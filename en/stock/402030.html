Public-sector AI revenue grows, but losses persist

Revenue rose for three straight years to KRW 34 billion in 2025, but operating losses have continued for four years and widened in the third quarter of 2026.

02

Business structure

Konan Technology Incorporation was founded in 1999 as an AI specialist built on text AI and vision AI technologies. Its main products are Konan LLM, a large language model; Konan Search, its established search solution; and vision AI tools for video analysis.

In the first three quarters of 2026, text AI accounted for 93.8% of cumulative revenue, while vision AI revenue fell to roughly KRW 0.98 billion from about KRW 2.13 billion a year earlier. LLM business revenue reached KRW 8.61 billion, or 54% of the total, and Konan Search contributed KRW 4.01 billion, or 25.2%.

LLM revenue rose from KRW 1.1 billion in 2024 to KRW 8.8 billion in 2025, shifting the revenue base away from chatbot and search products. Public institutions are the core customers, with public-sector clients generating 78.01% of 2025 revenue.

Notable projects include the Supreme Court's AI platform for trial support, the Ministry of Government Legislation's generative AI statute search, the Korea Gas Safety Corporation's company-wide generative AI service, and Korea Southern Power's AI deployment.

The company has recently extended its reach into manufacturing through an AX project with Seoyon E-Hwa. Competitively, it faces large IT services companies in the same sector for public AI contracts, and in March 2026 it formed a strategic partnership with NAVER Cloud on defense and manufacturing-focused LLMs.

Demand for chatbot and search products is being absorbed by LLM platforms, so revenue from legacy products continues to decline.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual results show revenue growth alongside persistent losses. Revenue rose from KRW 15.4 billion in 2022 to KRW 24.4 billion in 2023, up 58.7%, then to KRW 26.3 billion in 2024, up 7.7%, and to KRW 34 billion in 2025, up 29.1%, making 2025 the largest year in the 2022–2025 period.

The company described 2025 revenue as its highest since its founding. However, a meaningful part of the increase came from the one-time recognition of revenue tied to the end of multi-year projects.

One analysis points to the end of a smart talent management system project in August 2025, which led to roughly KRW 7.48 billion of revenue and related outsourcing costs being recognized at once.

The operating loss widened from KRW 4.04 billion in 2022 to KRW 11 billion in 2023 and KRW 14.1 billion in 2024, before narrowing to KRW 9.87 billion in 2025. The operating margin deteriorated from -26.3% in 2022 to -53.6% in 2024 and improved to -29.0% in 2025.

The net loss attributable to controlling shareholders narrowed to KRW 9.68 billion in 2025 from KRW 13.6 billion in 2024. Gross profit has stayed at around KRW 4.9 to 5.0 billion for three years, which suggests that revenue growth has not yet translated into better cost economics.

The debt-to-equity ratio fell to 54.0% at the end of 2025 from 126.2% in 2024, largely reflecting a rights offering of about KRW 18.8 billion in October 2025 rather than operating improvement.

Operating cash flow was negative in every year from 2022 to 2025, at -KRW 4.69 billion, -KRW 6.84 billion, -KRW 2.25 billion and -KRW 7.51 billion. On a quarterly basis, the third quarter of 2025 stood out with revenue of KRW 16.5 billion and an operating loss of KRW 1.55 billion.

The fourth quarter of 2025 turned to an operating profit of KRW 127 million on revenue of KRW 10.1 billion. Revenue then fell to KRW 3.88 billion in the first quarter of 2026, recovered to KRW 4.17 billion in the second quarter and reached KRW 7.9 billion in the third quarter.

The third-quarter operating loss of KRW 2.3 billion, however, was larger than the KRW 1.55 billion loss a year earlier. The net loss attributable to controlling shareholders over the last four quarters totals KRW 9.38 billion.

05

Industry analysis

Demand for public-sector generative AI is expanding under the government's AI transformation (AX) push. Reports indicate the government budgeted about KRW 10 trillion for AI and AX programs in 2026, roughly three times the prior year.

Adoption is spreading from central ministries and local governments to public power companies and medical institutions. The company said LLM demand expanded to medical institutions, local governments and public power companies after its 2024 contract with Korea Southern Power.

Public procurement, however, makes quarterly revenue sensitive to budget execution and acceptance timing, and the company attributed the third-quarter 2026 revenue decline partly to project tendering, construction schedules and revenue recognition timing.

Competition is intense because large IT services companies in the same sector also pursue major public projects. Konan Technology holds its own LLM, search and vision AI technologies and is focusing on building generative AI deployment references.

In defense, the company has co-developed an AI pilot with KAI under a defense innovation R&D project since 2024 and is taking part in a Ministry of National Defense AX sprint program.

Demand for legacy chatbot and search products is being absorbed by LLM platforms, so the speed of product transition will shape business durability. Market demand is growing, but the company faces both bidding competition and the need to convert its product base.

06

Outlook

Backlog stood at KRW 22.5 billion at the end of the first half of 2026 and at about KRW 18.9 billion at the end of the third quarter.

The third-quarter backlog includes a manufacturing AX project at Seoyon E-Hwa worth KRW 1.46 billion, a generative AI statute search system for the Ministry of Government Legislation worth KRW 1.27 billion, and an AI platform for Supreme Court trial support worth KRW 1.09 billion, which will be recognized as revenue as contracts are executed.

A March 2026 report said the company had set a target of returning to operating profit in the fourth quarter of 2026.

In its third-quarter 2026 quarterly report, the company said it will expand generative AI, AI agent and industry-specific AI businesses, and pursue productization and reuse of software assets to improve development efficiency and run its business with a focus on profitability.

Project milestones in 2026 included a statute search contract with the Ministry of Government Legislation in June, a company-wide generative AI service for the Korea Gas Safety Corporation in July, and the start of a manufacturing AX project at Seoyon E-Hwa in August.

The most direct near-term variable is a bidding restriction. The company disclosed on November 11, 2026 that it had received a bidding ban on contracts with the national government and local governments, decided on November 2, with a restriction period from November 23 to December 22, 2026.

The company plans to file for a suspension of enforcement and a cancellation lawsuit, and says that if the suspension is granted, its bidding eligibility will not be affected until a final ruling.

The actual financial impact will depend on the volume of tenders scheduled during the restriction period and on whether the suspension is granted. In the 2026 annual report, the next periodic filing, the fourth-quarter operating profit and the speed at which backlog converts into revenue are the key points to check.

08

Bull factors

Revenue mix shifting toward LLM

LLM revenue rose from KRW 1.1 billion in 2024 to KRW 8.8 billion in 2025, roughly eightfold. LLM accounted for 54% of cumulative revenue in the first three quarters of 2026, making it the core of the revenue mix.

Proponents point to new LLM deployments broadening the revenue base even as chatbot and search revenue declines.

Government AI budget and backlog

Reports indicate the government set the 2026 AI and AX budget at about KRW 10 trillion, roughly three times the prior year. Reported backlog was KRW 22.5 billion at the end of the first half of 2026 and about KRW 18.9 billion at the end of the third quarter. If contracts convert to revenue, the backlog could support quarterly results.

Cost improvement and narrower losses

According to a company announcement in August 2026, the operating loss for the first half of 2026 narrowed 19.3% year on year, and gross profit turned positive from a year-earlier loss. Cumulative operating loss for the first three quarters of 2026 was also 8.9% lower than a year earlier. Headcount reduction and other cost measures are continuing.

09

Bear factors

Public-sector dependence and bidding ban

Public-sector clients generated 78.01% of the KRW 34 billion in 2025 revenue, so any restriction on bidding feeds directly into results. The bidding ban decided on November 2, 2026 applies from November 23 to December 22. Its impact depends on the outcome of the suspension request, and the timing of that outcome has not been set.

Persistent losses and weaker quarters

The operating loss has persisted for four consecutive years since 2022, and the company has reported losses in each quarter from the first to the third quarter of 2026. Third-quarter 2026 revenue fell 52.0% year on year, and the operating loss widened from a year earlier.

The net loss attributable to controlling shareholders over the last four quarters totals KRW 9.38 billion, so revenue recovery has not yet fully translated into profit improvement.

Liquidity squeeze and governance pressure

Cash and cash equivalents fell from KRW 7.5 billion at the end of 2025 to KRW 3.6 billion at the end of the first half of 2026. Operating cash flow was negative in each year from 2022 to 2025 even after a rights offering of about KRW 18.8 billion in October 2025.

The CEO has pledged about 31.7% of his shares as collateral, and concerns about control weakening are being raised as a loan matures at year-end.

10

Risk factors

Policy and contract risk

Because the business depends on public contracts, a bidding restriction directly limits the company's ability to win new work. The company plans to seek suspension of the ban and file a cancellation lawsuit, and says bidding eligibility would be unaffected until a final ruling if suspension is granted.

Whether suspension is granted is uncertain, and the size of any revenue gap depends on the tender volume scheduled during the restriction.

Revenue recognition timing

Some analysts argue that part of the 2025 revenue increase came from one-time recognition when multi-year projects ended. The end of a smart talent management system project in August 2025 reportedly brought about KRW 7.48 billion of revenue into a single period.

The company attributed the third-quarter 2026 decline to project tendering, construction timing and revenue recognition timing, so quarterly results can swing with project schedules.

Cannibalization and financial burden

Revenue from legacy chatbot and search products is declining as demand moves to LLM platforms. A March 2026 report quoted the company as having no plans for additional rights offerings, but with cash balances falling, funding methods will remain a focus.

The improvement in the debt ratio owes more to the capital raise than to better operating profitability, which is worth keeping in view.

12

Overall view

Konan Technology Incorporation's 2025 revenue rose 29.1% to KRW 34 billion, and its revenue mix is shifting from chatbot and search products toward LLM. However, operating losses have continued for four consecutive years since 2022, and third-quarter 2026 revenue fell 52.0% while the operating loss widened.

Part of the 2025 revenue increase reflects one-time recognition tied to the end of multi-year projects, so the durability of quarterly results warrants separate attention.

Public-sector clients accounted for 78.01% of 2025 revenue, and a bidding restriction applies from November 23 to December 22, 2026, making the suspension outcome and any gap in tendering a near-term variable. Cash balances have fallen, and the CEO's pledge of shares adds financial and governance pressure.

Government AI budget growth and the backlog support the business base. The points to confirm are whether the targeted fourth-quarter 2026 operating profit is achieved and how quickly the backlog converts into revenue.
