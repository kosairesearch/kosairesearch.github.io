02

Business structure

BIO Port Korea (188040) is a food distribution specialist that generates sales across snacks, liquid tea, beverages and merchandise. Based on second-quarter 2026 sales, merchandise accounted for 32%, snacks 26%, beverages 22% and liquid tea 20%, while health functional foods were 1%.

Its flagship Yuja ginger tea is the core liquid tea item, and Korea Investment & Securities noted it generated KRW 13.6 billion in sales for the first three quarters, up 27% year on year.

The snack line includes sweet potato fries, shrimp chips and grilled squid snacks, and a snack product launched domestically in July 2025 was expanding into the United States and Greater China. The export share has climbed steadily, with exports rising from 22% in 2022 to 38% in 2023 and 49% in 2024.

Domestic and Costco sales across seven countries make up more than 70% of revenue, and the company has supplied Costco for about two decades. Its production capacity was roughly KRW 130 billion in 2024, split between snacks, liquid tea and beverages.

The company said it is internalizing its snack production lines and expanding OEM work to strengthen cost competitiveness. Snacks compete with large food makers such as Orion and Nongshim, but BIO Port's structure is built on overseas retail channel placement and export distribution.

04

Earnings analysis

On a reported basis, sales rose from KRW 68.7 billion in 2023 to KRW 73.3 billion in 2024, up 6.8% year on year, and then fell to KRW 70.9 billion in 2025, down 3.4%. Among 2023 to 2025, 2024 was the largest year and 2023 the smallest.

Operating profit was KRW 4.92 billion in 2023 and KRW 5.24 billion in 2024, up 6.5%, before falling to KRW 830 million in 2025, down 84.2%. The operating margin moved from 7.2% in 2023 and 7.1% in 2024 to 1.2% in 2025.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders went from KRW 4.11 billion in 2024, up 22.0%, to a KRW 1.4 billion loss in 2025, a swing into deficit. Total net income, including non-controlling interests, was also a KRW 1.4 billion loss in 2025.

Korea Investment & Securities attributed the 2025 weakness to tariff uncertainty, delivery delays tied to a labor audit at a key customer, and a worse product mix as sales of fried squid snacks declined. Fried squid sales fell 55% year on year to KRW 5.4 billion.

Quarterly results in 2026 moved between recovery and reversal. First-quarter 2026 sales were KRW 21.4 billion with operating profit of KRW 765 million, turning positive, and second-quarter sales rose to KRW 21.8 billion with operating profit of KRW 1.53 billion, up 99.5% from the prior quarter.

Third-quarter sales fell 11.1% to KRW 19.4 billion, with an operating loss of KRW 133 million and a net loss attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 745 million. Net income attributable to controlling shareholders over the last four quarters totaled KRW 1.76 billion.

The company attributed first-half 2026 profit growth to export expansion through global retail channels, portfolio diversification and lower fixed-cost burden. Given the wide swings in quarterly margins, fixed-cost absorption and product mix appear to be the key variables.

05

Industry analysis

Exports of Korean food products are tied to global retailers widening their interest in Korean ready meals and snack categories.

In a June 2025 report, Alphasquare pointed to global retailers focusing on Korean microwave-based meals and said category expansion could be a meaningful turning point for average selling price and customer reach. The same report also noted that valuation re-rating is under way among Korean food makers.

The company's investor relations material cites the spread of Korean content and a growing base of K-food consumers as the main drivers of export growth. In the cycle, the export tailwind coexists with external variables such as tariffs and Costco-related compliance.

In 2025, tariff issues, stricter labor compliance requirements at Costco and one-off listing costs were identified as factors that slowed growth.

Among peers, BIO Port is a smaller, export-heavy mid-cap food company compared with large makers such as Orion, Nongshim and Samyang Foods, and its sales scale and overseas channel structure differ substantially.

For that reason, the pace of its own export channel expansion and the stability of quarterly profit are more relevant comparison points than industry averages.

Outlook

The core of the 2026 outlook offered by the company and brokers is expansion through overseas retail channels. In a November 26, 2025 report, Korea Investment & Securities projected KRW 100 billion in 2026 sales, up 39%, and KRW 7 billion in operating profit, up 241%, with a 7.0% operating margin.

The same report named the China entry effect, wider liquid tea placements and new merchandise launches as the main growth drivers. In an August 2025 report, Hanwha Investment & Securities expected 2026 annual sales to exceed KRW 100 billion.

First-half 2026 sales were KRW 43.2 billion on a provisional basis, so reaching a KRW 100 billion annual level would require a sharp increase in the second half.

Third-quarter 2026 sales of KRW 19.4 billion and an operating loss of KRW 133 million make the gap between that outlook and current trends the main item to check.

The 2025 operating profit of KRW 830 million also came in below the KRW 2.1 billion estimate that Korea Investment & Securities had given, which is relevant when judging whether forecasts are met.

In its 2026 investor material, the company cited easing of external uncertainty such as tariffs, in-house snack lines with expanded OEM work and new product development as grounds for a turnaround. For the 2026 fiscal year, the target dividend payout ratio is 20 to 30 percent of standalone net income.

The company completed open-market purchases of 204,371 treasury shares, about 4.4% of total shares issued, and plans to cancel them in full. The next checkpoint is how much annual operating margin and Costco channel expansion appear in the 2026 annual report.

08

Bull factors

Expanding overseas retail channels

The export share reached 49% in 2024, and Yuja ginger tea sales rose 27% year on year to KRW 13.6 billion in the first three quarters of 2025. First-half 2026 sales were KRW 43.2 billion on a provisional basis, up 19.7% from the prior-year period. The China entry and wider liquid tea placements are cited as growth drivers.

Signs of profit recovery

Second-quarter 2026 operating profit was KRW 1.53 billion, up 99.5% from the prior quarter, and the first-half operating margin improved 3.3 percentage points to 5.3%. The company cited export growth, portfolio diversification and lower fixed-cost burden as drivers. Recovery from a 1.2% operating margin in 2025 is a positive signal.

Shareholder returns and insider buying

The target dividend payout ratio for the 2026 fiscal year is set at 20 to 30 percent of standalone net income. The company bought 204,371 treasury shares on the open market, about 4.4% of total shares issued, and plans to cancel them in full. The CEO and other senior executives also bought company shares with personal funds.

Bear factors

Volatile quarterly earnings

Third-quarter 2026 sales fell 11.1% from the prior quarter to KRW 19.4 billion, with an operating loss of KRW 133 million and a net loss attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 745 million. The second-quarter profit reversed in the third quarter, so the profitability has not been stable on a quarterly basis. Net income attributable to controlling shareholders over the last four quarters totaled KRW 1.76 billion.

Concentration in customers and channels

Domestic sales and Costco sales across seven countries account for more than 70% of revenue, so dependence on a single retail channel is high. In 2025, stricter labor compliance requirements at Costco caused delivery delays that weighed on results.

Because channel policy changes or order adjustments flow directly into revenue, this structure is a bearish factor.

History of lowered forecasts

At the listing, the 2025 sales target was KRW 95 billion with a 10% operating margin, and it was later cut to KRW 85 to 90 billion with a 7% margin. The 2025 operating profit of KRW 830 million fell short of the KRW 2.1 billion estimate from Korea Investment & Securities.

Reaching the KRW 100 billion sales forecast for 2026 requires a sharp second-half increase, so forecast delivery is an open test.

10

Risk factors

Tariff and trade risk

Changes in US tariff policy directly affect the cost base and delivery terms of an export-oriented business. Korea Investment & Securities identified tariff uncertainty, together with delivery delays, as a main reason 2025 results fell short. Even if the trade environment improves, margins may still vary depending on pricing adjustments by channel.

Cost and product mix risk

In the second quarter of 2025, rising logistics costs held the operating margin to 3.9%, and lower sales of higher-margin snacks changed the product mix. The decline in fried squid sales was also cited as a cause of mix deterioration. If launch delays or weak sales of higher-margin products recur, the operating margin could fall again.

Ownership concentration and float

The largest shareholder group holds around 40% of shares, which limits the number of freely traded shares. With a small market capitalization, thin trading volumes can amplify price swings. Treasury share cancellation and insider purchases are positive for shareholder value, but they do little to increase free float.

Overall view

BIO Port Korea restored first-half 2026 profitability through overseas channel expansion and cost structure improvements, and both operating profit and margin improved from the prior-year period.

However, the operating loss and net loss attributable to controlling shareholders in the third quarter of 2026 mean quarterly stability is not yet confirmed. The 2025 decline, with operating profit down 84.2% and a swing to loss at the net level, should be read alongside the recent recovery.

The structure in which customer concentration and tariffs directly affect results is a bearish factor that exists alongside the bullish signals. On shareholder returns, the dividend payout target, treasury share cancellation and insider buying are documented facts.

The share price trades below book value per share, while earnings-based measures remain sensitive to quarterly swings. The next checkpoints are the annual operating margin and the effect of channel expansion beyond Costco in the 2026 annual report, along with first-quarter 2027 results.
