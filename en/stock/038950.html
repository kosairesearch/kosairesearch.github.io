02

Business structure

Finedigital is a KOSDAQ-listed manufacturer founded in 1992 that develops and sells wired and wireless communication devices, including in-car dashcams, navigation units and golf distance measuring devices.

As of the end of 2025, the revenue mix was 76.57% from merchandise (navigation and dashcams), 20.81% from products (navigation and dashcams) and 2.61% from building leasing, so device sales account for most of the top line.

Domestic sales accounted for 91.4% and exports for 8.6% in the same period, indicating heavy reliance on the Korean market. Between August and October 2025 the company launched several new dashcam models across price tiers, and in November 2025 it supplied dashcams for a new model of a domestic automaker.

The company has said it is strengthening both premium and entry-level lineups to expand market share. R&D spending in 2025 was KRW 9.90 billion, equal to 15.75% of revenue. The cost of sales ratio was 68.39% in 2025, down from 71.83% in 2022.

On the demand side, growing awareness of vehicle safety and the spread of factory-fitted dashcams on new cars are cited as supporting factors. Competitive strength is tied to expanding installation on new vehicles and broadening the product range.

Earnings analysis

Annual revenue declined from KRW 91.1 billion in 2022 to KRW 87.7 billion in 2023 (down 3.7% year on year), KRW 70.8 billion in 2024 (down 19.3%) and KRW 62.9 billion in 2025 (down 11.2%). 2022 was the largest year and 2025 the smallest within the 2022-2025 period.

Operating results moved from an operating loss of KRW 3.7 billion in 2022 to a smaller loss of KRW 660 million in 2023, before widening to KRW 4.58 billion in both 2024 and 2025. The operating margin went from -4.1% in 2022 to -0.8% in 2023, -6.5% in 2024 and -7.3% in 2025.

Losses widened in the same years that revenue fell. Net income attributable to controlling shareholders was a loss of KRW 1.83 billion in 2022, a profit of KRW 2.13 billion in 2023 (a turn to profit), a loss of KRW 1.63 billion in 2024 and a loss of KRW 1.14 billion in 2025.

The 2023 profit arose despite an operating loss, which points to non-operating items playing a material role. Net income including non-controlling interests was a loss of KRW 2.11 billion in 2022, a profit of KRW 1.97 billion in 2023, a loss of KRW 1.59 billion in 2024 and a loss of KRW 1.22 billion in 2025.

Operating cash flow was -KRW 5.04 billion in 2022, KRW 1.97 billion in 2023, KRW 3.19 billion in 2024 and -KRW 5.87 billion in 2025.

Quarterly revenue was KRW 15.7 billion in Q3 2025, KRW 16.1 billion in Q4 2025 (up 2.7% quarter on quarter), KRW 14.7 billion in Q1 2026 (down 8.8%), KRW 13.5 billion in Q2 2026 (down 8.1%) and KRW 15.5 billion in Q3 2026 (up 15.1% quarter on quarter, down 1.0% year on year).

Operating profit of KRW 129 million in Q4 2025 was followed by operating losses of KRW 1.62 billion in Q1 2026, KRW 1.77 billion in Q2 2026 and KRW 1.32 billion in Q3 2026. Net income attributable to controlling shareholders totaled a loss of KRW 2.13 billion over the last four quarters. Whether the KRW 1.49 billion profit in Q4 2025 was one-off should be checked in the quarterly notes.

Industry analysis

Demand for dashcams is linked to rising awareness of vehicle safety and the growing practice of fitting them to new cars as standard, which supports a structural demand base.

Even so, Finedigital's revenue has declined since 2023, and Q3 2026 revenue fell 1.0% year on year, so a clear demand recovery has not yet appeared. About 91.4% of 2025 revenue came from Korea, which ties results closely to new-car sales and consumption at domestic automakers.

Most revenue comes from merchandise and product sales, so revenue moves with the product mix. The cost of sales ratio fell to 68.39% in 2025 from 2022, but the operating margin remained negative. R&D equal to 15.75% of revenue means the company must compete on both price and features.

Listed electronics peers such as Samsung Electro-Mechanics and LG Innotek focus on components like semiconductor parts and camera modules, so their customers and margin structures differ from those of a finished dashcam maker.

Direct comparison with sector averages is therefore less informative than tracking the company's own quarterly trend. On the cycle, the three-year revenue decline points to a stage where a bottom is still being tested.

Smaller quarterly losses and a recent profitable quarter

The Q3 2026 operating loss of KRW 1.32 billion was smaller than the KRW 1.77 billion loss in Q2 2026. Operating profit of KRW 129 million in Q4 2025 shows the business has briefly returned to profit. If quarterly losses keep narrowing, that would support a case for margin improvement.

Low leverage and a sizable equity base

Bear factors

Three straight years of revenue declines

Extended losses and a wider gap in 2026

Operating losses have continued for four straight years from 2022 through 2025, and the 2025 loss of KRW 4.58 billion matched 2024. The company has posted operating losses for three consecutive quarters since Q1 2026, and the Q3 2026 loss was larger than a year earlier. A longer loss period raises the risk of eroding equity.

Unstable cash generation

Operating cash flow swung from -KRW 5.04 billion in 2022 to KRW 1.97 billion in 2023, KRW 3.19 billion in 2024 and -KRW 5.87 billion in 2025. Cash outflow from operations in 2025 coincided with the operating loss. If cash flow does not improve alongside earnings, room for investment and dividends narrows.

Risk factors

Demand and domestic concentration

About 91.4% of 2025 revenue came from Korea, so the company is directly exposed to new-car sales and domestic consumption. Q3 2026 revenue was down year on year, which signals that demand has not clearly recovered. How much of the new automaker supply translates into sales is a variable.

Cost and R&D burden

The cost of sales ratio was 68.39% in 2025, and R&D spending equaled 15.75% of revenue. Even though the cost ratio fell, the fixed-type R&D outlay makes it hard for the operating margin to turn positive. The effects of product mix shifts and pricing on margins need to be tracked quarter by quarter.

Volatility from non-operating items and metric interpretation

In 2023 the company posted a profit attributable to controlling shareholders despite an operating loss, and the Q4 2025 profit attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 1.49 billion came alongside a small operating profit.

The gap between that figure and net income including non-controlling interests is also meaningful. Judging performance on operating profit alone can miss distortions from non-operating items.

Overall view

Finedigital is a manufacturer centered on dashcams and navigation units, and most of its 2025 revenue came from device sales. Revenue fell from KRW 91.1 billion in 2022 to KRW 62.9 billion in 2025, declining for three consecutive years, and operating losses have continued for four straight years from 2022.

Operating losses have persisted for three consecutive quarters from Q1 2026, and the Q3 2026 loss was larger than a year earlier. On the other side, Q3 2026 revenue rose from the prior quarter, and the year-on-year decline narrowed to 1.0%.

The balance sheet is lightly levered with a debt ratio of 8.8%, but operating cash flow turned to -KRW 5.87 billion in 2025. The price-to-book ratio sits low against historical year-end levels, while a price-to-earnings ratio cannot be calculated during the current loss period.

Bullish factors are the narrowing revenue decline, the smaller quarterly losses and the low debt burden. Bearish factors are the extended losses and the deterioration in cash flow.

The next checkpoints are full-year 2026 results and Q4 2026 operating results, and a return to a positive operating margin would be the most direct sign of a business recovery.
