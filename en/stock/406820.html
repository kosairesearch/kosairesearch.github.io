02

Business structure

Beautyskin, founded in 2012, is a Korean cosmetics company that absorbed JS Global in 2021, gaining a China distribution network and the Wonjin brand, before listing on KOSDAQ in July 2023.

Its business spans OEM/ODM manufacturing, in-house brands, and product distribution; as of March 2026, OEM/ODM accounted for the largest share of revenue at 44%, followed by health supplements at 32%, product distribution at 12%, food processing at 7%, and other activities at 5%.

Its flagship brands, Wonjin Effect and Dr. Wonjin, are dermo-cosmetic lines developed with input from dermatologists and originally targeted post-procedure skin care demand; the company also operates the URIID and Glow Loudey brands.

The distribution business acts as an agent bringing Korean and other cosmetics brands into China and Southeast Asia, and the company has historically been involved in the China market entry of brands such as It's Skin, AHC, and VT.

The OEM/ODM segment handles contract manufacturing for client brands as well as the company's own private-label production.

Beyond its core cosmetics business, Beautyskin has expanded into health supplements and food processing, taking equity stakes in companies such as Pessle (health supplements) and Circular Bio One, while also pursuing unrelated investments in seafood distribution, insect farming, and tea distribution, which has drawn market scrutiny over capital allocation focus.

In OEM/ODM, Beautyskin competes with larger contract manufacturers such as Cosmax, Kolmar Korea, and Cosmecca Korea, while in brand distribution it competes with companies possessing strong China and Southeast Asia networks such as Silicon 2 and C&C International. The largest shareholder is CEO Kim Jong-soo, who held a 43.25% stake as of the first-half 2026 report.

04

Earnings analysis

Beautyskin's annual revenue rose 37.3% from KRW 58.9 billion in 2022 to KRW 80.9 billion in 2023, stayed essentially flat at KRW 80.9 billion in 2024, and then fell 32.1% year over year to KRW 54.9 billion in 2025, marking two consecutive years of decline since the 2023 peak.

Operating income swung from a profit of KRW 4.05 billion in 2022 to an operating loss of KRW 6.08 billion in 2023, narrowed to a loss of KRW 1.21 billion in 2024, and then widened again to a loss of KRW 3.16 billion in 2025, resulting in three straight years of operating losses from 2023 through 2025.

Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders similarly flipped from a profit of KRW 3.36 billion in 2022 to a loss of KRW 8.64 billion in 2023, before the loss narrowed to KRW 5.06 billion in 2024 and further to KRW 1.73 billion in 2025.

Over the same period, the operating margin moved from 6.9% in 2022 to -7.5% in 2023, -1.5% in 2024, and -5.8% in 2025.

On a quarterly basis, revenue declined from KRW 14.5 billion in Q2 2025 to KRW 11.5 billion in Q3 (down 21.3% quarter over quarter) and KRW 10.2 billion in Q4 (down 10.7%), before rising to KRW 12.5 billion in Q1 2026 (up 22.4%) and KRW 17.5 billion in Q2 2026 (up 39.6% quarter over quarter and 20.1% year over year), marking two consecutive quarters of growth following the Q4 2025 trough.

Operating income turned positive at KRW 123 million in Q3 2025, swung back to a loss of KRW 779 million in Q4, continued in loss at KRW 1.48 billion in Q1 2026 with the loss widening, and then returned to profit at KRW 413 million in Q2 2026, a turnaround from both the prior quarter and the same quarter a year earlier.

Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders diverged from this operating recovery, swinging to a loss of KRW 2.03 billion in Q1 2026 and widening further to a loss of KRW 2.81 billion in Q2 2026 versus both the prior quarter and the year-earlier quarter, bringing the cumulative net loss over the trailing four quarters (Q3 2025 through Q2 2026) to KRW 2.94 billion.

Operating cash flow swung sharply year to year, from KRW 3.09 billion in 2022 to negative KRW 18.3 billion in 2023, KRW 8.89 billion in 2024, and KRW 610 million in 2025, indicating that cash generation from operations has yet to stabilize.

05

Industry analysis

Korea's cosmetics OEM/ODM industry broadly grew from 2021 through 2023, and Beautyskin was counted among companies posting double-digit multi-year growth during that period, but the picture diverged across companies from 2024 onward.

Global interest in K-beauty products has remained steady, but competition from low-priced local brands in China and rising influencer marketing costs are seen as having eroded the profitability of traditional distribution structures.

Beautyskin has used the Wonjin brand and China distribution network it gained through the JS Global merger to support the market entry of several Korean brands, but its own China business has underperformed expectations in recent years, prompting the recent distributor change.

Within the industry, large contract manufacturers such as Cosmax and Kolmar Korea maintain stable order bases built on scale and a broad roster of global clients, whereas smaller distribution-and-brand operators like Beautyskin show greater volatility due to higher dependence on specific regions and customers.

At the same time, the rise of overseas distribution platforms such as Silicon 2 has diversified the competitive landscape for K-beauty distribution beyond China.

Against this backdrop, Beautyskin's relatively small revenue scale, lower profitability, and diversification into non-core businesses together position it as comparatively less stable than larger industry peers.

06

Outlook

The most important variable for Beautyskin's future earnings path is the KRW 30 billion supply contract signed with GUANGZHOU QIANYU BRAND MANAGEMENT in China in September 2026.

The deal replaced the company's previous China distributor with a new local partner, with the contract value set based on a minimum purchase amount and a penalty clause if the counterparty fails to meet that minimum.

The payment structure calls for a 5% advance payment within 90 days of signing, followed by 50% of each order amount at order placement and the remaining 50% at shipment.

Because the contract runs through December 2029, the full KRW 30 billion will not be recognized as revenue at once; one estimate suggests that spreading the contract evenly over its term implies an annual supply volume equivalent to roughly 17% of 2025 revenue, with actual contribution depending on future order and delivery volumes.

In addition, in October 2026 the company signed a further KRW 5.0 billion cosmetics supply agreement with Wellmake and Laon Commerce, extending its distribution channel expansion.

Management has indicated it intends to focus on its core cosmetics business following the China distributor change, though with health supplements and food processing already accounting for a meaningful share of revenue, balancing diversification against core business recovery remains a challenge.

From July 2027, the KOSDAQ market-cap listing maintenance requirement is set to rise to KRW 30 billion, making the company's market capitalization trend a variable to watch in terms of continued listing as well.

Bull factors

Signs of a Return to Operating Profit

Operating profit turned positive at KRW 413 million in Q2 2026, a swing from both the prior quarter and the year-earlier quarter, following an earlier profitable quarter of KRW 123 million in Q3 2025.

Revenue has also grown for two consecutive quarters in Q1 and Q2 2026 following the Q4 2025 trough, signaling early stages of a top-line recovery.

However, net profit attributable to controlling shareholders has remained in loss over the same period, meaning the improvement at the operating level has yet to flow through to the bottom line.

China Distribution Network Overhaul

In September 2026 the company replaced its existing China distributor and signed a KRW 30 billion supply contract with GUANGZHOU QIANYU BRAND MANAGEMENT, marking a reset of its China operations.

The contract value equals 54.62% of 2025 consolidated revenue, and if orders and deliveries proceed as planned, it could serve as a platform for expanding China-bound sales.

Around the same time, the company also signed an additional KRW 5.0 billion contract with Wellmake and Laon Commerce, broadening its distribution channels.

Diversified Revenue Streams

As of March 2026, health supplements accounted for 32% of revenue, giving the company multiple revenue streams alongside its core OEM/ODM business at 44%. Amid continued weakness in the cosmetics business, these newer segments can partly supplement the revenue base. Whether this diversification strengthens the core business's competitiveness, however, remains to be confirmed.

Bear factors

Three Straight Years of Operating Losses

Operating income posted losses for three consecutive years from 2023 through 2025, with net profit attributable to controlling shareholders also in the red over the same period. 2025 revenue fell sharply from the 2023 peak to KRW 54.9 billion, reflecting both shrinking scale and weakening profitability simultaneously.

While operating profit turned positive in Q2 2026, the quarterly results have alternated between profit and loss repeatedly, making it premature to call this a confirmed trend reversal.

Growing Balance Sheet Strain

The debt-to-equity ratio has risen steadily from 99.4% in 2022 to 168.8% in 2025, and total liabilities of KRW 55.1 billion at the end of 2025 substantially exceeded total equity of KRW 32.6 billion.

Operating cash flow has also been highly volatile year to year after a sharp outflow of negative KRW 18.3 billion in 2023, and has not settled into a stable generating pattern. Against this backdrop, the exercise of convertible bond conversion rights could add further share dilution risk.

Expansion into Non-Core Investments

Amid continued weakness in its core cosmetics business, the company has been noted for expanding into areas with no direct link to cosmetics beyond health supplements and food processing, including seafood distribution, insect farming, and tea distribution.

There is market concern over continuing such unrelated investments funded by external capital while the balance sheet is deteriorating. Whether these new ventures will become a genuine profit base remains uncertain.

Risk factors

Earnings Volatility

Net losses attributable to controlling shareholders continued for two consecutive quarters in Q1 and Q2 2026, with a persistent gap between operating-level recovery and bottom-line results.

If actual revenue recognition from the new China supply contract lags the implied schedule, the anticipated top-line recovery could be delayed. The repeated swings between quarterly profit and loss also add uncertainty to any trend projection.

Financial and Dilution Risk

With the debt-to-equity ratio rising from 99.4% in 2022 to 168.8% in 2025, the conversion right on the 7th convertible bond is scheduled to open on December 26, 2026, raising the possibility of further share dilution.

If balance sheet improvement remains slow amid ongoing interest expense pressure, financing conditions could deteriorate further. A past large-scale bonus share issuance that significantly increased shares outstanding has also been a dilutive factor for per-share metrics.

Listing Maintenance and Volatility Risk

The KOSDAQ market-cap listing maintenance requirement is set to rise to KRW 30 billion from July 2027, meaning that given the current market capitalization level, a share price decline could increase the burden of maintaining the listing.

The stock has also been designated for investor caution in the past following a sharp short-term price surge, and it is classified as relatively volatile in terms of trading flows. A shareholder base with a high proportion of small individual holders is another factor contributing to volatility.

Overall view

Beautyskin has seen revenue decline for two consecutive years since its 2023 peak and has posted operating losses for three straight years from 2023 to 2025, yet signs of a possible bottoming are emerging with the Q2 2026 return to operating profit and two consecutive quarters of revenue growth.

At the same time, net profit attributable to controlling shareholders has remained in loss for both Q1 and Q2 2026, showing that the operating-level improvement has not yet fully carried through to the bottom line.

The KRW 30 billion China supply contract signed in September 2026, which came alongside a distributor change, represents a structural shift, and given its multi-year term, the pace of actual revenue conversion will be key.

At the same time, a rising debt ratio, potential convertible bond dilution, expansion into investments unrelated to the core business, and a tightening KOSDAQ market-cap listing requirement remain balance sheet and governance-related concerns.

Overall, the company is in a phase where early operating improvements coexist with financial and diversification-related uncertainties, making it worthwhile to track coming quarterly results, the revenue contribution from the China contract, and how the convertible bond situation unfolds.
