02

Business structure

BeautySkin is a comprehensive cosmetics company spanning contract manufacturing (OEM/ODM), its own brands, and product distribution, and listed on KOSDAQ in July 2023.

According to corporate data as of March 2026, its revenue mix was roughly 44% OEM/ODM, 32% health supplements, 12% product distribution, 7% food processing, and 5% other, meaning health-supplement and food-processing operations carry meaningful weight alongside cosmetics manufacturing.

The company absorbed JS Global in a 2021 merger, and in 2026 it acquired a stake in health-supplement maker Fessle, bringing its consolidated subsidiary count to five.

On the production side, the company holds annual OEM/ODM capacity of 59 million mask-pack units and 54 million basic-cosmetics units, leaving room for utilization gains if order volumes expand.

Headcount stands at around 126 employees, and founder-CEO Kim Jong-su holds a concentrated 43.25% stake (6,113,920 shares) as the largest shareholder. Minority shareholders number 6,917 but hold only 1,266,979 shares, meaning the free float itself is relatively limited for a small-cap name.

In the broader cosmetics OEM/ODM industry, top-tier players Cosmax, Kolmar Korea, and Cosmecca Korea maintain dominant market share, leaving BeautySkin positioned as a smaller player structurally dependent on specific brand and regional orders.

In January 2026 the company carried out a bonus share issue of roughly 10.6 million common shares, substantially expanding shares outstanding.

04

Earnings analysis

On an annual basis, revenue was about KRW 58.9 billion with operating profit of roughly KRW 4.0 billion (6.9% operating margin) in 2022, but 2023 revenue grew 37% to about KRW 80.9 billion even as the company swung to an operating loss of roughly KRW 6.1 billion (-7.5% margin).

In 2024, revenue was roughly flat at about KRW 80.9 billion while the operating loss narrowed to about KRW 1.2 billion (-1.5% margin), and in 2025 revenue dropped 32% to about KRW 54.9 billion as the operating loss widened again to about KRW 3.2 billion (-5.8% margin).

On an owners' net-income basis, the company moved from a roughly KRW 3.4 billion profit in 2022 to losses of about KRW 8.6 billion in 2023 and KRW 5.1 billion in 2024, before the loss narrowed to about KRW 1.7 billion in 2025 — a pattern driven more by swings in non-operating items than by the operating line itself.

Quarterly, Q3 2025 briefly turned operating-profitable with about KRW 11.5 billion in revenue and roughly KRW 0.1 billion in operating profit, while Q4 2025 saw revenue slip to about KRW 10.2 billion and an operating loss of about KRW 0.8 billion, yet owners' net income swung to a roughly KRW 2.1 billion profit, highlighting the outsized role of non-operating factors.

In 2026, Q1 was weak with revenue of about KRW 12.5 billion, an operating loss of roughly KRW 1.5 billion, and a net loss of about KRW 2.0 billion, before Q2 revenue recovered to about KRW 17.5 billion with operating profit turning positive at roughly KRW 0.4 billion — even as the owners' net loss widened further to about KRW 2.8 billion.

Over the most recent four quarters (Q3 2025 through Q2 2026), the combined owners' net loss totaled roughly KRW 2.9 billion, with continued divergence between operating and net results amid high quarterly volatility.

On the cash-flow side, operating cash flow deteriorated sharply to about negative KRW 18.3 billion in 2023, recovered to about positive KRW 8.9 billion in 2024, then slowed again to roughly KRW 0.6 billion in 2025 — suggesting the cash underpinnings of any profitability recovery remain unsettled.

Industry analysis

South Korea's cosmetics OEM/ODM industry has continued to grow overall, buoyed by strong K-beauty export momentum. According to analysis by Cosin Korea, combined revenue at 81 domestic cosmetics OEM/ODM companies rose 11.8% year over year in 2025.

Over the same period, top-tier players Cosmax, Kolmar Korea, and Cosmecca Korea maintained dominant market share, pointing to deepening polarization within the industry.

On the export side, mask-pack exports maintained solid growth as of August 2026, with export value growth outpacing volume growth, signaling a premiumization trend.

Looking at the broader listed cosmetics sector, Q1 2026 consolidated revenue rose 11.5% year over year to about KRW 728 billion and operating profit rose 31.6% to about KRW 78.9 billion, indicating a generally healthy industry backdrop.

However, BeautySkin's shares saw a relatively sharp pullback during that period, which the industry attributed to volatility concentrated in small and mid-cap names facing short-term rally fatigue and earnings uncertainty.

In other words, sector-wide export expansion and earnings improvement coexisted with a 32% drop in BeautySkin's own revenue, suggesting company-specific order and customer-mix changes are driving results more than the broader cycle.

For a small to mid-sized OEM/ODM player, securing new overseas accounts remains the key variable in translating industry tailwinds into actual results.

06

Outlook

The most notable near-term variable is the KRW 30 billion cosmetics supply contract signed in September 2026 with China's GUANGZHOU QIANYU BRAND MANAGEMENT.

The contract covers China excluding Taiwan and runs from September 15, 2026 through December 31, 2029, with a 5% advance payment due within 90 days of signing and subsequent payments split 50% at order placement and 50% at shipment on a per-order basis.

This single contract equals 54.6% of trailing revenue, making the pace of actual order execution the key determinant of how quickly it shows up in results.

Around the same time, in October 2026, the company also signed an additional KRW 5 billion cosmetics supply contract (9.1% of revenue) with Welmake and Raon Commerce, indicating simultaneous order wins across both domestic and overseas channels.

With health-supplement maker Fessle brought in as a subsidiary during 2026, how much of a diversification effect from outside cosmetics shows up in coming quarters is also worth watching.

That said, given the structural revenue decline that persisted through 2025 and the first half of 2026, whether the new contracts can fully offset the prior revenue gap needs to be confirmed sequentially through quarterly disclosures.

On the financial side, the call option exercise window on the KRW 10 billion 6th-series convertible bond is set to expire in October 2026, and whether the company exercises or forfeits that option will determine whether bondholders can subsequently exercise conversion rights.

Conversion requests on the 7th-series convertible bond are scheduled to open on December 26, 2026, leaving potential share supply as a variable that could affect trading dynamics in the following quarter.

Bull factors

Revenue diversification via a large China contract

The 30 billion KRW supply contract with a Chinese partner signed in September 2026 is a large-scale order equivalent to 54.6% of recent revenue, with a contract period set long through 2029.

As the structure includes advance payments and staged payments by order, there is a possibility that as execution gets into full swing, it will be gradually reflected in quarterly revenue.

While high dependence on a single customer is conversely a risk factor, securing a new overseas channel itself could become a foundation for an earnings turnaround.

Operating profit has turned positive in some recent quarters

Operating profit turned positive in Q3 2025 and Q2 2026, at approximately 100 million KRW and approximately 400 million KRW respectively, signaling improvement in the cost structure.

The fact that there were periods of improved gross margin even amid declining revenue shows that cost and operational efficiency measures are partially working. However, as losses and profits have alternated quarter to quarter, further confirmation is needed regarding sustainability.

Idle capacity and business diversification

With OEM/ODM production capacity of 59 million mask packs and 54 million basic cosmetics units annually, there is room for profitability improvement through higher utilization rates if orders increase.

Portfolio restructuring is also underway to reduce dependence on the single cosmetics business, as health supplement company Fescle is being incorporated as a subsidiary in 2026.

Bear factors

Revenue has contracted for a fourth straight year

Revenue, which was about 80.9 billion KRW in 2023, remained at a similar level in 2024 before sharply declining by 32% to about 54.9 billion KRW in 2025, and Q1 2026 revenue also remained below the same period of the prior year.

In a phase where the revenue base itself is shrinking, there may be a time lag before the effects of new contracts become visible.

Widening gap between operating and net results

While Q2 2026 operating profit was positive (about 400 million KRW), net loss attributable to controlling shareholders rather expanded to about 2.8 billion KRW, and the combined net loss attributable to controlling shareholders over the trailing four quarters reached about 2.9 billion KRW.

This means that volatility in non-operating gains and losses is driving actual results, and with operating cash flow also slowing to about 600 million KRW in 2025, the qualitative foundation for a recovery in profitability is not yet clear.

Rising leverage and mezzanine supply overhang

The debt ratio rose markedly from 99.4% in 2022 to 168.8% in 2025.

In addition, the issuer call option on the 100 billion KRW 6th series convertible bonds is set to expire in October 2026, and conversion requests for the 7th series convertible bonds become possible from December 26, 2026, raising concerns about equity dilution due to potential share issuance.

Risk factors

Customer concentration risk

The contract with China's GUANGZHOU QIANYU accounts for 54.6% of recent revenue, indicating very high dependence on a single overseas customer.

If that customer's order plans change or the distribution and consumption environment in China deteriorates, a gap could emerge between the contracted amount and actual execution.

Exchange rate fluctuations and changes in the trade environment are also variables that must be managed more closely as the share of overseas revenue grows.

Mezzanine and overhang risk

The 6th series convertible bonds have a structure in which the issuer's call option takes precedence over the conversion request right, so the future supply-demand dynamics could vary depending on whether the call option is exercised.

Following the adjustment of the conversion price due to the free rights offering in January 2026, conversion requests for the 7th series convertible bonds are scheduled to begin from late December, and concerns over equity dilution from potential new share issuance persist.

Financial soundness risk

The debt ratio has risen for three consecutive years, and operating cash flow also slowed to about 600 million KRW in 2025, indicating that internal cash-generating capacity is not yet stable.

Some financial data providers have also presented a low Altman Z-Score, a measure of insolvency risk, making reliance on external financing and interest burden management ongoing variables to watch.

Overall view

BeautySkin finds itself seeking a potential inflection point via a large September 2026 China supply contract, against a backdrop of sharply lower revenue from 2023 through 2025 and quarter-to-quarter swings in profitability.

Operating profit has turned positive in some quarters, but owners' net income remains driven by non-operating factors and has widened into deeper losses in other quarters, meaning the gap between operating performance and bottom-line results has not yet closed.

Financially, a rising debt ratio coincides with an overlapping schedule of convertible-bond call-option expiry and conversion-request openings, adding capital-structure variables that need to be monitored alongside the business.

The broader industry is benefiting from expanding K-beauty exports, but BeautySkin's own results have diverged from that trend, suggesting company-specific order and customer dynamics matter more than the sector cycle.

The actual pace of execution on the China contract, together with the call-option and conversion-request timeline, is likely to be the key variable shaping both earnings and share supply-demand over the coming months.

Any assessment would benefit from sequentially tracking upcoming quarterly disclosures and contract execution rather than relying on a single data point.
