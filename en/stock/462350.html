Innospace: Launch Cadence Gates the Earnings Turn

Innospace's 2025 revenue rose to KRW 2.75 billion, but its operating loss widened to KRW 72.2 billion, so a narrowing of losses still depends on launch success and launch cadence that have not yet been proven.

02

Business structure

Innospace is a private small-launch company that places small satellites into low Earth orbit; it is headquartered in Sejong and led by CEO Kim Soo-jong.

Its main vehicle, HANBIT-Nano, is a two-stage small launch vehicle that uses a hybrid propulsion design combining solid fuel with a liquid oxidizer, and its stated goal is to commercialize launch services. The company emphasizes that this vehicle is not subject to the U.S.

International Traffic in Arms Regulations (ITAR), and presents that as a differentiator when selling to overseas satellite customers.

In October 2025 the company said the Korea AeroSpace Administration granted it a commercial launch license, the first of its kind for a domestic private firm, and in December 2025 it attempted the first commercial launch from the Alcântara Space Center in Brazil. That launch lifted off but did not reach orbit.

Its customer base consists of launch service contracts with five customers in Brazil, India and Korea, and the mission was scheduled to carry eight regular payloads, including customer satellites.

The suborbital rocket SEBIT is a single-stage vehicle designed to carry payloads to altitudes below 100 km and uses a 4-tonne-class hybrid engine.

SEBIT was developed to test technologies in high-altitude and microgravity conditions, and the company plans to use it to build a commercial base for suborbital testing and verification services. In April 2026 reports said the company signed a 2028 satellite launch contract with Japan's Lusix.

In November 2026 launch services were listed by the Ministry of Trade, Industry and Energy as a trade-offset item, the first time a launch service has been included on that list. The business structure reads as one in which the number of launches, the number of contracts and payload scale drive revenue.

04

Earnings analysis

On a filed annual basis, revenue went from KRW 342 million in 2022 to KRW 231 million in 2023 (down 32.4% year on year), fell to KRW 14.71 million in 2024 (down 93.6%), and then rose to KRW 2.75 billion in 2025 (up 18,573.4%), making 2025 the largest year and 2024 the smallest of 2022 to 2025.

Operating losses were KRW 25.8 billion in 2022, KRW 15.9 billion in 2023, KRW 32.9 billion in 2024 and KRW 72.2 billion in 2025, and the company recorded losses in all four years, which is four consecutive years of losses from 2022 to 2025.

The 2025 operating loss of KRW 72.2 billion widened from the prior year, so the higher revenue did not translate into a smaller loss.

The operating margin was -7,546.0% in 2022, -6,883.1% in 2023, -223,717.9% in 2024 and -2,630.5% in 2025, and because revenue was extremely small in some years, the ratio is of limited use for comparisons across years.

Controlling-shareholder net losses were KRW 48.3 billion in 2022, KRW 83.2 billion in 2023, KRW 33.3 billion in 2024 and KRW 75.1 billion in 2025, and the net loss including non-controlling interests is filed at the same amounts.

Operating cash flow was -KRW 23.3 billion in 2022, -KRW 13.3 billion in 2023, -KRW 33 billion in 2024 and -KRW 51.2 billion in 2025, an outflow in all four years with the largest outflow in 2025.

At the end of 2025 total equity was KRW 52 billion, all of it attributable to controlling shareholders, and total liabilities were KRW 18 billion, giving a debt ratio of 34.7%, up from 21.3% in 2023 and 26.5% in 2024. The 2022 debt ratio is shown as a negative value and is excluded from simple comparison.

Quarterly revenue was KRW 1.09 billion in Q3 2025, KRW 960 million in Q4 2025 (down 12.3% quarter on quarter), KRW 1.4 billion in Q1 2026 (up 45.6%), KRW 2.47 billion in Q2 2026 (up 76.9%) and KRW 1.35 billion in Q3 2026 (down 45.3% quarter on quarter, up 23.6% year on year), and Q2 2026 was the largest of the last five quarters on a filed basis.

Quarterly operating losses were KRW 18.6 billion in Q3 2025, KRW 23 billion in Q4 2025, KRW 12.4 billion in Q1 2026, KRW 15.8 billion in Q2 2026 and KRW 21.5 billion in Q3 2026, with losses in five consecutive quarters and a wider loss in Q3 2026 than a year earlier.

Controlling-shareholder net losses over the last four quarters (Q4 2025 to Q3 2026) totaled KRW 75.4 billion, ranging from KRW 12.5 billion to KRW 25.6 billion per quarter.

Revenue swings sharply from quarter to quarter, while operating losses of KRW 12.4 billion to KRW 23 billion recur steadily, which reads as a cost base that does not move with revenue.

05

Industry analysis

One aerospace specialist said that as of 2026 the supply of rockets falls far short of demand for small satellites. A market is also forming for small launch vehicles that can launch on demand rather than fitting satellites into the schedules of large rockets.

Constellation deployments, including large satellite networks, are cited as a factor supporting demand for small launches. However, the market is still at the stage of building a commercial launch track record.

There have been no orbital deliveries since the first commercial launch in December 2025, and reports in March 2026 said a vehicle's mission was halted after a combustion chamber rupture 33 seconds after liftoff.

In the domestic supply chain, the company adopted the satellite dispenser technology of the supplier Space Bay, which previously flew a 6U-class small satellite dispenser on the fourth Nuri launch in 2025.

On the technology side, development of the core technology for LiMEK-04, a 0.4-tonne-thrust liquid methane engine intended for the upper stage of the next vehicle HANBIT-Micro, has been completed.

A June 2024 brokerage report assumed ten launches a year and a 2026 normalization of the business, but repeated delays and mission halts have moved actual launches away from that assumption.

Taken together, the company holds the first commercial launch license granted to a domestic private firm, by the company's own account, while its launch record in orbit is still being built.

06

Outlook

The nearest checkpoint is the follow-on test flight of SEBIT. On November 9, 2026, the company said it set a launch window from November 16 to December 11, 2026 (Brazil local time), with a target launch date of November 29.

After its first test flight, SEBIT had its engine thrust raised from 3 tonnes to 4 tonnes and its flight control algorithm improved, so whether the improved performance is confirmed in this flight is the starting point for follow-on commercialization.

The final report from Brazil's aviation accident investigation and prevention center on the first test flight was made public on November 5, 2026, and the official investigation was closed after approval by the Brazilian Air Force.

HANBIT-Nano's launch was originally targeted for Q3 2026, moved to November, and has now been pushed again to the first half of 2027.

The reasons given for the earlier shift to November were manufacturing and verification schedules reflecting the investigation results, the review process for launch permits at the Korea AeroSpace Administration, and local launch operations schedules.

CEO Kim Soo-jong has said that because launch involves many variables, adjustments to schedules and operating plans are possible. The suborbital testing and verification service is intended to build flight data and operating experience as a basis for commercialization.

The satellite launch contract with Japan's Lusix, reported in April 2026, has a 2028 implementation timeline. The 2026 annual business report, the next periodic filing, will confirm full-year 2026 revenue and operating loss on a filed basis.

08

Bull factors

A regulatory differentiator

HANBIT-Nano is presented as a launch vehicle that is not subject to the U.S. International Traffic in Arms Regulations (ITAR).

In November 2026 launch services were listed as a trade-offset item by the Ministry of Trade, Industry and Energy, and the company said this opens a path for use as an item of intergovernmental industrial cooperation. For overseas satellite customers weighing ITAR constraints, this feature could be emphasized in sales.

Accumulating flight data with SEBIT

After its first test flight, SEBIT's engine thrust was raised from 3 tonnes to 4 tonnes, and the final investigation report on the first flight was made public, closing the official investigation.

If the suborbital testing and verification service is commercialized, flight data and operating experience will accumulate across follow-on flights. The company explains that this process forms the basis for orbital launch capability.

An order pipeline

The company has launch service contracts with five customers in Brazil, India and Korea, and in April 2026 a 2028 satellite launch contract with Japan's Lusix was reported. An expert view that demand for small launch vehicles is rising while launch supply is short supports this demand trend. If these contracts lead to actual launches, the revenue structure could change.

Bear factors

Repeated launch delays

The first commercial launch in December 2025 did not reach orbit after liftoff, and the follow-on HANBIT-Nano launch moved from Q3 to November and then to the first half of 2027. Reports in March 2026 said a vehicle's mission was halted after a combustion chamber rupture 33 seconds after liftoff. Because launches generate revenue, schedule slippage translates into uncertainty about when earnings can turn.

Loss structure and cash outflow

The 2025 operating loss was KRW 72.2 billion, and operating cash flow was -KRW 51.2 billion, an outflow in all four years. Controlling-shareholder net losses over the last four quarters totaled KRW 75.4 billion, and the Q3 2026 operating loss of KRW 21.5 billion was wider than a year earlier.

The fact that losses and cash outflows did not shrink even in a year of higher revenue is cited as a bearish factor.

Revenue volatility and funding

Revenue fell to KRW 14.71 million in 2024 before rebounding to KRW 2.75 billion in 2025, but quarterly revenue dropped 45.3% from KRW 2.47 billion in Q2 2026 to KRW 1.35 billion in Q3 2026.

The post-IPO investment in April 2026 and the capital increase history in June and July 2026 show that funding has been continuing. If funding continues, dilution of shareholder stakes could increase.

Risk factors

Launch technology and reliability

In the first commercial launch in December 2025 the vehicle did not reach orbit, and during preparations for that launch, avionics signal anomalies, a cooling unit fault and valve operation problems were reported in succession.

Mission performance and operational stability across each flight phase must be verified in subsequent flights. If technical verification is delayed, commercial launch schedules and fulfillment of customer contracts could slip together.

Schedule and licensing

HANBIT-Nano's launch was first delayed by two to three months due to manufacturing and verification schedules reflecting the investigation results, the launch permit review at the Korea AeroSpace Administration and local operations in Brazil, and has since been pushed to the first half of 2027.

The transition of the operating system at the Alcântara Space Center was also cited as a variable in launch slot planning. Schedule variables keep shifting the timing of any earnings forecast.

Financial and funding

If funding continues amid operating losses and cash outflows, dilution of shareholder stakes could increase. The post-IPO investment in April 2026 and the capital increase history in June and July 2026 reflect this pattern.

If operating losses continue to grow relative to total equity of KRW 52 billion, financial headroom could narrow.

Overall view

Innospace has recorded operating losses every year from 2022 to 2025, and its 2025 revenue of KRW 2.75 billion was the largest of those years. However, the 2025 operating loss widened to KRW 72.2 billion, and operating cash outflow grew to -KRW 51.2 billion.

On a quarterly basis, Q2 2026 revenue of KRW 2.47 billion was the largest of the last five quarters, but Q3 2026 revenue fell to KRW 1.35 billion and the operating loss was KRW 21.5 billion.

The business depends on launch success and launch cadence, and the first commercial launch in December 2025 did not reach orbit, while the follow-on HANBIT-Nano launch has been pushed to the first half of 2027.

Positive factors include the commercial launch license for a domestic private firm, overseas customer contracts, selection of launch services as a trade-offset item, and the engine thrust upgrade on SEBIT.

On the other side, controlling-shareholder net losses totaled KRW 75.4 billion over the last four quarters, and continued capital raising, including the April 2026 post-IPO investment, is a bearish factor.

The next checkpoints are the SEBIT follow-on test flight targeted for November 29, 2026 and full-year results in the 2026 annual business report.
