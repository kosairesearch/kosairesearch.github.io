Report overview

A Holding Company Between Book Value and Subsidiary Dividends

Nongshim Holdings is a pure holding company whose core affiliates, Nongshim and a 31.94%-owned listed affiliate, reach its books only through equity-method accounting and dividends. Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders fell sharply in the third quarter of 2026, and the gap between book value and share price remains a key issue.

02

Business structure

Nongshim Holdings was founded in 2003 through a spin-off of the investment business of Nongshim and operates as a pure holding company focused on controlling and investing in subsidiaries and providing management support.

As of the end of June 2025, its listed subsidiaries were Nongshim (32.72% stake) and Yulchon Chemical (31.94% stake), and it also holds unlisted affiliates including Nongshim Taekyung, Nongshim Development and Nongshim Engineering.

Corporate monitoring data groups the consolidated business into holding investment, manufacturing, engineering and leisure segments.

The main operating subsidiary, Nongshim, makes and sells instant noodles, snacks and beverages, and launched Shin Ramyun Gold and Shin Ramyun Rose to mark the 40th anniversary of Shin Ramyun.

Nongshim and Yulchon Chemical are equity-method affiliates rather than consolidated subsidiaries, so their revenue and operating profit are not added to the holding company's consolidated income statement.

Instead, affiliate earnings build up as book income in proportion to ownership, and cash arrives only through affiliate dividends.

Seu, a maker of soy sauce and seasonings that counts Nongshim as a major customer, has been brought in as a wholly owned subsidiary, and the company plans a structure in which Seu handles primary processing and Nongshim Taekyung handles secondary processing.

Nongshim's overseas business is led by its US unit, which has expanded sales through channels such as Costco and Walmart, and overseas sales accounted for 39.4% of Nongshim's consolidated revenue in the first three quarters of 2026.

04

Earnings analysis

Revenue rose from KRW 674.6 billion in 2022 to KRW 769.5 billion in 2023 (up 14.1% year on year), KRW 798.3 billion in 2024 (up 3.7%) and KRW 858.8 billion in 2025 (up 7.6%), and it increased for three consecutive years from 2023 through 2025. Within 2022 to 2025, 2025 was the largest year by revenue.

Operating profit rose from KRW 52.8 billion in 2022 to KRW 75.3 billion in 2023 (up 42.6%) and KRW 86.6 billion in 2024 (up 15.0%), then fell to KRW 81.1 billion in 2025 (down 6.4%), with 2024 the largest year for operating profit.

The operating margin moved from 7.8% in 2022 to 9.8% in 2023 and 10.9% in 2024, before easing to 9.4% in 2025.

Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders rose from KRW 34.1 billion in 2022 to KRW 67.1 billion in 2023 (up 96.9%), KRW 74.8 billion in 2024 (up 11.3%) and KRW 114.6 billion in 2025 (up 53.3%), increasing for three consecutive years from 2023 through 2025.

In 2025 operating profit fell while attributable net profit rose by 53.3%, and press coverage has pointed out that much of the profit is equity-method income rather than cash.

Net profit including non-controlling interests was KRW 34.2 billion in 2022, KRW 67.3 billion in 2023, KRW 74.9 billion in 2024 and KRW 114.6 billion in 2025, a gap from attributable profit that was small in each year.

Operating cash flow was KRW 9.74 billion in 2022, -KRW 16.7 billion in 2023, KRW 55.2 billion in 2024 and KRW 24.7 billion in 2025, with large swings year to year. Total liabilities at the end of 2025 were KRW 515.8 billion and the debt ratio rose to 39.0% from 32.4% in 2024, while total equity was KRW 1.32 trillion.

Quarterly operating profit fell from KRW 22.8 billion in the third quarter of 2025 to KRW 14.6 billion in the fourth quarter (down 36.1% from the prior quarter), recovered to KRW 30.9 billion in the first quarter of 2026 (up 111.9%) and KRW 34.6 billion in the second quarter (up 12.0%), and then dropped to KRW 18 billion in the third quarter of 2026 (down 48.0% from the prior quarter and down 21.1% year on year).

Third-quarter 2026 revenue was KRW 267.5 billion, up 0.5% year on year, but net profit attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 12.5 billion, down 80.8%. According to corporate monitoring data, consolidated revenue in the first half of 2026 rose 42.4% year on year and operating profit rose 49.8%.

05

Industry analysis

Holding company earnings are linked to the operating environment of Nongshim through equity-method accounting and dividends, so food-sector demand and cost trends flow into book income.

In its preliminary third-quarter 2026 results released on November 13, 2026, Nongshim reported consolidated revenue of KRW 929.6 billion and operating profit of KRW 49.7 billion, with operating profit down 8.6% year on year.

The company attributed the pressure to higher prices for raw materials such as palm oil, along with rising shipping and energy costs for exports. It also described a domestic market squeezed by slowing consumption and intensifying competition.

In the same preliminary results, sales to outside customers from its domestic units rose 4.6% to KRW 633.2 billion, and US unit revenue grew 14.3% to KRW 139.9 billion.

Higher exchange rates and oil prices pushed up packaging and other material costs, and Nongshim's standalone operating profit in the second quarter of 2026 fell 32.0% from the first quarter.

Whether the group can defend margins through price increases and new products while raw material costs rise is a key point across the food sector.

On the holding discount, a merger with Nongshim is cited as the most direct way to remove it, but analysts argue that a merger at market prices would reduce the chairman's effective stake, which weakens the incentive.

06

Outlook

The next periodic report for Nongshim Holdings is the 2026 annual report, which will also disclose full-year results and the year-end dividend. The company has never disclosed a corporate value-up plan of its own, while Nongshim announced its first such plan in May 2025 and a new one in July 2026.

Funds for the Seu acquisition were raised through corporate bonds and short-term borrowings, and the standalone capital structure shifted to a net-borrowing position.

A credit rating agency, in a report covered by the press, expected that once Seu's earnings normalize, borrowings would be gradually reduced using dividend income. Nongshim is reported to be pushing ahead with large investments, including a logistics center in Ulsan and an export-only plant in Busan.

For affiliate dividends to rise, Nongshim's payout ratio would need to increase, and by one press estimate a 10-percentage-point increase would add only about KRW 5 billion a year in dividends to the holding company.

The next checkpoint is fourth-quarter 2026 operating profit measured against the KRW 14.6 billion posted in the fourth quarter of 2025. Whether the in-house processing benefits from the Seu integration show up in operating profit is another item to confirm in upcoming disclosures.

Whether Nongshim's overseas growth continues in the second half and domestic cost pressure eases will shape the direction of both affiliate earnings and the holding company's book income.

Bull factors

An affiliate growing overseas

Nongshim reported that its third-quarter 2026 preliminary revenue rose 6.7% year on year, driven by growth at its US and China units and stronger exports. The overseas share of consolidated revenue for the first nine months was 39.4%, up 2.6 percentage points from a year earlier. US segment operating profit was reported to have increased from KRW 4.5 billion to KRW 12.7 billion.

Three years of rising attributable profit

Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders increased for three consecutive years from 2023 through 2025, rising 53.3% to KRW 114.6 billion in 2025. Corporate monitoring data show consolidated revenue up 42.4% and operating profit up 49.8% in the first half of 2026. The operating margin reached 10.9% in 2024, which provides a historical reference point for margin recovery.

The book-value gap and payout structure

Equity-method carrying value was KRW 1,044.8 billion, while the market value of the same stakes was reported at about KRW 900 billion. The company has a policy of returning at least half of standalone operating profit within affiliate dividends, and the 2025 year-end dividend increased from the prior year. A credit rating agency projected normalization of Seu's earnings.

Bear factors

Profit decline in the third quarter of 2026

Operating profit in the third quarter of 2026 was KRW 18 billion, down 21.1% year on year, and net profit attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 12.5 billion, down 80.8%.

Operating profit in the fourth quarter of 2025 (KRW 14.6 billion) and the third quarter of 2026 (KRW 18 billion) were both below the first and second quarters of 2026 (KRW 30.9 billion and KRW 34.6 billion). The reversal in quarterly profit is the main weakness in the recent data.

The gap between book income and cash

According to press coverage, the share of Nongshim's attributable net profit that is recorded in the holding company's books through its stake is about KRW 55.7 billion. Commentary notes that much of the holding company's profit is equity-method income rather than cash. Operating cash flow fell from KRW 55.2 billion in 2024 to KRW 24.7 billion in 2025.

Governance and leverage

The debt ratio rose from 32.4% in 2024 to 39.0% in 2025, and funds for the Seu acquisition came from corporate bonds and short-term borrowings. The company has not disclosed a corporate value-up plan, and the largest shareholder, who is the chairman, also serves as chairman of the board. Nongshim has published its own value-up plans, which contrasts with the holding company's approach.

Risk factors

Costs and currency

Nongshim faces pressure on domestic profitability from higher palm oil and other raw material costs, along with rising shipping and energy expenses. Its foreign-exchange-related results swung from a KRW 1.3 billion gain in the third quarter of 2025 to a KRW 12.9 billion loss in the third quarter of 2026. The holding company receives this pressure only indirectly through equity-method income and dividends.

Holding discount and governance

The holding company has not published a value-up plan of its own, and analysts argue there is little incentive for a merger with Nongshim. The largest shareholder holds 42.92%, and some analysts see limited triggers for narrowing the gap between book value and share price. The holding discount may persist as a structural issue rather than one driven by affiliate results.

Leverage and cash flow

The debt ratio stood at 39.0% at the end of 2025, and borrowings used for the Seu acquisition are reflected in the balance sheet. Operating cash flow was KRW 24.7 billion in 2025, down from KRW 55.2 billion in 2024.

Because debt reduction relies on affiliate dividends, any change in dividend flows would also change financial flexibility.

Overall view

Nongshim Holdings is a pure holding company with stakes in Nongshim and a 31.94%-owned listed affiliate. Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders rose 53.3% to KRW 114.6 billion in 2025, while operating profit fell 6.4% to KRW 81.1 billion.

In the third quarter of 2026, attributable net profit dropped 80.8% to KRW 12.5 billion, interrupting the earlier trend. Much of the profit is equity-method income, and cash reaches the holding company only through affiliate dividends, so book income and cash flow should be read together.

The gap between the KRW 1,044.8 billion equity-method carrying value and the roughly KRW 900 billion market value of the same stakes is presented as a product of the holding structure, dividend policy and governance.

Positive factors include overseas growth at Nongshim, three consecutive years of rising attributable profit and a dividend policy tied to affiliate income.

Negative factors include the quarterly profit decline, the gap between book income and cash, the higher debt ratio and the absence of a value-up plan at the holding company.

The next checkpoints are the preliminary fourth-quarter 2026 results and the 2026 annual report, and the key question is whether they show Seu's integration effect and a steady dividend flow.
