Report overview

A Holding Company Riding Subsidiary Earnings, With the Discount Still in Place

Nongshim Holdings draws most of its recurring income from dividends paid by its subsidiaries. Profit attributable to controlling shareholders rose in 2025 while operating profit fell, and in the third quarter of 2026 both operating profit and controlling-shareholder profit declined year on year.

  1. 1

    Profit attributable to controlling shareholders rose 53.3% year on year to KRW 114.6 billion in 2025, helped by a bargain purchase gain from the August 2025 acquisition of a seasoning and soy-materials maker, while operating profit fell 6.4% to KRW 81.1 billion.

  2. 2

    Third-quarter 2026 operating profit was KRW 18 billion, down 21.1% year on year, and profit attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 12.5 billion, down 80.8%. Second-quarter 2026 operating profit of KRW 34.6 billion is the highest of the last five quarters.

  3. 3

    Earnings that subsidiaries retained have built up book value while the share price has stayed within a range for years. The company was included in the list of 151 low-PBR companies that Korea Exchange published in November 2026.

  4. 4

  5. 5

    Operating profit recovered in the first half of 2026 but dropped in the third quarter, while a press tally shows first-half profit attributable to controlling shareholders up about 45% year on year, so the direction of results differs quarter to quarter.

02

Business structure

Nongshim Holdings was spun off from Nongshim in 2003 as a pure holding company, and it controls six subsidiaries and two second-tier subsidiaries. The consolidated group runs four segments: holding investments, manufacturing, engineering, and leisure.

In the 2025 half-year report, segment revenue before consolidation adjustments was KRW 288.3 billion for manufacturing, about KRW 38.4 billion for engineering, KRW 10.7 billion for leisure, and KRW 21.8 billion of dividend income from holding investments.

Manufacturing accounts for most of the group, and its core is Nongshim, which makes instant noodles, snacks, and beverages. Nongshim holds the number one share of the domestic instant noodle market and a top-tier share in the United States.

It is vertically integrated with a seasoning affiliate, Nongshim Tae-kyung, and a packaging-materials affiliate, which supports cost control and supply stability. In the first half of 2025, the related company Nongshim accounted for about 29.1% of group revenue, and direct sales made up 59.4%.

Dividend income is the core of the holding company's earnings: dividend income rose to KRW 31.7 billion in 2025 from KRW 21.8 billion in 2024, and most recurring income comes from subsidiary dividends. Engineering (food logistics plants and construction) and leisure (golf course operations) are small.

On August 1, 2025, the group acquired 100% of a soy-sauce and seasoning materials maker for about KRW 100 billion, widening its business scope.

04

Earnings analysis

On a disclosed annual basis, revenue rose from KRW 674.6 billion in 2022 to KRW 769.5 billion in 2023 (up 14.1% year on year), KRW 798.3 billion in 2024 (up 3.7%), and KRW 858.8 billion in 2025 (up 7.6%).

Revenue has grown for three consecutive years from 2023 through 2025, and 2025 was the largest year in the 2022 to 2025 period. Operating profit climbed from KRW 52.8 billion in 2022 to KRW 75.3 billion in 2023 (up 42.6%) and KRW 86.6 billion in 2024 (up 15.0%), making 2024 the largest year.

Operating profit then fell 6.4% to KRW 81.1 billion in 2025, and the operating margin moved from 7.8% in 2022 to 9.8% in 2023 and 10.9% in 2024 before easing to 9.4% in 2025. Revenue kept rising through 2025, but the margin turned down after peaking in 2024.

Profit attributable to controlling shareholders moved from KRW 34.1 billion in 2022 to KRW 67.1 billion in 2023 (up 96.9%), KRW 74.8 billion in 2024 (up 11.3%), and KRW 114.6 billion in 2025 (up 53.3%), rising for three consecutive years from 2023.

Non-recurring items played a large part in the 2025 increase: the company said the bargain purchase gain from the acquisition was reflected, and that KRW 44.7 billion gain equals roughly 40% of the year's controlling-shareholder profit.

Net profit including non-controlling interests was also KRW 114.6 billion in 2025, essentially the same as controlling-shareholder profit. Operating cash flow was KRW 9.74 billion in 2022, -KRW 16.7 billion in 2023 (a net outflow), KRW 55.2 billion in 2024, and KRW 24.7 billion in 2025.

Total equity at the end of 2025 was KRW 1.32 trillion, all attributable to controlling shareholders, and total liabilities were KRW 515.8 billion. The debt ratio rose from 28.9% in 2022 to 39.0% in 2025.

Quarterly, operating profit was KRW 22.8 billion in the third quarter of 2025 and KRW 14.6 billion in the fourth quarter, then recovered to KRW 30.9 billion in the first quarter of 2026 and KRW 34.6 billion in the second quarter before falling to KRW 18 billion in the third quarter of 2026, down 21.1% year on year.

Profit attributable to controlling shareholders over the last four quarters (fourth quarter of 2025 to third quarter of 2026) totaled KRW 79.5 billion. The third quarter of 2025 came just after the August 1 acquisition, so that year-earlier comparison may include one-time effects.

05

Industry analysis

In the domestic instant noodle market, the subsidiary Nongshim is the number one player, and it also holds the top spot in snacks. Coverage around late 2025 often framed competition around Samyang Foods' fire-noodle products.

In its first-half 2026 results, the subsidiary Nongshim cited a weak domestic consumer market and intensifying competition, and said overseas business growth and margin improvement led the results. With domestic demand flat, exports and overseas subsidiaries have become the growth engine.

Some analysts say growth in North America has slowed somewhat, but expansion in Europe and Asia is offsetting that. On costs, press reports say high exchange rates and high oil prices pushed up packaging and other material prices.

In the second quarter of 2026, the subsidiary Nongshim's standalone operating profit was KRW 32.1 billion, down 32.0% from the first quarter. For the holding company, dividends from subsidiaries directly affect income whenever Nongshim's earnings move.

One credit assessment notes that listed subsidiary stakes worth about KRW 915 billion partly offset the structural subordination typical of holding companies.

06

Outlook

The investment plan the holding company has confirmed concerns capital spending at the subsidiary Nongshim. Nongshim is pursuing new facilities, including the Noksan export-dedicated plant and the Ulsan Samnam logistics center, and it cites expanding exports as the rationale.

A credit rating agency assessed that the investment burden is manageable given the subsidiaries' operating cash generation and ample liquidity. The same assessment said the holding company's credit standing should remain stable as long as key subsidiaries keep their credit ratings.

At the holding level, dividend income rose to KRW 31.7 billion in 2025 from KRW 21.8 billion, and recurring income increased to KRW 33.0 billion. A November 2026 press report said total dividends of KRW 13.9 billion were about 53% of parent-only net profit.

A simple calculation suggests that if Nongshim raised its payout ratio by 10 percentage points, dividends flowing to the holding company would increase by roughly KRW 5 billion a year.

On earnings, second-quarter 2026 operating profit of KRW 34.6 billion was the highest of the last five quarters, and the drop to KRW 18 billion in the third quarter is the starting point for the next read.

When the 2026 annual report confirms fourth-quarter operating profit and the full-year operating margin, it will show whether the first-half recovery carried through the year. The timing of operation and the final spending on the new facilities will depend on future disclosures.

08

Bull factors

Overseas expansion at the subsidiary is lifting earnings

The subsidiary Nongshim holds a top-tier share in the United States and is widening overseas sales. Some assessments say North American growth has slowed somewhat, but expansion in Europe and Asia offsets that.

Wisereport's tally shows consolidated revenue up 42.4% and operating profit up 49.8% in the first half of 2026. Because the holding company's income follows subsidiary results, better overseas profitability can flow through to higher dividend income.

Financial capacity and an asset base

At the end of March 2026, the combined net borrowing of Nongshim Holdings, Nongshim and the packaging affiliate was negative KRW 652.9 billion, meaning net cash. Parent-only debt ratio in the first quarter of 2026 was 13.8%, and borrowings dependence was 8.7%.

Listed subsidiary stakes worth about KRW 915 billion are seen as partly offsetting the structural subordination of a holding company. A credit rating agency expects the company's credit standing to remain stable as long as key subsidiaries keep theirs.

Rising dividend income and room for more payouts

Dividend income at the holding company rose sharply to KRW 31.7 billion in 2025 from KRW 21.8 billion in 2024, and recurring income increased to KRW 33.0 billion. One assessment says recurring income covers recurring expenses by 187.1%.

A simple calculation suggests that a higher payout ratio at Nongshim would add to dividends received by the holding company. Because the holding company's own income depends on subsidiary dividends, this flow matters.

Bear factors

Third-quarter 2026 profit decline and flat revenue

Third-quarter 2026 operating profit was KRW 18 billion, down 21.1% year on year, and profit attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 12.5 billion, down 80.8%. Revenue for the quarter was KRW 267.5 billion, up only 0.5% from a year earlier, so top-line growth also stalled.

The third quarter of 2025 came after the acquisition, which made the profit comparison base higher. Compared with the second quarter of 2026, operating profit fell 48.0%.

Lower operating profit and reliance on non-recurring gains

Operating profit fell 6.4% to KRW 81.1 billion in 2025, and the operating margin slipped from 10.9% to 9.4%. Profit attributable to controlling shareholders rose 53.3% in the same year, but a KRW 44.7 billion bargain purchase gain from the acquisition made up about 40% of it.

Net profit rose while operating profit declined, so the increase leaned on a one-time gain. Because that gain is one-time in nature, it will not recur in the same way in later years.

Rising debt and slower cash generation

The debt ratio climbed from 28.9% in 2022 to 39.0% in 2025, and total liabilities at the end of 2025 were KRW 515.8 billion. Operating cash flow fell from KRW 55.2 billion in 2024 to KRW 24.7 billion in 2025. In 2023, operating cash flow was a net outflow of KRW 16.7 billion.

After the acquisition, parent-only finances moved into net borrowing, and the remaining cash was used to fund the deal.

Risk factors

Raw materials and FX

The subsidiary Nongshim's costs are sensitive to packaging and other material prices as well as the exchange rate. If high exchange rates and high oil prices persist, cost pressure could rise again, affecting both Nongshim's earnings and dividends to the holding company.

As overseas sales grow, exchange-rate swings matter more to the bottom line. The key question is how much of any cost increase can be passed on through prices.

Capex and funding

Nongshim is pursuing new facilities, including the Noksan export-dedicated plant and the Ulsan Samnam logistics center. A credit rating agency judged the investment burden manageable given operating cash generation and liquidity. If spending grows, it could compete with dividend increases for available funds. The timing of completion and when spending shows up in results will depend on future disclosures.

Governance and shareholder return policy

One analysis argues that a merger at market prices would cut the chairman's effective stake in Nongshim from about 14% to the 8% range, giving little incentive to merge. Buybacks have been mentioned as a way to narrow the discount, but commentary says funding is limited while the company carries borrowings. If subsidiary dividend policy does not change, cash flowing to the holding company stays constrained.

12

Overall view

Nongshim Holdings is a pure holding company that earns most of its recurring income from subsidiary dividends, so its results depend heavily on the operating trend at the subsidiary Nongshim and on one-time gains.

Revenue rose for three consecutive years from 2023 through 2025, and profit attributable to controlling shareholders also rose for three consecutive years over the same period, but operating profit peaked at KRW 86.6 billion in 2024 and fell to KRW 81.1 billion in 2025.

The KRW 114.6 billion controlling-shareholder profit in 2025 included a KRW 44.7 billion bargain purchase gain, so the rise in profit is hard to read as a sign of stronger core business.

Operating profit recovered in the first and second quarters of 2026, but fell to KRW 18 billion in the third quarter, down 21.1% year on year. The debt ratio climbed from 28.9% in 2022 to 39.0% in 2025, and parent-only finances moved into net borrowing after the acquisition.

The share price has stayed within a range for years and the company appears on the list of low-PBR companies, a situation tied to earnings that accumulate at subsidiaries without fully reaching the holding company as cash.

Strengths include overseas expansion, rising dividend income, and financial capacity, while weaknesses include the third-quarter profit decline, reliance on one-time gains, and higher debt. The next checkpoints are the fourth-quarter operating profit in the 2026 annual report and first-quarter 2027 results.
