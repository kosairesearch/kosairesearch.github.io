02

Business structure

Shinhan Global Active REIT listed on KOSPI in July 2024 as the first domestic REIT focused on global real estate funds. It does not own properties directly; through a 100%-owned subsidiary it invests in three U.S. open-end real estate funds and distributes the dividend income to shareholders.

At listing, the holdings were USGB at 51.8%, PRISA at 39.8%, and CBRE USCP at 8.4%. The underlying assets span U.S. government-leased infrastructure, logistics, and residential properties. According to the company's DART disclosure, its revenue consists of dividend income the subsidiary receives from the funds.

Shinhan REITs Management handles operations and sales, and this is the third listed REIT from that manager after Shinhan Alpha REIT and Shinhan Seobu T&D REIT.

Press reports said the FBI Charlotte asset at USGB raised rent by 32% and completed a 20-year long-term lease renewal, and that this asset accounts for 4.3% of USGB's net asset value. The combined occupancy of National Landing Center 1 and 2 reached about 60%.

In Korea, the company has repaid its borrowings and is preparing new investments. Around the listing, some industry participants argued that U.S. REIT ETFs offer a cheaper way to invest in overseas property.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual revenue rose from KRW 3.29 billion in the fiscal year ended August 2023 to KRW 3.49 billion in the fiscal year ended August 2024, an increase of 6.1%, making FY2024 the largest year in the period.

It then fell 12.3% to KRW 3.06 billion in the fiscal year ended August 2025 and 15.8% to KRW 2.57 billion in the fiscal year ended August 2026, the smallest year in the period.

Operating profit dropped from KRW 1.17 billion to KRW 147 million (down 87.4%) in the fiscal year ended August 2024, then rose to KRW 1.97 billion (up 1,235.6%) and KRW 2.29 billion (up 16.2%) in the next two fiscal years. Operating margin moved from 35.6% to 4.2%, then 64.4% and 88.9%.

Margin expanded while revenue shrank, which suggests cost levels fell by more than revenue did, but the line items should be checked in the full periodic report.

Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders was a loss of KRW 13 billion, KRW 10.8 billion, and KRW 7.24 billion in the fiscal years ended August 2023, 2024 and 2025, then turned positive at KRW 2.09 billion in the fiscal year ended August 2026.

Net profit including non-controlling interests was the same KRW 2.09 billion for that year. In the fiscal year ended August 2025, operating profit was KRW 1.97 billion but net loss was KRW 7.24 billion, so the gap between the operating and net lines was wide.

Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders over the last four quarters, from the fourth quarter of 2025 (September to November) through the third quarter of 2026 (June to August), totals KRW 2.09 billion.

Operating cash flow moved from KRW -1.21 billion and KRW -2.79 billion in the fiscal years ended August 2023 and 2024 to KRW 2.78 billion and KRW 1.86 billion in the fiscal years ended August 2025 and 2026. The debt ratio fell from 166.2% to 38.6% and 11.7%, then edged up to 17.5% in the fiscal year ended August 2026.

Press reports said a KRW 30 billion first capital increase at the subsidiary was used to finalize FX hedge settlement and fully repay a credit line.

05

Industry analysis

When the company listed in 2024, the REIT sector had posted negative returns for close to a year, and Shinhan Global Active REIT drew attention as a listing that could reverse that trend.

At the time, many industry participants argued that U.S. property prices had not adjusted enough, and others pointed to remaining adjustment and downside risk. Some market participants noted that its price-to-NAV ratio of 0.7 to 0.8 times at listing was above the domestic listed REIT average of 0.67 times.

In 2025, the sponsor cited macro factors such as interest rates and tariffs as the reason for the share price decline. Interest rates and exchange rates can affect the valuation of the underlying funds and the cost of currency hedging.

For the second quarter of 2026, press reports said the USCP fund's total return of 1.3% was its best since 2023, with residential assets such as rental housing and student housing improving.

Because the company is concentrated in overseas funds, it is more exposed to the U.S. property cycle and the exchange rate than to the domestic REIT cycle. Compared with sister REITs Shinhan Alpha REIT and Shinhan Seobu T&D REIT, its assets differ in both geography and structure.

Demand at listing was relatively strong, with only 7 of 23 listed REITs reaching triple-digit retail subscription ratios as of 2024.

06

Outlook

According to the investor briefing material the sponsor presented on May 28, 2026, the company is reviewing balance sheet improvement and new asset investments as ways to raise shareholder value. These plans are subject to legal review and board approval.

The first phase covered preparing FX hedge settlement funds and clearing the credit line, and the second phase covers new asset investment that is in progress.

Press reports later said the KRW 30 billion first capital increase was used for FX hedge settlement and full repayment of the credit line, and that the hedge ratio was cut from 100% to 50% of NAV.

Reports also said the company plans a KRW 35 billion second capital increase within 2026 to invest in domestic property preferred shares, with KRW 35 billion to KRW 40 billion available for new investments. The company has set a goal of a dividend yield of 6% to 7% from these investments.

On the U.S. side, as of the first quarter of 2026, leasing talks for National Landing Center II and an extension with an existing tenant were in progress, and marketing for new tenants was under way at Waterfront Station after a retail tenant's lease ended.

Press reports later said the FBI Charlotte lease extension talks concluded with a long-term renewal. Lease expirations at Original Liberty Center and Three Whiteflint are scheduled for 2027, and follow-on leases are being pursued.

PRISA is selling non-core assets and reshaping its portfolio toward residential, logistics and necessity retail.

08

Bull factors

Improving U.S. leasing indicators

Press reports said the FBI Charlotte asset at USGB raised rent by 32% and was renewed on a 20-year lease. The combined occupancy of National Landing Center 1 and 2 reached about 60%. Because the stability of fund distributions depends on lease continuity, these changes matter for the earnings base. The impact of any single lease is limited, since this asset accounts for 4.3% of USGB's net asset value.

Earnings direction has turned

Operating profit has risen in each of the two fiscal years since the low in the fiscal year ended August 2024. Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders turned positive in the fiscal year ended August 2026. Operating cash flow has been positive since the fiscal year ended August 2025.

The operating margin of 88.9% in the fiscal year ended August 2026 is the highest of the four fiscal years shown.

Balance sheet cleanup under way

Press reports said a KRW 30 billion first capital increase at the subsidiary was used to finalize FX hedge settlement and fully repay the credit line. The hedge ratio was reduced from 100% to 50% of NAV. Total liabilities were KRW 20.6 billion at the end of August 2026, with a debt ratio of 17.5%. Preparing new investment funds from a low borrowing base is a financial strength.

09

Bear factors

Revenue down two years in a row

Revenue fell from KRW 3.49 billion in the fiscal year ended August 2024 to KRW 3.06 billion in the fiscal year ended August 2025 and KRW 2.57 billion in the fiscal year ended August 2026. Because revenue consists of fund dividend income, lower fund distributions pass straight through to revenue.

A DART disclosure on revenue below KRW 5 billion was filed on October 13, 2026. Since the profit recovery came alongside shrinking revenue, its durability needs further confirmation.

Asset value decline and shareholder discontent since listing

Press reports said asset value fell about 15% after the pre-IPO round, and in June 2025 some shareholders asked for liquidation. The sponsor attributed the decline to interest rates, exchange rates and property market conditions and said a rebound was possible.

The gap between the high expected dividend yield presented at listing and actual distributions remains to be verified in periodic reports.

Overseas fund concentration and dilution risk

Assets are concentrated in three U.S. property funds, and occupancy varies widely across assets, as with National Landing Center 1 and 2. If the KRW 35 billion second capital increase proceeds, existing shareholders' stakes could be diluted. If returns on the domestic preferred shares fall short of the target, the dividend base could also weaken.

10

Risk factors

Currency and interest rate risk

Because the assets are stakes in U.S. property funds, currency and interest rate moves affect fund valuations and distributions. Lowering the hedge ratio from 100% to 50% of NAV increased exposure to exchange rate moves. Property price adjustments driven by U.S. interest rates also remain a risk.

Capital raise execution and dilution

Press reports say a KRW 35 billion second capital increase is planned within 2026. Depending on whether and when it proceeds and on its terms, the value of existing shareholders' stakes could be affected.

The pace at which the raised funds turn into domestic preferred share investments also matters for the dividend target.

Fee and management structure

Industry participants criticized the presence of redemption fees on a listed REIT, in addition to management and acquisition and disposition fees. The circumstances of asset transfers from a related party also led to shareholder disputes in 2025. Such a structure can widen the gap between distributable cash flow and shareholder returns.

12

Overall view

Shinhan Global Active REIT is a REIT with overseas property exposure built on stakes in U.S. open-end real estate funds, and it recorded a turn to positive net profit attributable to controlling shareholders and higher operating profit in the fiscal year ended August 2026.

Revenue, however, has fallen for two consecutive years since the peak in the fiscal year ended August 2024, and revenue in the fiscal year ended August 2026 is the smallest in the period.

The profit recovery depends on the cost structure and the flow of fund dividend income, so whether the revenue base recovers is the central point to watch. On leasing, the long-term renewal at the FBI Charlotte asset and the rise in National Landing Center occupancy are positive signs.

On the balance sheet, the FX hedge settlement, the repayment of the credit line and a debt ratio of 17.5% point to a more stable capital structure.

On the other side, execution of the KRW 35 billion second capital increase, exposure to exchange rates and interest rates, and the gap between the high expected dividend yield presented at listing and actual distributions remain open issues.

The DART disclosure on revenue below KRW 5 billion filed on October 13, 2026 also shows that the revenue base is still small. The next checkpoints are the outcome of the capital increase, the trend in quarterly net profit attributable to controlling shareholders, and the results of 2027 lease expirations.
