02

Business structure

Aiji net is an insurtech company that runs Bodak, a MyData-based app for insurance check-ups and recommendations, as its platform, and operates two B2B solutions alongside an insurance agency (GA) business through a subsidiary.

According to a Kiwoom Securities analyst's April 2026 note, platform insurance commissions accounted for 97.7% of 2025 revenue, with platform listing fees at 1.7% and B2B solutions at 0.6%.

Because revenue is concentrated in insurance consultation platform commissions, changes in the insurance sales environment and commission rules feed directly into results.

The company says it analyzes MyData information collected with customer consent using AI and supplies the refined data as databases (DBs) to insurers and GAs. In the first quarter of 2026, it said it had widened its client base from subsidiary-centered supply to major life insurers and large GAs.

Its partnerships with external GAs began in August 2025, and the company says they now make up 17% of total revenue.

On competition, the Kiwoom Securities analyst described competition as limited relative to strong demand for platforms in the insurance sector, and noted that Kakao and NAVER were also selected as MyData operators.

Bodak has passed 2.2 million cumulative downloads and more than 450,000 cumulative diagnoses, according to the same note.

In April 2026 the company signed an MOU with a global digital-asset insurance firm to pursue virtual-asset insurance solutions, and in September 2026 it signed a business agreement with an insurance agency group to co-develop MyData-based asset management services.

The detailed revenue split between DB sales and platform commissions should be checked against the periodic reports' segment disclosures.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual revenue rose from KRW 13 billion in 2023 to KRW 23.3 billion in 2024, up 79.5%, and then to KRW 39 billion in 2025, up 67.2%, marking two consecutive years of growth from 2024 through 2025.

Operating results moved from an operating loss of KRW 3.23 billion in 2023 to an operating profit of KRW 352 million in 2024, a turn to profit, and then to KRW 3.33 billion in 2025, up 844.7%.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders swung from a net loss of KRW 12.6 billion in 2023 to KRW 152 million in 2024, a turn to profit, and then to KRW 2.56 billion in 2025, up 1,584.4%.

Net income including non-controlling interests shows the same figures for each year, at a loss of KRW 12.6 billion in 2023, KRW 152 million in 2024 and KRW 2.56 billion in 2025. The operating margin improved from -24.9% in 2023 to 1.5% in 2024 and 8.5% in 2025, and 2025 remains in single digits.

The debt ratio, calculated as total liabilities over total equity, was -129.4% in 2023, which is negative and therefore hard to compare directly with later years, then 233.8% in 2024 and 127.5% in 2025. At the end of 2025, total equity stood at KRW 22.1 billion and total liabilities at KRW 28.2 billion.

Operating cash flow moved from -KRW 2.14 billion in 2023 to KRW 1.41 billion in 2024 and KRW 1.62 billion in 2025.

Quarterly results show revenue of KRW 8.95 billion and operating profit of KRW 607 million in the third quarter of 2025, then a jump to KRW 14.8 billion of revenue, up 64.9% quarter on quarter, and KRW 2.82 billion of operating profit, up 364.8%, in the fourth quarter.

The company attributed the fourth-quarter improvement to more partnership-based DB supply, which eased fixed-cost pressure, and to a standardized revenue recognition approach.

First-quarter 2026 revenue fell 40.8% to KRW 8.73 billion and operating profit fell 77.5% to KRW 636 million, before the second quarter recovered to KRW 9.21 billion of revenue, up 5.5%, and KRW 1.1 billion of operating profit, up 73.7%.

Third-quarter 2026 revenue reached KRW 10.3 billion, up 11.9% quarter on quarter and 15.1% year on year, while operating profit of KRW 641 million fell 42.0% quarter on quarter and rose only 5.6% year on year.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders over the most recent four quarters totaled KRW 3.76 billion.

05

Industry analysis

The fee structure for insurance sales channels is changing in stages. From July 2026, a cap known as the 1200% rule has applied to initial recruitment commissions for GA-affiliated agents, and a staged commission payout system is scheduled to take effect in 2027.

The company expects these changes to raise interest in customer acquisition and consultation efficiency across the insurance industry, which should lift demand for DB products used in sales.

Industry reports describe Korea's insurtech market as expanding quickly on the back of smartphone adoption and IT infrastructure, with B2B business growing as insurers adopt engine technology.

On the macro side, the company and press coverage describe rising oil prices and weaker consumer sentiment linked to the US-Iran conflict as increasing volatility across the insurance market. Kakao and NAVER were also selected as MyData operators, so platform competition overlaps with large platform players.

In scope, Aiji net combines its own insurance consultation platform, GA partnerships and insurer-facing DB supply, while its revenue scale and business mix differ sharply from NAVER and Kakao.

The key industry variable is whether DB sales growth continues alongside regulatory changes to sales channels, and quarterly revenue mix will show this over time.

06

Outlook

A company official said in a November 2026 press report that 2027 DB sales would substantially exceed the 2026 level, citing more purchases from existing clients, price increases and new customers.

The CEO, Kim Ji-tae, framed the goal as a business structure in which DB generation and refinement technology drives external sales, platform use and service-linked revenue.

To expand in 2027, the company is reviewing new businesses that would extend MyData use from insurance into financial and lifestyle services such as banking, investment, cards and telecom.

On the platform side, a May 2026 plan called for gradually extending AI consultation features, now offered only to customers assigned to in-house agents, to all users. On overseas expansion, the company named establishing a foothold in the Vietnamese market as a 2026 priority in March 2026.

In virtual-asset insurance, the company said it would pursue joint solutions following its April 2026 MOU. The company's quantitative targets, as far as they have been made public, remain directional comments on DB sales growth.

The next checkpoints are how the 2026 annual report describes the DB share within its business segments, and whether DB sales growth in the first quarter of 2027 translates into a recovery in operating profit.

08

Bull factors

Expansion of DB sales

The company said cumulative DB sales through the third quarter of 2026 reached KRW 2.35 billion, about 3.6 times 2025 full-year DB sales of KRW 650 million. A company official said 2027 DB sales would substantially exceed the 2026 level, citing more purchases from existing clients, price increases and new customers. Whether DB sales become the main earnings engine is presented as the key variable for growth.

Direction of margin improvement

The operating margin rose from -24.9% in 2023 to 1.5% in 2024 and 8.5% in 2025. The company cited expanded partnerships and AI-based operating efficiency as drivers of first-quarter 2026 profit improvement. Whether the fixed-cost burden keeps falling as revenue grows is the next point to confirm.

Regulatory changes as an external driver

Since July 2026, a cap known as the 1200% rule has limited initial recruitment commissions for GA-affiliated agents, and a staged commission payout system is scheduled for 2027.

The company expects these changes to raise insurance-industry interest in consultation efficiency and customer acquisition, which should lift DB demand. That DB demand increase is the company's expectation, and actual purchase volumes must be confirmed through quarterly results.

09

Bear factors

Quarterly earnings volatility

After KRW 2.82 billion of operating profit in the fourth quarter of 2025, quarterly operating profit has swung between KRW 636 million in the first quarter of 2026, KRW 1.1 billion in the second quarter and KRW 641 million in the third.

Third-quarter revenue rose 11.9% quarter on quarter, but operating profit fell 42.0%. Net income attributable to controlling shareholders fell 10.9% year on year to KRW 605 million, so revenue growth has not yet produced higher profit.

Concentration of commission income

Platform insurance commissions made up 97.7% of 2025 revenue, leaving the business concentrated in a single income source. Changes in insurance sales regulation or GA commission policy could hit consultation demand and contract conversion together.

Unlike the company's view that regulatory change is an opportunity, changes in sales cost structure could also weigh on results.

Shareholding and supply-demand

As of June 2026, Kim Chang-gyun and five related parties held 26.91%. A Kiwoom Securities analyst's April 2026 note flagged the need to watch overhang and post-listing supply-demand issues. Foreign ownership was 2.03% as of June 2026, so shifts in supply and demand could have a large effect.

Risk factors

Regulation and commission rules

Insurance commission rules and MyData regulations form the core of the business. Application of the 1200% rule and the 2027 payout system could change how GAs recruit and how DB demand develops.

Because information must be processed and supplied within the scope of customer consent, regulatory compliance is a key management point.

Gap between separate and consolidated results

Press coverage of third-quarter 2026 results reported separate-basis revenue of KRW 6.5 billion and separate-basis operating profit of KRW 3.3 billion, against consolidated operating profit of KRW 641 million.

Because the gap between separate and consolidated figures is large, the profit structure of subsidiary and GA operations should be checked in the periodic reports. Differences between company announcements and disclosed figures require attention.

Execution of new businesses

New businesses such as virtual-asset insurance, Vietnam expansion and financial and lifestyle services have not yet appeared in reported results. How investment costs and partnership outcomes feed into profit after 2027 remains uncertain. Rising spending on new businesses could slow the operating margin recovery.

Overall view

Aiji net shifted from an operating loss in 2023 to an operating profit of KRW 3.33 billion in 2025 while growing revenue to KRW 39 billion, making it an insurtech company whose growth is increasingly tied to its AI-processed MyData database business.

According to company announcements, cumulative DB sales through the third quarter of 2026 were about 3.6 times the 2025 full-year level.

Third-quarter 2026 revenue of KRW 10.3 billion marked a second straight quarterly increase, but operating profit of KRW 641 million remained below the fourth-quarter 2025 peak, and net income attributable to controlling shareholders declined year on year.

Bullish factors include DB sales expansion, the rise in operating margin to 8.5% in 2025, and expectations for DB demand from insurance commission changes.

Bearish factors include concentration in platform commissions, quarterly earnings volatility, and supply-demand pressure from concentrated major shareholding and low foreign ownership.

On valuation, the KRW 3.76 billion of net income attributable to controlling shareholders over the latest four quarters leaves a wide range of interpretation for earnings-based multiples, which needs confirmation from further quarterly results.

The next checkpoints are the 2026 annual report, first-quarter 2027 results, and the trajectory of DB sales after the staged commission payout starts in 2027.
