02

Business structure

Dongbang Transport & Logistics, founded in January 1965, is built around freight truck transportation, port cargo handling and ship transportation.

Based on the 2026 second-quarter sales mix reported by the corporate data aggregator Butler, freight truck transportation accounted for 36%, port cargo handling 28%, ship transportation 21%, other operations 14%, and merchandise sales and IT services 1%.

Road transport and port handling together exceed 60% of sales, so port cargo volumes and trucking demand weigh heavily on results. The shipping unit operates dedicated RO-RO vessels and dedicated ships for steel, including POSCO steel product coastal shipping.

The company has a logistics and warehousing partnership with Coupang, and the new barge Dongbang No. 10001 called at Ulsan port in March 2025. A corporate monitoring commentary dated June 2026 said the company is expanding into large logistics centers and online logistics.

Its industry peers include HMM, Hyundai Glovis, Pan Ocean and CJ Logistics among large shipping and integrated logistics groups, and Sebang, Sun Kwang and Hanjin among port and logistics operators. Kim Hyung-gon and three related parties hold 22.95% of common shares, and treasury shares are 0.37%.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual revenue rose from KRW 758.5 billion in 2022 to KRW 767.8 billion in 2023 (up 1.2% year on year), KRW 871.6 billion in 2024 (up 13.5%) and KRW 915.6 billion in 2025 (up 5.0%). Revenue increased for three consecutive years from 2023 through 2025, and 2025 was the largest year of the 2022 to 2025 period.

Operating profit peaked at KRW 39 billion in 2024 before falling 18.1% to KRW 32 billion in 2025, and the operating margin slipped from 4.5% to 3.5% over the same span.

Profit attributable to controlling shareholders rose from KRW 817 million in 2022 to KRW 14.8 billion in 2023 (up 1,713.8%), reached KRW 20.5 billion in 2024, and then fell 16.0% to KRW 17.2 billion in 2025.

Net income including non-controlling interests was KRW 17.4 billion in 2025, close to the controlling-shareholder figure. Operating cash flow dropped to KRW 9.74 billion in 2025 from KRW 56.9 billion in 2024.

On a quarterly basis, operating profit peaked at KRW 11.8 billion in the third quarter of 2025, then fell to KRW 4.31 billion in the fourth quarter (down 63.4% from the prior quarter), when the company posted a net loss attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 632 million after turning from profit to loss.

In the first quarter of 2026, revenue was KRW 241.5 billion (down 4.2% from the prior quarter), operating profit KRW 6.54 billion, and profit attributable to controlling shareholders KRW 2.25 billion, a return to profit.

Operating profit fell 19.1% to KRW 5.28 billion in the second quarter of 2026, then rose to KRW 9.84 billion in the third quarter, up 86.2% from the prior quarter but down 16.5% from a year earlier on revenue of KRW 225.4 billion (down 5.2% year on year).

A corporate monitoring commentary on the first quarter of 2026 said new volume wins lifted revenue despite weaker global trade, while profitability eased due to protectionism, freight rate volatility and carbon costs.

Profit attributable to controlling shareholders over the last four quarters totals KRW 4.65 billion, so quarterly swings are large and the direction is best read alongside the annual trend.

05

Industry analysis

The transport and logistics industry is sensitive to cargo volumes and trade conditions. A corporate monitoring commentary said global economic slowdown reduced cargo volumes in 2026, while protectionism, freight rate volatility and carbon costs weighed on profitability.

Freight truck transportation (36%) and port cargo handling (28%) make up most of Dongbang's sales mix, so domestic cargo volumes and port throughput directly affect results. Ship transportation (21%) relies on dedicated steel RO-RO vessels and POSCO steel coastal shipping, so it tracks steel cargo trends.

Among listed peers, HMM, Hyundai Glovis, Pan Ocean and CJ Logistics operate on a larger fleet or integrated network scale than Dongbang. Dongbang's position rests on trucking and handling capacity tied to specific shippers and routes, which is a niche rather than a broad platform.

The push into large logistics centers and online logistics is an effort to widen its volume base beyond that niche. Because the cost base is fixed-heavy, lower volumes can hit the operating margin quickly, which explains the uneven quarterly pattern in revenue and profit.

Operating profit fell 18.1% to KRW 32 billion in 2025, and the operating margin dropped to 3.5%. Operating profit fell 19.1% in the second quarter of 2026 and was still 16.5% lower than a year earlier in the third quarter. Operating cash flow fell to KRW 9.74 billion in 2025 from KRW 56.9 billion in 2024, which leaves debt service capacity as a concern.

Instability in quarterly results

The company posted a net loss attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 632 million in the fourth quarter of 2025, and operating profit declined in the second quarter of 2026 after the first-quarter return to profit.

Profit attributable to controlling shareholders over the last four quarters totals only KRW 4.65 billion. Large quarterly swings make it hard to tell whether the recovery is temporary or sustained.

Risk factors

Global cargo volumes and economic conditions

A corporate monitoring commentary on the first quarter of 2026 cited lower cargo volumes from the global economic slowdown as a factor in weaker profitability. Lower volumes feed directly into trucking and port handling revenue, and with a fixed-cost-heavy structure they can compress the operating margin. Whether new volume wins offset the decline is the key question.

Protectionism, freight rates and carbon costs

The corporate monitoring commentary named protectionism, freight rate volatility and carbon cost charges as factors that reduce profitability. The shipping business is directly exposed to freight swings and carbon regulation. How much of these costs can be passed through in rates determines margin defense.

Management transition and governance

In March 2026 the CEO role changed from joint CEOs Kim Hyung-gon and Park Chang-gi to Jung Un-geon. Outside director Ji Hee-jin was appointed on March 27, lowering the outside-director ratio to 25%.

With major shareholders holding 22.95%, the change in management direction raises questions about continuity of medium-term strategy.
