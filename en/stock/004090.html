02

Business structure

Korea Petroleum Industries Company is a chemical maker centered on asphalt waterproofing sheets, with three segments: asphalt, chemicals and synthetic resins.

Based on consolidated revenue before intercompany adjustments, asphalt was 49.31%, chemicals 33.55% and synthetic resins 19.78% in 2022, and in 2024 the split was 53.36%, 29.86% and 19.86%, according to the financial data provider FnGuide.

A rising asphalt share means greater exposure to construction demand, and both the company and brokerages describe construction investment as a driver of asphalt results.

In chemicals, the company said at its provisional first-quarter 2026 results, released on May 11, 2026, that it will expand higher-value products such as solvents for electronic materials and packaging for fine chemicals.

In synthetic resins, the company said segment sales rose year on year in 2025 on growth in blow containers for fine chemical applications. Domestic sales made up 80.8% of 2025 consolidated revenue, while exports made up 19.2%.

As new businesses, a domestic brokerage said in a March 2026 report that eco-friendly recycling, railway track systems, blow molding and EP would be reflected in earnings in sequence after 2026.

The cost of sales ratio stood at 89.00% of revenue in 2022 and 88.78% in 2025, so raw material price swings largely determine margins. Because feedstock is tied to crude prices, the company said securing raw materials early helped offset Middle East-related price volatility.

Earnings analysis

Annual revenue went from KRW 747.9 billion in 2022, the largest year in the period, to KRW 679.2 billion in 2023, the smallest year, down 9.2% year on year. It then rose to KRW 721.2 billion in 2024, up 6.2%, before falling to KRW 696.9 billion in 2025, down 3.4%.

Operating profit fell from KRW 18 billion in 2022 to KRW 15.2 billion in 2023 (down 15.6%), KRW 13.9 billion in 2024 (down 8.4%) and KRW 11.8 billion in 2025 (down 14.9%), a three-year consecutive decline from 2023 through 2025, and the operating margin slipped from 2.4% to 1.7%.

Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders rose to KRW 14.8 billion in 2023, up 33.4%, the peak of the period, then fell 12.1% to KRW 13.1 billion in 2024 and 60.1% to KRW 5.21 billion in 2025.

Net profit including non-controlling interests was KRW 5.07 billion in 2025, a different figure from the controlling-shareholder line. The company attributed the 2025 decline to weaker construction investment, a slowdown in downstream industries, a sluggish petrochemical market and a high exchange rate.

Operating cash flow was KRW 29.8 billion in 2022, KRW 5.37 billion in 2023, KRW 13.1 billion in 2024 and KRW 6.57 billion in 2025. Quarterly revenue ran KRW 172.8 billion in 3Q 2025, KRW 173.1 billion in 4Q 2025, KRW 171.2 billion in 1Q 2026, KRW 231.6 billion in 2Q 2026 and KRW 201.9 billion in 3Q 2026.

Operating profit was KRW 4.14 billion in 1Q 2026, KRW 13.8 billion in 2Q 2026 (up 232.9% from the prior quarter) and KRW 2.64 billion in 3Q 2026 (down 80.9% quarter on quarter and down 41.9% year on year).

In its provisional first-quarter 2026 results, the company reported operating profit up 335.0% year on year and a return to net profit, citing raw material procurement and better selling prices.

Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders for the last four quarters, from 4Q 2025 to 3Q 2026, totals KRW 14.3 billion, with KRW 11.7 billion in 2Q 2026 accounting for the largest share, so quarterly swings are large.

Whether one-off factors drove the 2Q 2026 jump is a question to check in the quarterly report notes and results materials.

Industry analysis

Demand for Korea Petroleum Industries Company rests on two pillars: construction activity and the petrochemical cycle. In 2025 the company cited weaker construction investment, a slowdown in downstream industries, a sluggish petrochemical market and a high exchange rate as reasons for lower results.

Against that, expectations of a construction recovery tied to government housing supply expansion were included in the company's February 2026 disclosure. A domestic brokerage said in a March 2026 report that a construction recovery would strengthen existing businesses.

On raw materials, Middle East geopolitical tension widened oil and feedstock price volatility in 1Q 2026, and the company said early procurement and flexible production helped it respond.

Sales rose sharply in 2Q 2026 after a weak 2025 market, but whether this reflects a petrochemical recovery or a temporary factor is a question for the periodic reports.

Because asphalt, tied to construction demand, is a larger share of the mix than commodity petrochemicals, its sensitivity to the cycle differs from that of large petrochemical peers. With the operating margin at 1.7% in 2025, profit swings depend heavily on cost structure and the ability to adjust selling prices. The company said it aims to improve profitability by expanding higher-value products.

Bull factors

Quarterly profit recovery

Operating profit rose 29.8% quarter on quarter to KRW 4.14 billion in 1Q 2026 and jumped 232.9% to KRW 13.8 billion in 2Q 2026. Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders reached KRW 11.7 billion in 2Q 2026, up 632.3% from the prior quarter and well above the KRW 357 million low of 4Q 2025. Evidence of a profit rebound since late 2025 is the core of the bullish case.

Higher-value products and cost gains

For 1Q 2026, the company credited higher sales volumes, cost-competitive inventory and a growing share of higher-value products. It also said it will strengthen its business around solvents for electronic materials and packaging for fine chemicals.

The bullish argument is that a higher mix of such products could lift an operating margin that was 1.7% in 2025.

Hopes for a construction recovery

Expectations of a construction revival are building on government housing supply expansion. A domestic brokerage said in a March 2026 report that a construction recovery would strengthen existing businesses and support higher-margin lines such as automotive refrigerants and high-quality containers. The company has set a 2026 target of KRW 818.4 billion in revenue and KRW 24.7 billion in operating profit.

Bear factors

Three straight years of lower operating profit

Operating profit declined for three consecutive years from 2023 through 2025, falling 14.9% to KRW 11.8 billion in 2025. The operating margin dropped from 2.4% in 2022 to 1.7% in 2025. The cost of sales ratio remained high at 88.78% in 2025, so cost pressure continues to weigh on margins.

Profit slowed again in 3Q 2026

Operating profit in 3Q 2026 was KRW 2.64 billion, down 80.9% from the prior quarter and down 41.9% year on year. Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 643 million, down 44.2% year on year. The bearish argument is that quarterly swings make it hard to judge whether the 2Q 2026 profit was sustainable.

Risk of renewed construction and currency pressure

If construction investment weakens again, petrochemical conditions stay sluggish and the exchange rate stays high, the earnings recovery could be delayed, as those were the causes the company gave for the 2025 decline. The debt ratio rose to 100.5% at the end of 2025 from 94.6% in 2024. Raw material and oil price volatility also remains a variable that can move margins.

Risk factors

Feedstock, oil and currency

Because feedstock is linked to crude prices, Middle East tension can affect cost of sales and inventory valuation. The company credited early raw material procurement for 1Q 2026, but there is no assurance the same approach will work in later quarters. A high exchange rate was also cited as a factor in the 2025 decline.

Construction cycle and policy

Asphalt demand, a large part of revenue, tracks construction investment. Weaker construction investment pulled results down in 2025, and whether government housing supply expansion turns into real demand is the key question. If the policy effect arrives late, the path to the 2026 target stays under pressure.

Market supervision and share price volatility

The disclosure record includes a June 18, 2025 designation as a short-term overheated stock, with trading moved to single-price auctions for three trading days. On March 5, 2026, a prior notice of investment warning designation was published.

Such designations are tied to share price overheating, and a change in trading method can affect liquidity and price formation.

Overall view

Korea Petroleum Industries Company is a chemical maker with asphalt, chemicals and synthetic resins segments, and its operating profit and operating margin have trended lower since 2022.

Operating profit fell for three consecutive years from 2023 through 2025, and net profit attributable to controlling shareholders has declined for two years since peaking in 2023.

Quarterly profit swings widened in 2026, with KRW 4.14 billion in 1Q, KRW 13.8 billion in 2Q and KRW 2.64 billion in 3Q, and 3Q was below the prior-year quarter.

The company points to higher-value products and raw material management as drivers of better profitability, while construction recovery and policy expectations are cited as demand factors.

On the other side, a cost of sales ratio near 88%, oil and currency swings and a possible return of weak construction investment remain bearish factors.

The price-to-book ratio sits below 1x, but earnings-based readings vary widely with the quarterly swings, so the balance of bullish and bearish factors does not clearly tilt either way.

The next checkpoints are the fiscal 2026 provisional results, expected in early February 2027 based on prior-year timing, and the 2026 annual report, due by March 31, 2027.
