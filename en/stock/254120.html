Report overview

Orders Rebound as Losses Persist at Xavis

Xavis posted KRW 9.23 billion in revenue for Q3 2026, rising for a second straight quarter, but an operating loss of KRW 2.42 billion kept the company in the red for five consecutive quarters. New orders are rising faster than sales, while mass-production costs continue to weigh on profit.

  1. 1

    Revenue recovery: sales rose in Q2 and Q3 2026, and Q3 revenue of KRW 9.23 billion was up 31.6% year on year.

  2. 2

    Order intake: Q3 2026 new orders of KRW 12.19 billion were roughly 2.6 times the prior-year level and exceeded same-quarter revenue by KRW 2.96 billion.

  3. 3

    Profitability gap: the Q3 2026 operating loss widened to KRW 2.42 billion, with costs from new equipment development, early mass production and capacity expansion reflected.

  4. 4

    Capacity expansion: the second plant started operating in August 2026, which the company's production capacity is reported to have been raised by 1.5 times.

  5. 5

    Diversification: a supply contract for electrode-cell automation equipment for AI servers in the United States, worth KRW 3.58 billion or 12.82% of 2025 revenue, was disclosed on November 4, 2026.

02

Business structure

Xavis is a KOSDAQ-listed machinery and equipment company that builds X-ray based inspection equipment, with a core business in inspection tools used in semiconductor and secondary battery manufacturing.

Based on Q2 2026 revenue, the business segments were split into Xscan at 51%, Fscan at 29% and other at 20%, according to an aggregated company profile by Butler.

Q3 2026 product sales were KRW 77.6 million up 48.7% from KRW 52.2 million a year earlier, making product sales the core of the KRW 9.23 billion total for the quarter, per the Q3 2026 quarterly report.

Battery demand has moved with the investment schedules of battery makers, which the company tied to weak electric vehicle sales and a chasm phase in its 2025 Q3 results, per FnGuide.

The company said it has launched a rotating-optics battery CT, a prismatic battery foreign-matter inspection system for ESS, a 3D AXI system and a CT for glass-substrate TGV applications. In Q3 2026 the first mass shipments of the rotating-optics battery CT and the ESS prismatic battery inspection system were made.

The CEO said 28% of revenue was invested in R&D first. Because inspection equipment sales depend on customers adopting a tool, mass-production quality and delivery performance will shape business stability.

The second plant began operating in August 2026, and total production capacity was reported to have expanded 1.5 times. The largest shareholder and related parties hold 29.90%, per FnGuide data with a last change dated July 25, 2023.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual revenue rose from KRW 22.3 billion in 2022 to KRW 28.7 billion in 2023, up 28.6% year on year, and to KRW 37.6 billion in 2024, up 31.0%, before falling to KRW 27.9 billion in 2025, down 25.8%. Among 2022 to 2025, 2024 was the largest year and 2022 the smallest.

Operating results moved from an operating loss of KRW 13.46 million in 2022 to an operating profit of KRW 841 million in 2023, a swing to profit, then KRW 1.21 billion in 2024, up 43.4%. In 2025 the company posted an operating loss of KRW 3.92 billion, a return to losses.

Operating margins were -0.1% in 2022, 2.9% in 2023, 3.2% in 2024 and -14.1% in 2025. Net income attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 363 million in 2022, a net loss of KRW 3.89 billion in 2023, KRW 1.32 billion in 2024 and a net loss of KRW 3.97 billion in 2025.

Net income including non-controlling interests was KRW 262 million in 2022, a net loss of KRW 3.96 billion in 2023, KRW 1.2 billion in 2024 and a net loss of KRW 4.12 billion in 2025.

The company attributed the 2025 swing to losses to lower sales from delayed investment in downstream industries and higher selling and administrative costs from R&D, per DigitalToday.

Quarterly, Q3 2025 revenue of KRW 7.01 billion came with an operating loss of KRW 336 million, and Q4 2025 revenue of KRW 7 billion came with a widened operating loss of KRW 3.25 billion.

Q1 2026 revenue of KRW 5.27 billion, down 24.6% quarter on quarter, carried an operating loss of KRW 774 million, and Q2 2026 revenue of KRW 8.08 billion, up 53.1%, carried an operating loss of KRW 675 million.

Q3 2026 revenue of KRW 9.23 billion, up 31.6% year on year, carried an operating loss that widened to KRW 2.42 billion. The sum of net losses attributable to controlling shareholders over the last four quarters was KRW 7.21 billion.

The rise in sales alongside a wider loss in Q3 2026 reflects installation and commissioning costs for new equipment, which were booked in cost of sales. The company said a cost-innovation task force is pursuing shared parts and better terms on key components.

Operating cash flow was -KRW 1.55 billion in 2022, -KRW 8.92 billion in 2023, KRW 10.8 billion in 2024 and -KRW 926 million in 2025. The debt ratio fell from 180.6% in 2022 to 36.6% in 2024 and rose to 53.6% in 2025.

05

Industry analysis

Xavis's main end markets are secondary battery and semiconductor inspection equipment demand. In its Q3 2025 results, the company tied weaker electric vehicle sales and a chasm phase to delayed factory expansion at battery makers, per FnGuide.

In this environment inspection equipment demand appears to hinge more on when customers switch technologies than on new capacity additions.

In Q3 2026 the company completed first mass shipments of its rotating-optics battery CT and ESS prismatic battery inspection system, which points to a push into ESS and other new applications beyond conventional battery lines.

For semiconductors, the company has put forward a CT for glass-substrate TGV applications as a direction for growth. The CEO said customers are choosing equipment based on the company's technology, though this is management's own assessment.

Within the KOSDAQ machinery and equipment sector Xavis is a small company, so order flow and the ability to deliver mass-production equipment carry relatively more weight.

In the United States, a supply contract for electrode-cell automation equipment for AI servers was signed, running from November 3, 2026 to May 23, 2027 as stated in the disclosure, which broadens demand beyond batteries. The single contract equals 12.82% of 2025 consolidated revenue, so concentration remains a factor.

06

Outlook

The clearest items for the company are order backlog and mass-production delivery. Backlog remaining at the end of June 2026 was reported at about KRW 13.75 billion, up 72% from the start of the year, per Korea Stock News.

Cumulative new orders for the first nine months of 2026 were KRW 26.23 billion, up 12.5% year on year, per Nae-oe Gyeongje TV. Q3 2026 new orders of KRW 12.19 billion exceeded same-quarter revenue, which points to revenue recognition in later periods.

The company decided to borrow KRW 3 billion in short-term funds from financial institutions for production and parts procurement, a sign that working capital needs have grown with order volume. With capacity raised 1.5 times by the second plant, the key question is how much of that capacity revenue fills from Q4 2026.

The U.S. AI server equipment contract is scheduled through May 23, 2027 according to the purchase order, so delivery and acceptance timing should be watched. The cost-innovation task force's effect will show up in cost of sales in future reports.

The 2026 annual report will finalize full-year results and Q4 2026 profit or loss. Whether the cost of new equipment ramp is one-off or structural will become clearer in results after Q1 2027. Whether the plans translate into profit improvement cannot be determined at this stage.

Bull factors

Order momentum improving

Q3 2026 new orders of KRW 12.19 billion were about 2.6 times the prior-year level and exceeded same-quarter revenue by KRW 2.96 billion. Backlog at the end of June 2026 was reported at about KRW 13.75 billion, up 72% from the start of the year. The pace at which orders convert into revenue will shape sales in coming quarters.

New products entering mass production

The company has also put forward a 3D AXI system and a CT for glass-substrate TGV applications. Product sales in Q3 2026 rose 48.7% year on year, and the range is widening beyond battery applications.

Capacity and application expansion

The second plant started operating in August 2026, and reported production capacity rose 1.5 times. A supply contract for AI server electrode-cell automation equipment in the United States, worth KRW 3.58 billion, equals 12.82% of 2025 revenue and runs from November 3, 2026 to May 23, 2027. Both capacity and application range are expanding.

09

Bear factors

Consecutive losses and a wider loss

Xavis has posted losses for five consecutive quarters from Q3 2025 to Q3 2026. The Q3 2026 operating loss of KRW 2.42 billion widened from the prior quarter, and the net loss attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 2.95 billion. Costs have not kept pace with the revenue increase.

Mass-production costs and cost pressure

Installation and commissioning costs for new equipment were booked in cost of sales and weighed on Q3 2026 results. Development, early mass-production and capacity-expansion costs also sit inside the operating loss. A cost-innovation task force is running, but its effect has not yet appeared in reported figures.

Customer capex swings and revenue volatility

Revenue in 2025 fell 25.8% year on year, which the company linked to delayed investment in downstream industries. Delayed battery factory expansions were also cited for lower Xscan sales.

Quarterly revenue swung from KRW 5.27 billion in Q1 2026 to KRW 9.23 billion in Q3 2026, and such volatility makes earnings harder to forecast.

10

Risk factors

Customer investment timing

Inspection equipment revenue depends on the capex schedules of battery and semiconductor customers. In Q3 2025 the company tied delayed factory expansions to lower sales. If customers delay technology transitions, order delivery can also slip, and backlog does not translate into revenue on a fixed timetable.

Mass-production execution and cost

If mass-production quality and cost do not stabilize, a return to profitability will be delayed. Installation costs for new equipment booked in cost of sales in Q3 2026 are one example. The operating loss widened to KRW 2.42 billion in that quarter, and the timing of cost-innovation gains is still unclear.

Financial capacity and funding

Total liabilities at the end of 2025 were KRW 13.2 billion, and the debt ratio was 53.6%. The company decided to borrow KRW 3 billion in short-term funds for production and parts procurement. If working capital needs grow while losses continue, borrowing costs could weigh more.

12

Overall view

Xavis showed signs of top-line recovery in Q3 2026, with revenue of KRW 9.23 billion and new orders of KRW 12.19 billion. However, an operating loss of KRW 2.42 billion extended a run of five consecutive loss-making quarters, so a return to profitability is not yet visible.

The second plant started in August 2026, raising capacity 1.5 times, and first mass shipments of new products were completed. The U.S. AI server equipment contract is an example of application expansion.

On the other side, installation and mass-production costs weighed on results, and revenue fell 25.8% in 2025 as customer investment was delayed. The debt ratio rose to 53.6% in 2025, and short-term borrowing has been decided.

The next checkpoints are the Q4 2026 profit or loss in the 2026 annual report, the size of the loss in the Q1 2027 quarterly report, and the delivery completion of the supply contract around May 23, 2027.
