02

Business structure

Tailim Packaging, founded in 1976, makes corrugated board and corrugated boxes, converting paperboard into corrugated sheets and then into boxes. Reported first-half 2026 consolidated revenue was KRW 371.7 billion for the corrugated board and box segment and KRW 54.8 billion for the paperboard segment.

In the first half of 2026, the box segment posted an operating loss of KRW 5.36 billion, while the paperboard segment earned operating profit of KRW 6.87 billion.

The split reflects diverging prices: paperboard selling prices rose while recycled fiber purchase costs fell, whereas box makers paid 8.1 percent more for base paper and raised selling prices by only 0.3 to 1.2 percent.

Its product lineup spans agricultural, food and beverage, appliance and parcel-delivery packaging, and a 2025 report said its lightweight box, at about 5 percent of sales, has shifted customer perceptions.

A Korea Paper Association survey put its share of paper box output at 22.2 percent in 2023, the top position, with the No. 2 producer at 20.2 percent. Industry participants describe the top five producers as an oligopoly that has persisted for years and that has contributed to flat earnings.

A September 2026 disclosure showed the company agreed to dispose of its 60.14 percent stake in paperboard subsidiary Dongwon Paper to parent Tailim Paper for KRW 26.6 billion, and to acquire Tailim Board shares for KRW 15.8 billion on the same day.

Paperboard production now sits with Tailim Paper and corrugated board and box manufacturing with Tailim Packaging, and the disposal amount equaled 8.22 percent of consolidated equity at the end of 2025.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual revenue fell from KRW 784 billion in 2022 to KRW 719.6 billion in 2023 (down 8.2% year on year) and KRW 715.4 billion in 2024 (down 0.6%), before rising to KRW 753.9 billion in 2025 (up 5.4%). Among 2022 to 2025, 2022 was the largest year by revenue and 2024 the smallest.

Operating profit declined from KRW 30.9 billion in 2022 to KRW 22.2 billion in 2023 (down 28.2%), then turned into an operating loss of KRW 16.6 billion in 2024. The 2025 operating loss of KRW 4.98 billion was smaller than the prior year's.

Operating margin moved from 3.9 percent in 2022 to 3.1 percent in 2023, -2.3 percent in 2024 and -0.7 percent in 2025.

Profit attributable to controlling shareholders fell from KRW 17.4 billion in 2022 to KRW 5.04 billion in 2023 (down 71.1%), then posted losses of KRW 22.1 billion in 2024 and KRW 9.34 billion in 2025, two consecutive loss years.

Net income including non-controlling interests, at a KRW 9.19 billion loss in 2025, is a different figure from the controlling-shareholder loss. A February 2026 report on the filing said management attributed the 2025 improvement to cost-structure and expense efficiency gains.

Quarterly, operating losses widened from KRW 1.13 billion in Q3 2025 to KRW 5.78 billion in Q4 2025, narrowed to KRW 3.53 billion in Q1 2026, and turned into operating profit of KRW 4.47 billion in Q2 2026.

Q2 2026 revenue of KRW 212.3 billion was the highest of the last five quarters, but Q3 2026 revenue fell to KRW 174.8 billion (down 11.6% year on year) and the company returned to an operating loss of KRW 3.4 billion.

A first-half 2026 consolidated net profit of KRW 292 million was driven by a KRW 4.57 billion income tax benefit, while pre-tax losses widened to KRW 4.28 billion. Controlling-shareholder net losses over the last four quarters totaled KRW 12.4 billion.

05

Industry analysis

The corrugated box industry is sensitive to domestic consumption and raw material prices, and analysts point to a domestic downturn that has continued since 2022. Around 2021, export packaging and parcel demand lifted corrugated prices sharply, but demand later slowed and prices turned weak.

In Q1 2026, corrugated sheet and box prices were reported down 8.4 percent and 4.8 percent year on year, respectively. Over the same period, prices for paper and corrugating medium rose slightly to KRW 428 per kilogram.

In the first half of 2026, the box segment's base paper purchase cost rose 8.1 percent to KRW 509 per kilogram, while sheet selling prices rose only 1.2 percent and box selling prices 0.3 percent.

Utilization improved over the same period, with sheet utilization rising from 81.59 percent to 89.6 percent and box utilization from 78.61 percent to 82.6 percent. Industry commentary says large players are holding on through volume-driven pricing, while smaller makers are losing ground on price competitiveness.

Analysts also cite limited growth in the paper industry and rising cost burdens as factors weighing on investment appeal. The paperboard segment benefited from diverging price trends, but the box segment remains in a period of limited ability to pass costs through to customers.

06

Outlook

The company said through a representative that price increases would begin to show in profitability from Q2 2026 onward, with gradual margin improvement expected. Q2 2026 operating profit of KRW 4.47 billion partly reflected this, but Q3 2026 returned to an operating loss of KRW 3.4 billion.

With Q3 revenue of KRW 174.8 billion, down 11.6 percent year on year, the key question is how much price gains offset lower volume.

In the paperboard segment, selling prices for paper and corrugating medium rose 16.3 percent to KRW 450 per kilogram, while domestic waste paper purchase costs fell 4.5 percent, so whether this spread holds will determine that segment's profit.

The stake disposal and acquisition decided in 2026 may change the consolidation scope and segment mix, so later filings should be read with that in mind. The group-level sale process is another variable.

A June 2026 report said Global Sewoo Group was pursuing a whole-company sale of its paper affiliates, while a September 2026 report said the group had halted the sale review in June, citing synergies among the paper companies.

Rising oil prices linked to the Middle East conflict were also cited as an external uncertainty. The next step is to confirm fourth-quarter and full-year 2026 price effects, operating margin and cash flow in the 2026 annual report.

08

Bull factors

Recovery in the paperboard segment

First-half 2026 operating profit in the paperboard segment reached KRW 6.87 billion, up 231.7 percent year on year. A 16.3 percent rise in selling prices alongside a 4.5 percent fall in waste paper costs widened the margin. That gain offset much of the box segment's loss.

Higher utilization and a profitable Q2

Sheet utilization rose from 81.59 percent to 89.6 percent, and box utilization from 78.61 percent to 82.6 percent. Q2 2026 operating profit of KRW 4.47 billion marked a return to profit, on revenue of KRW 212.3 billion, the highest of the last five quarters. Higher volume provides the base for the recovery.

Narrower losses and a slightly stronger balance sheet

The operating loss narrowed from KRW 16.6 billion in 2024 to KRW 4.98 billion in 2025. The debt-to-equity ratio eased from 115.2 percent to 112.1 percent over the same period.

Total equity at the end of 2025 was KRW 323.4 billion, and a September 2026 report said the group halted its whole-company sale review citing synergies among paper affiliates.

Bear factors

Difficulty passing on raw material costs

In the first half of 2026, base paper costs for the box segment rose 8.1 percent, while sheet and box selling prices rose only 1.2 percent and 0.3 percent. Sheet and box prices had fallen year on year in Q1 2026. Even after expected price increases, Q3 2026 still showed an operating loss of KRW 3.4 billion.

Weaker cash flow and debt burden

Operating cash flow turned negative at KRW -33.1 billion in 2025, after KRW 21 billion in 2024. Total liabilities stood at KRW 362.6 billion at the end of 2025. A Deal Site report said EBITDA fell from KRW 57.9 billion in 2022 to KRW 13.4 billion in 2024, and reached KRW 28.8 billion in 2025.

Revenue decline and ownership uncertainty

Q3 2026 revenue fell 11.6 percent year on year to KRW 174.8 billion. A June 2026 report said the group was pursuing a sale of its paper affiliates, while a September 2026 report said that review had been halted in June. The shift in consolidation scope could make period-to-period comparisons harder.

Risk factors

Industry and cost risk

Domestic demand remains weak and product prices are soft. Oil price increases tied to the Middle East conflict could raise both raw material and logistics costs. If base paper costs rise faster than selling prices, losses in the box segment could widen.

Financial and cash flow risk

The debt-to-equity ratio was 112.1 percent at the end of 2025, with total liabilities of KRW 362.6 billion. Operating cash flow was KRW -33.1 billion in 2025. Controlling-shareholder net losses over the last four quarters totaled KRW 12.4 billion, which limits internal capacity to repay debt.

Ownership and transaction risk

A September 2026 disclosure showed the decision to dispose of a 60.14 percent stake in Dongwon Paper and acquire Tailim Board shares. Conflicting reports on whether the group would sell its paper affiliates leave the future ownership structure unsettled. Follow-up filings and changes in the consolidation scope need to be monitored.

Overall view

Tailim Packaging combines a loss-making corrugated box business with a profitable paperboard segment. After returning to operating profit in Q2 2026, it reported an operating loss again in Q3.

On an annual basis, the operating loss narrowed from KRW 16.6 billion in 2024 to KRW 4.98 billion in 2025, but operating cash flow turned negative at KRW -33.1 billion in 2025.

At the end of 2025, the debt-to-equity ratio was 112.1 percent, with total liabilities of KRW 362.6 billion and controlling-shareholder equity of KRW 302.7 billion out of total equity of KRW 323.4 billion. Bullish factors include the paperboard recovery, higher utilization, the Q2 profit and the narrower 2025 loss.

Bearish factors include the difficulty of passing on costs, the Q3 loss, negative operating cash flow and uncertainty around the group's restructuring and sale talks.

Because net losses over the last four quarters rule out earnings-based multiples, asset-based multiples and the question of earnings recovery are interpreted differently. The next checkpoint is the full-year operating margin and cash flow in the 2026 annual report.
