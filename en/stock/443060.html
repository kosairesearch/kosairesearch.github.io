02

Business structure

HD Hyundai Marine Solution is the marine solutions arm of the HD Hyundai group, focused on aftermarket (AM) parts and services for ships, bunkering (marine fuel supply), eco-retrofit solutions and onboard digital solutions.

In the third quarter of 2026, bunkering revenue reached KRW 293.6 billion, up 38.5% year on year and 45.2% of total revenue. AM revenue was KRW 282.7 billion, up 15.7%, helped by ship parts sales and onshore power plant maintenance such as an Ecuador power facility service contract.

Eco solutions revenue was KRW 39.3 billion, up 17.3%, and digital solutions revenue was KRW 34.5 billion, up 48.1%. The cumulative fleet generating AM revenue reached 10,376 vessels, of which 905 run dual-fuel (DF) engines.

Cumulative long-term service agreement (LTSA) orders reached USD 765 million, with a backlog of USD 586 million. In early October 2026 the company decided to acquire Goltens, an engine service provider founded in New York, for about KRW 331.5 billion (about USD 247.5 million).

Goltens operates 28 service sites in 15 countries and employs about 1,300 skilled staff. Hana Securities noted that core business operating margin stayed stable at 26.7%, while Meritz Securities said in an April 2026 report that low-margin bunkering accounted for most of the external growth.

The company plans to use the deal to expand into vessels outside the HD Hyundai group and into third-party engine and equipment markets.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual results show revenue and profit rising together. Revenue went from KRW 1.43 trillion in 2023 to KRW 1.75 trillion in 2024 (up 22.0%) and KRW 1.98 trillion in 2025 (up 13.6%), rising for two straight years from 2024 through 2025, with 2025 the largest year in the 2023–2025 span.

Operating profit was KRW 201.5 billion in 2023, KRW 271.7 billion in 2024 (up 34.9%) and KRW 350.1 billion in 2025 (up 28.9%), also rising for two straight years. The operating margin climbed from 14.1% to 15.6% and then 17.7%, so profit grew faster than sales.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 151.1 billion, KRW 227.9 billion (up 50.8%) and KRW 269.6 billion (up 18.3%). Net income including non-controlling interests is reported at the same KRW 151.1 billion, KRW 227.9 billion and KRW 269.6 billion for the three years.

The debt-to-equity ratio fell from 173.6% in 2023 to 49.0% in 2024 before edging up to 54.3% in 2025, when total liabilities were KRW 447.6 billion and total equity was KRW 824.7 billion. Operating cash flow rose from KRW 72.9 billion in 2023 to KRW 246.5 billion in 2024 and KRW 324.1 billion in 2025.

Quarterly revenue went from KRW 513.2 billion in the third quarter of 2025 to KRW 516.2 billion, KRW 574.6 billion, KRW 580.4 billion and KRW 650.1 billion in the third quarter of 2026, rising for four straight quarters.

Operating profit over the same five quarters stayed in a narrow band of KRW 90.5 billion to KRW 97.6 billion. The third-quarter 2026 operating margin fell from 18.2% a year earlier to 14.7%, with a larger bunkering share cited as the main cause.

That quarter also absorbed a one-off Goltens deal cost of about KRW 9.0 billion and the effect of a weaker dollar, and an NH Investment & Securities report said operating profit missed market expectations by 9.7%.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders peaked at KRW 98.1 billion in the first quarter of 2026, then fell to KRW 83.0 billion and KRW 32.6 billion.

Pre-tax income for the third quarter was KRW 40.7 billion, and Samsung Securities treated a non-operating translation loss on foreign-currency deposits as temporary. Over the last four quarters, net income attributable to controlling shareholders totals KRW 287.2 billion.

05

Industry analysis

Brokers differ on the shipbuilding cycle. Samsung Securities said in an October 2026 report that a growing operating fleet widens the potential customer base, making the company relatively insulated from concerns that new-build demand has peaked.

Larger new-build deliveries feed early spare-parts supply and AM revenue, and rising new-build volumes have shown up as higher orders for digital solutions.

Environmental rules and fuel diversification are also demand drivers, and the company says growing demand for gas-fuel retrofits and carbon-reduction solutions supports its plans. Samsung Securities also cited LNG facility retrofit demand linked to the Middle East conflict in an April 2026 report.

In bunkering, the easing of Middle East supply disruptions and firmer Dubai crude prices were cited as sales drivers. On the negative side, NH Investment & Securities estimated that the weaker dollar reduced third-quarter operating profit by roughly KRW 5.0 billion to KRW 7.0 billion.

Daol Investment & Securities noted that AM revenue carries a lead time of about three months, so currency effects arrive with a lag. Yuanta Securities said in a May 2025 report that competition is limited in ship AM and commercial vessel MRO. Hana Securities expects expanded engine capacity at group affiliates to support future sales.

06

Outlook

On the company's own schedule, the Goltens acquisition is due to close within 2026, with post-merger integration starting in the fourth quarter. The company said the purchase price can be paid from cash on hand, with no plan for additional borrowing.

Backlogs include USD 132 million for eco-retrofit orders and KRW 271.7 billion for digital control orders. For LTSA, the company secured 80 new contracts and 20 renewals.

In onshore power engines, the company is pursuing a long-term maintenance agreement with Aperion covering more than seven years, and NH Investment & Securities expects related revenue from the second half of 2029.

The floating regasification unit (FSRU) retrofit work has seen delayed orders, and Samsung Securities said the order momentum is a matter of timing. On logistics, the company built a Singapore hub alongside Busan, which it says can cut parts lead times by up to 16 days and transport costs by up to 38%.

Meritz Securities projected in an April 2026 report that product mix improvement would come between the fourth quarter of 2026 and the first quarter of 2027. SK Securities said in a September 2026 report that the company stands to benefit from expanded HiMSEN engine capacity.

08

Bull factors

Goltens widens the AM footprint

Goltens operates 28 sites in 15 countries with about 1,300 staff, and analysts say its network can be combined with the company's parts and solutions supply. Daol Investment & Securities sees room to expand into vessels outside the HD Hyundai group and into third-party engine and equipment markets. Hana Securities sees synergies as a long-term opportunity.

Consecutive AM growth and order backlog

AM revenue set a record for the seventh straight quarter. The LTSA backlog grew to USD 586 million, and 80 new contracted vessels were secured. Hana Securities expects wider LTSA coverage and a higher share of dual-fuel engines to support future sales.

Onshore power and data-center engine servicing

Onshore power revenue, including an Ecuador power facility service contract, has started to show up in results. Yuanta Securities described the medium-term investment appeal from the power engine business as secured. Maintenance for data-center power engines is drawing attention as a future growth driver.

09

Bear factors

Quarterly margin decline and a larger bunkering share

The third-quarter operating margin fell from 18.2% a year earlier to 14.7%. Bunkering's share of total revenue rose from 38.5% in the prior quarter to 45.2%. Meritz Securities described bunkering as a low-margin business, and the gap between revenue growth and profit growth was most visible in the third quarter.

One-off costs and currency drag

A one-off Goltens deal cost of about KRW 9.0 billion hit third-quarter operating profit. The weaker dollar added to the pressure, and net income attributable to controlling shareholders fell 59.1% to KRW 32.6 billion.

Operating profit missed market expectations by 9.7%, although some analysts say it is in line once the one-off cost is excluded.

Delayed orders and peak-cycle concerns

Orders for FSRU retrofits have been delayed, and floating data-center retrofit orders are moving more slowly than the market expected. Concerns about a peak in the shipbuilding cycle still come up in the market. If order timing slips, visibility on new revenue sources could fall.

10

Risk factors

Currency

A stronger won weighs on AM revenue and profit. Daol Investment & Securities pointed out that AM revenue carries a lead time of about three months, so it is necessary to check how much currency pressure has been passed on in selling prices. Hana Securities noted that AM revenue still set a record for seven straight quarters despite the stronger won.

Integration of Goltens

The Goltens purchase price of about KRW 331.5 billion is being funded from cash on hand. Integration starts in the fourth quarter of 2026, so the expected synergies could be delayed during the transfer of staff, systems and customers.

Daol Investment & Securities considers how far group parts and solutions sales extend to outside customers more important than Goltens's own growth.

Bunkering volatility and the cycle

The company attributed bunkering growth to the easing of Middle East supply disruptions and firmer Dubai crude prices. Because bunkering is a low-margin business, revenue and margin could move together if supply conditions shift. Concerns about a shipbuilding peak could also feed volatility in income tied to new-build vessels.

12

Overall view

The third quarter of 2026 showed revenue and profit moving in different directions for HD Hyundai Marine Solution. Revenue rose 26.7% year on year to KRW 650.1 billion, and the AM segment set a record for the seventh straight quarter.

Operating profit rose only 2.2% to KRW 95.7 billion, well below the revenue growth rate, and the operating margin fell to 14.7% as bunkering's share grew, the won strengthened and Goltens deal costs weighed.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders fell 59.1% to KRW 32.6 billion, and the last four quarters total KRW 287.2 billion. Annually, revenue, operating profit and net income attributable to controlling shareholders rose in both 2024 and 2025, and the operating margin climbed from 14.1% to 17.7%.

Strengths include AM expansion through Goltens, a growing LTSA backlog and demand for onshore power and data-center engine servicing. Weaknesses include the lower quarterly margin, currency pressure and delayed orders.

Brokers cut target prices in late October while keeping their ratings, and views on the shipbuilding peak remain divided. The next checkpoints are the Goltens closing by the end of 2026, the operating margin in preliminary fourth-quarter 2026 results, and the 2026 annual report due by March 31, 2027.
