Q2 Profit Turn Amid Lingering Balance-Sheet Strain

Huneed Technologies saw both revenue and operating profit fall sharply in 2025, but narrowed its operating loss and returned to controlling-interest net profit in the second quarter of 2026, even as borrowings rose and operating cash flow stayed negative.

02

Business structure

Founded in 1968, Huneed Technologies is a specialist in avionics and defense communication systems that listed on the KOSPI in 1991 and adopted its current name in 2000.

The business is broadly split between a domestic defense segment centered on tactical communication systems and an overseas avionics parts segment serving aircraft and defense manufacturers.

In the defense segment, the company has held exclusive supply status for the High Capacity Trunk Radio System (HCTRS) within the Tactical Information Communication Network (TICN) program, though the revenue mix is shifting toward maintenance-type performance-based logistics contracts such as the one signed with Hanwha Systems rather than new production.

In avionics, The Boeing Company is both a major shareholder and customer, with Huneed supplying avionics equipment and wiring harnesses for the H-47 transport helicopter among other platforms.

More recently, the company has expanded contracts with General Atomics Aeronautical for unmanned aerial vehicle wiring systems and avionics equipment, broadening its customer base.

Huneed has also pursued adjacent technology partnerships, including business cooperation with Airbus Helicopters and joint development of LiFi in-cabin communication with France's Latécoère.

In Korea's defense and avionics electronics market, companies such as Hanwha Systems, LIG Defense&Aerospace and Korea Aerospace Industries occupy adjacent competitive and collaborative positions.

The company is also cultivating drone and electronic-warfare network businesses based on MANET/FANET technology, though these are understood to still represent a modest share of total revenue.

Earnings analysis

On an annual basis, revenue rose from KRW222.5 billion in 2022 to KRW228.8 billion in 2023 (+2.9% year on year) and KRW230.8 billion in 2024 (+0.9%), before falling to KRW137.2 billion in 2025 (-40.6%), with 2024 marking the largest revenue year and 2025 the smallest within the 2022-2025 period disclosed.

Operating profit rose from KRW12.7 billion in 2022 to KRW17.4 billion in 2023 (+37.1%), then fell to KRW9.13 billion in 2024 (-47.4%) before swinging to an operating loss of KRW12 billion in 2025.

Controlling-interest net income followed the same pattern, rising from KRW9.26 billion in 2022 to KRW17.9 billion in 2023 (+93.1%), falling to KRW10.7 billion in 2024 (-39.9%), and swinging to a controlling-interest net loss of KRW9.99 billion in 2025.

The operating margin improved from 5.7% in 2022 to 7.6% in 2023, then deteriorated to 4.0% in 2024 and -8.8% in 2025, reflecting a clear profitability decline.

On a quarterly basis, the company posted operating losses for five consecutive quarters from Q2 2025 through Q2 2026, with the loss peaking at KRW5.43 billion in Q4 2025 before narrowing to KRW3.27 billion in Q1 2026 and KRW428 million in Q2 2026.

Quarterly revenue also swung sharply, falling to KRW21.6 billion in Q1 2026 (-33.9% quarter on quarter) before rebounding to KRW29.3 billion in Q2 2026 (+35.3% quarter on quarter, -6.5% year on year).

Controlling-interest net income turned positive at KRW243 million in Q2 2026, a swing to profit both quarter on quarter and year on year, though the sum of the trailing four quarters (Q3 2025-Q2 2026) remains a controlling-interest net loss of KRW5.34 billion.

At end-2025, total equity stood at KRW162.8 billion, entirely attributable to controlling shareholders, against total liabilities of KRW148.9 billion, while operating cash flow was negative for two consecutive years at -KRW34.5 billion in 2024 and -KRW36.4 billion in 2025, a continuing strain on the financial structure.

Industry analysis

The avionics and defense communications industry in which Huneed operates is broadly split between Korea's Defense Acquisition Program Administration-led military communications programs and the global supply chains of aircraft makers such as Boeing, Airbus and General Atomics.

The domestic defense segment is in a transition period, as large programs such as TICN move from production into maintenance and performance-based logistics phases, shrinking the scale of new orders compared with prior years.

By contrast, industry observers note that overseas aviation production and maintenance demand, which was depressed after the COVID-19 pandemic, has entered a recovery phase, and Huneed's export share was reported at around 30% as of the third quarter of 2025.

The unmanned aerial vehicle and electronic-warfare network market is growing globally alongside rising defense budgets, and Huneed is expanding into this area by supplying components to UAV makers including General Atomics.

In the domestic competitive and collaborative landscape, companies such as Hanwha Systems, LIG Defense&Aerospace and Korea Aerospace Industries occupy adjacent positions in communications and avionics equipment, while overseas tactical-communications specialists such as Intracom Defense Electronics are cited as competitors.

With a relatively small market capitalization and limited production scale, Huneed maintains a position as a niche-focused supplier relative to larger defense prime contractors.

Prior to that, in May 2026 the company signed two separate H-47 avionics equipment supply contracts with The Boeing Company worth KRW12.45 billion and KRW6.96 billion, each running through 2031.

As of the third quarter of 2025, the company's total order backlog was reported at around KRW283.6 billion, and a new contract with General Atomics in January 2026 was reported to have lifted the cumulative overseas aviation backlog to KRW248.2 billion.

That forecast reflects the analyst's view as of its publication date and should be checked against subsequent regulatory filings. Company representatives explained in a January 2026 media interview that short-term borrowing and bond issuance were arranged preemptively to fund the fulfillment of overseas order backlog.

A key point to monitor is whether the profit turn and loss-narrowing trend seen in the second quarter of 2026 continues when the next quarterly regulatory filing for the third quarter of 2026 is released.

Bull factors

Multi-Year Contracts Improve Revenue Visibility

The wiring harness supply contract signed with General Atomics Aeronautical in October 2026 (about USD77.16 million, running through November 2029) and the H-47 avionics contracts with The Boeing Company signed in May 2026 are multi-year agreements extending through 2029-2031.

These contracts could help offset the revenue gap seen in 2025 as they are recognized over time. The order backlog was also reported at around KRW283.6 billion as of the third quarter of 2025, providing a base of volume for future revenue recognition.

Quarterly Earnings Have Improved Sequentially

The operating loss narrowed sequentially from KRW5.43 billion in Q4 2025 to KRW3.27 billion in Q1 2026 and KRW428 million in Q2 2026. Controlling-interest net income also turned positive at KRW243 million in Q2 2026, both quarter on quarter and year on year.

Revenue also rebounded to KRW29.3 billion in Q2 2026 after bottoming at KRW21.6 billion in Q1 2026, suggesting a possible trough has passed.

Customer and Business Diversification Under Way

Beyond its traditional customer The Boeing Company, Huneed is expanding UAV component supply contracts with General Atomics Aeronautical and continuing cooperation with Airbus Helicopters and Latécoère.

Domestically, the revenue base is being restructured away from new TICN production toward performance-based logistics contracts such as the one with Hanwha Systems.

A brokerage report has also cited an expected expansion of the drone (UAV) and electronic-warfare network (MANET/FANET) business in the second half of 2026.

Bear factors

Sharp 2025 Earnings Decline and Swing to Loss

2025 revenue fell 40.6% year on year to KRW137.2 billion, with both operating profit and controlling-interest net income swinging to losses of KRW12 billion and KRW9.99 billion, respectively. The operating margin also deteriorated sharply from 4.0% in 2024 to -8.8% in 2025. This has been attributed to a revenue gap left as the core TICN new-production volume was worked through.

Growing Balance-Sheet Strain

Operating cash flow was negative for two consecutive years, at -KRW34.5 billion in 2024 and -KRW36.4 billion in 2025. The debt ratio also rose back up, from 40.3% at end-2024 to 91.5% at end-2025.

In January 2026, the company arranged KRW30 billion in short-term borrowing and issued exchangeable bonds, which it described as preemptive funding to support the fulfillment of overseas order backlog.

Significant Quarter-to-Quarter Revenue Swings

Quarterly revenue has swung widely, falling from KRW38 billion in Q3 2025 to KRW21.6 billion in Q1 2026 before rising again to KRW29.3 billion in Q2 2026. This reflects the project-based revenue recognition inherent to the defense and avionics industry.

On a trailing four-quarter basis, the company still shows a controlling-interest net loss of KRW5.34 billion, so whether quarterly improvement translates into a full annual recovery requires further confirmation.

Risk factors

Financial Soundness

Operating cash flow was negative for two consecutive years in 2024-2025, and the debt ratio climbed back up to 91.5% at end-2025. Liquidity was reinforced through short-term borrowing and exchangeable bond issuance in early 2026, but this creates future repayment and refinancing obligations. If the profitability recovery is delayed, the need for additional funding could increase.

Revenue Volatility

Given the project-based nature of the defense and avionics business, revenue is highly dependent on individual project progress and delivery schedules, resulting in large quarter-to-quarter swings.

As the domestic TICN program shifts from new production to maintenance, the contribution from new production-type revenue may diminish compared with prior years. There is also a lag before newly signed overseas contracts are recognized as actual revenue.

Customer Concentration and Foreign Exchange

Revenue is relatively concentrated among a small number of large customers such as The Boeing Company and General Atomics Aeronautical, and major contracts are denominated in US dollars, exposing the company to exchange-rate fluctuations.

Indeed, the October 2026 contract value was converted to Korean won using the exchange rate prevailing on the contract date. Domestic defense revenue is also subject to the budget allocation and program schedules of Korea's Defense Acquisition Program Administration.

Overall view

Huneed Technologies swung to losses on both revenue and operating profit in 2025 before showing a sequential narrowing of operating losses through 2026 and returning to controlling-interest net profit in the second quarter.

During the same period, multi-year contracts with General Atomics Aeronautical and The Boeing Company were signed in succession, partially reinforcing the order backlog and revenue visibility.

However, two consecutive years of negative operating cash flow, a debt ratio that rose back to 91.5% at end-2025, and funding through short-term borrowing and exchangeable bonds remain areas warranting continued scrutiny of the balance sheet.

On a trailing four-quarter basis the company still shows a controlling-interest net loss, and whether the Q2 profit turn is a one-off or the start of a sustained recovery requires confirmation from upcoming quarterly results.

From an industry perspective, the key variable is whether the revenue gap left by the wind-down of domestic TICN production can be filled by growth in overseas avionics and drone businesses.

On valuation, the shares trade below book net asset value, but conventional earnings-based metrics remain constrained by losses over the trailing four quarters.

Ahead of any investment decision, it will be important to track upcoming quarterly regulatory filings and further order disclosures to confirm whether the improvement in profitability and cash flow is sustained.
