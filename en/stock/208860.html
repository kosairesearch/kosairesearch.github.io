02

Business structure

In November 2025, Dasan DMC absorbed DMC, an auto-parts affiliate of the Dasan Group, and its business mix shifted toward automotive electronics and parts. According to a Dealsite report, the legacy software units Automotive, IoT and HIM accounted for only about 3% of revenue in Q1 2026.

In the same quarter, the automotive parts segment generated KRW 31.9 billion, about 97% of total revenue. The parts business supplies Hyundai Motor, KIA and other global automakers as well as major Tier1 suppliers.

On the software side, the Automotive unit worked with a domestic telecom operator to develop telematics services for a global automaker, which launched with the vehicle in June 2026. The company says it also won a follow-on second service development project.

Customers are concentrated among automakers and first-tier suppliers, so an order change from one client can flow directly into revenue.

Competition splits in two directions: in vehicle software and IT services, peers such as Hyundai Autoever and LG CNS are the natural comparisons, while in parts the company overlaps with established Tier1 suppliers.

Management is pursuing a model that uses the parts unit's global network to offer connected-car platforms and software services together. In November 2026, a cooperation agreement with POSCO International and IBCT on Catena-X data ecosystem work was reported.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual results show revenue expansion alongside earnings swings. Revenue rose from KRW 3.49 billion in 2022 to KRW 4.94 billion in 2023 (up 41.4%), KRW 11.2 billion in 2024 (up 127.0%) and KRW 113.7 billion in 2025 (up 914.2%), growing three consecutive years from 2023 through 2025.

Within 2022 to 2025, 2025 was the largest year and 2022 the smallest. Operating loss narrowed from KRW 5.25 billion in 2022 to KRW 2.99 billion in 2023, then swung to operating profit of KRW 1.79 billion in 2024. Operating profit reached KRW 2.76 billion in 2025, up 54.4%.

However, operating margin fell from 16.0% in 2024 to 2.4% in 2025, because revenue growth of 914.2% far outpaced operating profit growth of 54.4%, a dilution pattern tied to the merger.

Attributable net results ran from a loss of KRW 946 million in 2022 and a loss of KRW 6.03 billion in 2023 (a continued deficit) to a profit of KRW 3.25 billion in 2024 (a swing to profit), then a loss of KRW 7.03 billion in 2025 (a swing to deficit).

Consolidated net loss including non-controlling interests was KRW 6.74 billion in 2025, a different figure from the attributable number. For the 2025 deterioration, the company cited higher other expenses from impairment of intangible assets.

Debt-to-equity rose from 13.3% in 2024 to 102.9% at end-2025, when total equity was KRW 55.6 billion, all attributable to controlling shareholders, and total liabilities were KRW 57.2 billion.

Operating cash flow moved from -KRW 7.64 billion in 2022 to -KRW 1.24 billion in 2024 and turned positive at KRW 5.36 billion in 2025. Quarterly results are more mixed.

Revenue was KRW 35.5 billion in Q3 2025, KRW 35.4 billion in Q4 2025 (down 0.2% QoQ), KRW 33 billion in Q1 2026 (down 7.0%), KRW 36.1 billion in Q2 2026 (up 9.6%) and KRW 31.2 billion in Q3 2026 (down 13.6% QoQ and 12.2% YoY).

Operating profit reached KRW 2.95 billion in Q2 2026 (up 141.3% QoQ) before falling to KRW 636 million in Q3 2026 (down 78.4% QoQ, up 121.9% YoY).

Attributable net income swung to a loss of KRW 10.1 billion in Q4 2025, recovered to KRW 2.16 billion in Q1 2026 and KRW 3.93 billion in Q2 2026, then returned to a loss of KRW 1.43 billion in Q3 2026. Attributable net losses over the last four quarters total KRW 5.45 billion.

05

Industry analysis

Demand from the downstream market is driven by the shift toward software-defined vehicles and the spread of connected cars at global automakers. As in-vehicle software and data functions grow, OEMs and Tier1 suppliers have more room to partner with outside software providers.

The EU digital product passport (DPP) rules require digital recording and sharing of raw material, carbon emissions and recycling data for products.

Korean parts suppliers that deal with German automakers must respond to data-linkage demands based on Catena-X, and Dasan DMC has positioned a one-stop support framework through its agreement with POSCO International and IBCT. Auto-parts revenue moves with automaker production volumes and new-model program schedules.

Q3 2026 revenue fell 12.2% year over year, and whether that drop came from automaker volume factors can be checked in the segment disclosures of the periodic report. Competitive positioning splits along two lines.

In software and IT services, Hyundai Autoever and LG CNS are listed IT and software peers that compete for automotive software demand. In parts, the company competes with established Tier1 suppliers for positions in the Hyundai Motor and KIA supply chains.

The company describes itself as the only automotive Tier1 company in the Dasan Group, and says it will play a role in extending AI semiconductors and advanced electronics into automotive. Plans to expand connected-car and mobility platforms carry more weight when paired with the automaker customer base. Because the company is small, a single large-customer order schedule can move quarterly results substantially.

06

Outlook

The company has outlined three plans. It intends to pursue additional new orders from global automakers in the second half of 2026, to offer connected-car platforms and software services alongside auto parts using its parts network, and to build a one-stop framework for Catena-X compliance.

The connected-car telematics service formally launched in June 2026 with vehicle release, and a follow-on second service development project was won. How much of that order flows into revenue can be checked in segment disclosures of the periodic reports.

The Catena-X agreement was reported in November 2026, and no disclosed figures yet show when or how much revenue it will contribute. A near-term variable is the February 27, 2026 termination of the global telematics platform development contract with LG U+, which carried a termination amount of KRW 1.848 billion.

Per a press report, that amount equaled 37.4% of recent revenue at the time of disclosure. The Q4 2026 results have not yet been covered by a periodic report. Given that full-year 2025 results were disclosed on February 11, 2026, full-year 2026 preliminary results in early 2027 are the next checkpoint.

Over the medium term, the question is whether stability in auto-parts revenue and growth in connected-car service revenue advance together. With Q3 2026 revenue down to KRW 31.2 billion, recovery in auto-parts orders is the key item to confirm in coming quarters.

A reported office consolidation, still underway as of the 2026 reporting, is another area where cost-structure changes can be tracked.

Bull factors

Expanded revenue base

Revenue reached KRW 113.7 billion in 2025, up 914.2%, and the automotive parts segment, about 97% of Q1 2026 revenue, broadened the base. Operating profit of KRW 2.95 billion in Q2 2026 rose 141.3% from the prior quarter, showing the potential for quarterly profit improvement. That improvement, however, is a single-quarter result.

Connected-car services moving to commercial stage

The connected-car telematics service formally launched in June 2026 with vehicle release. The company won a second follow-on service development project and said it will pursue additional new orders from global automakers in the second half of 2026. If software revenue grows alongside parts revenue, the business mix could diversify.

Regulation-driven demand

Demand arising from the EU digital product passport rules led the company to sign a Catena-X agreement with POSCO International and IBCT. Automotive data-linkage requirements apply across Korean parts suppliers, which frames them as a new business opportunity. No disclosed figures yet show the timing or size of revenue contribution from the agreement.

Bear factors

Quarterly earnings volatility

Attributable net losses over the last four quarters total KRW 5.45 billion, and Q3 2026 returned to a deficit with a net loss of KRW 1.43 billion. Revenue for the same quarter was KRW 31.2 billion, down 12.2% year over year.

Operating profit rose year over year, yet net results remained in deficit, so the durability of earnings still needs confirmation.

Customer concentration and contract termination

Automotive parts make up about 97% of revenue, which means heavy dependence on automaker customers. The global telematics platform contract with LG U+ was terminated on February 27, 2026, with a termination amount of KRW 1.848 billion. The structure means even one contract termination can affect quarterly revenue.

Shift in financial structure

The debt ratio rose from 13.3% in 2024 to 102.9% at end-2025, with total liabilities of KRW 57.2 billion. Operating margin fell from 16.0% in 2024 to 2.4% in 2025. With operating margin lower, whether the impact of the impairment loss on net results continues in later quarterly earnings is the point to check.

10

Risk factors

Customer concentration and contract structure

Automotive parts represent a high share of revenue, and key customers are automakers and first-tier suppliers. The 2026 LG U+ termination was disclosed as resulting from a contract termination between the prime contractor and the client. Because external contract structures sit outside the company's control, order-volatility risk is high.

Accounting and impairment risk

The 2025 deterioration in net results was explained by higher other expenses from impairment of intangible assets. Future impairment reviews of assets that grew through the merger could again affect net results. Total liabilities at end-2025 were KRW 57.2 billion, close in size to total equity of KRW 55.6 billion.

Ownership structure and trading

DASAN Networks, the de facto controlling shareholder, reduced its holding by 1,842,105 shares in January 2026 after exercising a put option on convertible bonds, leaving a 61.53% stake. A concentrated shareholding can limit the free float available for trading. Governance matters should be tracked through shareholder meetings and later disclosures.

Overall view

Dasan DMC became an auto-parts-centered company with KRW 113.7 billion in 2025 revenue after the DMC merger. The annual path from an operating loss in 2022 to operating profit in 2024 and KRW 2.76 billion in 2025 points to recovering earnings capacity.

At the same time, the 2025 attributable net loss of KRW 7.03 billion and a debt ratio of 102.9% at end-2025 show that financial burden has increased. Quarterly operating profit fell from KRW 2.95 billion in Q2 2026 to KRW 636 million in Q3 2026, and attributable results returned to a loss of KRW 1.43 billion.

Attributable net losses over the last four quarters total KRW 5.45 billion. On the business side, the commercial launch of the connected-car telematics service, a follow-on second project and the Catena-X agreement are new-business signals.

However, customer concentration in auto parts, at about 97% of revenue, and the LG U+ contract termination add to order volatility. With strengths and weaknesses coexisting, the next checkpoints are the full-year 2026 preliminary results and the operating margin and net results in the 2026 annual report.
