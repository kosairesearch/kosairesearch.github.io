02

Business structure

KoreaGas's core business is importing liquefied natural gas (LNG) and wholesaling it to city gas distributors and power generators in Korea, alongside overseas resource development equity holdings.

Domestic wholesale raw material costs are linked to import prices, so international LNG prices and the won-dollar exchange rate drive the cost base. Supply is split between residential city gas and power generation, and residential selling prices are subject to a government-managed tariff system.

When tariffs lag costs, raw material receivables accumulate. At end-September 2026, total receivables stood at KRW 14.65 trillion, of which residential city gas receivables were KRW 13.8 trillion, the largest share. Power generation receivables were KRW 533.7 billion.

The overseas portfolio includes the Canadian LNG project, the Australian GLNG project, the Mozambique Coral floating LNG (FLNG) project, and assets in Myanmar, among six major sites.

According to the company, the combined Q3 2026 operating profit of these six sites was KRW 409.9 billion, versus KRW 105.4 billion a year earlier. Overseas profits are sensitive to LNG selling prices and crude oil prices.

City gas retailers such as Samchully and Seoul City GAS also buy gas from KoreaGas, so wholesale prices and the tariff framework affect the earnings structure across the sector.

04

Earnings analysis

On a reported basis, annual revenue fell from KRW 51.7 trillion in 2022 to KRW 44.6 trillion in 2023 (down 13.9% year on year), KRW 38.4 trillion in 2024 (down 13.8%), and KRW 35.7 trillion in 2025 (down 6.9%), a three-year decline from 2023 through 2025. 2022 was the largest year of 2022 to 2025.

In Q1 2026, revenue fell year on year even as won-dollar exchange rates rose and gas sales volumes increased, with lower LNG import prices cited as the main factor.

Operating profit moved from KRW 2.46 trillion in 2022 (operating margin 4.8%) to KRW 1.55 trillion in 2023 (margin 3.5%, down 36.9%), the smallest year of the period, before rising to KRW 3 trillion in 2024 (margin 7.8%, up 93.3%), the largest year. 2025 operating profit was KRW 2.1 trillion (margin 5.9%, down 30.0%).

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders went from KRW 1.49 trillion in 2022 to a net loss of KRW 761.2 billion in 2023, a swing from profit to loss, then returned to profit at KRW 1.15 trillion in 2024, before falling to KRW 133 billion in 2025 (down 88.4%).

Total net income including non-controlling interests was KRW 132.3 billion in 2025, a different figure from the controlling-shareholder number. Quarterly, Q4 2025 stands out with a controlling-shareholder net loss of KRW 406.9 billion.

A securities firm said settlement costs and subsidiary impairment losses were recognized in that period. Q1 2026 marked the peak, with operating profit of KRW 910 billion and controlling-shareholder net income of KRW 548.6 billion.

Q2 (operating profit KRW 675.3 billion, controlling-shareholder net income KRW 339.1 billion) and Q3 (operating profit KRW 587.8 billion, controlling-shareholder net income KRW 266.7 billion) each declined for two consecutive quarters.

Still, Q3 operating profit rose 51.1% and controlling-shareholder net income rose 206.4% year on year. The main driver of the Q3 improvement was overseas operations, where the company said Canadian LNG operating profit rose from KRW 9.5 billion to KRW 245.4 billion year on year.

The debt ratio fell from 499.6% in 2022 to 396.6% in 2025, and total equity at end-2025 was KRW 10.8 trillion. Operating cash flow swung from negative KRW 14.6 trillion in 2022 to positive figures of KRW 5.89 trillion in 2023, KRW 3.63 trillion in 2024, and KRW 6.54 trillion in 2025.

05

Industry analysis

City gas demand is highly seasonal, driven by winter heating, while power generation demand depends on electricity supply conditions.

Raw material costs track international LNG prices and exchange rates, whereas residential selling prices depend on the timing of tariff adjustments, so when international prices rise, the lag between wholesale and retail appears as receivables.

In 2026, higher crude oil prices lifted overseas LNG selling prices and resource development profits, but the same price increase also raises domestic import costs.

NH Investment & Securities said rising LNG prices are negative because they feed into higher domestic import costs, but that overseas profit gains offset this. Daishin Securities estimated that every USD 1 per barrel increase in crude oil improves annual net cash flow by roughly KRW 50 billion to KRW 70 billion.

Because KoreaGas handles domestic LNG imports and wholesale, its cost position differs from city gas retailers. Its overseas footprint includes producing assets in Canada, Australia, and Mozambique.

NH Investment & Securities also noted that in discussions about a possible integration with the Korea National Oil Corporation, the treatment of the two companies' financial structures is the key issue. As overseas resource development grows as a share of profit, oil price movements have a larger effect on results.

Outlook

No formal annual earnings guidance from the company was available, so the outlook rests mainly on securities firm estimates. In a May 2026 report, Hyundai Motor Securities projected 2026 consolidated revenue of KRW 36.8 trillion and operating profit of KRW 2.14 trillion.

That estimate predates the Q3 2026 results, so the Q4 and full-year numbers will serve as the comparison point. In an October 2026 report, Daishin Securities raised its earnings forecast to reflect higher crude oil prices, and expected overseas profit gains to slightly exceed the increase in receivables.

The same report said receivables would keep rising because of LNG import price increases and the freeze on residential city gas tariffs. KB Securities said in an August 2026 report that overseas oil field results were positive but collection of receivables would inevitably be delayed.

Hana Securities said in a September 2026 report that although receivables are growing, dividend concerns are limited once the exchange rate is considered. NH Investment & Securities noted in a September 2026 report that the domestic segment is weak while the overseas segment improves.

The effect of a possible integration with the Korea National Oil Corporation on earnings and dividends will depend on how the two companies' financial structures are treated. Whether receivables begin to decline, and whether tariff adjustments take place, are the key variables for the earnings path.

Bull factors

Wider overseas resource development profits

According to the company, the six major overseas sites together posted Q3 2026 operating profit of KRW 409.9 billion, up from KRW 105.4 billion a year earlier. Canadian LNG operating profit rose from KRW 9.5 billion to KRW 245.4 billion and was the main contributor.

KB Securities said in a November 16, 2026 report that major oil field operating profit improved 288.9% year on year and drove the earnings increase.

Cash flow sensitivity to higher oil prices

In an October 21, 2026 report, Daishin Securities estimated that every USD 1 per barrel rise in crude oil improves annual net cash flow by roughly KRW 50 billion to KRW 70 billion. Because overseas asset selling prices are linked to oil, higher prices directly lift overseas profits. The same report expected the overseas profit gain to slightly exceed the rise in receivables.

Improving balance sheet trend

Operating cash flow was KRW 6.54 trillion positive in 2025. Hana Securities said in a September 2026 report that dividend concerns are limited once the exchange rate is considered, even as receivables rise.

Bear factors

Outstanding raw material receivables reached KRW 14.65 trillion at end-September 2026, up KRW 466.9 billion from end-June. Residential city gas receivables account for KRW 13.8 trillion of that total.

KB Securities said in an August 2026 report that overseas oil field results were positive, but collection delays are unavoidable.

Daishin Securities estimated in an October 2026 report that domestic wholesale operating profit would fall year on year, reflecting settlement effects. If the residential tariff freeze continues, cost increases are hard to pass through to retail prices.

Earnings volatility

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders swung from a KRW 761.2 billion loss in 2023 to KRW 133 billion in 2025, a fall of 88.4%. Q4 2025 posted a controlling-shareholder net loss of KRW 406.9 billion. Controlling-shareholder net income declined for two consecutive quarters in Q2 and Q3 2026.

10

Risk factors

Crude oil and LNG prices

Overseas profits and domestic import costs are tied to the same price variables. Lower oil prices reduce overseas profits, while higher prices raise import costs and receivables. Results after Q4 2026 could move in either direction depending on this balance.

Overseas production site operations

Overseas profits from six sites drove the Q3 2026 operating profit increase, and Canadian LNG operating profit rising to KRW 245.4 billion became the center of that gain.

If production at other sites such as the Australian GLNG and Mozambique Coral FLNG projects is disrupted or delayed, the overseas profit upswing could weaken. When profits are concentrated in a few sites, a change at one project can have a large effect on consolidated operating profit.

Restructuring and possible integration with KNOC

Integration talks with the Korea National Oil Corporation are underway, and NH Investment & Securities said in a September 2026 report that the treatment of the two companies' financial structures is central. The integration method could change how debt is handled and how overseas assets are allocated. Specific timing and terms need to be confirmed through later disclosures.

Overall view

KoreaGas's operating profit peaked at KRW 3 trillion in 2024 and fell to KRW 2.1 trillion in 2025, but quarterly results in 2026 improved year on year on the back of higher overseas resource development profits.

Q3 2026 operating profit was KRW 587.8 billion, up 51.1% from a year earlier, and the last four quarters of controlling-shareholder net income total KRW 747.5 billion. The debt ratio fell from 499.6% in 2022 to 396.6% in 2025.

On the other side, raw material receivables rose to KRW 14.65 trillion at end-September 2026, and with residential city gas tariffs frozen, the domestic wholesale segment continues to carry a burden.

Bullish factors are the oil price sensitivity and profit growth of overseas operations, while bearish factors are accumulating receivables and import cost pressure. Target prices from securities firms are estimates built on each firm's own assumptions and were set at different dates.

The next checkpoints are Q4 and full-year 2026 results, the 2026 annual report, and whether tariffs are adjusted and receivables decline.
