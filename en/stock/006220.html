02

Business structure

Jeju Bank is a regional bank founded in 1969 to support the economy of Jeju Province, and Shinhan Financial Group held about 64.0% of its shares as of end-March 2026. Deposits and loans form the core business, supplemented by cards, foreign exchange, bancassurance and trust services.

Won-denominated loans stood at KRW 6.67 trillion at end-June 2026, of which 90.5% were in Jeju. Corporate loans reached KRW 4.27 trillion, up 2.9% from year-end 2025, and large-corporate loans rose 36.2% to KRW 292.5 billion from KRW 214.7 billion.

Household loans were KRW 2.07 trillion at end-September 2026, up 11.2% from year-end 2025. Total deposits and funding reached KRW 7.72 trillion at end-September 2026, up 16.1% from year-end 2025, with fixed-term deposits the largest component at KRW 5.35 trillion.

Earnings depend mainly on net interest income, and net fee income in 3Q 2026 rose 36.7% year on year. Management views population decline and a weaker tourism economy as limits on growth within Jeju, and has therefore expanded corporate banking in Seoul and Busan and through outside partnerships.

The main pillar of this push is financing for companies using ERP software, built around a partnership with Douzone Bizon and the 'DJ Bank' brand. Among regional banks, Jeju Bank is in the middle of reducing a loan book that is more than 90% tied to its home province.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual results moved from a peak in 2022 to a trough in 2023 and then recovered. Operating profit was KRW 27 billion in 2022, the largest year in the 2022 to 2025 span, and KRW 2.62 billion in 2023, the smallest.

Operating profit fell 90.3% in 2023 and net income attributable to controlling shareholders fell 77.6% to KRW 5.1 billion. Operating cash flow that year was KRW 222.8 billion, up from KRW 114.4 billion in 2022.

In 2024 operating profit rose 335.5% to KRW 11.4 billion, and net income attributable to controlling shareholders rose 104.2% to KRW 10.4 billion.

In 2025 operating profit fell 13.0% to KRW 9.93 billion, while net income attributable to controlling shareholders rose 33.7% to KRW 13.9 billion, so the two measures moved in opposite directions. The factors behind that divergence cannot be separated using the disclosed figures alone.

Operating cash flow swung from -KRW 140.9 billion in 2024 to KRW 24.9 billion in 2025. On a quarterly basis, operating profit fell from KRW 4.88 billion in 3Q 2025 to KRW 1.62 billion in 4Q 2025, a 66.8% quarter-on-quarter drop, and reached a low of KRW 947 million in 1Q 2026.

It then rose 427.7% to KRW 5 billion in 2Q 2026 and reached KRW 6.37 billion in 3Q 2026, up 27.5% quarter on quarter and 30.6% year on year.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders went from KRW 1.66 billion in 4Q 2025 to KRW 4.21 billion in 1Q 2026, KRW 4.76 billion in 2Q and KRW 5.3 billion in 3Q, three straight quarterly increases. Over the last four quarters, that line totaled KRW 15.9 billion.

In the first half of 2026, provisions for credit losses fell 17.3% year on year, and rising interest and fee income also helped, according to press reports. Over the same period, the loss in other operating income widened.

05

Industry analysis

Jeju Bank's operating base is effectively Jeju Island. At end-June 2026, 90.5% of loans were in Jeju, so the book is strongly exposed to local economic and demographic trends. Management believes limits in the local industrial structure and a weaker tourism economy constrain growth within the province.

It has therefore widened corporate banking by using a Seoul-area financial network and offices outside Jeju. Expansion of structured finance such as club deals, sell-downs and project finance is cited as the main channel for loans outside the province. Household lending is also growing.

Of the KRW 2.08 billion increase in loans between end-March and end-September 2026, household loans accounted for KRW 1.68 billion, or about 81%, so both corporate and household books are driving growth.

On the funding side, deposits reached KRW 7.47 trillion at end-September 2026, up more than KRW 1.3 trillion in one year, which press reports describe as the fastest growth in five years.

Among regional banks, the Shinhan Financial Group affiliation and the ERP-linked financing experiment are cited as points of differentiation.

Asset quality indicators improved, with the substandard-and-below loan ratio at 1.50% at end-June 2026 and 1.48% at end-September, yet the absolute amount of substandard-and-below loans increased over the same period. If the local economy weakens further, the quality of the regional loan book becomes the key variable.

06

Outlook

According to company profile data, Jeju Bank is pursuing an ERP banking model for 2026, a focus on SOHO businesses, AX and DX implementation, stronger capital efficiency and an upgraded consumer-protection governance framework.

In corporate banking, it is working to win ERP-using companies nationwide through its partnership with Douzone Bizon and the 'DJ Bank' brand. Its first-half 2026 earnings commentary said corporate lending grew mainly on funding demand from small and medium enterprises and small merchants.

In loan data, however, large-corporate lending grew fastest at 36.2%. Capital is the next test. The CET1 ratio was 12.13% at end-September 2026, down 1.19 percentage points from year-end 2025. Over the same period, the bank issued KRW 48 billion of hybrid capital securities, which raised additional Tier 1 capital.

In the end-June analysis of capital ratio movements, retained earnings lifted the CET1 ratio by 6 basis points, while the rise in risk-weighted assets reduced it by 105 basis points.

The next checkpoint is the year-end capital ratio and risk-weighted asset size in the 2026 annual report, which must be filed by March 31, 2027. Based on the timing of the 1Q 2026 release in mid-May 2026, the 1Q 2027 results are expected around mid-May 2027.

Balancing loan growth against capital ratios, and the credit-loss trend in loans outside Jeju, will shape results after 2027.

Valuation is usually read through the relationship between earnings trends and price relative to book value, rather than the share price alone. Over past year-ends, the price-to-book ratio alternated between above and below 1x.

In 2023, when earnings were small, the price-to-earnings ratio spiked sharply, and as profit recovered the multiple compressed, a pattern that has repeated. The price-to-book ratio is currently below 1x, and market capitalization is below the book value attributable to controlling shareholders.

How the market weighs the durability of earnings relative to the capital base can lead to different readings. The dividend payout ratio fell from 63% in 2023 to 31% in 2024 and 27% in 2025, as net income grew. Durability of earnings depends on whether the bank can keep growing loans while holding its capital ratios. Valuation reflects both of these factors together.

08

Bull factors

Quarterly earnings rebound

Operating profit rose from a KRW 947 million low in 1Q 2026 to KRW 5 billion in 2Q and KRW 6.37 billion in 3Q, and net income attributable to controlling shareholders has grown for three straight quarters. 3Q 2026 operating profit was up 30.6% year on year.

Shift toward corporate banking

Large-corporate loans rose 36.2% to KRW 292.5 billion at end-June 2026, and loans booked in Seoul and Busan reached 9.5% of the total. The strategy to reduce dependence on Jeju is visible in the shift in the loan mix. Structured finance and ERP-linked corporate lending are cited as new growth channels.

Group backing and deposit growth

Shinhan Financial Group, the largest shareholder with about 64.0%, is backing an ERP banking experiment at group level. Deposits reached KRW 7.47 trillion at end-September 2026, up more than KRW 1.3 trillion in a year. A broader funding base supports loan growth.

09

Bear factors

Falling capital ratios

The CET1 ratio fell from 13.32% at year-end 2025 to 11.95% at end-June 2026 and was 12.13% at end-September. Risk-weighted assets rose about 8.8% in the first half of 2026. The increase in risk-weighted assets outweighed the boost from retained earnings and drove the decline.

High quarterly volatility

Quarterly operating profit fell from KRW 4.88 billion in 3Q 2025 to KRW 1.62 billion in 4Q and KRW 947 million in 1Q 2026 before recovering. Net income attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 1.66 billion in 4Q 2025. The loss in other operating income widened to KRW 44.6 billion in 3Q 2026 from KRW 41.1 billion a year earlier.

Rising absolute credit losses

The substandard-and-below loan ratio improved, but the amount rose from KRW 99.9 billion at year-end 2025 to KRW 101.0 billion at end-June 2026. Overdue loan balances also rose from KRW 101.2 billion to KRW 106.6 billion. As the loan book expands quickly, the rising absolute amount of problem loans is a bearish factor.

10

Risk factors

Capital regulation risk

If loan growth continues to outpace capital build-up, the capital ratios could keep declining. The preliminary BIS total capital ratio was 15.92% at end-September 2026, down from 16.24% at year-end 2025.

Part of the capital build depends on external issuance such as hybrid securities, so funding conditions are a variable.

Concentration in the local economy

At end-June 2026, 90.5% of loans were in Jeju, so a worsening local economy could feed quickly into asset quality. A prolonged decline in population and tourism would weigh on regional loan demand and repayment capacity. Growth outside Jeju is reducing this concentration, but that share is still 9.5%.

Internal control matters

A February 2026 press report said the bank self-detected that the sales share of affiliated fund products exceeded internal limits, and that its internal controls had worked. Given that the strategy includes upgrading consumer-protection governance, whether sales controls take hold is a point to watch. The outcome of any regulatory review has not been reported.

What to watch next

  1. Early 2027 (timing per the company's preliminary-results notice)

    Compare 2026 full-year operating profit and net income attributable to controlling shareholders with the 2025 figures of KRW 9.93 billion and KRW 13.9 billion. The key question is whether the quarterly momentum of 2026 carries into the full-year numbers.

  2. By March 31, 2027

    The 2026 annual report will show year-end CET1 and BIS ratios, risk-weighted assets and substandard-and-below loan amounts. Whether capital ratios recover from the September 2026 level will determine how the capital-related bearish factor evolves.

  3. Around mid-May 2027 (based on the 1Q 2026 release timing)

    Check 1Q 2027 operating profit and net income attributable to controlling shareholders. The points to watch are whether a low similar to the KRW 947 million of 1Q 2026 recurs and whether the credit-cost trend holds.

  4. Around mid-August 2027 (based on the 2026 half-year release timing)

    The 2027 half-year results and report will show the pace of corporate loan growth alongside changes in the CET1 ratio. The key question is whether loan growth and capital recovery happen at the same time.

  5. Timing undetermined (when capital-related filings are made)

    Watch for securities filings tied to capital raising, such as hybrid capital or subordinated debt. Issue size and terms directly affect the pace of capital-ratio recovery and the cost of capital.

Overall view

Jeju Bank's net income attributable to controlling shareholders has risen two years in a row since the 2023 low, and quarterly operating profit climbed from KRW 947 million in 1Q 2026 to KRW 5 billion in 2Q and KRW 6.37 billion in 3Q, so the recovery is visible at the quarterly level as well.

On the other side, loans grew to KRW 6.67 trillion at end-June 2026 and the CET1 ratio fell to 11.95%. Corporate banking outside Jeju and ERP-linked finance are new growth channels, yet 90.5% of loans remain in Jeju, so local economic exposure persists.

Substandard-and-below and overdue loan amounts also rose, so loan growth and asset-quality management need to be read together. The price-to-book ratio is below 1x, but that does not by itself guarantee the durability of earnings. The next checkpoints are the year-end capital ratios in the 2026 annual report and 1Q 2027 results.
