02

Business structure

Agent AI runs renewable power generation as its core business and holds artificial intelligence (AI) as a new business line. Its 2025 annual business report, filed with the Korea Exchange, lists renewable energy as the main business and classifies AI as one of its new businesses.

A renewable-segment contract example is a supply agreement for a Anseong solar power plant disclosed on March 20, 2026, worth KRW 1.7 billion with a contract period ending June 30, 2026.

On the AI side, the company develops an intelligent agent solution that carries out tasks based on user intent, and it has launched EarthRadar, a platform combining global satellite data with AI analysis.

A subsidiary has started an AI drug-discovery platform and begun external validation of six candidate compounds at a European contract research organization. Another subsidiary, Robotaxi Corp., is building infrastructure for a robotaxi service based on Tesla's FSD software and plans to expand hubs to major cities.

The largest shareholder group holds 39.01% as of September 18, 2026. The segment split of the KRW 13.1 billion 2025 revenue is a point to confirm in the notes to the business report.

Renewable power is highly sensitive to government policy and market rules, while the AI business sits alongside intensifying platform competition among global big tech companies. Revenue linkage between the two businesses has not yet been confirmed in disclosed figures.

04

Earnings analysis

On a disclosed annual basis, revenue fell from KRW 33.5 billion in 2022 to KRW 18.2 billion in 2023 (down 45.7% year on year) and KRW 11.8 billion in 2024 (down 35.2%), before rebounding to KRW 13.1 billion in 2025 (up 11.2%). Within 2022 to 2025, 2022 was the largest revenue year and 2024 the smallest.

Over the same span the operating loss grew each year, from KRW 2.84 billion in 2022 to KRW 4.76 billion, KRW 5.01 billion and KRW 9.32 billion in 2025, and the operating margin moved from -8.5% to -26.1%, -42.4% and -71.0%. The key point in 2025 is that revenue rose while the operating loss widened.

In its 2025 consolidated results explanation, the company cited higher cost of sales on construction work, higher selling and administrative expenses such as salaries and commissions, and larger non-operating losses from bad-debt provisions and equity-method investment results.

Parent-attributable net losses were KRW 17.8 billion in 2022, KRW 7.43 billion in 2023, KRW 13.5 billion in 2024 and KRW 18.5 billion in 2025, narrowing in 2023 before widening again.

Consolidated net loss including non-controlling interests was KRW 18.7 billion in 2025, a different figure from the parent-attributable loss.

Quarterly revenue was KRW 3.17 billion in 3Q 2025, KRW 1.15 billion in 4Q 2025 (down 63.7% from the prior quarter), KRW 2.72 billion in 1Q 2026 (up 136.4%), KRW 2.93 billion in 2Q 2026 (up 7.5%) and KRW 2.15 billion in 3Q 2026 (down 26.5% from the prior quarter and down 32.2% year on year).

In 4Q 2025 the operating loss of KRW 5.17 billion and the parent-attributable net loss of KRW 13.5 billion both widened together. In 3Q 2026, the parent-attributable net loss of KRW 3.72 billion was far larger than the operating loss of KRW 671 million, suggesting non-operating items had a large effect.

At end-2025, total equity was KRW 37.5 billion and total liabilities KRW 19.9 billion, with the debt ratio at 53.0%, slightly above 51.8% in 2024. Parent-attributable equity is listed at KRW 37.7 billion, so the gap with total equity is a point to check in the financial statement notes.

Operating cash flow was -KRW 13.3 billion in 2022, -KRW 5.12 billion in 2023 and KRW 1.01 billion in 2024, the only positive year in the span, before turning to -KRW 10.6 billion in 2025.

05

Industry analysis

Domestic solar installations have stalled as government policy shifts. WiseReport, a securities information site, summarized that the abolition of the Renewable Portfolio Standard (RPS) and the introduction of an auction system have weakened the business environment for renewable power.

Wider use of bidding and auctions can reduce the predictability of power project returns and increase volatility in project planning and revenue. The company's renewable revenue depends on power plant construction and equipment supply contracts, so the size of individual contracts can move quarterly revenue sharply.

The 136.4% rise in 1Q 2026 revenue and the 26.5% drop in 3Q 2026 should be read alongside contract timing. The AI market carries large growth forecasts. SK AX data projects that the enterprise AI agent software market will expand from KRW 2 trillion in 2025 to KRW 61 trillion in 2030.

Gartner, however, expects more than 40% of agentic AI projects to be cancelled by the end of 2027 due to cost, unclear value and weak risk controls.

As global big tech companies such as Microsoft, Google and AWS strengthen agent platforms, a small AI player must address platform competition and differentiation at the same time. Analysts have described agentic AI as sitting at the peak of inflated expectations.

Whether the AI business can offset the earnings gap in renewable power has not yet been confirmed in disclosed figures.

06

Outlook

The company's current initiatives fall into three areas. First, its AI drug-discovery platform has started operating through a subsidiary, and external validation of six candidate compounds has begun at a European contract research organization. No date has been set for disclosure of results.

Second, the EarthRadar satellite data platform, after its formal launch, manages about 290 major regions in real time, and the company has said cumulative analysis signals reached 2,451 and cumulative satellite verification footage reached 11,513.

These are company-reported figures and should be read separately from any monetization scale. Third, the robotaxi business is building Tesla FSD-based infrastructure and plans to expand hubs in major cities, but the investment scale and revenue model have not been confirmed in disclosures.

On the financial side, the March 2026 rights offering decision plans to raise KRW 18.7 billion in total, with KRW 15.9 billion for operating funds and KRW 2.8 billion for debt repayment. How much of the operating loss this funding can cover is the next financial checkpoint.

In renewable power, the Anseong solar supply contract disclosed in March 2026 ended its contract period on June 30, 2026, so whether new contract disclosures follow will indicate whether revenue can recover.

The company's progress against any annual revenue or profit targets cannot be tracked, as no such figures appear in the disclosures reviewed.

The next regular report is the 2026 annual business report, and the annual operating loss size and the direction of operating cash flow in that report are the next confirmation points.

Bull factors

Operating loss narrowing

The operating loss shrank for three straight quarters, to KRW 1.98 billion in 1Q 2026, KRW 1.47 billion in 2Q and KRW 671 million in 3Q, after KRW 5.17 billion in 4Q 2025. Margin improvement depends on quarterly revenue scale, so it needs to be checked alongside any revenue recovery.

Net profit in 2Q 2026

Parent-attributable net profit reached KRW 1.65 billion in 2Q 2026, turning positive from the prior quarter's loss. The operating loss in that quarter was KRW 1.47 billion, which suggests non-operating items were positive. The cause and durability should be checked in the quarterly report notes.

AI business pipeline

The AI drug-discovery platform has entered external validation of six candidate compounds, and the EarthRadar satellite data platform has launched formally. Together with the robotaxi infrastructure plan, the scope of business is widening. Revenue contribution from these businesses has not yet been confirmed in disclosed figures.

Bear factors

Four years of losses and wider deficits

The operating loss grew every year, from KRW 2.84 billion in 2022 to KRW 9.32 billion in 2025, marking four straight years of losses from 2022 to 2025. The operating margin fell from -8.5% to -71.0%.

In 2025 revenue rose 11.2% but the operating loss widened, so top-line growth did not translate into better profitability.

Frequent capital raising and dilution risk

A March 2026 rights offering plans to raise KRW 18.7 billion in total, and Daily Union has highlighted frequent convertible bond issuance and cash outflow pressure. Operating cash flow turned to a KRW 10.6 billion outflow in 2025 from a positive figure the prior year. Repeated fundraising could increase dilution for existing shareholders.

Policy shifts and renewed quarterly loss

Analysts point to stalled domestic solar installations as policy changes, with the RPS abolition and auction system weakening the renewable business environment. The parent-attributable net loss of KRW 3.72 billion in 3Q 2026 reversed the 2Q profit back into a loss. Parent-attributable net losses over the last four quarters total KRW 17.6 billion.

10

Risk factors

Liquidity and funding risk

Operating cash flow was a KRW 10.6 billion outflow in 2025, and parent-attributable net losses have continued for four consecutive years. Under this structure, reliance on rights offerings and convertible bonds grows.

If funding increases the share count, per-share metrics could be diluted, making the method and scale of raising capital the key point to monitor.

Business and policy volatility

Renewable revenue fell 45.7% in 2023 and 35.2% in 2024 before rebounding in 2025. Quarterly revenue also swung from KRW 1.15 billion in 4Q 2025 to KRW 2.72 billion in 1Q 2026. If the market shifts toward bidding and auctions, revenue could swing more depending on whether contracts are secured.

New-business execution and governance risk

The six AI drug-discovery candidates are in external validation, and the revenue models for EarthRadar and the robotaxi business have not been confirmed in disclosures.

With the largest shareholder group holding 39.01%, and businesses spread across several subsidiaries, the allocation of resources and transparency of management decisions both need close attention.

Overall view

Agent AI's 2025 revenue rose to KRW 13.1 billion, yet its operating loss widened to KRW 9.32 billion, extending a run of losses that has lasted four straight years from 2022. In 2026 the operating loss narrowed for three consecutive quarters, and 2Q 2026 showed a parent-attributable net profit of KRW 1.65 billion.

However, 3Q 2026 returned to a parent-attributable net loss of KRW 3.72 billion, and parent-attributable net losses over the last four quarters total KRW 17.6 billion. On the capital side, a March 2026 rights offering decision and frequent convertible bond issuance point to heavier reliance on outside funding.

On the business side, renewable power is contracting under policy changes, while the company is expanding into an AI drug-discovery platform, a satellite data platform and robotaxi infrastructure, though the revenue contribution from these has not been confirmed in disclosures.

On valuation, market capitalization sits well below book equity and no PER can be calculated. The next confirmation point is the annual operating loss and operating cash flow in the 2026 annual business report, followed by whether losses continue in the 1Q 2027 quarterly report.
