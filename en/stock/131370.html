02

Business structure

Rsupport is a remote-software company founded in 2001 that builds solutions for connecting to and supporting or controlling other people's PCs and devices.

Its core products are the remote-support solution RemoteCall and the remote-control solution RemoteView, followed by the video conferencing product RemoteMeeting and the AI meeting-minutes service AI:repoto.

According to a third-party summary of the 2025 business report, remote-support and remote-control products made up about 92.5% of revenue (KRW 451.7 billion), while video conferencing, AI meeting minutes and other businesses accounted for about 7.5% (KRW 36.8 billion).

The company says more than 60% of total revenue is generated overseas. On the same summary basis, the Japanese subsidiary RSUPPORT K.K. generated about 44% of consolidated operating revenue (KRW 212.6 billion, versus KRW 193.6 billion a year earlier), making Japan the largest single market.

In Japan, NTT Docomo includes Rsupport's solution as a standard feature of its add-on service Anshin Remote Support, which is reported to have more than 20 million users.

Customers range from call centers and IT help desks to financial firms operating in separated network environments, manufacturing OT networks and public institutions. The company says RemoteView is supplied to about 21,000 Japanese companies.

The competitive landscape is led by overseas vendors that drive product supply and technology development, with Cisco Webex and LogMeIn frequently cited as representative players. The company says it invests 20% of annual revenue in research and development.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual results show profitability falling faster than revenue. Revenue rose from KRW 48.6 billion in 2022 to KRW 50.4 billion in 2023 (up 3.7% year on year). The 2022 decline was attributed to weak results in the RemoteView remote-control line, and the 2023 recovery to expansion in the RemoteCall remote-support line.

Revenue fell to KRW 47.5 billion in 2024 (down 5.7%) and recovered to KRW 48.8 billion in 2025 (up 2.8%). Operating profit peaked at KRW 7.93 billion in 2023 (up 9.4%), then fell to KRW 3.68 billion in 2024 (down 53.6%) and KRW 2.39 billion in 2025 (down 34.9%), a two-year decline in a row.

The operating margin dropped from 15.7% in 2023 to 4.9% in 2025, so profitability weakened even in 2025, when revenue grew.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders fell from KRW 7.26 billion in 2023 to KRW 1.78 billion in 2025, while net income including non-controlling interests was KRW 1.8 billion in 2025.

The debt-to-equity ratio rose to 25.6% in 2024 and fell to 13.7% in 2025, and total equity at the end of 2025 was KRW 96.6 billion. Operating cash flow was KRW 9.59 billion in 2022, KRW 6.74 billion in 2023, KRW 6.61 billion in 2024 and KRW 6.84 billion in 2025, staying in the KRW 6.6 to 6.9 billion range since 2023.

Quarterly results show an operating loss of KRW 961 million in the third quarter of 2025, followed by a wider loss of KRW 1.23 billion in the fourth quarter.

The first quarter of 2026 narrowed the loss to KRW 751 million, and the second quarter turned to an operating profit of KRW 2.58 billion on revenue of KRW 14.2 billion. Third-quarter 2026 revenue was KRW 9.34 billion, down 13.4% year on year, and the operating loss widened to KRW 1.11 billion.

The controlling-shareholder net loss for the last four quarters totaled KRW 1.03 billion. The second-quarter profit came in a quarter when revenue rose to KRW 14.2 billion, which suggests quarterly profit moves closely with revenue scale.

05

Industry analysis

Demand for remote support and control software grew with the spread of remote work and non-face-to-face operations. Reports said the company recorded revenue of KRW 464 billion and operating profit of KRW 185 billion (a 40% margin) in 2020 amid a surge in contactless demand, and revenue reached KRW 525 billion in 2021.

Since then the operating margin has fallen to 4.9% in 2025, a wide gap from the pandemic period. The market is led by overseas vendors, and in a December 2022 report the company said RemoteCall held a domestic remote-support market share of first place, second in Asia and seventh globally.

In Japan, the company secured local carrier channels through its work with NTT Docomo, and a 2025 report cited the company's claim of first place in Japan's remote-solution market.

On the demand side, natural disasters have lifted usage: the company said RemoteCall remote-support cases averaged 8,778 per day in July 2024, up from a prior daily average of 7,128.

The launch of RemoteView OT, which controls remote access to manufacturing OT networks, has pointed to industrial security as a new source of demand. On the technology side, each change in the operating system platforms of supported devices requires fast development of support software.

The company highlights that more than 60% of its revenue comes from overseas, a trait it describes as rare among enterprise software firms.

06

Outlook

The nearest item to confirm is whether the overseas partner structure translates into results. On September 3, 2026, the company held an official launch event for RemoteCall and RemoteView in Jakarta, Indonesia.

The company plans to supply enterprises and institutions through about 80 reseller partners held by its exclusive local distributor PAT, building sales, operations and technical support on that base. The company has said it will apply this partner-centered structure to other Southeast Asian countries.

On the product side, RemoteCall with AI consultation summaries and RemoteView with AI reporting were presented as the core of its Southeast Asian partnerships. In Japan, at Japan IT Week 2026, the company presented an integrated DX strategy combining RemoteView with the anti-ransomware product White Defender.

In August 2026 the company announced free provision of RemoteView, RemoteCall and RemoteMeeting to businesses, local governments and recovery groups affected by the Kumamoto earthquake, with free use through January 31, 2027.

RemoteView OT, a product for manufacturing OT networks, is described as offering zero-trust security controls and AI-based anomaly detection. The AI meeting-minutes service AI:repoto has also been newly launched.

The next checkpoint is 2026 full-year revenue and operating margin, which will appear in the annual business report due by the end of March 2027. Whether the third-quarter 2026 loss widening reflects overseas channel costs can be compared in the first-quarter 2027 quarterly report.

08

Bull factors

Operating cash flow held up while profit fell

Operating cash flow was KRW 6.74 billion in 2023, KRW 6.61 billion in 2024 and KRW 6.84 billion in 2025, staying in the KRW 6.6 to 6.9 billion range while profit fell. Cash generation not breaking down during the profit decline is cited as a bullish point.

The second quarter of 2026 produced a quarterly operating profit of KRW 2.58 billion on revenue of KRW 14.2 billion.

Lower debt burden

The debt-to-equity ratio at the end of 2025 was 13.7%, with total liabilities of KRW 13.2 billion against total equity of KRW 96.6 billion. The ratio had risen to 25.6% in 2024 and fell within a year, which points to a lighter financial burden. With a low debt-to-equity ratio, the company has some room in funding research and overseas partner expansion.

Japan base and overseas partner channels

The Japanese subsidiary accounts for about 44% of consolidated operating revenue, and NTT Docomo's add-on service is reported to have more than 20 million users. In September 2026 a launch event was held in Indonesia, built on a network of about 80 resellers.

If the partner-centered distribution model extends to more Southeast Asian countries, the overseas revenue base would broaden.

09

Bear factors

Operating profit down two years in a row

Operating profit fell from KRW 7.93 billion in 2023 to KRW 3.68 billion in 2024 and KRW 2.39 billion in 2025, a two-year decline in a row. The operating margin of 4.9% in 2025 is far below 14.9% in 2022. Profit did not improve even though revenue recovered to KRW 48.8 billion in 2025, which is cited as a bearish point.

Wider loss and falling revenue in Q3 2026

Third-quarter 2026 revenue was KRW 9.34 billion, down 13.4% from a year earlier. The operating loss of KRW 1.11 billion was wider than a year earlier, and the net loss attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 1.23 billion.

Even with the second-quarter profit of KRW 2.11 billion attributable to controlling shareholders, the four-quarter total remains a loss of KRW 1.03 billion.

Competition from overseas vendors and Japan dependence

The remote-support market is described as one where overseas vendors lead product supply and technology development. With the Japanese subsidiary at about 44% of consolidated operating revenue, a slowdown in Japanese demand could widen swings in results.

Annual revenue has stayed between KRW 47.5 billion and KRW 50.4 billion since 2023, which points to limited top-line growth.

10

Risk factors

FX and overseas revenue mix

Overseas sales account for more than 60% of total revenue, so won-translated results can be affected by exchange-rate moves. With the Japanese subsidiary contributing about 44% of consolidated operating revenue, the yen translation level feeds directly into won-based results. Even with a low-debt balance sheet, this factor can enlarge quarter-to-quarter swings.

Competition and operating-system changes

Because overseas vendors lead the remote-support market, competition is an ongoing condition. Upgrades to device operating-system platforms and new OS releases require the company to develop support software quickly. If this development burden continues without revenue growth, it will weigh on the operating margin.

R&D burden and fixed costs

The company says it invests 20% of annual revenue in research and development. In quarters when revenue declines, this spending continues and adds to the operating loss. The third-quarter 2026 operating loss of KRW 1.11 billion widened from a year earlier.

12

Overall view

Rsupport is a company centered on remote-support and remote-control software, with more than 60% of revenue generated overseas and a large contribution from its Japanese subsidiary. Revenue of KRW 48.8 billion in 2025 is close to KRW 48.6 billion in 2022, so the top line has been flat over three years.

Operating profit peaked at KRW 7.93 billion in 2023 and fell to KRW 2.39 billion in 2025, and after a second-quarter profit in 2026 the third quarter returned to an operating loss of KRW 1.11 billion.

The financial position is relatively stable, with a debt-to-equity ratio of 13.7% and operating cash flow of KRW 6.84 billion at the end of 2025. The price-to-book ratio sits in a low range relative to net assets, but the net loss over the last four quarters means no earnings-based multiple is calculated.

Bullish factors include stable cash generation, low debt, and the Japan base and Indonesian partner channels, while bearish factors include the two-year profit decline, the third-quarter revenue drop, competition from overseas vendors and R&D burden.

The next checkpoints are the 2026 annual business report at the end of March 2027 and the first-quarter 2027 quarterly report in mid-May 2027.
