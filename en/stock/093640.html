02

Business structure

Korea Robot Manufacturing was founded in December 1998 and listed on KOSDAQ in October 2007, focusing on the design and sale of multimedia semiconductors. As a fabless company, it operates no production lines and outsources manufacturing of its developed products to foundry companies.

According to the company profile, its foundry capacity is secured through Samsung Electronics and Fujitsu. Its semiconductor products include AP ICs, Optical MP3 ICs, Multimedia Interface ICs and DAB ICs, and most sales come from the AP IC segment.

The semiconductor division also offers automotive electronics solutions such as cluster, IVI and mobile connectivity, built on in-vehicle chip technology. The other pillar is robotics and drones.

The company has signed MOUs with domestic and foreign drone makers to enter the global drone market and has invested in Hoverfly Technologies, a US defense drone company, to expand its core component supply. Segment weights differ by source.

Butler's tally of 2Q 2026 revenue puts robots at 60% and multimedia semiconductors at 40%, while WiseReport's first-half 2026 summary describes the semiconductor and robot/drone units as holding similar revenue shares. WiseReport's 2024 domestic and export breakdown also includes a quadruped robot product.

The business structure reads as two pillars: fabless chip design that depends on outsourced manufacturing, and robotics and drones.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual revenue fell from KRW 12.9 billion in 2022 to KRW 12.4 billion in 2023 (down 3.6% year on year), KRW 11.2 billion in 2024 (down 9.8%) and KRW 9.03 billion in 2025 (down 19.3%), declining for three consecutive years from 2023 through 2025.

Among 2022 to 2025, 2022 was the largest year by revenue and 2025 the smallest.

Operating profit of KRW 184 million in 2022 turned into an operating loss of KRW 5.87 billion in 2023, and losses then widened to KRW 9.88 billion in 2024 and KRW 10.2 billion in 2025, with the operating margin falling from 1.4% in 2022 to -113.0% in 2025.

WiseReport's cumulative 2025 third-quarter analysis cited lower semiconductor division revenue, early investment costs in the robot business, weaker demand and production line setup costs as the drivers of the widening loss.

R&D spending rose from KRW 1.46 billion in 2023 (11.77% of revenue) to KRW 2.22 billion in 2024 (19.86% of revenue, consolidated basis), raising the burden relative to sales.

Net loss attributable to owners of the parent was KRW 9.1 billion in 2022, KRW 14.1 billion in 2023, KRW 25.9 billion in 2024 and KRW 14.1 billion in 2025, with the 2025 loss smaller than the year before.

Quarterly revenue peaked at KRW 2.72 billion in the fourth quarter of 2025 (up 36.1% from the prior quarter), dropped to KRW 1.56 billion in the first quarter of 2026 (down 42.9%), then rose for two consecutive quarters to KRW 1.59 billion in the second quarter (up 2.3%) and KRW 2.24 billion in the third quarter (up 40.8% quarter on quarter and up 12.0% year on year).

The operating loss narrowed from KRW 2.77 billion in the third quarter of 2025 to KRW 1.22 billion in the third quarter of 2026, but widened to KRW 2.37 billion in the second quarter of 2026.

Among the last five quarters, the net loss attributable to owners of the parent was largest in the fourth quarter of 2025 at KRW 6.52 billion, and the last four quarters totaled KRW 11.6 billion.

Operating cash flow was negative in all four years, at -KRW 2.13 billion in 2022, -KRW 10.7 billion in 2023, -KRW 4.92 billion in 2024 and -KRW 4.15 billion in 2025.

Total equity stood at KRW 70.4 billion and total liabilities at KRW 35 billion at the end of 2025, and the debt ratio fell from 113.8% in 2023 to 49.8% in 2025.

05

Industry analysis

The multimedia semiconductor business is a fabless model built on chip design assets, so production capacity depends on foundry companies. The company profile names Samsung Electronics and Fujitsu as its foundry routes, meaning changes in outsourced production terms flow directly into its operations.

WiseReport describes the company as responding to the reshaping of global supply chains away from China.

The automotive electronics business built on in-vehicle chip technology centers on cluster, infotainment and mobile connectivity products, and the company says it is also pursuing autonomous driving platform projects.

The robot business is still at an investment stage, and WiseReport's cumulative 2025 third-quarter summary cited early investment and production line setup costs as earnings pressures. National defense market entry was presented as a central theme in the same summary.

Set against same-sector listed peers such as DB HiTek, Wonik IPS and PSK, the differences in revenue scale and operating structure are clear. The position of the semiconductor cycle is hard to pin down from company data alone and is better read alongside industry indicators.

The company's direction can be summarized as two pillars: foundry-dependent chip design and a robotics and drone business.

Risk factors

Dilution risk

A convertible bond issuance decision has been disclosed, and if conversion occurs later the number of shares would rise and per-share metrics could be diluted. The conversion terms and timing must be checked in the original disclosure. Total equity of KRW 70.4 billion is sizable, but operating cash outflows continued in all four years.

Outsourced production and supply chain risk

The company has no production lines of its own and relies on foundry companies, with Samsung Electronics and Fujitsu named as its foundry routes. It says it is responding to the reshaping of global supply chains away from China, but changes in outsourced production terms can have a large effect on revenue and costs. Production capacity depends on external foundry schedules.

New business investment burden

Early investment costs in the robot business and higher production line setup costs were cited as main causes of the deterioration in the cumulative 2025 third-quarter results. R&D spending was KRW 2.22 billion in 2024, equal to 19.86% of revenue. Because the timing of robot and drone revenue is uncertain, the pace of earnings recovery is hard to gauge.

Overall view

Korea Robot Manufacturing is a fabless company with both a multimedia semiconductor business and a robotics and drone business.

Revenue declined for three consecutive years from 2023 through 2025, but has risen for two consecutive quarters from the second quarter to the third quarter of 2026, showing a recovery in quarterly sales.

Operating losses have continued for three straight years since 2023 and every one of the last five quarters was in the red, although the third-quarter 2026 operating loss of KRW 1.22 billion was smaller than a year earlier.

Operating cash flow was negative in all four years of the disclosed period, and the debt ratio at the end of 2025 was 49.8%, lower than in 2023.

Bullish factors include the quarterly revenue recovery, the parallel two-pillar business and the lower debt ratio, while bearish factors include prolonged operating losses, a shrinking revenue base and possible dilution from the convertible bond decision.

On valuation, the price-to-earnings ratio is not calculated, and the price-to-book ratio sits within the range of annual values since 2022. The next checkpoints are the operating margin in the 2026 annual report and the revenue trend in the first quarter of 2027.
