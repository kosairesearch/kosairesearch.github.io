02

Business structure

Founded in 2019, Kanaph Therapeutics operates a platform that analyzes large-scale human genome data through its proprietary disease signature discovery system to identify drug targets.

Its business model follows a relay structure in which preclinical assets are out-licensed early to domestic pharmaceutical companies, developed through early clinical trials with partners, and then re-licensed to global pharmaceutical companies once clinical data is secured.

According to a February 2026 report by Eugene Investment & Securities, the 2024 product revenue mix was 82.4% from out-licensing and 17.6% from research services.

The company's active pipeline totals seven assets, comprising two bispecific antibodies, three small-molecule compounds, and two ADC assets including a platform.

Key pipeline assets include KNP-101, a bispecific antibody targeting anti-FAP and mutant IL-12 co-developed with Dong-A ST; KNP-701, a bispecific ADC simultaneously targeting EGFR and cMET co-developed with GC Biopharma; KNP-502 and KNP-504, dual EP2/EP4 inhibitor and SOS1 inhibitor assets that have been fully out-licensed to Oscotec and Yuhan, respectively; and KNP-301, a wet age-related macular degeneration candidate.

KNP-502 was out-licensed to Oscotec in 2022, and the U.S. FDA has since approved an IND for a Phase 1 clinical trial, with commercialization now progressing.

Strategic investors include GC Biopharma, the second-largest pre-IPO shareholder with a 13.06% stake, and Kang Jung-suk, Chairman of Dong-A Socio Holdings, the third-largest shareholder with an 8.5% stake, with Lotte Biologics also holding an equity stake.

Domestically listed peers working in bispecific antibody and ADC modalities include ABL Bio, LigaChem Biosciences, and Alteogen.

04

Earnings analysis

Kanaph Therapeutics posted 2025 revenue of KRW 5.56 billion, up 30.8% from KRW 4.25 billion in 2024. Over the same period, the operating loss narrowed to KRW 4.27 billion from KRW 4.72 billion, a continued loss with a reduced magnitude, and the operating margin improved from -111.1% in 2024 to -76.8% in 2025.

In contrast, the net loss attributable to controlling interests widened to KRW 35.6 billion in 2025 from KRW 28.3 billion in 2024, a continued loss with an expanded magnitude, and net loss including non-controlling interests was likewise KRW 35.6 billion.

The widening of the net loss despite the narrower operating loss appears to reflect changes in non-operating items.

At the end of 2025, total equity stood at KRW 12.4 billion, matching the controlling interest equity, while total liabilities were KRW 3.08 billion, producing a debt ratio of 24.9%, an improvement from -125.8% in 2024.

Operating cash flow deteriorated to -KRW 7.17 billion in 2025 from -KRW 3.23 billion in 2024, reflecting increased R&D spending. On a quarterly basis, revenue declined 4.1% quarter over quarter to KRW 74.96 million in Q2 2026 from KRW 78.16 million in Q1 2026.

The operating loss narrowed to KRW 4.7 billion in Q2 2026 from KRW 5.42 billion in Q1 2026, marking a continued loss with a reduced magnitude over two consecutive quarters, while the net loss attributable to controlling interests followed the same pattern, narrowing to KRW 4.34 billion from KRW 5.32 billion.

The sum of net losses attributable to controlling interests over the most recent four quarters (Q1 2025 through Q4 2025) was KRW 35.6 billion.

05

Industry analysis

Across the global pharmaceutical industry, out-licensing transactions for oncology and ophthalmology treatments using next-generation modalities such as bispecific antibodies and ADCs remain active.

Domestically, companies including ABL Bio, LigaChem Biosciences, and Alteogen have signed licensing agreements with global pharmaceutical firms, intensifying competition in the bispecific antibody and ADC space.

Kanaph's KNP-701 was selected for a government-backed SME technology innovation program (Scale-up TIPS) and received partial development funding in 2025.

The company presented at the World ADC conference in San Diego in October 2026, where it disclosed key data on its next-generation bispecific ADC pipeline asset KNP-701 to raise its global profile.

However, the company has drawn criticism that it lacks a track record of global out-licensing deals and human clinical trial data, and some observers note that its core pipeline remains at the preclinical stage, raising questions about feasibility.

In South Korea's biotech sector, a wave of biotech IPOs including Kanaph's in 2026 has been interpreted as a signal of recovering investor sentiment toward biotech that had previously been subdued.

06

Outlook

In a September 2026 interview, the company outlined its post-IPO strategy as 'Kanaph 2.0,' stating that it would directly invest in core pipeline assets through clinical stages before pursuing out-licensing deals with global pharmaceutical companies.

CEO Lee Byung-chul set a goal of pursuing an out-licensing deal within the year for the wet AMD candidate KNP-301. During the June 2026 BIO USA conference in San Diego, the company said it was in discussions with multiple global companies regarding KNP-301 licensing and expects the possibility of signing a term sheet.

At a February 2026 IPO briefing, the company set a target to achieve at least one new clinical entry and one out-licensing or co-development outcome annually, aiming for a breakeven conversion by 2028. KNP-504, out-licensed to Yuhan, is reportedly targeting clinical entry within the year.

The SHP2 inhibitor KNP-503 is reportedly in licensing discussions with a domestic pharmaceutical company.

On October 26, 2026, the company said it was shifting its business development strategy to directly conduct early clinical trials for key pipeline assets to raise their value, while also actively pursuing global out-licensing.

Bull factors

Early Out-Licensing Model with Multiple Partnerships

Kanaph has signed a cumulative five out-licensing and co-development agreements with Dong-A ST, GC Biopharma, Oscotec, Yuhan, and Lotte Biologics.

As of the end of the prior year, cumulative out-licensing value excluding royalties stood at roughly KRW 774.8 billion, with cumulative upfront payments already received of about KRW 15.9 billion. Among these, KNP-502 has received a U.S. FDA Phase 1 IND approval and is advancing toward commercialization.

Revenue Growth and Improving Operating Loss Ratio

2025 revenue grew 30.8% year over year to KRW 5.56 billion, and the operating margin improved from -111.1% to -76.8%. The operating loss also narrowed from KRW 5.42 billion in Q1 2026 to KRW 4.7 billion in Q2 2026.

While losses continue given the company's R&D-intensive nature, the direction of profitability improvement has been maintained.

Multi-Modality Pipeline and Strategic Shareholder Base

The company holds seven pipeline assets spanning bispecific antibodies, small molecules, and ADCs.

Strategic investors including GC Biopharma, Dong-A Socio Holdings Chairman Kang Jung-suk, and Lotte Biologics have set a three-year lock-up period and agreed to jointly exercise voting rights during that time, establishing a basis for long-term cooperation. This structure is viewed as helping to diversify drug development risk.

Bear factors

Widening Net Loss and Continued Cash Outflow

The net loss attributable to controlling interests widened to KRW 35.6 billion in 2025 from KRW 28.3 billion in 2024. Operating cash flow also deteriorated to -KRW 7.17 billion in 2025 from -KRW 3.23 billion in 2024. As R&D investment increased, the income statement showed a correspondingly wider loss.

Gap in Global Licensing Track Record and Clinical Data

As of October 2026, the company has drawn criticism for lacking global out-licensing experience and human clinical trial data. This weakness, flagged since the IPO, is compounded by the fact that core pipeline assets remain at a pre-clinical-entry stage. Such gaps could affect negotiating leverage in talks with global pharmaceutical companies.

Small Absolute Revenue Base

Q2 2026 revenue was KRW 74.96 million, down 4.1% from the prior quarter. With annual revenue still in the low single-digit billions of won, its contribution relative to operating costs remains limited.

Even with continued revenue growth, the financial structure makes an exit from operating losses difficult in the near term.

Risk factors

Clinical and Licensing Delay Risk

Core pipeline assets such as KNP-301 and KNP-101 remain in preclinical or early clinical stages, so delays in clinical entry or global licensing beyond expectations could undermine the assumptions underlying valuation.

If the company's stated goal of at least one out-licensing deal per year is not achieved, it could also affect the 2028 breakeven target.

Liquidity and Funding Risk

As an R&D-intensive company, Kanaph continues to show negative operating cash flow, and the outflow widened in 2025 compared to the prior year. If additional upfront payments or milestone inflows from licensing deals do not materialize before IPO proceeds are exhausted, further fundraising could become necessary.

Partner Dependence and Governance Risk

A substantial portion of revenue stems from a small number of out-licensing and co-development agreements, meaning the termination or modification of any single contract could materially affect results.

The relatively high equity stakes held by strategic investors such as GC Biopharma and Dong-A Socio Holdings also raise the possibility that governance interests may diverge from those of general shareholders.

Overall view

Kanaph Therapeutics is a drug development company that has built a multi-modality pipeline spanning bispecific antibodies, small molecules, and ADCs on top of a human genome-based target discovery platform. 2025 revenue grew 30.8% year over year to KRW 5.56 billion, and the operating loss narrowed to KRW 4.27 billion from the prior year, but the net loss attributable to controlling interests widened to KRW 35.6 billion.

Both the operating loss and net loss continued into Q1 and Q2 2026, though the loss size showed a modest quarter-over-quarter narrowing trend.

Under its 'Kanaph 2.0' strategy, the company is simultaneously pursuing direct clinical-stage development of core pipeline assets and global out-licensing, targeting a breakeven conversion by 2028.

However, it has yet to secure a track record of global licensing deals or human clinical data, making future clinical progress on assets such as KNP-301 and the conclusion of licensing agreements key indicators of the pace of earnings improvement.

Before making investment decisions, it would be useful to monitor quarterly profit and loss trends, the pace of cash consumption, and milestone achievement across the pipeline.
