02

Business structure

CMTX makes silicon parts and materials used in semiconductor manufacturing equipment, and listed on KOSDAQ on November 20, 2025.

A pre-listing analysis dated October 30, 2025 showed that silicon parts accounted for 97.5% of 2024 sales, other products such as ceramic and sapphire parts for 2.0%, and merchandise for 0.5%.

Press reports say the company performs the full process in-house, from ingot production through machining, cleaning and inspection, and that its subsidiary produces silicon ingots, internalizing about 90% of the silicon material it currently uses.

The main parts wear down in plasma processes, and industry reports say shorter replacement cycles are emerging as process nodes shrink. The business model is direct supply to chipmakers through the aftermarket, with customers reported to include Samsung Electronics, Micron, TSMC and Kioxia.

Production is based in Gumi, Gyeongbuk, with annual ingot capacity of 200 tons, and a second plant (M Campus) of about 15,000 pyeong is under construction next to the existing site.

Overseas revenue in the first half of 2026 was KRW 59.9 billion, up from KRW 47.6 billion a year earlier, and China accounted for about 75% of overseas sales at KRW 44.9 billion. Customer A still accounted for 74.7% of first-half 2026 revenue.

Competitive positioning is discussed mainly in terms of the shift of memory customers toward aftermarket sourcing and the degree of in-house material supply.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual results show sales and operating profit growing together. Revenue rose from KRW 108.7 billion in 2024 to KRW 160.6 billion in 2025, up 47.8% year on year, and operating profit rose from KRW 23.6 billion to KRW 51.8 billion, up 119.1%.

The operating margin climbed from 21.8% in 2024 to 32.3% in 2025, and a company official described 2025 as a record result.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders, however, went from KRW 13.5 billion in 2024 to a net loss of KRW 25.1 billion in 2025, and net income for the year turned into a net loss of KRW 21.5 billion.

The company attributed the swing to recognizing valuation losses on RCPS, and a rise in operating profit alongside a net loss shows how valuation items can move net income sharply.

Leverage moved from a debt-to-equity ratio of -575.2% in 2024, reflecting full capital impairment, to 51.0% at the end of 2025, when total equity was KRW 182.4 billion (KRW 178.1 billion attributable to controlling shareholders) and total liabilities were KRW 93 billion.

Operating cash flow was KRW 35.5 billion in 2024 and KRW 36.2 billion in 2025.

Quarterly figures show operating profit of KRW 15.5 billion in the third quarter of 2025, KRW 10 billion in the fourth quarter (down 35.3% from the prior quarter), and a recovery to KRW 13.5 billion in the first quarter of 2026 and KRW 15.6 billion in the second quarter.

In the third quarter of 2026, revenue reached KRW 48.4 billion, up 18.1% year on year, but operating profit fell to KRW 10.5 billion, down 32.2% year on year, and net income attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 5.08 billion, down 67.0% year on year.

Press reports attributed the third-quarter margin decline to higher operating costs from new facilities and hiring. Net income attributable to controlling shareholders over the most recent four quarters (Q4 2025 to Q3 2026) totaled KRW 32.2 billion.

05

Industry analysis

Demand from the front-end market moves with utilization and process investment at memory makers and foundries such as TSMC. In June 2026 a company official said memory fabs were running at full capacity and that the company was also running its production lines at full capacity.

Press reports said expanding AI server investment had lifted NAND prices and memory fab utilization.

The same report said 70% to 80% of TSMC's 2026 capital spending was concentrated on leading-edge processes, which it expected to widen the application of CMTX parts, and it expected 18 products to pass qualification tests and 5 to enter mass production in 2026.

In cycle terms, demand is benefiting from higher memory utilization and from replacement needs created by finer process nodes, while in the third quarter of 2026 the cost of new capacity weighed on margins.

On competitive positioning, press coverage described CMTX as the only domestic company covering the full process from ingot production through inspection. IBK Securities cited the high degree of in-house material supply as a source of cost and quality competitiveness.

Changes in the pace of aftermarket sourcing and in customers' equipment investment plans could still move demand sharply.

06

Outlook

The company has said it will continue to grow by expanding supply to customers. A KRW 17.7 billion new facility investment disclosed on December 9, 2025 is aimed at expanding production capacity and building long-term growth infrastructure, with an investment period from January 1 to December 31, 2026.

Through the B building of Gumi plant No. 2, annual capacity is planned to rise about 80% from KRW 250 billion to about KRW 450 billion, with completion in December 2026 and mass production from January 2027. With utilization at about 80%, reports expected greater capacity to take on new orders after the expansion.

On September 28, 2026, IBK Securities initiated coverage with a Buy rating and a target price of KRW 115,000, a figure reflecting its estimate at that date. The same report said the customer base had broadened beyond Samsung Electronics to Micron and TSMC and that operating margins in the 30% range were continuing.

On diversification, sales to customers other than the main customer rose by about 95% in the first half of 2026. The points to confirm are whether the new capacity runs as planned and how far the higher third-quarter operating costs carry into the next quarters' margins.

08

Bull factors

Operating margin in the 30% range

The operating margin rose from 21.8% in 2024 to 32.3% in 2025, and according to IBK Securities it held at 31.6% in the first half of 2026. The in-house supply of about 90% of silicon material is cited as a cost advantage. Bulls argue that if this margin holds, higher sales after the expansion could translate into profit.

Overseas sales and customer diversification

Overseas sales in the first half of 2026 reached KRW 59.9 billion, about KRW 12.3 billion more than a year earlier, and sales to customers other than the main customer rose about 95%. Customer A's share fell from 84.6% to 74.7% over the same period. Taiwan and Singapore sales also roughly doubled, which bulls read as a sign of broader supply.

Capacity expansion and utilization headroom

The B building expansion at Gumi plant No. 2 is planned to lift annual capacity from KRW 250 billion to about KRW 450 billion, with mass production scheduled for January 2027. With utilization at about 80%, the expansion is seen as creating room to take new orders. IBK Securities presented a Buy view and a KRW 115,000 target price on September 28, 2026.

09

Bear factors

Higher operating costs in the third quarter

Operating profit in the third quarter of 2026 was KRW 10.5 billion, down 32.2% year on year and 32.7% from the prior quarter. Press reports attributed the lower profitability to higher operating costs from new facilities and hiring. Bears worry that costs could keep rising before the new capacity contributes.

Concentration in customers and regions

Customer A still accounted for 74.7% of first-half 2026 revenue. China accounted for about 75% of overseas sales, at KRW 44.9 billion. Bears argue that changes in one customer's equipment investment or regional demand flow directly into revenue.

Net income swings from valuation items

Net loss attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 25.1 billion in 2025, and the net loss for the year was KRW 21.5 billion, even though operating profit was KRW 51.8 billion. The company attributed this to RCPS valuation losses.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders also fell 65.1% in the fourth quarter of 2025 and dropped to KRW 5.08 billion in the third quarter of 2026.

Risk factors

Customer and regional concentration

Customer A accounted for 74.7% of first-half 2026 revenue, and about 75% of overseas sales came from China. Changes in a major customer's investment plans or regional regulation could significantly affect revenue and profit. Diversification is under way, but the pace of the shift still needs to be confirmed.

Execution risk and cost burden from expansion

Completion of the B building is planned for December 2026, with mass production from January 2027. Any delay or low initial utilization could leave depreciation and labor costs weighing on profit first. The rise in operating costs in the third quarter of 2026 illustrates this pressure.

Valuation items and legal uncertainty

Net income swung into a loss in 2025 because of RCPS valuation losses. A June 2026 brokerage report cited the outcome of an appeal in the litigation with Lam Research as an uncertainty. Valuation items and litigation results can move net income independently of operating performance.

Overall view

CMTX grew 2025 revenue to KRW 160.6 billion and operating profit to KRW 51.8 billion, expanding both scale and profitability.

However, valuation losses on RCPS turned net income attributable to controlling shareholders into a loss of KRW 25.1 billion in 2025, so the gap between operating performance and net income was wide.

Quarterly revenue rose to KRW 44.1 billion, KRW 47.9 billion and KRW 48.4 billion in 2026, but third-quarter operating profit fell to KRW 10.5 billion, down 32.2% year on year, and press reports attribute this to higher costs from new facilities and hiring.

Strengths include the operating margin in the 30% range, wider overseas sales, the Gumi plant No. 2 expansion and in-house silicon material supply. Weaknesses include Customer A's 74.7% share, concentration of overseas sales in China and net income volatility from valuation items.

Completion of the B building is planned for December 2026 with mass production from January 2027, so whether margins recover will be seen in quarters after that.

The 2026 annual business report, the next periodic filing, must be submitted by March 31, 2027, and it will show the valuation items and customer mix on a disclosed basis.
