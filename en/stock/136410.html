02

Business structure

Assems is a specialist in hot-melt film adhesives, founded in 2003. Based on 2024 figures, eco-friendly materials, including solvent-free films and functional coatings, accounted for 63.4% of sales, automotive functional fabrics for 28.5%, and other items such as processing and equipment for 8.1%.

In amounts, that is KRW 362 million for eco-friendly materials, KRW 163 million for automotive fabrics and KRW 47 million for other items.

Global brands including Nike, Adidas and New Balance are cited as key footwear customers, and the company says its share has grown as these customers adopt more eco-friendly materials.

In automotive, the company supplies hot-melt sunroof materials, and a 2025 brokerage report listed NX5 mass production planned for 2026 and NQ6 for 2027. The overseas production base is also expanding.

In its 2025 annual results announcement, the company said utilization at its Vietnamese and Indonesian subsidiaries rose sharply and that a higher overseas sales share helped lower production costs.

By product, release-paper-free film sales rose 11.6% quarter on quarter in the second quarter of 2026, and press reports say Ultranet sales rose 105.4% quarter on quarter in the third quarter of 2026.

Waterless dyeing yarn was presented as developed in a 2022 brokerage report, but press reports say its launch slipped as product registration talks with global customers took longer.

Compared with larger listed chemical companies, Assems' sales base is small, so individual product developments carry more weight in its results.

04

Earnings analysis

On a filed-results basis, annual revenue fell from KRW 53.1 billion in 2022 to KRW 47.7 billion in 2023 (down 10.0% year on year), then rose to KRW 57.2 billion in 2024 (up 19.8%) and KRW 60.3 billion in 2025 (up 5.4%), with revenue increasing for two consecutive years from 2024 to 2025.

Operating profit went from KRW 8.69 billion in 2022 to KRW 6.01 billion in 2023 (down 30.8%), the low point, before recovering to KRW 9.22 billion in 2024 (up 53.5%) and KRW 12 billion in 2025 (up 30.1%).

The operating margin was 16.4% in 2022, 12.6% in 2023, 16.1% in 2024 and 19.9% in 2025, the highest of the 2022–2025 period.

The company attributes the profit recovery to a higher share of high-margin new products, lower production costs from a larger overseas subsidiary sales share, and lower raw material purchase prices.

Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders went from KRW 3.12 billion in 2023 (down 56.9%) to KRW 6.02 billion in 2024 (up 92.8%) and KRW 8.82 billion in 2025 (up 46.5%).

Net profit including non-controlling interests was KRW 8.98 billion in 2025, reported separately, and the gap between the two figures is small.

On the balance sheet, the debt-to-equity ratio fell from 68.6% in 2022 to 55.4% in 2025, and total equity at the end of 2025 was KRW 73.9 billion, of which KRW 71.6 billion belonged to controlling shareholders.

Operating cash flow rose from KRW 691 million in 2022 to KRW 8.03 billion in 2025, moving in the same direction as profit. Within the filed quarters from the third quarter of 2025 to the third quarter of 2026, the third quarter of 2025 had the highest revenue (KRW 15.1 billion) and operating profit (KRW 3.83 billion).

Operating profit then fell to KRW 1.05 billion in the fourth quarter of 2025, down 72.5% quarter on quarter. It recovered to KRW 2.42 billion in the first quarter of 2026 (up 129.9%), then KRW 2.22 billion in the second quarter and KRW 2.25 billion in the third quarter.

Third-quarter revenue of KRW 13 billion, however, was down 14.0% year on year, so profit recovery has not yet come with sales growth.

05

Industry analysis

Assems' core demand is tied to the footwear consumption cycle and the automotive new-model cycle. In its first-half 2026 results briefing, the company said a slowdown in global consumer markets and production adjustments by customers had some effect.

Footwear demand is seasonal and concentrates around global consumer peaks such as Black Friday, Singles' Day and the back-to-school season, which weighs more heavily on second-half sales. The company has highlighted that sales expand as the year progresses.

In automotive, volumes depend on whether specific vehicle models adopt the material, so mass-production timing for a given model feeds directly into results. Demand for eco-friendly materials is another variable.

A 2025 brokerage report noted that the company developed recycled materials in September 2024 in response to European environmental regulation. On competition, the company says its share of eco-friendly adhesives has grown as global customers adopt more of its products.

Raw material prices also drive profitability; a 2024 brokerage analysis said stabilizing prices for inputs such as TPU resin and fabrics could widen margin gains.

Compared with larger listed chemical companies, Assems' sales base is small, so customer wins and the speed of product adoption matter more for its position than the stage of the industry cycle.

06

Outlook

The company reported first-half 2026 revenue of KRW 25.4 billion and operating profit of KRW 4.6 billion, both down year on year, and said it had laid the groundwork for a second-half recovery. It expects second-half benefits from global consumer peaks including Black Friday, Singles' Day and the back-to-school season.

It plans to expand supply to global brands and grow Ultranet and functional film sales to diversify its product portfolio. Its statements are directional rather than quantitative.

In its first-quarter 2026 results briefing, it said sales were somewhat constrained by external conditions but that higher-margin product mix confirmed profitability gains. Press reports say volumes rose in the third quarter as more models adopted Ultranet.

Third-quarter sales were still below the prior-year level, so whether fourth-quarter peak-season volumes lift revenue year on year is the next checkpoint. In automotive, the key question is whether the planned 2026 NX5 mass production shows up in actual volumes.

The timing of waterless dyeing yarn remains uncertain, since it depends on product registration talks with global customers. Sequential recovery is visible, but a year-on-year revenue recovery has not yet appeared.

08

Bull factors

Wider adoption of eco-friendly materials

The company says its share grew as global customers including Nike, Adidas and New Balance adopted more eco-friendly materials. A larger share of high-margin eco-friendly adhesives underpinned the 19.9% operating margin in 2025. In 2024, that segment generated KRW 362 million, or 63.4% of sales. If this share holds, the improved product mix can keep feeding into profitability.

Cost benefits from the overseas base

The company says utilization at its Vietnamese and Indonesian subsidiaries rose sharply in 2025, and a larger overseas sales share lowered production costs. Lower raw material purchase prices added to the improvement in cost of sales. If this structure holds, margins could be defended even if sales grow modestly. The overseas base was built to meet customers' localization needs, so it can also support new volume wins.

Rising automotive and Ultranet volumes

In automotive, a 2025 brokerage report listed mass production of the NX5 in 2026 and the NQ6 in 2027. Press reports say Ultranet sales rose 105.4% quarter on quarter in the third quarter of 2026, as supplier customers expanded and existing customers added models. If this continues, it could support a move out of the current sales decline.

Bear factors

Sales and profit both down in the third quarter

Third-quarter 2026 revenue of KRW 13 billion fell 14.0% year on year, and operating profit of KRW 2.25 billion fell 41.3%. First-half 2026 operating profit of KRW 4.6 billion was also below the prior-year level. The company said external factors had some effect. If sales do not recover in the next quarter, doubts may grow over whether margin gains can be sustained.

Large swings in quarterly profit

Operating profit fell to KRW 1.05 billion in the fourth quarter of 2025, down 72.5% quarter on quarter, then rose to KRW 2.42 billion in the first quarter of 2026, up 129.9%.

Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders totals KRW 4.51 billion over the last four quarters, and the quarter-to-quarter swings reduce how predictable results are. At this size, order changes from one or two customers can move quarterly profit considerably.

Dependence on consumer demand and customer production

The company said a slowdown in global consumer markets and customer production adjustments affected first-half results. Footwear volumes move with inventory cycles at key customers, so slower inventory drawdowns could push back the second-half recovery.

Press reports say the launch of waterless dyeing yarn slowed as product registration talks with global customers took longer.

Risk factors

Customer and demand concentration

Key sales rest on a small number of global customers in footwear and automotive. Changes in customers' production plans or inventory policies can move quarterly revenue quickly. The year-on-year decline in third-quarter 2026 revenue is linked to such external factors. The pace of customer diversification determines how large this risk is.

Raw material and overseas production variables

Lower raw material purchase prices helped cost of sales in 2025, but if prices reverse, margins could come under pressure. Utilization at overseas subsidiaries also depends on local demand and operating efficiency. As the overseas production share grows, local operating factors carry more weight in results.

Scale and earnings volatility

With a market capitalization of about KRW 34.5 billion, the company is a small-cap, and its sales base is small next to larger listed chemical companies. Small caps can have limited trading liquidity, so share price moves can become larger than earnings changes. When quarterly profit swings this much, annual results alone make trends harder to read.

Overall view

Since the 2023 low, Assems' revenue, operating profit and net profit attributable to controlling shareholders rose for two consecutive years through 2025, and the operating margin climbed to 19.9%.

The main drivers of the profit recovery, according to filings and company statements, are a larger share of high-margin eco-friendly adhesives, higher utilization at overseas subsidiaries and lower raw material prices.

On the other side, third-quarter 2026 revenue fell 14.0% year on year and operating profit fell 41.3%, so it is hard to say growth and profit are recovering together.

Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders totals KRW 4.51 billion over the last four quarters, with wide quarterly swings, so trends are better read quarter by quarter.

Bullish factors cited include wider Ultranet adoption and new automotive models, while bearish factors include dependence on consumer demand and customer production decisions.

The next checkpoints are whether fourth-quarter 2026 peak-season sales rise year on year, and whether the 2026 preliminary full-year results, expected around late February 2027, show the operating margin holding near 20%.
