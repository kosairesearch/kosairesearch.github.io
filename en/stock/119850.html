02

Business structure

GnCenergy manufactures and installs emergency and commercial generators and runs biogas power generation.

Its businesses fall into four segments: diesel engine and gas turbine generators, biogas generators, mechanical equipment such as cooling, heating and air-conditioning systems, geothermal systems and fuel cells, and power generation.

In 2024, diesel engine and gas turbine generators accounted for 78.6% of revenue, mechanical equipment for 10.6%, power generation for 8.7% and biogas generators for 2.0%. Power sales jumped from the third quarter of 2025 as revenue from a power plant in Papua New Guinea began to be recognized.

Data-center routes accounted for about 82% of generator revenue in the first quarter of 2025, above a 2024 average of about 64%. Brokerage estimates put data-center generators at about 70% of total revenue.

Data-center generator revenue is recognized through EPC work carried out over 1.5 to 2.5 years after construction starts. Customers are mainly large corporates running domestic AI data-center projects.

Market share depends on the basis used: IBK Securities put the company's share of the domestic data-center backup generator market at about 70%, while a 2025 press report cited a 40% share of a domestic market of KRW 400 billion.

Globally, Generac's data-center backup generator orders have grown quickly since early 2026, which is changing the competitive picture.

04

Earnings analysis

On a disclosed basis, annual revenue rose from KRW 148.5 billion in 2022 to KRW 166.4 billion in 2023 (up 12.0%), KRW 226.3 billion in 2024 (up 36.0%) and KRW 262.6 billion in 2025 (up 16.0%), growing for three consecutive years from 2023 to 2025.

Operating profit went from KRW 6.09 billion in 2022 to KRW 11 billion in 2023, KRW 31.7 billion in 2024 (up 187.6%) and KRW 49.4 billion in 2025 (up 55.7%), and the operating margin moved from 4.1% to 6.6%, 14.0% and 18.8%.

In the first-quarter 2025 review, analysts cited a higher recognition rate for data-center generators and a better mix as the reasons for the strong results.

Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 27.4 billion in 2022, KRW 11.4 billion in 2023 (down 58.4%), KRW 38.5 billion in 2024 and KRW 39.9 billion in 2025, rising for two consecutive years from 2024 to 2025.

In 2022, net profit attributable to controlling shareholders was well above operating profit of KRW 6.09 billion, which suggests non-operating items played a large role that year.

Net profit including non-controlling interests was KRW 40 billion in 2025, a different figure from profit attributable to controlling shareholders. Operating cash flow moved from KRW -30.7 billion in 2022 to KRW 58.4 billion in 2023, then KRW 13 billion in 2024 and KRW 41.1 billion in 2025.

The debt-to-equity ratio rose from 45.9% in 2022 to 67.4% in 2025, with total liabilities of KRW 149.3 billion and total equity of KRW 221.5 billion, of which KRW 218.8 billion belonged to controlling shareholders, at the end of 2025.

Quarterly revenue bottomed at KRW 55.4 billion in the first quarter of 2026 (down 16.1% from the prior quarter) and then rose to KRW 66.5 billion in the second quarter and KRW 83.6 billion in the third quarter, growing for two consecutive quarters.

Operating profit fell from KRW 12.8 billion in the third quarter of 2025 to KRW 4.4 billion in the first quarter of 2026, then recovered to KRW 10.2 billion in the second quarter and KRW 7.5 billion in the third quarter.

Samsung Securities described the first-quarter 2026 weakness as caused by foreign exchange effects, and said a contract with an FX settlement clause limited the impact from the second quarter.

In the third quarter of 2026, revenue rose while net profit attributable to controlling shareholders swung to a loss of KRW 11.4 billion, and the sum of the last four quarters' net profit attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 20.5 billion.

05

Industry analysis

Demand for emergency generators is rising alongside AI data-center investment. An emergency generator supplies power to servers and cooling equipment when outages or power anomalies occur, and because a data center's core equipment depends on uninterrupted power, it needs a reliable backup power system.

In a September 2026 report, IBK Securities estimated that the 8.4 GW of projects scheduled through 2029 alone would open a procurement market of more than KRW 1 trillion a year.

Emergency generators are often ordered before data-center construction begins, so they convert into new orders faster than other equipment, according to the same analysis.

The global trend points the same way: IBK Securities said Generac's data-center backup generator orders grew from USD 100 million at the start of 2026 to USD 1.2 billion in July and USD 2.4 billion in September. Domestically, brokers view GnCenergy as holding about 70% of the data-center backup generator market.

Higher-margin gas-turbine backup generators may gain weight, which is cited as a variable for profitability. On the supply side, Samsung Securities summarized that backup generator orders were KRW 330 billion in 2025 and that the company targets 20% annual growth.

Unit order prices rose about 20% as price increases were passed through to contracts. Production capacity expansion and changes in profitability are cited as the main factors that will shape future results.

Outlook

GnCenergy's emergency generator backlog stood at KRW 414.7 billion at the end of the first half of 2026. Six new data-center backup generator orders totaling KRW 254.1 billion were disclosed after July 2026.

In a July 2026 corporate day review, Samsung Securities put the company's 2026 backlog target at KRW 500 billion. The same report said full-year 2026 operating profit growth would be in the mid-single digits because of first-quarter FX effects, but that profitability could recover from 2027.

IBK Securities forecast new orders of KRW 470 billion in 2026, KRW 900 billion in 2027 and KRW 1 trillion in 2028. The same firm estimated the backlog would reach KRW 640 billion in 2026, KRW 1.2 trillion in 2027 and KRW 1.7 trillion in 2028.

It expected large orders of 40 MW and above to concentrate from the second half of 2026 into 2027, and said some sites would begin recognizing high-margin gas-turbine generator revenue from the second half of 2026. These figures are broker estimates, and actual orders and timing may differ.

Production capacity expansion and changes in profitability are also cited as factors that will shape future results. The next checkpoints are whether the third-quarter loss trend continues in the fourth-quarter 2026 preliminary results, and the year-end backlog and full-year operating margin in the 2026 annual report.

Bull factors

Analysts attributed the strong first quarter of 2025 to a higher recognition rate for data-center generators and a better mix.

Bear factors

Third-quarter revenue growth without profit growth

Third-quarter 2026 revenue was KRW 83.6 billion, up 20.2% year on year, yet operating profit fell 41.2% to KRW 7.5 billion. Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders swung to a net loss of KRW 11.4 billion.

The quarter shows revenue growth that did not carry through to profit, leaving the stability of earnings as an open question.

Revenue concentrated in one end market

Data-center routes accounted for about 82% of generator revenue in the first quarter of 2025, and data-center generators make up about 70% of total revenue, according to brokerage estimates. If orders concentrate in large projects, revenue recognition could also bunch into specific quarters. Results are therefore highly exposed to one industry's investment cycle.

Rising debt and volatile cash flow

The debt-to-equity ratio rose from 45.9% in 2022 to 67.4% in 2025. Operating cash flow fell to KRW 13 billion in 2024 before recovering to KRW 41.1 billion in 2025. If order growth raises working-capital needs, cash flow could become more volatile.

Risk factors

Foreign exchange

Samsung Securities attributed weak first-quarter 2026 results to foreign exchange effects and said a contract with an FX settlement clause limited the impact from the second quarter. As of the third quarter of 2023 on a year-to-date basis, overseas raw material purchases equaled 30.7% of revenue. A high reliance on overseas inputs means currency moves can pass through to costs quickly.

Lag between orders and revenue

Data-center generators are recognized as revenue through EPC work over 1.5 to 2.5 years after construction begins. Even as orders rise, it takes time for them to become revenue and profit.

The forecast that large orders will cluster from the second half of 2026 into 2027 is only an estimate, and if procurement timing shifts, revenue recognition may shift with it.

Earnings volatility and financial burden

Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders fell 58.4% in 2023 before recovering in 2024 and 2025. A net loss attributable to controlling shareholders occurred in the third quarter of 2026, and the last four quarters total KRW 20.5 billion.

With the debt-to-equity ratio at 67.4% in 2025, larger earnings swings would raise the importance of monitoring the financial burden.

Overall view

GnCenergy holds a high share of the domestic data-center backup generator market, and its emergency generator backlog rose to KRW 414.7 billion at the end of the first half of 2026. Revenue grew from KRW 148.5 billion in 2022 to KRW 262.6 billion in 2025, and the operating margin rose from 4.1% to 18.8%.

However, third-quarter 2026 revenue rose to KRW 83.6 billion while operating profit fell to KRW 7.5 billion and the company posted a net loss attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 11.4 billion.

Brokers attributed the weak first quarter of 2026 to FX effects, which they said eased from the second quarter as an FX settlement clause took effect. IBK Securities projected 2026 new orders of KRW 470 billion and about KRW 1 trillion by 2028, but these are broker estimates.

The gap between orders and revenue recognition, the concentration of large projects and the dependence on data centers could all translate into earnings volatility. The debt-to-equity ratio also rose from 45.9% in 2022 to 67.4% in 2025, so changes in financial leverage are worth tracking.

The next checkpoints are the fourth-quarter 2026 preliminary results and the operating margin and year-end backlog in the 2026 annual report.
