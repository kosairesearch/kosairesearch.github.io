Data Center-Driven Generator Demand Amid Earnings Volatility

GNC Energy, a leading player in Korea's emergency generator market, has posted rising revenue and operating margins on the back of AI data center power infrastructure expansion, while quarterly earnings volatility has also widened.

02

Business structure

Founded in 1989 and listed on KOSDAQ in 2013, GNC Energy operates emergency and commercial power generation, environmental facility design and construction, and fuel cell power generation businesses.

Its core revenue comes from emergency generators supplied to data centers (IDC) and R&D centers, and it has signed a series of supply contracts with major data center operators including Kakao, LG Uplus, KT Cloud, and LG CNS.

The company holds an estimated 60-70% share of Korea's domestic emergency generator market, a position attributed to its ability to source large engines from global manufacturers such as Cummins and Caterpillar and its proprietary load-testing facility in Dangjin, South Chungcheong Province.

Beyond generators, the company has diversified into power sales through biogas generation, HVAC and renewable energy equipment, and equity-method fuel cell power generation. In 2024, it acquired 100% of a Papua New Guinea-based power generation entity, newly incorporating overseas power generation into its portfolio.

Diesel engine and generator-related sales reportedly make up the largest share of revenue, with power generation and mechanical equipment segments accounting for the remainder.

Compared with many competitors that act as domestic dealers for a single foreign brand or lack in-house testing facilities, the company is seen as holding an edge in site-specific design and delivery reliability.

04

Earnings analysis

Consolidated revenue rose to KRW 262.6 billion in 2025 from KRW 226.3 billion in 2024, while operating profit expanded sharply to KRW 49.4 billion from KRW 31.7 billion, lifting the operating margin from 14.0% to 18.8%.

Net profit attributable to owners stayed roughly flat at KRW 39.9 billion in 2025 versus KRW 38.5 billion in 2024.

Looking at the 2022-2025 trend, revenue rose every year from KRW 148.5 billion to KRW 166.4 billion, KRW 226.3 billion, and KRW 262.6 billion, while operating margin steadily improved from 4.1% to 6.6%, 14.0%, and 18.8%, reflecting clear operating leverage in the generator business.

However, 2022 net profit of KRW 27.4 billion notably exceeded operating profit of KRW 6.1 billion, suggesting a significant contribution from non-operating items that year.

On a quarterly basis, Q3 2025 posted the highest profitability in the recent window, with revenue of KRW 69.5 billion, operating profit of KRW 12.8 billion (margin of about 18.4%), and net profit of KRW 11.9 billion, before Q4 2025 profitability eased to revenue of KRW 66.0 billion, operating profit of KRW 9.4 billion, and net profit of KRW 5.3 billion.

Q1 2026 revenue fell to KRW 55.4 billion and operating profit dropped sharply to KRW 4.4 billion; according to WiseReport, consolidated revenue and operating profit declined 16.4% and 70.6% year-on-year, respectively, in that quarter.

Yet net profit rose to KRW 12.3 billion that same quarter, actually higher than the prior quarter, suggesting non-operating items drove the divergence in net profit direction.

Q2 2026 saw a rebound to revenue of KRW 66.5 billion, operating profit of KRW 10.2 billion (margin around 15.3%), and net profit of KRW 14.2 billion, the highest net profit among the recent five quarters, bringing the trailing four-quarter (Q3 2025-Q2 2026) sum of owners' net profit to approximately KRW 43.7 billion.

05

Industry analysis

Korea's data center market is expanding rapidly on the back of surging AI infrastructure investment, and according to the Korea Data Center Council, domestic private data center revenue has been growing at roughly 10% annually.

On June 29, 2026, the government unveiled its 'Korea Great Leap' three mega-projects plan, including an 18.4GW expansion of AI data centers, providing policy support for power infrastructure investment.

Since the electricity grid impact assessment took effect in June 2024, securing sufficient power supply for large-scale data centers in the greater Seoul area has become more difficult, driving development to spread toward regional locations.

AI data centers require high power density, and securing excess power capacity of 100-150% of contracted capacity is becoming standard; NH Investment & Securities noted on July 23, 2026 that this trend is structurally increasing demand volume for emergency generators.

In terms of generation technology, global cloud service providers and AI operators increasingly favor gas turbines over diesel, and gas turbines carry higher unit prices than diesel, which also affects order values.

On the competitive front, many rivals are domestic dealers for a single foreign brand or lack proprietary testing facilities, giving companies with diverse engine sourcing and in-house load-testing capability a relative advantage.

In renewable energy, the Biogas Promotion Act is progressively raising mandatory production ratios—for the public sector from 50% in 2025 to 60% in 2035, and for the private sector from 10% in 2025 to 50% in 2035—creating a medium-to-long-term opportunity for the power sales segment as well.

06

Outlook

The company has stated that it expanded large emergency generator production capacity through an expansion of its Dangjin, South Chungcheong Province plant, a move interpreted as a response to rising data center-related orders.

Its order backlog grew from KRW 161 billion at end-2022 to KRW 399.6 billion at end-2025, and a credit rating firm estimated it could reach roughly KRW 485.3 billion as of H1 2026.

Recently confirmed individual contracts include a generator supply deal with Samsung SDS for a Gumi AI data center (contract value of KRW 29.748 billion, equivalent to 11.3% of 2025 revenue, running through May 31, 2029, disclosed on July 2, 2026) and a generator sales and supply contract with Samsung C&T for the Yongin Deokseong AI data center (disclosed on July 15, 2026).

LG Uplus has stated plans to cooperate with GNC Energy to proactively secure core power infrastructure for AI data centers.

NICE Investors Service assessed that while ongoing investment related to AI data centers and fuel cell facilities may constrain cash flow to some degree, the company's net cash position and improved profit-generating capacity should allow it to manage funding needs.

However, the company's business report was subject to a correction request from the Korea Exchange in March 2026 due to insufficient disclosure, meaning detailed figures should be verified against the final amended filing.

Amid continued government policy to expand AI data centers and major conglomerates pursuing ultra-large-scale projects, whether additional single supply contracts are disclosed going forward will be a key variable for earnings visibility.

08

Bull factors

Dominant Position in the Data Center Generator Market

The company holds an estimated 60-70% share of Korea's domestic emergency generator market, with its ability to source large engines from global manufacturers such as Cummins and Caterpillar and its proprietary load-testing facility in Dangjin serving as barriers to entry.

Because AI data centers require high power density, securing excess power capacity beyond contracted levels is becoming standard, which analysts say is structurally increasing generator demand.

A track record of contracts with major operators such as Samsung SDS and Samsung C&T has reportedly given it priority status for new orders.

Confirmed Profit Leverage in the Earnings Turnaround

From 2022 to 2025, revenue grew from KRW 148.5 billion to KRW 262.6 billion while operating margin improved from 4.1% to 18.8%, demonstrating clear operating leverage in the generator business. 2025 operating profit expanded significantly to KRW 49.4 billion year-on-year.

Some analysis suggests further margin improvement is possible as the mix shifts toward higher value-added products such as gas turbines.

Expanding Order Backlog and Accumulation of Large Contracts

The order backlog grew from KRW 161 billion at end-2022 to KRW 399.6 billion at end-2025, and is estimated to have expanded to roughly KRW 485.3 billion in H1 2026.

A series of disclosed contracts with large data center clients, including a KRW 29.7 billion Samsung SDS Gumi deal running through May 2029 and a Samsung C&T contract for the Yongin Deokseong project, support revenue visibility.

Government policy support, including an 18.4GW AI data center expansion plan, is also cited as a favorable backdrop.

09

Bear factors

Significant Quarter-to-Quarter Earnings Volatility

Operating profit fell 70.6% year-on-year in Q1 2026 before rebounding in Q2, illustrating substantial quarter-to-quarter variance. Because revenue and profit recognition are concentrated around project completion and delivery timing, single-quarter results are difficult to extrapolate into an annual trend.

There have also been quarters in which net profit moved in a different direction than operating profit, indicating a meaningful influence from non-operating items.

Cost Exposure to Raw Materials and Foreign Exchange

Because the company sources large engines from overseas manufacturers such as Cummins and Caterpillar, exchange rate fluctuations can directly affect costs. The long lag between order timing and project completion means unforeseen currency or raw material price swings can erode margins on contracts signed earlier.

Margin improvement from the shift toward gas turbines also tends to swing depending on which contracts complete in which period, rather than appearing smoothly on a quarterly basis.

Limited Formal Brokerage Coverage

Even though multiple brokerages publish research on the company, most maintain a Not Rated status without issuing formal investment opinions or target prices, leaving thin official consensus. This is cited as a factor that can widen information asymmetry and share price volatility.

As a KOSDAQ small-cap stock, short-term volatility driven by supply-and-demand flows also warrants consideration.

10

Risk factors

Working Capital and Cash Flow Volatility

NICE Investors Service noted that because the accounts receivable collection period is longer than the payables period, working capital burden exists, and timing gaps between project-based progress billing and raw material payment create inherent working capital volatility.

It also noted that continued investment related to AI data centers and fuel cell facilities could constrain cash flow to some degree going forward.

Policy and Permitting Risk

Systems such as the electricity grid impact assessment governing data center permitting could affect project groundbreaking and completion schedules. If the government's AI data center expansion policy proceeds more slowly than planned or is delayed, the timing of related generator orders could also be pushed back.

Disclosure Correction History and Information Risk

The company's business report was subject to a correction request from the Korea Exchange in March 2026 due to insufficient disclosure, meaning detailed financial and business information should be reverified against the final amended filing. This correction history is a point investors should note in terms of disclosure reliability.

12

Overall view

GNC Energy has shown a steady improvement in revenue and operating margin from 2022 through 2025, underpinned by its oligopolistic position in Korea's domestic emergency generator market and structural demand growth tied to AI data center power infrastructure expansion.

However, quarter-to-quarter earnings variance has widened, as seen in the 70.6% year-on-year drop in Q1 2026 operating profit followed by a Q2 rebound, making it difficult to draw firm trend conclusions from any single quarter.

While accumulating large contracts with clients such as Samsung SDS and Samsung C&T has grown the order backlog, and government AI data center expansion policy is cited as a favorable backdrop, no official consensus has formed since most brokerages have yet to issue formal investment ratings or target prices.

Exposure to raw materials and foreign exchange, working capital volatility, and potential policy or permitting delays are factors that warrant balanced consideration.

Going forward, Q3 earnings disclosure, any additional large contract announcements, and the final amended business report should be monitored together to assess both earnings visibility and disclosure reliability.
