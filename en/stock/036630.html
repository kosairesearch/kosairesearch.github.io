Sejong Telecom Turns Profitable in Q3 Amid Restructuring

Sejong Telecom returned to operating profit in Q3 2026, but four straight years of operating losses through 2025 and a debt ratio that rose to 105.8% keep its balance sheet in focus.

  1. 1

    Q3 2026 operating profit was KRW 4.72 billion, swinging from a year-earlier loss, on revenue of KRW 72.8 billion.

  2. 2

    Revenue rose for three straight quarters from Q1 2026 through Q3 2026, but Q3 revenue grew only 1.3% year over year.

  3. 3

  4. 4

    The debt ratio climbed from 36.8% in 2022 to 105.8% in 2025, and in 2026 the company decided on a KRW 28 billion third-party allotment rights issue to its largest shareholder.

  5. 5

02

Business structure

Sejong Telecom spun off its wired telecom business into a separate unit in April 2024 and split off its AI and blockchain business in April 2025. In 2025 it sold its mobile virtual network operator business, which it had run for 12 years, to Eyesvision.

NewsWave reported that the deal had been negotiated at about KRW 18 billion but was ultimately sold for KRW 1 billion. The report attributed the exit to accumulated losses and a market increasingly dominated by large corporations.

The core business now centers on infrastructure construction, including electrical, telecom, fire-protection and civil works.

NewsWave also reported that the telecom segment, including the mobile virtual network business, posted 2024 revenue of KRW 75.4 billion and an operating loss of KRW 17 billion on a standalone basis. That figure is a standalone value from press coverage, not a consolidated disclosure.

Consolidated revenue in 2025 was KRW 284.2 billion, down 23.5% from the prior year, which shows the effect of the restructuring on the top line. SK Telecom, KT and LG Uplus are subscriber-based mobile operators, so their business model differs from Sejong Telecom's construction-centered operations.

In 2026 the company decided on a KRW 28 billion third-party allotment rights issue to its largest shareholder, with KRW 22 billion earmarked for operating funds for its electrical construction business and KRW 6 billion for facility funds to acquire real estate.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual revenue rose from KRW 324.3 billion in 2022 to KRW 339.3 billion in 2023 (up 4.6% year over year) and KRW 371.4 billion in 2024 (up 9.5%), then fell to KRW 284.2 billion in 2025 (down 23.5%). Among 2022 to 2025, 2024 was the largest year and 2025 the smallest.

Operating results were an operating loss of KRW 5.48 billion in 2022, KRW 3.11 billion in 2023 (losses continued, with a smaller loss), KRW 12.1 billion in 2024 (losses continued, with a wider loss), and KRW 10.8 billion in 2025 (losses continued, with a smaller loss), so the company posted losses for four consecutive years from 2022 to 2025.

The operating margin went from -1.7% to -0.9%, -3.3% and -3.8%. In 2025 the loss amount narrowed, but revenue fell 23.5%, leaving the margin below its 2022 level.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders was a loss of KRW 38.9 billion in 2022, a profit of KRW 43.4 billion in 2023 (a swing to profit), a loss of KRW 33.5 billion in 2024 (a swing to loss) and a profit of KRW 8.1 billion in 2025 (a swing to profit).

Press coverage said the 2025 profit also reflected a gain on disposal of investment assets.

Net income including non-controlling interests was a loss of KRW 38.2 billion in 2022, a profit of KRW 44.3 billion in 2023, a loss of KRW 33.2 billion in 2024 and a profit of KRW 8.01 billion in 2025, a different measure from controlling-shareholder net income.

Quarterly, the operating loss narrowed from KRW 3.58 billion in Q3 2025 to KRW 864 million in Q4 2025, then widened to KRW 1.59 billion in Q1 2026. Q2 2026 returned to an operating profit of KRW 1.38 billion, and Q3 2026 operating profit of KRW 4.72 billion rose 242.7% from the prior quarter.

Revenue bottomed at KRW 67.7 billion in Q4 2025 and reached KRW 72.8 billion in Q3 2026. Controlling-shareholder net income over the last four quarters totaled only KRW 3.56 billion, so quarterly swings look large.

Operating cash flow was KRW 34.5 billion in 2024 and KRW 12.9 billion in 2025, while total liabilities rose to KRW 183 billion at the end of 2025, so both figures need to be read together.

05

Industry analysis

In the telecom sector, the three major mobile carriers earn from subscriber-based services. The mobile virtual network operator market is being reshaped around large corporations. NewsWave pointed to that reshaping and intensifying competition as the background to Sejong Telecom's large losses.

Some investment-banking observers argued the company failed to build the competitiveness of a smaller operator in a crowded market.

Because the company exited the mobile business and shifted its weight to infrastructure construction, the key industry variable now is the flow of electrical and telecom construction orders.

Large carriers are recovering: SK Telecom reported operating income above KRW 500 billion for a third straight quarter in Q3 2026, and said its wireless revenue recovered gradually after a shift to customer-lifetime-value marketing.

That recovery belongs to subscriber-based operators, so it does not carry over directly to Sejong Telecom's construction-centered results.

In cycle terms, the mobile business has moved to the wind-down stage, while the infrastructure construction business is still before its order flow shows up as sustained annual profit.

06

Outlook

According to press coverage, after exiting the mobile business Sejong Telecom is likely to focus on new areas such as electrical construction and 5G private networks. Of the KRW 28 billion rights issue proceeds in 2026, KRW 22 billion is planned for operating funds for the electrical construction business.

KRW 6 billion is planned for facility funds to acquire real estate, but the acquisition target has not been disclosed. Some market observers read the company as having entered a full-scale growth investment phase after restructuring.

The same coverage said it will be hard to recover the losses from the telecom business in a short period. The third-party allotment to the largest shareholder was issued at a premium and raises funds inside the group rather than through outside mezzanine financing or third-party investors.

A charter change that reduced the issuance limits for convertible and warrant bonds is also read as a move to lower reliance on outside funding. The next checkpoint is the 2026 annual report.

The key questions are whether Q4 2026 operating results continue the profit run seen in Q2 and Q3 and how the full-year operating result turns out. Whether Q2 and Q3 2026 operating profit more than offset the Q1 2026 operating loss of KRW 1.59 billion is also a benchmark for reading the full-year trend.

08

Bull factors

Operating profit returns in Q3 2026

Q3 2026 operating profit was KRW 4.72 billion, a swing from a year-earlier loss and a 242.7% rise from the prior quarter. Revenue has grown for three straight quarters since its Q4 2025 low. Operating profit has now been positive in two consecutive quarters, following Q2 2026 at KRW 1.38 billion.

Capital reinforced through insider funding

In 2026 the company decided on a KRW 28 billion third-party allotment rights issue to its largest shareholder. KRW 22 billion of the proceeds is planned for operating funds for the electrical construction business.

Because the largest shareholder funded it instead of outside mezzanine or third-party investors, the move is read as lowering reliance on outside capital.

Restructuring and cash generation

Selling the mobile business and splitting off the wired and AI-blockchain units pruned lower-return activities. Some market observers read this as a sign of entry into a full growth-investment phase. Operating cash flow was positive, at KRW 34.5 billion in 2024 and KRW 12.9 billion in 2025.

Bear factors

Four straight years of operating losses

The company posted operating losses from 2022 through 2025, with a KRW 10.8 billion loss in 2025. The 2025 operating margin of -3.8% was lower than the -1.7% recorded in 2022. The loss amount narrowed as revenue shrank, but the margin did not improve.

Rising debt and reliance on the largest shareholder

The debt ratio rose from 36.8% in 2022 to 105.8% in 2025, and total liabilities were KRW 183 billion at the end of 2025. Q1 2026 still posted an operating loss of KRW 1.59 billion. Fundraising continues to depend on support from the largest shareholder.

Revenue decline and one-off gains

Revenue fell 23.5% to KRW 284.2 billion in 2025. Press coverage said the 2025 swing to profit was partly due to a gain on disposal of investment assets. Controlling-shareholder net income over the last four quarters totals only KRW 3.56 billion.

10

Risk factors

Portfolio restructuring risk

Selling the mobile business and splitting off the wired and AI-blockchain units changed the business mix significantly. Whether order flow in the core infrastructure construction business feeds through to earnings is the key question. How quickly the new businesses show up in reported results is also still unclear.

Capital and dilution risk

The debt ratio reached 105.8%, and the company reinforced its capital with a KRW 28 billion rights issue in 2026. Press coverage reported that after exiting the mobile business it carried out free capital reduction, paid-in capital reduction and a par-value consolidation. Repeated capital raises would raise the risk of dilution for existing shareholders.

Industry and competition risk

The mobile market has been reshaped around large corporations, and observers say smaller operators face worse competitive conditions. Large carriers' Q3 2026 results recovered, but Sejong Telecom's business area is different.

Its funding structure, which leans on the largest shareholder, is also a point to monitor for management independence.

12

Overall view

Sejong Telecom returned to operating profit with KRW 4.72 billion in Q3 2026, and revenue has grown for three straight quarters since Q1 2026. That is a clear change from the operating losses that continued through Q4 2025.

However, the company posted operating losses in each year from 2022 through 2025, and its 2025 operating margin of -3.8% was still negative. The debt ratio rose from 36.8% in 2022 to 105.8% in 2025, increasing the financial burden.

The KRW 28 billion rights issue in 2026 strengthened capital, but it was funded by the largest shareholder and that should be read together with it. Exiting the mobile business and splitting off the wired and AI-blockchain units changed the business structure, and the core business is now infrastructure construction.

Strengths are the quarterly turn to profit and the completed restructuring; weaknesses are continued annual losses and rising debt. The next checkpoints are the 2026 annual report and the Q1 2027 quarterly report.
