02

Business structure

Sigong Tech plans, designs, manufactures and installs exhibition objects, exhibition facilities and interiors for museums, science centers and expos.

Company-information sites that break down business segments put exhibition objects, facilities and interiors at the bulk of revenue, with investment-property leasing, including self-storage, making up the rest.

The exhibition share is listed as 89% on one site and 94% on another, so exact segment figures should be checked against the periodic filing's business section.

Its disclosed project record includes the Oman pavilion at the 2025 Osaka Expo, the 2023 Qatar Expo pavilion, the Korean pavilion at the 2015 Milan Expo and the theme pavilion at the 2017 Astana Expo.

Clients are mainly public institutions and local governments, including Incheon International Airport Corporation, the Ministry of Oceans and Fisheries, Wonju City, the Gyeongnam Office of Education and LH, while private interior works have included sites for Lotte E&C and Shinsegae E&C.

The 2023 November quarterly report's contract list shows wide differences in completion rates across projects, so revenue appears to cluster by quarter depending on award timing and project deadlines.

Overseas, the company holds a Myanmar unit in which it owns more than 50% and a Chinese affiliate, and an overseas subsidiary investment in Mongolia was reported in May 2026.

It emphasizes immersive VR and AR media content and said it held or was pursuing 90 intellectual property rights as of the November 2023 quarterly report. Competition centers on public tenders, so price terms and build record appear to drive award outcomes.

Earnings analysis

On a disclosed annual basis, revenue rose from KRW 100.2 billion in 2022 to KRW 134.9 billion in 2023 (up 34.6% year on year), KRW 147.1 billion in 2024 (up 9.1%) and KRW 168.4 billion in 2025 (up 14.4%), with growth in each of the three years from 2023 to 2025.

The year 2025 was the largest revenue year in the 2022 to 2025 period.

Operating profit moved from an operating loss of KRW 5.2 billion in 2022 to operating profit of KRW 5.61 billion in 2023, then KRW 6.43 billion in 2024 (up 14.7%) and KRW 15.1 billion in 2025 (up 134.5%), growing for two consecutive years from 2024 to 2025.

The operating margin climbed from -5.2% in 2022 to 4.2% in 2023, 4.4% in 2024 and 9.0% in 2025, so revenue growth translated into margin improvement.

Profit attributable to controlling shareholders went from KRW 6.92 billion in 2022 to KRW 8.31 billion in 2023 and KRW 30.5 billion in 2024 (up 266.5%), then held flat at KRW 30.5 billion in 2025 (up 0.1%). Net income including non-controlling interests was also disclosed at KRW 30.5 billion in both 2024 and 2025.

The gap between operating profit and net profit appears to reflect non-operating items: a separate-basis 2025 operating profit of KRW 15.0 billion was accompanied by non-operating income reported at about KRW 25.6 billion in one data set, suggesting that non-operating gains contributed substantially to 2025 net profit.

Quarterly revenue went from KRW 38 billion in the third quarter of 2025 to KRW 41.8 billion in the fourth quarter (up 9.9% quarter on quarter), then KRW 24.6 billion in the first quarter of 2026 (down 41.2%), KRW 23.8 billion in the second quarter and KRW 21.2 billion in the third quarter, a decline in three consecutive quarters.

Operating profit fell from KRW 3.9 billion in the fourth quarter of 2025 to an operating loss of KRW 2.31 billion in the first quarter of 2026, returned to a profit of KRW 315 million in the second quarter, and turned to an operating loss of KRW 1.62 billion in the third quarter.

Profit attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 19.6 billion in the fourth quarter of 2025 accounts for most of the KRW 20 billion total over the last four quarters, followed by losses of KRW 2.48 billion in the first quarter of 2026 and KRW 5.92 billion in the third quarter.

Operating cash flow was KRW 7.28 billion in 2023, KRW 20 billion in 2024 and KRW 12.5 billion in 2025, positive for three years after a negative KRW 3.2 billion in 2022.

Industry analysis

Demand for Sigong Tech's exhibition work depends on public and institutional orders such as museum and science center construction, local government tourism facilities and participation in international expos. As a result, revenue tends to move with the cycle of large events.

The company's track record runs through the Milan Expo in 2015, the Astana Expo in 2017, the Qatar Expo in 2023 and the Osaka Expo in 2025, and the Korean pavilion contract for the 2027 Belgrade Expo links to the next event cycle.

A company-information site describes the company as the number one player in the exhibition culture market by share. Bidding structures can bring intense price competition, so winning prices and cost control act as variables for profitability.

The rise of the operating margin to 9.0% in 2025 can be explained by fixed-cost absorption from higher revenue, and the quarterly revenue decline in 2026 appears to have reversed that effect, leaving quarterly operating results alternating between losses and profits.

The current position of the exhibition cycle reads as a gap in awards between large events, and the size and timing of the next event contracts are likely to determine how fast earnings recover. Margin improvement remains an operating lever for the company.

Whether the revenue decline through the third quarter of 2026 reflects a specific project gap cannot be pinned down from disclosures alone. Revenue direction after the fourth quarter of 2026 will likely depend on the size of new awards and the completion progress of ongoing projects.

Bull factors

Profit recovery in 2025

Operating profit in 2025 reached KRW 15.1 billion, up 134.5% from the prior year, while the operating margin rose from 4.4% to 9.0%. After a turn to operating profit in 2023 from a loss in 2022, operating profit grew in both 2024 and 2025. Operating cash flow of KRW 12.5 billion in 2025 stayed positive, which bulls cite as support for the earnings.

Overseas expo pipeline

The Korean pavilion contract for the 2027 Belgrade Expo, running from June 23, 2026 to November 30, 2027, is worth KRW 8.84 billion, about 5.2% of 2025 revenue. The company also has a track record in large overseas events such as the Oman pavilion at the 2025 Osaka Expo. Bulls point out that continued overseas event awards could fill gaps left by domestic public orders.

Balance sheet room and a return to quarterly profit

The debt ratio fell to 29.0% at the end of 2025 from 44.2% in 2024, and total equity stood at KRW 185.6 billion. The second quarter of 2026 returned to operating profit of KRW 315 million and profit attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 8.8 billion. Bulls see the balance sheet and the second-quarter profit as grounds to weather loss-making periods.

Bear factors

Three straight quarters of revenue decline

Revenue peaked at KRW 41.8 billion in the fourth quarter of 2025 and then fell to KRW 24.6 billion, KRW 23.8 billion and KRW 21.2 billion over the next three quarters. Third-quarter 2026 revenue was down 44.2% from a year earlier. Bears argue that new revenue has not replaced the large projects that have ended.

Recurring quarterly operating losses

Operating results were a loss of KRW 2.31 billion in the first quarter of 2026 and a loss of KRW 1.62 billion in the third quarter, with only the second quarter in profit. The third quarter also saw a net loss attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 5.92 billion. Bears argue the evidence that annual profitability holds up at the quarterly level is still thin.

Possible reliance on non-operating gains

One data set puts 2025 non-operating income on a separate basis at about KRW 25.6 billion, against separate-basis operating profit of KRW 15.0 billion. That gap suggests non-operating items contributed substantially to 2025 net profit.

Profit attributable to controlling shareholders was flat at KRW 30.5 billion in both 2024 and 2025, which bears read as a sign that the earnings base may not be durable.

Risk factors

Bid pricing and construction cost control

Because Sigong Tech wins its projects through public tenders, its construction margins can fall directly when winning bids move close to cost. If construction costs rise faster than contract amounts, profitability after completion could come in below expectations.

With the operating margin reaching 9.0% in 2025, whether new bids can hold margins at that level will be a key variable for the durability of earnings.

Overseas contracts and variability

The company has said the amount and construction period of the Belgrade Expo contract may change as work progresses. Operating overseas units in China, Myanmar and Mongolia exposes the company to local business conditions and currency movements. As overseas work grows, contract terms and local management costs are likely to rise with it.

Affiliate stake transactions and funding

A KOSDAQ-listed affiliate was reported to have seen the company raise its common-stock stake to 47.58%. Expanding an affiliate stake requires cash outlays and can affect consolidated results and cash flow. If the affiliate's share price stays weak, the value of that stake could also become a burden.

Overall view

Sigong Tech's 2025 annual results showed revenue of KRW 168.4 billion, operating profit of KRW 15.1 billion and an operating margin of 9.0%, reflecting a recovery in earnings power. Revenue and operating profit have risen together since 2023, and operating cash flow has stayed positive for three years.

Revenue has nevertheless fallen for three straight quarters in 2026 after peaking in the fourth quarter of 2025, and the first and third quarters recorded operating losses. On a separate basis, non-operating items appear to have contributed substantially to the 2025 net profit.

The balance sheet looks stable with a 29.0% debt ratio and KRW 185.6 billion of total equity, and the KRW 8.84 billion Korean pavilion contract for the 2027 Belgrade Expo shows the overseas award flow.
