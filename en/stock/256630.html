02

Business structure

Point Engineering, founded in 1998, makes aluminum components for semiconductor and display equipment and has been listed on KOSDAQ since 2019. Its main products are diffusers, susceptors and shadow frames, and it handles surface treatment such as anodizing in-house.

Its consolidated subsidiaries include ABM, which provides semiconductor equipment repair services, and ADL Korea, which distributes and repairs DC power supplies. In a product-level revenue breakdown, diffuser-related items rank at the top, indicating a large weight for display-equipment items.

According to the company profile, its key customers include domestic display and memory makers such as LG, Samsung and SK hynix, and it also supplies parts to global equipment makers. The semiconductor side centers on coating parts, and demand for these tends to move with high-end chip production volumes.

The new MEMS pin foundry business started development in 2020, and in 2024 the company unveiled buckling, spring and micro cantilever probe pin lineups and moved into mass production.

The company positions these pins as an alternative for testing high-density chips such as HBM, but it said at the 2024 announcement that pin foundry revenue was not yet large.

Competition is tied to domestic firms that have localized equipment parts, and in probe and pin testing, customer-by-customer qualification determines the pace of supply. The business is tied both to the display investment cycle and to the production schedules of its semiconductor customers.

04

Earnings analysis

On a disclosed basis, revenue fell from KRW 39.4 billion in 2022 to KRW 26.2 billion in 2023, a 33.6% decline that made 2023 the smallest year in the period.

Revenue then rose to KRW 31 billion in 2024 (up 18.6%) and KRW 36 billion in 2025 (up 16.0%), growing for two consecutive years from 2024 to 2025, while 2022 was the largest year of 2022 to 2025.

Operating profit went from KRW 462 million in 2022 to an operating loss of KRW 8.97 billion in 2023, and the operating loss narrowed to KRW 1.31 billion in 2024. In 2025, operating profit returned to KRW 832 million, an operating margin of 2.3%.

Attributable net profit was KRW 1.35 billion in 2024, but in 2025 it swung to an attributable net loss of KRW 5.92 billion, and net profit including non-controlling interests moved in the same direction.

A news report on the March 2026 filing said that coating business strength improved operating results in 2025, but an impairment of KRW 9.44 billion on idle Pin Foundry equipment pushed the company into a net loss. The deepest margin deterioration came in 2023, when the operating margin fell to -34.3%.

Operating cash flow dropped from KRW 9.72 billion in 2022 to KRW 428 million in 2023, then recovered to KRW 5.61 billion in 2024 and KRW 8.78 billion in 2025.

Quarterly, the fourth quarter of 2025 showed revenue of KRW 9.44 billion and operating profit of KRW 606 million, a sharp improvement on the prior quarter, yet an attributable net loss of KRW 6.29 billion emerged.

In the first quarter of 2026, revenue was KRW 5.8 billion, with an operating loss of KRW 2.06 billion and an attributable net loss of KRW 1.24 billion.

The second quarter returned to operating profit at KRW 1.25 billion on KRW 10.4 billion of revenue, and in the third quarter revenue reached KRW 11.1 billion with operating profit of KRW 1.44 billion, so revenue rose for two consecutive quarters.

However, the third quarter still carried an attributable net loss of KRW 259 million, meaning the operating recovery has not yet carried through to net profit. The four quarters from the fourth quarter of 2025 to the third quarter of 2026 total an attributable net loss of KRW 6.16 billion.

05

Industry analysis

The display-equipment parts market is viewed as growing over the medium term as the industry shifts from LCD to OLED. In the near term, however, demand for CVD equipment parts has weakened as Chinese LCD oversupply has pushed down LCD prices and panel makers have cut investment.

That pressure shows up in the first-quarter 2026 revenue decline of 30.9% year on year reported by a data provider. On the other side, the company says new Chinese customer investment has secured more than one year of new display-part production schedules.

On the semiconductor side, coating supply volumes are recovering in line with major customers' schedules. The display business thus faces both an investment contraction and a pocket of new spending, while semiconductors are in a recovery phase.

The company argues that growth in AI computing and high-density chips such as HBM will raise demand for fine pins and test technology, a view from its 2024 announcement. Current market share or industry size figures are not covered in this report.

Some analysts note that overseas customer verification and standardization are still under way, which limits the near-term revenue contribution of the new PF business.

Listed peers in semiconductor test equipment and probe-related businesses, such as TSE and HANMI Semiconductor, provide a reference point for the industry structure.

06

Outlook

The company's stated direction for 2026 is to strengthen the foundation for a turnaround by recovering its existing business and starting full-scale PF supply. In a May 2026 announcement, the company said it began full-scale PF supply to global customers from the second quarter.

Coating supply in the semiconductor division is recovering in line with major customers' schedules, and the display division has more than one year of new production schedules secured from expanded Chinese customer investment.

In its corporate value-up plan disclosed on April 20, 2026, the company set out goals to strengthen profitability in semiconductor and display process parts, expand high-margin product volumes through fine-process capital spending, keep the display business as a stable cash generator, and extend its portfolio into MEMS pin foundry.

On shareholder returns, the plan states the company qualifies as a high-dividend company and falls under the rules applied to dividends in a loss year. The company also cautioned that the plan contains forward-looking information and may change with market and business conditions.

The first checkpoint is how large PF revenue appears in the 2026 annual report. The second is whether impairment on idle equipment is recognized again, since a KRW 9.44 billion impairment drove the 2025 net loss. The third is how quickly new display production schedules convert into revenue. The fourth is whether the third-quarter 2026 attributable net loss of KRW 259 million turns into a profit.

08

Bull factors

Operating-level turnaround

Operating profit turned positive at KRW 832 million in 2025 and stayed positive at KRW 1.25 billion in the second quarter and KRW 1.44 billion in the third quarter of 2026. Revenue grew 16.0% in 2025 to KRW 36 billion and rose for two consecutive quarters in the second and third quarters of 2026. The move from operating losses to profits can be read as a starting point for recovery at the net-income level.

New PF supply begins

The company said in May 2026 that it began full-scale MEMS pin foundry supply to global customers from the second quarter of 2026. It has argued that rising demand for high-density chip testing requires fine pins.

Unlike its existing display and coating businesses, this could give it a new demand base in semiconductor testing.

Financial capacity and cash generation

At the end of 2025, the debt-to-equity ratio was 38.0%, total liabilities were KRW 25.4 billion and total equity was KRW 66.7 billion. Operating cash flow recovered from a low of KRW 428 million in 2023 to KRW 5.61 billion in 2024 and KRW 8.78 billion in 2025. Positive operating cash flow despite net losses can be seen as a financial buffer.

Bear factors

Weaker display demand

First-quarter 2026 revenue was KRW 5.8 billion, down 30.9% year on year. The company cited seasonal weakness and customer production schedule changes, while outside analysis pointed to Chinese LCD oversupply and reduced panel investment. Recovery in display-equipment parts demand is a precondition for a broader earnings recovery.

Continued net losses

The 2025 attributable net loss of KRW 5.92 billion marked a swing back to losses, and the third quarter of 2026 still recorded an attributable net loss of KRW 259 million. Even with operating profit back in the black, a continued net loss keeps earnings-based assessment difficult.

Asset impairment and new-business burden

A news report said the 2025 net loss reflected a KRW 9.44 billion impairment on idle Pin Foundry equipment. The company has acknowledged that the PF business incurred losses for several years because of capital spending burdens. With new-business revenue still limited, low utilization of equipment leaves room for further impairments.

10

Risk factors

Customer and investment-cycle dependence

Revenue from display-equipment parts depends heavily on investment decisions by panel makers, including in China. If Chinese oversupply persists, LCD investment and parts demand could weaken further. On the semiconductor side, quarterly volumes can swing with major customers' production schedules.

Delayed qualification of new business

The PF business is still going through overseas customer verification and standardization, which limited its first-quarter revenue contribution. If verification takes longer, the pace of supply expansion could fall short of expectations. Until the new business offsets losses in the core business, earnings volatility will remain.

Impairment risk and ownership concentration

A KRW 9.44 billion impairment on idle equipment contributed to the 2025 net loss. Additional impairments could widen net losses again. With the largest shareholder group holding a stake of around 49%, the free float is limited, which can affect share price volatility.

Overall view

Point Engineering's disclosed results can be summarized as 2025 revenue of KRW 36 billion, a swing to operating profit of KRW 832 million and an attributable net loss of KRW 5.92 billion.

In 2026, operating profit stayed positive in the second and third quarters and revenue rose for two consecutive quarters, but the third quarter still carried an attributable net loss of KRW 259 million.

Supporting factors include the operating profit trend, the start of MEMS pin foundry supply in the second quarter, a debt-to-equity ratio of 38.0% and recovering operating cash flow.

Offsetting factors include weaker display-equipment parts demand, attributable net losses of KRW 6.16 billion over the last four quarters and the possibility of further impairment on idle equipment.

On valuation, the price-to-book ratio of about 0.58 times is low relative to net assets, but no earnings-based multiple can be calculated. The current stage therefore depends on whether operating recovery translates into net-income profitability, and the facts support a neutral reading.

The next checkpoints are the preliminary 2026 full-year results and the annual report, where PF revenue scale and impairment treatment will become visible. This report offers no investment opinion or target price.
