02

Business structure

Seondo Electric was founded in 1972 as a maker of heavy electrical equipment and listed on the KOSPI in 1989. The company manufactures gas-insulated switchgear (GIS) and circuit breakers for industrial plant power facilities and supplies them to Korea Electric Power Corporation (KEPCO) and major plant builders.

In an April 28, 2026 disclosure, it said it had halted its Beomjeon, SLBC and electrical construction businesses and refocused on switchboards, protective switchboards and switchgear.

Its portfolio also includes digital protection relays (IED) and power control systems, which it reportedly developed in-house and supplied to semiconductor production lines and nuclear power plants.

Eco-friendly products include switchgear based on insulation technology that does not emit SF6, and orders for these have increased. Subsidiaries include a financial investment company and an agricultural corporation that grows blueberries.

A March 2026 contract with KEPCO covered a 25.8kV eco-friendly switchgear package and six other items, worth about KRW 2.6 billion, equal to 10.86% of recent revenue.

In heavy electrical equipment the company competes with LS ELECTRIC, Hyosung Heavy Industries and HD Hyundai Electric, while Cheryong Electric is noted as a leader in transformers.

Because the business now centers on three product groups, revenue mix depends on switchboard, protective switchboard and switchgear orders as well as the control equipment backlog.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual revenue fell from KRW 64 billion in 2022 to KRW 41.6 billion in 2023 (down 35.0% year on year) and KRW 24 billion in 2024 (down 42.4%), then rose to KRW 38.2 billion in 2025 (up 59.2%). Of the 2022 to 2025 period, 2022 was the largest year and 2024 the smallest.

The company attributed the 2025 revenue increase to higher orders. Operating losses of KRW 11.3 billion in 2022, KRW 7.21 billion in 2023 and KRW 5.75 billion in 2024 narrowed each year, and 2025 turned to operating profit of KRW 1.25 billion, a 3.3% margin versus -24.0% in 2024.

Press reports described this as the end of five consecutive years of operating losses from 2019 through 2024. Controlling shareholders' net loss was KRW 14.3 billion in 2022 and KRW 16.4 billion in 2023; it turned to net income of KRW 9.79 billion in 2024 and then fell 45.9% to KRW 5.29 billion in 2025.

The company attributed part of the 2024 result to a gain on disposal of property, plant and equipment from the sale of Plant 1, so that year includes a one-time element. Net income including non-controlling interests was KRW 5.28 billion in 2025.

Net income exceeding operating profit in 2025 indicates a meaningful contribution from non-operating items. Quarterly results peaked in Q4 2025, with revenue of KRW 18.9 billion (up 354.4% from the prior quarter), operating profit of KRW 2.32 billion and controlling shareholders' net income of KRW 2.36 billion.

Revenue then fell to KRW 5.06 billion in Q1 2026 (down 73.2%) with an operating loss of KRW 496 million, and to KRW 3.01 billion in Q2 2026 with an operating loss of KRW 1.03 billion.

Q3 2026 revenue was KRW 7.9 billion (up 162.0% quarter on quarter and 90.3% year on year) with operating profit of KRW 292 million, but controlling shareholders' net income was only KRW 1.75 million. Controlling shareholders' net income for the latest four quarters totals KRW 138 million.

At the end of 2025, total equity was KRW 36.6 billion (KRW 35.7 billion attributable to controlling shareholders) and total liabilities were KRW 37.1 billion. Operating cash flow was negative in each year from 2022 to 2025, and in 2025 the outflow was KRW 1.79 billion versus KRW 9.27 billion in 2024.

05

Industry analysis

Demand from the downstream market is explained along two lines. The company attributed part of its 2025 earnings recovery to replacement demand for Korea Electric Power Corporation's aging grid and to higher demand for power equipment driven by AI semiconductor demand from SK hynix.

Press reports say power equipment makers are enjoying a boom tied to the AI data center build-out. One analysis says power demand, once driven by industrial activity and factory utilization, is now shifting toward data centers and semiconductor clusters.

Some media describe global grid infrastructure replacement as a supercycle. Whether this demand converts into order backlog and revenue has to be checked against quarterly disclosed contract values. The March 2026 KEPCO contract was about KRW 2.6 billion, equal to 10.86% of recent revenue.

In competition, Seondo Electric faces large heavy electrical equipment makers such as LS ELECTRIC, Hyosung Heavy Industries and HD Hyundai Electric, and Cheryong Electric is noted in transformers. The company is pursuing new eco-friendly products and overseas expansion on the basis of ISO quality certification.

The cycle position depends on whether contract disclosures convert into quarterly revenue, and the swing in revenue between Q4 2025 and Q1 2026 shows how volatile that conversion has been.

06

Outlook

The company has set out strengthening operational capacity, expanding order-taking activity, developing new products and diversifying its product line as its core strategy.

Its improvement plan calls for continued business focus, keeping a majority of outside directors on the board, setting up an outside-director nomination committee and a transparency management committee, and appointing a full-time auditor as the next auditor.

It is also pursuing legal recovery of damages and has committed to block the largest shareholder from participating in management for three years. The KEPCO contract ran from March 18 to September 30, 2026, so later orders will have to be confirmed through new disclosures.

On the product side, orders for SF6-free insulation switchgear have increased and control equipment backlog has built up.

Press reports cited expectations for rail power supply system orders as a driver of the share price after trading resumed; those expectations are media interpretation and should be distinguished from contract disclosures.

The next checkpoint is the 2026 annual report, which will confirm Q4 2026 results and full-year operating cash flow in regulatory filings.

08

Bull factors

Exit from losses and balance sheet repair

Operating profit turned positive at KRW 1.25 billion in 2025, with a 3.3% margin. The company credits a credit rating upgrade with a recovery in orders. Its improvement plan also reported a sharp improvement in the debt ratio and a credit rating increase.

Two demand drivers: grid replacement and AI

The company says replacement of KEPCO's aging grid and higher AI semiconductor demand are lifting power equipment demand. It supplies GIS and circuit breakers to KEPCO and major plant builders, giving it exposure to both demand lines. The control equipment order backlog that has built up is cited as another positive.

Eco-friendly products and business focus

Orders for SF6-free insulation switchgear have increased. The company says that narrowing its focus to switchboards, protective switchboards and switchgear, along with restructuring, improved profitability. Operating profit of KRW 292 million in Q3 2026 is consistent with that direction.

09

Bear factors

Thin earnings base and quarterly volatility

Operating losses of KRW 496 million in Q1 2026 and KRW 1.03 billion in Q2 2026 were followed by operating profit of KRW 292 million in Q3, while controlling shareholders' net income in Q3 was only KRW 1.75 million. Net income for the latest four quarters totals KRW 138 million. Quarterly swings are large, and continuity of profits is not yet established.

Limited revenue recovery and continued cash outflow

Revenue fell from KRW 64 billion in 2022 to KRW 24 billion in 2024 and recovered to KRW 38.2 billion in 2025, still below the 2022 level. The 2025 outflow was smaller at KRW 1.79 billion, but the pattern of cash outflow has not reversed.

Q4 2025 revenue of KRW 18.9 billion made up a large share of the year, so revenue is concentrated in a few quarters.

Governance history and residual listing risk

Trading was suspended in March 2022 and resumed on April 29, 2026, after the exchange kept the company listed. Auditors gave adverse opinions for fiscal 2021 and 2022 after former executives were accused of embezzlement and breach of trust. The company says civil suits against the parties involved are ongoing.

Risk factors

Trading volatility and market alerts

Shares hit the daily limit up two sessions in a row after trading resumed, and a possible investment caution designation was announced. The market alert system runs through caution, warning and risk stages, and warning or risk designations can lead to trading halts. Prices may move apart from reported results, so the size of price swings is itself a risk.

Capital base and debt burden

At the end of 2025, total liabilities of KRW 37.1 billion were roughly equal to total equity of KRW 36.6 billion, and the debt ratio was 101.5%. The ratio had reached 302.2% in 2023. With operating cash flow negative from 2022 to 2025, external funding needs could raise the debt burden. Liabilities that are large relative to equity are a financial risk.

Order timing and quarterly revenue swings

The March 2026 KEPCO contract of about KRW 2.6 billion equaled 10.86% of recent revenue and ran through September 30, 2026. Because orders convert to revenue at different times each quarter, quarterly results swing sharply, as the gap between KRW 18.9 billion in Q4 2025 and KRW 5.06 billion in Q1 2026 shows. Operating losses can persist during periods without new orders.

Overall view

Seondo Electric returned to operating profit of KRW 1.25 billion in 2025, and its debt ratio fell from 302.2% in 2023 to 101.5% in 2025. Revenue recovered from KRW 24 billion in 2024 to KRW 38.2 billion in 2025.

On the other side, 2026 quarterly results alternated between losses in the first two quarters and a profit in the third, and net income for the latest four quarters totals KRW 138 million. Operating cash flow was negative in each year from 2022 to 2025.

The governance history from the 2022 trading suspension, the adverse audit opinions and the ongoing civil suits also remains. Grid replacement and AI data center demand are cited as favorable market conditions, but whether that demand converts into quarterly orders and revenue is still to be confirmed. The next checkpoint is the 2026 annual report, followed by the Q1 2027 quarterly report.
