Seondo Electric: 2025 Turnaround Wobbles Again in 2026

Seondo Electric swung back to an operating profit in 2025 after five years of losses, but has posted two consecutive quarterly operating losses in early 2026, underscoring the volatility of its earnings.

02

Business structure

Seondo Electric is a heavy electric equipment maker founded in 1972 and listed on the KOSPI in 1989.

Its core products are power conversion equipment supplied to power generation, transmission, and distribution plants, including gas-insulated switchgear (GIS), vacuum circuit breakers, protection relay panels (IED), switchgear, and control systems.

The company has recently been diversifying its product lineup by developing eco-friendly switchgear that does not use SF6 gas.

Its main customers include Korea Electric Power Corporation (KEPCO), the Korea National Railway, SK hynix, and Daewoo E&C — mostly public institutions and large corporations — and quarterly revenue tends to swing widely given the order-based nature of the business.

The company also holds a financial-investment subsidiary, SD Partners, and an agricultural subsidiary engaged in blueberry farming, alongside its core electric equipment business.

Management was replaced in 2022 after embezzlement and breach-of-trust allegations against the former CEO came to light, and the new management has pursued normalization centered on strengthening sales capability, new product development, and product diversification.

Within the industry, Seondo Electric is a smaller player compared with large transformer and heavy electric equipment makers such as LS Electric, HD Hyundai Electric, and Hyosung Heavy Industries.

Switchgear and relay panels appear to form the core of revenue, followed by GIS, and the company has recently been expanding into higher-voltage GIS products.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual revenue fell from KRW 64 billion in 2022 to KRW 41.6 billion in 2023 (down 35.0%) and KRW 24 billion in 2024 (down 42.4%), before rebounding 59.2% to KRW 38.2 billion in 2025.

Operating losses of KRW 11.3 billion in 2022, KRW 7.21 billion in 2023 (losses continuing, narrowing), and KRW 5.75 billion in 2024 (losses continuing, narrowing) gave way to an operating profit of KRW 1.25 billion in 2025.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders swung from a loss of KRW 14.3 billion in 2022 and KRW 16.4 billion in 2023 (losses continuing, widening) to a profit of KRW 9.79 billion in 2024, before declining 45.9% to KRW 5.29 billion in 2025.

The 2024 profit, despite an operating loss of KRW 5.75 billion, was driven by a one-off gain from the disposal of fixed assets including the sale of Plant No.1, illustrating how non-operating items have swayed bottom-line results.

The operating margin moved from a range of -17.6% to -24.0% in 2022-2024 to 3.3% in 2025, and the debt-to-equity ratio fell sharply from 302.2% in 2023 to 101.5% in 2025.

On a quarterly basis, revenue of KRW 7.84 billion and operating profit of KRW 117 million in Q2 2025 deteriorated to revenue of KRW 4.15 billion (down 47.1%) and an operating loss of KRW 15.58 million (loss turned) in Q3 2025, then rebounded sharply to revenue of KRW 18.9 billion (up 354.4%) and operating profit of KRW 2.32 billion (profit turned) in Q4 2025.

However, revenue fell to KRW 5.06 billion (down 73.2%) with an operating loss of KRW 496 million (loss turned) in Q1 2026, followed by revenue of KRW 3.01 billion in Q2 2026 (down 40.4% QoQ and 61.5% YoY) and an operating loss of KRW 1.03 billion (losses continuing, YoY loss turned), marking two consecutive quarters of operating losses and controlling-shareholder net losses (KRW 900 million in Q1, KRW 1.33 billion in Q2).

As a result, the cumulative controlling-shareholder net loss over the trailing four quarters (Q3 2025-Q2 2026) came to KRW 1.36 billion, suggesting the large revenue recognition in Q4 2025 may have been a temporary effect.

Operating cash flow was negative for three straight years in 2022-2024 (-KRW 3.03 billion, -KRW 9.4 billion, -KRW 9.27 billion) before the outflow narrowed to -KRW 1.79 billion in 2025.

05

Industry analysis

South Korea's power equipment sector is enjoying a broad order boom driven by rising AI data center-related power demand, KEPCO's aging grid replacement, and expanding investment in renewables and HVDC.

The Ministry of Trade, Industry and Energy has been pursuing a three-year, KRW 120 billion localization program for large-capacity voltage-source HVDC converter transformers starting in 2025, with HD Hyundai Electric, Hyosung Heavy Industries, LS Electric, and Iljin Electric participating as lead agencies.

HD Hyundai Electric has said it expects the boom to continue and is investing KRW 400 billion to expand transformer capacity at its Ulsan plant and an Alabama facility in the United States.

Sanil Electric and others are rapidly increasing orders for specialty transformers for U.S.-bound ESS and renewable energy projects, a shift seen as moving the industry toward higher value-added products.

Against this backdrop, Seondo Electric occupies a position supplying comparatively smaller-scale equipment such as GIS, vacuum circuit breakers, and relay panels, focused mainly on domestic demand from KEPCO, the Korea National Railway, and semiconductor fabs.

Unlike the large transformer and HVDC players, its export exposure and ultra-high-voltage transformer business appear limited, though it has recently expanded into 72.5kV-class GIS, moving into the rail and high-speed rail power equipment market.

While the broader sector-wide power-grid investment upcycle is benefiting most players, the scale of benefit appears to differ by company size and product portfolio.

08

Bull factors

First Operating Profit in Five Years and Trading Resumption

Operating profit of KRW 1.25 billion in 2025 ended five consecutive years of operating losses from 2019 through 2024. Order recovery following a credit rating upgrade and expanding demand from KEPCO and SK hynix were cited as drivers of the improvement.

Share trading, halted since March 2022, resumed in April 2026 after the Korea Exchange decided to maintain the listing, formalizing the company's return to the capital markets.

Diversifying Order Sources and Higher-Voltage Product Expansion

Order sources are broadening beyond eco-friendly switchgear for KEPCO to power equipment for semiconductor fab lines for SK hynix and 72.5kV GIS for high-speed rail for the Korea National Railway.

The SK hynix contract in particular runs as a long-term agreement through August 2028, which could support a stable revenue base. The 72.5kV GIS orders mark an expansion of the company's technical scope from existing 29kV electric railway equipment to higher-voltage substation equipment for high-speed rail.

Improving Balance Sheet Trend

The debt-to-equity ratio fell sharply from 302.2% in 2023 to 101.5% in 2025, and the operating cash outflow narrowed from -KRW 9.4 billion in 2023 to -KRW 1.79 billion in 2025. Total equity at the end of 2025 stood at KRW 36.6 billion, moving away from earlier concerns about capital impairment in 2022-2023. That operating cash flow remains negative, however, is a point worth continuing to monitor.

Bear factors

Two Consecutive Quarters of Losses Again in 2026

Revenue, which had surged to KRW 18.9 billion in Q4 2025, fell for two straight quarters to KRW 5.06 billion in Q1 2026 and KRW 3.01 billion in Q2 2026, while operating losses widened from KRW 496 million in Q1 to KRW 1.03 billion in Q2 2026.

Controlling-shareholder net losses of KRW 900 million in Q1 and KRW 1.33 billion in Q2 2026 leave open the possibility that the 2025 profit turnaround was closer to a one-off event. The cumulative controlling-shareholder net loss over the trailing four quarters totaled KRW 1.36 billion.

Quarterly Volatility Inherent to the Order-Based Business

Quarterly revenue swings have been extreme, surging 354.4% from KRW 4.15 billion in Q3 2025 to KRW 18.9 billion in Q4 2025 before plunging 73.2% again in Q1 2026.

The structure, in which revenue is concentrated in particular quarters depending on when large contracts are delivered and inspected, makes it difficult to judge a trend from a single quarter's results. This volatility could recur going forward.

Legacy Governance Risk and Non-Core Subsidiaries

The company went through a listing-eligibility review following a former CEO's alleged embezzlement and breach of trust worth roughly KRW 16.8 billion in 2022. While the listing was maintained, the legacy governance risk could still weigh on investor confidence.

In addition, its financial-investment subsidiary SD Partners and agricultural subsidiary (blueberry farming) are classified as non-core assets with little direct connection to the core heavy electric equipment business.

10

Risk factors

Governance and Management Risk

In 2022, embezzlement and breach-of-trust allegations against the former CEO led to an audit opinion disclaimer and a listing-eligibility review, which resulted in nearly four years of trading suspension.

Although this was resolved with the April 2026 decision to maintain the listing, residual effects from the past incident and uncertainty around the major shareholder structure warrant continued monitoring. Governance stability following the management change is also worth tracking.

Order and Revenue Volatility Risk

Revenue is structurally concentrated in specific quarters, with wide swings illustrated by the sharp Q4 2025 surge followed by two consecutive declines in Q1-Q2 2026. Because delivery and inspection schedules for large contracts drive quarterly results, order backlog and delivery timing need to be monitored together.

If revenue dependence on specific customers or ordering agencies rises, changes in those customers' ordering schedules could directly affect results.

Competitive and Industry Risk

Compared with large heavy electric equipment makers such as LS Electric, HD Hyundai Electric, and Hyosung Heavy Industries, Seondo Electric is at a disadvantage in revenue scale and capital strength, which could intensify competition for large project awards.

While the broader power equipment sector upcycle continues, the scale of benefit may differ by company depending on product portfolio and customer base. If the power grid investment cycle slows, smaller companies could face longer gaps between orders.

Overall view

Seondo Electric achieved a turning point toward management normalization in 2025 with its first operating profit in five years and the resumption of trading, but the direction of earnings has become uncertain again in 2026 with two consecutive quarters of operating and net losses.

Revenue surged to KRW 18.9 billion in Q4 2025 before declining for two straight quarters in Q1 and Q2 2026, and the cumulative controlling-shareholder net loss over the trailing four quarters totaled KRW 1.36 billion.

On the positive side, order sources are diversifying across KEPCO, SK hynix, and the Korea National Railway, and the debt-to-equity ratio has fallen sharply.

On the negative side, residual effects from the past embezzlement and breach-of-trust case and the inherent quarterly volatility of the order-based business remain significant.

Given that 2024's net profit, despite an operating loss, was driven by a one-off asset disposal gain, it will be important going forward to examine the factors behind any gap between operating and net results when assessing earnings quality.

The next quarterly report and new order disclosures are likely to be key variables for gauging the direction of earnings from the second half of 2026 onward.
