Report overview

Non-Housing Growth and the 2026 Turnaround Check

Kolon Global booked large losses in 4Q 2025 and then posted operating profit in each of the first three quarters of 2026, so the next checkpoint is whether the 2026 annual guidance is met.

  1. 1

    Revenue in 3Q 2026 was KRW 747.8 billion and operating profit was KRW 34.2 billion, up 23.1% and 23.5% year on year. The company said the construction segment's cost ratio fell to 87.4% from 89.0% a year earlier.

  2. 2

    4Q 2025 showed an operating loss of KRW 52.7 billion and a net loss attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 149.6 billion. The company said it recognized uncertain costs at four sites in that quarter to remove financial uncertainty.

  3. 3

  4. 4

02

Business structure

Kolon Global runs two main businesses: construction and AM/leisure. The construction segment covers housing, civil engineering and building work as well as non-housing projects such as plants and office buildings.

According to company figures, construction-segment revenue for 2025 was KRW 2.31 trillion with operating profit of KRW 6.1 billion. In 3Q 2026 construction revenue was KRW 624.5 billion and operating profit was KRW 31.6 billion, up 17.1% and 21.0% year on year.

Major 3Q orders included the Ansan Hyundai 1st Apartment project (KRW 218.3 billion), the Majang-dong District 3 small-scale redevelopment (KRW 103.5 billion), the Samsung Electronics Pyeongtaek P5 auxiliary building (KRW 84.1 billion) and the Hanam Gyosan environmental facility project (KRW 37.4 billion).

Of the KRW 3.06 trillion in new orders in 2025, non-housing work accounted for KRW 1.66 trillion, more than half.

At the end of 2025 the company completed its merger with golf, resort and hotel operator MOD and asset manager Kolon LSI, and it said the AM/leisure business is expected to secure annual revenue of KRW 280 billion and operating profit of KRW 20 billion.

Construction earnings move with sales and order timing, while leisure and asset management provide steadier cash flow.

In competition, large builders such as Samsung C&T, Hyundai Engineering & Construction, Daewoo Engineering & Construction, GS Engineering & Construction and DL E&C compete with mid-sized peers in housing and civil work.

The Simmtech Cheongju 2 plant contract signed in November 2026 (KRW 77.7 billion) is a non-housing order for an electronics component maker's factory.

04

Earnings analysis

On the annual figures in regular filings, revenue went from KRW 2.6 trillion in 2022 to KRW 2.66 trillion in 2023, up 2.4%, and KRW 2.91 trillion in 2024, up 9.3%, before falling to KRW 2.68 trillion in 2025, down 7.8%. Among 2022 to 2025, 2024 was the largest year and 2022 the smallest.

Operating profit dropped from KRW 166.7 billion in 2022 to KRW 12.8 billion in 2023, down 92.3%, and the company then posted an operating loss of KRW 56.7 billion in 2024, a swing into loss. In 2025 operating profit was KRW 3.65 billion, a return to profit, but the operating margin was only 0.1%.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 145.5 billion in 2022, KRW 284 million in 2023 (down 99.8%) and KRW 24.1 billion in 2024 (up 8,394.7%). It turned into a net loss of KRW 191.2 billion attributable to controlling shareholders in 2025.

Net income including non-controlling interests was a loss of KRW 200.4 billion in 2025. Hyundai Motor Securities attributed the 2025 year-end write-off of about KRW 240 billion to higher civil-engineering costs, unsold housing units and weaker economics of regional housing cooperative projects.

On a quarterly basis, operating profit moved from KRW 27.7 billion in 3Q 2025 to an operating loss of KRW 52.7 billion in 4Q 2025, then to KRW 22 billion in 1Q 2026, a return to profit.

It rose to KRW 52.2 billion in 2Q 2026, up 137.2% from the prior quarter, and eased to KRW 34.2 billion in 3Q 2026, down 34.4% from the prior quarter. Net income attributable to controlling shareholders in 3Q 2026 was KRW 9.74 billion, down 70.1% from the prior quarter.

Over the last four quarters, from 4Q 2025 to 3Q 2026, the combined net loss attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 95.8 billion. Operating cash flow was KRW 243.9 billion in 2022, negative at KRW -148.4 billion in 2023 and KRW -213.3 billion in 2024, then turned to KRW 72.4 billion in 2025.

05

Industry analysis

Construction earnings swing sharply with housing sales, interest rates and cost variables such as materials and labor. Hyundai Motor Securities said in a June 2026 report that it expected a conservative industry backdrop to continue and lowered its target price-to-book multiple.

The same report expected cost ratios to keep declining because materials price increases would have only a limited effect. The largest swing factor for the sector is housing.

The large losses booked at the end of 2025 were attributed to higher civil-engineering costs, unsold housing and weaker economics at regional housing cooperative projects.

Against this backdrop Kolon Global is shifting its mix toward non-housing work such as public projects, industrial complexes, plants and office buildings.

Large builders have broader order bases in overseas plants and major maintenance projects, while Kolon Global's smaller revenue base may limit its capacity to bid for large projects. Its differentiation as a mid-sized builder rests on adding the AM/leisure business, which earns income less tied to construction cycles.

Because a housing-heavy mid-sized builder is more exposed to a downturn in the construction cycle, the pace of this portfolio shift is central to the company's outlook. Housing sales conditions and the direction of interest rates can be read indirectly from next-quarter results and order flow.

06

Outlook

The company's 2026 guidance calls for KRW 4.5 trillion in new orders, KRW 3.1 trillion in revenue and KRW 120 billion in operating profit.

Cumulative new orders through 3Q 2026 were KRW 1.78 trillion, so progress against the order target is low and the pace of orders in the final quarter is the key test. 3Q new orders of KRW 792.1 billion included large projects such as the Ansan Hyundai 1st Apartment project and the Majang-dong District 3 redevelopment.

Quarterly revenue was KRW 631.2 billion in 1Q 2026, KRW 750.5 billion in 2Q and KRW 747.8 billion in 3Q, and operating profit was KRW 22 billion, KRW 52.2 billion and KRW 34.2 billion.

Even if this pattern continues into 4Q, reaching the KRW 120 billion annual operating profit guidance will depend on the profit level in the remaining quarter. Hyundai Motor Securities forecast in a June 2026 report annual revenue of KRW 3.016 trillion and operating profit of KRW 120.2 billion for 2026.

The Simmtech Cheongju 2 plant contract was signed on November 16, 2026, with construction scheduled to start on December 21, 2026 and run until June 30, 2028 on an 18-month build period, making it a checkpoint for non-housing revenue from 2027.

The AM/leisure target of KRW 280 billion in revenue and KRW 20 billion in operating profit is the yardstick for the merger's effect. The company has also said it will expand dividends to support shareholder value.

The progress of the four sites where losses were booked early in 4Q 2025 will determine whether further losses emerge. The 2026 annual report will show the profit and order results for these sites.

08

Bull factors

Profit Run After Early Loss Clearance

The company said it cleared financial uncertainty by booking costs for four sites early in 4Q 2025. Operating profit then followed at KRW 22 billion in 1Q 2026, KRW 52.2 billion in 2Q and KRW 34.2 billion in 3Q. The recovery in quarterly profit after a one-time clearance is the core bullish argument.

Improving Construction Cost Ratio

The construction segment's cost ratio in 3Q 2026 was 87.4%, down from 89.0% a year earlier. Hyundai Motor Securities said in a June 2026 report that steady non-housing revenue and a normalizing housing margin should keep cost ratios falling. This is a visible improvement in a core operating metric.

Cash Build and Dividend Plans

The company reported cash and cash equivalents of KRW 318.9 billion at the end of 3Q 2026, up KRW 117.7 billion from the prior quarter. It said it will work to raise shareholder value through larger dividends. Liquidity and a stated shareholder-return plan together form a bullish factor.

09

Bear factors

Loss History and Housing Volatility

Regular filings show an operating loss of KRW 56.7 billion in 2024 and a net loss attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 191.2 billion in 2025. Causes cited include higher civil-engineering costs and unsold housing. The fact that housing volatility can move earnings quickly is a bearish factor.

Order Target Still to Be Met

Cumulative new orders through 3Q 2026 were KRW 1.78 trillion, a wide gap to the KRW 4.5 trillion guidance. Operating profit in 3Q 2026 fell 34.4% from the prior quarter to KRW 34.2 billion. The order and quarterly profit pace needed to reach the annual target has not yet been confirmed, which is a bearish point.

High Leverage and Cash Flow Swings

Total liabilities at the end of 2025 were KRW 2.27 trillion, and the debt ratio of 332.3% remains high. Operating cash flow was negative at KRW -148.4 billion in 2023 and KRW -213.3 billion in 2024 before turning to KRW 72.4 billion in 2025. Whether cash flow has stabilized on a sustained basis is not yet established, which is a bearish point.

10

Risk factors

Housing Market and Unsold Units Risk

A slowdown in housing sales could raise unsold inventory and delay construction and sales schedules. Unsold housing was among the causes cited for the 2025 year-end write-offs. As long as housing remains part of the mix, changes in the sales market feed directly into earnings.

Project Cost and Further Loss Risk

Higher civil-engineering costs and weaker project economics were behind the large 2025 losses. The company booked costs for four sites, Daejeon Seonhwa 3rd, Daejeon Bongmyeong, Incheon Songdo and Gwangju Docheok, but further costs during the wind-down remain possible. Progress at these sites is the key driver of earnings volatility.

Balance Sheet and Interest Rate Risk

The debt ratio at the end of 2025 was 332.3%, down from 403.0% in 2022 but still high. Construction companies face interest costs and housing demand that both move with rates. A change in the rate environment could affect financing costs and project financing terms.

12

Overall view

Kolon Global's regular-filing results show revenue moving within a range of KRW 2.6 trillion to KRW 2.9 trillion, with large swings in operating profit.

The operating loss in 2024 and the net loss of KRW 191.2 billion attributable to controlling shareholders in 2025 show the burden created by housing costs and unsold units.

After booking costs for four sites early in 4Q 2025, operating profit continued through 1Q to 3Q 2026, and the construction cost ratio improved in 3Q. However, cumulative new orders through 3Q 2026 were KRW 1.78 trillion, a wide gap to the KRW 4.5 trillion target that anchors the annual guidance.

Hyundai Motor Securities said in a June 2026 report that the recovery began after the large year-end 2025 write-off, though this is one brokerage's view. On the balance sheet, the debt ratio fell to 332.3% at the end of 2025 from 403.0% in 2022 but remains high.

The next checkpoints are the 4Q 2026 preliminary results and the 2026 annual report, where the KRW 120 billion operating profit guidance and the outcome at the four sites will be confirmed together.
