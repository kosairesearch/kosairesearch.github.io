Firstec: Defense Earnings Recovery Meets Quarterly Swings

02

Business structure

Firstec, founded in 1975, is a defense company whose main revenue comes from selling defense products. Its product range centers on launch control equipment and actuators and is widening to cooling units and automatic fire suppression systems.

Its major programs include the T-50 supersonic advanced trainer, the KUH Surion helicopter and the K-9 self-propelled howitzer. In guided weapons, press reports describe Firstec as supplying the actuator drive, a core component of the Cheongung-II system.

In June 2025 it signed a contract worth KRW 27.7 billion related to Cheongung for Iraq, adding to its order backlog. Its main customers are prime contractors such as Hanwha Aerospace, Hyundai-Rotem and Korea Aerospace Industries, and Firstec's results tend to improve as their orders grow.

It is also expanding development and production investment in unmanned aircraft and urban air mobility under large state-led programs. Press reports say the company aims to enter the top ten domestic players in ground and naval guided weapons.

In an April 2026 interview with Korean media, the company said it had received Boeing's top Gold rating in supplier quality assessments for two consecutive years. Competitively, Firstec sits in a supply role, providing core components and equipment rather than complete weapon systems.

Earnings analysis

Annual revenue rose from KRW 160.2 billion in 2022 to KRW 173.1 billion in 2023 (up 8.1%), KRW 207.3 billion in 2024 (up 19.7%) and KRW 294.8 billion in 2025 (up 42.2%), growing for three straight years from 2023 through 2025. 2025 was the largest year in the 2022 to 2025 period.

Operating profit fell to KRW 2.51 billion in 2023 (down 19.4%), the low point, then rose to KRW 4.35 billion in 2024 (up 73.4%) and KRW 10.7 billion in 2025 (up 145.2%), increasing for two straight years.

The operating margin moved from 1.9% in 2022 to 1.4% in 2023, 2.1% in 2024 and 3.6% in 2025, so margin gains lagged the 42.2% revenue growth. Press coverage attributed the faster profit growth in 2025 to stable defense product sales and cost reduction.

Profit attributable to controlling shareholders rose from KRW 1.79 billion in 2022 to KRW 4.62 billion in 2023, KRW 10.9 billion in 2024 and KRW 14.7 billion in 2025, increasing for three straight years from 2023.

Operating cash flow was KRW 1.23 billion in 2022, KRW 37.9 billion in 2023 and KRW 74.8 billion in 2024, then turned to -KRW 20.2 billion in 2025.

Quarterly operating profit was KRW 2.85 billion in the third quarter of 2025, KRW 4.87 billion in the fourth quarter, KRW 2.28 billion in the first quarter of 2026, KRW 3.41 billion in the second quarter and KRW 1.42 billion in the third quarter, which was down 50.2% from a year earlier.

Profit attributable to controlling shareholders bottomed at KRW 1.68 billion in the first quarter of 2026 and then rose to KRW 2.69 billion and KRW 4.02 billion over the next two quarters. Over the last four quarters, profit attributable to controlling shareholders totaled KRW 16.4 billion.

05

Industry analysis

Defense demand is tied to national defense budgets, so it is described as less directly exposed to the business cycle. Defense improvement spending for 2026 rose sharply from the prior year, extending the expansion of the defense budget.

When Middle East tensions rose in early 2026, demand for drones and precision guided weapons drew attention and Firstec's share price swung sharply both up and down.

Press reports said that, as Israel and Iran use reconnaissance and attack drones, Firstec was named a beneficiary because of its vertical takeoff drone system technology. The same reports noted that buying tends to flood in briefly when armed conflict issues emerge and then reverses once tensions ease.

Some market participants, however, argue that this trend reflects a real improvement in fundamentals. For prime contractors, order expansion at Hanwha Aerospace, Hyundai-Rotem and Korea Aerospace Industries flows through to Firstec's results, so where those companies sit in their order cycles matters.

Competitively, Firstec supplies drive mechanisms and launch control equipment rather than competing as a complete-system maker.

Its precision control technology for unmanned aircraft and aerospace is cited as a medium-term growth factor, with revenue contribution becoming visible through individual contract disclosures.

Press coverage in April 2026 said the company continued to win large state-led programs. Its unmanned aircraft and urban air mobility businesses are receiving expanded development and production investment, so the timing of their revenue contribution is a point to watch.

Bull factors

Defense budget tailwind

Defense improvement spending for 2026 rose sharply from the prior year, extending the expansion of the defense budget. Demand is tied to the budget rather than the business cycle, which gives component suppliers a steady base. As prime contractors win more orders, Firstec's deliveries rise with them.

Scale of the 2025 profit recovery

Operating profit rose 145.2% to KRW 10.7 billion in 2025, well ahead of 42.2% revenue growth. Profit attributable to controlling shareholders rose 34.9% to KRW 14.7 billion and has grown for three straight years since 2023. Press coverage cited stable defense product sales and cost reduction behind the expansion.

Export contracts and quality rating

A June 2025 Cheongung-related contract for Iraq worth KRW 27.7 billion is an example of rising overseas orders. In an April 2026 interview, the company said it had earned Boeing's top Gold supplier rating for two consecutive years. This adds to its track record as a supplier for export programs.

Bear factors

Quarterly earnings volatility

Third-quarter 2026 operating profit was KRW 1.42 billion, down 50.2% from a year earlier and 58.4% from the prior quarter. Quarterly revenue also swung, falling 26.9% from KRW 108.5 billion in the fourth quarter of 2025 to KRW 79.3 billion in the first quarter of 2026.

A 3.6% operating margin is thin for this revenue base, so quarterly swings can raise questions about earnings durability.

Balance sheet strain and cash flow

The debt ratio was 388.8% at the end of 2025, with total liabilities of KRW 320.6 billion far above equity of KRW 82.5 billion. Operating cash flow fell from KRW 74.8 billion in 2024 to -KRW 20.2 billion in 2025. Cash generation has not kept pace with the rise in reported profit, which is a bearish factor.

Theme-driven trading and investment warning

The shares were designated as an investment-warning stock on April 22, 2026, and the exchange said they could be re-designated if they rise further after the release. Press reports noted a pattern in which buying surges whenever Middle East tensions escalate. Such flows can move the price regardless of earnings, which weighs on the outlook.

Risk factors

Timing of new-business returns

Unmanned aircraft and urban air mobility businesses are in a stage of expanding development and production investment. Investment costs arise first during the development period, while revenue contribution appears after mass production and delivery, so a time gap can emerge.

If schedules of large state-led programs shift, this gap could lengthen and delay profit improvement. If the timing of revenue contribution slips, it may take longer for the effects of expanded investment to show in results.

Defense budget review risk

The 2027 national defense budget will set the scale of defense improvement spending through National Assembly review. Whether the rise in 2026 defense improvement spending continues into the 2027 budget could change the medium-term direction of demand for defense components.

If the budget increase is trimmed or program priorities are adjusted during review, order timing for large state-led programs could be delayed. This is a policy variable that could affect Firstec's component supply volumes and order timing.

Pressure to meet the revenue target

The company is aiming to lift 2026 revenue above KRW 400 billion. From KRW 294.8 billion in 2025, that requires growth of more than 35%, while quarterly revenue from the first to the third quarter of 2026 has stayed around KRW 80 billion.

Meeting the target would require a sharp rise in fourth-quarter revenue, so deliveries and revenue recognition could concentrate at year-end. Missing the target could lower expectations for annual growth.

Overall view

Firstec's revenue has grown for three straight years since 2023, and in 2025 revenue rose 42.2% while operating profit rose 145.2%. The 3.6% operating margin is still low for the revenue base, and third-quarter 2026 operating profit fell 50.2% from a year earlier. Quarterly revenue and profit remain volatile.

Bullish factors include the expansion of defense budgets and improvement spending, the pass-through structure from prime contractor orders, overseas contracts and Boeing's quality rating.

Bearish factors include the thin margin, a debt ratio of 388.8%, the swing of operating cash flow into negative territory in 2025, and trading swings tied to Middle East news that led to an investment-warning designation.

Profit attributable to controlling shareholders has nonetheless risen for two consecutive quarters since the first quarter of 2026. The next checkpoints are the annual operating margin and operating cash flow in the 2026 annual report, and the trend of operating profit in the first quarter of 2027.
