Ubion's AI Platform Shift Faces Quarterly Earnings Swings

Ubion is shifting from a base of domestic university LMS leadership toward an AI platform business; it returned to profit in the fiscal year ended September 2025, but a second-quarter 2026 operating loss keeps quarterly earnings volatile.

  1. 1

  2. 2

  3. 3

02

Business structure

Ubion, founded in 2000, reports its business in two segments: edutech and education services. According to data aggregator Butler, edutech accounted for 61% of revenue and education services for 39% in the third quarter of 2025 on a calendar basis.

The core of the edutech segment is the learning management system Coursemos, which combines learning content with counseling and career-support tools on one platform.

A press report citing company investor materials put Ubion first in the domestic university LMS market with a 43% share, and said existing university clients moving to AI-LXP lifted average revenue per client sharply.

In the corporate market, Ubion won Samsung Electronics' LMS project in 2024, the first such award in the industry, and has since signed learning-platform contracts with other corporate clients. In June 2026 the company said it signed an additional customization contract with Samsung Electronics worth KRW 3.0 billion.

The education-services segment consists of the Wowpass brand for finance, business and accounting courses, an online lifelong education institute offering credit-bank programs, and a cyber training academy for corporate employees. A study-abroad support business launched in 2024 extends that segment further.

When preparing its listing in 2021, the company said more than 30% of its total revenue came from overseas orders. Competition is framed as a contest to hold share in university LMS and, in the corporate market, to win orders against large IT companies whose businesses overlap with education platforms.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual results show a clear recovery in the fiscal year ended September 2025.

Revenue was KRW 31.8 billion in the fiscal year ended September 2023, KRW 33.3 billion in the fiscal year ended September 2024 (up 4.6% year on year), and KRW 33.7 billion in the fiscal year ended September 2025 (up 1.2%), so revenue grew for two consecutive fiscal years from the fiscal year ended September 2024 through the fiscal year ended September 2025, and the fiscal year ended September 2025 was the largest year in the period.

Operating profit went from KRW 385 million in the fiscal year ended September 2023 to an operating loss of KRW 2.02 billion in the fiscal year ended September 2024 (a swing to loss from profit), then back to operating profit of KRW 1.41 billion in the fiscal year ended September 2025 (a swing back to profit).

Operating margins were 1.2%, -6.1% and 4.2% across the three fiscal years.

Net loss attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 601 million in the fiscal year ended September 2023, KRW 2.8 billion in the fiscal year ended September 2024 (losses continued and widened), and net income of KRW 1.31 billion in the fiscal year ended September 2025 (a swing to profit).

Net income including non-controlling interests was a net loss of KRW 601 million, a net loss of KRW 2.8 billion and net income of KRW 1.31 billion in the same three years.

Operating cash flow improved from -KRW 477 million and -KRW 1.61 billion in the first two years to KRW 4.74 billion in the fiscal year ended September 2025.

At the end of September 2025, total equity was KRW 7.7 billion (KRW 7.6 billion attributable to controlling shareholders) and total liabilities were KRW 15 billion; the debt ratio was 148.3%, 263.6% and 194.6% across the three years. Quarterly results are volatile.

After revenue of KRW 9.28 billion and an operating loss of KRW 523 million in the second quarter of 2025, third-quarter 2025 revenue fell 52.2% to KRW 4.43 billion, and fourth-quarter revenue rose 83.5% to KRW 8.14 billion.

First-quarter 2026 revenue rose 56.5% from the prior quarter to KRW 12.7 billion and produced operating profit of KRW 1.12 billion, a swing to profit.

Second-quarter 2026 revenue fell 35.1% from the prior quarter to KRW 8.26 billion (down 11.0% year on year), and an operating loss of KRW 1.51 billion returned, with the year-on-year loss widening.

Net loss attributable to controlling shareholders over the last four quarters (third quarter 2025 to second quarter 2026) totaled KRW 380 million. The breakdown of the cost structure behind the second-quarter 2026 operating loss is a point to confirm in the periodic report.

05

Industry analysis

Ubion's edutech market is in a transition toward AIED (AI in Education), which embeds AI into the learning process. Demand for services that combine learning with AI grew after generative AI emerged, and this is cited as a background to the company's earnings recovery.

In Korea, the introduction of AI digital textbooks has sustained expectations for edutech, but concerns remain that a shrinking school-age population limits core business growth.

A financial-investment industry expert said the corporate value of education companies depends on whether they integrate AI and DX technologies. In domestic university LMS, Ubion's first-place 43% share means the migration of existing university clients to AI-LXP is its main growth axis.

In the corporate education market, the company is building B2B references by widening contracts with corporate clients after the Samsung Electronics LMS award. Because this market is contested by large IT companies, the award or loss of one or two large clients can move quarterly revenue significantly.

In the cycle, earnings have continued to recover since the swing to profit in 2025, but the second-quarter 2026 operating loss shows profitability is not yet stable. Relative to peers, Ubion's position is strong in domestic university LMS, while expanding overseas and corporate-market shares remains a task.

06

Outlook

In an announcement on May 15, 2026, the company reported preliminary standalone revenue of KRW 20.7 billion for the first half of its fiscal year (October 2025 to March 2026), describing it as the largest half-year revenue in its history.

In the same release it said it expects earnings growth to continue in the second half and will focus on expanding its Coursemos LXP share and winning large projects. In June 2026 it announced an additional LMS customization contract with Samsung Electronics worth KRW 3.0 billion.

The existing Samsung Electronics subscription contract, worth KRW 3.6 billion, runs from November 21, 2024 to November 20, 2027, so whether it is renewed will affect revenue flow from 2027.

The company has set medium-term targets for 2030 of KRW 65 billion in revenue, an operating margin of 10% or more, and a total shareholder return ratio of 25%.

Moving the operating margin from 4.2% in the fiscal year ended September 2025 to 10% or more would require improvement in business indicators such as unit pricing and contract structure.

The next checkpoint is the third-quarter 2026 periodic report (July to September), and whether its operating results align with the second-half growth the company has described is the benchmark.

The key variables for the outlook are whether unit-price gains from the university LMS shift to AI-LXP continue and whether corporate references convert into additional orders.

08

Bull factors

University LMS leadership and the AI-LXP shift

A press report citing company materials puts Ubion first in domestic university LMS with a 43% share, and says average revenue per client rose sharply as existing university clients moved to AI-LXP. Further migration potential among existing clients such as Yonsei University and Hanyang University is cited as large.

The company's announcement that first-half 2026 standalone revenue was the largest half-year in its history is consistent with this trend.

Expanding corporate-market references

Ubion won Samsung Electronics' LMS project in 2024, the first such award in the industry, and disclosed a KRW 3.6 billion subscription contract in November 2024. In June 2026 it said it had signed an additional customization contract with Samsung Electronics worth KRW 3.0 billion.

If contracts with large corporate clients continue, the share of B2B revenue could rise, which is cited as a positive factor.

Profitability recovery and medium-term targets

The operating margin moved from -6.1% in the fiscal year ended September 2024 to 4.2% in the fiscal year ended September 2025. The company has set targets for 2030 of KRW 65 billion in revenue, an operating margin of 10% or more, and a total shareholder return ratio of 25%.

Whether these targets are reached depends on how much business indicators such as unit pricing and the share of large contracts improve.

Bear factors

Wider quarterly earnings swings

Second-quarter 2026 revenue fell 35.1% from the prior quarter to KRW 8.26 billion, and an operating loss of KRW 1.51 billion returned. The company had recorded operating profit of KRW 1.12 billion in the first quarter of 2026, so the direction of profit reversed within one quarter. Net losses attributable to controlling shareholders over the last four quarters totaled KRW 380 million.

Shrinking school-age population and weaker education services

Across the education sector, concerns have been raised that a shrinking school-age population limits core business growth. The education-services segment accounted for 39% of revenue as of the third quarter of 2025 and is exposed to declining enrollment in entrance-exam and adult-education markets. Changes in student numbers at universities are also a medium-term variable that links to the LMS client base.

Financial burden and a small equity base

At the end of September 2025, total liabilities of KRW 15 billion exceeded total equity of KRW 7.7 billion, a debt ratio of 194.6%. The ratio fell from 263.6% in the fiscal year ended September 2024 but remains high.

Operating cash flow improved to KRW 4.74 billion in the fiscal year ended September 2025, but was negative in the fiscal years ended September 2023 and September 2024.

Risk factors

Customer concentration risk

Corporate-market revenue relies heavily on large contracts such as the one with Samsung Electronics. The existing subscription contract runs until November 20, 2027, and if it is not renewed a revenue gap could emerge.

Quarterly revenue has moved sharply with the timing of large contracts, as seen in the third quarter of 2025 and the first quarter of 2026.

Policy and market risk

Policy expectations such as AI digital textbooks have influenced sentiment and demand expectations for edutech companies, but a change in policy direction could alter demand outlooks. The university market is affected by a shrinking school-age population and university finances. The education-services segment is exposed to declining enrollment in entrance-exam and adult-education courses.

Listing-maintenance threshold risk

According to press reports, the market-capitalization threshold for continued listing is set to rise to KRW 15 billion in 2026, KRW 20 billion in 2027 and KRW 30 billion in 2028. The current market value sits close to the 2027 reference line, so maintaining market value when the threshold rises is a challenge. In its value-up disclosure in early 2026, the company also presented plans to expand shareholder returns.

Overall view

Ubion is an edutech company expanding from its leadership in domestic university LMS into AI-LXP and the corporate market.

In the fiscal year ended September 2025 it recorded revenue of KRW 33.7 billion, operating profit of KRW 1.41 billion and net income attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 1.31 billion, turning from loss to profit, with an operating margin of 4.2%.

However, in the second quarter of 2026 it recorded revenue of KRW 8.26 billion and an operating loss of KRW 1.51 billion, so quarterly earnings remain volatile.

Net losses attributable to controlling shareholders over the last four quarters totaled KRW 380 million, so a price-to-earnings ratio is not calculated, and the price-to-book ratio sits in a premium range over net assets.

Positive factors include unit-price gains from the AI shift, large corporate references such as Samsung Electronics, and medium-term profitability targets; negative factors include quarterly earnings swings, a shrinking school-age population and debt burden.

The next checkpoints are the third-quarter 2026 quarterly report and the annual business report for the fiscal year ending September 2026, and how operating results and the debt ratio trend in those filings will be the benchmark.
