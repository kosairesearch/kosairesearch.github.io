Fadu: Profit Turn Lifts Results, Sustainability in Focus

Fadu's operating profit has risen for two straight quarters since turning positive in the first quarter of 2026, but profit attributable to shareholders has swung between quarters, with a KRW 60 billion loss in the second quarter of 2026 and a cumulative KRW 53.7 billion loss over the latest four quarters.

  1. 1

    Revenue growth: Third-quarter 2026 revenue was KRW 143.6 billion, up 459.8% year on year, and has increased for three straight quarters from the fourth-quarter 2025 low.

  2. 2

    Operating profit: Third-quarter 2026 operating profit was KRW 36.1 billion, a swing from an operating loss of KRW 11.4 billion a year earlier.

  3. 3

    Net profit swings: Profit attributable to shareholders was a KRW 60 billion loss in the second quarter of 2026 and a KRW 34.2 billion profit in the third, and the latest four quarters total a KRW 53.7 billion loss.

  4. 4

    New orders: Disclosed new orders through October 2026 reached about KRW 456.3 billion, more than four times 2025 annual revenue of KRW 92.4 billion, and press reports noted that not all of it will be recognized as revenue this year.

  5. 5

    Balance sheet: The debt ratio rose to 518.5% at end-2025 from 37.2% in 2024, with total equity of KRW 19.8 billion against total liabilities of KRW 102.6 billion.

02

Business structure

Fadu is a fabless company whose main product is SSD controllers for data centers. An SSD controller is the system chip that manages how data is read and written inside an SSD, and the company says its PCIe Gen5 controllers are its current core supply to several global big-tech customers.

Data presented at the March 2026 shareholders' meeting showed SSD controller sales of about KRW 64.2 billion in 2025, up from KRW 23.9 billion in 2024 and close to 70% of total revenue.

The company says the controller share of revenue rose from about 55% in 2024 to about 70% in 2025 and about 80% in the first quarter of 2026.

The rest comes from SSD finished-product sales; in January 2026 the company disclosed a KRW 47 billion SSD supply contract and a KRW 20.3 billion controller supply contract, and in February a KRW 30.5 billion SSD order from an aerospace customer.

Customers have broadened to global hyperscalers, and the company also says it has added a new server OEM customer. Since 2023 the company has reshaped its portfolio around controllers in cooperation with global NAND flash makers.

It is extending from power solutions such as PMIC and PLP IC into signal restoration and high-speed signal processing chips, and it unveiled file-semantic SSD (FS-SSD) technology at the OCP Global Summit on October 15, 2026.

Competition comes from other SSD controller designers; market share figures for individual rivals are not confirmed.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual revenue fell from KRW 56.4 billion in 2022 to KRW 22.5 billion in 2023 (down 60.2% year on year), then rose to KRW 43.5 billion in 2024 (up 93.6%) and KRW 92.4 billion in 2025 (up 112.4%), an increase in two consecutive years. 2025 was the largest year of 2022 to 2025, and the company described it as its highest annual revenue since its founding.

Operating profit went from KRW 1.51 billion in 2022 to an operating loss of KRW 58.6 billion in 2023, widened to an operating loss of KRW 95 billion in 2024, and narrowed to an operating loss of KRW 65.5 billion in 2025, with operating losses in each of the three years from 2023 through 2025.

Operating margin was -260.6% in 2023, -218.5% in 2024 and -70.8% in 2025. Profit attributable to shareholders was a loss of KRW 227.5 billion in 2022, KRW 56.7 billion in 2023, KRW 90.6 billion in 2024 and KRW 75.6 billion in 2025, a loss in each of the four years from 2022 through 2025.

Why the 2022 loss was so large despite a small operating profit is not confirmed. Operating cash flow was negative KRW 3.7 billion in 2025, a much smaller outflow than negative KRW 108.8 billion in 2024.

In the fourth quarter of 2025 the operating loss widened to KRW 29.5 billion, with the company saying KRW 11 billion of Gen6 controller development costs were recognized in that quarter and that development spending has now wrapped up, so revenue can grow without higher SG&A.

Quarterly revenue bottomed at KRW 23.9 billion in the fourth quarter of 2025 (down 6.9% from the prior quarter) and then rose three straight quarters to KRW 59.5 billion in the first quarter of 2026, KRW 73.2 billion in the second and KRW 143.6 billion in the third, when revenue was up 459.8% year on year and 96.2% quarter on quarter.

Operating profit turned to KRW 7.69 billion in the first quarter of 2026, then rose to KRW 16.2 billion in the second quarter (up 111.1%) and KRW 36.1 billion in the third (up 122.6%), two straight quarters of increase since the first-quarter low.

Profit attributable to shareholders swung between quarters, with a KRW 10.4 billion profit in the first quarter, a KRW 60 billion loss in the second and a KRW 34.2 billion profit in the third, and the latest four quarters together total a KRW 53.7 billion loss.

The non-operating items behind the gap between the second quarter's operating profit and its shareholder loss are not identified, so the quarterly report should be checked for their composition.

05

Industry analysis

End demand comes from expanding AI data center investment. According to the company, the rising share of high-performance NAND-based SSDs in AI data centers has sharply increased demand for SSD controllers.

The enterprise SSD demand for AI data centers that began in the second half of 2025 was described as moving in the same direction as Nvidia's comments on inference storage demand.

The company says its enterprise SSD controller business is less exposed to supply-driven cycles and seasonality and is built on long-term supply contracts, but market size and share statistics supporting this are not confirmed.

Disclosed results show that revenue recovered to KRW 92.4 billion in 2025 after 2024, a year the company said suffered from reliance on its Gen4 products.

On competition, the company says it has secured a position in the PCIe Gen5 controller market, and according to its investor relations material it has received a controller market co-creation award two years running.

Its CXL business has been slowed while it watches market conditions, and its HBF memory strategy will move only once the market is judged to be clearly open.

The CEO's view is that continued AI-driven data growth will push data that memory cannot hold onto SSDs, so the sector outlook leans heavily on the direction of AI infrastructure spending.

06

Outlook

In its July 2026 preliminary results, the company expected revenue and profit in the second half of 2026 to be far larger than in the first half. In its October 2026 announcement of third-quarter results, it said it expects growth to continue into the fourth quarter.

In its February 2026 disclosure, the company expected 2026 annual revenue growth to exceed the growth rate of 2025 over 2024. These are directional statements from the company; no numerical guidance was confirmed.

On product timing, the company has finished development of its Gen6 controller and plans full-scale sales from late 2026. Its Gen7 controller is in development with a target of customer sample supply in 2028.

On orders, disclosed new orders through October 2026 reached about KRW 456.3 billion, but the timing of revenue recognition depends on each contract's supply schedule and recognition conditions, so order growth does not translate directly into quarterly revenue.

For new businesses, the company is pursuing expansion into FS-SSD and power and signal chips. At the March 2026 shareholders' meeting, the CEO said there was no plan for a rights offering in the core business, while adding that funding may be needed depending on how any new business starts.

Points to check next are the scale of Gen6 revenue at year-end, whether fourth-quarter revenue holds near the third-quarter level, and how quickly orders convert into quarterly revenue.

08

Bull factors

Shift to a controller-led revenue mix

The company says the controller share of revenue rose from about 55% in 2024 to about 70% in 2025 and about 80% in the first quarter of 2026. Controllers are presented as a higher-margin product line than finished SSDs, and this mix shift coincides with the operating profit turn in the first quarter of 2026. Operating profit rising in the second and third quarters also appeared alongside this structural change.

Order scale and wider customer base

Disclosed new orders through October 2026 reached about KRW 456.3 billion, more than four times 2025 annual revenue of KRW 92.4 billion. The company says its customer base has widened to global hyperscalers and server OEMs.

Contracts of KRW 47 billion, KRW 20.3 billion and KRW 30.5 billion were confirmed through disclosures in January and February 2026.

Next-generation products and wider scope

The company has completed development of its Gen6 controller and plans full-scale sales from late 2026. Whether the 2028 customer sample target is met is the checkpoint for Gen7 development.

The company is widening its scope from power solutions into signal restoration and high-speed signal chips, aiming to become a comprehensive AI data center solution fabless company.

09

Bear factors

Lag between orders and revenue

How much of the roughly KRW 456.3 billion in disclosed new orders becomes revenue in each quarter depends on supply timing and recognition conditions. Press reports also noted that not all of it will be recognized as revenue this year.

Whether fourth-quarter 2026 revenue holds near the third-quarter level has not yet been confirmed by disclosure. The gap between announced order volume and quarterly revenue is a variable in reading the results.

Net losses and quarterly swings

Profit attributable to shareholders was a KRW 60 billion loss in the second quarter of 2026, running in the opposite direction from operating profit of KRW 16.2 billion. The latest four quarters together total a KRW 53.7 billion loss attributable to shareholders.

Whether the operating profit turn has translated into steady net profit attributable to shareholders has not yet been confirmed.

Product concentration

With controllers at about 80% of first-quarter 2026 revenue, dependence on a single product line has risen. Results could be sensitive to competition and pricing changes in the controller market.

The company is growing power and signal chip businesses, but the effect of diversification can only be judged once their revenue share is confirmed in disclosures.

10

Risk factors

Customer and technology transition risk

Because supply runs to several global big-tech customers, order changes from a single customer can weigh heavily on quarterly revenue. If Gen6 controller sales at year-end are delayed, the revenue growth phase could shift later. The CXL business is being slowed, and HBF will move only when the market is judged to open.

Balance sheet and funding

Total liabilities of KRW 102.6 billion at end-2025 exceeded total equity of KRW 19.8 billion, leaving a debt ratio of 518.5%. The reason liabilities rose is not confirmed. Operating cash flow narrowed to negative KRW 3.7 billion in 2025, but it was negative KRW 108.8 billion in 2024, and that history also matters.

Legal risk and funding room

At the March 2026 shareholders' meeting, the CEO said the impact of legal risk on the company would be fully explained as the process continues. The specifics and timeline of the matter are not confirmed.

The same meeting said there is no plan for a rights offering in the core business, while noting that funding may be needed if a new business starts.

Overall view

Fadu recorded operating losses from 2023 through 2025 before turning its operating profit positive in 2026. Third-quarter 2026 revenue of KRW 143.6 billion and operating profit of KRW 36.1 billion are the company's best quarter on a disclosed basis, and the growth is centered on SSD controller sales.

Disclosed new orders through October 2026 reached about KRW 456.3 billion, more than four times 2025 annual revenue.

However, a KRW 60 billion loss attributable to shareholders in the second quarter of 2026 and a cumulative KRW 53.7 billion loss over the latest four quarters show that operating profit has not consistently translated into net profit.

The debt ratio of 518.5% and equity of KRW 19.8 billion at end-2025 are also facts to weigh when assessing financial capacity. This report does not present a view on share price direction or investment decisions.

Points to confirm are the year-end revenue contribution from Gen6, the direction of fourth-quarter 2026 net profit, and how fast orders convert into revenue, along with progress on the legal risk matter.
