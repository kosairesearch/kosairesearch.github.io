Fadu: Data-Center Orders Grow as Profitability Turns

Fadu's revenue has risen for three straight quarters since the first quarter of 2026 and operating profit has returned to the black, but the company recorded net losses attributable to controlling shareholders for four straight years from 2022 to 2025 and its debt ratio has risen sharply, so the durability of the earnings recovery is still being tested.

  1. 1

    Disclosed new orders reached about KRW 456.3 billion through October 2026, more than four times 2025 annual revenue of KRW 92.4 billion. Not all of these orders will be recognized as revenue within the same year.

  2. 2

  3. 3

    Net losses attributable to controlling shareholders persisted for four straight years from 2022 to 2025, and the four quarters from the fourth quarter of 2025 through the third quarter of 2026 total KRW 53.7 billion in losses. The debt ratio at the end of 2025 was 518.5%.

02

Business structure

Fadu is a fabless company that designs controllers and SSD modules for enterprise solid-state drives (eSSD) used in data centers. Its core product is the SSD controller, and according to a Dealsite report the controller accounted for 80% of revenue in the first quarter of 2026.

Beyond controllers, SSD finished-product supply contracts are also growing. In January 2026 the company signed a KRW 47.0 billion SSD supply contract with Taiwan's Macnica Galaxy, and in February it secured a KRW 30.5 billion SSD finished-product contract with a global aerospace company.

Its main customers are global big-tech firms, hyperscale data-center operators and overseas NAND flash makers. The company said in 2026 it added several global hyperscalers to its customer base.

It currently supplies Gen5 products to major tech companies and has completed development of the Gen6 controller, which it plans to sell in earnest from late 2026.

Its business is widening from power management ICs (PMIC) and power loss protection ICs (PLP IC) into signal restoration and high-speed signal solutions, which the company describes as a shift toward a comprehensive AI data-center solutions fabless company.

On the competitive side, weakness at rival suppliers has been cited as a factor behind the expansion of big-tech eSSD orders.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual revenue fell from KRW 56.4 billion in 2022 to KRW 22.5 billion in 2023, a 60.2% decline, and then rose to KRW 43.5 billion in 2024, up 93.6%, and to KRW 92.4 billion in 2025, up 112.4%, marking two consecutive years of growth. Of the 2022-2025 period, 2025 was the largest year.

Operating profit went from KRW 1.51 billion in 2022 to an operating loss of KRW 58.6 billion in 2023, when the company turned to a loss, widened to an operating loss of KRW 95 billion in 2024, and narrowed to an operating loss of KRW 65.5 billion in 2025.

The company has posted operating losses for three consecutive years from 2023 to 2025. The operating margin was -260.6% in 2023, -218.5% in 2024 and -70.8% in 2025, with 2025 showing the smallest loss.

Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders was a loss of KRW 227.5 billion in 2022, a loss of KRW 56.7 billion in 2023, a loss of KRW 90.6 billion in 2024 and a loss of KRW 75.6 billion in 2025, a string of four consecutive years of losses from 2022.

Net loss including non-controlling interests, which is a different figure, was KRW 76.2 billion in 2025. Operating cash flow was -KRW 108.8 billion in 2024 and -KRW 3.7 billion in 2025, a sharp narrowing of the outflow.

Quarterly revenue went from KRW 25.6 billion in the third quarter of 2025 and KRW 23.9 billion in the fourth quarter (down 6.9% from the prior quarter) to KRW 59.5 billion, KRW 73.2 billion and KRW 143.6 billion in the first through third quarters of 2026, three consecutive quarters of growth.

Third-quarter 2026 revenue rose 459.8% year on year.

Operating profit moved from an operating loss of KRW 29.5 billion in the fourth quarter of 2025 to a KRW 7.69 billion profit in the first quarter of 2026, then rose to KRW 16.2 billion in the second quarter (up 111.1% quarter on quarter) and KRW 36.1 billion in the third quarter (up 122.6%), two consecutive quarters of growth.

Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders swung from a loss of KRW 60 billion in the second quarter of 2026 to a profit of KRW 34.2 billion in the third quarter.

Because operating profit and net profit attributable to controlling shareholders did not move in the same direction in some quarters, the gap should be checked against the income statement line items in the annual report.

At the end of 2025 total equity was KRW 19.8 billion, with KRW 19.3 billion attributable to controlling shareholders, and total liabilities were KRW 102.6 billion, lifting the debt ratio from 37.2% in 2024 to 518.5% in 2025.

05

Industry analysis

Demand for enterprise SSDs is rising alongside investment in AI data centers. According to market researcher TrendForce, the top five enterprise SSD suppliers' combined revenue in the third quarter of 2025 rose 28% from the prior quarter to USD 6.54 billion.

Morgan Stanley's report on NAND in the AI era projects that AI-related NAND will account for 34% of total market value by 2029. The same report identified the controller as the key determinant of eSSD performance, reliability and endurance.

Reporting in the second half of 2025 noted that global NAND makers were raising prices as demand expanded. NAND prices tend to move sharply with supply cycles, so a change in pricing or demand could shift the timing of customer orders.

On competition, analysts argue that Fadu has moved beyond reliance on a single customer and broadened its base across customers and the NAND ecosystem.

In Korea, large memory makers such as Samsung Electronics and SK hynix produce NAND, so their capital spending and pricing indirectly shape the ordering environment for Fadu's customers.

Outlook

The company expects growth to continue in the fourth quarter of 2026. Its Gen6 controller will be sold in earnest from late 2026, and development of the next-generation Gen7 has been reported as underway. In March 2026 Fadu said it plans to unveil its first proof of concept for AI data-center storage during 2026.

The same month it appointed Kim Jin-soo, a professor at Seoul National University, as chief research officer, and said it is preparing to become a storage-systems company. On orders, new orders disclosed through October 2026 total about KRW 456.3 billion.

The company said a KRW 47.0 billion contract signed in January 2026 would be fully recognized as revenue in the second half of this year. Not all disclosed orders will be recognized as revenue this year, because timing depends on supply schedules and revenue recognition conditions.

A KRW 50.0 billion eSSD controller contract with an overseas NAND maker was disclosed in May 2026. The pace at which the order backlog converts into revenue and the scale of Gen6 sales will shape the coming earnings path.

08

Bull factors

Order backlog and room for conversion

Disclosed new orders through October 2026 total about KRW 456.3 billion, more than four times 2025 annual revenue of KRW 92.4 billion. Quarterly revenue has risen for three straight quarters since the first quarter of 2026.

Not all orders will be recognized this year, but conversion as supply schedules are met leaves room for further quarterly growth.

Gen6 transition and existing customer base

Fadu has completed development of its Gen6 controller and plans to sell it in earnest from late 2026. Because Gen5 products are currently supplied to major technology companies, the existing customer relationships are cited as a foundation for next-generation supply. Once Gen6 sales begin, changes in the revenue mix can be observed.

Diversifying customers

The company said in 2026 that it added several global hyperscalers to its customer base. Its counterparties have expanded to overseas NAND makers, a Taiwan partner and an aerospace company. Analysts say the company has moved away from reliance on a single customer.

09

Bear factors

Accumulated losses and a thin equity base

Net losses attributable to controlling shareholders persisted for four straight years from 2022 to 2025, and the most recent four quarters total KRW 53.7 billion in losses. Total equity at the end of 2025 was KRW 19.8 billion, smaller than total liabilities of KRW 102.6 billion.

With a short record of profitability, the capacity of equity to absorb accumulated losses is limited, which is a bearish factor.

Volatility in quarterly earnings

Operating profit in the second quarter of 2026 was KRW 16.2 billion, but net loss attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 60 billion. In the third quarter, operating profit of KRW 36.1 billion and net profit attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 34.2 billion marked a turnaround.

Because operating results and bottom-line earnings diverged from quarter to quarter, the durability of profit needs confirmation over additional quarters.

Governance and legal risk

Prosecutors indicted the company's founder, and a criminal trial is under way. The founder stepped down from his post, and the company moved to a single-representative structure with a reshuffled board.

The stock exchange once judged that the listing's continued suitability needed review over the omission of material matters during the IPO process.

10

Risk factors

Customer and contract concentration

Orders for eSSD products depend heavily on purchases from a small number of customers. A contract first signed in November 2025 at KRW 21.5 billion was revised to KRW 47.0 billion after volume increases and higher semiconductor prices. Changes to contract terms and scale can shift the timing when the backlog turns into revenue.

Financial structure

The debt ratio at the end of 2025 was 518.5%, up sharply from 37.2% in 2024. Total equity was KRW 19.8 billion and total liabilities were KRW 102.6 billion. The reasons for the rise in liabilities and repayment plans should be checked in the notes to the financial statements in the annual report.

Industry and pricing cycle

Demand for eSSD depends heavily on AI data-center investment. If NAND prices or hyperscaler capital spending plans change, the timing and scale of orders may shift. If memory prices enter a correction phase, customers' inventories and ordering patterns may also change.

Overall view

Fadu's enterprise SSD controller and SSD finished-product orders expanded sharply in 2026, lifting quarterly revenue for three consecutive quarters, and operating profit rose for two consecutive quarters after turning positive in the first quarter of 2026.

New orders disclosed through October 2026 of about KRW 456.3 billion exceed four times 2025 annual revenue, which suggests a broader base for conversion into revenue.

On the other side, the company posted net losses attributable to controlling shareholders for four straight years from 2022 to 2025, and the most recent four quarters total KRW 53.7 billion in losses.

The debt ratio at the end of 2025 was 518.5%, with total liabilities of KRW 102.6 billion against equity of KRW 19.8 billion. In the second quarter of 2026, operating profit was positive yet net loss attributable to controlling shareholders reached KRW 60 billion, so quarterly swings remain large.

The indictment of the founder and the criminal trial remain governance risks. Bullish factors are order growth, customer diversification and Gen6 sales, while bearish factors are the durability of profits and the financial structure.

The next checkpoints are preliminary fourth-quarter 2026 results, the full-year figures and order backlog in the 2026 annual report, and the pace at which Gen6 sales are recognized.
