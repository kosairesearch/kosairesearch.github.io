02

Business structure

Stonebridge Ventures was founded in May 2017 as a spin-off from the venture capital arm of the private equity firm Stonebridge Capital and listed on KOSDAQ in February 2022. Its income comes from management fees on the funds it forms and performance fees tied to exit returns.

Alphasquare's breakdown of revenue shows fund operations at 89.70% and proprietary accounts at 10.24%, so fund-related income makes up most of the business. Assets under management rose from KRW 933.9 billion at the end of 2021 to about KRW 1.7 trillion, according to a Korea Economic Daily report from August 2026.

The Stonebridge AI Global No.1 fund (commitments of KRW 225.08 billion) and No.2 fund (commitments of KRW 99.92 billion), whose formation meeting was held at the end of July 2026, total KRW 325 billion.

About 60% of capital is to be invested across the AI value chain, including AI infrastructure, AI models, AI applications and AI transformation, and the remaining 40% in adjacent areas such as biotech and deep tech.

The company runs a multi-vintage structure of 18 funds formed since 2015, spreading exit timing across fund vintages. CEO Yoo Seung-woon has led the company since 2019 and cites large markets and founding-team capability as core investment criteria.

Portfolio companies awaiting exits include NearThink-type AI names such as Nearus Lab, Inzenia Therapeutics, Adel, Nexeye and Allgnize.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual revenue fell from KRW 29.6 billion in 2022 to KRW 19 billion in 2023 (down 35.8% year on year) and to KRW 14.2 billion in 2024 (down 25.4%), before rising to KRW 25.5 billion in 2025 (up 80.2%). Among 2022 to 2025, 2022 was the largest year for revenue and 2024 the smallest.

Operating profit fell from KRW 7.79 billion in 2022 to KRW 6.44 billion in 2023 (down 17.3%) and KRW 3.37 billion in 2024 (down 47.6%), then rose to KRW 13.5 billion in 2025 (up 299.0%).

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 11.1 billion in 2025 (up 383.5%), the largest year of the period, and KRW 2.3 billion in 2024 was the smallest. The regulatory filings show net income for each year at the same figure as controlling-shareholder net income.

The operating margin was 26.3% in 2022, 33.9% in 2023, 23.8% in 2024 and 52.7% in 2025, the highest of the four years. Operating cash flow was -KRW 13.4 billion in 2022 and -KRW 7.88 billion in 2023, turned positive at KRW 8.34 billion in 2024 and KRW 2.55 billion in 2025, a smaller amount than 2024.

On a quarterly basis, revenue rose from KRW 5.25 billion in the third quarter of 2025 to KRW 11.5 billion in the fourth quarter (up 119.7% quarter on quarter), and operating profit rose to KRW 8.52 billion (up 221.4%).

Revenue peaked at KRW 19.6 billion in the first quarter of 2026, with operating profit of KRW 15.2 billion and net income attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 13.4 billion.

In the second quarter, revenue was KRW 15 billion (down 23.6% quarter on quarter), operating profit KRW 1.73 billion (down 88.7%) and controlling-shareholder net income KRW 584 million.

In the third quarter, revenue was KRW 9.75 billion, operating profit KRW 4.86 billion and controlling-shareholder net income KRW 4.39 billion. Revenue has declined for two consecutive quarters from the second quarter of 2026 through the third quarter.

For the second-quarter drop, the company cited performance-fee-linked personnel costs recognized when revenue is booked and lower equity-method gains after a market correction.

Reports put first-half 2026 performance fees at KRW 26.5 billion (KRW 12.9 billion in the first quarter and KRW 13.6 billion in the second), and described first-half results as the highest for any half-year period.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders over the last four quarters (fourth quarter of 2025 through third quarter of 2026) totals KRW 25.6 billion.

05

Industry analysis

Venture capital income tends to cluster around the exit dates of the funds it has formed. The company said it adopted a multi-vintage approach that spreads fund formation years to smooth this pattern.

Reports say first-half 2026 exit proceeds reached KRW 232.9 billion, above the KRW 230.7 billion recorded for all of 2025. Over the same period, performance fees of KRW 26.5 billion were added, sharply raising fund-related investment partnership income.

A Korea Economic Daily report from August 2026 placed the firm within the top five of independent venture capital firms. Because exits depend on IPOs and trade sales, the domestic IPO market and listing schedules can affect the timing and size of exit returns.

The company attributed the second-quarter fall in equity-method gains partly to a stock market correction, which shows that the value of its holdings moves with market conditions. Growth in fund formation has widened the management-fee base, which is a scale-related point of differentiation.

Performance versus peer firms, however, depends on exit results and fund-level returns that are not yet fully disclosed, so a firm ranking cannot be set from the current public data. The industry is in a cycle where fund exits and the listing market drive results from quarter to quarter.

06

Outlook

The company said it is weighing how to balance new fund formation, follow-on investment and expanded shareholder returns, using profits and cash secured in the first half. Shareholder returns, including possible dividends, are under review and the specific form has not been fixed.

Companies cited as exit pipeline candidates include Nearus Lab, Inzenia Therapeutics, Adel, Nexeye and Allgnize, and reports say their KOSDAQ listings are imminent. The company said funds formed from 2018 onward will also move into the exit phase in turn.

The AI Global No.1 and No.2 funds are meant to widen investment capital and the management-fee base. Because the funds were only just formed, the pace of investment and their contribution to earnings should be checked in future filings. The CEO noted that exit results may vary from quarter to quarter.

The next periodic report is the 2026 annual report, where the full-year operating margin, operating cash flow and whether a shareholder-return policy is fixed can be confirmed. The timing and size of exits will depend on the listing schedules of individual portfolio companies and market conditions.

08

Bull factors

Exit proceeds and a performance-fee base

Reports say exit proceeds were KRW 230.7 billion in 2025 and reached KRW 232.9 billion in the first half of 2026 alone, above the prior full year. Operating profit in 2025 was KRW 13.5 billion, up 299.0%, with an operating margin of 52.7%. Reports cite KRW 26.5 billion of first-half 2026 performance fees as the main driver of the gain.

Wider AUM and management-fee base

Reports say assets under management grew from KRW 933.9 billion at the end of 2021 to about KRW 1.7 trillion. The AI Global No.1 and No.2 funds, totaling KRW 325 billion and formed at the end of July 2026, add a new source of management fees. The company said the formation secured new investment capital.

Financial capacity and shareholder-return review

The debt-to-equity ratio was 9.6% at the end of 2025, with total liabilities of KRW 8.97 billion against equity of KRW 93.8 billion. The company said it is reviewing an expansion of shareholder returns, including dividends. Whether a policy is fixed can be checked in the 2026 annual report.

Bear factors

Swings in quarterly profit

Operating profit fell to KRW 1.73 billion in the second quarter of 2026, down 88.7% quarter on quarter, and controlling-shareholder net income was KRW 584 million. Revenue declined for two consecutive quarters from the second quarter through the third quarter of 2026, with third-quarter revenue at KRW 9.75 billion. Even if the company's explanation of temporary factors holds, the quarterly swings remain large.

Reliance on performance fees

Alphasquare's breakdown puts fund operations at 89.70% of revenue. Revenue of KRW 14.2 billion in 2024 was the smallest of the period, and operating cash flow of KRW 2.55 billion in 2025 was below the KRW 8.34 billion of 2024. If listing schedules for companies awaiting exit slip, recognition of performance fees could be delayed.

Sensitivity to markets and accounting

The second quarter included an equity-method loss of KRW 1.54 billion, and the company said a stock market correction reduced equity-method gains. Personnel costs linked to performance fees were recognized when the fees were booked, which pushed operating profit lower. Reports also describe a series of conversion-price cuts through refixing on mezzanine investments.

10

Risk factors

Exit timing and size

Performance fees are recognized as funds are liquidated and exits occur, so quarterly variation is large. The company itself said exit results may vary from quarter to quarter. If listings of the five companies awaiting exit are delayed, expectations for second-half 2026 results could be revised.

Market and accounting sensitivity

Valuations of investee holdings move with markets, so equity-method gains can shrink in a correction. Personnel costs tied to performance fees are booked when the fees are recognized, which can lower operating profit. Refixing cases on mezzanine investments have been reported as a drag on the valuation of those positions.

Capital allocation and concentration

Sixty percent of the new AI fund capital targets the AI value chain, which exposes the firm to shifts in that sector. The balance between reinvestment and expanded shareholder returns is still under board discussion. Operating cash flow was negative in 2022 and 2023, so the stability of cash generation needs to be confirmed.

12

Overall view

Earnings at Stonebridge Ventures recovered sharply from the 2024 low, with operating profit of KRW 13.5 billion and an operating margin of 52.7% in 2025. The recovery was driven by performance fees from fund exits, and reports cite KRW 26.5 billion of such fees in the first half of 2026.

Assets under management grew to about KRW 1.7 trillion, and the AI Global funds formed at the end of July 2026 total KRW 325 billion. On the other side, operating profit fell to KRW 1.73 billion in the second quarter of 2026, and revenue declined for two consecutive quarters.

The debt-to-equity ratio was 9.6% at the end of 2025, so financial burden is light, but the stability of operating cash flow still needs confirmation. Valuation reads differently depending on whether net income over the last four quarters (KRW 25.6 billion) or full-year 2025 results is used as the base.

The next checkpoints are the listing progress of the five exit candidates and the 2026 annual report's full-year results and shareholder-return policy.
