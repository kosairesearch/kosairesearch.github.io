02

Business structure

Hwang Kum Steel & Technology is a stainless steel (STS) processing and distribution company founded in 1986.

Its Ansan Siheung plant operates as a coil center that cuts and processes coil sourced from domestic and overseas steelmakers for nationwide supply, and it keeps a standing inventory of about 25,000 tons to meet urgent orders.

The Dangjin plant sits on a site of roughly 49,000 square meters and has plating and surface-treatment lines with annual processing capacity of about 8,000 tons, producing color stainless and surface-finished products such as bead-blasted and mirror finishes.

Its designation as a POSCO STS cooperative processing center in December 2021 expanded its handling of domestic stainless products, which had previously been weighted toward imports.

The product mix spans general grades and premium grades such as STS316L, 321 and 310S, duplex steel and nickel alloys, and the company has designated the premium grades as strategic items and expanded stock for them. The business is reported as a single STS segment.

Consolidated subsidiaries include a stainless pipe maker, a bridge design and construction company and a laser-cutting affiliate in which the company holds a 37.5% stake. In December 2025, a subsidiary was merged into the bridge company.

Customers are mainly domestic construction and industrial processors, and the company positions itself as a comprehensive STS supplier combining a nationwide distribution network with processing equipment.

04

Earnings analysis

On a consolidated basis from the periodic filings, revenue peaked at KRW 333.4 billion in 2022 and rose to KRW 355.1 billion in 2023 (up 6.5%). It then fell to KRW 321.1 billion in 2024 (down 9.6%) and KRW 316.5 billion in 2025 (down 1.4%), declining for two consecutive years from 2024 to 2025.

Among 2022–2025, 2023 was the largest year and 2025 the smallest. Operating profit fell from KRW 46.5 billion in 2022 to KRW 29.3 billion in 2023 (down 37.0%), KRW 19.4 billion in 2024 (down 33.8%) and KRW 18 billion in 2025 (down 7.1%), declining for three consecutive years from 2023 to 2025.

The operating margin fell from 13.9% in 2022 to 8.2%, 6.0% and 5.7%. Press reports cited weak domestic and imported stainless demand and lower prices as the drivers in 2023, and higher raw material purchase costs alongside lower STS selling prices in 2024.

Profit attributable to controlling shareholders fell from KRW 40.4 billion in 2022 to KRW 24.1 billion in 2023 (down 40.3%) and KRW 16.3 billion in 2024 (down 32.3%), then recovered to KRW 18 billion in 2025 (up 10.3%).

Net income including non-controlling interests was KRW 21.2 billion in 2025, higher than the controlling-shareholder figure because of the non-controlling share. The fact that controlling-shareholder profit rose while operating profit fell in 2025 suggests non-operating items may have contributed.

Operating cash flow was KRW 15.2 billion in 2022, KRW 53.7 billion in 2023, KRW 20.6 billion in 2024 and KRW 24.1 billion in 2025. Quarterly results turned higher from the second half of 2025.

Revenue rose from KRW 79.5 billion in Q3 2025 to KRW 85.3 billion in Q4 (up 7.3% quarter on quarter), and operating profit rose from KRW 4.02 billion to KRW 4.74 billion (up 17.8%).

Q1 2026 revenue was KRW 87.7 billion and operating profit KRW 8.78 billion (up 85.4%), followed by Q2 revenue of KRW 104 billion and operating profit of KRW 12.4 billion (up 40.8%).

Q3 2026 revenue was KRW 94.1 billion (down 9.5% quarter on quarter, up 18.4% year on year) and operating profit was KRW 9.32 billion (down 24.6% quarter on quarter, up 131.7% year on year). Profit attributable to controlling shareholders over the last four quarters totaled KRW 30.8 billion.

05

Industry analysis

Stainless steel is used across construction, chemical and petrochemical plants, semiconductors, shipbuilding and power generation, and demand in each of these sectors feeds into results differently.

According to the company commentary summarized on the Kiwoom-style market data site Wisereport (as of September 6, 2026), growth in chemicals, petrochemicals, semiconductors, shipbuilding and power generation expanded demand for high-alloy materials, which improved results in the stainless segment.

The cycle has also shown weaker phases in press reports. In 2023, domestic and imported stainless demand weakened and product prices fell, and a 2025 report described continued global demand slowdown and a downturn in the domestic steel market.

Margins are exposed to the timing gap between raw material costs and selling price adjustments. On competitive position, the December 2021 designation as a POSCO STS cooperative processing center was a turning point, giving the company standing as a regular distributor of domestic stainless mill products.

A 2025 report noted the company had recorded high profitability among specialized STS processors. A numerical peer comparison of profitability was not available in public sources, so the assessment remains qualitative.

The company is shifting equipment and inventory toward color stainless, surface finishing and premium grades to move beyond commodity products.

06

Outlook

In its Q3 2026 results announced on November 12, 2026, the company emphasized a profit structure centered on specialty grades.

It plans to widen standing inventory for 316L plate up to 120 mm thick and round bar up to 500 mm in diameter, and to introduce nationwide urgent-delivery service with the aim of becoming a specialty alloy stockist in Asia.

On capacity, a 2025 mid-year report described adding PVD equipment and expanding INCO lines at the Dangjin plant, which could lift capacity by about 150% versus the existing level.

In the same period, a hairline line capable of processing 18,000 tons a year was reported as scheduled for installation at the Ansan headquarters plant within the third quarter of 2025. These were plans as of the reporting dates, and their current operating status is not established in the sources.

On products, the company's TITAMAX product was reported as newly selected for POSCO's premium building material brand INNOVILT. The company said it plans to pursue Good Recycle certification and related steps toward fully domestic premium eco-friendly stainless building materials.

Collaboration with a laser-cutting affiliate is intended to strengthen delivery and pricing responsiveness. A headwind is that if the domestic steel slump and global demand slowdown persist, the volume benefit from new equipment could arrive later than planned.

08

Bull factors

Specialty-grade mix and quarterly profit recovery

Q1 2026 operating profit reached KRW 8.78 billion, up 85.4% quarter on quarter, and Q2 rose to KRW 12.4 billion. The company attributes this to pruning low-margin items and expanding premium grades.

Because the annual operating margin fell from 13.9% in 2022 to 5.7% in 2025, a better mix leaves room for margin recovery.

Equipment expansion and specialty inventory strategy

Plans to add PVD equipment and expand INCO lines at Dangjin were reported in mid-2025 as potentially lifting capacity by about 150% versus existing levels. A hairline line for 18,000 tons a year was described for the Ansan plant. The company also plans to widen standing inventory of premium grades and pair it with urgent-delivery service.

POSCO cooperative center status and balance sheet

Since its December 2021 designation as a POSCO STS cooperative processing center, the company has handled large volumes of domestic stainless products. At the end of 2025 total liabilities were KRW 115.3 billion, and the debt ratio held at 26.5% in 2025 after 29.1% in 2022 and 25.2% in 2024.

Controlling-shareholder equity of KRW 385.5 billion within total equity of KRW 435.1 billion gives a sizable capital base.

09

Bear factors

Three-year decline in operating profit

Operating profit fell from KRW 46.5 billion in 2022 to KRW 29.3 billion in 2023, KRW 19.4 billion in 2024 and KRW 18 billion in 2025, declining for three consecutive years from 2023 to 2025. Revenue also declined in 2024 and 2025.

The 2025 operating margin of 5.7% is well below the 13.9% of 2022, and whether 2026 quarterly gains reverse the trend will only be settled by full-year results.

Exposure to raw material costs and selling prices

The company attributed the 2024 decline to higher raw material purchase costs and lower STS selling prices, as reported in the press. In 2023, weak stainless demand and falling prices also weighed on results.

Profit is sensitive to the timing between cost and price, and a renewed fall in commodity-grade prices could reapply margin pressure despite the premium-grade shift.

Thin liquidity and concentrated ownership

Major shareholders hold 56.42% and the free float is 43.51%, as of October 30, 2026. A limited float can leave trading thin and price moves less responsive to earnings. Foreign ownership is 5.26%.

The group also has several layers of subsidiaries, so changes in group structure would need to be tracked for their effect on minority holders.

Risk factors

Demand and price cycle

Prolonged weakness in the domestic steel market and slower global demand could pressure both volumes and selling prices. Because the 2023 and 2024 declines stemmed from demand softness and price swings, the same drivers could recur. A higher premium-grade mix cushions this, but its share of total revenue is not disclosed in public sources.

Counterparty credit and receivables

According to press reports, the company manages operating risk by requiring cash sales from new or financially weak customers and accepting promissory notes from distributors that pledged collateral.

In a downturn, distributor credit deterioration could slow collections, and the effect of changes in sales terms on revenue and cash flow should be watched quarter by quarter.

Governance and group restructuring

In December 2025, a subsidiary was merged into the bridge company, and in October 2025 another company was brought into the group as a subsidiary. Restructuring can improve efficiency, but changes in consolidation scope alter comparability of results. With a high major-shareholder stake, the effect of ownership changes on decisions also warrants attention.

Overall view

Hwang Kum Steel & Technology is a specialized stainless steel processing and distribution company whose annual revenue and operating profit on a filed basis have declined since the 2023 peak. Operating profit fell from KRW 46.5 billion in 2022 to KRW 18 billion in 2025, and the operating margin dropped to 5.7%.

Quarterly operating profit in 2026 was KRW 8.78 billion in Q1, KRW 12.4 billion in Q2 and KRW 9.32 billion in Q3, above the second half of 2025, and the company attributes this to pruning low-margin items and expanding premium grades.

Supporting factors include the premium-grade mix, the POSCO cooperative center status, planned capacity additions and a sizable equity base.

Offsetting factors include three consecutive years of declining operating profit, margin exposure to raw material and selling prices, and thin liquidity with a concentrated ownership structure. Whether the profit recovery persists through a softer demand cycle has not yet been confirmed. The next checkpoints are the operating margin in the 2026 annual report and the Q1 2027 quarterly report.
