Steel Pipe Maker's Losses Meet a SpaceX Supply Boost

02

Business structure

KBI Dong Yang Steel Pipe was founded in 1973 and listed on the KOSPI market in 1977, and it is headquartered in Cheonan, Chungcheongnam-do, with a plant in Chungju, Chungcheongbuk-do, and a sales office in Yongsan-gu, Seoul.

Its core steel pipe business produces piping pipe, gas pipe, oil pipe, steel pipe piles, water-coated pipe, and offshore wind foundation pipe, supplying key materials for domestic waterworks and gas pipeline construction.

In 2025 it became the first steel pipe maker in the industry to start producing BH (Built-up H-Beam), building annual capacity of 40,000 tons to broaden its product lineup.

Its wholly owned subsidiary KBI Alloy produces copper alloy made by blending 99.9%-purity electrolytic copper with chromium, beryllium, and zirconium, supplying wire and power-generation equipment applications. Its major customers include SeAH Steel and Master Pipe Distribution Company, LLC in the United States.

Domestically it supplies structural steel pipe to Samsung Electronics' Pyeongtaek P4 fab and SK hynix's Yongin cluster. The company is shifting from a domestic-focused structure toward export orientation, developing new accounts across the Middle East, Africa, and South America.

It recently changed its corporate name to KBI Dong Yang Steel Pipe after 52 years, aiming to strengthen synergy among KBI Group affiliates and reinforce brand identity.

Its steel pipe segment is sensitive to the construction cycle and infrastructure investment, while its copper alloy segment tracks the auto industry but retains relatively steady demand given its diverse applications.

04

Earnings analysis

Consolidated revenue rose from KRW 266.2 billion in 2022 to KRW 278.3 billion in 2023, then declined for two straight years to KRW 250.3 billion in 2024 and KRW 234.8 billion in 2025.

Operating profit fell sharply from KRW 7.74 billion in 2022 to KRW 2.2 billion in 2023, rebounded to KRW 6.68 billion in 2024, then turned into an operating loss of KRW 1.62 billion in 2025.

Net profit attributable to owners swung from a KRW 3.97 billion gain in 2022 to losses in each of 2023 through 2025, with the loss widening to KRW 19.6 billion in 2024 before narrowing to KRW 3.1 billion in 2025.

The company cited weak demand from downstream industries and profitability deterioration from US steel tariffs as the main reasons for the earnings swing.

On a quarterly basis, revenue peaked at KRW 89.1 billion in the fourth quarter of 2025, up 88.7% quarter on quarter, before declining for two consecutive quarters to KRW 67 billion in the first quarter of 2026 and KRW 60.3 billion in the second quarter.

Operating profit posted losses for three straight quarters from the second to the fourth quarter of 2025, turned positive at KRW 4.19 billion in the first quarter of 2026, then reverted to a loss of KRW 82.67 million in the second quarter.

The second-quarter 2026 net loss attributable to owners was KRW 2.31 billion, a continued loss from a year earlier, and the trailing four quarters (third quarter 2025 through second quarter 2026) totaled a net loss of KRW 4.39 billion.

The year-end 2025 debt-to-equity ratio fell sharply to 69.4% from 139.7% in 2023, signaling an improving capital structure, even as operating cash flow reversed to negative KRW 31.6 billion in 2025 from positive KRW 32.9 billion in 2024, indicating weaker cash generation.

05

Industry analysis

The United States imposed a 25% tariff on steel and aluminum in March 2025 and raised it to 50% in June of the same year, directly hitting profitability across Korea's steel pipe industry.

Korea's steel pipe exports to the United States fell about 11% year on year in 2025 to roughly 969,616 tons, the lowest level in five years. Integrated pipe maker SeAH Steel also swung to a loss in the third quarter of 2025 amid a construction downturn combined with the burden of US tariffs.

Entering 2026, however, a rebound emerged as US data center and infrastructure construction boosted local steel demand, with pipe becoming the largest single export item to the United States by volume.

Domestically, a sluggish construction market and reduced infrastructure spending remain a headwind for structural pipe demand.

By contrast, Samsung Electronics' planned KRW 450 trillion investment over five years including its Pyeongtaek P5 line, along with SK hynix's expanded Yongin cluster investment, are cited as a new growth driver for structural pipe products such as square pipe and BH.

Orders have also continued in the domestic offshore wind foundation pipe market, including a KRW 21.16 billion contract with Samil C&S, partly offsetting the segment's traditional reliance on the construction cycle.

With Mexico and the European Union also raising or signaling 50%-level steel tariffs, the possibility that low-priced Chinese steel shut out of the US market flows into third countries remains a downward pressure shared across smaller pipe makers.

06

Outlook

The company carried out a KRW 50 billion rights offering in September 2025 to secure raw materials ahead of large overseas projects, and is preparing to supply roughly 50,000 tons of pipe worth about KRW 60 billion for the Wei Lan Hai Changhua offshore wind foundation project in Taiwan.

A roughly KRW 97.4 billion steel supply contract signed with Iraq's MAJID MOZAN GHTAMI COMPANY in December 2025 was a sizable transaction relative to recent revenue, but a March 2026 disclosure confirmed shipments had been suspended due to the fallout from the US-Iran conflict, with the contract's end date changed to undetermined.

In October 2026 it was confirmed that the company is supplying about 950 tons of high-strength pipe (640 tons in April, plus 205 and 105 tons in October) produced at its Cheonan and Chungju plants for SpaceX's rocket launch facilities in Florida and Texas.

That volume, however, is only about 8% of its roughly 12,000-ton Iraq pipe contract and around 2% of its annual 50,000-ton supply agreement with a US distributor.

The company said this delivery track record could put it in a favorable position for future SpaceX orders, but the scale and timing of any additional orders have not yet been determined.

Domestically, the company states that expanding semiconductor investment by Samsung Electronics and SK hynix leaves medium-to-long-term order opportunities open for structural pipe products such as square pipe and BH.

08

Bull factors

Structural Pipe Demand from Semiconductor Fab Construction

As large-scale semiconductor investment accelerates at Samsung Electronics' Pyeongtaek P5 line and SK hynix's Yongin cluster, there is potential for growing medium-to-long-term demand for structural pipe products such as square pipe and BH.

The company has an existing supply track record at both sites, positioning it to pursue order opportunities as investment expands.

The BH production line, which began operating in 2025 with annual capacity of 40,000 tons, is a new growth avenue that can create synergy with its existing round and square pipe product lines.

Export Diversification and a New SpaceX Reference

The company is pursuing multiple overseas projects, including an Iraq oil and gas pipeline contract, a Taiwan offshore wind foundation project, and an annual supply agreement with a US distribution network, in an effort to reduce its reliance on the domestic market.

The SpaceX rocket launch facility pipe supply confirmed in October 2026 involved only a modest volume but established a reference of having passed rigorous quality verification. The company has stated it plans to use this as a stepping stone to pursue further orders in the US space infrastructure market.

Improving Capital Structure and Cost Response

The debt-to-equity ratio eased from 139.7% in 2023 to 69.4% in 2025, indicating a lighter capital structure. In the first quarter of 2026, the company posted operating profit of KRW 4.19 billion, briefly breaking a run of quarterly losses that had persisted since the second quarter of 2025.

It is also using funds raised through a rights offering to pre-purchase raw materials, a strategy aimed at strengthening its ability to respond to large overseas projects.

09

Bear factors

Profitability Pressure from US Steel Tariffs

Since the United States raised steel and aluminum tariffs to 50% in June 2025, the profitability of Korean pipe exporters to the US has broadly deteriorated.

Korea's pipe exports to the US fell 11% year on year in 2025 to the lowest level in five years, and the company itself cited weak downstream demand and the tariffs as the main reasons for its 2025 swing to a loss.

Industry concerns persist that a return to prior profit levels will be difficult as long as the tariff environment remains in place.

High Volatility in Quarterly Results

Operating profit alternated between losses and gains across five consecutive quarters from the second quarter of 2025 to the second quarter of 2026, swinging back to a loss in the second quarter of 2026 right after turning positive in the first quarter.

Net profit attributable to owners also swung between gains and losses over the same period, totaling a net loss of KRW 4.39 billion over the trailing four quarters.

This volatility stems from a business structure that is sensitive to multiple external variables, including the construction cycle, exchange rates, and tariffs.

Execution Uncertainty on Large Orders

The KRW 97.4 billion Iraq supply contract has had shipments suspended due to the fallout from the US-Iran conflict, with its end date changed to undetermined, making it difficult to gauge when revenue will be recognized.

The 950-ton SpaceX supply is also small relative to the company's other large contracts, and its earnings contribution could remain limited unless followed by additional orders. As reliance on large overseas projects grows, the risk of schedule changes driven by geopolitical factors or customer circumstances persists.

Risk factors

Trade and Tariff Risk

The US 50% tariff on steel and aluminum has remained in place since June 2025 and has been expanding to cover derivative products, a tightening trend. The European Union, Canada, and Mexico have also been raising tariffs on imported steel in succession, which could offset the benefits of export diversification.

If low-priced Chinese steel shut out of the US market flows into third-country markets, price competition could intensify further.

Large Order Execution Risk

The Iraq oil and gas project has had shipments suspended indefinitely due to Middle East instability, with the timing of resumption dependent on external factors such as a resolution of the conflict.

The Taiwan offshore wind project could also be affected by changes in permitting or construction schedules given the nature of offshore work. The possibility that the SpaceX supply remains a one-time reference, rather than leading to additional orders, cannot be ruled out.

Financial and Trading Risk

Operating cash flow reversed to negative KRW 31.6 billion in 2025 from positive KRW 32.9 billion in 2024, signaling weaker cash generation. Funding through a rights offering improves financial flexibility but comes with the possibility of equity dilution from new share issuance.

The stock has also shown sharp short-term swings driven by thematic news, which is a point investors should note.

Overall view

KBI Dong Yang Steel Pipe has faced persistent earnings weakness, with revenue declining for two straight years since 2023 and both an operating loss and a net loss recorded in 2025, though signs of improving financial structure also emerged, including a brief return to operating profit in the first quarter of 2026 and a falling debt-to-equity ratio.

In October 2026, confirmation of a steel pipe supply to SpaceX's rocket launch facilities sent shares to the daily upper limit, though the volume involved is small relative to the company's other large contracts such as the Iraq deal and the US distribution agreement, suggesting the significance lies more in establishing a quality reference than in a material earnings contribution.

The KRW 97.4 billion Iraq export contract remains in limbo with shipments suspended amid Middle East instability, and the US 50% steel tariff continues to weigh structurally on industry-wide profitability.

On the other hand, structural pipe demand tied to expanding semiconductor investment by Samsung Electronics and SK hynix, along with the Taiwan offshore wind project, are cited as medium-to-long-term growth drivers.

The volatility seen in alternating quarterly operating and net results may persist for some time, making it important to track upcoming quarterly reports and the execution of large orders going forward.
