02

Business structure

KBI Dong Yang Steel Pipe is a pipe manufacturer founded in 1973, with its headquarters in Cheonan, Chungcheongnam-do and its plant in Chungju, Chungcheongbuk-do.

Structural steel pipes, gas pipes and oil transmission pipes form the core of its business, and the company also manufactures and sells copper alloy made from 99.9% electrolytic copper blended with chromium and beryllium.

The company positions itself as a large-diameter pipe specialist, and a 2025 press report described four core product lines: offshore wind substructures, large square pipes for seismic-resistant construction, piping and line pipe, and H-beams.

Its BH beam line, which the company described as a first for the pipe industry, has an annual capacity of 40,000 tons. Key counterparties include EPC contractors, equipment suppliers and steel frame builders that buy its seismic-resistant pipes.

The offshore wind contract signed in May 2026 with Samil CNS runs through September 15, 2027. Segment revenue split is not broken out here, but pipe operations are the core business and are tied to construction activity, SOC investment and steel prices.

Overseas, the company has pursued supply to an Iraqi oil and gas pipeline project and to U.S. distributors. In domestic competition, large pipe makers such as SeAH Steel are the main rivals, and the company emphasizes custom small-batch production, short lead times and welding technology.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual revenue rose from KRW 266.2 billion in 2022 to KRW 278.3 billion in 2023, the largest year in the 2022 to 2025 span, a 4.6% increase. It then fell 10.1% to KRW 250.3 billion in 2024 and 6.2% to KRW 234.8 billion in 2025, marking two consecutive years of decline.

Operating profit dropped from KRW 7.74 billion in 2022 to KRW 2.2 billion in 2023, a 71.6% decline, recovered to KRW 6.68 billion in 2024, a 203.8% increase, and then turned into an operating loss of KRW 1.62 billion in 2025. The operating margin moved from 2.9% in 2022 to 0.8% in 2023, 2.7% in 2024 and -0.7% in 2025.

Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders went from KRW 3.97 billion in 2022 to a loss of KRW 1.56 billion in 2023, widened to a loss of KRW 19.6 billion in 2024, and narrowed to a loss of KRW 3.1 billion in 2025, which means three consecutive years of losses from 2023 to 2025.

For 2025, the company cited weaker downstream demand and the profitability hit from U.S. steel tariffs. Operating cash flow swung from an inflow of KRW 32.9 billion in 2024 to an outflow of KRW 31.6 billion in 2025.

On a quarterly basis, Q4 2025 revenue of KRW 89.1 billion rose 88.7% from the prior quarter, yet an operating loss of KRW 703 million persisted. Q1 2026 posted an operating profit of KRW 4.19 billion, a swing back to profit.

Q2 2026 returned to an operating loss of KRW 82.67 million, and Q3 2026 widened the operating loss to KRW 3.28 billion, marking two consecutive quarters of operating losses.

However, Q3 2026 net profit attributable to controlling shareholders turned positive at KRW 7.05 billion, and the sum over the last four quarters was KRW 4.37 billion. Because operating and net profit moved in different directions, the composition of non-operating items is a point to check in the next annual report.

05

Industry analysis

Demand for steel pipe tracks construction activity, SOC investment and plant orders. Weak downstream construction demand and the 50% U.S. steel tariff were cited as the main drivers of the 2025 profit decline.

The company said in September 2025 that protectionism and high tariffs by major trading partners had weakened its export price competitiveness.

In the United States, the removal of steel export quotas led the company to sign a memorandum of understanding with a major distributor for annual sales of at least 50,000 tons, according to a 2025 report.

Domestically, reports expected demand for structural pipes such as BH beams to grow with semiconductor plant construction. Offshore wind substructures are a field in which domestic orders and a Taiwan project are proceeding in parallel.

The cycle is tied to construction, SOC and overseas project awards, so earnings swing widely. Against peers such as SeAH Steel, the company emphasizes large-diameter capability, welding technology and short-lead-time small-batch supply.

Prices of hot-rolled coil and heavy plate, its raw materials, directly affect costs, and the company said proceeds from its 2025 rights offering would be used to secure raw materials in advance.

06

Outlook

The core of the 2026 outlook cited by the company and the press is overseas orders. A February 2026 report pointed to global orders in Iraq, the United States and Taiwan as drivers of earnings recovery, and the Iraqi export contract was reported at KRW 97.4 billion.

The Yeonggwang Yawol offshore wind contract signed in May 2026 is worth KRW 21.164 billion, with a term from May 14, 2026 to September 15, 2027. The contract carries no down payment or advance payment, and payment falls due within 60 days of billing.

The company disclosed that the contract amount and period may change depending on construction progress.

In September 2025, the company outlined plans for annual sales of at least 50,000 tons through U.S. distribution and about 80,000 tons, worth roughly KRW 128 billion, through cooperation with equipment makers and EPC contractors.

For a Taiwan offshore wind substructure project, it said it was preparing to supply 50,000 tons of pipe with raw material value of about KRW 60 billion. A report also said first-half 2026 revenue rose 29.3% year on year and operating profit turned positive, while net income was still a loss.

The next points to watch are how quickly this order backlog converts into quarterly revenue and whether Q3 2026 revenue of KRW 81.1 billion can be sustained.

Two consecutive quarters of operating losses

Risk factors

Tariff and trade environment

The 50% U.S. steel tariff was cited as a main reason for the 2025 profit decline. Planned U.S. distribution volumes and export pricing can change with trade conditions. The company itself flagged protectionism as a factor weighing on export price competitiveness.

Raw material prices and cash flow

Changes in hot-rolled coil and heavy plate prices feed directly into costs. Operating cash flow turned to an outflow of KRW 31.6 billion in 2025. Proceeds from the rights offering are planned for advance raw material purchases, so working capital needs could shift with raw material prices.

Order execution and revenue timing

The disclosure states that the offshore wind contract amount and period may change with construction progress. With no down payment or advance, collection timing depends on the 60-day payment term after billing. Earnings volatility could rise depending on how quickly overseas project volumes turn into quarterly revenue.

Overall view

KBI Dong Yang Steel Pipe recorded net losses attributable to controlling shareholders for three consecutive years from 2023 to 2025, and in 2025 swung to an operating loss of KRW 1.62 billion. The company cited weak downstream demand and U.S. steel tariffs as the main causes.

Quarterly results in 2026 have moved between profit and loss, with an operating profit of KRW 4.19 billion in Q1 and a net profit attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 7.05 billion in Q3, while operating losses persisted for two consecutive quarters in Q2 and Q3.

On the order side, a KRW 21.164 billion offshore wind pipe contract and a KRW 97.4 billion Iraqi export contract have been reported. The debt-to-equity ratio fell to 69.4% from 139.7% in 2023, while operating cash flow turned to an outflow of KRW 31.6 billion in 2025.

The next points to confirm are the direction of full-year 2026 operating profit, the composition of non-operating items in the 2026 annual report, and how fast the order backlog converts into quarterly revenue.
