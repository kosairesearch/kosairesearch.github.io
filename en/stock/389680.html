02

Business structure

Udmtek, founded in 2007, develops AI-based integrated digital manufacturing (UDM) solutions that interpret control logic and process data from automated equipment.

According to the company, a UDM solution is built from stage-specific products covering design, build, commissioning, operation and maintenance of equipment, lines and factories. Its flagship product is PLC eXpert, and the OPTRA Platform was developed in March 2025.

The stated target applications are PLC-based equipment in automotive, display and automation industries.

Revenue comes mainly from project-based smart factory and digital twin development contracts, so quarterly sales swinging from KRW 479 million in the third quarter of 2025 to KRW 1.31 billion in the fourth quarter can be read as tied to contract timing.

The company was selected as an AI factory specialist firm in August 2025, received Inno-Biz certification in December 2025, and had a US patent registered in January 2026. Its 2026 company profile lists 44 employees on a national pension basis.

Same-industry listed peers include Samsung SDS, Hyundai Autoever and POSCO DX Company, but the revenue gap is wide, which limits direct metric comparison. The company says its platform has been applied and validated at multiple global manufacturers.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual revenue was KRW 7.42 billion in 2023, KRW 6.07 billion in 2024 (down 18.1% year on year) and KRW 3.21 billion in 2025 (down 47.1%), falling for two consecutive years from 2024 to 2025, with 2023 the largest year and 2025 the smallest in 2023–2025.

Operating income moved from a profit of KRW 155 million in 2023 to an operating loss of KRW 2.01 billion in 2024, the year it turned to a loss, and the loss widened to KRW 3.65 billion in 2025. The operating margin fell from 2.1% in 2023 to -33.1% in 2024 and -113.5% in 2025.

Because the company's main cost items are labor and R&D, the sharp revenue decline appears to have left fixed-type costs flowing straight into losses, which is the basic reading.

The controlling-shareholder net loss was KRW 5.79 billion in 2023, KRW 5.2 billion in 2024 and KRW 3.5 billion in 2025, a third consecutive loss year with a narrowing loss, and the net loss including non-controlling interests was reported at the same amounts.

At the end of 2025, total equity was KRW 10.5 billion, all attributable to controlling shareholders, and total liabilities were KRW 4.38 billion.

Operating cash flow moved from an inflow of KRW 281 million in 2023 to an outflow of -KRW 1.34 billion in 2024 and -KRW 2.79 billion in 2025, negative for two consecutive years.

Quarterly, sales peaked at KRW 1.31 billion in the fourth quarter of 2025, up 174.3% from the prior quarter, when the operating loss was KRW 243 million, smaller than KRW 976 million in the third quarter of 2025.

Sales then fell for three consecutive quarters to KRW 890 million, KRW 743 million and KRW 614 million in 2026, while operating losses widened to KRW 559 million, KRW 693 million and KRW 1.92 billion. The controlling-shareholder net loss over the last four quarters totaled KRW 3.28 billion.

05

Industry analysis

Industrial smart factory software demand moves with manufacturers' equipment investment and digital transformation budgets.

The company describes its solutions as used in automotive and display industries and other automated equipment settings, so investment flows in those customer groups connect most directly to its revenue.

The position in the cycle is hard to judge with figures, so whether revenue recovers in step with manufacturing investment is the key point to watch. Among same-industry peers, Samsung SDS, LG CNS, Hyundai Autoever and POSCO DX Company are large IT service companies with broad customer bases and greater capital.

Udmtek instead emphasizes PLC-based equipment and process data solutions. The company presents field validation of its own platform as a differentiator. With 2025 revenue at KRW 3.21 billion, however, that differentiation needs to show up in customer expansion and in revenue before it translates into earnings.

Certifications and patents document the technology base but do not by themselves guarantee revenue growth. Compared with large peers, the company's results appear to depend more on the continuity of project wins than on scale.

Widening losses and cash outflows

Operating cash flow was -KRW 1.34 billion in 2024 and -KRW 2.79 billion in 2025. Losses and cash outflows are growing together.

No earnings base for valuation

10

Risk factors

Customer capex swings

If customers in automotive, display and other automated equipment industries cut investment, project orders may also decline. The company says its solutions are used in PLC-based equipment in these industries, so investment flows in these customer groups connect directly to revenue.

A slowdown in manufacturing investment could delay the timing of new contracts and reduce order sizes. This risk stems from customers' investment decisions, separate from the company's own win efforts.

Loss of key developers

The company develops its solutions with a workforce of 44 employees, and product upgrades depend on the skills of its R&D staff. If key development engineers leave, feature improvements to PLC eXpert and the OPTRA Platform could be delayed. Technical support quality for existing customers could also decline. Slow hiring could limit the capacity to execute new projects.

Commercialization of new products

The OPTRA Platform was developed in March 2025, and the company says its own platform has been applied and validated at multiple global manufacturers. If these deployments do not lead to new contracts and recurring revenue, recovery of development investment could be delayed.

Overseas customer expansion may depend on validation periods. If commercialization proceeds more slowly than expected, the timing of R&D cost recovery could also slip.

The controlling-shareholder net loss narrowed from KRW 5.79 billion in 2023 to KRW 3.5 billion in 2025 but widened to KRW 1.91 billion in the third quarter of 2026.

Quarterly sales have declined for three straight quarters through the third quarter of 2026, and the third-quarter operating loss of KRW 1.92 billion was the largest of the last five quarters. On the balance sheet, the debt ratio fell to 41.7% at the end of 2025 from 102.6% in 2023.

Operating cash flow, however, was negative in 2024 and 2025, and the controlling-shareholder net loss over the last four quarters totaled KRW 3.28 billion. Technology credentials, the project-based revenue model and the fixed-cost burden are the main variables that will shape future results.
