Report overview

Profit recovery led by the high-school unit amid demographic risk

Digital Daesung's operating profit rebounded from its 2024 low on the strength of its high-school education unit, but the shrinking school-age population remains a structural question for the company.

02

Business structure

Digital Daesung was founded in 2000 and listed on KOSDAQ in 2003 as an education content company. It serves students from elementary and middle school through high school graduates and repeat test-takers, operating online and offline lectures, textbooks, academy franchises and boarding academies.

Based on 2023 segment revenue, the mix was 20.28% elementary and middle school, 78.89% high school and 0.83% other, so the high-school segment accounts for most of sales.

The core of the high-school segment is its online college-entrance lecture brand, and a company official said in May 2026 that this brand accounts for about half of total company revenue.

Boarding academy operations run through Gangnam Daesung Boarding School (QUETTA, the medical school dormitory), and KRW 8.8 billion of medical dormitory revenue from September to December 2024 was consolidated.

Its Korean-language mock-exam brand has supplied programs to more than 800 academies nationwide as a B2B model, according to press reports. In early childhood and elementary reading education, its Hanwoori brand reported more than 100,000 paid members.

In online college-entrance lectures it competes with peers including MegaStudy, and the company says it keeps instructors in each subject area, which spreads the risk of losing any single instructor.

The structure was built through a series of acquisitions, starting with the 2010 merger with the Daesung MyMac lecture platform and including the 2024 acquisition of the medical school dormitory. In the second quarter of 2026, the sale of its stake in a Vietnam joint venture was completed.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual revenue rose from KRW 210.8 billion in 2022 to KRW 211.5 billion in 2023 (up 0.3%), KRW 217.7 billion in 2024 (up 2.9%) and KRW 253.8 billion in 2025 (up 16.6%). Revenue has grown for three consecutive years from 2023 through 2025, with 2025 the largest year and 2022 the smallest in the 2022 to 2025 period.

Operating profit fell from KRW 28.4 billion in 2022 to KRW 25 billion in 2023 (down 11.8%) and KRW 23.7 billion in 2024 (down 5.3%), then recovered to KRW 31.6 billion in 2025 (up 33.1%). The company said in a February 2026 filing that its 2025 revenue and operating profit were the highest in its history.

Operating margin went from 13.5% in 2022 to 11.8% in 2023 and 10.9% in 2024 before recovering to 12.4% in 2025.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders fell from KRW 21.3 billion in 2022 to KRW 12.5 billion in 2023 (down 41.1%), then rose two years in a row to KRW 15.2 billion in 2024 (up 21.1%) and KRW 20.9 billion in 2025 (up 37.9%).

Net income including non-controlling interests was KRW 25.6 billion in 2025, a different measure, with the gap reflecting the non-controlling share. By quarter, the third quarter of 2025 had revenue of KRW 80.2 billion, operating profit of KRW 21.2 billion and controlling-shareholder net income of KRW 16.1 billion.

The fourth quarter of 2025 swung to an operating loss of KRW 4.92 billion and a controlling-shareholder net loss of KRW 3.41 billion, with revenue of KRW 53.9 billion (down 32.8% from the prior quarter).

The first quarter of 2026 returned to profit, with revenue of KRW 69.8 billion, operating profit of KRW 10.2 billion and controlling-shareholder net income of KRW 5.54 billion.

Second-quarter controlling-shareholder net income of KRW 18.3 billion (up 230.0% from the prior quarter) included a gain on the sale of the Vietnam joint venture stake, according to the company, so it should be read as a one-time factor.

In the third quarter revenue reached KRW 89 billion (up 10.9% year on year), operating profit KRW 27.5 billion (up 29.4%) and controlling-shareholder net income KRW 21.7 billion (up 35.0%).

Controlling-shareholder net income over the last four quarters, from the fourth quarter of 2025 through the third quarter of 2026, totaled KRW 42.2 billion.

05

Industry analysis

Digital Daesung's core customers are high-school students preparing for college entrance and repeat test-takers, and demand moves with competition for top-tier university seats and medical school admissions.

The company argues that top-tier university seats are fixed even as the student population shrinks, so demand concentrates on established leading brands. Across the education sector, however, press coverage notes that concern about the shrinking school-age population as a medium-term growth constraint remains strong.

A government survey that ranked Korean-language private tutoring spending growth first was also cited, and the company uses this as evidence of steady demand in that subject. A company official said demand from repeat test-takers is expanding ahead of the 2028 college-entrance exam reform.

Harder English and Korean exams last year and the shift of students toward social studies subjects were cited by the company as additional drivers of demand for high-school lectures.

In online college-entrance lectures, the company competes with MegaStudy and others, and it said the subscriber base and revenue of its leading online lecture brand reached record levels.

The company also stresses a Korean-language education chain that runs from early reading programs to the college-entrance Korean exam. The company says profitability improved across all business units from the first to the third quarter of 2026, which it presents as evidence of a shift into an earnings recovery phase.

Shifts in demand caused by the shrinking school-age population and admissions reforms, however, affect the whole industry.

06

Outlook

The company said in March 2026 that it targets full-year 2026 operating profit in the KRW 50 billion range. Operating profit for the first nine months of 2026 was preliminarily reported at KRW 52.7 billion in a filing on November 4, 2026, which is above that target level.

Heungkuk Securities projected 2026 consolidated revenue of KRW 287.3 billion and operating profit of KRW 48.6 billion. CEO Kim Hee-seon said 2026 is an intermediate stage rather than the peak of the growth cycle and that the company expects to exceed this year's record results again in the following year.

The company said its 2028 Daesung Pass for students preparing for the 2028 exam has been selling well since October, and it cited enrollment results for the boarding academy winter school as a signal of demand.

On shareholder returns, the company retired 627,972 treasury shares in September 2026, equal to 2.3% of total shares issued. From September 22, 2026, it began buying treasury shares worth KRW 10 billion for cancellation, according to a November 5, 2026 report. The next checkpoint is the fourth quarter of 2026.

Because the fourth quarter of 2025 swung to an operating loss right after the peak third quarter, the fourth-quarter 2026 operating profit will be a key variable for whether the full-year target is reached.

Bull factors

Operating leverage in the high-school unit

The preliminary operating profit for the first nine months of 2026 was KRW 52.7 billion, already above the full-year 2025 figure of KRW 31.6 billion, according to a November 4, 2026 filing. The company said operating profit from its leading online lecture brand doubled year on year over the first nine months.

Its explanation is that added revenue on an existing platform is flowing through to profit, which is the basis for the margin recovery.

Expanding shareholder-return policy

The company has a stated policy to return at least 50% of controlling-shareholder net income over 2025 to 2028. The fiscal 2025 cash dividend totaled KRW 13.3 billion, a 63.8% payout ratio that met the requirements for a high-dividend company. In 2026 it retired 627,972 treasury shares and is buying back KRW 10 billion in additional shares.

Brand demand during admissions reform

The company explains that expanding demand from repeat test-takers underpins sales of this brand. The company reports that its 2028 Daesung Pass has been selling well since October. Its argument is that proven brands and content gain prominence during periods of admissions change.

Bear factors

Structural pressure from a shrinking school-age population

Press coverage notes that concern about the shrinking school-age population as a long-term constraint remains strong across the education sector. The company's growth depends on demand concentrating in leading brands, so whether that concentration holds as the pool of test-takers shrinks is the open question.

The company's argument that top-tier university seats are fixed answers this concern, but any shift in demand must be confirmed in reported results.

Quarterly volatility and one-time gains

The fourth quarter of 2025 swung to an operating loss of KRW 4.92 billion and a controlling-shareholder net loss of KRW 3.41 billion. The company said the second-quarter 2026 controlling-shareholder net income of KRW 18.3 billion included a gain on the sale of its Vietnam joint venture stake. How stable quarterly profit is excluding such items can only be seen over the short period reported so far.

Debt burden and capital allocation

Total liabilities at the end of 2025 were KRW 175.5 billion, above total equity of KRW 170.5 billion. The debt-to-equity ratio fell from 109.8% in 2024 to 102.9% in 2025 but remains well above the 48.6% in 2022 and 46.7% in 2023. As buybacks and dividends expand, whether financial capacity can support them needs to be confirmed.

10

Risk factors

Admissions-policy risk

Subject-level demand may shift depending on the detailed design of the 2028 college-entrance exam reform. Such shifts can move sales across particular instructors and products.

The company views periods of admissions change as favorable for brand strength, but a change in the exam design could also change the demand structure.

Concentration and instructor risk

The high-school segment accounted for 78.89% of revenue in 2023, and the company says its leading online lecture brand represents about half of total revenue. Because the business is concentrated in college-entrance education, demand changes in one segment have a large effect on group results.

The company says its subject-by-subject instructor teams spread the risk of any single departure, but the effect of losing top instructors has to be confirmed in reported quarters.

Balance-sheet and capital-return risk

With total liabilities above total equity, the company is running buybacks alongside dividends. The fiscal 2025 cash dividend of KRW 13.3 billion equaled roughly 64% of controlling-shareholder net income of KRW 20.9 billion.

Keeping the payout level steady while quarterly profit fluctuates requires cash flow support, and operating cash flow in 2025 was KRW 55.5 billion.

Overall view

Digital Daesung recorded full-year 2025 revenue of KRW 253.8 billion and operating profit of KRW 31.6 billion, the largest year in the 2022 to 2025 period. Operating profit in 2026 rose in the second and third quarters after a return to profit in the first quarter.

The high-school unit, including the online lecture brand and boarding academies, sits at the center of the recovery, and the company attributes the gains to operating leverage and fixed costs already covered.

The preliminary nine-month 2026 operating profit of KRW 52.7 billion is above the company's full-year target range. On the other side, the shrinking school-age population, demand shifts from the 2028 exam reform, quarterly losses and the effect of one-time gains still need confirmation.

Shareholder returns rest on a policy to return at least half of controlling-shareholder net income over 2025 to 2028, backed by treasury share retirements and buybacks. The debt-to-equity ratio fell from 109.8% in 2024 to 102.9% in 2025 but remains above the 2022 level.

The next checkpoints are the fourth-quarter 2026 result and the confirmed figures in the 2026 annual report. This report does not offer an investment opinion, rating or target price.
