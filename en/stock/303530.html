02

Business structure

Innodep, founded in January 2008, develops intelligent video security and integrated control platforms. Its core business applies artificial intelligence to video from surveillance devices and builds that analysis into control-center systems, with the VURIX VMS platform as its flagship product.

The platform uses an open structure that connects hundreds of camera models and IoT devices. Several outlets describe the company as the leader in municipal integrated control centers in Korea, with more than half of that market.

Maintenance and other service revenue act as a recurring base, and an independent research note put maintenance sales at KRW 5.7 billion in 2023, KRW 6.4 billion in 2024 and KRW 7.6 billion in 2025. Service revenue including maintenance reached KRW 2.72 billion in the third quarter of 2026, up 36.1% year on year.

Its main customers are local governments and public agencies, and in February 2026 it was selected for a Gwangju smart-services project with a total contract value of KRW 6.35 billion, of which its consortium share is 70%.

Its product line is widening to the VUCatcher AI video analysis engine, the VUNex next-generation platform, the Kinderlog child-development observation platform, and mobile AI control built with a robot maker. Overseas, it signed a KRW 7.3 billion AI control contract with the government of Belize in June 2026. Hanwha Vision and IDIS are also named as related video-security companies.

04

Earnings analysis

On DART-filed annual figures, revenue rose from KRW 76.4 billion in 2022 to KRW 84 billion in 2023 (up 10.0% year on year) and KRW 86.8 billion in 2024 (up 3.3%), then fell to KRW 70.7 billion in 2025 (down 18.5%). Among 2022 to 2025, 2024 was the largest year for revenue and 2025 the smallest.

Operating profit was KRW 413 million in 2022 and KRW 636 million in 2023 (up 53.9%), before the company posted an operating loss of KRW 1.93 billion in 2024, and the 2025 operating loss widened to KRW 2.9 billion. The operating margin moved from 0.8% in 2023 to -2.2% in 2024 and -4.1% in 2025.

Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 1.03 billion in 2023 (up 82.4%), then a net loss of KRW 1.54 billion in 2024 and KRW 3.02 billion in 2025, making 2024 and 2025 two consecutive loss years.

Net income including non-controlling interests is recorded at KRW 563 million in 2022, KRW 1.03 billion in 2023, a net loss of KRW 1.54 billion in 2024 and a net loss of KRW 3.02 billion in 2025.

Operating cash flow was -KRW 3.48 billion in 2022, KRW 4.15 billion in 2023, KRW 5.38 billion in 2024 and -KRW 4.33 billion in 2025.

The debt ratio rose from 41.5% in 2022 and 38.1% in 2023 to 70.1% in 2024 and 101.2% in 2025, and at the end of 2025 total equity was KRW 34.6 billion against total liabilities of KRW 35 billion.

Quarterly results show an operating loss of KRW 2.16 billion in the third quarter of 2025, an operating profit of KRW 2.54 billion in the fourth quarter that marked a swing to profit, and an operating loss of KRW 3.65 billion in the first quarter of 2026 that marked a return to loss.

The operating loss then narrowed to KRW 601 million in the second quarter and KRW 139 million in the third quarter of 2026. Quarterly sales moved from KRW 28.2 billion in the fourth quarter of 2025 to KRW 8.52 billion, KRW 19.2 billion and KRW 14.3 billion over the next three quarters.

Net loss attributable to controlling shareholders over the last four quarters totaled KRW 1.44 billion.

A news report on the third-quarter filing said the separate-basis operating profit was KRW 150 million, while the consolidated result remained a loss, and the company attributed the improvement to fixed-cost cuts, a larger maintenance business and project-level profit management.

05

Industry analysis

The video security market is widening from simple CCTV monitoring into intelligent safety management that combines AI video analytics, facial recognition, access control and integrated control.

Public-sector and corporate security budgets, along with upgrades to smart-city and industrial-site safety infrastructure, are cited as medium-term demand drivers.

An October 2026 press review of 13 video-security companies described a split between winners and laggards in their results, and an industry source said technology strength, public contract wins and actual revenue contribution should be weighed together.

Innodep is described as the leader in Korea's municipal integrated control centers, with more than half of that market, a share based on company statements and press releases. Hanwha Vision and IDIS are also named among related video-security companies.

Because public-sector work depends on tender timing and budget execution, its results tend to follow procurement schedules.

In private markets the company targets mid-sized demand such as retail, warehouses, schools and office buildings through VUNex, with proof-of-concept work underway with two Japanese retail brands and a Thai firm.

Overseas, the Belize contract and a Guatemala City deployment serve as references for Latin America, while North American proof-of-concept work and partnership talks continue. Whether public AI-transition demand and private expansion move at pace will shape the sector's direction.

06

Outlook

The company has set a target of an annual operating profit on both separate and consolidated bases for 2026, citing an order backlog of about KRW 42.1 billion and an improved cost structure as its basis.

In a shareholder letter published in February 2026, it said carry-over orders for 2026 were about KRW 22.6 billion, up roughly 36% year on year. It has also won a contract to convert 8,942 CCTV cameras in Ulsan Metropolitan City to a Korean NPU-based AI control system.

Trials of the VLM-based solution with major local governments, including Ulsan, are scheduled to run through the end of 2026.

The mobile patrol-robot project plans to fix its core functions by the end of 2026 and to start proof-of-concept work with local governments and the national police agency in the first half of 2027.

On capital policy, an extraordinary shareholders' meeting on December 18, 2026 will vote on transferring KRW 20 billion of capital reserves to retained earnings, which the company says would lay the groundwork for cash dividends and share buybacks.

An independent research house noted on November 17, 2026 that the transfer amount does not itself represent the size of shareholder returns, which will depend on future earnings and cash flow.

The next points to track are whether the 2026 annual operating profit is confirmed and how quickly the order backlog converts into revenue.

Bull factors

Growing recurring revenue base

Maintenance sales rose from KRW 5.7 billion in 2023 to KRW 6.4 billion in 2024 and KRW 7.6 billion in 2025, and an independent research house projected that annual contract-based maintenance would exceed KRW 10 billion in 2026.

Maintenance contracts that follow installed public control systems are cited as a factor supporting revenue stability.

Order backlog and overseas references

In June 2026 the company signed an AI control expansion contract worth about KRW 7.3 billion with the government of Belize, and the selection through public bidding was highlighted. The company plans to extend the Latin American reference into North America and Asia.

Improving third-quarter profit trend

The consolidated operating loss narrowed to KRW 139 million in the third quarter of 2026 from KRW 2.16 billion a year earlier. Press reports said the gross margin rose about 5 percentage points and selling and administrative expenses fell, which supported the separate-basis profit. The company has set a target of an annual operating profit for 2026.

Bear factors

Wider losses in 2025

The 2025 operating loss of KRW 2.9 billion was larger than the KRW 1.93 billion loss of 2024, and the net loss attributable to controlling shareholders widened to KRW 3.02 billion. The operating margin fell to -4.1% from -2.2% in 2024.

The cumulative operating loss for January to September 2026 was about KRW 4.39 billion, down from about KRW 5.43 billion a year earlier, but still a loss.

Heavier balance-sheet burden

The debt ratio climbed from 41.5% in 2022 to 101.2% in 2025. Operating cash flow was -KRW 4.33 billion in 2025. Total liabilities at the end of 2025 reached KRW 35 billion, roughly the same size as total equity of KRW 34.6 billion.

Sales decline and quarterly swings

Revenue for 2025 was KRW 70.7 billion, down 18.5% year on year. Cumulative sales for January to September 2026 were about KRW 42 billion, below about KRW 42.6 billion a year earlier. Quarterly sales also swung widely, from KRW 8.52 billion in the first quarter of 2026 to KRW 19.2 billion in the second quarter.

Risk factors

Public procurement timing

A large share of revenue is tied to local-government and public-agency projects, so quarterly results can swing sharply with acceptance and delivery timing.

Sales fell from KRW 28.2 billion in the fourth quarter of 2025 to KRW 8.52 billion in the first quarter of 2026 before recovering to KRW 19.2 billion in the second quarter. The speed at which the order backlog converts into revenue will determine how much this risk weighs.

Share price volatility and trading controls

The stock was designated a short-term trading caution stock for June 23, 2026, after a 17.34% fall over three days, with concentrated selling by a small number of accounts cited as the reason. Higher designation stages, investment warning and investment risk, can lead to restrictions on trading, including suspension.

New-business execution and shareholder returns

VUNex and the mobile AI control business are largely at the proof-of-concept stage, and the patrol-robot PoC is planned to start in the first half of 2027. Whether the KRW 20 billion capital-reserve transfer leads to actual dividends or share buybacks depends on earnings and cash flow. Operating cash flow was negative in 2025, which may constrain the capacity to execute returns.

Overall view

Innodep recorded an operating loss of KRW 2.9 billion in 2025, extending its losses, but its consolidated operating loss narrowed to KRW 139 million in the third quarter of 2026. It posted an operating profit of KRW 2.54 billion in the fourth quarter of 2025, so quarterly results remain volatile.

Recurring maintenance revenue grew to KRW 7.6 billion in 2025 and the order backlog expanded to about KRW 42.1 billion, which are cited as strengths. On the other side, 2025 revenue fell 18.5%, the debt ratio climbed to 101.2% and operating cash flow was -KRW 4.33 billion in 2025, increasing the financial burden.

New businesses such as the Belize contract and AI robot control show growth potential, but many are still at the proof-of-concept stage.

With strengths and weaknesses both present, the points to confirm next are full-year operating profit in the 2026 annual report, the trend in the debt ratio, and how quickly the order backlog converts into revenue.
