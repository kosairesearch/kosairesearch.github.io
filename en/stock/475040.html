02

Business structure

StradVision is a vehicle vision AI software company founded in 2014.

Its core product is the SVNet object recognition solution, which analyzes camera footage to identify vehicles, pedestrians, lanes and traffic signals, and its product line covers front vision for driving, surround vision for parking and multi-camera multi-vision.

The company highlights that its software does not depend on a specific chip or camera and says it supports more than 30 automotive semiconductor platforms. Its customers are global automakers (OEMs) and Tier-1 suppliers.

The company says its software is applied to 50-plus vehicle models from 13 global OEMs, with cumulative installations above 5 million vehicles.

Revenue comes in two forms: development services (NRE) billed per customer project during development, and licensing fees that accumulate with vehicle sales after mass production. Licensing was about 14% of revenue in 2025, so the development-service model still dominates.

Overseas customers accounted for 86% of 2025 revenue. The product scope is widening from ADAS toward end-to-end (E2E) autonomous driving, and the company also points to robotics, defense, infrastructure, aviation and drones.

In 2026, the company says it recorded revenue from Level 2 and 2+ global supply contracts, Indian commercial vehicle and Middle East electric vehicle projects, and pre-loading its software onto chips from companies such as AMD. Aptiv, Hyundai Motor Group and LG Electronics participate as strategic investors.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual revenue was KRW 7.17 billion in 2023, KRW 11.5 billion in 2024 (up 60.9% year on year) and KRW 18.1 billion in 2025 (up 56.9% year on year). It rose in both 2024 and 2025, and 2025 was the largest year of the 2023 to 2025 period.

Over the same span, the operating loss was KRW 64.9 billion in 2023, KRW 63.9 billion in 2024 and KRW 58.6 billion in 2025, which makes three consecutive years of operating losses, with the loss narrowing in 2024 and 2025.

Net loss attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 54.4 billion in 2023, KRW 65.5 billion in 2024 (losses widened) and KRW 62.2 billion in 2025 (losses narrowed). The net loss including non-controlling interests matches the controlling-shareholder figure in each year.

The operating margin improved from -905.1% in 2023 to -553.5% in 2024 and -323.6% in 2025, so the loss shrank relative to revenue. The debt ratio was -119.7% in 2023 and -119.9% in 2024; a negative ratio means total equity was negative in those years.

Total equity at the end of 2025 was KRW 25.3 billion, all attributable to controlling shareholders, against total liabilities of KRW 27.2 billion, giving a debt ratio of 107.5%. Operating cash flow was -KRW 44.1 billion in 2023, -KRW 50.9 billion in 2024 and -KRW 47.1 billion in 2025, an outflow in each year.

Quarterly revenue went from KRW 1.15 billion in Q3 2025 to KRW 10.4 billion in Q4 2025 (up 800.0% quarter on quarter), then fell to KRW 3.57 billion in Q1 2026 (down 65.6%). It then rose to KRW 6.42 billion in Q2 and KRW 8.97 billion in Q3 2026, an increase in each of the two quarters from Q2 to Q3.

Q3 2026 revenue was up 677.8% year on year. The company said cumulative revenue through Q3 2025 was 42.7% of full-year revenue, with the rest concentrated in Q4.

Operating losses were KRW 19.9 billion in Q3 2025, KRW 8.69 billion in Q4 2025, KRW 13.2 billion in Q1 2026, KRW 11.1 billion in Q2 2026 and KRW 9.06 billion in Q3 2026. The loss widened in Q1 2026 and narrowed quarter on quarter in Q2 and Q3.

Some press reports described three consecutive quarterly declines starting from Q1, but on the filed figures the Q1 2026 loss was larger than the Q4 2025 loss.

Net loss attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 19.5 billion in Q3 2025, KRW 8.76 billion in Q4 2025, KRW 13.1 billion in Q1 2026, KRW 11.3 billion in Q2 2026 and KRW 10.3 billion in Q3 2026. The total for the latest four quarters, from Q4 2025 through Q3 2026, was KRW 43.5 billion.

05

Industry analysis

The auto industry is being reorganized from hardware-centric to software-defined vehicles (SDV), and wider ADAS adoption is lifting demand for camera-based recognition software.

In its 2026 IPO materials, the company projected that vehicles fitted with its software under customers' production plans would reach about 1.2 million in 2026, 3.5 million in 2027 and 10 million in 2028.

These are company projections, and the company itself notes that mass-production volumes can change with customers' production plans. On timing, automotive software revenue tends to concentrate at year-end, when OEM development sign-offs and mass-production inspections cluster.

Company materials show first-half revenue in 2023 to 2025 at 31% to 36% of each year's total, with more than 60% of revenue booked in the second half. On competition, the company emphasizes that its software is not tied to a specific chip, which it presents as a differentiator.

Whether OEMs and chipmakers develop recognition software in-house or buy it from outside suppliers will shape the size of the opportunity. The company points to more than 5 million vehicles with its software in mass production and three consecutive years on the Emerging AI+X Top 100 list in image and video processing.

Among domestic IT and software names, Hyundai Autoever is a comparison point because it also works in automotive software, although the scale and revenue structure differ considerably.

The company also says that intense global competition for GPU capacity makes secure training infrastructure central to development speed.

06

Outlook

In the securities registration statement for its IPO, the company set a 2026 full-year revenue target of about KRW 30.8 billion. At its IPO press briefing, it presented a 2026 target of KRW 30.0 billion, up about 66% from the prior year.

Because the two documents describe the target differently, the figures should be checked against the regular reports once they are filed. By the company's own tally, cumulative revenue through Q3 2026 reached 61.4% of the registration-statement target.

The company expects annual results to grow as OEM development sign-offs and mass-production schedules concentrate in Q4. About KRW 39.0 billion of new development contracts signed in the first half of 2026 will be recognized as revenue across 2026 and 2027, followed by licensing revenue after mass production.

The company said the second half should reflect a global supply contract with Aptiv worth KRW 23.7 billion, an Indian commercial vehicle project and the AMD automotive AI platform collaboration.

The company calls 2027 the year when major projects, including a next-generation platform for a global Tier-1 supplier, enter mass production, and it treats that point as the turning point for its business structure.

It has set targets of a quarterly profit during 2027 and a full-year operating profit in 2028, and it presented revenue of KRW 147.9 billion for 2029 as a long-term goal. Its next-generation E2E autonomous driving product is being co-developed with a global Tier-1 supplier, with completion targeted for 2027.

The company says IPO proceeds will fund GPU servers, cloud infrastructure, algorithm development, R&D hiring and new businesses. These targets are the company's own, and whether they are met will be confirmed through future regular reports.

Bull factors

Revenue visibility from new contracts

The company says about KRW 39.0 billion of development contracts secured in the first half of 2026 will be recognized across 2026 and 2027. A KRW 23.7 billion global supply contract with Aptiv is also expected to appear in second-half results. Cumulative revenue through Q3 2026 has surpassed full-year 2025 revenue.

Mass-production track record and platform breadth

The company says cumulative installations exceed 5 million vehicles and that its software is covered by supply contracts across 50-plus models from 13 global OEMs.

Support for more than 30 automotive chip platforms is presented as a strength, because automakers changing chip suppliers would not need to redevelop the software. That breadth could act as an entry barrier in competitive bidding.

Narrowing operating loss and better margin

The operating loss narrowed to KRW 58.6 billion in 2025 from the prior year, and the operating margin improved from -905.1% in 2023 to -323.6% in 2025. The loss narrowed quarter on quarter in both Q2 and Q3 2026. The fact that losses shrank while revenue grew is a positive factor.

Bear factors

Three years of losses and cash outflow

Operating losses and net losses attributable to controlling shareholders persisted for three consecutive years from 2023 to 2025, and operating cash flow was negative in each year. Operating cash flow was -KRW 47.1 billion in 2025.

The combined net loss attributable to controlling shareholders over the latest four quarters was KRW 43.5 billion.

Concentrated revenue and a small licensing share

Licensing was about 14% of 2025 revenue, and overseas customers accounted for 86%. Because revenue clusters in Q4, it fell from KRW 10.4 billion in Q4 2025 to KRW 3.57 billion in Q1 2026, a 65.6% quarter-on-quarter decline. Given that quarterly swings are large, a delay in one contract can weigh heavily on a single quarter's results.

Profit timing depends on mass production

The profit targets, a quarterly profit during 2027 and a full-year profit in 2028, both assume next-generation projects enter mass production on schedule. The company has also said mass-production volumes can change with customers' production plans. The operating loss widened in Q1 2026, so the path to these targets could be uneven from quarter to quarter.

10

Risk factors

Mass-production timing and customer volumes

Licensing revenue builds only after projects under development reach mass production on schedule. The company notes that mass-production volumes depend on customers' production plans. If production starts slip, the gap between the decline in development-service revenue and the rise in licensing revenue could widen.

Investment needs and technology transition costs

The company is shifting investment from forward-sensor work toward E2E and new businesses, and says spending on GPU servers and cloud infrastructure will continue. If costs rise in this process, the pace of loss reduction could be slower than expected. The company says data automation and shared platforms should lower overall development costs.

Post-listing supply and demand and overseas exposure

The company has said short-term supply and demand after the listing has drawn attention. With overseas customers making up 86% of 2025 revenue, the business is also exposed to the timing of overseas customer projects and to currency movements. The share price can move in ways that diverge from reported results because of these factors.

Overall view

StradVision's revenue rose from KRW 7.17 billion in 2023 to KRW 18.1 billion in 2025, and cumulative revenue through Q3 2026 has passed full-year 2025 revenue. Operating losses, however, were recorded in each year from 2023 to 2025, and operating cash flow was -KRW 47.1 billion in 2025.

The operating loss widened to KRW 13.2 billion in Q1 2026 and then narrowed in Q2 and Q3. The revenue model is still built mainly on development services, with licensing at about 14% of 2025 revenue.

The company expects revenue to grow in 2026 and 2027 based on about KRW 39.0 billion of new development contracts and a KRW 23.7 billion Aptiv supply contract, and it presents 2027 mass production as the start of its business transition.

On the positive side are more than 5 million vehicles in production and support for more than 30 chip platforms. On the negative side are continuing losses and dependence on the mass-production schedule.

The company's targets of a quarterly profit in 2027 and a full-year profit in 2028 have not yet been confirmed by reported results. The next checkpoint is the 2026 annual report.
