Hantop: Shrinking Sales and a Fair Trade Fine Under Appeal

Hantop turned to a controlling-shareholder net profit in 2025, but its sales have declined for four straight quarters through the third quarter of 2026, operating losses have continued, and the company has filed an administrative suit against a Fair Trade Commission fine of about KRW 24.3 billion.

  1. 1

    Third-quarter 2026 revenue was KRW 17.5 billion, down 15.8% year on year, and the company posted an operating loss of KRW 1.15 billion.

  2. 2

    Full-year 2025 net income attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 2.09 billion, a swing from a loss in 2024, while operating profit fell 22.0% to KRW 2.92 billion.

  3. 3

    A Fair Trade Commission fine of about KRW 24.3 billion was recognized in one go in the first half of 2026, enlarging the separate net loss to KRW 29.2 billion, and the company has sought to annul the fine and to suspend its enforcement.

02

Business structure

Hantop is a flour-milling and feed company classified under the KOSDAQ food and tobacco sector and the consumer staples group. The company says its flour-milling division keeps a relatively stable operating flow because staple goods have low price elasticity.

The feed division decided in late October 2024 to halt production, effective from the first day of the following month. Even so, the 2025 earnings commentary cited intensified competition in feed sales as one reason for the overall decline.

For the 2024 improvement, a company official pointed to halting a loss-making business unit and company-wide cost reduction. The company is pursuing new businesses including confectionery and bakery manufacturing and food-ingredient wholesale and retail.

In 2026 it has also been reviewing use of investment property and its headquarters site alongside cash assets, while carrying out cost cuts. A flour-centered structure is sensitive to raw-material and procurement costs, so cost variables and pricing policy weigh heavily on results.

04

Earnings analysis

On a consolidated basis, annual revenue declined from KRW 99.1 billion in 2022 to KRW 94.8 billion in 2023 (down 4.3% year on year), KRW 84.9 billion in 2024 (down 10.5%), and KRW 82.4 billion in 2025 (down 2.9%). Among 2022 to 2025, 2022 was the largest year by revenue and 2025 the smallest.

Operating profit rose from KRW 333 million in 2022 to KRW 912 million in 2023 (up 174.1%) and KRW 3.75 billion in 2024 (up 311.1%), then fell to KRW 2.92 billion in 2025 (down 22.0%). Operating margin moved from 0.3% to 1.0%, 4.4%, and 3.5%, peaking in 2024 and easing slightly in 2025.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders swung from losses of KRW 4.73 billion in 2022, KRW 10.3 billion in 2023 and KRW 5.36 billion in 2024 to a profit of KRW 2.09 billion in 2025. Total net income moved in the same direction, with 2025 at KRW 2.09 billion.

On a quarterly basis, revenue fell for four straight quarters from KRW 20.7 billion in Q3 2025 to KRW 17.5 billion in Q3 2026. Operating profit of KRW 773 million in Q3 2025 turned into an operating loss of KRW 889 million in Q1 2026, followed by losses of KRW 1.25 billion in Q2 and KRW 1.15 billion in Q3.

The Q2 2026 net loss attributable to controlling shareholders widened to KRW 26.9 billion, a period in which a Fair Trade Commission fine of about KRW 24.3 billion was recognized as a provision and a one-off non-operating expense.

Q3 2026 returned to a net profit attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 234 million. On a separate basis, the first half of 2026 showed revenue of KRW 32.9 billion, an operating loss of KRW 1.9 billion and a net loss of KRW 29.2 billion.

Operating cash flow was -KRW 11.5 billion in 2022, KRW 8.69 billion in 2023, KRW 22.93 million in 2024 and KRW 2.45 billion in 2025. At end-2025, total liabilities were KRW 91 billion and total equity was KRW 47.3 billion, with a debt-to-equity ratio of 192.3%.

05

Industry analysis

Flour milling and feed rest on staple-goods demand, which the company describes as relatively insulated from economic swings. Currency is a direct cost variable, however: the company said the first half of 2026 reflected cost pressure from a sharp rise in the exchange rate and foreign-currency losses.

The same period was also affected by price cuts across the industry in response to a Fair Trade Commission investigation, which shows that the sector as a whole faces pricing and regulatory forces at once.

The 2025 earnings commentary attributed the feed sales decline to intensified competition, and the company responded by halting production in that unit. Industry-average cycle indicators are not presented as numbers in this report.

In competitive terms, Hantop is weighted toward raw and intermediate supply rather than branded consumer goods, so pricing power and the ability to pass through costs drive results.

The company frames its service to trading partners as a way to build its brand, and its push into confectionery, bakery and food-ingredient distribution runs in the same direction as its new-business plan. Overall, flour demand is steady, but costs, pricing and regulation all bear on results at the same time.

06

Outlook

For the second half of 2026, the company set out plans to reduce foreign-exchange losses as the currency stabilizes, to improve profitability by bringing product prices up to realistic levels, to raise production efficiency, and to cut costs company-wide.

Whether these plans translate into profit will show up in quarterly operating margins. On the fine, the company has filed an administrative suit at the Seoul High Court to annul the penalty and filed a request to suspend payment and corrective orders.

It says it will argue in court that the fine is unjustified and will disclose the outcomes of the suspension request and the suit as they come. After receiving the fine notice, it plans to apply for installment payment alongside the suit to address liquidity.

On assets, it is reviewing use of investment property and its headquarters site and is pursuing a value-enhancement plan that includes open-market share purchases by the largest shareholder.

In July 2026 the board approved a 5-for-1 reverse split, which changed the par value from KRW 500 to KRW 2,500 and reduced the total shares from 32,316,799 to 6,463,359, and the company said it aims to ease short-term supply-demand swings by managing free float.

The key checkpoints are whether price increases halt the sales decline and whether currency stability eases cost pressure.

Bull factors

Operating margin recovery

Operating margin rose from 0.3% in 2022 to 4.4% in 2024 and held at 3.5% in 2025. The level stayed above 2022, which suggests cost cuts and production restructuring had some effect. Losses in 2026 quarters mean the sustainability of this trend still needs confirmation.

Asset monetization and shareholder-return plans

The company is weighing use of investment property and its headquarters site, and is pursuing a value-enhancement plan that includes open-market share purchases by the largest shareholder. Using non-cash assets could support liquidity. The size and timing of execution remain to be confirmed through disclosures.

Staple demand and planned price realignment

The company says the flour division runs relatively steadily because staple goods have low price elasticity. It plans to improve profitability through price realignment. Whether price increases can halt the sales decline is the test for this bullish factor.

Bear factors

Persistent revenue decline

Revenue fell for four straight quarters from KRW 20.7 billion in Q3 2025 to KRW 17.5 billion in Q3 2026, and Q3 2026 was down 15.8% year on year. Full-year 2025 revenue was down 2.9% and smaller than the KRW 99.1 billion of 2022. Competition in feed and the halt of production there have narrowed the revenue base.

Fine and widening losses

A Fair Trade Commission fine of about KRW 24.3 billion was reflected in first-half 2026 results, widening the separate net loss to KRW 29.2 billion. Operating losses continued from Q1 to Q3 2026, and the Q2 net loss attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 26.9 billion. The final burden of the fine is not settled until the suit concludes.

Currency and cost pressure

The company said cost burden and foreign-exchange losses tied to a sharp currency rise weighed on first-half 2026 results. How much of the cost increase can be passed through depends on pricing, and industry-wide price cuts may limit that room. Currency stabilization is a premise of the company's second-half plan.

Risk factors

Legal and regulatory risk

The administrative suit and the request to suspend the fine have not yet produced a result. The ruling could change the fine burden and its accounting treatment. The company says it will disclose outcomes as they come, and industry-wide price cuts tied to the regulatory probe are another risk factor.

Cost and currency volatility

The flour division's costs shift with raw-material prices and the exchange rate. Raw-material swings were cited as a drag on first-half 2025 results. In first-half 2026, currency spikes and foreign-exchange losses added to non-operating pressure. If the currency turns volatile again, the second-half plan could be undermined.

Balance sheet and liquidity

At end-2025, total liabilities of KRW 91 billion exceeded total equity of KRW 47.3 billion, with a debt-to-equity ratio of 192.3%. Net losses attributable to controlling shareholders over the last four quarters total KRW 29 billion. The company plans to pair installment payments with the suit to address liquidity. The timing and size of asset sales or share purchases will affect financial stability.

Overall view

Hantop is a flour-milling and feed company that returned to a controlling-shareholder net profit in 2025, a sign of earnings recovery. Yet revenue fell for four straight quarters into 2026, and operating losses have lasted three quarters.

The first half of 2026 carried a Fair Trade Commission fine of about KRW 24.3 billion, which widened the net loss. The company has filed a suit and a suspension request and set out second-half plans on price realignment, currency stability and cost cuts.

Asset use, open-market share purchases by the largest shareholder and the reverse split are also under way as value-enhancement steps. The shares trade below net asset value, but four quarters of net losses leave no earnings-based multiple.
