Report overview

Memory Fab Orders: H2 Revenue Conversion Is the Test

Wonik IPS has built up a much larger order backlog from Samsung Electronics' and SK hynix's memory capacity expansion. Its reported first-half 2026 results, however, combine delayed revenue recognition with a large non-operating gain, so the next thing to check is whether equipment revenue actually lands in the second half.

02

Business structure

Wonik IPS was created in 2016 when Wonik Holdings spun off its semiconductor, display and solar equipment businesses, and it relisted on KOSDAQ the same year.

Its core products are front-end deposition and thermal processing tools: its CVD, ALD and metal deposition equipment is used in key DRAM, NAND and logic process steps.

By Meritz Securities' tally, 2025 revenue was about KRW 707 billion from semiconductors and about KRW 202.6 billion from display, so semiconductors made up roughly three quarters. In Q1 2026 semiconductors accounted for KRW 145.9 billion (88%) and display for KRW 19 billion (12%), a heavier tilt toward chips.

Samsung Electronics is the largest customer. It is also a major shareholder with about 7.5%, second to the Wonik Holdings group at about 33%.

The company supplies Samsung Electronics' memory and foundry operations as well as SK hynix's memory lines, and Kiwoom Securities said new tools such as ALD for DRAM and NAND and HBM equipment are driving a structural upgrade. Overseas, it supplies China's CXMT, and a new overseas customer is evaluating its CVD tools.

In deposition it competes with Jusung Engineering, Eugene Technology and Tesco at home and with large global toolmakers abroad. Its position rests on installed share in domestic customers' mass-production lines and on demand for localized equipment.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual revenue fell 31.8% from KRW 1.01 trillion in 2022 to KRW 690.3 billion in 2023. It then rose to KRW 748.2 billion in 2024 and KRW 909.8 billion in 2025, two consecutive years of growth from 2024 through 2025.

Operating results swung from an operating profit of KRW 97.6 billion in 2022 to an operating loss of KRW 18.1 billion in 2023, returned to a profit of KRW 10.6 billion in 2024, and rose 593.6% to KRW 73.8 billion in 2025.

Operating margin recovered from -2.6% in 2023 to 1.4% in 2024 and 8.1% in 2025, still below the 9.6% of 2022. Quarterly operating profit declined from KRW 36.5 billion in Q2 2025 to KRW 27.5 billion in Q3, KRW 17.2 billion in Q4 and KRW 10.7 billion in Q1 2026.

Meritz Securities estimated that Q4 2025 carried about KRW 15 billion of one-off costs, including year-end bonuses. The same broker said Q1 2026 operating profit was helped by a richer semiconductor and parts mix and a reversal of inventory provisions.

Q2 2026 revenue of KRW 216.5 billion rose 31.3% from the prior quarter but fell 10.6% year on year, and operating profit of KRW 18.4 billion was down 49.6% year on year. Net profit attributable to owners that quarter reached KRW 89.9 billion, up 225.2% year on year.

Pre-tax profit in the preliminary filing was about KRW 101.2 billion, so most of the profit came from outside operations. Whether that gain is one-off needs to be checked in the notes to the half-year report.

05

Industry analysis

The cycle started with a memory shortage driven by server demand. That shortage has spread to commodity DRAM and NAND, lifting prices across both, and capacity expansion is seen as the fundamental fix.

Meritz Securities expects equipment move-ins to keep rising through 2028 as Samsung Electronics and SK hynix open clean rooms in sequence. Kiwoom Securities laid out a customer investment sequence of DRAM in 2026 followed by NAND and eSSD in 2027.

Shinhan Securities said a larger share of long-term supply agreements could make memory conditions less volatile than in past cycles.

In contrast, BNK Investment & Securities said in August 2026 that equipment makers' earnings improve with a lag while their shares move with customer demand, which it judged had already peaked.

Competitively, Wonik IPS is a domestic deposition specialist that counts Samsung Electronics' memory and foundry arms and SK hynix as customers. SK Securities said its value-chain weight is large because order value per unit of DRAM investment is high.

Still, revenue remains heavily tied to the capital spending of two domestic customers, so results swing sharply with the pace of their investment.

06

Outlook

Brokers expect 2026 to be weak in the first half and strong in the second. Revenue for overseas customers that slipped out of Q2 should start to be booked in Q3, at the same time as equipment move-ins for the two domestic memory makers' expansions.

NH Investment & Securities expects the second half to account for about 70% of full-year revenue and sees roughly KRW 1.2 trillion of annual revenue as achievable even after lowering display expectations.

Meritz Securities said shipments are concentrated in the second half: V9 NAND conversion (partly in Q3 2026, mostly in Q4), the Taylor foundry fab (H2 2026) and Greater China display (mostly Q4 2026).

According to an October 2026 report, Shinhan Securities found that some Taylor-related revenue shifted to Q4 but that the annual planned volume remains on track. Over the medium term, an added growth variable is whether CVD tools for a new overseas customer move from evaluation in 2026 to volume supply in 2027.

Neither a periodic report nor preliminary results for Q3 2026 have been released yet. The second-half outlook therefore rests on broker estimates rather than reported results.

08

Bull factors

Expansion schedule running to 2028

After Samsung Electronics' Pyeongtaek P4 and SK hynix's M15X, new fab clean rooms are due to open one after another. Meritz Securities expects equipment move-ins to rise through 2028.

Shinhan Securities said in August 2026 that pulled-forward memory expansion timelines give high earnings visibility through at least 2028. Given the lag between orders and revenue, the backlog built in the first half underpins second-half revenue.

Broader products and customers

Its tool lineup covers DRAM and NAND conversions as well as new foundry investment. New products such as ALD and HBM equipment are entering customer lines, and foundry tools for Samsung Electronics' Taylor fab are scheduled to ship in the second half of 2026.

If a new overseas customer's CVD evaluation leads to volume supply, reliance on the two domestic customers could ease.

Solid cash flow and balance sheet

Operating cash flow improved to KRW 156.6 billion in 2025 from -KRW 12.3 billion in 2023. The debt ratio fell from 29.0% in 2022 to 20.1% in 2025. That leaves room to fund the heavier working capital that equipment builds require during an upturn without outside borrowing.

09

Bear factors

Delayed recognition and year-on-year decline

Q2 2026 revenue and operating profit missed market expectations and fell year on year. Some Taylor-related revenue has reportedly slipped again into Q4. If shipments keep sliding, the weak-first-half, strong-second-half thesis itself could be pushed into 2027.

Gap between net and operating profit

Net profit attributable to owners in the first half of 2026 totaled about KRW 112 billion, nearly four times the roughly KRW 29.1 billion of operating profit in the same period. When non-operating gains are this large, multiples based on net profit can flatter the core business's earning power. Operating margin has also fallen from the 15% range in Q2 2025 to 6-8% in Q1-Q2 2026.

Debate over the cycle peak

BNK Investment & Securities judged in August 2026 that customer demand had passed its peak. It cut its 2026 revenue and operating profit estimates by 5% and 12%. Because equipment earnings lag customer investment, a turn in memory prices or demand could trigger broad downward revisions at once.

10

Risk factors

Customer concentration

Samsung Electronics is both the largest customer and a major shareholder with about 7.5%. Changes in its fab-by-fab investment schedule or process choices can swing quarterly results sharply. The 31.8% revenue decline and swing to an operating loss in 2023 also stemmed from customers cutting equipment orders.

Earnings volatility

Equipment is booked as revenue only after the order, build, delivery and acceptance, so results vary widely from quarter to quarter. Revenue falling 40.0% from KRW 275 billion in Q4 2025 to KRW 164.9 billion in Q1 2026 is one example. One-off items such as bonuses and provision reversals also move quarterly margins considerably.

External and business environment

US tariff policy and export controls on equipment bound for China are variables for overseas growth plans. Expectations for the display segment were cut sharply in the first half of 2026, and with Greater China shipments concentrated in Q4 it is exposed to delays. If the new overseas customer's evaluation is delayed or dropped, the diversification story weakens.

What to watch next

  1. Mid-November 2026

    In the Q3 preliminary results and the Q3 report, check whether overseas-customer and Taylor-related revenue deferred from Q2 was actually recognized, and whether operating margin returned to the 15% range of Q2 2025.

  2. Mid-November 2026

    Use the order disclosures and income-statement notes in the Q3 report to track the backlog, and check whether the Q2 non-operating gain recurs, as a gauge of earnings quality.

  3. Late January 2027

    Check 2027 capital spending guidance in Samsung Electronics' and SK hynix's Q4 earnings releases. Whether NAND and eSSD investment expands will set the size of 2027 orders.

  4. Around late February 2027

    Check whether Q4 and full-year 2026 preliminary results reach the roughly KRW 1.2 trillion annual revenue level NH Investment & Securities outlined. The result will show whether Q4-concentrated shipments went to plan.

  5. During 2027

    Watch whether CVD tools for the new overseas customer move from evaluation to volume supply. Success would be the first sign of change in a revenue base concentrated on two domestic customers.

12

Overall view

Wonik IPS moved past its 2023 loss to recover to KRW 909.8 billion in revenue and KRW 73.8 billion in operating profit in 2025. Memory expansion also lifted its backlog sharply in Q1 2026. Yet first-half 2026 operating profit stayed near the prior-year level, and most of Q2 net profit came from outside operations.

The bullish case rests on customer clean-room schedules running to 2028, broader products and customers, and strong cash flow. The bearish case points to the risk of repeated shipment delays, the gap between net and operating profit, and the debate over a cycle peak.

The Q3 results in November 2026 will be the first evidence to separate the two views: whether deferred revenue is actually booked and margins recover. This report is for information only and contains no investment opinion or buy/sell recommendation.
