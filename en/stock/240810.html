Report overview

Wonik IPS: The Backlog Now Has to Become Revenue

Wonik IPS is at the stage where orders built up by memory capacity expansion should start turning into second-half revenue. However, its reported first-half 2026 results mix delayed revenue recognition with one-off non-operating gains, so third-quarter results will be the first real test of that transition.

  1. 1

    Revenue rose 21.6% year on year to KRW 909.8 billion in 2025, and operating profit grew 593.6% to KRW 73.8 billion. This extended into a second year the recovery that began after the KRW 18.1 billion operating loss of 2023.

  2. 2

    Second-quarter 2026 revenue was KRW 216.5 billion and operating profit KRW 18.4 billion, down 10.6% and 49.6% year on year. SK Securities said revenue from overseas customers slipped into the next quarter because lead times differ by region.

  3. 3

    Net profit attributable to owners rose 225.2% year on year to KRW 89.9 billion in the same quarter. SK Securities attributed this largely to roughly KRW 80 billion of valuation gains on investment assets booked below the operating line.

  4. 4

    According to SK Securities, new orders reached KRW 627.5 billion in the first quarter of 2026, lifting the order backlog to KRW 400.4 billion at quarter-end.

  5. 5

    On October 15, 2026, doubts about the returns on AI investment and worries about CXMT catching up hit the market together, and the KOSPI fell 7.57%. Chip equipment stocks, Wonik IPS included, dropped by double-digit percentages that day.

02

Business structure

Wonik IPS was created in 2016 through a spin-off of the semiconductor, display and solar-cell equipment businesses of Wonik Holdings. Its main products are semiconductor deposition and thermal-processing tools, and its CVD, ALD and metal deposition systems are used in core DRAM, NAND and logic process steps.

Its flagship tool is the Gemini PECVD platform, which led the revenue recovery when its domestic customer resumed investment in 2025. According to Meritz Securities, semiconductors generated KRW 145.9 billion, or 88%, of first-quarter 2026 revenue, and display equipment generated KRW 19.0 billion, or 12%.

Meritz estimated that parts made up about 21% of semiconductor revenue in that quarter; parts revenue recurs more steadily than tool sales, which helps keep earnings stable.

Key customers are Samsung Electronics' memory and non-memory divisions and SK hynix's memory business, and SK Securities expected revenue tied to Samsung Electronics' Taylor fab in the US to start in the third quarter of 2026.

Overseas revenue comes from customers in the US and China, and SK Securities calculated that the overseas share fell from 34.7% to 25.4% in the second quarter of 2026.

In deposition, the company competes with large global equipment makers and, at home, with deposition specialists such as Jusung Engineering, Eugene Technology and Tesco.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual revenue fell 31.8% from KRW 1.01 trillion in 2022 to a low of KRW 690.3 billion in 2023. It then rose for two straight years to KRW 748.2 billion in 2024 and KRW 909.8 billion in 2025, although 2022 remains the largest year in the 2022-2025 period.

The company swung to an operating loss of KRW 18.1 billion in 2023, returned to an operating profit of KRW 10.6 billion in 2024 and earned KRW 73.8 billion in 2025. Operating margin went from 9.6% to -2.6%, then recovered to 1.4% and 8.1%, which clearly shows how higher revenue eased the burden of fixed costs.

Net profit attributable to owners rose 305.0% to KRW 84 billion in 2025, and operating cash flow doubled from KRW 78.3 billion in 2024 to KRW 156.6 billion.

Quarterly operating profit fell from KRW 36.5 billion in the second quarter of 2025 to KRW 27.5 billion, KRW 17.2 billion and KRW 10.7 billion in the following three quarters, then rose again to KRW 18.4 billion in the second quarter of 2026.

First-quarter 2026 revenue of KRW 164.9 billion was down 40.0% from the previous quarter. SK Securities put this down to tools being delivered early in the prior quarter, and Meritz Securities noted that a reversal of inventory provisions lifted margins that quarter.

Second-quarter 2026 net profit attributable to owners of KRW 89.9 billion depended more on non-operating items than on revenue of KRW 216.5 billion.

The preliminary release on August 6, 2026 showed pre-tax profit of KRW 101.2 billion, and SK Securities estimated that roughly KRW 80 billion of it came from valuation gains on investment assets.

As a result, the KRW 173.1 billion of net profit attributable to owners over the last four quarters includes this one-off gain and looks larger than recurring operating earnings.

05

Industry analysis

In end markets, rising demand for memory in AI servers is driving continued capacity expansion at Korean memory makers. SK Securities said that the pulled-forward build-out schedules at Samsung Electronics' Pyeongtaek P4 and SK hynix's M15X led to Wonik IPS's order growth.

In a May 2026 report, SK Securities projected that SK hynix's DRAM investment in 2027 could reach up to twice the 2026 level. In an October 2026 report, Meritz Securities expected tool move-ins to continue through 2028 as both customers bring new cleanrooms online one after another.

In mid-October 2026, however, doubts about how long the cycle can last grew. CXMT unveiled plans for next-generation DRAM, warnings emerged that the memory market could slide into oversupply by 2029, and skepticism grew over whether AI infrastructure spending will pay off.

The KOSPI closed down 7.57% on October 15, 2026, and among materials, parts and equipment stocks, Wonik IPS and Jusung Engineering fell more than 13% and 12%, respectively.

Competitively, Wonik IPS supplies tools to Samsung Electronics' memory and foundry investments as well as SK hynix's memory investments, giving it a relatively broad customer base among Korean equipment makers.

06

Outlook

No numeric company guidance was found, so the outlook rests on broker estimates and customers' investment schedules. In an October 2026 report, Meritz Securities estimated Wonik IPS's third-quarter revenue at KRW 342.5 billion and operating profit at KRW 58.3 billion, up 28% and 112% year on year, respectively.

Meritz projected a 48% compound annual growth rate in operating profit for 2026-2028.

On shipment timing, Meritz said in a May 2026 report that V9 NAND tools would ship partly in the third quarter and mostly in the fourth, with Taylor foundry fab tools in the second half and Greater China display tools concentrated in the fourth quarter.

SK Securities expected Samsung Electronics to start placing purchase orders for its P5 fab in the second quarter of 2027. On products, Kiwoom Securities pointed in a March 2026 report to new tools, including ALD systems for DRAM and NAND and HBM equipment, as a driver of structural improvement.

On the other hand, NH Investment & Securities expected in a June 2026 report that display revenue this year would fall short of expectations. Second-half results therefore depend on whether the backlog converts to revenue as planned and how far the lower-margin display business recovers.

08

Bull factors

Backlog supports second-half revenue

SK Securities put the backlog at KRW 400.4 billion at the end of the first quarter of 2026, up in a single quarter from around KRW 300 billion at the end of 2025. Because orders built up ahead of revenue, overseas shipments that slipped out of the second quarter could be booked from the third quarter onward.

If Meritz Securities' third-quarter operating profit estimate of KRW 58.3 billion is met, a single quarter would deliver most of the KRW 73.8 billion earned in all of 2025.

Broadening customers and processes

Beyond Samsung Electronics' memory lines, revenue sources now include the Taylor foundry fab, SK hynix's M15X and overseas customers in the US and China. According to Meritz Securities, the semiconductor share of revenue reached 88% in the first quarter of 2026, which, together with parts sales, lifted gross margin.

If new ALD and HBM tools gain traction, the range of tools it supplies would widen and its market share could rise.

Balance sheet built for downcycles

The debt ratio was 20.1% at the end of 2025, the lowest in the 2022-2025 period, with total liabilities of KRW 194.6 billion against total equity of KRW 970 billion.

Operating cash flow of KRW 156.6 billion in 2025 far exceeded net profit attributable to owners of KRW 84 billion, showing that profits are converting well into cash. Even if quarterly swings typical of the equipment sector widen, the company can fund working capital and capital spending without outside borrowing.

09

Bear factors

Revenue timing is unreliable

In the second quarter of 2026, regional lead-time differences pushed overseas revenue into the next quarter and results missed market forecasts. Tool revenue is booked only after customer move-in and acceptance, so even small schedule changes can swing quarterly results sharply.

With third-quarter estimates set high, any further slippage of deferred shipments could widen the gap between results and expectations.

Net profit includes one-offs

Second-quarter 2026 net profit attributable to owners of KRW 89.9 billion includes about KRW 80 billion of investment valuation gains, by SK Securities' estimate.

Operating profit of KRW 18.4 billion in the same quarter was down 49.6% year on year, so core earnings have not yet returned to their second-quarter 2025 level. First-quarter 2026 margins were also helped by a reversal of inventory provisions, so recurring earnings need to be looked at separately.

Fears of a memory cycle peak

In October 2026, CXMT unveiled next-generation DRAM plans, and warnings emerged that the memory market could fall into oversupply by 2029. Skepticism about the returns on AI infrastructure spending, combined with concerns about rising US rates, raised the possibility that customers could slow their capacity additions.

Because equipment revenue is tied directly to customers' capital spending, even small changes to expansion plans hit orders immediately.

10

Risk factors

Customer concentration

Most revenue comes from investment by Samsung Electronics and SK hynix. The company has already seen, in 2023, that when these two customers cut spending, revenue can fall 31.8% and the company can slip into an operating loss. Schedule changes at a single fab flow directly through to company-wide results.

China and trade policy

SK Securities cited growing supply to China's CXMT as a growth driver. However, changes to US export controls on chip equipment to China could directly affect this revenue. CXMT is also emerging as a competitor to the company's Korean customers, which weighs on their investment over the long term.

Display segment and margin mix

Meritz Securities assessed that the display segment is less profitable than semiconductors. NH Investment & Securities expected 2026 display revenue to come in below expectations. If Greater China display shipments cluster in the fourth quarter, revenue could rise while the company-wide margin falls.

What to watch next

  1. Mid-November 2026

    In third-quarter preliminary results and the quarterly report, check whether overseas revenue deferred from the second quarter was actually booked. The gap against Meritz Securities' estimates (revenue KRW 342.5 billion, operating profit KRW 58.3 billion) is the key measure.

  2. Mid-November 2026

    In the order status section of the third-quarter report, check whether the backlog rose or fell from KRW 400.4 billion at the end of the first quarter. This shows both how fast orders are converting to revenue and how much new business is coming in.

  3. Late January 2027

    In Samsung Electronics' and SK hynix's fourth-quarter results, check their 2027 capital spending plans. The key question is whether expansion speed changes after the oversupply worries that surfaced in October 2026.

  4. Late February 2027

    In fourth-quarter and full-year 2026 preliminary results, check whether V9 NAND and Greater China display shipments were concentrated in the fourth quarter, and what drove the margin.

  5. Second quarter of 2027

    Check whether the Samsung Electronics P5 tool orders projected by SK Securities actually begin. This will be the next basis for gauging order flow beyond 2027.

12

Overall view

Wonik IPS's reported results show the effect of resumed memory investment, with a recovery from a 2023 loss to KRW 73.8 billion of operating profit in 2025. In the first half of 2026, operating profit was lower than a year earlier, and second-quarter net profit relied heavily on investment valuation gains.

The bull case points to the larger backlog, a broader customer base and a low-leverage balance sheet. The bear case points to delayed revenue recognition, one-offs in earnings, and the memory oversupply and China catch-up worries that surfaced in October 2026.

The share price trades at high multiples relative to both the industry average and net assets, so it can react sharply to whether results meet expectations. Third-quarter results and the backlog figure due in mid-November 2026 will be the first evidence of which view holds.
