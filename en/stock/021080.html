02

Business structure

Atinum Investment is a venture capital firm founded in 1988 that invests in small and mid-sized growth companies and earns fees for managing the partnerships that hold those stakes. Investment partnership income accounts for most of revenue, at 96.04% according to an Alphasquare breakdown.

Its earnings come from management fees tied to assets under management, performance fees paid when partnerships distribute gains at liquidation, and equity-method gains that reflect changes in the value of portfolio stakes held by those partnerships.

Management fees for 2025 fell 13.9% year on year to KRW 21.5 billion, while the company said growth in its results was driven by larger fund exits. Equity-method partnership gains rose 1,728% to KRW 19.2 billion, but these are valuation-based figures rather than realized cash.

Its flagship 2023 growth partnership, sized at KRW 860 billion and formed in September 2023, had deployed about 44.3% of commitments as of a November 2025 report.

The firm runs a one-fund model in which all investment professionals work on a single large fund, and it ranked fourth among venture capital managers by assets at year-end 2025.

A November 2026 report put its assets under management at KRW 1.86 trillion, while another tally put year-end 2025 AUM at about KRW 2.063 trillion, so figures differ by source. Exit proceeds in 2025 reached KRW 311.9 billion, up 67.4% year on year.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual revenue rose from KRW 101 billion in 2022 to KRW 135.8 billion in 2023 (up 34.5% year on year), fell to KRW 51.7 billion in 2024 (down 61.9%), and recovered to KRW 70.6 billion in 2025 (up 36.4%). Across 2022–2025, revenue was largest in 2023 and smallest in 2024.

Operating profit fell from KRW 35.2 billion in 2022 to KRW 24.8 billion in 2023 (down 29.5%) and KRW 13.5 billion in 2024 (down 45.5%), then turned up to KRW 32 billion in 2025 (up 136.8%). Operating profit was largest in 2022 and smallest in 2024, and operating margin moved through 34.8%, 18.2%, 26.1% and 45.3%.

The 2023 revenue spike came from performance fees on the liquidation of a high-growth corporate partnership, while the 2024 decline reflected performance fees falling from KRW 113.2 billion to KRW 22 billion.

In 2025 exits from major portfolio companies continued, with exit proceeds reaching KRW 311.9 billion, up 67.4%, which underpinned the recovery.

Controlling-shareholder net income was KRW 28.2 billion in 2022, KRW 20.1 billion in 2023 (down 28.8%), KRW 10.8 billion in 2024 (down 46.0%) and KRW 25.1 billion in 2025 (up 131.4%). Net income including non-controlling interests was disclosed at the same yearly amounts.

In Q3 2025 revenue was KRW 24.2 billion, operating profit KRW 18.2 billion and controlling-shareholder net income KRW 14.7 billion.

Q4 2025 revenue fell to KRW 15.9 billion (down 34.4% quarter on quarter), operating profit to KRW 570 million (down 96.9%), and the company swung to a controlling-shareholder net loss of KRW 200 million.

Q1 2026 returned to profit, with revenue of KRW 8.44 billion, operating profit of KRW 4.34 billion and controlling-shareholder net income of KRW 3.35 billion; press reports put that quarter's performance fees at KRW 1.9 billion and equity-method gains at KRW 2.9 billion.

Q2 2026 revenue was KRW 2.22 billion (down 73.7% quarter on quarter), with an operating loss of KRW 5.07 billion and a controlling-shareholder net loss of KRW 3.42 billion.

Q3 2026 revenue was KRW 3.81 billion (up 72.0% quarter on quarter, down 84.2% year on year), with an operating loss of KRW 616 million and a controlling-shareholder net loss of KRW 380 million, a narrower loss.

Quarterly disclosures alone do not show how much of the revenue came from performance fees versus management fees. Controlling-shareholder net losses over the last four quarters (Q4 2025 to Q3 2026) totaled KRW 643 million.

05

Industry analysis

Venture capital earnings depend on the state of the exit market, since performance fees vary with how and when portfolio companies are listed or sold. Atinum Investment ranked fourth among venture capital managers by assets under management at year-end 2025, according to press reports.

It was selected as a large-category management company under the government-backed National Growth Fund's indirect investment scheme, with the state policy lender acting as anchor investor; the target commitment for that category is KRW 500 billion, and the final size can reach up to 200% of target.

A November 2026 Hankyung report noted criticism that startup valuations in the Korean venture market are becoming inflated. Industry observers said the firm sustained its ability to run large funds and deliver exits even as rate hikes weakened VC investor sentiment.

The company said it has invested in physical AI startup Holiday Robotics and smart factory automation firm Dyim Research. As of the first three quarters of 2023, Atinum was the only listed venture capital firm with operating revenue above KRW 50 billion.

Alphasquare data for the 17-company venture capital industry show Atinum's 2025 operating margin above the industry average.

In cycle terms, the sequence of a performance-fee peak in 2023, a decline in 2024, a rebound in exits in 2025 and quarterly losses in 2026 places the firm in the later part of an exit cycle, with its precise position depending on fund liquidation timing.

06

Outlook

The company's target is to complete formation of its next mega fund within 2026. Hankyung reported that the industry expects it to exceed the size of the previous KRW 860 billion fund.

The company has said that a larger fund is not necessarily better and that it has not fixed a final size, pushing back on talk of a KRW 1 trillion vehicle.

The KRW 860 billion 2023 partnership had deployed about 44.3% of commitments as of a November 2025 report, and at that time industry forecasts saw deployment passing 50% if the pace held.

A company official said the firm will focus on supporting existing portfolio companies at each growth stage rather than sourcing many new targets. The 2018 growth partnership matures on December 27, 2026, and the New Paradigm partnership matures on February 18, 2027.

A company official said the major exits from the high-growth corporate partnership are complete, so further performance fees from that vehicle are unlikely. Securing a policy-backed anchor through the National Growth Fund makes the formation of new partnerships a key variable for future performance-fee flows.

The next periodic report is the 2026 annual report, which will include Q4 2026 results and full-year operating profit and controlling-shareholder net income.

08

Bull factors

Large-fund capacity and a policy-backed anchor

Atinum runs a KRW 860 billion partnership and was reported as a final selection for the large category of the National Growth Fund's indirect investment scheme, managed by the state policy lender. The target commitment for that category is KRW 500 billion, and the final size can reach up to 200% of target.

Some in the industry see the next fund reaching KRW 1 trillion, but the company has said it has not fixed a size.

Exit track record and high-return portfolio

Exit proceeds in 2025 reached KRW 311.9 billion, up 67.4% year on year. The liquidated high-growth corporate partnership distributed 4.9 times invested capital to its investors, with a reported gross IRR of 32.9%. A portfolio company, JI Biomedical, was cited as returning about ten times the original investment.

High operating margin and low leverage

The operating margin of 45.3% in 2025 was the highest of 2022–2025. The debt ratio at year-end 2025 was 10.1%, with total liabilities of KRW 20.7 billion. Q1 2026 returned to profit, and press reports put that quarter's management fees at KRW 3.7 billion plus performance fees of KRW 1.9 billion.

09

Bear factors

Dependence on performance fees and quarterly losses

Revenue in 2024 fell 61.9% and operating profit fell 45.5%, with the drop attributed mainly to lower performance fees. Operating losses followed in Q2 and Q3 2026, and controlling-shareholder net losses over the last four quarters totaled KRW 643 million. Q3 2026 revenue of KRW 3.81 billion compares with KRW 24.2 billion in Q3 2025.

Falling management fees and exhausted growth-fund performance fees

Management fees for 2025 fell 13.9% year on year to KRW 21.5 billion. A company official said the major exits from the high-growth corporate partnership are complete, making additional performance fees from it unlikely. The 84.2% year-on-year drop in Q3 2026 revenue appears connected to this shift.

Startup valuation froth and weaker VC sentiment

A November 2026 Hankyung report noted criticism that startup valuations in the Korean venture market are inflating this year. Investment bankers have also described VC investor sentiment as weakened by rate hikes.

Because lower portfolio valuations reduce exit proceeds and performance fees together, this is a bearish factor.

10

Risk factors

Timing of performance fees

Performance fees depend on the timing of partnership liquidations and profit distributions, which the company cannot fully control. Operating losses continued in Q2 and Q3 2026, and quarterly disclosures do not separate out the performance-fee amount.

Distribution results from the partnership maturing in December 2026 could shift the direction of upcoming earnings.

Size and timing of the next fund

The next mega fund's formation is targeted within 2026, but its final size has not been set. A gap exists between the industry's expectation of exceeding the prior fund and the company's emphasis on investment quality over size. Final formation under the National Growth Fund's large category also remains pending.

Portfolio valuation and market backdrop

Equity-method gains reflect the current value of held stakes, so they can swing widely before realization. If criticism of inflated startup valuations persists, both valuation marks and exit timing could be affected.

The company has said it will focus on follow-on investment in existing portfolio companies rather than new targets.

12

Overall view

Atinum Investment earns from management fees and performance fees on the investment partnerships it runs, so its results depend heavily on the timing of fund exits. Annual results across 2022–2025 moved sharply with performance fees.

Revenue peaked at KRW 135.8 billion in 2023, fell to KRW 51.7 billion in 2024, and recovered in 2025 to operating profit of KRW 32 billion and controlling-shareholder net income of KRW 25.1 billion.

Bullish factors include management of a KRW 860 billion partnership, selection for the National Growth Fund's large category, KRW 311.9 billion in 2025 exit proceeds, a 45.3% operating margin in 2025, and a 10.1% debt ratio.

Bearish factors include operating losses in Q2 and Q3 2026, controlling-shareholder net losses over the last four quarters totaling KRW 643 million, and reduced prospects for further performance fees after the major exits from the high-growth partnership.

Reports of inflated startup valuations and weaker VC sentiment are variables for the exit environment. On valuation, PER cannot be calculated and the price-to-book ratio sits well below 1x, a discount to net assets whose meaning depends on whether the profit recovery is sustained.

The next checkpoints are the distribution outcome of the partnership maturing in December 2026, the formation and size of the next mega fund, and full-year results in the 2026 annual report.
