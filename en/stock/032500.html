02

Business structure

KMW is a telecom equipment maker with two businesses: RF (radio frequency) products and LED lighting. According to WiseReport's company monitor, the RF business accounted for about 72% of sales in the first half of 2026, and its main customers include Samsung and global telecom equipment makers such as ZTE.

The remaining sales come from LED lighting, which the same source said weakened as the lighting market contracted. Hana Securities described KMW as a virtual front-runner in global base station filter and antenna markets (report dated March 17, 2026).

The business is project-driven and tied closely to carriers' capital spending cycles, so the timing of orders from a single customer can move quarterly sales.

Hana Securities viewed KMW as a vendor to Samsung, which holds a market share above 30%, and expected Samsung-supplied 5G equipment for Verizon to be likely in 2027 (report dated May 19, 2026). The same firm also flagged the possibility of supplying Ericsson.

On competition, Hana Securities argued that weaker positions of rivals could widen KMW's market share in antenna, filter and system products across the US, Europe and Korea (report dated February 3, 2026).

These are brokerage views, and the disclosed quarterly sales in Q1 to Q3 2026 stayed in a range of KRW 20.7 billion to KRW 24.3 billion.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual results show losses in every year from 2022 to 2025, with sales moving in both directions. Sales fell from KRW 179.5 billion in 2022 to KRW 100.1 billion in 2023, down 44.2% year on year, and reached the smallest level in 2024 at KRW 87.4 billion, down 12.7%.

Sales recovered to KRW 97.5 billion in 2025, up 11.6%, and 2022 was the largest year in the 2022 to 2025 window. Operating losses widened from KRW 45 billion in 2022 to KRW 62.9 billion in 2023 and then narrowed to KRW 45.5 billion in 2024 and KRW 23.8 billion in 2025.

Operating margins were -25.1%, -62.8%, -52.1% and -24.4% for 2022 through 2025. The year with the sharpest sales decline, 2023, also had the widest loss, which suggests that fixed-cost pressure from shrinking revenue was the main driver of weaker profitability.

Net losses attributable to controlling shareholders were KRW 26.7 billion, KRW 65.3 billion, KRW 38.6 billion and KRW 27 billion for 2022 through 2025, and total net loss was disclosed at the same levels.

The debt-to-equity ratio rose from 43.9% in 2022 to 88.0% in 2025, with total equity of KRW 131.4 billion, all attributable to controlling shareholders, and total liabilities of KRW 115.6 billion at year-end 2025.

Operating cash flow was negative in all four years, at -KRW 6.86 billion in 2022, -KRW 33.4 billion in 2023, -KRW 27 billion in 2024 and -KRW 17 billion in 2025.

Quarterly, sales in Q3 2025 were KRW 20.5 billion with an operating loss of KRW 7.02 billion, and Q4 2025 sales rose to KRW 25.6 billion, up 24.9% from the prior quarter, with an operating loss of KRW 5.1 billion.

In Q1 2026 sales were KRW 21.5 billion, down 16.1% quarter on quarter, and the operating loss widened to KRW 5.73 billion. In Q2 2026 sales recovered to KRW 24.3 billion, up 13.2%, but the operating loss widened to KRW 6.26 billion.

Q3 2026 sales were KRW 20.7 billion, down 14.8% quarter on quarter and up 1.1% year on year, and the operating loss of KRW 5.17 billion was smaller than both the prior quarter and the same quarter a year earlier.

The net loss attributable to controlling shareholders was largest in Q2 2026 at KRW 14.9 billion and narrowed to KRW 6.81 billion in Q3 2026, though it was wider than a year earlier.

The sum of net losses attributable to controlling shareholders over the last four quarters, from Q4 2025 to Q3 2026, was KRW 27.6 billion.

Industry analysis

The telecom equipment cycle depends heavily on whether US carriers resume large-scale investment. Hana Securities cited AT&T's plan to raise average annual capital spending in 2026 to 2030 to about 2.5 times the prior-year level as evidence of expanding US network investment (report covered on March 17, 2026).

The same firm expected network build-out after the US spectrum auction scheduled to start in June to lift equipment demand.

On the other side, WiseReport's company monitor identified carriers' capital spending adjustments and network investment efficiency measures as factors that weighed on RF segment results in the first half of 2026.

Tighter US and EU restrictions on Chinese telecom equipment and components are another variable Hana Securities highlighted (report dated February 3, 2026). On where the cycle stands, Hana Securities pointed to a potential sharp rise in 2027 earnings, while also noting that share prices tend to move ahead of earnings.

On competition, the choice of vendors by Samsung Electronics and Ericsson is seen as a key determinant of market share. Hana Securities said it raised its target multiple in May 2026, reflecting share price gains across domestic and overseas telecom equipment makers (report dated May 19, 2026).

Whether sector expectations are already priced in, and whether they convert into sales, is the central industry question.

Bull factors

Expanding US carrier capital spending

Hana Securities cited AT&T's plan to raise average annual capital spending in 2026 to 2030 to about 2.5 times the 2025 level as a basis for earnings improvement (report covered on March 17, 2026). The same firm judged that AT&T's expansion was likely to lead to higher capital spending at Verizon.

It also argued that network build-out after the US spectrum auction would lift equipment demand. Whether this flow reaches 2027 sales is the test for the bullish case.

Possible expansion of vendor positions

Whether Samsung-supplied equipment actually reaches Verizon in 2027 is the first checkpoint for an expanded vendor position (Hana Securities outlook, report dated May 19, 2026). The same report also raised the possibility of being selected as an Ericsson supplier.

Given its position in global filter and RU markets, the firm also cited the company's small market capitalization as a reason. If vendor selections materialize, the sales base could broaden.

Benefit from tighter rules on Chinese equipment

Hana Securities said tighter restrictions on Chinese telecom equipment and components in the US and Europe raise the likelihood of KMW being selected as an Ericsson vendor (report dated February 3, 2026).

It also argued that weaker rivals could widen KMW's share in antenna, filter and system products across the US, Europe and Korea. If Chinese suppliers continue to be displaced in existing supply chains, the opportunity would fall to non-Chinese suppliers. The timing at which these rules translate into actual orders is the item to check.

The operating loss narrowed in Q3 2026, but the net loss attributable to controlling shareholders was wider than a year earlier. A February 2026 report expected earnings recovery only from the second half onward, and a March 2026 report expected a Q4 return to profitability, yet losses continued through Q3.

Sales decline and capex adjustments

A contracting LED lighting market was also cited as a factor behind continued weakness. Until expectations for US investment translate into actual sales recovery, the shrinking top line remains a burden.

Risk that expectations are already priced in

Hana Securities analyst Kim Hong-sik said share prices typically move ahead of earnings, so expectations for next year's improvement are likely to be priced in first (report covered by Edaily on March 17, 2026). The same analyst added that substantial gains may already have occurred once profitability is confirmed.

The firm also noted that a recovery in results would take time in the near term. If expectations are priced in before earnings confirm them, share price volatility could rise.

Risk factors

Financial structure

The debt-to-equity ratio rose from 43.9% in 2022 to 88.0% in 2025, and total liabilities at year-end 2025 were KRW 115.6 billion. Operating cash flow was negative in every year from 2022 to 2025, meaning operations did not generate funds in that period.

Total equity at year-end 2025 was KRW 131.4 billion, and continued losses could reduce that capacity over time. External financing plans would also affect shareholder value and should be checked against disclosures.

Customer and policy concentration

WiseReport's company monitor describes the RF business as relying on a few global equipment makers, including Samsung and ZTE. If US and EU restrictions on Chinese equipment eased or changed direction, the premise of vendor selection could weaken.

Delays in the US spectrum auction or carrier investment schedules would push back the timing of brokerage forecasts. Earnings are tied to specific customers and regional policy, which is the structural risk.

Reliance on brokerage estimates

Projections of a sharp rise in 2027 earnings rest on brokerage estimates, while disclosed results show quarterly losses from Q3 2025 through Q3 2026. Hana Securities lowered its 2026 estimate slightly but kept its 2027 and 2028 estimates unchanged.

If these forecasts miss, the higher target multiple used in the target price could also be revised. Until results confirm the estimates, changes in forecasts themselves remain a source of uncertainty.
