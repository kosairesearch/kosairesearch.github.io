02

Business structure

Ecopro Materials was founded in 2017 and listed on KOSPI in 2023, supplying high-nickel ternary cathode precursors for lithium-ion batteries. Its business is split into a raw-material refining process (RMP) and a precursor process (CPM).

With the acquisition of Indonesian nickel smelter PT Green Eco Nickel, the company says it has completed its nickel supply chain and internalized sulfate-based metal refining to strengthen cost competitiveness.

The smelter was reported on a separate-company basis through 2025 and has been consolidated since the first quarter of 2026, so year-on-year comparisons are not on the same perimeter. Revenue rests on two pillars: precursor sales and intermediate nickel products such as mixed hydroxide precipitate (MHP).

Precursor volumes depend on how key customers schedule production-line changes, and in the third quarter of 2026 a product transition at major customers reduced precursor shipments. Meanwhile, the Indonesian smelter's utilization has been recovering gradually, lifting MHP sales.

Winning North American customers is the main volume lever: NH Investment & Securities, in a December 2025 report, projected 2026 precursor sales of 31,000 tons, with new-customer volume reaching about half of the total. Ecopro and 20 related parties held 45.45% as of October 8, 2026. The company sits in the same cathode and precursor supply chain as POSCO Future M and L&F.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual revenue rose from KRW 665.2 billion in 2022 to KRW 952.5 billion in 2023 (up 43.2% year on year), then fell to KRW 299.8 billion in 2024 (down 68.5%). It recovered to KRW 392.5 billion in 2025 (up 30.9%); 2023 was the largest year and 2024 the smallest in the 2022-2025 window.

Operating profit fell from KRW 39 billion in 2022 to KRW 8.77 billion in 2023 (down 77.5%), and the company swung to an operating loss of KRW 64.7 billion in 2024. The operating loss widened to KRW 65.4 billion in 2025, making 2024 and 2025 two consecutive loss years.

Operating margin went from 5.9% in 2022 to 0.9% in 2023, -21.6% in 2024 and -16.7% in 2025. Net income attributable to controlling shareholders moved from KRW 5.05 billion in 2023 to a net loss of KRW 42.7 billion in 2024, then returned to a KRW 24.1 billion profit in 2025.

Net income turning positive in a year of operating losses suggests non-operating items and accounting effects played a role.

NH Investment & Securities estimated in December 2025 that a one-off gain of about KRW 18 billion from a change in depreciation useful lives would be reflected in the fourth quarter, and that excluding it, fourth-quarter operating profit would have been a loss.

Quarterly revenue went from KRW 63.2 billion in the third quarter of 2025 to KRW 115.2 billion in the fourth quarter (up 82.4% quarter on quarter), KRW 166.5 billion in the first quarter of 2026 (up 44.6%), KRW 178.8 billion in the second quarter (up 7.4%) and KRW 181.1 billion in the third quarter (up 1.3%), four consecutive quarters of increase.

Operating results moved from a KRW 25.1 billion loss in the third quarter of 2025 to a KRW 3.35 billion profit in the fourth quarter, then KRW 15.7 billion in the first quarter of 2026 (up 368.7% quarter on quarter), and the first-quarter improvement reflects the consolidation of Green Eco Nickel.

The second quarter returned to an operating loss of KRW 10.6 billion, and the third-quarter operating loss widened to KRW 29 billion, two consecutive loss quarters.

The third-quarter weakness was attributed to lower precursor volumes from a product transition at major customers, combined with inventory valuation losses from weaker metal prices and exchange rates. Net loss attributable to controlling shareholders over the latest four quarters totaled KRW 158 billion.

When the third-quarter figures were released on November 5, 2026, the company described them as preliminary and unaudited.

05

Industry analysis

Downstream demand is being shaped by weakness in the North American electric vehicle market. NH Investment & Securities, in a December 2025 report, expected weaker downstream demand after the end of US EV purchase subsidies, while still seeing room for shipment growth through new customers.

From a cycle perspective, the company had counted on demand from supply chains seeking to reduce China exposure, but analysts noted that weak competitiveness among end customers and policy changes have delayed earnings recovery.

On the 2025 third-quarter earnings call, the company said some downstream customers were seeking non-China materials to qualify for the US advanced manufacturing production credit (AMPC), and that some were converting EV lines to ESS production in response to rising energy storage demand.

On inputs, weaker mineral prices and exchange-rate swings enlarged inventory valuation losses and weighed on third-quarter 2026 profitability. On competitiveness, the company's inclusion of its Indonesian nickel smelter in consolidated results is presented as a cost differentiator.

Compared with cathode and precursor peers such as POSCO Future M and L&F, owning a nickel refining subsidiary is the structural feature that distinguishes this company.

The pace of demand recovery may vary from quarter to quarter, depending on North American customers' inventory adjustments and production-line schedules.

06

Outlook

The company says it will step up precursor shipments to new North American customers, secure a US black mass tolling customer, and expand MHP sales as the Green Eco Nickel smelter returns to normal utilization.

Global Economic reported on November 17, 2026 that fourth-quarter 2026 would bring new North American precursor shipments and plans to expand a US recycling business. Whether these plans show up in revenue will become visible in the preliminary fourth-quarter 2026 results.

On the 2025 third-quarter earnings call, the company said shipments to new customers had started within that quarter and expected utilization to improve from the second half of 2026.

On capacity, it plans to complete a 33,000-ton CPM3 precursor plant, but said expansion was postponed and tied to the timing of entry into several projects, so the completion date may shift with customer schedules.

On earnings, NH Investment & Securities forecast in December 2025 that the company could return to an annual profit in 2026.

Based on the November 5, 2026 release, cumulative operating losses for the first three quarters of 2026 were KRW 23.9 billion on a preliminary basis, a smaller loss than a year earlier but still short of the profit turnaround that was forecast. The next checkpoint is whether annual operating profit moves out of loss in the 2026 annual report.

08

Bull factors

Expanding North American customer shipments

NH Investment & Securities, in a December 2025 report, said new North American customer supply could raise utilization. The company has also outlined plans to step up new North American precursor shipments in the fourth quarter of 2026.

A growing share of new-customer volume would provide the basis for a sales recovery, which is the core of the bull case.

Raw-material integration and smelter recovery

Consolidating PT Green Eco Nickel and internalizing sulfate-based refining is presented as a cost-competitiveness argument. In the third quarter of 2026, smelter utilization recovered gradually and MHP sales rose. The first-quarter 2026 operating profit turnaround also reflected the consolidation of this subsidiary.

Easing financial burden

The debt-to-equity ratio moved from 102.0% in 2022 and 38.2% in 2023, to 74.9% in 2024 and 51.1% in 2025, a lower level overall. Total liabilities were KRW 584.4 billion at the end of 2025, against total equity of KRW 1.14 trillion. Operating cash flow turned from negative KRW 134.6 billion in 2024 to positive KRW 94.4 billion in 2025.

Bear factors

Widening operating and net losses

The operating loss widened in the third quarter of 2026 from a year earlier. Net loss attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 68 billion, a swing from profit a year earlier. Over the latest four quarters, that net loss totaled KRW 158 billion, so the losses have not been confined to one or two quarters.

Customer transitions and inventory valuation losses

The third-quarter weakness was attributed to two overlapping factors: lower precursor sales from a product transition at major customers, and larger inventory valuation losses from weaker metal prices and exchange rates. Falling metal prices lower inventory values and weigh directly on profit. If volume recovery is delayed, this pressure could recur in quarterly results.

North American demand and policy variables

NH Investment & Securities expected weaker downstream demand after the end of US EV purchase subsidies. Another assessment noted that weak competitiveness among end customers and policy changes have delayed the earnings recovery. Softness in North American EV demand is a variable that can affect shipment volumes directly.

Risk factors

Customer schedules and product transitions

Precursor sales depend on the production plans and inventory adjustments of major customers. In the third quarter of 2026, product transitions at those customers again reduced volumes. As new customers gain weight, schedule changes at any single customer could move quarterly results.

Metal prices and exchange rates

Weaker global mineral prices and currency swings enlarged inventory valuation losses, according to the explanation for the third quarter. Without a rebound in prices, valuation pressure will keep adding volatility to quarterly profit. Raw-material price trends also affect results from the refining business.

Accounting treatment and audit outcomes

The third-quarter figures were released as preliminary and unaudited, and the company said confirmed numbers could change after audit. Results were reported on a separate basis through 2025 and on a consolidated basis from 2026, so year-on-year comparisons cover different perimeters.

Analysts earlier said the timing of consolidating the nickel refining results was uncertain because of audit delays.

Overall view

Ecopro Materials' revenue base has grown substantially in 2026. Third-quarter 2026 revenue was KRW 181.1 billion, up 186.8% year on year. Over the same period, the operating loss widened to KRW 29 billion, and the net loss attributable to controlling shareholders over the latest four quarters totaled KRW 158 billion.

Bullish factors include planned North American new-customer shipments, the consolidation and recovering utilization of the Indonesian nickel smelter, and a debt-to-equity ratio that fell from 102.0% in 2022 to 51.1% in 2025.

Bearish factors include lower volumes from customer product transitions, inventory valuation losses tied to metal prices and exchange rates, and weak North American EV demand. The two sides deserve equal weight.

For the bullish factors to show up in reported results, the first items to confirm are the preliminary fourth-quarter 2026 results and whether annual operating profit moves out of loss in the 2026 annual report.
