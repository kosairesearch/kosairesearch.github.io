LIG Accuver: Back in the Black, Proof Still Pending

LIG Accuver swung to an operating loss in 2025 and returned to operating profit in the second and third quarters of 2026, but the 2025 operating cash outflow and an audit key matter on product sales authenticity remain open items to watch.

02

Business structure

LIG Accuver was founded in 2000 and listed on KOSDAQ in 2005, starting with telecom test and measurement equipment and small cells. The business is now split into a telecom segment and an automotive segment.

The telecom segment covers wireless network optimization, big data, telecom test and measurement, small cells and defense products, while the automotive segment handles automotive semiconductor distribution and V2X (vehicle-to-everything) solutions.

Automotive segment sales reached KRW 88.7 billion in 2025, or 47.7% of total revenue, according to a Newsworker analysis. Media reports described the shift from telecom test equipment toward automotive semiconductor distribution as rapid.

Automotive distribution margins were reported to remain in the 4% to 5% range in the first half of 2026 (E-Today). Automotive semiconductor distribution sits with a wholly owned subsidiary, and V2X with a 57.5%-owned unit.

Defense revenue from electronic warfare equipment supplied to defense system makers began in earnest in 2025. Major customers reportedly include Hyundai Mobis, KT, LIG Nex1 and LG Uplus.

In March 2026, the company changed its name to LIG Accuver and said it would expand into AI, satellite communications, defense, V2X and mobility.

04

Earnings analysis

On a disclosed annual basis, revenue rose from KRW 132.9 billion in 2022 to KRW 137.4 billion in 2023 (up 3.4% year on year) and KRW 189.7 billion in 2024 (up 38.1%), then fell to KRW 186.1 billion in 2025 (down 1.9%). Among 2022 to 2025, 2024 was the largest year and 2022 the smallest.

Profitability moved in the opposite direction. Operating profit declined from KRW 14.3 billion in 2022 to KRW 10.3 billion in 2023 (down 28.3%) and KRW 2.44 billion in 2024 (down 76.3%), then turned to an operating loss of KRW 1.12 billion in 2025.

The operating margin fell from 10.8% in 2022 to 7.5% in 2023, 1.3% in 2024 and -0.6% in 2025. Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders followed the same path, from KRW 13.5 billion in 2022 to KRW 2.53 billion in 2024 (down 76.6%), and then a net loss of KRW 1.2 billion in 2025.

Net profit including non-controlling interests was KRW 2.91 billion in 2024 and a net loss of KRW 1.2 billion in 2025. The cash flow swing was sharper. Operating cash flow went from an inflow of KRW 18 billion in 2024 to an outflow of KRW 8.5 billion in 2025.

Media reports noted that receivables rose from KRW 357.4 million-scale levels in 2024 to KRW 458.2 million-scale levels in 2025, and that inventory accumulation outpaced sales growth. Quarterly results bottomed in the third quarter of 2025, with revenue of KRW 41.6 billion and an operating loss of KRW 2.52 billion.

The fourth quarter of 2025 posted revenue of KRW 61.7 billion (up 48.3% from the prior quarter) and operating profit of KRW 7.78 billion, a turn to profit. The first quarter of 2026 returned to an operating loss of KRW 276 million on revenue of KRW 45.5 billion (down 26.3% quarter on quarter).

The second quarter of 2026 brought revenue of KRW 63.7 billion and operating profit of KRW 5.64 billion, and the third quarter brought revenue of KRW 77.4 billion (up 21.5% quarter on quarter) and operating profit of KRW 9.35 billion (up 65.6%). Revenue has risen for two consecutive quarters.

Trailing four-quarter net profit attributable to controlling shareholders totaled KRW 19.1 billion. Securities analysts cited the start of integrated test equipment sales to LG Uplus and the expansion of North American small cells as drivers from the second quarter of 2026.

05

Industry analysis

The telecom equipment cycle weakened visibly in 2025. As global carriers cut 5G capital spending, sales of test and measurement equipment and small cells became more volatile, and this is cited as a backdrop to the 2025 swing to a loss. Analysts say the direction began to change in 2026.

Hana Securities pointed to rising test equipment demand tied to the shift to 5G standalone (SA) networks and to recovering small cell sales in Japan and North America as growth drivers for 2027.

In Korea, test equipment sales linked to LG Uplus are growing, and overseas, small cell businesses in Japan and North America are recovering. On the spectrum side, domestic and overseas frequency auctions and wider use of higher-frequency bands are cited as possible catalysts for carrier equipment investment.

In automotive, rising semiconductor content per vehicle supports growth, but analysts also note that revenue is exposed to swings in the global economy and the chip industry. In defense, electronic warfare equipment supply adds demand that does not follow the telecom spending cycle.

Positioning-wise, a company built around telecom test equipment and small cells is widening into automotive electronics and defense. Two variables will shape the industry view: how fast the telecom spending cycle recovers and the margin structure of the automotive business.

06

Outlook

When the company changed its name in March 2026, it said it would focus on developing a data pipeline for AI model training, AI-RAN performance verification, field test automation for non-terrestrial network (NTN) direct-to-device service, and next-generation automotive eCall verification systems.

The most concrete contract so far is the LG Uplus Core Probe supply agreement disclosed on September 3, 2026. The contract is worth KRW 29.18 billion, runs from September 3, 2026 to March 31, 2027, and has a total supply period of 209 days. It was the company's first contract disclosure of the year.

In a report covered in September 2026, Hana Securities estimated 2026 revenue of KRW 245.6 billion, up 32.0% from 2025, and operating profit of KRW 19.9 billion, a return to profit. The same report projected 2027 revenue of KRW 311.7 billion, operating profit of KRW 35.7 billion and an operating margin of 11.5%.

These are securities-firm estimates, not company guidance. In a report covered in the first half of 2026, Hana Securities also said test equipment sales could rise after a U.S. frequency auction in the second half of 2026.

V2X was described as a mid-to-long-term growth pillar because the company holds both test equipment and modules that apply directly to connected cars. The next checkpoint is annual revenue and operating margin in the 2026 annual report, which will also include fourth-quarter 2026 results.

08

Bull factors

Quarterly earnings recovery

Operating profit has been positive in both the second and third quarters of 2026, following the fourth quarter of 2025. Third-quarter operating profit rose 65.6% from the prior quarter, and revenue has grown for two consecutive quarters. Securities analysts described revenue and profit improving together, as reported by Pinpoint News.

Telecom capex cycle and 5G SA demand

Hana Securities emphasized rising test equipment demand from the shift to 5G standalone networks and the start of integrated test equipment sales to LG Uplus. A recovery in small cell sales in Japan and North America was also cited.

Domestic and overseas frequency auctions and wider use of higher-frequency bands were presented as additional growth drivers.

Defense and V2X as new pillars

Electronic warfare equipment sales to defense system makers began in earnest in 2025. V2X is cited as a mid-to-long-term growth pillar because the company holds modules as well as test equipment. Rising semiconductor content per vehicle is cited as a growth factor for the automotive segment.

09

Bear factors

Reversal in operating cash flow

Operating cash flow swung from an inflow of KRW 18 billion in 2024 to an outflow of KRW 8.5 billion in 2025. Receivables rose over the same period, from the KRW 357.4 million-scale level to KRW 458.2 million-scale, according to a Daily Yonhap report.

Inventory edged up to the KRW 301.5 million-scale level, raising questions about how quickly assets convert to cash.

Audit key audit matter

The auditor's opinion on the 2025 annual report was unqualified, but the audit report listed 'authenticity of product sales' as a key audit matter. Together with analysis that inventory accumulation outpaced sales growth, this has raised concern about the quality of growth. This item will be checked again in the next annual report.

Thin distribution margins and telecom losses

Automotive distribution margins were reported to remain in the 4% to 5% range (E-Today). Customer concentration, with more than half of sales tied to a single first-tier partner, was also flagged as an area to improve.

The telecom segment was reported to have posted a separate-basis operating loss of KRW 1 billion in the first half of 2026, so the pace of recovery in the core business needs monitoring.

10

Risk factors

Accounting and working capital

The key risk is that operating cash flow turned to an outflow while receivables and inventory rose. Because the audit key matter points to the authenticity of sales, audit results in the annual report are an important checkpoint. Whether working capital pressure leads to more short-term borrowing is also worth monitoring.

Customer concentration and margins

The automotive business operates with 4% to 5% distribution margins and high concentration on a single first-tier partner. A change in orders from one customer could quickly move revenue. Whether defense and V2X sales offset this structure is the next question.

Telecom capex cycle and policy timing

Telecom equipment sales swing sharply from quarter to quarter with carriers' capex cycles. If frequency auction timing slips or results fall short, the recovery in test equipment demand could be delayed.

Having faced a telecom downturn in 2025, the company's sales need to be checked each quarter to confirm the cycle is resuming.

12

Overall view

LIG Accuver's operating profit fell from KRW 14.3 billion in 2022 to KRW 2.44 billion in 2024 before the company swung to a KRW 1.12 billion operating loss in 2025. Over the same period revenue peaked at KRW 189.7 billion in 2024 and eased to KRW 186.1 billion in 2025. Quarterly results changed direction in 2026.

Operating profit was KRW 5.64 billion in the second quarter and KRW 9.35 billion in the third, and third-quarter revenue of KRW 77.4 billion rose 86.0% year on year. Growth pillars are automotive semiconductor distribution, telecom test equipment and small cells, and defense electronic warfare equipment.

Supporting factors include the earnings recovery, the LG Uplus contract, demand linked to 5G SA and spectrum, and expansion into defense and V2X.

Negative factors include the 2025 operating cash outflow, rising receivables and inventory, the audit key matter on product sales authenticity, and thin distribution margins with customer concentration.

Hana Securities maintained a KRW 80,000 target price as reported on September 16, 2026, but that is one securities firm's view. The next checkpoint is the annual operating margin and operating cash flow in the 2026 annual report.
