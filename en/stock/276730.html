02

Business structure

HanWool & Jeju was founded in 2015, listed on KOSDAQ in 2021, and changed its name from Jeju Beer in April 2025. Beer accounts for about 67% of revenue and other business for about 33%, based on the second quarter of 2026.

Its flagship product is Jeju Wit Ale, and domestic sales account for 99.05% of product revenue for Jeju Wit Ale and related items as of December 2025, with exports at 0.95%. Brewery tour sales are recorded as 100% domestic in the same filing data.

The company acquired a frozen-food maker, now named Olgot, in 2024 to broaden its food and beverage business, but press reports said that subsidiary turned to a net loss after the acquisition. Its brewing know-how came through a partnership with Brooklyn Brewery of New York, according to the company profile.

The company attributed the 2025 revenue decline to lower product sales amid intensifying competition in the liquor market. Its largest shareholder is HanWool Semiconductor, and overseas buyers and investors took part in the Local Startup Festa in Jeju held from November 4 to 6, 2026, according to the company. The company has moved to a single-CEO structure to streamline decision-making.

04

Earnings analysis

On the basis of the DART-filed annual figures, revenue fell from KRW 24 billion in 2022 to KRW 22.4 billion in 2023 (down 6.3% year on year), KRW 18.3 billion in 2024 (down 18.6%), and KRW 13.9 billion in 2025 (down 24.0%), a decline in each of the three years from 2023 to 2025.

Within 2022 to 2025, 2022 was the largest year and 2025 the smallest. Operating losses were KRW 11.6 billion in 2022, KRW 11 billion in 2023, KRW 4.83 billion in 2024 and KRW 4.9 billion in 2025, so the company has been loss-making for four straight years from 2022 to 2025.

The operating margin was -48.4% in 2022, -49.0% in 2023, -26.4% in 2024 and -35.3% in 2025, so the loss ratio narrowed in 2024 and widened again in 2025. In 2025 operating losses grew slightly even as revenue fell 24.0%.

Net loss attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 24.6 billion in 2022, KRW 12.6 billion in 2023, KRW 20.9 billion in 2024 and KRW 11.5 billion in 2025, a loss in each of the four years, and total net loss including non-controlling interests followed the same path.

The debt ratio rose from 80.4% in 2022 to 118.2% in 2023, 298.7% in 2024 and 278.1% in 2025, and at the end of 2025 total liabilities of KRW 44.4 billion sat against total equity of KRW 16 billion, of which KRW 15.8 billion belonged to controlling shareholders.

Operating cash flow was negative in all four years, at -KRW 9.7 billion in 2022, -KRW 9.52 billion in 2023, -KRW 3.4 billion in 2024 and -KRW 8.34 billion in 2025.

Quarterly revenue was KRW 3.37 billion in the third quarter of 2025, KRW 3.82 billion in the fourth quarter, KRW 3.33 billion in the first quarter of 2026, KRW 3.8 billion in the second quarter and KRW 4.14 billion in the third quarter of 2026, while the operating loss in the third quarter of 2026 was KRW 2.16 billion, wider than a year earlier.

The net loss attributable to controlling shareholders in the third quarter of 2026 was KRW 4.48 billion, larger than the operating loss, which points to costs below the operating line widening the gap; the detail should be checked in the footnotes of the third-quarter 2026 quarterly report.

The combined net loss attributable to controlling shareholders over the last four quarters, from the fourth quarter of 2025 to the third quarter of 2026, was KRW 18.7 billion. The company said on November 13, 2026 that it will offset about KRW 5.3 billion of first-half losses against its deficit.

05

Industry analysis

HanWool & Jeju's core market is the domestic beer and liquor market, and the company attributed its 2025 revenue decline to intensifying competition in liquor sales. Its company profile states that its founding goal was to popularize craft beer, and it emphasizes an automated brewing system.

Its strategy leans on regional branding and brewery tours as differentiators, but large liquor makers have more distribution reach and brand scale, which could leave it at a disadvantage in pricing and shelf competition.

Compared with listed food and beverage companies, its revenue base is small, and the scale gap with large liquor and beverage makers such as Hite Jinro and Lotte Chilsung Beverage is wide.

On overseas expansion, the company said buyers from Hong Kong and other markets showed interest at the Local Startup Festa in November 2026. Whether that interest has translated into sales cannot yet be confirmed from filed figures.

Separately, the share price has stayed below KRW 1,000, which has raised the possibility of designation as a KOSDAQ administrative issue stock, a constraint that is company-specific rather than industry-wide.

The purchases on the open market by the largest shareholder and management are described by the company as a measure to restore trust with the market.

06

Outlook

The company will put a 5-for-1 share consolidation amendment and a deficit offset plan for the first half of 2026 before an extraordinary shareholder meeting on November 27, 2026. The consolidation combines five common shares with a KRW 100 par value into one common share with a KRW 500 par value.

The company plans to offset first-half losses of about KRW 5.3 billion, reviewed by its external auditor, by reducing share premium within capital reserves and transferring the same amount to retained earnings. Once that offset is complete, cumulative deficit offsets in 2026 will reach about KRW 89.7 billion.

The company has explained that the offset does not increase total equity but reclassifies capital line items.

The roughly KRW 19.7 billion of capital raised in 2026 consists of about KRW 9.9 billion from a rights offering, about KRW 3.6 billion from conversion of convertible bonds and about KRW 6.2 billion from warrant exercises.

The largest shareholder, HanWool Semiconductor, subscribed for 300,000 new shares in the November 2026 rights offering, about 11.1% of the planned new issuance. The company is reviewing plans to sell existing investment assets to secure liquidity, and the specific assets and timing have not yet been announced.

The CEO has set strengthening core business competitiveness and improving profitability as priorities. The company said shareholder return measures will be reviewed in light of operating results, financial conditions and distributable earnings. The next checkpoint is the annual operating margin and operating loss scale in the 2026 annual report.

Bull factors

Revenue up for two straight quarters

Revenue rose to KRW 3.8 billion in the second quarter of 2026 and KRW 4.14 billion in the third quarter, two consecutive quarterly gains. Third-quarter revenue was up 22.9% year on year. Compared with three straight years of annual declines through 2025, the quarterly trend shows a rebound signal.

Quarterly revenue is only modestly above the third quarter of 2025, so whether the gains persist needs confirmation from upcoming filings.

Capital raise and debt converted into equity

About KRW 19.7 billion of capital was added in 2026 through a rights offering of about KRW 9.9 billion, conversion of convertible bonds of about KRW 3.6 billion and warrant exercises of about KRW 6.2 billion. The company said that converting CB and BW liabilities into equity reduced repayment pressure.

The company's stated plan is that reclassifying cumulative deficits of about KRW 89.7 billion would allow shareholder return measures to be considered once profits accumulate.

Insider buying by the largest shareholder and management

On November 2, 2026, the largest shareholder HanWool Semiconductor bought 37,862 shares, CEO Kim Baek-san bought 57,910 shares and Deputy CEO Kim Dong-hyun bought 33,230 shares on the open market, 129,002 shares in total.

The company described the purchases as a response to the risk of designation as an administrative issue stock. The largest shareholder also subscribed for 300,000 new shares in the rights offering that month. The company presents the direct cash commitment as evidence of its resolve to stabilize management.

Bear factors

Five straight quarters of operating losses, with a wider gap

Operating losses have run for five consecutive quarters from the third quarter of 2025 through the third quarter of 2026. The operating loss in the third quarter of 2026 was KRW 2.16 billion, wider than a year earlier.

Net loss attributable to controlling shareholders reached KRW 4.48 billion in the third quarter of 2026, and the four-quarter total is KRW 18.7 billion. If revenue growth does not come with an improved cost structure, a move back to operating profit remains some distance away.

Four straight loss years and three straight years of revenue decline

Revenue declined for three straight years from 2023 through 2025, falling to KRW 13.9 billion in 2025. The company has been loss-making for four years from 2022 through 2025, and the operating margin was -35.3% in 2025. The company cited intensifying liquor-market competition for the 2025 revenue decline.

Revenue has not returned to the KRW 24 billion level of 2022, and the core business recovery is not yet confirmed in annual results.

Leverage and erosion risk to equity

The debt ratio rose from 80.4% in 2022 to 298.7% in 2024 and 278.1% in 2025. Total liabilities at the end of 2025 were KRW 44.4 billion, well above total equity of KRW 16 billion. Operating cash flow was negative in all four years through 2025. Because the deficit offset does not increase total equity, the financial burden remains.

Risk factors

Listing status and administrative issue designation

The share price staying below KRW 1,000 has raised the possibility of designation as a KOSDAQ administrative issue stock. The company says the open-market purchases by the largest shareholder and management and the 5-for-1 consolidation address this risk.

Even if the consolidation is approved at the November 27, 2026 meeting, the trading structure will take time to settle, so price and volume changes after the consolidation should be watched.

Share dilution

Conversion of CBs and exercise of BWs in 2026 increased the share count, and a rights offering was also completed. The capital raise converted debt into equity, but existing shareholders' ownership percentages declined by the same token. Further conversions or exercises could keep increasing the share count.

The consolidation will reduce the share count to one-fifth, but the remaining balance of convertible securities must be checked in filings.

Business focus and liquidity

The company is reviewing the sale of existing investment assets to secure liquidity, and the assets and timing have not yet been announced. Press reports said a subsidiary acquired to diversify into food and beverage underperformed.

With the core beer business facing both revenue decline and losses, asset sales could narrow room for investment in the core business. The timing and terms of any disposal plan should be checked when announced.

Overall view

HanWool & Jeju is a beer company whose annual revenue fell for three straight years and which recorded losses for four straight years from 2022 through 2025.

Quarterly revenue has risen for two consecutive quarters in 2026, but operating losses have continued for five straight quarters, and the third-quarter loss was wider than a year earlier.

On the financial side, the 2026 capital raise of about KRW 19.7 billion and the deficit offset are restructuring the balance sheet, but the debt ratio of 278.1% remains high.

The open-market purchases by the largest shareholder and management and the largest shareholder's rights offering subscription are presented by the company as evidence of responsible management. A share price below KRW 1,000 and the possibility of administrative issue designation are an important constraint.

The price-to-book ratio is below 1x, but earnings-based valuation measures cannot be calculated because of losses. The next checkpoints are the outcome of the November 27, 2026 extraordinary shareholder meeting, the disposal plan for investment assets, and the operating margin in the 2026 annual report.
