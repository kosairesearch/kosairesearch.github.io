Business structure

Interm makes industrial audio equipment, centered on PA (public address) systems, wireless devices and sound products.

According to company profile data, the revenue mix is given as products (PA, SR, wireless and others) at 46.6%, rental and other income at 28.46%, and merchandise (microphones, projectors and others) at 24.95%, though the reference period for this mix is not stated.

Rental and other income accounting for more than a quarter of revenue means that a non-manufacturing revenue stream sits alongside product sales. The PA (public address system) market share is put at 60% as of June 2026.

The consolidated group includes Inter-M Dongguan Electronics, which manufactures electronic products in Dongguan, China.

In overseas business, railway vehicle electronics is being pursued; the profile summary says the company completed the first delivery of electronics for Turkish State Railways EMU vehicles and is expanding exports to Europe, India and Asia.

R&D expense was KRW 3.67 billion in the year ended September 2025, or 6.11% of revenue (consolidated basis, company profile data). On competition, the only available evidence is press coverage saying Interm keeps a high share in the public address market; competitor-level share figures were not confirmed.

Because its scale and product range differ from large electronics and components listed companies, direct peer comparison is limited.

Earnings analysis

On an annual basis, sales were KRW 51 billion in the year ended September 2023, KRW 60.3 billion in the year ended September 2024 (up 18.4% year on year), and KRW 60.1 billion in the year ended September 2025 (down 0.5% year on year). The year ended September 2024 was the largest sales year among the three.

Operating profit fell from KRW 10.8 billion in the year ended September 2023 to KRW 4.4 billion in the year ended September 2024 (down 59.1% year on year) and KRW 3.99 billion in the year ended September 2025 (down 9.5% year on year), declining two years in a row from the year ended September 2024 through the year ended September 2025.

The operating margin moved from 21.1% to 7.3% and then 6.6%, so revenue held up while profitability narrowed sharply.

Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 8.23 billion in the year ended September 2023, KRW 2.31 billion in the year ended September 2024 (down 71.9% year on year), and KRW 2.05 billion in the year ended September 2025 (down 11.3% year on year).

Net profit including non-controlling interests was reported at the same amounts over the same period. At the end of September 2025, total equity was KRW 42.2 billion, all of it attributable to controlling shareholders, and total liabilities were KRW 47 billion.

The debt-to-equity ratio moved from 117.5% to 113.2% and then 111.3%. Operating cash flow was KRW 4.03 billion, KRW 2.68 billion, and KRW 9.52 billion in the years ended September 2023, 2024 and 2025, rising in the latest year while operating profit declined.

On a quarterly basis, sales were KRW 15 billion in 2Q 2025, KRW 18.9 billion in 3Q 2025 (up 26.2% from the prior quarter), and KRW 12.9 billion in 4Q 2025 (down 31.7%), then KRW 17.8 billion in 1Q 2026 and KRW 19.1 billion in 2Q 2026 (up 7.1% from the prior quarter), rising for two straight quarters.

Operating profit bottomed at KRW 53 million in 4Q 2025 (down 97.8% from the prior quarter), then reached KRW 456 million in 1Q 2026 and KRW 1.75 billion in 2Q 2026 (up 283.8% from the prior quarter), also rising for two straight quarters.

In 1Q 2026, operating profit was positive but net profit attributable to controlling shareholders was a loss of KRW 97.64 million, a swing into loss; in 2Q 2026 it returned to a profit of KRW 1.9 billion.

The sum of net profit attributable to controlling shareholders over the last four quarters, from 3Q 2025 to 2Q 2026, was KRW 3.5 billion. The disclosed data alone does not explain why 1Q 2026 showed positive operating profit alongside a net loss attributable to controlling shareholders.

Industry analysis

A 2021 securities research report estimated that Korean speaker shipments rose from KRW 315.5 billion in 2014 to KRW 345.6 billion in 2019, an average annual increase of 1.84%.

That data is several years old and cannot be carried over to today's market size, and no recent market-size figure was confirmed in this review. The same report said the company tried to widen its private-sector and global mix during a period when public-sector contracts were suspended.

Recent press coverage says Interm keeps a high share of the public address market, and company profile data puts its PA market share at 60%. However, one analysis notes that sales have stayed around KRW 60 billion since 2020, so market growth has not translated into sustained top-line growth for long stretches.

Railway vehicle electronics tends to concentrate revenue around delivery timing, which can amplify quarterly swings. Whether the 2Q 2026 sales recovery reflects industry conditions or individual orders cannot be separated from the disclosures alone.

Compared with other KOSDAQ electronics and components companies, Interm is small, so direct comparison with sector averages is limited.

Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders over the last four quarters totals KRW 3.5 billion, and the wide quarter-to-quarter swings are the basis of this bear point.

Market-cap standard and listing pressure

Overall view

Interm is an industrial audio equipment maker centered on PA public address systems. Across the years ended September 2023 to September 2025, sales stalled around KRW 60 billion, operating profit fell two years in a row, and the operating margin dropped from 21.1% to 6.6%.

In 2026, the company swung to a net loss attributable to controlling shareholders in 1Q and returned to a profit in 2Q, with 2Q sales of KRW 19.1 billion and operating profit of KRW 1.75 billion, each rising for two straight quarters.

Treasury share purchases and a cancellation resolution continued the shareholder-return activity, and the 60% PA market share and overseas railway electronics business are presented as strengths.

On the other side, quarterly volatility, the lower annual operating margin, the planned rise in the market-cap standard for administrative issues, and the delisting injunction proceedings remain weaknesses.

Because strengths and weaknesses appear together, the key checkpoint is whether the operating margin and net profit attributable to controlling shareholders hold up in the annual business report for the year ended September 2026.
