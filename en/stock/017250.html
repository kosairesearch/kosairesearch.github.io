Interm: Whether the Return to Profit Can Hold

02

Business structure

Interm was founded in 1983 as a provider of audio, video and communication solutions and listed on KOSDAQ in January 1996. The company offers integrated audio, video and communication solutions and positions itself as a leader in public address broadcasting.

According to WiseReport's company monitor, revenue is split into products (PA, SR, wireless and others) at 46.6%, rental and other income at 28.46%, and merchandise (microphones, projectors and others) at 24.95%.

A dataset from around 2024 put audio equipment-related sales at about 67% and rental income at about 32%, so the segment mix varies with the timing and classification used. Its PA (public address system) market share was shown as 60% as of June 2026.

Major customers are large venues such as performance halls and stadiums, and disclosed orders include stage materials and installation for Seoul Arena worth KRW 1.58 billion (26.2% of revenue) and a PAS, PIS and CCTV procurement contract for electric train sets worth KRW 634 million (10.5% of revenue).

Expansion of overseas rail projects is a pillar the company cites for restoring corporate value. R&D spending in the fiscal year ended September 2025 was about KRW 3.67 billion, or 6.1% of revenue.

On competition, price pressure from low-cost Chinese products has been cited as the main cause of past revenue contraction.

04

Earnings analysis

On an annual basis, revenue rose from KRW 51 billion in the fiscal year ended September 2023 to KRW 60.3 billion in the fiscal year ended September 2024, up 18.4%, and then eased slightly to KRW 60.1 billion in the fiscal year ended September 2025, down 0.5%.

The fiscal year ended September 2024 was the largest year and the fiscal year ended September 2023 the smallest within the FY2023 to FY2025 range.

Operating profit fell from KRW 10.8 billion to KRW 4.4 billion (down 59.1%) and then to KRW 3.99 billion (down 9.5%), so it declined for two consecutive fiscal years from the fiscal year ended September 2024 through the fiscal year ended September 2025.

The FY2023 operating profit included a KRW 9.37 billion litigation provision reversal; excluding it, as the company explained, FY2024 operating profit would have risen 214.7% year on year.

The post-FY2023 decline therefore reflects largely the absence of that one-off reversal, and the company attributed underlying improvement to lower selling and administrative expenses, cost reduction and process improvements.

Even so, operating margin fell from 21.1% in the fiscal year ended September 2023 to 7.3% and 6.6% in the two following years.

Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 8.23 billion, KRW 2.31 billion (down 71.9%) and KRW 2.05 billion (down 11.3%), and total net profit including non-controlling interests was reported at the same amounts.

At the end of September 2025, total equity was KRW 42.2 billion, all attributable to controlling shareholders, against total liabilities of KRW 47 billion, and the debt-to-equity ratio declined from 117.5% to 111.3% over the period.

Operating cash flow rose from KRW 4.03 billion and KRW 2.68 billion in the first two years to KRW 9.52 billion in the fiscal year ended September 2025. Quarterly revenue bottomed at KRW 12.9 billion in Q4 2025 (down 31.7% from the prior quarter), with operating profit of KRW 53 million (down 97.8%).

Revenue then rose to KRW 17.8 billion in Q1 2026 and KRW 19.1 billion in Q2 2026, two consecutive quarterly increases. Q2 2026 operating profit was KRW 1.75 billion, up only 0.5% year on year.

Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders moved to a KRW 97.64 million loss in Q1 2026 before returning to KRW 1.9 billion in Q2 2026, and the last four quarters (Q3 2025 to Q2 2026) total KRW 3.5 billion.

05

Industry analysis

The public address and public audio market to which Interm belongs depends on installed demand from performance halls, stadiums and public facilities. Interm's PA market share was shown as 60% as of June 2026, which places it among the leading positions in domestic public address broadcasting.

Annual revenue, however, has stayed around KRW 60 billion since 2020. Past revenue contraction is attributed to price competition from low-cost Chinese products, a debarment that limited participation in public works bidding from May 2020 to April 2021, and event delays linked to COVID-19.

Over the same period, operating losses widened from KRW 3.9 billion in 2019 to KRW 15.5 billion in 2020 and KRW 17.8 billion in 2021. Recent growth levers cited are large domestic venue orders and expansion of overseas rail projects.

By industry classification, Interm sits within KOSDAQ's electronics sector under IT hardware, though its operations combine audio equipment manufacturing and sales with rental income. On competition, Interm holds a high PA share but remains exposed to price pressure from low-cost imports. Buybacks and cancellations are a parallel shareholder-value measure that addresses the stalled revenue base.

06

Outlook

The key test is whether the Q2 2026 revenue growth and return to profit persist. Q2 2026 revenue of KRW 19.1 billion was up 27.4% year on year, while operating profit of KRW 1.75 billion rose only 0.5%, so whether revenue growth is translating into margin improvement needs to be confirmed in upcoming quarters.

Share buybacks totaling KRW 402.8 million were completed between February 25 and April 9, 2026, and the 996,542 shares acquired between February and September 2026 were retired. After the cancellation, listed shares stand at 20,048,925, which reduces the share count as a shareholder-return measure.

KOSDAQ's market-capitalization threshold for avoiding management-issue designation rises to KRW 30 billion from July 2027. A October 2026 news report calculated that the share price would need to rise about 34.6% on the listed share count for the company to meet that threshold.

Overseas rail expansion and large performance-hall stage orders are cited as candidates for growth, but individual orders are disclosed case by case and should be read alongside the quarterly revenue trend. Quantitative guidance is not covered in this report. The Q4 2026 quarterly report is scheduled to be filed in mid-February 2027.

Bear factors

Two consecutive years of operating profit decline and thin margins

Operating profit fell from KRW 10.8 billion in the fiscal year ended September 2023 to KRW 3.99 billion in the fiscal year ended September 2025, and operating margin dropped from 21.1% to 6.6%.

The FY2023 figure included a KRW 9.37 billion provision reversal, and the company says FY2024 operating profit would have risen without it. Even so, operating margin has stayed around 6% to 7% since the fiscal year ended September 2024.

Stalled revenue and quarterly volatility

Revenue peaked at KRW 60.3 billion in the fiscal year ended September 2024 and eased to KRW 60.1 billion in the fiscal year ended September 2025. News reports show consolidated revenue of KRW 102.6 billion in 2018 fell to KRW 63.4 billion in 2020 and has since stayed near KRW 60 billion.

Operating profit was KRW 53 million in Q4 2025, and Q1 2026 posted a net loss attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 97.64 million.

Price competition and past bidding restrictions

Price competition from low-cost Chinese products has been cited as the main cause of past revenue contraction. The company was barred from public works bidding from May 2020 to April 2021. Because individual orders carry large revenue shares, a gap in orders can translate into quarterly revenue swings.

The company was also designated an unfaithful-disclosure company on June 23, 2023, over delayed disclosure related to litigation.

Quarterly earnings volatility

Operating profit in Q4 2025 fell 97.8% from the prior quarter to KRW 53 million. Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders swung to a KRW 97.64 million loss in Q1 2026 before returning to profit in Q2. Whether quarterly profits can persist beyond a couple of quarters has yet to be established.

Order concentration and price competition

Seoul Arena stage materials equal 26.2% of revenue and the rail procurement contract equals 10.5%, so single orders are large relative to sales. If orders do not arrive steadily, revenue gains could reverse. Competition from low-cost Chinese products has been cited as a cause of past revenue stagnation.

Overall view

Interm's revenue has stalled near KRW 60 billion after peaking at KRW 60.3 billion in the fiscal year ended September 2024.

Operating profit fell from KRW 10.8 billion in the fiscal year ended September 2023, which included a KRW 9.37 billion provision reversal, to KRW 3.99 billion in the fiscal year ended September 2025, and operating margin dropped from 21.1% to 6.6%.

Strengths include Q2 2026 revenue of KRW 19.1 billion, up 27.4% year on year, the return to a KRW 1.9 billion profit attributable to controlling shareholders, market value below total equity, and treasury share cancellation.

Weaknesses include the Q1 2026 net loss attributable to controlling shareholders, the Q4 2025 operating profit low of KRW 53 million, Q2 2026 operating profit up only 0.5% year on year, and price competition from low-cost Chinese products.

The debt-to-equity ratio fell to 111.3% and operating cash flow rose to KRW 9.52 billion in the fiscal year ended September 2025, yet whether the profit recovery holds quarter to quarter rests on a still-short record. The market-capitalization threshold effective from July 2027 remains an external variable.

The next checkpoints are the operating margin in the annual report for the fiscal year ending September 2026 and the operating profit trend in the October to December 2026 quarterly report.
