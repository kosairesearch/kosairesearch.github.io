02

Business structure

Daewon is a general construction company engaged in housing development, sales and civil engineering works. The company's business overview classifies it as a general construction company, so its business structure differs from the refining label shown on the card.

Segment revenue in 2025 was 58.5% from building construction contracts, 14.4% from civil engineering contracts and 12.7% from a segment labeled Moban, according to disclo's compilation. Building and civil engineering contracts together accounted for 72.9% of that revenue.

In the same compilation, domestic sales made up 97.9% and overseas sales 2.1%. Its main apartment brand is CANTAVIL, and the company says it will keep developing under this brand in Korea and overseas, according to PrimeEconomy.

Its representative contract, the Namju-dong No. 8 redevelopment construction project, is valued at KRW 138.31 billion and runs until June 2028, according to a corporate-analysis site.

Its wholly owned textile-products subsidiary is slated for absorption into the parent, with the merger agreement dated October 19, 2026, according to Digital Today.

In competition, the company emphasizes a stable earnings structure centered on its own projects and its financial strength, while the broader construction sector has contracted. With 97.9% of sales from Korea, the domestic housing market and construction pace are the central variables for the business.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual revenue rose from KRW 358.3 billion in 2022 to KRW 436.1 billion in 2023, up 21.7% year on year, before falling to KRW 277.2 billion in 2024, down 36.4%, and KRW 112.1 billion in 2025, down 59.5%. Among 2022 to 2025, 2023 was the largest year and 2025 the smallest.

The operating margin was 5.0% in 2022, -14.2% in 2023, 0.1% in 2024 and -12.3% in 2025. Operating profit of KRW 17.8 billion in 2022 became an operating loss of KRW 62.1 billion in 2023, turned to an operating profit of KRW 195 million in 2024, and became an operating loss of KRW 13.8 billion in 2025.

Parent net income was KRW 19.5 billion in 2022, a parent net loss of KRW 57.4 billion in 2023, parent net income of KRW 2.83 billion in 2024 and a parent net loss of KRW 8.45 billion in 2025. Net income including non-controlling interests, a separate figure, was a net loss of KRW 8.5 billion in 2025.

Even in 2024, when the company was profitable, operating cash flow was negative at -KRW 74.9 billion, and in 2025 it was -KRW 69.4 billion, with all four years from 2022 to 2025 negative.

The debt ratio climbed from 48.6% in 2022 to 82.4% in 2025, when total equity was KRW 279.3 billion, of which KRW 278.1 billion belonged to parent shareholders, and total liabilities were KRW 230.2 billion.

Quarterly revenue rose to KRW 28.5 billion in the fourth quarter of 2025, up 25.3% from the prior quarter, and peaked at KRW 29 billion in the first quarter of 2026. It then fell to KRW 27.1 billion in the second quarter and KRW 18.5 billion in the third quarter, two consecutive quarterly declines.

Operating results moved from a KRW 40.51 million operating profit in the first quarter of 2026 to a KRW 11.69 million operating loss in the second quarter and a KRW 3.27 billion operating loss in the third quarter, two consecutive loss quarters. Parent net losses over the last four quarters total KRW 828 million.

The detailed causes of the revenue decline and the size of any one-off items in quarterly results are matters to check in the notes to the annual business report.

Industry analysis

The card shows a refining sector label, but the company's business overview classifies Daewon as a general construction company, so the industry analysis rests on housing and construction demand.

Domestic housing sales and construction orders are the core of the business, and domestic sales were 97.9% of revenue in 2025. PrimeEconomy reported that defaults and workout applications by construction companies have added to liquidity stress across the industry.

In the same report, the company said it stands out on financial stability while the construction sector as a whole has contracted. That is the company's own position, so any differentiation should be tested against its revenue and profit trend.

Revenue fell 59.5% in 2025, and operating losses continued in the second and third quarters of 2026, suggesting that order backlog is converting into revenue slowly.

If the company's own-project focus holds, its risk profile differs from one dependent on contracted work, but it remains exposed to the pace of housing sales and collections. To gauge the company's position in the cycle, the useful yardstick is how quickly order backlog turns into revenue and then into cash.

The company talks about expanding overseas under the CANTAVIL brand, but overseas sales were only 2.1% of revenue in 2025.

Bull factors

Long-dated construction backlog

Quarterly profit turns

First-quarter 2026 operating profit was KRW 40.51 million, turning to profit, with a parent net profit of KRW 2.5 billion. The second quarter of 2026 also recorded a parent net profit of KRW 1.36 billion, so the parent recorded profit in two consecutive quarters. Moving out of the net-loss stretch of the third and fourth quarters of 2025 reads as a recovery signal.

Equity base and stated financial stability

Total equity at year-end 2025 was KRW 279.3 billion, of which KRW 278.1 billion belonged to parent shareholders. According to press coverage, the company presents financial stability as a strength amid industry liquidity stress. It also stresses an earnings structure centered on its own projects as a point of difference.

Bear factors

Revenue fell 36.4% in 2024 and 59.5% in 2025. Operating losses appeared for two consecutive quarters in the second and third quarters of 2026, with the third-quarter loss widening from the prior quarter to KRW 3.27 billion. The parent net loss turned negative again at KRW 3.91 billion in the third quarter.

Negative operating cash flow

Operating cash flow was negative in all four years from 2022 to 2025, from -KRW 8.11 billion to -KRW 69.4 billion. Even in 2024, when parent net income was positive, operating cash flow was -KRW 74.9 billion. The gap between book profit and cash collection is large.

Rising debt ratio

The debt ratio rose every year, from 48.6% in 2022 to 62.4% in 2023, 67.1% in 2024 and 82.4% in 2025. Total liabilities at year-end 2025 were KRW 230.2 billion. In an environment of industry liquidity stress, that debt load can affect financing terms.

Risk factors

Construction costs and collections

A corporate-analysis site says the recovery of profit depends on cost ratios and collections on long-term contracts such as the Namju-dong No. 8 project. If costs exceed contract value or collections slip, losses may persist. The KRW 3.27 billion operating loss in the third quarter of 2026 can be read as an indicator of this risk.

Industry liquidity and housing demand

According to press coverage, defaults and workout applications by construction companies are adding to industry liquidity stress. With 97.9% of sales from Korea, a slowdown in domestic housing sales would directly affect orders and revenue. Overseas sales of 2.1% offer limited diversification.

Dividend funding and subsidiary merger execution

The March 27, 2026 shareholders' meeting approved a cash dividend with a total payout of about KRW 2.6 billion. Net losses continued over the same period, so the funding source must be checked against operating cash flow.

The absorption merger of the subsidiary is set for January 1, 2027, and whether the procedure stays on schedule is also a point to check.

Overall view

Daewon is a general construction company centered on housing and civil works, and revenue fell for two consecutive years after its 2023 peak of KRW 436.1 billion.

The company moved into operating and parent net losses in 2025, with an operating loss of KRW 13.8 billion and a parent net loss of KRW 8.45 billion, and after an operating profit in the first quarter of 2026, two consecutive quarters of operating losses followed.

Parent net losses over the last four quarters total KRW 828 million, a small amount, but operating cash flow was negative throughout the period from 2022. On the financial side, total equity was KRW 279.3 billion and total liabilities KRW 230.2 billion at year-end 2025, with the debt ratio at 82.4%.

Strengths include the KRW 138.31 billion Namju-dong No. 8 long-term contract, the return to operating profit in the first quarter of 2026, and an equity base mostly held by parent shareholders. Weaknesses are the revenue decline, two consecutive loss quarters and negative operating cash flow.

The next scheduled events are board approval of the subsidiary merger on November 19, 2026 and the merger effective date of January 1, 2027.

The 2026 annual business report will fix fourth-quarter and full-year 2026 results, and cost ratios and collections on long-term contracts remain the central variables for future results.
