Daewon: Earnings Swing Between Profit and Loss

Daewon, a general contractor built around its CANTAVIL housing brand, has seen revenue fall for two straight years after peaking in 2023, while operating profit and loss have alternated, widening earnings volatility.

02

Business structure

Daewon is a general contractor founded in 1972 and headquartered in Cheongju, North Chungcheong Province, operated under a co-CEO structure.

The company builds and sells apartments and mixed-use residential buildings under its own CANTAVIL housing brand, while also handling commercial building construction and civil works such as industrial complex development and road construction.

In fiscal 2025, building construction contracts accounted for 58.5% of revenue, the largest segment, followed by civil engineering contracts at 14.4% and a textile commissioned manufacturing and sales segment at 12.7%.

Domestic sales made up 97.9% of revenue while overseas sales were only 2.1%, suggesting the contribution from Daewon's past Vietnam housing and industrial-park development initiatives to current revenue appears limited.

The CANTAVIL brand has a supply track record across Seoul's Mok-dong and Apgujeong areas as well as Hanam Misa, Dongtan Station, Guri Inchang, Uiwang Naeson-dong, Cheongju Sangdang, Cheonan Buldang, Daejeon Jukdong, and Busan Yangjeong.

More recently, the company began marketing the 612-unit CANTAVIL The Edition in Gimpo's Bukbyeon 2 district in November 2025, while move-ins began at CANTAVIL The Suite near Singeomdan Jungang Station in Incheon in June 2026.

The textile segment has been run through a wholly owned subsidiary, which the company decided in October 2026 to absorb into the parent, with the merger scheduled to take effect on January 1, 2027.

Compared with large builders, Daewon ranks lower in capital base and construction-capacity rankings, but it is classified as a mid-tier builder that has built brand recognition through a strategy focused on its own development projects in prime metropolitan locations.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual revenue rose 21.7% from KRW 358.3 billion in 2022 to KRW 436.1 billion in 2023, the largest figure in the 2022-2025 span, before falling for two consecutive years to KRW 277.2 billion in 2024 (down 36.4%) and KRW 112.1 billion in 2025 (down 59.5%), making 2025 the smallest year.

Operating profit swung from operating income of KRW 17.8 billion in 2022 to an operating loss of KRW 62.1 billion in 2023, then back to operating income of KRW 195 million in 2024, before turning to an operating loss of KRW 13.8 billion in 2025, flipping sign almost every year.

The operating margin likewise moved from 5.0% to -14.2% to 0.1% to -12.3% over the same period.

Net income attributable to controlling interests went from KRW 19.5 billion in 2022 to a net loss of KRW 57.4 billion in 2023, back to net income of KRW 2.83 billion in 2024, and then to a net loss of KRW 8.45 billion in 2025, while net income including non-controlling interests followed a similar path from KRW 19.5 billion to a net loss of KRW 57.4 billion, to KRW 2.63 billion, and to a net loss of KRW 8.5 billion.

On a quarterly basis, the operating loss widened from KRW 5.75 billion in the second quarter of 2025 to KRW 7.77 billion in the third quarter, then narrowed to KRW 1.59 billion in the fourth quarter, before turning to operating income of KRW 40.51 million in the first quarter of 2026 and back to an operating loss of KRW 11.69 million in the second quarter.

Net income attributable to controlling interests over the same span moved from a net loss of KRW 3.12 billion in the second quarter of 2025 to a wider net loss of KRW 5.03 billion in the third quarter, a narrower net loss of KRW 777 million in the fourth quarter, net income of KRW 2.5 billion in the first quarter of 2026, and net income of KRW 1.36 billion in the second quarter, down 45.7% quarter on quarter but a turn to profit year on year.

The trailing four quarters from the third quarter of 2025 through the second quarter of 2026 still show a combined net loss attributable to controlling interests of KRW 1.95 billion, meaning the cumulative position remains in loss territory despite the quarterly swings.

On the balance sheet, the debt-to-equity ratio climbed from 48.6% in 2022 to 82.4% in 2025, and operating cash flow was negative for four straight years: -KRW 8.11 billion in 2022, -KRW 56.1 billion in 2023, -KRW 74.9 billion in 2024, and -KRW 69.4 billion in 2025, indicating that a recovery in cash generation has yet to be confirmed.

05

Industry analysis

According to the Korea Research Institute for Construction Policy, domestic construction investment is projected to recover only modestly, by around 2%, in 2026 after falling roughly 9% in 2025.

The Korea Institute of Civil Engineering and Building Technology forecast that construction orders in the second half of 2026 would rise 8.9% year on year to KRW 240.8 trillion, with construction investment up 0.3% to KRW 266.1 trillion.

Nationwide housing sale prices are expected to be led by a 4.5% gain in the Seoul metropolitan area, while regional areas are projected to rise only about 0.5%, pointing to a widening regional divide.

Post-completion unsold units remained in the range of roughly 27,000 to 28,000 units as of the second half of 2025, a historically elevated level that keeps housing-business risk elevated outside the capital region.

Against this backdrop, the institute noted that the recovery felt by private non-residential, regional, and smaller builders would likely remain limited.

Daewon has concentrated its own-account housing sales in the Seoul metropolitan area, including Mok-dong, Apgujeong, Gimpo, and Incheon, giving it a portfolio weighted toward the region expected to outperform under this diverging market.

However, the company trails large builders in capital base and construction-capacity rankings, which could put it at a relative disadvantage in order competition, and its textile segment operates on a different demand cycle from construction, with limited impact on overall results given its small scale.

Risk factors

Industry Risk

Domestic construction investment is projected to recover only modestly, by around 2%, in 2026 after falling roughly 9% in 2025.

Post-completion unsold units stood at roughly 27,000 to 28,000 units as of the second half of 2025, a historically elevated level that keeps housing-business risk elevated outside the capital region.

Daewon is weighted toward the metropolitan area, but if the broader sector recovery is delayed, its sales and construction schedules could be affected.

Financial Risk

The debt-to-equity ratio rose from 48.6% in 2022 to 82.4% in 2025, and operating cash flow was negative for four consecutive years. If pre-sale cash inflows and construction progress do not proceed as planned, liquidity pressure could increase.

The balance between shareholder-return measures such as treasury share retirement and financial soundness management is a point to monitor going forward.

Business Restructuring and One-Off Cost Risk

As shown by the 59.5% drop in 2025 revenue, gaps between project completions and new starts amplify earnings volatility. The October 2026 decision to absorb the textile subsidiary could also generate one-off merger-related costs or accounting effects.

Because the textile segment operates on a different demand cycle than construction, visibility into segment profitability after the merger also warrants attention.

Overall view

Revenue fell for two straight years in 2024 and 2025 after peaking in 2023, and operating profit alternated between gains and losses every year from 2022 through 2025.
