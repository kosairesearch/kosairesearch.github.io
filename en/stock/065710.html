02

Business structure

Seoho Electric designs and supplies electrical and electronic control systems and drive control systems for cranes used at ports, shipyards and steel mills. The company points to its crane automation experience, in-house systems and engineering staff as its technology edge.

Major customers reported in press coverage include POSCO, Hyundai Steel and domestic thermal power plants, and the company has worked with global crane makers such as Doosan Enerbility, Hyundai Samho Heavy Industries and China's ZPMC.

Press reports also name Samsung Heavy Industries, HD Hyundai Heavy Industries and HJ Heavy Industries as clients.

In ports, it supplies ARMG and QC automation systems for PSA's TUAS Phase 1 in Singapore, and it handled equipment control and terminal operating systems at Busan New Port's Western Container Terminal Phase 2-5. Reports also say the Phase 2-6 transfer crane project was contracted with HJ Heavy Industries.

The company mainly wins crane projects from large shipping companies and government agencies. Because its orders are largely made to order, quarterly results can swing sharply with the timing of awards and project progress.

Demand rests on shipbuilding, steel and power plant investment alongside port automation spending, so the customer industries' cycles shape earnings volatility. Compared with other listed electrical equipment companies, its concentration on port and shipyard crane controls is the clearest structural difference.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual revenue rose from KRW 60.2 billion in 2022 to KRW 66.2 billion in 2023 (up 10.0% year on year), fell to KRW 46.5 billion in 2024 (down 29.8%), and then jumped to KRW 116.6 billion in 2025 (up 151.0%). Among 2022 to 2025, 2025 was the largest year and 2024 the smallest.

Operating profit went from KRW 12.5 billion in 2022 and KRW 13 billion in 2023 (up 4.1%) to KRW 1.65 billion in 2024 (down 87.3%), before recovering to KRW 25.3 billion in 2025 (up 1,439.0%). The operating margin slipped from 20.7% in 2022 and 19.6% in 2023 to 3.5% in 2024 and then rose to 21.7% in 2025.

Digital Today reported that the company cited a decline in orders as the main reason for weak 2024 results. The Asia Economy-linked Etoday report noted that the KRW 25.3 billion operating profit in 2025 was the second-largest figure after KRW 27.1 billion in 2008.

Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders went from KRW 11.3 billion in 2022 and KRW 15.5 billion in 2023 to KRW 11.5 billion in 2024 (down 25.8%), then to KRW 20.7 billion in 2025 (up 80.3%).

Operating cash flow went from KRW 15.7 billion in 2022 and KRW 10.8 billion in 2023 to negative KRW 2.71 billion in 2024, then turned to KRW 36.3 billion in 2025.

The debt ratio rose to 24.6% at the end of 2025 from 14.2% in 2024, and total equity of KRW 83 billion included KRW 81.9 billion attributable to controlling shareholders. The payout ratio for the 2025 fiscal year was disclosed at 130.9%, and the dividend amount exceeded net profit.

On a quarterly basis, sales fell from KRW 30.8 billion in Q3 2025 to KRW 20.7 billion in Q4 2025 (down 32.7% quarter on quarter), rebounded to KRW 25.9 billion in Q1 2026 (up 25.2%), and then declined to KRW 24 billion in Q2 (down 7.6%) and KRW 23.4 billion in Q3 (down 2.3% quarter on quarter and down 23.9% year on year).

Operating profit was KRW 6.2 billion in Q3 2025, KRW 4.05 billion in Q4 2025 (down 34.7%), KRW 8.04 billion in Q1 2026 (up 98.8%), KRW 4.2 billion in Q2 (down 47.8%) and KRW 5.94 billion in Q3 (up 41.4% quarter on quarter, down 4.3% year on year).

Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders moved from KRW 7.8 billion in Q3 2025 to KRW 4.46 billion in Q4, KRW 4.92 billion in Q1 2026 and KRW 5.22 billion in Q2, before a net loss attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 235 million in Q3 2026 turned the result to a loss.

The sum of net profit attributable to controlling shareholders over the last four quarters is KRW 14.4 billion. The item-level cause of the swing to a controlling-shareholder net loss despite positive operating profit should be checked in the income statement notes of the quarterly report.

05

Industry analysis

Demand for port crane controls depends on trade volumes and port automation investment. According to an Etoday report, Korea's annual exports exceeded USD 700 billion for the first time in 2025, and that trend was tied to rising demand for port systems.

The same report noted that shipyard expansion and large vessel construction steadily generate crane demand. A shipbuilding order boom was also cited as a basis for continued sales growth.

The company says that port automation and the upgrading of large cranes are structurally expanding demand for electrical and electronic control systems, but that is management's view and should be checked against order flow data.

The company was reported to have kept average utilization at 100% through the third quarter of 2025.

In competition, Seoho Electric works alongside crane makers such as Doosan Heavy Industries and Hyundai Samho Heavy Industries, and China's ZPMC has also been named as a partner, so relationships with crane makers appear to shape how it wins projects.

Compared with other listed electrical equipment companies, its focus on port and shipyard crane control systems is the distinguishing feature. Sector-wide cycles, however, depend on shipbuilding orders and port investment plans, so the order flow of its customer industries needs to be checked alongside its own results.

Outlook

The most recent order in the sources is a single-sale supply contract disclosed on August 28, 2026, covering a KRW 13.8 billion crane control system for NANTONG HUADONG HEAVY MACHINERY CO., LTD.

The contract can be read as backlog that could feed revenue after Q3 2026, but its revenue recognition timing has not been disclosed. In a value-up plan disclosed on March 27, 2026, the company said it would pursue profit-focused management, sustainable dividends and new market development.

The plan also covers product competitiveness, production efficiency and stronger R&D. A treasury share disposal was described as securing funds to buy imported raw materials needed for higher sales, along with operating funds.

Press coverage reports that the Busan New Port Western Container Terminal Phase 2-6 project is under way. Because orders are made to order, quarterly sales could move sharply, so whether sales resume growth from Q4 2026 is a question to watch. Which new markets the company will enter has not been specified.

The 2026 annual report is the next regular filing, and changes in contract backlog and operating margin there are the next checkpoints.

Bear factors

Sales declined quarter on quarter in both Q2 and Q3 2026, marking two straight quarterly declines. Q3 2026 sales fell 23.9% year on year. The annual revenue recovery in 2025 has not carried into the quarterly trend.

Swing to a controlling-shareholder loss in Q3

Equity decline and accounting questions

10

Risk factors

Earnings volatility from order timing

Company commentary notes that port crane control systems are made to order, so results shift with order timing and project progress. When the 2024 operating margin fell to 3.5%, the company cited lower orders as the main cause. High reliance on large single contracts can widen quarterly variation.

Contract assets and accounting judgment

The recoverability of contract assets remains a key audit matter. The reasons for the decline in total equity at the end of 2025 should be checked together with the statement of changes in equity in the regular filing. Depending on contract progress, the timing of revenue and contract asset recognition can change.

Customer cycles and dividend burden

Key customers are tied to capital spending cycles in steel, shipbuilding and power generation, so slower investment there could affect orders. The 130.9% payout ratio for the 2025 fiscal year exceeds net profit, so a decline in earnings would raise the burden of maintaining shareholder returns.

Overall view

Seoho Electric supplies control systems for port and shipyard cranes, and its 2025 results rebounded sharply to KRW 116.6 billion in revenue and KRW 25.3 billion in operating profit. The operating margin rose to 21.7% in 2025 from 3.5% in 2024.

In 2026, however, quarterly sales peaked at KRW 25.9 billion in Q1 and then fell for two straight quarters, and Q3 brought a net loss attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 235 million.

Positive factors include port automation and shipbuilding demand, the KRW 13.8 billion contract disclosed in August 2026, and the shareholder return plan.

Negative factors include quarter-to-quarter swings from made-to-order projects, the decline in total equity at the end of 2025, and audit questions on the recoverability of contract assets.

Valuation should be read together with the KRW 14.4 billion four-quarter sum of net profit attributable to controlling shareholders and the 130.9% payout ratio for the 2025 fiscal year.
