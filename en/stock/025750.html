02

Business structure

Hansol HomeDeco, founded in 1991, makes interior building materials, led by MDF (medium-density fiberboard), flooring and wall finishing products. A business-mix summary on a corporate information site puts wood products at 99.9% of the business.

In October 2022 the company spun off its total-interior business into a separate unit, which completed liquidation in June 2025. After that exit, sales rely on wood products and on construction and remodeling demand.

Storyboard, a high-margin product line, recorded its highest monthly sales volume on record in March 2026, according to the company. Stronger remodeling demand raised distributor orders, and the showroom opened in May 2025 along with YouTube and influencer marketing supported brand awareness, the company said.

In construction, a bathroom dry-wall waterproofing system co-developed with DL E&C received construction new technology certification and has been delivered to about 33,000 households, with application expanding to top-tier builders and LH. Overseas, the consolidated subsidiaries are Hansol NewZealand Ltd.

(forestry) and Hansol Homedeco Vietnam Co., Ltd. (flooring manufacturing). The company says its cogeneration business provides stable energy supply and cost savings. In competition, Dongwha Enterprise is mentioned as a comparison in laminate flooring and eco-friendly boards. Sales are exposed to construction activity and remodeling demand at the same time.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual sales rose from KRW 283.4 billion in 2022 to KRW 300 billion in 2023 (up 5.9% year on year) and KRW 327.3 billion in 2024 (up 9.1%), then fell to KRW 279.8 billion in 2025 (down 14.5%).

Operating profit moved from KRW 2.71 billion in 2022 to an operating loss of KRW 2.11 billion in 2023, a swing back to profit at KRW 11.4 billion in 2024, and KRW 2.33 billion in 2025 (down 79.6%). Operating margin was 1.0% in 2022, -0.7% in 2023, 3.5% in 2024 and 0.8% in 2025.

Net loss attributable to controlling shareholders widened from KRW 4.87 billion in 2022 to KRW 14.2 billion in 2023, KRW 16.9 billion in 2024 and KRW 22.3 billion in 2025, a four-year run of losses.

In 2024 a KRW 11.4 billion operating profit still came with a KRW 16.9 billion controlling-shareholder net loss, which points to a large burden below the operating line. An aggregated dataset shows interest expense alone at KRW 4.496 billion in Q4 2025.

Quarterly results ran as follows: Q3 2025 sales of KRW 72 billion with an operating loss of KRW 553 million; Q4 2025 sales of KRW 64.9 billion with operating profit of KRW 108 million; Q1 2026 sales of KRW 69.1 billion with operating profit of KRW 2.71 billion (up 2,414.9% quarter on quarter); Q2 2026 sales of KRW 70.4 billion with operating profit of KRW 3.15 billion; and Q3 2026 sales of KRW 67.3 billion (down 6.5% year on year) with an operating loss of KRW 1.47 billion.

In Q4 2025 operating profit of KRW 108 million sat beside a controlling-shareholder net loss of KRW 23.4 billion. In an aggregated dataset, pre-tax results including special items (-KRW 23.067 billion) and excluding them (-KRW 1.646 billion) differ by more than KRW 20 billion, suggesting one-off items widened the loss.

The Q1 2026 improvement, as the company described it, came from high-margin Storyboard sales, remodeling demand, stable cogeneration income and cost savings. Q1 sales were down 7.0% year on year, so the gain came from margins rather than volume.

In the aggregated data, cost of sales fell from about 88% of sales in Q3 2025 to about 82% in Q2 2026.

Q3 2026 turned to an operating loss, and the controlling-shareholder net loss of KRW 10.8 billion was much larger than the operating loss, likely reflecting non-operating items whose details should be checked in the annual report.

Q2 2026 controlling-shareholder net income was KRW 9.01 million, down 99.7% from the prior quarter. The four quarters from Q4 2025 to Q3 2026 total a controlling-shareholder net loss of KRW 30.6 billion.

At the end of 2025 total liabilities were KRW 128.3 billion and total equity KRW 99.6 billion, and operating cash flow fell from KRW 26.1 billion in 2024 to KRW 12.3 billion in 2025 but stayed positive.

05

Industry analysis

Demand for wood-based materials runs along two lines: construction activity and remodeling. Press coverage tied the weak 2025 results to a deterioration in construction activity. The cumulative 2025 Q3 explanation pointed to lower domestic MDF prices and weak construction as reasons wood-products demand fell.

The Q1 2026 explanation again cited lower domestic MDF prices and a slowdown in construction as drags on sales. On the other side, the company said remodeling demand, centered on existing homes, has been rising.

On inputs, a summary on a financial data site describes falling log prices tied to slower growth in China, which led the company to halt logging and consider a shift toward higher-value businesses such as carbon credits.

Press coverage says the company is widening its eco-friendly lineup across MDF, flooring and wall finishes. In competition, Dongwha Enterprise is mentioned as a comparison in laminate flooring and eco-friendly boards.

Distribution runs through distributor orders and deliveries to builders, so it matters which channel drives the pace of recovery. The position in the cycle is hard to pin down: operating profit improved from Q1 2026 after the 2025 decline, but Q3 2026 sales fell again year on year.

Outlook

In its Q1 2026 results announcement, the company said it would strengthen the base for sustainable growth through expanded higher-value products, margin improvement and a stable balance sheet.

The bathroom dry-wall waterproofing system that received construction new technology certification has been delivered to about 33,000 households, and its application is expanding to top-tier builders and LH.

The company said Storyboard recorded its highest monthly sales on record in March 2026, so whether high-margin sales hold quarter after quarter is the next point to watch.

On shareholder returns, the company said it planned to present a medium-term roadmap at its regular shareholders' meeting in March 2026, covering higher dividends, a review of share buybacks and cancellations, and stronger investor relations. In April 2026 the company consolidated its shares on a five-to-one basis.

On the balance sheet, the company said it is reducing borrowings and current liabilities while bringing down its debt ratio. Sustaining the operating margin recovery depends on keeping the share of high-margin products such as Storyboard.

At the same time, domestic MDF prices and construction activity remain the variables that determine how fast sales recover. Confirmed figures will be available in the 2026 annual report.

08

Bull factors

Operating profit recovery led by high-margin products

Operating profit was KRW 2.71 billion in Q1 2026 and KRW 3.15 billion in Q2, two consecutive profitable quarters. The company cited growth in high-margin Storyboard sales as the main driver. Whether the operating margin can again exceed the 3.5% level of 2024 is the next point to check.

Remodeling demand and certified new product

The company said expanding remodeling demand increased distributor orders. The bathroom dry-wall waterproofing system has been delivered to about 33,000 households after receiving construction new technology certification, and its use is expanding to top-tier builders and LH.

The remodeling channel, outside new construction, could partly cushion sales swings, which is the basis of this bull case.

Cash generation and lower debt ratio

Operating cash flow stayed positive at KRW 12.3 billion in 2025. The debt-to-equity ratio fell from 134.5% in 2024 to 128.8% in 2025, and the company said it is reducing borrowings and current liabilities.

Operating cash flow was positive in every year from 2022 to 2025, a sign that cash generation continued despite the accounting losses.

09

Bear factors

Four consecutive years of controlling-shareholder net losses

Net losses attributable to controlling shareholders ran for four consecutive years from 2022 to 2025, and the 2025 loss of KRW 22.3 billion was larger than the KRW 14.2 billion of 2023. The four quarters from Q4 2025 to Q3 2026 total KRW 30.6 billion in net losses. The fact that net losses persisted even in years with operating profit is the core bear point.

Operating loss returns in Q3 2026

Q3 2026 sales were KRW 67.3 billion, down 6.5% year on year, and the company swung to an operating loss of KRW 1.47 billion. The controlling-shareholder net loss of KRW 10.8 billion was much larger than the operating loss. The failure of the Q1 and Q2 operating profits to carry into Q3 is the central bear argument.

Lower MDF prices and slower construction

Sales fell 14.5% to KRW 279.8 billion in 2025, and the cumulative 2025 Q3 explanation cited lower domestic MDF prices and weak construction as drags on demand. The same factors were cited again for the sales decline in Q1 2026. Halting logging after log prices fell highlights the uncertainty in a raw-material-based business.

10

Risk factors

Construction activity and MDF prices

Domestic MDF prices and construction activity directly move the company's sales. The sales declines in 2025 and in Q1 2026 were explained by the same factors. If the construction slowdown lasts, growth in remodeling demand alone may not fill the gap in sales.

Raw materials and forestry assets

Falling log prices tied to slower growth in China led the company to halt logging. Sourcing and the value of forestry assets are exposed to price swings. How much the shift toward higher-value businesses such as carbon credits will contribute to actual earnings remains to be confirmed.

Balance-sheet burden and earnings volatility

Total liabilities at the end of 2025 were KRW 128.3 billion, larger than total equity of KRW 99.6 billion. An aggregated dataset shows a sharp rise in the pre-tax loss in Q4 2025 driven by special items.

The company has also noted that provisional results can differ from final figures, so quarterly numbers should be rechecked in the annual report.

Overall view

Hansol HomeDeco's reported results show improvement at the operating level alongside losses at the net level. Net losses attributable to controlling shareholders ran for four consecutive years from 2022 to 2025, and 2025 sales fell 14.5% to KRW 279.8 billion.

Operating profit was positive in Q1 and Q2 2026, and for Q1 the company cited high-margin Storyboard sales, remodeling demand and cost savings from cogeneration. Q3 2026 brought an operating loss of KRW 1.47 billion and a controlling-shareholder net loss of KRW 10.8 billion.

The last four quarters total KRW 30.6 billion in controlling-shareholder net losses, and total liabilities exceed total equity.

Bull factors include remodeling demand, the certified building product and positive operating cash flow, while bear factors include domestic MDF prices, weak construction activity and the Q3 reversal.

Valuation is described by relative positioning against book value, since no earnings-based multiple can be calculated, and that reading also depends on whether the earnings recovery continues.

The next checkpoints are the Q4 2026 provisional results around January 2027 and the 2026 annual report due by the end of March 2027.
