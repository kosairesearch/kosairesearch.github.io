02

Business structure

Woori Financial Group holds Woori Bank as its core subsidiary and combines card, capital, securities, asset management and life insurance affiliates into a integrated financial group.

Non-bank businesses accounted for 9.5% of group profit at the end of 2025, and the company aims to raise that share to around 20% in 2026.

Cumulative net income for January to September 2026 was KRW 2.40 trillion at Woori Bank, KRW 121.6 billion at Tongyang Life Insurance, KRW 133.7 billion at Woori Card, KRW 114.6 billion at Woori Financial Capital, KRW 28.2 billion at Woori Investment & Securities and KRW 25.4 billion at Woori Asset Management.

Loan growth at the bank is led by corporate lending, and Woori Bank's won-denominated loans reached KRW 313 trillion at end-September 2026, up 4.1% from end-2025.

The company has set a medium-term goal of shifting its loan mix from a 50:50 split of corporate and household lending at end-2025 to 60% corporate and 40% household.

Investment banking fees across the group more than doubled year on year in the January to September 2026 period, driven by collaboration between the bank and the securities arm. The company credits the insurance acquisition for lifting non-interest income.

Industry commentary suggests competition among financial holding companies is shifting from scale of profit toward capital capacity and the durability of shareholder returns.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual operating profit under the DART annual reports was KRW 4.43 trillion in 2022, fell to KRW 3.5 trillion in 2023 (down 21.0% year on year), recovered to KRW 4.26 trillion in 2024 (up 21.6%) and was KRW 3.67 trillion in 2025 (down 13.6%).

Profit attributable to owners bottomed at KRW 2.51 trillion in 2023 (down 20.2%), then rose 23.1% to KRW 3.09 trillion in 2024 and 1.2% to KRW 3.12 trillion in 2025, a two-year run of gains. Both operating profit and attributable profit were largest in 2022 within the 2022 to 2025 window.

Net income including non-controlling interests, which differs from attributable profit, was KRW 3.23 trillion in 2025. The 2025 result included one-off gains such as the bargain purchase gain from the insurance acquisition, so year-on-year comparisons need that adjustment.

The debt-to-equity ratio fell from 1,419.2% in 2022 to 1,364.7% in 2024, then rose to 1,488.7% in 2025, when total equity was KRW 37.9 trillion, of which KRW 36 trillion belonged to owners of the parent, against total liabilities of KRW 563.6 trillion.

Operating cash flow swung from KRW 18.6 trillion in 2022 to KRW 1.62 trillion in 2023, -KRW 6.13 trillion in 2024 and KRW 13.6 trillion in 2025. The insurance revenue line was KRW 1.01 trillion in 2025, with no data disclosed for 2022 to 2024, and it covers insurance subsidiaries only rather than group revenue.

Quarterly operating profit bottomed at KRW 709.1 billion in Q4 2025 and then rose to KRW 808.2 billion, KRW 1.32 trillion and KRW 1.42 trillion across the next three quarters.

Attributable profit fell to KRW 328.4 billion in Q4 2025 from KRW 1.24 trillion in Q3 2025, then rose to KRW 604.3 billion, KRW 1 trillion and KRW 1.05 trillion over 2026, and the last four quarters total KRW 2.99 trillion.

In Q3 2026 operating profit rose 43.6% from a year earlier while attributable profit fell 15.7%, so the two measures moved in opposite directions.

Media attributed the decline to a base effect from the insurance acquisition, while the company said cumulative net income for January to September 2026 rose 18.7% excluding one-off items.

The CET1 ratio rose from a preliminary 12.9% at end-2025 to a preliminary 13.8% at end-September 2026, and the cumulative credit cost ratio for January to September 2026 fell 0.10 percentage points to 0.42%.

05

Industry analysis

Bank earnings are moving on two fronts, interest margins and loan growth. A securities-firm analysis of the first half of 2026 described the net interest margin as rising, while a summary of the Q3 2026 earnings call recorded a decline.

Higher long-term rates reduced securities-related gains, partly offsetting the Q3 rise in non-interest income. Lending is growing led by corporate loans, and Woori Bank's won-denominated loans stood at about KRW 313 trillion at end-September 2026.

Media analysis says competition among holding companies is shifting from the size of profit toward capital capacity and the durability of shareholder returns.

Woori Financial Group says it is currently the only domestic bank holding company paying tax-exempt dividends, and commentary notes that its capital position, previously weaker than some rival groups, is improving. The lower credit cost ratio is a supportive factor for asset quality.

The CET1 ratio of a preliminary 13.8% at end-September 2026 is one the company has highlighted as a capital buffer. Numerical comparisons with large peers such as KB Financial Group and Shinhan Financial Group should be checked against each company's periodic reports on the same basis.

06

Outlook

In its 2026 corporate value-up plan, the company set goals of reaching a CET1 ratio of 13% or higher ahead of schedule, lifting the non-bank share of earnings to 20% and raising annual dividends per share by 10% or more.

Buybacks and cancellations were KRW 200 billion in the first half, with another KRW 150 billion planned for the second half, bringing the annual total to KRW 350 billion.

A KRW 150 billion treasury share trust contract signed on July 24, 2026 runs until January 22, 2027, and the number of shares actually acquired may vary with the share price.

At the Q3 2026 earnings call, CFO Kwak Sung-min said personally that the 2027 nominal total shareholder return should be at least around 50%, and the company is reviewing that level.

The company planned loan growth of about 5% in won-denominated lending for 2026 and aims to deploy at least KRW 17.5 trillion, including KRW 3.6 trillion for investments such as participation in the National Growth Fund and KRW 13.9 trillion in loans.

On capital restructuring, a press report from April 2026 described a KRW 1 trillion additional capital injection into the securities arm and a share exchange to make Tongyang Life Insurance a wholly owned subsidiary.

Because capital use is rising, maintaining a CET1 ratio of 13.2% or higher is the precondition for expanding shareholder returns.

The next checkpoints are whether the CET1 ratio stays at target at end-2026, the capital and cash flow lines in the 2026 annual report, and whether the 2027 return plan is formally announced.

Bull factors

Wider Non-Bank Earnings Contribution

Cumulative non-interest income for January to September 2026 was KRW 1.64 trillion, up 13.9% from a year earlier. The company cites the insurance acquisition and intra-group synergies as drivers.

If the target to lift the non-bank earnings share from 9.5% in 2025 to 20% is reached, the earnings mix would spread beyond banking.

Higher Capital Ratio and Expanded Returns

The CET1 ratio rose from 12.9% at end-2025 to a preliminary 13.8% at end-September 2026. The company completed KRW 200 billion of buybacks and cancellations in the first half and plans another KRW 150 billion in the second half, which media described as the largest annual amount since the group was formed. Funds available for tax-exempt dividends were reported at about KRW 6.3 trillion.

Earnings Recovery and Stable Credit Costs

Operating profit has risen for three straight quarters since the Q4 2025 low, reaching KRW 1.42 trillion in Q3 2026. Cumulative operating profit for January to September 2026 was KRW 3.54 trillion, up 19.5% from a year earlier. The credit cost ratio fell to 0.42%, consistent with improving asset quality.

Bear factors

Margin Pressure and Lower Securities Gains

A summary of the Q3 2026 earnings call recorded a decline in the net interest margin. Higher long-term rates reduced securities-related gains and partly offset growth in non-interest income. A change in market rate direction could slow the growth in interest income, which is the main downside factor.

Earnings Volatility After a Base Effect

Attributable profit in Q3 2026 rose from the prior quarter but fell from a year earlier. Media attributed the gap to a base effect from one-off gains in the comparison period, including the bargain purchase gain tied to the insurance acquisition. Attributable profit fell to KRW 328.4 billion in Q4 2025, so quarterly earnings have shown wide swings.

Rising Capital Use and Cash Flow Swings

An April 2026 press report described a KRW 1 trillion additional capital injection into the securities arm and a share exchange to bring Tongyang Life Insurance fully under the group. Capital directed to these steps could reduce room for buybacks and dividends.

Operating cash flow was -KRW 6.13 trillion in 2024, and the debt-to-equity ratio rose to 1,488.7% in 2025 from 2024.

10

Risk factors

Rates and Market-Linked Income

Higher long-term rates have reduced securities-related gains. If the margin keeps narrowing over several quarters, interest income growth could slow. With the rate path uncertain, quarterly earnings may remain volatile.

Loan Mix Shift and Asset Quality

The company aims to raise corporate lending toward 60% of the loan book over the medium term. Growth concentrated in corporate loans could raise credit costs if business conditions weaken. The current credit cost ratio of 0.42% is low, but asset quality indicators need continued monitoring during the mix shift.

Regulation, Tax and Operational Risk

The tax-exempt dividend depends on a tax framework and on meeting the high-dividend company criteria. Charges tied to LTV rules were cited as a burden in the past. One analysis flagged cyber security as a challenge as important as earnings.

12

Overall view

Woori Financial Group's attributable profit for Q3 2026 was KRW 1.05 trillion, the second straight quarter above KRW 1 trillion, and operating profit has risen for three consecutive quarters since the Q4 2025 low.

The company and media point to a larger contribution from non-bank affiliates and stable credit costs as the drivers. On the other side, attributable profit fell 15.7% from a year earlier in Q3, and a narrower net interest margin and lower securities-related gains from higher long-term rates remain headwinds.

The CET1 ratio rose to a preliminary 13.8% at end-September 2026, and buybacks of KRW 350 billion plus tax-exempt dividends form the core of shareholder returns.

However, operating cash flow was -KRW 6.13 trillion in 2024 and the debt-to-equity ratio rose to 1,488.7% in 2025, so year-to-year volatility remains a point to track.

The securities capital injection and insurance subsidiary restructuring increase capital use, making the balance between faster shareholder returns and keeping the CET1 ratio at 13.2% or higher the central point to watch.

The next checkpoints are the year-end 2026 capital ratio, the January 22, 2027 expiry of the treasury share trust, and the March 31, 2027 filing deadline for the 2026 annual report.
