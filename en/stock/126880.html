02

Business structure

JNK Global was spun off in 1998 from the heater division of Daelim Engineering and specializes in industrial fired heaters.

Its core work covers process design, fabrication, installation and commissioning of fired heaters used in refinery and petrochemical plants, and the company describes itself as the only dedicated industrial fired heater specialist in Korea.

Based on Q2 2026 revenue, heaters accounted for 97% and hydrogen for 3% according to a corporate information aggregator. The hydrogen business traces back to hydrogen production technology begun in 2013 as a national project, centered on reformers for hydrogen refueling stations.

In 2022 the company won a 3,000 kg-per-day hydrogen extraction EPC project from SK Materials, extending its reach into private industrial customers. In March 2024 the company renamed itself from JNK Heaters to JNK Global, citing a strategy to strengthen new businesses centered on hydrogen infrastructure.

Its customers are mainly overseas refinery and petrochemical companies, including Saudi Aramco and the Kuwait National Petroleum Company (KNPC). According to the company, it has delivered more than 350 fired heaters across the Middle East, Africa, India, North America, South America and Southeast Asia.

Revenue swings from quarter to quarter depending on the timing and size of large overseas projects. On November 17, 2026 the company said it would strengthen its growth base through large BPCL and Dangote orders and an expanded hydrogen business.

The hydrogen segment remains small, but the company is expanding it with a target of KRW 100 billion in hydrogen revenue.

04

Earnings analysis

On the disclosed annual basis, revenue went from KRW 197.1 billion in 2022 to KRW 166.7 billion in 2023 (down 15.4%) and KRW 138.6 billion in 2024 (down 16.8%), then recovered to KRW 147.3 billion in 2025 (up 6.2%). Across 2022 to 2025, 2022 was the largest year and 2024 the smallest.

Operating profit went from KRW 6.93 billion in 2022 to KRW 4.14 billion in 2023 (down 40.3%), KRW 3.64 billion in 2024 (down 12.0%) and KRW 3.15 billion in 2025 (down 13.4%), declining for three consecutive years from 2023 through 2025. The operating margin was 3.5% in 2022, 2.5% in 2023, 2.6% in 2024 and 2.1% in 2025.

Revenue grew in 2025 but operating profit fell again, so the top-line rebound did not carry through to profit. Net income attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 7.39 billion in 2022, KRW 3.78 billion in 2023, KRW 91.5 billion in 2024 and KRW 2.92 billion in 2025, with 2024 the largest and 2025 the smallest.

The 2024 figure is far above that year's operating profit of KRW 3.64 billion, so its drivers need to be read separately from core operations. Net income including non-controlling interests was reported at the same values in all four years.

The debt ratio peaked at 156.5% in 2023 and fell to 76.2% in 2024 and 58.2% in 2025. Operating cash flow was -KRW 6.25 billion in 2022, KRW 55 billion in 2023, KRW 9.6 billion in 2024 and -KRW 15.3 billion in 2025.

Quarterly results swung from a Q3 2025 revenue of KRW 29.3 billion and operating loss of KRW 678 million to a Q4 2025 revenue of KRW 52 billion and operating profit of KRW 3.33 billion. Q1 2026 revenue was flat at KRW 52 billion, while operating profit fell 82.6% quarter on quarter to KRW 579 million.

Q2 2026 revenue rose to KRW 71.4 billion but posted an operating loss of KRW 223 million and an attributable net loss of KRW 677 million.

Q3 2026 revenue reached KRW 92.9 billion (up 30.0% quarter on quarter and 217.3% year on year), with operating profit of KRW 12.3 billion and attributable net income of KRW 7.31 billion, up 569.6% year on year. According to the company's November 17, 2026 announcement, the Q3 operating margin was 13.2%. Attributable net income over the last four quarters (Q4 2025 to Q3 2026) totaled KRW 10.6 billion.

05

Industry analysis

Demand for industrial fired heaters follows capital spending cycles at refinery and petrochemical plants.

Because the company's stated delivery regions are mainly overseas, including the Middle East, India, North America, South America and Southeast Asia, its results are more sensitive to the timing of overseas orders than to domestic investment.

The company describes itself as the only dedicated industrial fired heater specialist in Korea and highlights its ability to handle work from design through commissioning. Its competitive set centers on developed-country peers, and the company says it aims to rank among the global top tier in the field.

The hydrogen infrastructure business is shaped by domestic policy. A March 2024 report said the company was pursuing on-site hydrogen refueling stations based on natural gas and LPG reforming hydrogen production units, in line with fine dust and renewable energy policy.

The same report said the company had secured hydrogen production technology through a partnership with a Canadian company that has biogas purification technology. The refueling station business combines on-site production with off-site delivery of hydrogen produced elsewhere.

The position in the industry cycle is hard to pin down, but annual revenue bottomed in 2024 and rose in 2025, and quarterly revenue has risen since Q4 2025. The company attributed the Q3 2026 revenue increase to large overseas projects.

Within the mechanical and equipment sector, the company is a small-to-mid-sized player, and its order scale and financial capacity are smaller than those of large plant contractors.

Risk factors

Order concentration and timing

Revenue depends on large overseas refinery and petrochemical projects, so order timing and size drive quarterly results. Flat Q1 2026 revenue at KRW 52 billion illustrates the point. A delay in a major project could leave a gap in quarterly results.

Cost and execution risk

Overseas EPC projects can carry added costs from exchange rate and raw material swings and from execution delays. The operating margin's fall to 2.1% in 2025 may reflect cost structure. Cost ratios, more than order size, determine how much profit actually stays with the company.

Small-cap liquidity and price volatility

As a small-cap KOSDAQ mechanical and equipment stock, trading volume and price swings can be larger than for large caps. Quarterly earnings alternating between profit and loss also make the share price direction harder to read. Price moves can also be shaped by flows unrelated to earnings.
