Novarex: Q3 2026 Swings to Net Loss After Record Q1

Novarex's earnings recovered in 2025, but sales and operating profit have declined for two consecutive quarters since the first quarter of 2026, and the third quarter of 2026 turned to a net loss attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 1.9 billion. The completion of the Osong No. 2 plant is the next checkpoint.

02

Business structure

Novarex is a health functional food ODM and OEM manufacturer founded in November 2008 and listed on KOSDAQ in November 2018. The company positions its holding of one of the largest sets of individually recognized raw materials in Korea as the foundation of its competitiveness.

In formulation technology it highlights sustained-release tablet technology and its 'Switchu' formulation technology.

Sales are split between domestic and overseas, and according to company figures released for the first quarter of 2026, domestic sales were KRW 77.3 billion, up 30.4% year on year, while overseas sales were KRW 43.5 billion, up 38.5%.

Domestically, DS Investment attributed the rise in customer orders to a broader health functional food lineup at new distribution channels such as Olive Young and convenience stores. Overseas, growth factors include higher sales of Vitapack and fish oil products in China and expanding exports to Asia.

The company said it is developing new customers in China while widening cooperation in the United States, Europe, and the Middle East and halal markets. On competition, the company points to its speed in proposing formulations and blends and its quality control capabilities.

On the production side, it is pairing line efficiency investment at its existing No. 1 plant with the construction of a new Osong No. 2 plant, which has already broken ground. Quarterly sales are influenced by the structure of ODM work, where volumes move with customer orders.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual sales moved from KRW 281.7 billion in 2022 to KRW 302.2 billion in 2023 (up 7.3%) and KRW 297.7 billion in 2024 (down 1.5%), before reaching KRW 404.3 billion in 2025 (up 35.8%), the largest year in the 2022 to 2025 period.

Operating profit was KRW 25.2 billion in 2022, KRW 25.5 billion in 2023 (up 1.2%), KRW 23.2 billion in 2024 (down 9.1%), and KRW 43.1 billion in 2025 (up 85.6%), with 2024 the smallest year.

Operating margin fell from 8.9% in 2022 to 7.8% in 2024 before rising to 10.6% in 2025, so the profit recovery outpaced sales growth. Net income attributable to controlling shareholders rose for three consecutive years from 2023 through 2025, reaching KRW 35.4 billion in 2025, up 53.9%.

Net income including non-controlling interests was KRW 36 billion in 2025, close to the controlling-shareholder figure. Operating cash flow rose from KRW 13.3 billion in 2024 to KRW 16.7 billion in 2025 but remained below KRW 25.3 billion in 2023 and below 2025 net income.

The debt-to-equity ratio climbed from 31.5% in 2024 to 41.2% in 2025, with total liabilities of KRW 104.9 billion and total equity of KRW 254.6 billion at year-end 2025.

Quarterly sales went from KRW 113.8 billion in the third quarter of 2025 to KRW 100.7 billion in the fourth quarter (down 11.6% quarter on quarter), then peaked at KRW 120.8 billion in the first quarter of 2026 (up 20.0%).

Sales then fell to KRW 101.8 billion in the second quarter (down 15.7%) and KRW 92.5 billion in the third quarter (down 9.1% quarter on quarter and down 18.7% year on year), marking two consecutive quarterly declines.

Operating profit went from KRW 14.1 billion in the first quarter of 2026 to KRW 11 billion in the second quarter and KRW 6.55 billion in the third quarter (down 49.3% year on year).

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders moved from KRW 14.6 billion in the first quarter of 2026 to KRW 7.67 billion in the second quarter, then turned to a net loss of KRW 1.9 billion in the third quarter.

The company announced preliminary first-half 2026 sales of KRW 222.6 billion (up 17.3%) and operating profit of KRW 25.1 billion (up 29.2%) on August 14, 2026. Whether the third-quarter swing to a loss stemmed from one-off items or operating factors cannot be separated from the disclosed figures alone.

05

Industry analysis

Expectations for the health functional food market rest on rising elderly population and growing health awareness. On distribution, new channels such as convenience stores and Olive Young are widening health functional food lineups, which has increased customer orders for ODM suppliers.

On products, the company stresses that faster shifts in consumer trends have made the speed of formulation and blend development a competitive factor. In a May 2026 report, DS Investment said Novarex's plant utilization in the first quarter of 2026 was 113% and had been above 100% since the second half of last year.

Utilization above 100% points to a need for capacity expansion, which the company is addressing through the new Osong No. 2 plant alongside line efficiency work at the No. 1 plant. DS Investment noted that domestic sales of KRW 77.3 billion in the first quarter of 2026 were the highest quarterly level on record.

Overseas, DS Investment judged that the export slowdown it had worried about was a matter of timing, but sales fell in the second and third quarters, so the durability of overseas momentum is a point to confirm in upcoming quarterly results.

Relative positioning against peers can be described around the company's holding of individually recognized raw materials and its formulation technology.

Within the food sector, DS Investment said it lowered its target multiple from 12 times to 10 times, the same as peers, to reflect a decline in the sector's relative attractiveness. New customer development in China and cooperation in the Middle East and halal markets could spread export exposure across more channels.

Outlook

On August 14, 2026, the company announced preliminary first-half results and said it will continue expanding production infrastructure, widening global customer access, and strengthening formulation technology in the second half.

In May 2026, the company said the Osong No. 2 plant had broken ground and that annual capacity would roughly double from about KRW 500 billion to KRW 1 trillion once it is completed in the second half of 2027.

In the same period, DS Investment projected 2026 sales of KRW 481.6 billion (up 19%), operating profit of KRW 54.2 billion (up 26%), and an operating margin of 11.3%.

Disclosed sales for the first three quarters of 2026 add up to KRW 315.1 billion and operating profit to KRW 31.65 billion, so reaching that projection would require fourth-quarter sales of KRW 166.5 billion and operating profit of KRW 22.55 billion.

The gap is tied to the fact that the projection was made in May 2026, so whether it has been revised is the next point to confirm. On shareholder returns, the company carried out a share cancellation at the end of March 2026 and said it would keep expanding shareholder-friendly policies.

On exports, the question is how far new customers in China and cooperation in the United States, Europe, and the Middle East and halal markets feed into sales after the second half of 2026.

On finances, the key checkpoint is how far the KRW 104.9 billion in total liabilities at year-end 2025 grows with capacity investment. Once the 2026 annual report discloses fourth-quarter results and full-year operating margin, the gap between the projection and actual results will be clearer.

Bear factors

Third-quarter operating profit of KRW 6.55 billion was down 49.3% year on year. Net income attributable to controlling shareholders swung to a net loss of KRW 1.9 billion.

Cash flow and debt burden

Operating cash flow in 2025 was KRW 16.7 billion, below net income of KRW 36 billion. The debt-to-equity ratio rose from 31.5% in 2024 to 41.2% in 2025, with total liabilities of KRW 104.9 billion. If capacity investment continues, the burden on this cash flow could become more visible.

Gap between projection and results

A large gap exists between the KRW 481.6 billion full-year 2026 sales projection DS Investment presented in May 2026 and the KRW 315.1 billion disclosed for the first three quarters combined. Because the projection dates from May 2026, it is not yet clear whether the subsequent quarterly trend has been reflected in it.

DS Investment's view that the export slowdown was a timing issue was tested by the sales decline in the third quarter.

Risk factors

Demand and order volatility

Because quarterly sales move with customer order volumes, a drop in orders as in the second and third quarters of 2026 can reduce profit quickly. Third-quarter operating profit fell 49.3% year on year.

Whether the order swings reflect channel restructuring or temporary adjustments cannot be separated from the disclosures alone.

Capex and completion timing

The company plans to complete the Osong No. 2 plant in the second half of 2027, while DS Investment projects capacity of KRW 800 billion to KRW 1 trillion by the end of 2027, so timing and scale differ. A delay would prolong pressure on a plant running at 113% utilization and could cost orders. During the investment period, the debt ratio and cash flow may come under strain.

Overseas exposure and regulation

Overseas sales of KRW 43.5 billion in the first quarter of 2026 accounted for roughly a third of total sales. Export flows can shift with overseas customer orders, country-specific rules, and currency movements. Because health functional food regulation differs by country, it affects the pace of entry into new markets.

Overall view

Novarex grew scale in 2025, with sales of KRW 404.3 billion and operating profit of KRW 43.1 billion, and its operating margin recovered to 10.6%.

However, sales peaked at KRW 120.8 billion in the first quarter of 2026 and then declined in the second and third quarters, and in the third quarter operating profit fell to KRW 6.55 billion and the company posted a net loss attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 1.9 billion.

Bullish factors include strong overseas growth, new customers in China and the Middle East, and a plan to double production capacity. Bearish factors include consecutive quarterly declines, a rising debt-to-equity ratio, and operating cash flow trailing net income.

The gap between DS Investment's May 2026 projection and the disclosed first-to-third-quarter totals leaves the projection versus actual results as an open issue. Valuation sits with price-to-book below 1 times, but the swing to a loss widens the range of interpretation for earnings-based multiples.

The next checkpoints are the 2026 annual report, the first-quarter 2027 results, and completion of the Osong No. 2 plant.
