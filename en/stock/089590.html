02

Business structure

Jejuair is a low-cost carrier in Korea, and passenger air transport is the core of its business. Passengers in the first quarter of 2026 totaled 3,311,358, up 24.2% year on year and the highest among domestic LCCs, with a load factor of 91.9% against a national carrier average of 88.8%, according to the company.

Japan routes account for the largest share at 45% of passenger revenue, and Japan route revenue rose 53% year on year in the same quarter, according to one brokerage analyst. Revenue from China and Greater China routes rose 76%, and domestic route revenue rose 43%.

Southeast Asia routes have been cut due to weak demand, while seats on Japan and China routes have been expanded. The fleet is being shifted from leased Boeing 737-800 aircraft to newer 737-8 aircraft owned by the company.

The company brought in two 737-8 aircraft in the first quarter of 2026 and plans to add five more by year-end to the ten it already holds. Hanwha Investment & Securities estimates that switching to the 737-8 cuts costs by about 14%.

Nine domestic LCCs compete in the market, and one analyst argued that the sector needs restructuring so that only firms able to survive independently remain.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual revenue rose from KRW 702.5 billion in 2022 to KRW 1.72 trillion in 2023, up 145.4% year on year, and reached KRW 1.94 trillion in 2024, the largest year in the 2022 to 2025 span. Revenue fell 18.4% to KRW 1.58 trillion in 2025.

Operating loss of KRW 177.5 billion in 2022 became operating profit of KRW 169.8 billion in 2023, which then fell to KRW 79.9 billion in 2024, down 52.9%, before turning into an operating loss of KRW 111.7 billion in 2025. Operating margin was -25.3% in 2022, 9.8% in 2023, 4.1% in 2024 and -7.1% in 2025.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 134.3 billion in 2023 and KRW 21.7 billion in 2024, down 83.8%, before a net loss of KRW 116.4 billion in 2025. Net income including non-controlling interests was disclosed at the same amounts for each of these years.

The company said 2025 fuel costs fell about 16% year on year, reflecting gains from newer, more fuel-efficient aircraft. Even so, operating cash flow in 2025 was KRW 6.1 billion, far below KRW 388.2 billion in 2023 and KRW 163.3 billion in 2024.

The debt ratio rose from 442.6% in 2022 to 754.4% in 2025, when total equity was KRW 275.2 billion and total liabilities were KRW 2.08 trillion. The quarterly path moved faster.

After an operating loss of KRW 55 billion in the third quarter of 2025, operating profit of KRW 17.8 billion came in the fourth quarter, and the first quarter of 2026 reached KRW 69 billion, up 287.4% from the prior quarter.

The second quarter of 2026 returned to an operating loss of KRW 45 billion, and the third quarter operating profit of KRW 55.9 billion marked a turn to profit from a year earlier. Third-quarter revenue of KRW 497.7 billion rose 28.2% year on year.

However, third-quarter net income attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 195.9 billion included a one-time gain from selling owned aircraft, and a lower won-dollar rate improved foreign currency translation gains.

One brokerage analyst also noted a one-off maintenance cost of about KRW 40 billion in the same quarter. For this reason, the KRW 149.8 billion total for the last four quarters should be read with these one-off items in mind.

05

Industry analysis

Demand differs by route. Hana Securities noted in a November 4, 2026 report that outbound demand is growing mainly toward China and Japan, while travel demand to Southeast Asia has fallen more than 10% year to date. One analysis said that cutting weak Southeast Asia routes at high oil prices helps reduce costs.

KB Securities estimated that international passenger unit revenue rose 38% year on year in the third quarter of 2026, but it remained below the level of the first quarter before the war began. On fuel, Hanwha Investment & Securities raised its 2027 jet fuel price assumption to an average of USD 131 per barrel.

A stabilizing won-dollar exchange rate improved foreign currency gains tied to aircraft lease payments and fuel settlements. On competition, Hanwha analyst Park Su-young said that route and schedule overlaps after a competitor's merger could ease some supply competition.

One analyst also argued that the nine domestic LCCs need restructuring to identify those able to survive on their own. In terms of the cycle, the fourth quarter of 2026 is a seasonally weak period, while third-quarter 2026 results beat market expectations by a wide margin. The durability of the sector recovery depends on both fares and fuel prices.

06

Outlook

The company said it will keep a stable profit trend in the fourth quarter. On the fleet, it plans to add five 737-8 aircraft by the end of 2026.

Hanwha Investment & Securities put its 2026 operating profit estimate at KRW 95 billion and its 2027 estimate at KRW 144 billion; these are broker estimates, not disclosed results. The same broker said its estimates are open to upside depending on jet fuel prices.

By contrast, KB Securities expects a fourth-quarter operating loss if international passenger fares fail to reflect higher fuel prices. The same report said the benefit from lower fuel prices in 2027 could be diluted.

Hanwha said the fourth quarter is seasonally weak and fuel prices are higher, so third-quarter-level profit is hard to expect, but a return to the operating losses of the past is limited.

Newer aircraft accounted for about one-third of the fleet at the end of the third quarter of 2026, and the share is set to rise to 50% by the end of 2027, according to the same broker.

The next checkpoints are fourth-quarter international fares, the 2027 jet fuel price, and whether the newer-aircraft share expands as planned.

08

Bull factors

Japan and China route demand and network flexibility

Hanwha Investment & Securities assessed that flexible capacity adjustments matched to strong demand on Japan and China routes would drive earnings improvement.

The first-quarter 2026 load factor of 91.9% exceeded the national carrier average of 88.8%, and one analyst estimated Japan route revenue rose 53% year on year in that quarter. Cutting Southeast Asia routes and allocating seats to higher-yield routes is cited as a factor behind the third-quarter turn to profit.

Cost savings from fleet modernization

One estimate puts cost savings from switching to the 737-8 at about 14%, covering lease, maintenance and fuel costs. The company reported that 2025 fuel costs fell about 16% year on year.

Newer owned aircraft made up one-third of the fleet at the end of the third quarter of 2026 and are set to reach 50% by the end of 2027.

Signs of balance sheet improvement

KB Securities viewed the reduction in fuel use and the fall in the debt ratio positively. The same firm estimated that third-quarter fuel consumption fell 13% year on year.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders over the last four quarters totals KRW 149.8 billion, contrasting with the 2025 full-year net loss of KRW 116.4 billion.

09

Bear factors

Possible return to a fourth-quarter loss

KB Securities said international passenger fares remain low and that the fourth-quarter operating result could swing back to a loss. Hanwha Investment & Securities also noted the fourth quarter is seasonally weak and fuel prices are rising, making third-quarter-level profit hard to expect. It also said a return to past-style operating losses looks limited.

One-off factors in third-quarter profit

Third-quarter net income attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 195.9 billion included a one-time gain from aircraft sales. The same quarter also carried a one-off maintenance cost of about KRW 40 billion.

The second quarter recorded a net loss attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 49.2 billion, underlining the large swings in quarterly profit.

Southeast Asia demand and slow fare recovery

Hana Securities pointed out that travel demand to Southeast Asia has fallen more than 10% since the start of the year. KB Securities analyzed that fares did not fully pass through fuel costs that surged after the second quarter.

International passenger fares rose year on year in the third quarter but remained below the first-quarter level.

10

Risk factors

Fuel prices and exchange rate

Jet fuel prices are a key variable for earnings, and Hanwha Investment & Securities assumed an average of USD 131 per barrel for 2027. The same broker said there is room to raise operating profit estimates if prices come in lower than expected.

If prices stay high, whether fares can pass through the cost becomes the central question. The won-dollar rate also affects foreign currency gains and losses tied to lease and fuel payments.

Balance sheet

Total liabilities at the end of 2025 were KRW 2.08 trillion against total equity of KRW 275.2 billion, with a debt ratio of 754.4%. Operating cash flow in 2025 was only KRW 6.1 billion.

Aircraft sales and lease returns have been cited as easing financial burdens, but sale gains are one-off in nature, so their sustainability must be checked separately.

Route concentration and industry restructuring

The larger the share of Japan and China routes, the more sensitive results become to demand shifts on those routes. With nine domestic LCCs and restructuring discussed, the competitive landscape could change depending on how the industry is reorganized.

On the other hand, route overlap adjustments after a competitor's merger are cited as a factor that could ease supply competition.

Overall view

Jejuair shifted from an annual loss in 2025 to an operating profit in the third quarter of 2026. Brokers and the company attribute the turn to demand on Japan and China routes, fuel and maintenance savings from fleet modernization, and higher passenger volumes.

However, third-quarter net income attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 195.9 billion included one-time aircraft sale gains, and the second quarter recorded a net loss of KRW 49.2 billion.

Some brokers expect the fourth quarter could return to a loss because of seasonal weakness and fuel costs, and their views diverge here. Target prices presented were KRW 9,000 by Hanwha Investment & Securities on November 10, 2026 and KRW 6,400 by KB Securities on November 13, 2026.

With the debt ratio at 754.4% at the end of 2025 and operating cash flow of KRW 6.1 billion, the durability of cash generation is an item to verify.

The next checkpoints are the 2026 annual report due by late March 2027, fourth-quarter international fares, and whether the additional 737-8 deliveries are completed by year-end.
