02

Business structure

Black YAK I&C is an industrial safety product maker established in 2013. It first ran an online shop for its parent group BLACKYAK, but handed that business over in 2018 and took on an industrial safety division, which turned it into a safety-equipment specialist.

Its core product is safety footwear, and its line now extends to safety apparel, safety gear and fire-protection protective equipment. The company runs two brands, BLACKYAK WORKWEAR and WORXONE.

Its main customers are industrial sites such as construction, aviation, maintenance and transportation, along with large facilities such as semiconductor fabs and data centers.

Sales run through roughly 100 dealers and, through a corporate sales team created in a reorganization reported in August 2026, through business-to-business (B2B) channels.

First-half 2026 safety-product sales on a standalone basis were KRW 21.1 billion, up 14.3% year on year, while consolidated revenue for the same period was KRW 41.5 billion. The gap reflects results from a subsidiary consolidated in 2025.

The company's first-half 2026 results briefing cited fab construction and the expansion of clean fire-suppression gas business as drivers. In competitive terms, the company's own 2023 estimates put market share at 13.5% for K2 Safety, 8.2% for Black YAK I&C and 3.6% for Jiben Safety.

04

Earnings analysis

On the basis of filed annual results, revenue rose from KRW 35.2 billion in 2023 to KRW 37.7 billion in 2024, up 7.1%, and then to KRW 57.3 billion in 2025, up 51.9%. Revenue increased for two consecutive years from 2024 through 2025, and 2025 was the largest year of the 2023-2025 period.

Operating profit went from KRW 8.06 billion in 2023 to KRW 8.3 billion in 2024, up 2.9%, and then to KRW 9.28 billion in 2025, up 11.8%, also rising for two consecutive years from 2024 through 2025.

However, the operating margin fell from 22.9% in 2023 to 22.0% in 2024 and 16.2% in 2025, meaning profit grew more slowly than revenue as the business expanded.

Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders went from KRW 6.21 billion in 2023 to KRW 6.98 billion in 2024, up 12.4%, then dropped 98.7% to KRW 92.18 million in 2025.

The company posted a net loss of KRW 5.64 billion in 2025, and because that figure includes non-controlling interests, it moved in a different direction from the profit attributable to controlling shareholders.

The 2025 disclosure on the large swing cited the first-time consolidation of a subsidiary, merger costs tied to the SPAC listing, and costs from acquiring the subsidiary. Operating cash flow fell from KRW 7.54 billion in 2023 and KRW 826 million in 2024 to -KRW 4.73 billion in 2025.

The debt-to-equity ratio rose from 83.6% in 2023 and 39.8% in 2024 to 152.2% in 2025, when total liabilities of KRW 58.4 billion exceeded total equity of KRW 38.4 billion.

On a quarterly basis, 4Q 2025 revenue was KRW 20.2 billion, up 27.2% from the prior quarter, and operating profit was KRW 2.8 billion, up 13.3%. 1Q 2026 revenue slipped 17.4% to KRW 16.7 billion and operating profit dipped 7.4% to KRW 2.59 billion, before 2Q 2026 revenue rose to KRW 24.9 billion and operating profit to KRW 5.87 billion.

The company linked the 2Q jump to fab-related safety products and fire-protection equipment, which generate sales in stages from construction start through completion. Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders for the four quarters from 4Q 2025 through 3Q 2026 totals KRW 15.7 billion.

The 4Q 2025 figure of KRW 8.16 billion exceeded that quarter's operating profit of KRW 2.8 billion by a wide margin, which suggests non-operating items were large in that period.

05

Industry analysis

Korea's industrial safety protection market, according to a size estimate in the company's merger securities filing, was KRW 856.7 billion in 2021 and was projected to grow at an average of 6.3% a year to KRW 1.2352 trillion in 2027.

Expansion of Korea's Serious Accidents Punishment Act to workplaces with fewer than 50 employees in 2024 raised protective-equipment demand among smaller sites, and the company has built a corporate sales team to pursue B2B business on that basis.

The main end-market driver is large industrial sites where semiconductor fab construction and data-center expansion overlap. A company official said the second half of the year was expected to outperform the first half as major domestic chipmakers' large fab investments and data-center expansion coincide.

Because fab-related sales are recognized in stages from construction start through completion, the duration of that demand is the key variable.

In competition, the company's own 2023 estimates placed market share at 13.5% for K2 Safety, 8.2% for Black YAK I&C and 3.6% for Jiben Safety, and reports described the market as lacking a clear leader. The company is therefore a second-tier player in a fragmented field.

Its standalone safety-product sales grew 14.3% in the first half of 2026, which suggests the general industrial customer base is holding up alongside the fab-related demand. Policy is a variable, since demand driven by the Serious Accidents Punishment Act could shift with changes in its scope or enforcement.

06

Outlook

In its first-half 2026 results briefing, the company said it expected second-half results to exceed the first half. The same briefing described a strategy of diversifying revenue by pursuing the B2B market through the corporate sales team.

Revenue in 3Q 2026 was KRW 17.9 billion, below KRW 24.9 billion in 2Q, and operating profit was KRW 2.83 billion, below KRW 5.87 billion in 2Q, so whether the second-half outlook translates into 4Q results is the first checkpoint.

On shareholder returns, the company has implemented a tiered dividend that favors general shareholders, as promised before its listing, and reports said it plans to raise the dividend scale and pay an interim dividend.

A company official said it would keep pursuing corporate value enhancement, including shareholder returns, on the back of earnings growth and a stronger balance sheet. Some reports said brokerage-industry observers expect this year's profit to rise on the subsidiary's contribution.

However, with the debt-to-equity ratio at 152.2% at year-end 2025, the borrowing structure and improvement in operating cash flow are the main variables for the outlook. The 2026 annual report, the next regular filing, will show full-year operating margin and operating cash flow.

Bull factors

Demand from chip fabs and data centers

Safety and fire-protection sales are recognized in stages from fab construction start through completion. Revenue in 2Q 2026 reached KRW 24.9 billion and operating profit KRW 5.87 billion, both up sharply from the prior quarter.

The company expected second-half results to beat the first half as chipmaker fab investment and data-center expansion coincide.

Subsidiary consolidation and diversification

Consolidated revenue rose 51.9% to KRW 57.3 billion in 2025 after the subsidiary was brought into the group. Standalone safety-product sales grew 14.3% to KRW 21.1 billion in the first half of 2026 within KRW 41.5 billion of consolidated revenue.

Clean fire-suppression gas expansion has been cited as a driver, widening the contribution beyond safety footwear.

More concrete shareholder-return policy

Reports say the company carried out its pre-listing shareholder-return pledge through a tiered dividend that favors general shareholders. Plans to raise the dividend scale and pay an interim dividend were also reported. The company said it would keep pursuing corporate value enhancement including shareholder returns.

09

Bear factors

Sharp drop in 2025 net profit

The disclosed drivers were the first-time consolidation of a subsidiary, SPAC merger costs and acquisition costs. Because net profit fell even as operating profit rose, questions remain about earnings quality.

Balance-sheet and cash-flow strain

The debt-to-equity ratio jumped to 152.2% at year-end 2025 from 39.8% in 2024. Total liabilities of KRW 58.4 billion exceed total equity of KRW 38.4 billion. Operating cash flow turned negative at -KRW 4.73 billion in 2025, making borrowing and working-capital management important tasks.

High quarterly volatility

1Q 2026 revenue fell 17.4% from the prior quarter to KRW 16.7 billion, and after the 2Q surge, 3Q 2026 operating profit of KRW 2.83 billion fell well short of 2Q's KRW 5.87 billion. The operating margin also fell from 22.9% in 2023 to 16.2% in 2025.

Quarterly results appear to swing with project timing, which limits how far recent quarters can be extrapolated.

Risk factors

Demand concentration risk

A large part of recent growth relies on the investment cycle in chip fabs and data centers. If that investment slows or is delayed, staged project revenue could decline quickly. Changes in the scope and enforcement of the Serious Accidents Punishment Act are also a demand variable.

Financial and cash-flow risk

The debt-to-equity ratio rose to 152.2% at year-end 2025, with total liabilities of KRW 58.4 billion exceeding total equity of KRW 38.4 billion. The KRW 4.73 billion operating cash outflow in 2025 could raise reliance on borrowing.

How debt taken on in the subsidiary consolidation and acquisition relates to cash flow is a point to check in the next annual report.

Governance and profit-attribution risk

Reports around the 2024 listing said the largest shareholder held about 65%, and some linked the structure to succession within the founding family. As the gap between net profit and profit attributable to controlling shareholders widened, how earnings are allocated among shareholders matters. For minority holders, the durability of the shareholder-return policy is central.

Overall view

Black YAK I&C's revenue rose from KRW 35.2 billion in 2023 to KRW 57.3 billion in 2025, and operating profit rose for two consecutive years from 2024 through 2025.

However, the operating margin fell from 22.9% in 2023 to 16.2% in 2025, and net profit attributable to controlling shareholders fell 98.7% to KRW 92.18 million in 2025, reflecting merger and acquisition costs together with non-controlling interest effects.

In 2Q 2026, safety demand tied to chip fabs and data centers, along with fire-protection equipment sales, produced revenue of KRW 24.9 billion and operating profit of KRW 5.87 billion. In 3Q 2026, revenue was KRW 17.9 billion and operating profit KRW 2.83 billion, both below 2Q.

On the balance sheet, the 152.2% debt-to-equity ratio and the KRW 4.73 billion operating cash outflow in 2025 are the main burdens. Shareholder returns are taking shape through the tiered dividend and interim dividend plan.

The next checkpoints are the operating margin and cash flow in the 2026 annual report and the operating profit trend in the 1Q 2027 quarterly report.
