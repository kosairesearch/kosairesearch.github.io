Report overview

Profit Recovery on Certification and Proof Services; Net Income Is the Next Test

Korea Information Certificate Authority, Incorporated (KICA) lifted its operating margin to 19.5% in 2025 and rebuilt its earnings base, but net income attributable to controlling shareholders has fallen for two straight quarters since its 1Q 2026 peak, so the next test is the net income trend.

  1. 1

    Revenue reached KRW 138.5 billion in 2025, up sharply from KRW 87.8 billion in 2022, and 2025 is the largest revenue year in the 2022–2025 period.

  2. 2

    Operating profit was KRW 27.1 billion in 2025, and the operating margin of 19.5% was the highest among the 2022–2025 years.

  3. 3

    Net income attributable to controlling shareholders rose 134.5% to KRW 26.2 billion in 2025, but after KRW 6.24 billion in 1Q 2026 it declined to KRW 5.24 billion in 2Q and KRW 4.64 billion in 3Q 2026.

02

Business structure

Korea Information Certificate Authority, Incorporated (KICA; stock code 053300) was founded in July 1999 and was the first government-accredited certification authority in Korea. It started with public certificate issuance and expanded into OTP, electronic documents and e-contract services.

Per the 2025 business report, the certification and security segment's revenue mix was certificate issuance 50.33%, OTP and financial solutions 18.32%, electronic documents 17.28%, other businesses such as SSL and e-contracts 10.78%, and toolkits and maintenance 3.29%.

That segment's total revenue was KRW 86.61 billion, a different scope from consolidated revenue of KRW 138.5 billion. Certificate revenue was KRW 43.28 billion in 2023, KRW 43.56 billion in 2024 and KRW 43.59 billion in 2025, which is essentially flat.

OTP and financial solution revenue fell from KRW 17.80 billion in 2023 to KRW 15.87 billion in 2025.

Electronic document revenue, mainly university and hospital certificate issuance, rose from KRW 9.29 billion in 2024 to KRW 14.96 billion in 2025, and the company describes university certificate services as a source of stable demand.

In September 2025 it launched a mobile certificate submission service, KICA Certificate. Its customers include financial firms, public agencies and corporations, and its public certificates can be used regardless of which bank or financial institution is involved.

Overseas, it has export records across more than 20 countries in Africa and Southeast Asia, and at RSAC 2026 it showed a solution for building certification systems in developing countries. In May 2024 it absorbed an online electronic certification company, and it aims to become a certification and proof platform.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual revenue rose from KRW 87.8 billion in 2022 to KRW 92.2 billion in 2023 (up 5.0% year on year), KRW 121.9 billion in 2024 (up 32.2%) and KRW 138.5 billion in 2025 (up 13.6%), extending three consecutive years of growth from 2023.

The 2024 jump coincides with the effective date of the Digital Zone absorption merger (May 1, 2024), and how much of that merger effect is reflected in the numbers needs to be checked in the business report notes.

Operating profit fell from KRW 15.7 billion in 2022 to KRW 14 billion in 2023 (down 11.1%), which marked the low point, and then rose to KRW 18.9 billion in 2024 (up 35.0%) and KRW 27.1 billion in 2025 (up 43.5%), a two-year run of growth.

The operating margin moved from 17.9% in 2022 to 15.1% in 2023, 15.5% in 2024 and 19.5% in 2025, with a sizable recovery in 2025. The company attributed the 2025 operating profit increase to higher revenue in its advertising business and an improved cost structure.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders grew from KRW 6.19 billion in 2022 to KRW 26.2 billion in 2025, a 134.5% increase in 2025.

Net income including non-controlling interests was KRW 28.1 billion in 2025, a different measure, and the company explained that lower non-operating expenses, including valuation losses on investment stocks, supported that figure.

Because operating profit grew 43.5% while attributable net income grew 134.5%, non-operating factors appear to have played a meaningful part in the 2025 net income improvement.

Operating cash flow fell from KRW 28.9 billion in 2024 to KRW 15.4 billion in 2025, and the debt ratio rose from 31.4% in 2022 to 74.6% in 2024 before easing to 69.0% in 2025.

Quarterly revenue went from KRW 33.2 billion in 3Q 2025 to KRW 38.6 billion in 4Q 2025 (up 16.3% quarter on quarter), then KRW 35.6 billion in 1Q 2026, KRW 36.9 billion in 2Q 2026 and KRW 35.7 billion in 3Q 2026 (up 7.5% year on year).

Quarterly operating profit recovered from KRW 5.82 billion in 4Q 2025 to KRW 9.12 billion in 1Q 2026, and KRW 9.22 billion in 3Q 2026 was up 10.9% year on year.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders, however, fell from KRW 6.24 billion in 1Q 2026 to KRW 5.24 billion in 2Q and KRW 4.64 billion in 3Q, down 25.2% year on year in 3Q. In 3Q 2026 operating profit rose while attributable net income fell, so the two measures moved in opposite directions.

05

Industry analysis

Demand for public certificates in Korea is strongly shaped by changes in electronic signature rules. After the full revision of the Electronic Signature Act removed discrimination among electronic signature methods, the company faces competition from a range of electronic signature technologies.

In its business report, the company noted that a decline in electronic signature service revenue could increase the volatility of total revenue.

In response, its 2026 corporate value enhancement plan targets stability in core certification, e-contract and certificate services, and it pursues M&A to diversify the portfolio.

In proof services, the company describes university certificate issuance as a source of stable demand, and it says that the expansion of online business is widening where certificates are used. In security, it is developing new products including post-quantum cryptography and biometric OTP.

Overseas demand centers on building certification systems for developing-country governments and financial institutions.

Among IT and software peers on the Korean market, Ahnlab is a comparable security company, but KICA differs in that certificate issuance accounts for about half of segment revenue, so its structure is more certification-centered.

In cycle terms, certificate revenue has plateaued while proof and new product revenue is expanding, indicating a transition phase.

Outlook

In its 2026 corporate value enhancement plan, disclosed on April 22, 2026, the company set goals of strengthening its core businesses, pursuing M&A, investing in AI and PQC, and expanding shareholder returns.

It said M&A is aimed at diversifying the business portfolio and securing growth drivers, and the targets and timing will become clear when individual deals are disclosed.

The treasury share cancellation was set in motion by a capital reduction decision on March 11, 2026, covering 1,850,265 common shares, equal to 4.36% of common shares on a common-share basis, with a record date of April 30, 2026.

A KRW 5.68 billion treasury share purchase trust agreement signed with Kiwoom Securities on April 10, 2026 contemplated full cancellation of the acquired shares after the contract ended, and the contract period ran through October 12, 2026.

On dividends, the 2025 payout ratio was disclosed at 30.5%, with total dividends of KRW 7.99 billion, and the prior-year dividend amount rose 31.24% from the year before. Overseas, the company used RSAC 2026 to promote its track record of operating national certification systems in more than 20 countries.

How the M&A plans and the AI and PQC investments play out will determine the progress of the portfolio expansion. The 2026 annual business report will show the fourth-quarter and full-year 2026 operating margin, the outcome of the treasury share cancellation, and notes on any M&A.

08

Bull factors

Operating margin recovery and cost structure

The operating margin reached 19.5% in 2025, the highest of the 2022–2025 years, and operating profit rose from KRW 18.9 billion in 2024 to KRW 27.1 billion in 2025. The company credited advertising revenue growth and an improved cost structure.

Quarterly operating profit held near KRW 9 billion through 2026, at KRW 9.12 billion in 1Q, KRW 8.54 billion in 2Q and KRW 9.22 billion in 3Q.

Proof and electronic document revenue broadens the base

Electronic document revenue rose from KRW 9.29 billion in 2024 to KRW 14.96 billion in 2025, partly offsetting the plateau in certificate revenue. The company says university certificate services secure stable demand.

The September 2025 launch of the KICA Certificate mobile submission service extends the proof business. Quarterly revenue rose from KRW 33.2 billion in 3Q 2025 to KRW 38.6 billion in 4Q 2025.

Expanded shareholder return policy

In March 2026 the company decided to cancel 1,850,265 treasury shares, and in April it signed a KRW 5.68 billion treasury share purchase trust. The 2025 payout ratio was disclosed at 30.5%, and the prior-year dividend amount rose 31.24% from the year before. The company stated that it qualifies as a high-dividend company under the Restriction of Special Taxation Act.

09

Bear factors

Slowing net income and reliance on non-operating items

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders fell from KRW 6.24 billion in 1Q 2026 to KRW 5.24 billion in 2Q and KRW 4.64 billion in 3Q, down 25.2% year on year in 3Q. In 4Q 2025 it was KRW 1.84 billion, down 70.4% from the prior quarter, which shows wide quarterly swings.

Lower non-operating expenses, including valuation losses on investment stocks, contributed to the 2025 net income improvement.

Flat certificate revenue and a decline in OTP

Certificate issuance revenue barely grew, from KRW 43.28 billion in 2023 to KRW 43.59 billion in 2025. OTP and financial solution revenue fell over the same period, from KRW 17.80 billion to KRW 15.87 billion.

With discrimination among electronic signature methods removed, public certificates now compete with a broader set of electronic signature technologies.

Leverage and cash flow pressure

The debt ratio rose from 31.4% in 2022 to 74.6% in 2024 and stood at 69.0% in 2025. Operating cash flow fell from KRW 28.9 billion in 2024 to KRW 15.4 billion in 2025. If M&A and treasury share purchases proceed at the same time, funding allocation could become a constraint.

10

Risk factors

Regulation and competition

With discrimination among electronic signature methods removed, competition from biometric and simplified authentication may intensify. The business report itself flags the risk that a decline in electronic signature revenue could widen overall revenue swings.

Because certificate issuance accounts for about half of segment revenue, this trend has a large bearing on results.

Earnings volatility and non-operating items

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders fell 70.4% quarter on quarter in 4Q 2025, and the 2025 net income improvement relied partly on lower investment valuation losses. If non-operating items swing, net income can fall even as operating profit grows. The 3Q 2026 divergence, with operating profit up and attributable net income down, illustrates this structure.

Capital allocation and M&A execution

Portfolio diversification through M&A depends on the performance of acquired businesses and integration costs. With the debt ratio having risen from 31.4% in 2022 to 69.0% in 2025, running M&A alongside treasury share cancellation could narrow financial flexibility. The company also plans investment in AI and PQC at the same time.

12

Overall view

KICA built its 2025 results of KRW 138.5 billion in revenue and KRW 27.1 billion in operating profit by adding electronic documents, advertising and overseas system building to its core public certificate business.

The 2025 operating margin of 19.5% was the highest of the 2022–2025 years, and net income attributable to controlling shareholders rose 134.5% to KRW 26.2 billion.

Part of that net income gain came from lower non-operating expenses, and in 2026 attributable net income has fallen for two straight quarters after its 1Q peak of KRW 6.24 billion.

Certificate revenue has been flat since 2023, while electronic document revenue rose from KRW 9.29 billion in 2024 to KRW 14.96 billion in 2025. On shareholder returns, the company has pursued treasury share cancellation and a treasury share purchase trust, and the 2025 payout ratio was disclosed at 30.5%.

The debt ratio rose from 31.4% in 2022 to 69.0% in 2025, and operating cash flow fell to KRW 15.4 billion in 2025. Strengths include the margin recovery and the expansion of proof services, while weaknesses include slowing net income, competition in electronic signatures and higher financial leverage.

The next checkpoints are the full-year figures in the 2026 annual business report and the net income trend in the 1Q 2027 quarterly report.
