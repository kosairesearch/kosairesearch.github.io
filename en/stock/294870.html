02

Business structure

Ipark Hyundai Development Company is a construction company centered on housing, with main business lines in urban redevelopment and reconstruction contracts, outsourced housing construction (contract work) and self-developed housing for sale.

The company reported that it won KRW 4.80 trillion in urban redevelopment orders in 2025.

In 2026, starting with the Seongnam Taepyeong 3 district in July, it secured construction rights in Uijeongbu Jangam 2, Busan Yeonsan 13 and Gunpo Geumjeong Station Area 1, bringing cumulative urban redevelopment orders to KRW 2.21 trillion.

Press coverage attributed the third-quarter 2026 margin improvement to better profitability in outsourced housing and higher revenue from self-developed housing.

The company positions large Seoul redevelopment projects as mixed-use development linked to nearby sites rather than simple construction work, and cites this as evidence of its developer capabilities.

Its IPARK brand is used to win trust from homeowner associations and project owners, and the industry view is that brand recognition weighs heavily in construction-company selection.

Its main competitors are large construction companies bidding on Seoul redevelopment projects, and that competition is intense and centered on brand and project terms. The company's urban redevelopment orders are concentrated in core locations in Seoul and the metropolitan area.

Segment revenue shares are not covered here, so the business structure is viewed through annual results and the order flow.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual revenue rose from KRW 3.3 trillion in 2022 to KRW 4.19 trillion in 2023 (up 27.1% year on year) and KRW 4.26 trillion in 2024 (up 1.6%), then fell to KRW 4.15 trillion in 2025 (down 2.6%). Among the 2022-2025 years, 2024 was the largest and 2022 the smallest.

Operating profit went from KRW 116.4 billion in 2022 to KRW 195.3 billion in 2023 (up 67.8%), KRW 184.6 billion in 2024 (down 5.5%) and KRW 248.6 billion in 2025 (up 34.7%), with 2025 the largest year in the period. The operating margin was 3.5%, 4.7%, 4.3% and 6.0%, with 2025 the highest of the four years.

Profit attributable to controlling shareholders peaked in 2023 at KRW 173.1 billion (up 243.6%), then came in at KRW 155.7 billion in 2024 (down 10.0%) and KRW 158.1 billion in 2025 (up 1.6%).

In 2025 operating profit rose 34.7% while attributable profit rose only 1.6%, and the items behind that gap cannot be pinned down from the disclosed figures alone. Net profit equaled attributable profit in both 2024 and 2025.

Quarterly revenue went from KRW 1.05 trillion in the third quarter of 2025 to KRW 1.03 trillion in the fourth quarter (down 2.7% quarter on quarter), with the first quarter of 2026 at KRW 673.9 billion (down 34.3%) as the low point.

Revenue then rose in the second quarter to KRW 914.6 billion (up 35.7%) and in the third quarter to KRW 1.08 trillion (up 18.3%), marking two consecutive quarterly increases.

Operating profit bottomed at KRW 41.3 billion in the fourth quarter of 2025 (down 43.3%) and rose for three consecutive quarters to KRW 80.1 billion, KRW 122.7 billion and KRW 165.8 billion.

Profit attributable to controlling shareholders rose from KRW 11.4 billion to KRW 111.3 billion over the same span, and the last four quarters total KRW 255.7 billion. Press reports said the third-quarter operating profit beat a market consensus of KRW 148.3 billion.

Media attributed the gain to better profitability in outsourced housing and higher self-developed housing revenue, with higher selling and administrative costs from the expansion of urban redevelopment orders partly offset by that gain.

Operating cash flow went from negative KRW 1.74 trillion in 2022 to KRW 640.2 billion in 2023, KRW 314.4 billion in 2024 and KRW 2.81 billion in 2025, leaving a gap between profit recovery and cash collection. The debt-to-equity ratio fell from 152.8% in 2022 to 136.5% in 2025.

05

Industry analysis

Press reports say the 2026 urban redevelopment and reconstruction market is expected to grow more than 20% from the prior year to roughly KRW 80 trillion. Competition among large construction companies has intensified over contract-heavy sites in Apgujeong, Yeouido, Mokdong and Seongsu.

Daewoo Engineering & Construction reported that cumulative new orders through the third quarter of 2026 reached KRW 12.5 trillion, up 11.9% from the same period a year earlier.

In bidding for redevelopment work, large construction companies compete on brand and project terms, and industry analysis holds that brand recognition weighs heavily in homeowner association selection.

Ipark Hyundai Development Company expanded its redevelopment order base, with KRW 4.80 trillion in 2025 and KRW 2.21 trillion cumulative in 2026. The company positions mixed-use development and linked projects as its competitive edge and is pursuing orders in core Seoul locations.

Housing sale market conditions and interest rates determine when self-developed housing revenue is recognized. Even as redevelopment orders grow, the timing of revenue depends on construction progress and sales timing, so profit improvement may not appear evenly from quarter to quarter.

06

Outlook

At the start of 2026 the company set a revenue plan of KRW 4.23 trillion, up from KRW 4.15 trillion in 2025. The annual new-order target was raised on October 26, 2026 to KRW 10.1 trillion from about KRW 6.5 trillion at the start of the year.

New orders were KRW 1.30 trillion in the first half and KRW 3.56 trillion in the third quarter alone, bringing cumulative orders to KRW 4.86 trillion at the end of September. Reaching the new target requires KRW 5.24 trillion more as of the end of September.

The Banghwa 2 and Sinwol 7-dong 1 redevelopment projects were selected as preferred bidders with construction costs of about KRW 293.8 billion and KRW 937.5 billion, but general assembly approval and final contracts remain.

The Sookdae-ipgu Station Area long-term rental housing redevelopment in Yongsan has an expected construction cost of about KRW 540 billion, with the company the sole bidder in the first round and the second-round bidding closing on November 30, 2026.

Projects such as Hayan Jugong 6 and 7 complexes, Mokdong 11 complex and Seongsu 2 district are also contested, and several are estimated at more than KRW 1 trillion in total project cost.

Yuanta-style brokerage research from Yujin Investment expects construction starts on large general building projects to pick up from 2027, supporting growth in revenue. Industry observers note that failing to win the key sites could leave the annual order target out of reach.

Bull factors

Third-quarter earnings capacity

Better profitability in outsourced housing and higher self-developed housing revenue were cited as the main drivers of the higher margin. Quarterly operating profit has risen for three consecutive quarters since the fourth-quarter 2025 low.

Expanding order flow

Third-quarter 2026 new orders were KRW 3.56 trillion, about 2.7 times the combined first-half total of KRW 1.30 trillion. On that basis the company raised its annual new-order target into the KRW 10 trillion range. Cumulative orders of KRW 4.86 trillion at the end of September equal 74.3% of the previous target.

Construction starts expected from 2027

Yujin Investment expects construction starts on large general building projects to become substantial from 2027, supporting revenue growth. Whether these starts translate into revenue will be visible in quarterly results from 2027.

Bear factors

Gap between profit and cash flow

Operating profit rose to KRW 248.6 billion in 2025, yet operating cash flow fell to KRW 2.81 billion from KRW 314.4 billion in 2024. The decline has continued since KRW 640.2 billion in 2023. The divergence between profit and cash flow is a point to recheck in the 2026 annual report.

Hurdle to reach the order target

As of the end of September, KRW 5.24 trillion of additional orders would be needed to reach the new target. The Banghwa 2 and Sinwol 7-dong 1 projects that support the raised target are still at the preferred-bidder stage, with general assembly approval and final contracts pending. Industry observers say failing to win key sites could make the target difficult to reach.

Flat revenue and first-quarter low

Revenue in 2025 was KRW 4.15 trillion, down 2.6% year on year. First-quarter 2026 revenue of KRW 673.9 billion marked the quarterly low. Reaching the 2026 revenue plan of KRW 4.23 trillion depends on whether the second- and third-quarter recovery continues.

Risk factors

Order competition and contract finalization

In Seoul redevelopment bidding, large construction companies compete on brand and project terms. Many candidate projects are still at the preferred-bidder stage, so delays in general assembly approval or final contracts could postpone order recognition. Winning large sole-bid projects is cited as a key measure of competitiveness.

Balance sheet and cash flow

Total liabilities at the end of 2025 were KRW 4.39 trillion, with a debt-to-equity ratio of 136.5%. Operating cash flow of KRW 2.81 billion is weak, so faster redevelopment order growth could raise working-capital needs as construction progresses. This should be checked in the cash flow statement of the 2026 annual report.

Selling and administrative costs and offset effects

Press analysis said selling and administrative costs rose somewhat with the expansion of urban redevelopment orders in the second half, and that better profitability in outsourced housing offset that increase. Whether the offset continues depends on construction progress and sales performance. If these costs grow faster than orders, the margin gain could narrow.

Overall view

Profit attributable to controlling shareholders reached KRW 111.3 billion in the third quarter of 2026, so the direction of earnings recovery is clear. The annual new-order target was raised into the KRW 10 trillion range, with third-quarter orders surging.
