02

Business structure

Zero to Seven was founded in 2000 and listed on KOSDAQ in 2013. It started in CRM services, ended its fashion business on August 31, 2022, and narrowed its focus to infant skincare and POE packaging.

Its core Gungjung Bichaek brand is a skincare line for infants and young children, sold through online channels in Korea and abroad, including Amazon. The packaging unit makes POE (Peel-off End), powdered-product can lids used in infant formula cans, and the company says it exports to roughly 20 countries.

Segment mix varies by source: one tally puts cosmetics at 54% and packaging at 46% of 2026 second-quarter sales, while another puts infant skincare at 55% and POE at 45% as of March 2026.

Gungjung Bichaek entered Malaysia in May 2025, Singapore in June, Thailand in September and Indonesia in November, followed by the United Arab Emirates in January 2026 and five European Amazon marketplaces in April 2026.

The company attributes sales growth to a stronger online mix and to early-morning delivery and easy-payment checkout. The company stresses its competitive position in POE, while earlier reports noted that export sales, centered on China, were a large share of the business.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual revenue fell from KRW 90.4 billion in 2022 to KRW 69.4 billion in 2023 (down 23.2% year on year), slipped to KRW 66.9 billion in 2024 (down 3.6%), and rose to KRW 71 billion in 2025 (up 6.1%). 2022 was the largest year and 2024 the smallest in the 2022 to 2025 window.

Operating income dropped from KRW 9.33 billion in 2022 to KRW 2.32 billion in 2023 (down 75.1%) and KRW 1.53 billion in 2024 (down 34.3%), then rebounded to KRW 2.52 billion in 2025 (up 64.8%).

Operating margin went from 10.3% in 2022 to 3.3% in 2023, 2.3% in 2024 and 3.5% in 2025, staying in the low single digits since 2023.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 4.13 billion in 2022, KRW 964 million in 2023 (down 76.6%), KRW 500 million in 2024 (down 48.1%) and KRW 3.45 billion in 2025 (up 589.9%). Net income including non-controlling interests matched these figures in each year.

The company attributed the 2025 improvement to higher sales in its Gungjung Bichaek cosmetics business. Debt-to-equity was 24.9% in 2022, 9.7% in 2023, 11.5% in 2024 and 10.1% in 2025.

At the end of 2025, total equity was KRW 83.4 billion, all of it attributable to controlling shareholders, and total liabilities were KRW 8.39 billion. Operating cash flow fell from KRW 12.6 billion in 2022 to KRW 5.19 billion in 2023 and KRW 1.29 billion in 2024 before recovering to KRW 3.34 billion in 2025.

Quarterly revenue was KRW 16.4 billion in the third quarter of 2025, KRW 16.9 billion in the fourth quarter and KRW 16.7 billion in the first quarter of 2026, then jumped to KRW 20.7 billion in the second quarter of 2026 (up 24.3% quarter on quarter) before falling to KRW 15.9 billion in the third quarter (down 23.2% quarter on quarter and down 2.8% year on year).

Quarterly operating income was KRW 419 million in the third quarter of 2025, KRW 20.9 million in the fourth quarter (down 95.0% quarter on quarter), KRW 644 million in the first quarter of 2026 and KRW 1.77 billion in the second quarter (up 174.5%), the largest of the last five quarters, before KRW 241 million in the third quarter (down 42.5% year on year).

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders rose to KRW 2 billion in the second quarter of 2026, then turned to a net loss of KRW 801 million in the third quarter.

Operating income stayed positive in the third quarter while controlling-shareholder earnings turned negative, so the two measures moved in opposite directions. Over the last four quarters, net income attributable to controlling shareholders totaled KRW 3.47 billion.

05

Industry analysis

Both businesses, infant skincare and POE packaging, are indirectly tied to birth rates and the infant formula market. The company cited intensifying competition in the domestic children's apparel market, linked to falling birth rates, as a reason for exiting fashion in 2022.

The company has said Gungjung Bichaek once ranked first for infant and child sun care on a Chinese online mall, and in March 2026 it announced an eighth consecutive year of winning a national brand award. Overseas sales are spreading beyond China into Southeast Asia, the Middle East, Europe and North America.

The POE business was centered on infant formula, but the company says it is widening applications to adult nutrition products and coffee. Earlier press reports linked infant formula registration procedures to lower POE sales, and China-centered exports were hit by an economic slowdown and regulation.

The company says packaging sales depend on whether demand in Oceania, a key export region, recovers. The company positions Gungjung Bichaek as a specialist in infant skin and says its target customers differ from those of large general cosmetics makers, even where categories overlap.

Because the company describes its POE business as the only domestic maker of powdered-product can lids, there is no clear one-to-one peer comparison for that unit.

Most listed consumer and distribution companies in the same sector are centered on retail channels such as department stores, convenience stores and rental services, so their business models differ from these two units.

Bear factors

Third-quarter swing to a loss

Third-quarter 2026 revenue of KRW 15.9 billion fell 23.2% from the prior quarter and 2.8% from a year earlier. The company posted a net loss of KRW 801 million attributable to controlling shareholders. Bears point to the loss at the net level even though operating income stayed positive.

Thin margins and a small earnings base

Operating margin was 3.3% in 2023, 2.3% in 2024 and 3.5% in 2025, well below 10.3% in 2022. Net income attributable to controlling shareholders totals KRW 3.47 billion over the last four quarters, a small base in which one weak quarter weighs heavily on the total. Bears view the thin margin as leaving little room for quarterly volatility.

Packaging exposure and quarterly volatility

Earlier reports tied POE packaging sales to infant formula registration and conditions in export markets. Quarterly revenue rose 24.3% in the second quarter of 2026 and then fell 23.2% in the third, and whether that pattern repeats is hard to gauge. Bears argue that dependence on a single product line makes the swings difficult to absorb.

Risk factors

Concentration and channel dependence

After exiting fashion in 2022, the company narrowed to two units, Gungjung Bichaek and POE packaging. Online sales and Amazon channels for Gungjung Bichaek can be affected by platform policy and fee changes. Weakness in either unit feeds directly into total revenue.

Overseas regulation and export conditions

Earlier reports said China-centered exports were hit by economic slowdown and regulation, disrupting normal operations. Changes in infant formula registration were also reported to have hit POE sales. Even as the number of overseas markets grows, country-specific rules and customs conditions can affect results.

Small-cap liquidity and ownership concentration

The largest shareholder and related parties held 57.79% as of April 2026, and the free float ratio was 39.89%. With a limited free float, a small-cap stock can see larger trading swings and price moves. Ownership concentration affects both decision-making and share supply and demand.
