Business structure

Victek began in 1990 as Victek Power Systems and listed on KOSDAQ in 2003 as a defense electronics maker. Defense operations accounted for 99% of first-quarter 2026 revenue of KRW 209 million?

Corrected: Defense operations accounted for 99% of first-quarter 2026 revenue of KRW 20.9 billion, with civilian businesses such as real-time location tracking systems making up just 1%.

Its main products are electronic warfare direction-finding systems, military power supplies, identification friend-or-foe equipment, tactical information communication (TICN) units and electro-optical equipment.

In submarine electronic warfare, the company localized equipment for the first time in Korea and now mass-produces electronic warfare gear for the 209-class submarine, while developing localized core components for the 214-class.

In power systems, it supplies power units for the L-SAM multifunction radar to Hanwha Systems, and its thermal imaging units for the K2 tank are tied to ground-weapon exports.

In space, it signed supply contracts with Hanwha Systems in February 2026 for power modules for a small SAR test satellite, worth KRW 2.88 billion.

Its main customers are the Defense Acquisition Program Administration (DAPA), LIG Defense&Aerospace and Hanwha Systems, so it operates as a component supplier to large systems integrators. Its aircraft-parts subsidiary handles parts supply and PBL and MRO services.

New businesses in preparation include ESS battery management systems for eco-friendly ships, standard-type batteries for ground defense systems, and integrated anti-drone solutions.

04

Earnings analysis

On a disclosed annual basis, revenue fell from KRW 74.4 billion in 2022 to KRW 54.9 billion in 2023, a decline of 26.1%, then rose to KRW 71.5 billion in 2024 (up 30.1%) and KRW 83 billion in 2025 (up 16.0%). Among 2022 to 2025, 2025 was the largest year and 2023 the smallest.

Operating profit went from KRW 1.65 billion in 2022 to an operating loss of KRW 4.34 billion in 2023, a swing into deficit, then turned to operating profit of KRW 1.82 billion in 2024 and rose to KRW 4.37 billion in 2025, up 140.5%. Operating margin was 2.2% in 2022, -7.9% in 2023, 2.5% in 2024 and 5.3% in 2025.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders was a loss of KRW 232 million in 2022 and a larger loss of KRW 4.19 billion in 2023, then turned to a profit of KRW 3.82 billion in 2024 and reached KRW 4.4 billion in 2025, up 15.3%.

Net income including non-controlling interests moved in the same direction over the same years. At the end of 2025, total equity was KRW 60.6 billion, all of it attributable to controlling shareholders, and total liabilities were KRW 65.6 billion.

The debt ratio rose from 57.6% in 2022 to 108.2% in 2025, so liabilities grew alongside the recovery in sales and profit. Operating cash flow was KRW 15.8 billion in 2022, an outflow of KRW 18.6 billion in 2023, then KRW 11.3 billion in 2024 and KRW 9.1 billion in 2025.

By quarter, the fourth quarter of 2025 was the highest in the disclosed set, with revenue of KRW 28.8 billion, operating profit of KRW 2.01 billion and net income attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 3.27 billion.

The first quarter of 2026 fell to revenue of KRW 20.9 billion, operating profit of KRW 195 million and a net loss attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 419 million.

The second quarter of 2026 brought revenue of KRW 22.6 billion and operating profit of KRW 624 million, but a net loss of KRW 567 million attributable to controlling shareholders continued.

In the third quarter of 2026, revenue of KRW 21.8 billion fell 11.0% year on year, while operating profit of KRW 1.63 billion rose 14.5% and net income attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 777 million turned positive.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders over the latest four quarters totaled KRW 3.06 billion. Project-level revenue recognition timing and development spending are cited as the reasons quarterly earnings swing.

05

Industry analysis

Growth in Korean defense exports is the main driver behind Victek's order flow. SK Securities assessed that Victek's parts are included across the major K-defense export items, and explained that export growth translates directly into higher revenue.

Because the exposure spans submarines, ground forces and missile defense rather than a single weapon system, the business is spread across several platforms.

The export chain also runs from prime contractors down to component suppliers: Hyundai-Rotem leads overseas sales of the K2 tank, which ties demand to suppliers of thermal imaging and electronics.

Market commentary has linked rising tension in the Middle East and Europe to expected order growth for domestic makers of precision strike and ground systems. A defense sector analyst, however, said Victek has shifted from an event-driven stock tied to North Korea risk into an export-led name expanding global share.

Among peers, Victek sits in the supply chains of large integrators such as Hanwha Systems, LIG Defense&Aerospace and Korea Aerospace Industries, and differentiates through its own ACES electronic warfare products in specific niches.

With civilian business at only 1% of first-quarter 2026 revenue, its exposure to the defense demand cycle is nearly complete. Because revenue timing depends on budget execution and contract timing, sector tailwinds do not translate directly into quarterly results.

Diversifying across platforms

Bear factors

Higher working capital and development spending tied to order growth could raise reliance on borrowing, so the debt trend in the next annual report is a key checkpoint.

Revenue decline and R&D burden

Third-quarter 2026 revenue of KRW 21.8 billion fell 11.0% year on year. R&D spending was KRW 2.2 billion in the first quarter of 2026, equal to 10.6% of revenue, and R&D equaled 12.9% of 2025 revenue.

Such spending is an upfront cost for adopting new systems, and the speed of profit recovery depends on whether it leads to production contracts.

Risk factors

Contract terms may change

The KRW 10.5 billion contract with Hanwha Systems carries a disclosed amount and period, but amounts and periods can change as the program proceeds. Disclosed order values may not map one-to-one onto recognized revenue, so comparing the disclosed amounts with quarterly revenue is necessary.

Customer concentration

Key customers are concentrated among the Defense Acquisition Program Administration, LIG Defense&Aerospace and Hanwha Systems.

Delays in these prime contractors' programs or export contracts could push back revenue recognition for component supply, and with civilian business at 1% of revenue, alternative demand sources are limited.

Budget execution and event-driven trading

Defense demand depends on the timing of budget execution and export contracts. A Jobpost report listed backlog conversion into revenue, project-level cost ratios, defense budget execution and cash flow as items to watch. The stock has moved sharply with geopolitical events, so price moves and business results can diverge.
