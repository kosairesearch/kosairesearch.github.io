Icure: Cosmetics ODM Drives Turnaround After Trading Halt

Icure Pharmaceutical Incorporation resumed trading in September 2026 after a 13-month halt, posting its first quarterly operating profit in nine years on cosmetics ODM growth, even as its pharmaceutical segment slows and governance risks persist.

  1. 1

    Q2 2026 operating profit reached KRW 2.4 billion, turning positive year-on-year, while revenue rose 49.1% year-on-year to KRW 37.7 billion.

  2. 2

    Trading, halted since August 2025 following the former largest shareholder's indictment for breach of trust and embezzlement, resumed on September 3, 2026.

  3. 3

    A KRW 25 billion third-party capital increase to Solum Cosmetics and others was completed, changing the company's largest shareholder.

  4. 4

    Cosmetics ODM drove the earnings recovery, while pharmaceutical revenue declined and the U.S. clinical trial for the donepezil patch was voluntarily withdrawn.

  5. 5

02

Business structure

Icure Pharmaceutical Incorporation is a KOSDAQ-listed company built on transdermal drug delivery system (TDDS) technology, operating two core businesses: pharmaceuticals and cosmetics ODM (original design manufacturing).

Its flagship pharmaceutical product is the dementia treatment patch 'Donerion Patch,' co-developed with Celltrion, under a domestic supply contract running through 2033. Per disclosed contract terms, the total supply value is KRW 390.7 billion, with average annual supply of about KRW 32.6 billion.

The cosmetics ODM business produces basic skincare, hydrogel mask packs, and soft capsules, and since 2025 has brought hydrogel mask pack production in-house after liquidating its joint venture, Cosmax Icure.

Cosmetics ODM clients include Korean beauty company APR and global firm Amway, and expanded sales to these newer customers have driven recent earnings improvement. In contrast, pharmaceutical revenue has been declining due to a restructuring of its oral prescription drug (ETC) business.

In 2026, a third-party capital increase made Solum Cosmetics, a subsidiary of Solum, the new largest shareholder, with Shinshin Pharmaceutical and the private equity fund DeepDive K-Beauty 2026 also participating. As a result, the stake of former largest shareholder and ex-CEO Choi Young-kwon has been reduced.

04

Earnings analysis

On a disclosed consolidated basis, revenue fell 4.3% from KRW 59.4 billion in 2022 to KRW 56.8 billion in 2023, then rose for two consecutive years to KRW 69.2 billion in 2024 (up 21.8%) and KRW 83.4 billion in 2025 (up 20.5%).

Over the same period, the operating loss narrowed each year from KRW 26.8 billion in 2022 to KRW 22.7 billion in 2023, KRW 17.2 billion in 2024, and KRW 12 billion in 2025, a fourth consecutive year of losses, while the operating margin improved from -45.1% to -14.3%.

However, net income attributable to controlling shareholders swung to a profit of KRW 1.05 billion in 2024 before reverting to a net loss of KRW 33.6 billion in 2025, a shift attributable in part to non-operating expenses including roughly KRW 8 billion in withholding tax tied to a tax authority's income disposition related to the former CEO.

Quarterly, revenue bottomed at KRW 10.9 billion in Q4 2025, down 56.8% quarter-on-quarter, with the net loss attributable to controlling shareholders widening to KRW 18.1 billion that quarter.

Revenue then rebounded for two consecutive quarters, reaching KRW 32.2 billion in Q1 2026 (up 196.3% quarter-on-quarter) and KRW 37.7 billion in Q2 2026 (up 17.0% quarter-on-quarter, up 49.1% year-on-year).

Operating profit turned positive at KRW 208 million in Q1 2026 and expanded to KRW 2.4 billion in Q2 2026, also turning positive year-on-year.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders likewise turned positive at KRW 2.32 billion in Q2 2026, versus both the prior quarter and year-ago quarter, though the trailing four quarters (Q3 2025 through Q2 2026) still show a cumulative net loss attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 20 billion.

At year-end 2025, the debt-to-equity ratio rose sharply to 270.0% from 118.8% a year earlier, total equity shrank to KRW 24.5 billion (controlling interest of KRW 26.3 billion), while operating cash flow improved to -KRW 2.68 billion from -KRW 17.1 billion the prior year.

05

Industry analysis

Icure's two business lines sit in different industry cycles. Cosmetics ODM is riding the global expansion of K-beauty brands, with expanded sales to new customers including Korean beauty company APR and global firm Amway serving as the key recent growth driver.

Competition among ODM makers with diversified product lines spanning hydrogel mask packs and soft capsules continues, and Icure has responded by increasing in-house production after liquidating a joint venture.

By contrast, the transdermal patch prescription drug market is comparatively small and slow-growing; donepezil, the active ingredient in its dementia treatment, sits in a mature market mixing originator oral drugs and generics, meaning patch-form penetration takes time. The company voluntarily withdrew its U.S.

FDA Phase 1 clinical trial plan for the donepezil patch on May 21, 2026, clouding the timeline for what had been a pillar of its global blockbuster strategy.

The domestic pharmaceutical segment continues to see revenue declines tied to a restructuring of its oral prescription drug (ETC) business, suggesting cosmetics ODM could account for a growing share of consolidated results near term.

Within the KOSDAQ bio/pharma sector, Icure is classified as a small-to-mid-cap company centered on contract manufacturing and improved generics, distinct from large biosimilar or novel drug developers.

06

Outlook

In a May 2026 interview, the company stated it had sold off non-core businesses and roughly a dozen subsidiaries that had caused inefficient management, focusing on its core pharmaceutical and cosmetics ODM businesses to lift revenue from KRW 50 billion in 2024 to KRW 82.8 billion in 2025, with a 2026 target of a record annual revenue and a turn to positive EBITDA.

In practice, H1 2026 standalone revenue of KRW 69.7 billion grew 80.2% from KRW 38.7 billion a year earlier, with operating profit turning positive at KRW 2.8 billion, tracking close to that target.

Solum, the parent of new largest shareholder Solum Cosmetics, is a mid-sized electronic components company that posted consolidated revenue of KRW 1.7002 trillion and operating profit of KRW 46.5 billion, raising the possibility of further financial backing, though no concrete business cooperation plan has been disclosed.

The pharmaceutical segment has no clearly stated alternative overseas path since the U.S. trial withdrawal, making the pace of domestic donepezil patch prescription growth and the revenue contribution of existing overseas license deals (with Menarini and Pharmbrands, among others) key items to watch.

Following trading resumption, it will also be important to confirm whether operating cash flow, distorted by adjusted receivable and payable timing, normalizes alongside continued governance stabilization.

Additional costs remain possible depending on the outcome of the damages lawsuit and criminal trial against the former CEO.

08

Bull factors

High Growth and Customer Diversification in Cosmetics ODM

Cosmetics ODM revenue reached KRW 63.2 billion in 2025, up 146% from KRW 25.7 billion a year earlier, driven largely by expanded sales to new customers such as APR and Amway. Growth continued into H1 2026, with standalone revenue up 80.2% year-on-year.

Shifting to in-house production after liquidating a joint venture also created room for margin improvement. An expanding global K-beauty customer base may also reduce reliance on any single client.

First Quarterly Operating Profit in Nine Years

Per disclosed figures, operating profit of KRW 208 million in Q1 2026 expanded to KRW 2.4 billion in Q2, up 1,054.6% quarter-on-quarter and turning positive year-on-year as well.

Standalone operating profit for H1 2026 turned positive for the first time in nine years, which can be read as a structural improvement signal. This aligns with the steady narrowing of the annual operating margin from -45.1% in 2022 to -14.3% in 2025.

Trading Resumption and Fresh Capital from New Largest Shareholder

Trading resumed after about 13 months following the Korea Exchange's decision to maintain the listing, resolving the liquidity gap.

Capital was bolstered via a KRW 25 billion third-party allotment capital increase, and new largest shareholder Solum Cosmetics voluntarily agreed to a three-year lock-up on its entire stake through September 2029, adding share stability.

Participation by the Solum group, a mid-sized electronic components company, can be seen as reinforcing financial credibility.

09

Bear factors

Structural Slowdown in Pharma and Setback to Overseas Strategy

2025 pharmaceutical revenue of roughly KRW 18.9 billion fell 22% year-on-year due to a restructuring of the oral prescription drug business. Compounding this, the company voluntarily withdrew its U.S.

FDA Phase 1 trial plan for the donepezil patch in May 2026, effectively halting the core pillar of its global expansion strategy. While patch contract manufacturing capability remains, the growth narrative tied to its own drug pipeline has weakened.

Weaker Balance Sheet and Accumulated Net Losses

The disclosed debt-to-equity ratio jumped from 118.8% in 2024 to 270.0% in 2025, while total equity shrank to KRW 24.5 billion. The trailing four quarters (Q3 2025 through Q2 2026) show a cumulative net loss attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 20 billion. With the quarterly profit turnaround only two quarters old, its durability still requires further confirmation.

Lingering Governance Risk

A tax authority income disposition tied to the former CEO's past breach-of-trust case created an approximate KRW 8 billion withholding tax obligation, causing temporary funding strain.

In H1 2026, a series of legal disputes continued, including injunctions to suspend the former CEO's duties, injunctions for accounting book inspection, and requests for court permission to convene shareholder meetings.

The damages lawsuit and criminal trial against the former CEO remain ongoing, leaving open the possibility of additional costs depending on the outcome.

Risk factors

Legal and Governance Risk

The company filed a damages claim against the former CEO to recover the withholding tax payment and obtained a court-ordered asset seizure, but the actual recoverable amount remains uncertain pending the outcome of the criminal and civil proceedings. Additional litigation costs or provisions, if they arise, could further burden financial metrics.

Share Overhang Risk

The 24,177,949 new shares issued via the third-party allotment represent 64.4% of shares outstanding prior to the increase, and are subject to a one-year lock-up from the additional listing date. When the lock-up expires, increased float could affect supply and demand.

Pharmaceutical Pipeline Uncertainty

The voluntary withdrawal of the U.S. FDA Phase 1 trial plan has clouded the global expansion path for the donepezil patch as a novel drug asset.

Domestic pharmaceutical revenue has also trended lower for a second straight year, raising the risk that growing reliance on cosmetics ODM could weaken the balance of the business portfolio.

Overall view

Icure Pharmaceutical Incorporation emerged from a trading halt that began in August 2025, resuming trading in September 2026, and on a disclosed basis posted operating profit in both Q1 and Q2 2026, breaking a four-year streak of annual losses.

At the center of this shift is strong growth in cosmetics ODM, bolstered by new customers such as APR and Amway, while the pharmaceutical segment has relatively contracted amid declining revenue and the withdrawal of its U.S. clinical trial.

Financially, a KRW 25 billion capital increase and the shift of the largest shareholder to Solum Cosmetics resolved much of the governance risk, but a year-end 2025 debt-to-equity ratio of 270.0% and a trailing four-quarter cumulative net loss attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 20 billion indicate the balance sheet remains tight.

The criminal trial and damages lawsuit involving the former CEO, along with the lock-up expiration schedule for newly issued shares, remain variables to watch going forward.

Ultimately, how this stock is assessed hinges on how many more quarters the cosmetics ODM-led profit turnaround continues, and whether governance and legal risks resolve without further costs. The conclusion on whether to invest is left to each reader's own judgment.
