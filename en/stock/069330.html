02

Business structure

UID is a display materials and components company headquartered in Cheongju, North Chungcheong Province, and is classified under the display equipment and components sector on KOSDAQ.

Its core operation is indium-tin-oxide (ITO) coating of touch panel glass using inline sputtering, alongside legacy production of tempered glass filters for PDP panels. Through its subsidiary GD, the company has internalized the panel etching (slimming) process to improve efficiency alongside ITO coating.

The company is now pursuing a transition from traditional display materials into energy storage systems (ESS) and chemical materials. In December 2025 it acquired BSS, a specialist in ESS containers (enclosures), gaining capability to supply custom ESS containers to domestic battery makers.

Leveraging GD's hazardous-chemical handling capability, including hydrofluoric acid, the company is also pursuing supply of high-value-added chemical materials for semiconductor and etching processes, though this is still at an early stage.

In July 2026 it signed an MOU with Global Topnet to jointly develop new materials for high-performance computing (HPC) and next-generation ESS, aiming to commercialize integrated solutions for AI data centers.

UID is thus in a transitional period, running its legacy display materials business alongside new growth areas spanning ESS, chemical materials, and data center infrastructure.

04

Earnings analysis

UID's annual results swung from profit in 2022 to a loss in 2023, before a gradual recovery through 2024-2025.

In 2022, revenue was KRW 62.5 billion with operating profit of KRW 3.9 billion, a 6.2% operating margin, but in 2023 revenue fell to KRW 53.0 billion with an operating loss of KRW 0.33 billion and an owners' net loss of KRW 3.3 billion.

In 2024, revenue edged up to KRW 53.6 billion, operating profit turned positive at KRW 0.24 billion with an owners' net profit of KRW 0.66 billion, though the operating margin was a thin 0.5%.

In 2025, revenue slipped slightly to KRW 49.7 billion, but operating profit rose to KRW 1.3 billion, lifting the operating margin to 2.6%, with owners' net profit also around KRW 1.3 billion.

Quarterly results show notable volatility: in Q2 2025 operating profit was KRW 0.1 billion yet the company posted an owners' net loss of KRW 0.25 billion, while Q3 and Q4 2025 operating profit improved to KRW 0.66 billion and KRW 0.73 billion respectively, with net profit staying positive.

In Q1 2026, revenue jumped to KRW 15.6 billion with operating profit of KRW 3.49 billion and owners' net profit of KRW 3.73 billion, a sharp jump versus the prior quarter, but in Q2 2026 revenue fell to KRW 12.5 billion with an operating loss of KRW 0.74 billion and an owners' net loss of KRW 0.47 billion, swinging back into the red.

Summed over the most recent four quarters (Q3 2025 through Q2 2026), owners' net profit was about KRW 5.0 billion, a figure heavily lifted by the large Q1 2026 gain.

On cash flow, operating cash flow rose from KRW 0.45 billion in 2023 to KRW 2.9 billion in 2024 and KRW 3.7 billion in 2025, suggesting somewhat improved earnings quality, while the debt ratio jumped from 62.4% in 2022 to 130.9% in 2023 and has since held at an elevated 121.8% (2024) and 120.5% (2025).

05

Industry analysis

The downstream touch panel and small/mid-size display market has matured along with smartphone and tablet demand, slowing the traditional growth drivers for domestic ITO coating and lamination suppliers.

UID is one of a small number of firms that has internalized both inline-sputtering ITO coating and etching processes through its subsidiary, giving it niche process expertise, but its revenue scale has shrunk from KRW 62.5 billion in 2022 to roughly KRW 53.0 billion in 2023-2025.

Against this backdrop, demand for semiconductor-process chemical materials has remained comparatively resilient, and the etching-material segment the company is seeking to enter is cited as an area with growth potential.

The ESS market is seeing rising demand for container-type products amid renewable energy expansion and growing power demand, and BSS, the company UID acquired, has a track record of supplying customized containers to domestic battery makers.

Expanding AI data center investment has also highlighted demand for cooling and fire-suppression solutions for high-performance computing racks, and the Global Topnet partnership can be read as an attempt to address this trend.

However, ESS, semiconductor materials, and data center infrastructure are all new territories for UID, and with a shorter track record than incumbent players, its competitive position in these areas remains at an early stage.

06

Outlook

Following the BSS acquisition, the company has set a target of 20% annual growth for that business and is working on normalization, stating it expects full-scale business expansion from the second half.

The chemical materials business aims to supply materials for semiconductor and etching processes leveraging GD's hazardous-chemical handling capability, but it remains at an early stage, with any expansion into in-house production still under review.

Under the Global Topnet MOU, the two companies plan to develop and build a mass-production system for an all-in-one modular data center combining new-material-based high-performance server racks with UID's customized container systems, and are also considering expansion into edge data centers for industrial complexes and special-purpose infrastructure such as autonomous vessels.

Management has stated it will strengthen investor relations alongside pursuing these new businesses. On financing, a third-party allotment rights offering in April 2026 raised about KRW 2.0 billion for operating funds, with 1,863,931 new shares listed on May 27, 2026, bringing total shares outstanding to 16,952,574.

However, these new businesses have not yet translated into meaningful revenue contribution, so progress will need to be confirmed through future quarterly results via contract announcements or utilization indicators.

Bull factors

Recovering Profitability

Operating results moved from a loss in 2023 to profits in 2024-2025, with the 2025 operating margin improving to 2.6% from 0.5% a year earlier. Despite quarterly volatility, profitability was generally maintained from Q3 2025 onward.

Operating cash flow also rose from KRW 0.45 billion in 2023 to KRW 3.7 billion in 2025, suggesting some improvement in earnings quality.

Diversification Into New Businesses

The company is simultaneously pursuing multiple new growth pillars: ESS containers (BSS), semiconductor chemical materials (GD), and AI data center new materials (Global Topnet MOU). If successfully established, these could reduce reliance on the single display materials business. BSS has a track record of supplying customized containers to domestic battery makers.

Niche Process Technology

Inline-sputtering ITO coating technology combined with in-house etching (slimming) capability through its subsidiary provides process-linkage efficiency. This is a niche area held by only a few players, which can act as an entry barrier.

However, this technical capability has not directly translated into revenue growth amid slowing downstream display demand.

Bear factors

Quarterly Earnings Volatility

Results swung sharply from operating profit of KRW 3.49 billion in Q1 2026 to an operating loss of KRW 0.74 billion in Q2 2026. This suggests specific quarters may have been influenced by non-recurring factors rather than a repeatable operating trend. BSS previously went through court receivership due to liquidity issues, and normalization work is ongoing following the acquisition. The chemical materials business is also at an early stage, with the specific timing and scale of revenue contribution yet to be confirmed. If these new businesses take longer to establish, achieving the targeted growth could be delayed.

Elevated Debt Ratio and Equity Dilution

The debt ratio has remained elevated in the 120-130% range since 2023. A third-party allotment rights offering in April 2026 increased shares outstanding, diluting existing shareholders. Should investment in new businesses continue, the possibility of further capital raises cannot be ruled out.

Risk factors

New Business Execution Risk

The ESS, chemical materials, and data center infrastructure businesses are all at an early stage without visible revenue contribution, making it uncertain whether planned growth targets will be met. Additional investment costs may arise during the business transition. Commercialization and mass-production timelines for each business could also be delayed.

Acquired-Subsidiary Normalization Risk

BSS has a history of court receivership, and if normalization is delayed, the expected synergies may take longer to materialize. Expanding the business requires first restoring customer trust and securing new orders. Normalization costs could also exceed initial expectations.

Capital Structure and Liquidity Risk

With the debt ratio at an elevated level around 120% and operating cash flow showing quarterly volatility, reliance on external financing may continue. A third-party allotment rights offering was indeed carried out in April 2026. Any further equity issuance or borrowing could affect existing shareholder value.

Overall view

UID is in a transitional phase, building on its traditional display ITO coating business while simultaneously pursuing multiple new ventures in ESS containers, semiconductor chemical materials, and AI data center new materials.

Annual results moved from a loss in 2023 to profits in 2024-2025 with an improving operating margin, but 2026 has shown extreme quarterly volatility, with operating profit swinging from KRW 3.49 billion in Q1 to an operating loss of KRW 0.74 billion in Q2.

The new businesses are taking shape through the BSS acquisition, GD's entry into chemical materials, and the Global Topnet MOU, but all remain at an early stage without meaningful revenue contribution yet.

Financially, the debt ratio remains elevated around 120%, and a third-party rights offering in April 2026 diluted existing shareholders by increasing shares outstanding. The price-to-book ratio sits below 1x, trading at a discount to book value, though the company pays no dividend.

Investors will need to continue monitoring upcoming quarterly filings and order/contract confirmations across the new business lines to track the direction of earnings and the progress of the business transition.
