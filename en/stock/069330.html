02

Business structure

U.i.display began in 1990 as Yuinjeongkwang, changed its name in 2000, and listed on KOSDAQ in August 2003 as a display materials and parts company. Its core business is inline sputtering coating of indium tin oxide (ITO) onto glass used in LCD and touch-panel modules.

Through subsidiary GD, acquired around 2018, the company internalized the etching (slimming) process that thins display panels, linking etching and ITO coating into one integrated production flow.

GD is classified as a semiconductor and display equipment manufacturer and was previously under court receivership before being acquired by U.i.display.

More recently, the company has laid out three new growth pillars alongside its legacy display materials business: automotive materials, energy storage systems (ESS), and chemical materials.

The chemical materials initiative aims to leverage GD's capability in handling hazardous chemicals such as hydrofluoric acid to supply higher value-added materials for semiconductor and etching processes, though this remains at an early stage.

The company is also working with GlobalTopNet to commercialize an integrated HPC/ESS cooling solution for AI data centers, combining a fluorinated-ketone cooling and fire-suppression material with U.i.display's battery enclosure (container) technology.

Major customers have historically included domestic display makers and overseas panel manufacturers, though a detailed, product-level revenue breakdown for recent quarters is not separately disclosed.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual revenue fluctuated from KRW 62.5 billion in 2022 to KRW 53 billion in 2023 (down 15.2% year-on-year), KRW 53.6 billion in 2024 (up 1.1%), and KRW 49.7 billion in 2025 (down 7.2%), with 2022 the largest year and 2025 the smallest among the four.

Profitability moved independently of revenue: operating profit of KRW 3.87 billion in 2022 swung to an operating loss of KRW 325 million in 2023, then turned profitable again at KRW 244 million in 2024, before rising 433.4% year-on-year to KRW 1.3 billion in 2025.

The company attributed the 2024 turnaround to higher sales at its DM division, and attributed the 2025 profit growth despite lower revenue to cost reductions.

Controlling-interest net profit followed a similar path, swinging from KRW 3.77 billion in 2022 to a net loss of KRW 3.31 billion in 2023, turning profitable at KRW 660 million in 2024, and rising 94.7% to KRW 1.29 billion in 2025.

On a quarterly basis, operating profit improved for four consecutive quarters, from KRW 103 million in Q2 2025 to KRW 664 million in Q3 2025 (up 546.2% quarter-on-quarter), KRW 727 million in Q4 2025 (up 9.4%), and KRW 3.49 billion in Q1 2026 (up 380.0%).

However, in Q2 2026 revenue fell to KRW 12.5 billion, down 19.9% quarter-on-quarter and 5.5% year-on-year, while the company swung to an operating loss of KRW 740 million both quarter-on-quarter and year-on-year, and the controlling-interest net loss widened to KRW 467 million, also a quarter-on-quarter swing with the year-on-year loss persisting.

The sum of controlling-interest net profit across the last four quarters (Q3 2025–Q2 2026) reached KRW 5.01 billion, exceeding the full-year 2025 figure of KRW 1.29 billion, suggesting continued cumulative profit contribution even with the Q2 dip.

The operating margin improved gradually from 6.2% in 2022 to -0.6% in 2023, 0.5% in 2024, and 2.6% in 2025, while operating cash flow rose steadily from KRW 452 million in 2023 to KRW 2.95 billion in 2024 and KRW 3.75 billion in 2025.

05

Industry analysis

The ITO coating and display materials segment that U.i.display operates in is a niche distinct from large-cap semiconductor equipment and materials names.

Among peers classified in the same sector, there are large companies tied to HBM and advanced packaging equipment such as HANMI Semiconductor and EO Technics, or semiconductor cleaning and etching materials such as PSK and Tokai Carbon Korea, but U.i.display differs significantly from these in end-market exposure and business scale.

The company is attempting to move beyond its traditional reliance on touch-panel and LCD coating demand by expanding into automotive materials, ESS, and chemical materials, which can be read as a response to structurally stagnant or slowing demand for display-grade ITO coating.

Internalizing etching and chemical materials through subsidiary GD represents an attempt to expand into semiconductor-process material supply, but this field already has established large material suppliers, meaning a new entrant may need time to secure a meaningful revenue share.

The AI data-center cooling and ESS integrated solution touches on a growth theme currently drawing industry attention, but U.i.display's initiative with its partner remains at an early joint-development stage, with commercialization timing and revenue contribution not yet confirmed.

In 2026, a surge in small-amount public offerings among small-cap KOSDAQ companies emerged, largely driven by the late-July increase in the offering cap from under KRW 1 billion to under KRW 3 billion.

U.i.display's own decision in April 2026 to pursue a third-party allotment rights offering for working capital is consistent with this broader shift in the fundraising environment for small KOSDAQ manufacturers.

06

Outlook

The company's stated strategy centers on managing the profitability of its existing display ITO coating business while pursuing top-line expansion through three new businesses: automotive materials, ESS, and chemical materials.

The chemical materials initiative is at the stage of reviewing supply of semiconductor- and etching-process materials based on GD's hazardous chemical handling capability, with a reported plan to eventually expand toward supplying self-manufactured products.

The HPC/ESS integrated cooling solution aims to commercialize an all-in-one solution for AI data centers by combining GlobalTopNet's new cooling and fire-suppression material with U.i.display's battery enclosure technology, though specific supply contracts or revenue recognition timing have not yet been disclosed.

Financially, the company raised roughly KRW 2 billion in working capital through an April 2026 third-party allotment rights offering, which it stated would be used for research and development, personnel costs, and domestic and overseas sales and marketing.

Largest shareholder and CEO Park Jong-su increased his holdings through open-market purchases disclosed in March 2026, raising the combined stake of the largest shareholder and related parties into the 40% range, a fact that can be read as reflecting an insider perspective alongside ownership stability.

Since the earnings improvement that continued through Q1 2026 reversed in Q2, whether second-half results rebound or quarterly volatility persists is something to confirm through upcoming quarterly filings.

With the new businesses still at an early stage, profitability management of the existing display and equipment operations is likely to remain the main driver of results in the near term rather than a rapid shift in revenue mix.

Bull factors

Improving Earnings Structure

The company swung from an operating loss in 2023 to consecutive profitable years in 2024 and 2025, lifting the operating margin from -0.6% in 2023 to 2.6% in 2025. Cost reductions and higher DM-division sales were cited as the drivers of the 2025 and 2024 turnarounds, respectively.

Operating cash flow also rose steadily from KRW 452 million in 2023 to KRW 3.75 billion in 2025, showing that earnings improvement has translated into cash generation.

Business Diversification Effort

Having built an integrated etching-to-ITO-coating production line through subsidiary GD, the company is broadening its new-business pillars into automotive materials, ESS, and chemical materials. It is also pursuing commercialization of an HPC/ESS integrated cooling solution for AI data centers with GlobalTopNet. This can be read as an attempt to reduce reliance on legacy display ITO coating demand.

Increased Largest-Shareholder Stake

Largest shareholder and CEO Park Jong-su was disclosed to have increased his stake through open-market purchases in March 2026. The combined stake including related parties rose into the 40% range. This is a relevant fact from a management control stability perspective.

Risk factors

Balance Sheet and Past Designation History

U.i.display has a history of being designated for administrative issues or having trading suspended due to past consecutive operating losses.

The situation has improved with profitable years in 2024 and 2025, but the debt-to-equity ratio has stayed in the 120% range since 2023, meaning leverage pressure has not been fully resolved. Renewed deterioration in operating results could bring balance-sheet concerns back into focus.

Dilution and Small-Offering Risk

U.i.display has conducted repeated small-amount third-party rights offerings for working capital during 2026, consistent with a broader surge in small-amount public offerings across KOSDAQ small-caps.

Such offerings are sometimes exempt from mandatory lock-up requirements, raising the possibility that new shares reach the market relatively quickly. If further capital raises occur, the resulting dilution from an increased share count warrants monitoring.

End-Demand and New-Business Uncertainty

The core legacy ITO coating business is tied to touch-panel and LCD demand and is therefore sensitive to shifts in end-market demand. New businesses in automotive materials, ESS, chemical materials, and HPC cooling remain at an early stage with unverified revenue contribution.

The possibility that new-business investment increases cost burdens enough to offset the profitability of the existing business cannot be ruled out.

Overall view

U.i.display showed a recovery in profitability, swinging from a loss in 2023 to consecutive profitable years in 2024 and 2025, but operating and controlling-interest net losses reappeared in Q2 2026, reconfirming the company's quarterly earnings volatility.

The sum of controlling-interest net profit over the last four quarters exceeds the full-year 2025 figure, indicating continued cumulative profit contribution, but the most recent quarter alone makes it difficult to assert that profitability holds every quarter.

The company is attempting to diversify its business structure beyond legacy ITO coating through new ventures in automotive materials, ESS, chemical materials, and HPC cooling solutions, but most of these remain at an early stage with unconfirmed revenue contribution timing.

Financially, the debt-to-equity ratio has stayed in the 120% range while small-amount rights offerings for working capital recurred through 2026, meaning dilution potential should be weighed alongside any valuation assessment.

The largest shareholder's increased stake is a relevant fact for management stability but is not itself a determinant of future earnings direction.

Upcoming quarterly reports and disclosures related to the new businesses will be needed to further assess whether the Q2 2026 loss was a temporary adjustment or the start of a trend reversal.
