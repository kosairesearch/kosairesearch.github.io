02

Business structure

T.S.Investment is a venture capital (VC) and private equity (PEF) manager with a strength in M&A for small and mid-sized and venture companies. Its income comes from fee income, including management and performance fees from funds, and from equity-method and fair-value gains or losses on its fund stakes.

Assets under management were reported at KRW 1,171.7 billion at end-2025, and VC commitments in the same year totaled KRW 158.3 billion according to a Thebell tally. Dry powder, meaning uncalled fund capital, was reported at KRW 217.9 billion at end-2025.

Its core product is M&A funds, including an M&A growth fund of KRW 111.2 billion and a scale-up fund of KRW 110 billion formed in 2024. Press reports say the sale of its Freelife stake generated about KRW 62.8 billion in recoveries, but that figure is at fund level and does not map directly onto company-level profit.

On the policy side, the company was selected as a GP in 2025 for the KDB secondary fund program and the agriculture forestry fisheries fund secondary segment, receiving KRW 48 billion in commitments.

In 2026 it was reported as the sole general partner selected in the corporate succession M&A segment of the 1st regular fund-of-funds program, securing KRW 40 billion in commitments. Six managers applied for that segment, a 6-to-1 competition ratio.

Lee Dong-hyun was appointed CEO at the end of 2025, and the company is strengthening its M&A-centered strategy under the new leadership. Its revenue model differs from listed financial peers such as Mirae Asset Securities and NH Investment & Securities, which are centered on brokerage businesses.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual revenue fell from KRW 27.3 billion in 2022 to KRW 26.2 billion in 2023 (down 4.0% year on year) and then to KRW 17.6 billion in 2024 (down 32.7%), before rising to KRW 19.6 billion in 2025 (up 11.5%). Among 2022 to 2025, 2022 was the largest year and 2024 was the smallest.

Operating profit was KRW 11.6 billion in 2022 and KRW 14.3 billion in 2023 (up 22.9%), but 2024 turned to an operating loss of KRW 3 billion. Press reports attributed the 2024 loss to lower fee income together with larger equity-method valuation losses from falling fair values of fund assets.

In 2025 operating profit returned to KRW 9.02 billion, with an operating margin of 46.0%. Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders moved from KRW 10.3 billion in 2023 to a net loss of KRW 2.44 billion in 2024, then to KRW 7.02 billion in 2025.

Net income including non-controlling interests, a different figure, was KRW 7 billion in 2025 and should be read separately. Operating cash flow declined from KRW 9.95 billion in 2022 and KRW 10.8 billion in 2023 to KRW 3.94 billion in 2024 and KRW 239 million in 2025.

The debt ratio went from 34.2% in 2022 to 9.7% in 2024 and then 27.3% in 2025, when total equity was KRW 98.4 billion, equity attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 91.3 billion, and total liabilities were KRW 26.8 billion.

On a quarterly basis, Q4 2025 revenue of KRW 6.43 billion and operating profit of KRW 7.26 billion were the high points, while Q1 2026 posted an operating loss of KRW 2.7 billion and a net loss attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 2.25 billion.

Q2 2026 returned to an operating profit of KRW 5.42 billion, and Q3 2026 revenue of KRW 6.21 billion (up 50.1% year on year) came with operating profit of KRW 2.19 billion (up 117.0% year on year).

Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders over the latest four quarters totaled KRW 9.07 billion, and the drivers of the quarterly swings should be checked against the equity-method and fair-value components in the quarterly reports.

05

Industry analysis

The VC and PEF fund management market in which T.S.Investment operates is structured so that returns vary widely with the timing of investment execution and exits. A Numbers report noted that VC investment carries high volatility but offers the potential for high multiples in early-stage companies.

The same report said M&A investments have comparatively lower expected multiples, and that the company is using its VC segment to supplement its return metrics.

Policy capital plays a large role, as fund-of-funds programs such as the Korea Fund of Funds, the KDB recovery-market support fund and the agriculture forestry fisheries fund are used to raise fund capital. In 2026 the company was selected as the sole GP in the corporate succession M&A segment.

Secondary funds acquire portfolio stakes held by existing venture funds, which increases liquidity in the exit market. According to Thebell, the company is pursuing a portfolio strategy spanning secondary, buyout and growth-stage investments.

Within the corporate succession M&A segment, the competition ratio was 6 to 1, and the company previously won a GP role in a culture-sector fund-of-funds program in a consortium with NH Investment & Securities.

Listed financial peers such as KB Financial Group and Mirae Asset Securities have bank- and brokerage-centered models, so they are difficult to use as direct comparison benchmarks. Fund commitment and exit volumes for the Korean VC market as a whole should be read separately from this company's results.

06

Outlook

In May 2026, press reports said the company had begun forming its 18th M&A fund, targeting at least KRW 100 billion, backed by KRW 40 billion in commitments from the fund-of-funds corporate succession M&A segment.

The first closing target reported at the time was October 2026, a date that has already passed, so the final fund size and closing outcome are the next points to confirm.

Dry powder of KRW 217.9 billion at end-2025 can be deployed into new investments, so the pace of fund formation is directly linked to the pace of investment execution. A January 2026 report said the company aims to form an M&A fund of KRW 200 billion.

A November 2025 report said the company also plans to form a fund specialized in proptech and a dedicated M&A fund.

The same reporting said an anchor limited partner had not yet been secured, and that the company intended to take part actively in policy fund programs such as the fund-of-funds regular program and the National Growth Fund.

Given the management and performance fee structure, fee income tends to track fund size and the pace of investment. Because equity-method results move with the fair value and exit timing of fund assets, quarterly profit may not move in step with fund formation.

The company has described a strategy that balances the stability of M&A with the growth potential of VC, and the timing of how that strategy shows up in earnings can be checked in the annual and quarterly reports.

Bull factors

Scale of AUM and policy capital

Assets under management were reported at KRW 1,171.7 billion at end-2025, with dry powder of KRW 217.9 billion. In 2026 the company was selected as the sole GP in the corporate succession M&A segment and secured KRW 40 billion in commitments.

The 18th M&A fund is targeting at least KRW 100 billion, so completing its formation could broaden the base of fee income.

2025 earnings recovery and high operating margin

Operating profit for 2025 was KRW 9.02 billion, turning around from a 2024 operating loss, with an operating margin of 46.0%. Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders recovered to KRW 7.02 billion from a net loss in the prior year.

Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders over the latest four quarters totaled KRW 9.07 billion, and operating profit was positive in both Q2 and Q3 2026.

Debt burden below the 2022 level

Total equity was KRW 98.4 billion at end-2025, and total liabilities were KRW 26.8 billion. The debt ratio rose to 27.3% in 2025 from 9.7% in 2024, but it remains below the 34.2% recorded in 2022. Financial capacity to support fund commitments and new investments is cited as a positive factor.

09

Bear factors

Large swings in quarterly results

Q1 2026 recorded an operating loss of KRW 2.7 billion and a net loss attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 2.25 billion. After returning to operating profit in Q2, Q3 operating profit of KRW 2.19 billion fell 59.6% from the prior quarter.

The 2024 annual loss shows that both quarterly and annual results are sensitive to fund valuation gains and losses.

Shrinking operating cash flow

Operating cash flow fell to KRW 239 million in 2025, a wide gap from the 2022 and 2023 levels. Cash generation shrank sharply even as profit recovered in 2025. Because cash inflows depend on fee collection and fund exit timing, a continued gap between earnings and operating cash flow is cited as a bearish factor.

Discount in market valuation

Market capitalization has been below equity attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 91.3 billion at end-2025. The price-to-book ratio has stayed under 1 at year-end since 2022.

This could be read as the market assigning a low value to the fund assets and the durability of exit results, and how long the discount persists is uncertain.

Risk factors

Fund valuation and exit timing risk

A decline in the fair value of assets held by funds can increase equity-method valuation losses and reduce both operating and net profit at the same time. The 2024 loss was explained through this channel. Delays in exit timing or changes in valuation assumptions could enlarge quarterly swings.

Fund formation and policy capital dependence

The 18th M&A fund targets at least KRW 100 billion, of which the KRW 40 billion fund-of-funds commitment is only a part. A January 2026 report said an anchor limited partner had not yet been secured. Fund size and timing could vary with the outcomes of policy programs, so the pace of growth in fee income is uncertain.

Changes in financial indicators

The debt ratio rose from 9.7% in 2024 to 27.3% in 2025. Operating cash flow fell to KRW 239 million in 2025. Whether quarterly profit holds steady after the Q1 2026 loss, and how the higher debt ties to fund commitment capital, are points to monitor.

Overall view

T.S.Investment is a small and mid-sized and venture fund manager centered on M&A funds, and its results have recovered with a return to annual operating profit in 2025 after an operating loss in 2024.

Quarterly results in 2026, however, have swung widely, with an operating loss in Q1, a return to operating profit in Q2, and a decline in operating profit in Q3 from the prior quarter.

Positive factors include assets under management of KRW 1,171.7 billion, dry powder of KRW 217.9 billion, and selection as the sole GP in the corporate succession M&A segment.

Negative factors include operating cash flow of KRW 239 million in 2025, a rise in the debt ratio from 9.7% in 2024 to 27.3% in 2025, and profit volatility driven by fund valuation gains and losses.

With market capitalization below equity attributable to controlling shareholders, market valuation appears tied to the durability of fund formation and exit results.

The next checkpoints are the full-year operating margin and operating cash flow in the 2026 annual report, the equity-method results in the Q1 2027 quarterly report, and the final size of the 18th M&A fund. This summary organizes facts drawn from disclosures and press reports.
