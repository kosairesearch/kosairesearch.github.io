Report overview

Chip Orders Rise, Profit Recovery Still Unproven

Advanced Process Systems says cumulative new chip-equipment orders passed KRW 100 billion in November 2026, but its operating margin fell to 7.2% in 2025 and attributable net income swung to a KRW 9.09 billion loss in Q3 2026, so profit recovery remains to be confirmed.

02

Business structure

Advanced Process Systems started in display manufacturing equipment and is building chip equipment into its second growth pillar. On the display side, the company supplies excimer laser annealing (ELA) and laser lift-off (LLO) tools used in OLED production.

Parts sales that follow equipment shipments are also growing, and the company said parts revenue rose 25% year on year in the first half of 2026. The core chip product is rapid thermal processing (RTP) equipment, which heats wafers to high temperatures for a short time and then cools them quickly.

The company says it entered chip equipment in 1999 with a tool co-developed with Samsung Electronics and has since expanded from NAND flash into DRAM. Chip equipment sales were KRW 65.2 billion in 2025, which the company describes as its largest annual figure, and chips still account for under 20% of total revenue.

The company targets a chip revenue share of 20% in the short term and 30% by 2030. Its RTP share among domestic memory makers is rising, and RTP is being applied in more front-end steps. Newly won chip orders also include laser tools that apply laser control technology validated in OLED to wafer processes.

For advanced packaging, the company is pursuing commercialization of laser debonders and laser dicing tools used in HBM stacking. On the customer side, OLED tools depend on display panel makers, and a 2024 brokerage report linked lower utilization at Chinese panel makers to weaker parts sales. The company cites about 30 years of volume supply experience and short lead times as its strengths.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual revenue rose from KRW 486.6 billion in 2022 to KRW 533.6 billion in 2023, a 9.7% increase that marks the peak, and then fell 3.2% to KRW 516.7 billion in 2024 and 10.9% to KRW 460.1 billion in 2025, a two-year decline.

Operating profit fell three years in a row, from KRW 90.5 billion in 2022 to KRW 60.1 billion in 2023, KRW 47.2 billion in 2024 and KRW 33.3 billion in 2025, and 2025 was the smallest year of the period. In 2023 operating profit dropped 33.6% even though revenue grew, which is the notable feature of that year.

The operating margin declined every year, from 18.6% in 2022 to 11.3% in 2023, 9.1% in 2024 and 7.2% in 2025. Attributable net income fell from KRW 82.2 billion in 2022 to KRW 23.8 billion in 2025, and the 54.1% drop in 2025 was the largest annual decline in the period.

On a quarterly basis, revenue peaked at KRW 196.1 billion in Q1 2026 and then fell to KRW 162.8 billion in Q2 and KRW 129.2 billion in Q3, two consecutive quarterly declines.

Operating profit went from KRW 18 billion in Q1 2026 to KRW 9.59 billion in Q2 and KRW 4.04 billion in Q3, and the Q3 figure was down 69.8% from a year earlier.

Attributable net income turned into a net loss of KRW 9.09 billion in Q3 2026, and the sum of attributable net income over the latest four quarters is KRW 30.2 billion. In its first-half 2026 announcement, the company said OLED equipment sales rose about 99% year on year, which lifted first-half results.

Media reports of Q3 2026 revenue of KRW 488.2 billion and operating profit of KRW 31.6 billion are nine-month cumulative figures and broadly match the sum of the filed quarterly values.

05

Industry analysis

Demand for display equipment entered a recovery phase in 2026. The company says equipment investment by smartphone and display makers has resumed, and first-half 2026 OLED equipment sales rose about 99% year on year.

In the OLED production equipment market, the company has secured a high share and supply experience for LTPS-based ELA tools. On the chip side, the company links its order flow to global chipmakers expanding production capacity.

Its RTP share among domestic memory makers is rising, and demand for high-spec RTP tools for next-generation DRAM and chip automation equipment has grown. Competitively, the company points to more than a decade of equipment supply references and short lead times.

Parts revenue builds up from follow-on demand tied to installed equipment, so wider equipment supply forms the base for long-term revenue. Listed chip equipment peers include HANMI Semiconductor, Jusung Engineering and Wonik IPS, while this company is categorized by its RTP and laser tool lines.

On the display side, it is pursuing commercialization of ALD equipment for HMO oxide backplanes to broaden its equipment range into deposition tools. If display and chip capacity cycles move together, both businesses could grow in tandem, but how well one offsets a slowdown in the other is the point to watch.

06

Outlook

The company's medium-term direction is to raise chip equipment's revenue share to 20% in the short term and 30% by 2030. Cumulative new chip orders passed KRW 100 billion as of November 2026, and the company says this backlog will be recognized in revenue over the years through 2027.

When it announced first-half results in August 2026, the company had set a target of KRW 100 billion in new chip orders within the year, and that target was reached by November.

The company expects that expanding RTP applications and customer capacity additions could bring extra supply to similar processes within the same fab.

Laser tools are at the stage of widening their process applications and customer base, and laser debonders and dicing tools for advanced packaging are being pushed toward commercialization. The company said on November 17, 2026 that it is pursuing commercialization of ALD equipment for HMO.

A domestic brokerage (Leading Investment & Securities) estimated 2026 consolidated revenue at KRW 602.9 billion and operating profit at KRW 58.9 billion in a September 2026 report. Cumulative operating profit through Q3 2026 is KRW 31.6 billion, so reaching that estimate would require a sizable recovery in Q4.

Shareholder returns are being pursued under a three-year medium-term policy from 2024, and the 2025 year-end dividend and share buybacks have been completed. The size and timing of the next return policy are the next points to confirm.

The next periodic report is the 2026 annual report, and whether the annual operating margin rebounds from 7.2% in 2025 is the key test.

Bull factors

The company says this backlog will be recognized through 2027. As the backlog grows, whether chips become a growth pillar after display is the point to verify.

RTP share and laser equipment expansion

The company says its RTP share among domestic memory makers is rising and that RTP is being applied in more front-end steps. Laser tools built on OLED technology were added to new orders for wafer processes. Laser debonders and dicing tools for HBM packaging are also being pushed toward commercialization.

Shareholder returns and balance sheet capacity

A KRW 5 billion dividend tied to the 2025 settlement was paid, and a KRW 3 billion treasury share cancellation was decided on November 9, 2026. The company has pursued a three-year medium-term return policy since 2024. Total equity stood at KRW 367.6 billion at the end of 2025, which forms the capital base for returns.

Bear factors

Operating margin in a multi-year decline

The operating margin declined each year, from 18.6% in 2022 to 11.3% in 2023, 9.1% in 2024 and 7.2% in 2025. Operating profit has fallen for three consecutive years from 2023 to 2025. Attributable net income dropped 54.1% in 2025, the largest decline in the period.

Quarterly slowdown and Q3 2026 net loss

Revenue peaked at KRW 196.1 billion in Q1 2026 and then declined in Q2 and Q3. Q3 operating profit was KRW 4.04 billion, down 69.8% from a year earlier. Attributable net income swung to a KRW 9.09 billion loss, reversing the direction of quarterly profit.

OLED dependence and a low chip-revenue starting point

OLED-related sales account for most of total revenue. Chips make up under 20% of revenue, a low starting point. Because chip orders are recognized over the years through 2027, there is a lag before orders show up in reported results.

Risk factors

Customer capex cycle

Display equipment revenue depends heavily on panel makers' capex and utilization. A 2024 brokerage report said parts sales weakened after average utilization at Chinese panel makers fell by more than 10%.

OLED equipment sales rose in the first half of 2026, but if customer investment slows again, equipment and parts revenue could both weaken.

Timing of new-product commercialization

Laser tools for HBM packaging, dicing tools and ALD equipment for HMO are still being pushed toward commercialization. They become revenue only when customers adopt them in volume production and place new orders.

As the gap between a September 2026 estimate and nine-month cumulative operating profit of KRW 31.6 billion shows, estimates could diverge widely if new products are slow to reach revenue.

Balance sheet and payout sustainability

The debt-to-equity ratio rose to 85.3% in 2025 from 78.2% in 2024, with total liabilities of KRW 313.4 billion. Operating profit and attributable net income declined, and Q3 2026 turned into a loss.

Dividends and share cancellations continue, but the size of the next return policy is still under review, so its sustainability remains to be confirmed.

Overall view

Advanced Process Systems has seen cumulative new chip orders pass KRW 100 billion as of November 2026, which raises visibility on its shift toward chips.

On the other hand, DART-filed results show operating profit and attributable net income declining three years in a row from 2023, and the operating margin falling from 18.6% in 2022 to 7.2% in 2025.

Quarterly figures in 2026 also declined for two consecutive quarters after the Q1 peak, and Q3 turned into an attributable net loss of KRW 9.09 billion.

Strengths include chip orders recognized through 2027, a rising RTP share, new laser and ALD product lines, and a shareholder return policy covering dividends and share cancellations.

Weaknesses include reliance on OLED equipment, a low starting point with chips under 20% of revenue, the lag before orders convert to revenue, and quarterly earnings volatility.

The points where these threads meet are whether the operating margin rebounds in the 2026 annual report and how quickly orders convert into sales.

The gap between a September 2026 brokerage estimate of KRW 58.9 billion in 2026 operating profit and nine-month cumulative operating profit of KRW 31.6 billion shows how far apart interpretations can be. In short, order indicators have improved, but profitability has not yet confirmed a recovery.
