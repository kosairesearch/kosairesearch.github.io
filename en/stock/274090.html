02

Business structure

Kencoa Aerospace was founded in 2013 in Sacheon, Gyeongsangnam-do, and its core businesses are aerostructure and component manufacturing, special materials for space and aviation, and aircraft maintenance, repair and overhaul (MRO).

The company's investor materials list commercial and defense aircraft, freighter conversions, engines and space launch system components, and it is extending into advanced air mobility (AAM).

Production sites are split between Sacheon in Korea and Georgia and California in the United States, with the US subsidiary supplying US defense and space customers directly.

A September 2024 securities report put the airline MRO group, including PTF Conversion (passenger-to-freighter conversion), at about 44% of revenue and aerospace raw materials at about 23%, and said defense accounted for 46% of the backlog at that time.

Those are 2024 figures, so the latest segment mix should be checked in the annual report.

Per the company's website, customers include Boeing, Lockheed Martin, Gulfstream, Spirit AeroSystems, Pratt & Whitney, NASA, SpaceX and Blue Origin, alongside Korean Air, Korea Aerospace Industries, Hanwha Systems, Hanwha Aerospace and the ROK Air Force.

Domestic peers of similar profile include smaller aviation component makers such as Ains Sky and Controlmax. The company positions its US production and AS9100 certification as differentiators, and it works as a supplier to large Korean defense and aviation companies.

04

Earnings analysis

On a disclosed basis, revenue rose from KRW 75.9 billion in 2022 to KRW 91.1 billion in 2023, a 20.2% increase and the largest year in the period. It then fell 5.5% to KRW 86.2 billion in 2024 and 11.3% to KRW 76.4 billion in 2025, two consecutive years of decline.

Operating profit dropped 89.0% from KRW 7.99 billion in 2022 to KRW 877 million in 2023, and the company swung to an operating loss of KRW 5.35 billion in 2024. The operating loss widened to KRW 9.23 billion in 2025, so 2024 and 2025 were both loss years.

Operating margin fell from 10.5% in 2022 to 1.0% in 2023, -6.2% in 2024 and -12.1% in 2025. Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 3.24 billion in 2022, turned into a loss of KRW 1.85 billion in 2023, and then widened to a loss of KRW 7.71 billion in 2024 and KRW 15.5 billion in 2025.

Net profit including non-controlling interests showed the same path, with a net loss of KRW 15.5 billion in 2025. Operating cash flow was negative at KRW -3.84 billion in 2022, KRW -7.29 billion in 2023 and KRW -22.6 billion in 2024, before narrowing to KRW -617 million in 2025.

Quarterly revenue went from KRW 17.8 billion in the third quarter of 2025 to KRW 20.2 billion in the fourth quarter, then peaked at KRW 29.2 billion in the first quarter of 2026. It then declined to KRW 28.5 billion in the second quarter and KRW 26 billion in the third quarter, two consecutive quarters of decline.

Operating losses of KRW 4.31 billion and KRW 3.23 billion in the third and fourth quarters of 2025 gave way to operating income of KRW 218 million in the first quarter of 2026, KRW 1.18 billion in the second and KRW 833 million in the third.

Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 939 million in the first quarter of 2026 and KRW 333 million in the second, before a loss of KRW 3.57 billion in the third quarter. The last four quarters together show a net loss attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 12 billion.

Operating profit held up in every quarter of 2026 while the net line stayed negative in some quarters, so the gap between the operating and net lines is the point to track in the quarterly filings.

05

Industry analysis

In commercial aviation, the main drivers are passenger-to-freighter conversion (PTF Conversion) and maintenance demand.

A September 2024 securities report said delayed aircraft retirements linked to Boeing's quality issues were supporting conversion demand, and that PTF work had become a core business within two years of entering the market.

In space, the company's supply of components for the SLS launch system, a core part of the Artemis program, was reported to run through Boeing, and a company official said the realization of the SLS backlog was proceeding as planned.

In defense, the company's role as a defense offset trade partner is presented as a distinguishing feature. A January 2026 report mentioned a possible delivery of military transport aircraft parts worth roughly KRW 4 trillion, but this is a reported possibility rather than a confirmed contract.

There is a lag between order intake and revenue recognition. Customer-specific orders, production certification and delivery schedules directly affect when revenue is booked, so a stronger demand cycle does not translate into company results at a fixed pace.

Among domestic peers, Hanwha Aerospace, Korea Aerospace Industries, Hanwha Systems and Korean Air are both customers and large partners. The company says it works as a first-tier supplier to overseas customers, unlike many domestic aviation makers that sit at the second tier.

Against smaller component makers, its US production base and overseas quality certifications are relative strengths, while its market value and revenue scale are well below those of the large domestic defense and aviation groups.

06

Outlook

The company said the order backlog at the end of 2025 was over KRW 900 billion, and a January 2026 report projected that new orders in 2026 would exceed KRW 400 billion. That figure is a press projection, not an official annual guidance from the company.

On overseas orders, reports described a cooperation relationship with Embraer and a series of global aerospace quality certifications, including AS9100 and NADCAP.

A 2024 securities report expected production capacity to rise 1.5 times in 2025 and said a large expansion was underway, funded by investment from the private equity firm IMM. The speed at which the secured orders turn into revenue is the next point to watch.

Quarterly revenue has eased from KRW 29.2 billion in the first quarter of 2026 to KRW 28.5 billion and KRW 26 billion in the next two quarters, so how capacity additions and delivery schedules show up in quarterly sales is the key question.

When the fourth-quarter 2026 preliminary results are released, investors can check whether operating profit extends its run of positive quarters. In the 2026 annual report, the central items are whether annual revenue exceeds the KRW 76.4 billion recorded in 2025 and whether the operating loss turns into a profit.

The delivery timing of SLS-related components and the scale of convertible bond conversions disclosed alongside results also matter for share count and per-share value.

Because customer orders, certification and delivery schedules directly affect revenue recognition, a new order disclosure does not translate into quarterly results at once.

Bull factors

Large order backlog

A company official said the order backlog at the end of 2025 was over KRW 900 billion, which a report described as close to ten years of work. The company also said realization of its SLS backlog is proceeding as planned.

If these orders convert into revenue, they would provide a basis for growth well above the current revenue base.

Operating profit positive in recent quarters

Operating income turned positive at KRW 218 million in the first quarter of 2026 and stayed positive at KRW 1.18 billion in the second quarter and KRW 833 million in the third. Second-quarter operating income rose 443.6% from the prior quarter. This is a change from the operating losses reported in the third and fourth quarters of 2025.

Improved balance sheet

The debt ratio fell from 240.1% in 2022 to 45.2% in 2025. Total liabilities at the end of 2025 were KRW 72.5 billion against total equity of KRW 160.4 billion. A larger equity cushion gives the company more room to fund capacity investment and certification costs.

Bear factors

Prolonged net losses

Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders turned to a loss in 2023 and the loss widened in 2024 and 2025, reaching KRW 15.5 billion in 2025.

Over the last four quarters the net loss attributable to controlling shareholders totals KRW 12 billion, and the third quarter of 2026 still recorded a loss of KRW 3.57 billion. Operating profit has not yet carried through to the bottom line.

Negative operating cash flow

Operating cash flow was negative in every year from 2022 to 2025. It was weakest in 2024 at KRW -22.6 billion before narrowing to KRW -617 million in 2025. If cash generation does not improve, funding capacity expansion and working capital will depend more on outside financing.

Quarterly revenue slowdown

After peaking at KRW 29.2 billion in the first quarter of 2026, revenue fell to KRW 28.5 billion in the second quarter and KRW 26 billion in the third, two consecutive quarterly declines. Third-quarter revenue was up 46.7% from a year earlier but lower than the prior quarter.

Annual revenue also fell in both 2024 and 2025, so whether top-line recovery can be sustained is still unproven.

Risk factors

Pace of backlog conversion

Customer-specific orders, production certification and delivery schedules directly affect when revenue is recognized. Even with a large backlog, delays in certification or delivery push quarterly revenue later than expected. A high dependence on large programs such as SLS means schedule changes can flow through to earnings.

Dilution from convertible bonds

In April 2024, the private equity firm IMM acquired perpetual convertible bonds worth KRW 130 billion. A later disclosure of additional listings from conversion rights on domestic private CBs raised concerns about dilution and a larger free float. Conversion shares entering the market can affect both per-share value and supply and demand.

Share price volatility and liquidity

A report dated October 7, 2026 noted that daily trading volume exceeded 4 million shares, raising short-term turnover in the free float. When trading concentrates on one side, price swings can widen.

While earnings have not yet settled into a steady profit pattern, supply and demand factors can have an outsized effect on the share price.

12

Overall view

The current picture for Kencoa Aerospace has two sides. On one side are a backlog that the company official put at over KRW 900 billion at the end of 2025, a US production base, and a customer base spanning overseas aerospace and space companies.

On the other side are disclosed results showing net losses attributable to controlling shareholders since 2023, and a four-quarter net loss total of KRW 12 billion.

Operating profit has been positive in each quarter from the first to the third quarter of 2026, which supports a change in direction, but the return to a net loss in the third quarter shows that operating profit has not yet carried through to the bottom line.

Revenue has declined for two consecutive quarters from its first-quarter 2026 peak, and operating cash flow was negative in every year from 2022 to 2025. The debt ratio has come down to 45.2% in 2025, but dilution from convertible bond conversions remains a factor.

The next points to check are the preliminary fourth-quarter 2026 results, the 2026 annual report and the first-quarter 2027 results, with the speed at which orders turn into revenue and cash flow as the central question.
