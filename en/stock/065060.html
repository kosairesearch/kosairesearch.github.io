02

Business structure

Great New wave Coming was founded in 1997 to manufacture and sell apparel and accessories and listed on KOSDAQ in 2002. Based on a corporate information site's product list, the company runs its own brands including COVETBLAN, NONTYPE, TI FORMEN, THURSDAY ISLAND and TOINQ.

NONTYPE was launched in August 2024, the same month its own online shop opened. The same site's 2024 domestic and export breakdown shows the main brands' sales were recorded entirely in Korea.

The consolidated group includes a trading subsidiary in Ningbo, China (100% owned) and an investment-related affiliate, CU Capital Partners (39.10% stake, as of December 2024). The revenue split by brand and by channel is a point to verify in the detailed items of the periodic report.

On the distribution side, the channel mix is moving quickly from department stores toward outlets and online. The customer base is centered on domestic consumers, and the backdrop for demand is a prolonged domestic slowdown plus weaker consumer sentiment from high interest rates and inflation.

On competition, low-priced products from Chinese e-commerce platforms are cited as a factor intensifying price competition in casual apparel. Listed peers in the same sector include Youngone, F&F, LF, Handsome and Shinsegae International, so comparisons should account for differences in business structure.

04

Earnings analysis

On an annual basis, revenue was KRW 147 billion in 2023 (up 2.6% year on year), the largest year in the 2022–2025 period, followed by KRW 121.2 billion in 2024 (down 17.5%) and KRW 110.9 billion in 2025 (down 8.5%), making 2025 the smallest year.

Operating results moved from an operating loss of KRW 2.9 billion in 2022 to operating profit of KRW 7.9 billion in 2023, then back to an operating loss of KRW 3.93 billion in 2024 and KRW 409 million in 2025, a smaller loss.

The operating margin was -2.0% in 2022, 5.4% in 2023, -3.2% in 2024 and -0.4% in 2025, with 2023 the only positive-margin year in the period.

Shareholder net income of KRW 1.45 billion in 2023 became a net loss of KRW 8.2 billion in 2024 and KRW 8.55 billion in 2025, two consecutive loss years with a wider loss in 2025.

Net income including non-controlling interests was KRW 1.45 billion in 2023, a net loss of KRW 8.2 billion in 2024 and a net loss of KRW 8.55 billion in 2025, and it should be read separately from the shareholder figure.

On a quarterly basis, the third quarter of 2025 had revenue of KRW 25.6 billion and operating profit of KRW 266 million, then the fourth quarter had revenue of KRW 29.3 billion (up 14.5% from the prior quarter) and an operating loss of KRW 25.08 million.

The first quarter of 2026 had revenue of KRW 22.5 billion and an operating loss of KRW 1.1 billion, a wider loss. The second quarter of 2026 had revenue of KRW 31.7 billion (up 41.0% from the prior quarter) and operating profit of KRW 4.31 billion.

The third quarter of 2026 had revenue of KRW 22.5 billion (down 29.2% from the prior quarter and down 12.1% year on year) and an operating loss of KRW 1.39 billion.

Operating results and shareholder net income moved in different directions across quarters, with positive shareholder net income of KRW 2.58 billion in the second quarter and KRW 1.31 billion in the third quarter, so how much non-operating items contributed is a point to check in the periodic report notes. Operating cash flow turned positive at KRW 1.13 billion in 2025 from negative KRW 7.73 billion in 2024.

Industry analysis

The consumer backdrop is described as prolonged domestic weakness combined with high interest rates and inflation, which has dampened consumer sentiment.

Distribution is shifting quickly from department stores to outlets and online, so apparel companies that built sales around department stores face a channel reshuffle.

A corporate information site's first-quarter 2026 earnings commentary notes that raw material costs rose on the Middle East war and currency swings continue to pressure costs. The same source says competition intensified because of low-priced offerings from Chinese e-commerce platforms.

On the cycle, Great New wave Coming had operating profit in 2023 but weaker revenue and results in both 2024 and 2025, and the second-quarter 2026 return to operating profit is a rebound at the quarterly level.

Whether that rebound came from revenue recovery or cost improvement is a point to check in the quarterly detail of the periodic report.

Against peers such as Youngone, Handsome and F&F, direct comparison is limited because brand structures and export shares differ, and Great New wave Coming is distinct for its domestically dependent brand portfolio. Operating margin is the profitability measure that matches the reported figures.

Because channel shifts and cost pressure are happening at the same time, improvement signals should be confirmed through the operating margin trend over several quarters.

Profit returned in Q2 2026

Second-quarter 2026 revenue of KRW 31.7 billion rose 41.0% from the prior quarter, and operating profit of KRW 4.31 billion marked a return to profit. Cash generation and lower leverage

Risk factors

Financial and capital structure

The company decided on November 16, 2026 to acquire KRW 2.5 billion in face value of the 16th convertible bonds issued privately on September 10, 2025, before maturity. The consideration of KRW 2.715 billion exceeds the face value. The bonds mature on September 10, 2029.

Because non-controlling interests make up a large share of equity relative to KRW 40.8 billion in shareholder equity, the capital base may have limited room to absorb continued losses.

Channel shift and consumer environment

A prolonged domestic slowdown and high interest rates with inflation are weighing on consumer sentiment. As sales shift from department stores to outlets and online, brand-level sales must be reallocated.

Because the main brands were recorded entirely in Korea in 2024, the company is directly exposed to any domestic consumption slowdown.

Costs, currency and competition

Raw material costs have risen on the Middle East war and currency swings are pressuring costs. Low-priced products from Chinese e-commerce platforms are intensifying price competition.

If costs cannot be passed through to selling prices, the operating margin could stay slightly negative as in 2025 or deteriorate further.
