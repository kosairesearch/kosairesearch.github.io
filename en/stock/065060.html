GNCO Turns Profitable in Q2, Yet Annual Losses Still Loom

GNCO swung to operating profit and net profit attributable to owners in Q2 2026 both quarter-on-quarter and year-on-year, but two straight years of annual operating losses in 2024-2025 and repeated capital transactions leave financial burdens still worth watching.

02

Business structure

GNCO (Great New wave Coming), founded in 1997 and listed on KOSDAQ in 2002, is a casual apparel specialist operating a multi-brand portfolio that includes Thursday Island, Nontype, PLADS7, Etaffe, and TOINQ.

Its product range spans comfortable casual wear, boundary-crossing genre fashion, and niche perfume and lifestyle beauty items, reflecting a niche strategy aimed at specific consumer segments.

Distribution is centered on department store channels, supplemented by experiential pop-up marketing to reinforce offline touchpoints.

Subsidiaries include a new-technology venture finance company, a North America-focused fashion distribution unit, an Isuzu commercial vehicle distribution unit, and a trading subsidiary based in Ningbo, China, spreading the business beyond core apparel into finance, mobility, and overseas distribution.

The controlling shareholder is Creo SG, which, including special related parties, reported a 57.78% stake as of an April 2026 filing. A notable feature of the governance structure is the repeated participation of Curo Holdings-affiliated related parties in rights offerings and debt-to-equity conversions.

Within the same industry sit large fashion names such as Handsome, LF, and Shinsegae International alongside smaller listed peers such as Chokwang Leather and AGABANG&COMPANY, placing GNCO as a relatively small-cap mid-tier casual specialist.

In 2025 the company decided on a KRW 18 billion rights offering and subsequently signaled expanded marketing investment built on offline performance including department-store pop-up sales.

This business structure means both the cyclicality of the core apparel business and the volatility of non-apparel subsidiaries feed into consolidated results.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual revenue rose from KRW 143.3 billion in 2022 to a peak of KRW 147 billion in 2023, then declined for two consecutive years to KRW 121.2 billion in 2024 and KRW 110.9 billion in 2025.

Operating profit swung from an operating loss of KRW 2.9 billion in 2022 to an operating profit of KRW 7.9 billion in 2023, before reverting to an operating loss of KRW 3.93 billion in 2024 and continuing in loss at KRW 409 million in 2025, though the loss size narrowed.

Net profit attributable to owners likewise turned profitable at KRW 1.45 billion in 2023 before posting net losses attributable to owners of KRW 8.2 billion in 2024 and KRW 8.55 billion in 2025, with the loss widening in the latter year.

On a quarterly basis, operating loss of KRW 484 million in Q2 2025 turned into operating profit of KRW 266 million in Q3, reverted to an operating loss of KRW 25.08 million in Q4, and widened further to an operating loss of KRW 1.1 billion in Q1 2026, reflecting significant quarter-to-quarter swings.

In Q2 2026, revenue reached KRW 31.7 billion (up 41.0% quarter-on-quarter and up 2.0% year-on-year), accompanied by operating profit of KRW 4.31 billion and net profit attributable to owners of KRW 2.58 billion, both turning positive quarter-on-quarter and year-on-year.

The trailing four-quarter (Q3 2025 through Q2 2026) cumulative net loss attributable to owners was KRW 2.43 billion, markedly smaller than the full-year 2025 net loss attributable to owners of KRW 8.55 billion.

Operating cash flow improved from negative KRW 5.41 billion in 2022 to positive KRW 16.3 billion in 2023, deteriorated again to negative KRW 7.73 billion in 2024, and turned slightly positive at KRW 1.13 billion in 2025.

The debt-to-equity ratio rose from 83.1% in 2022 to 88.7% in 2024 before easing to 82.3% in 2025, with total equity of KRW 128.3 billion at end-2025 including KRW 40.8 billion attributable to owners of the parent.

05

Industry analysis

Korea's fashion and apparel sector is sensitive to shifts in domestic consumption sentiment, and mid-tier department-store-centric casual brands face competitive pressure from both large fashion conglomerates and online platforms.

While large listed players such as Handsome, LF, and Shinsegae International lead the market with multi-brand portfolios and distribution networks, smaller companies such as GNCO pursue differentiation through niche brands and experiential marketing.

Persistent cost pressure from raw materials and labor is a sector-wide challenge for margin defense. The company itself cited a slowdown in the apparel market and weakening consumer spending sentiment as a backdrop for its 2025 capital raising.

Fashion as a sector tends to rebound during consumption recovery phases of the cycle, but there is currently limited evidence that the broader industry has entered a clear upturn.

Expansion into niche perfume and lifestyle beauty items can be read as an attempt to reduce reliance on traditional apparel revenue, potentially serving as a point of differentiation versus peers.

06

Outlook

The company allocated all proceeds from its 2025 rights offering to repaying convertible bonds, and in May 2026 it decided on an additional third-party allotment to controlling shareholder Creo SG and affiliates aimed at balance-sheet improvement through debt-to-equity conversion.

Around the same time it completed a 5-for-1 share consolidation that adjusted par value from KRW 500 to KRW 2,500, reducing the number of outstanding shares.

On the operating side, the company has signaled expanded marketing investment built on the performance of experiential offline marketing such as department-store pop-ups, alongside continued efforts to improve profitability through AI-driven inventory optimization.

In Q1 2026, consolidated revenue fell 9.9% year-on-year and the operating loss widened sharply, but Q2 showed improvement in both revenue and profit, signaling a potential rebound amid continued quarterly volatility.

Whether this improvement translates into a full-year swing to profit, however, has not yet been confirmed through periodic disclosures.

While repeated capital transactions such as rights offerings and share consolidations contribute to balance-sheet repair, equity dilution and trading volatility tied to concentrated minority-account activity remain variables to monitor.

Key points to watch going forward include whether the operating profit trend persists in coming quarters and whether balance-sheet improvement efforts translate into reduced interest expense burden.

Bull factors

Q2 Turnaround Signal

Q2 2026 revenue of KRW 31.7 billion, operating profit of KRW 4.31 billion, and net profit attributable to owners of KRW 2.58 billion all turned positive both quarter-on-quarter and year-on-year. This stands out as the clearest improvement signal amid the quarterly volatility seen since Q2 2025.

Whether this pattern continues into subsequent quarters will be a key indicator of the earnings trajectory ahead.

Ongoing Balance-Sheet Repair

Proceeds from the 2025 rights offering were used entirely to repay convertible bonds, and a further rights offering in May 2026 aimed at debt-to-equity conversion continued efforts to reduce liabilities.

The debt-to-equity ratio eased from 88.7% in 2024 to 82.3% in 2025, and operating cash flow turned positive at KRW 1.13 billion in 2025. The cumulative effect of these measures could potentially ease interest expense burdens over time.

Diversified Business Base

Beyond core apparel, revenue sources are spread across subsidiaries in new-technology venture finance, North American fashion distribution, Isuzu commercial vehicle sales, and a China trading unit.

The expansion of the brand portfolio into niche perfume and lifestyle items also reduces reliance on traditional apparel sales. However, segment-level revenue breakdowns are not disclosed in detail in periodic reports, which limits interpretation.

Bear factors

Revenue Declined for Two Straight Years

Revenue declined for two consecutive years after peaking at KRW 147 billion in 2023, falling to KRW 110.9 billion in 2025. Weakening consumer sentiment and a slowdown in the apparel market were cited directly by the company as backdrop in its capital-raising disclosures. When the revenue base itself contracts, cost cuts alone may be insufficient to defend profitability.

Repeated Capital Transactions and Dilution

Capital structure changes were frequent, including a 90% capital reduction in 2025, two rights offerings, and a May 2026 share consolidation plus an additional third-party allotment. Throughout this process the stake of the controlling shareholder and related parties rose as debt-to-equity conversions recurred.

While frequent capital transactions can aid balance-sheet repair, they can also impose dilution burdens on existing shareholders.

Continued Losses and a History of Widening Deficits

Operating profit posted losses for two consecutive years in 2024 and 2025, and net profit attributable to owners also recorded losses over the same two years, with the loss widening in 2025. Quarterly results showed continued losses in Q4 2025 and Q1 2026, reflecting significant volatility.

Whether the Q2 2026 swing to profit is a one-off or a sustained trend still requires further confirmation through subsequent periodic reports.

Risk factors

Consumer Spending Risk

Apparel consumption is sensitive to disposable income and consumer sentiment, and a prolonged slowdown in the end market could delay revenue recovery. The company itself referenced a slump in the apparel market and weakening spending sentiment in its 2025 capital-raising disclosure.

Given heavy reliance on department-store channels, the business is exposed to shifts in offline consumption trends.

Financial and Liquidity Risk

The debt-to-equity ratio has remained in the mid-80% range, and recent years have seen repeated rights offerings tied to convertible bond and debt repayment. Operating cash flow has swung between positive and negative year to year, and the possibility of renewed fundraising needs cannot be ruled out. The fact that past fundraising has typically involved equity dilution is also worth noting.

Trading and Governance Risk

Multiple investment-caution designations in 2026 tied to concentrated minority-account trading indicate that the share price can be sensitive to trading-driven factors.

The pattern of the controlling shareholder and related parties repeatedly raising their stake through debt-to-equity conversions leaves open the possibility of further equity changes ahead.

The existence of intercompany debt guarantees and fund transfers within the affiliated group structure also merits continued attention.

Overall view

GNCO experienced revenue peaking and a brief profit in 2023 followed by two consecutive years of declining revenue and operating losses in 2024-2025, yet showed signs of change in Q2 2026 when revenue, operating profit, and net profit attributable to owners all turned positive both quarter-on-quarter and year-on-year.

Along the way, the company underwent a series of capital structure changes including a 90% capital reduction, two rights offerings, and a May 2026 share consolidation plus an additional third-party allotment, which contributed to balance-sheet repair while also leaving dilution burdens.

The debt-to-equity ratio fluctuated in the 80% range and operating cash flow direction varied year to year, so it remains premature to characterize financial stability as on a clearly improving trend.

On valuation, the price-to-book ratio sits below 1x and the dividend yield remains near zero, while a net loss over the trailing four quarters makes conventional price-to-earnings comparisons difficult.

Diversification through a niche brand portfolio and non-apparel subsidiaries could be a strength, but the two-year decline in the revenue base itself and the history of repeated capital transactions are factors to weigh alongside it.

Key points to watch going forward are whether the Q2 improvement trend continues in subsequent quarters and whether balance-sheet repair efforts translate into an actual easing of interest expense burdens.
