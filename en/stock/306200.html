Report overview

Profit returns on North American tubular demand; FX and cost lag are swing factors

SeAH Steel posted positive operating profit in each quarter from the first to the third of 2026, supported by North American tubular demand, but 2025 operating profit of KRW 49.6 billion remains far below the KRW 231.9 billion recorded in 2023.

02

Business structure

SeAH Steel is a domestic steel pipe maker whose core business is tubular products. Korea Ratings puts its share of domestic output at around 15% by production volume and says it remains the largest domestic producer.

Its main products are line pipe, structural pipe, large-diameter pipe, OCTG (oil country tubular goods) for oil and gas development, and oil transmission pipe.

In the first quarter of 2026, consolidated sales were KRW 448.6 billion, of which tubular product sales were KRW 417.8 billion and merchandise and other sales were KRW 30.8 billion.

Within tubular product sales in the same quarter, domestic sales were KRW 200.3 billion and export sales were KRW 217.5 billion, so exports exceeded domestic sales. North America, including Canada, accounted for 36.7% of consolidated sales in 2024, and the United States is the largest export market.

Because high-value export items such as OCTG and oil transmission pipe carry this exposure, US trade policy directly affects results. Hanwha Investment & Securities said North America's share of total sales fell from 47.2% in the second quarter of 2026 to 37.0% in the third quarter.

The largest shareholder is the holding company SeAH Steel Holdings, which held 50.1% at the end of 2024. Overseas tubular manufacturing and sales units stayed with the holding company after the 2018 spin-off, which Korea Ratings flagged as limiting SeAH Steel's business scope to domestic tubular operations. Standalone results therefore depend on how the domestic entity manages export volumes and selling prices.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual results declined for two consecutive years after the 2023 peak, with the decline running from 2024 through 2025. Sales rose from KRW 1.8 trillion in 2022 to KRW 1.86 trillion in 2023 (up 3.3% year on year), then fell to KRW 1.81 trillion in 2024 (down 2.8%) and KRW 1.48 trillion in 2025 (down 17.9%).

Operating profit moved from KRW 215.2 billion in 2022 to KRW 231.9 billion in 2023 (up 7.8%), then KRW 202.9 billion in 2024 (down 12.5%) and KRW 49.6 billion in 2025 (down 75.6%). Among 2022 to 2025, 2025 was the smallest year.

The operating margin went from 11.9% in 2022 and 12.5% in 2023 to 11.2% in 2024 and 3.3% in 2025.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders fell from KRW 188.8 billion in 2023 to KRW 137.1 billion in 2024 and KRW 30 billion in 2025, and net income including non-controlling interests was also KRW 30 billion in 2025. Operating cash flow went from KRW 299.9 billion in 2024 to -KRW 108.3 billion in 2025.

On a quarterly basis, the operating loss of KRW 5.19 billion in the third quarter of 2025 turned into operating profit of KRW 8.28 billion in the fourth quarter, then rose to KRW 24.1 billion in the first quarter of 2026 (up 191.2% from the prior quarter) and KRW 34.9 billion in the second quarter (up 44.9%).

In the third quarter of 2026, sales were KRW 477.2 billion (down 3.6% from the prior quarter and up 43.8% from a year earlier), while operating profit was KRW 20.2 billion (down 42.1% from the prior quarter).

Hanwha Investment & Securities said the won strengthened 5.4% while dollar-denominated export prices rose about 6%, leaving won-converted export prices roughly flat from the prior quarter.

The same report said high-priced hot-rolled coil bought in the second quarter entered production with a lag, compressing the spread on domestic sales. Net loss attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 2.78 billion in the third quarter, a swing from net profit of KRW 32.5 billion in the second quarter.

Securities analysts cited domestic construction weakness and the US 50% steel tariff as reasons for the weak fourth quarter of 2025.

05

Industry analysis

Industry analysts describe the global steel cycle as weighed down by weak end-demand and oversupply, which has intensified competition. Within this environment, SeAH Steel's tubular business improved on North American energy demand and data-center demand.

Company officials expect US prices for oil country tubular goods to keep rising. The company also expects demand for structural and stainless pipe used in data-center construction to expand. In the Middle East, restrictions on Strait of Hormuz transit have partly eased, but logistics uncertainty persists.

The United States applies a 50% tariff on steel and aluminum products under Section 232 of the Trade Expansion Act, and anti-dumping and other import restrictions are tightening, which keeps export volumes volatile.

On the cycle position, Hanwha Investment & Securities said US tubular prices may stay at high levels rather than fall sharply after a peak, which is a forecast rather than a settled fact. Korea Ratings says the company remains the largest domestic producer by production volume, with a share of around 15%.

Its differentiation within the sector lies in a higher mix of high-value energy products such as OCTG and oil transmission pipe. Domestic construction demand remains weak, which limits the scope for a recovery in domestic volumes.

06

Outlook

Holding company SeAH Steel Holdings expects demand for OCTG and oil transmission pipe to remain firm in the fourth quarter of 2026, driven by North American energy infrastructure investment.

It expects US prices for oil country tubular goods to keep rising and demand for structural and stainless pipe used in data-center construction to expand. The company said it plans to pursue selective sales strategies that weigh profitability by product in response to changes in US trade conditions.

Hanwha Investment & Securities estimates fourth-quarter 2026 operating profit at KRW 26.2 billion, up 29.7% from the prior quarter. It expects lower costs for hot-rolled coil bought with a lag and the higher US tubular prices seen since the third quarter to flow into export selling prices.

The same firm raised its 2027 operating profit estimate from KRW 108.2 billion to KRW 114.4 billion, reflecting a higher US hot-rolled coil price forecast. The same report, however, flagged the risk from follow-on project orders and downside risk from the exchange rate.

Fourth-quarter 2026 results are expected to be confirmed in a preliminary release in early 2027, and full-year 2026 results will be disclosed in the annual report.

08

Bull factors

Higher North American OCTG prices

Third-quarter 2026 sales were KRW 477.2 billion, up 43.8% from a year earlier, as both volumes and prices rose for North American OCTG. Operating profit of KRW 20.2 billion swung to profit from a year earlier.

Holding company SeAH Steel Holdings announced on November 4 that SeAH Steel's standalone operating profit for the third quarter was KRW 18.0 billion, a turn to profit.

Energy and data-center demand expected to persist

Holding company SeAH Steel Holdings expects demand for North American OCTG and oil transmission pipe to stay firm in the fourth quarter. It also expects demand for structural and stainless pipe used in data-center construction to grow. A company official expects US OCTG price increases to continue.

Cost lag easing and estimate upgrades

Hanwha Investment & Securities estimates fourth-quarter 2026 operating profit at KRW 26.2 billion and points to lower costs for hot-rolled coil entering production with a lag. The same firm raised its 2027 operating profit estimate from KRW 108.2 billion to KRW 114.4 billion. It attributed the change to a higher US hot-rolled coil price forecast.

09

Bear factors

Limits of price pass-through and FX

Hanwha Investment & Securities analyzed that the won strengthened 5.4% while dollar export prices rose about 6%, leaving won-converted prices roughly flat from the prior quarter. Domestic selling prices rose 8.5%, yet profitability worsened because of the hot-rolled coil cost lag. Third-quarter 2026 operating profit of KRW 20.2 billion fell 42.1% from the prior quarter.

US trade risk

The United States applies a 50% tariff on steel and aluminum products under Section 232 of the Trade Expansion Act. SeAH Steel names North America as its largest export region and said the tariff level, customer negotiations and volume shifts remain uncertainties in its financial estimates. As of its first-quarter 2026 report, the financial effect of the tariff had not been quantified.

Earnings volatility and a swing to loss

Operating profit of KRW 49.6 billion in 2025 is far below the KRW 231.9 billion peak in 2023. The third-quarter 2026 net loss attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 2.78 billion followed net profit of KRW 32.5 billion in the second quarter.

North America's share of sales also dropped from 47.2% to 37.0% between those quarters, signaling a shift in the export mix.

10

Risk factors

Trade and tariffs

The 50% Section 232 tariff and anti-dumping measures directly target the company's largest export channel. Americas sales accounted for 36.7% of consolidated sales in 2024. Export volumes and selling prices could swing with tariff levels and customer negotiations.

Currency

A stronger won offsets dollar-based price gains when converted into won-denominated results. Hanwha Investment & Securities cited the exchange-rate decline as a main cause of the weak third quarter.

The same report used follow-on project wins and downside exchange-rate risk as grounds for lowering its target price-to-book multiple.

Raw material and price cycle

The lag between hot-rolled coil costs and selling prices drives profitability. If US tubular prices pass their peak, selling prices could adjust quickly, and demand is tied to oil and gas investment cycles. Middle East logistics uncertainty could also affect export delivery schedules and costs.

12

Overall view

Since the 2023 peak, SeAH Steel's sales, operating profit and net income attributable to controlling shareholders declined through 2025, and the 2025 operating margin fell to 3.3%.

In 2026, higher North American OCTG prices and data-center tubular demand led a recovery, and operating profit was positive in the first, second and third quarters. However, third-quarter operating profit fell from the second quarter, and net income attributable to controlling shareholders swung to a loss.

The stronger won and the lag in passing through hot-rolled coil costs were cited as third-quarter margin drags. Positive factors include North American demand and prices, the company's fourth-quarter demand outlook, and the operating profit recovery that Hanwha Investment & Securities estimates for the fourth quarter.

Negative factors include exchange rates, trade variables such as the US 50% tariff, and large earnings volatility. Both sides carry comparable weight, and it is difficult to conclude that the balance has tilted one way.

Whether the earnings recovery holds can be checked against the fourth-quarter 2026 preliminary results and the operating margin in the 2026 annual report.
