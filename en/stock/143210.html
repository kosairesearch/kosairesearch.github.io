Report overview

Shrinking Sales and Audit Risk Weigh on Wheel Maker Hands

Hands has posted operating and net losses in every year from 2022 to 2025, its revenue has fallen for three straight quarters through Q3 2026, and an audit-opinion dispute now sits at the center of the outlook.

  1. 1

    Annual revenue reached KRW 784.8 billion in 2025, the highest of 2022 to 2025, yet the company still booked an operating loss of KRW 38.7 billion, extending a four-year run of losses.

  2. 2

    Q3 2026 revenue was KRW 129.7 billion, down 27.7% year on year and down for three consecutive quarters from the previous quarter.

  3. 3

    Operating profit turned positive in Q2 2026 at KRW 9.52 billion, but Q3 returned to an operating loss of KRW 16.2 billion.

  4. 4

    At the end of 2025, total equity stood at KRW 93.4 billion against total liabilities of KRW 678.3 billion, a debt ratio of 726.2%.

  5. 5

    Press reports said the company received a disclaimer of opinion in its latest audit report, which has put going-concern questions at the center of the debate.

02

Business structure

Hands manufactures aluminum wheels for automobiles, and aluminum wheels account for 100% of its business as of Q2 2026 revenue according to one aggregator. In 2024, aluminum wheel sales were split 19.51% domestic and 80.49% export, so overseas sales make up more than 80% of the total.

Its main customers are global automakers including Hyundai Motor Group, Renault-Nissan Group and GM Group, supplied on an OEM basis. Production sites are spread across Incheon headquarters, the Hwaseong Hands 6 plant, Qingdao Dongwha Casting in China and HANDS 8 S.A. in Morocco.

The company reportedly holds in-house mold design and two-cavity casting equipment capabilities. Reports cite the company as the number one domestic aluminum wheel maker and in the global top six, but these reports are dated.

On competition, its 2024 annual report cited intensifying competition from Chinese products as a factor behind weak operating results. As of the end of March 2025, the largest shareholder, CEO Seung Hyun-chang, held 40.51%, and related parties held 60.10%. R&D spending was 0.38% of revenue in 2023 and 0.34% in 2024.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual revenue fell from KRW 778.3 billion in 2022 to KRW 749.5 billion in 2023, down 3.7% year on year, then rose to KRW 764.4 billion in 2024, up 2.0%, and to KRW 784.8 billion in 2025, up 2.7%.

Revenue has grown for two straight years since the 2023 low, with 2025 the largest year and 2023 the smallest among 2022 to 2025. Operating losses were KRW 66.7 billion in 2022, KRW 21.5 billion in 2023, KRW 46.5 billion in 2024 and KRW 38.7 billion in 2025, a four-year run of losses.

Operating margins were -8.6% in 2022, -2.9% in 2023, -6.1% in 2024 and -4.9% in 2025, so higher sales did not translate into steadily narrowing losses.

Press coverage attributed the 2023 narrowing to lower selling and administrative expenses, while the company cited rising cost relative to selling prices and high SG&A in 2024.

Net losses attributable to controlling shareholders were KRW 77.8 billion in 2022, KRW 33.9 billion in 2023, KRW 121.5 billion in 2024 and KRW 88.8 billion in 2025, while the 2024 net loss including non-controlling interests was KRW 122.1 billion, a different figure.

The debt ratio rose to 726.2% at the end of 2025 from 343.0% in 2022 as equity was eroded by losses. Operating cash flow was KRW 2.43 billion in 2022, KRW 57.4 billion in 2023, KRW 22.9 billion in 2024 and KRW 41.5 billion in 2025.

On a quarterly basis, revenue peaked at KRW 185.1 billion in Q4 2025 and then fell to KRW 180.6 billion in Q1 2026, KRW 156.8 billion in Q2 and KRW 129.7 billion in Q3.

Operating results moved from a loss of KRW 9.05 billion in Q1 2026 to a profit of KRW 9.52 billion in Q2, then back to a loss of KRW 16.2 billion in Q3. Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 22.96 million in Q2 2026 and KRW 2.43 billion in Q3, a 10,482.8% increase from the prior quarter.

The four quarters from Q4 2025 to Q3 2026 together produced a net loss attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 85 billion. In Q4 2025, the net loss of KRW 55.2 billion far exceeded the operating loss of KRW 10.5 billion, which points to a large non-operating or one-off impact in that quarter.

05

Industry analysis

Demand for aluminum wheels is shifting toward higher-value products as automakers push premium models and SUVs and luxury vehicles drive demand for large-inch wheels. The company said in its cumulative 2025 third-quarter commentary that it is expanding capacity for high-spec products.

According to a Korea Ratings assessment from June 2025, revenue has held around KRW 750 billion since 2022 on a recovery in automaker demand and price pass-through.

The same assessment said that, given a high cost-of-sales ratio and losses at the Moroccan unit, a meaningful earnings recovery in the short term would be difficult. On costs, higher transportation, fuel and power costs were cited as pressures on profitability.

Intensifying competition from Chinese products is another factor behind weak operating results. Tariff uncertainty increased in early 2026 after a US Supreme Court ruling that the global reciprocal tariff system was unlawful.

With exports above 80% of aluminum wheel sales, the business is directly exposed to tariffs and changes in overseas automaker production. Because the company is concentrated in a single product, it bears raw material prices and the automaker production cycle directly. The cycle appears to be in a phase where revenue is holding up but profit conversion remains limited.

Outlook

Company-issued annual targets and order backlog data were not available for this report. The confirmed direction is cost adjustment and pricing.

In earlier press reports, the company said it was negotiating product price increases with customers to offset rising raw material and logistics costs, and was considering contract clauses to recover losses from raw material price swings.

Whether the Q2 2026 operating profit and the Q3 return to loss persist will show whether these measures are working. Korea Ratings and NICE Credit Rating both carried a B- rating as of March 2026. Reports of production adjustments and fixed-cost cuts in early 2026 should be read alongside the revenue decline.

Whether the three-quarter revenue decline extends into Q4 2026 is the next item to watch. Whether the audit opinion returns to unqualified and whether the going-concern language remains will be checked in the 2026 annual report. Whether operating margins hold in positive territory is the next checkpoint in the upcoming periodic report.

08

Bull factors

Demand for high-value large wheels

According to a Wisereport company commentary, demand for large-inch wheels for SUVs and premium vehicles is raising the share of higher-value products. The same source recorded revenue growth from expanded supply to major customers including Hyundai Motor Group, Renault-Nissan Group and GM Group.

The company is expanding capacity for high-spec products. Whether this translates into better operating margins is a point for the next periodic report.

Operating profit in Q2 2026

Operating profit in Q2 2026 was KRW 9.52 billion, turning positive from the prior quarter, and net profit attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 22.96 million. Korea Ratings recorded an earlier operating turnaround in Q1 2025 that came from new customer wins and price increases.

However, Q3 2026 returned to an operating loss of KRW 16.2 billion, so the durability of the profit has yet to be tested.

Price increases and cost pass-through

In earlier press reports, the company said it was negotiating product price increases with customers to offset higher raw material and logistics costs. It was also reportedly considering contract clauses to recover losses from raw material price swings.

Reports also cite its number one domestic position and standing in the global top six. Whether these adjustments are reflected in results during the 2026 revenue decline will be confirmed in later filings.

Bear factors

Four straight years of losses and shrinking equity

Operating losses and net losses attributable to controlling shareholders have persisted from 2022 through 2025. Total equity stood at KRW 93.4 billion at the end of 2025, and the debt ratio rose to 726.2%. Accumulated losses have reduced financial headroom while borrowing burdens have grown. Korea Ratings flagged a heavy financial burden and high liquidity risk as key concerns.

Three straight quarters of revenue decline

Revenue peaked at KRW 185.1 billion in Q4 2025, then fell to KRW 180.6 billion in Q1 2026, KRW 156.8 billion in Q2 and KRW 129.7 billion in Q3. Q3 2026 revenue was down 27.7% year on year. The same quarter returned to an operating loss of KRW 16.2 billion. A shrinking sales base makes it harder to sustain profitability, which is a bearish factor.

Audit opinion and listing-maintenance risk

Press reports said the company received a disclaimer of opinion in its latest audit report, after which trading in its shares was reportedly suspended. Coverage treated going-concern uncertainty as the central issue.

Intensifying competition from Chinese products and the loss-making structure at the Moroccan unit were also cited as weaknesses. Whether trading resumes and the listing is maintained will be shown in later filings.

Risk factors

Financial and liquidity

According to a Newsworker analysis, the current ratio fell from 63.2% in 2022 to 51% in 2024, which leaves less room to meet short-term debt. Viewed together with a debt ratio of 726.2% and total equity of KRW 93.4 billion, financing costs and debt repayment could limit the pace of any earnings recovery. Changes in borrowing structure and cash flow should be checked in the next periodic report.

Tariffs and export exposure

Exports accounted for 80.49% of aluminum wheel sales in 2024. Reports say tariff uncertainty increased in early 2026 after a US Supreme Court ruling on the global reciprocal tariff system. A shift in overseas automaker production or trade policy could affect both export volumes and prices. The effect of the tariff environment on revenue should be checked in later quarterly results.

Plant operations and facilities

Reports said a factory fire in 2025 halted production for more than a month and disrupted supply. Korea Ratings noted that yield improvement has been slow at the Moroccan plant. Any renewed halt or facility problem could affect both revenue and costs. Whether production has normalized and what investment plans are in place should be checked in upcoming filings.

Overall view

Hands recorded operating losses in each year from 2022 to 2025, and its 2025 revenue of KRW 784.8 billion was the highest of the period, yet its operating margin was -4.9%. Q2 2026 posted an operating profit of KRW 9.52 billion, but Q3 returned to an operating loss of KRW 16.2 billion.

Over the same stretch, revenue fell for three straight quarters, and Q3 was down 27.7% year on year. Financially, total equity of KRW 93.4 billion and a debt ratio of 726.2% at the end of 2025 indicate a heavy burden.

Bullish factors include rising demand for large-inch wheels, expanded supply to overseas automakers, and efforts to raise prices. Bearish factors include continued losses, financial strain, tariff uncertainty and competition from China, and the reported disclaimer of opinion.

The next checkpoints are the audit opinion and going-concern language in the 2026 annual report, and the operating margin in the Q1 2027 report.
