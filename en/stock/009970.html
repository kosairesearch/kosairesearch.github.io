Operating profit recovers while Q3 net income to parent falls sharply

02

Business structure

Youngone Holdings was founded in 1974 to manufacture and sell outdoor and sportswear, and since its 2009 spin-off it has operated as a pure holding company that owns stakes in subsidiaries. Its profit tracks subsidiary results, and dividends received from subsidiaries are the main source of income.

The core subsidiary Youngone makes and exports outdoor and sportswear on an OEM basis. The company said growth in the first half of 2026 was also led by this OEM business.

Production bases are spread across Bangladesh and Vietnam, and the company credits this diversified footprint and stable delivery capability with sustaining long-term partnerships with global brands. The domestic outdoor retail unit runs The North Face brand, so OEM and brand distribution sit together within the group.

Since 2015 the group has moved into global distribution through its stake in SCOTT, the Swiss bicycle brand. The SCOTT segment still posted an operating loss in 1Q 2026 but narrowed the loss sharply from a year earlier.

On the customer side, orders for Amer Sports, which operates Arc'teryx, increased and lifted Youngone's order intake.

04

Earnings analysis

On a disclosed basis, annual revenue fell from KRW 4.53 trillion in 2022 to KRW 4.36 trillion in 2023 (down 3.9% year on year) and to KRW 4.31 trillion in 2024 (down 1.1%), before recovering to KRW 4.9 trillion in 2025 (up 13.7%). Within 2022-2025, 2025 was the largest year for revenue and 2024 the smallest.

Operating profit fell from KRW 1 trillion in 2022 to KRW 872.6 billion in 2023 (down 12.9%) and KRW 517 billion in 2024 (down 40.7%), then rose to KRW 735.5 billion in 2025 (up 42.2%). Within 2022-2025, 2022 was the largest year for operating profit.

The operating margin moved from 22.1% to 20.0%, then 12.0%, then 15.0%. The margin has recovered from the 2024 low but has not returned to the 2022 level.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders fell from KRW 440.8 billion in 2022 to KRW 371.7 billion in 2023 (down 15.7%) and KRW 334 billion in 2024 (down 10.2%), then rose to KRW 367.7 billion in 2025 (up 10.1%).

Net income including non-controlling interests was KRW 898.1 billion, KRW 718.7 billion, KRW 495.6 billion and KRW 606.5 billion over the same years. The gap between the two measures is large, which means non-controlling interests account for a meaningful share and the two should be read separately.

Quarterly revenue rose from KRW 1.26 trillion in 3Q 2025 to KRW 1.45 trillion in 4Q 2025, with operating profit moving from KRW 196.6 billion to KRW 243.8 billion.

In 1Q 2026 revenue fell to KRW 1.08 trillion (down 25.6% from the prior quarter) and operating profit to KRW 157.8 billion (down 35.3%), marking the low point. In 2Q 2026 revenue reached KRW 1.39 trillion and operating profit KRW 195.7 billion, both higher.

In 3Q 2026 operating profit rose to KRW 197.8 billion, the second straight quarterly increase, while revenue of KRW 1.27 trillion grew only 0.5% year on year.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders in 3Q 2026 was KRW 16.7 billion, down 8.4% from the prior quarter in percentage terms of 83.5% and down 84.3% year on year.

Because operating profit held up while this net income fell sharply, the gap should be traced through items below operating profit, such as non-operating items, income tax and non-controlling interests.

Operating cash flow rose from KRW 519.2 billion in 2022 to KRW 701.8 billion in 2023 and KRW 781 billion in 2024, then fell to KRW 540.7 billion in 2025.

05

Industry analysis

The global apparel OEM industry contracted overall after the COVID-19 pandemic, but Youngone is viewed as having improved earnings through supply chain competitiveness. Industry observers point to its diversified production base and stable delivery capability. Views on the sector differ.

One reading cites a slowdown in the outdoor market as background to 1Q 2026 results. Another points to a global market recovery and rising demand for outdoor and sports products as drivers of improvement. Reports said 1Q 2026 results beat market expectations.

On orders, Amer Sports grew more than 40% locally on the opening of China's outdoor market, and this was seen as lifting Youngone's order intake. Currency effects are also large.

According to brokerage estimates, OEM dollar-based sales in 2Q were expected to rise about 8%, while the won-based growth rate was estimated at around 16%. Its position as the largest revenue company among listed domestic fashion firms shows its scale within the sector.

Because the group also contains brand retail and bicycle distribution, its profit structure is hard to compare directly with pure OEM producers.

Outlook

Cumulative results for the first half of 2026, as announced by the company, were revenue of KRW 2.464 trillion (up 12.8%) and operating profit of KRW 353.5 billion (up 19.8%). The company attributed this trend to growth in the subsidiary's OEM business.

On shareholder returns, it completed an early cancellation of 4% of shares in the first half and plans to cancel a further 5.5% in the second half, for a total of 9.5%. The company said this exceeds its existing medium-term return target. The standalone payout ratio is maintained at about 50% of net income.

A domestic brokerage forecast in a July 2026 report that 2Q revenue would reach KRW 1.1633 trillion and operating profit KRW 177.7 billion. Disclosed 2Q results were KRW 1.3854 trillion and KRW 195.7 billion. On orders, growth in Arc'teryx-related orders was mentioned in 1Q 2026.

Whether these orders translate into later quarterly revenue is a point to confirm in future quarterly reports. The SCOTT segment narrowed its loss in 1Q 2026, but no disclosure yet shows when it will return to profit.

The deadline for filing the 2026 annual report is March 31, 2027, and how that report explains the operating profit and net income gap in Q3 is the next checkpoint.

08

Bull factors

OEM recovery and back-to-back operating profit gains

Operating profit rose 42.2% to KRW 735.5 billion in 2025 and grew in both 2Q 2026 (KRW 195.7 billion) and 3Q 2026 (KRW 197.8 billion). The company announced first-half 2026 operating profit of KRW 353.5 billion, up 19.8%.

Because the earnings base rests on subsidiary OEM demand, whether this trend continues is the core of the bullish case.

Expanded shareholder returns

The company said it completed an early 4% share cancellation in 2026 and plans to cancel a further 5.5% in the second half, for 9.5% in total. Reports described this as above the existing medium-term return target. It also keeps a standalone payout ratio of about 50% of net income.

Outdoor order flow

Reports said 1Q 2026 revenue and operating profit exceeded market expectations. Amer Sports' growth in China's outdoor market was seen as lifting Youngone's orders. Whether those orders show up in later quarterly revenue is the checkpoint for the bullish scenario.

09

Bear factors

Sharp drop in Q3 net income to parent

Q3 2026 net income attributable to controlling shareholders fell to KRW 16.7 billion, down 83.5% from the prior quarter and 84.3% year on year. Operating profit rose in the same quarter, so the gap between the two is wide.

Over the last four quarters the total was KRW 341.2 billion, below full-year 2025 controlling-shareholder net income.

Won-based growth leaning on currency effects

A brokerage estimate put OEM dollar-based sales growth at about 8%, against roughly 16% on a won basis. A large part of won-based growth therefore comes from currency effects. If exchange rates move, the won-based growth rate could shift as well.

Incomplete margin recovery

The operating margin fell to 12.0% in 2024 and recovered to 15.0% in 2025, still below 22.1% in 2022. A slowdown in the outdoor market was also cited as a backdrop to 1Q 2026 results. Earnings have not yet returned to their earlier peak, which is the basis of the bearish case.

10

Risk factors

Currency and demand swings

Much of the won-based OEM revenue growth is estimated to come from currency effects. A slowdown in outdoor demand could feed directly into orders and utilization. Both factors can widen quarter-to-quarter swings in results.

Governance risk

Controversy over the high compensation of Vice Chairman Sung Rae-eun has been cited as a burden. Related-party shareholders hold close to half of the company, so the progress of governance improvements is a key issue. How far expanded shareholder returns ease these concerns will be visible in future disclosures.

SCOTT segment losses

The SCOTT segment posted an operating loss in 1Q 2026 but narrowed it sharply from the prior year's loss. When this segment returns to profit within group earnings is a point to confirm in upcoming regular reports. If segment results do not improve, earnings volatility at the holding level may persist.

12

Overall view

Youngone Holdings is a holding company that controls Youngone's OEM business, the domestic retail unit for The North Face, and SCOTT bicycle distribution. Consolidated revenue and operating profit recovered in 2025 from the 2024 low, and operating profit grew in both 2Q and 3Q 2026.

However, Q3 2026 net income attributable to controlling shareholders fell sharply to KRW 16.7 billion, diverging from operating profit, and the last-four-quarter total is below full-year 2025 controlling-shareholder net income. Currency effects are estimated to account for a large part of won-based revenue growth.

Risks also include a possible slowdown in the outdoor market and continued SCOTT losses. Shareholder returns are expanding, with 9.5% of shares being cancelled and a standalone payout ratio of about 50% of net income.

Strengths are the recovery in the OEM business and shareholder returns, while weaknesses are the Q3 net income drop, reliance on currency effects and governance controversy.

The next checkpoints are completion of the second-half share cancellation, how the 2026 annual report explains the Q3 net income gap, and OEM revenue trends in 1Q 2027.
