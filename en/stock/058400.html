02

Business structure

KNN (Korea NEW Network) is a privately owned broadcaster based in the Busan and Gyeongnam region, and it is moving away from a business model that relied heavily on broadcast advertising revenue.

The company is diversifying its income sources, and sponsorship and non-advertising revenue are taking a growing share of total sales. Expanding content production and sales and cultural businesses follows the same direction.

Consolidated revenue was KRW 66 billion in 2022, KRW 66.1 billion in 2023, KRW 68.9 billion in 2024 and KRW 68.5 billion in 2025, staying within a range of roughly KRW 66 billion to KRW 69 billion over the four years.

The 0.5% decline in 2025 is attributed to weaker dominance of terrestrial broadcasters in the advertising market as OTT services expand and viewing shifts to mobile. Advertising revenue depends directly on viewing habits and advertiser spending, so it responds quickly to changes in the media environment.

Income outside advertising can move through channels different from ad volume, which may diversify revenue sources. In competition, OTT platforms and IPTV, cable and general programming channels compete for viewing time and ad budgets.

Compared with national players such as CJ ENM and SBS in the same media and entertainment sector, KNN's regional base gives it a different business scope. At the end of 2025, total equity stood at KRW 228.5 billion and total liabilities at KRW 17.8 billion, a balance sheet with a relatively light debt burden.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual revenue rose from KRW 66 billion in 2022 to KRW 68.9 billion in 2024 before slipping 0.5% to KRW 68.5 billion in 2025. Within 2022 through 2025, 2024 was the largest revenue year and 2022 the smallest.

Operating profit was KRW 6.74 billion in 2022, KRW 6.09 billion in 2023 (down 9.6%), KRW 4.51 billion in 2024 (down 25.9%) and KRW 3.19 billion in 2025 (down 29.2%), declining for three consecutive years from 2023 to 2025. Operating margin fell from 10.2% in 2022 to 9.2% in 2023, 6.6% in 2024 and 4.7% in 2025.

Revenue was roughly flat while margin narrowed, so less profit was kept from the same sales base.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 7.39 billion in 2022, KRW 8.38 billion in 2023 (up 13.4%), KRW 8.06 billion in 2024 (down 3.8%) and KRW 11.2 billion in 2025 (up 39.3%), making 2025 the largest year of the four.

Because operating profit was the smallest that same year, non-operating items appear to have weighed heavily on net income.

Net income including non-controlling interests was KRW 7.38 billion in 2022, KRW 8.45 billion in 2023, KRW 8.11 billion in 2024 and KRW 11.3 billion in 2025, a separate measure from the figure attributable to controlling shareholders.

Operating cash flow rose from KRW 2.68 billion in 2022 to KRW 11.6 billion in 2023, KRW 10.4 billion in 2024 and KRW 17.6 billion in 2025.

Quarterly revenue was KRW 17.3 billion in the third quarter of 2025, KRW 23.5 billion in the fourth quarter (up 35.3% from the prior quarter), KRW 13.1 billion in the first quarter of 2026 (down 44.4%), KRW 14.2 billion in the second quarter and KRW 18 billion in the third quarter (up 27.5%).

Quarterly operating profit was KRW 1.81 billion in the third quarter of 2025, KRW 598 million in the fourth quarter, an operating loss of KRW 86.81 million in the first quarter of 2026 (a swing into loss), KRW 984 million in the second quarter (a swing into profit) and KRW 1.33 billion in the third quarter (up 35.6% from the prior quarter but down 26.1% from a year earlier).

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 2.63 billion in the third quarter of 2025, KRW 5.75 billion in the fourth quarter, KRW 6.84 billion in the first quarter of 2026 and KRW 18.9 billion in the second quarter, before a net loss of KRW 1.32 billion in the third quarter of 2026.

The four quarters from the fourth quarter of 2025 through the third quarter of 2026 total KRW 30.2 billion, and the second quarter of 2026 alone accounts for more than half of that sum.

05

Industry analysis

The broadcasting industry is undergoing a structural shift as OTT services expand and mobile viewing grows, dividing viewing budgets across more channels. The weakening dominance of broadcasters in the terrestrial advertising market is cited as the backdrop to KNN's stagnant revenue.

The growth of IPTV, cable and general programming channels within a converging broadcasting and telecom environment is another factor narrowing the relative position of terrestrial broadcasters.

Because this change looks more structural than cyclical, it is uncertain whether a recovery in the broader ad cycle alone would reverse the trend.

The company is expanding sponsorship, non-advertising income and content businesses in response, but the slight revenue decline in 2025 shows the effect has not yet produced clear top-line growth.

Within the KOSDAQ entertainment and culture sector, KNN is a regional private broadcaster and is small relative to the sector as a whole. Compared with national operators, its revenue leans more heavily on local advertisers and a regional viewer base.

The direction of KNN's revenue will depend on the recovery of the overall ad market and the trend in terrestrial viewership. Quarterly revenue rose in the second and third quarters of 2026 after a first-quarter low, a sign of recovery, but the third-quarter operating margin remains in the single digits.

Outlook

The corporate value enhancement plan that KNN disclosed on March 24, 2026 set out continued investment for sustainable growth and stable earnings generation as its goals. At the regular shareholders' meeting held the same day, the 32nd fiscal year financial statements were approved and a cash dividend was resolved.

On April 23, 2026, the company decided on a 10-for-1 common share consolidation, with the stated aims of stabilizing the share price and enhancing corporate value. Following the consolidation, the par value per share changes from KRW 500 to KRW 5,000.

Quarterly trends show revenue rising in the second and third quarters of 2026, and operating profit swinging back to a profit in the second quarter.

However, third-quarter operating profit fell year on year and a net loss attributable to controlling shareholders emerged, so whether revenue recovery is translating into earnings recovery needs further confirmation.

Preliminary results for the fourth quarter and full year 2026 are expected in early 2027, and the 2025 operating margin of 4.7% serves as the reference point for measuring the pace of recovery.

The 2026 annual business report must be submitted by March 31, 2027, at which point the annual revenue mix and operating cash flow can be checked. The dividend for 2026 is expected to be resolved at the following regular shareholders' meeting, which will also show how shareholder return policy is carried out.

Rising operating cash flow

Operating cash flow rose from KRW 2.68 billion in 2022 to KRW 11.6 billion in 2023, KRW 10.4 billion in 2024 and KRW 17.6 billion in 2025, the largest of the four years. This runs counter to the decline in operating profit over the same period, which suggests cash generation has held up separately from reported profit.

Sustained cash generation during a period of falling operating profit supports the company's financial capacity.

Quarterly profit recovery and revenue rebound

After an operating loss of KRW 86.81 million in the first quarter of 2026, operating profit returned to profit at KRW 984 million in the second quarter, and third-quarter operating profit rose 35.6% from the prior quarter to KRW 1.33 billion.

Revenue increased for two consecutive quarters from the second quarter through the third quarter of 2026. The corporate value enhancement plan disclosed voluntarily in March 2026, which set shareholder value enhancement as a goal, is also cited as an effort to improve.

Bear factors

Three years of falling operating profit and thinner margins

Operating profit declined for three consecutive years from 2023 through 2025, and KRW 3.19 billion in 2025 is less than half of KRW 6.74 billion in 2022. Operating margin fell from 10.2% in 2022 to 4.7% in 2025.

Third-quarter 2026 operating profit of KRW 1.33 billion was still down 26.1% year on year, so the scale of recovery remains limited.

Weakening terrestrial advertising structure

Expanding OTT services and the shift to mobile viewing have weakened the dominance of broadcasters in the terrestrial advertising market. Revenue fell 0.5% in 2025, and growth from KRW 66 billion in 2022 to KRW 68.5 billion in 2025 has not been pronounced.

Whether non-advertising income has grown enough to offset the revenue decline will need to be checked against the revenue mix in the 2026 annual business report.

Quarterly swing into loss and concentrated profits

The net loss attributable to controlling shareholders in the third quarter of 2026 was KRW 1.32 billion, a swing into loss from the prior quarter.

More than half of the KRW 30.2 billion total across the four quarters from the fourth quarter of 2025 through the third quarter of 2026 came from the second quarter of 2026, at KRW 18.9 billion. Profit concentrated in a few quarters makes the direction of quarterly results hard to predict.

Risk factors

Ad market and viewing environment

If OTT platforms and mobile viewing keep expanding, terrestrial ad volumes and rates could face further pressure. The growth of IPTV, cable and general programming channels within a converging broadcasting and telecom market is another factor dividing viewing time. A delayed recovery in the overall ad market could extend the period of flat revenue.

Earnings volatility and non-operating items

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders rose 39.3% in 2025 while operating profit fell 29.2%, so the two measures moved in opposite directions.

In the second quarter of 2026, net income attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 18.9 billion was far larger than operating profit of KRW 984 million, showing how volatile the earnings mix is. When non-operating items weigh heavily on net income, the uncertainty in interpreting results increases.

Share consolidation and shareholder return execution

The 10-for-1 common share consolidation disclosed in April 2026 was presented as aimed at stabilizing the share price and enhancing corporate value. How the change in share count and trading structure affects liquidity will need to be confirmed through later disclosures.

The degree to which the corporate value enhancement plan is carried out is another variable that will determine whether shareholder return policy is effective.

Overall view

KNN's annual revenue has held between KRW 66 billion and KRW 68.5 billion, while operating profit and operating margin have continued to decline. The company is expanding sponsorship, non-advertising income and content businesses as its broadcast-advertising-centered model weakens under OTT growth and mobile viewing.

Revenue rose for two consecutive quarters after the first-quarter 2026 low, and operating profit returned to profit in the second quarter.

By contrast, third-quarter 2026 operating profit of KRW 1.33 billion fell 26.1% year on year, and the company swung to a net loss attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 1.32 billion.

On the financial side, total equity of KRW 228.5 billion and a debt-to-equity ratio of 7.8% provide a cushion, and operating cash flow rose to KRW 17.6 billion in 2025. However, net income depends heavily on non-operating items and on specific quarters, so the stability of earnings is still being tested.

The company voluntarily disclosed a corporate value enhancement plan in March 2026 and decided on a 10-for-1 share consolidation in April 2026, and the execution of these measures and shareholder returns are the next points to watch.

The annual operating margin and revenue mix that will be confirmed in the 2026 annual business report is the key checkpoint.
