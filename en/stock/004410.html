02

Business structure

Seoul Food IND runs a bakery business and an environmental business. The bakery business centers on frozen dough and also produces snacks, mass-produced bread and breadcrumbs, while the environmental business builds and manages food waste drying units.

According to the company, frozen dough and pizza are its main products, and it cited growth in these two lines as a key driver of its 2025 results.

For the first nine months of 2025, bakery revenue growth was attributed to expanding exports of frozen dough and pizza and new sales to large retailers, catering and hotels in Korea. The company sees rising single-person households lifting demand for frozen dough and pizza.

It is widening sales channels, including online retail, and pairing them with aggressive marketing. Exports are centered on Asia, and the company plans to enter large marts in Vietnam. On the production side, the company introduced automated systems such as robotic arms on its lines to raise capacity and productivity.

In bakery, large baking companies such as Samlip are the main competitors, so Seoul Food IND is extending its reach through frozen dough, an intermediate-type product, and exports. Its CEO has described a strategy of offering premium bakery products at competitive prices.

04

Earnings analysis

Based on the regular filings, annual revenue was KRW 64.3 billion in 2022, KRW 64.8 billion in 2023 (up 0.7% year on year), KRW 58.6 billion in 2024 (down 9.6%), and KRW 68.6 billion in 2025 (up 17.1%). Among 2022 to 2025, 2025 was the largest year and 2024 the smallest.

Operating profit fell from KRW 1.31 billion in 2022 to KRW 587 million in 2023 (down 55.2%), and the company swung to an operating loss of KRW 2.23 billion in 2024. In 2025 it posted an operating loss of KRW 460 million, a continued loss with a smaller deficit, making 2024 and 2025 two consecutive loss years.

Operating margin was 2.0% in 2022, 0.9% in 2023, -3.8% in 2024 and -0.7% in 2025, so the deficit narrowed after the 2024 low.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 676 million in 2022, then a net loss of KRW 573 million in 2023, a wider net loss of KRW 2.99 billion in 2024, and a narrower net loss of KRW 1.6 billion in 2025, giving three consecutive loss years from 2023 to 2025.

Quarterly, revenue was KRW 17.1 billion in the third quarter of 2025 with operating profit of KRW 7.86 million, then KRW 16.8 billion in the fourth quarter (down 1.8% quarter on quarter) with operating profit of KRW 14.94 million (up 90.1%).

In the first quarter of 2026 revenue fell 10.6% to KRW 15 billion and the company posted an operating loss of KRW 122 million, the low point. In the second quarter revenue rose 11.9% to KRW 16.8 billion and operating profit was KRW 224 million, a swing to profit.

In the third quarter revenue was KRW 17 billion (up 0.7% quarter on quarter, down 1.1% year on year) and operating profit was KRW 313 million, up 3,887.0% year on year; revenue has grown for two consecutive quarters since the first quarter of 2026.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 63.34 million in the second quarter and KRW 43.18 million in the third quarter, after a net loss of KRW 485 million in the first quarter.

As a result, the net loss attributable to controlling shareholders over the last four quarters (fourth quarter of 2025 to third quarter of 2026) totals KRW 422 million. Operating cash flow was KRW 303 million in 2022, KRW 2.02 billion in 2023, KRW 2.59 billion in 2024 and KRW 919 million in 2025.

The company credited automation and growth in frozen dough and pizza for 2025, while the land revaluation that added KRW 5.2 billion to assets is a balance-sheet change rather than an identified source of operating gains.

05

Industry analysis

Demand for frozen dough and pizza is expanding as single-person households grow, a view that appears both in company statements and in press reports. Seoul Food IND says it is widening sales channels, including online retail, and aims to win share through aggressive marketing.

Its growth strategy follows the home meal replacement trend by offering premium bakery products at competitive prices. In a February 2026 report, its CEO said the company's brand ranking was around 20th and that it aims to move up to about 10th.

The Korean bakery market is dominated by large baking companies, so Seoul Food IND is extending its reach through frozen dough and exports. Compared with large bakers such as Samlip, the company is small in scale and distribution, so securing large retail accounts has an outsized effect on results.

Across the food sector, raw material and logistics costs have been a margin pressure, and Seoul Food IND's margins are exposed to cost moves in the same direction. In cycle terms, revenue rose in 2025 and then grew for two consecutive quarters in the second and third quarters of 2026, indicating a recovery phase.

The food waste drying unit in the environmental business has a different demand structure from bakery and is best viewed separately.

Revenue recovery

Revenue rose 17.1% to KRW 68.6 billion in 2025. Revenue has grown for two consecutive quarters from the second quarter of 2026 through the third quarter, reaching KRW 17 billion in the third quarter. The company attributes this to rising demand for frozen dough and pizza as single-person households grow.

Export expansion and automation

In February 2026 the company said it plans to enter large marts in Vietnam. It has said automation such as robotic arms raised capacity and productivity. It also described a land revaluation that added KRW 5.2 billion to assets as a foundation for financial stability.

Bear factors

Cumulative losses and consecutive loss years

Operating losses occurred in both 2024 and 2025, and net losses attributable to controlling shareholders ran for three consecutive years from 2023 to 2025.

The net loss attributable to controlling shareholders over the last four quarters totals KRW 422 million, and that period includes a first-quarter 2026 net loss of KRW 485 million. For this reason PER cannot be calculated.

Financial leverage burden

At the end of 2025, total liabilities were KRW 46.1 billion against total equity of KRW 26.8 billion, a debt ratio of 172.0%. The ratio fell from 188.6% in 2024 but remained above 150.2% in 2022. Operating cash flow dropped from KRW 2.59 billion in 2024 to KRW 919 million in 2025.

Thin earnings base and quarterly swings

Third-quarter 2026 operating profit was KRW 313 million on KRW 17 billion of revenue, a small profit base. In the first quarter of 2026 revenue fell 10.6% quarter on quarter to KRW 15 billion and the company posted an operating loss of KRW 122 million. Third-quarter revenue was 1.1% below the prior year, so the top line still trails the 2025 level.

Risk factors

Largest shareholder stake and governance

The largest shareholder's related parties held 20.10% as of November 13, 2026, with the change recorded as on-market purchases and the stated purpose linked to management control.

The increase can be read as a signal of commitment, but its effect on free float and share price movement cannot be gauged from the filing alone.

Balance sheet and liquidity

Total liabilities of KRW 46.1 billion exceed total equity of KRW 26.8 billion, so the company may be sensitive to changes in interest rates and borrowing conditions. The land revaluation added KRW 5.2 billion to assets, but it is a book-value change. With operating cash flow of KRW 919 million in 2025, room to repay debt may be limited.

Business concentration and execution

Results depend heavily on frozen dough and pizza, so shifts in demand or customer accounts for these lines flow directly into earnings. Exports to large marts in Vietnam have been described as planned rather than established. A key test is how far automation reduces quarter-to-quarter swings like the first-quarter 2026 loss.

Overall view
