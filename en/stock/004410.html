02

Business structure

Seoul Food IND was founded in 1955 and listed on the KOSPI market in 1973 as a specialized bakery company. Its core business spans two pillars: a bakery segment producing frozen dough, bread, snacks, and bread crumbs, and an environmental segment that manufactures and outsource-operates food-waste drying equipment.

As of the Q1 2023 quarterly filing, finished product sales accounted for 39.2% of revenue, merchandise sales for 49.3%, and the environmental segment for 11.5%.

Products are sold under several in-house brands, and frozen dough is considered the key growth driver, supported by rising single-person households and expanding demand for home meal replacement (HMR) products.

Clients have diversified toward large distributors and food companies including CJ Freshway, Shinsegae Food Inc. (including supply linked to Starbucks), Homeplus, Hyundai Department Store, and Paris Croissant, alongside exports to Vietnam and other overseas markets.

As of end-June 2026, the company had 229 employees, consistent with a small-to-mid-sized food company. Production was relocated to a new Chungju plant equipped with robotic-arm-based automation to raise capacity and efficiency.

The environmental segment is largely unaffected by seasonality, while the bakery segment's profitability is heavily influenced by grain input costs such as flour and sugar and by foreign exchange movements, and the company has been shifting its mix toward higher-margin frozen dough in a highly competitive market.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual revenue edged up from KRW 64.3 billion in 2022 to KRW 64.8 billion in 2023 (+0.7% year on year), then fell to KRW 58.6 billion in 2024 (-9.6% year on year), before rising to KRW 68.6 billion in 2025 (+17.1% year on year), the largest figure of the 2022-2025 period.

Profitability, however, diverged from the revenue trend. Operating profit fell from KRW 1.31 billion in 2022 to KRW 587 million in 2023 (-55.2% year on year), then swung to an operating loss of KRW 2.23 billion in 2024, and remained in loss at KRW 460 million in 2025, though the loss narrowed.

This marks two consecutive years of operating losses from 2024 through 2025.

Controlling-interest net profit moved from a KRW 676 million profit in 2022 to a KRW 573 million net loss in 2023, widened to a KRW 2.99 billion net loss in 2024, and then narrowed to a KRW 1.6 billion net loss in 2025, extending controlling-interest net losses to three consecutive years from 2023 through 2025.

On a quarterly basis, the operating loss of KRW 5.39 million in Q2 2025 turned into an operating profit of KRW 7.86 million in Q3, which rose 90.1% quarter on quarter to KRW 14.94 million in Q4, before swinging back to an operating loss of KRW 122 million in Q1 2026, and then to an operating profit of KRW 224 million in Q2 2026, a swing to profit versus both the prior quarter and the year-earlier quarter.

Controlling-interest net profit likewise swung to a profit of KRW 63.34 million in Q2 2026, versus a net loss of KRW 485 million in the prior quarter and a net loss of KRW 333 million in the year-earlier quarter.

Still, the sum of controlling-interest net losses over the trailing four quarters (Q3 2025-Q2 2026) was KRW 887 million, meaning a single profitable quarter did not offset the cumulative deficit. The operating margin worsened from 2.0% in 2022 to 0.9% in 2023 and -3.8% in 2024, before improving to -0.7% in 2025.

05

Industry analysis

South Korea's food industry faced broad growth pressure in Q2 2026 once export-heavy large-cap peers were excluded from the comparison, according to market forecast data compiled for major food companies, which cited a triple squeeze from packaging materials, raw materials, and foreign exchange.

Against this backdrop, rising single-person households and growth in the home meal replacement (HMR) market are viewed as structural tailwinds supporting demand for frozen dough and pizza products.

Still, the bakery market is dominated by larger food companies competing on proprietary brands and large-scale distribution networks, leaving smaller-revenue players like Seoul Food IND reliant on business-to-business supply relationships with specific clients such as CJ Freshway, Shinsegae Food Inc., and Homeplus rather than their own consumer brand scale.

Key raw materials such as flour and sugar are heavily import-dependent, making profitability directly sensitive to international grain prices and the won-dollar exchange rate.

Across the KOSPI market broadly, a delisting requirement targeting stocks trading below KRW 1,000 that took effect from July 2026 prompted a wave of smaller-cap, low-priced issues to pursue par value consolidations and other share-price management measures.

Outlook

The company continues to raise production capacity and efficiency based on robotic automation at its new Chungju plant, which could be a factor in future cost-structure improvement.

Securing an asset increase equal to roughly 8% of total assets through a land revaluation is viewed as a balance-sheet reinforcement measure. CEO Seo Seong-hun has stated a goal of climbing from roughly the 20th-ranked food brand to the top 10, though this is a company-stated target rather than a confirmed outcome.

On the export side, expanding overseas supply of frozen dough and pizza products and securing new domestic clients among large distributors, institutional catering, and hotels are cited as the main channels for revenue growth.

Even after the April 2026 par value consolidation, the possibility of further share-count changes from convertible bond conversions remains, warranting continued monitoring of per-share dilution.

Whether the swing to profit in Q2 2026 reflects a one-off or a sustained improvement will need to be confirmed through subsequent quarterly results.

Bull factors

Tailwinds from Frozen Dough and HMR Trends

Rising single-person households and HMR market growth are structural factors supporting demand for frozen dough and pizza products. The client base has expanded into large distributors, institutional catering, and hotels, alongside exports to Vietnam and other markets. This provides a potential growth channel even amid a domestic demand slowdown.

Automation Investment and Asset Value Reinforcement

Relocating production to the new Chungju plant and introducing robotic automation offers room for productivity and cost-structure improvement. An external land revaluation secured an asset increase equal to roughly 8% of total assets, partially reinforcing the balance sheet. These measures could form a basis for future operating efficiency gains.

Quarter-to-Quarter Earnings Volatility

The streak of profitability through Q4 2025 reverted to a loss in Q1 2026, indicating relatively low visibility into quarterly performance. This makes it difficult to prematurely characterize future quarters as a clear trend.

Elevated Leverage and Slowing Cash Flow

The debt-to-equity ratio stood at 172.0% at end-2025, down slightly from 188.6% in 2024 but still elevated. Operating cash flow fell to KRW 919 million in 2025 from KRW 2.59 billion in 2024, a signal that financial capacity has not fully improved.

Risk factors

Balance Sheet and Liquidity Risk

The debt-to-equity ratio of 172.0% at end-2025 is on the higher side within the sector, and operating cash flow declined from 2024 levels. Continued reliance on external financing amid ongoing cumulative losses could increase financial strain. Total equity of KRW 26.8 billion leaves relatively limited buffer compared with larger food companies.

Share Dilution Risk

A March 2026 disclosure confirmed a share-count change resulting from convertible bond conversion. If convertible bonds remain outstanding even after the 10-for-1 par value consolidation, further conversions could continue to add shares, a factor that could dilute per-share value.

Raw Material and Foreign Exchange Risk

Key raw materials such as flour and sugar are heavily import-dependent, making cost of goods sold directly sensitive to international grain prices and the won-dollar exchange rate.

Market forecasts have pointed to a triple squeeze from packaging materials, raw materials, and foreign exchange across the domestic food industry, and a smaller player like Seoul Food IND is not immune to this pressure. A rise in cost burden could reverse the loss-narrowing trend seen in 2025-2026.
