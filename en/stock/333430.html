02

Business structure

IL Seung was founded in 1999 as a maker and seller of marine equipment and was listed on KOSDAQ in 2021 through a merger. The company holds a subsidiary and operates four segments: environmental equipment, shipbuilding LNG, PIPE SPOOL, and materials.

Its main products are marine sewage treatment units (STP), scrubbers for sulfur oxide reduction, and LNG regasification units (LNG RU). The WiseReport company data service counts the PIPE SPOOL segment as more than half of total revenue.

PIPE SPOOL supplies ship piping to shipyards as block assemblies, so its sales move with shipyard build volumes and delivery timing. Demand for environmental equipment is linked to tighter vessel emission rules.

The shipbuilding equipment sector depends heavily on shipyard order flow, and some key components are imported. Peers such as Sejin Heavy Industries, Hyundai Hyms, and Oriental Precision & Engineering often move on the same days within the same theme, but main products, customers, and order timing differ by company.

A November 2026 news report described IL Seung as operating marine environmental equipment and shipbuilding equipment businesses. The revenue split between environmental equipment and PIPE SPOOL is a item to check in the notes to the regular filing.

04

Earnings analysis

In annual reported results, revenue fell from KRW 65.0 billion in 2022 to KRW 53.0 billion in 2023 (down 18.6% year on year), then to KRW 51.8 billion in 2024 (down 2.2% year on year) and KRW 50.7 billion in 2025 (down 2.2% year on year), declining for three consecutive years.

Operating profit rose from KRW 3.23 billion in 2022 to KRW 5.29 billion in 2023 (up 63.9% year on year), KRW 6.91 billion in 2024 (up 30.6% year on year) and KRW 10.9 billion in 2025 (up 57.1% year on year), increasing for three consecutive years, and 2025 was the largest year of 2022-2025.

The operating margin rose from 5.0% in 2022 to 10.0% in 2023, 13.3% in 2024 and 21.4% in 2025, so margins improved substantially even during the period when revenue declined. Figures that break down the cause of the margin improvement by segment remain a task to examine in the notes to the periodic reports.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders moved from KRW 2.42 billion in 2022 to KRW 3.84 billion in 2023 (up 59.0% year on year), KRW 1.74 billion in 2024 (down 54.8% year on year) and KRW 7.45 billion in 2025 (up 328.9% year on year), and 2025 was the largest year.

Over the same period, net income including non-controlling interests was KRW 6.52 billion in 2024 and KRW 13.7 billion in 2025, so the gap versus net income attributable to controlling shareholders is large.

At the end of 2025, total equity was KRW 90.9 billion and equity attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 45.8 billion, a structure in which the share of non-controlling interests is large.

Operating cash flow turned from KRW -1.69 billion in 2022 to KRW 12.5 billion in 2023, then declined to KRW 9.74 billion in 2024 and KRW 6.73 billion in 2025.

On a quarterly basis, results peaked in the fourth quarter of 2025 with revenue of KRW 15.3 billion, operating profit of KRW 5.53 billion and net income attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 4.59 billion, then turned down in the first quarter of 2026 with revenue of KRW 12.1 billion (down 20.8% quarter on quarter) and operating profit of KRW 2.3 billion (down 58.4%).

In the second quarter of 2026, results recovered, with revenue of KRW 16.2 billion (up 34.4% quarter on quarter), operating profit of KRW 5.93 billion (up 157.5%) and net income attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 4.54 billion (up 81.4%).

In the third quarter of 2026, revenue was KRW 13.6 billion (down 16.4% quarter on quarter and down 1.6% year on year), operating profit was KRW 3.3 billion (down 44.3% quarter on quarter and down 1.2% year on year), and net income attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 0.79 billion (down 82.7% quarter on quarter and down 48.2% year on year).

In the third quarter, net income attributable to controlling shareholders fell sharply relative to operating profit of KRW 3.3 billion, so where the difference between the two figures arose is the next point to check.

Industry analysis

Demand upstream remains supported by shipyards that keep high order backlogs. An October 2026 report said the shift to eco-friendly vessels and U.S. shipbuilding plans are lifting expectations for suppliers of engines, fittings, and forgings.

NH Investment & Securities said in a late-May 2026 outlook that cumulative global vessel orders in April 2026 reached 54.24 million GT, up 62% year on year. The same outlook raised its 2026 combined order estimate for major Korean shipbuilders from USD 45.8 billion to USD 49.9 billion.

On the other side, LNG carrier newbuilding prices rose only modestly, which the same analysis flagged as a constraint. Rising Chinese commercial orders are also increasing supply volumes for Korean engine makers, and Korean firms are described as keeping a technology edge in mid-to-large eco-friendly engines.

Shipbuilding equipment makers book results with a lag from order to delivery. IL Seung's focus on environmental equipment and PIPE SPOOL places it apart from engine and insulation makers, and reporting noted that earnings improvement differs across companies even within the same theme.

Bear factors

Large quarterly earnings swings

Across the last four quarters, net profit attributable to controlling shareholders totaled KRW 12.4 billion with wide quarterly gaps. In Q1 2026, revenue fell 20.8% and operating profit fell 58.4% quarter on quarter, which suggests results are sensitive to order and delivery timing.

Revenue decline and non-controlling interests

Net profit of KRW 13.7 billion in 2025 exceeded controlling-shareholder net profit of KRW 7.45 billion, and controlling-shareholder equity was KRW 45.8 billion of KRW 90.9 billion total equity. Sector headwinds

Analysts noted that LNG carrier newbuilding prices rose only modestly, so higher prices take time to reach equipment unit pricing. Reports also say Chinese yards are expanding commercial vessel orders, which changes the competitive landscape. Even with sector strength, earnings improvement differs across individual companies.

10

Risk factors

Dependence on shipyard orders

Shipbuilding equipment makers depend heavily on shipyard order volumes, and their competitiveness varies with technical capability. Revenue recognition lags order intake, so quarterly results can swing. A delay at a specific shipyard or project can shift revenue timing directly.

FX, raw materials, and imported parts

A significant share of key equipment components is imported, and raw material prices and delivery timing affect results. Exchange rate moves influence both imported component costs and overseas order pricing. If cost increases cannot be passed through to prices, margins can come under pressure.

Share price volatility and theme trading

In a July 24, 2026 morning market report on shipbuilding equipment stocks, IL Seung showed a 29.86% move. The stock tends to trade with peers in the same theme, so share price moves can diverge from company results. Price swings and reported earnings should be read separately.

Overall view

From 2022 to 2025, IL Seung's operating profit and operating margin improved while revenue declined for three straight years, so the earnings profile has shifted toward profitability rather than scale.

Net profit was KRW 13.7 billion in 2025 and controlling-shareholder net profit was KRW 7.45 billion, so the two figures should be read separately given the large non-controlling share.

External data shows sharp growth in revenue and profit in H1 2026, but in Q3 2026 revenue and operating profit slipped slightly below the prior-year quarter and controlling-shareholder net profit fell 48.2%.

Shipbuilding order strength and emission rules are cited as supportive backdrop, while limited LNG carrier price gains, competition from Chinese yards, and the lag from order to delivery remain headwinds. The valuation depends heavily on how durable earnings are, and the price-to-book ratio sits above 1x.
