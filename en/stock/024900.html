02

Business structure

DY Deokyang was founded in 1977 and is an automotive parts maker headquartered in Ulsan, with crash pads and cockpit modules as its core products. The company has been listed on the KOSPI since 1997, and it changed its name from Deokyang Industrial to DY Deokyang.

Its business rests on supplying interior parts to Hyundai Motor, and it has been involved from the early stage of new model development by producing parts molds. Since 2021 it has supplied BMA parts to Europe and the United States, broadening its overseas customer base.

In 2015 it acquired a stake in Beijing Mobis Zhongche from Hyundai Mobis and continues its China business. A opening ceremony for the Georgia plant of its North American unit, DY Deokyang America (DYA), was held in July 2026, and production equipment setup, die transfer and assembly line stabilization followed.

On October 29, 2026, the company said the Georgia plant had begun first mass production of interior and exterior auto parts. It has also moved into energy storage systems (ESS) for electric-propulsion ships. The largest shareholder group held 43.15% as of the last change date of August 22, 2024. Listed companies in the same automotive sector include Hyundai Mobis, Hanon Systems and Seoyon E-Hwa.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual results show diverging trends in revenue and profitability. Revenue rose from KRW 1.54 trillion in 2022 to KRW 1.88 trillion in 2023, up 21.7% year on year, and reached its peak of KRW 1.88 trillion in 2024, up 0.4%, which was the largest year in the 2022 to 2025 span.

It then fell to KRW 1.74 trillion in 2025, down 7.7%. Operating profit went from KRW 30.7 billion in 2022 to KRW 27.7 billion in 2023, down 9.6%, then KRW 8.23 billion in 2024, down 70.3%, and KRW 3.2 billion in 2025, down 61.1%, marking three consecutive years of decline from 2023 through 2025.

The operating margin narrowed from 2.0% in 2022 to 1.5% in 2023, 0.4% in 2024 and 0.2% in 2025, and margins also compressed in 2023 and 2024 even as revenue grew. Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders rose to KRW 16.8 billion in 2024, up 35.7%, then fell to KRW 1.24 billion in 2025, down 92.6%.

Net profit including non-controlling interests was KRW 16.7 billion in 2024, a different figure that should be read separately. According to a market data aggregator, first-half 2025 revenue fell 8.5% year on year, operating profit fell 51.7%, and net income turned to a loss.

By quarter, operating loss of KRW 3.64 billion in the third quarter of 2025 turned into operating profit of KRW 4.36 billion in the fourth quarter, then fell to KRW 2.62 billion in the first quarter of 2026, down 39.9% from the prior quarter.

The second quarter of 2026 posted an operating loss of KRW 7.92 billion, and the third quarter returned to operating profit of KRW 6.83 billion.

First-quarter 2026 revenue fell 1.6% and operating profit fell 15.0% year on year, with crash pads and door trim, whose sales track finished vehicle volumes directly, cited as the cause. Third-quarter 2026 revenue was KRW 333.5 billion, down 19.6% from the prior quarter and 19.4% from a year earlier.

Year-end 2025 total equity was KRW 114.9 billion, equity attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 119.4 billion, and total liabilities were KRW 476 billion.

Operating cash flow was negative KRW 12.1 billion in 2022 and turned positive at KRW 64.3 billion in 2023, KRW 34.7 billion in 2024 and KRW 41.6 billion in 2025.

05

Industry analysis

Automotive parts is a sector where quality and technology matter and where production and sales are tied to automaker orders, which creates scale economics. Recent business-cycle swings and shifts in the market environment have pressured earnings across the industry.

Crash pad and door trim sales, the company's core products, track finished vehicle sales volumes directly, and as of the first quarter of 2026 a slowdown in advanced-market growth was cited as a factor behind the decline.

Automakers are increasingly sourcing parts as integrated modules and systems, which makes module capability more important for suppliers. The company is responding through BMA parts supply to Europe and the United States while expanding new businesses.

Local production in North America is aimed at supplying automaker customers that have entered North America on time and improving logistics efficiency and cost competitiveness. The company said it is focusing resources on electrification to match the shift toward eco-friendly vehicles.

Its move into ESS for electric-propulsion ships can also be read as an attempt to expand beyond vehicles.

Margin comparisons with listed peers are only meaningful when fiscal-year disclosures are aligned, and as of the third quarter of 2026 the company's operating margin remains at a low level measured in fractions of a percent.

06

Outlook

The most recent confirmed business milestone is the first mass production at the Georgia plant of DY Deokyang America, DYA. The company expects revenue from locally produced products to be recognized in stages as the plant runs.

The company also said it won a bid to supply crash pads for the SUV hybrid model that will be the main vehicle at a key customer's Georgia plant, backed by technology and cost competitiveness from a new process.

Chief Executive Bae Un-gyo said the company will consolidate its North American growth base through new orders and expanded supply. However, specific annual revenue or profit targets from the company were not included in the material reviewed.

Strengths include revenue recognition from the North American base, the potential for more new orders, and the electrification and ESS initiatives. Weaknesses include declining sales of core products tied to weaker vehicle sales and continued losses.

Given three straight quarters of falling revenue through the third quarter of 2026, a key question is how much North American revenue offsets the drop in vehicle sales elsewhere. The scale of DYA revenue in quarterly filings will first appear in the first-quarter 2027 quarterly report. How far the operating margin recovers from 0.2% is another item to check in the next annual report.

Bull factors

North American base starts production

DYA's Georgia plant began first mass production on October 29, 2026, moving the North American base into revenue generation. The point at which local production revenue shows up in quarterly results is the next thing to check. It also said it secured a crash pad award for the SUV hybrid model at a key customer's new Georgia plant.

Operating profit returns to the black

Following operating profit of KRW 4.36 billion in the fourth quarter of 2025, the company posted KRW 6.83 billion in the third quarter of 2026, a second consecutive quarter in the black. The second quarter of 2026 had an operating loss of KRW 7.92 billion, so how long the profitable stretch lasts is the key test. The third-quarter swing also reversed the direction from a year earlier.

Gradual improvement in the balance sheet

The debt ratio fell from 488.2% in 2022 to 414.4% in 2025. Operating cash flow stayed positive after 2023, at KRW 64.3 billion in 2023, KRW 34.7 billion in 2024 and KRW 41.6 billion in 2025. The company has also continued paying cash dividends, which is a shareholder return factor.

Bear factors

Revenue down for three straight quarters

Revenue peaked at KRW 438.8 billion in the fourth quarter of 2025, then declined to KRW 428.1 billion in the first quarter of 2026, KRW 414.8 billion in the second and KRW 333.5 billion in the third. Third-quarter revenue fell 19.4% from a year earlier.

Because core product sales track finished vehicle volumes directly, slower vehicle sales flow straight through to revenue.

Thin margins and accumulated losses

The operating margin was 0.2% in 2025. Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders fell 92.6% to KRW 1.24 billion. The cumulative net loss attributable to controlling shareholders over the last four quarters is KRW 4.95 billion, and the third-quarter 2026 loss widened to KRW 8.04 billion.

Heavy debt load and customer concentration

Total liabilities at the end of 2025 were KRW 476 billion, well above total equity of KRW 114.9 billion. The debt ratio remained high at 414.4% in 2025. Because the business has been built on supplying Hyundai Motor, earnings can react sensitively to changes in that customer's production plans.

10

Risk factors

Vehicle sales and customer concentration

Crash pad and door trim sales track finished vehicle volumes directly. If slower growth in advanced markets persists, total revenue including overseas plants could face further pressure. The Hyundai Motor-centered customer structure also exposes the company to changes in that customer's production plans.

North American plant ramp-up and costs

DYA's Georgia plant has entered first mass production, but the pace and scale of revenue recognition have not yet appeared in filings. Early-stage costs from new equipment and local staffing could weigh on operating profit.

If North American production does not stabilize as planned, the effect of new awards may not translate into earnings.

Financial burden and new business investment

Total liabilities of KRW 476 billion and a debt ratio of 414.4% could become a financial burden if losses continue. New businesses such as ESS for electric-propulsion ships may require additional funding.

Even with operating profit back in the black, the widening net loss attributable to controlling shareholders means the non-operating cost structure needs to be examined.

Overall view

Since 2022 DY Deokyang's revenue has risen from KRW 1.54 trillion to KRW 1.74 trillion, while operating profit has fallen from KRW 30.7 billion to KRW 3.2 billion.

The third quarter of 2026 returned to operating profit of KRW 6.83 billion, but the net loss attributable to controlling shareholders widened to KRW 8.04 billion, so the earnings recovery is not yet complete.

Revenue has declined for three straight quarters since the fourth quarter of 2025, and core product sales are directly linked to finished vehicle volumes. Strengths include the first mass production at DYA's Georgia plant, new orders, a lower debt ratio and continued positive operating cash flow.

Weaknesses include a 0.2% operating margin, a cumulative net loss attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 4.95 billion over the last four quarters, and a still-high debt ratio. Valuation rests on net asset comparisons because no earnings-based multiple can be calculated until profitability is confirmed.

The next checkpoints are the annual profit in the 2026 annual report and the scale of North American revenue in the first-quarter 2027 quarterly report.
