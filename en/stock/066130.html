02

Business structure

Haatz (066130) was founded in 1988 to make and sell kitchen appliances and listed on KOSDAQ in 2003 as a mid-sized company.

Its core lines are range hoods, built-in appliances, residential ventilation systems such as heat exchangers, and air purifying ventilators, and it started a bath business in 2023 to widen its scope.

In a revenue-mix tally from FnGuide's sister profile service (the page does not state the measurement date), hoods account for about 33.8%, built-in appliances (merchandise) about 33.1%, ventilation construction income about 11.2%, bath products (merchandise) about 1.3%, and other items about 20.6%.

Sales depend on multiple channels, including apartment developers, mid-sized cabinet makers, distributors and online malls, with mid-priced range hoods and compact built-in appliances described as the core products.

The ventilation business supplies heat exchangers, ducts and bathroom ventilation to builders and is shaped by the housing performance grade labeling and the mandatory ventilation installation rules.

The company is described as the industry leader in range hoods (company estimate), and a market-share table shows 64% for range hoods and related products in its distribution and special-order division and 19% for the ventilation division, as of September 2025.

Recently it has expanded from hoods and induction cooktops into sink bowls, faucets and food waste disposers, pitching the whole kitchen as one solution. The affiliated group is listed as Byuksan, and the ties to that group are a point to check in periodic filings for transactions and governance. Headcount was 241 as of August 2026.

04

Earnings analysis

On a disclosed basis, revenue rose from KRW 143.1 billion in 2022 to KRW 154.6 billion in 2023 (up 8.0%) and KRW 186.5 billion in 2024 (up 20.6%), then fell 5.2% to KRW 176.9 billion in 2025. Among 2022 to 2025, 2024 was the largest year for revenue and 2022 the smallest.

Operating profit fell from KRW 7.18 billion in 2022 to KRW 6.28 billion in 2023 (down 12.5%), jumped to KRW 10.7 billion in 2024 (up 70.5%), and came to KRW 10.4 billion in 2025 (down 3.4%).

In 2023 revenue grew 8.0% while operating profit fell 12.5%, pushing the operating margin from 5.0% down to 4.1%; the margin then recovered to 5.7% in 2024 and reached 5.9% in 2025.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders went from KRW 4.59 billion in 2022 to KRW 6 billion in 2023 (up 30.7%), KRW 8.88 billion in 2024 (up 47.9%) and KRW 9.07 billion in 2025 (up 2.1%), growing for three consecutive years from 2023 to 2025.

In 2025 operating profit declined 3.4% while attributable net income rose 2.1%, so the items below operating profit that drove this gap are a point to confirm in the periodic report text.

Net income including non-controlling interests was disclosed at KRW 4.59 billion in 2022, KRW 6 billion in 2023, KRW 8.88 billion in 2024 and KRW 9.07 billion in 2025. The debt ratio rose from 38.6% in 2022 to 41.5% in 2023 and 43.8% in 2024 before falling to 36.4% in 2025.

Total equity at the end of 2025 was KRW 116.7 billion, of which the controlling shareholders' stake was also KRW 116.7 billion, against total liabilities of KRW 42.5 billion.

Operating cash flow went from KRW 33.5 million in 2022 to KRW 11 billion in 2023 and KRW 14.5 billion in 2024, then eased to KRW 8.54 billion in 2025.

Quarterly revenue was KRW 40.3 billion in the third quarter of 2025, KRW 47.7 billion in the fourth quarter (up 18.3% from the prior quarter), KRW 36.2 billion in the first quarter of 2026 (down 24.0%), KRW 46.8 billion in the second quarter (up 29.0%), and KRW 34.4 billion in the third quarter of 2026 (down 26.4% from the prior quarter and down 14.7% from a year earlier).

Operating profit was KRW 2.21 billion, KRW 2.89 billion, KRW 1.09 billion and KRW 3.37 billion in the third quarter of 2025 through the second quarter of 2026, then turned into an operating loss of KRW 457 million in the third quarter of 2026, a swing into loss from a year earlier.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders followed the same pattern, ending in a net loss of KRW 202 million in the third quarter of 2026, and the sum over the latest four quarters (fourth quarter of 2025 through third quarter of 2026) was KRW 6.39 billion.

With quarterly revenue ranging between KRW 34.4 billion and KRW 47.7 billion and profit swings this large, the cause of the third-quarter loss is a matter to confirm in the periodic report text and later disclosures.

05

Industry analysis

Haatz's revenue is tied to new housing construction volumes and remodeling demand. FnGuide's profile service describes new housing demand as weakened by the property downturn, with the company responding through new business expansion.

A 2020 securities report called range hoods and gas or electric cooktops essential remodeling items and cited rising remodeling demand as a positive factor. The ventilation business is described as growing steadily under the housing performance grade labeling and the mandatory ventilation installation rules.

As interest in air quality management grows, the air purifying ventilators are widening sales channels into office buildings and multi-use facilities.

In range hoods, the company is described as the industry leader (company estimate), and a market-share table shows 64% for range hoods and related products in the distribution and special-order division as of September 2025.

Its heavy reliance on apartment developers and mid-sized cabinet makers makes it sensitive to construction cycles. On the cycle position, the profile service frames the current stage as one of weakened new housing demand.

In kitchen products, the company is trying to become a supplier covering the whole kitchen through a broader product line. The bath business is being pushed toward the premium segment by strengthening design and convenience.

Outlook

The company has broadened its line from hoods and induction cooktops into sink bowls, faucets and food waste disposers, pitching the whole kitchen as one solution, and a corporate information site reported that it plans to examine whether this business can be applied locally.

The bath business is being pushed further into the premium segment by strengthening design and convenience. In ventilation, the company is developing products that exceed legal standards as demand for better air quality grows.

For the air purifying ventilators, widening sales channels into office buildings and multi-use facilities is listed as a challenge.

The 2026 annual business report, the next periodic filing, will fix fourth-quarter 2026 results in annual terms, so whether the third-quarter loss carried into the fourth quarter is the first point to check.

On revenue, the questions are whether the weakness in new housing demand persists and whether a quarter with a sharp revenue jump, like the second quarter of 2026, recurs. The operating margin was 5.9% in 2025, so whether the 2026 annual report keeps near that level is the key gauge of profitability direction.

Results from the product line expansion would show up in the revenue mix, so the revenue composition table in the annual report is the next point to check. The contribution of the bath and air purifying ventilator businesses to total revenue has to be confirmed from segment information in the periodic reports.

Bear factors

Swing into loss in the third quarter of 2026

The operating loss in the third quarter of 2026 was KRW 457 million and the net loss was KRW 202 million, both swinging into loss from a year earlier. Revenue fell 14.7% year on year to KRW 34.4 billion.

The four-quarter total of net income attributable to controlling shareholders stood at KRW 6.39 billion, and the weaker quarterly profit is a drag on the annual trend.

Large swings in quarterly results

Operating profit fell 62.3% from the prior quarter to KRW 1.09 billion in the first quarter of 2026, then rose 209.1% to KRW 3.37 billion in the second quarter. It then turned into an operating loss in the third quarter. Swings this large make it hard to judge the trend from one or two quarters.

Weaker new housing demand

A financial information site explains that weaker new housing construction demand from the property downturn is reducing sales. Revenue fell 5.2% in 2025 and operating profit fell 3.4%. Because the core products are tied to new housing construction, a continued slowdown would limit any revenue recovery.

Risk factors

Earnings volatility

The third-quarter 2026 operating loss of KRW 457 million and net loss of KRW 202 million show how quickly quarterly profit can turn negative. The gap between the second-quarter 2026 operating profit of KRW 3.37 billion and the first-quarter figure of KRW 1.09 billion is also wide.

When quarterly figures diverge from the annual trend, the baseline for assessing the business is harder to pin down.

Housing cycle

Range hoods and built-in appliances, the core lines, are tied to new housing construction and remodeling demand. A property slowdown cuts builder orders and makes revenue volatile. The 5.2% revenue decline in 2025 and the 14.7% year-on-year drop in third-quarter 2026 revenue are consistent with this risk materializing.

Liquidity and ownership structure

The company's affiliated group is listed as Byuksan, making the group relationship central to its ownership structure. With a small market capitalization, the share price can move sharply depending on trading volume and the free float. Related-party transactions and any change in ownership should be checked in the periodic filings.

Overall view

Haatz's revenue rose from KRW 143.1 billion in 2022 to KRW 176.9 billion in 2025, and its operating margin improved from 5.0% to 5.9%. Net income attributable to controlling shareholders grew for three consecutive years from 2023 to 2025, and the debt ratio fell to 36.4% at the end of 2025.

However, the company swung to an operating loss of KRW 457 million and a net loss of KRW 202 million in the third quarter of 2026, with revenue down 14.7% year on year to KRW 34.4 billion.

Quarterly profit has swung widely, and the core products are tied to new housing demand, so the annual and quarterly trends may diverge.

Strengths include financial capacity, annual earnings recovery and product line expansion, while weaknesses include the quarterly loss, sensitivity to the housing market and small-cap liquidity.

The next checkpoints are the provisional fourth-quarter 2026 results and the 2026 annual business report, where the key questions are whether the third-quarter loss carried into the fourth quarter and whether the operating margin holds near the 2025 level.
