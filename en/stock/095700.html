02

Business structure

Genexine's core asset is GX-I7 (efineptakin alfa), a long-acting interleukin-7 immuno-oncology candidate, and the company also develops biobetter products built on its long-acting hyFc platform.

GX-E4, an anemia treatment, and GX-H9, a growth hormone therapy for growth hormone deficiency, are reported to be in Phase 3 trials on the company website. The China launch timing for GX-H9 has been pushed back several times, from 2024 to 2025 and then to 2026, via its Chinese partner.

In a Phase 2 study of GX-I7 in recurrent glioblastoma, progression-free and overall survival did not show statistically significant differences versus historical controls, as reported in February 2025. GX-I7 is now being advanced through combination studies with MSD's pembrolizumab and Roche's bevacizumab.

In 2026 the bioPROTAC platform, which uses targeted protein degradation, became a new pillar.

GX-BP1 for lung cancer, GX-BP2 for atopic dermatitis, and GX-BP3 for spinocerebellar ataxia are in preclinical or proof-of-concept stages, and preclinical combination data for GX-BP1 were presented at the World Conference on Lung Cancer in September 2026.

Revenue appears to be driven by lumpy receipts such as licensing fees and milestones, and the company's Magok R&D center received its occupancy approval in January 2026.

Earnings analysis

On the disclosed annual figures, sales fell from KRW 16.1 billion in 2022 to KRW 4.43 billion in 2023, a 72.6% decline year on year, and to KRW 2.93 billion in 2024, down 33.7%. Sales recovered to KRW 4.57 billion in 2025, up 56.0% year on year. 2022 was the largest year of the four and 2024 the smallest.

Operating losses were KRW 33.7 billion in 2022, KRW 41.2 billion in 2023, KRW 37.2 billion in 2024 and KRW 36.2 billion in 2025, a loss in each of the four years. The operating margin moved from -208.7% in 2022 to -931.7% in 2023 and -1,270.0% in 2024, then improved to -790.8% in 2025.

The attributable net loss was KRW 56.8 billion, KRW 66.9 billion, KRW 63.7 billion and KRW 27.2 billion for 2022 through 2025, and the total net loss figures match these values. Operating cash flow was negative in every year, at -KRW 39.8 billion, -KRW 38.1 billion, -KRW 1.65 billion and -KRW 29.6 billion.

The debt ratio rose from 31.9% in 2024 to 50.5% in 2025, with total equity of KRW 246.8 billion at year-end 2025.

On a quarterly basis, sales were KRW 3.05 billion in Q3 2025, KRW 537 million in Q4 2025, KRW 415 million in Q1 2026, KRW 972 million in Q2 2026 and KRW 4.16 billion in Q3 2026, the last two quarters both increasing.

Operating losses over the same stretch were KRW 7.42 billion, KRW 12.8 billion, KRW 9 billion, KRW 10.5 billion and KRW 7.06 billion. Q3 2025 recorded an attributable net profit of KRW 21.1 billion despite an operating loss, which points to non-operating items driving the gap. Over the four quarters from Q4 2025 to Q3 2026, the attributable net loss totaled KRW 66.4 billion.

05

Industry analysis

In the bio-pharma sector, immuno-oncology development has shifted toward combinations with checkpoint inhibitors rather than stand-alone use. Genexine's clinical work with MSD's pembrolizumab and Roche's bevacizumab follows that path.

Combination studies, however, depend on partners' drug supply and trial design, and they become business value only when results are positive. The glioblastoma Phase 2 result, which did not clear statistical thresholds, illustrates how high the clinical bar is in this area.

Targeted protein degradation has expanded as researchers seek to address the off-target toxicity limits of small-molecule PROTACs, and the company argues that its nanobody-based binders can reach proteins that small molecules cannot bind.

Because clinical data for this modality are still limited, large pharma partners often ask for clinical evidence before licensing. Peers frequently compared with Genexine include Hanmi Pharm, Yuhan, Alteogen, ABL Bio and Peptron.

Genexine runs three axes at once, long-acting protein platforms, immuno-oncology and protein degradation, so its risk profile differs from that of a company with a single commercialized product.

GX-BP2, being developed for atopic dermatitis, was expected in February 2026 to enter preclinical development in Q4 2026.

Given this pattern, the company's outlook should be confirmed quarter by quarter through its filings.

The company disclosed in August 2026 that it would receive a USD 3 million milestone for completing a Phase 2 study of GX-I7 licensed to its affiliate and licensee, and in October 2026 it signed a Roche supply agreement for a glioblastoma combination study.

Further GX-I7 milestones and the scope of combination studies will depend on the clinical results. With the arbitration concluded in April 2026, the company said it could focus more on its core pipeline and global business expansion.

Bull factors

The August 2026 disclosure said a USD 3 million milestone tied to the Phase 2 completion of GX-I7 was confirmed and due within 30 days. Q3 2026 sales of KRW 4.16 billion rose sharply from the prior quarter, and sales increased in two consecutive quarters. This shows that contract receipts can lift reported revenue.

bioPROTAC pipeline expanding

GX-BP1 has completed animal studies and built an IND package for Phase 1 entry, and preclinical combination data for lung cancer were presented in September 2026. The company expects GX-BP2 to enter preclinical development in Q4 2026.

If the protein-degradation platform wins licensing interest, the business options expand regardless of current revenue scale.

Borrowings and cash outflows

Operating cash flow was -KRW 29.6 billion in 2025. In October 2026, a KRW 3 billion third-party rights issue was withdrawn after the allottee failed to pay.

Recurring delays and clinical setbacks

A Phase 2 study of GX-I7 in recurrent glioblastoma did not reach statistical significance for progression-free or overall survival, as announced in February 2025. The expected approval timing for GX-E4 in Korea has been pushed back several times, and the China launch of GX-H9 moved from 2024 to 2026. Risk factors

Funding

Short-term borrowings and facility-related debt coincide with continuing operating cash outflows, which may require additional funding. The withdrawn October 2026 rights issue suggests the financing route may be limited. Any capital raise could dilute existing shareholders.

Development timing and trial results

The results of GX-I7 combination studies and the clinical entry timing for GX-BP1 and GX-BP2 are central to the business. The company has repeatedly moved its timelines, so whether targets are met should be checked each quarter. Weak clinical data could also weaken negotiating positions in licensing talks.

Lumpy, one-off revenue

Quarterly sales swing widely, from KRW 3.05 billion in Q3 2025 to KRW 537 million in Q4 2025. The Q3 2026 increase appears to reflect milestone receipts, so whether such income recurs is an open question. If one-off receipts do not repeat, the underlying loss structure would become more visible.

Overall view

Genexine cleared its largest legal uncertainty in April 2026, when ICHOR Medical Systems' claim of about KRW 200 billion was dismissed in a final single-instance award.

On the licensing side, the August 2026 GX-I7 milestone disclosure and the October 2026 Roche supply agreement for a combination study were announced, and Q3 2026 sales of KRW 4.16 billion marked a second consecutive quarterly increase.

However, on the disclosed figures, operating losses have run for four straight years from 2022 through 2025, and the attributable net loss over the last four quarters totals KRW 66.4 billion. The strengths, namely the resolved legal overhang and visible licensing income, and the weaknesses, namely prolonged losses and borrowing pressure, are presented here with equal weight.
