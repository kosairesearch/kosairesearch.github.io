02

Business structure

Samhwa Networks is a video content producer founded in 1991 that plans and outsources dramas for terrestrial, cable and general programming channels. Job Korea's company profile shows a business mix of 88% drama, 10% building leasing and 2% music, meaning most revenue comes from drama production and rights supply.

The company says its long-standing relationships with broadcasters secure stable airing slots. It continues to use a system production method to manage production costs efficiently, and recently supplied titles to global OTT platforms such as Disney+.

A representative contract is the drama production supply deal with Studio S announced in June 2025, worth KRW 17.01 billion for a 14-episode series with a term ending January 15, 2026.

Suju Radar named a KRW 16.57 billion contract with PCCW, a KRW 4.75 billion second contract, and a definitive 2026 contract with a kt group studio unit as the next pipeline items. Because revenue is recognized contract by contract, results move sharply with annual broadcast slots and the number of productions.

In competition, the company vies for broadcaster slots with listed drama producers and distributors such as Studio Dragon and CJ ENM.

At the March 2026 shareholder meeting, business purposes including parking operations and restaurant operations were approved, and the company said the aim is to secure side income from idle space in its office building.

Earnings analysis

Annual revenue fell from KRW 82.3 billion in 2022 to KRW 62.4 billion in 2023 (down 24.2% year on year), KRW 46.7 billion in 2024 (down 25.1%) and KRW 37.3 billion in 2025 (down 20.2%).

Revenue declined for three straight years from 2023 through 2025, with 2022 the largest year and 2025 the smallest in the 2022 to 2025 period.

Operating profit of KRW 10.8 billion in 2022 turned into an operating loss of KRW 1.82 billion in 2023, returned to a profit of KRW 671 million in 2024, and swung back to an operating loss of KRW 1.55 billion in 2025. The operating margin moved from 13.1% in 2022 to -2.9% in 2023, 1.4% in 2024 and -4.2% in 2025.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 13.8 billion in 2022, a net loss of KRW 1.24 billion in 2023, KRW 1.86 billion in 2024 and a net loss of KRW 872 million in 2025, while net income including non-controlling interests was reported at the same values.

The company attributed the 2025 deterioration to higher production costs and fewer broadcast slots. Total equity at year-end 2025 was KRW 58.9 billion, all attributable to controlling shareholders, against total liabilities of KRW 919 million... more precisely KRW 9.19 billion.

Operating cash flow was KRW 8.02 billion in 2022, KRW 15.4 billion in 2023 and KRW 6.65 billion in 2024, then turned negative at -KRW 1.19 billion in 2025.

On a quarterly basis, Q3 2025 revenue of KRW 375 million and an operating loss of KRW 1.69 billion gave way to Q4 2025 revenue of KRW 35.6 billion and operating profit of KRW 2.82 billion, a 9,391.1% quarter-on-quarter revenue increase.

Q1 2026 revenue of KRW 353 million fell 99.0% from the prior quarter, then rose to KRW 391 million in Q2 and KRW 482 million in Q3, two straight quarterly gains. The Q3 2026 operating loss of KRW 1.66 billion widened from Q2 but narrowed from a year earlier.

The attributable net loss over the last four quarters totals KRW 936 million. The Q4 2025 profit reflects revenue concentrated in one quarter, so whether it is repeatable should be checked against the 2026 annual report's revenue breakdown.

05

Industry analysis

The drama production market is described as a combination of rising production costs, shrinking content investment and intensifying competition from global production environments. Fnguide's company summary likewise links these factors to lower drama production revenue.

The company itself cited higher production costs and fewer broadcast slots as the causes of its 2025 weakness. Broadcaster scheduling is the starting point of a producer's revenue, so when slot volumes shrink, the number of productions and rights income fall together.

As global OTT platforms have become a major source of production orders, domestic producers are moving toward handling both broadcast scheduling and platform supply. Samhwa Networks positions itself in this environment with a system production approach that controls production costs.

Against larger listed producers and distributors such as Studio Dragon and CJ ENM, its scale is smaller, and with cumulative revenue of about KRW 1.2 billion over Q1 to Q3 2026 it is more exposed to swings in the industry cycle.
