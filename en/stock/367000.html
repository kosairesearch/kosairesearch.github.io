Plateer: AX Mix Rises as Losses Narrow, Quarterly Swings Persist

Plateer lifted AX solution revenue to 56% of sales in the second quarter of 2026 and cut its cumulative January-to-September 2026 operating loss by 52.8% year on year, but quarterly results still swing with the timing of license revenue.

02

Business structure

Plateer is a vertical AI and digital transformation (DX) solutions company whose business runs on two axes: AX (AI transformation) solutions and DX solutions.

AX solution revenue reached KRW 5.77 billion in the second quarter of 2026, which was 56% of that quarter's sales, while the January-to-September 2026 cumulative share was 44%.

By business unit, the cumulative January-to-September revenue was reported as KRW 18.4 billion from agentic commerce (X2BEE, XGEN and related products, 67.7%) and KRW 8.8 billion from AI software infrastructure (Atlassian and GitLab related work, 32.3%).

Key products include the AI commerce platform X2BEE, the agentic AI platform XGEN, the conversational shopping agent genser, and the personalized marketing solution groobee.

Customers are mainly retailers, commerce operators and financial firms, including Lotte Homeshopping, Lotte E-commerce, Jeju Bank, iM Capital, Hyundai Livart Mall and Shinsegae I&C.

In DX, a KRW 29.4 billion three-year supply contract for Samsung Electronics, signed with Samsung SDS in October 2025, is reflected in second-quarter 2026 revenue.

Competitively, Plateer sits in the same IT and software sector as platform operators such as NAVER and Kakao and system integrators such as Samsung SDS, LG CNS and Hyundai Autoever, but it is a small-cap solutions firm. Its Japanese subsidiary, Platier Japan, is 100% owned and is the only consolidated subsidiary.

04

Earnings analysis

On a DART-filed basis, annual revenue fell from KRW 50 billion in 2022 to KRW 33.3 billion in 2023 (down 33.4%) and KRW 29.8 billion in 2024 (down 10.5%), before rebounding to KRW 38.9 billion in 2025 (up 30.4%). Among 2022 to 2025, 2022 was the largest year and 2024 the smallest.

Operating profit of KRW 3.18 billion in 2022 turned into an operating loss of KRW 4.54 billion in 2023, the loss widened to KRW 6.97 billion in 2024, and then narrowed to KRW 4.4 billion in 2025; the company has posted losses for three consecutive years from 2023 to 2025.

The operating margin moved from 6.4% in 2022 to -13.6% in 2023 and -23.4% in 2024, then improved to -11.3% in 2025. Net income attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 3.96 billion in 2022, followed by losses of KRW 3.24 billion in 2023, KRW 5.64 billion in 2024 and KRW 4.29 billion in 2025.

Net income including non-controlling interests was reported as KRW 3.96 billion in 2022 and net losses of KRW 3.24 billion, KRW 5.64 billion and KRW 4.29 billion in 2023 to 2025.

Operating cash flow was KRW 1.6 billion in 2022 and KRW 2.32 billion in 2023, then turned negative at KRW -3.61 billion in 2024 and KRW -2.67 billion in 2025.

The debt-to-equity ratio rose from 19.9% in 2022 to 73.6% in 2025; at the end of 2025 total equity was KRW 32.7 billion, all attributable to controlling shareholders, and total liabilities were KRW 24 billion.

On a quarterly basis, the fourth quarter of 2025 revenue of KRW 12.1 billion was the highest quarter since 2022 according to the company, and operating profit of KRW 1.63 billion marked a return to profit.

The first quarter of 2026 revenue was KRW 8.61 billion (down 29.0% from the prior quarter) with an operating loss of KRW 1.39 billion.

The second quarter of 2026 returned to profit with revenue of KRW 10.4 billion and operating profit of KRW 362 million, while the third quarter of 2026 revenue was KRW 8.22 billion (down 9.7% year on year) with an operating loss of KRW 1.82 billion.

The company attributes quarterly swings mainly to license revenue timing, since license revenue is booked at once when supply and inspection are complete; large second-quarter deliveries pulled revenue forward, while fixed costs such as personnel expenses stayed flat.

Part of the third-quarter net loss reduction came from FX and translation gains of KRW 1.61 billion recorded as non-operating income, and outsourcing costs fell 31.8% year on year.

The sum of net losses attributable to controlling shareholders over the latest four quarters (fourth quarter 2025 to third quarter 2026) was KRW 237 million.

05

Industry analysis

The key structural change in the market Plateer serves is that enterprise AI adoption is moving from pilot projects into production operations.

Discussions at a financial technology conference held in September 2026 centered on how AI agents are expanding into credit review, anomaly detection and customer service, with security and regulation shaping deployment strategies.

On the commerce side, the company says demand for agentic commerce is spreading across the market, but that view comes from the company itself and should not be read as an industry-wide demand indicator.

Whether the industry sits at a peak, trough or mid-cycle point cannot be determined from the evidence available; what can be observed is the rising share of AX revenue and the quarter-to-quarter timing gaps in large contracts.

Among IT and software peers, Samsung SDS, LG CNS and Hyundai Autoever are built around large enterprise system integration, while NAVER and Kakao operate platform-based businesses.

Plateer describes its own move away from a system-integration-centered structure toward higher-margin solutions, which places it between these two models. On product positioning, the company says XGEN has been deployed in projects that replace existing foreign systems at some retailers.

In finance, demand has followed questions about features for environments with network separation requirements. Overseas, Platier Japan is converting technical verification projects with e-commerce operators into formal contracts.

06

Outlook

The nearest identifiable milestone is the Shinsegae I&C project. The commerce platform contract signed in July 2026, worth KRW 7.96 billion, was still in planning and design during the third quarter, so its revenue impact was limited.

The company expects revenue recognition to expand from the fourth quarter of 2026 as the project moves into development and revenue is booked by progress.

Backlog at the end of the third quarter of 2026 was reported at KRW 12.9 billion, up 55% year on year, by Digital Daily; ZDNet reported KRW 13 billion for the same point, and the two figures differ slightly. Either way, the backlog is more than double the end-2025 level of KRW 5.7 billion.

New collaborations include a memorandum with Lotte E-commerce in November 2026 to supply its technology engine for the New LECS brand platform, and the company is also pursuing Tristar Toscar supply and other new fourth-quarter projects.

In Japan, Platier Japan converted technical verification with e-commerce operators into formal contracts, and the genser shopping agent has been deployed at a local food shopping mall and in fashion and beauty platforms. In finance, the company won the iM Capital AI platform project after Jeju Bank.

The company also cites moves to formal contracts after proof-of-concept work in defense, global semiconductors and large manufacturing. The chief executive has set a goal of completing annual profitability through larger commerce projects, wider sales of XGEN and genser, and Japanese agentic commerce results.

He also said quarterly figures will fluctuate while the broader trend has turned toward growth. Whether quarterly results match that trajectory will show up in upcoming reports.

Bull factors

Shift in revenue mix toward AX

AX solution revenue rose 89.3% year on year to KRW 5.77 billion in the second quarter of 2026, lifting its share of sales from 31% to 56%. Cumulative AX revenue for January to September was KRW 12.09 billion, up 35.7%. The company says the shift from system integration toward solutions is showing up in the numbers.

Smaller losses and cash flow turn

Cumulative operating loss for January to September 2026 was KRW 2.84 billion, down 52.8% year on year, and operating cash flow for the same period turned positive at KRW 3.62 billion. Outsourcing costs fell 21.9% and selling and administrative expenses fell 8.3%. Against the negative annual operating cash flow in 2025, this marks a change in direction.

Backlog and large contracts

Backlog at the end of the third quarter of 2026 was reported at KRW 12.9 billion, up 55% year on year.

Key contracts include a KRW 29.4 billion Samsung Electronics supply deal signed with Samsung SDS in October 2025, and a KRW 7.96 billion commerce platform project with Shinsegae I&C signed in July 2026, which the company expects to add more revenue from the fourth quarter.

09

Bear factors

Quarterly earnings volatility

After the second-quarter profit, the third-quarter 2026 operating loss returned to KRW 1.82 billion. Revenue fell 9.7% year on year to KRW 8.22 billion. Large license deliveries concentrated in the second quarter, while fixed costs such as personnel stayed level. The first quarter of 2026 also posted an operating loss of KRW 1.39 billion.

What drove the net loss reduction

The third-quarter net loss fell 62.1% year on year to KRW 880 million, but part of that came from FX and translation gains of KRW 1.61 billion booked as non-operating income. At the operating level, losses continued.

The net loss attributable to controlling shareholders over the latest four quarters still totals KRW 237 million, so whether operating-level performance is improving needs confirmation in upcoming quarters.

Balance sheet and cash flow

The debt-to-equity ratio rose from 19.9% in 2022 to 73.6% in 2025. Operating cash flow was negative for two consecutive years, at KRW -3.61 billion in 2024 and KRW -2.67 billion in 2025.

Advance receipts of KRW 5.27 billion and payables of KRW 20.9 billion rose by the end of the third quarter of 2026, which press reports attribute to new orders rather than borrowing. How fast those orders convert into revenue will determine the cash position.

Risk factors

Customer and contract concentration

The Samsung Electronics DX supply runs through a collaboration with Samsung SDS, so revenue recognition on the KRW 29.4 billion contract depends on that partner's schedule. The Shinsegae I&C project is recognized by development progress, so any schedule slip would directly affect quarterly results. A profile that depends on a few large contracts magnifies quarter-to-quarter swings.

Revenue timing and overseas expansion

License revenue is recognized in a lump when supply and inspection are complete, so quarterly sales and profit can move sharply. Platier Japan has converted proofs of concept into formal contracts, but the speed at which those contracts become revenue is the key question. As overseas operations grow in the quarterly mix, currency effects may also become larger.

Liquidity and funding

The debt-to-equity ratio reached 73.6% at the end of 2025, and operating cash flow was negative in 2024 and 2025. Cash cushions built on advance receipts depend on how quickly orders become revenue. If funding is needed, equity raising could dilute existing shareholders, which is a point to monitor.

Overall view

Plateer's revenue fell after 2022 before rebounding to KRW 38.9 billion in 2025, and its operating loss narrowed to KRW 4.4 billion that year as the company shifts toward AX and DX solutions.

Cumulative revenue for January to September 2026 was KRW 27.2 billion and the cumulative operating loss was KRW 2.84 billion, both improved year on year, but the third quarter alone saw revenue decline and a return to loss.

Strengths include a rising AX share of revenue, a larger backlog, a positive operating cash flow turn and revenue recognition from large contracts.

Weaknesses include quarter-to-quarter swings driven by license timing, the contribution of non-operating gains to the third-quarter net loss reduction, a rising debt-to-equity ratio and negative operating cash flow in 2024 and 2025. Giving equal weight to both sides, the quarterly record is still short.

On valuation, the price-to-book ratio sits below 1x and net losses have continued over the latest four quarters. The next checkpoints are revenue recognition on the Shinsegae I&C project in the fourth quarter of 2026, the preliminary full-year 2026 results, and the 2026 annual business report.
