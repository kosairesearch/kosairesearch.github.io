02

Business structure

Koses develops and manufactures back-end semiconductor equipment and laser application equipment, and has recently expanded into secondary battery manufacturing equipment and automation equipment for AI server electrode cells.

A Korean financial media report citing cumulative data for the first nine months of 2025 put the revenue mix at 34% semiconductor, 16% secondary battery and 26% laser application equipment.

Another report from the same outlet, using a third-quarter basis, gave 83.6% semiconductor and 16.4% secondary battery, so segment shares vary with the classification used.

On the semiconductor side, the company reportedly supplies back-end core equipment such as solder ball attach systems to major domestic chipmakers including Samsung Electronics and SK hynix. Other customers mentioned include ASE, Amkor, Enovix and Bloom Energy.

The Bloom Energy relationship began with a supply contract announced on December 2, 2025 worth about KRW 17.7 billion, and a December 15 disclosure set a contract of KRW 18.9 billion, equal to 27.29% of recent revenue.

The contract covers automation equipment for electrode cells used in AI server applications linked to solid oxide fuel cell (SOFC) production, and the disclosed contract period runs through July 13, 2026.

Bloom Energy is a major US maker of stationary fuel cells, giving Koses a route into power equipment for energy and data center markets beyond semiconductors. Its laser technology is also being applied to OLED manufacturing and precision laser cutting.

The annual scale of Bloom-related revenue can be gauged from segment information in the next annual business report.

04

Earnings analysis

Koses' annual revenue was KRW 72.9 billion in 2022, KRW 95.7 billion in 2023, KRW 69.2 billion in 2024 and KRW 82.4 billion in 2025. 2023 was the largest year and 2024 the smallest among 2022 to 2025, with 2024 revenue falling 27.7% year on year before rising 19.1% in 2025. Profit moved more sharply than revenue.

Operating profit fell from KRW 8.55 billion in 2022 to KRW 8.02 billion in 2023, a 6.2% decline, then turned into an operating loss of KRW 5.61 billion in 2024. Operating profit of KRW 17.4 billion in 2025 marked a return to profit, with an operating margin of 21.1%.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders swung from a loss of KRW 197 million in 2024 to KRW 13.2 billion in 2025, and total net income including non-controlling interests was also KRW 13.2 billion.

At the end of 2025, total equity was KRW 74 billion and total liabilities were KRW 18.2 billion, with the debt-to-equity ratio at 24.6%, down from 49.2% in 2023. Operating cash flow was negative KRW 15 billion in 2023, then KRW 13.1 billion in 2024 and KRW 15.6 billion in 2025.

Quarterly revenue was KRW 22.2 billion in the third quarter of 2025 and KRW 24.3 billion in the fourth quarter, then fell 48.0% quarter on quarter to KRW 12.6 billion in the first quarter of 2026, which marked the low point.

Revenue then rose to KRW 18.6 billion in the second quarter and KRW 62.2 billion in the third quarter, with third-quarter operating profit of KRW 24.3 billion up 581.2% year on year.

Operating profit moved from KRW 1.46 billion in the first quarter of 2026 to KRW 5.73 billion in the second quarter and KRW 24.3 billion in the third, a steepening recovery.

05

Industry analysis

Back-end semiconductor equipment orders move with the capital spending cycle of chipmakers. A first-quarter 2026 earnings commentary cited in a securities report attributed weak results in the chip equipment industry to reduced investment by downstream customers, cost cuts and delayed capex at device makers.

In contrast, SK Securities said in a February 26, 2026 report that surging power demand from AI data centers is driving a broader expansion in demand for solid oxide fuel cell (SOFC) equipment.

Kiwoom Securities, in a January 2026 report, projected data center power demand rising from 55 gigawatts (GW) in 2023 to 219 GW in 2030, and noted that Bloom Energy planned to expand production capacity to 2 GW by year-end.

Reports also linked US tax-credit requirements for a higher share of non-Chinese components to Korean battery and equipment makers moving US lines toward ESS applications. Listed domestic peers in chip equipment include HANMI Semiconductor, EO Technics, Wonik IPS and PSK.

Koses differs by applying its laser technology to expand into adjacent equipment areas. The larger the secondary battery equipment share becomes, however, the more its results will reflect that segment's cycle.

Outlook

In a February 26, 2026 report, SK Securities projected 2026 revenue of KRW 187.6 billion and operating profit of KRW 45.2 billion, and expected Bloom-related revenue of about KRW 80 billion this year and more than KRW 130 billion next year.

Kiwoom Securities, in a January 15, 2026 report, expected operating profit of KRW 47.1 billion. Both forecasts predate the quarterly disclosures for 2026, so direct comparison with current figures is limited.

In an October 20 report, SK Securities estimated third-quarter 2026 revenue at KRW 59.9 billion and operating profit at KRW 18.2 billion, and the disclosed third-quarter figures of KRW 62.2 billion and KRW 24.3 billion came in above those estimates.

The Bloom Energy contract period was disclosed as running through July 13, 2026, so whether volume continued afterward will show up in later disclosures and the periodic report. Whether Bloom's plan to expand capacity to 2 GW by year-end turns into actual orders is another point to watch.

Because first- and second-quarter 2026 revenue were low, fourth-quarter revenue will matter for reaching full-year forecasts. The 2026 annual business report will show revenue by segment and the scale of Bloom-related sales.

Bull factors

New demand tied to Bloom Energy

The Bloom Energy contract disclosed in December 2025 was KRW 18.9 billion, equal to 27.29% of recent revenue. On March 1, 2026, SK Securities assessed that the SOFC capacity expansion cycle had just begun. If Bloom's plan to reach 2 GW of capacity by year-end is realized, the link to equipment demand widens.

Rapid recovery in earnings

Operating profit in 2025 was KRW 17.4 billion, turning positive from a loss of KRW 5.61 billion in 2024, with an operating margin of 21.1%. Third-quarter 2026 operating profit of KRW 24.3 billion rose 581.2% year on year and exceeded SK Securities' October estimate of KRW 18.2 billion. Net income attributable to controlling shareholders over the last four quarters totaled KRW 25.9 billion.

Long-term power infrastructure demand

Kiwoom Securities projected data center power demand rising from 55 GW in 2023 to 219 GW in 2030. That trend could carry through to demand for fuel cell and electrode cell manufacturing equipment. Koses is applying the automation and laser technology it built in semiconductor equipment to this area.

Bear factors

Large swings in quarterly results

First-quarter 2026 revenue of KRW 12.6 billion fell 48.0% from the prior quarter, and fourth-quarter 2025 operating profit of KRW 3.02 billion fell 15.2% from the third quarter. The large third-quarter 2026 growth rates also reflect a low base.

Given the size of quarter-to-quarter swings, the durability of profit depends on additional quarterly disclosures.

Weakness in the semiconductor equipment cycle

A first-quarter 2026 earnings commentary cited in a securities report pointed to reduced investment by downstream customers and delayed capex as causes of weaker results. Revenue in the same quarter fell 32.9% year on year, and operating profit fell 63.1%. During periods when the semiconductor share of revenue is large, the company feels this cycle directly.

Customer concentration and contract continuity

The Bloom Energy contract was disclosed in December 2025 with a period running through July 13, 2026, and whether follow-on volume continues depends on later disclosures. The 2025 operating margin of 21.1% compares with 8.4% in 2023, but a single year does not establish durability. When customer concentration is high, the end of one contract can significantly affect revenue.

Risk factors

Customer and contract concentration

The disclosed period for the Bloom Energy contract runs through July 13, 2026. Because the contract equaled a sizable share of recent revenue, quarterly sales could swing again if follow-on orders stop. Detailed shares for other customers are not broken out in the disclosures reviewed.

Semiconductor capex cycle

A first-quarter 2026 earnings commentary identified reduced downstream investment and cost cuts at device makers as drags on results. Chip equipment orders can change quickly with customer capex plans, so the timing of demand recovery in the core business is uncertain. How the cycle shaped the higher 2025 operating margin is an item for the periodic reports.

Business shift and differences across sources

Koses is shifting its business mix from semiconductor equipment toward secondary battery and energy equipment, so its revenue composition is changing quickly. Media reports classify segment shares differently, so the same period can show different proportions depending on the source. The profitability of this shift can be compared only once segment disclosures accumulate.

Overall view

Koses moved from an operating loss in 2024 to operating profit of KRW 17.4 billion and a 21.1% operating margin in 2025, and disclosed third-quarter 2026 revenue of KRW 62.2 billion and operating profit of KRW 24.3 billion.

Automation equipment for AI server electrode cells supplied to Bloom Energy is cited as a main driver of the profit recovery. On the other side, quarterly results swing widely, with first-quarter 2026 revenue falling to KRW 12.6 billion, and the continuity of a few customer contracts strongly affects the profit path.

Cycle effects from a slowdown in chip equipment and a rising share of secondary battery equipment are also variables to weigh.

Securities forecasts were made long before the latest disclosures and are hard to compare directly with current figures, but the third-quarter disclosure did exceed SK Securities' October estimate.

On valuation, the stock trades at a substantial premium to net asset value, and how multiples are read depends on the pace of profit recovery. The next checkpoints are Bloom Energy's capacity expansion by year-end 2026 and the 2026 annual business report, and strengths and weaknesses should be weighed on equal footing.
