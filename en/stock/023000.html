02

Business structure

Samwonsteel is a vehicle spring steel maker founded in 1992. It was formed as a joint venture between a spring maker and a major steelmaker, and it completed its plant in 1994. The business is divided into rolling, leaf spring and steel processing segments, producing hot-rolled steel material and finished leaf springs.

Butler's breakdown based on second-quarter 2026 revenue shows steel processing products at 52%, spring products at 46% and others at 2%, while Alphasquare reports 52.90%, 44.40% and 2.70%, so the two sources differ somewhat. Those two product lines make up most of revenue.

Key products are hot-rolled steel, processed steel materials, pipes and leaf springs, and the company is classified under auto parts. Its hot-rolled products hold a dominant domestic position, while leaf springs operate in an oligopoly.

Customers include vehicle spring makers and automakers, and the company supplies spring makers in several regions besides its largest shareholder group. As of a March 2026 filing, the largest shareholder group held 54.81% of shares, with a 54.64% stake held by Daewon Kang Up.

The company is investing in steel processing, including building a new pipe forming plant in line with the automotive lightweighting trend.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual revenue fell from KRW 364.1 billion in 2022 to KRW 359.1 billion in 2023 (down 1.4% year on year) and KRW 343.6 billion in 2024 (down 4.3%), before rebounding to KRW 359.3 billion in 2025 (up 4.6%). Among 2022 to 2025, 2022 was the largest year and 2024 the smallest.

Operating profit went from KRW 16.5 billion in 2022 to KRW 16.5 billion in 2023 (down 0.2%), KRW 11.8 billion in 2024 (down 28.8%) and KRW 9.88 billion in 2025 (down 16.0%), declining for three consecutive years from 2023. Operating margin moved from 4.5% in 2022 and 4.6% in 2023 to 3.4% in 2024 and 2.7% in 2025.

In 2025 revenue grew, but that growth did not translate into higher profit, so the margin declined.

Profit attributable to controlling shareholders rose from KRW 13 billion in 2022 to KRW 16.6 billion in 2023 (up 27.6%), then fell to KRW 13.2 billion in 2024 (down 20.3%) and KRW 10.7 billion in 2025 (down 19.2%), declining for two consecutive years.

Net income was reported at KRW 13 billion in 2022, KRW 16.6 billion in 2023, KRW 13.2 billion in 2024 and KRW 10.7 billion in 2025. At end-2025, total equity was KRW 258.7 billion, all of it attributable to controlling shareholders, and total liabilities were KRW 48.9 billion.

The debt ratio fell from 27.3% in 2022 and 25.2% in 2023 to 16.0% in 2024, then edged up to 18.9% in 2025. Operating cash flow rose every year, from KRW 6.22 billion in 2022 to KRW 17.8 billion in 2023, KRW 19.2 billion in 2024 and KRW 33.3 billion in 2025.

Quarterly revenue went from KRW 93.2 billion in the third quarter of 2025 to KRW 82.5 billion in the fourth quarter (down 11.5% quarter on quarter), then KRW 84.8 billion in the first quarter of 2026, KRW 93.3 billion in the second quarter (up 10.0%) and KRW 86.8 billion in the third quarter (down 7.0% quarter on quarter and down 6.9% year on year).

Quarterly operating profit was KRW 4.17 billion, KRW 1.67 billion, KRW 2.44 billion, KRW 2.6 billion and KRW 1.72 billion across the third quarter of 2025 through the third quarter of 2026, with the latest quarter down 58.8% year on year. Profit attributable to controlling shareholders over the last four quarters totaled KRW 11.2 billion.

Bull factors

Recovery in auto production

Revenue rose 4.6% to KRW 359.3 billion in 2025. Whether the production outlook translates into sales and profit is the test for this bullish case.

Cash generation and financial capacity

Operating cash flow rose every year, from KRW 6.22 billion in 2022 to KRW 33.3 billion in 2025. Total liabilities at end-2025 were KRW 48.9 billion, and the debt ratio of 18.9% is at a low level. All of the KRW 258.7 billion in total equity is attributable to controlling shareholders. Cash generation rising while operating profit declined can be cited as a supporting point for the bullish case.

Steel processing expansion and overseas production

One assessment points to rising global market share as domestic automakers raise their overseas production share. Butler's count puts steel processing products at 52% of revenue based on second-quarter 2026.

Bear factors

Persistent margin decline

Operating margin fell from 4.5% in 2022 to 2.7% in 2025. Operating profit has declined for three consecutive years from 2023, and profit attributable to controlling shareholders fell for two consecutive years in 2024 and 2025. Revenue grew in 2025 while profit fell, so sales scale alone does not explain profitability. If margins do not recover, this bearish case persists.

Profit softness in 2026 quarters

Third-quarter 2026 revenue was KRW 86.8 billion, down 6.9% year on year. In the same quarter operating profit was KRW 1.72 billion, down 58.8%, and profit attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 1.82 billion, down 52.4%. Company Monitor described 2026 profitability as limited by cost burdens. Profit attributable to controlling shareholders over the last four quarters totaled KRW 11.2 billion.

Shareholder structure and float

The largest shareholder group held 54.81% of shares in a March 2026 filing, a high concentration. Company Monitor put the free float ratio at 37.55%. With low foreign ownership, trading liquidity may be constrained. A concentrated controlling stake means relatively few shares are available to minority holders.

Risk factors

Cost and profitability

Company Monitor described profitability as limited by cost burdens. With operating margin at 2.7% in 2025, even modest cost increases or unadjusted selling prices can have a large effect on profit.

Quarterly operating profit falling from KRW 2.6 billion in the second quarter of 2026 to KRW 1.72 billion in the third is consistent with this risk. Cost trends and pricing adjustments are the main items to monitor.

Auto demand and trade environment

Revenue depends on the auto production cycle. The 2026 production outlook assumes easing US trade risk and continued hybrid preference. If either condition changes, the demand outlook could shift. Third-quarter 2026 revenue falling year on year shows that demand volatility is already visible in results.

Concentration of business and ownership

Revenue is concentrated in two product lines, steel processing and springs. Demand or pricing shifts in either line would weigh on overall results. The high stake held by the largest shareholder group means changes in control or shareholder composition could affect the share price. The March 2026 filings reported executive changes alongside stake changes.

Overall view

Samwonsteel's 2025 revenue rose to KRW 359.3 billion, but operating profit fell to KRW 9.88 billion and the operating margin dropped to 2.7%. Operating profit has declined for three consecutive years from 2023, and profit attributable to controlling shareholders fell for two consecutive years in 2024 and 2025.

Bullish factors include the production outlook and the domestic dominance of hot-rolled products, while bearish factors include cost burdens and the declining margin trend. The high stake held by the largest shareholder group also limits the float and is worth considering.
