02

Business structure

Laseroptek is a specialist maker of laser devices used for skin aesthetics and the treatment of medical conditions. In a February 2026 interview, the company's CEO said the current revenue mix is about 80% aesthetics and 20% medical treatment, with a medium-to-long-term goal of raising the treatment share to 40%.

Its flagship pigment laser, 'HELIOS 785 PICO', recorded cumulative third-quarter 2026 sales of KRW 7.35 billion, up about KRW 2.1 billion from the prior-year period, according to press reports.

The 'LOTUS III' erbium:YAG laser, combined with a needle-free drug delivery system, posted cumulative sales of KRW 3.5 billion, up 63.1%. VASCURA 589, a 589-nanometer solid-state vascular laser launched in June 2026, targets vascular skin conditions such as hemangiomas as well as cosmetic procedures.

By region, cumulative third-quarter 2026 domestic sales were KRW 11.86 billion (up 32.9%), European sales KRW 2.26 billion (up 17.1%) and Latin American sales KRW 1.66 billion (up 368.8%).

In Brazil, the company holds an exclusive partnership with the local firm ENTERA and has secured health-regulator approval for three core products.

Domestic customers are mainly dermatology and plastic surgery clinics, and the company has built a direct sales network in western U.S. states and Texas and expanded into Canada. Litigation with a U.S. competitor that began in 2024 has moved to the merits trial stage and has weighed on U.S. revenue.

The structure therefore combines aesthetic equipment and consumables with distributor-led overseas sales, and broadening into medical treatment is the key test of revenue diversity.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual revenue rose from KRW 30 billion in 2022 to KRW 34.4 billion in 2023, a 14.3% increase, which marks the peak year. Revenue then declined 3.8% to KRW 33 billion in 2024 and fell 22.2% to KRW 25.7 billion in 2025, so revenue decreased for two consecutive years from 2024 through 2025.

Among 2022 to 2025, 2023 was the largest year and 2025 the smallest. Operating profit reached KRW 4.48 billion in 2023, up 78.5%, before turning into an operating loss of KRW 196 million in 2024, and the operating loss widened to KRW 12 billion in 2025.

The operating margin moved from 8.4% in 2022 and 13.0% in 2023 to -0.6% in 2024 and -46.9% in 2025.

Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders fell 99.4% to KRW 16.78 million in 2023, then became a net loss of KRW 783 million in 2024 and KRW 10.7 billion in 2025, which is two consecutive years of losses from 2024 through 2025.

Net income including non-controlling interests was a net loss of KRW 10.7 billion in 2025. Operating cash flow went from KRW 1.44 billion in 2022 to -KRW 196 million in 2023, -KRW 3.34 billion in 2024 and -KRW 6.39 billion in 2025.

A half-year 2025 press report pointed to lower U.S. revenue during litigation with a U.S. competitor, and to delayed Middle East shipments that pushed some sales into the third quarter.

On a five-quarter basis, third-quarter 2025 revenue of KRW 9.87 billion was the highest, and third-quarter 2026 revenue was KRW 7.22 billion, down 26.9% year on year.

Operating losses narrowed from KRW 2.97 billion in the third quarter of 2025 and KRW 4.49 billion in the fourth quarter to KRW 1.95 billion, KRW 991 million and KRW 341 million across the first three quarters of 2026. This extends five consecutive quarters of operating losses, while the size of the losses is shrinking.

By contrast, the net loss attributable to controlling shareholders in the third quarter of 2026 was KRW 2.63 billion, up from KRW 772 million in the second quarter and larger than the operating loss.

The four-quarter total of net losses attributable to controlling shareholders, from the fourth quarter of 2025 through the third quarter of 2026, was KRW 7.13 billion.

The company said cumulative third-quarter 2026 selling and administrative expenses fell 8.7% and bad-debt expense dropped sharply, which helped narrow the operating loss.

05

Industry analysis

Laseroptek sits in the electronics and components sector by classification, but its business is closer to medical devices. Laser demand for skin aesthetics and for medical treatment of skin conditions moves with both consumer beauty spending and clinic treatment demand.

In a January 2026 press report, the company said demand for high-power vascular lasers in the global skin-condition treatment and medical aesthetics market has been rising steadily. This is the company's own market view.

In the U.S., litigation with a competitor and the insurance code framework shape the operating environment. The company said the insurance code framework is being settled in a direction favorable to it.

Asia sales in the first half of 2026 were KRW 4.6 billion, up 101.9% year on year, supported by dual distributors and wider sales channels in Japan and the Middle East. In the first half of 2025, revenue fell 50.9%, which the company attributed mainly to the U.S. litigation and delayed Middle East shipments.

That pattern suggests reported results have moved more with company-specific litigation and geopolitical factors than with sector-wide conditions. Growth in the skin-booster market was cited as a driver of LOTUS III sales.

Competitive position depends on product differentiation, such as the 589-nanometer solid-state vascular laser and the needle-free drug delivery combination line, and on the outcome of the U.S. litigation.

Outlook

Fourth-quarter 2026 results will first be disclosed in the 2026 annual report. In its third-quarter 2026 release, the company said it aims to keep improving results in the fourth quarter.

VASCURA 589 was launched in June 2026, and the company plans to expand sales in the second half of 2026 and grow exports of its core products. U.S. FDA approval is being pursued with a target of the first quarter of 2027, and approval would determine the timing of entry into North America.

The company expects the local sales environment to improve if U.S. litigation risk eases and insurance code changes become visible, possibly as early as the first quarter of 2027.

In overseas markets, the company changed its distributor in Brazil, secured new supply contracts and opened a direct office to strengthen sales and customer support. The company has set a medium-to-long-term goal of raising the medical-treatment share of revenue to 40%.

On funding, the KRW 11.7 billion rights offering decided in February 2026 is reported to be allocated to raw material procurement, new product development, overseas certification and clinical work, and marketing. These plans are the company's own statements, and their effect will show up in later disclosed results.
