02

Business structure

Sands LAB is a cyber threat intelligence (CTI) specialist that supplies intelligence products used to anticipate and respond to cyber threats. Its main revenue has been generated from CTI product sales, although the reported revenue mix differs by source.

A financial information site put product sales at 99.47% and SI (system integration) at 0.53% as of late September 2026, while a press report on the first nine months of 2025 showed CTI revenue of KRW 1.009 billion and SI revenue of KRW 4.216 billion.

The different aggregation bases make a firm segment split difficult to establish. In SI, the company won the government's C-Brain project, a cybersecurity-focused generative AI build, as part of a consortium with PersonaAI.

The 2025 pilot was about KRW 5 billion, and the project was reported to move into its main phase in 2026 with a scale of KRW 15 billion.

Overseas, the company signed an export deal with a Japanese local distributor covering 1,000 units of MNX, its cloud and lightweight product, with annual production of roughly 4,000 units and further supply contracts reported as planned.

The company positions the export of its network detection and response (NDR) solution to Japan as a medium-term growth pillar. Its 2026 corporate value enhancement plan sets out technology competitiveness, profit-focused management, and R&D capability as core goals.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual revenue rose from KRW 9.3 billion in 2022 to KRW 11.7 billion in 2023, up 26.0% year on year, fell to KRW 8.3 billion in 2024, down 29.2%, and recovered to KRW 11.6 billion in 2025, up 40.0%. Among the years from 2022 to 2025, 2023 was the largest and 2024 the smallest.

Operating profit of KRW 2.09 billion in 2022 turned into an operating loss of KRW 884 million in 2023, which widened to KRW 3.71 billion in 2024 before narrowing to KRW 1.77 billion in 2025. The operating margin moved from 22.5% in 2022 to -7.5% in 2023, -44.7% in 2024 and -15.2% in 2025.

Operating and net results moved in different directions in some years; in 2023 attributable net income of KRW 439 million stood against an operating loss, which points to non-operating items affecting the bottom line.

In 2025 the net loss including non-controlling interests was KRW 465 million, while the attributable net loss was KRW 550 million, so the two figures should be read separately.

Operating cash flow swung from KRW 3.53 billion in 2022 to -KRW 8.03 billion in 2023 and then -KRW 212 million in 2024, before turning to KRW 325 million in 2025.

On a quarterly basis, revenue climbed from KRW 802 million in the third quarter of 2025 to KRW 6.39 billion in the fourth quarter, up 696.0% from the prior quarter, when operating profit of KRW 2.53 billion and an attributable net income of KRW 1.49 billion marked a return to profit.

Press coverage noted that CTI revenue typically concentrates in the fourth quarter. In 2026, revenue fell for three straight quarters, to KRW 1.83 billion in the first quarter (down 71.4% from the prior quarter), KRW 1.55 billion in the second and KRW 1.21 billion in the third.

Operating losses were KRW 1.01 billion in the first quarter, KRW 866 million in the second and KRW 1.31 billion in the third.

Industry analysis

The market Sands LAB serves is seeing cyber threats grow alongside the spread of AI, and companies adopting AI are creating new security demand. Press coverage interpreted these expectations as already reflected in the share price.

A report also highlighted that Nvidia's chief executive named cybersecurity as a next growth market for AI, which was cited as a driver of investor sentiment. In September 2026, the stock rose more than 20% in a single session on the AI security theme, a move that underscored its trading as a thematic stock.

Demand in the security sector follows the timing of security budgets at companies and public agencies, and Sands LAB's revenue shows a pattern of concentrating in the fourth quarter, when those budgets tend to be spent.

Ahnlab is a listed security peer in the same industry, while Sands LAB focuses on the specific CTI segment. CTI analysis of threat intelligence differs from general security products in that it blends product sales with build-out projects.

The C-Brain project serves as an example of public security demand translating into a product and project flow. Japan has emerged as a new overseas market, and how fast export contracts are recognized as revenue is a company-specific variable separate from the sector cycle.

Even with a supportive industry backdrop, quarterly results can swing widely depending on the timing of revenue recognition and contract delivery.
