NHN Bugs: Profit Turn Meets Continued Revenue Decline

NHN Bugs kept posting operating profit into the third quarter of 2026, but revenue has declined for three straight years through 2025, and the controlling-shareholder net loss widened again in that quarter to KRW 814 million.

  1. 1

    Operating profit in the third quarter of 2026 was KRW 221 million, turning positive from an operating loss of KRW 412 million a year earlier, though it was below the KRW 496 million of the prior quarter.

  2. 2

    Revenue peaked at KRW 11.3 billion in the fourth quarter of 2025 and then fell to KRW 10.7 billion, KRW 10.6 billion and KRW 9.96 billion in the first three quarters of 2026.

  3. 3

02

Business structure

NHN Bugs operates the Bugs music streaming platform. The company was founded in 2002 and listed on KOSDAQ in 2009. Its business is split into two main lines. The first is consumer music streaming centered on the Bugs app, and the second is business-to-business (B2B) music distribution.

According to the company's first-half 2026 earnings commentary from an independent data provider, B2B music distribution accounts for more than half of total revenue. The Essential curation brand has moved beyond the music app into other settings.

After home appliances from Samsung Electronics and other makers, an Essential app for Hyundai Motor Group's next-generation infotainment system launched in May 2026, and in June 2026 Essential was installed in the infotainment system of a new Toyota RAV4 through a telecom operator.

Essential subscribers stood at about 1.52 million as of June 2026. On August 20, 2026, the Bugs app home screen was reorganized into HOT & NEW, genre and theme tabs, and a 'MIX Playlist' reflecting listening history was added.

The domestic music streaming market is regarded as saturated, and Bugs is described as a first-generation music platform. Competing against large platforms, the company is widening revenue through B2B channels and adjacent areas such as cars and appliances.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual revenue fell from KRW 65.8 billion in 2022 to KRW 56.8 billion in 2023 (down 13.7% year on year), KRW 52.1 billion in 2024 (down 8.4%) and KRW 44.9 billion in 2025 (down 13.8%).

Revenue declined for three consecutive years from 2023 through 2025, with 2022 the largest year and 2025 the smallest in the 2022–2025 range. Profitability weakened over the same period. The operating margin slid from 3.2% in 2022 to 1.4% in 2023 and 0.7% in 2024 before reaching -1.6% in 2025.

Operating profit went from KRW 2.08 billion in 2022 to KRW 804 million in 2023 (down 61.4%) and KRW 357 million in 2024 (down 55.5%), and the company recorded an operating loss of KRW 735 million in 2025, turning into a deficit.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 2.53 billion in 2022, turned into a loss of KRW 5.07 billion in 2023, widened to a loss of KRW 12.7 billion in 2024, and narrowed to a loss of KRW 6.22 billion in 2025. Net income including non-controlling interests followed the same direction.

The debt ratio rose from 26.9% in 2022 to 34.6% in 2024 and was 31.8% in 2025. At year-end 2025, total equity was KRW 50.7 billion, all attributable to controlling shareholders, and total liabilities were KRW 16.1 billion.

Operating cash flow was KRW 2.47 billion in 2022, KRW 2.45 billion in 2023, KRW 8.84 billion in 2024 and KRW 5.37 billion in 2025, remaining positive even in the loss year.

Quarterly, an operating loss of KRW 412 million in the third quarter of 2025 became an operating profit of KRW 83.88 million in the fourth quarter, followed by KRW 175 million in the first quarter of 2026, KRW 496 million in the second and KRW 221 million in the third.

Revenue slid from KRW 11.3 billion in the fourth quarter of 2025 to KRW 9.96 billion in the third quarter of 2026, and third-quarter operating profit fell 55.4% from the prior quarter.

Controlling-shareholder net income was KRW 478 million in the first quarter of 2026 and KRW 240 million in the second, before a net loss of KRW 814 million in the third. The combined controlling-shareholder net loss over the last four quarters was KRW 4.9 billion. A corporate earnings commentary attributed the return to operating profit mainly to cost reduction.

Industry analysis

The domestic music streaming market is regarded as saturated. When NHN agreed in January 2026 to sell its Bugs stake, the reasons cited included market saturation and difficult profitability, and the parent said it would concentrate resources on core businesses such as AI, games and cloud.

Bugs is described as a first-generation music platform and competes with large platforms in the consumer subscription market. For that reason, the company is looking for revenue in B2B music distribution and adjacent channels such as cars and home appliances.

The August 2026 home screen redesign and the introduction of the MIX Playlist can also be read as efforts to widen user contact. On the earnings side, operating profit turned positive while revenue declined, a result that appears tied to cost restructuring.

An IT industry official said that regardless of whether the stake sale proceeds, reversing the revenue decline is the company's task. The same official said that business decisions to reverse shrinking revenue will be a variable for profitability.

Direct peer comparisons are limited, so comparisons with listed media and entertainment companies need to account for differences in business structure.

NHN Bugs has seen its operating margin fall while revenue declined from 2022 onward, but operating profit stayed positive from the first to the third quarter of 2026, reversing the earlier direction of profitability.

On an annual basis, 2025 included an operating loss of KRW 735 million and a controlling-shareholder net loss of KRW 6.22 billion. In the third quarter of 2026, operating profit was KRW 221 million, but the controlling-shareholder net loss widened to KRW 814 million.
