Dual: Shareholder Change Tests Margin Recovery

02

Business structure

Dual was founded in 1983 and makes automotive interior parts, including seat fabric, seat covering and airbag cushions, supplying automakers and parts groups such as Hyundai Mobis and a major airbag maker.

A market data aggregator put the 2023 third-quarter sales mix at roughly 70% seats, 24% fabric and 7% airbags, a snapshot of that period rather than a current split.

A securities research note from November 2023 said about 65% of cumulative sales in the first nine months of that year went to Hyundai Motor and a Hyundai Motor Group parts affiliate, including their subsidiaries.

Because seat covering depends mainly on the Hyundai Motor Group, earnings track automakers' production plans and new-model launches.

Overseas, the company runs seat covering and fabric subsidiaries in China, Portugal, Romania, the Czech Republic, Tunisia, Turkey, Mexico, Brazil and Indonesia, spreading production across several countries. R&D spending in 2025 equaled 1.09% of sales.

Listed companies in the same automotive classification include Seoyon E-Hwa and PIM Korea. The company does not disclose firm order backlog or product-level profit, which limits direct comparison of profitability across segments.

Press coverage of the share purchase agreement said the medium- to long-term strategic direction may change after the transaction.

04

Earnings analysis

Revenue rose from KRW 566 billion in 2022 to KRW 753.1 billion in 2023, up 33.1% year on year, then grew 2.7% to KRW 773.3 billion in 2024 and 3.0% to KRW 796.1 billion in 2025, so the pace of growth slowed.

Revenue increased for three consecutive years from 2023 through 2025, and 2025 was the largest year in the 2022 to 2025 period. Operating profit climbed from KRW 13.3 billion in 2022 to KRW 44.1 billion in 2023, up 231.5%, eased 2.7% to KRW 42.9 billion in 2024, and recovered to KRW 48.6 billion in 2025, up 13.2%.

The operating margin moved from 2.3% in 2022 to 5.9% in 2023, then held at 5.6% in 2024 and 6.1% in 2025. In 2025, operating profit grew faster than revenue, so margin improvement drove most of the gain.

Controlling-shareholder net income was KRW 7.27 billion in 2022, KRW 20.8 billion in 2023 (up 186.6%), KRW 16.1 billion in 2024 (down 22.6%) and KRW 28.8 billion in 2025 (up 78.7%).

Net income including non-controlling interests was KRW 31.3 billion in 2025, and the gap from controlling-shareholder income reflects the non-controlling share. Operating cash flow was KRW 50.7 billion in 2025, above operating profit of KRW 48.6 billion, and the debt ratio fell from 133.2% in 2022 to 96.7% in 2025.

Quarterly, the third quarter of 2025 showed operating profit of KRW 15.8 billion and controlling-shareholder net income of KRW 11.2 billion, but the fourth quarter fell to operating profit of KRW 2.93 billion, down 81.4% from the prior quarter, and a controlling-shareholder net loss of KRW 1.38 billion.

The first quarter of 2026 returned to operating profit of KRW 6 billion and controlling-shareholder net income of KRW 3.72 billion, and the second quarter improved to operating profit of KRW 8.27 billion and net income of KRW 7.6 billion.

The third quarter of 2026 brought revenue of KRW 188.1 billion, down 5.2% year on year, operating profit of KRW 6.29 billion, down 60.2%, and a controlling-shareholder net loss of KRW 3.7 billion.

Whether the fourth-quarter 2025 and third-quarter 2026 losses reflect one-off items cannot be judged from the disclosed figures alone, so the cost lines in the periodic reports need to be read alongside them.

05

Industry analysis

Automaker demand is the starting point for earnings. A first-quarter 2026 earnings commentary carried by a financial information site attributed weaker automaker demand, amid a global slowdown, softer consumer sentiment and job insecurity, to lower sales and profitability across the interior-materials segment.

The 5.2% year-on-year revenue decline in the third quarter of 2026 points in the same direction. Because revenue depends on Hyundai Motor Group production, changes in the group's output and model mix pass through directly.

Within the automotive sector, Dual is classified as a company specializing in interior materials such as seats, fabric and airbag cushions. Its overseas subsidiaries span Europe, Asia, North America and South America, allowing supply close to local automaker plants.

Mortrex's interior-flooring business is adding seat and fabric capabilities to offer packaged cabin materials per vehicle. How this combination changes contract structures with automaker customers will need to be checked against later disclosures and results.

Among the last five quarters in the disclosed record, the second quarter of 2026 had the largest revenue at KRW 209.2 billion, and the third quarter fell back from that level.

06

Outlook

The largest variable is the change of largest shareholder. On April 30, 2026, the largest shareholder IHC and related parties signed a share purchase agreement to sell their holdings to Mortrex's buyer, an acquiring company that will take a 62.23% stake for KRW 145.2 billion.

The scheduled closing date was disclosed as July 31, 2026, but the timing may shift depending on fulfillment of conditions precedent and payment of the balance. The acquirer plans to add seat and fabric capabilities to its flooring-centered interior business to broaden cabin portfolios across vehicles.

An extraordinary shareholders' meeting on June 30, 2026 placed on its agenda an approval for a plan covering treasury share holding and disposal. The 2025 year-end dividend was decided on February 24, 2026, with a total payout of KRW 3.4 billion and a record date of December 31, 2025.

The next checkpoint is whether the closing disclosure confirms the change of largest shareholder. Later filings should also show whether management composition and business direction shift.

In the 2026 annual report, the key items are whether the full-year operating margin exceeds the 6.1% of 2025 and whether the cost structure behind the fourth-quarter 2025 loss becomes visible.

08

Bull factors

Margin recovery and cash generation

The operating margin rose from 2.3% in 2022 to 5.9% in 2023 and reached 6.1% in 2025. Operating cash flow of KRW 50.7 billion in 2025 exceeded operating profit of KRW 48.6 billion, confirming cash conversion. The debt ratio fell from 133.2% in 2022 to 96.7% in 2025, reducing financial burden.

Expansion through the shareholder change

The acquirer said it will combine its flooring-centered interior business with Dual's seat and fabric capabilities to strengthen packaged cabin proposals. It described the combination as a base for expanding customers, regions and products. These are the acquirer's plans, and the actual order and revenue effects must be confirmed in later results.

Balance sheet and earnings rebound

Total equity at the end of 2025 was KRW 279.3 billion, of which KRW 276.2 billion belonged to controlling shareholders. Controlling-shareholder net income recovered from KRW 20.8 billion in 2023 and KRW 16.1 billion in 2024 to KRW 28.8 billion in 2025.

A total dividend of KRW 3.4 billion for 2025 was decided in February 2026, keeping shareholder returns in place.

Bear factors

Volatility in quarterly earnings

Operating profit in the fourth quarter of 2025 was KRW 2.93 billion, down 81.4% from the prior quarter, and the company swung to a controlling-shareholder net loss of KRW 1.38 billion. The third quarter of 2026 also recorded a controlling-shareholder net loss of KRW 3.7 billion.

Controlling-shareholder net income over the last four quarters totaled KRW 6.23 billion, well below the KRW 28.8 billion for full-year 2025.

Revenue decline and softer demand

Third-quarter 2026 revenue was KRW 188.1 billion, down 5.2% year on year. A first-quarter 2026 earnings commentary attributed weaker automaker demand to a global slowdown and softer consumer sentiment.

Lower profitability in the interior-materials segment was also cited, making demand recovery the key to earnings recovery.

Customer concentration and governance uncertainty

About 65% of cumulative sales in the first nine months of 2023 went to Hyundai Motor and a Hyundai Motor Group parts affiliate, indicating high dependence on one customer group. The company disclosed that the change of largest shareholder is not final until closing.

Press coverage has said the medium- to long-term strategy may shift, so the post-acquisition business structure remains uncertain.

Risk factors

Customer and demand concentration

Because the business depends heavily on Hyundai Motor Group, shifts in automaker output or model mix can move both revenue and margins. The third-quarter 2026 revenue decline and the demand slowdown cited in the first-quarter commentary are signs of this exposure.

Change in ownership control

A change in controlling shareholder can alter medium- to long-term strategy, business structure and the management of subsidiaries. Until the closing and management disclosures are filed, the extent of any strategic change is hard to gauge.

Share price volatility and trading concentration

On September 9, 2026, the share price surged 25% intraday from the prior close before giving back much of the gain. Trading volume rose more than 50-fold from the previous day, yet no clear new supply contract or earnings disclosure explained the move. Large price swings driven by short-term capital inflows are a risk in themselves.

Overall view

Dual is an automotive interior-materials company founded in 1983 that supplies seats, fabric and airbag cushions, mainly to Hyundai Motor Group-linked customers. From 2022 to 2025, revenue rose from KRW 566 billion to KRW 796.1 billion, and operating profit recovered to KRW 48.6 billion.

The operating margin increased from 2.3% in 2022 to 6.1% in 2025, and the debt ratio fell to 96.7%.

On the other side, a controlling-shareholder net loss in the fourth quarter of 2025 and another in the third quarter of 2026 of KRW 3.7 billion made quarterly earnings more volatile, and third-quarter 2026 revenue fell 5.2% year on year.

A share purchase agreement signed on April 30, 2026 has an acquiring company taking a 62.23% stake, and press coverage has pointed to a possible change in strategic direction.

Strength comes from margin recovery and a stronger balance sheet, while weakness comes from volatile quarterly earnings, softer automaker demand, customer concentration and uncertainty over the ownership change.

The next checkpoints are the 2026 annual report, due by March 31, 2027, first-quarter 2027 results, and the disclosure that closes the change of largest shareholder.
