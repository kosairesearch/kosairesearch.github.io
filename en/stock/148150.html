02

Business structure

Se Gyung Hi Tech was founded in 2006 as a specialty maker of functional films for displays, with foldable smartphone protective films as its core product. As of March 2026, the revenue mix was 39% optical film, 33% decorative film, 26% injection film and 2% merchandise.

The company has reportedly supplied optical film for Samsung Electronics' foldable phones on an exclusive basis since 2019, and it has continued to take part in advance development projects at Samsung Display.

It is also reported to hold a stable supply structure in ultra-thin glass (UTG) protective films for foldable panels. Decorative film covers the front and back decoration of smartphones and plastic substitutes for glass, while injection film covers protective parts attached inside the device.

Beyond Samsung Electronics and Samsung Display, its customer base has reportedly expanded to LG Display, OPPO and Xiaomi. Its competitive edge is linked to its in-house Micro Dry Deposition (MDD) process, which analysts credit with productivity and quality advantages over peers.

In 2023 it acquired Sesmat, a battery-materials company, extending into insulation films and electrode protection materials for batteries. Overseas production runs through its Vietnam and China subsidiaries, with Vietnam Plant 2 Building B completed in 2025 and a China facility relocated for capacity expansion. Segment revenue is presented only as shares of the mix.

04

Earnings analysis

On disclosed annual figures, revenue went from KRW 260.6 billion in 2022 to KRW 304.5 billion in 2023, up 16.8% year on year, and peaked at KRW 320.3 billion in 2024, up 5.2%. It then fell to KRW 277.9 billion in 2025, down 13.2%.

Operating profit was largest in 2023 at KRW 36.2 billion, up 352.4%, followed by KRW 31.5 billion in 2024, down 13.0%, and KRW 1.76 billion in 2025, down 94.4%. The operating margin moved from 3.1% in 2022 to 11.9% in 2023, 9.8% in 2024 and 0.6% in 2025.

The company attributed the 2025 weakness to lower volumes of glass-like plastic products and optical film, and to higher fixed costs such as depreciation after its Vietnam and China plants were completed for new businesses.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 48.8 billion in 2025, up 137.2% and the largest of the 2022 to 2025 period, while net income including non-controlling interests was KRW 47.8 billion, a different figure.

Because operating profit fell sharply while controlling-shareholder profit rose, the makeup of non-operating items is something to check in the annual report. Operating cash flow fell from KRW 65.2 billion in 2024 to KRW 14.6 billion in 2025.

The debt-to-equity ratio declined from 66.9% in 2024 to 47.6% in 2025, with total equity at KRW 234.7 billion and total liabilities at KRW 111.8 billion at year-end 2025. Quarterly results bottomed in Q1 2026, with revenue of KRW 61 billion, down 13.9% from the prior quarter, and an operating loss of KRW 2.17 billion.

Revenue then rose to KRW 86.3 billion in Q2 and KRW 97.8 billion in Q3, while operating profit reached KRW 3.33 billion in Q2 and KRW 5 billion in Q3.

The Q3 2026 net loss attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 8.19 billion moved in the opposite direction from that operating profit, so the gap between quarterly operating profit and controlling-shareholder profit is wide.

Controlling-shareholder net income for the last four quarters, from Q4 2025 to Q3 2026, totaled KRW 44.8 billion.

05

Industry analysis

Se Gyung Hi Tech's end market is closely tied to the new-model cycle for foldable smartphones. In a September 2026 report, Meritz Securities said that sales of protective films (PL) recovered on new foldable models from a major domestic customer, which drove top-line growth.

The same report said the company is the sole supplier for three models launched this year. Smartphone makers are diversifying form factors under cost pressure, and Meritz argued that the number of applicable products and mounting areas could grow as new form factors expand.

The company is also reported to be developing a film for a North American smartphone customer's new form factor marking its 20th anniversary.

Meritz said the product is likely to be applied to the customer's high-volume models and that, once mass production begins, related revenue could expand to more than KRW 100 billion a year. By contrast, 2025 coverage pointed to lower foldable phone shipments and slower market growth as reasons for weak results.

On competition, a 2024 brokerage analysis named the company as one of the few firms able to use the MDD process and cited that as a differentiator in UTG protective films. Coverage also noted that revenue timing in the battery insulation film business depends on qualification tests at domestic cell makers.

06

Outlook

Meritz Securities said that fixed-cost pressure from large investments made in 2025 continues to slow the pace of margin recovery more than expected. The same report described a full-scale rebound starting in the second quarter.

The company reportedly plans to reflect the North American customer's new form-factor film in its estimates once adoption is confirmed. Coverage from 2025 said the joint venture in Vietnam with China-based UTG maker SEED was expected to start generating revenue in the second half.

The company's history shows that in January and February 2026 it invested in SEED GLOBAL PTE. LIMITED with a 46.7% stake and in its Vietnam joint venture with a 49% stake.

Coverage from 2025 said its battery subsidiary was nearing qualification at a domestic cell maker, and whether mass-production revenue has started should be checked in the business description of the annual report.

At the extraordinary shareholder meeting on November 6, 2026, a partial amendment to the articles passed with 90.2% approval of shares voted, and Lee Eun-young, a senior executive officer, was elected as an inside director.

This governance change does not by itself alter the operating numbers but marks a change in management composition.

08

Bull factors

Sole-supply models driving volume

In a September 2026 report, Meritz Securities said the company is the sole supplier for three foldable models launched this year. The same report attributed top-line growth to a recovery in protective film sales.

Revenue rose from KRW 61 billion in Q1 2026 to KRW 86.3 billion in Q2 and KRW 97.8 billion in Q3, with Q3 up 25.0% year on year.

Quarterly operating profit recovery

Operating profit moved from a KRW 2.17 billion loss in Q1 2026 to KRW 3.33 billion in Q2, turning positive. Q3 operating profit reached KRW 5 billion, up 95.7% from a year earlier. Profitability that had fallen to a 0.6% operating margin in 2025 has changed direction on a quarterly basis.

The company tied the 2025 rise in fixed costs to overseas plant completions, so how well those costs are absorbed will determine how far the recovery goes.

New form factor and balance sheet

Meritz Securities said that if a film for a North American customer's new 20th-anniversary form factor enters mass production, related revenue could expand to more than KRW 100 billion a year. Adoption has not been confirmed, so whether it is reflected in estimates remains open.

On the balance sheet, the debt-to-equity ratio fell from 66.9% in 2024 to 47.6% in 2025, and total equity stood at KRW 234.7 billion at year-end 2025. Analysts point to this capital base as room to keep expanding into new businesses.

09

Bear factors

Dependence on Samsung foldables

Optical film has reportedly been supplied exclusively for Samsung Electronics' foldable phones since 2019. Revenue depends on one customer and specific set models, so a drop in new-model shipments can shake results. 2025 coverage cited lower foldable shipments as a factor in weak performance.

Customer diversification to LG Display, OPPO and Xiaomi has been pursued, but the sources do not quantify a meaningful reduction in dependence.

Incomplete margin recovery

The 2025 operating margin fell to 0.6%, down from 11.9% in 2023 and 9.8% in 2024. Meritz Securities said the pace of profitability recovery is slower than expected due to fixed-cost pressure from large investments.

In Q3 2026, the company posted an operating profit but swung to a net loss attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 8.19 billion. Whether quarterly profits can be sustained has not yet been tested.

Earnings quality and cash flow

Controlling-shareholder net income of KRW 48.8 billion in 2025 far exceeded operating profit of KRW 1.76 billion. Quarterly controlling-shareholder profit has also swung widely, from KRW 28.7 billion in Q4 2025 to KRW 3.33 billion in Q1 2026, KRW 21 billion in Q2 and a net loss of KRW 8.19 billion in Q3. Whether profit and cash move in the same direction still needs to be confirmed.

10

Risk factors

Pledge of major shareholder shares

A major shareholder holding report filed on November 12, 2026 lists about 21.24 million shares of the largest shareholder's holding company as subject to a pledge agreement. The reported loan amount is KRW 66 billion at an interest rate of 6.05%, with a 120% maintenance ratio.

The largest shareholder side's share ratio is reported at 43.04%. Changes in collateral value or pledge terms could affect the ownership structure and share price, so related disclosures need continued monitoring.

Customer and set-maker concentration

Revenue from optical film and PL protective film moves with the new-product schedules of major domestic set makers. A delay or discontinuation of a specific model could shift quarterly results sharply. Adoption of the new form-factor film has not been confirmed.

Joint ventures with a China-based UTG maker and overseas subsidiaries are being used for diversification, but the revenue contribution from these businesses is limited in the available sources.

Fixed costs and overseas plant burden

The company attributed the 2025 earnings decline partly to higher fixed costs, including depreciation from completed Vietnam and China plants built for new businesses. Q1 2026 posted an operating loss of KRW 2.17 billion. Profitability can swing quickly when fixed costs persist during low-volume periods. Whether plant utilization recovers is the key variable for the operating margin.

Overall view

The next checkpoints are adoption of the new form-factor film, Q4 and full-year 2026 results, and the 2026 annual report.
