Tovisco After the Automotive Split: Industrial Monitors at the Core

02

Business structure

Tovisco was founded in 1998 and listed on KOSDAQ in 2004 as a mid-sized company. Its core businesses are industrial monitors used in casino gaming equipment and other public information displays, automotive displays, and TFT-LCD modules. The product mix differs by data provider.

According to wisereport, automotive displays and related products accounted for about 56% of 2025 sales, industrial monitors about 40%, and other products about 4%. Alphasquare's breakdown shows industrial monitors at about 56% and automotive displays at about 39%.

In 2024, industrial monitors accounted for 43.2%, automotive displays for 51.7%, and other products for 5.0%. Demand for industrial monitors is driven by casino market expansion, larger screen sizes, and a rising share of higher-value products.

Automotive display capacity was strengthened through production line expansions starting in 2024. Through a spin-off split effective July 1, 2026, the automotive display unit was separated into a standalone company, and its results were excluded from Q3 2026 onward.

The U.S. is the core market for industrial monitors, accounting for 72.4% of that business in Q1 2026. U.S.-bound products are produced at the Songdo plant, while the Chinese subsidiary Dongguan Tovis mainly produces casino displays for Europe, Australia, and other non-U.S. markets.

Dongguan Tovis sales fell 32.5% from KRW 71.6 billion in 2024 to KRW 48.4 billion in 2025. R&D spending equaled 5.38% of sales in 2025.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual results show sales growth came first. Sales rose from KRW 328.2 billion in 2022 to KRW 447.4 billion in 2023, up 36.3% year on year, and to KRW 623.8 billion in 2024, up 39.4%. In 2025 sales reached KRW 640.3 billion, up 2.7%.

Sales have risen for three consecutive years from 2023 to 2025, and 2025 is the largest year in the 2022 to 2025 period. Operating profit was KRW 58.4 billion in 2024, up 127.5%, the largest figure in the period, before falling to KRW 57.2 billion in 2025, down 2.0%.

Securities analysts described 2024 operating profit as the company's best result since its founding. The operating margin was 2.1% in 2022, 5.7% in 2023, 9.4% in 2024, and 8.9% in 2025, easing slightly after 2024.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders swung from a loss of KRW 4.98 billion in 2022 to a profit of KRW 11.6 billion in 2023, turning profitable. It rose to KRW 57.7 billion in 2024, up 397.6%, and fell to KRW 43.1 billion in 2025, down 25.3%.

Net income including non-controlling interests was KRW 44.7 billion in 2025, a different figure from profit attributable to controlling shareholders. A 2026 media report noted that excluding foreign exchange gains, net income has in effect been declining.

Operating cash flow went from negative KRW 5.41 billion in 2022 to KRW 34.9 billion in 2023, KRW 61 billion in 2024, and KRW 53.5 billion in 2025. The debt ratio fell from 109.9% in 2022 to 82.8% in 2025.

Quarterly sales were KRW 164.7 billion in Q3 2025, KRW 155.5 billion in Q4 2025 (down 5.6% quarter on quarter), KRW 169.8 billion in Q1 2026, and KRW 188.1 billion in Q2 2026, the highest in the window.

Q3 2026 sales of KRW 85.9 billion, down 47.8% year on year, and operating profit of KRW 7.37 billion, down 55.6%, are the first quarter without the automotive unit, and the company said the change in business composition calls for care when comparing with prior periods. In the first half of 2026, sales rose 11.8% year on year and operating profit rose 3.1%.

05

Industry analysis

Analysts attribute growth in industrial monitors to casino market expansion, larger screen sizes, and a rising share of higher-value products. Casino equipment demand is shaped by regional economic conditions and capital spending cycles, so quarterly sales seasonality should be read alongside demand shifts.

In early 2025, around the release of Q1 2025 results, NH Investment and Securities expected the casino monitor business to enter its seasonal peak from the second half.

Growth in automotive displays was attributed to a rising number and size of in-vehicle displays, and that business now belongs to a separate company following the July 1, 2026 split.

Because most industrial monitor sales come from the Americas, U.S.-bound volumes are concentrated at the Songdo plant, which sits outside tariff exposure. Production at the Chinese base has been reorganized around markets outside the U.S., and the profitability of that setup has come under test.

Tovisco previously had two pillars, casino monitors and automotive displays, and the split places them in separate companies. The position in the industry cycle can only be judged once post-split quarterly sales accumulate after Q3 2026. Assessing the cycle can be done by tracking the U.S. sales share together with subsidiary results.

06

Outlook

Following the July 1, 2026 spin-off, the continuing company's results exclude the automotive unit, so consolidated results from Q4 2026 onward should be read on the same basis.

A share cancellation decision was disclosed on July 29, 2026, and a corrected major shareholder report on the treasury share purchase decision followed on July 31. Under the U.S. tariff environment, U.S.-bound industrial monitors are being reorganized toward greater production at the Songdo plant.

The Chinese subsidiary Dongguan Tovis now produces casino displays for Europe, Australia, and other markets outside the U.S. Casino market expansion and larger screens are cited by analysts as the medium-term demand basis for industrial monitors.

The 11.8% sales increase in the first half of 2026 reflects the pre-split structure and does not show post-split trends. The key point is the level of quarterly sales and operating margin at the continuing company now that the automotive unit has been separated.

The next regular report is the 2026 annual report, with a statutory filing deadline at the end of March 2027. When the annual report discloses product-level sales mix and subsidiary results, the post-split business structure will be clearest.

08

Bull factors

Improving profitability

The operating margin rose from 2.1% in 2022 to 9.4% in 2024 and held at 8.9% in 2025. Operating profit was KRW 7.04 billion in 2022, so the scale of profit has grown. Operating cash flow improved from negative KRW 5.41 billion in 2022 to KRW 53.5 billion in 2025. The debt ratio also fell over the same period, from 109.9% to 82.8%.

Casino monitor demand expansion

Analysts describe industrial monitors as growing steadily on casino market expansion, larger screen sizes, and a rising share of higher-value products. The U.S. accounted for 72.4% of this business in Q1 2026. The bull case rests on the view that the business can keep contributing if U.S. demand holds.

Shareholder return disclosures

A share cancellation decision was disclosed on July 29, 2026. A corrected major shareholder report on the treasury share purchase decision followed on July 31. Controlling shareholders' equity of KRW 280 billion at the end of 2025 is cited as a capital base supporting the case.

09

Bear factors

Sharp drop in Q3 2026 results

Q3 2026 sales were KRW 85.9 billion, down 47.8% year on year. Net income attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 409 million, down 96.9%. It has declined for two consecutive quarters starting in Q2 2026.

Because the spin-off is mixed into these figures, the full decline cannot be read as operating deterioration alone.

Tariff exposure and overseas production

The U.S. accounted for 72.4% of industrial monitor sales in Q1 2026. Subsidiary Dongguan Tovis saw sales fall 32.5%, from KRW 71.6 billion in 2024 to KRW 48.4 billion in 2025. Over the same period, its net income swung from profit to loss.

Slowing profit growth

Sales rose only 2.7% in 2025, and operating profit fell 2.0%. Net income attributable to controlling shareholders dropped 25.3% to KRW 43.1 billion.

Risk factors

Change in business structure

The July 1, 2026 spin-off of the automotive unit led to its results being excluded from Q3 2026. The company said the change in business composition calls for care when comparing with prior-year and prior-quarter periods. The quarterly time series is now broken, which shortens the period available for judging trends.

Trade and tariffs

Industrial monitors, with their heavy U.S. sales weighting, are exposed to changes in tariff conditions. Most U.S.-bound volume is concentrated at the Songdo plant, which adds production concentration risk. Continued weakness at the Chinese production base is an additional burden during the reorganization.

Currency and non-operating items

Media reports point to non-operating items such as foreign exchange gains and losses affecting net income. In 2024, net income of KRW 61.3 billion was higher than profit attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 57.7 billion. The gap between non-controlling interests and non-operating items affects how results are interpreted.

12

Overall view

Tovisco's operating margin rose from 2.1% in 2022 to 8.9% in 2025, and sales have grown for three consecutive years from 2023. However, sales growth slowed to 2.7% in 2025, and both operating profit and net income attributable to controlling shareholders declined.

The July 1, 2026 spin-off of the automotive unit changed the earnings structure, and Q3 2026 sales of KRW 85.9 billion and net income of KRW 409 million are the first post-split quarter, so direct comparison with the prior structure is difficult.

Bullish factors include casino market expansion for industrial monitors, a lower debt ratio, and a disclosed share cancellation decision. Bearish factors include two consecutive quarters of decline, tariff exposure in the U.S., and weak results at the Chinese subsidiary.

Market multiples are below the industry average and the price-to-book ratio is below 1x, a range that should be read alongside the earnings trend. The next checkpoints are Q4 2026 results, the 2026 annual report, and Q1 2027 results.
