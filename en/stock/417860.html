02

Business structure

Obzen was founded in 2000 as a full-stack AI data platform company built on AI and data science, and it listed on KOSDAQ in 2023. It provides end-to-end solutions that cover data collection, analysis and execution, with financial and retail industries as its main customers.

It delivers marketing management solutions under both subscription and build-to-install models. In the 2021 consolidated mix, professional services accounted for 73.4% of revenue, product license fees for 14.0%, product technology fees for 6.2% and product operating fees for 6.4%.

A large services share means revenue moves with individual projects, which is the backdrop to the company's push to grow subscription income. At listing, the company earmarked KRW 1.6 billion of IPO proceeds for SaaS operating costs and launched 'Obzen Daisy' to strengthen its subscription business.

The 2024 revenue decline was attributed to a slowdown in IT spending by major clients such as financial institutions, which delayed new solution adoption. The company also previously raised a Series A round from Naver Cloud.

Within the same IT and software sector sit larger listed companies such as NAVER and Samsung SDS, but Obzen differs greatly in scale. Revenue growth after Q1 2026 is confirmed in disclosed results, while details of the business mix shift should be checked in the next annual report.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual revenue fell from KRW 25.9 billion in 2022 to KRW 17 billion in 2023 (down 34.5% year on year) and KRW 15.2 billion in 2024 (down 10.5%), before recovering to KRW 23.4 billion in 2025 (up 54.0%). Among 2022 to 2025, 2022 was the largest year and 2024 the smallest.

Operating losses went from KRW 2.05 billion in 2022 to KRW 5.83 billion in 2023, a continued loss with a wider deficit, then narrowed to KRW 4.15 billion in 2024. In 2025 the company posted an operating profit of KRW 6.37 million, a swing to profit, with an operating margin of 0.0%.

Net loss attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 3.43 billion in 2025, wider than KRW 3.28 billion in 2024. The net loss persisting despite an operating profit appears linked to non-operating items, but their amounts are not broken out here.

Operating cash flow was -KRW 3.21 billion in 2023 and -KRW 3.57 billion in 2024, then turned to KRW 2.26 billion in 2025. On a quarterly basis, revenue rose from KRW 6.34 billion with operating profit of KRW 120 million in Q3 2025 to KRW 13.1 billion with an operating loss of KRW 465 million in Q1 2026.

Q2 2026 revenue was KRW 12.5 billion, down 4.4% from the prior quarter, and the operating loss widened to KRW 650 million, yet shareholder net income turned to KRW 4.32 billion.

In Q3 2026 revenue reached KRW 15.5 billion and operating profit KRW 1.33 billion, up 1,008.4% from a year earlier, while shareholder net income fell to KRW 1.04 billion, down 75.9% from the prior quarter. No Q4 2025 quarterly data is available, so that quarter is excluded from the trend comparison.

According to the company, cumulative operating profit for the first nine months of 2025 was KRW 900 million, improved from a KRW 3.8 billion loss a year earlier.

Because quarterly profit direction has shifted often, the drivers of quarterly swings in shareholder net income should be checked in the notes to the periodic reports.

05

Industry analysis

Obzen's business is sensitive to the IT spending cycles of corporate clients. The 2023 and 2024 revenue declines were attributed to clients such as financial institutions cutting IT budgets amid an economic slowdown.

The growth driver the company highlights is rising demand for AI solutions across financial and retail industries, a view based on the company's own description.

The same IT and software sector includes NAVER, Samsung SDS, Kakao, Hyundai Autoever and LG CNS, but these companies run larger platform or systems integration businesses, so their scale and customer base differ from Obzen's.

Obzen's competitive position is more accurately read within the narrow niche of AI marketing and data solutions for financial and retail clients.

Reports say the share price has moved more on themes than on earnings, with a notable example being a sharp rally when expectations rose around the new government's AI pledges. Indicators of the pace of industry recovery should be checked alongside quarterly disclosures.

06

Outlook

In a November 2025 statement, the company said it expected 2026 to be a real turnaround year, citing its AI technology and rising demand for AI solutions in financial and retail industries. Disclosed results show mixed direction in 2026, with operating losses in Q1 and Q2 and an operating profit in Q3.

Concluding that the profit trend has become established for the full year is therefore premature, and whether quarterly profits persist is the next checkpoint. Q4 2026 results will be reflected in the 2026 annual report as full-year figures.

Derivative valuation gains and losses tied to convertible bonds swing quarterly net results depending on the share price, and a January 2026 report said capital impairment rose to 94.95% after derivative losses were recognized.

That figure reflects the report's timing and should be kept separate from the current balance sheet. Whether subscription revenue's share grows should be checked against the revenue composition disclosed in the annual report. Targets and guidance should be discussed only to the extent they appear in official disclosures.

08

Bull factors

Shift to operating profit

Full-year 2025 operating profit turned positive at KRW 6.37 million. Third-quarter 2026 operating profit reached KRW 1.33 billion, up 1,008.4% from a year earlier. Operating cash flow also swung to KRW 2.26 billion of inflow in 2025, showing improvement in cash generation. The fact that operating gains carried into a profitable quarter is the core bullish point.

Continued top-line growth

Third-quarter 2026 revenue reached KRW 15.5 billion, up 143.9% from a year earlier. Full-year 2025 revenue rose 54.0% to KRW 23.4 billion. The company pointed to broad demand growth for AI solutions across financial and retail industries as its main growth engine. The confirmed expansion in revenue scale is the bullish factor.

Recent earnings recovery signals

Shareholder net income over the last four quarters totaled KRW 5.29 billion. In Q2 2026, shareholder net income was KRW 4.32 billion even with an operating loss of KRW 650 million. The company raised a Series A round from Naver Cloud after its technology was recognized. Technology credibility supports expectations for a continued earnings recovery.

09

Bear factors

No annual profit yet at the shareholder level

Net losses attributable to controlling shareholders continued for four consecutive years from 2022 through 2025. In 2025 the loss widened to KRW 3.43 billion from KRW 3.28 billion in 2024. The 2025 net loss including non-controlling interests was also KRW 3.43 billion.

A shift to profit at the shareholder level has not yet been confirmed on an annual basis, which is the bearish point.

Balance sheet pressure

Total liabilities at the end of 2025 were KRW 43.2 billion, larger than total equity of KRW 10.5 billion. The debt ratio rose from 195.0% in 2024 to 410.5% in 2025. A report said capital impairment reached 94.95% after derivative valuation losses were booked. The continued heavy debt load is the bearish point.

Conflicting quarterly direction

In Q2 2026 an operating loss of KRW 650 million coexisted with shareholder net income of KRW 4.32 billion. In Q3 operating profit turned positive, but shareholder net income fell 75.9% from the prior quarter.

Because operating and net results often point in different directions, the record of continuous operating profit is still short, which is the bearish point.

10

Risk factors

Financial and derivative risk

Total liabilities exceed total equity, and the debt ratio reached 410.5%. Reports describe the company booking derivative valuation losses equal to the gap between the convertible bond conversion price and the share price. Net results can swing with the share price, and repeated losses could add pressure to the capital structure.

Client IT spending cycle risk

Because key clients are financial and retail companies, the business is exposed to IT budget cuts during economic slowdowns. Reports say major clients such as financial institutions delayed adopting new solutions as they cut IT spending during the downturn. The 2024 revenue decline to KRW 15.2 billion illustrates this exposure.

Theme-driven share price volatility

Reports say the share price has moved more on themes than on fundamentals overall. Grouped with AI and Naver-related stocks, it has responded weakly to positive earnings news such as expected profit turnarounds. With quarterly net income changing direction, theme-driven trading can amplify price swings.

Overall view

Obzen showed signs of improving profitability, with an operating profit for full-year 2025 after a swing to profit and an operating profit again in Q3 2026. Because operating losses continued in Q1 and Q2 2026, it is too early to judge whether quarterly profits can persist.

Revenue recovered from KRW 15.2 billion in 2024, the low point, to KRW 23.4 billion in 2025 and KRW 15.5 billion in Q3 2026. However, shareholder net losses persisted for four consecutive years from 2022 through 2025, and the 2025 loss widened to KRW 3.43 billion.

The balance sheet burden also remains, with total liabilities of KRW 43.2 billion well above total equity of KRW 10.5 billion. Derivative valuation gains and losses on convertible bonds remain a variable that can flip the direction of net income.

Bullish factors are the swing to operating profit, revenue growth and the turn to positive operating cash flow, while bearish factors are continued net losses, a high debt ratio and volatile quarterly results.

The next checkpoints are the operating margin and shareholder net results in the 2026 annual report and any change in the debt structure. This report is provided for information only and contains no buy or sell opinion.
