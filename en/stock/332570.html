02

Business structure

Pentastone Electronics, founded in 2006 and listed on KOSDAQ in 2020, specializes in wireless communication components.

Its core products are power amplifier modules (PAM) for mobile communications and RF front-end modules (RF FEM), and it now reports three business lines: wireless communication components, automation equipment and servers.

Mobile electronic components fell from 86.7% of revenue in 2024 to 45.7% in 2025 and 17.3% in the first nine months of 2026, so the business mix has changed quickly. The shift began when the company acquired an automation equipment business in January 2025 and merged it in June 2025.

Automation equipment is built to customer order, so quarterly sales are uneven, at KRW 19.6 billion in Q1 2026, KRW 22.5 billion in Q2 and KRW 4.3 billion in Q3. The server business supplies AI and HPC servers and data center infrastructure in cooperation with global GPU and server platform makers.

High-performance server revenue reached KRW 109.3 billion in the first nine months of 2026, or 52.1% of the KRW 209.8 billion total, and one news report put servers at 79% of Q3 revenue.

Customer concentration is high: one customer (Customer B) accounted for KRW 104.96 billion, about half of nine-month revenue, and two customers each exceed 10%.

The company plans to extend its server work from sales, customization, installation and management into assembly, and to link it with semiconductor design and assembly, packaging, automation and automotive electronics.

In wireless components it competes with large electronics component makers, while in servers its breadth of partnerships with global server makers shapes competitiveness.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual revenue rose from KRW 51.4 billion in 2022 to KRW 68.1 billion in 2023 (up 32.6% year on year) and KRW 122.1 billion in 2024 (up 79.2%), then reached KRW 125.5 billion in 2025 (up 2.7%). It has grown three years in a row since 2023, and 2025 was the largest year of 2022 to 2025.

Operating profit was KRW 4.33 billion in 2022, KRW 6.42 billion in 2023 (up 48.5%) and KRW 10.8 billion in 2024 (up 67.7%), the highest of the period, before falling to KRW 3.91 billion in 2025 (down 63.7%), the lowest.

The operating margin moved from 8.4% in 2022 to 9.4% in 2023, 8.8% in 2024 and 3.1% in 2025, so the margin compressed even as revenue grew in 2025. 2025 also saw the acquisition of an automation equipment business in January and its merger in June, a period when the cost structure changed.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders moved from a loss of KRW 10.1 billion in 2022 to a profit of KRW 19.3 billion in 2023, back to a loss of KRW 7.83 billion in 2024, and to a profit of KRW 17.2 billion in 2025.

Total net income including non-controlling interests was KRW 16.4 billion in 2025, a different figure, and it sits far above operating profit of KRW 3.91 billion, so non-operating items matter for reading profit quality.

Quarterly revenue has risen for four consecutive quarters from Q4 2025 to Q3 2026, reaching KRW 99.7 billion in Q3, up 271.1% year on year.

Operating profit swung from an operating loss of KRW 1.18 billion in Q4 2025 to a profit of KRW 56.46 million in Q1 2026, then rose to KRW 4.13 billion in Q2 and KRW 13.1 billion in Q3.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders peaked at KRW 15.4 billion in Q1 2026 and then fell to KRW 5.72 billion in Q2 and KRW 767 million in Q3, while the last four quarters total KRW 27 billion.

Q3 gross margin was 18.4%, down from 24.8% a year earlier, and the product cost ratio rose from 76.6% in Q1 to 84.2% in Q2 according to news reports based on DART filings.

Operating cash flow turned to a net outflow of KRW 40.56 billion in Q3, and contract liabilities fell from KRW 76.68 billion at end-June to KRW 4.01 billion at end-September as contracts were delivered.

05

Industry analysis

Demand from the downstream market is driven by rising purchases of AI servers. The company attributes its profit recovery to stronger server purchasing demand and wider cooperation with several global top-tier server makers.

High-performance server revenue did not exist in 2025 and has grown into more than half of consolidated revenue in 2026. Server revenue, however, clusters in particular quarters because it depends on the delivery timing of large supply contracts.

A contract signed on April 30, 2026 (about KRW 102.9 billion, running to August 14, 2026) was fully recognized across Q2 and Q3, while a KRW 30.8 billion contract signed on July 22 had KRW 4.1 billion recognized in Q3 and the remaining KRW 26.7 billion scheduled for Q4.

Because server equipment and parts must be bought in advance and paid to suppliers first, working capital needs rise, and news reports say management identified cash management as a key task.

The wireless components business still centers on PAM and RF FEM, but its lower mobile share has shifted the earnings base toward servers and automation equipment.

In servers, volume depends on how global GPU and server platform makers allocate supply partners, and the company is adding partners beyond its existing ones. It is also reviewing component purchasing arrangements with several suppliers.

Automation equipment is built to order, so quarterly revenue depends on customer delivery and inspection schedules.

06

Outlook

The direction the company has stated centers on additional orders and a broader business scope. Alongside its Q3 disclosure on November 9, 2026, the company said it is expanding cooperation with several global top-tier server makers beyond its existing partners and pursuing additional orders.

It plans to extend server work from sales, customization, installation and management into assembly, and has also outlined ODM, maintenance and data center construction support.

It is also looking at server assembly and inspection opportunities by using its automation equipment business and the manufacturing capability of its consolidated subsidiary PS Onyx. The nearest checkpoint is whether the remaining KRW 26.7 billion of the July contract is recognized as Q4 revenue.

The KRW 22.0 billion exchangeable bond raised earlier was used as operating funds for server parts and equipment, and more server volume would also raise working capital needs. The contract size and supply timing of the additional business under review have not been fixed.

The company has said it is considering measures to raise corporate and shareholder value, but no specific measures have been disclosed. The 2026 annual report will be the main point for checking server revenue share, customer breakdown, operating margin and working capital changes.

08

Bull factors

Rapid server revenue growth

Q3 2026 revenue of KRW 99.7 billion rose 271.1% year on year. Nine-month high-performance server revenue of KRW 109.3 billion made up 52.1% of total revenue. A business that did not exist in 2025 grew to more than half of revenue within a single year.

Additional orders and wider customer base

The company said it is expanding cooperation with several global top-tier server makers beyond existing partners and pursuing additional orders. Of the KRW 30.8 billion contract signed in July, KRW 26.7 billion is scheduled for Q4 recognition. Plans to widen the scope into server assembly and ODM work were also presented.

Recovery in operating profit scale

Q3 operating profit of KRW 13.1 billion rose from KRW 1.46 billion a year earlier and was the largest of the last five quarters. Selling and administrative expenses held near KRW 5.2 billion, so much of the revenue gain carried through to operating profit. The earnings base widened as automation equipment and servers both contributed.

09

Bear factors

Customer concentration

Half of nine-month 2026 revenue came from one customer (Customer B), and two customers each account for more than 10%. Large-customer order and delivery schedules weigh heavily on quarterly results. A delay with one customer could move quarterly revenue sharply.

Cash flow and borrowing burden

Q3 operating cash flow was a net outflow of KRW 40.56 billion, and contract liabilities fell from KRW 76.68 billion to KRW 4.01 billion. Short-term borrowings rose from KRW 33 billion to KRW 35.13 billion, and KRW 22.02 billion was raised through exchangeable bonds. As advance-payment-type funding shrank, external funding covered more of working capital.

Quarterly earnings swings and cost pressure

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders fell from KRW 15.4 billion in Q1 2026 to KRW 767 million in Q3. Q3 gross margin of 18.4% was below 24.8% a year earlier. Automation equipment revenue fell from KRW 22.5 billion in Q2 to KRW 4.3 billion in Q3, exposing a gap in the non-server businesses.

10

Risk factors

Customer and contract execution

Quarterly revenue depends on the order and delivery schedule of a single large customer. If Q4 recognition of the July contract or the conversion of additional contracts is delayed, server revenue could drop from the Q3 level. The additional business under review has no confirmed contract size or supply timing.

Working capital and financial headroom

Large equipment and parts purchases must be prepaid to suppliers, which makes working capital demand heavy. The exchangeable bond raised funds but leaves repayment and interest obligations. The more server volume grows, the more prepaid funding is required.

Business transition and forecastability

With mobile parts and automation equipment shrinking, the earnings base has concentrated in servers. The product cost ratio rose to 84.2% in Q2, lowering gross margin, and whether that cost burden continues must be checked against the next quarter's gross margin. Order-based businesses with large quarterly swings make the earnings path harder to forecast.

12

Overall view

Pentastone Electronics is shifting from a wireless component maker into a company where AI server revenue accounts for more than half of sales.

Q3 2026 revenue of KRW 99.7 billion and operating profit of KRW 13.1 billion were the highest of the last five quarters, and nine-month revenue of KRW 209.8 billion has already passed full-year 2025 revenue. 2025 was the year operating profit was smallest at KRW 3.91 billion, and it also saw an acquisition and merger.

Earnings, however, swing sharply by quarter: controlling-shareholder net income fell from KRW 15.4 billion in Q1 2026 to KRW 767 million in Q3, and Q3 operating cash flow was a net outflow of KRW 40.56 billion.

The bullish factors are additional orders, wider cooperation with global server makers and business expansion, while the bearish factors are customer concentration, working capital needs, rising cost ratios and quarterly swings in automation equipment.

The next checkpoints are whether the KRW 26.7 billion contract balance is recognized in Q4 2026, whether additional supply contracts are signed, and the 2026 annual report due by the end of March 2027.
