02

Business structure

Rothwell International is a holding company set up in 2014 under Hong Kong law, and its operations are run by two Chinese subsidiaries, Yangzhou Boxing Technology Development Co. and Jiangsu Rothwell Electric Co.

Its core products are CAN control-system electronics, which handle digital communication between automotive measurement and control devices, and in-vehicle HVAC equipment such as air conditioners and heaters. Key subsidiary-level products include CAN Gateways, CAN display modules and dual Beidou/GPS driving recorders.

The third line is a household information-terminal business under a cooperation agreement with ZTE; a company commentary as of September 2025 says it is in progress, covering five years of supply and expanded capacity.

Customers are centered on automakers, and the CAN bus unit works with them from the new-model development stage, holding patents that the company says give it exclusive rights in auto parts.

The company is described as one of the foreign-owned firms in China's automotive electronics manufacturing market, and it is shifting toward technology-intensive automotive electronics.

The company cited an order from one major customer as the main driver of the 2025 revenue increase, which points to a structure where large individual orders weigh heavily on results. Revenue share by segment is not disclosed in verified form, so the split here is by product line only.

04

Earnings analysis

The figures below are DART periodic-report values. Rothwell reports in CNY, so KRW amounts are KRW-converted.

Revenue went from KRW 89.4 billion in 2022 to KRW 396.6 billion in 2023 (up 343.5%), KRW 542.3 billion in 2024 (up 36.7%) and KRW 768.7 billion in 2025 (up 41.8%), rising three years in a row from 2023 through 2025; 2025 was the largest year of 2022 to 2025.

Operating loss of KRW 6.94 billion in 2022 turned into operating profit of KRW 15.9 billion in 2023, rose to KRW 20.8 billion in 2024 (up 30.8%), then fell to KRW 11.4 billion in 2025 (down 45.4%).

Operating margin moved from -7.8% in 2022 to 4.0% in 2023, 3.8% in 2024 and 1.5% in 2025, so margins thinned while revenue grew.

Profit attributable to controlling shareholders swung from a loss of KRW 17 billion in 2022 to KRW 16.4 billion in 2023, then rose to KRW 18.5 billion in 2024 (up 12.8%) and KRW 20 billion in 2025 (up 8.4%), two years of growth in a row.

Net income including non-controlling interests was also reported at KRW 20 billion for 2025, so profit lines moved in different directions in 2025.

By quarter, Q4 2025 revenue of KRW 372.9 billion (up 167.6% quarter on quarter) was the peak, close to half of full-year 2025 revenue; that quarter posted KRW 7 billion operating profit and KRW 11.4 billion attributable profit.

Revenue then fell for three straight quarters: KRW 249.6 billion in Q1 2026 (down 33.1%), KRW 218.1 billion in Q2 (down 12.6%) and KRW 205.1 billion in Q3 (down 5.9%), though Q3 revenue was up 47.2% year on year.

Operating profit also declined for three straight quarters, to KRW 6.72 billion, KRW 5.42 billion and KRW 3.2 billion, with Q3 up 64.0% year on year. Attributable profit for the latest four quarters totaled KRW 29.9 billion.

The debt ratio rose from 42.0% in 2022 to 300.9% in 2024 before easing to 239.7% in 2025, and operating cash flow fell from KRW 179.7 billion in 2024 to KRW 60.1 billion in 2025.

05

Industry analysis

Rothwell's end markets are automotive electronics, in-vehicle HVAC and household information terminals. Automotive electronics demand tracks automaker production cycles and new-model launch timing.

The company is described as one of the foreign-owned firms in China's automotive electronics manufacturing market and is shifting toward technology-intensive products. China's EV market growth and battery-pack line expansion are cited as external growth drivers.

In 2020, the end of a Chinese purchase-tax cut combined with US-led trade friction caused a sharp fall in sales, showing sensitivity to policy and trade conditions. On the supply-demand cycle, quarterly revenue peaked in Q4 2025 and has declined through Q3 2026, which reads as a post-peak adjustment on quarterly data.

Compared with Hyundai Mobis, a leading Korean automotive electronics company, Rothwell's revenue is generated through its Chinese subsidiaries. Within its competitive set, the company differentiates through its CAN bus unit, which works with automakers from the new-model development stage.

Risk factors

Customer concentration and orders

The order the company cited for the 2025 revenue increase shows how heavily one customer contract can weigh on results. How long and how large that order's deliveries run beyond 2026 has not yet been settled. A scaled-down or delayed customer contract could weaken quarterly revenue further.

Policy, trade and geopolitics

In 2020, the end of a Chinese purchase-tax cut combined with US-led trade friction sharply reduced sales across all segments. If Chinese policy or US-China trade conditions shift again, lower automaker output could weigh on demand for CAN control systems and HVAC equipment. Because revenue is generated through Chinese subsidiaries, this exposure is direct.

FX and capex funding

Because the company reports in CNY, KRW figures move with the won-yuan exchange rate. The same yuan-denominated results will translate into different revenue and profit amounts in won as rates shift. Funding for the capacity expansion tied to the ZTE project may also be affected by currency moves.

Overall view
