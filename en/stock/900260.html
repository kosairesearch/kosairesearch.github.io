Report overview

Rothwell: Revenue Grows, Margins Thin

Hong Kong-based holding company Rothwell has grown revenue for four straight years on the back of GPON terminal supply to ZTE, but its 2025 operating margin fell to just 1.5%, widening the gap between top-line growth and profitability.

02

Business structure

Rothwell International Co., Ltd. is a holding company incorporated under Hong Kong law in 2014, with actual operations carried out through China-based subsidiaries including Yangzhou Boxin Technology Development Co., Ltd. (established 2005) and Jiangsu Rothwell Electric Co., Ltd. (established 2006).

The company's original business centered on CAN bus-based automotive electronic control components and automotive air-conditioning and heating systems, with product lines such as body control modules, HVAC controllers, integrated audio/entertainment systems, and smart remote keys.

At the end of 2021, the company signed a contract worth roughly RMB 1 billion (about KRW 1.6 trillion) with Chinese telecom equipment maker ZTE Corporation to enter the home information terminal (GPON) business, and formal deliveries began in the second half of 2022.

Since then, the GPON terminal segment has grown rapidly and now accounts for the large majority of total revenue, while the legacy CAN bus and HVAC segments have shrunk substantially as a proportion of sales.

The company states it continues R&D on advanced driver assistance systems (ADAS) as part of an effort to expand into next-generation automotive electronics solutions.

According to Wiseform data, Rothwell is described as holding a significant share among foreign-invested manufacturers in China's automotive electronics manufacturing market, though this figure is based on company disclosure and has not been independently verified against official statistics.

The customer base is heavily concentrated on ZTE, meaning changes in contract terms with this single customer directly affect results.

The company has also publicly referenced plans for an IoV (Internet of Vehicles) platform business developing connected car and autonomous driving solutions, along with a planned smart IoV science and technology industrial park.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual results show revenue of KRW 89.8 billion, an operating loss of KRW 7.0 billion, and a net loss of KRW 17.0 billion in 2022, as the COVID-19 pandemic, a slump in China's auto market, and rising raw material costs combined to push the company into the red.

Revenue jumped to KRW 398.1 billion in 2023, with the company turning profitable with operating profit of KRW 16.0 billion and net profit of KRW 16.4 billion, and growth continued in 2024 as ZTE-related supply ramped up, with revenue of KRW 544.2 billion, operating profit of KRW 20.9 billion, and net profit of KRW 18.5 billion.

However, in 2025 revenue rose again sharply to KRW 771.5 billion, yet operating profit fell to KRW 11.4 billion, causing the operating margin to decline from 3.8% to 1.5%.

Search results indicate that in the first half of 2025, revenue fell 2.9% and operating profit plunged 83.5% year on year, which is consistent with quarterly data showing operating profit of only KRW 2.26 billion in Q2 2025 and KRW 1.96 billion in Q3 2025.

In contrast, Q4 revenue surged to KRW 374.3 billion, with operating profit of KRW 7.03 billion and net profit of KRW 11.46 billion, meaning a substantial portion of annual results was generated in a single quarter, suggesting seasonal concentration in ZTE-related shipments.

In 2026, margins improved somewhat, with Q1 revenue of KRW 250.5 billion and operating profit of KRW 6.74 billion (2.7% margin), and Q2 revenue of KRW 218.9 billion and operating profit of KRW 5.44 billion (2.5% margin).

Over the trailing four quarters (Q3 2025 through Q2 2026), net profit attributable to owners totaled approximately KRW 29.7 billion, indicating a relatively stable annual profit run rate.

On the cash flow side, operating cash flow was very strong at KRW 180.3 billion in 2024 but dropped sharply to KRW 60.3 billion in 2025, highlighting a notable lag between revenue growth and cash generation.

05

Industry analysis

Rothwell's dominant GPON (Gigabit Passive Optical Network) terminal business is tied to the global expansion of fiber-optic broadband networks, a market where players such as Huawei, Nokia, ZTE, Cisco Systems, and Adtran are commonly cited as key competitors.

China's fiber-to-the-home penetration is already relatively advanced, but ongoing migration toward next-generation technologies such as XG-PON and NG-PON2 continues to generate equipment demand across the supply chain.

However, Rothwell's GPON revenue is effectively tied to the order schedule of a single customer, ZTE, meaning contract-specific order timing matters more to results than broader market supply-demand dynamics.

The company's legacy automotive electronics (CAN bus) and HVAC businesses remain exposed to cyclical factors such as China's vehicle purchase tax policy and the pace of the electric vehicle transition, though their reduced share of total revenue has correspondingly lowered their overall impact on group results.

As a China-based issuer listed on KOSDAQ, the company tends to have relatively limited information accessibility and disclosure transparency for domestic Korean investors, and its share price has at times moved sharply in connection with sector-theme trading rather than fundamentals alone.

In the competitive landscape, the automotive electronics segment involves numerous local Chinese and foreign-invested parts suppliers, and while the company describes itself as holding a substantial share among foreign-invested players, this claim has not been independently verified against official statistics.

06

Outlook

The company's near-term earnings trajectory appears to depend heavily on the volume and timing of GPON terminal orders from ZTE.

Search results indicate that the company is engaged in a five-year product supply arrangement with ZTE for home smart terminal projects, along with capacity expansion investment, suggesting the revenue base could be sustained over multiple years.

However, as seen in the first half of 2025, a reduction in orders in a given period can cause a sharp drop in revenue and profit, a pattern already evident in past results.

The company disclosed a decision to issue convertible bonds in June 2026, a financing method commonly used by small and mid-cap KOSDAQ issuers; the degree of future equity dilution will depend on the intended use of proceeds (capital investment versus debt repayment) and the conversion price and refixing terms.

In the automotive electronics segment, the company states it continues R&D on advanced driver assistance systems and development of connected car and autonomous driving solutions based on an IoV (Internet of Vehicles) platform, and has also referenced plans for a smart IoV science and technology industrial park, though specific construction timelines have not been confirmed.

Given the sharp rise in the debt ratio over the past three years, whether further financing or balance-sheet improvement measures follow will also be worth monitoring.

Bull factors

Net profit has remained positive for three straight years

After recording a net loss of KRW 17.0 billion in 2022, the company maintained steady profitability from 2023 to 2025, with net income of KRW 16.4 billion, KRW 18.5 billion, and KRW 20.1 billion, respectively.

The sum of controlling interest net income over the most recent four quarters (Q3 2025 to Q2 2026) also reached approximately KRW 29.7 billion, indicating that the scale of earnings has remained stable.

Given that the contract with ZTE is structured to continue over multiple years, there is a possibility that a certain level of revenue base will continue to be supported going forward.

Market valuation is low relative to earnings and assets

The current share price is trading at a relatively low multiple both in terms of recent net income and book value per share.

The fact that the market has not assigned a large valuation relative to the company's asset size or earnings flow can be seen as a factor that leaves room for re-rating if performance continues to remain stable going forward.

Revenue visibility underpinned by a multi-year supply contract

According to research materials found, it is understood that the company is in discussions with ZTE regarding a 5-year agreement for product supply and production capacity expansion.

This suggests that, rather than being a one-off order, the structure secures volume over multiple years, implying the possibility that a mid-to-long-term revenue base could continue despite quarterly volatility.

Bear factors

Operating margin declined even as revenue grew

In 2025, revenue significantly increased year-over-year to KRW 771.5 billion, but operating profit decreased to KRW 11.4 billion, lowering the operating margin from 3.8% to 1.5%.

Given that top-line growth does not directly translate into improved profitability, it will be necessary to monitor whether margins recover going forward.

Sharp rise in debt ratio and convertible bond issuance

The debt ratio rose sharply from 42.0% in 2022 to 239.7% in 2025. In June 2026, the company disclosed a decision to issue convertible bonds, and depending on the terms of the funding, the possibility of future equity dilution cannot be ruled out.

Earnings volatility from single-customer dependence

GPON revenue is structured to be effectively dependent on the order schedule of a single customer, ZTE, as demonstrated by the case in which revenue and operating profit significantly declined in the first half of 2025 compared to the same period the previous year.

This is a structure in which performance can fluctuate significantly if orders are delayed or reduced in a specific quarter.

Risk factors

Customer concentration risk

The majority of the company's revenue is generated from supplying GPON terminals to a single customer, ZTE. If the contract terms or order volume with this customer change, it could have a direct and significant impact on performance, as evidenced by the slowdown in performance in the first half of 2025.

Balance sheet and financing risk

The debt ratio has risen sharply over the past three years from 42.0% to 239.7%, and in June 2026, the company disclosed a decision to issue convertible bonds. Depending on the use of the raised funds and the conversion terms, the degree of financial burden or equity dilution may vary.

Information and regulatory risk specific to China-based KOSDAQ issuers

The company is a China-based enterprise listed in Korea with a holding company based in Hong Kong, and there have been cases of disclosure responses to requests for clarification of unusual price movements, suggesting that the stock price tends to fluctuate significantly over short periods due to theme-driven trading demand.

It should also be considered that domestic investors may have relatively limited access to accounting and disclosure transparency.

Overall view

Rothwell has steadily expanded revenue centered on GPON terminal supply to ZTE since turning profitable in 2023 from a loss in 2022, with 2025 revenue reaching KRW 771.5 billion.

However, over the same period the operating margin fell from 3.8% to 1.5%, and quarterly volatility has been considerable, with operating profit plunging 83.5% year on year in the first half of 2025.

The debt ratio rose sharply from 42.0% in 2022 to 239.7% in 2025, and the company continued to raise financing, deciding to issue convertible bonds in June 2026.

Net profit attributable to owners over the trailing four quarters has held at roughly KRW 29.7 billion, indicating relatively stable earnings, but the disconnect between revenue growth and profitability improvement remains a variable to watch.

As a Hong Kong-incorporated holding company and a China-based issuer listed on KOSDAQ, the company's heavy dependence on a single customer, ZTE, and the particular information-access characteristics of such issuers also warrant consideration.

Overall, the company appears to be in a phase where revenue expansion, margin pressure, and changes in balance-sheet structure are all unfolding at the same time.
