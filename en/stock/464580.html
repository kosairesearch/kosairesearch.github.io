Report overview

Losses Deepen as B2C Share Rises; Recovery Still Unproven

dotmill's revenue grew 43.2% in 2025, yet it posted operating losses in each quarter from Q1 2026 through Q3 2026, while self-operated B2C businesses are taking a larger share of sales.

02

Business structure

dotmill handles the full chain of its immersive media business in-house, from content planning and production to space design, construction and operation. Its business has three pillars.

B2G project awards from local governments and public institutions are the largest, including media art projects and theme park construction. B2B work for corporate clients is a small share.

B2C operations run immersive media theme parks in the Seoul metropolitan area and on Jeju Island, earning revenue from admissions, food and beverage, and merchandise. The segment mix varies by period.

A breakdown based on Q2 2026 revenue shows B2G at 69%, B2C at 27% and B2B at 3%, while cumulative figures for January to September 2026 were reported as 62.3%, 35.8% and 1.9% respectively (ZDNet Korea, November 17, 2026). Recent expansion has centered on B2C.

The Glow Safari venue at the Daegu 83 Tower complex opened in May 2026, and in June 2026 the company launched its own experience platform, bemill, which combines exhibitions, theme parks and tourism content. Global IP exhibitions such as One Piece and Dragon Ball Heroes: Rise also ran during 2026.

ARC Research cited the company's large base of immersive media references in Korea and its in-house content capability as strengths (September 2026).

04

Earnings analysis

Annual revenue rose from KRW 18.8 billion in 2023 to KRW 23.6 billion in 2024 (up 25.8% year on year) and KRW 33.8 billion in 2025 (up 43.2%), growing for two consecutive years from 2024 through 2025. 2025 was the largest year and 2023 the smallest within 2023 to 2025.

Operating profit grew from KRW 809 million in 2023 to KRW 1.02 billion in 2024 (up 26.5%), then fell to KRW 123 million in 2025, down 88.0%. The operating margin was 4.3% in 2023, 4.3% in 2024 and 0.4% in 2025, so the steepest margin decline came in the year revenue grew most.

In a February 2026 report, The Bell attributed the 2025 profit decline to operating costs from securing new revenue, including global IP collaborations, B2C expansion and responses to B2G tenders.

Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders swung from a KRW 1.21 billion loss in 2023 to KRW 960 million profit in 2024, then declined 17.2% to KRW 796 million in 2025. Net profit including non-controlling interests followed the same pattern for those years.

Operating cash flow turned positive in 2024 at KRW 3.31 billion, from negative KRW 2.35 billion in 2023, and rose to KRW 4.16 billion in 2025.

Quarterly revenue peaked at KRW 13.8 billion in Q3 2025, the highest of the last five quarters, then fell to KRW 8.9 billion in Q4 2025 (down 35.5% quarter on quarter), KRW 6.04 billion in Q1 2026, KRW 5.2 billion in Q2 2026 and KRW 5.81 billion in Q3 2026.

Operating profit dropped from KRW 1.8 billion in Q3 2025 to KRW 109 million in Q4 2025 (down 93.9%), then turned into operating losses of KRW 1.54 billion in Q1 2026, KRW 1.68 billion in Q2 2026 and KRW 3.22 billion in Q3 2026.

Net losses attributable to controlling shareholders over the last four quarters total KRW 5.37 billion. The company cites delayed public orders in the first half of 2026 and early costs for new businesses as the main causes.

05

Industry analysis

The immersive media industry is spreading into exhibitions, theme parks, performance and AI, and dotmill is focusing mainly on public media art and urban regeneration demand within that trend.

In a January 2026 report, Hana Securities said Korea's urban regeneration market is being reorganized around economic hub creation and private-led growth.

The same report put 2026 urban regeneration and balanced development budgets at about KRW 62.5 trillion, with about KRW 832.3 billion to go to three urban regeneration innovation districts. B2G revenue is recognized according to local government budget allocation and tender timing.

ZDNet Korea reported an analysis that shifts in local government project schedules around the June 2026 local elections affected dotmill's results. On global IP, the collaboration with Merlin Entertainments and participation in content for the Sphere in Las Vegas have been mentioned.

Analysts link the ASTRA CORE spherical display technology to demand for large spherical display projects such as the Sphere and COSM in Los Angeles. On B2C operations, FnGuide noted that the Seoul metropolitan and Jeju theme parks generate stable earnings but growth is limited by the economic slowdown.

In competition, dotmill presents its in-house structure spanning planning through operation as a differentiator.

06

Outlook

Based on a November 17, 2026 report, the company said that a normalization of B2G orders in 2027 and greater efficiency in B2C operations should lead to a full earnings recovery. It also said early construction spending should wrap up from Q4 2026 and that new B2C venues should reach full operation.

Brokerage forecasts are more ambitious. On September 30, 2026, Edaily reported a large project worth KRW 50 billion and headlined the possibility of 2027 revenue above KRW 90 billion.

In a September 2026 report, ARC Research analyst Kim Yong-ho presented a 2027 revenue mix of ASTRA CORE KRW 45 billion, Harry Potter IP KRW 15 billion and existing and other businesses KRW 35 billion, and projected entry into a growth phase.

Hana Securities, by contrast, had expected 2026 revenue of KRW 46.7 billion (up 54.9%) and a swing to operating profit in a January 2026 report, yet operating losses continued from Q1 through Q3 2026, widening the gap between that forecast and actual results.

On orders, the company disclosed three new projects from December 2025 to early January 2026, including Taean, Gangneung and a Daegu media art theme park, and the Daegu project carries an operating revenue-sharing structure.

On financing, the board approved additional short-term borrowing on November 6, 2026, lifting total short-term debt to KRW 19 billion. The interest rate, maturity and facilities to be funded were not specified in the disclosure.

The new IP exhibition the company planned to unveil within 2026 and its planned overseas expansion of the platform are items the company has described as plans.

Bull factors

Rapid rise in B2C share

B2C's share of Q3 2026 revenue rose to 58.8% from 27.4% in the prior quarter (ZDNet Korea, November 17, 2026). B2C earns admissions, food and beverage, and merchandise revenue directly from consumers, which is cited as a strength because it is less tied to local government tender timing. The Glow Safari venues, the bemill platform and global IP exhibitions support this business.

Global IP pipeline

A February 2026 report by The Bell said the collaboration with Merlin Entertainments and new partners widened the global IP pipeline. The bemill platform, launched in June 2026, includes plans for overseas expansion. The company said it would offer platform users benefits for a new IP exhibition to be unveiled within 2026.

Public orders and urban regeneration demand

The company disclosed three new projects from December 2025 to early January 2026, and Hana Securities said the rise in public media art projects is positive for its business expansion (Newspim, January 26, 2026).

The Daegu project carries an operating revenue-sharing structure, which analysts said could help improve profitability.

Bear factors

Revenue and operating results deteriorating together

Q3 2026 revenue was KRW 5.81 billion, down 57.9% year on year, and the operating loss reached KRW 3.22 billion. Operating losses have continued for three consecutive quarters from Q1 2026 through Q3 2026, and net losses attributable to controlling shareholders over the last four quarters total KRW 5.37 billion. The operating margin also fell to 0.4% in 2025.

Dependence on public orders and timing swings

Public project revenue depends on local government budget allocation and tender timing. The company itself cited delayed public orders in the first half of 2026 as a cause of the slowdown.

Cumulative B2G share for January to September 2026 was reported at 62.3%, so gaps in public orders feed directly into total revenue.

Expanded borrowing and financial burden

Short-term borrowing was increased by KRW 11.5 billion on March 19, 2026 and by KRW 3.865 billion on November 6, 2026, bringing the total to KRW 19 billion. The interest rate, repayment maturity and facilities to be funded were not specified in the disclosures.

The company relies on a guarantee under the Korea Credit Guarantee Fund's innovation icon program, but borrowing has grown while operating losses continue.

Overall view

Q3 2026 revenue was KRW 5.81 billion, down 57.9% year on year, while B2C's share of that quarter's revenue rose to 58.8%.

The company attributes the slowdown to delayed public orders and early costs for new businesses, and expects B2G orders to normalize in 2027, while some brokerages project entry into a growth phase in 2027.
