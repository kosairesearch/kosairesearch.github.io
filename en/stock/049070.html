Intops: Yield Recovery Now Tests Its Robotics Pivot

Intops swung to an operating loss in 2025 and returned to operating profit in the first quarter of 2026, but posted an operating loss of KRW 5.92 billion in the third quarter of 2026 as smartphone product mix shifted and customers trimmed inventory.

02

Business structure

Intops, founded in 1981, produces IT devices and molds, and operates across IT devices, home appliance assembly, auto parts, molds and other segments. IT devices generate the bulk of revenue, and about 70% of sales come from smartphone-related business with Samsung Electronics, according to a May 2026 press report.

Production sites include Intops Vietnam in Hanoi for IT devices, Platel Vina in Ho Chi Minh City for home appliance assembly, and Intops Tianjin in China for auto parts, while Sky Bridge in Washington State handles software development.

Intops Vietnam's revenue rose to KRW 1.09 billion in the first quarter of 2026 from KRW 1.015 billion a year earlier, and it returned to a net profit of KRW 3.9 billion.

By contrast, Platel Vina's first-quarter 2026 revenue fell to KRW 175 million from KRW 215 million in the first quarter of 2025, which the company attributed to intensifying competition in the TV market.

The robotics business uses an EMS model that builds customer-designed products from sourcing through assembly and inspection, extending into serving and wearable robots.

The company draws on years of serving-robot manufacturing and, as of the May 2026 report, had signed a memorandum with a wearable-robot firm and begun production preparations.

It also manufactures airframes for a defense ministry training drone program and has joined a semiconductor equipment parts supplier as a strategic investor. Because a single large electronics customer dominates demand, smartphone volume and product mix changes flow directly into results.

04

Earnings analysis

On a disclosed annual basis, revenue fell from KRW 1.11 trillion in 2022 to KRW 577.4 billion in 2023 (down 48.2% year on year), recovered to KRW 614.7 billion in 2024 (up 6.5%), and reached KRW 590.8 billion in 2025 (down 3.9%).

Operating profit dropped from KRW 142 billion in 2022 to KRW 18.3 billion in 2023 (down 87.1%) and KRW 3.78 billion in 2024 (down 79.3%), then turned into an operating loss of KRW 11.3 billion in 2025. The operating margin declined from 12.7% in 2022 to 3.2% in 2023 and 0.6% in 2024, and was -1.9% in 2025.

Revenue edged up in 2024 while profit fell, which points to margin pressure beyond sales volume.

According to brokerage analysis, the IT device segment lost KRW 14.4 billion through the first nine months of 2025, driven by yield problems on new automated lines at the Vietnam plant, compounded by a customer's wireless earbud quality issue and tighter production standards.

Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders fell from KRW 100.4 billion in 2022 to KRW 27.7 billion in 2023, KRW 21.5 billion in 2024 and KRW 16.2 billion in 2025, a decline in each of the three years from 2023 through 2025.

Net profit including non-controlling interests was KRW 8.16 billion in 2025, and the gap from controlling-shareholder profit reflects the non-controlling share.

On a quarterly basis, revenue was KRW 142.6 billion in the third quarter of 2025 with an operating loss of KRW 684 million, then KRW 142.1 billion with an operating loss of KRW 1.16 billion in the fourth quarter.

In the first quarter of 2026, revenue reached KRW 163.1 billion (up 14.8% quarter on quarter) and operating profit was KRW 6.39 billion, and the company said yield problems in its smartphone production had been resolved.

Second-quarter 2026 revenue was KRW 155 billion (down 5.0%), with operating profit of KRW 1.87 billion (down 70.8%).

Third-quarter 2026 revenue was KRW 130.6 billion (down 15.7% quarter on quarter and down 8.4% year on year), with an operating loss of KRW 5.92 billion, a wider loss than the KRW 684 million loss in the same quarter of 2025.

Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders went from KRW 5.47 billion in the third quarter of 2025 to KRW 12.4 billion, KRW 10.1 billion, and KRW 3.95 billion across the next three quarters, before a net loss of KRW 4.42 billion in the third quarter of 2026.

Over the most recent four quarters, from the fourth quarter of 2025 through the third quarter of 2026, net profit attributable to controlling shareholders totaled KRW 22 billion.

05

Industry analysis

Intops's results are closely tied to the smartphone cycle and to how its key customer places component orders. The company attributed the third-quarter 2026 adjustment to a product mix shift in the mobile market and conservative inventory management by its customer.

The company has also said that new smartphone launches raise manufacturing difficulty, since devices must be lighter and thinner while keeping durability, and that this led to yield problems in the second half of 2025.

That shows how higher quality standards from finished-device customers raise yield risk for contract manufacturers.

Alphasquare counts 54 listed companies in the mobile phone parts industry, and on an annual basis for 2025, Intops's operating margin was above the industry average but ranked in the lower-middle part of the group.

In home appliances, competition in the TV market has reduced sales at the Ho Chi Minh City subsidiary, so its customer and market exposure differs across segments.

On the robotics side, rising demand for serving and wearable robots has opened room to widen EMS customers, and in January 2026 LS Securities cited the company's existing manufacturing capacity in robot EMS as the basis for expecting customer diversification.

However, no disclosed figure confirms how large the robotics revenue is, so its contribution to total results is hard to quantify. While smartphone finished-device business accounts for most of revenue, volatility in demand and customer orders is likely to keep driving results.

06

Outlook

On November 13, 2026, when it reported third-quarter results, the company said it would pursue a rebound through investment in semiconductor equipment parts and new businesses in robotics, AI and drones.

In semiconductors, it joined a core parts supplier for wafer cleaning equipment as a strategic investor to build a base for new opportunities.

In robotics, based on its memorandum with a wearable-robot firm, it was in talks to bring production into full operation from the second half of 2026 and planned to expand its customer base, according to a May 2026 report.

In defense, it manufactures airframes for a training drone program, and a September 2026 report described a strategic partnership to expand its defense drone business. In AI, it is pursuing manufacturing with an AI chip company.

On the overseas side, the company profile says it is adding capacity at its Vietnam subsidiary to secure stable supply for its home appliance assembly business, which has a trading relationship with Samsung Electronics. The Ho Chi Minh City subsidiary is focused on winning new local customers.

In June 2026, the company announced plans to retire 733,000 treasury shares and began additional buybacks. The next items to watch are whether wearable robot production shows up in revenue, and whether the Vietnam capacity expansion runs alongside stable yields.

08

Bull factors

Yield issues resolved and back in profit

Operating profit of KRW 6.39 billion in the first quarter of 2026 marked a return to profit, and the company said yield problems in smartphone production had been resolved. Intops Vietnam also returned to a net profit of KRW 3.9 billion in the same quarter.

The cause behind the KRW 14.4 billion IT device loss through the third quarter of 2025 has eased, which marks a starting point for earnings recovery.

Financial room from a net cash position

Total equity was KRW 756.4 billion and total liabilities KRW 116.2 billion at the end of 2025, for a debt ratio of 15.4%. The company says its cash holdings exceed borrowings. That financial room supports new-business investment alongside shareholder returns such as share retirement and buybacks.

Expansion into robot EMS and defense drones

Building on years of serving-robot manufacturing, the company is widening its wearable-robot production, and a May 2026 report described a memorandum with a wearable-robot firm.

It manufactures airframes and quality control for a defense training drone program, and a September 2026 report covered a strategic partnership to expand its defense drone business. These are routes to a customer base beyond smartphones.

Bear factors

Dependence on smartphones and mix shift

About 70% of revenue comes from smartphone-related business with Samsung Electronics. In the third quarter of 2026, a product mix shift in mobile and conservative inventory management by the customer combined to produce revenue of KRW 130.6 billion and an operating loss of KRW 5.92 billion. When customer orders fall, utilization and profit move quickly.

Long-running decline in profitability

The operating margin fell from 12.7% in 2022 to 3.2% in 2023, 0.6% in 2024 and -1.9% in 2025. Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders declined for three straight years from 2023 through 2025.

Quarterly profit returned in early 2026, but second-quarter operating profit fell 70.8% from the prior quarter, and the third quarter returned to an operating loss.

Weak home appliance unit and overseas subsidiary

Platel Vina's revenue in the first quarter of 2026 was KRW 17.5 billion, down from KRW 21.5 billion a year earlier. The company cited intensifying competition in the TV market. If the home appliance unit does not recover, the buffer from non-smartphone businesses stays thin.

Risk factors

Customer concentration risk

Most revenue depends on smartphone business with Samsung Electronics, so model changes, inventory policies and order timing can move results sharply. The third-quarter 2026 adjustment coincided with the customer's conservative inventory approach. The pace of customer diversification determines how large this risk is.

Yield and quality risk

Yield problems on new automated lines at the Vietnam plant in 2025 caused a KRW 14.4 billion IT device loss through the third quarter. Higher design and durability standards for customer products are raising manufacturing difficulty. If yields slip again, the return to profit could reverse.

Uncertainty in monetizing new businesses

The company is pursuing robot EMS, drones and semiconductor equipment parts, but no disclosed figure confirms the revenue scale or profitability of these businesses.

Wearable-robot production was reported to be ramping in the second half of 2026, and whether mass production and customer additions follow remains to be seen. If new businesses do not fill the gap in existing lines, earnings volatility may persist.

Overall view

Intops's revenue fell from KRW 1.11 trillion in 2022 to KRW 590.8 billion in 2025, and its operating margin moved from 12.7% to -1.9%.

The return to operating profit in the first quarter of 2026 was attributed to normalized smartphone production yields, but the third quarter of 2026 recorded an operating loss of KRW 5.92 billion as product mix shifted and the customer adjusted inventory.

Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders totaled KRW 22 billion over the last four quarters, so earnings may be only at an early stage of recovery. On the balance sheet, total equity of KRW 756.4 billion and a net cash position act as a cushion.

Whether robot EMS, defense drones and semiconductor equipment parts contribute to results has not yet been confirmed by disclosed figures. Bullish factors include yield normalization and financial capacity, while bearish factors include smartphone dependence and the long decline in profitability.

The next checkpoints are the operating margin in the 2026 annual business report and whether new-business revenue appears in it.
