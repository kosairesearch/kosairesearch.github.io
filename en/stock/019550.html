Report overview

Profits Rest on Exits as Quarterly Earnings Fall for Three Quarters

SBI Investment KOREA has stayed profitable on an annual basis since turning to profit in 2023, but revenue and operating profit have fallen for three straight quarters after the 2025 Q4 peak, showing how strongly results depend on exit timing.

  1. 1

  2. 2

  3. 3

  4. 4

02

Business structure

SBI Investment KOREA is a venture capital company established in November 1986 under the Act on the Promotion of Venture Investment, and its core business is investing in small and early-stage companies and forming and managing venture investment funds.

Its business purposes also include forming and managing startup investment funds and private equity vehicles, and brokering mergers and acquisitions between companies.

Operating revenue splits into investment income, driven mainly by gains and valuation changes on investee assets, and fee income made up of management and performance fees from fund operations; investment income varies widely by quarter depending on exit timing.

Management and performance fees totaled KRW 7.7 billion in the first half of 2026, up 36.7% year on year.

Assets under management were about KRW 1.5 trillion at the end of June 2026 according to a company shareholder letter, while the DART quarterly report put the end-2025 figure at KRW 1,185.5 billion on a co-GP pro-rata basis, so the figures differ by measurement basis.

In 2024 the company formed four funds totaling KRW 379.5 billion, a record fundraising result, led by the KRW 184.1 billion KB-SBI Global Advanced Strategy private equity partnership.

Its portfolio spans materials, parts and equipment, bio and healthcare, media and content, AI and robotics, and regional holdings include secondary battery equipment maker CIS in Daegu, foldable display inspection equipment maker Innotech in Cheonan, and autonomous driving software company Ride Flux in Jeju.

The company describes itself as the first venture capital firm in Korea and uses the overseas network of its parent SBI Group to form cross-border investment funds. According to DealSite's tally, its 2025 investment execution of KRW 193.2 billion ranked sixth in the industry.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual results began with 2022 revenue of KRW 16.9 billion and an operating loss of KRW 15 billion, an operating margin of -88.8%. In 2023 revenue rose to KRW 29.3 billion, up 73.0% year on year, and operating profit of KRW 8.54 billion turned the company to profit, with a 29.2% operating margin.

In 2024 revenue slipped 2.0% to KRW 28.7 billion, but operating profit rose 37.5% to KRW 11.7 billion, with a 40.9% margin. Revenue reached KRW 32.7 billion in 2025, up 14.0% and the largest year in the 2022 to 2025 window, while operating profit fell 20.6% to KRW 9.32 billion, a 28.5% margin.

Net profit attributable to owners was KRW 8.32 billion in 2023, KRW 11.4 billion in 2024, up 36.9%, and KRW 9.32 billion in 2025, down 18.1%. Net profit including non-controlling interests was KRW 8.32 billion, KRW 11.4 billion and KRW 9.32 billion in the same years.

Operating cash flow went from KRW 9.92 billion in 2022 and KRW 20.4 billion in 2023 to KRW 21 billion in 2024 and -KRW 14.5 billion in 2025, moving in a different direction from reported profit.

Quarterly figures show the peak in the fourth quarter of 2025, when revenue reached KRW 19.9 billion, up 301.3% from the prior quarter, and operating profit reached KRW 5.26 billion, up 154.7%.

From the first quarter of 2026 onward, revenue fell for three straight quarters to KRW 8.11 billion, KRW 5.21 billion and KRW 3.32 billion, and operating profit fell to KRW 4.94 billion, KRW 2.62 billion and KRW 621 million.

Net profit attributable to owners in the third quarter of 2026 was KRW 621 million, down 75.6% from the prior quarter, and the last four quarters total KRW 13.4 billion.

First-half 2026 operating profit of about KRW 7.6 billion matches the sum of the first two quarters, which indicates that exit gains concentrated in earlier quarters drove the swing in quarterly results.

Industry analysis

In a DealSite report, a venture capital industry source said performance of venture investment funds improved despite the high-interest-rate environment.

In the same tally, the company ranked 15th among surveyed managers by assets under management at KRW 1,240.5 billion as of the first half of 2026, and 8th by undrawn commitments at KRW 250.7 billion.

Its new investment execution of KRW 71.3 billion in the first half of 2026 ranked 13th, two places higher than its AUM rank. Cumulative investment execution over the past ten years was about KRW 1.3 trillion, or roughly KRW 130 billion a year on average.

On the supply side, the industry reading is that large funds formed after the 2024 fundraising are now entering the investment phase, which may keep deployment levels elevated for a time.

On the policy side, a report said the company was selected as a general partner for the National Growth Fund investment program, and the company has said it aims to form that fund within 2026.

Competitively, its AUM ranks mid-table while its new investment ranks near the top, which suggests faster deployment relative to assets. Industry-wide exit volume figures were not presented, so the company's earnings should be read as dependent on individual portfolio exits.

Outlook

In coverage of its August 14, 2026 disclosure, the company said it plans to strengthen its stable fee base through fundraising in the second half of 2026.

The National Growth Fund is an expected commitment that was not included in assets under management as of end-June 2026, and it has been reported that the company aims to form the fund within 2026.

DealSite's tally put combined investable capital at about KRW 300 billion, made up of KRW 250.7 billion in undrawn commitments and a KRW 43 billion fund formed in July 2026.

A venture capital industry source said the large recently formed funds are entering the investment phase, which may keep deployment levels high for some time.

Retained deficit was reduced to KRW 9.6 billion in the first half of 2026, according to a Hankyung report, and DealSite reported that the company is expected to have the financial soundness to pay dividends from 2027 once the deficit is cleared.

Reports dated November 5, 2026 confirmed a plan for a second share consolidation, which is being discussed as a way to maintain the listing, and the company has reportedly completed treasury share cancellation.

Fourth-quarter 2026 results have not yet been filed in a periodic report, and the 2026 annual business report is the next one. The next checkpoints are therefore the annual operating margin, the remaining retained deficit and dividend decision in that report, and whether the National Growth Fund is formed.

Bull factors

Exit gains and a fee base

First-half 2026 operating profit of KRW 7.6 billion, up 277.4% year on year, is attributed mainly to exits from key portfolio companies. Management and performance fees rose 36.7% to KRW 7.7 billion, giving a fee base that operates alongside exit gains. The company's shareholder letter put assets under management at about KRW 1.5 trillion at the end of June 2026.

Investable capital and deployment pace

Combined investable capital of about KRW 300 billion includes KRW 250.7 billion in undrawn commitments and a fund formed in July 2026. First-half 2026 new investment of KRW 71.3 billion ranked 13th, two places above its AUM rank. The shift toward deployment follows the record fundraising completed in 2024.

Path to balance-sheet repair

The debt-to-equity ratio fell from 10.4% in 2022 to 4.6% in 2025, with total liabilities of KRW 6.82 billion and equity of KRW 146.9 billion at the end of 2025. A Hankyung report said the retained deficit was reduced to KRW 9.6 billion in the first half of 2026. DealSite reported expectations of dividend capacity from 2027 after the deficit is cleared.

Bear factors

Three straight quarters of decline

Revenue and operating profit have declined for three straight quarters since the 2025 Q4 peak, through the third quarter of 2026. Operating profit of KRW 621 million in the third quarter was down 69.9% year on year.

If the strong first half reflected exits concentrated in earlier quarters, a lull in exits would weigh on later results.

Cash flow diverging from profit

Operating cash flow moved from KRW 21 billion in 2024 to -KRW 14.5 billion in 2025, diverging from operating profit of KRW 9.32 billion in the same year. Whether this gap reflects higher investment deployment can only be determined once the cash flow line items in the annual report are fixed. Prolonged periods of profit without cash inflow could reduce financial flexibility.

Share price and listing conditions

A BLOTER report noted that the share price fell despite first-half operating profit rising to nearly four times the prior-year level. The company is pursuing a second share consolidation, and the stock has been described as remaining in penny-stock territory.

Maintaining the listing is presented as a task tied to both the pace of financial repair and share price performance.

Risk factors

Earnings volatility tied to exit timing

Investment income depends on gains from exits and valuation changes, so the timing of exits swings quarterly results sharply. Revenue fell from KRW 19.9 billion at the 2025 Q4 peak to KRW 3.32 billion in the third quarter of 2026.

The gap between quarters with concentrated exits and quarters without them is wide, which widens the range of possible outcomes.

Listing maintenance and share structure

With the share price in penny-stock territory, the company is pursuing a second share consolidation. A consolidation reduces the number of shares outstanding, and its ratio and timing will be confirmed through disclosures. Maintaining the listing may depend on clearing the retained deficit and sustaining profits.

Fundraising and industry conditions

The high-interest-rate environment could weigh on investor sentiment and the pace of fund formation across venture capital. The National Growth Fund remains an expected commitment, and whether it is formed within 2026 is not yet settled. A delay in fund formation could slow growth in assets under management and the management fee base.
