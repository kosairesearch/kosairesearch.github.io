Hanchem: OLED Dependence Meets a Q3 2026 Operating Loss

02

Business structure

Hanchem, founded in 1999, develops and produces carbon-compound advanced materials to customer specifications. Its core product line is OLED materials, and a 2025 year-end analysis put the emitting-layer share of its main OLED materials above 90%.

The company also makes semiconductor materials, catalyst materials and pharmaceutical materials, and runs contract research and manufacturing (CRO and CDMO) operations. Revenue moves with display demand and the timing of new product launches, which makes the company sensitive to OLED material orders.

A September 2026 earnings commentary pointed to dependence on a small number of large domestic display makers as a driver of the weaker results. The company is also pushing to expand its metallocene catalyst materials as a strategic share.

Its synthesis know-how includes more than 6,000 synthesis datasets and core techniques such as palladium (Pd) catalytic reactions and cryogenic reactions.

Large chemical companies such as LG Chem and Hanwha Solutions are reference points in the same sector, but differences in scale and product mix limit direct comparison.

At its 2024 listing, the plan was to add a new plant building at the Okcheon site in Chungbuk and install 13 additional reactors, bringing the total to 53 reactors from Q1 2026.

Earnings analysis

Annual revenue rose from KRW 26.9 billion in 2023 to KRW 36.7 billion in 2024, up 36.2% year on year, and then fell to KRW 33.9 billion in 2025, down 7.6%. Within 2023 to 2025, 2024 was the largest year and 2023 the smallest.

Operating profit was KRW 5.02 billion in 2023, KRW 6.54 billion in 2024 (up 30.2%) and KRW 4.85 billion in 2025 (down 25.8%), and the operating margin moved from 18.6% to 17.8% and then 14.3%. In 2025, lower revenue and cost pressure together weighed on profitability.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 4.16 billion in 2023, KRW 5.77 billion in 2024 (up 38.6%) and KRW 5.77 billion in 2025 (up 0.1%), an increase in both 2024 and 2025.

Because operating profit fell in 2025 while attributable net income held flat, the role of non-operating items and taxes should be checked in the business report notes. Net income including non-controlling interests was also KRW 5.77 billion in both 2024 and 2025.

Operating cash flow was KRW 865 million in 2023, KRW 8.11 billion in 2024 and KRW 5.58 billion in 2025.

By quarter, Q3 2025 revenue of KRW 8.16 billion and operating profit of KRW 1.29 billion were followed by Q4 2025 revenue of KRW 6.83 billion (down 16.3% quarter on quarter) and operating profit of KRW 229 million (down 82.3%).

Q4 2025 attributable net income of KRW 1.41 billion was far above that quarter's operating profit, which suggests non-operating items had a large effect. Q1 2026 revenue was KRW 6.3 billion with operating profit of KRW 461 million, and Q2 2026 revenue was KRW 6.66 billion with operating profit of KRW 414 million.

Q3 2026 revenue was KRW 5.4 billion, down 18.9% quarter on quarter and 33.8% year on year, and the quarter recorded an operating loss of KRW 705 million and a net loss attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 661 million, both swinging to losses. Attributable net income over the last four quarters, from Q4 2025 to Q3 2026, totaled KRW 1.78 billion.

Industry analysis

Forecasts point to mid-to-large OLED panel demand expanding into TV, IT and automotive applications, and the 2025 year-end analysis also cited the expansion of mid-to-large OLED as a growth factor.

By contrast, a September 2026 earnings commentary attributed lower OLED material sales to a slowdown in the display market and weaker smartphone and TV demand. The panel market therefore shows growth in some segments and softness in others at the same time.

Materials suppliers typically see orders rise and fall with panel makers' capacity additions and product launch cycles, so the cycle position is usually read from panel makers' investment plans. Hanchem's position is that of a mid-sized supplier focused on emitting-layer materials.

Lake Materials, a listed peer in OLED materials, is the closest comparison, while large chemical groups such as LG Chem and Hanwha Solutions operate across a much wider scope. Metallocene catalyst materials are presented as a route to broaden the business beyond OLED.

The heavy dependence on large domestic display makers explains why shifts in the industry pass through to earnings quickly.

Outlook

The only outlook items confirmed in public materials are the Okcheon plant expansion and the push to grow metallocene catalyst materials. At the 2024 listing, the expansion was presented as bringing 53 reactors into operation from Q1 2026.

A September 2025 report said more than 70% of the overall process had been completed, with completion and full-scale production expected the following year. The actual start of operations and utilization rate still need to be confirmed in later filings.

Whether the Q3 2026 operating loss is temporary or part of a continuing trend can be judged from Q4 results. Whether the 31.4% first-half revenue decline carries into the second half is the next checkpoint.

Wider panel demand across TV, IT and automotive could feed into emitting-layer material orders, but no specific order value has been disclosed.

The timing and scale at which metallocene catalyst materials become a meaningful revenue share can only be gauged once segment information appears in the annual business report. After the plant is complete, the effect of higher fixed costs on the operating margin will matter.

Comparing the 14.3% operating margin of 2025 with how quarterly losses develop will show how much the expansion and demand recovery each contribute.

Bull factors

Because emitting-layer materials make up more than 90% of the core product mix, a pickup in panel demand would feed directly into revenue.

Push to expand metallocene catalyst materials

The company is reported to be pushing to raise the strategic weight of metallocene catalyst materials as their use grows. Catalyst materials are presented as a way to widen the business beyond OLED. Their revenue contribution can only be gauged once segment information appears in the annual business report.

Low financial burden

The debt-to-equity ratio was 10.8% at the end of 2025, with total liabilities of KRW 6.38 billion. All KRW 59.3 billion of total equity belongs to controlling shareholders. Operating cash flow stayed positive at KRW 8.11 billion in 2024 and KRW 5.58 billion in 2025, showing cash generation even as earnings slowed.

Risk factors

Customer and demand concentration

Revenue is concentrated among a few large domestic display makers, so changes in panel investment or product schedules can hit sales all at once. The lower revenue in 2025 and in the first half of 2026 fits this pattern.

Progress on customer diversification can be checked in the major customer disclosures of the annual business report.

Execution of the plant expansion

The Okcheon expansion was planned at the 2024 listing to bring 53 reactors into operation from Q1 2026. A 2025 report said more than 70% of the process was complete, with completion and production expected the following year. How higher fixed costs after start-up feed into profitability should be checked in quarterly reports.

Earnings volatility and shareholder structure

Quarterly profit swings are large enough that a single quarter can turn into a loss, as happened in Q3 2026. The largest shareholder and related parties hold 41.28%, as of the last change date of April 27, 2026. Such concentration should be considered when reading float and trading flows.
