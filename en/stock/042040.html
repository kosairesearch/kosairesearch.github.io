KPM Tech: Deficits Narrowing Amid a Balance Sheet Overhaul

KPM Tech's sales rose to KRW 76.9 billion in 2025, but an operating loss of KRW 17.2 billion extended a four-year run of losses, and 2026 has brought a reverse stock split and a debt-repayment rights offering as part of a balance sheet overhaul.

02

Business structure

KPM Tech was founded in 1971 to localize plating chemicals and listed on KOSDAQ in 2003, manufacturing and selling surface treatment materials.

Its core businesses are surface treatment chemicals, including plating chemicals for PCB (printed circuit board) production and functional plating chemicals, and fully automatic plating equipment built to customer specifications for PCB, semiconductor and automotive component lines.

The surface treatment chemicals are a core material in PCB manufacturing that determines product durability and quality, and the company is pushing technology development and product upgrades through its in-house research institute.

A 2022 corporate analysis put the surface treatment business at about 80% of sales in the semiconductor manufacturing context. Based on the mix compiled by Butler for the second quarter of 2026, chemicals account for 18%, machinery for 3%, and the remaining 79% is classified as other.

Equipment orders move with investment levels in the PCB, semiconductor and electronics industries, and the company describes itself as a technology-leading supplier that is receiving orders.

The largest shareholder, Telcon RF Pharmaceutical, holds 80.82% as of September 2, 2026 according to WiseReport, leaving a free float ratio of 19.18%, so only a small share of the stock is actively traded.

04

Earnings analysis

On a disclosed annual basis, sales rose from KRW 41.3 billion in 2022 to KRW 45.5 billion in 2023, up 10.2% year on year, then fell to KRW 28.6 billion in 2024, down 37.0%, which was the smallest year in the period. Sales in 2025 reached KRW 76.9 billion, up 168.6%, making 2025 the largest year from 2022 to 2025.

The company attributed the main drivers of the 2025 sales and profit swings to higher sales and operating losses at its consolidated subsidiaries, according to a Digital Today report on the March 2026 disclosure.

The operating loss was KRW 448 million in 2022, KRW 14.5 billion in 2023, KRW 4.56 billion in 2024 and KRW 17.2 billion in 2025, so the company has posted losses in each of the four years from 2022 to 2025. The operating margin moved from -1.1% in 2022 to -31.8% in 2023, then -15.9% in 2024 and -22.3% in 2025.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders turned to a profit of KRW 5.05 billion in 2023 from a loss, swung back to a net loss of KRW 39.2 billion in 2024, and narrowed to a net loss of KRW 14.7 billion in 2025.

Net loss including non-controlling interests was KRW 30.9 billion in 2025, a different figure from the loss attributable to controlling shareholders. Operating cash flow was an outflow of KRW 19.9 billion in 2025, the largest of the four years.

Quarterly sales went from KRW 22.9 billion in the third quarter of 2025 to KRW 19.3 billion in the fourth quarter, down 15.9% from the prior quarter, then KRW 19.8 billion in the first quarter of 2026, KRW 20.1 billion in the second quarter and KRW 19 billion in the third quarter.

The operating loss narrowed from KRW 5.24 billion in the third quarter of 2025 to KRW 135 million in the second quarter of 2026, then widened to KRW 2.52 billion in the third quarter, which was wider than the prior quarter but narrower than a year earlier.

The net loss attributable to controlling shareholders was largest in the second quarter of 2026 at KRW 11.8 billion among the last five quarters and fell to KRW 3.4 billion in the third quarter. The combined net loss attributable to controlling shareholders over the last four quarters totals KRW 27.7 billion.

WiseReport summarized first-half 2026 consolidated sales as up 14.8% year on year and the operating loss as down 64.4%, as of August 31, 2026.

05

Industry analysis

The PCB industry, KPM Tech's core customer base, is growing around high-value products as AI and semiconductor demand expands, according to the company.

The company says that lighter and higher-performance electronics are raising the share of high-value products such as flexible PCBs, which in turn lifts demand for surface treatment technology.

WiseReport's corporate comment says plating equipment sales depend on investment levels in the PCB, semiconductor and electronics industries, and that the company is receiving more orders as a technology-leading supplier with a much smaller operating loss.

Sales fell to KRW 28.6 billion in 2024 and then jumped to KRW 76.9 billion in 2025, but the disclosure attributes the 2025 increase mainly to higher sales at subsidiaries.

That makes the 2025 figure hard to read as a full industry recovery and points instead to a business where equipment orders can swing sharply within a single year.

Quarterly sales of KRW 19.8 billion, KRW 20.1 billion and KRW 19 billion in the first three quarters of 2026 have hovered around KRW 20 billion, so the stability of orders is still at an early stage.

Surface treatment chemicals are consumed in PCB production, so their demand moves with production volumes and utilization.

On the competitive side, the company says it is strengthening equipment technology in line with the shift toward automation and higher precision in production lines, and it presents custom-built equipment as its differentiator.

06

Outlook

The company says that, on the back of its balance sheet improvement, it will strengthen the competitiveness of its surface treatment chemicals and fully automatic plating equipment to raise corporate value.

To offset accumulated deficits, the board approved a 10-to-1 reverse stock split in April 2026, and according to a Money Today report, the procedure was completed and trading resumed, with share capital reduced from KRW 24.7 billion to about KRW 2.47 billion.

On April 7, 2026, the board also approved a third-party allotment rights offering to the largest shareholder, Telcon RF Pharmaceutical, with proceeds used to repay debt through an offset against borrowings, in an amount of KRW 7.99999985 billion.

First-half 2026 results showed higher sales and a smaller operating loss, but third-quarter sales of KRW 19 billion were down 17.1% from a year earlier, so the quarterly trend is mixed.

The company points to growth in the high-value PCB market driven by AI and semiconductors as its opportunity, but no quantitative target or order backlog figure is available in the sources used for this report.

The next confirmation point is the annual operating loss and operating cash flow in the 2026 annual report, and after that the first-quarter 2027 report will show whether sales are recovering and how large the operating loss is.

08

Bull factors

Demand for high-value PCBs

The company says the high-value PCB market is expanding steadily as AI and semiconductor industries grow. As lighter and higher-performance electronics raise the share of flexible PCBs and other high-value products, demand for surface treatment technology is rising, according to the company.

Because its chemicals and plating equipment are used in this process, the company argues that the demand expansion could translate into business opportunities.

Narrowing operating losses

The operating loss narrowed from KRW 5.24 billion in the third quarter of 2025 to KRW 135 million in the second quarter of 2026, and in the third quarter of 2026 it was KRW 2.52 billion, smaller than a year earlier. WiseReport put the first-half 2026 consolidated operating loss as down 64.4% year on year. The narrower losses in some quarters can be read as a sign of improving business structure.

Balance sheet restructuring

In April 2026 the company decided on a 10-to-1 reverse stock split to offset accumulated deficits, and reports say the procedure was completed and trading resumed. The same spring, a third-party allotment rights offering to the largest shareholder was approved to raise funds for debt repayment. The company describes these steps as a foundation for improving its financial structure.

09

Bear factors

Four straight years of operating losses

The operating loss continued for four consecutive years, from 2022 through 2025. The 2025 operating margin was -22.3%, and the loss widened even though sales rose sharply from 2024. The bearish case rests on the fact that no return to operating profit appears in the disclosed results.

Volatile sales and a recent decline

Sales fell to KRW 28.6 billion in 2024 and then jumped to KRW 76.9 billion in 2025, a large swing between years. Third-quarter 2026 sales of KRW 19 billion were down 17.1% from a year earlier and 5.5% from the prior quarter. Whether the 2025 increase, which the company linked to subsidiary sales, will continue remains to be seen.

Financial strain and cash outflows

The debt-to-equity ratio rose from 32.2% in 2022 to 129.1% in 2025, and total liabilities at the end of 2025 were KRW 80.7 billion against total equity of KRW 62.5 billion. Operating cash flow was an outflow of KRW 19.9 billion in 2025.

With the largest shareholder holding 80.82% and a free float of 19.18%, funding and supply-demand conditions also add to the burden.

10

Risk factors

Financial and liquidity risk

Continued operating losses and negative operating cash flow have increased reliance on outside funding. A rights offering was carried out in 2026 to repay debt, and the debt-to-equity ratio stood at 129.1% at the end of 2025.

Of total equity of KRW 62.5 billion, KRW 55.6 billion is attributable to controlling shareholders, so further losses would reduce financial room.

Share structure and trading risk

In 2026, the share consolidation and capital reduction procedures suspended trading several times, and the number of shares fell sharply. The largest shareholder holds 80.82%, leaving a free float of 19.18%.

That can make the share price move sharply on small volumes, and because the unit of per-share metrics has changed, comparisons with older figures require care.

Industry cycle and order risk

Plating equipment sales depend on investment levels in the PCB, semiconductor and electronics industries. As the 37.0% sales drop in 2024 and the sharp rebound in 2025 show, annual results can swing widely.

With third-quarter 2026 sales down from a year earlier, whether orders continue is the key variable for a recovery.

Overall view

KPM Tech is a materials and equipment supplier that combines surface treatment chemicals with fully automatic plating equipment, so its direction depends on PCB and semiconductor investment and demand for high-value PCBs linked to AI.

Sales rose to KRW 76.9 billion in 2025, but the operating loss of KRW 17.2 billion and the -22.3% operating margin extended a four-year run of losses. The 2026 quarterly trend mixes a smaller operating loss with lower sales, and third-quarter sales of KRW 19 billion were down 17.1% from a year earlier.

On the financial side, the debt-to-equity ratio rose to 129.1% and operating cash flow stayed negative, while a reverse stock split and a debt-repayment rights offering are reshaping the balance sheet. Valuation sits well below book value per share, but no earnings-based multiple can be calculated.

The largest shareholder holds 80.82% and the free float is small, and because the share count has fallen, simple comparisons with older per-share figures require care. The next checkpoints are the annual operating loss and cash flow in the 2026 annual report, and whether sales recover in the first quarter of 2027.
