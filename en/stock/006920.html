Mohenz: Ready-Mix Concrete Swinging Between Losses and Profit

Mohenz earns nearly all of its revenue from ready-mix concrete, and after swinging to an annual loss in 2025, its quarterly results alternated between an operating profit in Q2 2026 and an operating loss in Q3 2026.

02

Business structure

Mohenz was founded in 1970 and listed on KOSDAQ in 1989 as a ready-mix concrete manufacturer and seller. Based on the revenue mix compiled by Alphasquare, ready-mix concrete accounts for 99.05% of sales and other products for 0.95%, making the business effectively single-segment.

A consolidated subsidiary runs welding operations alongside ready-mix production, widening its footprint in construction materials. Ready-mix is delivered as mixed concrete to construction sites, so the distance between plants and job sites and delivery efficiency affect cost and profitability.

Raw materials include cement, sand, gravel and admixtures, and cement price movements are a key variable for margins. The company has positioned quality competitiveness, wider sales coverage of existing products and more orders from large construction firms as its core direction.

Disclosed market share fell from 4.08% in 2017 to 3.16% in 2021, while Eugene recorded 3.56% in the same 2021 disclosure. A Q1 2026 results summary credits Mohenz with share gains despite a weak construction market, so interpretations of its share trend diverge.

Customers are mainly construction sites that use ready-mix, so construction volumes and timing of orders directly drive revenue swings.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual results show revenue turning down first, with profit following.

Revenue rose from KRW 90.1 billion in 2022 to KRW 111.4 billion in 2023 (up 23.6% year on year), then fell to KRW 100.5 billion in 2024 (down 9.8%) and to KRW 81.2 billion in 2025 (down 19.2%), declining for two consecutive years from 2024 through 2025.

Operating profit went from KRW 3.56 billion in 2022 to KRW 12.1 billion in 2023 (up 240.9%), the largest year in the 2022 to 2025 period, before falling to KRW 2.73 billion in 2024 (down 77.5%) and swinging to an operating loss of KRW 2.96 billion in 2025.

Operating margin was 4.0% in 2022, 10.9% in 2023, 2.7% in 2024 and -3.6% in 2025, falling after the 2023 peak. Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders dropped from KRW 8.27 billion in 2023 to KRW 580 million in 2024 (down 93.0%), and in 2025 turned into a net loss of KRW 2.63 billion.

Net loss including non-controlling interests was KRW 3.5 billion in 2025, larger than the controlling-shareholder loss, which points to losses at the non-controlling level as well.

Operating cash flow fell from KRW 2.65 billion in 2022 and KRW 9.28 billion in 2023 to KRW 1.02 billion in 2024 and KRW 126 million in 2025, so cash generation weakened alongside profit.

On a quarterly basis, revenue of KRW 20.4 billion and an operating loss of KRW 270 million in Q3 2025 gave way to revenue of KRW 21.8 billion and an operating loss of KRW 1.76 billion in Q4 2025, a wider loss.

In Q1 2026 revenue was KRW 17.8 billion (down 18.5% from the prior quarter) and the operating loss narrowed to KRW 747 million. Q2 2026 revenue rose to KRW 22.1 billion (up 24.7% from the prior quarter) and the company posted an operating profit of KRW 1.18 billion.

Q3 2026 revenue fell back to KRW 18.4 billion (down 9.8% year on year), and an operating loss of KRW 295 million resumed, wider than a year earlier. Net loss attributable to controlling shareholders over the last four quarters totals KRW 2.25 billion, and quarterly results still alternate between profit and loss.

05

Industry analysis

Ready-mix is delivered from plants to nearby sites, so demand moves directly with local construction volumes. A Q1 2026 results summary credits Mohenz with share gains despite a weak construction market, yet disclosed share fell from 4.08% in 2017 to 3.16% in 2021.

In the same 2021 disclosure, Eugene recorded a 3.56% share, higher than Mohenz. In an Alphasquare comparison of 35 construction materials stocks, Mohenz ranks 28th on annual revenue and 25th on operating margin, both in the lower part of the group.

Listed cement companies in the same sector, including Hanil Cement, SAMPYO Cement and Asia Cement, supply key raw materials for ready-mix, so raw material prices and supply terms are one axis of sector judgment.

When raw material costs rise and selling prices lag in passing them on, margins come under pressure, while stable inputs give room for margin recovery. Domestic construction orders and starts set the broad direction of ready-mix demand, so the pace of recovery in construction shapes the whole sector.

A structure that depends on large builders' orders secures volume, but prolonged delays in orders would lower plant utilization at the same time.

Bull factors

Quarterly profit returned

It followed a narrower operating loss of KRW 747 million in Q1 2026, giving a basis to judge whether the earnings trough has passed.

Easing leverage trend

The debt-to-equity ratio declined from 71.0% in 2022 to 63.0% in 2023 and 52.5% in 2024, then edged up to 55.6% in 2025. Total equity stood at KRW 38.4 billion and total liabilities at KRW 21.4 billion at end-2025. Borrowing burden has not expanded sharply despite annual losses, which gives a cushion against earnings swings.

Share gains cited

A Q1 2026 results summary credited Mohenz with share gains despite a weak construction market. Whether this translates into sustained quarterly revenue recovery can be gauged by whether revenue of KRW 22.1 billion in Q2 2026 holds in later quarters. This contrasts with the downward share trend shown in disclosures since 2017.

Risk factors

Construction cycle and project volumes

Ready-mix demand moves directly with construction volumes and order timing. A Q1 2026 results summary also pointed to a weak construction market. When volumes fall, plant utilization and sales drop together, which widens quarterly earnings swings.

Raw material and logistics costs

Cement, aggregates and delivery costs determine ready-mix cost. When input costs rise and selling prices are passed on with a lag, operating margin narrows. Whether the 2.7% operating margin in 2024 and -3.6% in 2025 reflect this pressure should be checked against cost items in the annual report.

Cash flow and non-controlling interest results

Operating cash flow shrank to KRW 126 million in 2025. The net loss of KRW 3.5 billion exceeded the controlling-shareholder loss of KRW 2.63 billion, indicating a share of losses sits in non-controlling interests. Results at the subsidiary level can widen swings in consolidated profit.

Overall view

Mohenz runs a single-business ready-mix model in which revenue and profit both declined after the 2023 peak, and the swing to annual operating and net losses was confirmed in 2025.

Q2 2026 returned to operating profit of KRW 1.18 billion and the Q1 loss narrowed, but Q3 2026 revenue fell to KRW 18.4 billion and an operating loss of KRW 295 million resumed. The debt-to-equity ratio fell from 71.0% in 2022 to 55.6% in 2025, so financial leverage has eased.

Operating cash flow, however, shrank to KRW 126 million in 2025, weakening cash support for earnings. Disclosed share fell from 4.08% in 2017 to 3.16% in 2021, while a Q1 2026 summary credited share gains.

Bullish factors are the quarterly return to profit and lower debt, while bearish factors are continued annual losses, weaker cash flow and a slow construction market.
