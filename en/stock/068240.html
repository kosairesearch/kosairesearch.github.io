Report overview

Fusion Orders Amid Losses, Balance Sheet Repair Is the Test

Dawonsys is a company where fusion power-supply orders and a plan to repair its balance sheet are both in focus, after an operating loss of KRW 120.7 billion and a capital deficit in 2025.

  1. 1

    On the disclosed annual figures, 2025 revenue fell 62.8% year on year to KRW 112.1 billion, and the company swung to an operating loss of KRW 120.7 billion.

  2. 2

  3. 3

02

Business structure

Dawonsys is described as a manufacturer of railway rolling stock and plasma power supplies. On the rail side, electric railcar sales have been the main pillar of the business, and the decline in electric railcar sales was cited as the first cause of the 2025 earnings deterioration.

On the plasma side, high-voltage power supplies used in fusion devices form the representative product line. The company said it was confirmed as the supplier of 24 high-voltage power units for the ITER project, and the order was reported at KRW 90 billion.

That order followed an earlier selection as supplier of superconducting power units for the Stage 2 phase. Its experience supplying, maintaining and operating power units for heating systems at the KSTAR superconducting fusion facility was cited as the basis for the technology assessment.

Customers fall into two groups: fusion projects involving international organizations and overseas partners, and domestic railway purchasers.

In rolling stock, delivery schedules and quality bear directly on purchaser evaluations, and a delay in railcar deliveries drew public criticism from the president, turning into a trust issue.

Compared with large power-equipment makers in the same electrical equipment sector, such as LS ELECTRIC, Hyosung Heavy Industries and HD Hyundai Electric, Dawonsys serves different product scopes and customers, so its delivery record in specialized fusion and rail power supplies is the main point of differentiation.

Because the financial position is closely tied to the ability to execute orders, it is worth reviewing alongside the business structure.

04

Earnings analysis

Based on reported results, revenue was KRW 209.2 billion in 2022, KRW 357.8 billion in 2023 (up 71.1% year on year), KRW 301.5 billion in 2024 (down 15.7% year on year), and KRW 112.1 billion in 2025 (down 62.8% year on year).

Revenue peaked in 2023 and fell for two consecutive years from 2024 to 2025, with 2023 the largest year and 2025 the smallest year between 2022 and 2025.

Operating profit moved from an operating loss of KRW 159.3 billion in 2022 to an operating profit of KRW 19.3 billion in 2023 (a turnaround to profit from the previous year) and an operating profit of KRW 7.65 billion in 2024 (down 60.3% year on year), then swung to an operating loss of KRW 120.7 billion in 2025 (a turnaround to loss from the previous year).

The operating margin was -76.1% in 2022, 5.4% in 2023, 2.5% in 2024, and -107.7% in 2025. The widening of the loss in 2025 is attributed to lower railcar revenue, greater raw material price volatility, and cost burdens from global supply chain disruptions (WiseReport).

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders was positive at KRW 3.71 billion in 2023 and KRW 11.3 billion in 2024, but turned to a net loss attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 197.0 billion in 2025.

Net loss including non-controlling interests was KRW 196.9 billion in 2025, a different figure from the net income attributable to controlling shareholders.

Operating cash flow was negative in all four years: -KRW 149.5 billion in 2022, -KRW 2.69 billion in 2023, -KRW 50.9 billion in 2024, and -KRW 82.2 billion in 2025.

On a quarterly basis, revenue fell from KRW 28.0 billion in Q3 2025 to KRW 4.66 billion in Q4, then rose to KRW 21.0 billion in Q1 2026, and in Q3 it was KRW 10.6 billion, down 62.0% year on year (there is no data for Q2 revenue).

The operating loss was KRW 19.3 billion in Q3 2025, KRW 63.1 billion in Q4, KRW 9.46 billion in Q1 2026, KRW 22.9 billion in Q2, and KRW 4.7 billion in Q3.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders was a profit of KRW 34.1 billion in Q1 2026, but the compiled data shows a pre-tax loss of KRW 15.2 billion excluding certain items, suggesting that the Q1 profit was largely driven by one-off factors (Google Finance compilation).

Net losses attributable to controlling shareholders followed, at KRW 25.0 billion in Q2 and KRW 19.7 billion in Q3, and the total net loss attributable to controlling shareholders over the most recent four quarters (Q4 2025 to Q3 2026) is KRW 149.5 billion.

05

Industry analysis

Dawonsys's end markets fall into two areas, electric railcars and fusion research projects. In 2025, lower electric railcar sales, volatile raw material prices and supply chain disruptions overlapped and weakened operating conditions (WISEreport).

In fusion, orders tend to come in stages from international projects such as ITER, and the company has added high-voltage power supply work after its earlier superconducting power unit supply role (Cheolgang Economic News).

If this continues, its track record in specialized power supplies becomes the basis of its competitiveness. In rail, delivery schedules and quality control bear directly on purchaser evaluations, and delivery delays have become a public issue, making trust recovery an important task (Newsis).

Listed peers in the same electrical equipment sector include LS ELECTRIC, Hyosung Heavy Industries, HD Hyundai Electric, Iljin Electric, Cheryong Electric and Sanil Electric.

Most of them focus on large power equipment or cable and distribution products, so their product scope differs from Dawonsys's rail and fusion power supplies. The position in the cycle for this end market depends on how continuously fusion orders are placed.

Outlook

The confirmed order the company has pointed to is the supply confirmation for 24 high-voltage power units for ITER, valued at KRW 90 billion (Cheolgang Economic News). An additional candidate, an ELM control power supply worth KRW 67 billion, was reported as expected to become clear in the fourth quarter of 2026.

If both are confirmed, the revenue base of the fusion segment would widen, though the timing and scale should be checked against the reporting dates. On the financial side, the disclaimer of opinion on the 2025 audit and the capital deficit led to listing-eligibility questions (Newsis).

In March 2026, a third-party rights offering and share sale to an engineering-sector mutual aid cooperative, aimed at a change in the largest shareholder, was pursued, but reports said completion became uncertain as the company faced possible delisting (Newsis).

A report in early April 2026 said the KRW 41.1 billion rights issue was fully withdrawn after the cooperative stepped back, and that the company was moving toward court receivership (a Korean business daily). The same report highlighted a guarantee burden of more than KRW 1 trillion that the cooperative had taken on.

Accumulated deficit at the end of 2025 was recorded at KRW 846.7 billion (Newsis). The next periodic report is the 2026 annual report, with a statutory filing deadline at the end of March 2027.

In that filing, the 2026 annual revenue and operating results, and the audit outcome after the 2025 disclaimer, are the next checkpoints.

Bull factors

A base of fusion power-supply orders

The company said it was confirmed as the supplier of 24 high-voltage power units for ITER, reported at KRW 90 billion. Its experience supplying, maintaining and operating heating-system power units at KSTAR was cited as the basis for its technology assessment.

Whether an ELM control power supply worth KRW 67 billion is won is expected to become clear in the fourth quarter of 2026.

A narrowing operating loss

The operating loss narrowed from KRW 63.1 billion in the fourth quarter of 2025 to KRW 9.46 billion in the first quarter of 2026, then rose to KRW 22.9 billion in the second quarter before falling to KRW 4.7 billion in the third quarter.

The third-quarter 2026 loss was smaller than the KRW 19.3 billion of the same quarter a year earlier. Whether this improvement reflects lower cost pressure can be checked in the next periodic report.

A record of past profits

Operating profit of KRW 19.3 billion in 2023 marked a swing from loss to profit, and profit attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 11.3 billion in 2024 rose 204.2% from the prior year. The operating margin was 5.4% in 2023 and 2.5% in 2024.

Profits in this period were modest, but they show that the rail and fusion power business was profitable in some years.

Bear factors

Capital impairment and listing eligibility

Total equity was a deficit of KRW 515.6 billion at the end of 2025, a full capital impairment. The disclaimer of opinion on the 2025 audit raised listing-eligibility questions, and reports said the company could be designated for unfaithful disclosure and face a substantive listing review. The accumulated deficit stood at KRW 846.7 billion.

Withdrawn rights issue and guarantee exposure

A report said a KRW 41.1 billion rights issue was fully withdrawn after the engineering mutual aid cooperative stepped back. If contract performance or defect guarantee claims arise, the financial burden could grow further.

Shrinking sales and cost pressure

Revenue fell 62.8% to KRW 112.1 billion in 2025, and third-quarter 2026 revenue of KRW 10.6 billion was down 62.0% from a year earlier. In 2025, lower electric railcar sales overlapped with cost pressure from raw materials and supply chain disruptions.

Risk factors

Financial structure and going-concern risk

Total equity was a deficit of KRW 515.6 billion at the end of 2025, and total liabilities were KRW 1.13 trillion. Operating cash flow was -KRW 82.2 billion in 2025. If audit conclusions or the going-concern assumption change, the effects on listing status and funding conditions would be direct.

Guarantee and contract performance risk

The more than KRW 1 trillion guarantee burden borne by the engineering mutual aid cooperative was reported as a key issue. Delays in railcar deliveries have turned into a trust issue, and contract performance or defect guarantee claims could create additional costs. This risk remains open until the guarantee structure is settled.

Order volatility and cost risk

Fusion orders depend on international project schedules, and whether the additional ELM control power supply order is won will become clear with fourth-quarter 2026 results. In rail, lower electric railcar sales fed directly into earnings. Raw material price swings and global supply chain disruptions could raise cost pressure again.

Overall view

Dawonsys recorded revenue of KRW 112.1 billion, an operating loss of KRW 120.7 billion and a controlling shareholder net loss of KRW 197 billion in 2025, and operating losses have continued for five consecutive quarters from the third quarter of 2025 through the third quarter of 2026.

The operating loss narrowed to KRW 4.7 billion in the third quarter of 2026, but the controlling shareholder net loss was KRW 19.7 billion. In fusion power supplies, confirmation as supplier of 24 high-voltage units for ITER and a further KRW 67 billion order candidate remain the basis of the business.

On the other side, the total equity deficit of KRW 515.6 billion at the end of 2025, the disclaimer of opinion, the withdrawn KRW 41.1 billion rights issue and the guarantee burden of more than KRW 1 trillion are the key issues in the financial structure. Both strengths and weaknesses coexist.

The next checkpoints are the outcome of the ELM order in the fourth quarter of 2026, decisions on court receivership and listing eligibility, and the 2026 annual report due at the end of March 2027.
