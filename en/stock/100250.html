02

Business structure

Chinyang Holdings is a holding company that controls its domestic operating subsidiaries, and its core businesses are plastic product manufacturing, automotive parts manufacturing and real estate leasing.

Consolidated subsidiaries include Chinyang Industry, the PU foam subsidiary, Chinyang Chemical, the automotive interior subsidiary, Chinyang AMC and Chinyang Mulsan. Sources differ on the subsidiary count, with one corporate database listing 7 and another listing 8.

Consolidated 2024 sales were split into product sales of KRW 227.9 billion, merchandise sales of KRW 54.4 billion and rental income of KRW 4.2 billion, based on the regular report breakdown. Product sales are the largest component, and merchandise sales include TDI trading.

The PU foam subsidiary reported cumulative sales of KRW 34.7 billion for the first nine months of 2025, with PU foam accounting for 99.6% of that total. PU foam is mainly used in automotive interiors, and its customer base centers on domestic carmakers.

The PVC artificial leather unit serves the same automotive interior market, and the automotive subsidiary makes interior trim. Certain automotive interior items were disclosed with export shares of around 65% in 2025.

Management consulting income equals about 0.9% of total operating revenue, so it is a small part of the holding company's income.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual sales rose from KRW 234.6 billion in 2022 to KRW 271.1 billion in 2023 (up 15.6%) and KRW 286.8 billion in 2024 (up 5.8%), then fell to KRW 279.8 billion in 2025 (down 2.5%). Across 2022 to 2025, 2024 was the largest year for sales and 2022 was the smallest.

Operating profit fell from KRW 5.23 billion in 2022 to KRW 3.52 billion in 2023 (down 32.8%), then rose to KRW 4.91 billion in 2024 (up 39.8%) and KRW 5.78 billion in 2025 (up 17.7%). Operating profit has increased for two consecutive years from 2024 through 2025.

The operating margin was 2.2% in 2022, 1.3% in 2023, 1.7% in 2024 and 2.1% in 2025, so higher sales have not lifted the margin above roughly 2%.

Profit attributable to owners peaked at KRW 36.1 billion in 2023 (up 47.7%), dropped to KRW 15.3 billion in 2024 (down 57.6%) and recovered to KRW 18.2 billion in 2025 (up 18.6%).

Net profit including non-controlling interests was KRW 27.9 billion, KRW 38.2 billion, KRW 20.9 billion and KRW 22.1 billion for 2022 through 2025, and it differs from profit attributable to owners. The debt-to-equity ratio climbed each year from 48.3% in 2022 to 55.7% in 2025.

Operating cash flow was KRW 8.23 billion in 2022, an outflow of KRW 6.24 billion in 2023, then KRW 23.4 billion in 2024 and KRW 23 billion in 2025. On a quarterly basis, Q3 2025 sales were KRW 67.7 billion with operating profit of KRW 1.2 billion.

Q4 2025 sales were KRW 65.1 billion, down 3.9% from the prior quarter, and the company posted an operating loss of KRW 724 million, a swing from profit to loss. Q1 2026 sales were KRW 73.7 billion, up 13.4% quarter on quarter, and operating profit turned positive at KRW 2.27 billion.

Q2 2026 sales were KRW 77.3 billion with operating profit of KRW 2.2 billion, down 3.0%.

Q3 2026 sales were KRW 72.2 billion, down 6.7% quarter on quarter and up 6.5% year on year, while operating profit was KRW 47.25 million, down 97.9% quarter on quarter and down 96.1% year on year, marking two consecutive quarterly declines from Q2 2026.

Profit attributable to owners was KRW 14.1 billion in Q3 2025, KRW 4.86 billion in Q4 2025, KRW 7.89 billion in Q1 2026 and KRW 90.6 billion in Q2 2026. The Q2 2026 figure was about 41 times that quarter's operating profit, which means non-operating items weighed heavily.

In Q3 2026 an operating profit close to zero coincided with a net loss attributable to owners of KRW 48.2 billion. The sum of profit attributable to owners over the last four quarters, from Q4 2025 to Q3 2026, was KRW 55.1 billion.

05

Industry analysis

Chinyang Holdings' earnings are tied directly to demand for automotive interior materials. Chinyang Chemical's PVC artificial leather is expanding into the automotive interior market on rising demand for vegan leather and the use of bio-based materials, according to a corporate database summary.

The automotive interior subsidiary is reported to be growing exports on new vehicle models developed around overseas trends. PU foam from the PU foam subsidiary is used in automotive interiors, so it moves with domestic vehicle production.

The company's investor materials cite the limits of the existing PU foaming and curing process and describe a new plant with automated batch production aimed at cost, quality and delivery competitiveness. The company also aims to secure a stable supply chain with quality certification for domestic carmakers.

Battery thermal management parts for electric vehicles, known as C-PAD, connect the group to the shift toward electrification. Competition centers on quality and delivery among automotive parts suppliers, and raw material price swings shape margins.

Whether the sharp drop in Q3 2026 operating profit reflects industry conditions or cost factors cannot be separated from the quarterly figures alone. Where the business sits in the cycle should be read together with auto production trends and the timing of the new plant's start-up.

06

Outlook

The concrete plan disclosed by the company is the new plant at the PU foam subsidiary. The rights offering of about KRW 19.2 billion is earmarked for plant construction and equipment relocation, with facility funding stated at KRW 19.16 billion.

If the parent takes up its allotment, its stake in that subsidiary could rise from up to 50.5% to as much as 54.55%. The new plant is meant to improve production efficiency and raise the share of higher-value polyurethane products.

The company expects better results and management efficiency from the investment, but it has not ruled out a negative impact if research and development results fall short or unexpected issues arise during construction.

Investor materials from 2025 also described expanding the real estate business and building new structures within the group. Quantitative guidance for the 2026 fiscal year has not been disclosed. The start-up timing of the new plant and any shift in product mix after start-up will be key variables for operating margin.

Whether the sharp drop in Q3 2026 operating profit reflects costs tied to the equipment transition should be tracked through later disclosures. Export growth and demand for vegan materials are positive factors, but whether higher volume translates into margins has not yet been shown in the figures.

Bull factors

New plant could reshape production

The new plant at the PU foam subsidiary is designed to add automated batch production that could improve cost, quality and delivery competitiveness. The plan also raises the share of higher-value polyurethane products. The company expects the investment to lift results. Removing limits in the existing foaming and curing process is the core rationale.

Larger stake and recovering earnings

Taking part in the rights offering could raise the parent's stake in the PU foam subsidiary to as much as 54.55% from up to 50.5%. Operating profit rose from KRW 4.91 billion in 2024 to KRW 5.78 billion in 2025, extending gains for a second straight year.

Profit attributable to owners recovered from KRW 15.3 billion in 2024 to KRW 18.2 billion in 2025. The recovery in earnings alongside a larger stake is cited as a positive.

Export and eco-material demand

The automotive interior subsidiary is reported to be growing exports on new vehicle models built around overseas trends. The PVC artificial leather unit is expanding into the automotive interior market on rising demand for vegan leather and bio-based materials.

Battery thermal management parts for electric vehicles also connect to electrification. Diversifying demand across core product lines is a positive factor.

Bear factors

Structurally thin operating margin

The operating margin was 2.2% in 2022, 1.3% in 2023, 1.7% in 2024 and 2.1% in 2025, never clearing the low 2% range. Operating profit was only KRW 5.78 billion on sales of KRW 279.8 billion in 2025. A thin margin leaves quarterly results exposed to small cost changes.

Reliance on non-operating items and quarterly losses

Profit attributable to owners of KRW 90.6 billion in Q2 2026 was about 41 times operating profit of KRW 2.2 billion, which shows a large non-operating contribution. In Q3 2026, operating profit fell to KRW 47.25 million and the company posted a net loss attributable to owners of KRW 48.2 billion.

Net results swing between profit and loss while operating profit stalls, which makes earnings harder to project.

Rising leverage and intragroup borrowing

In 2024 the chemical subsidiary raised about KRW 12 billion through a rights offering to repay debt, then borrowed KRW 20 billion and KRW 15 billion from affiliated companies. Added investment in the new plant could increase financial pressure. Complex fund flows within the group are a negative factor.

Risk factors

New plant execution risk

The company has acknowledged that weak research and development results or unexpected construction issues could hurt earnings. A delayed start-up would postpone the investment benefit while depreciation burdens rise first. The time needed to stabilize automated equipment is another variable. Whether the Q3 2026 profit drop overlapped with transition costs also needs monitoring.

Earnings volatility and related-party flows

Profit attributable to owners fell 57.6% in 2024 from KRW 36.1 billion in 2023. On a quarterly basis, the result moved from KRW 90.6 billion in Q2 2026 to a net loss of KRW 48.2 billion in Q3 2026. Borrowing and fund transfers between affiliates can affect both earnings and the balance sheet. When profit concentrates in one quarter, trends are harder to read.

Governance and shareholder dilution

At the March 2026 shareholder meeting, two inside directors were reelected, leaving three directors in total, one of whom is an outside director, a 33.3% outside director share. The board's independence is limited by that structure.

The rights offering covers about 6.7 million new shares, roughly 67.0% of existing shares, which dilutes existing holders unless they participate. Ownership structure will depend on who takes up the allotment.

Overall view

Chinyang Holdings' operating profit rose to KRW 5.78 billion in 2025, extending gains for a second straight year, and profit attributable to owners recovered to KRW 18.2 billion. However, the operating margin stayed between 1.3% and 2.2% from 2022 to 2025, so profit is thin relative to sales.

Profit attributable to owners of KRW 90.6 billion in Q2 2026 was about 41 times that quarter's operating profit, which points to a large non-operating contribution, and in Q3 2026 operating profit fell to KRW 47.25 million alongside a net loss attributable to owners of KRW 48.2 billion.

The KRW 55.1 billion total over the last four quarters is concentrated in a single quarter. Positive factors include the new plant investment at the PU foam subsidiary, the possibility of a larger stake, and demand from exports and eco-friendly materials.

Negative factors include a thin operating margin, a rising debt-to-equity ratio, intragroup borrowing and execution risk at the new plant. The price-to-book ratio is well below 1, which is a factual comparison against net assets, but the direction of earnings is hard to gauge from quarterly figures.

The next checkpoints are the full-year 2026 operating margin in the March 2027 annual report and the start-up timing of the new plant.
