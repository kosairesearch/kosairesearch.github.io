02

Business structure

Hyundai Movex runs logistics automation as its core business, centered on automated storage and retrieval systems (AS/RS).

It supplies shelves, stacker cranes, conveyance, conveyors and sorters as a package, and in the middle of a process it can substitute automated guided vehicles (AGV) or AMRs according to customer needs. Its in-house AGV, AMR and gantry robots were developed at the Cheongna R&D Center built in 2019.

The company reports three segments, logistics automation, platform screen doors (PSD) and IT services, and according to a November 2025 report, the 2024 revenue mix was 69% logistics automation, 23% screen doors and 8% IT services.

Of the backlog at the end of September 2026, logistics automation accounted for KRW 460.8 billion, screen doors KRW 43.4 billion and IT services KRW 11.2 billion.

Customers include tire makers, battery material and cell producers, food, retail and beauty companies, and overseas reference projects include battery plants in the United States and Canada and a subway PSD project in Sydney. Exports reached 60.1% of 2025 revenue.

Industry commentary notes that intra-group orders are a very small share, which supports the view of standalone competitiveness. The PSD segment faces limited market conditions beyond new lines, while renovation of aging subway stations is cited as a growth driver.

04

Earnings analysis

In the annual figures from DART regular reports, revenue went from KRW 210.5 billion in 2022 to KRW 267.8 billion in 2023 (up 27.3% year on year), KRW 341.4 billion in 2024 (up 27.5%) and KRW 393.9 billion in 2025 (up 15.4%), rising three years in a row from 2023 through 2025, with 2025 the largest year in the 2022 to 2025 span.

Operating profit moved differently, from KRW 12.2 billion in 2022 to KRW 4.14 billion in 2023 (down 65.9%), KRW 24.6 billion in 2024 (up 493.0%) and KRW 18.2 billion in 2025 (down 25.9%). 2024 was the peak year for operating profit, when the operating margin reached 7.2%.

In 2025 revenue grew but the operating margin fell to 4.6%. Coverage attributed the 2024 gains to revenue from large overseas projects in North America and Australia and to efficiency measures that absorbed supply chain disruption, while the 2025 profit decline was linked to one-off costs.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders fell 58.0% from KRW 26.9 billion in 2024 to KRW 11.3 billion in 2025. Operating cash flow was -KRW 26 billion in 2022, -KRW 9.94 billion in 2023, KRW 55.1 billion in 2024 and -KRW 1.43 billion in 2025, so 2024 was the only positive year.

At the end of 2025, total equity was KRW 160.3 billion and total liabilities KRW 123.1 billion, and the debt ratio had risen from 42.0% in 2022 to 76.8%.

On a quarterly basis, revenue moved from KRW 103.5 billion and operating profit of KRW 6.72 billion in Q3 2025 to KRW 113.9 billion and KRW 1.86 billion in Q4 2025, then to KRW 75 billion with an operating loss of KRW 640 million in Q1 2026.

Q2 2026 revenue was KRW 100.4 billion with operating profit of KRW 3.2 billion, a turn to profit. In Q3 2026 revenue was KRW 84.9 billion, down 18.0% year on year, and operating profit was KRW 4.31 billion, down 35.9%.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders swung to a loss of KRW 4.22 billion in Q3 2026 from a profit a year earlier, and the four quarters from Q4 2025 to Q3 2026 total KRW 8.21 billion.

05

Industry analysis

Demand for logistics automation equipment moves closely with domestic manufacturers' facility investment, and that investment tends to shrink during economic downturns. E-commerce expansion, however, continues to support demand for distribution center automation.

In North America, forecasts call for continued growth in energy storage system and battery material demand even as electric vehicle demand stagnates.

The tire industry is expected to expand global production bases under high-inch and premium strategies, and Korea's three major tire makers as well as overseas players are speeding up production network reorganization.

In a November 2025 report, a Shinhan Investment Corp. analyst said development of cleanroom products was nearly complete, enabling a push into domestic semiconductor cluster projects.

The same analyst expected the fastest order growth among domestic and overseas peers over the coming years, and that view is an analyst opinion to be checked against revenue trends such as the year-on-year decline in Q3 2026.

Industry commentary also notes that intra-group volume is minimal, so external order competitiveness directly drives results.

Outlook

Reports have pointed to the conversion of the KRW 515.5 billion backlog at the end of September 2026 into revenue as the key factor for results.

An industry outlook from November 2025 projected new orders of KRW 470 billion in 2025, KRW 520 billion in 2026 and KRW 550 billion in 2027, and since that view dates from November 2025, it should be compared with actual 2026 order intake.

A November 2026 report confirmed a KRW 72 billion order for a new Kumho Tire plant in Hampyeong, and separately a KRW 19 billion national R&D project tied to a Korea-US shipbuilding collaboration. Because orders tend to concentrate in the fourth quarter, order disclosures in Q4 2026 will shape the annual order picture.

A March 2026 estimate from Bookook Securities projected 2026 revenue of KRW 496.3 billion and operating profit of KRW 38.5 billion, and that is a March 2026 estimate; whether it is reached can be checked against the 2026 annual report, since the company's Q4 2026 results have not yet been filed in a regular report.

Expansion of smart logistics built on AI and robotics continues to be promoted by industry and government, while combining physical AI with automation equipment remains a medium-term project.

08

Bull factors

Larger Order Backlog

The backlog stood at KRW 515.5 billion at the end of September 2026, up from KRW 363.2 billion a year earlier. Logistics automation accounts for most of it at KRW 460.8 billion, with PSD and IT services making up the rest. A KRW 72 billion order for the Kumho Tire Hampyeong plant was reported in the same period. Bulls see the backlog as the basis for revenue growth once it converts.

Operating Profit Back in the Black

Operating profit went from a KRW 640 million loss in Q1 2026 to KRW 3.2 billion in Q2 and KRW 4.31 billion in Q3. The 2024 peak of KRW 24.6 billion and a 7.2% operating margin show that margins have reached higher levels before. Bulls treat the run of profits from Q2 through Q3 as a sign that the business is normalizing.

Overseas Expansion and New Segments

Exports reached 60.1% of 2025 revenue, up from 12.4% three years earlier. The customer base has widened to battery materials and cells, tires, food and retail.

The AGV and gantry robot technology from the Cheongna R&D Center and the nearly completed cleanroom product development are cited as foundations for entering new areas such as semiconductor clusters.

Bear factors

Revenue Growth Without Profit Growth

Revenue rose 15.4% in 2025, yet operating profit fell 25.9%. Q3 2026 revenue of KRW 84.9 billion was down 18.0% year on year. Q3 2026 operating profit of KRW 4.31 billion was positive but below the prior-year level. Bears point out that revenue growth has not translated into profit growth across 2025 and into Q3 2026.

Cash Flow and Balance Sheet Pressure

Operating cash flow was negative in 2022, 2023 and 2025, at -KRW 26 billion, -KRW 9.94 billion and -KRW 1.43 billion, with 2024 the only positive year at KRW 55.1 billion. The higher debt ratio, from 42.0% in 2022 to 76.8% in 2025, increased the financial burden.

A net loss attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 4.22 billion returned in Q3 2026. Bears view these three points as questions about earnings quality and financial capacity.

Capex Cycle and PSD Softness

Demand for logistics automation moves with domestic manufacturers' facility investment and can weaken in downturns. The PSD segment is described as having limited market conditions beyond new lines. Bears argue that softer demand in these two segments could slow the conversion of the backlog into revenue.

10

Risk factors

Project Execution and Costs

For large projects, total contract value can change as installation costs are added. Analysts linked the 2025 profit decline partly to one-off costs. With a large backlog, cost swings during project execution could move the margin.

Overseas Exposure and Receivables

With exports at 60.1% of 2025 revenue, exposure to exchange rates and to overseas customer payments has grown. Korea Credit News highlighted capital erosion and a sharp rise in bad-debt reserves at the Chinese subsidiary. As overseas projects increase, receivables and local subsidiary management become variables for results.

Customer Capex Cycles and Policy

Logistics automation investment depends on the investment plans of domestic manufacturers. North American battery and ESS investment is shaped by customer plans and policy amid slower electric vehicle demand.

If large orders concentrate in a few customers or industries, revenue can swing more sharply when order gaps appear.

Overall view

Hyundai Movex has built its revenue base in logistics automation and platform screen doors on a backlog of KRW 515.5 billion at the end of September 2026.

Operating profit stayed positive in Q2 and Q3 2026, but Q3 revenue fell 18.0% year on year and the company swung to a net loss attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 4.22 billion.

On an annual basis, revenue rose to KRW 393.9 billion in 2025 while operating profit fell to KRW 18.2 billion, so the link between revenue growth and profit growth is not yet clear. The debt ratio climbed from 42.0% in 2022 to 76.8% in 2025, and operating cash flow was -KRW 1.43 billion in 2025.

Exports at 60.1% of revenue bring growth opportunities along with receivables risk overseas. Market capitalization is high relative to net income of KRW 8.21 billion over the last four quarters and total equity of KRW 160.3 billion, so the pace at which orders become revenue is central to interpreting the valuation.

Bull factors are the order scale and the run of profits, while bear factors are the revenue decline, the net loss and cash flow. The next checkpoints are the year-end order disclosures in December 2026, the 2026 annual report due by late March 2027, and the Q1 2027 quarterly report in mid-May 2027.
