Copus Korea Tests Turnaround Under New Owners

02

Business structure

Copus Korea, founded in 2005 and listed on Kosdaq in 2019-2020 via a SPAC merger, is a content distribution and production company.

Its core business involves acquiring content rights from domestic production companies and broadcasters, customizing them (dubbing, subtitling), and supplying them to major Japanese broadcasters, DVD distributors, and OTT platform operators.

Secondary businesses include cosmetics e-commerce leveraging its network and subtitle production outsourcing services. In 2022, the company acquired drama production house Kim Jong-hak Production to strengthen its production capabilities and has participated in co-producing Netflix original content.

More recently, it has been pushing into a short-form drama platform called 'Pickmore' and concentrating resources on OSMU (One Source Multi Use) content planning and production as part of a new business pivot.

Following an ownership change process spanning June to September 2026, Artist Studio, an affiliate of Artist Company where actor Lee Jung-jae is the largest shareholder, secured effective control, and it formed a joint management arrangement with a voting-rights agreement alongside Studio Jidam and Maeil Broadcasting Network (MBN).

The new management stated it plans to combine Copus Korea's overseas, primarily Japanese, distribution network with the production capabilities of Artist Studio and Studio Jidam to build a vertically integrated value chain spanning planning, production, and domestic/international distribution.

Competitively, the company remains a small niche player relative to large content studios and general distributors, and it is now attempting to shift toward a production-centered business model through this ownership transition.

04

Earnings analysis

On an annual basis, 2022 revenue was KRW 29.27 billion with a solid 31.2% operating margin, though net income was still a loss of KRW 1.43 billion.

In 2023, revenue rose to KRW 44.65 billion but the operating margin fell sharply to 3.8%, while net income turned positive at KRW 3.18 billion (KRW 3.30 billion attributable to owners).

In 2024, revenue declined 6% to KRW 41.83 billion while the operating loss ballooned to KRW 29.36 billion, pushing the operating margin to -70.2%, and the net loss widened to KRW 25.12 billion (KRW 25.01 billion attributable to owners).

In 2025, revenue fell a further 21% to KRW 32.90 billion, but the operating loss narrowed to KRW 7.09 billion (-21.6% margin) and the net loss also narrowed to KRW 13.60 billion (KRW 13.37 billion attributable to owners).

On a quarterly basis, revenue bottomed at KRW 2.50 billion with an operating loss of KRW 3.14 billion in 2025Q2, then temporarily recovered to KRW 11.83 billion and KRW 14.59 billion in Q3 and Q4, before slowing again to KRW 10.80 billion in 2026Q1 and KRW 7.18 billion in 2026Q2.

The operating loss shrank to KRW 0.47 billion in 2025Q4 before widening again to KRW 2.49 billion in 2026Q1 and narrowing to KRW 1.08 billion in 2026Q2, reflecting significant quarter-to-quarter volatility.

Notably, net loss attributable to owners, which ranged from KRW 2.48 billion to KRW 5.75 billion in 2025Q2-Q4, shrank markedly to about KRW 87 million in 2026Q1 and KRW 321 million in 2026Q2, signaling a bottom-line improvement distinct from the operating trend.

Over the trailing four quarters (2025Q3-2026Q2), combined revenue was KRW 44.39 billion, the operating loss was KRW 6.48 billion, and net loss attributable to owners was KRW 6.92 billion, a milder loss range than the full-year 2024 and 2025 figures.

05

Industry analysis

Copus Korea's core end market, Japan's content distribution industry, is widely viewed as having entered a mature phase.

As global OTT platforms such as Netflix rapidly absorbed local Japanese users, the profitability of local broadcasters and OTT platforms deteriorated, which fed directly into reduced licensing demand for Korean broadcast content.

The company's drama production revenue reportedly fell sharply over the past year, and domestic broadcasters cutting back drama slates due to weaker profitability has also been cited as a factor behind the contraction in the production business.

At the same time, some observers note that the continued popularity of Korean-wave content and the still-high market share of local OTT platforms in Japan provide some support for negotiating leverage on new title distribution.

Competitively, Copus Korea remains a small niche player relative to large studios and comprehensive distributors, and it has recently sought new avenues through short-form drama and OSMU content aimed at emerging consumption trends.

Across Kosdaq more broadly, delisting-rule reforms have progressively tightened listing-maintenance requirements such as market capitalization and revenue thresholds, intensifying market scrutiny of small-cap companies with weak earnings.

06

Outlook

The new management consortium of Artist Studio, Studio Jidam (Jidam Media), and StoryArx Studio completed the ownership transition by injecting a combined KRW 28.2 billion through an old-share purchase (KRW 3.2 billion), a third-party placement (KRW 7.5 billion), and a fifth-series convertible bond (KRW 17.5 billion), with the placement payment completed in August 2026 and new share listing finalized in early September.

The company stated plans to strengthen its value chain from planning and production through distribution by securing new content IP, reinforcing production capabilities, and expanding global distribution, and a large-scale IP production contract is reportedly under negotiation.

However, since July 2026 the Kosdaq market-cap threshold for listing maintenance rose to KRW 20 billion and is set to rise again to KRW 30 billion from 2027, prompting a company representative to say the group is "preparing a proactive response plan." The recent increase in total shares outstanding from the placement and CB issuance is favorable for clearing the market-cap threshold but could dilute existing shareholders.

Meanwhile, some existing shareholders filed injunctions to block the new share issuance and the fifth-series CB issuance; both were dismissed by the court but are now under appeal, leaving legal uncertainty around the capital structure not fully resolved.

It has also been confirmed that financial investors such as GreenGrowth and Innovation No.1 Investment Partnership, who participated in an earlier stage of the ownership process, have already sold part of their acquired shares on and off the market, making potential further sales of remaining stakes (overhang) another variable to monitor.

Bull factors

Fresh Capital Injection and Business Combination by New Controlling Shareholder

Artist Studio, an affiliate of Artist Company, together with the Studio Jidam and Maeil Broadcasting Network (MBN) camp, secured control by injecting a combined KRW 28.2 billion through an old-share purchase, third-party placement, and CB subscription.

The new management stated it plans to build vertical integration spanning planning, production, and distribution by combining Copus Korea's overseas distribution network with its own production capabilities.

This capital injection and business-combination attempt could relieve prior liquidity pressure while opening a potential new growth axis.

Recent Narrowing of Quarterly Net Losses

Net loss attributable to owners narrowed sharply from a range of KRW 2.48-5.75 billion in 2025Q2-Q4 to roughly KRW 87 million in 2026Q1 and KRW 321 million in 2026Q2. Even as the operating loss persisted, this bottom-line improvement signal suggests that non-operating burdens may be easing. Whether this trend continues will need to be confirmed through upcoming quarterly results.

Pivot Toward Short-Form and IP-Driven New Businesses

The company is developing a short-form drama platform called 'Pickmore' and concentrating resources on OSMU-style content planning and production as a new business pivot.

The new management has also referenced securing new IP and pursuing a large-scale production contract, continuing a diversification effort away from the legacy Japan-focused distribution model. However, the scale and timing of new-business revenue contribution have not yet been confirmed with published figures.

Bear factors

Structural Contraction of the Core Distribution Business

Revenue fell from KRW 44.65 billion in 2023 to KRW 32.90 billion in 2025, and 2026Q2 revenue slowed again to KRW 7.18 billion from KRW 10.80 billion in Q1.

Licensing demand for Korean content continues to face structural decline as Japan's OTT market matures and global OTT platforms erode local platforms' economics, while domestic broadcasters' reduced drama slates have also weighed on production revenue.

Repeated Control-Change Attempts and Opaque Shareholding Structure

A control-change agreement with GreenGrowth and Innovation No.1 Investment Partnership signed in October 2025 was terminated in January 2026 after the buyer failed to pay the remaining balance, resulting in an unfair-disclosure designation.

Media reports subsequently raised questions about the opacity of certain acquiring parties' funding sources, and it was confirmed that a substantial portion of shares acquired by early investment partnerships had already been sold on and off the market.

This pattern of repeated control-change attempts and share sales could feed into overhang concerns regarding remaining share blocks.

Burden from Tightened Listing-Maintenance Requirements

Since July 2026, the Kosdaq market-cap threshold for listing maintenance rose to KRW 20 billion and is set to rise further to KRW 30 billion from 2027, under a tightened rule where a company falling below the threshold for 30 consecutive trading days is designated an administrative issue, and failure to recover above the threshold for 45 consecutive trading days within a subsequent 90-day window results in delisting.

While the recent increase in shares outstanding from the placement and CB issuance helps support market capitalization, the company will need to continuously manage its market cap going forward.

Risk factors

Financial Soundness

Equity attributable to owners fell sharply from KRW 56.80 billion in 2023 to KRW 25.05 billion in 2025, with large net losses recorded in both 2024 and 2025.

Since 2026, half-year complete capital impairment has also been added as grounds for a substantive delisting review, so further losses could draw greater market attention to capital soundness.

Legal Disputes

Injunctions filed by some existing shareholders to block the new share issuance and the fifth-series convertible bond issuance were dismissed by the court but are now under appeal.

Depending on the outcome, there could be implications for the legal stability of the recently completed capital raise and control-transfer process.

Business Transition Risk

The pivot toward a production- and IP-centered business model driven by the new management is still at an early stage, and concrete outcomes such as a large-scale IP production contract have not yet been confirmed through official disclosure.

Whether new businesses can offset the ongoing structural contraction of the legacy Japan-focused distribution business remains a task to be verified through future results.

Overall view

Copus Korea underwent a major change in 2026 as its controlling shareholder changed for the first time in 20 years, with a new consortium centered on Artist Studio, an affiliate connected to actor Lee Jung-jae, injecting a combined KRW 28.2 billion to complete the capital raise and new share listing by August-September.

Financially, after swinging from a profit in 2023 to large losses in 2024-2025, some improvement signals have emerged, with net loss attributable to owners narrowing sharply over the past two quarters.

On the other hand, the core Japan-focused content distribution business remains exposed to structural pressure from global OTT platforms eroding local markets and reduced domestic drama slates, and 2026Q2 revenue slowed again from the prior quarter.

In addition, the tightened Kosdaq market-cap listing-maintenance threshold effective from July 2026, the repeated failure of earlier control-sale attempts, and ongoing legal disputes over the new share and CB issuances remain variables to monitor.

Whether the new management's stated plan to build a production- and IP-centered value chain and pursue large-scale IP contracts translates into actual results, and whether the company continues to clear the market-cap requirement, are likely to be the key observation points going forward.
