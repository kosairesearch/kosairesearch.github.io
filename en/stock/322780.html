Report overview

Revenue Decline and Losses, Amid Recapitalization and Group Realignment

Copus Korea has posted operating losses for five straight quarters through Q3 2026 and revenue has fallen for three consecutive quarters, while a Q3 capital raise and a change in its controlling shareholder are reshaping its funding base and group content value chain.

02

Business structure

Copus Korea is a content distribution and production company founded in 2005 that listed on KOSDAQ in 2020 through a merger with a special purpose acquisition company.

Its core model is producing dramas and films in-house or securing finished titles, then supplying them to domestic and overseas broadcasters and platforms.

In 2022 it acquired a production company to broaden its production capacity, and among its four subsidiaries is a Japanese affiliate, which makes overseas distribution a second pillar. Its hit dramas and a Netflix original co-production point to a track record in production.

In 2026 the company said it was supplying a new romance title to ABEMA in Japan and focusing on revenue growth by combining Japanese distribution networks with its own production capability.

The company has been regarded as strong in distribution and sales, and its core structure is resale of in-house titles, first aired on general programming channels, to multiple domestic and foreign operators.

Compared with larger listed players such as CJ ENM and Studio Dragon, its production scale is smaller and it has relied more heavily on external funding. The Q3 2026 capital raise has temporarily strengthened its financial capacity.

04

Earnings analysis

On a disclosed annual basis, revenue rose from KRW 29.3 billion in 2022 to KRW 44.7 billion in 2023, up 52.6%, making 2023 the largest year of 2022 to 2025. Revenue then fell 6.3% to KRW 41.8 billion in 2024 and 21.4% to KRW 32.9 billion in 2025, a two-year decline from 2024 through 2025.

Operating profit was KRW 9.12 billion in 2022 and KRW 1.7 billion in 2023, down 81.4%, before the company swung to an operating loss of KRW 29.4 billion in 2024. The 2025 operating loss of KRW 7.09 billion was smaller, but 2024 and 2025 are two consecutive loss-making years.

The operating margin moved from 31.2% in 2022 to 3.8% in 2023, -70.2% in 2024 and -21.6% in 2025. In 2024 cost of sales exceeded revenue, leaving gross profit negative, a result that reflects cost pressure combined with the revenue decline.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders went from a KRW 1.43 billion loss in 2022 to a KRW 3.3 billion profit in 2023, then to a KRW 25 billion loss in 2024 and a KRW 13.4 billion loss in 2025.

Net income including non-controlling interests is a different figure: KRW 3.18 billion in 2023, a KRW 25.1 billion loss in 2024 and a KRW 13.6 billion loss in 2025. Operating cash flow was KRW 35.4 billion in 2023, well above that year's profit, then shrank to -KRW 577 million in 2024 and KRW 661 million in 2025.

Quarterly revenue peaked at KRW 14.6 billion in Q4 2025 and then declined for three straight quarters to KRW 10.8 billion, KRW 7.18 billion and KRW 1.99 billion in Q3 2026, down 83.2% from a year earlier.

Operating losses have run for five straight quarters since Q3 2025, and the Q3 2026 loss of KRW 3.68 billion widened from the prior quarter. The company attributed higher Q3 selling and administrative expenses partly to a one-time fee tied to its capital raise.

Net losses attributable to controlling shareholders for the last four quarters totaled KRW 7.99 billion. At the end of 2025, total equity was KRW 25.4 billion and total liabilities were KRW 41.5 billion, with the debt ratio easing from 194.7% in 2022 to 163.1% in 2025.

05

Industry analysis

The drama production and distribution industry generates revenue title by title, so quarterly results can swing sharply with broadcast scheduling and delivery timing. Producers typically air in-house titles domestically while selling to overseas platforms, and recover returns by reselling one IP to several channels.

Recently, demand for original co-productions from global OTT platforms has become an important variable, and Copus Korea has a Netflix original co-production track record. Larger peers such as CJ ENM and Studio Dragon are ahead in production scale and overseas networks.

Copus Korea is a smaller distribution and sales-focused operator that is trying to shift its position by securing production IP within its group. Japan is the market where the company is concentrating its distribution expansion, and its local affiliate and platform supply record form the base of overseas revenue.

The cycle position is better read from the company's own quarterly revenue than from industry-wide indicators, and revenue has declined for three straight quarters since the Q4 2025 peak.

Smaller distributors can face a weaker funding structure, so the pace at which new capital is deployed into production may shape the company's competitive position.

06

Outlook

In its Q3 2026 results announcement on November 13, 2026, the company said it would expand supply of proven IP and new content, deepen Japanese local collaboration and strengthen growth and global distribution competitiveness.

Linking the content production and distribution capabilities within its group was presented as the core axis. The company said it is pursuing content planning and distribution discussions with MBN, along with co-production work with Artist Studio's successor studio.

Because the controlling studio now belongs to a group that also holds MBN and drama IP, the company described a value chain spanning planning, production, domestic broadcast scheduling and overseas distribution.

The Q3 capital raise, which lifted cash and cash equivalents to KRW 20.8 billion, also gives it room to fund new productions. On costs, the company said it has pursued organizational restructuring and cost efficiency since its management change, and that personnel costs fell 5% from a year earlier.

However, the Q3 2026 operating loss of KRW 3.68 billion widened from the second quarter, so how quickly efficiency gains reach the bottom line remains an open task.

Because the delivery timing of newly scheduled titles drives quarterly revenue, the Q4 2026 and 2027 quarterly reports will show whether collaboration results reach revenue. Revenue contribution from overseas new releases, such as the title supplied to ABEMA in Japan, is another point to track.

08

Bull factors

Recapitalization expands financial room

The company said a Q3 2026 capital raise lifted cash and cash equivalents to KRW 20.8 billion and raised total equity by 22% from the start of the year. Against year-end 2025 liabilities of KRW 41.5 billion, this creates room to reduce reliance on borrowing.

Operating cash flow also turned to a KRW 661 million inflow in 2025, providing a minimal base of cash generation.

Group value chain integration under way

The controlling studio now belongs to the same group as MBN and an affiliate holding IP for a major drama. The company said this creates a value chain linking original IP planning, production, domestic broadcast scheduling and overseas distribution. Content planning and distribution talks with MBN and co-production with the studio are also under way.

Overseas platforms and Japanese distribution

The company has a Netflix original co-production record and a distribution network centered on its Japanese affiliate among its four subsidiaries. In 2026 it supplied a new title to ABEMA in Japan and said it would expand local collaboration.

The 2025 operating loss of KRW 7.09 billion was much smaller than the KRW 29.4 billion loss in 2024, so cost-structure efforts appear partly reflected in annual results.

Bear factors

Quarterly revenue decline and continued losses

Quarterly revenue fell from KRW 14.6 billion in Q4 2025 to KRW 1.99 billion in Q3 2026, down 83.2% from a year earlier. The operating loss of KRW 3.68 billion in Q3 2026 extends a five-quarter run of losses and is larger than in the prior quarter.

Listing-eligibility disclosure

On November 13, 2026, the company filed a KOSDAQ market notice after its separate-basis Q3 revenue was KRW 142.8 million, below KRW 300 million. Consolidated Q3 revenue was KRW 1.99 billion, so the two bases differ sharply. The outcome of the substantive review could change trading conditions and disclosure risk.

Governance and funding volatility

Reports in early February 2026 said a share sale agreement with the then-largest shareholder was canceled, and the planned rights offering and convertible bonds were withdrawn with it. A June 2026 filing then described a consortium led by the studio moving to become the largest shareholder.

Because an earlier acquisition fell through, the market remains cautious about further changes to the transaction structure.

Risk factors

Listing eligibility and disclosure risk

The most direct risk is the substantive review that follows the separate-basis Q3 revenue shortfall and the market notice. Reports on the earlier failed acquisition raised concerns about penalty points tied to withdrawn funding plans, so disclosure risks may overlap. Trading conditions could change depending on the review result and any sanctions that follow.

Delayed earnings recovery and scheduling dependence

Revenue has declined for three straight quarters and operating losses have persisted for five. Quarterly swings are large because results depend on title delivery and scheduling, which makes earnings hard to forecast.

With the Q3 2026 operating loss widening, the pace at which cost efficiency shows up in profit needs to be checked in the next regular report.

Balance sheet and use of funds

The debt ratio stood at 163.1% at the end of 2025, with total liabilities of KRW 41.5 billion. The Q3 capital raise increased cash, but continued losses could speed up its use. How the new funds are allocated between content production and the Japanese business is another key variable.

12

Overall view

Copus Korea's revenue fell for two straight years after peaking at KRW 44.7 billion in 2023, and it recorded operating losses in both 2024 and 2025. Quarterly revenue has declined for three straight quarters in 2026, and operating losses have run for five straight quarters.

On the other hand, the 2025 operating loss narrowed sharply from 2024, and operating cash flow turned from a loss in 2024 to an inflow in 2025.

The Q3 2026 capital raise lifted cash and cash equivalents to KRW 20.8 billion, and a value chain linking the group's drama IP with scheduling and overseas distribution has been presented as a new axis.

On the weaker side, a listing-eligibility review tied to the separate-basis Q3 revenue shortfall, a failed acquisition and withdrawn funding in early 2026, and a debt ratio of 163.1% all weigh on the outlook.

Strengths and weaknesses coexist, and the most direct checkpoint on earnings is the full-year revenue and operating results in the 2026 annual report due by the end of March 2027. On valuation, the facts are that the PBR sits below 1x and the PER cannot be calculated.
