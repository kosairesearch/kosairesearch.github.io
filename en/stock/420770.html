02

Business structure

GigaVis, founded in 2004, makes semiconductor equipment. Its automated optical inspection (AOI) tools find circuit defects on semiconductor substrates, and its automated optical repair (AOR) tools fix them with lasers.

Its core market is FC-BGA substrates used in AI servers and high-performance computing, and its integrated solution links inspection (AOI), verification (VRS) and repair (AOR) into one process.

According to BusinessPost, AOI equipment was the largest revenue item in 2025, and the company is expanding in-line automated systems that connect AOI, AOR and VRS on one production line. Its customers reportedly include Ibiden and Shinko Electric in Japan, Unimicron in Taiwan, and Samsung Electro-Mechanics in Korea.

In a February 2026 analysis, KB Securities named KLA of the United States, Camtek of Israel and Inspec of Japan as main competitors. The business is also moving beyond FC-BGA into next-generation packaging such as WLP, PLP and glass substrates.

The company launched an AOI tool for the redistribution layer (RDL) in WLP in 2024 and is pursuing supply of PLP inspection equipment to global chipmakers and outsourced semiconductor assembly and test (OSAT) firms.

Its business is tied to semiconductor cycles and customers' capital expenditure, so order volumes shift with the pace of investment. In 2026 it signed several large supply contracts with domestic and overseas substrate makers, widening its order base.

04

Earnings analysis

On a disclosed annual basis, revenue fell from KRW 99.7 billion in 2022 to KRW 91.4 billion in 2023, down 8.3% year on year, and then to KRW 26.1 billion in 2024, down 71.4%, which was the smallest year in the 2022 to 2025 span. Revenue recovered to KRW 52.4 billion in 2025, up 100.6%.

According to BusinessPost, the 2024 decline reflected customers delaying investment because of US-China tensions and some contract cancellations.

Operating profit was KRW 33.8 billion in 2022 (operating margin 33.9%) and KRW 35 billion in 2023 (38.3%), then turned into an operating loss of KRW 1.79 billion in 2024 (margin -6.8%). In 2025 operating profit of KRW 12.1 billion (margin 23.1%) returned the company to profit.

Profit attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 27.8 billion in 2022 and KRW 32.7 billion in 2023, the largest year in the span, then KRW 3.41 billion in 2024, down 89.6%, and KRW 15.5 billion in 2025, up 352.9%. Net income including non-controlling interests was KRW 15.5 billion in 2025.

Operating cash flow was KRW 35.8 billion in 2022, KRW 13.1 billion in 2023, KRW 2.99 billion in 2024 and KRW 25.7 billion in 2025. At the end of 2025, total equity was KRW 211 billion, all attributable to controlling shareholders, and total liabilities were KRW 28.3 billion.

Quarterly, revenue rose to KRW 31.7 billion in the fourth quarter of 2025, up 338.8% from the prior quarter, with operating profit of KRW 11.1 billion. It then fell to KRW 5.99 billion in the first quarter of 2026, down 81.1%, with an operating loss of KRW 971 million.

Revenue then rose to KRW 22.3 billion in the second quarter and KRW 26.9 billion in the third, marking two consecutive quarters of growth, with third-quarter operating profit of KRW 8.95 billion. Profit attributable to controlling shareholders over the latest four quarters totaled KRW 21.6 billion.

According to Daily Yonhap, cash and cash equivalents at the end of 2025 were KRW 39.78 billion, up 97.4% from the prior year.

05

Industry analysis

GigaVis's results are tied to the capital expenditure cycle of FC-BGA substrate makers. In February 2026, KB Securities said key customers Ibiden and Zhen Ding had raised their 2026 capital expenditure targets by 70%, and it expected expansion across substrate makers to continue.

In a June 2026 report, Meritz Securities judged that the current ABF substrate investment cycle would be considerably larger and more durable than past upcycles.

Looking at where the cycle stands, BusinessPost described 2024 as a year when customer investment delays hurt results, and demand for FC-BGA inspection and repair equipment rising alongside AI adoption and chip demand from 2025.

Competitors named in the coverage include KLA, Camtek and Inspec, and GigaVis differentiates itself by offering inspection and repair in one integrated solution. As substrate manufacturing gets more demanding, yield control becomes more important, which supports demand for inspection and repair tools.

In next-generation packaging, the industry sees glass substrate commercialization around 2028, so revenue from that segment remains a medium-term task. On the other side, the business is tied to customer capex, so a slowdown in AI investment could cut orders quickly.

GigaVis's position therefore combines technology leadership in FC-BGA inspection and repair with high sensitivity to customers' investment cycles.

06

Outlook

New orders disclosed for July through September 2026 totaled KRW 116.84 billion. In a September 30, 2026 report, Meritz Securities put the order backlog at that point at KRW 182.1 billion.

Contracts announced later added a KRW 46.94 billion domestic order on October 23, 2026 and a KRW 30.59 billion order from a Chinese substrate maker disclosed on October 28, 2026. Together those two contracts total about KRW 77.5 billion, roughly 1.5 times 2025 revenue.

Equipment under the domestic contract is scheduled for delivery by the end of December 2027, and the Chinese contract runs until October 30, 2027. The Chinese customer has not been disclosed at its request, but the company said it has fulfilled similar contracts with the same counterparty over the past three years.

On the capacity side, a ceremony for the new Dongtan headquarters building was held on September 11, 2026. As of BusinessPost's report, the company was building a new production base in Dongtan Industrial Complex with about KRW 50 billion of investment, targeting completion within 2026.

Industry observers expect annual production capacity to rise from about KRW 100 billion to around KRW 250 billion. In a November 1, 2026 report, iM Securities forecast 2026 revenue of KRW 97.1 billion, up 86% from the prior year, and operating profit of KRW 31.6 billion.

That is the brokerage's estimate, and reaching it would require a fourth quarter with substantial profit after the first three quarters disclosed so far. On the product side, PLP inspection equipment supply and glass substrate readiness are cited as the next growth axes.

Risk factors

Customer concentration and geopolitical risk

Key customers are global substrate makers in Japan and Taiwan, so overseas investment decisions, exchange rates and US-China technology tensions could shift the timing of orders.

BusinessPost reported that the 2024 revenue decline stemmed from customer investment delays and contract cancellations tied to those tensions. The Chinese counterparty is undisclosed, which makes it hard to see the customer mix in detail.

Technology shifts and competition

In FC-BGA inspection, GigaVis competes with KLA, Camtek and Inspec. Next-generation packaging such as PLP, WLP and glass substrates offers expansion potential, but commercialization timing is uncertain. The industry sees glass substrate commercialization around 2028, so revenue from that segment is a medium-term task.

Expansion execution and fixed-cost burden

About KRW 50 billion is being invested in a new production base in the Dongtan Industrial Complex. If operations start late or utilization is low, fixed-cost pressure could rise. The capacity gains industry observers expect are not yet reflected in disclosed results. How fast revenue follows the expansion will determine whether the recovery is sustained.

Overall view

GigaVis makes inspection and repair equipment for FC-BGA substrates. After an operating loss in 2024 and a return to profit in 2025, quarterly revenue has risen for two consecutive quarters through the third quarter of 2026.

On a disclosed basis, 2025 revenue was KRW 52.4 billion, operating profit KRW 12.1 billion and profit attributable to controlling shareholders KRW 15.5 billion, and total equity at the end of 2025 was KRW 211 billion.

New orders disclosed for the third quarter of 2026 totaled KRW 116.84 billion, and about KRW 77.5 billion of supply contracts were added in October, widening the order base.

Strengths lie in demand for AI server substrates and an integrated inspection and repair solution, while customers' capex cycle and progress in next-generation packaging are medium-term variables.

On the other side, quarterly revenue swings with shipment timing, as the first quarter of 2026 showed with an operating loss of KRW 971 million, and concentration among customers and any slowdown in investment leave structural risk of lower orders.

The brokerage's 2026 operating profit estimate of KRW 31.6 billion leans heavily on fourth-quarter results, so the gap versus actual results needs to be checked.
