02

Business structure

Daea TI develops, supplies and maintains railway and urban transit signaling control systems, operating through seven subsidiaries. Its segments are railway signaling (SIG), software (M&S) and transmission equipment (I&C).

In 2024, the railway signaling segment generated KRW 127.8 billion, or 85.7% of total sales, while M&S contributed KRW 9.9 billion and I&C KRW 11.2 billion. Key products include the Korean train control system KTCS-2, the radio-based KTCS-M and centralized traffic control (CTC) equipment.

Overseas sales were about KRW 5.9 billion in 2024, roughly 4% of the total, with projects in Egypt, the Philippines and Indonesia. In 2023 the company won a roughly KRW 156.8 billion contract for the second rail traffic control center in Osong.

Its customers are mainly domestic rail operators and urban transit authorities. Because revenue depends on large project awards and delivery schedules, its model differs from platform-driven peers such as NAVER and Samsung SDS.

A premium yogurt manufacturing and distribution business, started in the first half of 2025, sits apart from the rail core.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual sales were KRW 105.5 billion in 2022, KRW 112.3 billion in 2023 (up 6.4%), KRW 149.1 billion in 2024 (up 32.8%) and KRW 153.2 billion in 2025 (up 2.7%), with three consecutive years of growth from 2023. The 2024 jump was attributed to higher order intake.

Operating profit fell from KRW 9.4 billion in 2022 to KRW 3.56 billion in 2023 (down 62.1%), recovered to KRW 7.91 billion in 2024 (up 122.1%) and was KRW 7.56 billion in 2025 (down 4.4%). The 2024 recovery was attributed to a larger share of higher-value rail signaling work and cost management.

Operating margin was 8.9% in 2022, 3.2% in 2023, 5.3% in 2024 and 4.9% in 2025, so the 2022 level was not regained even as sales grew. Profit attributable to owners rose from KRW 8.42 billion in 2022 to KRW 10.7 billion, KRW 14.7 billion and KRW 20.5 billion in 2025, with three consecutive years of growth from 2023.

The 2025 increase was attributed mainly to higher gains on financial instruments held by subsidiaries, which lifted profit even as operating profit fell. Net profit including non-controlling interests was KRW 20.4 billion in 2025, a different figure from profit attributable to owners.

Quarterly profit attributable to owners peaked at KRW 6.33 billion in 2025 Q4, then fell to KRW 2.06 billion in 2026 Q1, KRW 1.93 billion in 2026 Q2 and KRW 226 million in 2026 Q3, three consecutive quarterly declines.

Sales in 2026 Q3 were KRW 35.8 billion, up 18.3% year on year, while operating profit that quarter was KRW 1.65 billion. Profit attributable to owners over the last four quarters totaled KRW 10.5 billion.

Operating cash flow went from KRW 48.6 billion in 2024 to -KRW 1.51 billion in 2025, and the debt ratio rose from 81.6% in 2022 to 118.4% in 2025.

05

Industry analysis

The rail signaling market is in a transition as the national rail network's train control standard moves to KTCS-2. Industry experts expect Daea TI, which has already completed field demonstrations, to be the biggest beneficiary of the nationwide network expansion.

The company won the KTCS-2 installation for the Gyeongbu high-speed line and the Seoul Line 5 signaling upgrade, and the shift of urban transit to CBTC is seen as a further tailwind for resignaling work.

A KRW 54.1 billion KTCS-2 installation spread across four lots was presented as a project to localize high-speed rail train control. The order backlog rose from KRW 250.3 billion in 2022 to KRW 435.9 billion in 2023 and KRW 501.1 billion in 2024.

Cumulative contracts totaled KRW 993.4 billion, of which KRW 492.2 billion had been delivered. The backlog figures are as of end-2024, so later backlog levels must be checked in the 2026 annual report. Overseas, the company is pursuing urban transit projects where KTCS-M could be applied.

Rail signaling is awarded as large individual projects, so its cycle differs from advertising- or platform-driven IT and software peers. Client budgets and work schedules determine when revenue is recognized, which makes quarterly results volatile.

Outlook

No annual guidance appears in the disclosure record reviewed, so the outlook rests on project schedules and disclosed contract periods. Sales in the first half of 2026 fell as signaling projects moved through their schedules, and sales recovered to KRW 35.8 billion in 2026 Q3, though profit has not yet followed.

The Busan urban transit train control contract runs through November 30, 2026, so how completion and settlement appear in the fourth-quarter results is a checkpoint. A project to integrate control systems across Seoul urban transit Lines 1 through 9 was reported with a December 2026 completion target.

The second rail traffic control center was reported with a June 2027 completion target, while another report cited a 2028 completion target and about 35% progress, so the completion timing should be checked against the company's project disclosures. In March 2026 the company decided on a KRW 1 billion share buyback.

At the March 2026 general meeting a third co-CEO, Jang Il-su, was added, and the company said the change was meant to improve management efficiency. In the yogurt business the company plans to expand its product lineup and enter online distribution. The next checkpoint is the 2026 annual report.

08

Bull factors

Revenue visibility from the order backlog

At end-2024, KRW 492.2 billion of cumulative contracts had been delivered, and the remaining backlog gives sales a base while large projects continue.

Potential beneficiary of the KTCS-2 nationwide rollout

As the national rail standard shifts to KTCS-2, the fact that Daea TI has already completed demonstrations is cited as a basis for its participation in the nationwide rollout.

The Gyeongbu high-speed line and Seoul Line 5 awards align with this shift, and the high-speed localization project is a key pillar of its participation.

Room from control-center integration and overseas expansion

A Seoul urban transit control integration project was reported with a December 2026 completion target. Overseas sales were about KRW 5.9 billion in 2024, around 4% of the total, and the company's overseas footprint is widening.

09

Bear factors

First-half 2026 declines

For the first half of 2026, consolidated sales fell 24.5% year on year, operating profit fell 50.1% and net profit fell 60.7%, according to a compilation dated September 2026. Railway work dominates revenue, so sales moved with project schedules. Sales bottomed at KRW 28.6 billion in 2026 Q1, and the third-quarter recovery did not carry through to profit.

Sharp drop in profit attributable to owners

Profit attributable to owners in 2026 Q3 was KRW 226 million, down 88.3% from the prior quarter and 94.3% year on year, while operating profit that quarter was KRW 1.65 billion. The gap between operating and net profit is wide.

Profit attributable to owners over the last four quarters totaled KRW 10.5 billion, with three straight declines since the 2025 Q4 peak.

Thinner margins and financial load

The operating margin fell from 8.9% in 2022 to 4.9% in 2025, and the debt ratio rose from 81.6% to 118.4%. Operating cash flow dropped from KRW 48.6 billion in 2024 to -KRW 1.51 billion in 2025. Because 2025 profit was helped by financial instrument gains, the core business earnings base needs separate checking.

Risk factors

Project timing and concentration

Railway signaling accounted for 85.7% of 2024 sales, so the business is concentrated in one segment. Delays or shifts in tender timing can move quarterly sales sharply. Sales fell 36.1% from the prior quarter in 2026 Q1, an example of how project timing feeds directly into quarterly results.

Balance sheet and cash flow

Total liabilities at end-2025 were KRW 182.4 billion, above total equity of KRW 154 billion, and the debt ratio stood at 118.4%. Operating cash flow was -KRW 1.51 billion in 2025 after KRW 48.6 billion in 2024. Given the swings, working capital and collection timing on construction payments matter.

New businesses and governance

The premium yogurt business began in the first half of 2025 and is far from the rail core, so resource allocation and management scope could become issues. A third co-CEO was added to the existing two. A related F&B brand is reported to be led by the chairman's son.

12

Overall view

Daea TI's disclosed results show a structure driven by delivery of railway signaling projects. From 2022 to 2025, sales rose from KRW 105.5 billion to KRW 153.2 billion, and profit attributable to owners rose from KRW 8.42 billion to KRW 20.5 billion.

The operating margin, however, fell from 8.9% in 2022 to 4.9% in 2025, and gains on financial instruments helped 2025 profit. On the strength side, the KRW 501.1 billion backlog at end-2024, the KTCS-2 nationwide rollout and the control integration projects support the revenue base.

On the weakness side, sales and profit fell in the first half of 2026, and profit attributable to owners dropped to KRW 226 million in 2026 Q3, so quarterly earnings are volatile. The debt ratio of 118.4% and operating cash flow of -KRW 1.51 billion in 2025 are financial items to watch.

The next checkpoints are the December 2026 control integration target, the full-year results and cash flow in the 2026 annual report, and the completion schedule for the second control center.

Because project timing sets the timing of results, the backlog and delivery progress are the reference for reading quarterly numbers.
