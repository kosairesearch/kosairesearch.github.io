Portfolio Expands to KRW1.25tn, Dilution in Focus

02

Business structure

Samsung FN REIT is a trust-type real estate investment company managed by Samsung SRA Asset Management, with Samsung Life Insurance and Samsung Fire & Marine Insurance serving as sponsors, investing in office assets located in core Korean business districts.

Its portfolio comprises six office buildings: FN Tower Daechi and S-1 Tower in Gangnam and downtown Seoul, FN Tower Sunhwa, FN Tower Pangyo in Seongnam, FN Tower Jamsil in Songpa, and A-One Tower Dangsan in Yeongdeungpo, whose acquisition was closed in August 2026.

Major tenants include group affiliates such as Samsung Life, Samsung Fire & Marine, S-1 Corporation, and Hanwha Systems, alongside outside tenants such as Woowa Brothers, with portfolio-wide occupancy reported in the high-90% range approaching full occupancy.

Samsung SRA Asset Management judged that the A-One Tower Dangsan asset fits the REIT's identity because its main tenants are Samsung financial affiliates, with Samsung Life Insurance Service, Samsung Life, Samsung Fire & Marine, and Samsung Card occupying 87.3% of the building.

Samsung SRA Asset Management serves as the asset manager, and on the funding side the company has relied on mortgage loans from affiliates such as Samsung Life and Samsung Fire & Marine, while also entering the public corporate bond market for the first time in 2026.

Competitively, it vies for asset acquisitions with other listed office and diversified REITs such as Lotte REIT, SK REIT, Hanwha REIT, and Shinhan Alpha REIT, and has recently broadened its focus beyond group-affiliated properties to prime offices outside the Samsung group.

Its business model centers on generating stable cash flow from rental income and distributing quarterly dividends, with acquisition funding drawn from a mix of rights offerings, corporate bonds, and mortgage loans.

04

Earnings analysis

Samsung FN REIT's consolidated revenue was nearly flat from KRW9,283 million in 2022 to KRW9,278 million in 2023, then rose steadily to KRW10,191 million in 2024 and KRW12,236 million in 2025.

The operating margin moved within a range, from 67.9% in 2022 to 60.5% in both 2023 and 2025 and 58.2% in 2024, reflecting the inherently high-margin nature of rental operations.

Owners' net income fell from KRW1,139 million in 2022 to KRW458 million in 2023 and KRW361 million in 2024, before rebounding sharply to KRW3,068 million in 2025, a pattern consistent with revenue growth accompanied by an improved cost structure.

Basic EPS followed the same pattern as owners' net income, declining through the downturn before rising sharply in line with the 2025 profit rebound.

Operating cash flow eased from KRW2,610 million in 2022 to KRW2,186 million in 2023, then expanded to KRW5,221 million in 2024 and KRW4,569 million in 2025, indicating an overall strengthening of rental-income-based cash generation.

Owners' equity dipped slightly from KRW378,252 million in 2022 to KRW373,271 million in 2023 before rising to KRW419,450 million in 2024 and KRW406,546 million in 2025, reflecting capital changes tied to asset acquisitions.

Total liabilities grew from KRW368,449 million in 2022 to KRW461,533 million in 2025, pushing the debt ratio up from 97.4% in 2022 to 113.5% in 2025, which shows that expanded borrowing for new asset acquisitions is being reflected in the balance sheet.

Industry analysis

Korea's listed REIT sector was cautious about new asset acquisitions through 2024-2025 amid high interest rates, but in 2026 transactions have resumed centered on quality offices and hotels, with REITs re-emerging as key buyers in the market.

REITs, which had been passive about acquiring new assets due to high interest rates through last year, are resuming investment centered on quality offices and hotels and emerging as key buyers in the transaction market.

At the same time, there is a growing effort across the industry to move away from the practice of funding acquisitions through rights offerings, which has repeatedly caused dilution and share price declines.

The industry theme of diversifying away from heavy reliance on rights offerings toward corporate bonds and other funding methods has gained prominence.

Samsung FN REIT joined this trend in 2026 by entering the public corporate bond market for the first time to fund the Jamsil building acquisition, reducing its reliance on rights offerings.

In the second half, portfolio rebalancing combining new asset acquisitions with divestitures is becoming more pronounced across the industry, and rather than acquiring large physical assets outright, REITs are expected to actively pursue smaller add-on acquisitions relative to existing holdings or joint-investment and preferred-equity structures.

However, funding costs remain a burden in a floating-rate environment, making prevailing market interest rates at the point of refinancing a key variable differentiating dividend capacity among individual REITs.

Risk factors

Interest Rate Risk

Some floating-rate borrowings, including mortgage loans and electronic short-term bonds, remain outstanding, meaning market rate movements can flow directly into interest expense. Refinancing terms for the KRW430 billion mortgage loan that matured in September are a key variable determining future funding costs.

The company is pursuing a higher share of fixed-rate debt, but the pace of transition depends on market conditions.

Capital Markets Funding Risk

Additional asset acquisitions may require repeated rights offerings or bond issuances. Frequent rights offerings could further dilute per-share metrics and existing shareholders' stakes. If capital market conditions deteriorate, uncertainty could arise regarding funding costs or full subscription.

Tenant and Asset Concentration Risk

Because major tenants across several assets are concentrated among Samsung financial affiliates, changes in that group's business strategy could affect leasing stability.

The newly acquired A-One Tower Dangsan is located in a non-core business district rather than a prime office area, which may carry different sensitivity to leasing market conditions. With a small number of assets in the portfolio, diversification benefits against individual asset risk are limited.

Overall view

Samsung FN REIT, backed by high-quality sponsors Samsung Life and Samsung Fire & Marine, expanded its asset base to roughly KRW1.25 trillion with the 2026 addition of A-One Tower Dangsan, and owners' net income rebounded sharply from KRW361 million in 2024 to KRW3,068 million in 2025.

However, this expansion has come with a rising debt ratio (from 97.4% in 2022 to 113.5% in 2025) and a 19-million-share rights offering decided in August 2026, with the final issue price due on October 29.

The company has shown a shift toward corporate bond issuance over rights offerings for funding, and dividend guidance has reportedly been raised to KRW69 per quarter.

On the other hand, the portfolio remains limited to six assets with high dependence on specific Samsung affiliate tenants, leaving concentration risk in place, while the share of floating-rate borrowing and refinancing terms remain variables to monitor.

Investors may wish to track the finalization of the rights offering price in late October, upcoming quarterly earnings disclosures, and the funding method chosen for any future asset acquisitions.
