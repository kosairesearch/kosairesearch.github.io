DYA: Sales Growth Meets the Profit Recovery Test

DYA's first-half 2026 revenue rose 47.3%, but its 2025 operating margin fell to 4.8% and its debt ratio stood at 744.7%, so whether sales growth converts into profit and a stronger balance sheet is the key open question.

  1. 1

  2. 2

  3. 3

  4. 4

02

Business structure

DYA was founded in 1960 as an automotive parts maker and entered the vehicle parts market in 2010 through a business transfer. Finished vehicle seats account for 99.99% of its main product sales mix, while rental income accounts for 0.01%.

The company produces seats, foam pads, and headrests and armrests across its Gwangju, Hwaseong, Injoo, and Ulsan production bases.

The Gwangju site supplies seats for Kia's Sportage, Seltos, EV5 and Bongo truck, and Casper seats; the Hwaseong site supplies seats for KG Mobility's Rexton and Musso Sports and for Kia's K9, EV9 and PV5.

Nine-month 2026 sales by site were KRW 517.3 billion at Gwangju, up 28.3% year on year, and KRW 171.1 billion at Hwaseong, up 208.6%. Hwaseong Plant 2 swung to operating profit from an operating loss a year earlier, driven by SP3 production.

Revenue depends on the production volumes of vehicle models at customers such as Hyundai Motor, Kia and KG Mobility. In October 2025 the company was selected as a supplier of finished seats for the PV7 and NQ6, and in June 2025 as a supplier of seat pads for the SX3.

R&D spending was KRW 7.25 billion in 2025, or 1.14% of revenue. The company was renamed from Daeyu Actek to DYA.

04

Earnings analysis

On an annual basis, revenue was KRW 1.4 trillion in 2022, the largest year in the disclosed period, fell 59.6% to KRW 566.9 billion in 2023 and 0.4% to KRW 564.9 billion in 2024, and recovered 12.1% to KRW 633.4 billion in 2025.

Operating results moved from an operating loss of KRW 32 billion in 2022 to operating profit of KRW 36.1 billion in 2023, a turn to profit, then KRW 37.5 billion in 2024 (up 3.8%) and KRW 30.2 billion in 2025 (down 19.4%). Operating margin was -2.3% in 2022, 6.4% in 2023, 6.6% in 2024 and 4.8% in 2025.

According to FnGuide's summary, revenue rose in 2025 but profitability was limited by cost burdens.

Net loss attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 56.5 billion in 2022 and widened to KRW 102.7 billion in 2023, a continued loss; it turned to a profit of KRW 5.18 billion in 2024 and back to a loss of KRW 635 million in 2025.

Net income including non-controlling interests was a loss of KRW 93.8 billion in 2022, a loss of KRW 195.3 billion in 2023, a profit of KRW 4.84 billion in 2024 and a loss of KRW 652 million in 2025. The debt ratio was 476.7% in 2022, 813.2% in 2023, 658.9% in 2024 and 744.7% in 2025.

At the end of 2025, total equity was KRW 46.5 billion, of which equity attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 46.6 billion, and total liabilities were KRW 346.3 billion.

Operating cash flow moved from an outflow of KRW 4.05 billion in 2022 and KRW 26.7 billion in 2023 to inflows of KRW 4.03 billion in 2024 and KRW 80 billion in 2025.

On a quarterly basis, the third quarter of 2025 had revenue of KRW 159.3 billion and operating profit of KRW 5.98 billion; the fourth quarter brought revenue of KRW 173.8 billion (up 9.1% from the prior quarter), but operating profit fell to KRW 3.15 billion (down 47.2%) and a net loss attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 9.6 billion turned it into a loss.

In the first quarter of 2026, revenue was KRW 189.3 billion (up 8.9% from the prior quarter), operating profit was KRW 7.29 billion (up 131.1%), and net profit attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 3.53 billion, a turn to profit. Operating profit for the same quarter fell 32.5% year on year.

First-half 2026 cumulative revenue was KRW 442.3 billion, operating profit KRW 20.8 billion and net profit KRW 18 billion.

Third-quarter 2026 three-month revenue was KRW 246.2 billion (up 54.6%), operating profit KRW 13.6 billion (up 128.2%) and net income KRW 8.7 billion (up 84.8%), per news reports of the quarterly report.

05

Industry analysis

Auto parts demand in Korea tracks vehicle production and sales volumes. A market commentary carried on a Korean financial information site said exports slowed on rising protectionism and higher inventories, while domestic demand is expected to recover gradually as rates stabilize and consumer sentiment improves.

DYA's volumes depend on the new-model production schedules of Hyundai Motor, Kia and KG Mobility, so each customer's model changeover cycle effectively sets DYA's cycle. The current driver is the expansion of volumes for electric vehicles (EVs) and purpose-built vehicles (PBVs).

The early sales momentum of Kia's EV5 and Kia's first dedicated PBV, the PV5, was cited as a factor behind first-half 2026 revenue growth. Conversely, declining sales of existing combustion-engine models could create volume gaps.

DYA is a Tier 1 supplier with direct supply relationships with automakers, delivering seats for multiple models. Measured by market capitalization, it is a small company within the KOSPI transport equipment and parts sector.

In India, DYA is pursuing entry into global supply chains through a joint venture with NDR Auto Components, responding to Hyundai Motor and Kia's expanded production in India.

Because overseas demand depends on automakers' production plans, the timing of the joint venture's operations and its volumes will be key variables.

06

Outlook

The mid-to-long-term targets the company has presented are revenue of KRW 700 billion in 2026, KRW 1.05 trillion in 2027 and KRW 1.2 trillion in 2028. Nine-month 2026 cumulative revenue of KRW 688.5 billion is close to the 2026 target of KRW 700 billion.

The operating margin target is at least 5% by 2028, and the first-half 2026 operating margin of about 4.7% is not far from that level.

Financial targets call for a debt ratio of 300% or below and a borrowings ratio of 50% or below by 2028, and the consolidated debt ratio at the end of September 2026 was 593.1%, lower than at year-end 2025.

The shareholder return target is a dividend payout ratio of 25% in 2028, with a plan to expand returns beyond that level afterward. Production will be reorganized by moving the production functions of Hwaseong Plant 1 and the Asan plant to the Injoo plant, consolidating seat and pad manufacturing.

Construction of the new Injoo plant runs from April 28, 2026 to August 31, 2027, and the company has said the investment amount and period may change during execution. A production base in Ulsan will serve new Hyundai Motor volumes, and the GV80 and GV70 armrest award announced on November 9, 2026 fits this direction.

In India, a joint venture agreement with NDR Auto Components is scheduled for November 17, 2026, with NDR holding 51% and DYA holding 49%. The next checkpoints are whether the 2026 annual revenue target is reached and how quickly the Injoo plant consolidation proceeds.

Balance Sheet Normalization Plan

Customer Concentration and Ramp-Up Risk

Risk factors

Customer and Model Concentration

Revenue is tied to the production plans of Hyundai Motor, Kia and KG Mobility. A slowdown in sales of existing combustion-engine models could leave volume gaps, and delays in ramping up new models would affect revenue targets.

New awards such as the armrest contracts need to be confirmed as turning into revenue at the mass-production stage.

Leverage and Borrowing Burden

The debt ratio was 744.7% at the end of 2025 and reached 813.2% in 2023. The plan to repay borrowings relies on operating cash and asset sale proceeds, so the cash generation of the core business is the key variable. The debt ratio fell to 593.1% at the end of September 2026, but it remains well above the 300% target.

Plant Reorganization and Overseas Expansion

Consolidating Hwaseong Plant 1 and the Asan plant into the Injoo plant is planned for completion in August 2027. If quality or utilization slips during the transfer, the profit recovery could be delayed.

The Indian joint venture is structured with DYA holding 49%, so operating arrangements with the local partner and volume visibility are variables.

Overall view

DYA's revenue and profit rose together in 2026. Nine-month cumulative revenue reached KRW 688.5 billion, up 49.8% year on year, and the first-half operating margin was about 4.7%.

However, annual operating profit fell 19.4% to KRW 30.2 billion in 2025, net income including non-controlling interests turned to a loss, and the debt ratio at the end of 2025 was 744.7%.

On the disclosed annual record, the operating margin ranged from -2.3% in 2022 to 6.6% in 2024, and the net loss attributable to controlling shareholders widened to KRW 102.7 billion in 2023.

Strengths include new-model awards, rising production volumes and a balance sheet improvement plan, while weaknesses include cost pressure, a high debt ratio and the burden of capital spending.

The next checkpoints are whether the 2026 annual revenue target is met, progress on the Injoo plant consolidation and execution of the India joint venture. The earnings path will likely depend on how well new-model ramp-ups stabilize and how costs are managed.
