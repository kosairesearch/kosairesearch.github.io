02

Business structure

DYA, founded in 1960, is an automotive seat maker that entered the auto parts market in 2010 through a business transfer. In 2025, finished vehicle seats accounted for 99.99% of sales, with rental income making up the remaining 0.01%, so the business runs on a single product line.

Its main customers are Hyundai Motor and Kia. The Gwangju seat business supplies Sportage, Seltos, EV5 and Bongo truck seats to Kia's Gwangju plant on an exclusive basis.

In June 2025 DYA was named seat pad supplier for the SX3, the Kona successor, and in October 2025 it was selected as a finished seat supplier for the PV7 (LW) and NQ6, the Sportage successor.

On 4 November 2026 it disclosed its selection as armrest supplier for the Genesis GV80 (JX2) and GV70 (JK2), with supply to Hyundai's Ulsan plant. Production is being reorganized across Gwangju, Hwaseong, Injoo and Ulsan.

The new Injoo plant involves KRW 42.79 billion of investment, with a period running from 28 April 2026 to 31 August 2027. R&D spending was 1.14% of sales in 2025. Largest shareholders and related parties held 45.24% as of 24 August 2026, and the free float ratio was 54.74%.

04

Earnings analysis

On a full-year basis from the periodic reports, sales fell from KRW 1.4 trillion in 2022 to KRW 566.9 billion in 2023 (down 59.6% year on year), dipped to KRW 564.9 billion in 2024 (down 0.4%), and rose to KRW 633.4 billion in 2025 (up 12.1%). Among 2022 to 2025, 2022 was the largest year and 2024 the smallest.

Operating results moved from an operating loss of KRW 32 billion in 2022 to operating profit of KRW 36.1 billion in 2023, a swing to profit, then KRW 37.5 billion in 2024 (up 3.8%) and KRW 30.2 billion in 2025 (down 19.4%).

The operating margin was -2.3% in 2022, 6.4% in 2023, 6.6% in 2024 and 4.8% in 2025, so margin compressed in 2025 even as sales grew.

Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders was a loss of KRW 56.5 billion in 2022 and a loss of KRW 102.7 billion in 2023, with the loss widening, before turning to a profit of KRW 5.18 billion in 2024 and a loss of KRW 635 million in 2025.

Total net income including non-controlling interests is a different figure: a loss of KRW 93.8 billion in 2022, a loss of KRW 195.3 billion in 2023, a profit of KRW 4.84 billion in 2024 and a loss of KRW 652 million in 2025.

In 2023 operating profit turned positive while the controlling-shareholder loss widened to KRW 102.7 billion, so the items that separate operating profit from net results need to be traced in the periodic report notes.

Operating cash flow moved from -KRW 4.05 billion in 2022 and -KRW 26.7 billion in 2023 to KRW 4.03 billion in 2024 and KRW 80 billion in 2025. The debt ratio rose from 476.7% in 2022 to 813.2% in 2023, then eased to 658.9% in 2024 and was 744.7% in 2025, still a high level.

On a quarterly basis, Q4 2025 was the low point, with sales of KRW 173.8 billion, operating profit of KRW 3.15 billion (down 47.2% from the prior quarter) and a controlling-shareholder net loss of KRW 9.6 billion, a swing from profit.

Sales then reached KRW 189.3 billion in Q1 2026, KRW 253 billion in Q2 and KRW 246.2 billion in Q3, with operating profit of KRW 7.29 billion, KRW 13.5 billion and KRW 13.6 billion, so operating profit rose for three consecutive quarters. Q3 operating profit grew only 1.1% from Q2, a slower pace.

Controlling-shareholder net profit peaked at KRW 14.5 billion in Q2 2026 and was KRW 8.73 billion in Q3, and the last four quarters total KRW 17.1 billion. The company reported cumulative January to September 2026 sales of KRW 688.5 billion and operating profit of KRW 34.4 billion in its quarterly report.

05

Industry analysis

Automotive seats are parts whose volume follows finished-vehicle production directly, so DYA's sales move with each customer model's output and the timing of new model launches. The company said on 18 August 2026 that first-half sales growth was led by stronger new-car sales at its key customers and new model ramp-ups.

Specifically, it cited higher sales of the EV5 and Seltos and an early sales boost for Kia's first dedicated PBV, the PV5. A securities firm summary based on the 2025 full-year results described exports as slowed by tighter trade protection and rising inventories, with domestic demand expected to recover gradually.

In November 2026, Hyundai Motor and Kia were expanding production in India, and DYA said it is pursuing a local joint venture in response. As electric vehicles and PBVs emerge as a new source of seat demand, the company says it is expanding new orders centered on these models.

In cycle terms, 2026 is a phase in which new-model volume is driving sales growth. A securities summary of the first quarter of 2026 also noted that seat profitability fell year on year, due to raw material price volatility and higher cost burdens.

Production is also allocated by model, with Gwangju focused on Kia models, Hwaseong on PBV volume and Ulsan on new Hyundai volume. Under this structure, weaker sales of a specific model feed directly into revenue, so customer sales trends are central to reading the industry.

06

Outlook

In its value-up plan of September 2026, the company set targets of KRW 1.2 trillion in sales and an operating margin of at least 5% for 2028.

As interim steps, it projected KRW 700 billion in sales for 2026 and KRW 1.05 trillion for 2027, and cumulative January to September 2026 sales of KRW 688.5 billion are close to the 2026 level. Reports said the first-half 2026 operating margin was about 4.7%, not far from the 5% 2028 target.

The financial targets are a debt ratio of 300% or below and a borrowings ratio of 50% or below by 2028, and the debt ratio at the end of the first half of 2026 was reported at 704.3%.

For shareholder returns, the company targets a dividend payout ratio of 25% by 2028 and plans to expand returns beyond that level afterward. The growth engine is new EV and PBV orders, adding future models while keeping existing internal-combustion volume.

On production, the Hwaseong No. 1 plant and the Asan plant functions are moving to the Injoo plant to consolidate seat and pad output, with first completion starting in October 2026 and integration planned by August 2027. A base in Ulsan will support new Hyundai volume.

The joint venture agreement with NDR Auto Components, completed on 23 November 2026, plans a 51% NDR and 49% DYA stake and targets supply opportunities linked to Hyundai and Kia's India production expansion. Achieving these targets depends on new orders moving into mass production and on production efficiency gains.

08

Bull factors

Quarterly profit recovery

Operating profit rose for three straight quarters, to KRW 7.29 billion in Q1 2026, KRW 13.5 billion in Q2 and KRW 13.6 billion in Q3. Q3 operating profit was up 128.2% year on year. The company said cumulative January to September 2026 sales rose 49.8% to KRW 688.5 billion.

Wider order pipeline

In November 2026 DYA disclosed its selection as armrest supplier for the Genesis GV80 and GV70, after being named a supplier for the PV7, NQ6 and SX3 in 2025. The completed joint venture agreement with NDR creates supply opportunities tied to Hyundai and Kia's India production growth. The company set a KRW 1.2 trillion sales target for 2028.

Balance sheet repair plan

The company targets a debt ratio of 300% or below and a borrowings ratio of 50% or below by 2028. Operating cash flow reached KRW 80 billion in 2025, up from KRW 4.03 billion in 2024. The plan is to repay borrowings with operating cash and proceeds from asset sales.

09

Bear factors

Heavy debt load

Total liabilities at the end of 2025 were KRW 346.3 billion, more than seven times total equity of KRW 46.5 billion. The debt ratio eased from 813.2% in 2023 to 744.7% in 2025 but remains high. A first-half 2026 debt ratio of 704.3% was reported, so the financial burden is still being reduced.

Volatile quarterly earnings

The controlling-shareholder net loss in Q4 2025 was KRW 9.6 billion, and the controlling-shareholder net profit in Q3 2026 was KRW 8.73 billion, down 39.7% from the prior quarter. For full-year 2025, the controlling-shareholder net loss was KRW 635 million and the total net loss was KRW 652 million. Operating profit has risen, but net earnings swing sharply from quarter to quarter.

Cost pressure and customer concentration

Finished vehicle seats account for 99.99% of sales and are concentrated in Hyundai and Kia models, so results depend on volume for specific models. A securities summary said Q1 2026 seat profitability fell year on year on raw material price volatility and higher cost burdens. The 2025 operating margin of 4.8% was below 6.6% in 2024.

10

Risk factors

Sustainability of new-model volume

The 2027 interim sales target of KRW 1.05 trillion rests on the assumption that the new-model volume that drove 2026 growth keeps rising. First-half 2026 growth was supported by higher sales of the EV5 and Seltos and early PV5 sales, so a slowdown in these models could slow the pace of expansion.

Cumulative January to September 2026 sales of KRW 688.5 billion are close to the 2026 target of KRW 700 billion, so if new-model volume levels off, the timing of reaching the targets could slip.

Customer concentration and production transition

Dependence on Hyundai and Kia models is high, and moving functions from the Hwaseong No. 1 and Asan plants to Injoo could disrupt mass-production transitions. Integration runs from first completion in October 2026 through August 2027. A change in customer production strategy would require reallocating volume across sites.

Execution risk in the overseas joint venture

In the planned Indian joint venture, NDR holds 51%, giving it majority ownership. The joint venture agreement is complete, but company formation, local supply contracts and the timing of revenue have not been disclosed.

The company says the India business will build overseas production experience, but that has not yet appeared in reported results.

12

Overall view

DYA's business is concentrated in finished vehicle seats. Sales rose 12.1% to KRW 633.4 billion in 2025, but operating profit fell 19.4% to KRW 30.2 billion and the controlling-shareholder result swung to a KRW 635 million net loss.

In 2026, quarterly sales and operating profit rose together, with Q3 operating profit at KRW 13.6 billion. New model orders and the India joint venture are building the base for sales expansion.

However, the balance sheet, with total liabilities of KRW 346.3 billion against total equity of KRW 46.5 billion, and the debt ratio targets remain works in progress.

Quarterly profit swings are large, and cost pressure and customer dependence are high, so whether quarterly trends carry into full-year results will be confirmed in the periodic reports.

The 2028 targets of KRW 1.2 trillion in sales and an operating margin of at least 5% can be compared with the first-half 2026 operating margin of about 4.7%.

The next checkpoints are the 2026 annual report due by the end of March 2027, the Q1 2027 quarterly report due by 15 May 2027, and the completion of Injoo integration in August 2027.
