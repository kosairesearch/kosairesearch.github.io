Report overview

Seat Maker Rebuilds Scale While Margin Volatility Persists

DYA supplies automotive seats to KIA and KG Mobility, and while quarterly revenue has risen for four straight quarters into 2026, operating margins have swung sharply from quarter to quarter.

  1. 1

    Q2 2026 revenue reached KRW 253 billion, up 33.7% quarter-on-quarter and 65.4% year-on-year.

  2. 2

    2025 operating profit was KRW 30.2 billion, down 19.4% year-on-year, with the operating margin falling to 4.8%.

  3. 3

  4. 4

02

Business structure

Founded in 1960 and converted into an automotive seat manufacturer after a 2010 business transfer, DYA is a KOSPI-listed auto parts company. Its core product is the finished vehicle seat assembly, which accounts for nearly all of its revenue.

The Gwangju seat division exclusively supplies seats for KIA's Gwangju plant models including the Sportage, Seltos, EV5, and Bongo truck, and also supplies Gwangju Global Motors for the Casper.

The Hwaseong seat division supplies KG Mobility with seats for the Rexton and Musso Sports, and supplies KIA's Autoland Gwangmyeong plant with seats for the K9, EV9, Ioniq 9, and PV5.

In 2025, DYA was selected by Hyundai Transys as the seat assembly supplier for the PV7 (LW) and NQ6 (the Sportage successor model), and in June 2025 was selected by Hyundai Motor as the seat pad supplier for the SX3 (the Kona successor model).

Customers are concentrated among KIA, KG Mobility, Hyundai Motor, Hyundai Transys, and Gwangju Global Motors, reflecting heavy reliance on a small group of automakers.

A notable feature of recent order wins is the expanding scope of supply toward dedicated EV and purpose-built vehicle (PBV) models such as the EV5, EV9, PV5, and PV7.

In April 2026, the company decided on a new KRW 42.8 billion facility investment to build an Insu plant, integrating PV7 (LW) seat production with its existing Asan plant.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual revenue fell sharply from KRW 1.4 trillion in 2022 to KRW 566.9 billion in 2023, a 59.6% decline, dipped further to KRW 564.9 billion in 2024 (down 0.4%), then rebounded 12.1% to KRW 633.4 billion in 2025. Among 2022-2025, revenue was largest in 2022 and smallest in 2024.

Operating profit swung from an operating loss of KRW 32 billion in 2022 to an operating profit of KRW 36.1 billion in 2023, rose 3.8% to KRW 37.5 billion in 2024, then fell 19.4% to KRW 30.2 billion in 2025, with the operating margin slipping from 6.6% to 4.8%.

Controlling-interest net losses widened from KRW 56.5 billion in 2022 to KRW 102.7 billion in 2023, turned to a profit of KRW 5.18 billion in 2024, then reversed back to a net loss of KRW 635 million in 2025.

Net income including non-controlling interests followed the same pattern, moving from a profit of KRW 4.84 billion in 2024 to a loss of KRW 652 million in 2025.

On a quarterly basis, revenue rose for four consecutive quarters, from KRW 152.9 billion in Q2 2025 to KRW 159.3 billion in Q3 2025, KRW 173.8 billion in Q4 2025, KRW 189.3 billion in Q1 2026, and KRW 253 billion in Q2 2026, with Q2 2026 up 65.4% year-on-year.

Operating profit, by contrast, fell from KRW 10.3 billion in Q2 2025 to KRW 5.98 billion in Q3 2025 and bottomed at KRW 3.15 billion in Q4 2025, before recovering for two straight quarters to KRW 7.29 billion in Q1 2026 and KRW 13.5 billion in Q2 2026.

Operating cash flow was negative at -KRW 4.05 billion in 2022 and -KRW 26.7 billion in 2023, turned positive at KRW 4.03 billion in 2024, and rose sharply to KRW 80 billion in 2025.

05

Industry analysis

Automotive seat manufacturing is a classic order-driven industry directly linked to automakers' production volumes and new model launch schedules.

DYA's revenue structure depends on the operating rates of a small number of automaker plants, including KIA's Gwangju and Autoland Gwangmyeong facilities, KG Mobility, and Gwangju Global Motors, placing it among the smaller-cap specialized parts suppliers within the KOSPI transportation equipment and parts sector.

As electrification advances, the growing share of dedicated EV models such as the EV5, EV9, and Ioniq 9, along with purpose-built vehicles like the PV5 and PV7, is raising the importance of seat makers' ability to respond to new platforms.

According to the company, the improvement in first-half 2026 results was directly driven by increased production volumes at customers, including expanded KIA new model sales, rising EV5 and Seltos sales, and strong initial sales of the PV5.

At the same time, an assessment was offered that raw material price volatility and rising cost burdens are weighing on profitability. There is also a view that parts suppliers with quality and delivery competitiveness stand to benefit relatively as automakers reorganize production bases and diversify supply chains.

Profitability across the industry tends to vary significantly by company depending on raw material and labor cost burdens and bargaining power with automakers over unit prices.

06

Outlook

In September 2026, DYA disclosed a value-up plan targeting annual revenue of KRW 1.2 trillion and an operating margin above 5% by 2028. The roadmap was described as prioritizing a shift toward profitability, financial soundness, and shareholder value rather than pure top-line expansion.

The company cited diversification toward EV- and PBV-centered portfolios as its core growth driver, stating it plans to gradually expand the application range of seats and pads by adding new orders on top of its stable base of existing mass-production models.

The April 2026 decision to build a new Insu plant with a KRW 42.8 billion investment aims to integrate PV7 (LW) seat production for Hyundai Transys with the existing Asan plant, forming part of the capacity expansion to meet new order volumes.

The company stated it plans to continue both top-line growth and margin improvement in the second half of 2026 by combining stable volumes from existing flagship models with expanded production of new models.

However, how much of this plan translates into actual quarterly results will need to be confirmed through regulatory filings. A September 2026 affiliate merger also altered the shareholding structure among the largest shareholder group, making governance stability another point worth monitoring.

08

Bull factors

Four Straight Quarters of Revenue Growth

Revenue rose for four consecutive quarters from Q3 2025 through Q2 2026, with Q2 2026 up 65.4% year-on-year. This was attributed to expanded new model sales at key customers and increased production volumes of new models such as the EV5, Seltos, and PV5. The revenue recovery has also fed through to a rebound in operating profit starting in Q1 2026.

New Orders Tied to Electrification and PBVs

In 2025, DYA was selected by Hyundai Transys as the seat assembly supplier for the PV7 (LW) and NQ6, and by Hyundai Motor as the seat pad supplier for the SX3.

The expansion of supply targets toward dedicated EV and purpose-built vehicle models can be read as evidence of order competitiveness during the electrification transition. In April 2026, the company also decided on an Insu plant investment to respond to this.

Improving Operating Cash Flow

Operating cash flow turned positive at KRW 4.03 billion in 2024 from -KRW 26.7 billion in 2023, then rose sharply to KRW 80 billion in 2025. Reports indicate that in the first half of 2026, the debt-to-equity ratio declined from 744.7% to 704.3% and the current ratio rose as well. The recovery in cash generation appears to be underpinning the improvement in the financial structure.

09

Bear factors

Margin Volatility and the 2025 Net Loss

The operating margin fell from 6.6% in 2024 to 4.8% in 2025, and the controlling-interest net result swung from a profit in 2024 back to a net loss of KRW 635 million in 2025.

Quarter to quarter, volatility was pronounced as well, with Q4 2025 operating profit falling to KRW 3.15 billion and a controlling-interest net loss of KRW 9.6 billion. Revenue recovery has not consistently translated into stable earnings improvement.

Raw Material Cost Pressure

While revenue in the seat segment expanded on the back of rising automaker production volumes, profitability was assessed to have declined due to raw material price volatility and increased cost burdens. A structural challenge remains in that revenue growth and margin improvement have not occurred simultaneously. This is also part of the backdrop for the company citing cost competitiveness as a key management goal.

High Leverage and Thin Equity Base

At the end of 2025, the debt-to-equity ratio stood at 744.7% and total equity was KRW 46.5 billion, a relatively thin capital base relative to asset and revenue scale. Although the ratio eased somewhat in the first half of 2026, it remains elevated.

A financial structure heavily reliant on external borrowing can be sensitive to changes in interest rates or lending conditions.

Risk factors

Customer Concentration

The bulk of revenue is concentrated among a small number of automakers, including KIA, KG Mobility, Hyundai Motor, and Hyundai Transys. Production disruptions, model discontinuations, or volume adjustments at any single customer could directly affect results. Reliance on specific production hubs such as Gwangju and Hwaseong is also significant.

Governance and Ownership Changes

In September 2026, an absorption merger between affiliates TPC and Youngil-E reshuffled the largest shareholder group's holdings, and in the same month TPC further increased its stake through an off-market purchase.

While the combined stake of the largest shareholder group remained at 45.24%, share movements among individual affiliates have continued. It will be worth monitoring how these ownership structure changes affect control or decision-making going forward.

Raw Material and Currency Exposure

Company commentary confirmed that raw material price volatility for seat production has affected profitability. The structure is such that bargaining power in price negotiations with automakers determines whether cost increases can be passed through. If raw material or currency conditions worsen, margins could face further pressure even amid revenue growth.

Overall view

DYA experienced large losses and a sharp revenue decline from 2022 to 2024, before rebounding in 2025 and showing four consecutive quarters of revenue growth into 2026.

However, the operating margin eased to 4.8% in 2025, and quarterly volatility remained notable, with profit bottoming in Q4 2025 before recovering for two straight quarters in Q1-Q2 2026.

Controlling-interest net income turned profitable in 2024, reverted to a loss in 2025, and has since returned to a profitable trend in 2026.

The company is pursuing a value-up plan targeting KRW 1.2 trillion in revenue and an operating margin above 5% by 2028, alongside new model order wins and production capacity expansion.

Factors warranting continued attention include a debt-to-equity ratio above 700% combined with a thin capital base, revenue concentration among a small number of automaker customers, and the ownership structure changes stemming from a September 2026 affiliate merger.

Going forward, upcoming regulatory filings will be key to confirming whether revenue growth translates into stable earnings improvement and whether the value-up plan is actually implemented.
