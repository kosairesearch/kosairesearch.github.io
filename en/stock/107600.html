02

Business structure

Sebitchem, a recycling specialist founded in 2001 under an earlier name, adopted its current name in 2005 and has since run a business recycling waste acids from semiconductor and display processes.

Its core technology extracts industrial chemicals such as phosphoric, acetic and sodium nitrate compounds from waste acid and supplies them back as base materials for fertilizers, metal surface treatment and chemical materials. The company began battery recycling R&D in 2011 and scaled the business from 2017.

According to company figures for the first half of 2026 (preliminary), semiconductor waste phosphoric acid recycling (AM-X) generated KRW 7.63 billion, the lithium carbonate platform (LiM-X) KRW 7.13 billion and black mass (BM-X) KRW 15.8 billion.

LiM-X grew 79% from KRW 3.98 billion a year earlier, and BM-X expanded sharply from KRW 1.87 billion. The company said it cut its cost ratio by shifting BM-X feedstock from process scrap to spent-battery-based black mass.

Waste acid from semiconductor and display plants is generated in large volumes, which supports feedstock supply. LX Investment acquired the company in early 2025, and the pretreatment capability it brought gives the company a full battery recycling process.

The company cited diversification of its global customer base as a driver of the first-half rebound. Unlike battery-only recyclers, Sebitchem also draws on a non-battery feedstock base in semiconductor waste acid.

04

Earnings analysis

On a disclosed annual basis, revenue fell from KRW 48.1 billion in 2022 to KRW 46 billion in 2023 (down 4.4%) and KRW 30.3 billion in 2024 (down 34.0%), before rising to KRW 38.4 billion in 2025 (up 26.5%). Among 2022 to 2025, 2022 was the largest year and 2024 the smallest.

Operating profit of KRW 10.2 billion in 2022 turned into an operating loss of KRW 4.9 billion in 2023, and the loss then widened to KRW 6.16 billion in 2024 and KRW 9.42 billion in 2025. The operating margin fell from 21.2% in 2022 to -10.7% in 2023, -20.3% in 2024 and -24.5% in 2025.

Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders moved from KRW 7.7 billion in 2022 to net losses of KRW 1.17 billion in 2023, KRW 10.7 billion in 2024 and KRW 18 billion in 2025, and net income including non-controlling interests followed the same path.

For 2025, the company attributed the deterioration to a continued slump in the secondary battery market, where new product launches lifted revenue but higher raw material costs from expensive inputs and early operating costs for new products widened the operating loss.

Asset value declines, including impairments and deferred tax effects, enlarged the net loss. The balance sheet also weakened, with the debt ratio rising from 41.9% in 2022 to 82.4% in 2025 and operating cash flow turning from KRW 4.34 billion in 2022 to negative KRW 12.6 billion in 2025.

Quarterly results turned in 2026. Q1 2026 revenue was KRW 13.5 billion with an operating loss of KRW 472 million, and Q2 2026 revenue of KRW 17.4 billion produced an operating profit of KRW 867 million.

Preliminary first-half figures announced on August 14, 2026 showed revenue of KRW 30.9 billion and operating profit of KRW 390 million, the first half-year operating profit in three years.

Q3 2026 revenue of KRW 10.3 billion fell 40.8% from the prior quarter and rose 43.7% year on year, but the operating loss of KRW 4.49 billion returned. Net loss attributable to controlling shareholders over the four quarters from Q4 2025 to Q3 2026 totaled KRW 15.3 billion.

05

Industry analysis

The secondary battery cycle remains in a slow recovery phase. In its disclosure for fiscal 2025, the company said the slump in the secondary battery market continued.

Spent battery volumes are expected to rise in 2026 from 2025, based on an average battery replacement cycle of eight to ten years and the full commercialization of electric vehicles from 2017, according to press reports.

The government has proposed measures to strengthen the circular use system, including support for private battery supply. Lithium prices showed signs of rebounding in an October 2025 press report, as supply risks such as halted operations at some Chinese mines came into focus.

For the industry as a whole, overcapacity concerns and high technology entry barriers are cited as structural risks. On competition, analysts have pointed to the competitiveness of Sebitchem's liquid-phase separation technology in recovering valuable metals.

Battery recycling requires solution-based refining and separation capabilities, and the company entered this business building on its waste-acid recycling expertise. Waste acid generated by semiconductor and display manufacturing gives the company a feedstock base that differs from battery-only peers. The company has also said it is watching the recycling of waste solar panels positively.

Q2 profit turn and expansion of black mass

BM-X revenue expanded sharply to KRW 15.8 billion from KRW 1.87 billion a year earlier. The company said the switch to spent-battery-based black mass lowered its cost ratio.

Full process coverage and lithium platform growth

Since LX Investment acquired the company in early 2025, it has been described as having a full battery recycling process. LiM-X revenue reached KRW 7.13 billion, up 79% from KRW 3.98 billion a year earlier. The company said it will expand overseas feedstock sourcing alongside global sales channels.

Bear factors

Renewed loss in Q3

Q3 2026 revenue of KRW 10.3 billion fell 40.8% from the prior quarter, and the operating loss was KRW 4.49 billion. The Q2 profit reversed within a single quarter. Given the swings in quarterly results, whether the first-half profit reflects a structural change will depend on subsequent quarters.

Three straight years of operating losses and weaker cash flow

The company recorded operating losses for three consecutive years from 2023 through 2025, and the 2025 operating loss widened to KRW 9.42 billion. Operating cash flow turned negative at KRW 12.6 billion in 2025. The debt ratio rose from 41.9% in 2022 to 82.4% in 2025.

Financial burden from capital spending

At the end of 2025, total equity was KRW 45.4 billion and total liabilities were KRW 37.4 billion. Press reports pointed to rising financial strain from expanded facility investment and overseas plant development. Improving financial indicators such as the interest coverage ratio was cited as a priority.

Risk factors

Raw material and metal prices

In 2025 the company cited higher raw material costs from expensive inputs as a driver of the operating loss. A reversal in lithium and other battery metal prices could affect LiM-X profitability and the BM-X cost ratio. An October 2025 press report linked the lithium price rebound to sector expectations.

Demand and sector structure

In its fiscal 2025 disclosure, the company said the slump in the secondary battery market continued. The sector is seen as carrying structural risks, including overcapacity concerns and technology entry barriers. Whether recycling revenue growth converts into actual profit is the key question.

Financial position and ownership

LX Investment, a private equity manager, acquired the company in early 2025, changing its ownership structure. A debt ratio of 82.4% and net losses enlarged by asset impairments could constrain financial flexibility.

Whether the agenda at the December 4, 2026 meeting is carried out will show the direction of shareholder returns.

Overall view

Sebitchem is a recycling company that has expanded from semiconductor waste-acid recycling into battery recycling. Preliminary first-half 2026 results showed revenue of KRW 30.9 billion and operating profit of KRW 390 million, the first half-year operating profit in three years.

However, Q3 2026 revenue of KRW 10.3 billion and an operating loss of KRW 4.49 billion brought the losses back. For 2025, the operating loss widened to KRW 9.42 billion and the net loss attributable to controlling shareholders reached KRW 18 billion, and the four-quarter total of such losses was KRW 15.3 billion.

Strengths lie in the revenue base of segments such as AM-X and BM-X and full process coverage, while weaknesses are quarterly volatility and a debt ratio of 82.4%.

The next checkpoints are the December 4, 2026 shareholders meeting agenda, the start of MAP commercial production in the second half of 2026, and the explanation of the Q3 loss in the fiscal 2026 annual report around March 2027.

The key questions are whether profitability continues quarter to quarter and whether the financial burden eases.
