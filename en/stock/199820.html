02

Business structure

Cheil Electric is a manufacturer of wiring devices and distribution boards founded in 1980, listed on KOSDAQ in 2020. The business rests on two pillars. In the domestic market it supplies wiring devices and indoor electrical products to construction companies and a national housing developer.

On the export side, PCB assemblies (PCB ASSY) are the main product; they are a core component of arc fault circuit interrupters and earth leakage breakers, and made up 42% of 2024 sales. Consolidated export revenue rose from KRW 61.2 billion in 2022 to KRW 61.8 billion in 2023 and KRW 78.2 billion in 2024.

Media reports say the company exclusively supplies AFCI PCB assemblies to the US power management company Eaton, though these reports are not company disclosures. Its subsidiaries make automotive communication cables and wire harnesses for electric and hydrogen vehicles.

New initiatives include entry into EV charging and supplying new apartment projects through its construction-company sales network. Unlike large transformer makers, its product mix centers on wiring devices, breakers and PCB assemblies, so direct product overlap with those peers is limited.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual revenue rose from KRW 132.2 billion in 2022 to KRW 144 billion in 2023 (up 9.0%), KRW 186.3 billion in 2024 (up 29.4%) and KRW 196 billion in 2025 (up 5.2%). Revenue grew for three straight years from 2023 through 2025, and 2025 was the largest year of 2022 to 2025.

Operating profit rose from KRW 7.39 billion in 2022 to KRW 11.8 billion in 2025 (up 32.1% year on year), and it also grew for three consecutive years from 2023. The operating margin fell from 5.6% in 2022 to 4.8% in 2024 before recovering to 6.0% in 2025.

Net profit attributable to owners of the parent peaked at KRW 10.3 billion in 2024 and fell to KRW 6.62 billion in 2025 (down 35.8%). Net profit including non-controlling interests was KRW 6.7 billion in 2025.

The gap between higher operating profit and lower net profit may reflect non-operating items or taxes, which the annual report should clarify. The debt ratio edged up from 41.1% in 2022 to 42.9% in 2025, with year-end 2025 total equity of KRW 158.5 billion and total liabilities of KRW 68 billion.

Operating cash flow went from KRW -6.29 billion in 2022 to KRW 17.6 billion in 2023, KRW 14.4 billion in 2024 and KRW 6.37 billion in 2025.

Quarterly, operating profit went from KRW 2.72 billion in the third quarter of 2025 to an operating loss of KRW 248 million in the fourth quarter, then back to a profit of KRW 2.52 billion in the first quarter of 2026 and KRW 3.01 billion in the second quarter.

Third-quarter 2026 operating profit was KRW 1.31 billion, down 56.6% from the prior quarter and 52.0% from a year earlier, with a net loss attributable to owners of KRW 1.21 billion. Revenue of KRW 49.7 billion rose 10.2% year on year, but profit fell, so higher sales did not translate into earnings. Net profit attributable to owners over the last four quarters totaled KRW 2.8 billion.

05

Industry analysis

The power equipment cycle is supported by AI data center expansion, renewable energy deployment and replacement demand for aging grids in North America. A 2024 report pointed to data center growth, renewables and aging North American grids as drivers of higher exports of transformers, distribution equipment and cables.

An April 2026 report noted that Hyosung Heavy Industries, HD Hyundai Electric and LS ELECTRIC each posted quarterly record results together. The same report said the industry has shifted toward supplier advantage, which has strengthened expectations for a multi-year earnings upcycle.

Risks flagged in that coverage include US tariff volatility, possible oversupply after aggressive capacity expansions, and share prices that already reflect much of the outlook. National data showed transformer exports in the first quarter of 2024 rising 81.8% year on year.

Cheil Electric's product mix centers on wiring devices, breakers and PCB assemblies rather than large transformers, so product overlap with these peers is limited. Its main link to the US market is the AFCI supply relationship with Eaton.

One commentary said sales could grow if mandatory arc fault circuit interrupter rules are enacted. Whether demand for its products moves with the broader cable and wiring cycle is a point to check in the annual report's customer disclosures.

06

Outlook

Among the company's publicized projects, the Vietnam plant is the one with a set location. The company bought a site of about 20,826 square meters in Hanoi's surrounding Thuy Tri district for its first overseas production base.

However, the construction start, initially expected at the end of 2025, was delayed by paperwork, and no start date has been confirmed. Whether overseas capacity reaches revenue depends on the construction and operation timeline.

The automotive cable and wire harness business through its subsidiary is already part of consolidated results. The company has also outlined supplying new apartment projects through its construction-company sales network and entering EV charging.

Demand for arc fault circuit interrupters hinges on whether mandatory installation rules are enacted, and one brokerage-linked commentary said revenue could grow if they are.

Eaton separately announced a USD 340 million investment to expand US three-phase transformer production, with production and hiring slated to begin in 2027 at its South Carolina facility. That is a transformer expansion, not the AFCI PCB assemblies Cheil Electric supplies. The next checkpoints are the business section of the 2026 annual report and first-quarter 2027 results.

Bull factors

Power equipment cycle and export PCBs

A April 2026 report said three major Korean power equipment makers posted record quarterly results. The industry has been discussed as supplier-led, with a possible multi-year earnings upcycle. PCB ASSY is the largest export product, and reports say it supplies AFCI PCB assemblies to Eaton. Export revenue rising from KRW 61.2 billion in 2022 to KRW 78.2 billion in 2024 supports the export pillar.

Cost restructuring and margin recovery

The 2025 operating margin recovered to 6.0% from 4.8% in 2024. The company said BOM stabilization and raw material improvements reshaped its cost structure, and that it cut shipping and other selling costs. Operating profit reached KRW 11.8 billion in 2025, up 32.1%, and has grown for three consecutive years from 2023.

Arc fault breaker rules and new businesses

Wise Report summarized that revenue growth is expected if arc fault breaker installation becomes mandatory. The subsidiary runs automotive communication cable and wire harness operations, and the company has outlined EV charging and new apartment supply plans. The revenue share of these new businesses is a figure to check in the annual report.

Bear factors

Earnings decline and slowing quarters

Net profit attributable to owners fell 35.8% to KRW 6.62 billion in 2025. Third-quarter 2026 operating profit of KRW 1.31 billion was down 52.0% year on year, and the company posted a net loss attributable to owners of KRW 1.21 billion.

Revenue has declined for two straight quarters since peaking in the first quarter of 2026, which weakens top-line momentum.

Concentration in export PCBs and customers

PCB ASSY made up 42% of 2024 sales, so one product line carries much of the business. Given the reported supply relationship with Eaton, demand shifts at one customer or in export markets could weigh heavily on results. The high export share also exposes the company to currency and tariff changes.

Overseas plant delay and execution risk

The Vietnam plant has been delayed by paperwork after the site purchase, and no construction start has been set. The subsidiary business, EV charging and new apartment supply have yet to be proven in revenue terms. Oversupply concerns in the sector also make the payoff from new capacity harder to gauge.

Risk factors

Customer and export concentration

PCB assemblies are central to exports, so supply may be heavily weighted toward a few overseas customers. US arc fault breaker policy and customer order shifts could change quarterly revenue quickly. Whether revenue keeps falling after the first-quarter 2026 peak is the point to watch.

Currency, tariffs and raw materials

A high export share means currency moves affect won-denominated results. US tariff volatility is a variable raised across the power equipment sector. Raw material price changes feed into cost structure, so whether the cost gains of 2025 hold is worth checking.

New business execution and earnings volatility

Delays at the Vietnam plant, subsidiary expansion and entry into EV charging are running at the same time, which can raise investment burdens and execution risk together. The third-quarter 2026 profit drop and swing to a net loss show how wide earnings swings can be. Non-operating items and taxes also make the net profit trajectory harder to read.

Overall view

Cheil Electric is an electrical components company whose two pillars are domestic wiring devices and PCB assembly exports. From 2022 to 2025 revenue rose from KRW 132.2 billion to KRW 196 billion and operating profit from KRW 7.39 billion to KRW 11.8 billion, showing the effect of cost restructuring.

Net profit attributable to owners, however, fell from KRW 10.3 billion in 2024 to KRW 6.62 billion in 2025, and the third quarter of 2026 swung to a net loss. Bullish factors include the strong power equipment cycle, export PCB supply, the margin recovery and the possibility of arc fault breaker rules.

Bearish factors include the decline since the first-quarter 2026 revenue peak, the sharp third-quarter profit drop, concentration in export products and the delayed Vietnam plant. The two sides currently carry similar weight.

The next points to check are how the gap between operating profit and attributable net profit is explained in the 2026 annual figures, and whether revenue declines stop in the first quarter of 2027.
