GeneOne Life Science: Restructuring Meets a Listing Review Crossroads

GeneOne Life Science is undergoing a Korea Exchange listing eligibility review following a production halt and complete capital impairment at its U.S. subsidiary VGXI, while simultaneously raising capital through a new investment partnership to stabilize management control and liquidity.

02

Business structure

GeneOne Life Science was founded in 1976 as a textile interlining maker and expanded into biopharmaceuticals by launching a new pharmaceutical-related business at the end of 2005.

Its current operations consist of a biopharmaceutical CDMO business, a nucleic-acid-based drug development business, and a residual textile interlining and consulting business.

By revenue, plasmid DNA CDMO work for clinical-grade DNA vaccines and gene therapy raw materials accounts for roughly 59%, textile interlining accessories (dress shirts, caps, and other apparel components) for about 37%, and consulting for about 4%.

The CDMO business is carried out through VGXI, a Texas-based subsidiary acquired in 2008, which produces cGMP-grade plasmid DNA at its Woodland and Conroe facilities for global gene therapy and DNA vaccine developers.

In new drug development, the company has completed overseas Phase 1 and domestic clinical trials for its MERS (GLS-5300) and Zika (GLS-5700) DNA vaccines, and has advanced clinical development of its SFTS (GLS-5140) and COVID-19 (GLS-5310) DNA vaccines.

In January 2026, building on the experience of VGXI manufacturing a personalized anticancer DNA vaccine used in an FDA-approved clinical trial, the company designated a DNA/mRNA-based personalized anticancer nucleic acid vaccine as its new core strategic pipeline.

Amid the Ebola outbreak in the Democratic Republic of Congo, the company's earlier Ebola DNA vaccine research, co-developed with Inovio Pharmaceuticals and the University of Pennsylvania and shown in a Phase 1 trial to generate T-cell immune responses, drew renewed attention.

In terms of competitive positioning, VGXI is classified as a small-scale specialist in plasmid DNA production, distinct in scale and scope from large biopharmaceutical CDMO players such as Samsung Biologics.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual revenue fell for three straight years, from KRW 48.7 billion in 2022 to KRW 40.2 billion in 2023 (down 17.5% year over year), KRW 35.7 billion in 2024 (down 11.2%), and KRW 23 billion in 2025 (down 35.5%).

The operating loss widened from KRW 40.1 billion in 2022 to KRW 48.4 billion in 2023, narrowed to KRW 43 billion in 2024, then widened again to KRW 44 billion in 2025, marking four consecutive years of operating losses.

The net loss attributable to controlling shareholders followed a similar pattern, widening from KRW 38.1 billion in 2022 to KRW 77.8 billion in 2023, narrowing to KRW 48.5 billion in 2024, then widening again to KRW 51.9 billion in 2025, extending the loss streak to four years.

The operating margin deteriorated from -82.3% in 2022 to -191.1% in 2025, indicating that fixed-cost pressure weighed on results even more heavily than the pace of revenue decline.

Quarterly results swung even more sharply: revenue fell for four consecutive quarters, from KRW 6.81 billion in Q2 2025 to KRW 5.08 billion in Q3 (down 25.5%), KRW 2.65 billion in Q4 (down 47.8%), KRW 2.58 billion in Q1 2026 (down 2.7%), and KRW 2.06 billion in Q2 2026 (down 20.3% quarter over quarter and 69.8% year over year).

The operating loss widened from KRW 10.2 billion in Q2 2025 to KRW 13.1 billion in Q4, narrowed sharply to KRW 2.34 billion in Q1 2026, then widened again to KRW 12.8 billion in Q2 2026, marking five consecutive quarters of operating losses.

The net loss attributable to controlling shareholders fluctuated over the same period from KRW 15.4 billion to KRW 11.5 billion, KRW 16.1 billion, KRW 2.38 billion, and KRW 18.9 billion, totaling KRW 48.9 billion over the trailing four quarters.

The debt-to-equity ratio jumped from 22.8% in 2022 to 191.5% in 2025, with total equity at KRW 40.9 billion (all attributable to controlling shareholders) and total liabilities at KRW 78.3 billion at the end of 2025, reflecting a rapid deterioration in the balance sheet, while operating cash flow posted net outflows of KRW 16.1 billion to KRW 24.1 billion every year from 2022 to 2025, underscoring a structural reliance on external financing.

05

Industry analysis

Plasmid DNA is a key raw material used in gene therapies, DNA vaccines, and mRNA vaccines, and according to the company, demand that slowed after the COVID-19 pandemic has recently shown signs of recovery.

The CDMO market has grown rapidly, led by large biopharmaceutical contract manufacturers such as Samsung Biologics, and GeneOne Life Science has sought to keep pace by expanding its CDMO business around VGXI, acquired in 2008 as its core production base.

However, the plasmid DNA CDMO segment in which VGXI operates differs in scale and customer base from large-molecule biopharmaceutical CDMO markets covering antibodies and cell and gene therapies.

Among Korea's listed biopharmaceutical companies, firms such as Samsung Biologics, Celltrion, and SK Bioscience occupy the upper tier of the CDMO market with large-scale facilities and stable order books, making the comparatively small VGXI's utilization rate and new order intake a key driver of earnings volatility.

In the first half of 2026, CDMO production was fully suspended at both of VGXI's Woodland and Conroe plants, with no revenue recognized in that segment, a situation the company described as a technical measure tied to process efficiency improvements and validation preparations.

On the drug development side, the company has entered globally watched technology areas such as personalized nucleic acid vaccines and infectious-disease DNA vaccine platforms, though it has yet to bring a proprietary product through to commercial approval.

06

Outlook

The company announced that VGXI's new facility, located at Deison Technology Park in Conroe, Texas, passed its completion inspection and received an occupancy permit on October 1, 2026, and said it plans to move in promptly to resume commercial plasmid DNA production.

Around the same time, VGXI signed an agreement with C2R in the United States to extend the exercise deadline for its buyback option on the Conroe plant from September 1 to December 15, 2026, with part of the total buyback amount of $39.9 million to be paid in October and the remaining $35.9 million to be settled by December 15.

The company disclosed that it has continued sales activity in the first half of 2026, including signing a $16.9 million letter of intent with a new client.

On the financing side, a KRW 15 billion third-party share placement is underway, with KRW 13 billion earmarked for a loan to cover VGXI plant operating expenses and headquarters operating costs and KRW 1.9 billion for repaying a loan from Sangsangin Savings Bank; once the placement is completed, Revolt Investment Partnership is set to become the largest shareholder with a 25.51% stake.

Separately, the company has outlined a strategy to cultivate its DNA/mRNA-based personalized anticancer nucleic acid vaccine as its next core pipeline, centered on upgrading its platform, partnering with companies specializing in cancer antigen selection, strengthening GMP manufacturing capability, and building a global network.

However, the Korea Exchange has designated GeneOne Life Science for a listing eligibility review due to the suspension of CDMO production and sales activity, and a corporate examination committee review is underway whose outcome will determine delisting, the granting of an improvement period, or the continuation of the trading suspension.

08

Bull factors

VGXI's Production Asset Base

According to a March 2026 report, GeneOne Life Science's assets, including its U.S. CDMO plants, were said to total KRW 120 billion. In October 2026, VGXI's new Conroe facility passed its completion inspection and received an occupancy permit, meeting the physical requirements to resume commercial production.

If operations normalize, there is room for CDMO revenue to be reinstated in line with a recovery in plasmid DNA demand.

Stabilized Ownership and Capital Infusion

Following a management change in August 2025, Dongbanseongjang Investment Partnership No. 1 increased its stake, and in 2026 a KRW 15 billion third-party placement to Revolt Investment Partnership is underway that would again change the largest shareholder.

The company said it plans to use the proceeds for VGXI plant operating expenses and loan repayment, easing near-term liquidity pressure.

It also won an injunction against a KRW 7.3 billion fine and a two-year ban on participating in government projects imposed by the Ministry of Health and Welfare, resolving part of a near-term risk.

New Pipeline Expansion

The company has designated a DNA/mRNA-based personalized anticancer nucleic acid vaccine as its new core strategic pipeline and is pursuing platform upgrades and stronger GMP manufacturing capability.

This strategy builds on the experience of VGXI manufacturing a personalized anticancer DNA vaccine used in an FDA-approved clinical trial.

Amid the Ebola outbreak in Africa, Phase 1 results from the company's Ebola DNA vaccine, co-developed with Inovio Pharmaceuticals and the University of Pennsylvania, drew renewed attention, highlighting its value as an infectious-disease response platform.

Bear factors

Sharp Revenue Decline and Deepening Losses

Annual revenue fell for three straight years, from KRW 48.7 billion in 2022 to KRW 23 billion in 2025, while the operating loss widened over the same period from KRW 40.1 billion to KRW 44 billion, extending the loss streak to four years.

Second-quarter 2026 revenue plunged 69.8% year over year to just KRW 2.06 billion, and the operating loss also widened from a year earlier. The debt-to-equity ratio surged from 22.8% in 2022 to 191.5% in 2025, reflecting a rapid deterioration in the balance sheet.

VGXI Production Halt and Complete Capital Impairment

VGXI's CDMO production was fully suspended at both of its facilities, Woodland and Conroe, in the first half of 2026, with no revenue recognized in that segment.

As of the end of 2025, VGXI was in a state of complete capital impairment, with liabilities of KRW 226.5 billion exceeding assets of KRW 126.7 billion, and GeneOne Life Science recognized an impairment loss of KRW 80.3 billion after reassessing the recoverability of long-term loans and accrued receivables owed by VGXI. This undermines confidence in the strategy of relying on the CDMO subsidiary for revenue recovery.

Listing Eligibility Review and Adverse Half-Year Review Opinion

The Korea Exchange designated GeneOne Life Science for a listing eligibility review due to the suspension of VGXI's CDMO production and sales activity, and a corporate examination committee review is underway whose outcome could include delisting.

On August 14, 2026, the company disclosed a half-year review finding of 'adverse opinion, disclaimer of opinion, or complete capital impairment,' heightening concerns about its ability to continue as a going concern.

If the trading suspension continues during the review process, shareholders' asset liquidity could be constrained.

Risk factors

Delisting and Trading Suspension Risk

The Korea Exchange's corporate examination committee is reviewing whether to delist GeneOne Life Science, grant an improvement period, or continue the trading suspension, citing the halt in CDMO production and sales.

The scope of the review has broadened as multiple issues have overlapped, including a change of listing following a 5-for-1 reverse stock split and an adverse half-year review opinion.

If the review results in delisting or a prolonged trading suspension, shareholders' ability to convert their holdings to cash could be severely constrained.

Financial and Liquidity Risk

The debt-to-equity ratio surged to 191.5% in 2025, and operating cash flow has posted net outflows for four consecutive years, making reliance on external financing structural. Frequent third-party share placements and convertible bond issuances raise the risk of further dilution for existing shareholders.

VGXI's complete capital impairment and the recognition of a large impairment loss suggest the potential for further deterioration in asset quality.

Governance and Legal Risk

The largest shareholder and chief executive have changed repeatedly since August 2025, and in 2026 the largest shareholder is set to change again, from Dongbanseongjang Investment Partnership No. 1 to Revolt Investment Partnership, creating uncertainty around management continuity.

A former chief executive has filed a $170 million lawsuit against GeneOne Life Science and VGXI, alleging breach of a departure agreement and defamation.

Because the new largest shareholder is an investment partnership established only six days before the placement was decided, questions have also been raised about the reliability of the capital payment.

12

Overall view

GeneOne Life Science has seen revenue decline for four consecutive years alongside four straight years of operating and net losses, and financial pressure has intensified in 2026 with the additional complications of VGXI's CDMO production halt and complete capital impairment.

The company is simultaneously pursuing liquidity through a KRW 15 billion third-party share placement and working toward resuming production following the occupancy permit for VGXI's new plant and the extension of its buyback option.

At the same time, a Korea Exchange listing eligibility review is underway, and its conclusion will determine whether the company retains its listing and when trading might resume.

The personalized anticancer nucleic acid vaccine and the Ebola and other infectious-disease DNA vaccine platforms presented as new core pipelines represent one pillar of a longer-term growth narrative, but neither has yet reached a commercial stage that contributes to revenue.

Governance stability also remains a variable to watch, given repeated changes in the largest shareholder and management team over a short period and an ongoing lawsuit involving a former chief executive.

Investors should track, in sequence, the outcome of the corporate examination committee's review, whether VGXI's operations actually resume, and how the proceeds of the share placement are ultimately deployed.
