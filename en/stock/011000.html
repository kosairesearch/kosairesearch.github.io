02

Business structure

GeneOne Life Science was founded in 1976 and listed on the KOSPI market in November 1987. Its business is split between a pharmaceutical segment that produces raw materials for clinical-stage DNA vaccines and gene therapies, and a fabric segment that handles apparel interlinings and processed fabrics.

The core pharmaceutical base is its US subsidiary VGXI, which operates a first plant in The Woodlands, Texas and a second plant in Conroe. VGXI disclosed that its CDMO production was fully halted throughout the first half of 2026 and that no CDMO segment revenue was recognized in that period.

The company attributed the halt to asset inspections and production-line adjustments needed to restart the plants, which temporarily suspended finished-product manufacturing. After its lease ended, the first plant was re-leased through January 31, 2037.

On customers, as of June 25, 2026 the company signed a letter of agreement worth USD 16.9 million with one new customer and is preparing a definitive contract, while two existing customers requested additional production quotes.

Looking at recent trends rather than segment weights, revenue depends heavily on whether CDMO production resumes. Against large CDMO players such as Samsung Biologics and Celltrion, which have larger facilities and customer bases, GeneOne is positioned as a smaller production base centered on two US plants. The company is widening its order base through a supply agreement for mRNA-grade DNA.

04

Earnings analysis

On a reported annual basis, revenue went from KRW 48.7 billion in 2022 to KRW 40.2 billion in 2023 (down 17.5% year on year), KRW 35.7 billion in 2024 (down 11.2%), and KRW 23 billion in 2025 (down 35.5%), so revenue declined for three consecutive years from 2023 through 2025.

Among 2022 to 2025, 2022 was the largest year and 2025 the smallest. Operating losses were KRW 40.1 billion in 2022, KRW 48.4 billion in 2023, KRW 43 billion in 2024 and KRW 44 billion in 2025, which means four consecutive years of operating losses from 2022 through 2025.

The operating margin moved from -82.3% in 2022 to -120.3% in 2023, -120.5% in 2024 and -191.1% in 2025. Because the operating loss did not shrink much while revenue kept falling, the margin deterioration accelerated.

Net loss attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 38.1 billion in 2022, KRW 77.8 billion in 2023, KRW 48.5 billion in 2024 and KRW 51.9 billion in 2025.

Net loss including non-controlling interests was KRW 38.2 billion, KRW 77.8 billion, KRW 48.5 billion and KRW 51.9 billion in the same years, so the two figures are close.

Operating cash flow was -KRW 24.1 billion in 2022, -KRW 21.7 billion in 2023, -KRW 16.1 billion in 2024 and -KRW 16.5 billion in 2025, a continued cash outflow.

The debt ratio rose from 22.8% in 2022 to 35.4% in 2023, 76.8% in 2024 and 191.5% in 2025, and all of the KRW 40.9 billion total equity at the end of 2025 belongs to controlling shareholders.

Quarterly revenue fell from KRW 5.08 billion in the third quarter of 2025 to KRW 2.65 billion in the fourth quarter (down 47.8% from the prior quarter), and KRW 1.78 billion in the third quarter of 2026 was down 13.3% from the prior quarter and 64.9% year on year.

Quarterly operating losses were KRW 11.7 billion in the third quarter of 2025, KRW 13.1 billion in the fourth quarter, KRW 2.34 billion in the first quarter of 2026, KRW 12.8 billion in the second quarter and KRW 5.41 billion in the third quarter of 2026, which was narrower than a year earlier.

Net loss attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 15.4 billion in the third quarter of 2026, smaller than KRW 18.9 billion in the prior quarter but wider than KRW 11.5 billion a year earlier, and the last four quarters total KRW 52.9 billion.

Whether the narrower losses came from revenue recovery or from cost adjustments should be settled by the line items in the next periodic report.

Over the same period, a KRW 5.1 billion convertible bond was paid in on May 7, 2026 and a KRW 15 billion third-party placement was scheduled for September 30, 2026, so outside funding kept covering the losses.

05

Industry analysis

The field GeneOne operates in is CDMO work supplying raw materials for clinical-stage DNA vaccines and gene therapies, so results depend on client clinical progress and pipeline demand.

The fact that the latest supply agreement centers on DNA for mRNA-grade raw material suggests that demand for mRNA-based drug development overlaps with the company's production base.

Read in context, a DNA production base that grew out of pandemic-era vaccine manufacturing appears to be moving toward raw-material demand for other uses.

Against competitors, Samsung Biologics and Celltrion are large players with major production capacity and biopharma manufacturing track records, while GeneOne's facility scale is comparatively small.

Even within the same biopharma sector, where Alteogen and HLB are assessed mainly on technology licensing and platforms, GeneOne's key variables are plant utilization and fulfillment of customer contracts.

The halt in plant operations in the first half of 2026 raised questions about the company's ability to deliver on contracts. The USD 16.9 million agreement and two additional quote requests from existing customers point to demand, but the figures only reach reported results once the definitive contract is signed.

Scarcity of production know-how could support long-term contract wins, yet contract terms and pricing have not been disclosed. The direction of the industry will depend on how steadily client clinical schedules and mRNA-related raw-material orders continue.

Outlook

In a first-half 2026 disclosure, the company said that after asset inspections and production-line adjustments to restart its plants, it expects to recognize revenue alongside the start of new projects in the second half.

In a report dated October 26, 2026, a company representative said production could fully resume from early November 2026 once effectiveness testing and staffing are completed. Whether production actually ran after that point does not yet appear in reported results.

The second plant's asset monetization schedule has reportedly been delayed, and the company decided to keep the first plant operating for that reason. Whether the USD 16.9 million agreement becomes a definitive contract is the biggest variable for the next revenue trend.

The company disclosed that its KRW 13 billion operating fund is to be fully used during 2026. The KRW 1.9 billion debt repayment is for a March 2026 borrowing from a savings bank, recorded at a 10% interest rate.

According to the placement disclosure, once payment is completed the largest shareholder is set to change from a shared-growth investment partnership to a private investment partnership holding 25.51%. The 6,224,066 new shares were disclosed as fully subject to a one-year lock-up.

A provisional shareholders' meeting was also convened for October 30, 2026, but its detailed outcomes are not part of the reported results here. The next formal checkpoint is whether revenue recovers and how large the operating loss is in the 2026 annual business report.

Risk factors

Financial and liquidity risk

A KRW 5.1 billion convertible bond was paid in on May 7, 2026, and a KRW 15 billion third-party placement followed in September 2026, so fundraising has recurred. With the debt ratio at 191.5% and operating cash outflows continuing at the end of 2025, the need for further funding could grow.

Because the new shares are under a one-year lock-up, the timing of any increase in tradable supply is also limited.

Production and contract execution risk

CDMO production was fully halted in the first half of 2026, and the reasons included qualification assessment preparation and production-line adjustments. The asset monetization of the second plant has been delayed, so the company is operating the first plant under a re-lease.

If the USD 16.9 million agreement does not become a definitive contract or production starts late, the timing of a revenue recovery could slip.

Governance and disclosure risk

A largest-shareholder change dated February 13, 2026 was disclosed late on March 17, leading to a flagged possible designation as an unfaithful-disclosure company, with a cumulative penalty score of 5 points over the past year.

Under the exchange rules, a penalty score of 10 or more triggers a one-day trading suspension on the designation date. Because the largest shareholder stake is set to shift to a private investment partnership, changes in management direction and governance will need to be checked together.

Overall view

GeneOne Life Science has seen revenue decline and four consecutive years of operating losses from 2022 through 2025, and in 2026 it is working to create conditions for revenue recovery through a US CDMO plant restart and a USD 16.9 million supply agreement.

The operating loss in the third quarter of 2026 was KRW 5.41 billion, narrower than a year earlier, but the net loss attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 15.4 billion, wider than a year earlier, so the line items point in different directions.

On the financial side, the debt ratio of 191.5%, total equity of KRW 40.9 billion and continued operating cash outflow at the end of 2025 mean the company depends heavily on outside funding.

Strengths cited include the supply agreement, the plant restart and the narrower operating loss, while weaknesses cited include four consecutive quarters of revenue decline, repeated capital raising through convertible bonds and placements, and a governance disclosure history.

The weight of these factors will shift depending on whether the definitive contract is signed and whether post-restart revenue appears in disclosures. The next formal checkpoint is the fiscal 2026 annual business report, due by March 31, 2027.

The fourth-quarter 2026 revenue trend and the second plant's asset monetization schedule will make the scale of the financial challenge clearer before then.
