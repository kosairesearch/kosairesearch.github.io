02

Business structure

Founded in 1988 through the acquisition of Samgang Pharmaceutical, ChoA Pharmaceutical is a finished-dosage drugmaker whose core business is manufacturing and selling pharmaceuticals and quasi-drugs, with Medipharm Co. as its main subsidiary.

Most revenue comes from over-the-counter pharmacy products and health foods, centered on traditional OTC categories addressing indigestion, gastric disorders, iron-deficiency anemia, liver disease and allergic rhinitis.

The company operates the Medipharm Pharmacy chain brand and sustains brand marketing through mass-media advertising and sponsorships of weekend dramas and professional baseball. Its children's nutrition brand Zalkton marked its 20th anniversary with cumulative sales surpassing 10.45 million units as of end-June 2026.

More recently, the company has diversified into pet care with a six-product Zalkgae veterinary medicine and supplement line launched in July 2025, alongside sports-nutrition items such as the Dalshot Energy Gel for runners.

Its overseas push centers on the New Hem Syrup and New Hem Forte Syrup iron supplements targeting emerging markets in Africa and Southeast Asia with high iron-deficiency anemia prevalence, while it has obtained approvals in Guatemala for Hepatos Syrup, a liver activator, and Elas-A Liquid, for venous and lymphatic circulation disorders, and is expanding its export network to countries including the Democratic Republic of the Congo.

In May 2026 the company also joined a Korea-Latin America biomedical partnership event to hold export consultations with buyers in Mexico and Brazil, laying groundwork for entry into the region.

The domestic market is populated by numerous small and mid-sized pharmaceutical and health-food companies, and because the bulk of revenue is derived from domestic products, the company remains exposed to intensifying distribution competition and shifting consumer trends.

Earnings analysis

Consolidated revenue fell for three consecutive years, from KRW 68.9 billion in 2022 to KRW 63.0 billion in 2023, KRW 62.7 billion in 2024 and KRW 59.3 billion in 2025.

Over the same period, operating profit worsened from a loss of KRW 480 million in 2022 (operating margin of -0.7%) to a loss of KRW 9.65 billion in 2024 (-15.4%), before narrowing somewhat to a KRW 6.6 billion loss in 2025 (-11.1%).

Owner net income likewise stayed in the red for three straight years, moving from a KRW 560 million profit in 2022 to losses of KRW 10.51 billion in 2023, KRW 9.6 billion in 2024 and KRW 6.8 billion in 2025.

Quarterly trends, however, show clearer signs of improvement recently: owner net loss was KRW 1.415 billion in Q2 2025, narrowed to KRW 274 million in Q3, widened again to KRW 3.064 billion in Q4, then swung to a profit of KRW 26.1 million in Q1 2026 and KRW 500 million in Q2 2026, marking two consecutive profitable quarters.

Operating losses, which had hovered around KRW 1.5 billion per quarter through 2025, narrowed to KRW 1.12 billion in Q1 2026 and just KRW 23 million in Q2 2026, approaching breakeven.

This improvement appears to reflect both a modest revenue pickup (KRW 14.7 billion in Q1 2026 and KRW 15.2 billion in Q2 2026) and cost control, while no specific one-off factor for the widened Q4 loss is identifiable from disclosures.

On the balance sheet, accumulated losses have cut owner equity by more than half, from KRW 52.8 billion in 2022 to KRW 25.0 billion in 2025, while the debt ratio rose sharply from 66.6% to 197.2% over the same period, indicating a weakened capital buffer.

Non-controlling interest equity has also been negative every year since 2022, pointing to accumulated deficits at the subsidiary level as well.

Industry analysis

Korea's OTC drug and health-food market is a mature segment with numerous small and mid-sized pharmaceutical companies competing, where distribution and marketing—rather than prescription dynamics—largely determine sales outcomes.

The pet care segment has drawn attention as a new growth area for the pharmaceutical industry, with rising pet ownership fueling expectations for market growth.

In emerging markets, demand for nutrition-related pharmaceuticals such as iron-deficiency anemia treatments remains steady, and competition continues among Korean small-cap drugmakers pursuing expansion into Africa, Southeast Asia and Latin America.

At the industry level, the biggest recent variable has been the overhaul of KOSDAQ's delisting regime.

In February 2026, the Financial Services Commission and Korea Exchange announced a reform plan to accelerate the removal of insolvent companies, moving up the schedule for raising the KOSDAQ market-cap listing maintenance threshold to KRW 20 billion in July 2026 and KRW 30 billion in January 2027.

A new "penny stock" rule targeting shares trading below KRW 1,000 also took effect from July 2026, with detailed criteria designed to prevent companies from circumventing the rule through simple share consolidations.

According to the KOSDAQ Association, as of August 24, 2026, 128 companies had market caps of KRW 20 billion or less, and adding those in the KRW 20-30 billion band brought the total to 349 companies, meaning many small-cap pharma and biotech names face similar listing-maintenance pressure.

Alongside ChoA Pharmaceutical, other low-priced peers such as Kyungnam Pharmaceutical have likewise pursued share consolidations to normalize their stock prices, a response spreading across the sector.

Bull factors

Capital Policy Response to Listing Requirements

The consolidation could also help mitigate future dilution concerns in the event of capital raising. These measures signal the company's active engagement in maintaining its listing status.

Bear factors

Consolidated revenue declined for three straight years, from KRW 68.9 billion in 2022 to KRW 59.3 billion in 2025, with weak domestic sales—which account for most of total revenue—cited as the main driver.

While emerging-market exports are expanding, their scale remains small, meaning it will take time before they can drive an overall revenue rebound. Some market observers view it as still too early to gauge results from the new businesses and export push.

Weakening Capital Buffer

Accumulated losses have cut owner equity by more than half, from KRW 52.8 billion in 2022 to KRW 25.0 billion in 2025, while the debt ratio surged from 66.6% to 197.2% over the same period. Non-controlling interest has also been negative every year, indicating accumulated deficits at the subsidiary level as well. This could translate into less favorable terms should the company need additional capital in the future.

Proximity to Market-Cap Listing Threshold

KOSDAQ's market-cap listing maintenance threshold rose to KRW 20 billion in July 2026 and will rise again to KRW 30 billion in January 2027.

If a company's market cap falls short of the threshold for 30 consecutive trading days, it is designated an administrative issue, and failure to recover above the threshold for 45 consecutive trading days within the following 90 trading days can lead to delisting.

The company's market capitalization has moved near these thresholds, making it a variable that warrants ongoing monitoring.

Risk factors

Listing Maintenance Regulatory Risk

The Financial Services Commission and Korea Exchange announced a delisting reform plan in February 2026 that moved up the schedule for raising KOSDAQ's market-cap threshold to KRW 20 billion in July 2026 and KRW 30 billion in January 2027.

A new delisting requirement targeting sub-KRW-1,000 penny stocks was also introduced, with detailed criteria designed to prevent simple circumvention through share consolidations.

Sustained shortfalls versus the threshold could lead to administrative-issue designation and potentially a substantive delisting review, warranting ongoing monitoring.

Financial Soundness and Funding Risk

With owner equity shrinking and the debt ratio rising amid continued accumulated losses, any future need for external funding through rights offerings, convertible bonds or bonds with warrants could raise dilution concerns for existing shareholders.

Market commentary has suggested the share consolidation was partly a preemptive step ahead of such a funding scenario. Delisting requirements related to full capital impairment have also recently been expanded to a semiannual basis, raising the importance of balance-sheet management.

Business and Market Risk

The core domestic product business, which accounts for most revenue, has continued to stagnate, with sales declining for three straight years. While emerging-market exports and new businesses such as pet care are underway, their scale remains modest, and it will take time to confirm their earnings contribution.

Some in the industry take a cautious view that it remains too early to judge the success of these new initiatives.

Overall view

ChoA Pharmaceutical has shown signs of earnings improvement, posting two consecutive quarters of owner net income in the black in the first half of 2026 while narrowing its operating loss to near breakeven.

At the same time, the company continues efforts to diversify its revenue base through expanded emerging-market exports and new businesses such as pet care.

However, annual revenue has declined for three straight years, driven mainly by weak sales of domestic products that make up the bulk of the top line, meaning a fundamental revenue recovery has not yet been confirmed.

Factors that must be weighed together include a weakened capital structure—with equity shrinking and the debt ratio rising amid accumulated losses—and a market capitalization that sits close to KOSDAQ's recently raised listing maintenance threshold (KRW 20 billion, rising to KRW 30 billion in 2027).

The 5-for-1 share consolidation that lifted the stock out of penny-stock territory can be read as a preemptive response to listing-maintenance risk, though industry observers note that a genuine recovery in sales and profitability must ultimately follow.

Overall, the company sits at a juncture where near-term earnings improvement coexists with longer-term revenue stagnation and listing-related regulatory risk, making it important to track both upcoming quarterly results and listing-related disclosures going forward.
