02

Business structure

M83 is a visual effects (VFX) specialist founded in 2020, focused on VFX production for films, dramas and OTT content. It listed on KOSDAQ in August 2024. The business has two pillars: VFX and content production, and AI infrastructure run by its 100%-owned subsidiary.

The subsidiary supplies GPU servers, high-performance storage and industrial servers and builds AI infrastructure as generative AI spreads.

In March 2026, it announced a series of server infrastructure contracts for AI and large-scale data processing with major domestic platform and portal companies, with a total value of KRW 77 billion.

Media reports said the subsidiary supplies standard AI infrastructure servers to Naver Cloud under an annual contract of roughly KRW 50 billion.

The subsidiary's weight in results is large: 2025 standalone revenue was KRW 10.8 billion against consolidated KRW 132.4 billion, and third-quarter 2026 standalone revenue was KRW 12.4 billion against consolidated KRW 97.6 billion.

In VFX, the company has worked on global OTT projects and large Chinese animation productions. In January 2025 it acquired a stake in Norway's VFX and animation company Gimpville and became its largest shareholder; at the end of the first half of 2026 its stake was 33.26%, and Gimpville is classified as an associate.

At the March 2026 general meeting, the company added business purposes including interior design, software development and operation, and e-commerce.

04

Earnings analysis

On a filed basis, annual revenue was KRW 42.1 billion in 2023, KRW 58.2 billion in 2024 (up 38.1% year on year) and KRW 132.4 billion in 2025 (up 127.6%), rising two years in a row from 2024 to 2025; 2025 was the largest year of the 2023 to 2025 period.

The company attributed the 2025 revenue increase to sales growth from the expanding business of its subsidiaries. Operating profit fell from KRW 4.37 billion in 2023 to KRW 1.45 billion in 2024 (down 66.7%), and in 2025 the company swung to an operating loss of KRW 8.97 billion.

The operating margin moved from 10.4% in 2023 to 2.5% in 2024 and -6.8% in 2025.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders went from KRW 5.76 billion in 2023 to KRW 2.8 billion in 2024 (down 51.4%), then turned into a net loss of KRW 6.73 billion in 2025; net income including non-controlling interests was a net loss of KRW 8.86 billion in 2025.

The company cited weakness in film and drama production across the broader industry and higher costs tied to business expansion as reasons for the weaker profitability.

The balance sheet also shifted: the debt-to-equity ratio at the end of 2025 was 113.6%, up from 48.2% in 2024, and operating cash flow went from KRW 6.68 billion in 2023 to -KRW 6.72 billion in 2025.

Quarterly, the company moved from an operating loss of KRW 2.18 billion in the third quarter of 2025 to an operating loss of KRW 2.04 billion in the fourth quarter and KRW 2.58 billion in the first quarter of 2026.

In the second quarter of 2026, revenue was KRW 49 billion (down 26.8% from the prior quarter) and operating profit was KRW 1.59 billion, a swing to profit.

In the third quarter of 2026, revenue rose to KRW 97.6 billion (up 99.3% from the prior quarter and up 196.0% year on year) and operating profit to KRW 8.76 billion (up 450.0% from the prior quarter), with net income attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 6.53 billion.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders for the last four quarters totals KRW 4.33 billion, and the company said third-quarter revenue and operating profit were record quarterly levels.

The company said the third-quarter growth came from AI infrastructure sales at its subsidiary, and that standalone operating profit of KRW 800 million kept the core VFX business in the black.

05

Industry analysis

Demand for VFX work is tied to the production cycle for film, drama and OTT content. In its 2025 results commentary, the company said VFX profitability weakened because of a temporary drop in domestic production and a pullback in investment sentiment.

As of the first quarter of 2026, the utilization of its Chuncheon filming facility was recovering, and the company said it was also pursuing expansion of filming infrastructure in other regions.

Overseas, it signed a global OTT project contract with a Japanese content producer in the first quarter of 2026, and it is discussing cooperation with major local platforms and producers in China.

In AI infrastructure, demand for GPU servers and high-performance storage is driven by the spread of generative AI, and the company said expanding investment in AI data centers and cloud infrastructure is lifting server demand.

The subsidiary has built AI infrastructure references with major internet platform companies including Naver and Kakao and is pushing into new markets such as financial institutions.

The company said that as its subsidiary's revenue scale grows, its bargaining power in system procurement and its scale economies have increased. For the two businesses, the key variables are whether the content production cycle recovers for VFX and whether data center investment continues for AI infrastructure.

An independent research house's analysis published on September 3, 2026 described M83 as expanding from VFX production services into a content and technology platform company. The same analysis did not present an investment rating or target price.

06

Outlook

The company said that in the second half of 2026, the expansion of its AI infrastructure business and the recognition of revenue from global projects would move into full swing.

In its third-quarter 2026 release, the company said it is pursuing new project orders in Japan, China and Europe and expanding AI server supply centered on its subsidiary. The global project with a Japanese content producer began to be recognized as revenue from the second quarter of 2026.

The company said its trust with large customers, its ability to secure stable volumes and its stronger bargaining position have made it possible to increase order volumes and secure profits.

In its 2025 results release, the company said that resuming delayed VFX projects and expanding new orders could restore revenue and profitability.

In its first-quarter 2026 release, it said it had confirmed both AI infrastructure growth and the possibility of a VFX core business recovery, and it expressed hope for a business overhaul and earnings turnaround this year.

An independent research analysis published in September 2026 expected high growth to continue in the second half, driven by AI infrastructure expansion and revenue recognition from global projects.

In China, follow-on orders may expand on the back of earlier large-project references, but the company is currently at the stage of discussing cooperation with local platforms.

The next checkpoint is how much AI infrastructure revenue and VFX operating profit each contribute in the fourth quarter of 2026 and in later periodic reports, and the split between standalone and consolidated figures shows the contribution of each business.

08

Bull factors

Rapid expansion of AI infrastructure sales

Third-quarter 2026 consolidated revenue was KRW 97.6 billion, up 196.0% year on year. The company said the subsidiary's first-half 2026 revenue of KRW 96.3 billion approached its full prior-year results. Server infrastructure orders disclosed in March 2026 totaled KRW 77 billion. The company said its larger revenue base strengthened procurement bargaining power and scale economies.

Improving profitability in the core VFX business

The company said standalone operating profit has been positive for three consecutive quarters, starting from a return to profit in the first quarter of 2026. Standalone operating profit for the third quarter of 2026 was KRW 800 million.

The company credited production process efficiency and cost reduction through AI tools for the improvement. According to the company, the factors that weighed on VFX profitability, which contributed to the 2025 annual loss, are easing.

Overseas projects and stake-based expansion

The company said it signed a global OTT project contract with a Japanese content producer in the first quarter of 2026, with revenue recognition beginning in the second quarter. Its 33.26% stake in Norway's Gimpville, as of the end of the first half of 2026, is used as a base for European and North American business.

An independent research house said follow-on orders could increase if Chinese content demand expands, building on references from large Chinese projects.

09

Bear factors

Dependence on the subsidiary and order volatility

Standalone revenue for 2025 was KRW 10.8 billion, only part of consolidated revenue of KRW 132.4 billion. In the third quarter of 2026, standalone revenue was KRW 12.4 billion against consolidated revenue of KRW 97.6 billion.

A large share of consolidated results depends on the timing and size of AI infrastructure contracts. Revenue fell 26.8% from the prior quarter in the second quarter of 2026, showing how large the quarter-to-quarter swings can be.

Balance sheet and cash flow pressure

The debt-to-equity ratio at the end of 2025 was 113.6%, sharply higher than 48.2% in 2024. Total liabilities of KRW 49.8 billion exceed total equity of KRW 43.8 billion. Operating cash flow shifted from KRW 6.68 billion in 2023 to -KRW 6.72 billion in 2025.

The first-quarter 2026 consolidated operating loss of KRW 2.58 billion shows that profit recovery has been uneven from quarter to quarter.

Pace of the VFX industry recovery

The 2025 operating loss was explained by a combination of weakness in film and drama production and business expansion costs. Standalone operating profit turned positive in the first quarter of 2026, but the amount was only KRW 100 million.

The recovery in utilization at the Chuncheon filming facility is encouraging, but the business remains dependent on the content production cycle. If the industry recovery is slow, the profit contribution from the core VFX business could stay limited.

10

Risk factors

AI infrastructure investment cycle

Server demand at the subsidiary depends on expanding investment in AI data centers and cloud infrastructure. If that investment cycle slows or customers change their facility plans, the order outlook could shift.

The company is pursuing new customers, but the share of current orders tied to a small number of large customers is a source of earnings volatility.

Focus risk from broader business scope

At the 2026 general meeting, the company added business purposes such as interior design, software development and operation, and e-commerce. As the business scope widens, management resources could be split between the core VFX business and AI infrastructure.

In 2025, costs tied to business expansion were cited as a reason for weaker profitability. The cost burden of growing several businesses at once could keep weighing on earnings.

Overseas project timing and stake structure

Revenue from the global project with a Japanese content producer has begun to be recognized, but in China the company is still at the stage of discussing cooperation with local platforms. Overseas revenue timing can shift with project schedules and local production conditions.

Gimpville moved from a largest-shareholder position acquired in January 2025 to an associate with a 33.26% stake at the end of the first half of 2026, so how this change in ownership is reflected in consolidated results needs to be confirmed.

12

Overall view

M83's filed results moved from an annual loss in 2025 to a consolidated operating profit in the third quarter of 2026. Revenue has risen two years in a row since 2024, reaching KRW 132.4 billion in 2025 and KRW 97.6 billion in the third quarter of 2026 alone.

The growth engine is the AI infrastructure business of its subsidiary, while the standalone core VFX business has also stayed in profit since the first quarter of 2026.

Bullish factors are the expansion of AI infrastructure orders, improved VFX cost structure, and a four-quarter total of net income attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 4.33 billion, which is out of the loss zone.

Bearish factors are the heavy reliance of consolidated results on the subsidiary, a debt-to-equity ratio of 113.6% and negative operating cash flow in 2025, and the order volatility shown by the decline in second-quarter 2026 revenue from the prior quarter.

Because the third-quarter operating profit of KRW 8.76 billion is a single-quarter figure, the key checkpoint is whether this level holds in the fourth quarter and in the next annual report.

The company presents VFX profitability recovery and AI infrastructure expansion as its two growth pillars, and their contributions can be separated using standalone and consolidated figures. On valuation, a low multiple against net assets coexists with volatile earnings.
