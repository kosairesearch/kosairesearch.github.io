02

Business structure

Daelim Paper, founded in 1984, makes corrugated base paper and listed on KOSDAQ in 1994. Corrugated paper and base paper account for roughly 99% of revenue. In 2025, 97.58% of corrugated base paper sales were domestic and 2.42% were exported.

A January 2022 technical analysis report described a 71% paper and 29% board split at the end of 2020. In 2017 the company acquired the board business and Paju plant of its affiliate Sambo Corrugated Board, linking base paper to board production. Its main inputs are waste paper and pulp.

The same report estimated that group companies held about 90% of the corrugated base paper market. A past press report noted that intra-group transactions fell from the 60% range to 13%. Peers in the paper sector include Hansol Paper, Moorim P&P and Hankuk Paper MFG.

Demand depends on packaging volumes from corrugated box makers, so parcel and food shipments are the key driver.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual revenue was KRW 187.4 billion in 2022, KRW 164.4 billion in 2023 (down 12.3%), KRW 166.3 billion in 2024 (up 1.1%) and KRW 168.8 billion in 2025 (up 1.5%). The 2023 level was the smallest year in the period, revenue rose for two consecutive years from 2024 to 2025, and 2022 was the largest year.

Operating profit fell from KRW 29.2 billion in 2022 to KRW 21.8 billion in 2023 (down 25.2%), KRW 9.83 billion in 2024 (down 54.9%) and KRW 5.28 billion in 2025 (down 46.3%), a three-year decline from 2023 to 2025. The operating margin moved from 15.6% to 13.3%, 5.9% and 3.1%.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders went from KRW 24.4 billion in 2022 to KRW 18.8 billion, KRW 13.2 billion and KRW 6.4 billion, a decline in each year from 2023 to 2025. Net income including non-controlling interests followed the same path.

The company attributed the 2025 decline mainly to lower product selling prices and higher costs from a contribution to its employee welfare fund. Operating cash flow fell from KRW 31.4 billion in 2022 to KRW 23.2 billion in 2025, though it was above the KRW 16.9 billion of 2024.

In the third quarter of 2025 revenue was KRW 43.2 billion with operating profit of KRW 909 million. The fourth quarter swung to an operating loss of KRW 3.53 billion on revenue of KRW 43.1 billion.

Operating profit returned to KRW 563 million in the first quarter of 2026, then reached KRW 6.24 billion in the second quarter on revenue of KRW 47.6 billion. Third-quarter 2026 revenue was KRW 46.8 billion, up 8.4% year on year, and operating profit was KRW 3.71 billion, up 308.5% year on year. Net income attributable to controlling shareholders over the latest four quarters totaled KRW 8.28 billion.

05

Industry analysis

Corrugated base paper is tied to packaging demand from parcel delivery, food and e-commerce, so volumes at customer industries drive demand. Prices move with raw material costs such as waste paper and pulp and with supply conditions, so the gap between selling prices and costs sets the margin.

The company cited lower selling prices as a main cause of the 2025 decline, which points to price pressure weighing on profit. Peers in the paper sector include Hansol Paper, Moorim P&P and Hankuk Paper MFG.

The company secured part of its base paper outlets through its linked board business, but past press reports show intra-group transactions have fallen sharply, which means more of its sales now face market competition.

The market share estimate from the January 2022 report reflects that period and should not be read as a current share. Judging where the industry sits in its cycle requires tracking base paper selling prices, waste paper prices and domestic corrugated box demand together.

06

Outlook

The outlook currently rests on the 2026 quarterly earnings trend and the dividend policy. Operating profit recovered in the second and third quarters of 2026, so whether that carries into full-year results is the first point to watch.

Revenue was KRW 41.9 billion in the first quarter of 2026, down 2.8% from the prior quarter, then rose to KRW 47.6 billion in the second quarter and KRW 46.8 billion in the third. Third-quarter revenue grew 8.4% year on year, while annual revenue grew only 1.5% in 2025.

At the annual shareholder meeting, the financial statements for the 42nd fiscal year were approved and a cash dividend was passed, as disclosed on March 26, 2026. Whether dividends continue regardless of earnings swings and what the next shareholder meeting decides are the items to track.

The direction of the operating margin will depend on whether base paper selling prices recover from 2025 levels and how waste paper and pulp costs move.

08

Bull factors

Quarterly operating profit recovery

Operating profit reached KRW 6.24 billion in the second quarter of 2026, up 1,007.1% from the prior quarter, and KRW 3.71 billion in the third quarter, up 308.5% year on year.

The shift from an operating loss of KRW 3.53 billion in the fourth quarter of 2025 to profit from the first quarter of 2026 onward is the core of the bullish case. The improvement comes after the annual operating margin had fallen to 3.1%.

Financial capacity and cash generation

Operating cash flow rose to KRW 23.2 billion in 2025 from KRW 16.9 billion in 2024. Cash generation held up while earnings declined, which supports the case for a financial cushion.

Integrated base paper to board chain

In 2017 the company acquired the board business and Paju plant of its affiliate Sambo Corrugated Board, linking base paper and board in one chain. A January 2022 report cited this structure as an integrated-business advantage. Coordinating raw material sourcing and production within the group is seen as a buffer during price swings.

09

Bear factors

Long decline in operating margin

The operating margin fell from 15.6% in 2022 to 13.3% in 2023, 5.9% in 2024 and 3.1% in 2025. Operating profit dropped from KRW 29.2 billion in 2022 to KRW 5.28 billion in 2025.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders declined for three consecutive years from 2023 to 2025, so one recovering quarter does not yet establish a full-year turnaround.

Price pressure and cost burden

In the fourth quarter of 2025 it posted an operating loss of KRW 3.53 billion and a net loss attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 2.24 billion. Without a recovery in selling prices, cost savings alone may not restore the operating margin.

Stagnant revenue and low growth

Quarterly revenue ran at KRW 43.2 billion in the third quarter of 2025, KRW 43.1 billion in the fourth quarter and KRW 41.9 billion in the first quarter of 2026. It rose to KRW 47.6 billion in the second quarter and KRW 46.8 billion in the third, but annual revenue grew only 1.1% in 2024 and 1.5% in 2025. Limited sales growth could cap how far profit can recover.

10

Risk factors

Raw materials and selling prices

Margins can swing sharply with waste paper and pulp costs and with corrugated base paper selling prices. Lower selling prices were already cited as a main cause of the 2025 decline. The spread between input costs and selling prices is the key variable for the operating margin.

Intra-group trading structure

A past press report showed intra-group transactions falling from the 60% range to 13%. As outlets widen beyond the group, a larger share of sales faces direct price competition. Changes in the ownership structure and affiliate trading should be tracked through the related-party disclosures in periodic reports.

Small-cap share price volatility

As a small KOSDAQ company, its share price can move sharply when trading volume is thin. Quarterly earnings swing widely, so the share price may react strongly around results announcements.
