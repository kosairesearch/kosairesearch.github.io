From Paint Maker to Specialty Materials: Earnings Swings Remain the Test

SP Samhwa is widening its paint-centred business into semiconductor packaging materials and overseas markets, but quarterly profit swung from a sharp second-quarter operating recovery in 2026 to a third-quarter operating loss.

02

Business structure

SP Samhwa traces its roots to Dong Hwa Industrial, founded in 1946, and is a paint maker that changed its name from Samhwa Paint Industrial in March 2026.

Paint and chemical products accounted for KRW 320.9 billion of the KRW 321.9 billion consolidated sales in the first half of 2026, or about 99%, so the business relies heavily on a single segment.

By region, domestic sales were KRW 249.8 billion (78%) and overseas sales were KRW 72.0 billion (22%), although some reports put the overseas subsidiary share at about 6.5% in the first half, so the figure depends on the measure used.

The domestic core is repainting and industrial paint, and the company says high-value specialty lines such as fire-resistant coatings, automotive refinish paints and UV-curable coatings drove growth.

The semiconductor materials business took shape after the second half of 2018, when the company acquired Daerim Chemical, now Samhwa Daerim Chemical, for KRW 7.9 billion and gained electronic materials manufacturing technology.

Its epoxy molding compound (EMC) is a packaging material that protects semiconductors from heat, moisture and impact. In March 2026 the company supplied MMB, an intermediate of EMC, to Samsung SDI, which then supplies EMC to Samsung Electronics, linking SP Samhwa into that supply chain.

The paint business is tied to construction activity and repainting demand, so a prolonged downturn in domestic construction limits sales growth. Its raw materials include solvents, polyols, epoxy resins and titanium dioxide, which are exposed to crude oil prices and logistics costs.

04

Earnings analysis

In the annual figures from DART filings, sales went from KRW 646 billion in 2022 to KRW 631.4 billion in 2023 (down 2.3% year on year), KRW 628.3 billion in 2024 (down 0.5%) and KRW 617.1 billion in 2025 (down 1.8%).

Sales fell for three consecutive years from 2023 to 2025, with 2022 the largest year and 2025 the smallest in the 2022 to 2025 period.

Operating profit rose from KRW 19.9 billion in 2022 to KRW 25.8 billion in 2023 (up 30.1%), then fell to KRW 19 billion in 2024 (down 26.5%) and KRW 9.55 billion in 2025 (down 49.7%), declining for two consecutive years after the 2023 peak.

The operating margin was 3.1% in 2022, 4.1% in 2023, 3.0% in 2024 and 1.5% in 2025, the lowest of the four years, so the margin fell more sharply than sales. Press reports attributed the 2025 earnings decline to weak domestic construction activity that slowed paint demand.

Controlling-shareholder net income went from KRW 5.6 billion in 2022 to KRW 16.2 billion in 2023 (up 188.5%), then KRW 15.5 billion in 2024 (down 4.3%) and KRW 7.68 billion in 2025 (down 50.4%). Net income including non-controlling interests, which is a separate figure, was KRW 7.78 billion in 2025.

The debt-to-equity ratio fell from 95.9% in 2022 to 82.3% in 2025, when total equity was KRW 340.4 billion, of which KRW 340.1 billion belonged to controlling shareholders, and total liabilities were KRW 280.1 billion.

Operating cash flow was KRW 32.2 billion in 2022, KRW 43.6 billion in 2023, KRW 30.2 billion in 2024 and KRW 22.2 billion in 2025.

On a quarterly basis, operating profit was KRW 1.35 billion in the third quarter of 2025 and KRW 3.63 billion in the fourth quarter, then KRW 1.82 billion in the first quarter of 2026 (down 49.9% from the prior quarter).

In the second quarter of 2026 sales reached KRW 177.5 billion and operating profit KRW 13.8 billion (up 655.2% from the prior quarter), with controlling-shareholder net income of KRW 9.54 billion.

In the third quarter of 2026 sales were KRW 148.7 billion (down 4.4% year on year), and the company posted an operating loss of KRW 1.64 billion, a swing from profit to loss year on year, with a controlling-shareholder net loss of KRW 6.6 billion.

Media reports said the company explained that raw material prices that rose on Middle East tensions were passed into costs with a lag, and that it absorbed a large part of the increase itself.

Controlling-shareholder net income for the last four quarters, from the fourth quarter of 2025 through the third quarter of 2026, totaled KRW 6.46 billion.

05

Industry analysis

The paint business is sensitive to domestic construction activity. The company said its repainting business kept solid results in the first half of 2026 despite the construction slowdown. A prolonged downturn, however, makes it hard for repainting demand to translate into overall sales growth.

On the raw material side, prices of solvents, polyols, epoxy resins and titanium dioxide rose in the first half of 2026 on higher crude oil prices, supply chain instability and higher logistics costs.

The company is responding by diversifying suppliers and developing alternative raw materials, and it plans to raise the revenue share of high-performance product lines. Semiconductor packaging materials are linked to the rise in demand for advanced chips as AI spreads.

The company aims to extend EMC into packaging for AI server DRAM and NAND flash. Because EMC volumes move with the design wins and production plans of specific customers, rising customer concentration is a source of volatility when the industry changes.

In paint, the company is pursuing differentiation through specialty products such as fire-resistant coatings and automotive refinish paints. Its competitiveness therefore depends more on high-value products and accumulated materials technology than on the scale of commodity paint.

06

Outlook

At an event on October 16, 2026 marking its 80th anniversary, the company unveiled a ten-year medium-to-long-term plan. Its three core strategies are expanding strategic business segments, strengthening future R&D, and smart process innovation.

Under segment expansion, the company plans to upgrade the value chain of its paint business and reshape its portfolio around future growth potential. It targets raising the overseas subsidiary share of sales from about 6.5% in the first half of 2026 to 30% within ten years.

R&D focuses on applying core paint technology to functional and eco-friendly materials. In an April 2026 report, the company said a new EMC product developed in 2026 would be used in next-generation devices. The company aims to extend EMC applications to packaging for AI server DRAM and NAND flash.

On the production side, it plans to build a data-driven integrated management system and smart production systems to standardize quality control. On raw materials, the continuation of supplier diversification and alternative material development is a point to watch.

The next checkpoints are how far overseas sales share actually moves and how the scale of semiconductor materials sales shows up in future DART-reported results.

08

Bull factors

Expansion into semiconductor packaging materials

EMC is a core packaging material that protects semiconductors from heat, moisture and impact, and SP Samhwa entered the supply chain in March 2026 by supplying the intermediate MMB to Samsung SDI. An April 2026 report said the EMC is linked to chips used in Samsung Electronics devices.

The company aims to extend the material to AI server DRAM and NAND flash. Supply results from technology built since the 2018 acquisition are cited as a bullish factor.

Overseas restructuring and a first-half profit rebound

A half-year report covered in the press showed consolidated operating profit of KRW 15.6 billion in the first half of 2026, up 239% year on year. The company attributed the improvement to expanded fire-resistant coating sales at key overseas sites and large project wins.

The company also said overseas high-value specialty products exceeded targets in the third quarter of 2026. The goal of lifting overseas share to 30% within a decade is read as a direction for broader business expansion.

Balance sheet improvement and a return to quarterly profit

The debt-to-equity ratio fell from 95.9% in 2022 to 82.3% in 2025, and total equity at the end of 2025 was KRW 340.4 billion. Press reports said the company returned to operating profit in the first quarter of 2026, with KRW 1.82 billion, after an operating loss a year earlier.

Operating profit recovered to KRW 13.8 billion in the second quarter of 2026. Greater financial capacity can cushion the company when raw material costs rise.

Bear factors

Raw material and currency pressure led to a third-quarter loss

The company posted an operating loss of KRW 1.64 billion in the third quarter of 2026, and the controlling-shareholder net loss was KRW 6.6 billion. The company said raw material prices that rose on Middle East tensions reached costs with a lag.

Press reports said profitability weakened because the company absorbed a large part of the cost increase itself. Higher exchange rates were also cited as a cost pressure in the first quarter of 2026.

Core paint sales shrinking under a construction slowdown

Sales declined for three consecutive years, from KRW 646 billion in 2022 to KRW 617.1 billion in 2025. Operating profit fell 49.7% to KRW 9.55 billion in 2025. Press reports said prolonged weakness in domestic construction continued to slow paint demand. Stagnant paint sales are cited as the reason for diversification.

Quarterly earnings volatility and an unproven business shift

Quarterly operating profit swung widely, from KRW 1.82 billion in the first quarter of 2026 to KRW 13.8 billion in the second and an operating loss of KRW 1.64 billion in the third. Controlling-shareholder net income for the last four quarters totaled only KRW 6.46 billion.

The share of semiconductor materials in total sales is not yet clearly visible. More time is needed before the business shift shows up as a structural change in sales and profit.

10

Risk factors

Raw materials and FX

Prices of solvents, polyols, epoxy resins and titanium dioxide move with crude oil prices and logistics costs. The third-quarter 2026 loss was explained as the result of a lagged pass-through of raw material increases and partial absorption by the company. How far these increases can be passed on to customers will determine how quickly profit recovers.

Customer concentration and supply chain

Semiconductor materials are tied to a supply chain running through Samsung SDI to Samsung Electronics. Design changes or volume adjustments by a specific customer could reach the company's results quickly.

Because adoption of new EMC products and production plans depend on customer schedules, the durability of supply results needs to be checked.

Execution of the business transition

The ten-year plan pushes strategic segment expansion, R&D and smart process innovation at the same time. Lifting overseas share to 30% depends on order wins and investment results at local sites.

How far the name change and portfolio reshaping can offset weaker profitability in the core paint business remains an open question.

Overall view

SP Samhwa is in a transition, widening a paint business of 80 years into semiconductor packaging materials and overseas markets. In DART-reported results, sales have fallen for three consecutive years since 2023, and operating profit has declined for two consecutive years after its 2023 peak.

In 2026, the company returned to operating profit in the first quarter and recovered to KRW 13.8 billion in the second, but it posted an operating loss of KRW 1.64 billion in the third as raw material costs lagged.

Bullish factors include entry into semiconductor packaging supply chains, expansion of high-value overseas products and a lower debt-to-equity ratio. Bearish factors include weaker paint demand from the construction slowdown, raw material and currency pressure, and large swings in quarterly profit.

The price-to-book ratio sits well below 1x, while earnings-based multiples depend on whether quarterly profit recovers. Both sides deserve equal weight, and the key question is whether the business shift shows up as a structural change in sales and profit.
