02

Business structure

Robotis develops, manufactures and sells actuators (the core components that drive and control robot joints and movement), and also offers precision reducers, humanoid robot platforms, physical AI software and autonomous delivery robots.

Founded in 1999 and listed on KOSDAQ in 2018, the company is expanding overseas sales through its U.S. unit. Its flagship Dynamixel line has been used in research robots since its brand launch in 2003, and small general-purpose actuators now supply research robots and humanoid developers in Korea and abroad.

Shinhan Investment summarized that Robotis supplies actuators to about 70 customers worldwide. Daiwa Securities assessed that its lightweight actuators weighing roughly 100 to 200 grams deliver 3 to 11 N·m of torque and offer a better torque-to-weight ratio than rivals.

On the production side, the company makes most key parts in-house except motors, and plans to produce motors directly at its Uzbekistan plant. Management attributed 2025's improvement to expanding exports to the U.S. and other overseas markets along with stronger demand from downstream industries.

Samsung Securities pointed to high growth in China-bound sales, supported by price competitiveness, as a customer driver to watch. Actuator sales remain the center of the business, while the humanoid finished-goods and data businesses are at an early stage with operations targeted for 2027.

Competition is intensifying as the market grows, with Chinese and other overseas makers pressing hard, according to press coverage.

04

Earnings analysis

On a disclosed basis, revenue rose from KRW 25.9 billion in 2022 to KRW 29.1 billion in 2023 (up 12.7% year on year), KRW 30 billion in 2024 (up 3.1%) and KRW 38.9 billion in 2025 (up 29.6%).

Revenue has increased for three consecutive years from 2023 through 2025, with 2025 the largest year and 2022 the smallest in the 2022-2025 period.

Operating results moved from an operating loss of KRW 2.16 billion in 2022, to an operating loss of KRW 5.3 billion in 2023 (losses continued and widened), and an operating loss of KRW 2.97 billion in 2024 (losses continued but narrowed), before turning to an operating profit of KRW 3.35 billion in 2025.

The operating margin moved from -8.4%, -18.2% and -9.9% in 2022 to 2024 to 8.6% in 2025. According to press coverage, management credited the 2025 improvement to easing fixed-cost pressure as sales grew, lower exhibition and promotion spending, and government support.

Net loss attributable to controlling shareholders widened from KRW 260 million in 2022 to KRW 1.35 billion in 2023 and KRW 3.14 billion in 2024, before turning to a net profit of KRW 5.07 billion in 2025.

Net profit including non-controlling interests was KRW 5.3 billion in 2025, a separate figure from the profit attributable to controlling shareholders. Operating cash flow was KRW 12.5 billion in 2022, -KRW 4.75 billion in 2023, KRW 5.36 billion in 2024 and KRW 7.08 billion in 2025.

The debt-to-equity ratio fell from 45.0% in 2022 to 1.8% in 2025, with total equity of KRW 314.1 billion at the end of 2025 (KRW 312.9 billion attributable to controlling shareholders) and total liabilities of KRW 5.63 billion.

Quarterly revenue went from KRW 9.24 billion in the third quarter of 2025 to KRW 11.6 billion in the fourth quarter (up 25.5% quarter on quarter), KRW 11.8 billion in the first quarter of 2026 (up 2.1%), KRW 15.4 billion in the second quarter (up 29.8%) and KRW 13.5 billion in the third quarter (down 12.4% quarter on quarter, up 45.8% year on year).

Quarterly operating results swung from an operating profit of KRW 2.06 billion in the fourth quarter of 2025 to an operating loss of KRW 11.8 billion in the first quarter of 2026, which Shinhan-cited brokers Samsung Securities and Hanwha Investment & Securities described as a one-time cost from transferring treasury shares as employee performance pay.

An operating profit of KRW 1.98 billion in the second quarter was followed by an operating loss of KRW 2.61 billion in the third quarter.

Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 2.99 billion in the fourth quarter of 2025 and KRW 2.5 billion in the second quarter of 2026, while the first quarter of 2026 posted a loss of KRW 10.1 billion and the third quarter a loss of KRW 1.96 billion. Over the last four quarters, net loss attributable to controlling shareholders totaled KRW 6.53 billion.

05

Industry analysis

Shinhan Investment framed the second half of 2026 as the first year of humanoid volume production and pointed to bottlenecks in core components such as actuators and 3D-printed parts as an investment theme.

Background cited included Tesla's early Optimus production plans and Hyundai Motor Group's robot application center. Daiwa Securities identified fine hand manipulation as the bottleneck limiting real-world humanoid deployment and judged that competitiveness in hand components will shape market positions.

Kyobo Securities expected global humanoid businesses to follow a path similar to early electric vehicles, with Chinese makers expanding abroad, new entrants emerging and component suppliers becoming simple vendors.

Within this landscape, Robotis is focusing on mass production of small general-purpose actuators to lower costs. DS Investment assumed Chinese humanoid output of 260,000 units by 2030 and noted that stronger Chinese demand could lift Q-Series and general actuator sales above existing forecasts.

Competition from Chinese and other overseas makers remains a variable for Robotis as well. Kyobo Securities listed Robotis as a top pick in its sector in a June 29, 2026 report, citing its global customer references and expansion toward second-tier humanoid customers.

Hanwha Investment & Securities also flagged supply disruptions for high-spec motors as a production variable for the sector.

06

Outlook

The core of the capacity plan is the new Uzbekistan plant. According to a Newspim report dated August 5, 2026, the company is building the plant with operations targeted for the fourth quarter of 2026, and its initial annual capacity is about 200,000 units.

In an August 24, 2026 report, Hanwha Investment & Securities said capacity could expand tenfold to about 3 million units within five years from the existing level of roughly 300,000 units a year.

DS Investment reported that the company aims to add 1 million units of annual capacity each year from 2027 and reach 5 million units a year by 2031.

On products, the Q-Series humanoid actuators are central to volume production, and FnGuide's company summary says the company plans to launch AI Sapiens and Dynamixel-Q in the second half of 2026.

The AI Sapiens humanoid was released in November 2026 and will begin customer deliveries in 2027, with 23 Q-Series actuators installed, according to DS Investment's November 12, 2026 report. A data business is planned to start as a Data Factory in 2027, once the Uzbekistan plant stabilizes. Estimates diverge.

FnGuide's consensus, reported in August 2026, put 2026 revenue at KRW 64.6 billion and operating profit at KRW 2.4 billion, while Hanwha Investment & Securities estimated 2026 revenue at KRW 60.6 billion and 2027 revenue at KRW 120.2 billion.

Samsung Securities estimated 2027 revenue of KRW 94 billion and operating profit of KRW 16 billion, and 2028 revenue of KRW 117 billion and operating profit of KRW 19 billion, treating 2028 as the first full year of profit generation (August 19, 2026).

DS Investment expects an operating loss in 2026 that would be near breakeven excluding the one-time first-quarter cost, and an annual operating profit in 2027.

08

Bull factors

Shipment growth outlook

DS Investment projected that Robotis actuator shipments will rise from 430,000 units in 2026 to 1.01 million units in 2027, about 2.4 times higher (report dated November 12, 2026). Hanwha Investment & Securities estimated 2026 shipments at 500,000 units and 2027 shipments at 885,000 units, up 76% (August 24, 2026). The two brokers start from different 2026 bases, but both point to higher volume.

Capacity expansion

The new Uzbekistan plant targets operations in the fourth quarter of 2026, with initial annual capacity of about 200,000 units. Hanwha Investment & Securities expects capacity to expand from roughly 300,000 units a year to 3 million units within five years (August 24, 2026).

If the expansion reaches full operation, easing fixed-cost pressure from higher output is cited as a path to better profitability.

Diversifying customer references

Samsung Securities said the customer base for global references is diversifying and that China-bound sales are growing rapidly (August 19, 2026). The AI Sapiens humanoid carries 23 Q-Series actuators, which DS Investment described as the first case of Q-Series use in a mass-produced robot. A Data Factory starting in 2027 is also cited as a potential new revenue source beyond actuator sales.

09

Bear factors

Quarterly earnings volatility

After the KRW 11.8 billion operating loss in the first quarter of 2026, the third quarter posted a KRW 2.61 billion operating loss, so quarterly profitability has not been consecutive.

Even allowing for the one-time cost in the first quarter, the third-quarter loss sits outside that explanation, so the durability of earnings power will need to be judged from later quarters.

Net loss attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 653 million over the last four quarters suggests the pace of recovery could be gradual.

Motor supply and revenue cut

Hanwha Investment & Securities said supply disruptions for high-spec motors would tilt revenue growth toward lower-priced actuators, lowering its 2026 revenue estimate from KRW 66.3 billion to KRW 60.6 billion (August 24, 2026).

The company makes most components in-house but still depends on outside sourcing for motors. Supply disruptions could shift the mix away from high-spec products and slow the pace of margin improvement.

Competition and utilization risk

Competition from Chinese and other overseas makers is expected to intensify as the market grows, according to press coverage. DS Investment expects that after 2028, capacity expansion could outpace demand, pushing plant utilization into the 60% range and slowing margin improvement.

The Chinese expansion and component vendorization that Kyobo Securities anticipated could also add pricing pressure.

Risk factors

Customer concentration

DS Investment noted that more than half of the projected shipment volume comes from initial supply requests from a single humanoid developer. If that customer's mass-production schedule slips, the overall shipment plan could be affected. Delays in humanoid commercialization could also push actuator demand later than expected.

Capex and utilization

Plans to add capacity every year raise depreciation and fixed-cost burdens.

The company raised facility and operating funds through a rights offering to existing shareholders in 2025, and cash and cash equivalents reached KRW 60.9 billion at the end of March 2026, up from KRW 31 billion at the end of 2025 (Newspim, August 5, 2026 report). If capacity expansion runs ahead of demand, utilization could fall, which is the medium-term risk.

Supply chain and technology competition

Supply disruptions for high-spec motors tilted the sales mix toward lower-priced products. DS Investment reported plans to produce motors directly at the Uzbekistan plant, but technology and quality variables will remain until that plan runs stably. Competition from Chinese and other overseas makers could add pressure on both pricing and technology.

Overall view

Robotis's revenue has grown steadily since 2022, reaching KRW 38.9 billion in 2025, and operating profit of KRW 3.35 billion marked a return to profitability that year. Quarterly results have been volatile in 2026.

The first-quarter operating loss of KRW 11.8 billion was driven largely by a one-time cost, and after a second-quarter profit, the third quarter posted a KRW 2.61 billion operating loss.

Strength factors cited across several broker reports include higher actuator shipment expectations, the Uzbekistan capacity expansion and broader global customer references.

Weakness factors include high-spec motor supply disruptions, competition from Chinese and other overseas makers, the possibility of lower utilization after 2028 and concentration in one customer's volumes.

Broker estimates differ widely, with Hanwha Investment & Securities at KRW 60.6 billion and FnGuide consensus at KRW 64.6 billion for 2026 revenue, and Hanwha at KRW 120.2 billion and Samsung Securities at KRW 94 billion for 2027.

Net loss attributable to controlling shareholders totaled KRW 653 million over the last four quarters, so the continuity of earnings still needs confirmation.

The next checkpoints are whether the Uzbekistan plant starts in the fourth quarter, how Q-Series and AI Sapiens deliveries progress in 2027, and the full-year operating margin in the 2026 annual report. Broker target prices are each firm's own assumptions, and this report does not offer an investment opinion.
