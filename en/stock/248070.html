02

Business structure

Solum is a developer and manufacturer of electronic components centered on electronic shelf labels (ESL) and electronic parts, founded in 2015 and listed on the KOSPI market in 2021.

Building on its in-house technology and intellectual property, it runs overseas subsidiaries in China, Vietnam, Mexico and India to produce and sell products. Its ICT division's ESL business continues to grow as digital conversion accelerates, and Solum holds a global ESL share of about 30%, ranking second worldwide.

In Q3 2025, the electronic components unit reported revenue of KRW 308.5 billion and operating profit of KRW 5 billion. Its ESL customers are mainly global retailers, and deliveries to Amazon's Whole Foods are expanding.

Securities analysts describe the ESL business as a stable cash-generating base, supported by demand for electronic replacements of paper price tags. The ANP (Automotive & Power) unit leads new businesses, widening into high-output power modules for EV chargers and power modules for AI data centers.

The cosmetics business expanded after the acquisition of GDK Cosmetics in June 2025 and iCure in June 2026. In competition, France's VusionGroup is ranked first in ESL, and its market capitalization is far larger than Solum's. Solum is also in ongoing ESL supply discussions with W, the world's largest retailer.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual results peaked in 2023. Revenue rose from KRW 1.69 trillion in 2022 to KRW 1.95 trillion in 2023, up 15.1% year on year, then fell 18.3% to KRW 1.59 trillion in 2024 before recovering 6.6% to KRW 1.7 trillion in 2025.

Operating profit was KRW 154.5 billion in 2023, the highest of the 2022 to 2025 period, and then declined for two straight years, to KRW 69.1 billion in 2024 and KRW 46.5 billion in 2025. The operating margin was 4.5% in 2022, 7.9% in 2023, 4.3% in 2024 and 2.7% in 2025.

According to an iM Securities analysis, the gross margin improved 0.2 percentage points from 2023 to 2025, but the SG&A ratio worsened by 5.3 percentage points, which weakened profitability.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 120.4 billion in 2023, KRW 39.5 billion in 2024 and KRW 13.4 billion in 2025, falling for two straight years after the peak.

Net income including non-controlling interests was KRW 14.2 billion in 2025, a separate figure from the controlling-shareholder measure. Operating cash flow turned to a KRW 5.33 billion outflow in 2024 before recovering to KRW 54.6 billion in 2025.

The debt-to-equity ratio fell from 256.7% in 2022 to 126.8% in 2025, with total liabilities of KRW 713.8 billion and total equity of KRW 562.8 billion at the end of 2025. Quarterly results recovered from the Q4 2025 low, when revenue was KRW 427.7 billion and operating profit KRW 8.06 billion.

Operating profit was KRW 21.7 billion in Q1 2026, up 168.8% from the prior quarter, KRW 21.8 billion in Q2 and KRW 17.5 billion in Q3, down 19.9% quarter on quarter but up 18.5% year on year.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders swung from a KRW 7.07 billion loss in Q4 2025 to profits in Q1 and Q2 2026, then turned to a KRW 15.9 billion loss in Q3 2026. The four quarters from Q4 2025 through Q3 2026 total a KRW 12.5 billion net loss attributable to controlling shareholders.

The items that separate operating profit from bottom-line results are the central point to track in this report.

05

Industry analysis

The ESL market is growing as demand to replace paper price tags with electronic labels expands, led by global retailers. iM Securities expects ESL revenue to recover from a trough of KRW 450 billion in 2024 to KRW 530 billion in 2025 and KRW 670 billion in 2026.

In the same analysis, ICT operating profit was projected to rise from KRW 38.2 billion in 2025 to KRW 66.7 billion in 2026 and KRW 94.2 billion in 2027. However, a slow recovery in mature European markets was flagged as a weak point. In North America, long-term projects with large retailers are becoming visible.

KB Securities estimated that Q1 2026 ESL revenue would rise about 40% year on year. In competition, Solum ranks second with a share of about 30%, and the gap with first-ranked VusionGroup is discussed in terms of market capitalization and earnings structure.

Industry analysts attribute that gap mainly to differences in how the business model and portfolio are valued rather than to revenue scale. The electronic components unit was expected to stay weak because of falling market share among Korean TV set makers and intensifying supply-chain competition.

EV charging infrastructure and AI data-center power solutions are cited as growth drivers. By cycle position, ESL is in a recovery phase, while electronic components and new businesses are still at the validation stage.

06

Outlook

The company has said its DC-DC converter business targets sales of about KRW 200 billion in 2027. The product is a high-voltage power control module installed inside the battery backup units that prevent server shutdowns during outages or voltage swings at AI data centers.

Demonstration work with a global CSP customer is mostly complete, but some sales-price negotiations remain before a supply contract is finalized.

For EV chargers, the 30kW power module has been followed by a 50kW model that has passed European and US certification, and a second plant in Mexico is expanding North American supply.

An April 2026 report said the ANP unit planned to enter automotive electronics in the first half of 2026 and widen into AI data-center power modules and EV chargers in the second half. In ESL supply talks with W, the world's largest retailer, the company expects visible results in the first half of 2027.

According to a Mirae Asset Securities report, the cosmetics business is projected to earn operating profit of KRW 13 billion in 2026, KRW 20 billion in 2027 and KRW 24 billion in 2028. A report cited in an early November 2026 article projected 2026 revenue of KRW 1.998 trillion and operating profit of KRW 79 billion.

That differs from the iM Securities forecast reported in February 2026 of KRW 1.8856 trillion in revenue and KRW 72 billion in operating profit. On a disclosed basis, Q3 2026 operating profit fell from Q2, so the gap between forecasts and the actual quarterly path needs to be checked against the next periodic report.

08

Bull factors

ESL share and retail references

Solum ranks second globally in ESL with a share of about 30%, and expanding deliveries to Whole Foods and cooperation with large North American retailers are cited as its base. iM Securities expects ESL revenue to recover to KRW 670 billion in 2026 after the 2024 trough. On profits, Q1 2026 operating profit rose 168.8% from the prior quarter to KRW 21.7 billion.

New business pipeline

The 50kW EV charger power module has passed European and US certification, and a second Mexico plant is expanding North American supply. The company has said it targets KRW 200 billion in 2027 sales from DC-DC converters for AI data centers. If these businesses convert to revenue, the ESL-centered structure would broaden.

Shareholder returns and governance

The company set a policy to return 20% to 30% of separate-basis net income to shareholders through 2026. Treasury share cancellation and a first dividend have been carried out.

CEO Jeon Seong-ho bought about KRW 21.1 billion of treasury shares directly, and the KRW 140 billion preferred stock was issued without a repricing clause.

09

Bear factors

Incomplete profit recovery

The 2025 operating margin of 2.7% was the lowest of 2022 to 2025, and an iM Securities analysis showed the SG&A ratio worsened by 5.3 percentage points since 2023. Q3 2026 operating profit of KRW 17.5 billion was accompanied by a KRW 15.9 billion net loss attributable to controlling shareholders. The four quarters through Q3 2026 total a KRW 12.5 billion net loss.

New businesses not yet proven in revenue

For DC-DC converters, sales-price negotiations remain before a supply contract is finalized. The ESL supply talks with W are at a stage where visible results are expected only in the first half of 2027.

The cosmetics business rests largely on forecasts following acquisitions, so its proven earnings scale remains limited.

Slow European recovery and a dominant leader

The slow recovery in mature European markets was flagged as a disappointment even in iM Securities' analysis. The market capitalization gap with VusionGroup, the global ESL leader, is attributed to differences in earnings structure.

If the recovery path after the 2024 trough diverges from forecasts, the overall ESL revenue assumptions could be affected.

10

Risk factors

Balance sheet and cash flow

Total liabilities at the end of 2025 were KRW 713.8 billion, and the debt-to-equity ratio was 126.8%. Operating cash flow turned to a KRW 5.33 billion outflow in 2024. With the KRW 140 billion preferred stock, treasury share cancellation and dividends running in parallel, the cash allocation burden could increase.

New business execution and certification

The KRW 200 billion 2027 sales target for DC-DC converters depends on finalizing supply contracts and securing mass-production sites. The company has not specified production capacity or plant locations.

EV charger power modules have passed certification, but the pace of North American supply growth depends on customer orders.

Currency, overseas production and customer concentration

Production is spread across China, Vietnam, Mexico and India, so the company is exposed to currency moves and local cost changes. Securities analysts saw the exchange-rate environment as a positive contributor to earnings.

Because ESL revenue depends on a limited number of global retailers, changes in large customers' orders could affect results.

Overall view

Solum's ESL business has been in a recovery phase since its 2024 trough, and revenue has risen for three consecutive quarters from Q1 through Q3 2026. Net income attributable to controlling shareholders was profitable in Q1 and Q2 2026, but Q3 2026 turned to a KRW 15.9 billion net loss.

On an annual basis, operating profit has fallen for two straight years after peaking in 2023, and the 2025 operating margin was 2.7%.

The key new businesses are the certification of EV charger power modules and the KRW 200 billion 2027 sales target for DC-DC converters, and neither has yet been confirmed through signed contracts or reported sales.

The share price trades below 1x book value, and the PER cannot be calculated because the last four quarters show a net loss. The next checkpoints are the 2026 annual business report and the visible results from ESL supply talks with W in the first half of 2027.
