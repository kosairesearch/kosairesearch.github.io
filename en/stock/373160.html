Report overview

Back in the Red After One Profitable Year, Mix Shifting Overseas

Day1 Company turned to a KRW 4.57 billion operating profit in 2025, but posted operating losses in the second and third quarters of 2026 as consumer course sales weakened and lease and depreciation costs rose, while overseas and enterprise businesses reached about a third of revenue.

  1. 1

    Full-year 2025 operating profit of KRW 4.57 billion and controlling-shareholder net income of KRW 4.74 billion marked a turn to profit, but operating losses have continued since the second quarter of 2026.

  2. 2

    B2C course sales were KRW 41.29 billion in January to September 2026, or 49.57% of revenue, while total revenue fell 7.4% year on year.

  3. 3

    Overseas and enterprise businesses together reached 33.22% of revenue in January to September 2026, gradually spreading revenue beyond individual courses.

  4. 4

    The third-quarter 2026 operating loss of KRW 3.46 billion is attributed to higher depreciation and lease interest from a new long-term office lease, combined with rising payroll costs.

  5. 5

    Operating cash flow reached about KRW 10.5 billion in January to September 2026, up 27.7% year on year, so cash generation improved even as book losses widened.

02

Business structure

Day1 Company is a full-stack producer of adult education content, with revenue split into four segments: consumer (B2C) online courses, enterprise training (B2B), public-sector training (B2G), and overseas (global) operations.

B2C is the largest segment, selling courses from Fastcampus, Colosso and Zerobase to individual learners, and it generated KRW 41.29 billion, or 49.57% of revenue, in January to September 2026.

According to the company's first-quarter 2026 release, B2B revenue was KRW 2.8 billion (up about 24% year on year), B2G was KRW 7.1 billion (up about 97%), and global was KRW 5.1 billion (up about 33%).

Day1 Company Japan's revenue for January to September 2026 was KRW 1.14 billion, up 41.8% year on year, and it recorded a net profit.

The company emphasizes a one-source multi-use (OSMU) model that reuses one piece of content across many channels, and said the B2B and B2G segments reached a 60.8% contribution margin in the third quarter of 2025.

Japan accounted for about 61% of cumulative global revenue of KRW 14.3 billion through September 2025, so reliance on that market is high.

In August 2026 the company acquired a 56.33% stake in Spacewhy, an enterprise AI transformation consulting firm, extending its business into building AI systems inside corporate operations.

In individual online courses, competition centers on securing instructors and platforms, and the company cites its reuse of in-house content across languages and channels as a cost advantage.

04

Earnings analysis

On an annual basis, revenue rose from KRW 116.6 billion in 2023 to KRW 127.7 billion in 2024 (up 9.5% year on year) and then fell to KRW 123.9 billion in 2025 (down 3.0%). Among 2023 to 2025, 2024 was the largest year and 2023 the smallest.

Operating profit moved from KRW 1.12 billion in 2023 to an operating loss of KRW 287 million in 2024, which the company attributed to one-off legal and accounting costs tied to its listing; 2025 then returned to an operating profit of KRW 4.57 billion, which one report described as the highest ever.

The operating margin went from 1.0% in 2023 to -0.2% in 2024 and 3.7% in 2025.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders moved from a loss of KRW 7.91 billion in 2023 and a loss of KRW 1.61 billion in 2024, which narrowed the loss, to a profit of KRW 4.74 billion in 2025; net income including non-controlling interests was KRW 4.74 billion in 2025 as well.

The company-wide contribution margin exceeded 50% for the first time in 2025, and the 53.1% contribution margin in the first quarter of 2026 was described by the company as a record high.

By quarter, third-quarter 2025 revenue of KRW 33.7 billion and operating profit of KRW 3.43 billion were reported as the highest quarterly operating profit on record, and fourth-quarter 2025 revenue of KRW 33.9 billion was the peak.

Revenue then fell for three consecutive quarters: KRW 30.3 billion in the first quarter of 2026, KRW 26.8 billion in the second, and KRW 26.2 billion in the third.

Operating profit was KRW 388 million in the first quarter of 2026, followed by operating losses of KRW 3.04 billion in the second quarter and KRW 3.46 billion in the third. For January to September 2026, revenue was KRW 83.28 billion, down 7.4% year on year, and the cumulative operating loss was KRW 6.12 billion.

On costs, payroll rose 15.4% from new subsidiaries and AI hiring, and a new long-term office lease lifted right-of-use assets from KRW 6.03 billion to KRW 19.56 billion and lease liabilities from KRW 6.82 billion to KRW 20.88 billion. Depreciation and amortization rose 16.9% to KRW 10.06 billion.

At year-end 2025, total equity was KRW 45.4 billion, all attributable to controlling shareholders, and total liabilities were KRW 36.4 billion; the debt ratio was reported as -212.1% in 2023, 157.8% in 2024 and 80.3% in 2025.

Operating cash flow was KRW 13.5 billion in 2023, KRW 2.99 billion in 2024 and KRW 8.28 billion in 2025. Controlling-shareholder net losses for the last four quarters (fourth quarter 2025 to third quarter 2026) totaled KRW 4.47 billion.

05

Industry analysis

The company identified the contraction of the consumer online education market as the main cause of weaker third-quarter 2026 results, and standalone revenue at the parent fell 25.8% year on year in that quarter. Signs of recovery in individual courses have not yet appeared in the 2026 quarterly revenue trend.

Enterprise demand is firmer: B2B revenue rose about 24% in the first quarter of 2026, and the company said contracts signed over the prior six months grew about 19%, which it presents as a leading indicator for B2B revenue.

B2G revenue rose about 97% in the same quarter, so public and enterprise demand is partly offsetting the softness in individual courses. Overseas demand in Japan has a distinctive profile.

A user analysis of the Colosso Japan site that the company released in September 2025 showed the highest share of users aged 18 to 24, followed by those aged 25 to 34, and the company linked this to young Japanese adults who prioritize quick career decisions and practical skills.

Day1 Company Japan's 41.8% revenue growth in January to September 2026 fits that pattern. On the supply side, the company says AI-based automation of subtitling, translation and dubbing has cut production costs. Whether this is a lasting cost advantage will show up in whether the contribution margin holds.

Among competitors, instructor and platform access and the efficiency of reusing content are the key variables.

Because B2C still makes up close to half of revenue, the company remains sensitive to the consumer market environment, and the position in the cycle will be gauged by how individual-course revenue moves over the coming quarters.

06

Outlook

At the end of 2025 the company said it plans to return at least 30% of consolidated net income over the next three years through share buybacks and cancellations and cash dividends. In 2026 it bought treasury shares worth KRW 1.9 billion, and in September it cancelled 224,999 shares, worth about KRW 950 million.

The 56.33% stake in Spacewhy, acquired in August 2026, is a vehicle for scaling the enterprise AI transformation business, so how much that acquisition contributes to consolidated results is a checkpoint.

The company has previously described plans to add six overseas markets, including Canada, the UK, France, Australia and Thailand, and Day1 Company Japan recorded a profit in January to September 2026.

Because the company presents contracts signed over the prior six months as a leading indicator for B2B revenue, how that figure feeds into B2B revenue from the fourth quarter of 2026 onward is another checkpoint.

Fixed costs, which the company cited as a main driver of the quarterly losses, are hard to absorb unless revenue recovers. Right-of-use assets and lease liabilities already rose sharply by September 2026, so how depreciation and lease interest flow into quarterly results is also a checkpoint.

The next confirmed reporting milestones are the 2026 annual report, which will contain fourth-quarter 2026 results, and the first-quarter 2027 quarterly report.

08

Bull factors

Growth in overseas and enterprise segments

The company reported that first-quarter 2026 B2B revenue rose about 24%, B2G about 97%, and global about 33%. Day1 Company Japan grew revenue 41.8% in January to September 2026 and recorded a net profit.

Overseas and enterprise businesses together reached 33.22% of revenue in the same period, easing reliance on individual courses.

Improving contribution margin

The company-wide contribution margin exceeded 50% for the first time in 2025. The company described the 53.1% contribution margin in the first quarter of 2026 as a record high. It attributes the gain to its OSMU model, which lowers instructor, production and marketing costs by reusing content across channels.

Financial stability and shareholder returns

Two domestic credit rating agencies upgraded the company's ratings in April 2026. Cash and cash equivalents at the end of September 2026 were KRW 10.6 billion, up from KRW 1.6 billion at the end of 2025.

The company has said it will return at least 30% of net income over three years and has already carried out treasury share purchases and cancellations in 2026.

09

Bear factors

Shrinking consumer course sales

Revenue for January to September 2026 fell 7.4% year on year, and standalone parent revenue dropped 25.8% in the third quarter. B2C still accounts for 49.57% of revenue, close to half. The company itself pointed to contraction in the consumer online education market as the main cause of weaker results.

Fixed costs and lease burden

Payroll rose 15.4% in January to September 2026 from new subsidiaries and AI hiring. A long-term office lease more than tripled right-of-use assets and lease liabilities. Depreciation and amortization rose 16.9% and lease interest rose 194%, which pulled operating results lower while revenue was shrinking.

Consecutive operating losses that widened

After an operating loss of KRW 3.04 billion in the second quarter of 2026, the third-quarter operating loss widened to KRW 3.46 billion. Controlling-shareholder net loss for the third quarter was KRW 4.14 billion.

For the last four quarters, the combined controlling-shareholder net loss was KRW 4.47 billion, which reversed much of the 2025 annual profit gain.

Risk factors

Business and market concentration

Individual online courses still account for close to half of revenue, and about 61% of global revenue came from Japan as of September 2025. A slowdown in consumer demand or a shift in Japanese demand could weigh on results at the same time.

Whether the content reuse model delivers the same efficiency in new markets will show up in quarterly contribution margins.

Acquisition and integration

In August 2026 the company acquired a 56.33% stake in Spacewhy, an enterprise AI transformation consulting firm. How much profit that business contributes and how integration costs flow into consolidated results is not yet visible in the reported figures.

New subsidiaries were cited as one driver of higher payroll, so cost management after the deal is a point to watch.

Financial capacity and shareholder returns

Total liabilities at the end of 2025 were KRW 36.4 billion, and lease liabilities reached KRW 20.88 billion by September 2026. Because the planned return of at least 30% of net income depends on earnings, continued losses would constrain that plan. How the scale of buybacks and cancellations adjusts alongside the loss trend is a point to monitor.

12

Overall view

Day1 Company moved from a net loss in 2023 to a narrower loss in 2024, and then to a 2025 operating profit of KRW 4.57 billion and controlling-shareholder net income of KRW 4.74 billion.

In 2026, however, revenue fell for three consecutive quarters, to KRW 30.3 billion, KRW 26.8 billion and KRW 26.2 billion, and operating results swung from a first-quarter profit to losses in the second and third quarters.

The decline is attributed to weaker consumer online course sales and higher fixed costs tied to office leases and payroll. By contrast, overseas and enterprise businesses reached 33.22% of revenue in January to September 2026, and Day1 Company Japan as well as the B2B and B2G segments continued to grow.

On the balance sheet, the debt ratio fell to 80.3% at the end of 2025, cash rose from the start of the year to the end of September 2026, and credit ratings were upgraded in April 2026.

However, controlling-shareholder net losses over the last four quarters totaled KRW 4.47 billion, and lease and depreciation burdens have grown. The business structure is in transition, and profitability shows recovery and deterioration side by side. The next confirmed checkpoints are the 2026 annual report and the first-quarter 2027 quarterly report.
