02

Business structure

Farmsco was founded in 1999 through a spin-off and runs feed manufacturing and sales, meat processing, hog farming and solar power businesses. Feed is the core business, and in the third quarter of 2023 the company's IR data put hog feed at 51.32% of domestic feed sales.

Feed sales volume rose from 1.53 million tons in 2022 to 1.61 million tons in 2023, according to company IR materials. A first-half 2026 performance summary attributed the feed segment's improvement to higher sales of hog feed and better product quality.

The processed food unit has contributed to sales growth through its Hipork brand meat and premium product strategy. The livestock unit raises and sells animals, and the company promotes its large domestic breeding farm and 3-site system as strengths.

Overseas, it operates a corn drying facility and a feed plant in Indonesia and set up PT CAHAYA TECHNOLOGY UNGGAS to widen its reach. Domestically, the Jeongeup plant is the main production base and has supplied more than 500,000 tons a year since 2023. In competition, Farmsco faces large livestock-linked feed players, including Harim.

04

Earnings analysis

On a disclosed basis, revenue fell from KRW 1.9 trillion in 2022 to KRW 1.55 trillion in 2025, declining for three consecutive years from 2023 (2022 was the largest year in the period).

Over the same span, operating profit rose from KRW 18.8 billion to KRW 80.2 billion and has increased for three consecutive years from 2023 (2025 was the largest year). Operating margin moved up in steps, from 1.0% in 2022 to 1.7%, 3.4% and 5.2% in 2025.

The company attributed the 2025 improvement to better results at subsidiaries, stable grain and pork carcass prices, and lower financing costs as currency volatility eased.

Profit attributable to owners went from net losses of KRW 32.8 billion in 2022, KRW 34.2 billion in 2023 and KRW 70.7 billion in 2024 to a profit of KRW 40.9 billion in 2025, a turnaround to the black. Net profit including non-controlling interests, a separate figure, was KRW 41.8 billion for 2025.

At end-2025, total liabilities were KRW 850.1 billion against total equity of KRW 223.4 billion, and the debt ratio fell from 606.8% in 2024 to 380.6%. Operating cash flow improved from -KRW 82.1 billion in 2022 to KRW 53.1 billion in 2025.

Quarterly operating profit went from KRW 27.5 billion in the third quarter of 2025 to KRW 15.7 billion in the fourth quarter (down 42.8% from the prior quarter), then KRW 19.7 billion, KRW 20.3 billion and KRW 19.2 billion across the first three quarters of 2026.

Third-quarter 2026 revenue of KRW 435.8 billion marked a second straight quarterly increase, while operating profit fell 30.0% year on year. Profit attributable to owners of KRW 29.7 billion in the same quarter was well above operating profit, so non-operating and one-off items need to be checked. Profit attributable to owners over the latest four quarters totals KRW 10.6 billion.

05

Industry analysis

The domestic livestock cycle is pressured by a shrinking animal herd, which drives feed demand. A report covering the third quarter of 2025 cited the decline in domestic livestock numbers and a high-exchange-rate environment as reasons feed sales slowed.

On the cost side, a strong dollar and high grain prices have weighed on production. In describing record monthly output at its Jeongeup plant in October 2026, the company also pointed to currency and grain price pressure and aging equipment.

The company said stable grain and pork carcass prices supported the 2025 profit improvement. That suggests margins rose in a period when feed costs and pork prices were both favorable. Still, where the industry sits in its cycle is hard to pin down, and this reading relies on the company's own explanation.

In competition, the contest for feed share with Harim is a key variable. Company IR materials said in the first quarter of 2024 that its share in the market structure against the Harim group had kept rising. Overseas, the Indonesia business is being pursued as a base for expanding production.

Outlook

The most concrete plan the company has stated is its expectation that annual output at the Jeongeup plant will top 560,000 tons. The Jeongeup plant produced 50,029 tons of compound feed in October 2026, a record monthly figure for the site.

A late-August 2026 report said the company began its 'Challenge 153' push in September 2026 to expand feed sales in the second half. On expansion, the Boryeong feed plant passed 7,000 tons a month within six months of opening, according to a December 2025 report, and the Indonesia push continues.

On financing, the guarantees disclosed on November 9, 2026 are a key variable. They include a KRW 36.0 billion guarantee for borrowers from BNK Capital (valid until March 17, 2027) and a guarantee of KRW 13.4 billion for borrowings of an Indonesian affiliate (from November 24, 2026 to January 27, 2028).

In January 2026, the company decided to lend KRW 14.7 billion to its Indonesian subsidiary to stabilize its financial structure. A securities data summary reported a net loss for the first half of 2026, so whether the third-quarter turnaround to profit holds is the next point to watch.

The 2026 annual business report, the next regular filing, will show fourth-quarter results and whether the annual output target was reached.

08

Bull factors

Turnaround to profit and recovering earnings power

Operating profit for 2025 rose 48.7% to KRW 80.2 billion, with operating margin reaching 5.2%. Profit attributable to owners turned positive at KRW 40.9 billion, and operating cash flow improved to KRW 53.1 billion. Compared with the years of losses, earnings power has moved in a recovering direction.

Record feed production and sales momentum

The Jeongeup plant produced 50,029 tons of compound feed in October 2026, a monthly record for the site. The company expects annual output to exceed 560,000 tons. Third-quarter 2026 revenue rose 11.0% year on year to KRW 435.8 billion.

Improving debt ratio and overseas expansion

The debt ratio fell from 606.8% in 2024 to 380.6% in 2025. In Indonesia, the company runs a corn drying facility and a feed plant and has set up a local company to widen its reach. The Boryeong feed plant also scaled up production quickly after opening.

09

Bear factors

Quarterly operating profit declines and volatility

Operating profit fell to KRW 15.7 billion in the fourth quarter of 2025, down 42.8% from the prior quarter. Third-quarter 2026 operating profit of KRW 19.2 billion was down 30.0% year on year. Revenue has grown in some quarters while profit has not kept pace.

Cumulative losses and a thin net profit base

Profit attributable to owners posted net losses from 2022 through 2024, and even after the 2025 turnaround the latest four quarters total only KRW 10.6 billion. The first and second quarters of 2026 both recorded net losses. With a thin earnings base, a single quarter can shift the overall picture.

Shrinking herds and cost pressure

A shrinking domestic livestock herd and a high-exchange-rate environment were cited as reasons feed sales slowed. A strong dollar and high grain prices raise production costs. The company itself also named aging equipment as a burden on production.

10

Risk factors

Contingent liabilities and borrowing burden

Total liabilities at end-2025 were KRW 850.1 billion, far above total equity of KRW 223.4 billion. Total guarantees outstanding, including the KRW 36.0 billion BNK Capital-related guarantee disclosed on November 9, 2026, reached KRW 275.2 billion, which is more than equity. A larger guarantee book would add to the financial burden.

Derivatives and currency risk

Cumulative derivatives trading losses of KRW 4.3 billion and valuation losses of KRW 12.2 billion for the first nine months of 2026 were disclosed. The company stated that the valuation losses do not involve a cash outflow. If currency and raw material volatility widens, trading losses could continue.

Industry and production asset risk

When shrinking herds, a strong dollar and rising grain prices occur together, feed demand and costs come under pressure at the same time. Aging equipment at plants such as Jeongeup could lead to production disruptions and higher maintenance costs. The 2025 improvement relied on stable grain and carcass prices, so sensitivity to price swings remains.

12

Overall view

Farmsco's operating profit and operating margin have improved in steps since 2022, and profit attributable to owners turned positive in 2025. Production records and revenue growth have continued into 2026, so the business trend is recovering.

On the other side, third-quarter 2026 operating profit fell year on year, and profit attributable to owners over the latest four quarters totals only KRW 10.6 billion. Total liabilities are more than three times equity, and total guarantees outstanding exceed equity.

Cumulative derivatives losses and swings in herd size, currency and grain prices also remain. The strengths are the earnings recovery and the improving debt ratio, and the weaknesses are earnings volatility and contingent liabilities.

The next points to check are the preliminary 2026 annual results and, in the 2026 annual business report, non-operating items and changes in the guarantee balance.
