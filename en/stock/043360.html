02

Business structure

Digital Graphics Incorporation's core business is industrial inkjet digital printers, with equipment sales and ink and parts sales together forming revenue.

The product mix in a Wisereport company profile (no reference date shown in the table) is ink 43.61%, textile printers 23.87%, parts and other 16.30%, signage printers 7.51%, and other 8.71%.

The product lines are UV and latex signage printers, food printers under the Sweet Box brand, and digital presses under the PRESTO brand. Food printers are described as holding a leading position in Korea, while digital presses target custom short-run printing in packaging.

In 2023 the company acquired d.gen to broaden its eco-friendly textile printing line, and it operates branches in the United States and Italy. Export shares differ widely by product: as of December 2025, textile printers were 91.97% exported, signage printers 90.55%, ink 71.13%, and digital presses 71.87%.

In the same table, food printers were 98.92% domestic, and real estate leasing in the other category was 92.06% domestic. R&D spending in 2025 was KRW 1.72 billion, or 8.06% of revenue. As of June 2026 the company had 67 employees, and the largest shareholder changed from Choi Kwan-soo to Choi Dong-ho in May 2025.

Because its scale is small and its focus is custom short-run printing and food and packaging applications, its business structure differs from that of large electronic component makers.

Earnings analysis

Annual revenue rose from KRW 16.1 billion in 2022 to KRW 16.7 billion in 2023 (up 3.7%), KRW 20.4 billion in 2024 (up 22.3%), and KRW 21.3 billion in 2025 (up 4.4%), increasing three years in a row from 2023 to 2025, with 2025 the largest year in the 2022 to 2025 period.

Operating profit of KRW 222 million in 2022 turned into an operating loss of KRW 2.67 billion in 2023, a swing from profit to loss. The loss widened to KRW 2.74 billion in 2024 and narrowed to KRW 767 million in 2025. Operating margin moved from 1.4% in 2022 to -16.0% in 2023, -13.4% in 2024, and -3.6% in 2025.

Losses continued in 2024 and 2025 even as revenue grew, which suggests cost and expense burdens offset the sales gains, but the line-item detail should be checked in the regular report notes.

Controlling net income was KRW 688 million in 2022, then a controlling net loss of KRW 2.15 billion in 2023, KRW 1.9 billion in 2024, and KRW 630 million in 2025, a narrowing trend after 2023. Total net income, including non-controlling interests, followed the same path, with a net loss of KRW 630 million in 2025.

Operating cash flow was KRW 2.39 billion in 2022 and minus KRW 3.27 billion in 2023, then turned positive at KRW 720 million in 2024 and stayed positive at KRW 433 million in 2025.

On a quarterly basis, revenue fell from KRW 5.27 billion in the third quarter of 2025 to KRW 5 billion in the fourth quarter (down 5.1% from the prior quarter), KRW 4.58 billion in the first quarter of 2026 (down 8.4%), and KRW 4.53 billion in the second quarter (down 1.2%), before reaching KRW 3.67 billion in the third quarter of 2026 (down 18.9% from the prior quarter and down 30.4% from a year earlier).

The operating loss was KRW 35.97 million in the third quarter of 2025, KRW 367 million in the fourth quarter, and KRW 298 million in the first quarter of 2026, then widened to KRW 343 million in the second quarter and KRW 758 million in the third quarter of 2026.

Controlling net income of KRW 114 million in the third quarter of 2025 turned into a controlling net loss of KRW 309 million in the fourth quarter, and the controlling net loss was KRW 1.1 billion in the third quarter of 2026, a swing from profit a year earlier.

The controlling net loss over the last four quarters totaled KRW 1.59 billion. The reason the third-quarter 2026 net loss exceeded the operating loss cannot be pinned down from the quarterly figures alone, so non-operating items need to be checked in the regular report.

Industry analysis

Digital Graphics Incorporation is classified in the electronics and components sector, but its revenue centers on industrial printers, so its link to sector indicators from peers such as Samsung Electro-Mechanics and LG Innotek is indirect.

Because export demand makes up a large share of sales, the won-dollar and won-euro exchange rates and overseas printing and advertising demand affect revenue directly. As of December 2025, textile printers and signage printers were each more than 90% exported.

Signage printers are described as expanding into eco-friendly UV and latex products. Food printers are described as holding a leading position in Korea, but demand is concentrated in food applications, so revenue swings can track demand from a narrow customer group.

Digital presses target short-run custom printing in packaging. The 2023 acquisition of d.gen added textile lines and engineers, which expands the company's position in eco-friendly textile printing.

In competitive terms, the company is positioned as a specialist equipment maker in food, packaging, and short-run custom printing. The cyclical position of the global printing equipment market is a key variable for results, but the available data do not give a figure that settles the direction of the cycle.
