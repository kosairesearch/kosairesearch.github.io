02

Business structure

HanWool Materials Science was founded in 2000 and listed on KOSDAQ in 2008. It is a communications equipment maker that has mainly supplied optical transmission equipment to major telecom carriers, and telecom equipment remains the core of its revenue.

Press reports put first-half 2026 segment sales at KRW 3.63 billion for telecom equipment and KRW 637 million for semiconductor materials. Semiconductor materials are handled by its subsidiary JK Materials (JKM), in which HanWool Materials Science holds a 55.1% stake.

JKM has run mass production mainly through PSM (photosensitive materials) products, and press reports describe its current output as relying on an outsourced-production structure.

The Sejong Campus completed construction in 2026 and passed the commercial production approval for PSM, and press reports said about KRW 63.8 billion had been invested in building it.

Telecom customers include domestic carriers, national and public agencies, and private network operators, and the company has supplied PTN equipment to KT's disaster-network projects since 2018.

In November 2026 it announced both a KRW 3.55 billion PTN construction contract with KT and its selection as final bidder for a KISTI test-environment project on heterogeneous quantum cryptography networks.

That project extends its optical transmission technology into interfacing and operating QKD and key-management equipment. Semiconductor materials remain small in scale compared with telecom equipment because the production base is still early.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual revenue went from KRW 23 billion in 2022 to KRW 37.6 billion in 2023, up 63.1% year on year, then fell 53.9% to KRW 17.3 billion in 2024 and 22.5% to KRW 13.4 billion in 2025. Among 2022 to 2025, 2023 was the largest year and 2025 the smallest.

Operating loss was KRW 9.36 billion in 2022 and narrowed to KRW 4.05 billion in 2023, then widened to KRW 12.9 billion in 2024 and KRW 24.6 billion in 2025. Operating margin was -40.6% in 2022, -10.8% in 2023, -74.7% in 2024 and -183.1% in 2025, so margins deteriorated sharply as revenue shrank in 2024 and 2025.

The company cited lower sales from a slowdown in downstream investment and delayed orders, inventory valuation losses, higher investment costs such as research expenses at consolidated subsidiaries, and equity-method losses from associates as drivers of the 2025 operating loss (based on the February 27, 2026 filing).

Controlling-shareholder net loss was KRW 14.9 billion in 2022, narrowed to KRW 5.71 billion in 2023, then widened to KRW 19.6 billion in 2024 and KRW 33.8 billion in 2025. The net loss including non-controlling interests was KRW 36.5 billion in 2025, larger than the controlling-shareholder figure.

Debt-to-equity rose from 92.1% in 2023 to 170.1% in 2024 and 229.2% in 2025. At the end of 2025 total equity was KRW 36.6 billion, of which KRW 33.8 billion belonged to controlling shareholders, and total liabilities were KRW 83.8 billion.

Operating cash flow was negative in every year, at -KRW 6.23 billion in 2022, -KRW 3.72 billion in 2023, -KRW 14.3 billion in 2024 and -KRW 14.6 billion in 2025.

Quarterly revenue rose from KRW 2.89 billion in the third quarter of 2025 to KRW 5.92 billion in the fourth quarter, fell to KRW 1.53 billion in the first quarter of 2026, and recovered to KRW 2.74 billion and KRW 2.98 billion in the second and third quarters.

Operating loss peaked at KRW 7.96 billion in the fourth quarter of 2025 and narrowed to KRW 3.65 billion, KRW 3.54 billion and KRW 2.72 billion over the first three quarters of 2026.

Controlling-shareholder net loss widened to KRW 7.41 billion in the second quarter of 2026 and narrowed to KRW 3.57 billion in the third quarter.

05

Industry analysis

Demand for the telecom business continues from network redundancy and rising traffic, but revenue moves with the timing of carrier investment (WiseReport company commentary, as of September 2026). The first-half 2026 narrowing of the operating loss is attributed partly to cost cuts in the telecom segment.

The disaster-network business began with KT network supplies from 2018 and has moved into replacing aging equipment, which shows that public network equipment replacement demand recurs on a cycle.

In semiconductor materials, PSM is a core patterning material used across electronic materials including semiconductors, IT and displays, and a 2024 press report put its global market at about KRW 1 trillion.

The company's chairman said the global TPF (semiconductor protective film) market could reach about KRW 20 trillion by 2030, a projection made by the company itself. According to a September 30, 2026 report, JKM joined the optically clear adhesive supply chain of China's TCL Group.

The company cited investment slowdowns in downstream industries and delayed orders as the cause of lower 2025 sales. Because PSM and TPF are markets where global materials companies are already established, domestic mass-production track record will be the first indicator of the company's position.

06

Outlook

The PTN construction contract signed with KT in November 2026 runs through November 30, 2027, and equals about 26% of 2025 revenue. Revenue recognition timing will be confirmed in later quarterly reports.

The KISTI project is a service to verify interoperability of heterogeneous quantum cryptography equipment, and the company has set a response standard of resolving failures within 72 hours.

Its subsidiary JKM plans to expand output in earnest after completing the Sejong Campus and receiving the PSM commercial production approval. The chairman said four of the eight mass-produced items will be made at the Sejong Campus and four will remain outsourced.

The company has stated a plan to raise TPF revenue to about KRW 500 billion by 2030 (chairman's remarks as reported). On the financing side, in October 2026 the company extended the maturity of its KRW 25.5 billion BW from January 25, 2027 to January 25, 2028, and the warrant exercise period was extended as well.

Press reports noted that the extension does not mean the potential selling overhang has been cleared, and share issuance from warrant exercises remains possible. The next checkpoints are the Sejong Campus contribution in the 2026 annual report and the progress of the KT contract.

Bull factors

Narrowing operating loss

Operating loss fell to KRW 2.72 billion in the third quarter of 2026 from KRW 7.96 billion in the fourth quarter of 2025. Quarterly losses declined in sequence, from KRW 3.65 billion in the first quarter of 2026 to KRW 3.54 billion in the second and KRW 2.72 billion in the third. Any improvement in operating margin depends on how quickly revenue recovers.

Contract wins and public network position

The PTN construction contract with KT for the disaster-safety network is KRW 3.55 billion, about 26% of 2025 revenue of KRW 13.4 billion. The company has supplied PTN equipment to KT's disaster-network projects since 2018, which has led to replacement demand.

In November 2026 it was also reported as the final bidder for a KISTI quantum cryptography test-environment project.

Subsidiary production base and eased debt timing

Subsidiary JKM completed the Sejong Campus and received the PSM commercial production approval, and joined TCL Group's OCA supply chain in September 2026. In October 2026 the company extended the KRW 25.5 billion BW maturity by one year to January 25, 2028, easing near-term repayment pressure. The company said it plans to focus on advancing its subsidiary's business.

Bear factors

Cumulative losses and balance sheet

Operating loss and controlling-shareholder net loss have been recorded in each year from 2022 to 2025, and the 2025 operating margin was -183.1%. Debt-to-equity rose from 92.1% in 2023 to 229.2% in 2025. Operating cash flow was -KRW 14.6 billion in 2025, so cash continued to flow out of operations.

Dilution from convertible and warrant bonds

Among the CB and BW issued since 2024, the portion converted or exercised into shares adds to the number of shares outstanding, and about KRW 9.3 billion was cancelled. The maturity extension of the KRW 25.5 billion BW does not remove potential dilution, and the warrant exercise period was extended as well.

Revenue decline and timing of new business

Revenue fell from KRW 37.6 billion at the 2023 peak to KRW 17.3 billion in 2024 and KRW 13.4 billion in 2025, which the company linked to weaker downstream investment and delayed orders.

About KRW 63.8 billion was invested in the Sejong Campus, but the timing of recovering that capital is uncertain until mass-production sales ramp up. Quarterly revenue of KRW 1.53 billion in the first quarter of 2026 was the low point of the last five quarters.

10

Risk factors

Funding and dilution

Remaining CB and BW balances and their convertible shares can lead to additional issuance depending on the share price and exercise terms. Whether the BW is repaid or converted before its January 25, 2028 maturity will shape financial stability.

The company expects the longer warrant exercise period to ease near-term overhang concerns, while press reports note that additional issuance remains possible.

Execution of subsidiary ramp-up

The Sejong Campus has received commercial production approval, but four of the eight mass-produced items will remain outsourced. The chairman acknowledged that the outsourced structure keeps scale small. If customer qualification or price negotiations take longer, the pace of revenue conversion could slow.

Telecom capex cycle and contract volatility

Telecom equipment revenue moves with carriers' investment timing, and the 2024 and 2025 declines were linked to weaker downstream investment and delayed orders. The KT disaster-network contract runs through November 30, 2027, so whether follow-on orders continue will become clear before it ends. One contract at about 26% of annual revenue means the company's revenue is concentrated in a few large awards.

Overall view

HanWool Materials Science's 2025 operating loss was KRW 24.6 billion, with an operating margin of -183.1%, the weakest year among 2022 to 2025. Quarterly operating losses narrowed and revenue recovered through 2026, with the third quarter at KRW 2.72 billion in operating loss and KRW 2.98 billion in revenue.

Bullish factors include the KT PTN contract and the KISTI quantum cryptography award, which broaden the telecom revenue base, the Sejong Campus commercial production approval and TCL supply chain listing at JKM, and the one-year extension of the KRW 25.5 billion BW that eases near-term repayment pressure.

Bearish factors include four consecutive years of losses from 2022 to 2025, a debt-to-equity ratio of 229.2% at the end of 2025, operating cash flow of -KRW 14.6 billion in 2025, potential dilution from roughly KRW 82.5 billion in CB and BW issued since 2024, and an uncertain timeline for recovering the about KRW 63.8 billion invested in the Sejong Campus.

Quarterly loss reduction and cumulative losses with balance sheet strain coexist. Valuation cannot be calculated on earnings because of the last four quarters' net loss, and the stock trades at a premium to net assets.

The next checkpoints are the 2026 annual report, due by late March 2027, including full-year operating margin and operating cash flow, and how Sejong Campus revenue appears in the Q1 2027 quarterly report due in mid-May 2027.
