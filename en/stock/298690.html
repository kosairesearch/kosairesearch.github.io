Air Busan Back in Quarterly Profit Ahead of 2027 Merger

Air Busan returned to operating profit of KRW 4.68 billion in the third quarter of 2026 on a sharp sales recovery, but its net loss attributable to controlling shareholders over the last four quarters totals KRW 36.5 billion, and the company is set to join a merged low-cost carrier led by Jin Air in March 2027.

02

Business structure

Air Busan is a low-cost carrier based at Gimhae International Airport in Busan, and its business centers on passenger air transport. As of the first half of 2026 it operated 21 aircraft and served 4 domestic and 23 international routes.

Its core routes are to Japan and Taiwan, and the company has presented the recovery of passenger demand on these routes as the key driver of its earnings improvement. During the 2026 summer season it operated four new routes on an ad hoc basis: Busan to Shizuoka and Takamatsu, and Incheon to Hong Kong and Chiang Mai.

The Busan-Shizuoka service is a route that had been concentrated around the Seoul metropolitan area and is now operated solely from Gimhae, which the company describes as part of its Busan-hub strategy.

Expanding ad hoc flights lets the airline adjust capacity to demand, a flexible route approach that the company pursues alongside efficient aircraft deployment. Its maintenance infrastructure is also built around Gimhae, and the company has indicated it will be retained after the merger.

Quarterly results are driven by the scale of international capacity and aircraft utilization, while fuel costs and exchange-rate moves feed through both operating costs and non-operating items.

On the Incheon-Chiang Mai route, Korean Air, T'way Air and Eastar Jet already operate, so competition is expected to intensify with Air Busan's entry.

04

Earnings analysis

On a disclosed basis, sales rose from KRW 405 billion in 2022 to KRW 890.4 billion in 2023, an increase of 119.9% year on year, and then reached KRW 1.01 trillion in 2024, up 13.1%, making 2024 the largest year in the disclosed period. Sales fell 17.3% to KRW 832.6 billion in 2025.

Operating results moved from an operating loss of KRW 81.3 billion in 2022 to an operating profit of KRW 159.8 billion in 2023, a swing to profit, and operating profit then declined 8.4% to KRW 146.3 billion in 2024. In 2025 the company returned to an operating loss of KRW 4.52 billion.

Operating margin was -20.1% in 2022, 17.9% in 2023, 14.5% in 2024 and -0.5% in 2025. Net income attributable to controlling shareholders swung from a loss of KRW 150.4 billion in 2022 to a profit of KRW 104.1 billion in 2023, then fell 97.7% to KRW 2.42 billion in 2024, and in 2025 turned to a loss of KRW 22.1 billion.

Net income including non-controlling interests was reported at the same amounts in each year. By quarter, the third quarter of 2025 was the weakest, with sales of KRW 176.3 billion, an operating loss of KRW 28.5 billion and a net loss attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 50.4 billion.

Sales then rose to KRW 235.4 billion in the fourth quarter of 2025 (up 33.5% from the prior quarter) and the operating loss narrowed to KRW 5.03 billion.

The first quarter of 2026 returned to operating profit of KRW 30.4 billion on sales of KRW 257.7 billion, but the second quarter posted an operating loss of KRW 35.5 billion and a net loss attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 61.8 billion on sales of KRW 235.3 billion.

The third quarter of 2026 recorded sales of KRW 247.8 billion, up 5.3% from the prior quarter and 40.6% from a year earlier, with operating profit of KRW 4.68 billion and net income attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 73 billion.

The company attributed the gap between net and operating profit in the third quarter mainly to non-operating gains from a lower won-dollar exchange rate, while higher fuel costs raised operating expenses in the same quarter.

Over the last four quarters, the net loss attributable to controlling shareholders totals KRW 36.5 billion. The preliminary cumulative operating loss for the first three quarters of 2026 was KRW 0.4 billion, announced on November 4, 2026.

05

Industry analysis

The earnings of Korean low-cost carriers move with passenger demand, fuel costs and the won-dollar exchange rate at the same time. In the third quarter of 2026 the company said strong passenger demand, more ad hoc routes and normalized fleet operations lifted sales through higher capacity.

Over the same period high fuel prices kept operating costs elevated, but sales growth more than offset them and operating profit turned positive. A lower won-dollar rate added foreign-currency translation gains that lifted net income, according to the company's disclosure.

The company expects uncertainty in the operating environment to persist because of external factors including the situation in the Middle East, and it has stressed cost control against fuel price and currency volatility.

On competition, Korean Air and Asiana Airlines are scheduled to launch as a merged full-service carrier on December 17, 2026, while Jin Air, Air Busan and a third carrier are scheduled to launch as a merged low-cost carrier on March 17, 2027.

On the Incheon-Chiang Mai route, Korean Air, T'way Air and Eastar Jet already operate, and Air Busan's entry is expected to intensify competition.

After the merger, the supply structure of Korean low-cost carriers will sit within one company, so route adjustments and aircraft allocation remain variables that could shift industry capacity.

06

Outlook

In explaining its third-quarter 2026 results, the company said it would put safety first and raise operating efficiency and profitability through efficient aircraft deployment and flexible route management.

It also set out a plan to focus on cost control and profitability improvement against high fuel prices and currency volatility.

The 2026 summer schedule included four new routes: Incheon to Hong Kong once daily, Busan to Takamatsu three times weekly, Incheon to Chiang Mai four times weekly, and Busan to Shizuoka three times weekly.

According to the integration plan submitted by Jin Air, Gimhae's international routes would rise from 28 to 33, and weekly flights from 281 to 339. After integration, the plan calls for adding aircraft of the combined airline to Busan-origin routes to strengthen the Gimhae international network.

The Ministry of Land, Infrastructure and Transport approved the merger on November 12, 2026, and the combined airline is scheduled to launch around Jin Air on March 17, 2027. Demands from Busan's local community to move the headquarters were not included in the integration plan.

The launch of the merged Korean Air and Asiana Airlines full-service carrier on December 17, 2026 runs alongside this, so the domestic airline competitive map is being reshaped by two integrations at once. The next checkpoints are the annual operating margin and debt-to-equity ratio in the 2026 annual report.

Bull factors

Quarterly profit return and sales recovery

Third-quarter 2026 sales rose 40.6% year on year to KRW 247.8 billion, and operating profit of KRW 4.68 billion marked a return to profit. The company cited strong passenger demand, more ad hoc routes and normalized fleet operations.

Operating profit of KRW 30.4 billion in the first quarter of 2026 also means the quarterly profit has not been a single occurrence.

Expansion plan for the Gimhae base

According to the integration plan submitted by Jin Air, Gimhae's international routes would increase from 28 to 33 and weekly flights from 281 to 339. The combined airline plans to add aircraft to Busan-origin routes. The maintenance infrastructure built around Gimhae is also set to be retained after the merger.

Non-operating gains from a stronger won

In the third quarter of 2026 the company said non-operating gains, including foreign-currency translation gains from a lower won-dollar rate, lifted net income. Net income attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 73 billion far exceeded operating profit of KRW 4.68 billion. Depending on the direction of the exchange rate, this factor can change from quarter to quarter.

Large swings in quarterly results

The third quarter returned to profit, but the swings between quarters remain very large.

The 2025 annual operating loss of KRW 4.52 billion and net loss of KRW 22.1 billion also fall within this volatility.

It expects uncertainty in the operating environment to continue due to external factors such as the situation in the Middle East. With the operating margin at -0.5% in 2025, a rise in fuel costs could slow the pace of earnings recovery.

Risk factors

Merger timing and structural change

The integrated low-cost carrier is scheduled to launch around Jin Air on March 17, 2027. How far routes, staff and maintenance functions remain in Busan after integration is a concern for the local community.

The headquarters relocation was not included in the integration plan, leaving the status of the Busan base as an open uncertainty.

Fuel price and currency volatility

High fuel prices and currency swings affect fuel costs and foreign-currency translation gains at the same time. Because third-quarter net income was heavily shaped by non-operating gains from a lower exchange rate, a reversal in the currency could change the net profit picture. The company has emphasized cost management against fuel and currency volatility.

Intensifying competition

Korean Air and Asiana Airlines are scheduled to launch as a merged full-service carrier on December 17, 2026. Korean Air, T'way Air and Eastar Jet already operate on the Incheon-Chiang Mai route, where competition is intense.

If the Jin Air-led low-cost carrier expands international routes from Gimhae, competitive pressure on Busan-origin routes could also rise.

12

Overall view

Air Busan's recent results show a change in direction. After a swing to an annual loss in 2025, the company posted quarterly operating profit in the first and third quarters of 2026, and third-quarter sales rose 40.6% year on year.

However, the second-quarter operating loss of KRW 35.5 billion and net loss attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 61.8 billion show that quarterly results still swing widely.

The preliminary cumulative operating loss for the first three quarters of 2026 was KRW 0.4 billion, so cumulative profitability has not yet turned positive. Third-quarter net income was largely shaped by non-operating gains from a lower exchange rate, so it should be distinguished from operating profit.

The balance sheet at year-end 2025 showed a debt-to-equity ratio of 801.0%, total equity of KRW 161.2 billion and total liabilities of KRW 1.29 trillion, a heavy debt burden.

Strengths including the sales recovery and weaknesses including cumulative losses, high fuel prices and intensifying competition remain on the same footing. The next checkpoints are the 2026 annual report and the route results of the merged low-cost carrier after March 17, 2027.
