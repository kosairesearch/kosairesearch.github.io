02

Business structure

Messeesang launched as a specialized exhibition business in 2008 and listed on KOSDAQ in 2023 through a SPAC merger. In its business structure, the exhibition-service segment covering planning, hosting, and operating exhibitions accounted for 95.2% of 2023 revenue, followed by exhibition-space rental at 4.4%.

Flagship shows include Korea Build, the country's largest construction and interior fair, the pet-industry show Megazoo (K-Pet Fair), and the childcare and education fair Kobe Baby Fair, alongside B2B events such as Busan Korea Build, KHF (International Hospital and Health-Tech Fair), KPEX (International Security Industry Exhibition), and the ESG Green Expo.

As of 2024, segment revenue was fairly evenly split, with construction/interior at 21.1%, pet at 17.3%, and childcare/education at 15.9%, while pet-related exhibitions alone represented about 20% of total revenue as of 2023 as the company's flagship B2C category.

The company diversified its portfolio through active M&A, acquiring Korea Build in 2016 and Kobe Baby Fair together with GoCamp (camping and leisure) in 2014, differentiating itself from rivals that focus on a single category.

It opened its own Suwon Messe venue in 2020 and began overseas expansion in 2023 by opening IICC in India, and it also operates Cheongju OSCO, Chungbuk Province's first large convention center that opened last September, under an entrusted management contract.

Korea's exhibition-organizer market is known to be fragmented with roughly 600 operators, but Messeesang is considered a top-tier player by number of events and visitor count.

Leveraging the B2B data capability of its parent, Sang Networks, a big-data-driven marketing platform that matches exhibitors with buyers also forms part of its business.

04

Earnings analysis

Messeesang's consolidated revenue expanded from KRW 50.8 billion in 2023 to KRW 67.1 billion in 2024 and KRW 72.8 billion in 2025. Operating profit rose from KRW 11.2 billion (22.0% margin) in 2023 to KRW 17.1 billion (25.5%) in 2024 and KRW 19.0 billion (26.1%) in 2025, showing margin improvement alongside growth.

Owner's net profit likewise increased clearly from KRW 5.3 billion in 2023 to KRW 13.6 billion in 2024 and KRW 15.5 billion in 2025.

On a quarterly basis, revenue reached KRW 21.9 billion with operating profit of KRW 6.3 billion in Q3 2025, but in Q4 2025 revenue fell to KRW 20.3 billion and operating profit to KRW 2.9 billion, pushing the operating margin down to the mid-teens percent, which is interpreted as reflecting venue operating costs that had been deferred to the second half.

The company itself explained at its Q2 disclosure that profit had temporarily risen because some first-half costs related to the Cheongju OSCO entrusted operation had not yet been recognized, with normal cost recognition expected in the second half.

Subsequently, Q1 2026 revenue fell seasonally to KRW 16.6 billion but the operating margin recovered to near 30%, and in Q2 2026 revenue reached KRW 19.4 billion with operating profit of KRW 6.6 billion, lifting the margin to around 34% as costs normalized and profitability clearly recovered.

Owner's net profit summed over the most recent four quarters (Q3 2025-Q2 2026) reached about KRW 17.4 billion, exceeding the full-year 2025 figure even on an annualized basis.

This trajectory reflects the combined effect of more frequent exhibitions, revenue contribution from self-operated and entrusted venues including Cheongju OSCO, and cost efficiency gains.

05

Industry analysis

Korea's MICE industry has moved past the contraction of the COVID-19 period into a phase of normalizing offline exhibition demand, and rising exhibition-service and rental revenue is described as reflecting this industry recovery.

Total domestic exhibition space, about 330,000 square meters in 2022, is projected to expand to roughly 510,000 square meters by 2030 through the Magok MICE complex, KINTEX Exhibition Hall 3, and the Jamsil Sports-MICE complex, which would also expand organizers' hosting capacity.

Korea's exhibition-organizer market is fragmented with roughly 600 operators; Messeesang ranks among the top tier by event count and visitor numbers but competes against many small and mid-sized organizers within individual industry categories.

KB Securities noted in an August 2025 report that expectations for the MICE industry tied to the APEC summit in Gyeongju were a checkpoint to watch, and that summit was subsequently held in Gyeongju, North Gyeongsang Province, in November 2025.

In the defense exhibition segment, merger discussions between the existing KADEX and the competing DX KOREA show, prompted by the Ministry of National Defense, failed to reach agreement, so the two events continue to be held separately in 2026.

Overseas, expansion centered on IICC in India continues, making overseas category growth in response to domestic market maturity a variable in the company's industry positioning.

06

Outlook

The most visible near-term event is 'KADEX 2026' at Cheongju OSCO in Chungbuk Province from October 6-10, 2026.

Messeesang serves as the organizing office, and with about 450 companies and 2,032 booths from 21 countries expected to be its largest edition yet, it could contribute simultaneously to exhibition revenue and Cheongju OSCO's entrusted-operation revenue.

Following restrictions on using Gyeryongdae after the defense ministry's merger recommendation, the venue was moved to Cheongju OSCO, raising schedule-disruption concerns, but the organizing committee stated it quickly confirmed an alternative venue to minimize harm to participating companies.

The company has also stated plans to hold existing B2C exhibitions such as Megazoo, Kobe Baby Fair, GoCamp, and Gungdi Pangpang Cat Festa across the country, while expanding new categories in beauty and law such as the Osong Cosmetics and Beauty Industry Expo, Beautysome Suwon, and LAW EXPO SEOUL.

Management has indicated a direction of strengthening cost competitiveness through internalizing venue operations while developing new categories to solidify its competitive position in the MICE market.

Overseas, continued expansion of local exhibitions through IICC in India could serve as a buffer against any slowdown in domestic growth. However, the specific timing and revenue contribution of these new exhibitions have not yet been officially confirmed in disclosures, so their actual impact bears watching.

Bull factors

Revenue and Profit Expanding Together

Revenue and operating profit rose every year from 2023 to 2025, with operating margin improving from 22.0% to 26.1%. In Q1-Q2 2026, the operating margin recovered to the 30% range, clearly showing profitability improvement after cost normalization.

The sum of owner's net profit over the most recent four quarters exceeds the full-year 2025 result on an annualized basis.

Large-Scale KADEX 2026 Exhibition Secured

'KADEX 2026', organized by Messeesang, is set to be its largest edition yet with about 450 companies and 2,032 booths from 21 countries, held at Cheongju OSCO in October 2026.

Since the company also holds the entrusted operation of Cheongju OSCO, both exhibition revenue and facility operating revenue could arise from the same event.

Overseas Expansion and Category Diversification

Following the opening of Suwon Messe in 2020 and IICC in India in 2023, the company has also taken on the entrusted operation of Cheongju OSCO, broadening its portfolio of self-operated and entrusted venues.

Plans to expand into new categories such as beauty and law also signal continued efforts to widen the business beyond its traditional focus on construction, pets, and childcare.

Bear factors

Quarterly Earnings Volatility

The operating margin fell to the mid-teens percent in Q4 2025 as venue operating costs deferred from earlier in the year were recognized.

Because profit can swing significantly by quarter depending on the timing of major exhibitions and cost recognition, it is difficult to judge trends from a single quarter's results alone.

Fragmentation of the Defense Exhibition Market

Despite the defense ministry's merger recommendation, talks between KADEX and the competing DX KOREA show failed, so the two events continue to be held separately.

As seen when KADEX's venue was hastily changed to Cheongju OSCO due to restrictions on using Gyeryongdae, uncertainty around access to defense-related infrastructure remains latent.

Fragmented Domestic Exhibition-Organizer Market

Korea's exhibition-organizer market is fragmented with roughly 600 operators, and many small and mid-sized competitors exist within individual industry categories.

If revenue dependence on a specific large exhibition such as KADEX grows, changes in that event's scale or schedule could have a correspondingly larger impact on results.

10

Risk factors

Seasonality and Event-Concentration Risk

Operating margins can fluctuate significantly if exhibition hosting and cost recognition are concentrated in a particular quarter. The margin decline in Q4 2025 is a representative example, and a similar cost-recognition pattern could recur in the future.

Venue Infrastructure and Access Risk

As shown by the restriction on using Gyeryongdae for KADEX, large exhibitions relying on state or military facilities can have their venue or schedule changed depending on infrastructure approval. This can affect not only exhibition revenue but also the utilization of venues under entrusted operation.

Economic Cycle and Marketing Budget Risk

Exhibiting companies' booth applications and marketing budgets can be influenced by economic conditions. In a downturn, reduced exhibition participation or smaller booth sizes by companies could weigh on revenue.

Overall view

Messeesang is Korea's largest exhibition organizer, having grown revenue and operating profit every year since 2023 on the back of more frequent exhibitions, self-operated and entrusted venue management, and overseas expansion.

The margin decline in Q4 2025 is interpreted as a temporary effect of cost-recognition timing, and the margin is confirmed to have recovered to the 30% range in the first half of 2026.

In the near term, 'KADEX 2026' at Cheongju OSCO in October 2026 stands out as an event that could contribute to both exhibition revenue and entrusted-operation revenue simultaneously.

However, as shown by the venue and schedule changes for major exhibitions, uncertainty around infrastructure access rights and the fragmented market structure of roughly 600 competing domestic organizers remain factors to continue monitoring.

The pace at which new-category expansion in beauty and law, along with overseas growth through IICC in India, translates into actual revenue will also shape the direction of future results.

Before making any investment judgment, it is worth checking the next quarter's preliminary results together with the actual outcome of KADEX 2026.
