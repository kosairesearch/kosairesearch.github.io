Samsung Climate Control: Testing Whether the Quarterly Profit Recovery Holds

02

Business structure

Samsung Climate Control is an automotive parts maker based in Changwon, South Gyeongsang, producing radiators and oil coolers that cool engine coolant, engine oil and transmission fluid, along with HVAC (heating, ventilation and air-conditioning) parts for vehicles.

Based on Q2 2026 revenue, the product mix was radiators 53%, oil coolers 31%, HVAC 13% and other automotive parts 3%, according to Butler company data. With those two heat-exchange products making up most of sales, results depend heavily on automaker production volumes and vehicle mix.

Automakers and large tier-one suppliers are the main counterparties, so production disruptions pass through to revenue quickly. JobKorea lists 138 employees as of June 30, 2026, and classifies the company as a mid-sized enterprise.

In thermal management, large specialists such as Hanon Systems compete alongside smaller domestic parts makers that focus on particular models and product lines. This product concentration brings engineering depth but limits scale and diversification.

Revenue was KRW 115.8 billion in 2024 and KRW 117.7 billion in 2025, both below the 2023 level of KRW 125.9 billion. A heating, ventilation and air-conditioning segment at only 13% of sales suggests that the company still has room to widen its business beyond its core products.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual revenue rose from KRW 95.7 billion in 2022 to KRW 125.9 billion in 2023 (up 31.5% year on year), then fell to KRW 115.8 billion in 2024 (down 8.0%) and rose to KRW 117.7 billion in 2025 (up 1.7%). Across 2022 to 2025, 2023 was the largest year for revenue and 2022 the smallest.

Operating profit fell from KRW 8.74 billion in 2022 to KRW 5.44 billion in 2023 (down 37.7%), rebounded to KRW 6.48 billion in 2024 (up 19.2%), and dropped to KRW 3.25 billion in 2025 (down 49.9%). Operating profit was largest in 2022 and smallest in 2025.

The operating margin moved from 9.1% in 2022 to 4.3% in 2023, 5.6% in 2024 and 2.8% in 2025. Margin fell in 2023 even though revenue grew 31.5%, which suggests cost structure has mattered more than sales scale for profitability.

Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 40.9 billion in 2022, KRW 13.8 billion in 2023 (down 66.2%), KRW 14.4 billion in 2024 (up 4.6%) and KRW 7.13 billion in 2025 (down 50.7%). In 2022 that figure far exceeded operating profit, pointing to a larger role for non-operating items that year.

Net profit including non-controlling interests was KRW 7.24 billion in 2025, not far from the KRW 7.13 billion attributable to controlling shareholders.

For 2025, the company cited higher raw material and labor costs and the recognition of financial product impairment losses as reasons for the decline in operating profit and net profit, according to a Digital Today report on the filing.

Quarterly operating profit went from KRW 1.02 billion in Q3 2025 to an operating loss of KRW 1.4 billion in Q4, then back to a KRW 1.6 billion profit in Q1 2026. Q2 2026 operating profit of KRW 2.5 billion, up 55.6% from the prior quarter, was the quarterly peak.

Q3 2026 operating profit fell to KRW 1.25 billion (down 50.1% from the prior quarter, up 21.9% year on year), while revenue of KRW 30 billion was down 2.0% year on year.

Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders fell from KRW 6.24 billion in Q2 to KRW 483 million in Q3 (down 82.4% year on year), and the four quarters through Q3 2026 total KRW 9.23 billion. Quarterly revenue moved between KRW 26.7 billion and KRW 31.9 billion, but profit swung far more widely.

Operating cash flow was KRW 16 billion in 2022, KRW 15.9 billion in 2023, KRW 12.9 billion in 2024 and KRW 15.4 billion in 2025, a steadier path than profit.

05

Industry analysis

Samsung Climate Control's end market is global automobile production and vehicle mix. Most of its revenue comes from radiators and oil coolers for automakers, so it is directly exposed to the production cycle.

In Q3 2026, Hyundai Motor was reported to have protected profitability despite labor strikes and production disruptions. Production disruptions at automakers feed straight into supplier utilization and quarterly revenue, so the 2.0% year-on-year revenue decline in Q3 2026 should be read alongside those factors.

Competition splits between large thermal-management specialists such as Hanon Systems and smaller domestic parts makers, and in scale and model diversification Samsung Climate Control sits closer to the smaller group.

The shift to electric vehicles changes the demand mix for engine cooling parts, and the share of revenue from EV thermal products is a point to track in the next annual report's product breakdown. On the HVAC side, Samsung Electronics completed its acquisition of the European HVAC group FläktGroup in November 2025.

It is also pursuing a deal for Modine Manufacturing, a US data center cooling company, and Bloomberg reported that an agreement could come within weeks, though no final decision has been made. Samsung Electronics expects data center HVAC demand to grow at about 18% annually.

Data center cooling and automotive thermal management involve different products and customers, so the link to Samsung Climate Control is indirect and the sector serves as a background indicator. Samsung Electronics' planned HVAC production line in Gwangju is scheduled to start operating in early 2028.

Outlook

The 2026 quarterly pattern reads as a first-quarter swing back to profit, a second-quarter peak and a third-quarter slowdown.

Q2 operating profit of KRW 2.5 billion was the high point, and Q3 operating profit of KRW 1.25 billion and net profit attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 483 million suggest earnings power has not yet settled.

Q3 revenue fell 5.8% from the prior quarter in the same direction as operating profit, so volume and cost factors need to be read together. Higher raw material and labor costs were cited as the main reasons for the 2025 decline, which makes cost trends the central variable for margin recovery.

Whether those cost pressures ease in 2026 will be the benchmark for whether the operating margin climbs back from 2.8% in 2025. The 2025 recognition of financial product impairment losses weighed on both operating profit and net profit, and whether that item is one-off or recurring is a point for later filings.

On the balance sheet, total equity was KRW 294.9 billion and total liabilities KRW 39.2 billion at end-2025, so the profit slump has not yet strained financial stability.

Operating cash flow of KRW 15.4 billion in 2025 was well above operating profit of KRW 3.25 billion, so profit metrics and cash generation should be considered together. The next checkpoints are the Q4 2026 preliminary results and the 2026 annual report.

In the 2026 annual report, the product revenue mix and how far the operating margin recovers from 2.8% will be the main benchmarks for the outlook.
