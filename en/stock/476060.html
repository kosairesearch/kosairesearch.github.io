02

Business structure

Onconic Therapeutics (Onconic Therapeutics) is a drug development company established in May 2020 that aims to sell its own drugs to secure revenue and then reinvest that cash into follow-on pipeline programs.

Its main product is Jacubo, a treatment for gastroesophageal reflux disease that the company describes as Korea's 37th domestically developed new drug, launched in Korea in October 2024 and now expanding into gastric ulcer indications. A Phase 3 trial to secure an additional indication is ongoing.

According to BusinessWatch, cumulative third-quarter 2026 Jacubo product sales reached KRW 60.1 billion, up 109% from KRW 28.7 billion a year earlier, and product sales are tallied separately from technology transfer income.

As of June 2026 press reports, the company had technology export and supply agreements covering 27 countries, and Phase 3 trials in China and India have been completed with approval procedures under way.

A recent mission in Mexico involved a marketing approval application, alongside a license and supply deal in Indonesia that extends reach into Latin America and Southeast Asia.

The anticancer pipeline centers on Nesuparib, a dual-target inhibitor, with clinical development in pancreatic, endometrial, ovarian and gastric cancers. The new pipeline program OCN-501 is a candidate intended to reduce muscle loss during weight reduction when combined with GLP-1 class obesity drugs. Because sales are concentrated in one product, prescription trends drive quarterly results.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual sales were KRW 21.1 billion in 2023, KRW 14.8 billion in 2024 (down 29.5% year on year) and KRW 53.4 billion in 2025 (up 259.8% year on year), making 2025 the largest year and 2024 the smallest within 2023-2025.

Operating profit went from KRW 2.23 billion in 2023 to an operating loss of KRW 4.8 billion in 2024, a swing to a loss, before returning to operating profit of KRW 12.6 billion in 2025. The operating margin was 10.6% in 2023, -32.3% in 2024 and 23.6% in 2025.

Profit attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 1.68 billion in 2023, a loss of KRW 8.09 billion in 2024 (a swing to a loss), and KRW 15.8 billion in 2025. Net profit including non-controlling interests was reported as KRW 1.68 billion, a net loss of KRW 8.09 billion and KRW 15.8 billion in the same years.

At the end of 2025, total equity was KRW 72 billion, all of it attributable to controlling shareholders, total liabilities were KRW 3.67 billion, and the debt ratio was 5.1%. Operating cash flow was KRW 4.15 billion in 2023, -KRW 7.84 billion in 2024 and KRW 11.4 billion in 2025.

On a quarterly basis, sales of KRW 19.2 billion and operating profit of KRW 8.32 billion in the third quarter of 2025 were followed by sales of KRW 15.6 billion (down 18.9% from the prior quarter) and operating profit of KRW 1.61 billion in the fourth quarter.

Sales rebounded to KRW 23 billion in the first quarter of 2026 (up 47.5% from the prior quarter) with operating profit of KRW 4.59 billion. In the second quarter of 2026, sales were KRW 24.4 billion and operating profit KRW 2.79 billion.

Third-quarter 2026 sales were KRW 17 billion (down 11.4% year on year), with an operating loss of KRW 4.35 billion, a swing from profit a year earlier.

The company attributed the sales decline to lower production during summer holidays and the operating loss to higher R&D spending from new pipeline research and expanded clinical trials at home and abroad.

According to BusinessWatch, cumulative R&D spending for the first three quarters of 2026 was KRW 20.7 billion, about 3.4 times the KRW 6.1 billion a year earlier, and the R&D-to-sales ratio rose from 16% to 32%.

The third-quarter loss attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 7.69 billion was larger than the operating loss, which press reports tie to a foreign currency translation loss on dollar-denominated assets.

The company's investor materials say a Chinese partner's milestone was booked in the first quarter of 2025, so annual results include one-time contract income. Profit trends depend on whether Jacubo's prescription growth can absorb the expansion in R&D, and they should be read alongside the quarterly pattern.

05

Industry analysis

In the reflux disease treatment market where Jacubo competes, prescription trends directly determine revenue.

According to UBIST, Jacubo's third-quarter 2026 outpatient prescription value was KRW 28.75 billion, up 109.6% year on year, and its monthly prescription value in October 2026 was KRW 10.27 billion, passing KRW 10 billion for the first time two years after launch.

Because prescription growth and company revenue growth move together, whether that growth continues and how share shifts against competing products are the key industry variables.

In oncology, competition among late-stage dual-target anticancer drugs is central, and in a Phase 1b study in metastatic pancreatic cancer, Nesuparib produced complete responses with long-term remission lasting more than 40 months in some patients.

A Phase 1b study involves a small patient population at an early stage, so whether later trials reproduce these results is the key test. In obesity, the company presents OCN-501 as a candidate designed to be combined with GLP-1 class obesity drugs to reduce muscle loss during weight reduction.

In terms of cycle position, the company is moving from commercializing its own product to a phase of sharply higher R&D investment, as the R&D-to-sales ratio rising to 32% in the first three quarters of 2026 shows.

On regulation, approval procedures in China and India and a marketing application in Mexico are in progress, and preparations for FDA pre-IND consultation are under way for entry into U.S. clinical trials.

Compared with other listed Korean biopharma companies, the company's stated distinction is pairing R&D with revenue from its own product.
