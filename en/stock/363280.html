02

Business structure

TY Holdings is a holding company that was spun off in September 2020 from the broadcasting, leisure and logistics businesses of Taeyoung Engineering & Construction. Its purpose is to manage and invest in affiliate shares, so consolidated results should be read as the sum of its subsidiaries' performance.

Broadcasting and leisure are the main revenue sources, followed by environment and construction businesses. The broadcasting segment centers on SBS and its subsidiaries, which handle broadcast channel operations, broadcast advertising and content production.

In leisure, the subsidiary Bluewon operates golf courses and resorts. The construction segment consists of Taeyoung Engineering & Construction and its subsidiaries, covering urban development, housing, social overhead capital projects, civil engineering, buildings and plants.

The logistics segment is a warehousing business run by Pyeongtaek Ssairo. At the time of the spin-off, the company acquired the SBS Media Holdings stake previously held by Taeyoung Engineering & Construction, making TY Holdings the largest shareholder of SBS Media Holdings.

Broadcasting results depend on advertiser demand and ratings competition, while golf results depend on visitor demand and membership markets, so the drivers differ by segment. Construction order intake and receivable collection are tied to the financial stability of the wider group.

Because this is a holding company, reported results reflect subsidiary performance as it passes through consolidation and dividends, so segment-level causes need to be read separately.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual revenue fell from KRW 353.6 billion in 2022 to KRW 325.4 billion in 2023, down 8.0% year on year, and dropped further to KRW 79.4 billion in 2024, down 75.6%.

Revenue was KRW 79.2 billion in 2025, down 0.2%, and has declined for three consecutive years from 2023 through 2025. 2022 was the largest year of the 2022 to 2025 period and 2025 the smallest. Profitability swung sharply at the operating line.

Operating profit of KRW 22.4 billion in 2022, with an operating margin of 6.3%, turned into an operating loss of KRW 354.1 billion in 2023, an operating margin of -108.8%.

The operating loss was KRW 80.9 billion in 2024 (margin -101.9%) and KRW 34.6 billion in 2025 (margin -43.6%), so losses narrowed in each of the last two years.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 91.5 billion in 2022, then a net loss of KRW 592.9 billion in 2023, KRW 125 billion in 2024 and KRW 94.1 billion in 2025, which is three consecutive years of losses.

Net income including non-controlling interests, a different measure, was a net loss of KRW 92.8 billion in 2025. Operating cash flow went from KRW 81 billion in 2022 and KRW 57.6 billion in 2023 to KRW 71 billion in 2024, then turned negative at -KRW 45.5 billion in 2025.

The debt-to-equity ratio rose from 47.6% in 2022 to 137.9% in 2023 and then eased to 79.4% in 2024 and 76.8% in 2025.

In the quarterly data, operating profit of KRW 140 million in the third quarter of 2025 gave way to an operating loss of KRW 31.2 billion in the fourth quarter, the largest operating loss among the reported quarters.

The company then recorded an operating loss of KRW 2.45 billion in the first quarter of 2026, followed by operating profit of KRW 5.14 billion in the second quarter, a turn to profit.

Third-quarter operating profit was KRW 2.97 billion, up 2,017.6% year on year, but third-quarter revenue of KRW 23.5 billion fell 10.9% from the prior quarter.

Net losses attributable to controlling shareholders narrowed to KRW 11.3 billion, KRW 6.6 billion and KRW 4.21 billion across the first three quarters of 2026, but the streak of three quarterly losses continues.

Operating profit has not yet translated into net profit, which suggests the burden sits below the operating line, such as financing costs and non-operating items, though the exact components should be checked in the periodic report.

Industry analysis

The holding company and conglomerate industry that TY Holdings belongs to covers 67 companies on an annual basis. Within this group, TY Holdings' operating margin of -43.63% ranks 67th, against an industry average of 19.89%.

Its debt-to-equity ratio of 76.75% sits below the industry average of 117.33% and ranks 31st in the group. The comparison shows a weak operating profit structure alongside a debt burden lighter than the industry average.

On the demand side, the company's revenue spans three different cycles: advertising markets for broadcasting, leisure demand for golf and resorts, and construction order flow.

Broadcasting is sensitive to advertising conditions and ratings, leisure to consumer conditions and visitor numbers, and construction to housing and social overhead capital orders and the collection of construction receivables.

Many holding companies in this sector are valued mainly on the value of their listed affiliates, but TY Holdings' operating results in broadcasting, leisure and construction directly drive consolidated earnings.

Compared with larger holding companies, offsetting between affiliates is limited and earnings volatility is high.

In competition, the broadcasting segment competes for advertising with terrestrial and general programming channels, the golf segment shares visitors with other domestic golf operators, and the construction segment competes for orders among large and mid-sized builders.

Risk factors

Group-related risk

During Taeyoung Engineering & Construction's liquidity crisis, TY Holdings shares were pledged as collateral to creditor banks, and assets were contributed for the affiliate's workout.

If group finances deteriorate further, the pledged shares and contributed assets could affect the holding company's finances and governance. Progress of the workout is a variable to monitor separately from the holding company's own results.

Free float and shareholder structure

The largest shareholder and related parties hold 33.63% of shares, and treasury shares account for 19.88%. Together these leave a limited share of stock that general shareholders can trade. Low liquidity can make the share price move sharply on small trades, so price moves need careful interpretation.

Earnings volatility and financial burden

The operating loss of KRW 31.2 billion in the fourth quarter of 2025 was the largest among the reported quarters. Total liabilities at the end of 2025 were KRW 755.7 billion against total equity of KRW 984.5 billion, so the debt burden remains. If broadcasting, leisure and construction weaken together, the operating loss could widen again.
