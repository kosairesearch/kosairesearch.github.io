02

Business structure

Samkee is a die casting specialist founded in 1978 that makes aluminum automotive parts. It runs production sites in Pyeongtaek, Seosan and Incheon in Korea and in Alabama in the United States, where the US production is handled by its subsidiary Samkee America.

The business is organized into alloy, casting and machining divisions and covers engine parts, transmission parts and EV parts for both combustion and electric vehicles.

The company describes an integrated system running from alloy production through shipment, and a clean room for die casting that it says is the first of its kind in Korea. Transmission parts account for the largest share of the product mix, followed by engine parts.

Sales growth in the first half of 2026 was driven by higher demand for EV and hybrid parts, third-generation powertrain orders and a wider range of hybrid transmission models, while cost pressure weighed on profitability over the same period.

New businesses include parts for global EV brands and battery and chassis components. In June 2026 the company was selected as a developer of an upper-body skeletal frame for a global robot maker's humanoid robot.

It uses existing equipment, begins with prototype development and initial delivery, and expects larger follow-on orders after specification evaluation. The similarly named Samkee Energy Solutions is a separate listed company that was spun off in October 2020 from Samkee, taking over its EV battery parts business.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual sales rose from KRW 524.1 billion in 2022 to KRW 537.4 billion in 2023 (up 2.5% year on year), KRW 542.1 billion in 2024 (up 0.9%) and KRW 570.5 billion in 2025 (up 5.2%). Sales increased for three consecutive years from 2023 through 2025, and 2025 was the largest year of 2022 to 2025.

Operating profit moved the other way, falling from KRW 11.5 billion in 2022 to KRW 7.81 billion in 2023 (down 31.9%) and KRW 5.14 billion in 2024 (down 34.1%). It recovered to KRW 8.28 billion in 2025 (up 60.9%) but remained below the 2022 level, which was the largest year for operating profit in the period.

Operating margin was 2.2% in 2022, 1.5% in 2023, 0.9% in 2024 and 1.5% in 2025, so margins stayed in the 1% to 2% range even as sales grew. Cost burden was also cited in the 2026 first-half commentary as a factor that weighed on profitability.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders swung from a loss of KRW 29.6 billion in 2022 to a profit of KRW 6.35 billion in 2023, then back to a loss of KRW 2.85 billion in 2024 and a larger loss of KRW 6.65 billion in 2025. The company posted losses in both 2024 and 2025.

Total net income including non-controlling interests was a loss of KRW 27.6 billion in 2022, a profit of KRW 8.27 billion in 2023, a loss of KRW 3.2 billion in 2024 and a loss of KRW 10.3 billion in 2025; the gap from controlling-shareholder figures comes from non-controlling interests.

On a quarterly basis, Q3 2025 sales were KRW 148 billion with operating profit of KRW 4.04 billion. Q4 2025 sales fell 2.4% quarter on quarter to KRW 144.4 billion, and the company swung to an operating loss of KRW 5.19 billion.

Sales then rose for three consecutive quarters from Q1 2026 (KRW 148.6 billion, up 2.9%) through Q3 2026. Q2 2026 sales rose 18.4% to KRW 176 billion and operating profit turned positive at KRW 1.16 billion. Q3 2026 operating profit reached KRW 11.4 billion, up 882.5% quarter on quarter and 181.1% year on year.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders, however, was only KRW 174 million in Q3 2026, down 90.3% year on year. The non-operating items and tax amounts that explain the gap between Q3 2026 operating profit and controlling-shareholder net income should be checked in the 2026 annual report.

Industry analysis

The auto parts industry is moving through electrification, with combustion-engine parts and EV and hybrid parts shifting in share together.

A April 2023 company analysis by the Korea IR Council judged that government regulation and incentives would push automakers toward EVs, making combustion-engine parts demand decline over the medium term.

The same report expected parts demand to hold up in the short term while automakers still produce combustion models. Hybrid demand revived in 2026, and auto parts stocks moved as a theme.

A news report dated October 8, 2026 grouped Sm Bexel, Hanon Systems, DN Automotive and Seoyon E-Hwa among electrification-related names.

An industry official said companies that diversified into hybrid and EV parts are showing visible earnings improvement, while urging investors' attention to overseas order backlogs and customer diversification rather than theme-driven moves. Samkee's first-half 2026 sales growth fits this pattern.

Because engine and transmission parts still make up a large share of the mix, the pace of electrification and automaker production structures directly affect results. On competitive position, Samkee emphasizes its aluminum die casting technology and its US production base.

Other listed names in the same sector include Hyundai WIA, Sungwoo Hitech, Myoung Shin Industry and Hallacast, and direct product-level comparisons should be checked against each company's disclosures.

The next regular filing is the 2026 annual report, which will confirm full-year operating margin, net income attributable to controlling shareholders and cash flow. The first-quarter 2027 report is the next checkpoint for whether quarterly profitability holds.

Risk factors

Timing of order conversion

The humanoid frame business is at prototype and initial delivery stage, and larger follow-on orders depend on specification evaluation with no disclosed timing. If orders convert to revenue later than expected, the earnings benefit will also be delayed. The timing of revenue recognition should be checked in the annual and quarterly reports.

Profitability and cost

Cost pressure weighed on profitability in the first half of 2026, and operating margin was 1.5% in 2025. If cost increases are not absorbed even as sales grow, operating profit could fall again. The Q4 2025 operating loss shows that quarterly results can be volatile.

Financial leverage

The debt ratio was 308.2% at end-2025, and operating cash flow fell to KRW 27.3 billion in 2025 from KRW 42.4 billion in 2022. Changes in borrowing costs or financing conditions could move earnings and cash flow together. Whether the financial structure improves can be checked in the 2026 annual report.
