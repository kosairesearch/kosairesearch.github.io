Bio-fuel leader eyes marine fuel as Q3 2026 turns to loss

KG Eco Solution is building on its domestic power-plant bio-fuel base to enter bio marine fuel, but a Q3 2026 loss attributable to shareholders and a Korea Fair Trade Commission cartel review are the near-term overhangs.

02

Business structure

KG Eco Solution operates as a holding-type company within the KG Group's automotive, steel and eco-energy lines, and its only directly operated business is bio-fuel oil manufacturing and sales.

Standalone revenue for January to September 2026 was KRW 92.5 billion and operating profit was KRW 12.9 billion, both preliminary. The company says it holds the number one share of the domestic power-plant bio-fuel market, with a cumulative supply of about 60,000 KL as of November 2025.

Its main selling points are that existing bunker-C power plant equipment can be used without modification and the greenhouse gas reduction effect.

Power-plant volumes are supplied through bids from utilities such as Korea South-East Power and Korea Midland Power, and this bidding market is also under Fair Trade Commission cartel review.

The bio marine fuel business is anchored by the Ulsan plant, which has annual capacity of 36,000 tons, and the company expects revenue of roughly KRW 50 billion from it. The company says bio marine fuel is priced USD 200 to 300 per ton below biodiesel, another eco-friendly fuel.

Customers are broadening from utilities to major refiners, and in Q2 2026 the company signed a supply contract with a large domestic refiner for high-quality bio-fuel oil. Affiliates include KG Mobility and a steel subsidiary, KG Steel, whose export share reached 53.1 percent in Q2 2026, the highest in its history. The largest shareholder is KG Chemical, a group company holding 42.64 percent.

04

Earnings analysis

The annual pattern is revenue growth alongside shrinking profit.

Revenue went from KRW 4.94 trillion in 2022 to KRW 7.26 trillion in 2023 (up 46.8 percent year on year), KRW 7.28 trillion in 2024 (up 0.4 percent) and KRW 7.58 trillion in 2025 (up 4.0 percent), rising for three straight years from 2023 to 2025, with 2025 the largest year of 2022 to 2025.

Operating profit went from KRW 356.2 billion in 2022 to KRW 309.8 billion in 2023 (down 13.0 percent), KRW 226.5 billion in 2024 (down 26.9 percent) and KRW 191.3 billion in 2025 (down 15.6 percent), falling for three straight years from 2023 to 2025, with 2022 the largest year.

As a result the operating margin slid from 7.2 percent in 2022 to 4.3, 3.1 and 2.5 percent in 2023 to 2025.

Attributable net income fell from KRW 488.6 billion in 2022 to KRW 66.4 billion in 2023 (down 86.4 percent), then rose two years running to KRW 76.3 billion in 2024 (up 14.9 percent) and KRW 82.4 billion in 2025 (up 8.1 percent).

Net income including non-controlling interests was KRW 183.7 billion in 2025 and should be read separately from attributable net income.

The debt-to-equity ratio moved from 96.6 percent in 2022 to 106.0, 110.0 and then 99.5 percent in 2025, while operating cash flow fell from KRW 338.2 billion in 2024 to KRW 262.8 billion in 2025.

On a quarterly basis, operating profit bottomed at KRW 8.66 billion in Q4 2025, down 85.3 percent from the prior quarter, then recovered to KRW 32.8 billion in Q1 2026 and KRW 51.7 billion in Q2 2026.

The Q2 consolidated gain was helped by a steel subsidiary, whose operating profit rose 16.9 percent year on year, while the company's standalone Q2 operating profit was only KRW 540 million. Q3 2026 brought an operating loss of KRW 22 billion and a loss attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 70.7 billion.

The deterioration came more from below the operating line: Q3 financial costs were KRW 186.6 billion, against roughly KRW 27 billion a year earlier, and currency losses accounted for KRW 141.6 billion of that.

Cumulative operating profit for January to September 2026 fell 65.8 percent year on year, per the November 11, 2026 disclosure. Attributable net income for the latest four quarters totals KRW 41.8 billion, but that total includes the Q3 loss, so quarterly swings are large.

05

Industry analysis

Bio-fuel oil and bio marine fuel sit in the market for eco-friendly substitutes to fossil fuels, and regulation is the basic driver of demand. From 2026 the EU emissions trading scheme applies in full to shipping, which is expected to structurally lift demand for alternative marine fuels.

The company argues that bio marine fuel is priced below biodiesel and can capture demand from shipping lines and refiners that now use biodiesel. That price comparison and demand outlook, however, rest on the company's own explanation.

Domestic power-plant bio-fuel oil is sold through utility tenders, and bids covering KRW 9.7 trillion are now under Fair Trade Commission review, a structural risk for this market.

In cycle terms, bio marine fuel is at an early commercial stage, so its earnings contribution depends on how fast volumes build after the Ulsan plant starts operating.

On competition, the company highlights its number one position in the domestic power-plant market, but its standing against overseas refiners and traders rests mainly on its own disclosures.

The company is classified under automobile bodies and trailers, yet unlike vehicle makers or parts suppliers its core is raw-material sourcing and refining. The company points to a process that refines low-cost, high-acid-value feedstock directly at high temperature, presenting this as a structural margin edge.

Whether that edge can reverse the margin slide to 2.5 percent in 2025 should be checked against quarterly margin trends.

06

Outlook

The company set a near-term revenue target of KRW 187.5 billion for 2026 and medium-term targets of KRW 300 billion for 2028 and KRW 700 billion for 2030, announced at a management briefing in February 2026.

The Ulsan plant, with annual capacity of 36,000 tons, held its completion ceremony in March 2026, and the company said it was coordinating quality standards with refiners so that transactions could become visible.

In Q2 2026 it signed a supply contract for several hundred tons of high-quality bio-fuel oil with a major domestic refiner and scheduled an initial delivery.

After setting up a dedicated R&D unit, it budgeted R&D at about 3 percent of revenue from 2026 and said it would raise that to 5 to 10 percent over the medium term. The company also said it would keep growing in the second half of 2026 through synergies across its business units.

The Q3 loss, however, exposed a gap between targets and reported results. Whether the near-term target is met can be checked in the 2026 annual report. Once the Fair Trade Commission sets the sanction level, the next checkpoint is whether the estimated fine is reflected in the financial statements.

Investors can also track whether quarterly operating margin recovers after the Q3 loss and whether financial costs and currency gains and losses normalize. Revenue from bio marine fuel volumes may become visible in results from the first half of 2027 onward.

08

Bull factors

Regulation-driven marine fuel demand

The EU emissions trading scheme now applies in full to shipping from 2026, and this is expected to lift demand for alternative marine fuels.

The company has added 36,000 tons of annual capacity at Ulsan and has set revenue targets of KRW 300 billion for 2028 and KRW 700 billion for 2030, though these are the company's own plans.

Leading domestic power-plant bio-fuel base

According to the company, it holds the number one share of the domestic power-plant bio-fuel market, with a cumulative supply of about 60,000 KL as of November 2025.

Existing power equipment does not need modification, and standalone operating profit for January to September 2026 was KRW 12.9 billion on a preliminary basis.

Operating profit recovery in Q2 2026

Q2 2026 consolidated operating profit was KRW 51.7 billion, up 6.9 percent year on year and 57.5 percent from Q1. It followed a Q1 operating profit of KRW 32.8 billion, and a steel subsidiary's profit gains helped support the consolidated result.

09

Bear factors

Q3 loss and financial costs

Q3 2026 consolidated operating loss was KRW 22 billion and the loss attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 70.7 billion.

Financial costs rose to KRW 186.6 billion in the quarter from about KRW 27 billion a year earlier, with currency losses of KRW 141.6 billion, and the below-the-line charge drove the net loss more than operations did.

Cartel review and no loss booked

In late July 2026 the Fair Trade Commission's examiners sent a report to seven companies including the company, recommending fines and criminal referrals.

The fine ceiling is 20 percent of related sales, and affected bids total KRW 9.7 trillion, while the company said in its Q3 report that the impact is hard to estimate and booked no loss.

Borrowings and guarantees

Short-term borrowings and current debt at the end of 2024 totaled KRW 1.29 trillion, more than double the prior year.

Two won-denominated guarantees total KRW 313.1 billion, equal to 56 percent of standalone equity, and with the operating margin at 2.5 percent in 2025, a heavier interest burden could weigh on earnings capacity.

10

Risk factors

Regulatory and legal risk

The Fair Trade Commission's bids under review total KRW 9.7 trillion, with a fine ceiling of 20 percent of related sales.

The company says the financial impact is hard to estimate and has not booked an expected fine as a loss, and the examiners' recommendations also include criminal referrals, so the outcome could affect both finances and operations.

Financial and liquidity risk

Card-sales collateral deals carry a clause allowing investors to be repaid early if the credit rating falls two or more notches to BB+ or below, and the current rating is BBB with a stable outlook. Currency losses were a large share of Q3 financial costs, adding volatility to the balance sheet.

Policy dependence and execution risk

Bio marine fuel growth relies on regulation-driven demand such as the EU ETS, so results depend on policy changes and how fast shipping lines adopt the fuel.

The company had said deals could become visible in the first half of 2026, yet standalone Q2 operating profit was only KRW 540 million, and capacity and R&D spending add cost pressure.

Overall view

KG Eco Solution combines a leading domestic power-plant bio-fuel business with bio marine fuel as a growth option. Revenue rose for three straight years from 2023 to 2025, but operating profit fell for three straight years and the operating margin slid to 2.5 percent.

Q2 2026 consolidated operating profit recovered, but Q3 2026 turned to an operating loss of KRW 22 billion and a loss attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 70.7 billion. The Q3 deterioration came more from financial costs and currency losses than from operations.

The Fair Trade Commission examiners' report, the absence of an estimated fine from the books, and borrowing and guarantee burdens remain key variables for financial assessment.

Supporting factors include the outlook for marine fuel demand under the expanding EU emissions trading scheme, the Ulsan capacity expansion and the KRW 300 billion revenue target for 2028. Weighing against it are the Q3 loss, possible cartel sanctions and the debt structure.

The next checkpoints are the Fair Trade Commission's sanction decision, Q4 2026 results and the operating margin and borrowings in the 2026 annual report.
