MSDI: Defense Pivot Hopes Meet Quarterly Losses

MSDI is pushing to add a missile lifecycle business to its image-sensor packaging core, but net losses in the second and third quarters of 2026 make execution of the defense investment the key checkpoint.

02

Business structure

Founded in 2003, MSDI is a packaging company whose core technology is NeoPAC chip-scale packaging for camera image sensors. It listed on KOSDAQ in 2016 through the technology-special route.

Shareholders approved a name change from RNT-X to MSDI at an extraordinary meeting on August 3, 2026, and the formal English name is MSDI Co., Ltd.

After a change in the largest shareholder and the appointment of CEO Hwang Seong-hwan in May 2026, the business purpose was expanded to include missile development and MRO (maintenance, repair and overhaul).

In July 2026 the company was designated a trading agent for military goods by the Defense Acquisition Program Administration. On October 6, 2026 it signed an MOU with Gumi city in Gyeongsangbuk-do for a precision-guided weapons plant, an MRO center and a research facility.

The company describes this as the first step toward an integrated missile complex that handles the full lifecycle of precision-guided weapons in one place.

By product line, first-half 2026 revenue was 76.1% mobile camera module parts painting, 13.8% image sensor packages and 10.1% automotive HVAC components, according to a Money Today report dated October 8, 2026. Because painting is the largest share, shipments of camera modules flow directly into results.

Customers sit in the supply chain for camera modules and image sensor electronics, and packaging technology and process yield are the main competitive factors.

In defense, the company plans to start with MRO and licensed production for early revenue and later expand into fighter system integration and independent development of longer-range missiles.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual revenue rose from KRW 16.3 billion in 2022 to KRW 32.3 billion in 2023 (up 97.7% year on year) and KRW 39.4 billion in 2024 (up 21.9%), then eased to KRW 38.4 billion in 2025 (down 2.5%). Among 2022 to 2025, 2024 was the largest year and 2022 the smallest.

Operating losses were KRW 4.92 billion in 2022, KRW 10.1 billion in 2023 (losses continued and widened) and KRW 6.88 billion in 2024 (losses continued and narrowed), before 2025 posted an operating profit of KRW 208 million, a turn to profit.

The operating margin was -30.2% in 2022, -31.4% in 2023, -17.5% in 2024 and 0.5% in 2025, the first positive reading in the period.

Net loss attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 5.25 billion in 2022, KRW 12.1 billion in 2023, KRW 14.6 billion in 2024 and KRW 3.29 billion in 2025, which makes four consecutive years of losses from 2022 to 2025.

Net loss including non-controlling interests was KRW 12.2 billion in 2023, slightly different from the KRW 12.1 billion attributable to controlling shareholders.

The debt-to-equity ratio climbed from 44.9% in 2022 to 66.5% in 2023, 72.9% in 2024 and 75.2% in 2025, when total equity was KRW 26.4 billion and total liabilities were KRW 19.9 billion.

Operating cash flow went from negative figures of KRW 4.56 billion in 2022, KRW 1.13 billion in 2023 and KRW 3.17 billion in 2024 to positive KRW 4.72 billion in 2025.

Quarterly revenue fell from KRW 11.9 billion in the third quarter of 2025 to KRW 7.9 billion in the fourth quarter (down 33.8% from the prior quarter), then recovered to KRW 9.68 billion in the first quarter of 2026 (up 22.5%).

It then declined for two consecutive quarters, to KRW 8.49 billion in the second quarter and KRW 6.71 billion in the third.

Operating profit of KRW 2.64 billion in the third quarter of 2025 turned into an operating loss of KRW 760 million in the fourth quarter, returned to a KRW 1.35 billion profit in the first quarter of 2026, then fell to KRW 512 million in the second quarter (down 62.1%) and swung to an operating loss of KRW 1.9 billion in the third quarter.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders was a loss in every quarter from the fourth quarter of 2025 except the first quarter of 2026, when it was KRW 1.36 billion, and it has been a loss for two consecutive quarters since the second quarter of 2026 (net loss of KRW 204 million in the second quarter and KRW 2.58 billion in the third).

The sum of controlling-shareholder net losses over the last four quarters, from the fourth quarter of 2025 to the third quarter of 2026, was KRW 5.22 billion.

05

Industry analysis

MSDI's core business is image-sensor packaging for cameras in smartphones, laptops and automobiles, so camera module shipments and the shift to higher pixel counts drive demand. This demand moves with set-maker production plans, so changes in customer orders feed quickly into revenue.

The company's painting segment supports mobile camera module parts processing and tracks utilization of the core business.

In defense, the Defense Acquisition Program Administration signed an agreement on a Gyeongbuk and Gumi defense innovation cluster in 2023 and plans to spend KRW 49.9 billion through 2027 on a specialized development institute, infrastructure and jobs programs.

A researcher at the independent firm Value Finder said recurring missile maintenance demand could form the basis of repeat revenue. The same researcher described the first-phase investment of about KRW 700 billion as a test of execution capacity.

Among listed defense companies, Hanwha Aerospace is a large peer by market value, but MSDI emphasizes a model in which one company handles development, production and MRO.

The company is reportedly pursuing joint development of the next version of a medium-to-long-range air-to-surface missile with a European missile specialist and domestic production. The core business sits in the camera module demand cycle, while defense is at an early execution stage.

Outlook

The center of the company's stated plan is the first phase of the Gumi defense complex, with about KRW 700 billion to be invested through 2029 to build a missile production plant and an MRO center. A second phase would expand the site into a full-lifecycle cluster that hosts domestic and foreign defense companies.

In October 2026 a company official said talks with global defense majors were in their final stage and that concrete results could be announced soon. However, the timing and contract size of any such announcement have not been disclosed.

On the funding side, the new shares from a third-party allotment rights offering of KRW 2.0 billion decided on September 29, 2026 are scheduled to list on November 17, 2026, and the proceeds are to be fully used as operating funds in 2026 for balance-sheet improvement and new growth initiatives.

A report said cumulative convertible bond issuance decisions reached KRW 46.4 billion, and the most recently disclosed seventh tranche of KRW 20.0 billion has a scheduled payment date of December 10, 2026. Compared with total equity of KRW 26.4 billion at the end of 2025, the KRW 700 billion first-phase plan is large.

The next periodic report is the 2026 annual report, which will contain fourth-quarter and full-year 2026 figures. The defense business will develop depending on whether investment execution and contract announcements follow, while the core business depends on whether camera-module revenue and operating profit recover.

Bull factors

Institutional footing for defense

Registration as a military-goods trading agent in July 2026 and the Gumi MOU in October 2026 gave the missile business an institutional foothold. Value Finder described a sequence of MRO and licensed production for early revenue, widening into fighter system integration. The European OEM-based one-stop lifecycle model was presented as a strength.

2025 profit turn and cash flow recovery

Operating profit of KRW 208 million in 2025 turned the year from loss to profit, and operating cash flow of KRW 4.72 billion turned positive. The operating margin also rose from -17.5% in 2024 to 0.5% in 2025.

A first-quarter 2026 operating profit of KRW 1.35 billion shows the profit turn has appeared within a quarter as well.

Image-sensor packaging technology base

The company holds NeoPAC CSP packaging technology, and 13.8% of first-half 2026 revenue came from image sensor packages. If camera module demand and the move to higher pixel counts continue, packaging volumes could benefit. Automotive HVAC parts add some breadth to the revenue mix.

Overall view

MSDI is widening from its image-sensor packaging core toward a full missile lifecycle business, and the August 2026 name change, the trading-agent designation and the Gumi MOU have given the defense plan its formal structure.

In the public results, 2025 operating profit of KRW 208 million turned the year positive and operating cash flow also turned positive. By contrast, third-quarter 2026 revenue of KRW 6.71 billion fell 43.7% from a year earlier and an operating loss of KRW 1.9 billion returned.

On the financial side, the debt-to-equity ratio rose from 44.9% in 2022 to 75.2% in 2025, and cumulative convertible bond decisions and potential dilution have been reported together.

The KRW 700 billion first-phase defense investment is large relative to the company's size, and whether cooperation with European OEMs and contract announcements turn into revenue is the key variable. Because the core painting segment is highly concentrated, camera module demand recovery also needs to be watched.

The next checkpoints are the listing of new rights-offering shares on November 17, 2026, the convertible bond payment on December 10, 2026, and the full-year results in the 2026 annual report.
