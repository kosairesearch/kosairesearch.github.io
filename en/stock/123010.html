02

Business structure

MSDI was founded in 2003 as Optopac and grew as a specialist in CMOS/CCD image sensor wafer-level chip-scale packaging (WLCSP) based on its patented NeoPAC technology. The company later became I-WinPlus and then Rnt-X before adopting its current name, MSDI, in August 2026.

In May 2026, the controlling shareholder changed to WithWin Investment Partnership No. 91, and CEO Sung-hwan Hwang, a former head of the Precision Guided Weapons Program Office at the Defense Acquisition Program Administration, was appointed, triggering a major shift in the company's business direction.

Independent research firm Value Finder reported on October 8, 2026 that first-half 2026 revenue was split among mobile camera module coating (76.1%), image sensor packaging (13.8%), and automotive air-conditioning parts (10.1%). In image sensor packaging, Israel's Shellcase has long been cited as a key competitor.

The company completed registration as a munitions trading agent with the Defense Acquisition Program Administration in July 2026 and formalized its defense pivot with the name change in August.

On October 6, it signed an MOU with Gumi city to build a precision-guided weapons plant, an MRO center, and a research institute, outlining a one-stop business model spanning the entire missile life cycle.

According to the Value Finder report, the plan is to generate initial revenue from MRO and licensed production, later expanding into fighter aircraft system integration and, longer term, independent development of ground-to-ground and anti-ship missiles.

For now, the legacy image sensor, camera module, and automotive parts businesses continue alongside the new defense initiative.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual revenue rose from KRW 16.3 billion in 2022 to KRW 32.3 billion in 2023 (up 97.7%), grew a further 21.9% to KRW 39.4 billion in 2024, then slipped 2.5% to KRW 38.4 billion in 2025.

Operating losses of KRW 4.92 billion in 2022 widened to KRW 10.1 billion in 2023 before narrowing to KRW 6.88 billion in 2024, and the company turned to an operating profit of KRW 208 million in 2025.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders deteriorated from a loss of KRW 5.25 billion in 2022 to KRW 12.1 billion in 2023 and KRW 14.6 billion in 2024, before narrowing to a loss of KRW 3.29 billion in 2025, marking four consecutive years of losses from 2022 through 2025.

The operating margin improved from -30.2% in 2022 to 0.5% in 2025, and operating cash flow, negative throughout 2022-2024, turned positive at KRW 4.72 billion in 2025.

On a quarterly basis, revenue jumped 53.5% quarter-on-quarter to KRW 11.9 billion in Q3 2025 with operating profit swinging to KRW 2.64 billion, but then fell 33.8% to KRW 7.9 billion in Q4 2025 with operating losses of KRW 760 million, underscoring significant volatility.

In Q1 2026, revenue reached KRW 9.68 billion with operating profit of KRW 1.35 billion, but by Q2 2026 revenue eased to KRW 8.49 billion (down 12.3% quarter-on-quarter, up 9.3% year-on-year) and operating profit shrank to KRW 512 million (down 62.1% quarter-on-quarter, though a turnaround from a year earlier).

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders also flipped from a profit of KRW 1.36 billion in Q1 2026 to a loss of KRW 204 million in Q2 2026, with the cumulative net loss attributable to controlling shareholders over the last four quarters (Q3 2025 to Q2 2026) totaling only KRW 237 million.

The swings in operating profit and net income have been larger than the swings in revenue, suggesting earnings quality remains unstable.

05

Industry analysis

The image sensor packaging (WLCSP) market is a mature industry tied to mobile and automotive camera demand, known for intense price competition and thin margins.

The mobile camera module coating business, now accounting for a large share of revenue, similarly depends on smartphone and notebook camera module production cycles. Automotive air-conditioning parts represent a smaller revenue stream exposed to fluctuations in the broader auto industry.

By contrast, the newly entered missile and defense sector is viewed as an area with expanding export opportunities amid rising geopolitical tensions and increased defense spending by various countries.

Gumi city, which has an established electronics and defense manufacturing base, is pursuing an industrial complex with local government backing.

However, the missile life-cycle business requires substantial capital and time across development, certification, and mass production, and as a new entrant the company's position relative to established defense primes remains at an early stage.

In the legacy image sensor packaging business, competitors such as Israel's Shellcase have existed for years, and price competitiveness from Chinese back-end packaging firms has also been a factor.

06

Outlook

A third-party rights issue of KRW 2.0 billion is due to be paid in on October 29, 2026, with the resulting 685,166 new shares scheduled to list on November 17, adding further dilution.

The 8th-series convertible bond carries a monthly conversion-price reset mechanism; the conversion price was lowered from KRW 3,360 to KRW 2,672 on September 11, increasing the number of convertible shares, and further repricing remains possible.

The first phase of the Gumi city plan calls for roughly KRW 700 billion to be invested by 2029 to build a missile production plant and MRO center, with site acquisition targeted for completion by the end of 2026.

The company has stated that negotiations with several global defense companies over joint missile development, production, and MRO facility establishment are in their final stages.

With a European missile specialist, the company is pursuing joint development of a next-generation medium/long-range air-to-surface missile for domestic production.

Since operating cash flow turned positive in 2025, the legacy business could contribute some funding, though its self-funding capacity is limited relative to the scale of the planned KRW 700 billion investment.

The next quarterly report (Q3 2026) will be worth checking for updated operating margin and capital-structure trends.

Bull factors

Turnaround and Improved Cash Flow

Operating profit turned positive at KRW 208 million in 2025, and operating cash flow also flipped positive to KRW 4.72 billion. In the first half of 2026, the operating margin improved to around 10%, continuing the profitability recovery. Despite quarterly swings, the company has moved away from the heavy losses seen in 2022-2024.

Momentum from the Missile Defense Pivot

The company brought in a professional CEO with a background at the Defense Acquisition Program Administration and signed a KRW 700 billion MOU with Gumi city for a defense industrial complex. Institutional groundwork, including registration as a munitions trading agent, has also been put in place.

Joint development talks with a European missile company are underway, and the company has stated that negotiations with global defense firms are in their final stages.

Patented Niche Technology and Some Added Financial Flexibility

The NeoPAC packaging technology is based on dozens of in-house patents. Revenue is spread across mobile camera module coating, image sensor packaging, and automotive air-conditioning parts. The swing to positive operating cash flow could provide some support for financing new investment.

Bear factors

Dilution and Overhang Pressure

Potential shares from convertible bonds and rights issues are estimated to exceed 110% of shares outstanding. On November 17, an additional 685,166 shares from a third-party allotment are set to list.

The 8th-series convertible bond resets its conversion price monthly, meaning a falling share price could further increase the number of convertible shares.

Execution Risk at an Early Stage of the Defense Pivot

The MOU with Gumi city represents only the first step of the business transition, and concrete contracts or orders have not yet been finalized. The funding plan for the roughly KRW 700 billion first-phase investment has also not been detailed.

Negotiations with the European missile company and global defense firms are still ongoing, with an uncertain completion timeline.

Volatility and Limited Profitability in the Legacy Business

Quarterly revenue and operating profit have fluctuated widely, limiting earnings predictability. The mobile camera module coating business, which accounts for a large share of revenue, is sensitive to customer order patterns and the broader economic cycle. Net income attributable to controlling shareholders over the last four quarters has remained in a loss.

Risk factors

Financial and Capital Structure Risk

The debt-to-equity ratio has trended higher, from 44.9% in 2022 to 75.2% in 2025. Repeated convertible bond issuances and third-party rights offerings are adding complexity to the capital structure. Further fundraising could increase both dilution and financial leverage pressure.

Business Transition Execution Risk

Building the missile production and MRO facilities requires several remaining steps, including site acquisition, certification, and overseas partnership agreements. Roughly KRW 700 billion is planned to be invested by 2029, but the specific funding structure has not yet been disclosed. A significant time lag may occur before the business transition is reflected in revenue.

Governance and Funding Transparency Risk

A June 2026 news report raised concerns that some entities behind recent convertible bond and capital-raising inflows had unclear identities. The company's history of multiple controlling-shareholder changes is also a factor worth monitoring from a governance-stability perspective. This funding history could affect the credibility of any future capital raises.

12

Overall view

MSDI gained some financial breathing room as operating profit and operating cash flow turned positive in 2025, and in 2026 the company has moved decisively to pivot into the missile defense business.

The KRW 700 billion MOU with Gumi city and the defense expertise of new management add specificity to the transition, but these have not yet translated into signed contracts or revenue contribution.

At the same time, potential shares from convertible bonds and rights issues exceed 110% of shares outstanding, keeping dilution risk in place, with new shares set to list in November.

The legacy image sensor, camera module, and automotive parts businesses remain volatile on a quarterly basis, and cumulative net income attributable to controlling shareholders over the last four quarters is still in a loss.

The company's history of repeated controlling-shareholder changes and questions about the transparency of some funding sources are also worth watching.

The next quarterly report, progress on securing the Gumi site, and the signing of overseas partnerships will be key indicators of how far the business transition actually advances.
