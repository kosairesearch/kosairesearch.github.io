Worldex: Operating Profit Holds While Net Result Turns Loss

WORLDEX INDUSTRY & TRADING CO. makes silicon and quartz parts for semiconductor etching, and its third-quarter 2026 operating profit rose year on year while profit attributable to controlling shareholders swung to a loss.

  1. 1

    Third-quarter 2026 revenue was KRW 76.5 billion, up 14.3% year on year, and operating profit was KRW 14.4 billion, up 10.9%.

  2. 2

    Net loss attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 3.11 billion, a swing into loss from the year-earlier quarter.

  3. 3

    Full-year 2025 operating profit was KRW 58.5 billion, down 16.7% from the prior year, and net income attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 41 billion, down 37.0%.

  4. 4

    Samsung Electronics and SK hynix together account for 45% of consolidated revenue, a high level of customer concentration.

  5. 5

    The 2026 value-up plan includes a KRW 260 billion investment and a target to raise the dividend payout ratio to 10%.

02

Business structure

WORLDEX INDUSTRY & TRADING CO. was founded in 2000 to manufacture semiconductor and electronic parts, listed on KOSDAQ in 2008, and grew into a materials company after acquiring the US silicon ingot and quartz specialist WCQ in 2009.

Its core products are silicon and quartz parts used in the etching stage of front-end chip manufacturing, and it also runs a fine ceramics business.

According to THE ELEC, silicon-based consumable parts accounted for KRW 151.6 billion, or 73% of company revenue, in the cumulative first three quarters of 2025, while quartz-based products reached KRW 36.7 billion (18%) and ceramics reached KRW 19.3 billion (9%).

In the same report the company said 45% of third-quarter revenue came from Samsung Electronics and SK hynix, supplying integrated silicon cathodes for dry etching to both and showerheads for 12-inch etch equipment to Samsung Electronics.

Overseas customers named include Micron, Kioxia and Intel, and 66% of the cumulative revenue came from outside Korea. Export share as of December 2025 was 63.4% for silicon products and 77.3% for other quartz products.

A February 2024 company analysis by Daishin Securities said earnings move with aftermarket parts adoption rates and customer chip shipments. R&D spending was 2,417 million won in 2025, equal to 0.83% of revenue. The company said its 2022 investment at its Gumi 5th complex significantly expanded production capacity.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual revenue rose from KRW 255.9 billion in 2022 to KRW 306.8 billion in 2024 before falling 4.9% to KRW 291.8 billion in 2025. The largest year in the 2022 to 2025 span was 2024.

Operating profit climbed from KRW 50.7 billion in 2022 to KRW 64.8 billion in 2023, up 27.9%, and KRW 70.3 billion in 2024, up 8.4%, before falling 16.7% to KRW 58.5 billion in 2025. The operating margin went from 19.8% in 2022 to 22.5% in 2023 and 22.9% in 2024, then eased to 20.1% in 2025.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders reached KRW 41 billion in 2025, down 37.0%, which makes 2025 the smallest year of the four. Net income including non-controlling interests matched the controlling-shareholder figure in each of the four years as filed.

Operating cash flow went from KRW 82.9 billion in 2023 to KRW 35 billion in 2024 and recovered to KRW 60.8 billion in 2025.

On a quarterly basis, revenue peaked at KRW 84.1 billion and operating profit at KRW 22.4 billion in the fourth quarter of 2025, then fell to KRW 69.1 billion and KRW 14.3 billion in the first quarter of 2026.

Revenue then rose for two consecutive quarters, to KRW 75.4 billion in the second quarter and KRW 76.5 billion in the third quarter of 2026, while operating profit was KRW 16.5 billion and KRW 14.4 billion.

In the third quarter of 2026 operating profit rose 10.9% year on year, but the company posted a net loss of KRW 3.11 billion attributable to controlling shareholders, a swing into loss from the year-earlier quarter. Net income attributable to controlling shareholders over the last four quarters totals KRW 59.7 billion.

Because operating profit and bottom-line results moved in opposite directions in the third quarter, the non-operating items in the 2026 annual report are the next point to confirm.

05

Industry analysis

The semiconductor consumables market that Worldex serves moves with wafer investment and fab utilization. A February 2024 company analysis by Daishin Securities noted that earnings were weak through the third quarter of 2023 because key customers cut output, while demand for AI server chips remained firm.

A first-quarter 2026 earnings commentary cited growth in AI, data center and HPC demand, the start of 2-nanometer GAA production, and expected expansion of equipment investment on higher HBM demand.

Securities analysts at the end of 2025 projected rising parts demand in 2026 from NAND layer-stacking and recovering utilization. In its third-quarter 2025 remarks, the company said the boom at domestic HBM makers had a spillover effect on its results.

Because its customer base leans toward memory makers, earnings track the memory industry cycle closely. Ceramics revenue fell to KRW 19.3 billion in the cumulative first three quarters of 2025, below the prior-year period, so the business remains centered on silicon and quartz.

Compared with listed materials and parts peers such as Soulbrain, Tokai Carbon Korea and Hansol Chemical, Worldex has high customer concentration within the narrow etching-process niche.

06

Outlook

The company's medium-term plan puts weight on facility investment and expanded shareholder returns. Edaily reported that the 2026 value-up plan includes a KRW 260 billion investment and a plan to raise dividends.

The payout ratio for the latest fiscal year was 4.03%, and the company said it would raise the ratio step by step toward a 10% target. The company said revenue has grown at an average of about 9% a year since its 2022 investment at the Gumi 5th complex.

On the technology side, the first-quarter 2026 commentary pointed to expected equipment investment on the start of 2-nanometer GAA production and rising HBM demand. An THE ELEC report headline said the early competitive edge from adopting 18-inch wafers was expected.

Securities analysts at the end of 2025 projected rising parts demand in 2026 for NAND layer-stacking and higher utilization. A 2026 first-half summary showed revenue up 2.7% and operating profit up 33.1% year on year, with net income turning positive.

The next checkpoint is whether the 2026 annual report shows capital spending and total dividends at the levels the plan describes.

Bull factors

AI and HBM demand flowing into etching-part demand

A first-quarter 2026 commentary pointed to AI, data center and HPC demand growth, the start of 2-nanometer GAA production and rising HBM demand as drivers of expanded equipment investment.

Worldex's silicon and quartz parts are consumables in the etching process, so larger memory investment can flow through to parts demand. Third-quarter 2026 revenue was KRW 76.5 billion, up 14.3% year on year.

Dividend expansion under the value-up plan

Edaily reported that the 2026 value-up plan includes a KRW 260 billion investment and a higher dividend payout. The payout ratio in the latest fiscal year was 4.03%, and the company said it would raise it step by step toward 10%.

Because the dividend yield sits below the industry average, the next point to confirm is how much total dividend payments actually rise.

Stronger balance sheet and cash generation

The debt ratio fell to 24.0% at the end of 2025 from 40.8% in 2022, and total equity stood at KRW 346.6 billion. Operating cash flow rose to KRW 60.8 billion in 2025 from KRW 35 billion in 2024. Net income attributable to controlling shareholders over the last four quarters totals KRW 59.7 billion.

Bear factors

Annual profit decline and a third-quarter net loss

Operating profit fell 16.7% to KRW 58.5 billion in 2025, and net income attributable to controlling shareholders fell 37.0%. In the third quarter of 2026, operating profit rose year on year to KRW 14.4 billion, but the company posted a net loss of KRW 3.11 billion attributable to controlling shareholders.

Because the two measures moved in opposite directions, the coming periodic reports will be the data that show whether earnings quality is improving.

Customer concentration and reliance on overseas demand

Samsung Electronics and SK hynix together account for 45% of revenue, so earnings are sensitive to their capital spending and output decisions. The export share was 67.3% as of December 2025, which leaves the company exposed to overseas demand and exchange-rate moves. Earnings weakened through 2023 when key customers cut output, which is the precedent behind this concern.

Pace of downstream recovery and cost pressure

A commentary on the first half of 2025 described profitability as limited by slow recovery in downstream demand and cost pressure. Revenue fell 17.9% quarter on quarter to KRW 69.1 billion in the first quarter of 2026.

Revenue then rose for two consecutive quarters, but the third-quarter 2026 increase was only 1.4% from the prior quarter.

10

Risk factors

Customer concentration and memory cycle

Because 45% of revenue comes from two memory customers, a cut in their investment or inventory adjustment can quickly change orders. The memory industry cycles between output cuts and capacity additions, so quarterly revenue can swing widely. The high overseas share also exposes the company to global demand shifts.

Volatility of net results and non-operating items

In the third quarter of 2026 operating profit was KRW 14.4 billion, yet the company posted a net loss of KRW 3.11 billion attributable to controlling shareholders, so the two measures moved in opposite directions. Non-operating items and tax expenses can heavily influence quarterly net income.

Net income jumped to KRW 29.8 billion in the fourth quarter of 2025 from the third quarter, and such swings make the trend harder to read.

Large investment burden and execution of return policy

The KRW 260 billion investment is large relative to total equity of KRW 346.6 billion at the end of 2025, so the pace of spending and how it is funded will shape the financial burden. The plan to raise the payout ratio to 10% could also deliver a smaller increase than targeted if earnings or investment spending shift. Capital expenditure details can be tracked in the capex items of periodic reports.

12

Overall view

Worldex is a materials company whose core products are silicon and quartz parts for etching, and its profile is defined by a customer base centered on Samsung Electronics and SK hynix and a high overseas revenue share.

Operating profit peaked at KRW 70.3 billion in 2024, and in 2025 both operating profit and net income attributable to controlling shareholders declined. In 2026 revenue rose for two consecutive quarters after a first-quarter low, and third-quarter operating profit grew year on year.

However, the third quarter brought a net loss of KRW 3.11 billion attributable to controlling shareholders, a swing from the prior year that leaves the direction of earnings still to be confirmed.

Bullish factors include demand for high-performance memory such as AI and HBM chips, a balance sheet with the debt ratio down to 24.0%, and the plan to raise the dividend payout ratio.

Bearish factors include the decline in 2025 annual earnings, concentration on memory customers, dependence on the memory cycle and volatility in net results.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders over the last four quarters totals KRW 59.7 billion, which shows earnings capacity remains, but quarter-to-quarter swings are wide. The next checkpoint is the non-operating income composition and capital spending detail in the 2026 annual report.
