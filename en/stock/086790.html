Report overview

Fee growth and payout pace are the focus

Hana Financial Group's quarterly profit attributable to owners stayed above KRW 1 trillion for three straight quarters in 2026, with fee income growth and share-buyback-led shareholder returns as its two main drivers.

  1. 1

    Profit attributable to owners recovered from KRW 569.4 billion in 4Q 2025 to KRW 1.21 trillion in 1Q 2026 and has stayed above KRW 1 trillion since.

  2. 2

  3. 3

02

Business structure

Hana Financial Group is a financial holding company centered on banking, with non-bank affiliates in securities, cards and capital.

The group's flagship bank earned KRW 1.1946 trillion in net income in the third quarter, and its cumulative net income through the third quarter reached KRW 3.3157 trillion, up 5.8% year on year. The bank's fee income rose 11.5% to KRW 874.0 billion over the same nine months, a record for the period.

At group level, cumulative net interest income was KRW 74.832 trillion and fee income KRW 21.428 trillion. Growth in fee income was driven by the wealth management (WM) and investment banking (IB) divisions.

Among non-bank units, the group's securities unit recorded cumulative net income of KRW 283.4 billion through the third quarter, up 67.1% year on year. The fee share of core income rose from 19.6% to 22.3% year on year. The flagship bank's net interest margin (NIM) was 1.57% in the third quarter.

Group total assets including trust assets were KRW 924.4456 trillion. Earnings rest on two pillars: net interest income from banking and fee income from non-bank operations.

The group is typically compared with KB Financial Group, Shinhan Financial Group and Woori Financial Group as one of the four major financial holding companies.

04

Earnings analysis

On the disclosed annual figures, insurance revenue (an insurance-related account, not total company revenue) rose from KRW 489.2 billion in 2022 to KRW 648.2 billion in 2025, up 12.9%, 9.7% and 6.9% in 2023, 2024 and 2025.

It has increased three years in a row from 2023 through 2025, and 2025 was the largest year of the 2022–2025 period. Operating profit went from KRW 4.69 trillion in 2022 to KRW 4.69 trillion in 2023 (up 0.1%), then rose 3.4% to KRW 4.86 trillion in 2024 and 10.2% to KRW 5.35 trillion in 2025.

Profit attributable to owners fell 4.2% to KRW 3.42 trillion in 2023, the smallest year of the period, before rising 9.3% in 2024 to KRW 3.74 trillion and 7.1% in 2025 to KRW 4 trillion.

Net income including non-controlling interests is a separate figure, ranging from KRW 3.64 trillion in 2022 to KRW 4.04 trillion in 2025. The debt-to-equity ratio fell from 1,411.1% in 2022 to 1,363.7% in 2024 before edging up to 1,377.9% in 2025.

Total equity at end-2025 was KRW 45.6 trillion, of which KRW 44.6 trillion belonged to owners of the parent. Operating cash flow swung from KRW 25.2 trillion in 2022 to -KRW 9.62 trillion in 2023, then narrowed its outflow to -KRW 2.45 trillion in 2024 and -KRW 1.96 trillion in 2025.

Quarterly operating profit fell from KRW 1.48 trillion in 3Q 2025 to KRW 890.4 billion in 4Q 2025 (down 39.8%), then rebounded to KRW 1.65 trillion in 1Q 2026 (up 85.7%) before KRW 1.59 trillion in 2Q and KRW 1.63 trillion in 3Q 2026.

Quarterly profit attributable to owners went from KRW 569.4 billion in 4Q 2025 to KRW 1.21 trillion, KRW 1.19 trillion and KRW 1.22 trillion in the three 2026 quarters. The four quarters from 4Q 2025 to 3Q 2026 total KRW 4.19 trillion.

Cumulative fee income through the third quarter rose 29.8%, and net interest margin fell from the prior quarter and fee income dropped on weaker stock trading.

05

Industry analysis

Bank earnings in Korea move on two axes, net interest margin and fee income. Quarterly net income stayed around KRW 1.2 trillion for three straight quarters, but investors appear to have focused on the decline in net interest margin and lower market-related income.

Net interest income faces pressure from the rate environment, while fee income is sensitive to trading volumes in equity markets. Fee lines were supported by demand in brokerage, trust, wrap accounts and M&A advisory, with cumulative fee income up 29.8%.

On policy, the company met the high-dividend company requirements last year and secured tax-exempt funding at this year's shareholder meeting. Brokers have argued that a higher dividend could steady the share price.

Analysts pointed to the normalization of non-bank earnings as the next task, and said dividend increases to qualify for separate taxation and a buyback-centered return policy could be a further driver of the share price.

In comparisons among the four major financial holding companies, non-bank earnings weight and the pace of shareholder returns are the common yardsticks.

Shinhan Investment raised the possibility of additional shareholder-return expansion in the second half, citing strong earnings capacity and stable capital ratios, while Sangsangin and Kiwoom also viewed the recovery in non-bank operations and profitability positively.

These views were issued right after the first-quarter results, so whether non-bank improvement continues after the third quarter is the key checkpoint.

06

Outlook

Share buybacks and cancellations for 2026 total KRW 949 billion. Including KRW 699 billion already repurchased through the third quarter, the cumulative 2026 total reached KRW 949 billion, up 25.8% from the 2025 full-year figure of KRW 754.1 billion.

In July, the board decided to buy back and fully cancel KRW 250 billion of its own shares, with the purchase period running from the 27th to October 22. On capital, the common equity tier 1 ratio estimate of 13.45% at end-September met the 13% or higher target set in its corporate value-up plan 2.0.

The company has set a target of a 50% shareholder return ratio by 2027. On dividends, the CFO said the cash dividend would again rise 10% from the prior year, and described a plan to gradually raise the cash dividend share instead of focusing only on buybacks and cancellations.

On further returns, the company said it would watch the non-bank earnings trend through the second quarter before deciding. Shinhan Investment projected in a report in early September 2026 that the total shareholder return ratio for the year would reach 51.0%.

The company did not give a plan for additional buybacks in the fourth quarter. The 2026 annual report will disclose the full-year return ratio and full-year results, which is the formal point to check whether the annual target was met.

08

Bull factors

Stable quarterly earnings

The company says its underlying earning power has strengthened, with profit near KRW 1.2 trillion each quarter this year. Profit attributable to owners was KRW 1.21 trillion in 1Q, KRW 1.19 trillion in 2Q and KRW 1.22 trillion in 3Q 2026. Cumulative core income through the third quarter reached KRW 9.626 trillion, up 14.2% year on year.

Diversifying fee income

Cumulative fee income through the third quarter rose 29.8%, driving solid growth. The fee share of core income increased from 19.6% to 22.3%. A larger fee base can partly offset swings in net interest income.

Larger shareholder returns

Buybacks and cancellations for 2026 total KRW 949 billion, up 25.8% from the prior year and described as the largest level on record. A Shinhan Investment report projected the 2026 total shareholder return ratio at 51.0%, up 4.2 percentage points from the prior year.

09

Bear factors

Pressure on margin and fees

Net interest margin declined from the prior quarter, and fee income also fell on slower stock trading. Third-quarter fee income was KRW 655.4 billion, down 20% from the prior quarter. When interest and fee income weaken together, the quarterly earnings cushion narrows.

Quarterly volatility

Operating profit fell 39.8% to KRW 890.4 billion in 4Q 2025 before jumping 85.7% to KRW 1.65 trillion in 1Q 2026, then dipped 3.6% in 2Q and rose 2.3% in 3Q. Quarter-to-quarter swings are large, so the full-year trend can only be confirmed through the annual totals.

Uncertainty on further buybacks

The company did not give a plan for additional fourth-quarter buybacks. Further buybacks and cancellations are subject to board approval. If the additional announcement that brokers expect does not come, return expectations could be revised.

10

Risk factors

Rates and net interest income

The flagship bank's net interest margin was 1.57% in the third quarter, a baseline for net interest income. If rate conditions compress deposit and loan spreads, both net interest income and core income would be affected. Cumulative net interest income reached KRW 74.832 trillion, so any margin decline carries a large effect.

Capital and asset quality

The CET1 ratio estimate at end-September rose 0.18 percentage points from the prior quarter to 13.45%. Cumulative credit cost through the third quarter was 0.30%. Capital headroom underpins dividends and buybacks, so higher credit costs could constrain the return plan.

Market-sensitive fee income

Investors appear to have focused on the lower margin and reduced market-related income. Brokerage and wrap account fees move with equity trading volume each quarter. With the fee share at 22.3%, a market slowdown could weigh more heavily on earnings.

12

Overall view

Hana Financial Group's insurance revenue and operating profit both rose across 2022 to 2025, and profit attributable to owners recovered after its 2023 low.

In 2026 quarterly profit attributable to owners stayed above KRW 1 trillion, at KRW 1.21 trillion, KRW 1.19 trillion and KRW 1.22 trillion in the first three quarters. Fee income rose 29.8%, diversifying the earnings base, and 2026 buybacks and cancellations expanded to KRW 949 billion.

On the other side, third-quarter net interest margin declined and fee income fell from the prior quarter. The next checkpoints are additional buybacks in the fourth quarter and the full-year return ratio in the 2026 annual report.

On valuation, the CFO's remarks on the price-to-book ratio and broker target prices should be read together. This report gives no investment rating, recommendation or target price and is provided for information only.
