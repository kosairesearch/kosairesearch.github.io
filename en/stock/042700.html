HANMI Semiconductor: HBM bonder demand drives earnings swings

HANMI Semiconductor's earnings depend heavily on demand for its HBM TC bonders, and quarterly results have swung widely since the fourth quarter of 2025.

02

Business structure

HANMI Semiconductor is a back-end semiconductor equipment maker whose core product is the thermo-compression (TC) bonder used to stack HBM (high-bandwidth memory). A TC bonder applies heat and pressure to fix chips as DRAM dies are stacked vertically, which makes it essential to HBM production.

Industry reports say the company holds the world's No. 1 share in TC bonders, and its main customers are SK hynix and Micron.

Its lineup centers on dual TC bonders such as the TC Bonder 4 for HBM4 and the upgraded TC Bonder 4.5 Griffin, while new TC bonders for large-area AI packaging and flip-chip (FC) bonders are cited as new growth drivers.

At a 2025 briefing the company said it had received orders from a major customer for flux-less bonders and was preparing deliveries. Overseas customers accounted for about 72 percent of total revenue, as announced on October 30, 2026.

Reports from 2025 said TC bonder margins overseas run 30 to 40 percent above domestic levels. Beyond subsidiaries in Taiwan, China and Singapore, the company set up a U.S. unit, HANMI USA, in San Jose, California.

A domestic rival, Hanwha Semitech, was added as an SK hynix TC bonder supplier, while Singapore-based ASMPT is cited as a competitor in HBM4 TC bonders. At a 2025 briefing the company forecast that overseas customer revenue would exceed twice the revenue from one domestic customer.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual revenue fell from KRW 327.6 billion in 2022 to KRW 159 billion in 2023, a decline of 51.5%, before recovering to KRW 558.9 billion in 2024, an increase of 251.5%.

Revenue of KRW 576.7 billion in 2025, up 3.2%, was the largest year of 2022 to 2025, and 2024 and 2025 were two consecutive years of growth following 2023.

Operating profit was KRW 111.9 billion in 2022, KRW 34.6 billion in 2023 (down 69.1%), KRW 255.4 billion in 2024 (up 638.7%) and KRW 251.4 billion in 2025 (down 1.6%), with 2024 the largest year of the four.

The operating margin was 34.1 percent in 2022, 21.7 percent in 2023, 45.7 percent in 2024 and 43.6 percent in 2025, staying at a high level even as revenue scaled up.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders went from KRW 92.3 billion in 2022 to KRW 267.2 billion in 2023 (up 189.6%), the largest year of the four, then to KRW 152.6 billion in 2024 (down 42.9%) and KRW 214 billion in 2025 (up 40.2%).

At the end of 2025, total liabilities were KRW 123 billion and total equity was KRW 690.3 billion, giving a debt ratio of 17.8 percent, below 31.4 percent in 2024. Operating cash flow was KRW 109.5 billion in 2022, KRW 45 billion in 2023, KRW 141.4 billion in 2024 and KRW 228.6 billion in 2025.

On a quarterly basis, revenue of KRW 166.2 billion and operating profit of KRW 67.8 billion in the third quarter of 2025 were followed by fourth-quarter revenue of KRW 83 billion (down 50.1% quarter on quarter) and operating profit of KRW 27.6 billion (down 59.3%).

In the first quarter of 2026 revenue was KRW 50.9 billion and operating profit KRW 8.46 billion (down 69.4% quarter on quarter), the low point of the last five quarters, a period when delayed HBM4 orders from customers were cited.

The second quarter of 2026 rebounded to revenue of KRW 251.2 billion (up 393.4%) and operating profit of KRW 130.3 billion (up 1,441.4%). Third-quarter revenue of KRW 181 billion and operating profit of KRW 90.8 billion fell quarter on quarter but rose 8.9 percent and 33.9 percent year on year.

Third-quarter net income attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 34.9 billion fell 46.8 percent year on year, moving in the opposite direction to operating profit, and the sum over the last four quarters was KRW 191.3 billion.

The third-quarter operating margin of 50.2 percent, announced on October 30, 2026, was 9.4 percentage points above 40.8 percent a year earlier, while the company pointed to the weaker won-translated value and some equipment revenue shifting into the fourth quarter as drags.

05

Industry analysis

The HBM market structure means that thermo-compression bonders become more important as DRAM stacks grow taller. Industry commentary says new TC bonder orders temporarily slowed during the shift from HBM3E (fifth generation) to HBM4 (sixth generation), and that the first quarter of 2026 was affected.

As HBM4 mass production at major customers picks up in the second half of 2026, TC bonder orders were expected to resume from the second quarter. CGSI Securities, a China-linked brokerage, said in a first-half 2026 report that the order cycle passed its trough in 2025.

SK hynix set up HBM4 mass production in September 2025, ordered TC bonders from HANMI and Hanwha Semitech in January 2026, and in June 2026 the company disclosed a KRW 44.2 billion TC Bonder 4.5 Griffin contract with SK hynix.

SK hynix has said it will run TC bonding alongside hybrid bonding through HBM4E, and reports suggest TC bonders are likely to remain in use up to 16-high stacks. At a 2025 briefing the company claimed no domestic peer could match it on TC bonder mass-production processes for HBM4.

On competition, SK hynix added a domestic rival as a supplier and placed HBM4 equipment orders with Singapore-based ASMPT in 2025, diversifying its sourcing.

The company expects shipments of TC bonders to North American customers to ramp up, and analysts are watching new equipment such as BOC COB bonders for 2.5D packaging and TC bonders for HBF as second-half revenue drivers.

06

Outlook

On October 30, 2026 the company said it expects record quarterly revenue in the fourth quarter. The explanation is that equipment revenue deferred from the third quarter will be recognized and supply to overseas customers will expand.

The company's outlook is that 2026 annual revenue will set another record, extending its run of record annual sales to a third consecutive year after 2024 and 2025.

Press reports say the company also expects record results to continue into 2027, supported by expanded HBM and advanced AI packaging investment at global chipmakers.

In a June 2026 report, Sangsangin Securities projected 2026 revenue of KRW 785 billion and operating profit of KRW 369.4 billion and raised the possibility of record results.

Analysts see TC bonder shipments to North American customers, along with new equipment such as BOC COB bonders for 2.5D packaging and TC bonders for HBF, as swing factors for second-half revenue. At a 2025 briefing the company said it planned to raise monthly TC bonder capacity from 35 units to 45 units in 2026.

The company paid a year-end dividend for 2025 and said it expects a dividend at or near record levels for 2026. Chairman Kwak Dong-shin said the U.S. subsidiary will support customers closely and that he expects full-scale equipment orders alongside the start of new U.S. plants, as stated in the first half of 2026.

The next checkpoints are whether deferred equipment revenue is recognized in the fourth quarter and whether overseas revenue stays near 72 percent of the total.

Bull factors

TC bonder share and the HBM4 transition

Reports say the company holds the world's No. 1 share in TC bonders. A KRW 44.2 billion TC bonder contract with SK hynix was disclosed in June 2026, in step with the ramp of its HBM4 production. The third-quarter 2026 operating margin of 50.2 percent was 9.4 percentage points above a year earlier, per the company.

Overseas mix and the U.S. footprint

Overseas customers make up about 72 percent of total revenue, according to the company's October 30, 2026 announcement. HANMI USA in San Jose, California is intended to widen equipment supply alongside U.S. chip investment. At a 2025 briefing the company forecast overseas customer revenue above twice that of one domestic customer.

New equipment lineup and dividend expectations

New TC bonders for large-area AI packaging and flip-chip bonders are cited as new growth drivers. After the 2025 year-end dividend payment, the size of the 2026 dividend is the next point to check.

Sangsangin Securities, in a June 2026 report, projected operating profit of KRW 369.4 billion and raised the possibility of record results.

Bear factors

Large swings in quarterly results

Quarterly revenue was KRW 83 billion in the fourth quarter of 2025 and KRW 50.9 billion in the first quarter of 2026, a wide spread. Operating profit in the first quarter of 2026 was KRW 8.46 billion, down 69.4 percent from the prior quarter.

Third-quarter 2026 net income attributable to controlling shareholders fell 46.8 percent year on year, even as operating profit rose.

Customer concentration and the HBM technology shift

Major customers are SK hynix and Micron, and results depend heavily on the timing of orders from the largest of them, SK hynix. SK hynix has said it will pursue TC bonding and hybrid bonding in parallel, which could change the mix of equipment demand over time. During the first-quarter 2026 HBM generation shift, orders temporarily declined.

Intensifying competition and supplier diversification

In 2025 SK hynix added a domestic rival as a TC bonder supplier, and reports said it ordered seven sets of HBM4 TC bonding equipment from Singapore-based ASMPT in October 2025. Industry commentary read this as volume moving to a third equipment source. More competitors could weaken the company's share and pricing leverage with customers.

Risk factors

FX and revenue recognition timing

The company cited the won-translated impact of a weaker dollar and the shift of some equipment revenue into the fourth quarter as drags on third-quarter results. If deferred equipment revenue is not recognized in the fourth quarter, quarterly swings could widen. With about 72 percent of revenue from overseas, currency moves feed directly into won-denominated sales.

Patent litigation

The company filed a patent infringement suit over TC bonders against a domestic rival at the end of 2024. In October 2025 that rival filed a suit against the company over core TC bonder technology. A prolonged dispute could push customers to split their equipment sourcing further.

Customer capex timing

Orders are closely tied to when major customers bring equipment into new fabs and packaging lines, such as SK hynix facilities in Cheongju. Contracts such as the KRW 44.2 billion award in June 2026 arrive in irregular batches, so disclosures are uneven. If customer investment plans shift, revenue recognition timing can slip as well.

Overall view

HANMI Semiconductor's earnings depend heavily on demand for TC bonders used in HBM stacking, and disclosed filings show revenue and profit recovering from the 2023 low through 2025.

Revenue of KRW 576.7 billion and operating profit of KRW 251.4 billion in 2025 were slightly below 2024, but the operating margin of 43.6 percent stayed at a high level.

Quarterly results in 2026 moved from a first-quarter low of KRW 50.9 billion in revenue to a second-quarter rebound and a third-quarter operating margin of 50.2 percent per the company, although third-quarter net income attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 34.9 billion fell year on year.

The bullish case rests on TC bonder share, overseas revenue of about 72 percent and the outlook for a third straight record revenue year. The bearish case rests on quarterly volatility, customer concentration, patent litigation and technology shifts such as hybrid bonding.

The current price-to-earnings multiple is high against the KRW 191.3 billion of controlling-shareholder net income over the last four quarters, so how much future growth the share price already reflects is the central question.

Company outlooks and brokerage estimates point in the same direction but have not yet been confirmed by filings. The next checkpoints are the fourth-quarter 2026 preliminary results and the 2026 annual report, whose operating margin and order flow will sharpen the balance between the bull and bear cases.
