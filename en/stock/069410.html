02

Business structure

nTelsCo, founded in 2000, is a software company whose main business is developing and supplying operational support systems for wired and wireless telecom operators.

It offers integrated solutions and platforms that manage service sign-up, activation, customer management, and product billing and settlement for telecom, broadcasting, internet, and IoT operators.

Its products are supplied within the integrated operational support solution segment of the three major Korean mobile carriers, and it supplies and operates solutions for the largest domestic mobile carrier.

As of end-December 2025, product revenue mix was KRW 66.8 billion in total with operational support systems at 42.87%, business support systems at 23.04%, service delivery platforms at 21.78%, and system integration at 12.31% (FnGuide basis).

The operational support system share rose from 32.95% in 2022 to 42.87% in 2025, while the system integration share fell from 18.56% to 12.31%.

Export shares for the same period were highest for business support systems at 34.16%, followed by service platforms at 5.19% and system integration at 3.54% (wisereport basis).

In February 2026 the company said it is shifting from an IoT-centred model toward a manufacturing AX platform and cloud-native solutions, and that new business accounted for more than 30% of revenue.

Notable new-business wins include a manufacturing AX platform and AI infrastructure for a global battery maker, an overseas disaster network project, and a large cloud managed service operation. Key products include the AI data platform Tarzan DB and the new billing product FOSSA Billing. Its consolidated subsidiary nTelsCo Labs, established in February 2012, develops and supplies software.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual revenue moved from KRW 53.6 billion in 2022 to KRW 44.9 billion in 2023, a 16.2% decline, then rose to KRW 47.9 billion in 2024, up 6.7%, and reached KRW 66.8 billion in 2025, up 39.4%. Revenue has grown for two consecutive years since the 2023 low, and 2025 was the largest year of 2022 to 2025.

Operating results went from an operating loss of KRW 395 million in 2022 to an operating loss of KRW 3.21 billion in 2023, with losses widening, then to an operating loss of KRW 243 million in 2024, with losses narrowing, before an operating profit of KRW 2.16 billion in 2025 marked a turnaround.

Operating margins were -0.7% in 2022, -7.2% in 2023, -0.5% in 2024, and 3.2% in 2025. Net income attributable to controlling shareholders went from KRW 115 million in 2022 to a net loss of KRW 1.33 billion in 2023, a turnaround to a profit of KRW 1.26 billion in 2024, and KRW 3.16 billion in 2025, up 150.0%.

Net income including non-controlling interests followed the same path, at KRW 3.16 billion in 2025. However, the cost of revenue ratio rose from 71.65% in 2022 to 75.52% in 2025 according to FnGuide, so margin improvement was limited even as revenue grew.

Operating cash flow was KRW 7.87 billion in 2025, up sharply from KRW 1.52 billion in 2024, and the reasons for the gap versus operating profit of KRW 2.16 billion are a point to check in the cash flow statement of the annual report.

On a quarterly basis, revenue was KRW 17.1 billion in the third quarter of 2025 and KRW 20.1 billion in the fourth quarter, up 17.5% from the prior quarter, then fell to KRW 16 billion in the first quarter of 2026, down 20.5%, and recovered to KRW 18.3 billion in the second quarter, up 14.3%, and KRW 19.7 billion in the third quarter, up 8.0% from the prior quarter and up 15.3% year on year.

Operating profit went from KRW 721 million in the fourth quarter of 2025, up 40.7% from the prior quarter, to KRW 146 million in the first quarter of 2026, down 79.7%, KRW 21.01 million in the second quarter, down 85.7%, and KRW 298 million in the third quarter, up 1,318.7% from the prior quarter but down 41.9% year on year.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders fell from KRW 936 million in the third quarter of 2025 to KRW 891 million in the fourth quarter, KRW 626 million in the first quarter of 2026, and KRW 366 million in the second quarter, then turned into a net loss of KRW 628 million in the third quarter of 2026, and the sum over the latest four quarters is KRW 1.25 billion.

Cumulative revenue for the first three quarters of 2026 was KRW 54.0 billion, up 15.8%, while cumulative operating profit was KRW 470 million, down 67.7% (Digital Daily, 17 November 2026, based on cumulative figures in the quarterly report).

Whether one-off items moved quarterly profit should be checked against the itemized breakdown in the quarterly income statement.

Outlook

In February 2026 the company set a goal of becoming an AX full-stack company in 2026, citing enhanced competitiveness of its manufacturing AX platform and integration of infrastructure and services.

The same announcement said it would strengthen strategic M&A and technology investment to raise corporate value, and the rise in the debt ratio should be read alongside how that plan is financed.

On 16 November 2026 the board resolved to cancel 333,410 treasury shares, equal to 3.25% of total issued shares and worth about KRW 2.51 billion at book value. The scheduled cancellation date is 25 November 2026, after which shares outstanding would fall from 10,244,824 to 9,911,414.

The company cited shareholder value enhancement, and a report noted that a revised Commercial Act effective March 2026 also requires cancellation of previously acquired treasury shares within a set period.

The company had paid out 140,000 treasury shares as employee bonuses in November 2025, so how treasury shares are used between compensation and shareholder returns is also worth watching.

In October 2026, CEO Choi Il-gyu increased his holding by 5,000 shares through open-market purchases, and key employees also bought shares, reported at roughly 17,000 shares in total. At the March 2026 annual general meeting, articles on performance-linked stock compensation and independent directors were added.

Its new FOSSA Billing product and the AI data platform Tarzan DB were announced for AIoT Korea Exhibition 2026, held from 3 to 5 November 2026.

Bull factors

Shift toward manufacturing AX and cloud

The company said new business accounted for more than 30% of revenue. Its manufacturing AX platform build for a global battery maker and an overseas disaster network project are cited as key wins. The operating margin moved from -0.5% in 2024 to 3.2% in 2025, back into positive territory. The company attributes part of the margin improvement to higher-value contract wins.

Expanded shareholder returns

On 16 November 2026 the board resolved to cancel all 333,410 treasury shares held. The shares represent 3.25% of total issued shares, and the cancellation involves no change to share capital because the shares were acquired within distributable profits. The company cited shareholder value enhancement as the purpose. Once completed, shares outstanding would fall to 9,911,414.

Improving cash generation

Operating cash flow was KRW 7.87 billion in 2025, up from KRW 1.52 billion in 2024. Compared with the outflow of KRW 3.58 billion in 2023, the recovery is substantial. Revenue has grown for two consecutive years through 2025 and has also increased in the second and third quarters of 2026.

Bear factors

Quarterly earnings slowdown in 2026

Operating profit was KRW 146 million in the first quarter of 2026, KRW 21.01 million in the second, and KRW 298 million in the third, all at low levels. Third-quarter operating profit fell 41.9% year on year.

Net loss attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 628 million in the third quarter compares with a profit a year earlier. Cumulative nine-month operating profit fell 67.7%.

Rising cost ratio and debt ratio

The cost of revenue ratio rose from 71.65% in 2022 to 75.52% in 2025 according to FnGuide. The debt ratio increased from 24.8% at end-2025 to 36% at end-September 2026. Higher cost burdens limit margin gains even when revenue grows, which is a weakness.

Customer concentration and telecom capex cycle

Its main customers are mobile carriers, and it supplies and operates solutions for the largest domestic carrier. Revenue is exposed to carriers' equipment and system investment decisions.

In 2020, COVID-19 curbed downstream investment and revenue fell from KRW 60.2 billion to KRW 56.7 billion, per a November 2021 technical analysis report.

Risk factors

Customer concentration and investment cycle

Revenue is weighted toward mobile carriers, so results depend on their systems investment decisions. A 2020 downturn in downstream investment reduced revenue, so the possibility of a cyclical reversal remains open.

Although new business exceeds 30% of revenue, that area also faces competition from large IT services providers.

Profitability and cost

The cost of revenue ratio rose from 71.65% in 2022 to 75.52% in 2025, making operating margin gains difficult. Quarterly operating profit stayed low in 2026, and the third quarter turned into a net loss. When profits are small, one-off costs or project-level swings can move quarterly results sharply.

Financial and capital allocation

The debt ratio rose to 36% at end-September 2026, while the company is pursuing treasury share cancellation of about KRW 2.5 billion alongside planned strategic M&A and technology investment. A shift in funding priorities could bring changes to the capital structure. As a small-cap stock, share price volatility and trading liquidity changes also warrant monitoring.

Overall view

nTelsCo's 2025 full-year revenue of KRW 66.8 billion and operating profit of KRW 2.16 billion confirmed a turnaround, and net income attributable to controlling shareholders rose 150.0% to KRW 3.16 billion.

Revenue grew for two consecutive quarters in 2026, but operating profit fell to KRW 146 million in the first quarter, KRW 21.01 million in the second, and KRW 298 million in the third, so the margin recovery has stalled midway.

The third quarter turned into a net loss of KRW 628 million attributable to controlling shareholders, and the debt ratio rose to 36% at end-September 2026.

Positive factors include new business above 30% of revenue centred on the manufacturing AX platform and cloud, operating cash flow of KRW 7.87 billion in 2025, and the decision to cancel 333,410 treasury shares.

Negative factors include a higher cost of revenue ratio, reliance on the telecom investment cycle, and volatile quarterly earnings. The price-to-book ratio sits well below 1 times, but the multiple should be read with a wide range until earnings persistence is confirmed.

The next checkpoints are the completion of the cancellation on 25 November 2026, the full-year results disclosure expected in early February 2027, and the operating margin and debt ratio in the 2026 annual report.
