02

Business structure

Shinwon, founded in September 1973, runs two segments: an export business and a fashion business. The export segment works on an OEM and ODM basis, taking orders from overseas buyers for knitwear, sweaters and handbags and producing them at bases in Guatemala, Vietnam, Indonesia and Nicaragua.

Its major customers are US-centered global retailers such as Gap, Old Navy, Walmart and Target, and about ten fixed buyers are reported. Based on 2024 figures compiled by FnGuide, the export segment made up 80.83% of revenue and the fashion segment 19.17%.

The fashion segment is a domestic business selling women's and men's apparel brands, and its domestic sales were reported to have fallen 2.93% to KRW 185.5 billion in 2023 from KRW 191.1 billion in 2022.

Press reports on the Q3 2026 disclosure put export revenue at KRW 290.7 billion, up 18.4% year on year, and fashion revenue at KRW 36.2 billion, up 2.1%, with an operating loss of about KRW 1.6 billion.

In export OEM, Shinwon is compared with listed peers such as Youngone and Hansae, but it also runs a domestic fashion brand business. Widening its production and sales base in Central America in recent years is seen as a core element of its export competitiveness.

Export production efficiency and overseas plant operations therefore drive results, while improving fashion-segment profitability remains an open task.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual results moved in opposite directions, with revenue rising while profit fell.

Revenue went from KRW 995.4 billion in 2022 to KRW 834.5 billion in 2023 (down 16.2% year on year), then to KRW 939.5 billion in 2024 (up 12.6%) and KRW 1.09 trillion in 2025 (up 16.3%), growing for two consecutive years, with 2025 the largest year of 2022–2025.

Operating profit went from KRW 33.4 billion in 2022 to KRW 23.4 billion in 2023 (down 29.8%), KRW 25.3 billion in 2024 (up 8.1%) and KRW 18.8 billion in 2025 (down 25.8%), with 2022 the largest and 2025 the smallest year.

Operating margin fell from 3.4% to 2.8%, 2.7% and 1.7% over the same period, so higher sales did not translate into better margins.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders dropped from KRW 18.1 billion in 2022 to KRW 5.93 billion in 2023 (down 67.3%), rose to KRW 6.31 billion in 2024 (up 6.4%), and then turned into a net loss of KRW 2.61 billion in 2025.

Total net income, including non-controlling interests, also swung to a net loss of KRW 1.63 billion in 2025. The debt-to-equity ratio rose from 126.2% in 2022 to 181.8% in 2025, when total liabilities were KRW 414.5 billion against total equity of KRW 228.1 billion.

Operating cash flow was KRW 40.5 billion in 2022, KRW 27 billion in 2023 and KRW 34.3 billion in 2024, before turning to -KRW 18.6 billion in 2025.

Quarterly results show a sharp rebound from a low point: operating profit of KRW 10.1 billion in Q4 2025 was up 567.1% from the prior quarter, but it fell to KRW 6.28 billion in Q1 2026 and KRW 3.82 billion in Q3 2026, although Q3 2026 was up 153.6% year on year.

Revenue has grown for three straight quarters, from KRW 280.8 billion in Q1 2026 to KRW 327 billion in Q3 2026. Net income attributable to controlling shareholders swung between a loss of KRW 896 million in Q2 2026 and a profit of KRW 527 million in Q3 2026, and totaled KRW 5.79 billion over the last four quarters. The detailed causes of the 2025 swing to a loss should be checked in the annual report notes.

05

Industry analysis

Demand in the downstream market moves with orders from US-centered global apparel retailers. Shinwon has expanded production and sales bases in Central America, including Guatemala and Nicaragua, to meet global apparel demand, and the press has cited this as a reason for the improvement in export results.

Export revenue was reported to have expanded to KRW 712.5 billion in 2021 and KRW 804.3 billion in 2022 before falling to KRW 649 billion in 2023. The 18.4% rise in Q3 2026 export revenue is explained by a combination of recovering orders and higher production volumes.

Youngone and Hansae, the comparison peers, are listed companies centered on large-scale OEM, while Shinwon differs in adding a domestic fashion brand segment to its export OEM business. The company has described the domestic fashion business as operating in an intensely competitive brand market.

In a July 2022 report, the company said it expected its domestic business, then loss-making due to intensifying competition, to return to profit in the second half, but the fashion segment still posted an operating loss in Q3 2026.

On the industry side, the export segment's results track order recovery directly, while the fashion segment's pace of profit improvement is likely to stay limited unless the domestic competitive landscape changes.

Bull factors

Export-led growth continues

Press reports put Q3 2026 export revenue at KRW 290.7 billion, up 18.4% year on year, with operating profit of about KRW 5.5 billion, up 76.3%. Company-wide revenue has grown for three consecutive quarters, from KRW 280.8 billion in Q1 2026 to KRW 327 billion in Q3 2026.

Cumulative operating profit for January to September 2026 rose 102.4%, a trend that can be cited as evidence of earnings recovery.

Quarterly profit rebound

Operating profit of KRW 10.1 billion in Q4 2025 rose 567.1% from the prior quarter, showing a quick recovery from a low point. Net income attributable to controlling shareholders turned to a profit of KRW 527 million in Q3 2026, compared with a loss of KRW 843 million in Q3 2025. Over the last four quarters, the total remains positive at KRW 5.79 billion.

Overseas production base and customer base

Production bases in Guatemala, Vietnam, Indonesia and Nicaragua allow the company to spread output across countries. Its customer base of global retailers such as Gap, Old Navy, Walmart and Target underpins export volumes.

Measured against total equity of KRW 228.1 billion at the end of 2025, market capitalization sits below book value, which can be read as a smaller price burden relative to assets.

Bear factors

Fashion-segment losses persist

The fashion segment's Q3 2026 revenue rose 2.1% to KRW 36.2 billion, but it posted an operating loss of about KRW 1.6 billion. In July 2022 the company expected its domestic business to return to profit in the second half, yet the fashion segment is still loss-making.

Unless the fashion segment returns to profit, export earnings may not fully translate into overall profit improvement.

Earnings volatility and the 2025 loss

Operating profit fell from KRW 33.4 billion in 2022 to KRW 18.8 billion in 2025, and net income attributable to controlling shareholders swung to a net loss of KRW 2.61 billion in 2025. A loss of KRW 896 million in Q2 2026 meant profits did not hold every quarter.

The KRW 5.79 billion total over the last four quarters is thin for a business with roughly KRW 1 trillion in annual revenue.

Financial burden and cash flow

The debt-to-equity ratio rose from 126.2% in 2022 to 181.8% in 2025, and total liabilities of KRW 414.5 billion at the end of 2025 exceed total equity of KRW 228.1 billion. Operating cash flow turned to -KRW 18.6 billion in 2025, a year in which operations consumed cash. If export volumes expand with less financial headroom, working-capital needs could also rise.

Risk factors

Currency and trade environment

Because the export segment accounts for more than 80% of revenue, currency moves and trade policy changes in major export markets such as the United States feed directly into results. A stronger won or changes in tariff conditions can affect export prices and margins at the same time. Hedging policy also shapes how much earnings fluctuate.

Customer and production concentration

With roughly ten fixed buyers, changes in orders from a handful of global retailers have a large effect on export volumes. Production is spread across Central America and Southeast Asia, but labor, political or logistics disruptions in a particular country could still halt output.

Fashion-segment channel restructuring

The fashion segment still posted an operating loss in Q3 2026, and whether its channel restructuring narrows that loss will shape the direction of profit. Press reports have noted a shift away from an in-house online mall toward major platforms, so further adjustments should be watched. Channel transition costs could also weigh on near-term profit.

Overall view

Shinwon's quarterly revenue has grown for three consecutive quarters through 2026, backed by a recovery in exports, and Q3 2026 operating profit rose 153.6% year on year.

However, annual operating profit fell to KRW 18.8 billion in 2025 and the company swung to a net loss attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 2.61 billion, so whether the quarterly recovery carries into full-year earnings has not yet been shown on an annual basis.

Bullish factors include recovering export volumes, expanding Central American production bases, and the quarterly profit rebound. Bearish factors include continuing fashion-segment losses, a lower operating margin, and a debt-to-equity ratio of 181.8% alongside negative operating cash flow in 2025.

On valuation, the share price sits below book value of total equity, but thin earnings limit how far earnings-based multiples can be interpreted. The next checkpoints are the operating margin in the 2026 annual business report, the trend of fashion-segment losses, and the direction of operating cash flow.
