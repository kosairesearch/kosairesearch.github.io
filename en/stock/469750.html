Report overview

Revenue Rebounds for Three Quarters, Profit Recovery Still Unproven

Ivisionworks is widening its secondary-battery inspection business into semiconductor inspection. Revenue has risen for three straight quarters through Q3 2026, but net profit attributable to controlling shareholders has yet to settle into a stable profit range.

  1. 1

    Annual revenue peaked at KRW 35.2 billion in 2024 before falling 34.6% to KRW 23 billion in 2025, when the company swung to an operating loss of KRW 391 million.

  2. 2

    Q3 2026 revenue reached KRW 6.48 billion, up 36.2% from the prior quarter but down 36.0% from a year earlier, while net profit attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 242 million fell 83.1% year on year.

  3. 3

    Operating profit turned positive at KRW 10.41 million in Q2 2026 and rose to KRW 744 million in Q3. The cumulative net loss attributable to controlling shareholders over the last four quarters is KRW 278 million.

  4. 4

    Beyond secondary-battery inspection, the company has filed a patent application for glass-substrate optical inspection, is developing wafer and HBM inspection equipment, and disclosed a KRW 3.8 billion supply contract in September 2026.

  5. 5

    The exchange designated the stock for investment caution in August and October 2026, and trading resumed on October 2 after the 5-to-1 share consolidation decided in July 2026.

02

Business structure

Ivisionworks was founded in 2015 and listed on KOSDAQ on September 3, 2024. It designs and supplies machine-vision inspection systems used in the electrode, assembly, packaging and module processes of secondary batteries.

In a breakdown compiled by JobKorea as of March 2026, installation, setup and CS accounted for about 80% of sales, inspection systems themselves for about 19%, and rental income for about 1%. The breakdown varies by reporting date and classification, but secondary-battery inspection makes up most of the business.

New system deliveries generate sales first, followed by setup and after-sales support, so results depend heavily on project-based orders and acceptance schedules. Customers are domestic battery makers.

The company said it supplied electrode-process equipment to SK On and notching-process equipment to Samsung SDI, and it claims to be the only domestic equipment maker to have delivered across the battery makers' whole process chain. Some overseas customers were also cited in a securities analyst's comment.

The business started with LCD and smartphone display appearance inspection, supplying units used on Samsung Electronics smartphones and Apple Watch displays according to press reports.

In semiconductors, it has filed a patent for optical inspection of micro-cracks in glass substrates and is developing inspection tools for wafer and HBM fine patterns.

On competition, the company says the machine-vision market is shifting toward price competition and aims to differentiate with proprietary hardware and software.

04

Earnings analysis

On a disclosed annual basis, revenue rose from KRW 23.2 billion in 2023 to KRW 35.2 billion in 2024, up 51.3% year on year, then fell to KRW 23 billion in 2025, down 34.6%. Among 2023 to 2025, 2024 was the largest year and 2025 the smallest.

The operating margin declined from 11.9% in 2023 to 6.8% in 2024 and -1.7% in 2025. Operating profit was KRW 2.77 billion in 2023 and KRW 2.39 billion in 2024, down 13.9%, before swinging to an operating loss of KRW 391 million in 2025.

Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 2.67 billion in 2023, turned into a loss of KRW 4.59 billion in 2024, and in 2025 remained a loss of KRW 392 million, smaller than the year before. The company has posted losses for two consecutive years, 2024 and 2025.

At the end of 2025, total equity was KRW 36.1 billion, all of it attributable to controlling shareholders, and total liabilities were KRW 10.4 billion. The debt-to-equity ratio fell from 93.2% in 2023 to 28.9% in 2025.

Operating cash flow moved from KRW 5.47 billion in 2023 to -KRW 455 million in 2024 and -KRW 155 million in 2025. Quarterly, Q3 2025 revenue of KRW 10.1 billion and operating profit of KRW 1.34 billion were the highest of the last five quarters.

Q4 2025 revenue fell 56.6% quarter on quarter to KRW 4.4 billion, with an operating loss of KRW 732 million and a net loss attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 551 million. Revenue then rose in each of Q1, Q2 and Q3 2026, to KRW 4.49 billion, KRW 4.76 billion and KRW 6.48 billion.

Q2 2026 operating profit of KRW 10.41 million was small but turned the quarter positive, and Q3 2026 operating profit of KRW 744 million rose 7,050.1% from that low base. Even so, Q3 2026 operating profit fell 44.4% year on year, and net profit attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 242 million fell 83.1%.

Press coverage linked the revenue decline and the profit deterioration to a slowdown in secondary-battery demand and price pressure from Chinese makers. Because quarterly revenue swings with the timing of large deliveries, earnings volatility is high.

05

Industry analysis

The secondary-battery equipment market has been weak since 2025. In its New Year message in January 2026, the company described 2025 as a difficult year for the domestic battery industry, hit by global supply-chain uncertainty and rapid catch-up by Chinese manufacturers.

CEO Gil Gi-jae said the battery market is slowing amid an EV demand lull and pressure from Chinese competitors. The company expects a gradual recovery as new battery form factors and wider application areas emerge, but this is its own view, and the timing of customer capex resumption is not clearly disclosed.

A Hana Securities analyst, in a report covering Q3 2025 results, said growth had slowed on a weaker secondary-battery market and delayed investment, and noted plans to broaden exposure to semiconductor, display, automotive and ESS equipment.

Press coverage describes semiconductor inspection as a high-barrier market where success can bring solid profitability, and the CEO said a qualified supplier relationship with Samsung Electronics could last five to six years.

The company also cites growth in AI semiconductors and physical AI as a background opportunity. On competition, it emphasizes its record supplying three domestic battery makers, and an analyst viewed its overseas customer exposure as helping keep losses narrower than peers.

06

Outlook

In a New Year message released on January 23, 2026, the company declared 2026 a year of renewed growth and set three priorities: strengthening AI inspection capability, securing proprietary solutions that combine hardware and software, and establishing a position in the semiconductor inspection market.

The plan includes mobilizing tangible and intangible assets and concentrated company-wide investment in semiconductor inspection.

On September 14, 2026, it disclosed a KRW 3.8 billion supply contract for secondary-battery inspection systems, running from September 14, 2026 to September 13, 2027, a supply period of 364 days. The counterparty was not disclosed.

Supply contracts disclosed from January 1 to September 14, 2026 totaled two deals and KRW 7.48 billion, up 4.6% from KRW 7.15 billion in the same period a year earlier. Revenue from disclosed contracts may be recognized over the contract period rather than at signing.

The company says it expects glass-substrate inspection equipment to be supplied to a major domestic semiconductor maker. In an August 12, 2026 disclosure, a display-substrate transfer equipment maker reported buying about 200,000 more shares of Ivisionworks, after holding 16.8%.

The two companies are studying cooperation on large-substrate transfer and precision inspection, and the shareholder said this is not tied to supply to any specific customer or project.

08

Bull factors

Three Straight Quarters of Revenue Recovery

Revenue fell to KRW 4.4 billion in Q4 2025 and then rose to KRW 4.49 billion in Q1 2026, KRW 4.76 billion in Q2 and KRW 6.48 billion in Q3. Operating profit turned positive at KRW 10.41 million in Q2 and reached KRW 744 million in Q3, a change in direction from losses.

Expansion into Semiconductor Inspection

The company has filed a patent application for optical detection of micro-cracks in glass substrates and is developing wafer and HBM fine-pattern inspection tools. Its 2026 New Year message named establishing semiconductor inspection systems as a core priority.

Press coverage notes that semiconductor equipment has high entry barriers, so once a relationship starts it can last for years.

Order Flow and Partnerships

Supply contracts disclosed from January 1 to September 14, 2026 totaled KRW 7.48 billion, up 4.6% from the same period in 2025. A KRW 3.8 billion secondary-battery inspection contract was disclosed on September 14, 2026.

A shareholder added shares in August 2026, and the two companies are studying cooperation in display-process transfer and inspection.

Bear factors

Battery Demand and Chinese Competition

Revenue fell 34.6% in 2025, and the company swung to an operating loss. The company itself says EV demand softness and price pressure from Chinese makers are slowing the battery market. Although management expects a recovery, a securities analyst pointed to delayed investment slowing growth.

Earnings Power Not Yet Restored

Q3 2026 revenue was down 36.0% year on year, and operating profit fell 44.4%. Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 242 million fell 83.1%.

Over the last four quarters the cumulative net loss attributable to controlling shareholders is KRW 278 million, so quarterly profit has not yet built into cumulative earnings.

Governance and Trading Volatility

At the March 30, 2026 annual general meeting, the proposal to elect an auditor failed for lack of a quorum, so the incumbent auditor continues until the next meeting.

The stock was designated for investment caution twice in 2026, in August for a sharp closing-price move and in October for concentrated trading in a few accounts. Following the share consolidation, shares outstanding stand at about 6.89 million.

10

Risk factors

Customer Concentration and Demand

Most sales depend on capital spending by a small set of domestic battery makers. If customer expansion slips, deliveries and acceptance timing move back and quarterly results swing sharply, as seen when Q4 2025 revenue fell 56.6% from the prior quarter.

Unproven Semiconductor Business

Glass-substrate, wafer and HBM inspection tools are described as being in development or at the patent-filing stage, and no disclosed sales contribution has been identified.

Because the company plans concentrated investment in this area, costs may rise before revenue, and customer qualification delays would push back payback.

Financial Buffer and Share Liquidity

Quarterly operating profit turned positive in 2026, but a cumulative net loss remains over the last four quarters. Operating cash flow was -KRW 155 million in 2025. After the 5-to-1 share consolidation and a trading halt of about three weeks, trading volume and price swings may be larger.

Overall view

Ivisionworks is a company whose secondary-battery inspection business led to a 34.6% revenue decline and an operating loss in 2025. In 2026, revenue has risen for three straight quarters through Q3, operating profit turned positive in Q2, and Q3 operating profit reached KRW 744 million.

However, Q3 2026 revenue and net profit attributable to controlling shareholders were still down year on year, and the cumulative net loss attributable to controlling shareholders over the last four quarters is KRW 278 million.

On the business side, development of glass-substrate, wafer and HBM inspection tools, a KRW 3.8 billion supply contract, and a display-substrate cooperation study are presented as new growth areas. Disclosed revenue contribution from these new areas has not yet been confirmed.

On battery demand, management is optimistic about a recovery while external research points to delayed investment, so the pace remains uncertain. On governance, the 2026 proposal to elect an auditor failed, and the stock was designated for investment caution twice in 2026.

Strengths and weaknesses coexist, and the 2026 full-year operating margin and the share of semiconductor inspection sales in the next annual report are the most important checkpoints.
