02

Business structure

DongshinEngineering&Construction is a general contractor founded in 1958, focused on civil engineering, building construction, housing and cultural-heritage works.

It adopted its current name in 1973 and runs building projects such as public facilities, commercial buildings, apartments and BTL projects alongside civil works including roads, bridges, subways and water and sewer systems.

Civil engineering is the core, accounting for 64% of domestic revenue in the second quarter of 2026, with domestic building at 29% and other businesses at 7%, according to Butler's data.

Public-sector orders from central and local governments make up a large share of its work, so public investment budgets and tender schedules directly affect order intake. Its home base is Daegu and North Gyeongsang Province, where it reports a solid market position built on decades of experience.

Recent awards include a Hanjae(Yuho) district sewage improvement project in Cheongdo County worth KRW 4.57 billion excluding VAT, equal to 13.70% of recent sales, and a school construction contract for Haemaru High School worth KRW 12.9 billion disclosed in October 2026.

Competition splits between large listed builders such as Hyundai Engineering & Construction and DL E&C and regional mid-sized general contractors, and the company belongs to the latter group, competing mainly in regional public and private projects.

The expansion of lowest-price tender systems has been cited as a constraint on profitability improvement. Its free float ratio is about 32.65%.

Earnings analysis

Annual results turned after peaking in 2023. Sales rose from KRW 50.3 billion in 2022 to KRW 82.7 billion in 2023, a 64.5% increase, then fell 16.6% to KRW 69 billion in 2024 and 51.6% to KRW 33.4 billion in 2025, two consecutive years of decline. 2023 was the largest year and 2025 the smallest within 2022 to 2025.

Operating profit grew from KRW 1.37 billion in 2022 to KRW 6.34 billion in 2023, up 364.1%, then fell 90.8% to KRW 586 million in 2024 before the company recorded an operating loss of KRW 2.21 billion in 2025, a swing into losses. Operating margin moved from 2.7% in 2022 to 7.7% in 2023, 0.8% in 2024 and -6.6% in 2025.

The company attributed the 2025 declines in sales and net income to lower order volume. Profit attributable to controlling shareholders peaked at KRW 7.83 billion in 2023 and then fell to KRW 3 billion in 2024 and KRW 147 million in 2025.

Net income stayed positive in 2025 despite the operating loss because non-operating items offset much of the operating shortfall, so the quality of earnings depends on whether core margins recover. Operating cash flow fell from KRW 6.65 billion in 2022 to KRW 3.57 billion in 2024 and then to -KRW 4.01 billion in 2025.

Quarterly results show an operating loss of KRW 2.05 billion in the third quarter of 2025, an operating profit of KRW 513 million in the fourth quarter, then operating losses of KRW 250 million in the first quarter of 2026 and KRW 573 million in the second quarter.

Third-quarter 2026 sales reached KRW 11.4 billion, up 10.9% year on year, and operating profit of KRW 546 million replaced a loss a year earlier. Profit attributable to controlling shareholders over the last four quarters totals KRW 3.23 billion, a short period for judging whether annual profitability has stabilized.

Industry analysis

Construction revenue is recognized as projects progress, so changes in order backlog show up in sales with a lag. The 2025 sales decline was attributed by the company to lower order volume, and first-quarter 2026 new orders rose year on year, according to WISE Report's data.

Industry constraints cited include a construction slowdown, expansion of lowest-price tender systems, raw material cost pressure and rising wages. Rising materials and labor costs and construction delays can affect cost ratios and unbilled contract balances, so cost control matters alongside sales volume.

Because public-sector orders make up a large share of its work, government and local budgets and tender schedules are the key cycle variables. Housing supply policy affects housing construction, but since civil engineering is its core, direct exposure to the housing cycle is comparatively limited.

Large listed builders such as Hyundai Engineering & Construction, Samsung C&T and DL E&C operate broader project portfolios, so direct comparison with DongshinEngineering&Construction is limited mainly to regional public and private civil and building work.

Its position is that of a mid-sized general contractor rooted in Daegu and North Gyeongsang Province, where regional order wins and cost ratio management matter more than scale.

Risk factors

Because public-sector orders make up a large share of its work, results depend on public investment budgets and tender timing. The 51.6% sales decline in 2025 was attributed to lower order volume, so a gap in awards can reduce sales quickly.

Cost and schedule risk
