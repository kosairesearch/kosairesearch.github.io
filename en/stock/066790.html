CCS: Quarterly Losses Narrow as Sales Shrink

02

Business structure

CCS (Korea Cable TV Chung-Buk System) was established in 1997 as a cable broadcasting operator in Chungju and other areas and is a comprehensive cable TV operator based in North Chungcheong Province. It received a government license as a second-tier comprehensive cable operator in 1998 and launched operations in 1999.

Its core businesses are comprehensive cable broadcasting and basic telecom services, covering cable TV, high-speed internet, internet telephony, and broadcast advertising.

By revenue mix in the second quarter of 2026, broadcasting accounted for 52%, advertising for 25%, internet for 12%, and other revenue for 10%, according to the data aggregator Butler.

High-speed internet began in 1999 in partnership with SK Broadband, and the company currently offers internet access of up to 100Mbps over its own network. Cable services run on the company's own network, and a related operator is used for installation and outsourced after-sales service.

Part of revenue consists of subscription fees and home shopping sales, and the company cited declines in both as drivers of the 2025 deterioration. Its main competitors are IPTV, satellite broadcasting, OTT services, and mobile video platforms, and competition with them is cited as a background to the weaker results.

The company is developing digital value-added services on its HFC network to raise ARPU and is preparing QPS bundled products that combine cable with MVNO-based telecom services.

The largest shareholder and one related party hold 14.01% of common shares, and treasury shares account for 7.16%, according to the WiseReport company monitor.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual revenue went from KRW 19.4 billion in 2022 to KRW 19.2 billion in 2023 (down 0.7% year on year), KRW 18.3 billion in 2024 (down 4.6%), and KRW 18.3 billion in 2025 (down 0.4%). 2022 was the largest year and 2025 the smallest within 2022 to 2025.

Operating profit fell from KRW 649 million in 2022 to KRW 454 million in 2023 (down 30.1%), and the company swung to an operating loss of KRW 850 million in 2024. The 2025 operating loss of KRW 5.02 billion continued the loss pattern and widened it.

The operating margin declined from 3.4% in 2022 and 2.4% in 2023 to -4.6% in 2024 and -27.5% in 2025. Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders peaked at KRW 2.53 billion in 2023 (up 209.2% year on year), then became a net loss of KRW 1.07 billion in 2024 and KRW 9.07 billion in 2025.

Net income including non-controlling interests followed the same direction, at a net loss of KRW 1.07 billion in 2024 and KRW 9.07 billion in 2025.

The company attributed the main drivers of the 2025 change to recognized impairment losses on property, plant and equipment and other expenses, according to a news report on its disclosure dated February 11, 2026.

It also cited lower subscription fee revenue from declining broadcasting and internet subscribers, lower home shopping sales, and higher commission expenses. In the first half of 2025, consolidated revenue fell 4.3% year on year while the operating loss widened by 183.9%, according to WiseReport.

Operating cash flow declined from KRW 4.57 billion in 2022 to KRW 4.23 billion in 2023 and KRW 2.52 billion in 2024, then turned negative at KRW -2.25 billion in 2025.

On a quarterly basis, the operating loss widened from KRW 926 million in the third quarter of 2025 to KRW 2.54 billion in the fourth quarter, then narrowed to KRW 2.13 billion, KRW 738 million, and KRW 486 million in the first through third quarters of 2026.

The net loss attributable to controlling shareholders was largest at KRW 6.99 billion in the fourth quarter of 2025 among the disclosed quarters and narrowed to KRW 291 million in the third quarter of 2026, with a four-quarter total of KRW 9.82 billion.

Quarterly revenue went from KRW 5.22 billion in the third quarter of 2025 to KRW 4.35 billion in the fourth quarter, rose to KRW 4.56 billion in the second quarter of 2026, and fell to KRW 4.42 billion in the third quarter. Losses narrowed in 2026, but revenue remains on a downward path.

05

Industry analysis

The domestic comprehensive cable TV market faces intensifying competition from IPTV, satellite broadcasting, OTT services, and mobile video platforms.

CCS's 2025 deterioration occurred within this competitive environment, and the company cited subscriber declines and lower subscription and home shopping revenue as the main factors. The company's stated direction is to add digital value-added services on its HFC network to raise average revenue per user (ARPU).

QPS bundles that combine cable with MVNO-based telecom services are under development. CCS is classified under KOSDAQ's entertainment and culture sector, and under WI26 as media and education according to WiseReport.

Owning its own network as a second-tier comprehensive cable operator is an infrastructure strength, but retaining subscribers in a regional market is the key issue given the challenge from large telecom operators and OTT platforms. The direction of the sector can be judged from subscriber trends and ARPU changes.

Outlook

The company has said it is expanding customer services, including smart IoT products, preparation for digital broadcasting, VOD bundled products, broadband network upgrades, and home network-based Internet of Things services.

Among these, the QPS bundled telecom product, aimed at raising ARPU, is seen as the key task that could change the revenue trend. Third-quarter 2026 revenue of KRW 4.42 billion fell 15.4% year on year, so whether quarterly revenue recovers is the first checkpoint.

The operating loss narrowed to KRW 2.13 billion in the first quarter of 2026, KRW 738 million in the second, and KRW 486 million in the third, and whether this narrowing carries through on an annual basis is the second checkpoint.

The preliminary 2025 annual results were disclosed on February 11, 2026, so the preliminary 2026 fourth-quarter and annual results are expected around mid-February 2027. The 2026 annual business report is due by March 31, 2027, and it will confirm full-year 2026 revenue and operating margin.

Whether the fourth-quarter 2026 operating loss returns to the KRW 2.54 billion level of the fourth quarter of 2025 is another item to monitor.

The net loss attributable to controlling shareholders narrowed to KRW 291 million in the third quarter of 2026, but a return to annual profit has not yet appeared in reported results.

Subscriber response to the bundled telecom products and whether subscription fee revenue recovers are the main variables for the outlook.

08

Bull factors

Narrowing Losses

The operating loss narrowed from KRW 2.54 billion in the fourth quarter of 2025 to KRW 486 million in the third quarter of 2026, and the net loss attributable to controlling shareholders shrank from KRW 6.99 billion to KRW 291 million over the same period.

The operating margin, which fell to -4.6% in 2024 and -27.5% in 2025, has room to improve as quarterly losses narrow. Whether this trend carries through on an annual basis is the key question.

Bundled Telecom Products

The company is developing QPS products that bundle MVNO-based telecom services to raise ARPU. Digital value-added services, VOD bundles, and broadband network upgrades are also cited as customer service expansion tasks. If these products lift revenue per subscriber, they could partly offset the current decline in sales.

Low Financial Leverage

Total equity at the end of 2025 was KRW 25.4 billion, all of which is attributable to controlling shareholders. Total liabilities were KRW 3.54 billion, and the debt-to-equity ratio was a low 13.9%.

The company's own network supports its internet service of up to 100Mbps, so its infrastructure assets underpin the business.

Bear factors

Persistent Revenue Decline

Revenue declined from KRW 19.4 billion in 2022 to KRW 18.3 billion in 2025, and third-quarter 2026 revenue of KRW 4.42 billion fell 15.4% year on year. Revenue rose quarter on quarter in the second quarter of 2026 but fell again in the third quarter.

If the subscriber and subscription fee base keeps shrinking, the narrowing of losses could occur without a sales recovery.

Competitive Pressure

Competition from IPTV, satellite broadcasting, OTT services, and mobile video platforms is cited as a background to the weaker results. The company is preparing bundled telecom products in response, but their contribution to earnings has not yet appeared in reported figures. If competing platforms expand faster than the company's response, subscriber losses could continue.

Weaker Cash Generation

The operating margin fell to -27.5% in 2025, and operating cash flow turned negative at KRW -2.25 billion. The net loss attributable to controlling shareholders totals KRW 9.82 billion over the last four quarters. Total equity at the end of 2025 was KRW 25.4 billion, so continued losses would reduce the capital cushion.

Risk factors

Structural Subscriber Decline

The company attributes lower subscription fee revenue to a decline in broadcasting and internet subscribers. Cable operators face rising pressure to retain subscribers amid competition from IPTV and OTT services. If the bundled telecom products fail to gain traction, the revenue decline could continue.

Financial Capacity and Cumulative Losses

The 2025 net loss of KRW 9.07 billion widened from KRW 1.07 billion in 2024, and the four-quarter total is KRW 9.82 billion. With operating cash flow also negative, continued losses could reduce investment capacity. How long total equity of KRW 25.4 billion can absorb losses at this scale is an item to watch.

Shareholding Structure and Float

The largest shareholder and one related party hold 14.01%, and treasury shares account for 7.16%, leaving a free float ratio of 78.83%. With a meaningful share held by insiders and treasury stock, a float concentrated among smaller shareholders can add to price volatility. Changes in holdings are disclosed separately on DART.
