02

Business structure

Wiworld was founded in July 2000 and listed on the Konex market on December 26, 2013. It specializes in satellite communication antennas, and its headquarters sit in the Daedeok Techno Valley in Yuseong-gu, Daejeon.

Its core products are tracking antennas that follow satellites automatically from moving platforms such as vehicles and vessels, together with satellite broadcast reception systems.

For overseas markets the company offers antenna lines including the AP, DP, VM, PT and SM series, alongside maritime communication solutions and broadcast advertising solutions.

On a standalone basis in 2025, product revenue was KRW 5,659 million and service revenue was KRW 738 million, so product sales make up most of the top line. The company has also developed K-band and X-band communication antennas for defense use.

Wiworld supplied vehicle satellite antennas to KT Skylife in 2002 and exported broadcast antennas to Thailand and New Zealand in 2007. It later widened its export reach through maritime VSAT antennas and bids for military vehicle antennas abroad.

According to its 2022 IR materials, the largest shareholder and related parties held 87.7% of the 2,487,252 shares outstanding, which leaves a small tradable float.

In the satellite antenna market, domestic players such as Intellian Technologies compete alongside global names including Honeywell International, Viasat, L3Harris Technologies and Cobham SATCOM. Wiworld is a niche manufacturer far smaller than these firms, and its revenue split by product line could not be confirmed.

03

Recent trends

On a standalone basis, Wiworld's 2025 revenue was KRW 6,554 million, up 56.4% from KRW 4,197 million in 2024. Product revenue rose from KRW 3,734 million to KRW 5,659 million, and service revenue rose from KRW 293 million to KRW 738 million. Gross profit increased 48.3%, from KRW 1,749 million to KRW 2,594 million.

Operating profit climbed from KRW 137 million in 2024 to KRW 881 million in 2025, and the operating margin improved from 3.3% to 13.4%. The annual general meeting on March 25 approved the 26th-term financial statements, and the filing shows net income of KRW 841 million, with earnings per share also positive. Total assets were KRW 11,288 million, total liabilities KRW 1,732 million and total equity KRW 9,556 million.

The longer record shows wide swings. According to the company's 2022 IR materials, revenue was KRW 6,387 million in 2019, KRW 5,121 million in 2020 and KRW 8,376 million in 2021. It reported operating losses of KRW 516 million in 2019 and KRW 684 million in 2020, then operating profit of KRW 1,587 million in 2021.

The 2025 operating margin remains below the 18.9% of 2021. PreStock News also noted that operating cash flow did not rise much despite higher net income.

The share price has been volatile as well. At 4:04 p.m. on March 25 it stood at KRW 1,400, down 12.50% from the previous day. Geumgang Ilbo reported that on April 3 it closed at KRW 1,450, up 14.17%. PreStock News said the stock returned 71.95% over about two weeks in the first half of April.

Morningstar lists a 52-week range of KRW 578 to KRW 1,411. The October 27, 2026 close was KRW 897, up 15.00% on the day, with a market capitalization of KRW 2.2 billion and turnover of KRW 23,322. That market value is below the KRW 9,556 million of total equity at the end of 2025. First-half or quarterly 2026 results could not be confirmed in our research.

Outlook

The satellite communication sector is driven by low-earth-orbit constellation build-outs and defense demand. Mordor Intelligence projects the satellite antenna market will grow from USD 7.14 billion in 2026 to USD 12.62 billion in 2031, a 12.07% compound annual rate.

IndexBox estimates the satellite phased array antenna market will expand at a 9.2% compound annual rate over 2026-2035.

The same reports, however, point to demand concentrated in mass-producible phased array terminals for low-orbit broadband. Where Wiworld's mechanically tracking antennas fit within this trend could not be confirmed in our research.

Hanwha Investment & Securities said in an April 2 report that mass-production orders for low-orbit satellites are likely to begin from 2026-2027, though that analysis centers on satellites and ground systems.

To hold the 13.4% operating margin of 2025, Wiworld needs product revenue to keep growing. Its revenue swung widely in 2019-2021 and again in 2024-2025. Because the company is small, a single order can shape annual results, which makes the quarterly order flow a key item to monitor.

Whether operating cash flow improves, as PreStock News flagged, also bears watching. The balance sheet is stable, so the company has capacity to keep funding research and development internally, but the scale of new product investment could not be confirmed.

The first-half 2026 report and subsequent quarterly filings are the most direct data for judging the current-year trajectory.

As a Konex stock it trades thinly, and absent disclosures such as a transfer listing or ownership changes, price volatility depends heavily on supply and demand. Whether liquidity improves is a separate matter from corporate earnings.

05

Bull factors

Revenue and profit growing together

Revenue in 2025 rose 56.4% and operating profit grew from KRW 137 million to KRW 881 million. Both product and service revenue increased, so growth did not rely on a single line. The operating margin widened from 3.3% to 13.4%, a sizable improvement in business metrics.

Stable balance sheet

At the end of 2025, total liabilities were KRW 1,732 million against total equity of KRW 9,556 million. PreStock News observed that the debt burden fell and retained earnings rose. With a light borrowing load, the company is less exposed to interest-rate swings.

Expanding satellite communication demand

Low-orbit broadband constellations and defense spending are lifting demand for satellite antennas. Mordor Intelligence expects the market to grow at a 12.07% compound annual rate through 2031. Wiworld has more than two decades of experience developing mobile tracking antennas and defense communication antennas.

Bear factors

Volatile earnings on a small revenue base

According to the 2022 IR materials, the company recorded operating losses in 2019 and 2020 before returning to operating profit in 2021. Operating profit in 2024 was only KRW 137 million. Whether the 2025 improvement continues will need to be confirmed by further results.

Weak cash conversion

PreStock News pointed out that operating cash flow did not rise much compared with the increase in net income. Higher receivables or inventory could be a cause, but the details could not be confirmed. Weak cash generation would limit capacity for research and capital spending.

Disadvantage against larger rivals

The satellite antenna market includes larger players such as Intellian Technologies, Honeywell International, Viasat and L3Harris Technologies. Low-orbit demand centers on mass-produced flat terminals, which makes economies of scale hard to match for a small manufacturer. How competitive Wiworld's products are in this segment could not be confirmed.

It is hard to rule out weaker demand for conventional tracking antennas used with geostationary satellites, or tougher price competition. Overall view

Wiworld's 2025 revenue of KRW 6,554 million and operating profit of KRW 881 million show improvement in both scale and profitability, and its balance sheet is stable. At the same time, earnings are volatile, as the operating losses of 2019-2020 and the low profit of 2024 illustrate.

The weak growth in operating cash flow relative to net income also remains unresolved. The satellite communication sector is projected to grow on low-orbit constellations and defense demand, but how far that growth reaches Wiworld's product lines could not be confirmed.
