02

Business structure

Wiworld is a satellite communication antenna manufacturer located in the Daedeok Techno Valley in Daejeon.

Its core products are mobile satellite tracking antennas mounted on vehicles, ships and camping vehicles, and the line has expanded into satellite TV reception antennas and two-way satellite communication antennas.

The vehicle lineup includes the V700 model for high-speed moving platforms, while the marine lineup is divided by size from 32 cm to 60 cm. For defense use, the company offers K-band and X-band communication antennas and 3-axis SOT antennas for military satellite communications.

According to the company's IR material, its export-oriented markets include Europe, Australia, the Middle East, Japan, South America and the United States.

Of 2025 revenue, product sales were KRW 5,658.75 million and service sales were KRW 738.02 million, so product sales made up about 86% of the total (calculated from disclosed revenue).

Competition comes from domestic and overseas satellite antenna makers and is shaped by how satellite broadcasting and telecom operators procure equipment.

In Korea, the company has a record of developing a BS antenna linked to KT Skylife's LTE TV reception equipment, and it previously supplied KT relay vehicle antennas. A corporate information aggregator lists 44 patents (16 registered) and INNO-BIZ and Main-Biz certifications.

03

Recent trends

On a disclosed basis, 2025 revenue rose to KRW 6.55 billion from KRW 4.19 billion in the prior year, an increase of KRW 2.37 billion or 56.4%. Product sales grew from KRW 3.73 billion to KRW 5.66 billion, while service sales increased from KRW 293 million to KRW 738 million.

Gross profit rose 48.3% to KRW 2.59 billion, implying a gross margin of about 39.6% (calculated from disclosed revenue). Operating profit increased from KRW 140 million to KRW 880 million, raising the operating margin to 13.4%.

The 26th-term financial statements were approved at the annual general meeting on March 25, 2026, and standalone net income was disclosed at KRW 841 million.

Operating cash flow did not expand in step with net income, while the net inflow from investing activities mainly reflected financial product management and redemptions rather than capital expenditure.

The share price closed at KRW 1,400 on March 25, 2026, down 12.5% from the previous day, and the all-time low of KRW 578 was recorded on March 20, 2025. As of 02:10 KST on November 11, 2026, the stock traded at KRW 701 with a market capitalization of about KRW 1.7 billion and zero trading value. Market capitalization was about KRW 4.1 billion as of March 12, 2026.

04

Outlook

The key question for future results is whether product sales growth continues through 2026. It is difficult to tell from current disclosures alone whether the 2025 revenue increase came from one-off orders or from a recurring export structure.

Sustaining a 13.4% operating margin requires managing gross margin and selling and administrative expenses together. Whether operating cash flow moves in the same direction as net income is the main gauge of earnings quality.

On the positive side, the export-heavy structure and accumulated defense communication antenna business are often cited. On the negative side, the small revenue base means that changes in a single customer or project can weigh heavily on overall results.

With zero trading value, the channel through which results could be reflected in the share price is itself limited. The next checkpoints are revenue and operating cash flow in the 2026 third-quarter report, followed by year-end settlement disclosures for fiscal 2026.

05

Bull factors

Revenue and profit growing together

Disclosed 2025 figures show revenue growing 56.4% and operating profit expanding to about KRW 880 million. Both product and service sales increased, so growth did not rely on a single revenue stream. Gross profit rising 48.3% can be read as a sign of improvement in cost structure and pricing. For a small manufacturer, this marks a fast step-up in earnings capacity.

Low debt and a solid equity base

As of year-end 2025, total liabilities were KRW 1.73 billion and total equity was KRW 9.56 billion. The debt-to-equity ratio works out to roughly 18% (calculated from disclosed balance sheet figures). Growth in retained earnings indicates that internal reserves are building up.

Because borrowing is light, the company retains relatively more financial room during periods of earnings volatility.

Export track record and defense products

It completed first-batch exports of defense communication antennas to Canada in 2015. Its K-band and X-band vehicle antennas are introduced as being used in military field operations. A cumulative record of overseas orders gives defense and military communications demand a potential role as one pillar of results.

06

Bear factors

Delayed cash conversion

Operating cash flow in 2025 did not expand in step with the rise in net income. The net inflow from investing activities reflected financial product management and redemptions more than capital spending. How much of the reported profit converts into cash is still unproven.

If the gap between book earnings and cash generation persists, the financial signals become harder to interpret.

Extremely thin trading liquidity

Trading value is recorded at zero, and market capitalization is about KRW 1.7 billion. Daily volume in March 2026 remained in the range of a few shares to a few hundred shares. In such thin trading, a handful of executions can move the price sharply.

A market where price formation is constrained also limits how earnings improvements are reflected in the share price.

Share price volatility and product dependence

The share price fell 12.5% on March 25, 2026, and market capitalization stood at about KRW 4.1 billion as of March 12, 2026. The current price of KRW 701 sits close to the KRW 578 low recorded on March 20, 2025.

With product sales making up about 86% of revenue, demand shifts in a single product category pass directly into reported results.

07

Risk factors

Macro

A high export share exposes the company to currency swings and economic conditions in its overseas markets. Shifts in defense budgets or foreign government procurement schedules can delay order timing. Changes in raw material and component prices can also feed into gross margin.

Equipment demand moves with capital spending cycles of satellite broadcasting and telecom operators. If satellite communication technology evolves quickly, replacement cycles for existing antenna products could shorten.

Changes in defense or public procurement standards could alter certification requirements and delivery structures.

As a KONEX-listed small cap, the stock has limited participation from institutions and foreign investors. Infrequent trading can slow how quickly disclosed information is reflected in the price. Financial figures are often disclosed on a standalone basis, which makes comparisons on a consolidated basis difficult.

08

Overall view

Disclosed 2025 results show clear strengths in simultaneous growth of revenue and operating profit alongside lower debt. Operating cash flow lagged the increase in earnings, and because most revenue comes from product sales, the company's results may be volatile.

The share price recorded an all-time low of KRW 578 on March 20, 2025, fell 12.5% to KRW 1,400 on March 25, 2026, and stood at KRW 701 as of November 11, 2026. Zero trading value and a market capitalization of about KRW 1.7 billion are constraints on trading participation and price formation.

Positive factors center on financial stability and the export base, while negative factors concentrate on cash conversion and liquidity. The central question at this stage is whether operating improvement and cash conversion of earnings are confirmed together.

Trends in operating cash flow and gross margin in future periodic disclosures will serve as the main reference indicators.
