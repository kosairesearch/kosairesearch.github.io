02

Business structure

Hyundai Engineering & Construction runs housing and urban redevelopment, plant, civil and infrastructure, and nuclear and energy businesses as a diversified builder. At the regular shareholders meeting on March 26, 2026, the company set 2026 targets of KRW 33.4 trillion in new orders and KRW 27.4 trillion in revenue.

Consolidated new orders in 2025 were KRW 33.4394 trillion and revenue was KRW 31.1 trillion. The company said that growth in energy orders, including nuclear, solar and offshore wind, pushed standalone annual orders above KRW 25 trillion, which it described as a record.

In urban redevelopment it passed KRW 10 trillion in annual orders for the first time and targets more than KRW 12 trillion with an eighth straight year as the top order-winner in 2026. The company is focusing on selective bidding in core areas of Seoul and the capital region.

Overseas, beyond a large Middle East plant, it plans an EPC contract for the SMR-300 with Holtec in the United States, the Kozloduy nuclear project in Bulgaria, and a design project for the Fermi nuclear site in Texas.

Strategic projects in cumulative orders through Q3 2026 included an electric arc furnace steel mill in the United States and an AI data center in Pohang.

Its clients span redevelopment associations and other domestic project owners, public and private developers, and overseas energy buyers, and its revenue is spread across urban redevelopment, energy plants and other segments.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual revenue rose from KRW 21.2 trillion in 2022 to KRW 29.7 trillion in 2023, up 39.6% year on year, and reached KRW 32.7 trillion in 2024, up 10.2%, making 2024 the largest year of 2022 to 2025. Revenue was KRW 31.1 trillion in 2025, down 4.9%.

Operating profit grew from KRW 574.9 billion in 2022 to KRW 785.4 billion in 2023, up 36.6%, then swung to an operating loss of KRW 1.26 trillion in 2024 before returning to an operating profit of KRW 653 billion in 2025.

Operating margin was 2.7% in 2022, 2.6% in 2023, -3.9% in 2024 and 2.1% in 2025, staying in the 2% range outside 2024. Net profit attributable to owners rose to KRW 535.9 billion in 2023, up 31.1%, then became a net loss of KRW 168.7 billion in 2024 and returned to a profit of KRW 373.1 billion in 2025.

Net profit including non-controlling interests was a net loss of KRW 766.2 billion in 2024 and a profit of KRW 559.1 billion in 2025, a different figure from profit attributable to owners.

The company described 2025 as a year of continued difficulty, citing geopolitical tensions, liquidity deterioration across the construction sector, construction cost disputes and regulatory risk.

Quarterly revenue went from KRW 7.83 trillion in Q3 2025 to KRW 8.06 trillion in Q4 2025, up 3.0% quarter on quarter, then KRW 6.28 trillion in Q1 2026, down 22.1%, KRW 6.84 trillion in Q2, up 8.9%, and KRW 6.24 trillion in Q3, down 8.8% quarter on quarter and down 20.3% year on year.

Quarterly operating profit rose from KRW 103.5 billion in Q3 2025 to KRW 180.9 billion in Q1 2026 and KRW 261.8 billion in Q2, then came to KRW 221.7 billion in Q3, down 15.3% quarter on quarter and up 114.2% year on year.

For the nine months through Q3 2026, the company attributed the 24.4% rise in cumulative operating profit to tighter cost control and a larger share of profitable projects, as stated in its preliminary release on October 30, 2026.

Net profit attributable to owners was KRW 114.6 billion in Q4 2025 and peaked at KRW 173.5 billion in Q1 2026, then declined for two consecutive quarters from Q2 through Q3 2026 to KRW 37.8 billion in Q3, down 13.4% year on year, and the last four quarters total KRW 442.3 billion.

Operating cash flow was negative every year, at KRW -143.5 billion in 2022, KRW -714.7 billion in 2023, KRW -118.8 billion in 2024 and KRW -748.3 billion in 2025.

05

Industry analysis

At its regular shareholders meeting on March 26, 2026, the company cited liquidity deterioration, construction cost disputes and construction regulatory risk as conditions across the sector in 2025.

Against that backdrop it is shifting its overseas portfolio from the Middle East and Southeast Asia toward advanced markets such as the United States, Europe and Australia, while aiming to raise profitability and stability together.

In energy, the company linked growing power demand from AI industry expansion to its push into nuclear and renewables. Its strategy covers large nuclear plants, SMRs, offshore wind, solar, and hydrogen and ammonia, as well as power grids such as HVDC and AI data centers.

The urban redevelopment market is characterized by selective bidding centered on core areas of Seoul and the capital region, and the company said it became the top urban redevelopment order-winner for a seventh straight year after passing KRW 10 trillion in orders in 2025.

In overseas housing, it plans to pursue entry into advanced markets where housing demand is expected to grow. As a gauge of where the cycle stands, its order backlog at the end of Q3 2026 was KRW 99.531 trillion, up 4.7% year on year and equivalent to about 3.6 years of work.

The company points to its global execution capability and technology as its differentiators in energy and infrastructure.

Outlook

Cumulative revenue of KRW 193.642 trillion through Q3 2026 equals 70.7% of the annual revenue target, and cumulative new orders of KRW 28.8746 trillion equal 86.5% of the annual order target (preliminary, announced October 30, 2026). The remaining gap to the annual order target is KRW 4.5254 trillion.

In energy, the company has outlined plans for an SMR-300 EPC contract with Holtec in the United States, the Kozloduy nuclear project in Bulgaria and a design project for the Fermi nuclear site in Texas, and the timing and value of these contracts remain at the planning stage.

In urban redevelopment it targets more than KRW 12 trillion in orders and an eighth straight year at the top, and its first 2026 win was the Gunpo Geumjeong-2 redevelopment project with total construction cost of about KRW 425.8 billion. On profitability, its strategy centers on cost control and selective bidding.

For shareholder returns, the 2026 shareholders meeting set the total dividend at KRW 90 billion, which the company said reflects both investment funding and returns to shareholders. The KRW 99.531 trillion order backlog equals about 3.6 years of work and is presented as the medium-term revenue base.

The next checkpoints are whether annual orders reach the KRW 33.4 trillion target in 2026, and whether Q4 2026 operating profit holds near the KRW 221.7 billion recorded in Q3.

Bull factors

Scale of backlog and cumulative orders

The order backlog at the end of Q3 2026 was KRW 99.531 trillion, up 4.7% year on year, which the company equates to about 3.6 years of work (preliminary, announced October 30, 2026). Cumulative new orders of KRW 28.8746 trillion rose 10.6% from a year earlier. Cumulative operating profit of KRW 664.5 billion rose 24.4% year on year.

Recovery in earnings power

Operating profit swung from a loss of KRW 1.26 trillion in 2024 to a profit in 2025, then reached KRW 180.9 billion in Q1 2026 and KRW 261.8 billion in Q2. Q3 2026 operating profit of KRW 221.7 billion rose 114.2% year on year. The company attributes the improvement to cost control and a larger share of profitable projects.

Planned expansion in energy and nuclear

The company named strengthening its competitiveness across the energy value chain as its core strategy for 2026. It plans to expand into large nuclear, SMRs, offshore wind, solar, power grids such as HVDC, and AI data centers.

It also outlined a shift in its portfolio from the Middle East and Southeast Asia toward advanced markets such as the United States, Europe and Australia.

Bear factors

Revenue decline and slower quarterly orders

Q3 2026 revenue of KRW 6.24 trillion fell 20.3% year on year, and cumulative revenue fell 15.8%. New orders in Q3 2026 of KRW 6.0516 trillion fell 35.5% year on year. Cumulative orders rose, but the quarterly pace slowed (preliminary, announced October 30, 2026).

Slowing quarterly net profit

Net profit attributable to owners peaked at KRW 173.5 billion in Q1 2026, then fell to KRW 116.4 billion in Q2 and KRW 37.8 billion in Q3, declining for two consecutive quarters. Q3 net profit attributable to owners was down 13.4% year on year. The total for the last four quarters is KRW 442.3 billion.

Cash flow and financial burden

Operating cash flow was KRW -748.3 billion in 2025 and negative in every year from 2022 to 2025. The debt ratio rose from 110.7% in 2022 to 174.8% in 2025, after peaking at 179.3% in 2024. Total liabilities at the end of 2025 were KRW 17.7 trillion.

Risk factors

Construction cost and margin risk

In 2024 cost pressure was severe enough to produce an operating loss of KRW 1.26 trillion and a net loss attributable to owners of KRW 168.7 billion. The company cited construction cost disputes and regulatory risk among 2025 business conditions. If costs rise again on overseas projects, operating margin could be pressured, and it was -3.9% in 2024.

Cash flow and financial leverage

Operating cash flow was KRW -748.3 billion in 2025, a large outflow relative to reported profit. The debt ratio was 174.8% and total liabilities were KRW 17.7 trillion. If large overseas nuclear and plant orders expand, working-capital needs could increase, so cash flow should be read alongside earnings.

Overseas project and policy risk

The company cited geopolitical tensions and liquidity deterioration across the construction sector as headwinds. The SMR, Bulgarian and U.S. nuclear projects are presented as plans, and timing and value have not been fixed.

Given the weight of Middle East projects in the past, regional risk and contract delays could cause earnings volatility.

Overall view

Hyundai Engineering & Construction has kept its earnings recovery going since returning to an operating profit in 2025, with cumulative operating profit through Q3 2026 rising year on year. Q3 2026 operating profit of KRW 221.7 billion rose 114.2%, while revenue of KRW 6.24 trillion fell 20.3%.

Net profit attributable to owners has declined for two consecutive quarters since peaking in Q1 2026, with a four-quarter total of KRW 442.3 billion. On orders, cumulative new orders of KRW 28.8746 trillion and an order backlog of KRW 99.531 trillion show the medium-term workload.

However, Q3 new orders fell 35.5% year on year, so the quarterly pace slowed. Bullish factors are the earnings recovery, growth in cumulative orders and expanding energy and urban redevelopment wins, while bearish factors are falling revenue, weaker quarterly net profit, and cash-flow and debt burdens.

The next checkpoints are whether the year-end order and urban redevelopment targets are met, and the cash flow disclosed in the 2026 annual report due by March 2027.
