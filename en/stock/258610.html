Caelum: Aero-engine parts growth amid earnings swings

02

Business structure

Caelum was founded in 2009 as a manufacturer of geothermal power equipment and listed on KOSDAQ in 2017. Based on the product sales mix compiled by an equity data service, smart connectivity accounts for 58.43%, aircraft parts for 36.55%, and geothermal power equipment for 5.02%.

The smart connectivity segment centers on kids' phones, and a subsidiary is pursuing overseas expansion of kids' phones and an in-vehicle OS platform service called FamilyCare. The aircraft parts segment is growing as mass production of Safran's LEAP-1B engine parts gains momentum.

The plant equipment segment spans chemical and LNG plant equipment and geothermal equipment, and the company is expanding its installation areas based on a patented low-temperature geothermal technology for sources below 100°C.

On a sales-split snapshot dated October 2, 2026, exports were 60.10% chemical and LNG equipment and 18.20% geothermal power equipment, while domestic sales were 16.40% chemical and LNG equipment and 5.40% trading goods. In late 2023 the company pursued the disposal of non-core businesses and the sale of a subsidiary.

It resolved in December 2025 to merge with its aero-engine parts subsidiary, and the merger was completed in March 2026. The company has said that after the merger it will integrate production systems and gradually review facility investment and capacity expansion in line with market demand.

It has also outlined plans to extend its scope to military and space applications. In geothermal power equipment, reports credit the company with a high market share built on its low-temperature geothermal patent.

04

Earnings analysis

On a DART-filed basis, annual revenue fell from KRW 86 billion in 2022 to KRW 84.5 billion in 2023, a decrease of 1.7% year on year, then rose to KRW 97.3 billion in 2024, an increase of 15.1%, making 2024 the largest year in the 2022 to 2025 period.

Revenue in 2025 was KRW 81.7 billion, down 16.1% year on year, the smallest year in that period. Operating losses were KRW 3.55 billion in 2022 and KRW 4.41 billion in 2023, with the losses widening, before operating profit of KRW 2.77 billion in 2024 marked a turn to profit.

In 2025 the company returned to an operating loss of KRW 813 million. The operating margin fell from 2.9% in 2024 to -1.0% in 2025. Owners-of-parent net profit turned positive at KRW 4.86 billion in 2024, but in 2025 it was a loss of KRW 2.52 billion.

Net income including non-controlling interests, a loss of KRW 2.75 billion in 2025, is a separate figure from owners-of-parent net income. The company attributed the main causes of the 2025 swing to lower plant order volume and a smaller value of kids' phone deliveries.

On a quarterly basis, revenue was KRW 21.8 billion in the third quarter of 2025 and KRW 11.6 billion in the fourth quarter, down 46.9% from the prior quarter, then rose to KRW 26.6 billion in the first quarter of 2026, up 130.0%, the peak.

Revenue then fell to KRW 13.7 billion in the second quarter of 2026, down 48.7%, and to KRW 8.52 billion in the third quarter, down 37.6% from the prior quarter and down 60.9% from a year earlier, marking two straight quarterly declines.

Operating profit was KRW 871 million in the first quarter of 2026, a turn to profit, rose to KRW 1.87 billion in the second quarter, up 114.7%, and then swung to an operating loss of KRW 1.01 billion in the third quarter.

Owners-of-parent net loss widened to KRW 2.98 billion in the third quarter, a larger loss than a year earlier. Owners-of-parent net losses over the last four quarters, from the fourth quarter of 2025 to the third quarter of 2026, totaled KRW 1.39 billion.

In the first quarter of 2026 the smart connectivity segment declined on weaker low-end smartphone demand, while the plant segment's chemical and LNG sales rose but profitability deteriorated.

At the end of 2025, total equity was KRW 45.5 billion, of which owners-of-parent equity was KRW 44.9 billion, and total liabilities were KRW 34.4 billion.

05

Industry analysis

Geothermal power equipment is Caelum's technological starting point. The company says its low-temperature geothermal patent for sources below 100°C is expanding its installation areas.

According to company materials, the industry view is that expanding AI infrastructure leads to higher power demand, and that geothermal is one of the most stable eco-friendly heat sources. On the plant side, the company has widened its scope into chemical plants and pressure vessels for LNG carriers and LNG terminals.

A May 2025 news report said that deliveries and orders from the global LNG and hydrogen plant company NIKKISO had been steady, and that delivery for the Shaheen project in Ulsan had succeeded.

On the aviation side, the LEAP engine family is produced by CFM International, a joint venture of Safran and GE, and Caelum supplies parts for that engine.

Kids' phones improved in the third quarter of 2025 on market growth and a standalone sales organization, but in the first quarter of 2026 the segment declined amid global economic uncertainty and weaker demand for low-end smartphones.

Caelum is classified under steel and metals, but its actual business spans power equipment, plant equipment, aircraft parts and kids' phones, which makes direct comparison with materials producers difficult.

Because geothermal equipment and plant supply depend on patents and delivery track records, the timing of new orders is a key source of volatility.

06

Outlook

The medium-term direction the company has stated centers on expanding its aircraft engine parts business. With LEAP-1B engine parts production ramping up, the March 2026 merger integrated production systems.

The company has said it will gradually review facility investment and capacity expansion in line with market demand, and that it will build manufacturing infrastructure that can serve military and space aviation as well as civil aircraft engine parts.

In kids' phones, the company is pursuing overseas expansion, and a subsidiary is also pursuing an in-vehicle OS platform service called FamilyCare. For the plant segment, lower order volume in 2025 was cited as a cause of the earnings swing.

In the first quarter of 2026, chemical and LNG plant sales rose but profitability deteriorated. Whether plant orders recover and whether plant margins improve are therefore the next checkpoints.

Since third-quarter 2026 revenue fell to KRW 8.52 billion, a key variable is whether fourth-quarter revenue stabilizes around that level or declines further. It also remains to be seen whether overseas kids' phone expansion translates into higher deliveries.

Because facility investment will be reviewed in stages according to demand, its scale and timing have not been set. The next periodic report is the 2026 annual report, which will show full-year operating margin and the detailed facility investment plan.

Bull factors

Expansion of aircraft engine parts business

Mass production of LEAP-1B engine parts has gained momentum, and the aircraft parts segment is growing. The March 2026 merger consolidated production systems.

The company has said it will gradually review facility investment and capacity expansion in line with market demand, and it has outlined a plan to extend into military and space applications.

Export-oriented plant and geothermal technology

A stock page snapshot dated October 2, 2026 shows exports at 60.10% for chemical and LNG equipment and 18.20% for geothermal power equipment. Reports credit the company with a high share of the geothermal equipment market, built on its low-temperature geothermal patent. A growing delivery record could widen its overseas order base.

Improved balance sheet and profit phases

The debt-to-equity ratio fell from 207.0% in 2023 to 75.5% in 2025. Operating cash flow turned positive at KRW 2.91 billion in 2024 and reached KRW 5.17 billion in 2025.

Operating profit turned positive in 2024 and again in the first quarter of 2026, with a further operating profit of KRW 1.87 billion in the second quarter, which points to recovering earnings capacity.

Bear factors

Consecutive quarterly revenue declines and renewed losses

Revenue fell to KRW 13.7 billion in the second quarter of 2026 and KRW 8.52 billion in the third quarter, marking two straight quarterly declines. The third quarter swung to an operating loss of KRW 1.01 billion, and the owners-of-parent net loss widened to KRW 2.98 billion from a year earlier. Owners-of-parent net losses over the last four quarters totaled KRW 1.39 billion.

Weaker kids' phone and smartphone demand

The company cited lower kids' phone delivery value as a cause of the 2025 earnings swing. In the first quarter of 2026 the smart connectivity segment fell on global economic uncertainty and a shrinking low-end smartphone market.

Overseas expansion and the FamilyCare service are under way, but their results have not yet shown up in reported earnings.

Plant order volatility and weaker profitability

Lower plant order volume was cited as a main cause of the 2025 deterioration. In the first quarter of 2026, chemical and LNG plant sales rose, yet profitability in the plant segment weakened. Because quarterly revenue swings sharply, earnings volatility remains high depending on order timing.

10

Risk factors

Order concentration and earnings volatility

Plant and geothermal equipment revenue moves sharply with individual orders. Quarterly revenue fell from KRW 21.8 billion in the third quarter of 2025 to KRW 8.52 billion in the third quarter of 2026, which illustrates the pattern. If orders cluster in particular periods, the direction of quarterly profit can change.

Financial headroom and cumulative losses

At the end of 2025, total equity was KRW 45.5 billion, of which owners-of-parent equity was KRW 44.9 billion, and total liabilities were KRW 34.4 billion. In 2023 the owners-of-parent net loss reached KRW 46 billion, a period in which the equity base was sharply hit. Continued quarterly net losses could weigh on the owners-of-parent equity base.

Execution of expansion and investment burden

After the merger, production integration, facility investment and capacity expansion are being reviewed in stages. The scale and timing of the investment have not been set.

Demand for aircraft engine parts depends on the production schedules of overseas engine makers, so if actual volumes differ from plans, the timing of returns on investment could be delayed.

12

Overall view

Caelum combines geothermal power equipment, chemical and LNG plant equipment, aircraft engine parts and kids' phones. The March 2026 merger with its aero-engine parts subsidiary broadened the production base of its aircraft parts business.

On the financial side, the debt-to-equity ratio fell from 207.0% in 2023 to 75.5% in 2025, and operating cash flow has been positive since 2024. Earnings, however, have changed direction often.

The company returned to an operating loss of KRW 813 million in 2025, and third-quarter 2026 revenue of KRW 8.52 billion marked a second straight quarterly decline, with an operating loss of KRW 1.01 billion.

The strengths are LEAP-1B parts production and a stronger balance sheet, while the weaknesses are order volatility and weaker kids' phone and smartphone demand. Based on current data, it is difficult to say that either side clearly outweighs the other.

The next checkpoints are fourth-quarter 2026 preliminary results, the full-year operating margin in the 2026 annual report, and the facility investment plan.
