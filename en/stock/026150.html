02

Business structure

TuksuConstruction was founded in 1971 as Tuksu Construction Industry and listed on KOSDAQ in 1997, specializing in civil and underground construction. It performs urban underground works with advanced equipment such as shield and semi-shield machines, jumbo drills and steel pipe jacking systems.

Its business segments cover railway and road grade-separation works, bridge and building foundations, shield works, tunnel and general civil engineering, and water supply, sewage and environmental facilities.

Segment revenue mix shifts from year to year with the size of individual contracts, so a single large award can weigh heavily on annual sales. A KRW 38.43 billion road facility contract in Muju disclosed in June 2026 equals about 25.8% of 2025 revenue of KRW 149 billion.

Public clients such as the Iksan Regional Korea Land and Infrastructure Office are central, and the company has also taken part in railway and tunnel works ordered by large contractors.

A 2025 securities report said the company had completed the P3 gas pipeline works for Samsung Electronics and was building the P4 to P5 line valued at KRW 207.3 billion.

On technology, non-open-cut methods such as shield and front jacking reduce ground damage and civil complaints, which makes them preferred in dense urban construction, and the company has secured 19 TBM units.

Overseas, the Malaysian business has been fully exited, while the Singapore business is undergoing a restructuring.

04

Earnings analysis

On an annual basis, revenue peaked at KRW 232.9 billion in 2023, the largest year of 2022 to 2025, and then fell 8.7% to KRW 212.5 billion in 2024 and 29.9% to KRW 149 billion in 2025, a second consecutive decline.

Operating profit was KRW 2.68 billion in 2022, KRW 2.49 billion in 2023 (down 7.2% year on year) and KRW 8.75 billion in 2024 (up 251.4%), before turning to an operating loss of KRW 31.6 billion in 2025. The operating margin fell from 4.1% in 2024 to -21.2% in 2025.

The 2025 losses concentrated in the fourth quarter, when revenue of KRW 14.3 billion fell 64.8% from the prior quarter and the operating loss widened to KRW 28.3 billion from KRW 3.94 billion in the third quarter.

Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders turned positive at KRW 4.66 billion in 2024 but swung back to a loss of KRW 19.5 billion in 2025, while net loss including non-controlling interests was KRW 18.2 billion.

Revenue of KRW 53.2 billion in the first quarter of 2026 rose 271.6% from the prior quarter, but second and third quarter revenue of KRW 55.4 billion and KRW 55.2 billion were roughly flat.

Operating profit was KRW 2.59 billion in the first quarter of 2026, KRW 1.99 billion in the second and KRW 1.38 billion in the third, all positive, and the second and third quarters each declined from the prior quarter.

Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 1.7 billion, KRW 1.66 billion and KRW 2.31 billion across those three quarters. A news report based on the quarterly filing said nine-month 2026 operating profit reached KRW 6 billion, compared with an operating loss of KRW 3.3 billion a year earlier. Operating cash flow was KRW -5.79 billion in 2024 and KRW 19.3 billion in 2025.

Industry analysis

The end market is centered on underground civil works in urban areas, driven by sustained mid-to-large infrastructure spending by the central and local governments.

A 2025 news report said the company was bidding on several large public infrastructure projects, including the metropolitan deep-underground tunnel project and the underground conversion of a regional rail line.

On power demand, a securities analyst view holds that expanding data center investment makes securing power infrastructure essential, which would benefit the company's mid-term orders.

LNG cogeneration plant tenders are also multiplying in Yongin's semiconductor cluster, Asan and Hwaseong, as AI data centers and chip fabs lift demand for stable energy supply.

Large contractors such as DL E&C, Hyundai Engineering & Construction and Daewoo Engineering & Construction have won awards in this pipeline, so competition is intense.

The company's differentiation lies in non-open-cut methods such as shield and front jacking, which limit ground damage and civil complaints in dense city sites. Its market capitalization is small relative to large contractors, so a single contract carries a relatively large weight in its revenue.

Cycle position is usually read through the trend in tender volumes and contract values, and public road and rail contract disclosures, such as the June 2026 Muju award, have continued to appear.

Risk factors

Order volatility

Revenue depends on large single contracts, so a gap in awards can move both sales and utilization. Whether the 2024 and 2025 revenue declines tracked order flow is a point for the annual report notes to clarify. With public clients dominant, budget timing and bid outcomes can shift project schedules.

Project profitability and cost risk

An operating loss of KRW 28.3 billion in the fourth quarter of 2025 shows losses concentrating in a single quarter. Whether this was one-off or a recurring cost problem needs to be confirmed in the regular filings.

With quarterly operating profit down for two consecutive quarters in 2026, project cost ratios and schedule control could again weigh on margins.

Balance sheet and overseas business risk

The debt ratio was high at 121.8% in 2025, and total equity fell to KRW 87.9 billion. The Malaysian business has been exited, while the Singapore business is still being restructured, so the cost and execution of overseas wind-downs are variables. With limited financial headroom, bonding and working capital needs on large awards could increase.
