Sales Growth Amid Raw-Material Strain: Testing Profit Resilience

02

Business structure

YG-1 was founded in 1981 to manufacture and sell cutting tools and listed on the KOSDAQ market in 1997. Its main products are milling, drilling, threading and turning tools, produced in solid and indexable types. These tools are used to machine various mold products and mechanical parts.

Cutting tools are consumable products used across secondary industries including IT devices, automobiles, shipbuilding and aircraft, so demand follows equipment operating rates. In 2025, about 87% of sales came from exports to more than 60 countries, including China, the United States, France and India.

The group holds 29 unlisted subsidiaries. A structure built around overseas subsidiaries appears to support this high export share. Brokerage reports describe YG-1 as the world's largest company by end-mill sales volume.

In 2026, a surge in tungsten prices sharply increased the funds needed for raw-material purchases, and whether higher costs can be passed on to product prices is cited as the key to improving cash flow.

The largest shareholder group, Song Ho-geun and 11 others, holds 31.03%, and IMC Benelux B.V. holds 14.52%, as of June 25, 2026. Demand for high-quality cutting tools is cited as the backdrop for the recent profit recovery.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual sales rose from KRW 549.8 billion in 2022 to KRW 553.2 billion in 2023 (up 0.6% year on year), KRW 575 billion in 2024 (up 3.9%) and KRW 639.4 billion in 2025 (up 11.2%), and grew for three consecutive years from 2023 through 2025. Among 2022 to 2025, 2025 was the largest year for sales and 2022 the smallest.

Operating profit fell from KRW 72.6 billion in 2022 to KRW 54.7 billion in 2023 (down 24.7%), the low point of the period, and then rose for two consecutive years to KRW 55.8 billion in 2024 (up 2.1%) and KRW 66.5 billion in 2025 (up 19.2%).

The operating margin moved from 13.2% in 2022 to 9.9% in 2023 and 9.7% in 2024 before recovering to 10.4% in 2025. Attributable net income went from KRW 32 billion in 2022 to KRW 23.4 billion in 2023 (down 26.8%) and KRW 16.7 billion in 2024 (down 28.8%), then recovered to KRW 25.3 billion in 2025 (up 51.6%).

Net income including non-controlling interests was KRW 25.3 billion in 2025. The company attributed the 2025 improvement to higher sales and cost reductions from internal efficiency gains, as reported after its February 10, 2026 filing.

Quarterly operating profit was KRW 22.6 billion in Q3 2025, KRW 19.3 billion in Q4 2025 (down 14.4% from the prior quarter), KRW 38.8 billion in Q1 2026 (up 101.0%), KRW 47.7 billion in Q2 2026 (up 22.9%) and KRW 37.4 billion in Q3 2026 (down 21.7%).

Sales have risen for four consecutive quarters through Q3 2026, but Q3 2026 sales of KRW 213.5 billion were essentially flat from the prior quarter, while operating profit declined.

Attributable net income was KRW 25.9 billion in Q1 2026, KRW 30.4 billion in Q2 and KRW 20.9 billion in Q3, down 31.4% from the prior quarter.

Press reports said first-half 2026 raw-material purchases rose 122.6% to KRW 282.3 billion, and operating cash outflow narrowed to KRW 3.8 billion from KRW 22.4 billion in the first half of 2025, even as inventory build widened working-capital outflows.

Operating cash flow was KRW 13.1 billion in 2022, KRW 40.2 billion in 2023, KRW 50.9 billion in 2024 and KRW 38.3 billion in 2025. The detailed cause of the Q3 2026 profit decline, including the timing of raw-material cost recognition, should be confirmed in the quarterly filings.

05

Industry analysis

Cutting tools are consumable products used across secondary industries including IT devices, automobiles, shipbuilding and aircraft, and they are generally described as having relatively stable demand.

Demand for high-quality cutting tools has grown recently, which is the backdrop for YG-1's pronounced profit recovery in 2026. Over the same period, tungsten prices surged in the raw-material market, raising YG-1's cost burden.

Margins and cash flow depend on how much of this cost change can be reflected in product prices. On the cycle, a press report indicates that first-half 2026 sales and operating profit set record highs.

At the same time, selling prices and raw-material costs rose together, so sales growth has not translated directly into cash generation. Brokerage reports describe YG-1 as the world's largest company by end-mill sales volume.

With exports at about 87% of sales, global manufacturing capital spending and exchange rates act as the main signals for the industry backdrop. Because its business structure differs from other listed machinery and equipment companies, direct numerical comparison with them is difficult.

The next periodic report is the 2026 annual report, and the next checkpoint is when annual cash flow and the debt ratio are confirmed in that filing. Fourth-quarter 2026 results will also be finalized in that report.

Bull factors

Recovery in earnings capacity

Operating profit rose 19.2% in 2025 to KRW 66.5 billion, and Q1 and Q2 2026 operating profit reached KRW 38.8 billion and KRW 47.7 billion. Compared with the KRW 54.7 billion in 2023, the smallest year in the 2022 to 2025 window, the earnings base has clearly recovered.

Global footprint and demand structure

About 87% of sales come from exports to more than 60 countries, and brokerage reports describe YG-1 as the world's largest company by end-mill sales volume. Cutting tools are consumable products used across secondary industries, which is cited as a basis for relatively stable demand. Rising demand for high-quality tools is presented as the backdrop for recent profit gains.

Signs of cash-flow improvement

Press reports said first-half 2026 operating cash outflow narrowed to KRW 3.8 billion from KRW 22.4 billion in the first half of 2025. Industry observers reportedly linked the smaller outflow to cost pass-through.

Operating cash flow for 2025 was KRW 38.3 billion, down from KRW 50.9 billion in 2024, though cash generation continued alongside earnings.

09

Bear factors

Raw-material costs and working-capital strain

First-half 2026 raw-material and other purchases rose 122.6% year on year to KRW 282.3 billion. Press reports said the working-capital cash outflow from inventory build widened from KRW 30.3 billion to KRW 170.7 billion. Until the tungsten cost increase is fully passed on, cost pressure may weigh on cash flow.

Higher borrowing and exhausted trade-finance line

The company fully used its KRW 10.7 billion trade-finance line, and short-term and near-term borrowings totaled KRW 598.9 billion, up more than KRW 100 billion from KRW 486.6 billion at the end of 2025. Interest paid rose to KRW 17.3 billion in the first half from KRW 15 billion a year earlier.

With the debt ratio reported at 197.6%, financial improvement remains a precondition for more trade-finance capacity.

Quarterly earnings volatility

Operating profit swung sharply by quarter, falling 14.4% in Q4 2025 to KRW 19.3 billion and 21.7% in Q3 2026 to KRW 37.4 billion. Attributable net income fell 31.4% from Q2 to Q3 2026, to KRW 20.9 billion. Annual operating profit also declined 24.7% in 2023, so earnings have shown this pattern before.

10

Risk factors

Raw material (tungsten) prices

Funding needs for raw-material purchases grew quickly in 2026 as tungsten prices surged. If higher costs cannot be passed on fully to product prices, both margins and cash flow may come under pressure.

Conversely, falling raw-material prices could increase quarterly earnings swings through inventory valuation and selling-price adjustments.

Borrowing and financing conditions

With short-term borrowing and interest costs rising, changes in interest rates or financing terms could constrain working-capital funding. Although policy financing is expanding, press reports say additional lines depend on improving the financial structure. Because the debt ratio is high, whether stability indicators improve will matter.

Export dependence and quarterly volatility

About 87% of sales come from overseas, which exposes the company to exchange-rate and foreign demand changes. Sharp quarterly swings in operating profit appeared in Q4 2025 and Q3 2026. This volatility makes the earnings trajectory harder to gauge.

Overall view

Based on DART-filed results for 2022 to 2025, YG-1 passed a low in 2023 and saw both sales and operating profit rise together in 2025. Quarterly sales have grown for four consecutive quarters through Q3 2026, and operating profit in Q1 and Q2 2026 reached high levels.

However, Q3 2026 operating profit fell 21.7% from the prior quarter, increasing quarterly earnings volatility. The tungsten price surge has raised raw-material purchases and inventory burdens, and press reports said the trade-finance line was fully used.

The debt ratio was 170.8% at the end of 2025 and, according to press reports, rose to 197.6% in the first half of 2026. Strengths include the earnings recovery, the high overseas sales share and a consumable demand structure, while weaknesses include cost pressure, higher borrowing and quarterly earnings volatility.

The next checkpoints are annual cash flow and the debt ratio in the 2026 annual report, and whether raw-material cost pass-through is reflected in margins.
