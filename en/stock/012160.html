Youngwire: Losses Narrowing, but Sales Still Falling

Youngwire swung to an operating profit in the second quarter of 2026, then returned to an operating loss in the third quarter, while revenue has declined for three straight years since 2023.

02

Business structure

Youngwire is a wire maker in the steel and metals sector whose core products are wire rope and cold-drawn wire, made by processing steel wire and strand.

The corporate summary on the financial information site fnguide lists its business segments as steel, automotive parts, logistics, packaging, cold-drawn wire, and AI services.

The core business is centered on high-value cold-drawn wire and wire rope, with development of high-strength products for automotive parts and construction machinery components. Its customers span automotive, construction, and machinery industries, which spreads its exposure to demand changes in any single sector.

In 2024 the company spun off its automotive parts division into a separate entity, Yh Auto, and acquired an 80% stake in EOES to expand its AI services business. The portfolio is moving toward exiting inefficient businesses.

Production at the Changwon plant has stopped and a sale is being pursued, and the Vietnam operation is also mentioned as a candidate for withdrawal. Direct support for Daehan Special Steel, a subsidiary reported to be in capital impairment, is also cited as a task for restoring financial soundness.

In competition, Youngwire is compared with domestic wire makers, notably Kiswire, which produces wire and wire rope.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual results show sales declines and widening losses occurring together. Revenue was KRW 527.4 billion in 2022, KRW 500 billion in 2023 (down 5.2% year on year), KRW 480.3 billion in 2024 (down 3.9%), and KRW 433.5 billion in 2025 (down 9.8%), so revenue has fallen for three consecutive years from 2023 to 2025.

Among the years from 2022 to 2025, 2022 was the largest and 2025 the smallest. Operating profit was KRW 4.23 billion in 2022, KRW 1.25 billion in 2023 (down 70.4%), and KRW 3.07 billion in 2024 (up 145.3%), before the company posted an operating loss of KRW 13.2 billion in 2025, a swing into the red.

The operating margin moved from 0.8% in 2022 to 0.3% in 2023 and 0.6% in 2024, then fell to -3.0% in 2025.

The net loss attributable to controlling shareholders grew from KRW 328 million in 2022 to KRW 6.4 billion in 2023, KRW 7.82 billion in 2024, and KRW 52.3 billion in 2025, marking four consecutive years of losses from 2022 to 2025.

The net loss including non-controlling interests was KRW 54.5 billion in 2025 and should be read separately from the controlling-shareholder figure.

For the cause of the 2025 swing, a Digital Today report on the disclosure published on February 27, 2026 pointed to lower sales from the halted and to-be-sold loss-making Changwon plant, along with one-off restructuring costs.

Classifying the plant site as an asset held for sale brought impairment losses and losses on disposal of property, plant and equipment, which widened the net loss. The debt-to-equity ratio fell from 170.3% in 2022 to 136.7% in 2023 and 136.8% in 2024, then rose to 143.3% in 2025.

Operating cash flow went from KRW -28.6 million in 2022 to KRW 24.6 billion in 2023, KRW 20 billion in 2024, and KRW 41.4 billion in 2025, so cash generation stayed positive despite the losses.

Quarterly, the operating loss narrowed from KRW 6.97 billion in the third quarter of 2025 to KRW 2.24 billion in the fourth quarter, then KRW 1.44 billion in the first quarter of 2026, before turning to operating profit of KRW 2.17 billion in the second quarter.

The third quarter of 2026 brought revenue of KRW 103.8 billion, down 2.8% from the prior quarter and 5.6% from a year earlier, and an operating loss of KRW 1 billion.

The net loss attributable to controlling shareholders over the last four quarters totals KRW 26.7 billion, and because its sign has changed from quarter to quarter, the durability of earnings remains a question for coming quarters.

05

Industry analysis

Youngwire's demand comes from the automotive, construction, and machinery industries, where its cold-drawn wire and wire rope serve as component materials.

Automotive parts demand moves with vehicle output and whether parts are adopted, while construction wire is linked to public civil engineering and social overhead capital budget flows.

A 2021 report also cited expanded government social overhead capital budgets as a factor supporting a recovery in construction wire sales.

In wire and steel-wire makers, the spread between the cost of raw steel wire and selling prices largely determines profitability, so fixed-cost burden shows up directly in results during periods of falling sales.

The 2026 decline in January-September revenue may be tied to lower production-line utilization during the business restructuring. Among competitors, Kiswire is the most direct comparison, as a wire specialist working with similar raw materials and product lines.

SeAH Steel focuses on steel pipes, so its product structure makes a direct comparison harder. The position of the steel cycle should be judged from current price and demand indicators, which are worth checking alongside the next quarterly results.

The AI services business serves different customers from the core steel operations, so it should be viewed separately from the steel cycle.

As a small-cap stock, Youngwire may see its share price move sharply around earnings releases and disclosures, so it is useful to separate cyclical factors from company-specific ones.

Outlook

The direction the company has publicly outlined dates back to its first-quarter 2025 results commentary.

According to the fnguide corporate summary, the company then cited the start of mass production for the TK1 model, planned mass production for the OV1 and SP3 models, and a won bid for the SX3, expecting stable volume over the following five years.

Because this reflects the company's view as of the first quarter of 2025, whether these programs reached 2026 revenue should be checked against the quarterly sales trend.

How closely the return to operating profit in the second quarter of 2026 is tied to these programs can be gauged from the revenue composition disclosed in the next annual report. For the AI services business, the key question is how the 80% stake in EOES contributes to consolidated results.

Asset sales have been presented as a pillar of financial restructuring, and progress on the Changwon plant sale can be checked in the 2026 annual report due by March 31, 2027. Whether the impairment losses and restructuring costs that drove the 2025 loss recur in 2026 is the first condition for an earnings recovery.

The 5-for-1 share consolidation took effect on June 16, 2026, and the par value changed from KRW 500 to KRW 2,500. The company said the consolidation does not constitute a capital reduction.

In the near term, the key points are whether quarterly revenue stays in a range near KRW 100 billion and whether operating profit remains in positive territory. The next check point is how the annual operating margin and debt ratio appear in the 2026 annual report.

08

Bull factors

Operating profit in Q2 2026

Operating profit of KRW 2.17 billion in the second quarter of 2026 turned positive, breaking a run of quarterly operating losses that began in the third quarter of 2025. Revenue for the same quarter was KRW 106.8 billion, up 10.7% from the prior quarter.

The third quarter brought an operating loss again, so whether the swing is durable will be tested in later quarters.

Narrowing quarterly losses

Quarterly operating losses shrank from KRW 6.97 billion in the third quarter of 2025 to KRW 2.24 billion in the fourth quarter and KRW 1.44 billion in the first quarter of 2026. The third-quarter 2026 operating loss of KRW 1 billion was smaller than the year-earlier quarter. The narrowing can be read as linked to the effects of the business restructuring.

Exits from inefficient operations

Inefficient operations are being exited, including the halted and planned sale of the Changwon plant and the withdrawal from the Vietnam operation.

Removing loss-making operations would reduce fixed-cost burden, and the company describes the process as a step toward improving profitability and focusing on core capabilities. The timing and scale of the exits can be checked in the 2026 annual report.

09

Bear factors

Persistent sales decline

Revenue fell for three consecutive years, to KRW 500 billion in 2023, KRW 480.3 billion in 2024, and KRW 433.5 billion in 2025. Per Vegan News, cumulative January-September 2026 revenue of KRW 307 billion was below the KRW 337.2 billion reported for the same period of 2025. Sustaining profitability on a shrinking revenue base requires improvement in cost and cost structure.

Continued net losses and wider deficits

The net loss attributable to controlling shareholders has run for four consecutive years from 2022 to 2025 and widened to KRW 52.3 billion in 2025. Over the last four quarters that loss totals KRW 26.7 billion.

Even in the second quarter of 2026, when operating profit turned positive, net income attributable to controlling shareholders was only KRW 41.03 million.

Balance sheet strain and subsidiary support

The debt-to-equity ratio rose to 143.3% in 2025 from 136.8% in 2024. Total liabilities at the end of 2025 were KRW 235.5 billion, exceeding total equity of KRW 164.3 billion. Direct support for Daehan Special Steel, which has been reported to be in capital impairment, could add to the financial burden.

10

Risk factors

Execution risk in restructuring

If the Changwon plant sale is delayed or fetches less than book value, further impairment losses could follow. If the restructuring costs and impairments behind the 2025 loss recur in 2026, the earnings recovery would be pushed back.

The Vietnam exit and subsidiary support may also require cash, so the pace and cost of the restructuring need checking each quarter.

Customer concentration and cycle risk

Revenue depends on demand for component materials from the automotive, construction, and machinery industries. A slowdown in vehicle production or a drop in construction orders could reduce sales further.

Because margins are squeezed when the spread between raw material costs and selling prices narrows, industry indicators should be read alongside quarterly results.

Financial buffer and AI business delivery

With the debt-to-equity ratio at 143.3%, continued operating losses would weaken the financial buffer. If the AI services business built through the 80% stake in EOES does not show results, it will be hard to offset weakness in the core business. As a small-cap stock, the shares may also be more volatile.

Overall view

Youngwire is in a long run of losses, with an operating loss in 2025 and four consecutive years of net losses attributable to controlling shareholders starting in 2022.

In 2026 the second-quarter return to operating profit and the narrowing third-quarter loss marked quarterly improvement, but the third quarter again showed an operating loss.

Revenue has declined for three consecutive years since 2023, and cumulative January-September 2026 revenue was also below the same period of 2025.

On the balance sheet, the debt-to-equity ratio rose to 143.3% in 2025, and of total equity of KRW 164.3 billion at the end of 2025, KRW 135.8 billion belongs to controlling shareholders. The bullish factors are the existence of a profitable quarter, the narrowing loss trend, and a price-to-book ratio well below 1x.

The bearish factors are falling sales, continued net losses, the burden of subsidiary support, and execution risk in restructuring.

The next checkpoints are the preliminary 2026 full-year results around late February 2027, the 2026 annual report due by March 31, 2027, and the first-quarter 2027 report due by May 15, 2027.
