02

Business structure

Shindo Eng. Lab is a KOSDAQ-listed maker of display manufacturing equipment. Its core products are laminators that bond cover glass and films onto display panels and degassing machines that remove micro-bubbles, which it supplies for OLED, foldable and micro LED processes, according to press reports.

The company said it developed a curved vacuum laminator in 2015 that was the first of its kind in the world, and it has used that technology to widen its business. The most prominent new business is manufacturing equipment for the membrane electrode assembly (MEA), a core component of hydrogen fuel cells.

The company says it applied the heat, vacuum and pressure control technology used in display equipment to the MEA process. Smart windows, which use liquid crystal inside glass to control light transmission, and micro LED back-end equipment are also presented as new businesses.

Smart windows are being developed with large Korean companies, and equipment has been delivered to the United States. On the customer side, the company supplies laminators to four major panel makers in China's curved display market, and its 2026 first-half earnings commentary says export share has risen.

In 2026, a KRW 5.78 billion contract with an LG Display Vietnam entity and a KRW 6.79 billion sales and supply contract in Vietnam were disclosed. The company argues that MEA equipment is a field where high material costs and process know-how raise barriers for later entrants.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual revenue was KRW 21.8 billion in 2022, KRW 20.8 billion in 2023 (down 4.9% year on year), KRW 10.6 billion in 2024 (down 48.9%), and KRW 31.9 billion in 2025 (up 201.2%). Of 2022 to 2025, 2025 was the largest year and 2024 the smallest.

For the 2025 increase, the company cited higher revenue from a rise in orders and improved profitability as the main factors, according to press reports.

Operating results went from an operating loss of KRW 3.73 billion in 2022, an operating loss of KRW 2.51 billion in 2023 (losses continued but narrowed), and an operating loss of KRW 4.1 billion in 2024 (losses continued and widened) to an operating profit of KRW 1.81 billion in 2025, which turned a loss into a profit.

The operating margin moved from -17.1% in 2022, -12.1% in 2023 and -38.7% in 2024 to 5.7% in 2025. Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders went from a loss of KRW 401 million in 2022, a loss of KRW 3.35 billion in 2023 and a loss of KRW 564 million in 2024 to a profit of KRW 1.81 billion in 2025.

Net profit including non-controlling interests, a separate line, was also KRW 1.81 billion in 2025. At the end of 2025, total equity was KRW 59.3 billion, all of it attributable to controlling shareholders, and total liabilities were KRW 3.35 billion.

Operating cash flow went from -KRW 1.08 billion in 2022 and KRW 1.3 billion in 2023 to KRW 11.1 billion in 2024, then turned to -KRW 5 billion in 2025.

Press reports said receivables reached KRW 3.62 billion in 2025, up 105.4% from the prior year, and that the gap between that growth and the 201.2% revenue growth suggests collections may have lagged.

The same reports said the 2025 audit report carried a clean opinion, but the appropriateness of revenue recognition was flagged as a key audit matter for a second straight year.

Quarterly results rose from revenue of KRW 4.42 billion and operating profit of KRW 871 million in the third quarter of 2025 to revenue of KRW 26.1 billion and operating profit of KRW 3.05 billion in the fourth quarter, and fourth-quarter revenue accounted for most of the KRW 31.9 billion annual total.

In the first quarter of 2026, revenue was KRW 1.34 billion and the operating loss was KRW 1.33 billion, a swing from profit. In the second quarter, revenue rose to KRW 7.62 billion but the operating loss widened to KRW 7.88 billion.

In the third quarter, revenue was KRW 1.77 billion and the operating loss narrowed to KRW 1.88 billion, though this was a swing to loss from the same quarter a year earlier. The Wisereport data platform summarized that second-quarter revenue rose while the operating loss widened on cost burdens.

Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders turned to a loss from the second quarter of 2026, and the total for the latest four quarters (fourth quarter of 2025 through third quarter of 2026) was a net loss of KRW 8.37 billion.

05

Industry analysis

Demand for display equipment fell sharply after COVID-19, and the company has focused on new businesses that apply its existing technology, according to press reports.

In China's curved display market, the company supplies laminators to four major panel makers, and its 2026 first-half earnings commentary says export share is rising. In hydrogen fuel cells, MEA manufacturing equipment is central, and press coverage notes that about 440 sheets are used per NEXO vehicle.

The equipment began as a joint development with Samsung SDI in 2012, and after SDI exited hydrogen, the company said it developed the technology and equipment on its own and built a mass-production system. The company said it has spent more than ten years on self-funded R&D in smart windows and hydrogen fuel cells.

Press coverage also presented a view that the hydrogen fuel-cell market has entered a full growth phase. However, no company-disclosed figure showing MEA equipment revenue was identified, so the proven contribution from this business is limited.

In competition, the company points to its 2015 curved vacuum laminating technology and its heat, vacuum and pressure control technology as points of difference. Smart windows are presented for use in car sunroofs and building glass, with work under way alongside large Korean companies.

The lumpy nature of display equipment orders, where large contracts cluster and gaps follow, showed up in the swing in quarterly revenue between the fourth quarter of 2025 and the first quarter of 2026.
