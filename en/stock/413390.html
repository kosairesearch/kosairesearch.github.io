02

Business structure

MOT is a secondary-battery assembly equipment specialist that converted to a corporation in 2019 and listed on KOSDAQ in 2024.

Based on 2026 second-quarter revenue, the segment mix is roughly 34% other secondary-battery manufacturing equipment, 32% Tab Welding, 15% Can Cap Welding, 12% Jelly Roll Insert and 7% automotive parts manufacturing equipment.

Tab welding equipment bonds tabs to the positive and negative electrodes so current can flow, while packaging equipment forms and seals the battery. The company emphasizes customized assembly processes tailored to cell specifications as its differentiator.

Its main customer is Samsung SDI, for which it supplies assembly equipment across successive battery generations. Next-generation items include equipment for all-solid-state, 46-series cylindrical and lithium iron phosphate (LFP) batteries.

In August 2026 MOT first shipped 46-series cylindrical battery assembly equipment and is pursuing entry into next-generation mass-production lines. The business is broadening from secondary-battery equipment into automotive core component automation.

In April 2026 it won an ERCV production automation line worth about KRW 2 billion, a component used in EGR systems, and said the win would support a push into EV and HEV component equipment. Because equipment revenue depends on customers' investment timing, dependence on a small number of customers remains high.

04

Earnings analysis

On a disclosed annual basis, revenue rose from KRW 73.7 billion in 2023 to KRW 84.9 billion in 2024, up 15.1% year on year, then fell to KRW 75.5 billion in 2025, down 11.1%. Among 2023 to 2025, 2024 was the largest year and 2023 the smallest.

Operating profit was KRW 4.3 billion in 2023, KRW 6.5 billion in 2024 (up 51.0%) and KRW 4.41 billion in 2025 (down 32.2%). The operating margin was 5.8% in 2023, 7.7% in 2024 and 5.8% in 2025, so 2024 combined higher sales with a higher margin, and 2025 returned the margin to the 2023 level as sales declined.

Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 2.65 billion in 2023, KRW 6.09 billion in 2024 (up 129.9%) and KRW 3.47 billion in 2025 (down 43.0%). Net profit including non-controlling interests was reported at the same levels for each year.

Operating cash flow moved from an outflow of KRW -12.8 billion in 2023 to an inflow of KRW 29.5 billion in 2024, then fell to KRW 4.72 billion in 2025.

The debt-to-equity ratio rose from 179.2% in 2023 to 91.0% in 2024 and 99.1% in 2025, with total equity of KRW 60.2 billion at the end of 2025, all of it attributable to controlling shareholders, and total liabilities of KRW 59.7 billion.

On a quarterly basis, the third quarter of 2025 showed revenue of KRW 22.9 billion, operating profit of KRW 3.85 billion and net profit attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 3.02 billion.

The fourth quarter of 2025 posted revenue of KRW 26.2 billion (up 14.6% from the prior quarter) and operating profit of KRW 988 million (down 74.4%). The first quarter of 2026 recorded revenue of KRW 11.4 billion (down 56.7%) and operating profit of KRW 152 million.

The second quarter of 2026 recorded revenue of KRW 31.1 billion (up 174.0%), operating profit of KRW 1.2 billion and net profit attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 1.09 billion.

The third quarter of 2026 recorded revenue of KRW 23.5 billion (down 24.6% from the prior quarter and up 2.5% year on year), operating profit of KRW 1.03 billion (down 73.3% year on year) and net profit attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 43.12 million (down 98.6% year on year).

Revenue jumped in the second quarter of 2026, yet operating profit stayed around KRW 1.2 billion, so the sales expansion did not translate into a better margin.

Over the last four quarters, from the fourth quarter of 2025 to the third quarter of 2026, net profit attributable to controlling shareholders totaled KRW 2.64 billion.

05

Industry analysis

MOT operates in secondary-battery manufacturing equipment. One analysis points to a temporary slowdown in electric vehicle demand, often called a chasm, which has weighed on investment sentiment toward battery equipment makers.

Offsetting this, the same analysis cites the possibility of customers resuming investment and growing demand for next-generation equipment such as all-solid-state and 46-series cylindrical batteries.

A news headline reported that MOT supplies key equipment for Samsung SDI's shift of investment from EV batteries toward energy storage systems (ESS). Press coverage has also highlighted wide performance differences among battery equipment makers.

Within this group, MOT is positioned in customized equipment tailored to specific cell specifications, such as tab welding and cap welding. Because it receives new line orders tied to next-generation cell transitions, the timing of its customers' line expansions and process changes largely drives its order flow.

The automotive component automation business is smaller in scale than the battery business but broadens the customer base, and its earlier EGR valve and cooler production equipment record was cited as a reason for the ERCV win.

Its competitive position rests on assembly technology tailored to cell specifications, while revenue volatility remains high because it depends on the investment schedules of large cell makers.

Outlook

MOT has several disclosed orders in its pipeline. The KRW 39.95 billion contract signed in May 2026 runs from May 15, 2026 to March 1, 2027.

The roughly KRW 10.1 billion single supply contract signed on October 20, 2026 runs from October 20, 2026 to April 20, 2027, with no down payment and with interim and final payments made after delivery and inspection. A May 18, 2026 Newspim report stated that the order backlog had surpassed KRW 80 billion.

In April 2026 the company said it expects its competitiveness in larger equipment projects, such as power relay actuator lines, to extend beyond the battery business. Management has said it will focus on revenue growth and a stronger profit structure.

The 46-series cylindrical equipment, first shipped in August 2026, is at the stage of entering mass-production lines. Because the contracts run from about nine months to nearly a year, the pace at which orders are recognized as revenue and profit is the next point to check.

Fourth-quarter 2026 results are not yet part of the disclosed figures. Once the 2026 annual report sets full-year revenue and operating profit, the sharp second-quarter revenue increase and third-quarter profit decline can be read in the full-year context.

Bull factors

Large order accumulation

Disclosed 2026 orders include KRW 7.99 billion in April, about KRW 2 billion for ERCV in April, KRW 39.95 billion in May and about KRW 10.1 billion in October, together exceeding KRW 50 billion. The May contract equals 52.95% of 2025 revenue. Press coverage reported an order backlog above KRW 80 billion, which supports revenue visibility.

Next-generation products and diversification

46-series cylindrical assembly equipment was first shipped in August 2026, and the company is pursuing entry into next-generation mass-production lines. Its ERCV line win in automotive component automation broadened the customer base beyond batteries. The company argues this diversification can reduce reliance on a single industry.

Shareholder return execution

In June 2026 MOT signed a trust agreement to buy back shares worth about KRW 3 billion, stating that most of the shares would be cancelled. In September 2026 it decided to cancel 294,660 treasury shares, about 3% of total shares issued. The company describes these steps as fulfilling its shareholder value plan.

Bear factors

Weakening earnings capacity

2025 operating profit fell 32.2% year on year to KRW 4.41 billion, and net profit attributable to controlling shareholders fell 43.0% to KRW 3.47 billion. Third-quarter 2026 operating profit was KRW 1.03 billion, down 73.3% year on year, and net profit attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 43.12 million. Even with second-quarter 2026 revenue rising to KRW 31.1 billion, operating profit was only KRW 1.2 billion.

Dependence on customer investment timing

The main customer is Samsung SDI, and order and revenue timing depends heavily on that customer's investment schedule. One analysis cited the slowdown in the secondary-battery industry and reliance on customer investment timing as risk factors.

Quarterly revenue swings, from KRW 11.4 billion in the first quarter of 2026 to KRW 31.1 billion in the second quarter, illustrate this structure.

Cash flow and balance sheet shifts

Operating cash flow fell from KRW 29.5 billion in 2024 to KRW 4.72 billion in 2025. The debt-to-equity ratio rose from 91.0% in 2024 to 99.1% in 2025. Total liabilities at the end of 2025 were KRW 59.7 billion, roughly the same size as total equity of KRW 60.2 billion.

10

Risk factors

Customer concentration and capex cycle

A significant part of revenue depends on a small number of large customers, including Samsung SDI. A cut or delay in customer investment could quickly reduce orders. If the slowdown in EV demand persists, battery equipment orders may also be delayed.

Delayed profit recovery

Second-quarter 2026 revenue rose to KRW 31.1 billion, but operating profit was only KRW 1.2 billion, and third-quarter operating profit was KRW 1.03 billion. Disclosed figures alone make it hard to separate how changes in revenue mix and cost structure affected margins. A slower profit recovery would also lengthen the period over which the valuation is read.

Contract timing and recognition risk

The KRW 39.95 billion contract runs from May 15, 2026 to March 1, 2027, and the roughly KRW 10.1 billion contract runs from October 20, 2026 to April 20, 2027. Payments are made in stages after inspection, so revenue and cash inflow arrive with a time lag.

Counterparty details are withheld from disclosures as trade secrets, which limits outside review of customer-level risk.

12

Overall view

MOT has shown notable order flow in 2026, with disclosed contracts of KRW 7.99 billion in April, KRW 39.95 billion in May and about KRW 10.1 billion in October. On the other hand, disclosed 2025 revenue and operating profit declined year on year, and third-quarter 2026 operating profit fell 73.3% year on year.

Second-quarter 2026 revenue rose to KRW 31.1 billion, but operating profit was only KRW 1.2 billion, which shows that the link between sales growth and profit is still weak. Dependence on Samsung SDI makes results sensitive to the customer's investment schedule, which is a structural factor.

The first shipment of 46-series cylindrical equipment and the entry into automotive component automation are factual signs of diversification. Share buybacks and cancellations are factual shareholder return measures.

The next checkpoints are how full-year operating margin is presented in the 2026 annual report and how revenue from the contracts is recognized in early 2027.
