Mini LED Ramp-Up Meets Five Straight Quarters of Operating Losses

Mini LED backlight supply to a North American customer is ramping and sales rose for two straight quarters, but operating losses have continued for five straight quarters, making the pace of profit recovery the central issue.

  1. 1

    Disclosed 2025 results show revenue of KRW 121.2 billion (down 17.5% year on year) and operating profit of KRW 287 million (down 95.8%), the weakest year in the 2022–2025 range.

  2. 2

    Q3 2026 revenue was KRW 38.2 billion, up 5.6% from the prior quarter and 29.5% from a year earlier, extending two straight quarters of growth.

  3. 3

  4. 4

  5. 5

02

Business structure

WOOREE E&L Haroutine is a KOSDAQ-listed company whose core business is LED packaging. It supplies LED packages, light bars and backlight units (BLU) used in TVs, monitors, notebooks and tablets, along with automotive LED and general lighting products.

Production bases are located in China and Vietnam, and a subsidiary provides vertical integration from LED packages to modules.

The growth driver in mini LED backlights began with mass production in the first half of 2026, now supplied at about 15,000 units per month to a large North American IT company's next-generation AI notebooks. The module uses local dimming that drives more than 1,000 LED chips in segments within a notebook-sized panel.

The technology is patented in Korea and Japan, with patent applications pending in the United States and Europe. In automotive, the company is working with a global automaker on RGB mini LED modules toward a Q4 2028 launch target.

The health segment centers on the HA.routine functional food brand, launched in 2021, and the company acquired WOO Green Science in 2025 to hold R&D through distribution. The LED business has been hit by weaker demand and price competition, while the functional food segment faces intensifying competition. Segment revenue shares are not disclosed in the filings used here and are not quantified.

04

Earnings analysis

On a disclosed annual basis, revenue rose from KRW 136.2 billion in 2022 to KRW 142 billion in 2023 (up 4.3%) and KRW 146.9 billion in 2024 (up 3.4%), then fell to KRW 121.2 billion in 2025 (down 17.5%). Across 2022–2025, 2024 was the largest year for revenue and 2025 the smallest.

Operating profit went from KRW 2.74 billion in 2022 to KRW 5.05 billion in 2023 (up 83.9%) and KRW 6.87 billion in 2024 (up 36.1%), then dropped to KRW 287 million in 2025 (down 95.8%). The operating margin slid from 4.7% in 2024 to 0.2% in 2025.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders edged up to KRW 5.42 billion in 2023 and KRW 5.51 billion in 2024, then turned to a loss of KRW 639 million in 2025. Net income including non-controlling interests was a loss of KRW 1.39 billion in 2025, a separate figure from the controlling-shareholder line.

Operating cash flow fell from KRW 28.4 billion in 2022 to KRW 8.74 billion in 2024 before recovering to KRW 10.1 billion in 2025.

Quarterly revenue fell from KRW 29.5 billion in Q3 2025 and KRW 25.7 billion in Q4 2025 to KRW 23 billion in Q1 2026, then rose to KRW 36.2 billion in Q2 2026 (up 57.2% sequentially) and KRW 38.2 billion in Q3 2026 (up 5.6%).

Operating losses have persisted for five straight quarters, from KRW 1.49 billion in Q3 2025 to KRW 5.77 billion in Q1 2026, then narrowed to KRW 4.56 billion in Q2 and KRW 1.19 billion in Q3 2026.

Losses attributable to controlling shareholders widened for four straight quarters, from KRW 1.88 billion in Q4 2025 to KRW 5.14 billion in Q3 2026, totaling KRW 14.2 billion over the last four quarters.

In Q3 2026 the attributable loss was much larger than the operating loss, and the reasons for that gap sit in non-operating items to be checked in the annual report notes. Revenue recovery has not yet translated into a proportional narrowing of operating losses.

05

Industry analysis

The company's core demand comes from display backlight markets, and the shift in notebook design is shaping mini LED demand. TrendForce expects the share of AI notebooks in global notebook shipments to rise from 37.5% in 2026 to 50% in 2027 and 84.9% in 2029.

AI-dedicated platforms from NVIDIA and Qualcomm are placing CPU, GPU and NPU in one device, which raises the importance of internal space and heat management. That in turn lifts demand for thin, high-quality display components.

The company itself expects mid- to long-term growth in the LED package market as mini and micro LEDs expand. Its traditional BLU and lighting business, however, faces weaker demand and intensifying price competition.

Its differentiators cited are local dimming, a thin module design and patents already registered in Korea and Japan. Market share and price comparisons against domestic and overseas LED and BLU rivals were not confirmed, so margin trends are the practical gauge.

In health products, competition is cited as a drag on results, so both businesses are exposed to price and volume competition.

06

Outlook

The company has outlined three concrete tracks. First, it is expanding supply of mini LED backlights to a North American customer, and it is considering capacity investment tied to higher finished-product sales.

Second, it plans to strengthen its position in AI devices by winning new global customers and developing next-generation product lines. Third, in automotive it is working with a global automaker on RGB mini LED modules with a Q4 2028 launch target.

Patent examination is ongoing in the United States and Europe for the drive technology. The company says the technology could extend to optical communication and AI infrastructure, but no specific revenue target or order size has been disclosed.

In health products, the 2025 acquisition of WOO Green Science links raw-material R&D to distribution, and product and channel expansion continues. The next checkpoint for the quarterly path is whether the Q3 2026 revenue gain turns into a further narrowing of operating losses. No annual guidance was identified, so the outlook rests on disclosed figures and announced schedules.

08

Bull factors

Mini LED customer win

A large North American IT company is taking mini LED backlights in mass production for AI notebooks at about 15,000 units per month. Supply has continued since mass production began in the first half of 2026, and the company has said it is considering capacity expansion.

Sales recovery

Revenue bottomed at KRW 23 billion in Q1 2026 and rose to KRW 36.2 billion in Q2 and KRW 38.2 billion in Q3, with Q3 up 29.5% from a year earlier. The operating loss narrowed from KRW 5.77 billion in Q1 to KRW 1.19 billion in Q3 2026.

Balance sheet and diversification

At year-end 2025 the debt ratio was 33.5%, with total liabilities of KRW 30.6 billion against KRW 91.4 billion of total equity. Automotive RGB mini LED work with a global automaker targets Q4 2028, and the health business now spans R&D through distribution.

09

Bear factors

Five straight quarters of operating losses

The operating margin fell to 0.2% in 2025 from 4.7% in 2024. Operating losses have run for five straight quarters through Q3 2026, and attributable net losses total KRW 14.2 billion over the last four quarters, with no profit turnaround yet disclosed.

Weak demand and competition in core businesses

The LED business saw weaker demand and tougher pricing, while health products faced intensifying competition. If price pressure persists, growth in mini LED may not be enough to offset losses in the legacy business.

Non-operating drag

Q3 2026 operating loss was KRW 1.19 billion while the attributable net loss was KRW 5.14 billion, a wide gap. The reasons sit in items outside operations and need to be confirmed in the annual report notes, since the current data does not identify them.

Risk factors

Customer concentration

The current mini LED backlight supply is described as going to a single large North American IT company. Order changes from one customer could move sales and utilization directly. New global customer wins are planned, but timing and results have not been disclosed.

Profitability and cost structure

With the operating margin at 0.2% in 2025, there is little buffer against cost or price changes. Whether production in China and Vietnam can absorb price pressure should be checked through quarterly margins. Capacity expansion would add fixed-cost burden.

Governance and related-party dealings

The largest shareholder held 31.69% at the end of 2025, according to press reports. Health supplement products are manufactured using the production facilities of an affiliated company. The terms and scale of related-party transactions should be checked in the periodic reports.

12

Overall view

WOOREE E&L Haroutine is widening its business from LED packages and BLUs into mini LED backlights and health supplements. Disclosed 2025 results show revenue of KRW 121.2 billion and operating profit of KRW 287 million, both sharply lower than in 2024.

Revenue rose for two straight quarters into 2026, reaching KRW 38.2 billion in Q3. Operating losses, however, have persisted for five straight quarters, and attributable net losses have widened for four straight quarters, totaling KRW 14.2 billion over the last four quarters.

Bullish factors include mass production for a North American customer, patent registrations and automotive collaboration. Bearish factors include price and demand competition in legacy LED and health products, plus a sizable non-operating drag.

The price-to-book ratio sits well below 1x, but the pace of profit recovery is the starting point for interpreting that value. The next checkpoints are the Q4 2026 result and, in the 2026 annual report, the non-operating items and equity movement.
