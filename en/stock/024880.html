02

Business structure

KPF makes industrial fasteners, automotive forged parts and marine cable.

Segment weights differ by source: a company profile puts fasteners at about 28%, automotive parts at about 23% and other businesses including marine cable at about 49%, while Butler's Q2 2026 breakdown shows 50% for businesses other than marine cable and 49% for fasteners and automotive parts.

Because the classifications differ, the two cannot be compared directly. As of year-end 2025, bolts, nuts and forged products were 32.52% domestic and 67.48% export sales. Consolidated subsidiaries include KPF VINA in Vietnam, KPF (Jinan) Bearing in China and trading companies in the United States.

Fasteners have expanded Vietnam capacity to raise the OEM customer share. Prices are adjusted through negotiation with customers for cost increases, so price changes reach shipped volumes with a lag.

Automotive forged parts follow vehicle production, and marine cable follows shipbuilding orders, so cycles in those end markets drive results.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual revenue fell for three straight years, from KRW 819 billion in 2022 to KRW 809.7 billion in 2023, KRW 782 billion in 2024 and KRW 743.9 billion in 2025; 2022 was the largest year in the 2022 to 2025 span.

Operating profit peaked at KRW 47.3 billion in 2023 and then declined for two consecutive years to KRW 39.4 billion in 2024 and KRW 35.4 billion in 2025. Operating margin moved from 5.8% in 2023 to 5.0% in 2024 and 4.8% in 2025, so lower margins accompanied the shrinking sales.

Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 21.4 billion in 2024, the highest of the four years, and KRW 19.4 billion in 2025. Net income including non-controlling interests was KRW 24 billion in 2024 and KRW 19 billion in 2025.

The debt-to-equity ratio fell from 215.5% in 2022 to 103.5% in 2025; at year-end 2025, total equity was KRW 328.8 billion, of which KRW 270.1 billion belonged to controlling shareholders, and total liabilities were KRW 340.4 billion.

Operating cash flow was KRW 73.4 billion in 2023, dropped to KRW 13.6 billion in 2024 and recovered to KRW 50.7 billion in 2025. Quarterly revenue rose from KRW 174.1 billion in Q3 2025 to KRW 213.6 billion in Q2 2026, and Q3 2026 revenue of KRW 211.3 billion was 1.1% lower than the prior quarter.

Operating profit was KRW 7.82 billion in Q1 2026 and KRW 8.92 billion in Q2 2026, then fell 90.1% to KRW 882 million in Q3 2026.

Q4 2025 produced a net loss of KRW 500 million attributable to controlling shareholders after a profit in Q3 2025; Q1 2026 returned to a profit of KRW 7.45 billion, and Q3 2026 swung back to a loss of KRW 5.77 billion.

In a provisional release dated November 5, 2026, cumulative operating profit for January to September 2026 was KRW 17.6 billion, down 47.1%. Revenue grew while profit shrank across 2026.

05

Industry analysis

The marine cable business tracks shipbuilding demand. A company-profile commentary links recovery in that unit to higher global cargo volumes, high oil prices and tighter decarbonization rules that lift eco-friendly ship orders.

Press coverage attributes the 2026 revenue gains to strong domestic manufacturing exports, infrastructure investment and overseas market development. Supply-chain shifts, with the United States and Europe reducing reliance on Chinese sourcing, have lifted demand for products made in Vietnam.

On costs, the prolonged Middle East conflict and the Russia-Ukraine war pushed oil prices up and raised manufacturing costs, while a weaker won-dollar exchange rate weighed on profitability. Q3 2026, with revenue up and margins down, is the clearest current read on where the cycle stands.

Pass-through of costs into prices is the key variable for responding to the industry. Automotive forged parts depend on vehicle production. KPF's competitive position rests on overseas production and a growing OEM share.

Outlook

The near-term priority is restoring profitability. According to an Asia Economy report, KPF is negotiating price increases with customers, and the company expects profitability to take time to recover through Q4 2026 because price adjustments take time to reach shipped volumes.

Overseas capacity expansion continues: a News1 summary says the second Vietnam plant is due to be completed within 2026, with production capacity added from 2027. A company-profile commentary describes the Texas plant's full operation in 2026 as shifting the supply base to local manufacturing.

The first near-term test is how much of the price increase shows up in Q4 2026 shipments. The prior annual results were released on March 17, 2026, so the 2026 annual report may follow a similar timeline, around March 2027.

That report should confirm Q4 results, full-year operating margin and profit attributable to controlling shareholders. Cost pass-through speed relative to exchange and oil moves remains open.

The contribution of the Texas plant and Vietnam expansion will become clearer in segment disclosures of upcoming periodic reports.

Bull factors

Expanding overseas production base

Vietnam subsidiary sales rose 17.0% in Q3 2026 as US and European buyers reduce reliance on China. The Texas plant is described as entering full operation in 2026, and the second Vietnam plant is planned for completion within the year. A broader overseas production base is the core bull argument.

Price negotiations and room for margin recovery

Price increase talks linked to cost increases are under way with customers. Because high-cost volumes shipped in Q3, pass-through into future shipments could change the margin trajectory.

Whether increases actually stick will set the direction of operating margins, though reports also flag that recovery may take until Q4.

Balance sheet improvement and cash generation

The debt-to-equity ratio fell from 215.5% in 2022 to 103.5% in 2025. Operating cash flow recovered to KRW 50.7 billion in 2025 from KRW 13.6 billion in 2024. A thicker equity base gives room to absorb overseas investment and cost swings.

09

Bear factors

Profit decline and renewed losses

Operating profit peaked at KRW 47.3 billion in 2023 and then fell two years in a row. Cumulative operating profit for January to September 2026 fell 47.1%, and Q3 2026 operating profit dropped 89.7% to KRW 882 million, with a net loss attributable to controlling shareholders. Profit falling while sales rise is the core bear argument.

Exposure to exchange rates and oil prices

Q3 margins were directly hit by a weaker won-dollar rate and an oil price spike. With export sales above two-thirds of volume, currency swings carry significant weight, and delayed cost pass-through shrinks profit even at the same revenue level.

Thin earnings base and uncertain recovery timing

Profit attributable to controlling shareholders totals only KRW 5.56 billion over the last four quarters. After a Q4 2025 loss, Q3 2026 also posted a loss, so quarterly earnings are unstable.

Reports indicate profitability may take until Q4 to recover, and evidence of recovery is limited until price increases reach shipments.

Risk factors

Exchange rate and oil price swings

Currency and oil moves hit raw material costs, manufacturing costs and export profitability together. In Q3 2026, a weaker won-dollar rate and an oil spike overlapped and operating profit fell to KRW 882 million. With high export exposure, adverse currency moves can shrink profit even as revenue grows. This is an external variable the company cannot control.

Delayed price pass-through

Price increase talks with customers are under way without a confirmed outcome. Because increases reach shipments with a lag, cost increases hit profit first. Prolonged talks could keep margin pressure in place beyond Q4, and negotiating leverage matters more in concentrated customer segments.

Overseas expansion and investment burden

The second Vietnam plant and planned 2027 capacity additions could raise investment outlays. Total liabilities of KRW 340.4 billion at year-end 2025 are close in size to total equity of KRW 328.8 billion. The pace at which the Texas plant contributes to revenue and profit will show in upcoming periodic reports. If investments do not translate into earnings, financial strain could rise again.
