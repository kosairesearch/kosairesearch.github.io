Report overview

Long-term contracts anchor earnings between freight recovery and fading property gains

Korea Line's long-term charter base supports its cash flow, and 2026 bulk and tanker freight gains have partly offset the smaller revenue base left by 2025 fleet sales and the absence of property income.

02

Business structure

Korea Line is a shipping affiliate of the SM Group, centered on dedicated dry bulk charters and spot-market dry bulk operations, and it also runs tanker, LNG carrier and PCTC services.

The company's own separate-basis breakdown for the first quarter of 2026 shows dedicated charters at 58.21%, spot-market dry bulk at 41.75% and other items at 0.04%, with POSCO, Korea Electric Power Corporation, Hyundai Glovis and GS Donghae Power among its main customers.

Brokerage reports put the long-term dedicated share at about 75% of revenue, which differs from the company's own split, so the weight of dedicated charters should be read by source. Dedicated charters offer stable income with limited exposure to freight swings, and fuel costs can be passed through to freight.

Spot-market operations are directly exposed to freight cycles and benefit first when bulk and tanker rates rise. Third-quarter 2026 reports show dry bulk revenue of KRW 202.1 billion, up 16.55%, tanker revenue of KRW 14.0 billion, up 22.21%, and LNG revenue of KRW 102.1 billion, up 22.14%.

In September 2026 the company signed a PCTC long-term charter with Hyundai Glovis worth KRW 73.0 billion, running for 5 years and 4 months from November. Compared with peers such as HMM and Pan Ocean, its fleet and business scope are narrower, which limits how much it can scale with market swings.

The company also ran a property business, but with the apartment sales completed in 2025, no property profit was booked in 2026.

04

Earnings analysis

On a reported annual basis, revenue fell from KRW 1.61 trillion in 2022 to KRW 1.4 trillion in 2023, down 13.3%, then rose to KRW 1.75 trillion in 2024, up 25.0%, the largest year of 2022 to 2025. Revenue was smallest in 2025 at KRW 1.28 trillion, down 26.9%.

Operating profit peaked in 2024 at KRW 328.6 billion, up 31.5%, and was lowest in 2025 at KRW 207.2 billion, down 37.0%. Brokerage reports attributed the 2025 decline to a smaller fleet after VLCC sales and the end of a long-term transport contract.

The operating margin rose from 16.6% in 2022 to 18.8% in 2024 before easing to 16.2% in 2025. Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders bottomed at KRW 68.3 billion in 2023, down 56.7%, and then rose for two straight years, to KRW 162.1 billion in 2024 and KRW 180.2 billion in 2025.

Net income including non-controlling interests was KRW 173.3 billion in 2025, a different figure. The debt-to-equity ratio fell from 151.7% in 2022 to 70.2% at the end of 2025, when controlling shareholders' equity stood at KRW 2.16 trillion.

On a quarterly basis, revenue slipped from KRW 318 billion in the third quarter of 2025 to KRW 296.2 billion in the fourth quarter, then bottomed at KRW 277.8 billion in the first quarter of 2026 and rose for two straight quarters to KRW 312.8 billion and KRW 342.5 billion.

Operating profit peaked at KRW 74.4 billion in the first quarter of 2026 and fell for two straight quarters to KRW 62.9 billion and KRW 58.8 billion.

In the third quarter of 2026, dry bulk operating profit improved to KRW 25.6 billion, while LNG operating profit fell 13.46% to KRW 24.4 billion, as reported, with cost of sales rising faster than sales. The absence of property profit in 2026 also weighed on operating results. Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders over the last four quarters totaled KRW 176.3 billion.

05

Industry analysis

Dry bulk and tanker freight rates both rose in 2026. Sangsangin Securities said the BDI for the first quarter of 2026 was up 75% from a year earlier, with the BDTI up 124% and the BCTI up 53% amid the US-Iran conflict.

Third-quarter 2026 cumulative weighted average freight per ton was USD 14.27 for bulk, USD 31.40 for LNG and USD 15.98 for tankers, above the 2025 annual averages of USD 11.26, USD 30.08 and USD 14.66. Drought restricted Panama Canal transits, which pushed ships onto longer detours and tightened vessel supply.

Tanker operations also benefited from Middle East-related improvement in crude and product tanker markets. Korea Line's position rests on a high share of long-term dedicated charters, where stability is the main strength rather than capturing sharp rate spikes.

Sangsangin noted that dedicated charters can pass fuel cost increases through to customers and that dollar-denominated freight offers a currency benefit. Its spot-market share of around 40% leaves room to benefit from freight upside.

Direct comparison with large carriers such as HMM is limited by scale, and the key question for the sector is how quickly freight gains reach contract-based earnings.

06

Outlook

The company says it will secure stable income through long-term transport contracts while continuing to invest with a focus on profitability as market conditions change.

The PCTC charter with Hyundai Glovis, worth KRW 73.0 billion over 5 years and 4 months and starting in November, makes the timing and size of its earnings contribution the next point to track. Reports put long-term contracts at 37, with an average remaining term of 6 years and 6 months, as of January 2026.

Coverage describes the company as cautious about its 2026 outlook. Cumulative operating profit for the first three quarters of 2026 was KRW 196.2 billion, up 25.54% from KRW 156.3 billion a year earlier, according to quarterly report coverage.

Because no property profit was booked in 2026, earnings must now come from shipping alone. Hana Securities, in a March 2026 report, said a recovery in bulk demand could lift spot-market revenue, a view that dates from that month.

The next regular filing is the 2026 annual report, which will fix fourth-quarter 2026 results and full-year segment sales. The points to track from here are how much the PCTC charter adds, whether bulk and tanker rates hold after year-end, and the cost of sales trend in LNG.

Cash and equivalents stood at KRW 350 billion at the end of 2025, which a brokerage report highlighted as a large share of market capitalization. On earnings, the share price can be compared with net profit attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 176.3 billion over the last four quarters.

08

Bull factors

Cash generation from long-term contracts

Long-term dedicated charters limit freight swings and secure steadier income. Operating cash flow rose from KRW 392.3 billion in 2022 to KRW 567.2 billion in 2024 and was KRW 442.6 billion in 2025.

The debt-to-equity ratio fell to 70.2% by the end of 2025, and the contract-based model is seen as underpinning financial stability.

Freight gains showing up in results

Cumulative third-quarter 2026 bulk freight reached USD 14.27 per ton, above the 2025 annual average of USD 11.26. Tanker operating profit rose 164.41% to KRW 5.1 billion over the same period. Dry bulk operating profit also grew 25.79% to KRW 25.6 billion, showing market gains reaching spot-market earnings.

Lower interest burden and a stronger balance sheet

Hana Securities noted in a March 2026 report that annual interest expense fell sharply from KRW 160 billion in 2024 to KRW 97 billion in 2025. The same report put year-end 2025 cash and equivalents at KRW 350 billion. Proceeds from fleet sales went into debt reduction and cash build-up, lowering external funding pressure.

09

Bear factors

Smaller revenue base and no property income

Revenue fell 26.9% to KRW 1.28 trillion in 2025, with the VLCC sale and the end of a CVC contract cited as causes. No apartment sale profit was booked in 2026, and cumulative revenue for the first three quarters was down 4.85% from a year earlier. Even with better shipping profitability, the revenue base has recovered only partially.

Two straight quarters of softer profit after the peak

Operating profit peaked at KRW 74.4 billion in the first quarter of 2026 and then fell to KRW 62.9 billion and KRW 58.8 billion in the next two quarters. Third-quarter operating profit was down 0.9% from a year earlier.

Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 42.6 billion, down 2.1%, and LNG segment operating profit fell 13.46%.

Lag in market gains and share-price theme risk

Brokerages have pointed out that most revenue comes from long-term dedicated charters, so freight spikes cannot reach reported earnings immediately. Sangsangin Securities said the share price jumped in early 2026 on freight expectations and Arctic route theme buying. When prices move ahead of operating data, a reversal is a commonly cited risk.

10

Risk factors

Freight, currency and fuel cost swings

With spot-market exposure near 40%, a fall in bulk or tanker rates would quickly reduce earnings. Freight is collected in dollars, so a weaker dollar against the won reduces the won value of profit. Dedicated charters can pass on fuel costs, but spot operations cannot always do so fully.

Geopolitics and route disruptions

Part of the 2026 freight gains rests on the US-Iran conflict and detours caused by drought at the Panama Canal. If those factors ease, tanker and bulk rates could reverse. Quarterly results could change direction once the drivers of higher rates fade.

Customer concentration and limited shareholder returns

Main customers include POSCO, Korea Electric Power Corporation, Hyundai Glovis and GS Donghae Power, so earnings are sensitive to contract renewals and customer conditions. Brokerage reports noted that no cash dividends were paid from 2021 to 2024. With large vessel investment needs, the clarity of the shareholder return policy remains limited.

Overall view

Korea Line has a structure in which long-term dedicated charters underpin cash flow, and its debt-to-equity ratio fell to 70.2% by the end of 2025, a sign of a stronger balance sheet.

In 2025, fleet sales, the end of a long-term transport contract and the loss of property income combined to shrink revenue and operating profit sharply.

In 2026, higher bulk and tanker freight has lifted third-quarter revenue from a year earlier, but operating profit has fallen for two straight quarters, leaving the post-peak trend as the open question.

Strengths include contract stability, freight gains and better finances, while weaknesses include a smaller revenue base, softer LNG profitability and the lag before freight reaches reported earnings. Shareholder returns remain unproven, given no cash dividends were paid from 2021 to 2024.

The next points to check are the operating margin in the 2026 annual report, how much the PCTC charter adds, and how quickly freight gains convert into profit in the first-quarter 2027 report.
