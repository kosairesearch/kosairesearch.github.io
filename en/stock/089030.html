From Losses to Profit: HBM Test Gear Carries the Recovery

Techwing returned to a full-year 2025 net profit attributable to controlling shareholders, and its third-quarter 2026 revenue of KRW 87.8 billion and operating profit of KRW 18.1 billion show a recovery led by memory test equipment, with HBM cube probers emerging as a new growth axis.

02

Business structure

Techwing makes back-end test equipment for semiconductors, including test handlers that move chips through testing and sort good and defective units.

Its core product is the memory test handler, supplemented by wafer test equipment and HBM test equipment, and its customers include SK hynix, Samsung Electronics and Micron.

A 2023 company analysis report put memory handlers at 31% of 2022 revenue and described the company as holding the global number one share in that segment, with Kioxia and Western Digital also listed among major customers.

In the first half of 2026, memory test equipment made up 47.4% of revenue, up from 31.3% for full-year 2025. Test equipment parts and peripherals accounted for another 44.9%, so hardware and components make up most of the revenue base.

The product line also includes non-memory handlers, module and SSD handlers, C.O.K replacement kits and interface boards.

The cube prober, which tests HBM stacks one unit at a time, is the main new growth candidate, offering vision precision of 5 micrometers or better and skipping some transfer steps to raise process efficiency. Its display inspection subsidiary served Samsung Display as a key customer, according to the same 2023 report.

Because customer investment levels drive memory handler demand, the business depends heavily on a small customer base.

04

Earnings analysis

Based on DART periodic reports, revenue went from KRW 267.5 billion in 2022 to KRW 133.6 billion in 2023, down 50.1%, then rose to KRW 185.5 billion in 2024, up 38.8%, and fell to KRW 159.1 billion in 2025, down 14.2%. 2022 was the largest year and 2023 the smallest among 2022 to 2025.

The 2023 decline came as a semiconductor downturn led customers to cut investment, and revenue and profit fell together. Operating profit dropped from KRW 57.7 billion in 2022 to KRW 3.16 billion in 2023, down 94.5%, recovered to KRW 23.4 billion in 2024, up 640.7%, and reached KRW 15.8 billion in 2025, down 32.4%.

Operating margin was 21.6% in 2022, 2.4% in 2023, 12.6% in 2024 and 10.0% in 2025, with the steepest margin drop coming in the year revenue fell most.

Profit attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 32.6 billion in 2022, turned into a net loss of KRW 9.31 billion in 2023, widened to a net loss of KRW 20.9 billion in 2024, and returned to a profit of KRW 9.4 billion in 2025. Net income including non-controlling interests was KRW 9 billion in 2025.

Operating cash flow was KRW 68.2 billion in 2022, KRW 20.4 billion in 2023, KRW 32.7 billion in 2024 and -KRW 16.1 billion in 2025.

On a quarterly basis, operating profit fell from KRW 7.11 billion in the third quarter of 2025 to KRW 582 million in the fourth quarter, then rose to KRW 9.7 billion, KRW 12.5 billion and KRW 18.1 billion in the first three quarters of 2026.

Profit attributable to controlling shareholders turned to a profit of KRW 5.19 billion in the second quarter of 2026 and rose to KRW 51.1 billion in the third quarter, well above that quarter's operating profit, so non-operating items and taxes may have played a large role and the breakdown should be checked in the periodic report notes.

The last four quarters together total KRW 45.6 billion of profit attributable to controlling shareholders. The debt ratio rose from 98.8% in 2022 to 198.7% in 2025, when total equity was KRW 212.1 billion and total liabilities were KRW 421.6 billion.

05

Industry analysis

Memory test equipment demand moves with the DRAM and NAND production cut and capacity expansion cycle.

A 2023 company analysis report said memory handler demand weakened under production cuts and capex limits, but that back-end memory tester demand would rise alongside capacity investment after DRAM production cuts ended. In 2026, HBM demand structure is shifting.

Micron has reportedly moved toward customer-tailored HBM from the HBM4E generation, and analysts have argued that as custom HBM grows, the role of cube probers that inspect stacks unit by unit will expand.

In competition, Japan's Advantest is cited as a leading supplier of HBM-related handlers, and a domestic peer was also reported in 2023 to have launched a product aimed at the same market.

Techwing claimed in 2023 reporting that its HBM handler reached up to 256 parallel units and that its memory handler technology could handle up to 512. The cube prober customer base has broadened to include SK hynix and Samsung Electronics.

Samsung Electronics placed its first volume order on January 20, 2025, followed by an additional order on March 10, reportedly larger than the initial one. Customer-specific customization in HBM equipment can lengthen qualification, so delivery timing can slip with customer validation schedules.

Because memory handler demand is tied directly to the investment cycles of its three main customers, the direction of their capex shapes quarterly revenue.

Outlook

The growth axis now visible is the HBM cube prober. Techwing said on March 5, 2026 that SK hynix had completed the final qualification test for the cube prober, and the company said it expects full-scale equipment deliveries to follow.

How quickly qualification turns into revenue depends on customer mass-production schedules. The public quarterly figures do not separate how much of the KRW 87.8 billion third-quarter 2026 revenue came from cube probers.

Timing risk is also visible: at the time of its third-quarter 2025 results, Hyundai Motor Securities analyst Park Jun-young said cube prober revenue recognition for SK hynix was delayed, falling short of market expectations.

A 2023 report said a commercial HBM test handler model would launch as early as March 2024, with customer-line entry possible in the second half of 2024 after customer qualification. Whether that product has since reached commercial use is a point to check in the business section of the periodic reports.

Additional orders following Samsung Electronics' first volume order have widened the cube prober customer base to two companies.

The next checkpoint is the fourth-quarter and full-year 2026 preliminary results, typically expected in early to mid-February 2027, to see whether the third-quarter operating profit level of KRW 18.1 billion is sustained.

The 2026 annual report, due by March 31, 2027, will show the customer revenue mix and cube prober business details. The first-quarter 2027 quarterly report, due by May 15, 2027, will show whether operating margin stays above 20% of revenue, as in the second and third quarters of 2026.

Risk factors

Major customers are a small group of memory makers, led by SK hynix, Samsung Electronics and Micron. If their capex narrows, memory handler orders and revenue recognition can move together. Revenue fell 50.1% in 2023 during the last downturn, which shows the scale of this risk.

Revenue recognition timing

Cube prober revenue can shift between quarters depending on customer mass-production schedules. In the third quarter of 2025, SK hynix-related revenue recognition was delayed, and results fell short of market expectations. Because quarterly revenue swings widely, quarterly results are sensitive to the schedule of a single customer.

Balance sheet and non-operating items
