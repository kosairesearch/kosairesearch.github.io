KolmarBNH: Profit recovery amid softer sales

KolmarBNH kept quarterly operating profit near KRW 10 billion through the first three quarters of 2026, yet revenue has declined for two straight quarters since the first-quarter peak. Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders was positive in each 2026 quarter, but the full-year 2025 result was a net loss.

02

Business structure

KolmarBNH is an original development manufacturer (ODM) focused on health functional foods (HFF) and cosmetics. A JobKorea company profile compiled from company filings shows the 2025 sales mix at 63% health functional foods, 33% cosmetics, 2% toothpaste and hand sanitizers, and 2% rental and other.

A cosmetics trade outlet's analysis of the 2024 annual report shows the food segment with sales of KRW 331.8 billion (54% share) and operating profit of KRW 12.4 billion, down 30% from the prior year, while the cosmetics segment posted sales of KRW 278.7 billion (45%) and operating profit of KRW 9.6 billion, down 12%.

Customers are split between global clients and key domestic clients, and the company cites supply experience in 26 countries and its ingredient, formulation and packaging technologies as strengths. Domestic distribution has expanded into Olive Young and warehouse-style pharmacies.

Production is improving as existing capacity, including the Sejong 3 plant, runs at higher utilization and lowers fixed-cost burden.

Restructuring, including the transfer of the cosmetics business and the exit of non-core operations, is under way, and for that reason 2026 results have been announced on a separate basis rather than a consolidated one.

In competition, ingredient and formulation capability and production scale determine how ODM suppliers win clients.

04

Earnings analysis

On audited annual filings, KolmarBNH's revenue rose from KRW 575.9 billion in 2022 to KRW 579.6 billion in 2023 (up 0.6% year on year) and KRW 615.6 billion in 2024 (up 6.2%), then fell to KRW 574.9 billion in 2025 (down 6.6%). Among 2022 to 2025, 2024 was the largest year and 2025 the smallest.

Operating profit fell from KRW 61.1 billion in 2022 to KRW 30.3 billion in 2023 (down 50.5%) and KRW 24.6 billion in 2024 (down 18.6%), then recovered to KRW 26.6 billion in 2025 (up 8.2%). The operating margin went from 10.6% to 5.2%, 4.0% and 4.6%, staying in the 4% to 5% range since 2023.

Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders fell from KRW 40.5 billion in 2022 to KRW 19.7 billion in 2023 (down 51.3%) and KRW 18.1 billion in 2024 (down 8.4%), before turning to a net loss of KRW 22.7 billion in 2025.

Net profit including non-controlling interests moved in the same direction, to a net loss of KRW 22.6 billion in 2025. Operating profit rose in 2025 while the bottom line went negative, which means losses arose below the operating line.

The debt ratio rose from 53.2% in 2022 to 82.8% in 2025, and at year-end 2025 total equity was KRW 355.5 billion, of which KRW 353.8 billion was attributable to controlling shareholders, against total liabilities of KRW 294.2 billion.

Operating cash flow went from KRW 5.33 billion in 2022 to KRW 56.3 billion in 2023, KRW 39.2 billion in 2024 and KRW 37.9 billion in 2025, staying above KRW 35 billion each year since 2023.

Quarterly, the fourth quarter of 2025 was the low point, with revenue of KRW 122.2 billion (down 19.5% from the prior quarter), operating profit of KRW 2.65 billion (down 72.7%) and a net loss attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 37 billion, which turned from profit to loss.

The first quarter of 2026 brought revenue of KRW 136.9 billion (up 12.0%), operating profit of KRW 10.3 billion (up 290.0%) and net profit attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 22.3 billion, a swing back to profit.

The company said that first-quarter net profit included a gain on disposal of a Kolmask-related subsidiary, so it should be read apart from operating trends.

In the second quarter, revenue was KRW 127.2 billion (down 7.1% from the prior quarter), operating profit KRW 10.2 billion (down 1.7%) and net profit attributable to controlling shareholders KRW 6.93 billion (down 68.9%).

In the third quarter, revenue was KRW 118.3 billion (down 7.0% from the prior quarter and down 22.1% from a year earlier), operating profit KRW 10.4 billion (up 2.6% quarter on quarter and up 7.3% year on year) and net profit attributable to controlling shareholders KRW 6.36 billion (up 3.0% year on year).

Over the last four quarters, net losses attributable to controlling shareholders total KRW 1.38 billion.

Separately, in preliminary separate-basis results announced on October 29, 2026, nine-month net profit of KRW 44.2 billion (up 305.3%) reflected KRW 27.0 billion in HNG dividend income recorded as non-operating income, according to the company. These preliminary figures precede the audited periodic report.

05

Industry analysis

Demand for health functional foods in Korea is supported by an aging population and rising interest in health and beauty-related consumption, which tends to direct orders toward ODM suppliers with ingredient and formulation capability.

The company's chief executive said orders from key clients keep growing as K-health functional foods gain standing in global markets. In the nine-month separate-basis preliminary results, growth in higher-margin overseas sales was cited as a main driver of operating profit. Regional exposure, however, varies widely.

Sales in China fell 35% year on year to KRW 33.9 billion in the first half of 2025, and US sales dropped 74% from the prior quarter in the fourth quarter of 2024, according to a trade outlet's analysis. That points to the need to diversify overseas clients so the business does not depend on a single region.

Cosmetics ODM faces continued weakness in China and overseas channels, while health functional foods are widening their base through domestic channels such as Olive Young.

Unlike Hanmi Pharm or Yuhan, which are centered on drug development and sales, KolmarBNH has a business structure weighted toward contract manufacturing.

In terms of cycle position, the business appears to be in a phase where newly added capacity is lifting utilization, though market-level figures that quantify this are limited in the disclosed data.

Whether production efficiency and client diversification advance together will likely determine its standing within the industry.

06

Outlook

In its half-year 2026 release, the company said it aims to sustain production efficiency and profitability in the second half. In its first-quarter release, it said it would keep expanding volume with existing clients and pursue new large overseas accounts.

The chief executive outlined a medium- to long-term plan to strengthen ingredient and formulation competitiveness on the health functional food ODM base and to deepen natural-materials research.

On the production side, stabilizing utilization at existing expanded capacity, including the Sejong 3 plant, has been cited as the basis for lower cost structure.

On restructuring, the transfer of the cosmetics business and the exit of non-core and underperforming operations changed how 2026 results are reported, with the company now disclosing results on a separate basis.

That makes quarterly consolidated results and separate-basis preliminary figures difficult to compare directly. The separate-basis nine-month result showed growth in overseas ODM sales lifting operating profit, which suggests that overseas orders shape the direction of results.

However, revenue has declined since the second quarter, leaving a gap between the second-half growth plan and the third-quarter sales trend. The 2026 annual report will show whether annual revenue and operating margin hold, which is the first formal test of the medium-term plan. The next quarters will show whether new overseas accounts grow enough to offset the decline in sales.

Bull factors

Operating profit held near KRW 10 billion a quarter

Operating profit was KRW 10.3 billion in the first quarter of 2026, KRW 10.2 billion in the second and KRW 10.4 billion in the third, holding in the KRW 10 billion range each quarter. The company attributed the improvement to higher utilization at expanded plants, including Sejong 3, and lower fixed-cost burden.

Preliminary separate-basis nine-month operating profit of KRW 31.68 billion, up 32.6%, was announced on October 29, 2026.

Expansion of overseas clients

The company cites expanding product lines at global clients and new overseas accounts as drivers of revenue. In the nine-month separate-basis results, growth in overseas ODM sales was said to strengthen a higher-margin profile.

The company says its supply experience in 26 countries and its ingredient, formulation and packaging technologies support clients entering overseas markets.

Effect of business restructuring

Transferring the cosmetics business and exiting non-core and underperforming operations is meant to focus the company on its health functional food ODM core. Reporting on a separate basis is the company's way of showing core performance on its own. Cosmetics operating profit fell 12% in 2024, so how the exits show up in reported results is the key question.

Bear factors

Consecutive revenue declines

Revenue fell to KRW 127.2 billion in the second quarter of 2026 (down 7.1% from the prior quarter) and to KRW 118.3 billion in the third (down 7.0%). Third-quarter revenue was down 22.1% from a year earlier, a sizable decline.

Operating profit held up despite the smaller top line thanks to cost improvements, but continued shrinkage could limit how far fixed costs are spread.

Lingering net losses

A net loss attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 22.7 billion in 2025 turned profit into loss, and the fourth quarter of 2025 alone recorded a KRW 37 billion loss. The last four quarters together still total a KRW 1.38 billion loss.

The company said the first-quarter 2026 swing to profit reflected a gain on disposal of a Kolmask-related subsidiary. Separating recurring operating earnings from one-time gains remains the open question.

Leverage and uneven overseas sales

The debt ratio rose from 53.2% in 2022 to 82.8% in 2025, with total liabilities of KRW 294.2 billion at year-end 2025. Operating cash flow stayed above KRW 35 billion each year from 2023.

Overseas regional sales are also uneven, with China sales down 35% in the first half of 2025 to KRW 33.9 billion and US sales down 74% from the prior quarter in the fourth quarter of 2024.

Risk factors

Execution of restructuring

The transfer of the cosmetics business and the exit of non-core and underperforming operations are running at the same time, changing the business and consolidation perimeter. The gap between consolidated and separate-basis results widens in the process, making quarterly comparisons harder. Restructuring costs or asset disposal gains would require additional adjustment when reading operating trends.

Client and regional concentration

Revenue growth leans heavily on orders from global clients. Sales in China and the United States have dropped sharply in the past, so a drop in orders from a single client or region can move quarterly revenue quickly. Whether new overseas accounts offset declines at existing clients determines the path to a revenue recovery.

Leverage and earnings volatility

The debt ratio rose to 82.8% in 2025, increasing financial burden. Net profit has swung with subsidiary disposal gains and non-operating items, and in 2025 operating profit rose while the bottom line posted a loss.

Even with operating profit holding near KRW 10 billion a quarter, the financial structure may remain under pressure until net profit stabilizes.

Overall view

KolmarBNH's operating profit held near KRW 10 billion a quarter through the first three quarters of 2026, showing a recovery in core profitability. Revenue, however, has declined for two straight quarters since the first-quarter peak, and third-quarter sales were down 22.1% from a year earlier.

Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders was positive in each 2026 quarter, but the 2025 full-year net loss and a KRW 1.38 billion combined loss over the last four quarters remain on record.

First-quarter 2026 net profit included a subsidiary disposal gain, so it should be read apart from core operating trends. The debt ratio rose from 53.2% in 2022 to 82.8% in 2025.

On the business side, the cosmetics transfer and non-core exits are under way, and overseas client growth has been cited as lifting operating profit. Strengths include cost improvements and overseas order growth, while weaknesses include falling revenue, unstable net profit and higher leverage.

The next checkpoints are annual revenue and operating margin in the 2026 annual report and first-quarter 2027 results.
