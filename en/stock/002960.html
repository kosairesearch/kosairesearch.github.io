02

Business structure

Hankook Shell OIL was founded in 1960 to make and sell lubricants and greases, formed a joint venture with Shell in 1969, and took its current name in 1987. Production is centered at its Busan Yongdang plant, which makes passenger car, commercial vehicle, industrial and marine lubricants and greases.

In the first-half 2025 semi-annual report, lubricant products made up about 80% of sales, greases about 4.5% and products bought for resale about 15%. About 58% of base oil purchases are domestic, sourced from suppliers including S-Oil and SK, and the remaining 42% is imported from Japan, Germany and Singapore.

About 82% of lubricant additives are imported. SK Innovation, S-Oil, Michang OIL IND and Kukdong OIL & Chemicals are named as domestic competitors. The filing describes the domestic lubricant market as oversupplied, with many manufacturers.

The company points to Shell brand image, service differentiation and new customer wins as its key competitive factors. Its premium push includes synthetic oils built on GTL base oil and the Midel electrical insulating fluid for new markets.

Wisereport's company monitor also lists a biodegradable lubricant acquisition and development work on data center cooling fluids as recent activity.

04

Earnings analysis

On the DART annual figures, sales rose from KRW 301.6 billion in 2022 to KRW 320.5 billion in 2023 (up 6.2% year on year), KRW 327.2 billion in 2024 (up 2.1%) and KRW 345 billion in 2025 (up 5.4%), increasing for three straight years from 2023 to 2025.

Operating profit rose from KRW 35.1 billion in 2022 to KRW 42.3 billion in 2023 (up 20.6%), KRW 46 billion in 2024 (up 8.6%) and KRW 52.8 billion in 2025 (up 14.9%). The operating margin climbed every year, from 11.6% in 2022 to 13.2%, 14.0% and 15.3% in 2025.

Profit attributable to controlling shareholders went from KRW 37.4 billion in 2023 (up 39.9%) to KRW 36.7 billion in 2024 (down 1.9%) before recovering to KRW 48.2 billion in 2025 (up 31.6%).

The company attributed the 2025 gain to continued sales growth and higher profit from premium lubricant sales, and also cited higher non-operating income from an adjustment to soil contamination restoration provisions.

Operating cash flow went from KRW 15.4 billion in 2022 to KRW 51.3 billion in 2025, after dipping to KRW 33.6 billion in 2024 from KRW 43.3 billion in 2023. The debt ratio fell from 52.6% in 2022 to 42.7% in 2025, with total equity of KRW 140.4 billion and total liabilities of KRW 59.9 billion at year-end 2025.

Quarterly operating profit fell from KRW 14.3 billion in the third quarter of 2025 to KRW 9.53 billion in the fourth quarter (down 33.4% on the prior quarter), then rebounded to KRW 16 billion in the first quarter of 2026 (up 67.9%).

It then declined to KRW 15.6 billion in the second quarter and KRW 13.2 billion in the third, while third-quarter sales of KRW 94.3 billion rose 9.6% year on year and operating profit fell 7.7%.

Profit attributable to controlling shareholders peaked at KRW 14 billion in the first quarter of 2026, then slipped to KRW 12.8 billion and KRW 9.55 billion, down 22.5% year on year in the third quarter. Over the last four quarters that total is KRW 48.5 billion.

Third-party monitor Wisereport put first-quarter 2026 separate-basis operating profit up 10.3% year on year, which is a different basis from the DART consolidated figures above.

05

Industry analysis

The domestic lubricant market is oversupplied, with many manufacturers competing for the same customers, according to the company's own business report. Domestic lubricant volumes moved from 1,095 million liters in 2022 to 1,098 million in 2023 (up 0.27%) and 1,103 million in 2024 (up 0.43%), a nearly flat trend.

Demand tracks automotive, marine and industrial activity, so manufacturing conditions feed into volumes. On the cost side, about 58% of base oil is bought domestically from refiners such as S-Oil and SK, so refiners' supply terms and prices affect the company indirectly. Refining margins are strong at the global level.

Shell plc reported an indicative refining margin of $42 per barrel for the third quarter of 2026, and CNBC described that level as a record. This is a refining indicator and does not map directly onto Hankook Shell OIL's lubricant earnings.

On brand, Shell says that, citing Kline & Company research, it has been the world's top lubricant seller by volume for 20 consecutive years. Domestically, SK Innovation and S-Oil compete alongside Michang OIL IND and Kukdong OIL & Chemicals, so premium mix and service quality are the main points of differentiation.

06

Outlook

The company's direction centers on expanding premium lubricants, higher-value products and eco-friendly lines. Major equipment upgrades at the Busan Yongdang plant have been completed, which the company says improves production efficiency and capacity.

The business report had scheduled that project to run from the first quarter of 2024 to the first quarter of 2025. Adoption of the Midel electrical insulating fluid is under way for new markets, and energy-saving lubricants are under review.

Wisereport's company monitor says the company is responding to ESG demand with a biodegradable lubricant acquisition and is developing data center cooling fluids and lubrication technology for electric vehicles.

After profit eased in the second and third quarters of 2026 from the first-quarter peak, the key question is how premium mix shows up in quarterly operating profit. Given that non-operating income helped the 2025 gain, the next annual report's split between operating and non-operating items will matter.

No disclosed revenue figures for the new businesses have been identified, so they can only be described in qualitative terms. Refiner margins and Shell group earnings could affect the company's raw material terms indirectly.

Bull factors

Margin improvement

The operating margin rose from 11.6% in 2022 to 15.3% in 2025, and the company credited premium lubricant sales for the 2025 profit gain. Operating profit has increased for three straight years from 2023 to 2025. A richer premium mix is the main explanation the company gives for the margin gains.

Financial capacity

Total equity at year-end 2025 was KRW 140.4 billion against total liabilities of KRW 59.9 billion. The debt ratio fell from 52.6% in 2022 to 42.7% in 2025. Operating cash flow reached KRW 51.3 billion in 2025, above profit attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 48.2 billion.

Brand and new business lines

Shell says, citing Kline & Company research, that it has been the top lubricant seller by volume for 20 consecutive years. The company is responding to eco-friendly and new-market demand through the Midel electrical insulating fluid, a biodegradable lubricant acquisition and data center cooling fluid development. Broader technology offerings are cited as a basis for long-term competitiveness.

Bear factors

Quarterly profit slowdown

First-quarter 2026 operating profit of KRW 16 billion was the peak, followed by KRW 15.6 billion and KRW 13.2 billion, two straight quarterly declines. Third-quarter sales rose 9.6% year on year while operating profit fell 7.7%.

Profit attributable to controlling shareholders fell to KRW 9.55 billion, down 22.5% year on year in the third quarter.

Oversupply and flat domestic volumes

The business report describes the domestic lubricant market as oversupplied, with many manufacturers. Domestic volume growth was only 0.27% in 2023 and 0.43% in 2024. In a market with many competitors, defending prices and customers can squeeze profit.

Raw material and import dependence

About 58% of base oil is bought domestically and the rest is imported, while about 82% of additives are imported. Adverse moves in raw material prices or exchange rates could pressure margins. Part of the 2025 profit gain came from non-operating income, which calls for a closer look at earnings quality.

10

Risk factors

Raw materials and FX

High reliance on imported additives and refiner-supplied base oil means raw material prices and exchange rates can move costs quickly. Changes in refiner supply terms could also disrupt procurement. How much cost increases can be passed on to selling prices will determine margin outcomes.

Domestic demand and competition

Domestic volumes have grown only around 0.3% to 0.4% a year since 2022. With many lubricant makers, price competition is intense. A slowdown in automotive, marine or industrial demand could pressure both volumes and prices.

Management and governance changes

The chief executive's term end date listed in the 2025 semi-annual report is November 22, 2026. The largest shareholder is a Shell group entity, so group strategy and personnel decisions can shape management.

Whether any executive change or group role adjustment alters the business direction needs to be checked in disclosures.

Overall view

Hankook Shell OIL posted its highest sales, operating profit and profit attributable to controlling shareholders of the 2022 to 2025 period in 2025, and its operating margin rose from 11.6% to 15.3%. The debt ratio fell to 42.7% and operating cash flow increased.

However, quarterly operating profit and profit attributable to controlling shareholders have fallen for two straight quarters since the first-quarter 2026 peak, and third-quarter profit declined even as sales rose. The domestic lubricant market is oversupplied and domestic volume growth has stayed near 0.3% a year.

A large share of base oil and additives is imported, so procurement terms and exchange rates matter. Part of the 2025 profit gain came from non-operating items, so earnings quality deserves attention.

On valuation, market capitalization is several times total equity and the dividend yield sits well above the KOSPI average.

The next checkpoints are the reasons behind the quarterly declines and the split of non-operating items in the 2026 annual report, along with disclosures around the chief executive term date of November 22, 2026.
