Property-Led Recovery, but Q3 2026 Turned to a Loss

02

Business structure

K-Top REITs was founded in 2010 and listed on KOSPI in 2012 as a self-managed real estate investment company. It invests in buildings across core commercial districts in Seoul, the metropolitan area and Busan, earning rental income, and the company has described its holdings as eight buildings.

Key assets mentioned in 2025 press coverage include the Judis Taehwa Building in Busan, the Seocho Building in Seoul, and part of the LG Palace Building connected to Hongik Univ. Station on Seoul Subway Line 2.

Coverage in 2025 said the Judis Taehwa Building's vacancy rate, 47.85% at the end of 2022, was brought to near zero after leasing to fashion, beauty and food-and-beverage tenants. Fair value of the Seocho Building was reported at KRW 32.7 billion at the end of 2024, above its purchase price of KRW 17.1 billion.

The Songpa office building acquired in 2021 was described as leased to the AJ Group on a whole-building basis. The company has used leverage to buy buildings and earns the spread between rental income and interest expense. Under the REIT act it must distribute most of its profit as cash dividends.

Revenue includes both rental income and fair-value gains on investment property. A REIT industry source described it as a small REIT with a sound asset base rather than a large one.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual revenue rose from KRW 21.6 billion in 2022 to KRW 26.9 billion in 2023, the largest year in the period, up 24.3% year on year. It then fell to KRW 12.1 billion in 2024, the smallest year, down 55.1%, before recovering to KRW 15.1 billion in 2025, up 24.8%.

Operating profit went from KRW 20.5 billion in 2023 to KRW 7.92 billion in 2024, down 61.4%, and then to KRW 10.6 billion in 2025, up 34.2%. Net income attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 6.22 billion in 2025, up 20.2% from KRW 5.17 billion in 2024, but still below KRW 11.6 billion in 2023.

Net income including non-controlling interests was KRW 6.51 billion in 2025, a different figure from controlling-shareholder net income. The operating margin fell from 81.1% in 2022 to 76.3% in 2023 and 65.5% in 2024, then partly recovered to 70.4% in 2025.

The company attributed the main driver of the 2025 revenue increase to fair-value gains on investment property, which it said are unrealized and not distributable. Operating cash flow moved from an outflow of KRW 6.56 billion in 2024 to an inflow of KRW 2.03 billion in 2025.

On a quarterly basis, Q4 2025 revenue was KRW 8.81 billion and operating profit KRW 7.76 billion, a sharp rise, after which revenue held at KRW 2.31 billion, KRW 2.36 billion and KRW 2.33 billion in Q1 to Q3 2026.

Q3 2026 operating profit was KRW 1.4 billion, up 31.6% year on year, yet the company posted a controlling-shareholder net loss of KRW 362 million.

Controlling-shareholder net income for the last four quarters totaled KRW 7.13 billion, and the fair-value share of the Q4 2025 jump cannot be separated from quarterly figures alone.

05

Industry analysis

A June 2026 press report said liquidity concerns at some REITs had increased unease across the listed REIT market. According to the same report, the average occupancy of 13 listed REITs tracked by the Korea REIT Association was 97.6% excluding hotel sectors, and dividend yields stayed in a 6% to 9% range.

The report said the financial soundness of major listed REITs had not deteriorated as much as market concern suggested. A December 2025 report cited industry expectations for a gradual recovery in the long-depressed REIT market from 2026.

K-Top REITs is among the smaller listed REITs and is typically compared with large REITs tied to conglomerates. A 2025 report noted that the company's portfolio is centered on physical properties with no exposure to project financing development.

The same 2025 coverage quoted a securities analyst who attributed the low share price, despite no obvious adverse factor, to limited investor attention. The analyst also noted that investors tend to approach mainly the conglomerate-affiliated REITs.

08

Bull factors

Rising value of physical assets

A 2025 press report said the Judis Taehwa Building's fair value was KRW 23.3 billion at the end of 2024, up 63% from its KRW 14.3 billion purchase price. The Seocho Building's fair value was KRW 32.7 billion at the end of 2024, above its KRW 17.1 billion purchase price.

The Judis Taehwa Building's vacancy fell from 47.85% at the end of 2022 to near zero. Higher asset values leave room for gains on sale.

Operating profit recovery since 2025

Operating profit reached KRW 10.6 billion in 2025, up 34.2% year on year. The operating margin rose from 65.5% in 2024 to 70.4% in 2025. Q3 2026 operating profit was KRW 1.4 billion, up 31.6% year on year. The fact that the annual recovery is visible in quarterly operating profit is a supporting factor.

Shareholder changes and funding capacity

A DART disclosure as of June 2026 showed Kim Cheol as the largest shareholder with 13.17%. In September 2026 a real estate company disclosed on-market purchases worth about KRW 400 million.

A 2025 report cited cash of KRW 23.5 billion at the end of Q1 2025, and the company pointed to this as capacity for new property purchases.

09

Bear factors

2024 earnings slump and cash outflow

Revenue in 2024 fell 55.1% year on year to KRW 12.1 billion, and operating profit dropped 61.4% to KRW 7.92 billion. Operating cash flow turned to an outflow of KRW 6.56 billion that year. The 2025 recovery to KRW 15.1 billion in revenue and an operating cash inflow of KRW 2.03 billion came after that drop.

Reliance on fair-value gains and margin decline

The company said fair-value gains on investment property, the main driver of 2025 revenue growth, are unrealized and not distributable. The operating margin fell from 81.1% in 2022 to 70.4% in 2025. The structure leaves earnings quality dependent on the share of these gains.

Q3 2026 loss and debt burden

Operating profit rose to KRW 1.4 billion in Q3 2026, but the controlling-shareholder result swung to a net loss of KRW 362 million. The debt ratio climbed from 98.1% in 2024 to 103.2% in 2025. Total liabilities of KRW 109.6 billion at the end of 2025 exceeded total equity of KRW 106.2 billion.

Risk factors

New business execution and licensing

A 2025 press report said the company planned to directly buy mortgage-backed NPLs and expand into financial-institution loans, while proceeding with registration as a loan purchase and collection business.

Whether the registration is completed, when the business starts, and what recoveries it yields are not yet determined. The company said it could still earn distributions as a creditor if auction sales fail, but execution will show up in future regular reports.

Funding and interest rates

Two loan extensions totaling KRW 10.6 billion and KRW 12.0 billion for facility funds were disclosed in August 2026. Both fall due in August 2027, so refinancing terms, including interest rates and collateral, could change. The KRW 10.6 billion loan is secured by a mortgage on the Shinwon Building.

Tenant concentration and shareholder structure

The Songpa building was described as leased to the AJ Group on a whole-building basis, which lowers vacancy risk but ties income to one tenant's renewals and finances. AJ Networks appears as a shareholder with 8.25%. Given the June 2026 largest-shareholder change disclosure, the governance structure could also shift.

Overall view

K-Top REITs is a small self-managed REIT centered on physical buildings. Its 2025 revenue of KRW 15.1 billion and operating profit of KRW 10.6 billion reversed the 2024 decline.

However, the main driver of 2025 revenue growth was fair-value gains on investment property, which the company said are unrealized and not distributable.

Quarterly revenue held at KRW 2.3 billion levels through Q1 to Q3 2026, and Q3 2026 swung to a controlling-shareholder net loss of KRW 362 million despite operating profit rising to KRW 1.4 billion.

The debt ratio rose to 103.2% at the end of 2025 from 98.1% in 2024, with total liabilities of KRW 109.6 billion exceeding total equity of KRW 106.2 billion. Supporting factors include lower vacancy and higher fair value at the Judis Taehwa Building and the operating profit recovery since 2025.

Offsetting factors include reliance on fair-value gains, the Q3 2026 loss, and the debt level. On funding, the August 2027 repayment dates are the next checkpoint, and on business, execution of the new NPL business is the next point to watch.

The 2026 annual business report due by late March 2027 is the nearest point at which full-year figures will be finalized.
