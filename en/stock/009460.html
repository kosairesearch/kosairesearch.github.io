Hanchang Paper: Quarterly Profit Recovery Under Way

02

Business structure

Hanchang Paper, founded in 1973, is a specialist white board maker that produces white board and manila board for tobacco cartons, premium packaging paper and food packaging. Most sales come from white board, with domestic and export shares of roughly 70% and 30%, according to a March 2026 press report.

Its core is premium white board, used in packaging for cosmetics, pharmaceuticals and electronics. The company says it is the only domestic operator with dedicated premium white board equipment, while general white board is made on separate lines. Cosmetics-related sales account for about 10% of total sales.

Press reports put its share of the domestic premium white board market at around 50%. The company has supplied packaging materials for smartphones, tablets and smartwatches to a leading domestic electronics maker for about 13 years.

On the price side, higher selling prices for general white board and expanded sales are cited as 2026 profit drivers. Most pulp is imported, so results are exposed to exchange rates, global pulp prices and freight costs.

Competition is shaped by strict quality standards and the difficulty of substituting premium board, which makes dedicated equipment and quality control a barrier to entry. In new business, the company is pursuing carrier tape base paper for MLCC production, and its CEO put the domestic market at about KRW 100 billion. Prototypes are being made on in-house equipment while quality improvements and external testing continue.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual sales rose from KRW 214.9 billion in 2022 to KRW 276 billion in 2023, up 28.4% year on year, and reached KRW 294.3 billion in 2024, up 6.6%, making 2024 the largest year of 2022 to 2025. Sales fell 9.3% to KRW 267 billion in 2025, and 2022 was the smallest year in the period.

Operating profit moved from KRW 1.29 billion in 2022 to an operating loss of KRW 11.5 billion in 2023, back to profit of KRW 1.14 billion in 2024, and then to an operating loss of KRW 5.19 billion in 2025.

Operating margin was 0.6% in 2022, -4.2% in 2023, 0.4% in 2024 and -1.9% in 2025, so the margin stayed thin even in the largest sales year.

Controlling-shareholder net income was KRW 1.26 billion in 2022, a net loss of KRW 17.5 billion in 2023, KRW 2.64 billion in 2024 and a net loss of KRW 13.1 billion in 2025, and net income including non-controlling interests followed the same path.

In 2025 the cost of sales ratio rose 2.5 percentage points to 92.9%, and the net loss was widened by an impairment loss on property, plant and equipment. Management cited weaker export prices, demand softness after the martial law episode and quality issues in board products.

Debt-to-equity reached 246.6% at end-2025, up from 204.7% a year earlier, while operating cash flow stayed positive at KRW 5.41 billion in 2025.

Quarterly results show an operating loss of KRW 386 million in 2025 Q3 and KRW 1.34 billion in Q4, followed by operating profit of KRW 1.69 billion in 2026 Q1 as domestic volumes recovered, selling prices rose and cost savings from prior capex flowed through.

Operating profit then reached KRW 3.96 billion in Q2, up 134.7% from the prior quarter, before dropping to KRW 956 million in Q3, down 75.9% quarter on quarter, while Q3 sales of KRW 69.6 billion rose 1.3% year on year.

Controlling-shareholder net profit was KRW 677 million in Q1, KRW 941 million in Q2 and KRW 1.06 billion in Q3, rising in both Q2 and Q3, and the last four quarters total a controlling-shareholder net loss of KRW 2.55 billion.

05

Industry analysis

Domestic paper makers started 2026 on a weak footing. According to a press report citing electronic filings with the Financial Supervisory Service, major domestic paper companies' Q1 sales fell 2.7% year on year and operating profit dropped 44.7%.

High exchange rates, oil-driven freight costs and slow stabilization of global pulp prices are seen as pressure on production costs. Hansol Paper and Moorim P&P also reported weak results over the same period.

Against that backdrop, Hanchang Paper returned to operating profit, which placed it in contrast with much of the sector. An industry official said results diverged according to each company's cost control and business restructuring capability.

The CEO said the industry had struggled for several years under rising supply and weaker global demand, but export prices have recovered and both price and volume have improved since 2025 second half.

On demand, premium white board is used in premium packaging for smartphones and cosmetics and is viewed as a product line less exposed to economic swings. Tighter environmental rules and the shift from plastic to paper packaging are also cited as medium-term demand drivers.

Because pulp prices and exchange rates largely shape margins across the sector, whether the industry upturn carries through to earnings is a point to track in quarterly cost ratios.

06

Outlook

In its Q3 2026 results, released in November 2026, the company said it would push premium white board sales in Q4 to defend profitability. The company argues that continued price increases and sales expansion in general white board will diversify its profit sources across products.

However, in its Q1 2026 release in May 2026 the company said costs of raw materials, auxiliaries and energy would make the environment less favorable from Q2 than in Q1.

Efficiency gains and productivity improvements from facilities invested in 2025 are seen as a base for second-half results as they reduce manufacturing costs. Cosmetics packaging is cited as a new growth avenue tied to rising K-beauty exports, and cosmetics-related sales are about 10% of the total.

Carrier tape base paper is being produced in prototype on in-house equipment, with quality standards being aligned with intermediate processors and external testing under way.

The CEO said the new equipment has reached a stabilization phase and that the company had once earned operating profit of KRW 15 billion to KRW 17 billion, so profit above that level is achievable in his view. This is management's own view rather than an externally verified forecast.

The next scheduled regulatory checkpoint is the 2026 annual business report, which is generally due by the end of March 2027. That report will fix full-year 2026 sales, operating profit, controlling-shareholder net profit and the debt-to-equity ratio.

Since Q3 operating profit fell from the prior quarter, whether the Q4 sales push translates into full-year profitability is the nearest checkpoint.

Bull factors

Leading share in premium white board

Press reports put its share of the premium white board market at around 50%, and the company says it is the only domestic operator with dedicated premium equipment. It has worked with a leading domestic electronics maker for about 13 years, giving it a large anchor customer. Premium board is cited as a product that is hard to substitute.

Quarterly profit run and cost improvement

Operating profit turned positive at KRW 1.69 billion in 2026 Q1, rose to KRW 3.96 billion in Q2, and reached KRW 956 million in Q3. Controlling-shareholder net profit was KRW 677 million in Q1, KRW 941 million in Q2 and KRW 1.06 billion in Q3, rising in Q2 and Q3. The company attributes the improvement to higher selling prices and the effect of facility investment on costs.

Balance sheet repair and new business

The company said a land revaluation raised assets by KRW 50 billion and lowered the debt-to-equity ratio to 152% in 2026 Q1. Carrier tape base paper, a new business, has a domestic market estimated at about KRW 100 billion, with prototype production and quality work under way. Commercialization timing and revenue contribution are items to confirm in future disclosures.

Bear factors

2025 swing to losses

Sales in 2025 fell 9.3% year on year to KRW 267 billion, with an operating loss of KRW 5.19 billion and a controlling-shareholder net loss of KRW 13.1 billion. The cost of sales ratio rose to 92.9%, and selling expense cuts did not prevent the loss. The company cited weaker export prices, softer demand and quality problems as overlapping causes.

Volatility in quarterly profit

Operating profit in 2026 Q3 fell 75.9% from the prior quarter to KRW 956 million. Controlling-shareholder net losses over the last four quarters total KRW 2.55 billion, with the Q4 2025 loss of KRW 5.23 billion weighing most. The company itself has flagged rising raw material and energy costs from Q2 onward.

High debt load and no dividend

Total liabilities at end-2025 were KRW 204.5 billion, well above total equity of KRW 82.9 billion, and the debt-to-equity ratio was 246.6%. With dividends effectively absent, shareholder returns depend on a sustained recovery in earnings. Press reports of a higher market-cap listing threshold in January 2027 add to the burden.

Risk factors

Raw material and energy costs

Most pulp is imported, leaving results exposed to exchange rates, global pulp prices, oil-driven freight and energy costs. The company said prolonged conflict could strain the supply of raw and auxiliary materials. Its outlook for a less favorable cost environment from Q2 is a key variable for earnings volatility.

Demand and export pricing

Exports account for about 30% of sales, so overseas demand and export prices feed directly into results. The company said export prices in 2025 were unusually low. A drop in volume from its electronics customers would also weigh on premium board sales.

Quality and listing requirements

Quality issues, including spots in white board products, weighed on 2025 results according to the company. A March 2026 press report said the market-cap listing threshold would rise to KRW 30 billion in January 2027, making compliance a risk item. The largest shareholder holds more than 40%, which shapes the free float.

Overall view

Hanchang Paper holds a share of roughly 50% in the premium white board market. After a 2025 full-year loss, operating profit and controlling-shareholder net profit were both positive in every quarter from 2026 Q1 to Q3. Operating profit rose to KRW 3.96 billion in Q2 and fell to KRW 956 million in Q3.

Controlling-shareholder net profit rose in Q2 and Q3, but the last four quarters still total a KRW 2.55 billion net loss.

The company says a land revaluation and a 152% debt-to-equity ratio in 2026 Q1 improved the balance sheet, yet total liabilities of KRW 204.5 billion at end-2025 still exceed total equity of KRW 82.9 billion.

Strengths include market share, dedicated equipment, a large customer relationship and new-business preparation. Weaknesses include raw material and energy costs, quarterly volatility and listing-related requirements.

The company's view that costs will be less favorable from Q2 is a key variable for whether the recovery lasts. The next checkpoint is the 2026 annual business report, which will fix full-year operating margin and the debt-to-equity ratio as disclosed results.
