OliPass Under Court Rehabilitation: Survival Is the Key Variable

OliPass is undergoing court-led corporate rehabilitation amid deepening capital impairment, making approval of its rehabilitation plan and continued listing status more decisive than any near-term partnering news.

  1. 1

    The company received a court rehabilitation order from Suwon Rehabilitation Court in June 2026; the creditor claims review period ends October 8, 2026, and the rehabilitation plan filing deadline was extended to November 16, 2026.

  2. 2

    Owners' equity fell to KRW 1.70 billion in 2025 from KRW 11.86 billion a year earlier, while the debt ratio surged to 917.8%.

  3. 3

    First-quarter 2026 revenue fell below KRW 300 million, triggering an additional listing eligibility review, but second-quarter 2026 revenue rebounded to KRW 1.17 billion, sharply narrowing operating and net losses.

  4. 4

  5. 5

02

Business structure

OliPass is a biotech developing RNA therapeutics based on its proprietary OPNA (OliPass Peptide Nucleic Acid) platform, which it positions as a cell-penetrating synthetic gene technology that does not require a separate delivery vehicle.

Its business model has run on two tracks: licensing out self-developed candidates after clinical validation, and a partnered "Target X" model in which collaborators fund research toward candidates against targets they specify.

Its flagship proprietary pipeline, the non-opioid analgesic OLP-1002, targets the SCN9A gene to selectively inhibit the Nav1.7 pain protein, but repeated controversy over the statistical significance of Phase 1b and 2a trial results in Australia and the UK has weakened its development momentum.

The clearest example of the Target X model is the September 2022 joint research agreement with Nasdaq-listed Vanda Pharmaceuticals, under which the contract was signed on September 30, 2022 with a term of within three years from signing, and OliPass received an upfront payment of USD 3,000,000 while Vanda Pharmaceuticals fully funds R&D costs incurred during the collaboration.

Amended disclosures tied to this agreement regarding receipt of R&D funding have continued to appear even in 2026, suggesting some partnering activity persists. However, with no commercialized proprietary product, most revenue depends on such partnering fees or small service-related income.

During a period of attempted ownership change, the company also explored diversification into an ALS drug candidate and PDRN-based aesthetic medicine, though these did not materialize into concrete results as the related capital raises were not completed.

In the domestic RNA therapeutics landscape, peers such as Olix Pharma and ST Pharm have drawn attention through big-pharma licensing deals and clinical data, while OliPass's financial crisis currently overshadows its technology narrative.

04

Earnings analysis

Revenue rose temporarily from KRW 2.30 billion in 2022 to KRW 5.29 billion in 2023, then collapsed to KRW 0.76 billion in 2024 and KRW 0.82 billion in 2025, indicating the commercial revenue base has largely disappeared.

Operating losses persisted for four straight years—KRW 24.13 billion (2022), KRW 13.71 billion (2023), KRW 10.12 billion (2024), and KRW 7.39 billion (2025)—and while the loss figure has shrunk, this coincided with an even sharper contraction in revenue, so it is not clearly a qualitative improvement.

Net loss attributable to owners fell from KRW 24.17 billion (2022) to KRW 12.95 billion (2023) and KRW 5.32 billion (2024), before widening sharply again to KRW 15.38 billion in 2025.

Behind this widening loss, a decline in R&D and product revenue along with rising financial costs such as interest expense from early redemption of convertible bonds were cited as key factors.

Owners' equity fell from KRW 10.46 billion (2022) to KRW 2.49 billion (2023), recovered temporarily to KRW 11.86 billion (2024) via capital raises, then dropped to KRW 1.70 billion in 2025, while total liabilities stayed broadly in the KRW 15 billion range, pushing the debt ratio from 121.6% in 2024 to 917.8% in 2025.

Quarterly revenue ran between roughly KRW 120-160 million from Q2 to Q4 2025 with operating losses of KRW 1.5-2.3 billion, and first-quarter 2026 revenue fell short of KRW 300 million, triggering an added listing eligibility review while net loss widened to KRW 1.92 billion that quarter.

In the second quarter of 2026, however, revenue jumped to KRW 1.17 billion while operating loss narrowed to KRW 117 million and net loss to KRW 570 million, suggesting a combination of partnering cash inflow and cost reduction.

Operating cash flow was consistently negative every year from 2022 to 2025, in the range of KRW 6.8-14.7 billion in outflows, indicating continuous cash burn regardless of reported earnings.

05

Industry analysis

The global RNA and oligonucleotide therapeutics industry appears to be in an early-to-mid growth cycle, with big pharma increasingly pursuing in-licensing and M&A.

Industry reports suggest 2026 will be an inflection point where RNA therapeutics are judged by "data" rather than "potential", and note that big pharma is moving beyond simple partnerships to acquire entire RNA platforms through trillion-won-scale licensing deals and M&A.

In Korea, companies such as Olix Pharma have drawn attention for its asymmetric siRNA-based self-delivery platform and the scalability of its MASH pipeline candidate OLX702A, with some firms driving sector re-rating through big-pharma partnerships and late-stage data.

By contrast, OliPass has produced limited follow-up data disclosure since controversy emerged over the statistical significance of its core OLP-1002 trials, and its Target X partnership with Vanda Pharmaceuticals has not progressed to a concrete license agreement or milestone announcement since the 2022 contract.

In contrast to peers such as Olix Pharma and ST Pharm, which have shown large licensing deals and expanding commercial manufacturing this year, OliPass's non-operating risks—court rehabilitation and capital impairment—are making it difficult to even assess its underlying platform technology on its own merits.

The result is a notable disconnect between broad industry growth expectations and the company-specific survival risk.

Outlook

In the near term, legal process outweighs operations as the key variable.

The creditor claims and secured claims review period was extended to run from July 23 to October 8, 2026, and the rehabilitation plan filing deadline was extended to November 16, 2026; the findings from creditor review and the content of the rehabilitation plan during this window will largely determine the degree of shareholder dilution and whether the business continues.

At the same time, the company decided on a KRW 739 million third-party placement to fund debt repayment, issuing 1.478 million new shares at KRW 500 each, scheduled to list on December 23, representing a small but additional dilution factor already on the calendar.

On the partnering front, amended disclosures regarding receipt of R&D funding tied to the Vanda Pharmaceuticals collaboration have recurred into 2026, suggesting intermittent, small non-operating cash inflows may continue.

However, no disclosure has yet confirmed concrete commercialization progress such as option exercise or a license agreement under that contract.

Regarding continued listing, KOSDAQ's market committee had already resolved to delist the stock in October 2025, and the exchange reaffirmed that delisting grounds had already arisen despite the rehabilitation filing, meaning resumption of delisting procedures cannot be ruled out regardless of whether the rehabilitation plan is approved.

Diversification ideas floated during a past attempted change of controlling shareholder—such as an ALS drug candidate and PDRN aesthetic medicine—have not been officially confirmed to have advanced further, as the related capital raises were never completed.

Bull factors

Intermittent Non-Dilutive Partnering Cash Inflows

The 2022 joint research agreement with Vanda Pharmaceuticals was structured with a USD 3,000,000 upfront payment and full R&D cost coverage, and amended disclosures on related R&D funding receipts have recurred into 2026, bringing in small but non-operating cash.

The surge in second-quarter 2026 revenue to KRW 1.17 billion may partly reflect such partnering-related receipts. This matters as it is nearly the only non-capital-markets funding channel available to the company while external investment remains blocked.

Narrowing Losses Signal in Q2 2026

Second-quarter 2026 operating loss narrowed to KRW 117 million and net loss to KRW 570 million, sharply down from the prior quarter's KRW 1.79 billion operating loss and KRW 1.92 billion net loss.

This appears to reflect a combination of higher revenue and reduced costs, possibly partly benefiting from headcount reductions carried out in a 2025 restructuring. However, it is a single quarter's data, so it is premature to call it an established trend.

Underlying Industry Demand for the Platform Technology Itself

The global RNA and oligonucleotide therapeutics market is seen as being in a growth phase marked by active big-pharma in-licensing and M&A, with indications expanding from rare diseases into chronic disease areas.

OliPass's emphasis on cell permeability in its OPNA platform could be cited as a point of differentiation in the industry's ongoing delivery-technology competition. Whether this factor can offset the company's current financial crisis is, however, a separate question.

Bear factors

Court-Led Rehabilitation and Possible Resumption of Delisting

The company received a court rehabilitation order from Suwon Rehabilitation Court in June 2026, with the creditor claims review period and rehabilitation plan filing deadline pushed back to October 8 and November 16, respectively.

The exchange has stated that delisting grounds had already arisen independently of the rehabilitation filing, meaning continued listing is not automatically guaranteed even if a rehabilitation plan is approved. There remains a persistent possibility that the legal process could lead to trading suspension or delisting.

Deepening Capital Impairment and Surging Debt Ratio

Owners' equity plunged from KRW 11.86 billion in 2024 to KRW 1.70 billion in 2025 within a single year, while the debt ratio surged from 121.6% to 917.8% over the same period.

This reflects how repeated losses have rapidly eroded capital, underscoring that normalizing the balance sheet without additional outside funding will be difficult. The recently decided small-scale capital raise of KRW 739 million is far too small to close this gap.

Weakened Clinical Momentum for the Core Pipeline

The flagship candidate OLP-1002 suffered a credibility hit after repeated controversy over the statistical significance of past Australian and UK trial results. Since then, no clear follow-up clinical data or additional global licensing achievement has been confirmed.

With no commercialized product in hand, the weakening momentum of the core pipeline raises questions about the company's medium-to-long-term revenue model itself.

Risk factors

Delisting and Trading Suspension Risk

KOSDAQ's market committee resolved to delist the stock in October 2025, and the exchange reaffirmed that delisting grounds had already arisen independent of the rehabilitation filing.

Trading currently continues due to an injunction staying that decision, but the procedure could resume depending on whether the rehabilitation plan is approved or on further court rulings. Investors should bear in mind the legal uncertainty that could lead to trading suspension or delisting.

Repeated Funding Setbacks and Further Dilution

The company has a history of delayed or failed third-party placement payments, including a large capital increase tied to a change of controlling shareholder that collapsed.

The placement decided in September 2026 was also small (KRW 739 million) at a low issue price of KRW 500 per share, which may be insufficient for operating needs beyond debt repayment. If further low-price placements or convertible bond issuances follow, existing shareholders could face repeated dilution.

Audit Opinion and Going-Concern Uncertainty

According to an August 2026 disclosure, the company was reported to be in a state of complete capital impairment, with procedures related to an additional listing eligibility review not yet having progressed. This implies that going-concern uncertainty could continue to be raised during external audits.

The possibility of further deterioration in audit opinions or new delisting grounds arising in future annual, semi-annual, or quarterly review reports cannot be ruled out.

12

Overall view

From 2022 to 2025, OliPass saw revenue shrink to near-negligible levels while operating and net losses persisted, with owners' equity collapsing and the debt ratio surging to 917.8%—a textbook picture of a company in financial distress.

Entering 2026, it moved into court-led rehabilitation, with the rehabilitation plan filing deadline and creditor claims review period extended to November and October, respectively, making the progress of this legal process the most important thing to watch over the coming months.

The sharp improvement in second-quarter 2026 revenue and profitability versus the prior quarter can be read as a positive sign, but it may have been influenced by non-operating factors such as the Vanda Pharmaceuticals partnership, making its durability uncertain.

At the same time, KOSDAQ's October 2025 delisting resolution remains under a stay, so the legal possibility of delisting procedures resuming, independent of rehabilitation plan approval, persists.

While the global RNA therapeutics industry is broadly viewed as being in a growth phase, OliPass's individual fate currently hinges far more on non-operating variables—court rehabilitation and capital raising—than on the industry cycle.
