Olipass at a Crossroads Amid Rehabilitation Proceedings

02

Business structure

Founded in 2006, Olipass listed on KOSDAQ in 2019 under the technology special listing track as an RNA therapeutics platform company.

The company's proprietary platform, branded OliPass PNA (OPNA), is a chemically modified peptide nucleic acid derivative designed for high cell permeability to support exon-skipping drug design.

Its flagship candidate, the non-opioid analgesic OLP-1002, underwent trials in the United Kingdom and Australia but failed to demonstrate statistical significance versus placebo in efficacy outcomes.

The company also runs an ALS program targeting STMN2 to normalize neurons derived from ALS patients, supported by research funding from the Korea Drug Development Fund.

In September 2022, Olipass signed a joint R&D agreement with Nasdaq-listed Vanda Pharmaceuticals to discover RNA drug candidates against one rare-disease target and one immuno-oncology target, receiving a $3 million upfront payment plus ongoing research funding.

Since 2024, controlling shareholders have changed repeatedly, moving to Invest Partners No.1 and later Genocure, and amid these transitions the company pivoted partly toward cosmetics and aesthetic medicine materials while struggling to secure external capital.

Annual revenue has remained around KRW 1 billion, consistent with a clinical/platform-stage biotech, and headcount had shrunk to roughly 13 employees as of August 2026.

The revenue model leans more on partnered joint research and licensing — a so-called target-X approach — than on commercializing an in-house approved drug.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual revenue rose from KRW 2.3 billion in 2022 to KRW 5.29 billion in 2023 (up 130.4%), then plunged 85.7% to KRW 756 million in 2024 before edging up 7.9% to KRW 816 million in 2025.

The 2023 revenue spike reflected a large year-over-year increase as disclosed, while 2024's sharp drop came as the comparable base effect faded.

Operating losses narrowed each year from KRW 24.1 billion in 2022 to KRW 13.7 billion in 2023, KRW 10.1 billion in 2024, and KRW 7.39 billion in 2025, marking four consecutive years of losses even as the loss size steadily shrank.

Net loss attributable to owners, however, moved in the opposite direction after 2024, falling from KRW 24.2 billion in 2022 to KRW 5.32 billion in 2024 before widening again to KRW 15.4 billion in 2025.

On a quarterly basis, operating losses were uneven from the second quarter of 2025 through the first quarter of 2026 — KRW 2.31 billion, KRW 1.54 billion, KRW 1.67 billion, and KRW 1.79 billion — before narrowing to KRW 117 million in the second quarter of 2026, extending the loss streak to five consecutive quarters.

Second-quarter 2026 revenue of KRW 1.17 billion surged 1,178.5% quarter-over-quarter and 634.3% year-over-year, a jump that coincided with disclosed receipts of joint R&D funding from Vanda Pharmaceuticals during 2026.

Net losses attributable to owners for the first and second quarters of 2026 were KRW 1.92 billion and KRW 570 million respectively, a narrowing trend but still two consecutive loss-making quarters.

On the balance sheet side, the debt-to-equity ratio jumped from 121.6% in 2024 to 917.8% in 2025, with total equity at just KRW 1.7 billion at year-end 2025, reflecting significantly weakened financial stability.

Operating cash outflow shrank from KRW 15.5 billion in 2022 to KRW 6.81 billion in 2025, but the company has still recorded a net cash outflow every year in the period.

05

Industry analysis

The global RNA and oligonucleotide therapeutics field has grown to around 14 commercialized drugs, but most are still used mainly in rare diseases due to toxicity concerns at high doses, making it a relatively early-stage industry.

Domestically, listed biotechs such as OliX Pharmaceuticals, ST Pharm, and LigaChem Biosciences operate in the oligonucleotide and antibody-conjugate space, forming the competitive set against which Olipass's platform and partnership track record are measured.

Olipass has promoted its OPNA platform's ability to penetrate cells without a separate delivery vehicle as a differentiator, but commercial validation of the platform has been delayed as its core clinical pipeline has run into uncertainty.

Olipass has been cited as the technology-special-listed biotech with the largest market cap decline, a case attributed to repeated clinical failures in its lead candidate combined with a qualified audit opinion and accumulated disclosure violations that triggered delisting procedures.

Unlike peers that have funded operations through licensing deals or clinical successes, Olipass's agreement with Vanda Pharmaceuticals remains effectively its sole active external partnership, narrowing its relative industry position.

Across KOSDAQ more broadly, a number of technology-special-listed biotechs face delisting triggers or non-standard audit opinions each year, so Olipass's situation is not unique within the sector, though the severity of its financial distress ranks among the more acute cases.

06

Outlook

In an official notice, the company stated that due to liquidity problems it filed for rehabilitation with the Suwon Rehabilitation Court on May 7, 2026, while affirming its intent to continue pursuing its RNA-based platform technology along with key pipelines including the non-opioid analgesic and the ALS treatment candidate.

The court commenced rehabilitation proceedings on June 5, 2026, declining to appoint a separate administrator and instead designating CEO Kim Tae-hyun as the debtor-in-possession.

The claims review period for rehabilitation claims and secured claims was extended to November 6, 2026, and the deadline for submitting the rehabilitation plan was revised to December 16, 2026, per amended disclosures.

First-quarter 2026 revenue falling below the KOSDAQ quarterly revenue threshold also triggered an additional listing eligibility review cause, which could factor into the exchange's forthcoming review.

The company's only ongoing external collaboration is the joint R&D program with Vanda Pharmaceuticals, which, if it reaches its rare-disease and immuno-oncology target discovery goals, could lead to option-exercise technology fees and separate licensing negotiations, according to the company.

The timing and scale of any such follow-on agreements have not been disclosed. Whether the rehabilitation plan wins court approval and how the Korea Exchange's listing eligibility review concludes are the central variables determining the company's future form.

No concrete business guidance on new clinical restarts or additional licensing deals has been confirmed in currently available disclosures.

Bull factors

Ongoing Joint R&D with Vanda Pharmaceuticals

The joint R&D agreement signed with Vanda Pharmaceuticals in September 2022 targets RNA drug candidates for a rare-disease target and an immuno-oncology target, with Olipass receiving a $3 million upfront payment plus research funding.

Disclosures of continued R&D funding receipts under this agreement have persisted into 2026, keeping an external funding channel open. If the research goals are met, it could lead to option-exercise technology fees and separate licensing negotiations.

Narrowing Quarterly Operating Losses

Operating losses narrowed sharply from KRW 1.79 billion in the first quarter of 2026 to KRW 117 million in the second quarter. On an annual basis as well, operating losses shrank for four straight years, from KRW 24.1 billion in 2022 to KRW 7.39 billion in 2025. Further loss reduction could emerge as cost restructuring proceeds under the rehabilitation process.

Proprietary Platform Technology Asset

Olipass holds proprietary OPNA platform technology based on cell-permeable PNA derivatives, which achieves exon skipping without a separate delivery vehicle, a technical asset that can be viewed as distinct from the clinical setbacks of its lead candidate.

The platform is also applied to its ALS treatment project supported by the Korea Drug Development Fund. New partnership agreements could revive the platform-based revenue model.

Bear factors

Complete Capital Impairment and Consecutive Audit Disclaimers

A 2026 half-year review found a capital impairment ratio of 117.40%, confirming complete capital impairment, and both the FY2025 annual audit report and the 2026 half-year review received opinion disclaimers.

Total equity stood at just KRW 1.7 billion at year-end 2025 with a debt-to-equity ratio surging to 917.8%, severely impairing financial soundness. This raises fundamental questions about the company's going-concern status.

History of Core Pipeline Clinical Failure

The lead candidate, non-opioid analgesic OLP-1002, failed to achieve statistical significance in trials, with efficacy in treated patients at times appearing lower than placebo.

This failure was a central factor cited in Olipass being identified as the technology-special-listed biotech with the largest market cap decline. The clinical value of alternative pipeline assets remains externally unvalidated.

Uncertainty Over Continued Listing

Olipass received a delisting decision from the Korea Exchange on October 14, 2025, but related procedures such as a review trading period have been held in abeyance following an injunction request, and first-quarter 2026 revenue falling short of the KOSDAQ quarterly revenue requirement triggered an additional listing eligibility review cause.

With rehabilitation proceedings ongoing, the possibility that the rehabilitation plan fails to gain court approval or that the exchange review ultimately results in delisting cannot be ruled out. Headcount shrinking to about 13 employees as of August 2026 also adds to concerns about operational capacity.

Risk factors

Listing Eligibility Risk

A delisting decision was issued in October 2025 but held in abeyance following an injunction request, and an additional listing eligibility review cause later arose from a quarterly revenue shortfall. The final outcome of the Korea Exchange's review could determine whether the listing is maintained. The progress of the rehabilitation proceedings is itself a variable that could influence the exchange's review.

Financial Structure and Governance Risk

Since 2024, the controlling shareholder has changed repeatedly, moving to Invest Partners No.1 and then Genocure, with external capital raises delayed or falling through multiple times along the way.

With a capital impairment ratio of 117.40% indicating complete impairment, any further delay in capital infusion could deepen financial distress. Declining headcount and frequent management turnover add to uncertainty about execution capacity.

Pipeline and Commercialization Risk

The lead candidate OLP-1002 has a track record of failing to achieve statistical significance in trials, leaving efficacy uncertainty even if clinical work resumes.

The monetization path is effectively concentrated in a single partnership with Vanda Pharmaceuticals, so inflows of external funding are heavily dependent on that agreement's progress. The timing and terms of any new partnership have not yet been disclosed.

Overall view

Olipass has posted operating and net losses for four consecutive years from 2022 to 2025, with total equity shrinking to KRW 1.7 billion at year-end 2025, and 2026 has layered on severe financial warning signs in the form of complete capital impairment and consecutive audit opinion disclaimers.

The company filed for rehabilitation in May 2026, received a commencement order in June, and has since had its rehabilitation plan submission deadline extended to December 16, 2026.

Second-quarter 2026 revenue rose sharply from the prior quarter alongside narrower operating losses, but this appears linked to funding received under the Vanda Pharmaceuticals R&D collaboration rather than a structural turnaround to profitability.

On the listing front, a delisting decision from October 2025 remains stayed by an injunction, while an additional listing eligibility review cause arose in 2026, leaving the exchange's ultimate decision uncertain.

Core assets such as the OPNA platform and the Vanda Pharmaceuticals agreement remain intact, but the lead pipeline's clinical failure history and repeated changes in controlling shareholders leave open questions about business continuity.

Before forming any investment judgment, the specific contents of the rehabilitation plan, the court's approval decision, and the Korea Exchange's final listing eligibility determination should be checked in sequence.
