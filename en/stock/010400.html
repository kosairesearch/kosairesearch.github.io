02

Business structure

Woojin I&S is a mid-sized mechanical and fire-protection facilities contractor founded in 1975, operating through two segments: General Facilities and High-Tech.

The General Facilities segment installs mechanical and fire-suppression systems for apartments, offices, hotels and data centers, and accounts for 92.92 percent of sales in the revenue mix compiled by Alphasquare.

The High-Tech segment manufactures and installs coating ducts used in semiconductor and display plants and represents 7.08 percent of the same mix. In General Facilities, the company has worked with Lotte Construction and Shinsegae E&C on large projects such as the Dunchon apartment complex and Starfield Cheongna.

The High-Tech segment builds on work at SK hynix's Yongin cluster and a Naver data center in Sejong, and according to the corporate monitoring report from WiseReport, a semiconductor facilities project at SK Ecoplant's Yongin cluster was under way as of September 2026.

The company is also expanding into specialty chemical-resistant coating ducts for secondary battery processes and coating of mixing tanks.

In December 2025 it disclosed single supply contracts for a mechanical works package at Siheung Eunhaeng Zone 2 (KRW 7.82 billion), a mechanical fire-suppression package at Samseon 5 Zone (KRW 13.9 billion), and a mechanical works contract for a Gasan-dong data center (KRW 9.85 billion). The structure relies on contracts with general contractors as the main order channel.

04

Earnings analysis

On a disclosed annual basis, sales rose from KRW 84 billion in 2022 to KRW 100.2 billion in 2023, KRW 137.6 billion in 2024 and KRW 164.5 billion in 2025, increasing for three consecutive years from 2023 through 2025, with 2025 the largest year in the 2022 to 2025 span.

The operating loss of KRW 5.61 billion in 2022 narrowed to KRW 3.32 billion in 2023 but widened to KRW 17.9 billion in 2024 before the company posted operating profit of KRW 4.98 billion in 2025, lifting the operating margin from -13.0 percent to 3.0 percent.

In its preliminary results announced on February 13, 2026, the company attributed the turnaround to higher construction sales from new clients and carried-over projects, improved cost ratios and settlement effects on early-stage site investments.

Controlling-shareholder net income moved from a loss of KRW 22.2 billion in 2024 to a profit of KRW 7.59 billion in 2025, while net income including non-controlling interests was also KRW 7.59 billion.

Quarterly sales peaked at KRW 51.1 billion in the third quarter of 2025 and then fell 18.0 percent quarter on quarter to KRW 41.9 billion in the fourth quarter, when the company recorded an operating loss of KRW 1.01 billion.

Sales then rose for three straight quarters in 2026, to KRW 48.1 billion, KRW 60.5 billion and KRW 69.8 billion, while operating losses of KRW 2.15 billion, KRW 1.86 billion and KRW 5.02 billion followed, with the third-quarter loss the largest of the four.

Third-quarter 2026 sales were up 36.6 percent from a year earlier, yet the operating line swung into loss. WiseReport's corporate monitor noted that large General Facilities projects lifted first-half 2026 sales but that cost burdens widened the segment's operating loss.

Controlling-shareholder net income was positive in the second and third quarters of 2026, at KRW 660 million and KRW 2 billion, and the cumulative controlling-shareholder net loss over the last four quarters was KRW 779 million.

05

Industry analysis

Within the construction and building materials sector, Woojin I&S functions more as a specialized mechanical and fire-protection contractor than as a general builder of the kind represented by Samsung C&T, Hyundai Engineering & Construction or DL E&C, which handle civil and plant work across the board.

Woojin takes on the mechanical package within projects led by general contractors, so its sales are heavily tied to their construction timetables and site progress. When work slips, orders carry over, and the company attributed the 2025 sales increase to carried-over projects reaching full execution.

Demand in the High-Tech segment is linked to capital spending on semiconductors and secondary batteries. WiseReport's corporate monitor pointed to rising AI-related semiconductor demand and the company's entry into coating of secondary battery mixing tanks as growth factors.

Conversely, the General Facilities segment expanded its operating loss despite higher project sales because of cost burdens, making cost control a central variable for results. On leverage, the company's debt-to-equity ratio sat below the average of the machinery sector in Alphasquare's annual comparison.

06

Outlook

Several contracts disclosed by the company extend revenue visibility across multiple years. The Siheung Eunhaeng Zone 2 mechanical works package is disclosed with a construction period running to September 30, 2027, while the Samseon 5 Zone mechanical fire-suppression package runs to March 31, 2027.

The water-supply connection heat transport works contracted with Cheongna Energy cover 2026 to 2028, with a contract value of KRW 1.0287 billion disclosed as equal to 6.26 percent of 2025 sales.

In High-Tech, semiconductor facilities work at SK Ecoplant's Yongin cluster was under way, and coating for secondary battery mixing tanks has been cited as a growth axis.

In new businesses, the company is working on AI-based water pipeline integrated control, having been selected together with iRoom Technology for a government program aimed at speeding up commercialization of AI products in the environment field, with a target of market launch within one to two years.

The program is expected to receive government support of roughly KRW 41 billion through 2027 across the overall project. The company has also said it is pursuing municipal water system modernization in Korea and entry into water infrastructure markets in the UAE, the United States and Indonesia.

The next checkpoints are whether the operating loss stops in the 2026 fourth-quarter and full-year preliminary results, and how construction volume and cost ratios are reflected in the 2026 annual business report.

Bull factors

Earnings Structure Turned Profitable

Operating margin improved from -13.0 percent in 2024 to 3.0 percent in 2025 after the company returned to operating profit. The company cited higher construction sales, better cost ratios and settlement effects on early-stage sites as drivers.

Sales rose for three consecutive years from 2023 through 2025, with 2025 showing both top-line growth and a recovery in profitability.

Order Flow Anchored by Large Contracts

In December 2025 the company disclosed contracts with a major contractor for the Siheung and Samseon 5 Zone projects, along with a Gasan-dong data center mechanical works award. A 2026 contract with Cheongna Energy covers heat transport works running from 2026 to 2028. Multi-year construction periods add to revenue visibility.

Links to Semiconductor, Battery and AI Infrastructure

The High-Tech segment is widening into semiconductor facilities and coating for secondary battery mixing tanks, and WiseReport's corporate monitor cited rising AI-related semiconductor demand as a growth factor.

The company was selected with iRoom Technology for a government program on AI product commercialization and will handle pipeline and site deployment. The setup extends existing construction capability into new fields.

Bear factors

Operating Losses Continue and Widen in 2026

The company has recorded operating losses for four straight quarters from the fourth quarter of 2025 through the third quarter of 2026. Third-quarter 2026 sales reached KRW 69.8 billion, but the operating loss widened to KRW 5.02 billion, the largest of the four quarters.

WiseReport's corporate monitor noted that higher project sales in General Facilities were offset by cost burdens.

Quarterly Volatility in Net Income

Controlling-shareholder net income was KRW 7 billion in the third quarter of 2025 and swung to a net loss of KRW 1.27 billion in the fourth quarter. The third quarter of 2026 showed KRW 2 billion, up 203.2 percent from the prior quarter but down 71.4 percent from a year earlier. Frequent reversals in quarterly net income limit how stable the earnings trend has been.

Dependence on General Contractors and Thin Trading

Major orders depend on contracts with general contractors, so sales track their construction schedules. Major shareholder interest is 52.23 percent and the free float ratio is 37.80 percent as of September 30, 2026, which limits the tradable share base. Foreign ownership is also low, so price moves can be sizable on modest trading volume.

Risk factors

Concentration in Contractors and Projects

Revenue is concentrated in subcontracting work with large general contractors and a small number of major sites. If a contractor delays a start or a site halts, carried-over volumes can slip as well. Many contracts run into 2027, so schedule management is a key driver of earnings swings.

Cost Burden and Settlement Risk

In the first half of 2026 the General Facilities segment's operating loss widened despite higher sales. The cost-ratio improvement and settlement effects on early-stage sites cited for the 2025 turnaround may not repeat. Failure to control costs could keep sales growth from reaching operating profit.

Execution Uncertainty in New Businesses

The AI water pipeline control business targets a market launch within one to two years and relies on government support. Plans to enter overseas water infrastructure markets are still at the stage of stated intent. If performance or commercialization timing differs from plan, new revenue contributions could be delayed.

Overall view

Woojin I&S moved from an operating loss in 2022 to an operating profit in 2025. Sales rose for three consecutive years from 2023 through 2025, with 2025 the largest year in the span.

However, operating losses have run for four straight quarters from the fourth quarter of 2025 through the third quarter of 2026, and the third-quarter 2026 loss of KRW 5.02 billion was the widest of the four. Whether sales growth converts into operating profit is the central issue.

Supporting factors include large contractor relationships, semiconductor and battery facilities work, and the AI water-control program. Offsetting factors include cost burdens, quarterly swings in net income, and limited trading liquidity given concentrated major shareholder ownership.

The next checkpoints are the 2026 fourth-quarter and full-year preliminary results and the operating margin in the 2026 annual business report.
