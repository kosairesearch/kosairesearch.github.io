North American channels and auto electronics: profit recovery still unproven

02

Business structure

Namsung was founded in 1965 and listed on the Korea Exchange's main board in 1989. As of the third quarter of 2025, its revenue mix was 92.6% from the Digital Electronics segment and 7.4% from the Asset Management segment.

The asset management business consists of real estate leasing, including Namsung Plaza and Crown Plaza in Geumcheon-gu and Guro-gu, Seoul. Its main products are digital mobile audio and video devices, GPS receivers, and smart keys.

The company exports under its own brands Dual, Jensen, and Axxera to the United States, Canada and other markets. Through its US subsidiary Namsung America, Inc., it supplies Walmart, Advance Auto Parts and O'Reilly Auto Parts, and Walmart is described as its key customer.

It also sells automotive products on Amazon and Walmart.com, and plans to widen its online distribution after building brand awareness through Walmart. The company is pushing its automotive electronics business, including smart key exports and advanced driver-assistance system development.

Its NS AI LINK in-vehicle AI device links generative AI on a vehicle and a smartphone, and the company is preparing a launch in North America using its local distribution and logistics infrastructure.

As of September 1, 2026, the largest shareholder group, led by Yoon Seong-ho and eight others, held 38.94% and treasury shares accounted for 10.72%.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual revenue fell from KRW 98.1 billion in 2022 to KRW 69.7 billion in 2023, a decline of 29.0%, and then rose to KRW 74.6 billion in 2024 (up 7.0% year on year) and KRW 77.5 billion in 2025 (up 4.0% year on year). 2022 was the largest year and 2023 the smallest within the 2022 to 2025 period.

Operating profit moved from KRW 2.54 billion in 2022 to an operating loss of KRW 6.08 billion in 2023, which marked a switch to a loss from a profit. The loss narrowed to an operating loss of KRW 1.81 billion in 2024, and 2025 posted operating profit of KRW 188 million, a return to profit.

Operating margin was 2.6% in 2022, -8.7% in 2023, -2.4% in 2024 and 0.2% in 2025.

Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 3.19 billion in 2022, a net loss attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 10.1 billion in 2023, KRW 4.43 billion in 2024, and a net loss attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 3.73 billion in 2025.

Net profit including non-controlling interests is a different figure, and for 2025 it was a net loss of KRW 3.74 billion.

According to a news report from March 2026, the company attributed the 2025 net loss to lower other income and financial income, even though operating profit improved on higher US exports and lower cost of sales and selling and administrative expenses.

On a quarterly basis, operating loss was KRW 1.24 billion in the third quarter of 2025 and KRW 561 million in the fourth quarter, before operating profit of KRW 1.15 billion in the first quarter of 2026.

Operating profit of KRW 3.29 billion in the second quarter of 2026 rose 186.7% from the prior quarter, and a report on the company's disclosure from August 6, 2026 linked it to strong sales of higher-value products for Walmart.

Third-quarter revenue of KRW 21 billion was up 18.0% year on year, but the net loss attributable to controlling shareholders widened to KRW 3.15 billion.

Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders for the last four quarters (fourth quarter of 2025 through third quarter of 2026) totals KRW 3.7 billion, and the first quarter of 2026, at KRW 4.38 billion, accounts for most of that.

At the end of 2025, total equity was KRW 105.5 billion and total liabilities were KRW 172.9 billion, giving a debt-to-equity ratio of 163.9%.

05

Industry analysis

Namsung's core products are automotive audio and video devices and smart keys sold in the aftermarket, and its main sales channel is automotive accessory retail in North America.

The in-vehicle information, entertainment and driver-assistance functions of cars are becoming more advanced, and Namsung is preparing AI devices and advanced driver-assistance systems in line with that trend.

Market size and share figures are not used in this report, so the discussion of industry conditions is qualitative.

Compared with other listed companies in the consumer and distribution sector, Namsung is closer to a manufacturer-exporter that supplies its own brands to overseas retail chains than to a domestic large retailer such as E-Mart or Lotte Shopping, and its revenue structure and risk profile differ significantly.

In North American automotive accessories, retailers' inventory and promotion policies can amplify swings in revenue, and Namsung depends heavily on a small number of channels. Competition in automotive electronics spans direct supply to automakers and brand competition in the aftermarket.

Whether Namsung's own-brand strength is more effective in one of these areas will become clear as the mix of revenue changes. Whether the diversification set out in its value-up plan shows up as a measurable share of revenue is also worth tracking.

06

Outlook

In its value-up plan disclosed on April 20, 2026, the company set out diversifying its business and revenue mix and expanding new revenue based on its North American distribution network as its core axes. The NS AI LINK in-vehicle AI device sits at the center of the new revenue plan.

When it reported second-quarter 2026 results, the company said Walmart sales of this product would be reflected in earnings in the second half, and a news report said an initial shipment worth about KRW 5 billion at consumer prices had already been supplied in the United States.

Continued growth of existing Dual-brand products is the other pillar of the second-half outlook the company presented.

However, third-quarter 2026 revenue of KRW 21 billion and operating profit of KRW 1.07 billion both declined from the second quarter, and the net loss attributable to controlling shareholders widened to KRW 3.15 billion, so the effect of second-half initiatives has not yet fully shown up in profit.

Expansion into online channels such as Amazon has been announced as a plan, and the timing and scale of execution have not been confirmed.

In automotive electronics, smart key exports and advanced driver-assistance system development are under way, and whether these two areas widen the revenue base is a medium-term point to monitor. Whether the diversification announced by the company changes the weight of the asset management segment also needs watching.

Because total liabilities exceeded total equity at the end of 2025, the financing method and cost for new businesses should be assessed alongside the earnings outlook.

08

Bull factors

Return to profit in the first half of 2026

Operating profit turned positive at KRW 1.15 billion in the first quarter of 2026 and rose to KRW 3.29 billion in the second quarter. Second-quarter revenue of KRW 21.6 billion was up 15.5% year on year, and the company linked it to strong sales of higher-value products for Walmart.

Reports indicate that net profit also turned positive in the first half of 2026. Whether this pattern held in the third quarter has to be read against the net loss attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 3.15 billion.

North American channels and planned expansion

Namsung has secured North American retail channels including Walmart, Advance Auto Parts and O'Reilly Auto Parts, and also sells automotive products on Amazon and Walmart.com. The company has said it will build brand awareness through Walmart before expanding into online channels.

Its US subsidiary and a logistics infrastructure built over roughly two decades give it a base for supplying new products quickly.

Planned second-half revenue from NS AI LINK

The NS AI LINK in-vehicle AI device connects generative AI and AI agents on a vehicle and a smartphone. When it reported second-quarter 2026 results, the company said Walmart sales of this product would be reflected in the second half.

A news report said an initial shipment worth about KRW 5 billion at consumer prices had already been supplied in the United States. The actual revenue scale will be visible in fourth-quarter 2026 results.

09

Bear factors

Third-quarter net loss and annual net loss

Third-quarter 2026 revenue of KRW 21 billion rose 18.0% year on year, but the net loss attributable to controlling shareholders widened to KRW 3.15 billion. On an annual basis, the net loss attributable to controlling shareholders for 2025 was KRW 3.73 billion.

Even when operating profit is positive, a decline in other income and financial income can push net profit into the red, and this is a weakness in the company's results.

Debt burden and negative operating cash flow

The debt-to-equity ratio rose from 126.9% in 2022 to 163.9% in 2025. At the end of 2025, total liabilities of KRW 172.9 billion exceeded total equity of KRW 105.5 billion.

Operating cash flow was negative each year, at -KRW 6.38 billion in 2022, -KRW 3.93 billion in 2023, -KRW 3.21 billion in 2024 and -KRW 5.37 billion in 2025. Cash has not come in from operations even in years when the company reported profit.

Thin operating margin and channel concentration

The operating margin turned positive at 0.2% in 2025, but it remains below the 2.6% recorded in 2022. This means revenue growth has not translated into margin improvement for an extended period.

Because key customers are concentrated among a few US retail channels such as Walmart, changes in those channels' inventory policies or promotions can feed through to results.

10

Risk factors

Customer concentration risk

A large share of revenue depends on a small number of US retail channels, including Walmart. A drop in orders or inventory adjustments by those channels could hit revenue and operating profit at the same time.

Whether the strong sales of higher-value products seen in the second quarter of 2026 are temporary or sustainable remains unresolved. Whether expansion into online channels will reduce this concentration depends on execution.

Balance sheet and cash flow risk

The debt-to-equity ratio is 163.9%, and total liabilities exceed total equity. Operating cash flow was negative in every year from 2022 to 2025. If the company needs funding for new businesses such as AI devices or automotive electronics investment, it may have to rely on borrowing or new equity issuance.

Ownership concentration and limited free float risk

As of September 1, 2026, the largest shareholder group led by Yoon Seong-ho and eight others held 38.94%, and treasury shares accounted for 10.72%. The free float ratio is about 50.34%, and foreign ownership is 1.45%.

With a limited share of stock traded in the market, the share price can react sharply to changes in supply and demand.

12

Overall view

Namsung is a small company that exports automotive electronics and digital devices through North American retail channels. Its return to operating profit in 2025 and the profit trend in the first half of 2026 are on record.

However, even with higher revenue in the third quarter of 2026, the net loss attributable to controlling shareholders widened to KRW 3.15 billion, so the durability of earnings is not yet proven.

A debt-to-equity ratio of 163.9% and negative operating cash flow in every year from 2022 to 2025 are the main financial issues. Bullish factors include the Walmart-centered North American network, the NS AI LINK device scheduled to show up in second-half revenue, and the diversification set out in the value-up plan.

Bearish factors include dependence on a small number of channels, volatility in non-operating income that drives net profit, and a limited free float because of the large stake held by the largest shareholder and treasury shares.

The next checkpoints are the fourth-quarter 2026 preliminary results, the 2026 annual report, and the channel mix of revenue in the first quarter of 2027. This report does not present an investment opinion or target price and is provided for information only.
