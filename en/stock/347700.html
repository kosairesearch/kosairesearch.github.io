02

Business structure

Sphere makes and supplies specialty alloys for space launch vehicles, used in core components such as engines and nozzles that must withstand high heat and corrosion. It works on an OEM basis and relies on a global network of outsourced manufacturing partners rather than expanding its own production capacity.

After its March 2025 merger with Sphere Korea, the company entered the specialty alloy business in earnest and has strengthened integrated support running from early product development through delivery.

Revenue has two streams, specialty alloy raw materials and processed products, and the share of lower-margin raw-material sales grew in the second quarter of 2026. Specialty alloy sales in that quarter rose about fivefold from a year earlier.

Its main customer is a U.S. global space launch company, and in August 2025 the company signed with that customer what it described as the first ten-year long-term supply contract of its kind worldwide.

Its competitive positioning rests on a global vendor network that shortens lead times and on raw-material sourcing that stabilizes supply. A nickel project in Indonesia has also been cited alongside the push to broaden the business toward raw materials.

Because revenue is concentrated in a few customers and launch programs, diversifying the customer base is a key variable for business stability.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual revenue was KRW 2.82 billion in 2022, KRW 3.33 billion in 2023 (up 18.0% year on year), and KRW 2.56 billion in 2024 (down 23.1%), before jumping to KRW 95.6 billion in 2025 (up 3,639.2%). 2025 was the largest year of 2022–2025 and 2024 the smallest.

The 2025 surge is explained by long-term specialty alloy contracts that began converting into sales after the March 2025 merger. Operating losses were KRW 5.61 billion in 2022 and KRW 9.59 billion in 2023, with the loss widening in 2023, before narrowing to KRW 6.37 billion in 2024.

Operating profit turned positive in 2025 at KRW 9.05 billion, a 9.5% operating margin. Net income attributable to controlling shareholders posted losses from 2022 to 2024 and turned to a KRW 170 million profit in 2025, while net income including non-controlling interests was reported at the same amounts in each year.

The operating margin moved from -199.0% in 2022, -288.4% in 2023 and -249.2% in 2024 to 9.5% in 2025. Operating cash flow was still negative in 2025 at KRW 14.7 billion.

Quarterly revenue rose from KRW 21.1 billion in Q3 2025 to KRW 33.8 billion in Q4 2025 (up 60.4% quarter on quarter), KRW 45.1 billion in Q1 2026 (up 33.2%) and KRW 77.1 billion in Q2 2026 (up 71.0%), before easing to KRW 74.3 billion in Q3 2026 (down 3.6%).

Operating profit moved from an operating loss of KRW 990 million in Q3 2025 to KRW 3.64 billion in Q4 2025, fell to KRW 711 million in Q1 2026 (down 80.5%), then rose to KRW 1.67 billion in Q2 and KRW 4.88 billion in Q3 2026.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 39.8 billion in Q1 2026 but turned to net losses of KRW 24.3 billion in Q2 and KRW 14.5 billion in Q3.

The gap between Q1 2026 operating profit of KRW 711 million and attributable net income of KRW 39.8 billion is large, so the non-operating items need item-level review in the annual report. On an operating basis, Q3 2026 turned profitable compared with the same quarter a year earlier.

05

Industry analysis

Demand for materials in the space industry is growing as SpaceX expands its launch operations and lunar and Mars exploration projects increase. In Korea, the new establishment of a national aerospace agency and rising R&D investment are feeding into policies to nurture the industry ecosystem.

Specialty alloys for launch vehicles must offer high heat resistance, corrosion resistance and precision machinability, so supply stability and quality capability act as entry barriers.

In cycle terms, the business appears to be entering a stage where launch production is ramping up, and according to a press report, Meritz Securities analyzed that the growth base for mid- to long-term earnings widens as global customers' launch production becomes full-scale.

The OEM model built on a global vendor network offers the advantage of expanding production capacity without building its own facilities, but quality and lead-time control in outsourced manufacturing are central to competitiveness.

Its positioning against peers is described as a combination of long-term contracts, raw-material sourcing and supply-chain management capability. As the share of raw-material sales grows, raw-material price swings such as nickel and the ability to pass costs on determine margins.

The 89.0% cost of sales ratio in Q2 2026 illustrates this point. The Indonesian nickel project connects with an industry trend of bringing supply chains closer to the company through raw-material ownership.

06

Outlook

The company has defined 2026 as a year of a 'quantum jump' and is presenting a growth turnaround. The ten-year long-term supply contract signed in August 2025 is cited as a foundation for sales after 2026.

Adding up the revenue disclosed through the third quarter gives roughly two-thirds of Meritz Securities' estimated full-year revenue, so how close fourth-quarter revenue comes to that estimate is the first checkpoint.

In the same report, Meritz Securities estimated 2026 operating profit at KRW 15.4 billion and analyzed that 2026 revenue would rise 210.7% from 2025. Reaching that estimate would require fourth-quarter operating profit to clearly exceed the quarterly average so far.

A press report has noted that operation of the Indonesian nickel project could change raw-material sourcing and the revenue mix. Whether the share of raw-material sales declines and processed-product sales recover is the indicator for judging room for operating margin improvement.

Changes in production and test schedules for U.S. launch programs can shift order timing and revenue recognition. The next regular report, the 2026 annual report, is where the full-year operating margin, non-operating items and operating cash flow will be confirmed.

Until then, the size and counterparty mix of supply contracts disclosed on an as-needed basis serve as interim indicators.

08

Bull factors

A shift in revenue scale

Revenue reached KRW 95.6 billion in 2025, dozens of times the KRW 2.56 billion of 2024. Quarterly revenue stayed high in 2026, at KRW 45.1 billion in Q1, KRW 77.1 billion in Q2 and KRW 74.3 billion in Q3, with Q3 up 252.2% from a year earlier.

Demand for specialty alloys is tied to launch production ramps, which gives the revenue base room to widen, and this is the core bullish argument.

Operating profit momentum

Operating profit rose in each of the second and third quarters of 2026 to KRW 4.88 billion in Q3, which turned profitable compared with the same quarter a year earlier. If the cost structure improves, this profit trend could continue, which is the bullish case on the operating line.

Long-term contracts and space demand

The ten-year long-term supply contract signed in August 2025 with a U.S. global space launch company is described as going beyond short-term trading. Meritz Securities estimated in an August 18, 2026 report that 2026 revenue would reach KRW 297.1 billion, up 210.7% from 2025.

SpaceX's expanding launch operations and lunar and Mars exploration demand also support the outlook for materials demand.

Bear factors

Continued net losses to controlling shareholders

Net losses attributable to controlling shareholders were KRW 24.3 billion in Q2 2026 and KRW 14.5 billion in Q3 2026, and totaled KRW 19.9 billion over the last four quarters. The Q3 loss narrowed from Q2 but remains a loss.

The gap between Q1 2026 operating profit of KRW 711 million and attributable net income of KRW 39.8 billion shows swings in non-operating items, which is the bearish case on earnings composition.

Rising cost ratio and margin pressure

Q2 2026 revenue of KRW 77.1 billion rose 308.6% from a year earlier, yet operating profit was only KRW 1.67 billion. Higher raw-material sales lifted the cost of sales ratio to 89.0% from 73.6% a year earlier.

If revenue growth continues without margin recovery, the benefit of top-line expansion shrinks, which is the bearish case on margins.

Customer concentration and overseas project burden

Revenue depends mainly on a U.S. global space launch company, so it is sensitive to that customer's program schedules. Operating cash flow was still negative in 2025 at KRW 14.7 billion.

The Indonesian nickel project is in its operating phase, leaving raw-material supply and overseas operating risks, which is the bearish case on the overseas business.

10

Risk factors

Customer and program concentration

Because revenue depends on long-term contracts with one overseas customer, delays in launch testing or production could shift revenue recognition. If customer diversification is slow, a change in a single program could move quarterly results as a whole. Monitoring changes in the customer mix through ad hoc disclosures is therefore important.

Cost ratio and margins

As raw-material sales grew, the cost of sales ratio rose to 89.0% in Q2 2026. If nickel and specialty alloy input prices rise and cannot be passed through to selling prices, the operating margin could fall again. The operating margin turned to 9.5% in 2025, but the recovery period is short, so the trend needs confirmation.

Financial burden and cash generation

At the end of 2025, total shareholders' equity was KRW 63.6 billion and total liabilities were KRW 31.7 billion. Investment burdens, including the overseas smelter stake, continue while operating cash flow was negative at KRW 14.7 billion in 2025. If cash generation does not recover, financing conditions and the pace of investment could be constrained.

Overall view

Sphere is a company whose revenue and profit both turned in 2025, with revenue of KRW 95.6 billion and operating profit of KRW 9.05 billion. Quarterly revenue in 2026 stayed high at KRW 45.1 billion, KRW 77.1 billion and KRW 74.3 billion, and operating profit rose for two consecutive quarters to KRW 4.88 billion in Q3.

Net losses attributable to controlling shareholders continued at KRW 24.3 billion in Q2 and KRW 14.5 billion in Q3, leaving a KRW 19.9 billion loss over the last four quarters. The 89.0% cost of sales ratio in Q2 2026 shows that a raw-material-heavy revenue mix can constrain margins.

Bullish factors are long-term supply contracts, rising space demand and the operating profit trend, while bearish factors are continued net losses, a higher cost ratio and customer concentration.

Meritz Securities estimated 2026 revenue at KRW 297.1 billion and operating profit at KRW 15.4 billion, and that estimate will be tested against fourth-quarter results.
