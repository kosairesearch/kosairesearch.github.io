02

Business structure

UniTest was founded in March 2000 and listed on KOSDAQ in December 2006. It is a back-end semiconductor test equipment maker that develops and sells memory module testers, memory component testers and burn-in testers, with final test equipment that inspects chip performance as its core product line.

The business is split between semiconductor test equipment and solar power. In first-half 2024 consolidated revenue, semiconductor test equipment accounted for 58.07%, the solar business for 37.91%, and consumable parts and maintenance for 4.02%; these ratios are as of first-half 2024.

In test equipment, HBM burn-in testers have emerged as a new growth driver. A March 2026 report said the company completed mass-production qualification testing of its HBM4 burn-in tester with SK hynix in February 2026.

SK hynix is the core customer, and the company is reported to have supplied SSD testers to Chinese memory makers CXMT and YMTC for several years.

In competition, an April 2025 report said only one global rival could perform HBM high-bandwidth performance testing, while several domestic burn-in tester makers were also in HBM4 qualification.

The new Pyeongtaek plant houses memory test equipment and next-generation solar cell lines, and its semiconductor capacity is reported to be two to three times that of the older Yongin plant.

04

Earnings analysis

On the disclosed annual figures, revenue rose from KRW 123.8 billion in 2022 to KRW 167.8 billion in 2023, a 35.5% increase, then fell 44.9% to KRW 92.4 billion in 2024 before rising 19.2% to KRW 110.2 billion in 2025. 2023 was the largest year and 2024 the smallest within 2022 to 2025.

Operating results moved from an operating loss of KRW 5.99 billion in 2022 to operating profit of KRW 7.14 billion in 2023, a turn to profit, and back to an operating loss of KRW 23.4 billion in 2024. The 2025 operating loss of KRW 21.1 billion continued the loss but was smaller than in 2024.

The operating margin moved from 4.3% in 2023 to -25.4% in 2024 and -19.1% in 2025. A 2024 report attributed weak first-half results to lower sales across all business segments, including semiconductor test equipment and solar.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders went from a profit of KRW 6.75 billion in 2023 to a net loss of KRW 26.5 billion in 2024 and KRW 20.8 billion in 2025.

Net income including non-controlling interests was a net loss of KRW 22.4 billion in 2025, a different figure from the controlling-shareholder result. Operating cash flow was KRW -19.8 billion in 2022, KRW 18.5 billion in 2023, KRW -20.4 billion in 2024 and KRW -22.8 billion in 2025.

Quarterly, Q3 2025 revenue was KRW 31.3 billion with an operating loss of KRW 443 million. Q4 2025 revenue was KRW 27.3 billion with an operating loss of KRW 12.3 billion, a widening loss. Q1 2026 revenue was KRW 22.4 billion with an operating loss of KRW 2.53 billion.

Q2 2026 revenue rose to KRW 50.5 billion, up 125.6% from the prior quarter, and operating profit of KRW 7.88 billion marked a turn to profit.

Q3 2026 revenue was KRW 49.4 billion, down 2.3% from the prior quarter and up 57.9% from a year earlier, with operating profit of KRW 15.1 billion, up 91.7% from the prior quarter. Net income attributable to controlling shareholders over the last four quarters totals KRW 6.88 billion.

05

Industry analysis

Semiconductor test equipment verifies chip performance and reliability in back-end processes, and recent demand has tracked investment in HBM for AI servers.

A May 2026 report described burn-in testers as core equipment that subjects finished semiconductors to high-temperature, high-voltage conditions and as essential equipment as HBM output expands.

Analysts say the memory market has turned to recovery, and an August 2026 report said major domestic memory makers are pursuing new facility investment to expand HBM capacity, which would also lift test equipment demand.

A 2025 report, by contrast, noted that test equipment demand contracted when the semiconductor downturn deepened, showing how sensitive equipment makers are to memory investment cycles. Chinese memory makers' capacity expansion in general-purpose memory also links to SSD tester demand.

In competition, one global tester rival handles HBM high-bandwidth performance testing, while domestic makers compete in burn-in tester qualification. Back-end peers such as HANMI Semiconductor are also exposed to HBM-related equipment demand.

Positioned in the cycle, the company is in a phase where test equipment revenue is rising as memory investment recovers, following losses in 2024 and 2025.

06

Outlook

A August 2026 report said the company expects second-half profit improvement to come from HBM-related equipment shipments, SSD G6 tester shipments and revenue recognition from solar projects.

In the disclosed results, Q3 2026 revenue of KRW 49.4 billion and operating profit of KRW 15.1 billion align with that trajectory. Analysts expect HBM4 burn-in testers, which carry higher margins than earlier products, to contribute to profit recovery.

A Korea Investment & Securities analyst said in a first-half 2026 report that the company could benefit from NAND conversion investment by domestic customers and new capacity additions expected in the second half of 2027.

Shipments to Chinese customers for SSD testers also continue, so the change in overseas revenue share is another item to track. However, HBM equipment timing has slipped several times with qualification testing, and in 2025 a DDR5 burn-in tester passed qualification without winning volume orders.

The next regular report is the 2026 annual report, and its disclosed full-year operating margin and debt ratio are the next checkpoints. The Q1 2027 results will show how much HBM4 burn-in tester revenue flows into quarterly sales.

Bull factors

HBM4 burn-in tester supply begins

A May 2026 report said the company disclosed that it had begun supplying HBM4 burn-in testers, and a KRW 29.2 billion equipment supply to SK hynix was reported the same month. Analysts said the tool was developed on the company's DDR5 burn-in technology and carries higher margins than earlier products.

Q3 2026 revenue of KRW 49.4 billion and operating profit of KRW 15.1 billion are confirmed in the disclosed results.

Chinese memory customers and solar backlog

A May 2026 report said the company has worked with Chinese memory makers CXMT and YMTC for several years and is believed to supply SSD testers. Analysts also said capacity expansion by Chinese makers could show up in equipment revenue from the second quarter. An August 2026 report put the solar EPC backlog above KRW 100 billion.

A turn in quarterly results

Operating profit turned positive at KRW 7.88 billion in Q2 2026 and rose to KRW 15.1 billion in Q3, up 91.7% from the prior quarter. Net income attributable to controlling shareholders turned to a profit of KRW 7.22 billion in Q2 2026.

Over the last four quarters, that line totals KRW 6.88 billion, a change from the annual losses of 2024 and 2025.

09

Bear factors

Annual losses and a low operating margin

The operating margin was -25.4% in 2024 and -19.1% in 2025, two consecutive years of operating losses. Q1 2026 still posted an operating loss of KRW 2.53 billion. The 2025 net loss attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 20.8 billion, a smaller loss but not yet an annual turn to profit.

Balance sheet and cash flow pressure

The debt ratio rose from 32.5% in 2022 to 82.3% in 2025. Operating cash flow stayed negative at KRW -22.8 billion in 2025. A report said short-term borrowings due within a year rose sharply at the end of 2024, and the company said it had increased borrowings to fund operations and facility investment.

Past supply delays and solar volatility

An April 2025 report said the DDR5 burn-in tester passed qualification but did not win volume orders. Solar EPC revenue also swung from KRW 50.1 billion in 2022 to KRW 71.9 billion in 2023 and KRW 23.5 billion in 2024, according to the same report. If HBM equipment timing slips with qualification schedules, the earnings path could be delayed as well.

10

Risk factors

Customer concentration risk

Revenue depends heavily on HBM burn-in tester demand centered on SK hynix. A May 2026 report cited a KRW 29.2 billion supply to SK hynix, but a change in that customer's investment schedule could swing quarterly revenue. As Chinese customers gain weight, overseas trade conditions also become a factor.

Memory investment cycle risk

A 2025 report said test equipment demand contracted when the semiconductor downturn deepened. Demand for test equipment is sensitive to the size and timing of memory capex. The 2024 and 2025 losses were tied to this cycle's revenue swings, so a delayed recovery could make the 2026 profit trend harder to sustain.

Balance sheet and cash flow risk

The debt ratio reached 82.3% at the end of 2025, far above 32.5% in 2022. Operating cash flow was negative in every year from 2022 to 2025 except 2023. If inventory and receivables keep growing, cash generation may lag profit recovery, which the 2026 annual report will show.

12

Overall view

UniTest recorded operating profit in Q2 and Q3 2026 as HBM4 burn-in tester supply and sales to Chinese memory customers came together. On the disclosed figures, Q3 2026 operating profit was KRW 15.1 billion.

On an annual basis, however, 2024 and 2025 were two consecutive years of losses, with a 2025 operating margin of -19.1%. The rise in the debt ratio to 82.3% at the end of 2025 and negative operating cash flow are also financial factors to weigh.

The bullish factors are the start of HBM equipment supply and the quarterly turn to profit, while the bearish factors are the annual loss record, financial burden, customer concentration and sensitivity to the memory investment cycle.

The solar business has volatile revenue and differs in character from the semiconductor test equipment business. Current multiples will be read differently depending on whether the profit recovery holds.

The next checkpoints are the full-year operating margin and debt ratio in the 2026 annual report, and how consistently HBM4 burn-in tester revenue appears in Q1 2027 results. This report is provided for information only and does not present an investment rating or target price.
