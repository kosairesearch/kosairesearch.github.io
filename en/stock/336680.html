02

Business structure

TOP RUN Total Solution, founded in 2004, is a parts maker for automotive electronics and displays that began with injection molding, circuits, precision optics and molds before expanding into automotive parts in 2020.

Its core product is the automotive backlight unit (BLU), supplied to major customers for instrument clusters, center information displays and head-up displays.

Reports indicate that automotive segment sales were KRW 299.8 billion in 2024, or 57.6% of total revenue of KRW 519.7 billion, and that BLUs alone accounted for KRW 195.4 billion, about 37%. First-half 2026 BLU sales reached KRW 121.6 billion, up more than 36.3% from KRW 89.2 billion a year earlier.

Overseas subsidiaries in Poland (KRW 96 billion), China (KRW 85.5 billion) and Vietnam (KRW 82.5 billion) also grew in the same period, according to reports.

On the OLED side, the company completed the acquisition of a 55% stake in an optical compensation equipment maker in January 2025, expanded into OLED inspection equipment and was registered as an official equipment partner of LG Display.

Its customers remain centered on LG Electronics' vehicle components division and LG Display, and in 2026 it added AUMOVIO in Germany, a Japanese premium automaker and a Vietnamese mobility company.

Reports describe the company as the only domestic maker that produces BLUs in-house, and it is moving toward module assembly by sourcing panels, BLUs and cover glass directly. The acquisition of LG Display's Nanjing vehicle-module business, disclosed in February 2026, was still being pursued as of September 2026.

04

Earnings analysis

On an annual disclosed basis, revenue rose from KRW 513.9 billion in 2023 to KRW 519.7 billion in 2024 and KRW 550.1 billion in 2025, increasing for two consecutive years from 2024 to 2025, with 2025 the largest year in the 2023 to 2025 period.

Operating profit moved the other way, from KRW 29.7 billion in 2023 to KRW 26 billion in 2024 and KRW 8.9 billion in 2025, declining for two consecutive years, and the operating margin fell from 5.8% to 5.0% and then 1.6%. Revenue grew while profit shrank.

In a nine-month 2024 report, the company attributed the margin decline to R&D spending, price increases and one-off costs tied to its listing. In 2025 it said rising cost pressure from global inflation and volatility in market selling prices changed its profit structure.

Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders went from KRW 22.7 billion to KRW 19.9 billion and then KRW 6.62 billion, also a two-year decline, while net profit including non-controlling interests was KRW 6.07 billion in 2025, a different figure.

Operating cash flow went from KRW 23.7 billion in 2023 and KRW 34.8 billion in 2024 to -KRW 9.11 billion in 2025, so cash flowed out even though accounting profit remained positive.

Quarterly, operating profit of KRW 5.76 billion in Q3 2025 fell to KRW 2.2 billion in Q4 2025, then turned into an operating loss of KRW 4.85 billion in Q1 2026. Q2 2026 revenue was KRW 178.8 billion with operating profit of KRW 299 million.

In Q3 2026, revenue of KRW 157 billion rose 5.8% year on year, but the company posted an operating loss of KRW 4.32 billion and a net loss attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 15.4 billion, a swing into loss from a year earlier.

Cash and cash equivalents fell from KRW 101 billion at end-2024 to KRW 66.1 billion at end-2025, while total liabilities rose from KRW 268.3 billion to KRW 363.1 billion.

05

Industry analysis

Automotive displays are evolving from instrument clusters into platforms for navigation, autonomous driving information and content. Automakers are increasing investment in larger, higher-resolution displays and user experience.

The company said the shift toward software-defined vehicles and larger, more premium in-car displays lifted demand for BLUs. LCD remains the mainstream in vehicle displays, but in a June 2025 report the CEO said OLED would rise quickly and that the company was pursuing vertical integration in OLED materials and parts.

On the other hand, mobile phones and large displays have stagnated, and some observers noted that total company revenue did not grow much during that period. Automotive segment sales were reported to be growing at double-digit rates each year.

In competition, the company is described as the only domestic maker that produces automotive BLUs in-house. A 2025 report said pricing negotiations with customers were under way to reflect inflation and other changes, and that a smooth negotiation could improve profitability.

The outcome of these price talks and the margin structure of module assembly will determine profitability alongside the BLU market. The swing from an operating loss in Q1 2026 to a small profit in Q2 2026 illustrates that sensitivity.

06

Outlook

In a September 2026 report, the company said its sales follow a pattern of a stronger first half and a weaker second half each year, and expected 2026 revenue to exceed the KRW 550.1 billion recorded in 2025 if the second-half BLU business proceeds as planned.

It also said that once the automotive display module business stabilizes, it expects an additional monthly revenue of more than KRW 80 billion.

The acquisition of the Nanjing vehicle-module business was still being pursued as of September 2026, and the company said completion would allow it to build an integrated production system linking BLUs and modules.

On orders, a module supply deal for digital clusters and center information displays for a German luxury automaker, worth KRW 52 billion over five years, is scheduled to start deliveries in 2027.

A separate five-year contract to supply digital clusters, center information displays and passenger displays to a Japanese premium automaker is also set to begin in 2027.

In a June 2026 report, the company said it won a 15.6-inch automotive BLU order from AUMOVIO, which will be used in a global automaker's center information display. The Vietnam unit began mass production of nine electric two-wheeler exterior parts in October 2026, a five-year supply worth KRW 100 billion.

In China, the company is supplying rear-seat entertainment display parts worth about KRW 58 billion to automakers including SAIC. Whether operating margins respond as these orders turn into revenue from 2027 onward is the next checkpoint.

08

Bull factors

BLU demand and record quarterly sales

First-half 2026 BLU sales rose more than 36.3% to KRW 121.6 billion, and Q2 2026 revenue of KRW 178.8 billion was described by the company as its quarterly high. The company attributes the demand to larger, more premium in-car displays and the shift toward software-defined vehicles. It also said automation and supply chain optimization improved its cost structure.

Order backlog and customer diversification

In January 2026 the company said its automotive display order backlog was about KRW 1.3 trillion, its highest level since founding. Contracts include a KRW 52 billion five-year German luxury automaker deal, about KRW 58 billion in Chinese rear-seat display supply and a KRW 100 billion five-year Vietnamese order. Its customer base is widening beyond the LG group.

Module shift and unit economics

The company says the finished unit price of a module is more than five times that of a standalone BLU. It attributed the Q2 2026 return to operating profit to cost improvements combined with higher sales. Completing the Nanjing module acquisition would create an integrated BLU-to-module production system.

Bear factors

Margin decline and return to losses

The operating margin fell from 5.8% in 2023 to 5.0% in 2024 and 1.6% in 2025. In Q3 2026 the company swung back to an operating loss of KRW 4.32 billion and a net loss attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 15.4 billion, and the last four reported quarters total a KRW 19.3 billion loss. Revenue growth has not yet translated into profit.

Cash flow and balance-sheet burden

Operating cash flow turned to -KRW 9.11 billion in 2025. Total liabilities rose from KRW 268.3 billion at end-2024 to KRW 363.1 billion at end-2025, with a debt ratio of 187.3%. In March 2026 the company raised about KRW 40 billion more through convertible instruments and redeemable preferred shares.

Customer concentration and M&A burden

Press reports point out that the core revenue structure remains tied to the LG group. Growth through acquisitions has raised reliance on external borrowing, and concerns about impairment of acquired businesses have been raised.

A related headline also referred to the termination of the Nanjing business transfer agreement, so the path for that deal could change.

Risk factors

Customer concentration

Critics argue that a large part of the automotive display business is tied to LG-affiliated customers. With automotive segment sales at 57.6% of total revenue in 2024, a change in orders from one customer or in the LG group's operating conditions could feed through quickly to results. The key question is how fast new customers grow as a share of revenue.

Acquisition execution

The Nanjing vehicle-module acquisition was still being pursued as of September 2026, and a related headline refers to a terminated transfer agreement. Costs tied to the OLED equipment subsidiary acquired in 2025 weighed on results, and impairment concerns have been raised. Whether and when the deal closes will change the growth path of the module business.

Funding and dilution

In February 2026 the company decided on a KRW 20 billion convertible bond (about 9.26% of total shares if converted), a KRW 7.5 billion perpetual convertible bond, and third-party preferred shares of 2,495,500 units (5.99% of total shares on conversion).

The conversion window for the convertible bond opens on March 3, 2027. Continued fundraising could add dilution and financial burden.

Overall view

TOP RUN Total Solution's revenue rose from KRW 513.9 billion in 2023 to KRW 550.1 billion in 2025, supported by demand for automotive backlight units, and the company described its Q2 2026 revenue of KRW 178.8 billion as its quarterly high.

However, operating profit declined from KRW 29.7 billion in 2023 to KRW 8.9 billion in 2025, and the operating margin fell from 5.8% to 1.6%.

After a small operating profit in Q2 2026, the company returned to an operating loss of KRW 4.32 billion in Q3 2026, so the link between revenue growth and profit recovery is not yet stable.

Supporting factors include an order backlog of about KRW 1.3 trillion, a customer base widening to Germany, Japan, China and Vietnam, and a shift toward higher-value module sales.

Offsetting factors include a net loss attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 19.3 billion over the last four reported quarters, operating cash flow turning negative in 2025, a debt ratio of 187.3% and about KRW 40 billion of additional funding in 2026.

A price-to-book ratio below 1 is one asset-based reference point, but no earnings-based PER is calculated. Whether the Nanjing module acquisition and the 2027 overseas OEM deliveries translate into reported profit is the central thing to watch. The next regular reports will show how the operating margin and operating cash flow move.
