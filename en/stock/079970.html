02

Business structure

Tobesoft has led the enterprise UI/UX development platform business since its founding in 2000. Its core product is Nexacro, a web-standards-based development platform supplied across manufacturing, finance and the public sector.

The company says it serves more than 2,700 corporate customers across virtually all industry sectors. Its TOBESOFT Pro series of system-building automation solutions, rolled out sequentially from 2024, was honored with a technology innovation award at the Internet Eco Awards 2026 in July 2026.

In public procurement, the company says it has ranked first in the UI/UX development platform category on the G2B digital service mall of the Public Procurement Service from 2024 through 2026.

Its references span public agencies, university academic systems, financial institutions and retailers, and a May 2025 report cited deliveries to IBK Industrial Bank and the National Health Insurance Service.

The company says its next-generation business system project for Korea Aerospace University was still under way as of August 2026. In May 2026 it unveiled N Pangea, an integrated low-code full-stack platform covering the development lifecycle, and in July 2026 it obtained AWS Partner Pass status to expand globally.

Competition is not purely head-to-head: at Suhyup's next-generation system, Tobesoft's X platform served as the screen development tool while LG CNS's DevOn was used as the framework.

04

Earnings analysis

On a reported annual basis, revenue went from KRW 43.2 billion in 2022 to KRW 43 billion in 2023 (down 0.5% year on year), KRW 28.1 billion in 2024 (down 34.6%) and KRW 26.8 billion in 2025 (down 4.8%).

Revenue declined for three consecutive years from 2023 through 2025, with 2022 the largest year and 2025 the smallest in the 2022–2025 period. The company attributed the 2025 revenue decline to the sale of inefficient consolidated subsidiaries as part of a restructuring.

Operating losses of KRW 3.82 billion in 2022 and KRW 3.48 billion in 2023 and KRW 1.98 billion in 2024 narrowed while the losses persisted, before the company posted an operating profit of KRW 743 million in 2025.

The operating margin moved from -8.8% and -8.1% and -7.0% to 2.8% in 2025, and the company cited lower SG&A expenses and lower non-operating costs as the drivers.

Controlling-shareholder net losses of KRW 15.6 billion in 2022, KRW 16.6 billion in 2023 and KRW 22.9 billion in 2024 widened over the period, before a controlling-shareholder net profit of KRW 989 million in 2025 marked a turnaround.

In 2024 the net loss widened even as the operating loss shrank, and past press coverage pointed to repeated bad-debt and impairment charges tied to other-company investments and loans to affiliates.

Net loss including non-controlling interests was KRW 15.7 billion in 2022, KRW 16.7 billion in 2023 and KRW 23 billion in 2024, before a net profit of KRW 969 million in 2025.

The debt ratio fell from 133.6% in 2022 and 134.8% in 2023 to 64.8% in 2024 and 51.2% in 2025, with total liabilities of KRW 11.4 billion and total equity of KRW 22.3 billion at year-end 2025.

Operating cash flow was KRW 1.2 billion in 2022, -KRW 3.84 billion in 2023, KRW 350 million in 2024 and KRW 3.27 billion in 2025. On a quarterly basis, revenue rose to KRW 6.98 billion in the fourth quarter of 2025, up 14.4% from the prior quarter, with operating profit of KRW 1.12 billion.

It then fell to KRW 5.85 billion in the first quarter of 2026, down 16.2% quarter on quarter, with an operating loss of KRW 34.74 million. The second quarter of 2026 returned to operating profit of KRW 567 million.

In the third quarter of 2026, revenue was KRW 5.88 billion (down 3.6% year on year), operating profit was KRW 1.05 billion (up 184.6% year on year) and controlling-shareholder net income was KRW 1.23 billion (up 102.6% year on year).

Revenue has held near KRW 6 billion while operating profit has grown, lifting the margin, and controlling-shareholder net income over the last four quarters totals KRW 2.85 billion, a pattern so far visible over only four quarters.

05

Industry analysis

Tobesoft operates in enterprise UI/UX and development platforms, a market tied to demand for AI transformation (AX). The company has made AI-driven development automation its core axis and is reshaping its business around a low-code platform covering the full development process.

Claiming first place in public procurement underlines that public-sector demand remains important to its revenue. However, in one press report, CEO-level executive Lee Woo-cheol argued that code and deliverables produced for public projects are hard to keep as company assets.

If development outputs cannot be reused across projects, the cumulative revenue effect may weaken. Peer LG CNS appears as a supplier of a framework used alongside Tobesoft's screen development tool, so both collaboration and competition coexist with large SI firms.

The scale gap with large listed IT companies such as NAVER, Samsung SDS and Kakao is wide.

Given its small revenue base, quarterly results can move sharply around the timing of specific public and financial-sector projects, as seen in the swing from the first-quarter 2026 operating loss to the strong third-quarter 2026 profit.

The 2025 financial statements carry an audit disclaimer on both separate and consolidated bases. KOSDAQ's market division said in April 2026 that the improvement period had ended, and a delisting decision follows through the exchange's listing review committee.

Because that outcome determines whether the stock stays listed, the process is the largest uncertainty separate from operating results.

Three years of revenue decline

Revenue declined for three consecutive years from 2023 through 2025, falling to KRW 26.8 billion in 2025 from KRW 43.2 billion in 2022. Third-quarter 2026 revenue of KRW 5.88 billion was down 3.6% year on year, so top-line growth has not yet appeared.

The sale of inefficient consolidated units that the company cited may be one-time or reflect a smaller core business, and the two are hard to separate.

Quarterly volatility and past investment losses

A first-quarter 2026 operating loss of KRW 34.74 million and controlling-shareholder net income of only KRW 20.01 million show how quickly quarterly profit can reverse. Past press reports pointed to repeated bad-debt and impairment charges from other-company investments and affiliate loans. Operating cash flow of -KRW 3.84 billion in 2023 also shows a period when cash generation was not stable.

Risk factors

Audit and listing process

The audit disclaimer on the 2025 statements overlaps with the delisting review. Whether the 2026 business report also carries a disclaimer is central to whether the company stays listed. If audit issues remain unresolved, trading conditions could change regardless of operating improvement.

Earnings durability and revenue trend

Quarterly profitability has been seen over only four quarters. Revenue has stayed near KRW 6 billion, so margin gains rely more on cost efficiency than on top-line growth. If cost savings run out or project wins slip, quarterly losses could reappear.

Public-sector model and asset accumulation

A model that depends on public procurement and large projects makes quarterly results sensitive to contract timing. The concern that public-project code and deliverables are hard to hold as company assets could weaken a repeatable revenue base. How far the low-code and AX shift changes this structure has not yet appeared in reported numbers.
