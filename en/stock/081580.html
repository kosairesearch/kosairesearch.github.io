02

Business structure

SUNGWOO ELECTRONICS CO was founded in 1987 and listed on KOSDAQ in 2007, with its headquarters, manufacturing facilities and research center in Ansan, Gyeonggi Province. Its business is split into three segments: mobile communication terminals, optical devices, and injection-molded products and others.

The mobile communication terminal segment makes Shield Can smartphone internal shielding parts, exterior parts and metal cases, and it also includes parts for laptop PCs.

The core of the optical devices segment is voice coil motors for autofocus and optical image stabilization units, which are actuator parts that shape camera performance.

The injection-molded products and others segment makes battery cases, smart cards and filler beauty devices, and press reports say its new businesses here are gaining weight. A newly established subsidiary was added to the consolidated group in 2025 to widen the business.

Production relies on the headquarters plant alongside factories in China and Vietnam, and overseas subsidiaries operate in Vietnam and Japan. Customers are global smartphone makers and camera module companies, and the company supplies them on a B2B basis.

Recently it has expanded into robot reducer-related parts, automotive electronic parts and top cap assemblies for cylindrical lithium-ion batteries.

Within the electronic components sector, a camera module maker is grouped as a similar company, while Samsung Electro-Mechanics and LG Innotek differ in scale and customer mix.

04

Earnings analysis

Revenue went from KRW 165.9 billion in 2022 to KRW 149.2 billion in 2023 (down 10.1%) and KRW 146.9 billion in 2024 (down 1.5%), then rose to KRW 182.7 billion in 2025 (up 24.4%), which was the largest year of 2022 to 2025 and 2024 the smallest.

A securities data site summary attributed the 2025 increase to on-device AI and foldable smartphone growth lifting demand for heat-dissipation solutions and interior and exterior parts.

Operating profit of KRW 2.81 billion in 2022 turned into an operating loss of KRW 4.46 billion in 2023, the loss widened to KRW 5.09 billion in 2024, and 2025 returned to operating profit of KRW 3.98 billion. The operating margin moved from 1.7% in 2022 to -3.0% in 2023 and -3.5% in 2024 before reaching 2.2% in 2025.

Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders went from KRW 5.64 billion in 2022 to a net loss of KRW 490 million in 2023 and KRW 4.19 billion in 2024, then turned to KRW 12.4 billion in 2025.

Net profit including non-controlling interests was KRW 12.1 billion in 2025, well above operating profit, and the news summary cited higher sales, cost reduction and valuation gains as drivers.

Operating cash flow was KRW 7.16 billion in 2022, KRW 6.45 billion in 2023 and KRW 217 million in 2024, before recovering to KRW 15.4 billion in 2025.

Quarterly, the third quarter of 2025 showed revenue of KRW 46.1 billion and an operating loss of KRW 243 million, and the fourth quarter moved to an operating profit of KRW 1.06 billion on revenue of KRW 47.5 billion.

The first quarter of 2026 recorded revenue of KRW 47.2 billion and operating profit of KRW 1.69 billion, while the second quarter fell to KRW 351 million in operating profit on revenue of KRW 46.4 billion, down 79.3% from the prior quarter.

The third quarter of 2026 brought revenue of KRW 50.7 billion, up 9.2% from the prior quarter and 9.8% from a year earlier, and operating profit of KRW 1.89 billion, up 439.1% from the prior quarter.

Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders over the last four quarters totaled KRW 8.81 billion, which shows how sharply quarterly profit can swing.

05

Industry analysis

In the smartphone market, on-device AI is increasing demand for thermal management parts that handle heat from the CPU and NPU, a favorable variable for SUNGWOO ELECTRONICS CO.

Rising shipments of foldable smartphones in China and other Greater China markets have also supported parts demand in the mobile communication terminal segment. With Apple entering the foldable market, the outlook is for the global foldable smartphone market to expand again from 2026.

This remains a market outlook, and when the company's revenue is recognized depends on the launch timing and volumes of set makers. Quarterly revenue rose from KRW 46.1 billion in the third quarter of 2025 to KRW 50.7 billion in the third quarter of 2026, which points to a gradual recovery.

On competition, domestic media reported that the company keeps a technical edge in its OIS optical business and is widening market share. In camera modules, a camera module maker is grouped as a similar company. Samsung Electro-Mechanics and LG Innotek are much larger in production capacity and customer breadth.

Because set-maker shipments and part adoption drive results in this structure, whether foldable and AI-phone adoption continues will be a key variable for the company's position in the sector.

Bear factors

Volatile quarterly profit

Operating profit fell to KRW 351 million in the second quarter of 2026 from KRW 1.69 billion in the first quarter, down 79.3%. The third quarter of 2025 had an operating loss of KRW 243 million. Quarterly profit can swing sharply with customer shipment timing.

A thin profitability base

The operating margin was 2.2% in 2025, a turn from losses in 2023 (-3.0%) and 2024 (-3.5%) but still a low level. Revenue fell 10.1% in 2023 and 1.5% in 2024. Whether the 2025 revenue recovery is a one-year rebound or a trend needs confirmation from further quarterly results.

Reliance on non-operating gains

Net profit of KRW 12.1 billion and net profit attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 12.4 billion in 2025 far exceed operating profit of KRW 3.98 billion. A news summary cited valuation gains among the drivers. Such gains may not recur every year, so they should be read alongside operating profit trends.

Risk factors

Customer and set-demand concentration

Results are tied to set shipments by global smartphone makers and camera module companies. If foldable volumes or AI-phone adoption come later than expected, revenue and operating profit could both move sharply. Order changes from a small number of customers can significantly shift quarterly results.

Overseas operations and costs

Production depends on a global setup that combines the domestic headquarters, factories in China and Vietnam, and subsidiaries in Vietnam and Japan. Exchange rate swings and changes in local labor and logistics costs can affect costs and margins. As overseas production grows, the effect of these cost variables becomes larger.

New-business execution and small-cap traits

Mass production and orders for new businesses such as robot reducer parts, automotive electronic parts and top cap assemblies have yet to show clearly in results. As a small-cap KOSDAQ stock, trading and price volatility can be large. Whether the new businesses can offset swings in the existing business is the key question.
