Lodmire Lifts Nine-Month Profit; Quarterly Losses Persist

NextEye swung to a nine-month 2026 operating profit on the back of its Lodmire beauty platform, yet it posted operating losses in the second and third quarters of 2026.

02

Business structure

The business driving NextEye's recent results is Lodmire, a global beauty platform. The company attributed the growth in nine-month 2026 results mainly to Lodmire's rapid expansion. Lodmire sells cosmetics and health functional foods on a membership basis, and press reports differ on how many countries it covers.

One report lists 11 countries and regions, including China, Hong Kong, Taiwan, Vietnam, Malaysia and Canada, while another says the number of countries entered has expanded to 20.

Its U.S. subsidiary Lodmire Bio reported nine-month 2026 revenue of KRW 174.4 billion and operating profit of KRW 5.4 billion, according to the company. That subsidiary accounts for most of the KRW 182.3 billion consolidated revenue for the same period.

Product lines include the anti-aging health supplement Iona Jin AKG, the ginseng-culture supplement Heracell One, and inositol, launched in April 2026. Membership grew from 200,000 at the end of 2025 to 410,000 at the end of the third quarter of 2026, and the company put the figure at 540,000 as of November 2026.

The second business is machine vision inspection equipment. A 2023 technical report listed LCD appearance inspection equipment, image-processing hardware and cosmetics as its main segments, and 2026 press coverage confirms the company still runs the inspection equipment business.

Although the company is classified under electrical equipment by industry code, the 2026 earnings driver is a consumer platform rather than power equipment, which makes direct revenue comparison with sector peers difficult.

Earnings analysis

Annual revenue fell from KRW 32.2 billion in 2022 to KRW 17.7 billion in 2023, a 44.9% decline, then rose to KRW 52 billion in 2024 (up 193.2% year on year) and KRW 120.1 billion in 2025 (up 130.7%), growing for two consecutive years from 2024 through 2025. 2025 was the largest year of the 2022 to 2025 period and 2023 the smallest.

Operating losses were KRW 4.7 billion in 2022, KRW 12.2 billion in 2023, KRW 5.48 billion in 2024 and KRW 35.6 billion in 2025, a loss in each of the four years. In 2025 revenue rose 130.7%, yet the operating loss widened to KRW 35.6 billion and the operating margin fell from -10.5% in 2024 to -29.6%.

The loss attributable to controlling shareholders grew to KRW 40.1 billion in 2025 from KRW 6.36 billion in 2024, and the net loss including non-controlling interests was KRW 41.3 billion. The debt ratio (total liabilities divided by total equity) rose from 28.2% in 2022 and 44.3% in 2024 to 139.1% in 2025.

At the end of 2025, total liabilities were KRW 55.5 billion and total equity was KRW 39.9 billion, of which the controlling shareholders' stake was KRW 43 billion. Operating cash flow was negative every year from 2022 to 2025, reaching -KRW 16.4 billion in 2025, the largest outflow of the four years.

On a quarterly basis, an operating loss of KRW 18.1 billion in the third quarter of 2025 and KRW 17.6 billion in the fourth quarter were followed by operating profit of KRW 3.56 billion in the first quarter of 2026.

The second quarter returned to an operating loss of KRW 362 million, and the third quarter posted a smaller loss of KRW 217 million.

Loss attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 7.52 billion profit in the first quarter of 2026, then KRW 847 million in the second quarter (down 88.7% from the prior quarter) and a net loss of KRW 1.96 billion in the third quarter.

The net loss attributable to controlling shareholders over the last four quarters totals KRW 19 billion.

According to the quarterly report filed on November 16, 2026, consolidated revenue for January to September 2026 was KRW 182.3 billion and operating profit was KRW 3 billion, against an operating loss of KRW 18 billion in the same period of 2025.

05

Industry analysis

NextEye is classified under electrical equipment, but its businesses fall into two distinct groups. One is machine vision inspection equipment, including LCD appearance inspection systems and image-processing hardware. The other is a consumer platform selling cosmetics and health functional foods on a membership basis.

A 2023 technical report described the company as a domestic leader in machine vision systems. Demand for inspection equipment tracks capital spending on manufacturing lines such as displays, a cycle that differs from the order cycle in power equipment.

The company said Lodmire's monthly sales set a record high for six consecutive months from February to July 2026, with July sales above KRW 24 billion compared with KRW 20.8 billion in June.

Sales in overseas markets move with country-specific regulation and local consumer trends, and membership-based selling rules differ from country to country.

The machine vision business competes with domestic and foreign inspection equipment makers, while Lodmire is compared with other cross-border direct-selling platforms.

Against sector peers such as LS ELECTRIC and HD Hyundai Electric, the demand drivers differ, so a comparison of sector-average positioning offers limited insight.

Earnings concentrated in one business

The nine-month profit leans heavily on Lodmire's operating income. Lodmire Bio's cumulative operating profit of KRW 5.4 billion is larger than the group's KRW 3 billion. If overseas platform sales slow, the cumulative profit trend could be disrupted, which is the main bear point.

Overall view
