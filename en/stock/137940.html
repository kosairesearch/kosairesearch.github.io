Report overview

NextEye's Beauty Pivot: Growth Meets Persistent Losses

NextEye has shifted its center of gravity from machine-vision inspection equipment to beauty and health-functional-food products, driving rapid revenue growth, yet the company posted operating losses and net losses attributable to controlling shareholders for four consecutive years from 2022 through 2025, meaning a durable return to profitability has not yet been confirmed.

02

Business structure

NextEye was founded in 1998 and listed on KOSDAQ in 2011, initially focusing on machine-vision-based inspection equipment used in semiconductor, display and secondary-battery processes.

Its product lineup included polarizing-film inspection systems, backlight-unit (BLU) inspection equipment, glass-edge crack detection (GECD) systems, image-capture board and image-processing hardware, and barcode readers.

The company holds eight subsidiaries, including Silonix, which specializes in image-processing hardware, cosmetics manufacturing and distribution entities in China, and a bio and cosmetics distribution entity in the United States.

In 2016 the controlling shareholder changed to Aesthetic International Beauty Chain Group and the company moved decisively into the beauty business, with Jin Gwang taking over as chief executive, a role he still holds.

The beauty and health-functional-food business is expanding through cooperation among U.S. subsidiary ROADMIR Bio, which oversees global network marketing, Hong Kong subsidiary ROADMIR International, and domestic affiliates.

As a result, the 2025 consolidated revenue mix shifted to 91% beauty and health-functional-food and 9% machine-vision inspection equipment, a complete reversal from the company's original business weighting.

The main growth channel has been the business-to-consumer e-commerce platform ROADMIR, whose subscriber base grew from 200,000 at the end of 2025 to 300,000 at the end of Q1 2026. The original core inspection-equipment business now remains a secondary segment with a single-digit revenue share.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual revenue fell 44.9% from KRW 32.2 billion in 2022 to KRW 17.7 billion in 2023, marking the low point, before rising for two consecutive years to KRW 52 billion in 2024 (up 193.2%) and KRW 120.1 billion in 2025 (up 130.7%), with 2025 the largest year and 2023 the smallest within the 2022-2025 span.

Operating performance, however, stayed in the red for four straight years: an operating loss of KRW 4.7 billion in 2022, KRW 12.2 billion in 2023 (loss widened), KRW 5.48 billion in 2024 (loss narrowed), and KRW 35.6 billion in 2025 (loss widened again).

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders followed the same four-year loss pattern, from a loss of KRW 4.78 billion in 2022 to KRW 16.5 billion in 2023 (widened), KRW 6.36 billion in 2024 (narrowed), and KRW 40.1 billion in 2025 (widened).

The operating margin deteriorated to -68.6% in 2023, improved to -10.5% in 2024, then worsened again to -29.6% in 2025, reflecting significant volatility.

On a quarterly basis, revenue rose for four consecutive quarters, from KRW 16.4 billion in Q2 2025 to KRW 44.1 billion in Q3 2025, KRW 53.8 billion in Q4 2025, KRW 54.2 billion in Q1 2026, and KRW 65.2 billion in Q2 2026.

Operating income, by contrast, alternated between profit and loss: a profit of KRW 1.87 billion in Q2 2025 turned to a loss of KRW 18.1 billion in Q3 2025, the loss continued but narrowed to KRW 17.6 billion in Q4 2025, turned to a profit of KRW 3.56 billion in Q1 2026, then turned back to a loss of KRW 362 million in Q2 2026.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders posted a profit of KRW 7.52 billion in Q1 2026 and remained profitable at KRW 847 million in Q2 2026, though down 88.7% quarter over quarter and 64.7% year over year; the fact that net income stayed positive even as operating income turned negative in Q2 2026 suggests non-operating items or tax effects cushioned the bottom line.

Operating cash flow was negative for four consecutive years from 2022 through 2025 (-KRW 2.91 billion, -KRW 1.71 billion, -KRW 5.34 billion, -KRW 16.4 billion), showing that cash generation actually worsened even as the top line expanded.

05

Industry analysis

NextEye is classified under the KOSDAQ electric-equipment sector, but since the bulk of its actual revenue comes from cosmetics and health-functional-food distribution, its business structure differs greatly from the large power-equipment makers grouped in the same sector.

South Korea's power-equipment industry has recently been booming to the point that artificial intelligence data centers are reshaping how order contracts are structured, with suppliers moving beyond standalone transformers and circuit breakers to bundle energy storage systems, generators and power-stabilization equipment into full data-center power-infrastructure packages.

The three major Korean power-equipment makers, Hyosung Heavy Industries, HD Hyundai Electric and LS Electric, had a combined order backlog of roughly KRW 32.7 trillion as of the first quarter of 2026, and industry sources note that order backlogs have already piled up for several years, meaning new orders placed today would not be delivered for years.

However, this power-equipment supercycle has little direct connection to NextEye's core revenue source, the beauty and health-functional-food business.

NextEye's original core business, machine-vision-based inspection equipment, belongs to the precision inspection equipment market serving semiconductor, display and secondary-battery processes, a cyclical structure tied to capital-expenditure cycles in those upstream industries.

The beauty and health-functional-food segment operates a network-marketing-based B2C distribution model exposed to competition in health-functional-food and cosmetics markets centered on China, Hong Kong and other ethnic-Chinese markets, and is influenced by country-specific distribution and sales regulations.

The company is expanding its top line by increasing the number of distribution countries and broadening its product lineup, but this market has relatively low entry barriers and correspondingly intense competition.

Outlook

Independent research firm Aris projected in a July 2026 report that, on the back of a normalization of the beauty and health-functional-food business, NextEye would record consolidated revenue of KRW 200 billion and operating profit of KRW 10 billion for full-year 2026, returning to profit after two years of losses.

The report did not present an investment rating or a target price.

The same report stated that ROADMIR's subscriber base grew from 200,000 at the end of 2025 to 300,000 at the end of Q1 2026, and that the company planned to expand its health-functional-food and cosmetics distribution footprint from 11 countries to 20 countries in 2026.

The report cited an anti-aging healthcare product called IEONA GENE AKG as a key expansion product, noting that its core ingredient, AKG, had been reported in animal studies at an overseas aging-research institute to extend healthy lifespan.

NextEye is reviewing an inspection-robot business combining artificial intelligence and humanoid robotics, built on its machine-vision division, as a candidate for its next new business, remaining at an early stage of exploring potential cooperation with a Chinese robot manufacturer.

At the end of June 2026 the company decided on a third-party allotment capital increase in which the largest shareholder participated, and the resulting 1,275,168 new shares were additionally listed on July 30, 2026.

First-half 2026 results were announced as consolidated revenue of KRW 119.4 billion, operating profit of KRW 3.2 billion, and net income of KRW 8.6 billion, with revenue reported to have grown 438.5% year over year and net income growing by more than 100%.

How far these expansion plans and capital raises translate into actual quarterly performance will need to be confirmed through future periodic disclosures.

Bull factors

Rapid Growth of the Beauty and Health-Functional-Food Platform

ROADMIR's subscriber base grew from 200,000 at the end of 2025 to 300,000 at the end of Q1 2026, and the company plans to expand its distribution footprint from 11 countries to 20 countries in 2026.

This segment generated 91% of 2025 consolidated revenue and drove a 438.5% year-over-year revenue increase in the first half of 2026. There were also reports that the health-functional-food product 'Hera Cell One' stabilized at a monthly revenue run rate of roughly KRW 2 billion. Product diversification is also progressing with the addition of the new anti-aging product IEONA GENE AKG.

A Proven Case of Quarterly Profitability

In Q1 2026 the company turned profitable with revenue of KRW 54.2 billion, operating profit of KRW 3.56 billion, and net income attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 7.52 billion.

Although operating income reverted to a loss in Q2 2026, net income attributable to controlling shareholders remained positive at KRW 847 million. Revenue has grown for four consecutive quarters, from KRW 16.4 billion in Q2 2025 to KRW 65.2 billion in Q2 2026, demonstrating continuity in top-line expansion.

New-Business Diversification and Insider Buying

NextEye is reviewing an inspection-robot business combining artificial intelligence and humanoid robotics based on its machine-vision technology and is exploring potential cooperation with a Chinese robot manufacturer.

Between October 14 and 16, 2026, managing director Lee Jae-won acquired an additional 15,934 common shares through open-market purchases. This new-business exploration and insider buying reflect diversification efforts and management attention, though they do not by themselves guarantee results.

Bear factors

Recurring Operating-Income Volatility

Operating income swung between profit and loss across five consecutive quarters, from a profit in Q2 2025 to losses in Q3 and Q4 2025, a profit in Q1 2026, and a loss again in Q2 2026.

On an annual basis, the company posted operating losses for four consecutive years from 2022 through 2025, and the 2025 operating loss of KRW 35.6 billion was wider than the prior year. This recurring pattern shows that revenue growth has not yet been matched by stable profitability.

Rising Financial Strain and Weakening Cash Generation

The debt-to-equity ratio jumped from 44.3% in 2024 to 139.1% in 2025, and operating cash flow was negative for four consecutive years from 2022 through 2025, with the shortfall widening to KRW 16.4 billion in 2025.

At the end of 2025, total equity stood at KRW 39.9 billion against total liabilities of KRW 55.5 billion, indicating a growing debt burden relative to equity. If reliance on external financing continues, ongoing scrutiny of financial stability will be warranted.

Dilution Concerns from Frequent Capital Raises

In 2026 the company carried out a 1-for-10 share consolidation followed by a third-party allotment capital increase in which the largest shareholder participated, with 1,275,168 new shares additionally listed on July 30, 2026.

These actions were explained as aimed at maintaining an appropriate float and securing funding, but if repeated, they can dilute per-share metrics and alter existing shareholders' proportional stakes. The limited trading volume typical of a small-cap stock is another factor to weigh.

Risk factors

Financial Soundness Risk

With the debt-to-equity ratio rising to 139.1% in 2025 and operating cash flow negative for four consecutive years, operating cash generation alone appears insufficient to sustain both business expansion and financial stability.

Total liabilities of KRW 55.5 billion exceeding total equity of KRW 39.9 billion at the end of 2025 also reflects a leveraged position. Should further external financing be required, the possibility of equity dilution or higher borrowing costs cannot be ruled out.

Business-Model and Regulatory Risk

With 91% of revenue concentrated in the network-marketing-based beauty and health-functional-food business, changes in country-specific distribution regulations or a slowdown in platform subscriber growth could have an outsized impact on results.

Expansion centered on China, Hong Kong and other ethnic-Chinese markets is sensitive to consumption trends and regulatory conditions in those regions. The original core machine-vision inspection equipment business has shrunk to a single-digit revenue share, limiting the diversification benefit it once provided.

Supply-Demand and Trading Risk

The stock has been designated an investment-caution issue on multiple occasions between June and July 2026 due to trading concentration in a small number of branches or accounts.

Major shareholder ownership, including that of largest shareholder Ample Ocean Limited, is relatively concentrated, and shares outstanding have continued to change due to frequent capital raises.

Limited trading volume and liquidity associated with the company's small market capitalization can be a factor that amplifies price volatility.

Overall view

NextEye has shifted its center of gravity from its original machine-vision inspection equipment business to beauty and health-functional-food distribution, posting two consecutive years of revenue growth in 2024 and 2025, with 2025 revenue of KRW 120.1 billion the largest figure within the 2022-2025 period.

Over the same period, however, operating losses and net losses attributable to controlling shareholders persisted for four consecutive years from 2022 through 2025, and the 2025 loss was wider than the prior year.

On a quarterly basis, the repeated pattern of turning profitable in Q1 2026 and reverting to loss in Q2 2026 shows that the gap between revenue growth and secured profitability has not yet narrowed.

The debt-to-equity ratio rising to 139.1% in 2025 and operating cash flow being negative for four consecutive years have increased reliance on external financing, and indeed the company carried out a share consolidation and a third-party allotment capital increase in succession during 2026.

Independent research firm Aris suggested in a July 2026 report the possibility of a return to profit with KRW 200 billion in annual revenue and KRW 10 billion in operating profit, but did not present a target price, and whether this outlook is borne out by actual quarterly results will need to be confirmed through future periodic disclosures.

A history of investment-caution designations and the limited trading liquidity typical of a small-cap stock are additional variables worth monitoring.
