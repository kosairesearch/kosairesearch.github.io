02

Business structure

PHA manufactures automotive door system parts. Its main products are door modules, tailgate latches, hood latches and hinges in the door and latch family, plus electrical components.

A brokerage analysis of the first quarter of 2025 noted that latch sales declined while door module and electrical component sales rose and supported top-line growth. Its customers are automakers including Hyundai Motor and KIA, so results track automaker production volumes.

A 2024 brokerage analysis said PHA would supply door modules, tailgate latches and hood latches to Hyundai Motor's U.S. EV plant, HMGMA. As of the first half of 2026, Korea accounted for about 65% of sales and the largest share of operating profit, according to WiseReport's aggregation.

Overseas bases include PHA Georgia in the United States and the new India plant, and PHA Georgia was set up to respond to expanding EV demand in North America. The company cites more than 400 patent applications and ISO certification as its technology base.

According to WiseReport, export accounted for 64.47% of automotive parts sales by the Korean entity as of December 2025, larger than domestic sales at 35.53%.

As a first-tier supplier to automakers, PHA is compared within the sector with listed peers such as Hyundai Mobis, HL Mando, Hyundai WIA, Seoyon E-Hwa and Sungwoo Hitech.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual revenue rose from KRW 1.02 trillion in 2022 to KRW 1.2 trillion in 2025. Growth was 10.8% in 2023, 1.6% in 2024 and 4.1% in 2025, and revenue has risen for three consecutive years from 2023 to 2025.

Operating profit climbed 111.1% from KRW 22.9 billion in 2022 to KRW 48.3 billion in 2023, peaked at KRW 51.5 billion in 2024, and then fell 5.9% to KRW 48.4 billion in 2025. The operating margin was 2.2% in 2022, 4.3% in 2023, 4.5% in 2024 and 4.0% in 2025, staying in the 4% range since 2023.

Profit attributable to owners rose from KRW 28.3 billion in 2022 to KRW 61.5 billion in 2023 (up 117.6%) and KRW 61.9 billion in 2024 (up 0.7%), then fell 25.3% to KRW 46.3 billion in 2025.

In the disclosed table, net profit including non-controlling interests carries the same figures as profit attributable to owners for these years.

In the first quarter of 2025, a brokerage noted North American sales rose 24% year on year on new plant operations and a weaker won, while the same firm flagged initial plant costs and U.S. tariffs as holding back operating profit.

Quarterly, revenue of KRW 290.6 billion and operating profit of KRW 9.53 billion in Q3 2025 rose to a peak of KRW 305.2 billion and KRW 13.3 billion in Q1 2026. Operating profit then fell for two consecutive quarters, to KRW 11.4 billion in Q2 2026 and KRW 8.46 billion in Q3 2026.

Q3 2026 revenue of KRW 314.1 billion rose 8.1% year on year, but profit attributable to owners swung to a net loss of KRW 5.1 billion. A Q1 2026 brokerage analysis said higher automaker output lifted sales, but slower EV demand and intensifying price competition hurt profitability.

Operating profit stayed positive while the owner-level result turned into a loss, so the effect of items below operating profit is a point to check in the next quarterly report.

At the end of 2025, total equity was KRW 844.9 billion and the debt ratio was 34.7%, while operating cash flow was KRW 88.6 billion, up from KRW 61.1 billion in 2024.

Industry analysis

The downstream market is driven by automaker production. A Q1 2026 brokerage analysis said higher export and domestic output lifted automaker production and was reflected in sales. The same analysis said slower growth in the EV market and intensifying price competition hurt profitability.

In May 2025, a brokerage expected annual revenue growth of about 5% through 2027 as the new U.S. and Indian plants came online despite weaker industry demand. Views on the cycle split between those who focus on rising production and those who focus on softer demand.

Tariffs are another variable, and the same brokerage saw local production replacing exports as easing the tariff burden. Q2 2026 revenue rose 3.8% from the prior quarter while operating profit fell 14.3%, and Q3 2026 revenue fell 0.9% from the prior quarter.

Within the sector, PHA is compared with listed auto parts makers such as Hyundai Mobis and HL Mando. Market share and order backlog figures are not part of the disclosed tables, so the cycle position has to be read from brokerage analysis and the earnings trend.

With the Korean entity's export share at 64.47%, FX rates and overseas production strategy feed directly into results.

Outlook

In May 2025, a brokerage said initial plant costs and U.S. tariffs would hold 2025 operating profit flat for a time, but that higher plant utilization would create fixed-cost leverage. The same report said shifting from exports to local production would ease tariff costs and lift the operating margin after 2026.

The estimates at that time were revenue of KRW 1.21 trillion and operating profit of KRW 51.3 billion for 2025. Disclosed 2025 results came in at revenue of KRW 1.2 trillion and operating profit of KRW 48.4 billion, below those estimates.

Q1 2026 operating profit of KRW 13.3 billion fell year on year, while Q1 2026 revenue of KRW 305.2 billion rose. On October 16, 2026, the board decided on a KRW 7 billion treasury share purchase trust contract with two brokerages.

The contract runs from October 19, 2026 to April 19, 2027, and the stated purpose is to cancel the repurchased shares to enhance shareholder value. The next periodic report, the 2026 annual report, is due by the end of March 2027 and will show Q4 profit and the full-year operating margin.

The Q1 2027 quarterly report will show whether profit attributable to owners returns to positive territory after the Q3 2026 net loss.

Bull factors

Expanding overseas production base

PHA Georgia was set up to respond to expanding EV demand in North America. A brokerage analysis summarized the U.S. new plant as operating from the second half of 2024 and the new India plant from the second half of 2025.

The same analysis said higher utilization would create fixed-cost leverage, and that shifting from exports to local production would reduce tariff costs. The disclosed 2025 operating margin of 4.0% was below 4.5% in 2024, so how much of this effect reaches margins is the item to check in the next periodic report.

Financial capacity and cash generation

Total equity at the end of 2025 was KRW 844.9 billion and total liabilities were KRW 293.3 billion. The debt ratio fell from 45.6% in 2022 to 34.7% in 2025. Operating cash flow was KRW 88.6 billion in 2025, up from KRW 61.1 billion in 2024.

Cash generation rose while the operating margin stayed in the 4% range from 2023, which underpins the balance sheet case.

Shareholder return activity

The treasury share purchase trust contract is worth KRW 7 billion. The contract is run through two brokerages from October 19, 2026 to April 19, 2027. The disclosed purpose is to cancel the repurchased shares to enhance shareholder value. Cash dividends are paid alongside, so share repurchases and dividends run in parallel.

Q1 2026 operating profit of KRW 13.3 billion was below the year-earlier level. The pattern shows margins not keeping pace with rising automaker production.

2025 profit decline and new plant costs

Price competition and below-the-line items

Overall view

PHA's disclosed results for 2022 to 2025 show revenue rising from KRW 1.02 trillion to KRW 1.2 trillion, but operating profit peaked at KRW 51.5 billion in 2024 and fell to KRW 48.4 billion in 2025. Profit attributable to owners also declined, from KRW 61.9 billion in 2024 to KRW 46.3 billion in 2025.

Operating profit fell for two consecutive quarters after peaking at KRW 13.3 billion in Q1 2026, and Q3 2026 turned into a net loss of KRW 5.1 billion attributable to owners.

Positive factors include the expansion of overseas production through PHA Georgia and the new India plant, a balance sheet with KRW 844.9 billion of equity and a 34.7% debt ratio at the end of 2025, and a KRW 7 billion treasury share purchase contract.

Negative factors include margin pressure from slower EV demand and price competition, heavy reliance on Korea, which accounts for about 65% of sales, and the Q3 2026 swing to a net loss.

The two sides carry comparable weight, and the key question is whether plant utilization and tariff costs actually translate into margins. Profit attributable to owners over the last four quarters totals KRW 43.5 billion.

The next checkpoints are the operating margin in the 2026 annual report and the direction of profit in the Q1 2027 quarterly report.
