02

Business structure

Hwacheon Machine Tool is a Gwangju-based machine tool manufacturer that produces and sells through domestic facilities and six overseas regions.

The company's business is split into two segments: the machinery segment, which makes and sells CNC lathes and other equipment, and the materials segment, which produces robot components and other items.

In 1978, the company established Korea FANUC as a joint venture with Japan's FANUC to produce NC lathe and industrial robot control system components, and Hwacheon Machine Tool currently holds a 5.4% stake in Korea FANUC.

Korea FANUC reportedly paid out roughly KRW 600 billion in dividends in 2023, from which Hwacheon Machine Tool is estimated to have received about KRW 32 billion, making this stake a notable source of non-operating income.

In 2019 the company acquired MTS to strengthen the upstream supply chain for core machine tool components, and in 2021 it established Sirius Investment to pursue new market opportunities.

In the domestic machine tool market, the company competes with players such as DN Solutions, SMEC, and a machine tool specialist spun off from Hyundai Wia, and in April 2026 it showcased an AI-linked autonomous manufacturing platform alongside these companies at the SIMTOS 2026 exhibition.

Separately, a similarly named but distinct company, Hwacheon Machinery (010660), is also listed on the KOSPI, and investors should be careful not to conflate the two companies' financial disclosures.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual revenue declined for three straight years, from KRW 244.1 billion in 2022 to KRW 225.7 billion in 2023 (down 7.5%), KRW 222.2 billion in 2024 (down 1.6%), and KRW 221.8 billion in 2025 (down 0.2%).

Over the same period, operating profit fell from KRW 8.18 billion in 2022 to KRW 4.56 billion in 2023 (down 44.3%), turned into an operating loss of KRW 741 million in 2024, and then swung back to an operating profit of KRW 4.22 billion in 2025.

Parent-attributable net profit dropped from KRW 37.5 billion in 2022 to KRW 10.6 billion in 2023 (down 71.8%) and KRW 8.65 billion in 2024 (down 18.1%), before recovering to KRW 11.4 billion in 2025 (up 31.5%).

On a quarterly basis, revenue rose from KRW 58.5 billion in Q2 2025 to KRW 65.7 billion in Q3 2025 (up 12.2%), then fell to KRW 51.2 billion in Q4 2025 (down 22.1%) alongside an operating loss of KRW 1.68 billion.

Revenue then recovered to KRW 55.2 billion in Q1 2026 (up 7.8%, with operating profit of KRW 1.87 billion marking a swing to profit) and to KRW 74.9 billion in Q2 2026 (up 35.8%, with operating profit of KRW 6.48 billion, up 246.9%), extending a two-quarter improvement streak from the Q4 2025 trough.

Parent-attributable net profit similarly fell to KRW 851 million in Q4 2025 before rising for two straight quarters to KRW 4.73 billion in Q1 2026 (up 456.1%) and KRW 9.31 billion in Q2 2026 (up 96.8%).

Parent-attributable net profit summed over the trailing four quarters (Q3 2025 through Q2 2026) came to KRW 20.5 billion. The operating margin slipped from 3.4% in 2022 to negative 0.3% in 2024 before recovering to 1.9% in 2025, while the debt ratio steadily declined from 37.3% in 2022 to 23.8% in 2025.

Operating cash flow rose from KRW 5.27 billion in 2023 to KRW 15.8 billion in 2025, suggesting relatively steadier cash generation than the swings in reported profit.

05

Industry analysis

The machine tool industry is cyclical, with demand tied to capital expenditure cycles in downstream sectors such as automotive, general machinery, semiconductors, shipbuilding, and defense.

Through 2026, expanded AI- and semiconductor-related investment has been credited with improving order indicators across the machine tool industry. Cumulative machine tool orders for January through April rose 11.2% year over year, with overseas orders up 25.7% over the same period.

SIMTOS 2026 in April drew 1,300 companies from 35 countries under the shared theme of 'AI autonomous manufacturing,' with major domestic players including DN Solutions, Hwacheon Machine Tool, a Hyundai Wia-affiliated machine tool maker, and SMEC competitively showcasing autonomous machining and unmanned production solutions.

Industry voices have also argued that, contrary to fears that the shift to electric vehicles would reduce machine tool demand, growing demand for battery, thermal-management, and new-material machining is expanding the industry overall.

At the same time, China's state-driven expansion of machine tool production capacity could intensify price competition in general-purpose models, even as a technology gap is said to persist in ultra-precision segments.

Hwacheon Machine Tool's exposure extends beyond the machine tool cycle itself through its materials segment producing robot components and its stake in Korea FANUC, linking it to the broader robotics and automation investment cycle as well.

08

Bull factors

Signs of an Earnings Trough Passing

With the operating loss of KRW 1.68 billion in Q4 2025 as the trough, both sales and operating profit increased for two consecutive quarters in Q1 and Q2 2026. Q2 operating profit reached KRW 6.48 billion, up 246.9% quarter-on-quarter and 212.2% year-on-year.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders also rose for two consecutive quarters over the same period, totaling KRW 20.5 billion over the trailing four quarters. Whether this trend continues into the second half of the year is the key question.

Improving Balance Sheet

The debt-to-equity ratio has steadily declined from 37.3% in 2022 to 23.8% in 2025. Operating cash flow also increased from KRW 5.27 billion in 2023 to KRW 15.8 billion in 2025, showing improving financial stability.

Total equity at the end of 2025 stood at KRW 339.5 billion, maintaining a large scale relative to market capitalization. The dividend yield has been formed at a level above the industry average.

Exposure to the Robotics and Automation Value Chain

The materials business division produces robot components and has been cited as the main driver behind the turnaround to profitability in 2025. The company also secures non-operating income sources related to robotics and automation through its 5.4% stake in FANUC Korea.

Industry indicators are also improving, with total machine tool orders from January to April 2026 up 11.2% year-on-year driven by expanded AI and semiconductor investment. At SIMTOS 2026, the company also showcased AI-integrated autonomous manufacturing solutions, keeping pace with competitors.

Bear factors

Stagnant Top Line

Annual sales peaked at KRW 244.1 billion in 2022 before declining for three consecutive years from 2023 to 2025, reaching only KRW 221.8 billion in 2025. Quarterly sales are also highly volatile, falling to KRW 51.2 billion in Q4 2025.

Whether the recovery in the first half of 2026 represents a structural rebound or a temporary phenomenon driven by quarterly order concentration needs to be confirmed through additional quarterly results.

Low and Volatile Operating Margin

The operating margin fell from 3.4% in 2022 to -0.3% in 2024, and remained low at just 1.9% in 2025, an absolute level that is still weak. Quarterly volatility is also significant, with an operating loss of KRW 1.68 billion recorded in Q4 2025. The observation period is still too short to conclude that earnings capacity has structurally improved.

Cyclical, Demand-Sensitive Industry

Demand for machine tools is a cyclical structure dependent on the capital investment cycle of downstream industries such as automobiles and general machinery. There are concerns that China's expanding production capacity could intensify price competition in general-purpose models.

If investment in downstream industries slows, the company's overall performance, including the materials business division, could become unstable again.

10

Risk factors

Industry and Order Volatility

The machine tool industry's demand is linked to the capital investment cycle of downstream industries, resulting in significant quarterly volatility in orders and sales.

The possibility cannot be ruled out that a period similar to Q4 2025, when sales fell 22.1% quarter-on-quarter and turned into an operating loss, could recur. A global economic slowdown or delays in downstream industry investment could lead to a gap in orders.

Overseas Operations and Currency Exposure

In addition to its domestic business sites, the company produces and sells products in six overseas regions, exposing it to exchange rate fluctuations and changes in local demand. Expanding production capacity by competing countries such as China could lead to price competition pressure in overseas markets.

Translation gains and losses may also occur in volatility as overseas subsidiary results are reflected in the consolidated financial statements.

Affiliate Structure and Potential for Confusion

Hwacheon Machinery (010660), which has a name similar to Hwacheon Machine Tools, is a separately listed company on the KOSPI, and care should be taken not to confuse the performance and disclosure figures of the two stocks.

As the owner family participates in management under a co-CEO system, additional verification is needed from an investor's perspective if transactions between affiliates or changes in ownership structure occur. How the market values non-operating assets, such as the FANUC Korea stake, is also a variable to watch.

Overall view

Hwacheon Machine Tool has shown signs of emerging from its Q4 2025 trough, supported by a 2025 swing to operating profit and two consecutive quarters of revenue and profit improvement in the first half of 2026.

That said, annual revenue has declined for three straight years since 2022 and the operating margin remains low in absolute terms, so whether this represents a structural recovery or part of normal quarterly volatility requires further confirmation.

The balance sheet has stabilized, with a declining debt ratio and improving operating cash flow, while the materials segment and the Korea FANUC stake provide diversification into the robotics and automation value chain.

On valuation, the stock is distinguished by an earnings multiple below, and a dividend yield above, sector averages. The industry backdrop combines signs of order recovery tied to AI and semiconductor investment with concerns over China-driven price competition.

Tracking the next quarterly and annual disclosures will be important to confirm whether the recent improvement trend continues.
