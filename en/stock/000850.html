Business structure

Hwacheon Machine Tool runs two business lines: a machinery segment that makes metal-cutting machine tools, and a materials segment that casts components for machine tools, automobiles, industrial machinery and robots.

The machinery side covers CNC lathes, milling machines and machining centers along with related parts, and it builds processing systems for everything from single parts to small-batch, high-mix production.

The materials segment supplies machine-tool castings alongside automotive and robot-related castings, and it also sells kitchenware and wooden patterns.

Finished machines are assembled at the headquarters and the Pyeongdong plant, castings are produced at the Yeonggwang plant, and an Indian subsidiary runs injection molding and mold making.

As of year-end 2021, the machinery segment accounted for 79.7% of sales and the materials segment for 20.3%, so the mix as of November 2026 may differ from that older split. Exports made up more than half of 2021 sales, with Germany, the United States and Singapore as the largest destinations.

Market coverage has ranked the company among Korea's three leading machine-tool specialists, and it points to the early domestic NC lathe localization as a technical foundation. Acquisitions of subsidiaries have widened its procurement base for key components, while an investment subsidiary explores new markets.

The core customer base is manufacturers that need metal machining, and the casting business ties into demand from automotive and robot applications.

Earnings analysis

Annual revenue went from KRW 244.1 billion in 2022 to KRW 225.7 billion in 2023 (down 7.5% year on year), KRW 222.2 billion in 2024 (down 1.6%) and KRW 221.8 billion in 2025 (down 0.2%).

Revenue has declined for three consecutive years from 2023 through 2025, with 2022 the largest year and 2025 the smallest in the 2022 to 2025 window.

Operating profit fell from KRW 8.18 billion in 2022 to KRW 4.56 billion in 2023 (down 44.3%), turned into an operating loss of KRW 741 million in 2024, and returned to operating profit of KRW 4.22 billion in 2025.

Operating margin was 3.4% in 2022, 2.0% in 2023, -0.3% in 2024 and 1.9% in 2025, so margins weakened alongside shrinking sales.

Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders went from KRW 37.5 billion in 2022 to KRW 10.6 billion in 2023 (down 71.8%) and KRW 8.65 billion in 2024 (down 18.1%), before rebounding to KRW 11.4 billion in 2025 (up 31.5%).

Operating cash flow dropped from KRW 31 billion in 2022 to KRW 5.27 billion in 2023 and then recovered to KRW 12.9 billion in 2024 and KRW 15.8 billion in 2025.

The debt ratio declined from 37.3% in 2022 to 23.8% in 2025, and at year-end 2025 total equity stood at KRW 339.5 billion with total liabilities of KRW 81 billion.

Quarterly sales were KRW 65.7 billion in the third quarter of 2025, KRW 51.2 billion in the fourth quarter (down 22.1% quarter on quarter), KRW 55.2 billion in the first quarter of 2026 and KRW 74.9 billion in the second quarter (up 35.8%).

Operating profit was KRW 3.68 billion in the third quarter of 2025, an operating loss of KRW 1.68 billion in the fourth quarter, KRW 1.87 billion in the first quarter of 2026 and KRW 6.48 billion in the second quarter (up 246.9% quarter on quarter), the highest of the last five quarters.

The third quarter of 2026 brought sales of KRW 59.5 billion (down 20.6% quarter on quarter and 9.5% year on year) and an operating loss of KRW 198 million, after operating profit a year earlier, along with a net loss attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 2.77 billion.

The last four quarters of controlling-shareholder net profit sum to KRW 12.1 billion, above the 2025 full-year figure of KRW 11.4 billion, but the quarterly swings are large enough that margin direction should be checked against the cost and selling expense lines in the annual report.

Industry analysis

Machine tools are an equipment industry highly sensitive to capital expenditure cycles. Domestic facility investment, automotive production and export orders drive demand, and because there is a lag between orders and production, inspection and revenue recognition, improving orders reach sales later.

Hwacheon Machine Tool holds both metal-cutting equipment and castings, so demand for finished machines and demand for automotive and robot castings can move on different timelines.

Expanded capital spending tied to AI, semiconductors and data centers is cited as an upside factor for the machine tool cycle, but how much of that demand reaches Hwacheon Machine Tool's sales should be gauged from quarterly revenue.

On competition, the company is regarded as one of Korea's three leading machine tool specialists and points to its early domestic NC lathe localization as a technical starting point.

Still, it depends heavily on imports for key components such as controllers and bearings, so exchange rates and overseas supply conditions affect the cost structure.

Because its export share is high, compliance with strategic-goods export control rules and trade sanctions in major markets determines whether overseas sales can be sustained. A key check for the industry is whether domestic facility investment indicators and export order flows move together.

The company's operating margin was 1.9% in 2025, and comparisons with listed peers should be made alongside revenue scale and margins.

Thin margins and cost structure

Risk factors

Industry and capex cycle

Machine tool demand follows domestic capital spending, automotive production and export orders. Because there is a lag before orders show up as revenue, a turnaround in the cycle may reach reported earnings late.

The materials business is also exposed to auto production, so the two segments may not move in the same direction.

Costs, FX and component sourcing

A significant share of key components such as controllers and bearings is imported. With a high export share, currency moves feed directly into pre-tax profit. Higher component prices or weaker logistics would add cost pressure on margins that are already thin.

Trade controls and export dependence

For machine tools with multi-axis compound processing technology, compliance with strategic-goods export control rules and trade sanctions in major markets determines whether exports can continue. As of year-end 2021, the top export destinations were Germany, the United States and Singapore. Tighter regulation in any of these markets could hit overseas sales directly.
