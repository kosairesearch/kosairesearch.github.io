DB Securities: Client Assets Grow, Earnings Swing

02

Business structure

DB Securities is a small-to-mid-sized brokerage within a group whose largest shareholder is a group insurance affiliate. Its main businesses are wealth management (WM), investment banking (IB), project financing (PF), proprietary investment (PI), and bond trading.

A June 2026 securities analysis reported that the WM segment returned to operating profit of KRW 11.9 billion in the first quarter of 2026 after a loss in the prior quarter, while the IB segment posted KRW 10.9 billion in the same quarter.

The retail base rests on brokerage commissions and sales of funds and ELS products, and reports said a strong stock market in the first half of 2026 lifted retail inflows and product sales that drove the WM rebound.

The company introduced a waiver of domestic stock trading commissions for new non-face-to-face accounts through the end of 2028 to win retail clients. Its asset management affiliate widened its fee base through corporate fund inflows and growth in ETF and public fund assets.

Its savings bank affiliate recorded a much smaller provisioning burden on real estate PF loans, which helped its turnaround. The IB unit is pursuing selective deals with strong credit amid a slowing property market, while steadily adding staff. A weakness cited is a narrower market share than larger brokerages.

The company says the rise in consolidated client assets to KRW 126.4 trillion broadens its base for WM fee income. PF exposure, with a high share of bridge loans and mezzanine and junior tranches, is still cited as a burden on financial management.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual results from regulatory filings show 2023 as the low point, followed by recovery in 2024 and 2025. Revenue in 2023 was KRW 1.21 trillion, down 1.3% year on year, and operating profit was KRW 21.3 billion, down 10.6%.

Revenue then rose to KRW 1.32 trillion in 2024 (up 9.2%) and operating profit to KRW 61.9 billion (up 190.5%). In 2025 revenue reached KRW 1.92 trillion (up 45.8%) and operating profit KRW 116.7 billion (up 88.7%), the largest year of 2022–2025.

Profit attributable to controlling shareholders swung from a net loss of KRW 77.39 million in 2022 to a turnaround to profit of KRW 6.17 billion in 2023, then rose to KRW 50.1 billion in 2024 and KRW 81.4 billion in 2025, two consecutive years of growth.

Net income including non-controlling interests was KRW 95.5 billion in 2025, a different figure from the controlling-shareholder profit and to be read separately. The operating margin rose from 1.9% in 2022 and 1.8% in 2023 to 4.7% in 2024 and 6.1% in 2025.

Quarterly, revenue rose to KRW 589.8 billion in the fourth quarter of 2025 (up 51.7% from the prior quarter) while operating profit fell to KRW 15.7 billion (down 64.4%).

Revenue recovered to KRW 1.08 trillion in the first quarter of 2026 (up 82.3% quarter on quarter) with operating profit of KRW 30.2 billion (up 92.0%), and the second quarter reached KRW 47.5 billion in operating profit, the highest of the last five quarters, on revenue of KRW 1.27 trillion.

In the third quarter of 2026 revenue was KRW 976.6 billion, up 151.1% year on year, yet operating profit fell to KRW 11.6 billion (down 73.8%) and controlling-shareholder profit to KRW 6.8 billion (down 79.0%).

Revenue growth did not carry through to profit, so the quality of brokerage and investment income needs separate attention. Controlling-shareholder profit over the last four quarters totaled KRW 81.6 billion.

On the balance sheet, the debt-to-equity ratio rose from 808.9% in 2024 to 1,028.9% in 2025, with total liabilities of KRW 13 trillion and total equity of KRW 1.26 trillion at end-2025. Operating cash flow was KRW -1.02 trillion in 2025.

For a brokerage, operating cash flow is heavily driven by changes in customer deposits and margin lending, so it does not necessarily move in line with reported profit.

05

Industry analysis

The core demand drivers for brokerages are domestic stock trading volume and client deposits, and brokerage commissions and investment income move with both.

In the first half of 2026, reports described a recovery in trading volume and a larger asset management market that improved the earnings profile of small and mid-sized brokerages.

In the third quarter of 2026, however, a weaker stock market reduced brokerage and investment income, so quarterly results swung sharply within the same year.

Margin lending demand tends to rise in active markets, and margin lending limits are tied directly to equity capital, so capital raising links to earning capacity.

DB Securities said its standalone equity capital passed KRW 1 trillion at the end of the first half of 2026, the first time it reached that level since its founding. The property PF market remains under pressure, and brokerages are responding with selective deals that have strong credit.

Relative to larger brokerages, DB Securities is generally viewed as having a narrower market share. Capital strengthening and the PIB (private banking and investment banking linkage) model are presented as ways to narrow that gap.

Based on the reported information, the sector appears to alternate between phases of trading volume recovery and correction.

Outlook

When it reported first-half 2026 results, the company said it expected greater market volatility and a weaker second half, and it said it would protect stable earnings through its financial strength and portfolio.

It also said it would focus on raising shareholder value by upgrading its core businesses and strengthening the operating capability of the PI segment. In its third-quarter 2026 release, a company official said it would expand shareholder returns under its corporate value enhancement plan.

With KRW 1 trillion of equity, the company plans to expand investment product supply and margin lending to clients and strengthen its PIB model. Acquisition finance and venture capital supply are presented as core areas for higher-return business expansion.

The waiver of domestic stock trading commissions for non-face-to-face accounts runs through the end of 2028, which could affect the trend in commission revenue. Reports also said the company is pursuing an early position in security token offerings (STO) and other new businesses.

A June 2026 media report said the company aims to return to annual net income of KRW 100 billion, while 2025 net income was KRW 95.5 billion. The next checkpoints are the annual operating margin and capital trends in the 2026 annual report, and WM and IB segment profit in the first quarter of 2027.

08

Bull factors

Expanding client asset base

Consolidated client assets reached KRW 126.4 trillion at the end of September 2026, up 14.4% from the end of 2025. Inflows continued even as the market weakened, which the company links to a broader base for brokerage and wealth management fees. The commission waiver and expanded fund and ELS sales are tools to grow that base.

Capital strengthening and financial headroom

Standalone equity capital passed KRW 1 trillion in the first half of 2026, and a KRW 150 billion hybrid capital issue was completed in June. The consolidated net capital ratio rose to 421.3%, up 77.4 percentage points from end-2025.

Because margin lending limits are tied to equity, this headroom widens the room to expand business when markets are active.

Earnings recovery and group support

Operating profit was KRW 30.2 billion in the first quarter of 2026, up 92.0% quarter on quarter, and KRW 47.5 billion in the second quarter was the highest of the last five quarters. Controlling-shareholder profit grew for two consecutive years from 2024 to 2025.

The creditworthiness of the group centered on its insurance affiliate is cited as a factor that supports the company's own credit rating.

09

Bear factors

High sensitivity to market downturns

Third-quarter 2026 operating profit fell 73.8% year on year to KRW 11.6 billion, and controlling-shareholder profit fell 79.0% to KRW 6.8 billion. The company attributed the decline to lower brokerage and investment income amid a weak stock market.

Because quarterly results shift sharply with trading conditions, whether the recovery holds depends on trading volume trends.

High leverage and negative operating cash flow

The debt-to-equity ratio rose from 808.9% in 2024 to 1,028.9% in 2025, with total liabilities of KRW 13 trillion at end-2025. Operating cash flow was KRW -1.02 trillion in 2025, a large gap from operating profit of KRW 116.7 billion in the same year. Capital raising added headroom, but liabilities still far exceed equity.

PF exposure and a narrow market share

PF exposure, with a high share of bridge loans and mezzanine and junior tranches, is still cited as a burden on financial soundness. With the property market slowing, PF stress could again weigh on reported results. A narrower market share than larger brokerages is also cited as a disadvantage in scale.

10

Risk factors

Market and trading risk

Brokerage commissions and investment income are sensitive to domestic trading volume and interest rate moves. As in the third quarter of 2026, a weak market can cut quarterly operating profit sharply.

Bond trading is exposed to rate volatility, and the company issued KRW 200 billion in public bonds in early September 2026 to improve its response to rate changes.

Credit and PF risk

PF exposure with a high share of bridge loans and junior tranches leaves the company exposed to losses if the property market weakens further. The company limits IB underwriting to strong deals, but expanding margin lending also raises credit risk on borrowing clients. Provisioning and exposure disclosures in the annual report will be needed to assess this.

Capital and fee policy risk

Margin lending limits are linked to equity, so the pace of capital strengthening limits the pace of business expansion. The waiver of domestic stock trading commissions for non-face-to-face accounts through 2028 could reduce commission revenue in the near term. If PI segment investment results weaken, the capital-building effect could also be constrained.

Overall view

DB Securities is a small-to-mid-sized brokerage whose revenue, operating profit, and controlling-shareholder profit recovered from the 2023 low through 2025. Operating profit of KRW 116.7 billion in 2025 and a 6.1% operating margin were the highest since 2022.

However, third-quarter 2026 operating profit fell to KRW 11.6 billion as the stock market weakened, showing how volatile quarterly results can be. Consolidated client assets rose to KRW 126.4 trillion and standalone equity passed KRW 1 trillion, which strengthens the fee base and capital headroom.

On the other side, the debt-to-equity ratio climbed to 1,028.9%, PF exposure remains a burden, and operating cash flow in 2025 was KRW -1.02 trillion. On valuation, the price-to-book ratio sits well below 1x, while the earnings-based multiple moves with quarterly profit swings.

The next checkpoints are the annual operating margin and capital trends in the 2026 annual report, and first-quarter 2027 results. Strengths and weaknesses coexist in this setting.
