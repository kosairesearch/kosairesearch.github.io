02

Business structure

Samick THK is a machinery and equipment company whose core business is LM System linear motion components. The company says it began producing LM guides in Korea for the first time in the 1990s and grew into a domestic leader in the field.

It now focuses on automation equipment and robot industry development alongside its LM System business, while preparing to become a comprehensive global automation component maker. The business is broadly divided into the LM System segment and the Mechatro System segment, which covers automation equipment.

A April 2023 press report said the Mechatro System segment's share of revenue rose from 38% of the total in 2021 to nearly half in 2022. This reflects a shift toward equipment as demand for robots and facilities grew, showing the company moving from parts supply toward system supply.

The company has been regarded as a leader in robot components and industrial automation parts and equipment, and a press report said it expanded into contract manufacturing (OEM) of autonomous mobile robots for domestic robot companies.

Its main customers are semiconductor and secondary battery manufacturing facilities, so capital spending cycles in those industries strongly shape the direction of revenue.

A decline of 27.6% in first-half 2025 revenue has been attributed to lower facility investment, which shows the sensitivity of this structure in recent results. The latest segment revenue amounts can be checked in the segment information note of the annual business report.

04

Earnings analysis

Samick THK's results declined for three straight years from 2023 through 2025 after peaking in 2022, and quarterly figures began showing signs of recovery in 2026.

Annual revenue fell from KRW 339.1 billion in 2022 to KRW 315.6 billion in 2023 (down 6.9% year on year), KRW 304.4 billion in 2024 (down 3.6%), and KRW 222.2 billion in 2025 (down 27.0%). Among 2022 to 2025, 2022 was the largest year and 2025 the smallest.

Operating profit dropped from KRW 20.6 billion in 2022 to KRW 6.94 billion in 2023 (down 66.4%) and KRW 565 million in 2024 (down 91.9%), and turned into an operating loss of KRW 35.5 billion in 2025. The operating margin declined from 6.1% in 2022 to 2.2% in 2023, 0.2% in 2024, and -16.0% in 2025.

Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders fell from KRW 16.6 billion in 2022 to KRW 985 million in 2023 (down 94.1%), rose to KRW 1.53 billion in 2024 (up 55.7%), and then turned into a net loss of KRW 45 billion in 2025.

Net income including non-controlling interests shows the same direction, with a net loss of KRW 45 billion in 2025.

On a quarterly basis, the operating loss widened from KRW 11 billion in the third quarter of 2025 to KRW 11.4 billion in the fourth quarter, narrowed to KRW 3.81 billion in the first quarter of 2026, and turned into operating profit of KRW 1.69 billion in the second quarter.

In the third quarter of 2026, revenue reached KRW 90.4 billion (up 16.4% from the prior quarter) and operating profit reached KRW 4.05 billion (up 139.5% from the prior quarter). Revenue has risen for four consecutive quarters from the fourth quarter of 2025 through the third quarter of 2026.

Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders was a net loss of KRW 18.23 million in the second quarter of 2026, a narrower loss, and turned into a profit of KRW 2.33 billion in the third quarter. However, the net loss attributable to controlling shareholders over the last four quarters totals KRW 5.64 billion.

Operating cash flow went from negative KRW 13 billion in 2022 to KRW 5.52 billion in 2023, KRW 27 billion in 2024, and KRW 24.1 billion in 2025, remaining positive.

The debt ratio rose from 110.7% in 2022 to 149.5% in 2025, and at the end of 2025 total equity was KRW 157.4 billion, all attributable to controlling shareholders, against total liabilities of KRW 235.4 billion.

The size of one-off items should be checked in the notes to the periodic report, so whether the quarterly profits in 2026 continue is the key point for interpreting these results.

Industry analysis

Samick THK's results are tightly linked to the capex cycle of its downstream industries. A FnGuide company overview compiled during 2025 said recovery in sales is expected as the OLED market expands and demand recovers in memory semiconductors and secondary batteries.

The same overview attributed the 27.6% drop in first-half 2025 revenue and the swing to an operating loss to lower facility investment. In an April 2023 press report, the company's CEO said that, given industry characteristics, revenue tends to fall sharply the year after a large investment year.

The decline in revenue for three straight years since 2023 is consistent with that explanation. In robotics, demand expansion for autonomous mobile robot OEM work and for robot companies is cited as the basis for diversification.

The company's own profile also places automation equipment and robot industry development at the center, which shows its customer base widening from semiconductor and secondary battery facilities toward robotics and automation demand.

Its competitive position relies on its status as a leader in domestic LM guides, and market share versus listed peers should be checked against disclosures. On the cycle, the rise in quarterly revenue in 2026 can be read as an early sign of recovery in facility demand, but interpretations may differ.

Whether the decline since 2023 or the increase in 2026 represents the trend will be judged from quarterly disclosures after 2027. Changes in semiconductor and secondary battery investment plans and the company's quarterly order flow are the indicators that will show whether demand is recovering.

Bull factors

Third-quarter revenue of KRW 90.4 billion was 68.8% higher than a year earlier. The shift from annual losses to quarterly profits provides an early basis for a recovery in results.

Rising Mechatro System share and robotics diversification

A April 2023 press report said the Mechatro System segment's share of revenue rose from 38% in 2021 to nearly half in 2022. As the share of automation equipment grows, the business is shifting from component supply toward system supply.

Expansion into contract manufacturing of autonomous mobile robots for domestic robot companies also supports the diversification case. Whether these businesses reduce the volatility of the capex cycle should be verified with segment revenue in the periodic reports.

Positive operating cash flow despite reported losses

Operating cash flow remained positive at KRW 5.52 billion in 2023, KRW 27 billion in 2024, and KRW 24.1 billion in 2025. In 2025 the company recorded an operating loss of KRW 35.5 billion and a net loss attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 45 billion, yet cash generation continued.

This pattern shows the company generating cash separately from its reported profit measures. The composition of cash flows should be checked in the detailed line items of the cash flow statement.

Bear factors

The operating loss in 2025 was KRW 35.5 billion, and the operating margin fell to -16.0% from 6.1% in 2022. The net loss attributable to controlling shareholders reached KRW 45 billion, reversing a profit in 2024. As revenue declined to KRW 222.2 billion, margin pressure intensified.

How far the 2026 quarterly profits offset this loss on an annual basis is the key checkpoint for the bearish case.

Rising debt ratio and financial burden

The debt ratio rose from 110.7% in 2022 to 149.5% in 2025. Total liabilities at year-end 2025 were KRW 235.4 billion, exceeding total equity of KRW 157.4 billion. If equity capacity shrinks while losses continue, financial flexibility could be constrained. The debt structure and repayment schedule should be checked in the notes to the periodic reports.

Three years of revenue decline and a gap to medium-term goals

Revenue fell for three consecutive years from 2023 through 2025, reaching KRW 222.2 billion in 2025. The gap from the KRW 700 billion revenue target for 2026 presented in an April 2023 press report is large. The CEO referred to revenue falling sharply the year after a heavy investment year.

If the capex cycle turns down again, quarterly profits could disappear quickly, which is central to the bearish case.

Risk factors

Governance and legal uncertainty

A May 2025 press report said the company issued its first official statement after trading on the main market was halted over alleged embezzlement and breach of trust by a former executive. The company denied any plan to enter construction and said it would work to normalize trading.

Some small shareholders reportedly moved to form a minority shareholder association. The outcome of legal proceedings and the progress toward normal trading should be confirmed through follow-up disclosures.

Dependence on the capex cycle

The main customers are semiconductor and secondary battery manufacturing facilities, and revenue depends heavily on investment in those industries. First-half 2025 revenue fell 27.6%, with lower facility investment cited as the cause. If investment cuts persist, the swing to quarterly profit could reverse. Changes in investment plans in related industries should be monitored each quarter.

Earnings durability and few profitable quarters

Operating profit has been positive in only two quarters, the second and third of 2026. Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders has been positive in only the third quarter, and the net loss over the last four quarters totals KRW 5.64 billion.

With the debt ratio at 149.5%, swings in revenue are reflected strongly in profit, which can make quarterly results volatile. Whether profits continue in the fourth quarter and the first quarter of 2027 is the item to confirm.
