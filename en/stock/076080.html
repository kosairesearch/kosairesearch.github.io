Profit Turn Tests Order Recovery

02

Business structure

Welcron Hantec is an environmental plant and plant machinery company that, according to its own description, designs and builds energy-saving equipment, water and wastewater treatment plants for power stations and industrial sites, pharmaceutical and bio plants, and cathode material and separator equipment for secondary batteries.

Its business is broadly split between plant and construction-civil segments, and press reports said that in the first three quarters of 2026 the construction-civil segment led revenue growth while the plant segment passed its full-year 2025 revenue within three quarters.

The core of the plant business is water treatment engineering, procurement and construction (EPC); as of a company announcement in September 2026, it had won successive water and wastewater treatment contracts for the Andong combined-cycle unit 2 and the Haman combined-cycle project of two Korean power generators.

In secondary battery materials, it has supplied concentration and crystallization equipment for lithium sulfate, lithium hydroxide and lithium carbonate crystallization plants, as well as nickel sulfate and cobalt sulfate lines.

Overseas, its Chinese subsidiary booked four energy equipment projects for lithium-ion battery separator plants through the third quarter of 2026, totaling KRW 24.2 billion, about 5.8 times the KRW 4.2 billion booked for full-year 2025.

In environmental businesses, the company is targeting demand for high-concentration nitrogen wastewater treatment using ANAMMOX-based technology as biogas production mandates extend to private operators, and it cites a hydrogen-free anaerobic wastewater plant rated at 5,000 tonnes per day at a paper mill in Siheung.

In construction, press reports dated August 2026 cited a project of roughly KRW 80 billion, including the Bukwirye Village new build for a KB-affiliated senior care operator.

Customers span power generators, general contractors, secondary battery materials makers, and paper, food and pharmaceutical companies, and competition is mostly project-by-project bidding.

Listed companies grouped in the same sector include KEPCO Engineering & Construction Company and KEPCO Plant Service & Engineering.

04

Earnings analysis

Revenue peaked at KRW 333.2 billion in 2023, then fell 21.4% to KRW 261.9 billion in 2024 and 36.3% to KRW 166.7 billion in 2025, a two-year decline. Among 2022 to 2025, 2023 was the largest year and 2025 the smallest.

Operating profit was KRW 1.35 billion in 2022, KRW 1.22 billion in 2023 (down 9.4%) and KRW 1.95 billion in 2024 (up 59.1%), before the company recorded an operating loss of KRW 18.1 billion in 2025, a swing into loss.

The company attributed the 2025 revenue decline to lower new orders in its plant segment, tied to reduced capital spending and deferred investment, and to weak construction demand.

The 2025 net loss attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 43.4 billion reflected impairment of uncollected construction receivables at an affiliate, impairment assessments on renewable energy affiliate investments and loans, and provisions for the Shihwa MTV project financing.

The operating margin moved from 0.4% in 2022 and 0.4% in 2023 to 0.7% in 2024 and -10.9% in 2025, while the debt ratio rose from 210.4% in 2022 to 741.0% in 2025, with total equity of KRW 13.7 billion and total liabilities of KRW 101.3 billion at year-end 2025.

Quarterly operating losses of KRW 7.94 billion in the third quarter of 2025 and KRW 8.63 billion in the fourth quarter were followed by operating profit of KRW 1.59 billion in the first quarter of 2026, a turn to profit.

Operating profit was KRW 1.27 billion in the second quarter of 2026, down 20.0% from the prior quarter, and KRW 2.85 billion in the third quarter, a swing to profit from a year earlier. Revenue in the third quarter of 2026 was KRW 39.7 billion, down 10.0% year on year, so top-line recovery is still underway.

Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 20 billion in the second quarter of 2026 and KRW 450 million in the third quarter, a 97.8% drop, and press reports said the second-quarter figure reflected a KRW 19.0 billion provision reversal recorded as non-operating income, a one-off item that should be read apart from core earnings.

The four quarters from the fourth quarter of 2025 through the third quarter of 2026 together total a net loss attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 14.3 billion, driven largely by the KRW 31.2 billion loss in the fourth quarter of 2025.

05

Industry analysis

Welcron Hantec's main end markets are secondary battery materials investment, power generation capacity additions, waste and biogas treatment infrastructure, and construction orders.

In secondary batteries, a financial information site's summary of the first three quarters of 2025 said plant revenue fell as investment was delayed by the repeal of US tax credits and electric vehicle fire concerns.

Press reports dated late October 2026 said the government planned to put in place, within 2026, battery support measures that include tax incentives for domestic production and stronger cooperation between cell makers and materials, parts and equipment companies.

On power, press reports from October 2026 said a draft of the 12th Basic Plan for Electricity Supply and Demand was expected to reset LNG capacity from 67.0 GW to at least 71.5 GW.

An analyst at a domestic research firm estimated that assuming an average build-out of 2.2 GW a year through 2038 could create at least KRW 70 billion of new annual orders for the company, but that is an assumption-based estimate rather than a confirmed order plan.

In waste, expanding restrictions on direct landfilling of household waste are expected to raise demand for energy recovery and solid refuse fuel processing.

In biogas, the production target system was extended to private operators from 2026, and the company expects demand for organic waste treatment and high-concentration nitrogen wastewater treatment to grow.

In terms of cycle position, secondary battery equipment investment is waiting on policy and new orders to restart, while power, waste and biogas demand is forming as mandates expand.

In competition, the company is classified as an environmental plant contractor with membrane filtration, evaporation, concentration and crystallization technologies.

06

Outlook

In a September 2026 announcement, the company said it would expand its environmental plant EPC business while continuing to select orders for profitability and to manage costs project by project.

On orders, its water and wastewater projects for the Andong combined-cycle unit 2 and the Haman combined-cycle project are building a track record in power-sector water treatment.

In construction, it won a project of roughly KRW 80 billion including the Bukwirye Village new build, but the timing of revenue recognition depends on construction progress.

Its Chinese subsidiary booked KRW 24.2 billion through the third quarter of 2026 and is negotiating roughly KRW 14 billion in additional projects with Chinese secondary battery materials makers.

The company plans to expand high-concentration nitrogen wastewater orders as biogas mandates extend to private operators, and it has announced plans to enter water treatment for advanced industries such as semiconductors.

Analysts see power-sector water treatment EPC demand rising if the 12th Basic Plan for Electricity Supply and Demand is finalized with more LNG capacity. The first checkpoint is whether annual revenue in the 2026 business report exceeds the KRW 166.7 billion recorded in 2025.

Whether operating profit stays positive once the one-off provision reversal is excluded is the second item to confirm in the same report.

08

Bull factors

Power-sector water treatment EPC wins

The company has won successive water and wastewater treatment contracts for the Andong combined-cycle unit 2 and the Haman combined-cycle project, building EPC experience in power-sector water treatment.

An analyst at a domestic research firm argued that its LNG plant water and wastewater experience would benefit if the 12th Basic Plan adds LNG capacity. Continued power-sector orders could be one lever for rebuilding revenue from its trough.

Surge in Chinese subsidiary orders

The Chinese subsidiary booked four separator plant projects totaling KRW 24.2 billion through the third quarter of 2026, about 5.8 times the KRW 4.2 billion booked for full-year 2025. It is negotiating roughly KRW 14 billion in further projects with Chinese battery materials companies.

The company points to concentration, crystallization and solvent and energy recovery technologies as a way to cut customers' production costs and energy use.

Lower cost ratio and easing balance sheet strain

Press reports say the cumulative cost of sales ratio for the first three quarters of 2026 fell 7.1 percentage points to 89.4%, and selling and administrative expenses dropped sharply. Reports also said the debt ratio at the end of the third quarter of 2026 was 216.4%, far below the year-end 2025 level.

If the profit turn reflects a structural change in project cost ratios, that would support the case for sustained operating profit.

09

Bear factors

Shrinking revenue and order gaps

Revenue fell in 2024 and 2025, a two-year decline, and the KRW 166.7 billion recorded in 2025 is about half of the KRW 333.2 billion peak in 2023. The company cited lower new orders in both plant and construction as the reason for the 2025 decline.

Revenue in the third quarter of 2026 was KRW 39.7 billion, down 10.0% year on year, so the top line has not yet turned.

Reliance on one-off gains

Press reports said the KRW 20 billion second-quarter net profit attributable to controlling shareholders was largely driven by a KRW 19.0 billion provision reversal.

Operating profit in the same quarter was only KRW 1.27 billion, and the third-quarter net profit attributable to controlling shareholders fell 97.8% to KRW 450 million. With core quarterly profit still small, how much earnings power remains once one-off items such as reversals are removed is an open question.

Balance sheet and cash flow strain

At the end of 2025, total liabilities were KRW 101.3 billion against total equity of KRW 13.7 billion, a debt ratio of 741.0%. Press reports said cumulative operating cash flow for the first three quarters of 2026 was a net outflow of KRW 18.8 billion, compared with a net inflow in the same period of 2025.

Over the four quarters through the third quarter of 2026, the net loss attributable to controlling shareholders totaled KRW 14.3 billion, which adds to the pressure.

Risk factors

Order volatility

Plant orders move sharply with the timing of capital spending and policy schedules. In 2025, deferred investment reduced new plant orders and revenue fell 36.3%. If the delay in secondary battery investment and weak construction demand overlap again, another order gap could emerge.

Provision and litigation exposure

Press reports said the provision charge tied to litigation over the Shihwa MTV project rose to KRW 6.3 billion in the first three quarters of 2026, above the prior-year level. Because earnings were lifted by a provision reversal, additional charges or litigation outcomes could again swing reported profit. In 2025, impairment of uncollected construction receivables at an affiliate added to earnings volatility.

Policy timing and cash flow

The battery support measures, the 12th Basic Plan for Electricity Supply and Demand, and the expansion of biogas mandates have either not been finalized or are at an early stage. If policy timing slips, order expectations could be pushed back.

Operating cash flow was a net inflow of KRW 14.9 billion in 2025 but was reported as a net outflow for the first three quarters of 2026, and rising working capital needs could add pressure as orders grow.

12

Overall view

Welcron Hantec's losses widened in 2025, with an operating loss of KRW 18.1 billion and a net loss attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 43.4 billion, but it has posted quarterly operating profit since the first quarter of 2026, a sign of a change in its earnings base.

Press reports attribute much of the profit turn to a lower cost ratio and easing bad-debt burdens.

However, the KRW 20 billion second-quarter net profit attributable to controlling shareholders reflected a KRW 19.0 billion Shihwa MTV provision reversal, so the improvement should be divided between core recovery and one-off factors.

Revenue declined in both 2024 and 2025, and in the third quarter of 2026 it was down 10.0% year on year, so the top-line recovery is still in progress.

The KRW 24.2 billion in orders at the Chinese subsidiary, the power-sector water treatment EPC record, and biogas, battery and power policy are cited as grounds for an order recovery.

On the other side, the debt ratio of 741.0% at the end of 2025, a four-quarter net loss attributable to controlling shareholders totaling KRW 14.3 billion, and the possibility of policy delays are weaknesses.

The next checkpoint is annual revenue and operating margin in the 2026 business report, followed by whether profit holds in the first-quarter 2027 report.
