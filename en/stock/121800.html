02

Business structure

Vidente was founded in March 2002 as a specialist in broadcast displays and sells high-performance HD broadcast monitors and related equipment under its own TVLogic brand. Its customers include major global broadcasters such as BBC, CNN and NBC, and its lineup covers wall-mount, viewfinder and editing/CG monitors.

In a compilation by AlphaSquare, the LVM series accounts for 28.52% of revenue, the LUM series for 23.17%, rental revenue for 15.63%, the F-Series for 11.50% and the RKM for 8.66%. The same compilation puts other product revenue at 6.73% and other revenue at 5.80%.

In the 2025 disclosed revenue mix, domestic sales were 28.40% and exports 71.60%, so overseas exposure exceeds 70%. The information site Wisereport places Vidente third globally by units sold and notes that the shift to digital broadcasting and wider UHD adoption are lifting demand.

Reports of a domestic share above 90% exist, but those articles are several years old. The company acquired a large stake in Bithumb Holdings and expanded into blockchain and digital asset investment.

In 2025 it entered the fire damper control market with a smart damper actuator prototype and plans to extend into building energy management systems. Disclosures do not split earnings by business segment, so the contribution of non-broadcast businesses is difficult to quantify.

Earnings analysis

Annual revenue fell from KRW 15.8 billion in 2022 to KRW 10 billion in 2023, down 36.8% year on year, and then declined 3.8% to KRW 9.63 billion in 2024, the smallest year in the 2022 to 2025 span. Revenue recovered to KRW 10.1 billion in 2025, up 5.3% year on year.

The operating loss widened from KRW 2.71 billion in 2022 to KRW 9.89 billion in 2023 and KRW 11.1 billion in 2024, then narrowed to KRW 7.22 billion in 2025, and the company has been in operating loss for four consecutive years from 2022 to 2025.

The operating margin moved from -17.1% in 2022 to -98.7% in 2023 and -114.7% in 2024, improving to -71.1% in 2025. The cost of sales ratio compiled by FnGuide also fell from 102.81% in 2024, meaning cost of sales exceeded sales, to 72.41% in 2025.

For the 2025 improvement, the company cited lower inventory valuation losses and lower non-recurring costs such as prior-year re-audit expenses, and it reported valuation losses on an equity-method investment (based on a March 16, 2026 disclosure report).

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders moved from a net loss of KRW 161.7 billion in 2022 and KRW 25.9 billion in 2023 to a profit of KRW 67.3 billion in 2024. A profit that large against an operating loss of KRW 11.1 billion suggests non-operating items weighed heavily in 2024.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders fell 87.0% to KRW 8.76 billion in 2025, while net income including non-controlling interests was KRW 8.65 billion, a different figure.

On a quarterly basis, the operating loss has been recorded in each of the five quarters from Q3 2025 to Q3 2026, and Q2 2026 revenue of KRW 3.11 billion was the largest of the five disclosed quarters. Q3 2026 revenue of KRW 2.31 billion fell 25.9% from the prior quarter and 7.0% from a year earlier.

Net loss attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 3.2 billion... specifically KRW 32 billion in Q4 2025, KRW 40.6 billion in Q1 2026, KRW 2.22 billion in Q2 2026 and KRW 14.5 billion in Q3 2026, totaling KRW 89.3 billion over the last four quarters.

Wisereport's first-half 2026 compilation shows revenue up 26.4% year on year, operating loss down 43.3%, and net income turning to a loss.

Bull factors

Narrowing operating losses

The operating loss narrowed from KRW 11.1 billion in 2024 to KRW 7.22 billion in 2025, and the operating margin improved from -114.7% to -71.1%. According to Wisereport's compilation, the first-half 2026 operating loss fell 43.3% year on year. Quarterly losses narrowed to KRW 573 million in Q4 2025 and KRW 303 million in Q1 2026.

Financial capacity

Total equity at year-end 2025 was KRW 644.6 billion, total liabilities were KRW 58.5 billion, and the debt ratio stood at 9.1%. The ratio moved from 30.8% in 2022 to 14.0% in 2023 and 16.1% in 2024 before falling further. The low debt load provides room to absorb operating losses while funding new businesses.

Broadcast equipment base and overseas demand

Vidente's monitors serve major global broadcasters including BBC, CNN and NBC. Wisereport places the company third globally by units sold and cites digital broadcasting and UHD expansion as demand drivers. Exports were 71.60% of 2025 disclosed sales.

Bear factors

Continued net losses attributable to controlling shareholders

Net losses attributable to controlling shareholders were KRW 32 billion in Q4 2025, KRW 40.6 billion in Q1 2026 and KRW 14.5 billion in Q3 2026, totaling KRW 89.3 billion over the last four quarters. Q3 2026 marked a swing to loss from a year earlier. Net income attributable to controlling shareholders fell 87.0% in 2025, leaving a thinner earnings base.

Revenue volatility

Q3 2026 revenue of KRW 2.31 billion fell 25.9% from the prior quarter and 7.0% from a year earlier. Annual revenue also declined 36.8% in 2023 and 3.8% in 2024. Because quarterly sales depend on broadcaster tender wins, revenue is volatile.

Governance and new business results

With both the director and full-time auditor nominations having failed, how the board and audit positions are filled is the next point to check. The company has invested in new businesses, including a stake in Bithumb Holdings, but their results are not split out in disclosures. Operating losses continuing while new-business results remain unclear is a bearish factor.

Risk factors

Operating losses have continued for four consecutive years from 2022 to 2025 and for five straight quarters since Q3 2025. Net losses attributable to controlling shareholders have run four quarters in a row. If losses persist without a return to profit, the equity base could shrink.

Governance

Director and full-time auditor nominations were both voted down at the February 2026 extraordinary shareholders' meeting, showing shareholder resistance to the management slate. Changes in board composition could add uncertainty over business direction and capital allocation.

Exports, currency and new businesses

The 2025 export share of 71.60% exposes the company to overseas trade conditions and currency swings. New businesses such as the smart damper are not broken out in disclosures, which makes their performance hard to measure. If new investments fail to translate into earnings, they could add to pressure from the core business losses.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders fell from KRW 67.3 billion in 2024 to KRW 8.76 billion in 2025, and net losses over the last four quarters total KRW 89.3 billion. Revenue and operating loss improved year on year in the first half of 2026, but net income swung to a loss.

However, the rejection of director and full-time auditor nominations at the February 2026 extraordinary shareholders' meeting leaves governance as an item to watch.
