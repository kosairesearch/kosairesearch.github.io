Operating profit rises while net profit falls at a holding company

02

Business structure

SINGSONG HOLDINGS was founded in 1970 as a pure holding company, moved into the holding structure through a 2009 spin-off, and took its current name after a 2010 merger. In November 2013 it became the first holding company to list directly on the KOSPI.

Its subsidiaries fall into two groups: a food unit that makes and sells soy sauce, red pepper paste and soybean paste, and a grain and starch unit that produces gluten and wheat starch while also trading overseas grain such as rice. Both are wholly owned by the holding company.

The company runs five business segments: food, overseas grain trading, materials, real estate investment, and investment. The overseas segment is built on food-grain supply and distribution, and management says it is developing stable supply sources in major farming countries and expanding raw-meat distribution.

The real estate segment leases three office buildings in the Yeouido area of Seoul, which are regarded as a steady source of cash flow.

A 2020 press report said the holding company's income mainly comes from management consulting fees charged to subsidiaries, so the parent's earnings depend heavily on subsidiary performance.

As of August 18, 2026, the largest shareholder and related parties held 58.21 percent, and treasury shares stood at 9.79 percent as of November 3, 2026.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual revenue went from KRW 209.4 billion in 2022 to KRW 193.4 billion in 2023 (down 7.6 percent year on year) and KRW 164.6 billion in 2024 (down 14.9 percent), then recovered to KRW 187.8 billion in 2025 (up 14.1 percent). 2022 was the largest year in the 2022 to 2025 span and 2024 the smallest.

Operating profit slipped from KRW 8.57 billion in 2022 to KRW 8.38 billion in 2023 (down 2.3 percent), edged up to KRW 8.4 billion in 2024 (up 0.2 percent), and rose to KRW 10.3 billion in 2025 (up 22.7 percent). It has grown for two straight years from 2024 through 2025, and 2025 was the largest year in the span.

The operating margin rose from 4.1 percent in 2022 to 4.3 percent, 5.1 percent and 5.5 percent in 2025, so margins improved even in 2024 when revenue fell.

Profit attributable to controlling shareholders jumped from KRW 1.5 billion in 2022 to KRW 8.77 billion in 2023 (up 485.2 percent), then fell to KRW 5.43 billion in 2024 (down 38.1 percent) and KRW 3.64 billion in 2025 (down 33.0 percent), declining for two straight years. 2023 was the largest year for this line.

Because net profit rose sharply while operating profit barely moved in 2023, non-operating items and taxes appear to have weighed heavily on the bottom line. The company attributed the 2025 change mainly to lower financial income and higher income tax expense.

Net profit including non-controlling interests was KRW 3.54 billion in 2025, a different figure from profit attributable to controlling shareholders, so the two should be read separately.

Operating cash flow went from KRW 4.7 billion in 2022 and KRW 8.51 billion in 2023 to KRW 2.26 billion in 2024, then turned negative at minus KRW 8.94 billion in 2025.

The debt ratio was 152.1 percent in 2022, 134.6 percent in 2023, 138.8 percent in 2024 and 152.1 percent in 2025, when total equity was KRW 108.9 billion, equity attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 107.7 billion and total liabilities were KRW 165.6 billion.

Quarterly revenue peaked at KRW 62.9 billion in Q4 2025 (up 62.2 percent from the prior quarter), then held near KRW 45 to 46 billion in each quarter of 2026.

Q3 2026 operating profit was KRW 3.65 billion, recovering from KRW 1.52 billion in Q2 2026, while profit attributable to controlling shareholders fell to KRW 84.79 million in Q2 before returning to KRW 1.79 billion in Q3.

Over the last four quarters, from Q4 2025 to Q3 2026, that profit attributable to controlling shareholders totaled KRW 6.91 billion.

05

Industry analysis

Demand for the food unit's products comes from consumption of soy-based condiments, and margins for soy sauce, red pepper paste and soybean paste move with raw-material costs and sourcing conditions.

The grain and starch unit's results are directly exposed to wheat and rice prices, supply conditions and overseas sourcing. Food security has drawn attention to the importance of grain distribution, but the effect is hard to measure from segment figures and is better tracked through the earnings trend.

On the cycle, a 2020 press report said cumulative standalone net losses since the 2009 holding-company reorganization reached KRW 36 billion and that the company posted losses every year except 2015. The same period saw the grain subsidiary fall into a capital-impairment crisis and undertake a rights offering.

A 2016 controversy at an affiliate was followed by a shift to professional management, according to press coverage. Profit attributable to controlling shareholders was positive in 2025, and operating profit rose, so the trend has moved from accumulated losses toward recovery.

Listed holding companies such as SK Square, Hanjinkal and Lotte operate different business mixes, including investment, airline and retail, so direct comparison of performance metrics is limited.

Competitive position is therefore better judged by the strength of the operating subsidiaries and the stability of the office rental base.

Bull factors

Operating profit recovery and margin gains

Operating profit reached KRW 10.3 billion in 2025 and has risen for two straight years, while the operating margin improved from 4.1 percent in 2022 to 5.5 percent in 2025. Margins rose to 5.1 percent in 2024 even as revenue fell 14.9 percent. Improving core operating profit is the main bullish factor. Whether the trend holds will be visible in the operating margin reported in the 2026 annual report.

Buybacks, cancellation and dividend resolutions

The buyback approved on September 17, 2026 was completed on November 2, leaving treasury shares at 9.79 percent, and the company plans to cancel all acquired shares. A further purchase of 40,000 shares was approved on November 4, 2026, with a period running to February 4, 2027.

The board also approved a year-end dividend in February 2026. Consecutive shareholder-return actions are a bullish factor.

Yeouido office leasing and asset base

The real estate segment leases three office buildings near Yeouido and is regarded as a steady source of cash flow. The price-to-book ratio is below 1 times, so the shares trade at a discount to net asset value. The asset base could cushion earnings swings, which is the bullish argument. How much of total profit comes from rental income still needs to be checked against segment disclosures.

Bear factors

Profit attributable to controlling shareholders down two straight years

After the 2023 peak, profit attributable to controlling shareholders declined in both 2024 and 2025. The company cited lower financial income and higher income tax expense as the main reasons for the 2025 decline.

Net profit has fallen even as operating profit rose, so non-operating items and taxes are shaping earnings quality. This is the bearish argument.

Quarterly earnings volatility and negative operating cash flow

Profit attributable to controlling shareholders fell to KRW 84.79 million in Q2 2026 before rebounding to KRW 1.79 billion in Q3, which was still down 12.0 percent year on year. Q3 operating profit rose while attributable profit declined, a divergence. Operating cash flow turned negative at minus KRW 8.94 billion in 2025. This is the bearish argument.

Dependence on subsidiaries and debt guarantees

The holding company's income and financial burden are tied to subsidiary performance and debt guarantees. A 2020 press report said cumulative standalone net losses since 2009 reached KRW 36 billion, and the grain subsidiary once faced a capital-impairment crisis that required a rights offering.

Subsidiary debt-guarantee decisions were disclosed again in June and July 2026. Because subsidiary weakness passes straight through to the parent, this is a bearish factor.

Risk factors

Subsidiary guarantees and debt burden

Subsidiary debt-guarantee decisions were disclosed several times in June and July 2026. The debt ratio was 152.1 percent at the end of 2025, the same level as in 2022, with total liabilities of KRW 165.6 billion. Rising guarantees could add contingent burdens at the holding company level.

Swing to negative operating cash flow

Operating cash flow went from KRW 2.26 billion in 2024 to minus KRW 8.94 billion in 2025. Profit attributable to controlling shareholders also declined over the same period, so how dividends and buybacks are funded is a key point to watch. If cash generation does not recover, room for shareholder returns could be constrained.

Grain price and trade swings

The overseas segment relies on grain and raw-meat distribution, so changes in grain prices, exchange rates and supply can move revenue and margins. Revenue fell 14.9 percent in 2024 and rose 14.1 percent in 2025, showing wide swings. With the largest shareholder and related parties holding 58.21 percent, the free float may be limited.

Overall view

SINGSONG HOLDINGS's operating profit and operating margin rose in 2025, pointing to a recovery in its core operations. Profit attributable to controlling shareholders, however, has declined for two straight years since its 2023 peak, and operating cash flow turned negative in 2025.

Quarterly results in 2026 have been volatile, with Q3 operating profit up 141.0 percent from the prior quarter while Q2 attributable profit fell to KRW 84.79 million. The debt ratio of 152.1 percent at the end of 2025 matches the 2022 level.

On shareholder returns, the company has continued with share buybacks, a plan to cancel the repurchased shares, and a year-end dividend approved in February 2026.

The Yeouido office base and a price-to-book ratio below 1 times are asset-side bullish factors, while reliance on subsidiary results and guarantees is the main bearish factor. The next checkpoints are the end of the buyback period on February 4, 2027 and the 2026 annual report. This report does not include a buy or sell opinion or a target price.
