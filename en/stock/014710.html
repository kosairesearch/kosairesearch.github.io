02

Business structure

Sajo SEA Food is a seafood processing and distribution company founded in 1980 and affiliated with the Sajo group.

According to a Mirae Asset Securities report covering 2011, sashimi-grade tuna processing and distribution accounted for 64% of revenue of KRW 280.8 billion, with general seafood processing at 16%, seasoned laver at 9%, direct fishing at 6%, and other items at 3%.

That mix is about fifteen years old and may differ today, but the tuna-centered structure still defines the business. Its laver is sold under the Haepyo brand of the affiliate Sajodaerim. A March 2025 press report said food-segment export revenue in 2024 was about KRW 6.7 billion, up 30.4% from the prior year.

The company attributed the 2025 consolidated revenue increase of KRW 238 billion to higher fish prices and diversification of its customer base. It has also joined government programs to find export customers.

Competition runs on two fronts: in tuna and other seafood processed foods it faces large food companies such as Dongwon Industries, and in laver exports competition among domestic producers is seen as intensifying.

Because tuna processing is central, raw material sourcing is shaped by conditions in the distant-water fishing industry.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual results began in 2022 with revenue of KRW 201.1 billion and operating profit of KRW 13.6 billion. In 2023, revenue fell 12.5% to KRW 176 billion and the company swung to an operating loss of KRW 34.8 billion.

In 2024, revenue rose 7.0% to KRW 188.3 billion and operating profit turned positive at KRW 9.52 billion. Revenue in 2025 rose 26.4% to KRW 238 billion, the largest year in the 2022 to 2025 period, and it has increased for two consecutive years since 2023.

Operating profit rose 27.9% to KRW 12.2 billion in 2025, but the operating margin was 5.1% in both 2024 and 2025, below 6.8% in 2022. Operating profit stayed positive while the bottom line did not.

Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders turned into a loss of KRW 28.2 billion in 2025, and the total net loss including non-controlling interests was KRW 28.4 billion. The company said equity-method losses, reflecting higher non-operating costs at affiliates, drove the swing.

Quarterly figures show operating profit of KRW 3.16 billion in the third quarter of 2025, then KRW 1.35 billion in the fourth quarter, down 57.1% quarter on quarter, when controlling-shareholder net loss reached KRW 41.2 billion.

Operating profit recovered to KRW 2.59 billion in the first quarter of 2026, up 91.5%, and to KRW 10.5 billion in the second quarter, up 304.9%, the largest figure among the last five reported quarters.

In the third quarter of 2026, revenue reached KRW 75.3 billion, up 9.1% year on year, but operating profit fell to KRW 230 million, down 92.7% year on year, and the controlling-shareholder result turned to a net loss of KRW 4.41 billion.

The combined controlling-shareholder net loss for the last four quarters is KRW 40.1 billion. Operating cash flow moved from KRW 22.1 billion inflow in 2024 to -KRW 34.4 billion in 2025.

05

Industry analysis

Sajo SEA Food operates across processed tuna and other seafood products and their raw material supply. One domestic research note estimated the global tuna market at about USD 45 billion in 2024, with Japan, Europe and the United States accounting for more than 70% of consumption.

The same note described distant-water fishing as highly volatile, affected by climate phenomena such as El Niño, tighter fishing regulations and higher fuel prices. Raw material price swings can lift revenue, but margins depend on the ability to pass costs through.

The rise in fish prices cited as a driver of 2025 revenue illustrates this link. In laver, national export value reached USD 997 million in 2024, up 25.8% from the prior year. Output fell from 2,223 tonnes to 1,992 tonnes in the same year, while prices rose 14.1%, according to a 2025 press report.

Exports grew, but competition among exporters was reported to be intensifying. In processed foods, the company competes with large food companies such as Dongwon Industries. On the cycle, the 2023 operating loss was followed by operating profit recovery in 2024 and 2025.

However, the sharp drop in third-quarter 2026 operating profit means quarterly swings will determine whether that recovery holds.

06

Outlook

The company disclosed a 2026 corporate value-up plan, which press coverage described as its first such plan. Its pillars are profitability-focused management, strategic investment and stable shareholder returns, with a stated plan to pursue cost efficiency alongside expanded investment.

The plan includes strategic investment aimed at finding new growth drivers and strengthening synergies among core businesses. Dividends were not paid in 2024 but were resumed, and a cash dividend was approved at the annual general meeting on March 25, 2026.

The company's 2025 payout ratio was reported at about 25%, and it qualifies as a high-dividend company under the Restriction of Special Taxation Act. On the business side, the next checkpoint is the operating margin, which was 5.1% in both 2024 and 2025 and will be shown in the 2026 annual report.

On the financial side, the debt ratio rose to 88.7% at the end of 2025 from 42.1% in 2024. Equity totaled KRW 208.3 billion at year-end 2025, with KRW 206.3 billion attributable to controlling shareholders, and total liabilities were KRW 184.8 billion.

Fourth-quarter and full-year 2026 results will be fixed in the 2026 annual report. Whether operating profit recovers from the KRW 230 million posted in the third quarter of 2026 will define the year-end result.

Laver is produced from October to the following April, so the results of the 2026 season will be reflected in the first half of 2027.

Bull factors

Recovery in operating profit

Operating profit recovered from an operating loss of KRW 34.8 billion in 2023 to KRW 9.52 billion in 2024 and KRW 12.2 billion in 2025, up 27.9%. Operating profit in the second quarter of 2026 was KRW 10.5 billion. Revenue in that quarter rose 24.0% quarter on quarter to KRW 83.9 billion. The shift from losses back to profit is cited as the main bullish argument.

Resumed shareholder returns

The company, which paid no dividend in 2024, approved a cash dividend at its annual general meeting on March 25, 2026. The 2025 payout ratio was reported at about 25%, and the company qualifies as a high-dividend firm. The resumption is read as a signal of continued shareholder returns despite weak earnings. The size of future dividends may still move with earnings.

Revenue growth and value-up plan

Revenue in 2025 rose 26.4% to KRW 238 billion and has grown for two consecutive years since 2023. The company disclosed a 2026 value-up plan centered on profitability-focused management, strategic investment and stable dividends. It also stated it would pursue cost efficiency alongside expanded investment. Combined revenue growth and a shareholder-return framework form the bullish case.

Bear factors

Persistent net losses

Controlling-shareholder net profit of KRW 11.8 billion in 2024 turned into a net loss of KRW 28.2 billion in 2025. In the third quarter of 2026, the net loss of KRW 4.41 billion marked a swing to loss year on year.

Non-operating items and equity-method losses pulling the bottom line below operating profit form the bearish argument.

High quarterly volatility

Third-quarter 2026 revenue rose 9.1% year on year to KRW 75.3 billion, but operating profit fell 92.7% to KRW 230 million. The controlling-shareholder net loss in the fourth quarter of 2025 reached KRW 41.2 billion.

Operating profit dropped sharply in the third quarter after KRW 10.5 billion in the second quarter, so quarterly direction has changed often. The lack of sustained earnings is the bearish point.

Balance sheet and cash flow strain

The debt ratio climbed from 42.1% in 2024 to 88.7% at the end of 2025, and total liabilities reached KRW 184.8 billion. Operating cash flow swung from a KRW 22.1 billion inflow in 2024 to -KRW 34.4 billion in 2025. Including 2022 at -KRW 16 billion and 2023 at -KRW 8.81 billion, three of the four years were negative. Higher borrowing combined with cash outflows could limit room for investment and dividends.

Risk factors

Equity-method and non-operating swings

The company cited equity-method losses from higher non-operating costs at affiliates as the reason for the 2025 net loss. Such losses from affiliates moved the bottom line into the red even though operating profit stayed positive. These items move separately from operating results and are hard to forecast. The KRW 41.2 billion controlling-shareholder net loss in the fourth quarter of 2025 illustrates the effect.

Raw materials, catch and export competition

Because tuna processing is central, raw material sourcing depends on climate, fishing regulation and fuel prices in distant-water fishing. Higher fish prices lifted 2025 revenue, so failing to pass costs through would pressure margins. Laver exports grew in 2024, but competition is reported to be intensifying. Whether the diversification of export customers can be sustained is the next variable.

Financial leverage and cash flow

The debt ratio rose from 42.1% in 2024 to 88.7% at the end of 2025, more than doubling. Total liabilities of KRW 184.8 billion against equity of KRW 208.3 billion show a larger debt burden. Operating cash flow turned negative in 2025, which may have raised reliance on borrowing.

If planned investment expansion and resumed dividends draw on the same cash, financial headroom would narrow further.

Overall view

Sajo SEA Food has shown an earnings recovery in its core business, moving from an operating loss in 2023 to a profit in 2024 and higher operating profit in 2025. Operating profit of KRW 10.5 billion in the second quarter of 2026 was the largest of the last five reported quarters.

In the third quarter of the same year, operating profit fell to KRW 230 million, and the 2025 controlling-shareholder net loss of KRW 28.2 billion and the four-quarter combined net loss of KRW 40.1 billion point to a large effect from equity-method and other non-operating items.

The debt ratio rising to 88.7% at the end of 2025 and operating cash flow of -KRW 34.4 billion are also financial items to track.

Bullish factors include the operating profit recovery, resumed dividends and the 2026 value-up plan, while bearish factors include net losses, quarterly volatility and higher financial leverage. The shares trade at a discount to book value, but no earnings-based multiple can be calculated.
