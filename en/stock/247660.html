02

Business structure

Nanocms develops light-control materials based on nano-scale chemical structure design, producing anti-counterfeiting pigments for security printing, specialty IT materials and power semiconductor materials. The company was founded in 2003 and listed on KOSDAQ in March 2021.

Its core product lines are near-infrared absorbing and reflecting pigments and UV organic fluorescent pigments used in national security printing for banknotes, passports and ID cards. Infrared emitters are described as a major source of revenue.

Confirmed customers include the Korea Minting and Security Printing Corporation and domestic manufacturers, and the October 2026 contract was a pigment supply to a domestic manufacturer produced in-house.

The security printing market has long been dominated by Switzerland's SICPA, which holds a near-monopoly built on patents, making entry difficult for smaller players.

Nanocms has worked around this by developing new products that do not infringe SICPA's patents, which led to a supply win for the Korea Minting and Security Printing Corporation.

Separately, it has been mass-producing silicon carbide (SiC) powder on processing equipment completed in August 2024, aiming at EV inverter and fast-charger demand. It is also pursuing UV-absorbing materials for cosmetics and brand-protection materials for food products. The business therefore combines an established security-materials axis with a newer SiC processing axis.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual revenue fell from KRW 7.41 billion in 2022 to KRW 4.58 billion in 2023, a 38.2% decline that made 2023 the smallest year in the period. Revenue then rose to KRW 4.65 billion in 2024, up 1.5%, and to KRW 5.29 billion in 2025, up 13.8%, marking two straight years of growth; 2022 was the largest year in the period.

Operating profit of KRW 555 million in 2022 turned into an operating loss of KRW 3.16 billion in 2023, and the loss peaked at KRW 8.86 billion in 2024. The 2025 operating loss of KRW 1.99 billion was smaller, but the company has posted losses for three consecutive years.

Operating margin moved from 7.5% in 2022 to -69.0% in 2023, -190.5% in 2024 and -37.7% in 2025. The company attributed the narrower 2025 operating loss to higher revenue and lower cost of sales.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders swung from KRW 206 million in 2022 to a net loss of KRW 4.14 billion in 2023, a net loss of KRW 8.84 billion in 2024 and a net loss of KRW 2.2 billion in 2025.

Net income including non-controlling interests, which is a separate figure, was a net loss of KRW 2.2 billion in 2025. On a quarterly basis, the operating loss was KRW 41.78 million in Q3 2025 and KRW 830 million in Q4 2025, when revenue fell 45.9% from the prior quarter.

Q1 2026 revenue of KRW 2.33 billion produced operating profit of KRW 241 million, and Q2 2026 revenue of KRW 2.78 billion produced operating profit of KRW 869 million. Q3 2026 revenue of KRW 1.55 billion was down 12.9% year on year, and the company posted an operating loss of KRW 342 million.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders over the last four quarters, from Q4 2025 to Q3 2026, totaled KRW 692 million. Operating cash flow moved from -KRW 814 million in 2022 and -KRW 1.6 billion in 2023 to KRW 162 million in 2024 and KRW 280 million in 2025.

05

Industry analysis

Demand in the company's end markets splits into two streams. Security printing is centered on national documents such as banknotes, passports and ID cards, so demand depends on tender timing and replacement cycles.

As of a 2021 report, SICPA's patents acted as a barrier to entry in this market, and Nanocms chose to target niches with products that avoid those patents.

The other stream, SiC power semiconductors, is linked to demand for EV inverters and fast chargers because of its high-temperature, high-frequency operation and high-voltage handling.

Nanocms has processing equipment in place and is pursuing material sales, but SiC revenue is not reported separately in the financial disclosures, so its contribution is hard to gauge. Competition in security materials carries high technical barriers but a limited customer base.

In SiC processing, multiple material suppliers exist, so pricing and quality approval are the factors that separate competitors. Q3 2026 revenue fell year on year while the operating loss widened, indicating that any recovery in end-market demand has not yet been fully reflected in results.

In cycle terms, security materials offer stable but limited growth, while SiC offers growth potential that is still at the stage of confirming supply track records.

08

Bull factors

Return to quarterly operating profit

Operating profit of KRW 241 million in Q1 2026 marked a turn to profitability, and Q2 2026 operating profit of KRW 869 million rose 261.1% from the prior quarter. Net income attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 965 million in Q2 2026.

Compared with the operating losses in Q3 and Q4 2025, the earnings structure has improved. Because Q3 2026 returned to an operating loss, the durability of profitability is still at the confirmation stage.

Pigment supply contract and customer base

The KRW 916.5 million pigment supply contract signed in October 2026 equaled 17.33% of 2025 standalone revenue, and the filing states the counterparty, a domestic manufacturer, has a history of similar contracts over the past three years.

Supply to the Korea Minting and Security Printing Corporation and the development of products that avoid SICPA's patents support the company's customer base in security materials. This base underpins the possibility of repeat orders in the security materials business.

SiC processing facilities and a new business axis

The company has been mass-producing SiC powder on processing equipment completed in August 2024 and has been preparing full-scale supply since 2025 after customer-by-customer quality approvals. SiC is a durable, high-efficiency material with demand in electric vehicles and semiconductors.

If SiC processing becomes an established revenue axis alongside the security materials core, the business mix would diversify.

Bear factors

Q3 2026 operating loss and revenue decline

Q3 2026 revenue of KRW 1.55 billion fell 44.3% from the prior quarter and 12.9% year on year. The quarter posted an operating loss of KRW 342 million and a net loss attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 332 million. Profitability from Q2 reversed within a single quarter, leaving the direction of quarterly results unstable.

Three consecutive annual losses and funding pressure

Operating losses and net losses attributable to controlling shareholders have continued from 2023 through 2025, with the 2025 operating loss at KRW 1.99 billion. The August 2026 third-party rights issue raised KRW 2 billion, but the detailed uses of the proceeds have not been separately disclosed. Reports say part of the purchase funds rely on borrowing, which keeps financial burden in focus.

Governance uncertainty and market listing risk

A lawsuit related to a management control dispute was disclosed on February 9, 2026, and on July 29, 2026 the exchange issued a market notice flagging a possible designation as a managed issue because market capitalization was below KRW 20 billion.

The largest-shareholder change in August 2026 is reshaping management direction. During this period of governance restructuring, continuity of business strategy is uncertain.

Risk factors

Customer and order concentration

The confirmed large supply contracts, such as the KRW 916.5 million order in October 2026, represent a meaningful share of revenue in a single deal. Quarterly revenue can change sharply depending on a few customers' schedules. The 44.3% quarter-on-quarter revenue decline in Q3 2026 shows how order timing can swing results.

Uncertain contribution from SiC

The SiC processing equipment was completed in August 2024, yet SiC revenue is not separately reported in the financial disclosures. Whether customer quality approvals and expanded mass supply translate into actual revenue still needs confirmation. If the facilities fail to convert into sales, fixed costs may keep weighing on profit and loss.

Governance and rights-issue dilution

The August 2026 rights issue issued new shares equal to 20.42% of the shares outstanding before the offering. How the new largest shareholder will participate in management and whether there is a business link remain unsettled. The lack of detailed disclosure on fund use adds to the attention needed on the governance change.

Overall view

Nanocms recorded annual revenue growth for two straight years after the 2023 low, but operating results have been loss-making for three consecutive years since 2023. The 2025 operating loss narrowed to KRW 1.99 billion, and the company posted quarterly operating profit in Q1 and Q2 2026.

However, Q3 2026 revenue fell to KRW 1.55 billion and the quarter returned to an operating loss of KRW 342 million. Net income attributable to controlling shareholders over the last four quarters totaled KRW 692 million, a small base that magnifies quarterly swings.

The business centers on pigments for national security printing and has added SiC processing as a new axis. The August 2026 rights issue and change in largest shareholder alter both the capital structure and governance, making the use of proceeds and the new shareholder's role key items to watch.

Bullish factors include the experience of quarterly profits, the pigment supply contract and a lower debt-to-equity ratio. Bearish factors include persistent annual losses, the renewed Q3 loss and governance uncertainty. The next confirmation points are the 2026 business year annual report and the Q1 2027 quarterly report.
