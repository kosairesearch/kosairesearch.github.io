02

Business structure

KyungdongPharm, founded in 1976 and listed on KOSDAQ, is a pharmaceutical maker focused on manufacturing and selling medicines. According to wisereport as of June 9, 2026, its pharmaceutical segment accounts for about 97% of sales, with leasing and other businesses making up the rest.

Within pharma sales, its lead line accounts for about 66% and its second line for about 27%. JobKorea's business analysis as of March 2026 shows a different split, with 27%, 22% and 20% for three product groups, so the product weights vary by data provider.

Its sales organization moved to a contract sales organization (CSO) model in 2023, and a News Way report said profitability improved clearly after that shift.

Its core lineup centers on chronic disease treatments, and Nasopra is a combination of an NSAID and a gastroprotective agent made with six-layer coating technology. Its tegoprazan generic won a patent trial on the crystal form, allowing market entry after the substance patent expires in August 2031.

In hypertension, Baldipin Plus was approved as the first triple-combination antihypertensive in Korea (reported November 17, 2025). Its sales channels are widening from internal medicine toward pediatrics, ENT and pharmacies.

In biologics, it is developing a dupilumab biosimilar and co-developing a semaglutide-based long-acting obesity injection with a Korean biotech partner. On the production side, a new plant in Yangam-myeon, Hwaseong, Gyeonggi is planned to raise capacity more than threefold.

04

Earnings analysis

On a DART-disclosed basis, sales fell from KRW 182.7 billion in 2022 to KRW 162.7 billion in 2023, a decline of 11.0%, then rose to KRW 193.9 billion in 2024, up 19.2%, and to KRW 196.3 billion in 2025, up 1.2%. 2025 was the largest sales year in the 2022–2025 period.

Operating profit went from KRW 8.31 billion in 2022 to an operating loss of KRW 25 billion in 2023, the year the sales organization moved to a CSO model, then to KRW 2.62 billion in 2024 and KRW 7.67 billion in 2025, up 192.2%. The operating margin rose from 1.4% in 2024 to 3.9% in 2025.

The company attributed this to cost-of-production improvements from manufacturing efficiency and a leaner selling and administrative cost structure as the CSO system stabilized.

Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders swung from a loss of KRW 20.7 billion in 2023 to KRW 5.47 billion in 2024 and KRW 8.53 billion in 2025, up 55.9%. Total net income including non-controlling interests, a separate figure, was KRW 8.45 billion in 2025.

Quarterly sales were KRW 47.8 billion in Q1 2026 (down 8.3% from the prior quarter), KRW 52.3 billion in Q2 (up 9.3%) and KRW 53.1 billion in Q3 (up 1.5%, or 8.6% year on year), marking two consecutive quarters of growth.

Q2 operating profit was KRW 3.7 billion, up 53.6% from the prior quarter, and Q3 operating profit was KRW 3.88 billion, up 25.6% from a year earlier. Media reports on the company's disclosures said operating profit had stayed positive for nine straight quarters since Q2 2024.

Q3 2026 net loss attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 1.81 billion, moving in the opposite direction from operating profit, and the detailed drivers require a breakdown of the income statement.

Over the last four quarters, from Q4 2025 to Q3 2026, net profit attributable to controlling shareholders totaled KRW 11.6 billion. At the end of 2025, total liabilities were KRW 93 billion against total equity of KRW 228.9 billion, and the debt-to-equity ratio rose to 40.6%.

Operating cash flow turned positive at KRW 16.9 billion in 2025 from negative KRW 8.07 billion in 2024, while the company repaid KRW 8.6 billion of short-term borrowings and raised its cash holdings to KRW 28.4 billion.

05

Industry analysis

A government plan to revise drug pricing has raised expectations that companies with heavy generic and reimbursed-drug exposure could face profitability pressure. KyungdongPharm focuses on prescription drugs, so it must keep the improved profitability of the first half while also growing sales.

Combination drugs for hypertension, dyslipidemia and diabetes are described as growing quickly in chronic disease treatment. Combination products are valued for improving treatment effect while making dosing easier.

Biosimilars and obesity treatments are still in development, and some observers say near-term results are hard to expect. Its selection as lead institution for a national project on domestic production of essential-medicine active ingredients adds a policy dimension around supply-chain stability.

Widening its sales reach from internal medicine into pediatrics, ENT and pharmacies is part of its growth approach under the CSO model. As a synthetic-drug generic maker, its business structure differs from biologics-focused listed companies such as Samsung Biologics and Celltrion.

The company has entered a phase where cost and selling-expense improvements translate into profit, but it also needs drug-pricing clarity and sales growth at the same time.

06

Outlook

In its 2026 corporate value-up plan, the company set a target of KRW 230 billion in 2026 sales. Cumulative Q1–Q3 2026 sales were KRW 153.2 billion, so reaching the target would require about KRW 76.8 billion in Q4, based on the sum of disclosed quarterly figures.

The company said first-half sales of KRW 100.1 billion were the first time its half-year sales passed KRW 100 billion. A company official said it aims for KRW 230 billion in annual sales, citing the settled marketing agency structure and cost reduction, and expects growth to continue in the second half.

Under its PACE 2028 strategy, the company has set a long-term target of KRW 500 billion in annual sales by 2030 and plans to secure at least KRW 500 billion of production capacity through the new plant.

The new plant is described as a base for strengthening higher-value pipelines in obesity and diabetes treatments and anticancer drugs. In the pipeline, development of a dupilumab biosimilar was reported in December 2025.

In obesity treatment, the goal is to extend the dosing interval of a once-weekly semaglutide product to once a month. On shareholder returns, the company plans to keep its dividend policy and pursue share cancellation.

The next checkpoints are whether second-half sales track toward the target and whether biologics investment translates into business results.

Risk factors

Policy and drug pricing

Revision of drug pricing could weigh on profitability for companies with high generic and reimbursed-drug exposure. As a prescription-drug focused company, KyungdongPharm may be affected by changes in pricing policy. Its operating margin direction could shift depending on the final content and timing of the revision.

Pipeline execution

Biosimilars and obesity treatments are still in development, and some see near-term results as difficult. Delays or clinical outcomes could affect earnings plans as the company expands into biologics. Whether medium-term R&D converts into business results is the key question.

Financial and investment burden

In December 2025 the company issued KRW 10 billion of exchangeable bonds on its treasury shares and signed a KRW 50 billion facility loan from the Korea Development Bank. The debt-to-equity ratio reached 40.6% in 2025. With the new plant and higher R&D spending under way, financing and depreciation costs could weigh on earnings.
