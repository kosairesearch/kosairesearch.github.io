Sanil Electric: Record Orders Meet Shipment Delays

02

Business structure

Sanil Electric makes special transformers and reactors and supplies them mainly to overseas power grid and renewable energy markets centered on the United States.

In 2023, product sales were 84.7% transformers and reactors and 14.3% soft starters, while end-market shares were 67.3% renewable energy, 24.6% power grid and 8.1% industrial (per a 2024 securities firm IPO report). Exports exceeded 80% of 2023 sales, and the US accounted for roughly 70%.

Major customers include power grid infrastructure operators, global inverter makers, renewable energy developers and domestic and foreign EPC contractors. In 2026 the mix shifted further toward special transformers.

According to Yuanta Securities' analysis, special transformer sales for renewable energy, data centers and energy storage reached KRW 132.4 billion in the second quarter of 2026, about 81% of total revenue, while power grid sales fell 32% from the prior quarter to KRW 26.6 billion.

On the customer side, Bloom Energy, a US fuel cell on-site power company, stands out. About 30% of second-quarter 2026 new orders were related to Bloom Energy, following a KRW 50.3 billion data center order in April 2026 and additional follow-on orders. Production is centered on the Ansan plant.

Per a KB Securities report published August 31, 2026, the purchase agreement for land and buildings for a third plant was completed on August 26, 2026.

The company competes with listed electrical equipment makers such as LS ELECTRIC, Hyosung Heavy Industries and HD Hyundai Electric, and securities firms have described its growth and profitability indicators as ahead of global peers.

04

Earnings analysis

On a disclosed annual basis, revenue rose from KRW 214.5 billion in 2023 to KRW 334 billion in 2024 (up 55.7% year on year) and KRW 501.9 billion in 2025 (up 50.3%). Revenue increased for two consecutive years from 2024 through 2025, and 2025 was the largest year of 2023 to 2025.

Operating profit was KRW 46.6 billion in 2023, KRW 109.2 billion in 2024 (up 134.4%) and KRW 178.6 billion in 2025 (up 63.5%), also rising for two consecutive years. The operating margin moved from 21.7% to 32.7% and then 35.6% over the same period, so margin gains came alongside revenue growth.

Profit attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 39.1 billion in 2023, KRW 83.7 billion in 2024 (up 114.3%) and KRW 148.9 billion in 2025 (up 78.0%). Net income including non-controlling interests was reported at the same amounts for each year.

Operating cash flow was KRW 35.2 billion in 2023, KRW 15.5 billion in 2024 and KRW 115.5 billion in 2025. The debt-to-equity ratio fell from 51.9% in 2023 to 13.5% in 2024 and 16.5% in 2025.

At the end of 2025, total equity was KRW 585.5 billion, total liabilities were KRW 96.5 billion, and equity attributable to controlling shareholders was also KRW 585.5 billion.

On a quarterly basis, revenue moved from KRW 132.7 billion in the third quarter of 2025 to KRW 142.1 billion in the fourth quarter, KRW 150.3 billion in the first quarter of 2026 and KRW 164.2 billion in the second quarter.

Third-quarter 2026 revenue of KRW 146 billion fell 11.1% from the prior quarter but rose 10.0% from a year earlier. Operating profit went from KRW 62 billion in the second quarter of 2026 to KRW 44.5 billion in the third quarter, down 28.2% quarter on quarter and up 4.6% year on year.

Korea Investment & Securities analyzed on November 5, 2026 that weather-related shipment delays deferred about KRW 78 billion of revenue recognition, and that the won-dollar exchange rate decline and higher raw material prices weighed on profitability.

The same firm viewed these factors as temporary and pointed to revenue scale as the key to margin recovery. Profit attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 41.1 billion in the third quarter of 2026, down 24.7% from the prior quarter and up 11.5% year on year. The sum for the last four quarters (fourth quarter 2025 through third quarter 2026) is KRW 184.6 billion.

05

Industry analysis

On the demand side, the replacement of aging US power grids and rising data center power demand support the transformer market. Yuanta Securities, citing US Energy Information Administration data, reported that US new energy storage system installations in the first half of 2026 rose 35% year on year.

The same firm said data center power demand should keep growth solid in the second half. Korea Investment & Securities said the company is winning new customers across renewable energy, energy storage and data centers, and that European orders are rising after the US.

On tariffs, one reported analysis said distribution and low-voltage transformers are not subject to Section 232 steel tariffs, and that special transformers, over 60% of sales, are shipped on FOB terms, which limits the practical tariff burden.

SK Securities assessed that the US tariff change centered on extra-high-voltage transformers, effective April 2026, has a limited effect on the company, whose main product is distribution transformers.

On the cycle, grid transformer shipments were weak in 2025 due to customer inventory adjustments, and Kyobo Securities and IBK Securities expected a recovery phase in 2026 after inventories were depleted, according to reports published in January 2026.

On competitive position, Bloom Energy's formal vendor registration and entry into the 154kV extra-high-voltage transformer market are cited as differentiators.

By contrast, NH Investment & Securities explained that GE Vernova's acquisition of Prolec GE had raised concerns about stalled special transformer volumes from a key customer.

Outlook

Order flow is running ahead of the annual order target for 2026. Korea Investment & Securities said cumulative new orders through the third quarter of 2026 reached KRW 701.4 billion, achieving the annual order guidance ahead of schedule.

Yuanta Securities analyzed the order backlog at KRW 556.7 billion in the second quarter. Shinhan Investment Corp. said shipped but unrecognized revenue totaled about KRW 78 billion.

Reports say a second plant expansion is planned for completion in 2027, and that a larger capacity could support annual sales on the order of KRW 1 trillion.

Production for the 154kV extra-high-voltage transformer line is planned to start construction in the fourth quarter of 2026 using the remaining half of the Ansan second-plant site, with production starting in 2028 and annual capacity of roughly KRW 150 billion to KRW 200 billion.

NH Investment & Securities projected KRW 250 billion of sales growth from the 154kV expansion (reported May 21, 2026). KB Securities said completing the third plant purchase could secure up to KRW 1.2 trillion of capacity by 2028 (August 31, 2026 report).

Korea Investment & Securities estimated 2027 revenue would rise 35.3% year on year, with operating profit of KRW 327.7 billion and an operating margin of 36.7% (November 5, 2026 report).

Yuanta Securities projected 2026 revenue of KRW 690.5 billion and operating profit of KRW 258.8 billion, an estimate reported in September 2026 that predates the third-quarter results.

08

Bull factors

Record quarterly order intake

Second-quarter 2026 new orders reached KRW 243.5 billion, up 163.4% year on year and a quarterly record (Yuanta Securities).

Third-quarter 2026 new orders of KRW 278.9 billion set a new quarterly high, and cumulative new orders of KRW 701.4 billion reached the annual guidance early, according to Korea Investment & Securities. The second-quarter backlog stood at KRW 556.7 billion.

Expanding data center references

Yuanta Securities interpreted repeated Bloom Energy orders, rather than a single project, as a sign that the company's vendor position in the data center on-site power value chain is taking shape.

KB Securities reported that talks with other customers on data center transformer test deliveries are under way after the Bloom order. Yuanta Securities said more new customers and applications could broaden the revenue base from 2027 onward.

High margins and peer comparison

The operating margin rose from 21.7% in 2023 to 35.6% in 2025 and reached 37.8% in the second quarter of 2026 (Yuanta Securities analysis). LS Securities assessed that the company leads global peers on revenue growth, profitability and earnings growth. That assessment depends on the high-margin special transformer mix being maintained.

09

Bear factors

Third-quarter shipment delays and FX

Third-quarter 2026 operating profit of KRW 44.5 billion fell 28.2% from the prior quarter. Korea Investment & Securities said it fell short of market expectations. The shortfall reflected weather-related shipment delays, a weaker won-dollar rate and higher raw material costs. Quarterly results can swing with exchange rates and shipment timing, which is the bearish case.

Concentration in customers and regions

The US accounted for roughly 70% of sales in 2023. About 30% of second-quarter 2026 new orders were related to Bloom Energy. Power grid sales fell 32% from the prior quarter to KRW 26.6 billion in the same quarter, indicating a tilt toward renewable energy and data center demand.

Expansion costs and schedule dependence

Expansion raises supply capacity but brings plant stabilization, hiring needs and depreciation costs, as reported in the press. If the second-plant completion planned for 2027 or the 154kV line planned for 2028 slips, conversion of the order backlog into revenue could be delayed. NH Investment & Securities also noted concerns about volume stagnation following a customer's acquisition.

10

Risk factors

FX and tariffs

The won-dollar exchange rate was cited as a factor weighing on third-quarter 2026 profitability (Korea Investment & Securities).

Because the product mix is centered on distribution transformers, the tariff effect is described as limited, but policy changes affecting extra-high-voltage transformers are a separate item to monitor. With exports exceeding 80% of sales in 2023, currency and trade policy changes feed directly into results.

Supply schedule and shipments

Weather-related shipment delays deferred roughly KRW 78 billion of revenue recognition. The 2027 second-plant completion and the 2028 start of 154kV production are tied to schedules, so slippage could delay capacity growth and revenue conversion. Productivity issues during plant stabilization fall into the same category.

Customer concentration and policy

Bloom Energy-related orders accounted for about 30% of second-quarter 2026 new orders, a high dependence on one customer. Changes in US grid policy or data center investment flows could alter the pace of orders.

KB Securities viewed US grid policy as a tailwind, but a shift in policy direction could challenge that premise.

Overall view

Sanil Electric's revenue, operating profit and profit attributable to controlling shareholders rose for two consecutive years from 2023 through 2025, and its operating margin climbed from 21.7% to 35.6% over the same period.

In the third quarter of 2026, revenue rose 10.0% year on year, but operating profit fell from the prior quarter on shipment delays and exchange rate effects. Order indicators point in a different direction from quarterly earnings.

New orders of KRW 243.5 billion in the second quarter and KRW 278.9 billion in the third were both reported as quarterly highs, and the backlog stood at KRW 556.7 billion at the end of the second quarter of 2026.

Bullish factors include order expansion, data center and Bloom Energy references, and a high operating margin. Bearish factors include shipment timing swings, exchange rates, dependence on specific customers and expansion costs.

Target prices from securities firms were presented between August and November 2026, and each rests on different assumptions. The next checkpoints are the fourth-quarter 2026 results, the 2026 annual report and the second-plant completion schedule. This report does not present an investment opinion or target price.
