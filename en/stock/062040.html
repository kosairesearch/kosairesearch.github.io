02

Business structure

Sanil Electric, founded in 1987, is a transformer maker that centers on special transformers and serves three fields: data centers, power grids and renewable energy. The company describes itself as a total transformer solution provider reaching from distribution to transmission markets.

Special transformer sales in Q2 2026 were KRW 132.4 billion, up 51.7% year on year, while grid transformer sales fell 30.6% to KRW 26.6 billion over the same period, according to an IBK Securities report. Special transformers account for about 90% of new orders and order backlog, per the same report.

The company previously sold mainly through indirect channels involving GE and the TMEIC joint venture of Toshiba and Mitsubishi, but management says its customers now include data center developers, EPC contractors and energy storage companies.

In April 2026 it signed a KRW 50.3 billion data center transformer supply contract with Bloom Energy and later became a formal Bloom vendor. Reports say more than 90% of revenue comes from exports, with the US accounting for over 70%.

In on-site power inside data centers, Eaton, ABB and Schneider Electric have mainly served the market, and Sanil Electric is entering it.

In extra-high-voltage transformers, Hyosung Heavy Industries, HD Hyundai Electric and LS ELECTRIC already have capacity, while Sanil Electric's portfolio remains centered on low-voltage distribution units.

04

Earnings analysis

Based on filed annual results, sales were KRW 214.5 billion in 2023, KRW 334 billion in 2024 (up 55.7% year on year) and KRW 501.9 billion in 2025 (up 50.3%). Sales rose in both 2024 and 2025, and 2025 was the largest year of 2023 to 2025.

Operating profit went from KRW 46.6 billion in 2023 to KRW 109.2 billion in 2024 (up 134.4%) and KRW 178.6 billion in 2025 (up 63.5%). Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 39.1 billion in 2023, KRW 83.7 billion in 2024 (up 114.3%) and KRW 148.9 billion in 2025 (up 78.0%).

Net profit including non-controlling interests was reported at the same levels for each year. The operating margin rose from 21.7% in 2023 to 32.7% in 2024 and 35.6% in 2025. Operating profit grew faster than sales, which brokers attribute to a higher mix of high-margin special transformers and operating leverage.

IBK Securities also cited lower selling and administrative expenses as a contributor to margin gains in Q2 2026. The debt ratio fell from 51.9% in 2023 to 13.5% in 2024 and 16.5% in 2025.

Total equity at the end of 2025 was KRW 585.5 billion, all attributable to controlling shareholders, and total liabilities were KRW 96.5 billion. Operating cash flow was KRW 35.2 billion in 2023, KRW 15.5 billion in 2024 and KRW 115.5 billion in 2025.

On a quarterly basis, sales were KRW 142.1 billion in Q4 2025 (up 7.1% from the prior quarter), KRW 150.3 billion in Q1 2026 (up 5.8%) and KRW 164.2 billion in Q2 2026 (up 9.2%). Q3 2026 sales were KRW 146 billion, down 11.1% from the prior quarter but up 10.0% year on year. Q3 2026 operating profit was KRW 44.5 billion, down 28.2% from the prior quarter and up 4.6% year on year.

05

Industry analysis

Transformer demand is rising with the expansion of AI data center power loads and the replacement cycle of the US grid. Reports in 2026 said US transformer supply continues to lag demand, and North American power equipment projects often face delays due to a shortage of transformers.

Euroinvest Securities analysts said Bloom Energy's expanding capacity and a rising adoption of solid oxide fuel cells for on-site data center power created delivery opportunities for Sanil Electric.

On the grid side, IBK Securities said grid transformer sales in 2025 were weak as supply shortages eased and shipment schedules were adjusted during tariff-sharing talks with customers. The same report expected a recovery in 2026 as customer inventories were largely consumed.

The big three domestic power equipment makers, centered on extra-high-voltage transformers, are reported to average operating margins in the 10% to 20% range, which is well below Sanil Electric's level.

Competitors already have extra-high-voltage capacity, and Sanil Electric is entering that segment with production targeted for 2028. A Mirae Asset Securities analyst said Sanil Electric is the most aggressive investor in capacity expansion among Korean power equipment makers.

06

Outlook

The company's 2026 order guidance is KRW 700 billion. LS Securities said first-half 2026 cumulative orders rose 61% year on year and expects full-year orders to exceed that guidance. The Bloom Energy project is disclosed to supply through the first quarter of 2027.

Beyond Bloom, the company is reportedly in talks with other data center power utilities on special transformer supply. A KRW 42.6 billion contract for BESS and renewable distribution transformers in Europe was signed in June 2026.

LS Securities said a KRW 51.2 billion reactor order for a large US solar project from TMEIC USA was the largest single order on record, and expects it to be reflected in sales through 2027.

The dedicated 154 kV transformer line will be built on the remaining 18,500 square meters of the Ansan No. 2 plant starting in Q4 2026, with production set to begin in 2028 and an added capacity of KRW 150 billion to KRW 200 billion in annual sales.

Yuanta Securities noted that a gap can exist between order growth and sales growth, and that orders secured now could convert into sales from 2027 onward. The key check is whether order growth is accompanied by higher shipments and a sustained high-margin product mix.

08

Bull factors

Direct data center orders expanding

A KRW 50.3 billion data center transformer contract with Bloom Energy was disclosed in April 2026, and follow-on volume has reportedly been secured since Sanil Electric became a formal Bloom vendor.

Yuanta Securities said only the first of three project phases is reflected so far and put cumulative Bloom-related orders at about KRW 75 billion. Q2 2026 new orders reached KRW 243.5 billion, up 163.4% year on year, and the order backlog rose 32.7% to KRW 556.7 billion, according to IBK Securities.

High margins versus industry peers

The operating margin rose from 21.7% in 2023 to 35.6% in 2025, compared with reported average margins of 10% to 20% for the big three domestic power equipment makers. Brokers forecast 2026 operating margins in the high-30% range.

The margin gain is attributed to a higher share of special transformers together with lower selling and administrative expenses.

Capacity expansion and entry into extra-high-voltage

A Mirae Asset Securities analyst said Sanil Electric is the most aggressive capacity investor among Korean power equipment makers. The company is considering extra-high-voltage transformer expansion to move beyond its low-voltage distribution focus.

Brokers expect total capacity could reach as much as KRW 1.1 trillion by 2031 after new facilities are completed.

09

Bear factors

Q3 quarterly profit decline

Q3 2026 sales were KRW 146 billion, down 11.1% from the prior quarter, and operating profit was KRW 44.5 billion, down 28.2%. Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders fell 24.7% to KRW 41.1 billion, after reaching KRW 54.6 billion in Q2 2026. Year on year, all three measures still rose, but operating profit growth slowed to 4.6%.

Grid transformer weakness and extra-high-voltage gap

Grid transformer sales in Q2 2026 were KRW 26.6 billion, down 30.6% year on year, according to an IBK Securities report. Sanil Electric does not yet have extra-high-voltage transformer capacity, while Hyosung Heavy Industries, HD Hyundai Electric and LS ELECTRIC already do. Closing this portfolio gap depends on the new plant investment now underway.

Cash generation swings and capex burden

Operating cash flow fell from KRW 35.2 billion in 2023 to KRW 15.5 billion in 2024 before recovering to KRW 115.5 billion in 2025. IB Tomato reported that Q1 2026 operating cash flow was KRW 38.1 billion, 2.3 times the prior-year figure of KRW 16.8 billion. Large capital expenditures for capacity expansion continue, so cash outflow pressure remains.

10

Risk factors

Tariffs and trade policy

A large share of revenue comes from the US, so the outcome of tariff talks could directly affect results. A October 2025 Yuanta Securities report said the FOB delivery structure and a high share of items outside Section 232 limit the effective tariff burden. Still, shipment schedules were adjusted during tariff-sharing talks in 2025, so policy risk remains.

Customer concentration and follow-on orders

Cumulative Bloom-related orders are about KRW 75 billion, and Yuanta Securities sees room for second and third phase follow-on orders. If follow-on orders are delayed or fail to materialize, the pace of data center sales growth could slow.

LS Securities said talks with other data center power providers are ongoing, but whether they turn into contracts is not yet clear.

Extra-high-voltage expansion timing and site

The 154 kV line is targeted to start production in 2028, and any delay could weaken competitiveness in extra-high-voltage bidding. The remaining Ansan No. 2 site limits volume capacity, and the company is reportedly looking for a larger site to cover four to five years of demand. Competitors are already expanding extra-high-voltage capacity, so the pace of entry matters.

12

Overall view

Sanil Electric's sales, operating profit and net profit attributable to controlling shareholders rose in both 2024 and 2025 after 2023, and its operating margin climbed from 21.7% to 35.6%.

Q2 2026 sales of KRW 164.2 billion were the highest quarterly figure in the filed record, and new orders of KRW 243.5 billion topped KRW 200 billion for the first time on a quarterly basis.

In Q3 2026, however, sales of KRW 146 billion, operating profit of KRW 44.5 billion and net profit attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 41.1 billion all fell from the prior quarter.

The Bloom Energy orders and direct data center supply are converting into bookings, but the decline in grid transformer sales and the lack of extra-high-voltage capacity remain unresolved. US tariff policy and the timing of follow-on orders are also variables that could shape results.

The next checkpoints are Q4 2026 results, completion of Bloom supply in Q1 2027, and whether the extra-high-voltage line starts production in 2028.
