02

Business structure

Woorison F&G is an agricultural corporation founded in March 2002 and listed on KOSDAQ on July 27, 2016, operating as a meat processing and distribution company. The company says its portfolio links livestock production through processing and distribution.

Its main lines are finishing pigs, finishing cattle, processed and distributed products, poultry, and overseas operations. One tally of second-quarter 2026 sales puts the livestock and processing and distribution business at 80%, overseas at 13%, and poultry at 7%.

Another tally splits the mix into finishing pigs 48%, processing and distribution 26%, overseas 14%, poultry 8%, and other 4%, so segment definitions differ by source. Consolidated sales were KRW 287.1 billion in 2025, and the 2024 level of KRW 292.7 billion was the largest year in the 2022 to 2025 period.

In 2025 the overseas division's sales fell on a disease issue, while higher hog prices lifted operating profit and net income. The controlling shareholder is a holding company that bought common shares on the open market in May and September 2025. The board has eight directors, two of them outside directors.

04

Earnings analysis

On the disclosed annual figures, sales were KRW 286.9 billion in 2022, KRW 279.3 billion in 2023 (down 2.6% year on year), KRW 292.7 billion in 2024 (up 4.8%), and KRW 287.1 billion in 2025 (down 1.9%). Sales stayed within a narrow band, with 2024 the largest year and 2023 the smallest.

Operating profit fell from KRW 28.5 billion in 2022 to KRW 4.63 billion in 2023 (down 83.7%), then rose to KRW 27.5 billion in 2024 (up 494.1%) and KRW 32.7 billion in 2025 (up 18.8%), a two-year run of gains.

The operating margin was 9.9% in 2022, 1.7% in 2023, 9.4% in 2024 and 11.4% in 2025, the highest of the four years.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders fell from KRW 25 billion in 2022 to KRW 1.55 billion in 2023 (down 93.8%), then recovered to KRW 15.1 billion in 2024 (up 872.5%) and KRW 23.8 billion in 2025 (up 58.3%).

Net income including non-controlling interests was KRW 23.6 billion in 2025, a different figure from the attributable amount. The filings attribute the 2025 sales decline to a disease issue at the overseas division and the operating profit increase to higher hog prices.

Quarterly, the third quarter of 2025 showed sales of KRW 71.4 billion, operating profit of KRW 18.6 billion and attributable net income of KRW 16.1 billion.

The fourth quarter of 2025 showed sales of KRW 75.3 billion, up 5.5% from the prior quarter, but an operating loss of KRW 3.22 billion and an attributable net loss of KRW 2.58 billion.

The first quarter of 2026 had sales of KRW 71.1 billion, down 5.6%, and operating profit of KRW 2.66 billion, a return to profit, with attributable net income of KRW 1.1 billion.

The second quarter of 2026 had sales of KRW 77 billion, up 8.3%, operating profit of KRW 17 billion, up 540.0%, and attributable net income of KRW 12.6 billion.

The third quarter of 2026 had sales of KRW 77.9 billion, up 1.2% from the prior quarter and 9.1% year on year, but operating profit of KRW 13.6 billion fell 20.1% from the prior quarter and 27.1% from a year earlier, with attributable net income of KRW 8.98 billion.

Over the last four quarters, attributable net income totaled KRW 20.1 billion. The quarterly swing between loss and profit means annual totals alone understate how uneven the earnings path has been.

05

Industry analysis

The hog business is driven by hog prices and feed and rearing costs. The company cited higher hog prices as the driver of the 2025 increases in operating and net profit.

FnGuide company data show that in early 2025 hog shipments declined, but demand recovery after COVID-19 and higher feed grain prices and rearing costs lifted hog prices and improved gross profit and operating profit. The same mechanism works in reverse when prices fall, which is a basic feature of the business.

On the environmental side, pig waste treatment is costly, and tighter environmental regulation has raised related costs across the industry. Animal disease affects both supply and overseas sales.

The company listed a system for responding to disease outbreaks among its priority tasks in its value-up plan, and in 2025 the overseas division's sales fell because of a disease issue. Compared with the KOSDAQ food and tobacco sector aggregate, the company's sales scale is small.

On the cycle, higher hog prices lifted 2025 profit, while the 27.1% year-on-year drop in third-quarter 2026 operating profit suggests the same price tailwind has weakened. Because prices, disease and environmental costs move together, quarterly results need to be read with all three factors in view.

Outlook

The corporate value-up plan, resolved by the board on October 21, 2026 and disclosed on October 22, commits to keeping the shareholder return ratio at 30% or higher and to maintaining and expanding a stable cash dividend over the medium to long term.

The plan includes an analysis of the causes of the low price-to-book ratio and a plan to improve it. Its priority tasks are productivity gains, stronger cost competitiveness, and a system for responding to disease outbreaks. The 2025 payout ratio was 43.6%, and the dividend total for 2025 rose 114.3% from 2024.

On the shareholder side, the controlling shareholder's holding company disclosed open-market purchases of 690,000 shares in May 2025 and 1.4 million shares in September 2025, equal to 2.1% and 4.2% of its holdings.

At the annual general meeting on March 24, 2026, shareholders approved the 25th-term financial statements and elected three executive directors, including one newly elected. Sales rose in the second and third quarters of 2026, but operating profit fell in the third quarter.

The next task is to separate how much of the second-quarter 2026 operating profit recovery of KRW 17 billion came from hog prices and how much from a recovery at the overseas business. Once the 2026 annual report discloses segment sales and overseas results, that split can be measured.

Risk factors

Hog prices and feed costs

Sales and operating profit depend heavily on hog prices. Higher prices lifted 2025 operating profit, but a reversal would work in the opposite direction. If feed grain prices and rearing costs rise together, the benefit of higher hog prices can be offset.

Animal disease

The 2025 disease issue at the overseas division led to lower sales. The company has listed a disease-response system among its priority tasks. An outbreak in domestic rearing or overseas operations could bring production disruption and sales restrictions at the same time.

Environmental regulation and waste costs

Pig waste creates environmental costs for treatment facilities, and tighter environmental regulation is raising related costs across the industry. Stricter rules can lift both capital spending and operating costs, which can pressure margins.

The annual amount of these costs is not broken out in the disclosures covered here, so the cost structure should be checked in the annual report.

Overall view
