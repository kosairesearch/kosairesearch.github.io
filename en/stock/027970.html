02

Business structure

Hankuk Paper MFG is a specialty paper maker of industrial packaging paper, operating in two segments: printing paper and paperboard. According to company profile data, printing paper accounted for 75.3% of 2025 sales and paperboard for 24.7%.

Its main printing-paper products are the copy paper Milk and the premium copy paper Silk, and the company profile says it is strengthening its position as a domestic copy-paper producer. The paperboard segment centers on white board and other industrial packaging paper.

The August 2023 merger with the former Hankuk Paper brought the printing-paper business into consolidated sales and enlarged the revenue base. A 2023 brokerage report attributed growing white-board demand to higher online shopping and food-delivery use.

Press reports on the first half of 2026 put printing-segment sales at KRW 320 billion, up 7.8%, and package-segment sales at KRW 99.3 billion, up 5.6%. The premium lineup includes the eco-friendly packaging materials Green Shield and G-Flex and the premium packaging paper KAce Beauty.

On the production side, the company is pursuing cost competitiveness, including a new combined boiler at the Onsan mill. Printing paper faces price competition as new capacity in Southeast Asia comes online.

Listed companies in the same industry classification include Hansol Paper, Moorim P&P, and Sambo Corrugated Board.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual sales rose from KRW 227.3 billion in 2022 to KRW 712 billion in 2023, an increase of 213.2%, as the merged printing-paper business was consolidated. Sales peaked at KRW 792.1 billion in 2024, the largest year in the 2022 to 2025 range, before falling 4.8% to KRW 753.7 billion in 2025.

Operating profit went from KRW 3.5 billion in 2022 to KRW 13.5 billion in 2023 (up 285.4%) and KRW 19.3 billion in 2024 (up 43.1%), then dropped to KRW 3.12 billion in 2025 (down 83.8%). The operating margin moved from 1.5% in 2022 to 1.9% in 2023 and 2.4% in 2024, before falling to 0.4% in 2025.

Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 2.26 billion in 2022, turned to a net loss of KRW 66.6 billion in 2023, returned to a KRW 4.1 billion profit in 2024, and turned to a net loss of KRW 33.5 billion in 2025.

Operating cash flow was KRW 6.28 billion in 2022, KRW 101.9 billion in 2023, -KRW 21.3 billion in 2024, and KRW 50.1 billion in 2025. In Q4 2025 an operating loss of KRW 3.26 billion coincided with a controlling-shareholder net loss of KRW 39.1 billion, a gap that points to large non-operating items.

In Q1 2026 operating profit of KRW 4.56 billion turned positive, but a fine booked as a provision widened the controlling-shareholder net loss to KRW 47.6 billion. In Q2 2026 operating profit was KRW 4.68 billion and controlling-shareholder net profit was KRW 1.15 billion, a swing to profit.

Q3 2026 sales were KRW 203.4 billion, up 12.0% from a year earlier, with operating profit of KRW 1.64 billion and controlling-shareholder net profit of KRW 5.58 billion. The controlling-shareholder net loss over the last four quarters totaled KRW 79.9 billion.

05

Industry analysis

Printing paper is a market where shrinking paper consumption and a weak economy have combined with new capacity in Southeast Asia to intensify competition. A company performance comment noted that Hankuk Paper MFG has defended profitability through cost reduction in this environment.

The company said printing paper recovered in Q2 2026 on strong exports to North America. Paperboard is supported by demand from online shopping and food delivery, but a 2023 brokerage analysis judged the growth of the market the company participates in to be limited.

The package segment returned to profit in Q2 2026, with rising demand for packaging materials cited as the driver. On raw materials, pulp prices and the exchange rate were cited as pressures that widened first-half losses.

An industry source said the second half will be a contest over whether the recovery in exports and packaging offsets the fine, financing costs and cost pressures.

Competitors in the same paper and wood industry classification, including Hansol Paper, Moorim P&P and DaelimPaper, compete in printing-paper pricing and export markets.

On April 23, 2026, the Korea Fair Trade Commission imposed fines totaling KRW 338.3 billion on six paper producers, including Hankuk Paper MFG, over printing-paper price collusion. The case shows that regulatory pressure spans the printing-paper industry.

06

Outlook

The company said it will step up market efforts in the second half of 2026, centering on high-value products including the eco-friendly packaging materials Green Shield and G-Flex and the premium packaging paper KAce Beauty.

In the package segment, it plans to continue sales growth through product-line expansion and stronger service. It also said it will flesh out results from nanocellulose and other eco-friendly, high-performance materials to build medium- and long-term growth.

In renewable energy, the company is expanding the land it can offer for wind-power projects under a wind-power MOU signed with Geumyang Green Power in March 2024.

On capital structure, the reduction of 27,517 common shares, with a cancellation date of April 28, 2026, is expected by analysts to improve the balance sheet.

The company plans to pay the fine in two years of installments starting from the payment date assigned once the suspension period ends or is lifted, and a company official said there are no separate external borrowing plans.

Press reports also said a damages lawsuit related to the paper collusion case is pending, so any additional cost remains a variable depending on the outcome. The next points to check are the Q4 and full-year 2026 preliminary results and the operating margin and operating cash flow in the 2026 annual report.

08

Bull factors

Operating profit stayed positive through 2026

Operating profit was KRW 4.56 billion in Q1 2026, KRW 4.68 billion in Q2 and KRW 1.64 billion in Q3, so all three quarters stayed in profit. In Q2 strong North American exports for printing paper coincided with the package segment's return to profit.

The company said profit-focused operations and better supply-chain management shortened delivery times and raised production responsiveness.

Eco-friendly and premium product strategy

The company is centering its second-half push on eco-friendly and premium products, including Green Shield, G-Flex and KAce Beauty. A cost-competitiveness strategy, including a new combined boiler at the Onsan mill, is running in parallel. The Q2 2026 operating profit recovery may serve as one gauge of how well this strategy is working.

Improving balance sheet

The debt-to-equity ratio fell from 98.4% in 2022 to 63.7% in 2025, and total equity at the end of 2025 was KRW 406.2 billion. A capital reduction that cancelled 27,517 common shares, with a cancellation date of April 28, 2026, improved the capital structure.

The company said the fine is planned for two years of installments, which would reduce the immediate cash burden.

09

Bear factors

Fine and cumulative net losses

With a fine of more than KRW 49 billion booked in Q1 2026, the controlling-shareholder net loss widened to KRW 47.6 billion. The fine was recognized as an accounting loss first, and because the cash outflow has not yet occurred, it could become a cash burden later.

Cash flow and borrowing pressure

Press reports said operating cash flow turned negative again in the first half of 2026, after a KRW 21.3 billion outflow in 2024 and a KRW 50.1 billion inflow in 2025. The same reports said short-term borrowings rose by more than 20%. Higher working-capital needs, including growth in receivables, were cited as the cause.

Printing-paper demand and cost pressure

Shrinking printing-paper consumption and Southeast Asian capacity additions have intensified competition, and the operating margin fell to 0.4% in 2025. Press reports said pulp prices and the exchange rate weighed on first-half results.

An industry source said the key question is whether second-half export and packaging recovery can offset the fine, financing costs and cost pressures.

10

Risk factors

Regulation and litigation

The KRW 49.057 billion Fair Trade Commission fine and a damages lawsuit related to the paper collusion case could add further costs. Quarterly net results have become more volatile since the fine was booked. The effect of the installment schedule on cash flow has not been settled.

Raw materials and FX

Pulp prices and exchange-rate moves directly press production costs. With the operating margin at 0.4% in 2025, there is little cushion, so cost changes can quickly flow through to profit. Pulp and exchange-rate burdens were both cited as factors that widened first-half losses.

Liquidity

Short-term borrowings rose by more than 20%, and operating cash flow turned negative in the first half. The company said it has no external borrowing plans, but if working-capital pressure persists, financial flexibility could narrow. The fine equals 12.08% of the company's equity, another factor to weigh.

Overall view

Hankuk Paper MFG is a paper maker with both printing-paper and package businesses. Operating profit fell to KRW 3.12 billion in 2025, but operating profit and controlling-shareholder net profit were both positive in Q2 and Q3 2026.

A Fair Trade Commission fine booked as a provision in Q1 2026 widened the controlling-shareholder net loss to KRW 47.6 billion, and the net loss over the last four quarters totaled KRW 79.9 billion.

Positive factors include the recovery in North American exports, the package segment's return to profit, the eco-friendly premium product line, and a lower debt-to-equity ratio.

Negative factors include the cash outflow from the fine, higher short-term borrowings, the first-half operating cash flow turn to negative, and competition from weak printing-paper demand and Southeast Asian capacity.

On valuation, the price-to-book ratio is well below 1x, but because of the net losses over the last four quarters, a price-to-earnings ratio cannot be calculated.

The next points to check are the Q4 and full-year 2026 preliminary results, the operating margin and cash flow in the 2026 annual report, and the installment payments and lawsuit progress. This report is for information only and does not include a buy or sell opinion or a target price.
