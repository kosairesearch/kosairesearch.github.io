02

Business structure

WonIk owns about 30% of Wonik Holdings (23,170,763 shares, per a January 2026 report), making it a mid-tier holding company.

Wonik Holdings groups Wonik IPS (semiconductor equipment), Wonik QNC (semiconductor parts) and WONIK Materials (semiconductor materials) under its umbrella, so WonIk sits at the top of their ownership chain on an indirect basis.

Wonik Holdings also holds a 96.66% stake in the industrial robot maker Wonik Robotics, extending the group into robotics. Press reports from January 2026 described the Wonik Holdings group as more than 30 companies, including unlisted affiliates, functioning as a de facto group holding company.

Within semiconductor equipment, Wonik IPS is described as the group's flagship company. Expectations for its orders have been linked to the stated memory investment plans of Samsung Electronics and SK hynix.

A May 2024 report also pointed to the cosmetics business of subsidiary CMS Lab, which supplies brands through Daiso and Olive Young, as a driver of WonIk's share price at that time. The group therefore spans semiconductor equipment and materials alongside consumer businesses. WonIk's own 2025 sales were KRW 150.7 billion.

04

Earnings analysis

On a reported basis, sales rose from KRW 108.1 billion in 2022 to KRW 129.9 billion in 2023 (up 20.2% year on year), KRW 138.7 billion in 2024 (up 6.8%) and KRW 150.7 billion in 2025 (up 8.6%), growing for three consecutive years from 2023 through 2025. 2025 was the largest year of 2022 to 2025.

Operating profit went from KRW 7.41 billion in 2022 to KRW 12.5 billion in 2023 (up 68.6%), the largest year in the period, then fell to KRW 9.32 billion in 2024 (down 25.4%) and KRW 8.92 billion in 2025 (down 4.2%), declining for two consecutive years.

Sales growth did not carry through to margins, as the operating margin slipped from 9.6% in 2023 to 6.7% in 2024 and 5.9% in 2025.

Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders moved from KRW 38.2 billion in 2022 to a net loss of KRW 2.97 billion in 2023, a swing into loss, then turned to a profit of KRW 21.1 billion in 2024, and fell 45.6% to KRW 11.5 billion in 2025.

In 2022, net profit attributable to controlling shareholders was far above operating profit of KRW 7.41 billion, which points to a large contribution from non-operating items.

In 2023, a net loss of KRW 2.63 billion occurred despite operating profit of KRW 12.5 billion, indicating that items outside operations pulled earnings down. Operating cash flow went from negative KRW 3.06 billion in 2022 to KRW 9.93 billion in 2023 and KRW 10 billion in 2024, then dropped to KRW 1.09 billion in 2025.

Quarterly results were also volatile. The fourth quarter of 2025 recorded an operating loss of KRW 5.43 million, followed by operating profit of KRW 2.67 billion in the first quarter of 2026, and the second quarter of 2026 reached sales of KRW 54 billion and operating profit of KRW 3.92 billion.

Third-quarter 2026 sales were KRW 46.6 billion, up 17.6% year on year, but operating profit of KRW 1.45 billion fell 32.5%, and net profit attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 1.56 billion fell 81.1%.

Over the last four quarters (fourth quarter of 2025 through third quarter of 2026), net profit attributable to controlling shareholders totaled KRW 14.1 billion, above the KRW 11.5 billion recorded for the full year 2025.

Industry analysis

WonIk's earnings are tied to the semiconductor equipment and materials cycle through its listed affiliates. A January 2026 report said Wonik Holdings' share price rose sharply as the semiconductor supercycle lifted results at related subsidiaries such as Wonik IPS and WONIK Materials.

A December 2025 report cited memory investment plans from Samsung Electronics and SK hynix as a factor supporting expectations for process equipment orders. Wonik IPS disclosed on November 9, 2026 that its provisional operating profit for the third quarter of 2026 was KRW 66.65 billion, up 142.03% year on year.

Over the same period, Wonik IPS sales rose 25.83% to KRW 337.79 billion, while its net profit fell 88.22% to KRW 3.33 billion. WONIK Materials also reported provisional third-quarter 2026 operating profit of KRW 20.34 billion, up 43.69% year on year, on the same day.

Both figures are unaudited and may change before final results. Against the operating gains at these equipment and materials units, WonIk's own 2025 operating profit declined to KRW 8.92 billion, so how far subsidiary upturns reach WonIk's own profit is the central industry question.

Bear factors

Own operating profit down two years in a row

Operating profit peaked at KRW 12.5 billion in 2023 and then fell to KRW 9.32 billion in 2024 (down 25.4%) and KRW 8.92 billion in 2025 (down 4.2%). The operating margin dropped from 9.6% in 2023 to 5.9% in 2025. Margin erosion while sales grew is the core bearish argument.

Third-quarter earnings slowdown and quarterly volatility

Third-quarter 2026 sales of KRW 46.6 billion rose 17.6% year on year, but operating profit of KRW 1.45 billion fell 32.5%. Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 1.56 billion dropped 81.1%.

Compared with KRW 11.2 billion in the second quarter, the gap between quarters makes the earnings trend inconsistent.

Weaker cash generation and holding-structure drag

Operating cash flow fell from KRW 10 billion in 2024 to KRW 1.09 billion in 2025. Because a holding company's earnings depend on its affiliates, a slowdown at the subsidiaries can quickly widen swings at the parent. The decline in profit in 2025 after the 2024 return to profit is also cited as a bearish factor.

Risk factors

Semiconductor up-cycle reversal

The results of affiliates Wonik IPS and WONIK Materials depend on memory chip investment cycles. A change in investment stance could reduce equipment and materials orders quickly, altering how fast affiliate gains reach WonIk's own profit. Third-quarter 2026 provisional figures may still be adjusted in the audit process.

Quarterly volatility and holding-company structure

WonIk's quarterly operating profit moved from a loss in the fourth quarter of 2025 to KRW 3.92 billion in the second quarter of 2026. A holding company's results can swing with how affiliate income is recognized. In a mid-tier holding structure, stake relationships could also change with shifts in group ownership.

Cash flow and balance-sheet pressure

Operating cash flow fell to KRW 1.09 billion in 2025, and total liabilities at the end of 2025 stood at KRW 95.6 billion. If facility or affiliate investment rises while profit is weak, cash headroom could tighten. The debt ratio has fallen, but whether cash flow recovers remains an open issue.
