02

Business structure

Songwon Industrial was founded in 1965 and listed on the KOSPI in 1977. It produces polymer stabilizers that protect plastics from performance degradation, and its products fall into industrial chemicals and performance chemicals.

Based on classifications by the data provider WiseReport, industrial chemicals account for 72.53% of product revenue and performance chemicals for 27.47%.

In the second quarter of 2026, according to the half-year review report, industrial chemicals generated KRW 242.5 billion and performance chemicals KRW 86.0 billion, which together match group revenue of KRW 328.5 billion for the quarter.

Adjusted operating profit for the quarter was KRW 36.3 billion in industrial chemicals and KRW 3.5 billion in performance chemicals, so profit was concentrated in industrial chemicals.

By region, Other Asia was the largest at KRW 101.0 billion, followed by Europe at KRW 80.3 billion, the Americas at KRW 78.8 billion, Korea at KRW 50.2 billion, and the Middle East and Africa at KRW 18.2 billion.

Korea accounts for roughly 15% of quarterly revenue, so exchange rates and overseas demand feed directly into results. The company has manufacturing sites in Korea, Germany, the United States, China and India, and WiseReport counts 15 subsidiaries and 2 branches.

The company attributed first-half 2026 profitability gains to higher sales volumes and its pricing policy. Its competition is with global additive makers, and the breadth of its overseas production and sales network is central to its business structure.

04

Earnings analysis

The annual trend has been downward since 2022. Revenue fell from KRW 1.33 trillion in 2022 to KRW 1.03 trillion in 2023, down 22.5% year on year, edged up to KRW 1.07 trillion in 2024 (up 3.9%), and dropped to KRW 1.04 trillion in 2025 (down 2.9%).

Operating profit was KRW 185.1 billion in 2022, with an operating margin of 13.9%, then KRW 58.5 billion in 2023 (margin 5.7%, down 68.4%) and KRW 62.8 billion in 2024 (margin 5.9%, up 7.4%).

It reached KRW 21.8 billion in 2025, a 2.1% margin and a 65.2% decline. 2025 was the smallest operating profit year of 2022 to 2025. Net income attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 131.9 billion in 2022, KRW 34.8 billion in 2023, KRW 45.1 billion in 2024 and KRW 2.34 billion in 2025.

Net income including non-controlling interests is reported at the same yearly values. Quarterly, Q3 2025 revenue was KRW 258.6 billion with operating profit of KRW 5.39 billion. Q4 2025 revenue fell 7.4% quarter on quarter to KRW 239.4 billion and posted an operating loss of KRW 3.08 billion.

Q1 2026 revenue was KRW 269.2 billion with operating profit of KRW 26.6 billion, a turn to profit. Q2 2026 revenue rose 22.0% quarter on quarter to KRW 328.5 billion and operating profit rose 49.9% to KRW 39.8 billion.

Q3 2026 revenue was KRW 301.2 billion, down 8.3% quarter on quarter, and operating profit was KRW 23.7 billion, down 40.4%. Based on the Q3 2026 earnings release, cumulative operating profit for the first nine months of 2026 was KRW 90.1 billion, up 261.5% from the prior-year period.

The Q2 operating profit includes a remeasurement effect of about KRW 10.5 billion, which came from a change in how an employee benefit liability is paid. Because this item does not recur, it should be treated as one-off when reading the quarterly trend.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders over the latest four quarters, from Q4 2025 to Q3 2026, totaled KRW 58.0 billion. Operating cash flow was KRW 101.6 billion in 2022, KRW 166.8 billion in 2023, KRW 80.7 billion in 2024 and KRW 53.5 billion in 2025, positive in all four years. Total equity at end-2025 was KRW 760.1 billion and total liabilities were KRW 387.2 billion.

05

Industry analysis

Polymer stabilizers are a downstream business tied to plastics processing demand, and raw material prices and supply disruptions drive margins.

According to the company-level commentary on second-quarter 2026 results, Middle East conflict pushed up demand and created supply shortages that expanded sales volumes and margins for polymer stabilizers. The same commentary said fuel and lubricant additives also performed well.

The recent price strength therefore reflects geopolitical supply disruption to a large degree, and calling it structural demand growth would be premature. If supply normalizes, volumes and margins could reverse, so quarterly margin trends should be watched to judge where the cycle stands.

In its first-half 2026 results, the company attributed profitability gains to higher sales volumes, effective pricing and operational execution. First-half profit also includes the remeasurement effect, so operating improvement and accounting effects should be read separately.

The company operates in specialty additives, including antioxidants and PVC stabilizers, and differs in scale and structure from large petrochemical groups. Its manufacturing bases across Korea, Germany, the United States, China and India help spread regional demand differences.

Bull factors

Return to operating profit

Cumulative operating profit through Q3 2026 was KRW 90.1 billion, a sharp increase on the prior-year period, in contrast to full-year 2025 operating profit of KRW 21.8 billion.

Profit concentrated in industrial chemicals

In Q2 2026, adjusted operating profit in industrial chemicals was KRW 36.3 billion, well above KRW 3.5 billion in performance chemicals. On a first-half basis, the figures were KRW 54.8 billion and KRW 11.6 billion. The company attributed the improvement to higher sales volumes and its pricing policy. Commentary on the results also linked higher polymer stabilizer demand to Middle East disruption.

Improved financial structure

The debt ratio declined from 78.3% in 2022 to 54.4% in 2023, 56.1% in 2024 and 50.9% in 2025. Total liabilities at end-2025 were KRW 387.2 billion and total equity was KRW 760.1 billion. Operating cash flow stayed positive in each year from 2022 to 2025. This gives the company a financial cushion through periods of industry volatility.

Bear factors

Profit including one-off effects

Q2 2026 operating profit includes a remeasurement effect of about KRW 10.5 billion from a change in how an employee benefit liability is paid. The Q1 2026 release also said profit rose partly because previously recognized expenses were partly reversed.

Excluding these items, underlying quarterly profit may read lower than the reported figures. Because remeasurement does not recur, it is hard to treat as sustainable earnings.

High earnings volatility

The 2025 operating margin fell to 2.1%, and net income attributable to controlling shareholders was only KRW 2.34 billion. Q4 2025 produced an operating loss of KRW 3.08 billion and a net loss attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 2.74 billion.

In Q3 2026, revenue fell 8.3% quarter on quarter and operating profit fell 40.4%. Quarterly swings are large, so the return to profit is hard to treat as settled.

Limited revenue growth

Annual revenue fell from KRW 1.33 trillion in 2022 to KRW 1.04 trillion in 2025. Apart from a 3.9% gain in 2024, the revenue trend has not expanded clearly. Because the profit recovery leans on margins and one-off effects, a limited revenue base could make it hard to sustain. If Middle East disruption eases, demand and supply conditions could change.

Risk factors

Geopolitics and raw materials

Stronger polymer stabilizer demand and tighter supply are linked to Middle East conflict. Because overseas sales are large, exchange rates and overseas demand feed directly into results. Margins depend on how far raw material costs pass through to selling prices. These are external factors the company cannot fully control.

Accounting effects and earnings durability

Q1 and Q2 2026 earnings reflect a remeasurement effect on an employee benefit liability. The effect does not recur, so it is a poor baseline for future quarters. Whether operating profit excluding it stays positive is the key question. Accounting changes can blur the reading of results.

Financing costs and cash flow

Financing costs in the first half of 2026 totaled KRW 21.9 billion. Operating cash flow declined from KRW 101.6 billion in 2022 to KRW 53.5 billion in 2025. A continued decline in cash generation could affect dividend and investment capacity. Even as debt has fallen, financing costs remain a factor to track in results.

Overall view
