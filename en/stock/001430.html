02

Business structure

SeAH Besteel Holdings is a holding company whose operating business sits in its special steel subsidiaries.

The main subsidiaries are SeAH Besteel, which makes carbon-alloy special steel bars, Changwon Special Steel, which makes stainless wire rod and bars, and an aerospace and defense materials unit that supplies high-strength aluminum products.

In 2025, Changwon Special Steel posted revenue of KRW 1.3991 trillion and operating profit of KRW 53.9 billion, while SeAH Besteel posted operating profit of KRW 32.1 billion.

The aerospace and defense materials unit reported its best operating result since its founding, with revenue of KRW 128.7 billion and operating profit of KRW 24.6 billion. That unit accounted for 3.5% of group revenue but 24.1% of group operating profit in 2025, highlighting its high-value profile.

End markets include automotive parts for hybrid vehicles, construction and industrial machinery, defense and heavy equipment, AI semiconductor equipment, and energy and power plants. The aerospace business rests on long-term supply contracts with global aircraft makers such as Boeing and Airbus.

The largest competitive variable is low-priced Chinese special steel bar imports, whose volume into Korea rose 70.8% year on year in Q3 2026.

Overseas, the U.S. superalloy unit SST has secured annual capacity of 6,000 tons of master alloy and metal powder for casting, and a 20,000-ton-per-year stainless seamless steel pipe plant is under construction in Saudi Arabia.

04

Earnings analysis

Consolidated revenue fell from KRW 4.39 trillion in 2022 to KRW 4.08 trillion in 2023, down 6.9%, and then to KRW 3.64 trillion in 2024, down 11.0%, before recovering modestly to KRW 3.65 trillion in 2025, up 0.4%. Among 2022 to 2025, 2022 was the largest year and 2024 the smallest.

Operating profit rose from KRW 127.9 billion in 2022 to KRW 196.7 billion in 2023, up 53.8%, then dropped to KRW 52.3 billion in 2024, down 73.4%, and returned to KRW 98.4 billion in 2025, up 88.1%. Operating margin moved from 2.9% in 2022 to 4.8% in 2023, 1.4% in 2024 and 2.7% in 2025.

The company said one-off costs, including a provision related to ordinary wage claims, were booked in 2024, and that the 2025 recovery reflected higher sales of high-value products such as stainless steel and high-strength aluminum, flexible pricing, and the base effect of those one-offs.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 91 billion in 2022, KRW 128.3 billion in 2023 (up 41.0%), KRW 20.2 billion in 2024 (down 84.2%) and KRW 56.2 billion in 2025 (up 177.7%).

Net income including non-controlling interests was KRW 56.1 billion in 2025, a different figure that should be read separately. Operating cash flow rose from KRW 32.9 billion in 2022 to KRW 318 billion in 2024 and then fell to KRW 113.5 billion in 2025.

The total liabilities-to-equity ratio was 93.7% in 2022, 79.7% in 2023, 88.4% in 2024 and 100.4% in 2025, the highest of the four years. At end-2025, total liabilities were KRW 1.96 trillion and total equity was KRW 1.95 trillion.

Quarterly, Q3 2025 revenue was KRW 925.9 billion with operating profit of KRW 27 billion and net income attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 33.7 billion.

Q4 2025 operating profit was KRW 8.86 billion and the company posted a net loss attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 1.48 billion, a swing into loss. Q1 2026 operating profit was KRW 31.1 billion with net income attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 21.7 billion, a return to profit.

Q2 2026 revenue was KRW 1.14 trillion, operating profit KRW 59.8 billion and net income attributable to controlling shareholders KRW 34.7 billion, the largest operating profit among the last five reported quarters.

Q3 2026 revenue was KRW 1.06 trillion, up 14.1% year on year, but operating profit was KRW 14.4 billion, down 75.9% from the prior quarter and 46.4% from a year earlier, and the company posted a net loss attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 13.1 billion, a swing into loss.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders for the latest four quarters (Q4 2025 to Q3 2026) totaled KRW 41.9 billion.

05

Industry analysis

In special steel bars, import flows shape pricing. Chinese special steel bar imports into Korea reached 276,424 tons in Q3 2026, up 70.8% from 161,873 tons a year earlier, and the company said it could not fully pass raw material cost increases into selling prices because of low-priced imports.

The company has said it is actively responding to anti-dumping issues on special steel and bars to defend against low-priced imports, and an anti-dumping tariff decision has been cited as a key variable for the sector. End-market demand is mixed.

Traditional markets such as construction and industrial machinery have been slow to recover, while sales of high-value products for hybrid vehicles and other eco-friendly mobility, defense and heavy equipment, AI semiconductor equipment, and energy and power plants have grown.

Temporary production disruptions at domestic automakers also weighed on sales. On the input side, rising prices of steel scrap, nickel and stainless steel scrap are compressing roll margins, and higher Middle East-related shipping costs and currency volatility add to costs.

On operating profit, the cycle went from KRW 196.7 billion in 2023 to KRW 52.3 billion in 2024 and recovered to KRW 98.4 billion in 2025, while Q3 2026 operating profit fell 46.4% year on year.

The aerospace and defense materials business grew both sales and operating profit on expanding global aviation demand and defense market growth, but in Q3 2026 shipments were delayed by schedule adjustments at a major customer. The company plans to raise the share of high-value products to strengthen competitiveness and profitability.

06

Outlook

The company has pointed to five items to watch.

First, the U.S. superalloy unit SST is focusing group resources on stabilizing commercial production in the second half of 2026, backed by vacuum induction melting and powder equipment that give it annual capacity of 6,000 tons; equipment stabilization and output expansion are under way.

Second, the second Changnyeong plant of the aerospace and defense materials unit is set to build a first-phase line of 770 tons per year, with a KRW 58.8 billion investment, and begin operating in the second half of 2027, supported by long-term supply contracts with Boeing and Airbus.

Third, a 20,000-ton-per-year stainless seamless steel pipe plant in Saudi Arabia is under construction, and the company is expanding supply of dry storage canisters for spent nuclear fuel.

Fourth, the company maintains a target to pay out at least 30% of consolidated net income in dividends from 2024 through 2027; the 2025 total dividend was about KRW 38.5 billion, with a consolidated payout ratio of 68.7%.

Fifth, in its Q3 2026 disclosure the company said it will raise the share of high-value products for eco-friendly vehicles, semiconductors, defense and energy, and defend profitability through process efficiency and cost reduction. Its value-up plan progress report reaffirmed medium-term targets through 2027.

Variables to check in upcoming results are roll margin recovery, the effect of anti-dumping measures, Middle East shipping costs and currency movements, and how fully raw material costs are passed through to selling prices.

Bull factors

Expansion in High-Value Aerospace and Defense Materials

The aerospace and defense materials unit posted record annual results in 2025, with revenue of KRW 128.7 billion and operating profit of KRW 24.6 billion, contributing 24.1% of group operating profit on 3.5% of group revenue.

Backed by long-term supply contracts with Boeing and Airbus, the second Changnyeong plant's first phase of 770 tons per year is planned to start in the second half of 2027. Combined with SST's 6,000-ton superalloy capacity in the U.S., the company says its supply scope in aerospace and space supply chains will broaden.

Recovery in Operating Profit

Operating profit rose 88.1% from KRW 52.3 billion in 2024 to KRW 98.4 billion in 2025, and operating margin improved from 1.4% to 2.7%. Operating profit in Q1 and Q2 2026 was KRW 31.1 billion and KRW 59.8 billion, both higher than the preceding quarter. Net income attributable to controlling shareholders for the latest four quarters totaled KRW 41.9 billion.

Shareholder Return Policy and Value-Up Plan

The company keeps a target to pay at least 30% of consolidated net income in dividends from 2024 through 2027. The 2025 total dividend was about KRW 38.5 billion, a consolidated payout ratio of 68.7%.

In its value-up plan progress report, the company reaffirmed medium-term targets through 2027 and said its price-to-book ratio had fallen from the 2025 level.

Bear factors

Low-Priced Chinese Imports and Narrower Roll Margins

Chinese special steel bar imports into Korea reached 276,424 tons in Q3 2026, up 70.8% year on year. SeAH Besteel's standalone revenue rose 16.0% to KRW 575.9 billion, but its operating profit fell 34.4% to KRW 3 billion. Higher volumes and selling prices did not offset narrower roll margins.

Cost, Logistics and Currency Pressure

The company could not fully pass through higher costs for steel scrap, nickel and stainless steel scrap. Higher logistics costs tied to Middle East instability and currency volatility also weighed on roll margins.

Changwon Special Steel's Q3 2026 revenue rose 17.8%, yet its operating profit fell 27.8%, showing the same pattern.

Swing to Losses and Balance Sheet Strain

Net loss attributable to controlling shareholders reached KRW 13.1 billion in Q3 2026, swinging from a profit a year earlier, and a loss of KRW 1.48 billion was also posted in Q4 2025. The liabilities-to-equity ratio of 100.4% at end-2025 was the highest of 2022 to 2025.

Operating cash flow fell from KRW 318 billion in 2024 to KRW 113.5 billion in 2025, and whether cash generation holds up during ongoing investment is a bearish factor.

10

Risk factors

Trade and Policy

Anti-dumping measures are discussed as a tool against low-priced imports, but their scope and effectiveness depend on the final decision. A limited tariff would keep price competition with Chinese bars intense and could delay roll margin recovery.

A stronger measure could change the price negotiation environment in the domestic market. This is an area the company cannot control on its own.

Raw Materials, FX and Logistics

Raw material prices such as steel scrap and nickel are often reflected in selling prices with a lag, which shakes roll margins. Shipping costs tied to Middle East instability and currency volatility are cost items the company has limited control over. If pass-through lags, operating profit can fall sharply, as it did in Q3 2026.

Overseas Subsidiaries and New Investment

SST is in the stage of stabilizing equipment and expanding output, with commercial production targeted for the second half of 2026. The first phase of the second Changnyeong plant requires KRW 58.8 billion and is planned to start in the second half of 2027.

In Q3 2026, shipments at the aerospace and defense materials unit were delayed by schedule changes at a major customer, and operating profit fell 31.8%. Investment returns may arrive later than planned if customer schedules shift.

Overall view

In Q3 2026, SeAH Besteel Holdings' revenue rose 14.1% to KRW 1.06 trillion, but operating profit fell 46.4% to KRW 14.4 billion and the company posted a net loss attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 13.1 billion, a swing into loss.

The drivers were the rise in low-priced Chinese special steel bar imports and the inability to fully pass through raw material, logistics and currency costs. In the same quarter, the aerospace and defense materials subsidiary weakened on customer shipment delays.

Even so, operating profit for 2025 rose 88.1% to KRW 98.4 billion, and net income attributable to controlling shareholders over the latest four quarters totaled KRW 41.9 billion, a profit.

Bullish factors include growth room in high-value aerospace and defense materials, the commercial start of SST, and the 2024 to 2027 dividend policy.

Bearish factors include import competition and narrower roll margins, the 100.4% liabilities-to-equity ratio at end-2025, the highest of 2022 to 2025, and lower operating cash flow. Both sets of factors carry comparable weight.

The next checkpoints are the effect of anti-dumping measures, SST's commercial production, the operating margin and debt ratio in the 2026 annual report, and the roll margin trend in Q1 2027. This report is for information only and does not include an investment opinion or target price.
