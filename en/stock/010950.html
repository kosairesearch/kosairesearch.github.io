Refining and lubricant recovery meets the Shaheen test

Third-quarter 2026 operating profit rose to KRW 1.41 trillion, but the Shaheen project ramp-up from early 2027 is the next test of the company's shift in business mix.

02

Business structure

S-Oil is a refiner that runs a refining unit at its core, alongside lubricant base oils and petrochemicals. Its largest shareholder is Saudi Aramco, and the TC2C process at the center of the Shaheen project is also an Aramco technology.

Petrochemicals currently account for about 12% of production, and the company plans to raise that share to 25% once Shaheen is fully operating.

Shaheen has absorbed a total investment of KRW 9.258 trillion and is being built at the Onsan National Industrial Complex in Ulsan, the largest petrochemical investment in South Korea.

Once complete, it is designed to produce 1.8 million tons of ethylene and 770,000 tons of propylene per year, and up to 1.32 million tons of polyethylene. The company plans to split Shaheen's sales roughly evenly between domestic and export markets, with pipeline supply to Ulsan petrochemical companies.

In the third quarter of 2026, the lubricant unit posted revenue of KRW 1.54 trillion and operating profit of KRW 742.7 billion, while the refining unit recorded operating profit of KRW 690.4 billion.

Domestic petrochemicals are in a restructuring phase, with plans to cut 2.7 to 3.7 million tons of NCC capacity amid oversupply led by China. Shaheen is designed with lower production costs, but industry views differ on whether it should be included in the capacity cuts.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual revenue was KRW 42.4 trillion in 2022, the largest year of the 2022 to 2025 span, followed by KRW 35.7 trillion in 2023 (down 15.8% year on year), KRW 36.6 trillion in 2024 (up 2.5%) and KRW 34.2 trillion in 2025 (down 6.5%).

Operating profit went from KRW 3.41 trillion in 2022 to KRW 1.35 trillion in 2023 (down 60.2%), KRW 422.2 billion in 2024 (down 68.8%) and KRW 235.6 billion in 2025 (down 44.2%), declining for three consecutive years from 2023 to 2025.

The annual operating margin fell from 8.0% in 2022 to 0.7% in 2025, while the liabilities-to-equity ratio rose from 131.2% to 198.8% over the same period.

Net profit attributable to owners fell from KRW 2.1 trillion in 2022 to KRW 948.8 billion in 2023 (down 54.9%), swung to a net loss of KRW 193 billion in 2024, and returned to a profit of KRW 177 billion in 2025.

Net income including non-controlling interests was KRW 177 billion in 2025 and a net loss of KRW 193 billion in 2024, a separate line from profit attributable to owners.

Quarterly operating profit moved from KRW 229.2 billion in the third quarter of 2025 to KRW 371.9 billion in the fourth quarter (up 62.3%) and KRW 1.23 trillion in the first quarter of 2026 (up 231.0%).

Second-quarter 2026 operating profit fell 21.6% to KRW 965 billion, which the company attributed to the disappearance of a one-off inventory-related gain in the refining unit, a decline it said record lubricant profit largely offset.

Third-quarter 2026 operating profit rose 45.8% to KRW 1.41 trillion, with the refining unit contributing KRW 690.4 billion and the lubricant unit KRW 742.7 billion, and the company cited higher crude prices, the lagging effect on product prices and firmer refining margins.

Net profit attributable to owners rose from KRW 63.2 billion in the third quarter of 2025 to KRW 1.44 trillion in the third quarter of 2026, and the last four quarters total KRW 2.9 trillion.

Cumulative operating profit for January to September 2026 was KRW 3.6 trillion on a preliminary basis announced on November 2, 2026, turning from an operating loss in the same period of 2025.

Operating cash flow was KRW 1.28 trillion in 2022, KRW 2.53 trillion in 2023, KRW 1.47 trillion in 2024 and KRW 3.94 trillion in 2025.

05

Industry analysis

The refining cycle turned favorable in 2026 as supply-side shocks overlapped. The company said refining margins rose sharply in the second quarter of 2026 amid tight product supply.

It also described global crude inventories as below the bottom of their five-year range, with gasoline and diesel stocks near the same floor and well below a year earlier. U.S. gasoline inventories fell to historic lows even with refining capacity running at maximum.

In lubricant base oils, the Middle East accounts for about 30% of global Group III supply, and production disruptions and logistics constraints tied to the Strait of Hormuz pushed spreads to record levels.

The Strait of Hormuz blockade was temporarily lifted at the end of June 2026, and crude prices fell sharply at that point, which also needs to be weighed.

Petrochemicals are a different story, with large capacity additions led by China driving oversupply and pushing domestic players toward asset sales and restructuring. In refining, the domestic price cap could limit how much S-Oil benefits from a favorable market environment.

Until Shaheen begins operating, the company's earnings depend heavily on refining conditions and lubricant supply-demand balance.

06

Outlook

The company expects its crude distillation unit (CDU) utilization rate to rise from 81% in the third quarter of 2026 to about 90% in the fourth quarter. It plans to raise purchases of crude with medium-level heaviness and adjust vessel-to-vessel transfers and shipping routes to lift utilization.

For the fourth quarter, management said strong market conditions should continue, supported by low global product inventories and the start of the heating oil season.

In lubricant base oils, management expects tight Group III supply to persist beyond the fourth quarter and into 2027, as Middle East disruptions limit output. Foreign exchange gains are expected to shrink in the fourth quarter as the won strengthens.

The Shaheen project plans to complete trial runs and commercial readiness by December 2026, with sequential start-up beginning in January 2027 with the polyethylene units. Press reports indicate that about two to three months would be needed before full commercial operation.

The company has signed annual supply contracts with olefin monomer customers and an export marketing agreement for polyethylene with SABIC.

On early Shaheen profitability, the company said it is difficult to comment at this point given the volatility linked to the Middle East war, but it expects improved petrochemical spreads over the medium to long term to feed positively into earnings.

Yuanta Securities, in a June 2026 report, said the Shaheen facilities could contribute to profit from the fourth quarter of 2026. That timing differs from the company's early 2027 commercial operation target, so both schedules should be tracked side by side.

For shareholder returns, the company reaffirmed a plan to pay dividends of at least 20% of annual net profit for fiscal 2025 and fiscal 2026.

08

Bull factors

Strong refining margins and lubricant spreads

In the third quarter of 2026, the refining unit posted operating profit of KRW 690.4 billion, and the lubricant unit reached KRW 742.7 billion, which the company called a quarterly record.

Middle East production disruptions combined with the Strait of Hormuz blockade pushed lubricant spreads to record levels in the second quarter of 2026. The Middle East accounts for about 30% of Group III base oil supply, and the company expects recovery to take a considerable time.

If this supply structure persists, the lubricant unit could keep contributing to earnings, which is the bullish case.

Cost competitiveness of Shaheen

Yuanta Securities estimated in a September 2025 report that Shaheen's ethylene break-even point, excluding feedstock costs, is $171 per ton, which it said is $79 below the $250 per ton for existing domestic NCC operators.

The company also said the Shaheen facility has very low costs and a distinct price competitiveness. The TC2C technology, which converts crude and heavy fractions into petrochemical feedstock, is being applied commercially for the first time in the world, and that is cited as a differentiator.

If this cost advantage translates into earnings, it would support the planned shift of petrochemicals to 25% of output.

Earnings recovery and shareholder return policy

Net profit attributable to owners turned from a net loss of KRW 193 billion in 2024 to a profit of KRW 177 billion in 2025, and reached KRW 1.44 trillion in the third quarter of 2026. The company reaffirmed a plan to pay dividends of at least 20% of annual net profit for fiscal 2025 and fiscal 2026.

Management also said dividends per share would rise meaningfully as earnings grow. The combination of earnings recovery and a stated payout policy is cited as the bullish case.

09

Bear factors

Earnings tied to Middle East geopolitics

In the second quarter of 2026, refining operating profit fell as a one-off inventory-related gain from the prior quarter disappeared, and the company said lubricant profit largely offset the decline.

Third-quarter results were also attributed mainly to higher oil prices linked to Middle East tensions and a recovery in refining margins. One press report said current profitability is hard to view as a long-term earnings base.

If Middle East conditions ease, the favorable environment for refining margins and lubricant spreads could weaken, which is the bearish case.

Shaheen ramp-up and petrochemical oversupply

The petrochemical unit posted an operating loss in the third quarter of 2026, and profitability in petrochemicals, the core of the business shift, has not yet been demonstrated.

With China-led capacity additions deepening global oversupply, there are concerns that Shaheen's polyethylene output of up to 1.32 million tons could burden existing domestic producers. Shaheen must secure target yields and quality during initial trial runs and commercial operation. If product prices fall, a cost advantage alone may not be enough to secure profitability.

Government regulation and financial burden

The domestic price cap could prevent S-Oil from fully benefiting from a favorable market by imposing government price controls. The government's petrochemical restructuring also remains a variable, and the company said it supports the policy goal and is participating in a voluntary agreement.

The liabilities-to-equity ratio rose from 131.2% in 2022 to 198.8% in 2025, adding to the financial burden. Depreciation and financing costs will be fully reflected in earnings after Shaheen starts operating.

10

Risk factors

Geopolitics and crude sourcing

Conflict in the Middle East, shipping risks in the Red Sea and outages at Russian refineries could affect crude sourcing and product supply. The company said it is operating its facilities by securing alternative crude and diversifying shipping routes. It also said the third-quarter CDU utilization of 81% reflected a crude slate weighted toward light crude.

Currency swings

Won strength weighed on operating profit, while foreign exchange gains contributed to non-operating income in the third quarter. The company expects those gains to shrink in the fourth quarter. Currency movements can amplify swings in net profit through non-operating items.

Early Shaheen operations

Shaheen is the first commercial deployment of the TC2C technology, so reaching target yields and quality may take time. Earnings contribution depends on both higher utilization and secured sales channels. The company said early profitability is difficult to state at this point.

12

Overall view

S-Oil's third-quarter 2026 revenue was KRW 12 trillion and operating profit KRW 1.41 trillion, showing a clear earnings recovery. Still, annual operating profit fell from KRW 3.41 trillion in 2022 to KRW 235.6 billion in 2025, and the operating margin dropped to 0.7%.

The company attributed third-quarter gains mainly to higher oil prices linked to Middle East tensions and a recovery in refining margins. The lubricant unit recorded a quarterly record for operating profit, and the refining unit also grew profit on better margins.

The petrochemical unit posted an operating loss in the third quarter, and the Shaheen project is scheduled to begin sequential operation from January 2027. Shaheen is promoted for its cost competitiveness, but it also carries oversupply and early-operation risks.

The liabilities-to-equity ratio stood at 198.8% at the end of 2025, above 131.2% in 2022. The next checkpoints are the December 2026 trial-run results, progress after January 2027, the fourth-quarter 2026 results and the 2026 annual business report.
