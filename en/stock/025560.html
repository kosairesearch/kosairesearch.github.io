Order backlog and new capacity lift sales; earnings volatility remains the test

02

Business structure

Mirae makes inspection equipment used in semiconductor back-end processes, and its business runs on two pillars. The first is the automatic test equipment (ATE) division, centered on semiconductor test handlers.

The second is the non-standard component insertion machine (MAI) division, built on surface-mount technology (SMT) mounters. In the first half of 2026, the ATE division accounted for 90.6% of sales, and overseas sales made up 77.8% over the same period.

MAI equipment serves IT and automotive electronics component production; it is sold continuously to the country's leading home-appliance maker, and its quarterly sales topped KRW 3.4 billion, according to a July 2026 report.

Reported customers include SK hynix, China's ChangXin Memory Technologies (CXMT), and Infineon of Germany. In 2025, rising semiconductor capital spending in China lifted ATE sales, but one assessment said profitability remained limited by cost burdens.

In 2026 a newly acquired subsidiary was consolidated into the financial statements, enlarging the company's scale. Press reports say the company holds 612 patent items, comprising 512 registered domestic patents and 100 pending applications.

On a standalone basis for the first nine months of 2026, the core business recorded sales of KRW 71.5 billion and an operating margin of 27.1%.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual results have recovered from a trough in 2023. Sales fell 61.3% from KRW 56.2 billion in 2022 to KRW 21.7 billion in 2023, then rose to KRW 27 billion in 2024 (up 24.4% year on year) and KRW 50.8 billion in 2025 (up 87.8%), so sales grew two years in a row from 2024 through 2025.

Among the years from 2022 to 2025, 2022 was the largest. Operating profit of KRW 8 billion in 2022 turned into an operating loss of KRW 16 billion in 2023, then returned to a profit of KRW 12.1 billion in 2024.

The operating margin was highest in 2024 at 44.8% and fell to 18.0% in 2025, when operating profit of KRW 9.14 billion declined 24.6% year on year. In other words, 2025 saw sales rise while profit fell, so revenue and margin did not move in the same direction.

Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders swung from a loss of KRW 29 billion in 2023 to a profit of KRW 6.6 billion in 2024 and KRW 10.1 billion in 2025, up 53.1%.

Net income including non-controlling interests, a loss of KRW 31.5 billion in 2023, was larger than the controlling-shareholder loss of KRW 29 billion. Operating cash flow went from KRW 22.35 million in 2022 and KRW 6.69 billion in 2023 to KRW 329 million in 2024, then turned negative at -KRW 5.18 billion in 2025.

On a quarterly basis, sales bottomed at KRW 16.6 billion in the fourth quarter of 2025 and then grew for three consecutive quarters, reaching KRW 20.7 billion, KRW 30.8 billion and KRW 41.8 billion in the first through third quarters of 2026.

Third-quarter 2026 sales rose 84.8% year on year, while operating profit of KRW 7.45 billion fell 28.1% from the prior quarter but rose 24.1% from a year earlier. Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 4.97 billion dropped 53.7% from the prior quarter and 22.5% from a year earlier.

The 2026 quarters include the effect of consolidating the newly acquired subsidiary Atlas Link, and cumulative controlling-shareholder net profit over the latest four quarters totals KRW 23.9 billion.

05

Industry analysis

Demand for inspection equipment is being driven by wider semiconductor applications such as AI, big data, the Internet of Things and autonomous driving, according to both company and brokerage commentary.

Mirae's ATE division's sales are structured to rise with that trend and with expanding semiconductor capital spending in China. Its past supply of inspection equipment to China's memory maker ChangXin Memory Technologies (CXMT) was highlighted in a July 2026 report.

Chinese capital spending, however, is a policy-sensitive variable that raises dependence on one region's demand. The 77.8% overseas sales share shows a clearly export-oriented structure.

The SMT equipment market behind MAI is linked to home-appliance and automotive electronics component production, and rising demand for automotive electronics was cited as a background for MAI sales.

On the earnings trend, the company moved from an operating loss in 2023 to recovery in 2024 and 2025, and its cumulative 2026 sales grew sharply. Its differentiators cited include the two-pillar SMT and handler technology base and a portfolio of 612 patent items.

A 2026 report pointed to acquisitions accelerating its entry into high-value AI semiconductor equipment markets. An independent research firm's November 2026 report focused on order expansion, lower fixed-cost burden and the effect of capacity additions.

06

Outlook

The company said it intends to keep its sales growth on track in the fourth quarter of 2026. It stated that it already holds order volume close to its individual-basis cumulative third-quarter sales of KRW 71.5 billion.

An independent research firm put unrecognized order backlog at about KRW 49.5 billion at the end of the first half of 2026. According to the same report, the company signed a supply contract worth KRW 53.07 billion with YILING TRADING in September 2025, with delivery due by May 2027.

Annual capacity is set to rise 2.8 times, from 200 units at the existing Cheonan plant to 560 units including 360 units from a new Asan line. Because the existing equipment ran at a 97.5% utilization rate, the report expects relief from production bottlenecks.

The company also said it will keep running its second plant at full capacity and continue R&D for new products. How quickly the capacity additions convert into sales will show up in deliveries through May 2027 and in later quarterly results.

Consolidating the new subsidiary has also changed the makeup of consolidated revenue, which makes it harder to separate the core business from the subsidiary's contribution. The next checkpoints are the full-year operating margin in the 2026 annual report and revenue recognition around the May 2027 delivery deadline.

08

Bull factors

Order backlog and capacity expansion

Unrecognized order backlog was about KRW 49.5 billion at the end of the first half of 2026. A supply contract worth KRW 53.07 billion signed with YILING TRADING in September 2025 runs through delivery by May 2027.

Annual capacity is set to rise 2.8 times, from 200 units at the Cheonan plant to 560 units including a new Asan line, and an analysis expects relief from bottlenecks given the 97.5% utilization of existing equipment.

Lower SG&A burden and margin recovery

Per the independent research firm, the SG&A ratio fell from 36.9% in the first half of 2025 to 9.1% in the first half of 2026. As fixed costs were absorbed by higher sales, the operating margin excluding one-off items was put at 27.2% for the first half.

The standalone operating margin for the first nine months of 2026 was also 27.1%. Profitability has recovered from the 2023 operating loss through a return to profit in 2024.

Subsidiary consolidation and technology assets

Consolidation of the newly acquired subsidiary helped lift third-quarter 2026 sales 84.8% year on year. Press reports say the company holds 612 intellectual property items, including 512 registered domestic patents and 100 pending applications.

One assessment says the MAI division, which serves IT and automotive electronics components and topped KRW 3.4 billion in quarterly sales, helps absorb cyclical shocks.

Bear factors

Sales growth diverging from profit

Third-quarter 2026 sales rose 35.8% from the prior quarter, but operating profit fell 28.1%. Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 4.97 billion fell 53.7% from the prior quarter.

In 2025 sales rose 87.8% while operating profit fell 24.6%, so sales growth has not translated directly into profit growth.

Concentration in customers and regions

The ATE division accounted for 90.6% of first-half 2026 sales, and overseas sales were 77.8%. Because Chinese capital spending is cited as a main demand driver, results may be sensitive to Chinese customers' investment policies and export rules. In 2025, capital spending tied to China's semiconductor policy was also cited as a factor lifting sales.

Loss history and cash flow reversal

The company recorded an operating loss of KRW 16 billion and a controlling-shareholder net loss of KRW 29 billion in 2023. Operating cash flow turned negative at -KRW 5.18 billion in 2025.

Because the 2026 results include the effect of the subsidiary consolidation, the core business's growth is hard to gauge separately.

Risk factors

Customer and China exposure

Sales are concentrated in semiconductor inspection equipment, with overseas sales at 77.8%. Heavy reliance on Chinese memory makers' capital spending means that changes in Chinese policy or export rules could affect order flow. Delays in some customers' investment schedules could increase quarterly sales volatility.

Profitability and cost burden

One assessment said that in 2025 ATE sales rose but profitability stayed limited by cost burdens. The drop in operating margin from 44.8% in 2024 to 18.0% in 2025 shows how much margins can swing. Third-quarter 2026 operating profit fell from the prior quarter even as sales increased. Expanding capacity may also raise fixed costs and depreciation.

Subsidiary consolidation and financing

Consolidating the newly acquired subsidiary has changed the mix of sales and profit, making the core business trend harder to read. Mirae has operated under a new ownership structure since being acquired by LOA Holdings Company Group.

In 2024 it decided on a rights offering to raise KRW 41 billion for facility and other funds. Continued spending on capacity and acquisitions could add financial burden.

Overall view

Mirae's cumulative sales of KRW 93.3 billion and operating profit of KRW 22.7 billion through the third quarter of 2026 show expansion beyond its full-year 2025 results. Growth is centered on semiconductor inspection equipment, which made up 90.6% of first-half 2026 sales.

Unrecognized backlog of about KRW 49.5 billion and the planned 2.8-fold capacity increase are cited as support for sales visibility. On the other side, the operating margin fell from 44.8% in 2024 to 18.0% in 2025, and third-quarter 2026 operating profit fell 28.1% from the prior quarter even as sales rose.

The consolidated subsidiary and reliance on Chinese capital spending remain variables that could change the direction of results. Given the 2023 loss history and the swing to negative operating cash flow in 2025, the durability of earnings is a matter for quarterly results to confirm.
