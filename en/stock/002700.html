02

Business structure

Shinil Electronics positions itself as a general appliance maker that sells seasonal heating and cooling products alongside kitchen and household appliances, but fans remain the core of its sales.

Fan sales reached KRW 96.4 billion in 2025, up about 5% from the prior year, and their share of total revenue was 52.1% in 2023, 51% in 2024 and 49.7% in 2025.

Summer products such as dehumidifiers and portable air conditioners brought in KRW 12.9 billion in 2025, about 13% higher, while winter products including fan heaters and PTC heaters grew about 23.1%. General appliances such as kitchen and household goods grew to more than 20% of total sales.

New categories include food waste disposers, food warmers and the SAYES luggage brand, and the company described the luggage line as its first move outside appliances since its founding, although most of these products rely on Chinese OEM production and contribute limited short-term sales.

Merchandise sales accounted for 89.3% of 2025 revenue, and overseas sales were KRW 1.26 billion, or 0.6% of the total. Industry commentary describes a crowded market where large domestic brands, many smaller brands and Chinese brands compete together.

Inflows of low-priced Chinese products have weakened competitiveness in the cheapest segment, while motor technology and design differentiation are reported to be widening the premium range. The company has said it is reviewing in-house production for OEM items that reach a certain sales scale.

04

Earnings analysis

On the filing basis, annual revenue went from KRW 202.7 billion in 2022 to KRW 184.3 billion in 2023 (down 9.1%) and KRW 178.3 billion in 2024 (down 3.2%), then recovered to KRW 194 billion in 2025 (up 8.8%). Revenue was largest in 2022 and smallest in 2024 across 2022 to 2025.

Operating profit fell from KRW 2.83 billion in 2022 to KRW 2.01 billion in 2023 (down 28.9%), then rose to KRW 4.03 billion in 2024 (up 100.6%) and KRW 5.24 billion in 2025 (up 29.9%), growing for two consecutive years.

The operating margin rose from 1.4% in 2022 and 1.1% in 2023 to 2.3% in 2024 and 2.7% in 2025, but it still sits around the 2% level. Industry commentary attributes the 2025 improvement to a combination of weather-driven demand and financial restructuring effects.

Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders went from KRW 1.04 billion in 2022 to KRW 1.06 billion in 2023 (up 1.5%), KRW 1.43 billion in 2024 (up 35.8%) and KRW 4.21 billion in 2025 (up 193.2%), rising for three straight years from 2023.

The 2025 jump in net profit well exceeded the rise in operating profit, and reports point to improved financial income from the weaker won.

Quarterly, operating profit slipped from KRW 1.96 billion in the third quarter of 2025 to KRW 552 million in the fourth quarter (down 71.8% from the prior quarter) on revenue of KRW 47.6 billion.

The first quarter of 2026 turned to an operating loss of KRW 247 million on revenue of KRW 33.4 billion, and the second quarter swung back to a KRW 6.98 billion operating profit on revenue of KRW 71.9 billion, up 115.3% from the prior quarter.

Third-quarter 2026 revenue of KRW 47.8 billion fell 9.0% year on year and operating profit of KRW 1.52 billion fell 22.1%, yet net profit attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 3.13 billion rose 209.3%, so the two moved in opposite directions.

Financial costs in the first quarter of 2026 reached KRW 1.538 billion, reported as 2.8 times the prior-year level. Over the last four quarters, net profit attributable to controlling shareholders totaled KRW 7.77 billion.

At the end of 2025, total equity was KRW 83.2 billion, all of it attributable to controlling shareholders, and total liabilities were KRW 55.6 billion. Operating cash flow went from negative KRW 24.6 billion in 2022 to positive figures of KRW 20 billion in 2023, KRW 12.4 billion in 2024 and KRW 14.8 billion in 2025.

05

Industry analysis

Demand for seasonal appliances depends heavily on weather. Summer 2025 was among the hottest seasons on record in Korea, and Shinil Electronics' cumulative cooling-appliance sales growth in that period was reported at 19% year on year. Dehumidifier unit sales surged 36% within June and July alone.

In the second half, fan heaters and other heating products carried demand, with sales up 30% over October and November. The company says that climate-driven growth in seasonal demand, together with the rise of single-person households, has broadened the market base.

Industry observers expect demand for small appliances to keep rising as single-person households and compact living spaces spread. On pricing, inflows of low-priced Chinese products have intensified competition in the cheapest segment. The effect of the weaker won cuts both ways.

A first-quarter 2026 earnings comment described the weaker won as strengthening price competitiveness, while the company's heavy reliance on merchandise sales and overseas sourcing means dollar exchange rates and import costs can also add pressure.

Seasonal products are reported to make up more than 70% of sales, so weather sensitivity is central to the company's position. In a market where large brands, many smaller brands and Chinese brands compete, Shinil is differentiating through product features rather than competing on price alone.

06

Outlook

The company plans to keep its seasonal core while widening its general appliance lineup to smooth out seasonality.

New products since 2025 include the SAYES luggage line, food warmers and food waste disposers, and an AI voice-recognition air circulator has been introduced alongside export attempts through overseas trade shows.

An August 2026 report said peak July and August sales had not yet been reflected, leading some to expect annual profit gains. For the first half, revenue was reported up 12.5% year on year and operating profit more than doubled.

However, the third-quarter 2026 filing showed revenue of KRW 47.8 billion, down 9.0% year on year, and operating profit of KRW 1.52 billion, down 22.1%.

Because the reported expectations and the third-quarter figures diverge, whether the summer peak effect holds up quarter by quarter is the key variable for the next results.

Inventory valuation gains of KRW 1.606 billion were reported for the first half of 2026, and it is not yet clear whether such reversals will recur in later quarters.

The company is reviewing a shift to in-house production for OEM items that sell above a certain volume, which could raise unit profitability if implemented. The next annual report will show inventory and short-term borrowing trends, which will indicate how the balance sheet is changing.

08

Bull factors

Seasonal demand and weather tailwinds

Fan sales reached KRW 96.4 billion in 2025, up about 5%, while summer products such as dehumidifiers and portable air conditioners rose about 13% to KRW 12.9 billion. Winter products including fan heaters and PTC heaters grew about 23.1%, so seasonal lines grew together.

The heatwave-driven demand in the second quarter of 2026 translated into revenue of KRW 71.9 billion and operating profit of KRW 6.98 billion.

Recovery in earnings power

Operating profit fell from KRW 2.83 billion in 2022 to KRW 2.01 billion in 2023, then rose to KRW 5.24 billion in 2025 with two consecutive years of growth. Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders has risen for three straight years since 2023, reaching KRW 4.21 billion in 2025. The debt-to-equity ratio moved from 86.7% in 2022 to 66.9% in 2025, a direction that reduces financial burden.

Diversification and a possible shift in production

General appliances have grown to more than 20% of sales, with new categories such as food waste disposers, food warmers and luggage added. The company said it is reviewing in-house production for OEM products that sell above a certain volume. Industry commentary sees room for higher per-unit profitability if such a shift is carried out.

Bear factors

Seasonality and quarterly volatility

Third-quarter 2026 revenue of KRW 47.8 billion fell 9.0% year on year, and operating profit of KRW 1.52 billion fell 22.1%. The first quarter of 2026 posted an operating loss of KRW 247 million. Because the fan-led model makes summer peak sales decisive, quarterly results swing widely.

Thin margins and reliance on Chinese OEM supply

The 2025 operating margin of 2.7% leaves a thin profit cushion on KRW 194 billion of revenue. Merchandise sales were 89.3% of 2025 revenue, and overseas sales were KRW 1.26 billion, only 0.6% of the total.

Industry commentary also points to inflows of low-priced Chinese products eroding competitiveness in cheap segments.

Inventory valuation swings and borrowing

In 2025 the company booked an inventory valuation loss of KRW 1.798 billion, reversing the reversal gain of 2024. Short-term borrowings rose from KRW 16.6 billion in 2024 to KRW 26.3 billion in 2025, mostly usance import financing according to reports. Financial costs of KRW 1.538 billion in the first quarter of 2026 were cited as a factor in the wider net loss.

10

Risk factors

Weather dependence

Weather decides unit sales, so cooler summers or a shorter peak season could quickly weigh on quarterly revenue. Industry commentary credits weather conditions as a major driver of the 2025 improvement. The third-quarter 2026 decline can be read as one example of this structure.

Exchange rates and input costs

With merchandise sales at 89.3% and heavy sourcing from China, dollar exchange rates and import prices feed directly into costs. A weaker won is seen as supporting price competitiveness, but rising import prices can compress margins. With a 2.7% operating margin in 2025, the cushion for absorbing cost swings is thin.

Inventory, borrowing and non-operating items

An inventory valuation loss occurred in 2025 and short-term borrowings increased. Higher financial costs widened the net loss in the first quarter of 2026. These items can move against operating profit, so net profit attributable to controlling shareholders has been more volatile than operating profit.

12

Overall view

Shinil Electronics has seen operating profit rise for two straight years after its 2023 low, and net profit attributable to controlling shareholders has grown for two consecutive years since 2023. Yet fans still account for nearly half of sales, so weather-driven volatility remains structural.

In 2026, a second-quarter swing to profit, a third-quarter slowdown and a first-quarter loss all occurred within the same year, highlighting the role of seasonality and inventory valuation. The 2025 operating margin of 2.7% shows the margin structure is still thin.

On the balance sheet, the debt-to-equity ratio is lower than in 2022, but short-term borrowings have risen and an inventory valuation loss was booked in 2025.

The last four quarters' net profit attributable to controlling shareholders totaled KRW 7.77 billion, a period in which the gap between operating and non-operating results matters. The next checkpoint is the annual operating margin and the inventory and borrowing trends in the 2026 annual report.
