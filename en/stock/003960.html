Report overview

Sales Grew, but Profits Turned to Losses

Sajodaerim's 2025 sales rose to KRW 3.5 trillion, yet its operating margin fell to 2.7%, and it has posted attributable net losses in each of the last two quarters.

02

Business structure

Founded in 1964, Sajodaerim manufactures and distributes processed foods and handles food manufacturing and distribution within its group. Based on 2023 total sales before its acquisitions were consolidated, food manufacturing accounted for 81%, livestock 15%, overseas fishing 3%, and leasing and other 1%.

Within food manufacturing, edible oils and fats were estimated at 17%, fish cakes and surimi at 11%, soybean meal by-products at 9%, canned tuna at 7%, and soy and fermented seasonings at 4%.

Growth in scale came mainly from acquisitions: in February 2024 the company agreed to buy the starch and sugar maker Ingredient Korea and renamed it, and it also agreed to acquire the food-service and distribution company Foodist, which was reported to have sales of roughly KRW 1 trillion.

Among key products, market shares in 2025 (September) were 42.44% for fish-paste products, 31.40% for cooking oil and 27.30% for fish cakes. Exports were about KRW 106 billion in 2023, and the export share of OEM, pollock and cod-related product sales was 9.36% as of December 2024.

Competition is intense because technology in food manufacturing has become standardized, so similar new products tend to follow quickly. The largest shareholder, Sajo Industries, together with related parties held 58.52%, per a report on 2023 consolidated results.

04

Earnings analysis

On an annual basis, sales were KRW 2.02 trillion in 2022, KRW 2.06 trillion in 2023 (up 2.2%), KRW 2.64 trillion in 2024 (up 28.1%) and KRW 3.5 trillion in 2025 (up 32.4%), rising three consecutive years from 2023 to 2025.

Operating income went from KRW 97.7 billion in 2022 to KRW 128.6 billion in 2023 (up 31.7%) and KRW 133 billion in 2024 (up 3.4%), then fell to KRW 94.7 billion in 2025 (down 28.8%). 2024 was the largest year for operating income across 2022 to 2025.

Operating margin was 4.8% in 2022, 6.2% in 2023, 5.0% in 2024 and 2.7% in 2025, so margins did not keep pace with sales growth. Attributable net income was KRW 71.2 billion in 2022, KRW 91.3 billion in 2023 and KRW 91.8 billion in 2024 (up 0.5%), then turned to an attributable net loss of KRW 78.6 billion in 2025.

Net income including non-controlling interests was a loss of KRW 73.6 billion in 2025, a different figure because non-controlling holders recorded profit.

Quarterly, operating income fell from KRW 31.7 billion in the third quarter of 2025 to KRW 16.1 billion in the fourth quarter (down 49.1% quarter on quarter), when attributable net loss was KRW 128.6 billion.

It recovered to KRW 23.8 billion in the first quarter of 2026 (up 47.4%), then declined to KRW 20.4 billion in the second quarter (down 14.0%) and KRW 6.35 billion in the third quarter (down 68.9%), two consecutive quarterly declines.

Third-quarter 2026 sales were KRW 927.3 billion, up 5.5% from the prior quarter but down 1.0% year on year. Attributable net income was KRW 14.3 billion in the first quarter of 2026, then losses of KRW 114.5 billion in the second quarter and KRW 3.26 billion in the third quarter.

Attributable net losses over the last four quarters total KRW 232 billion. Operating cash flow was KRW 61.2 billion in 2022, KRW 174.8 billion in 2023, KRW 153 billion in 2024 and KRW 62.7 billion in 2025.

The debt-to-equity ratio rose to 153.2% in 2024 and 174.7% in 2025, and attributable equity was KRW 652.3 billion at the end of 2025.

05

Industry analysis

Food manufacturing is marked by rapid copycat launches, which tends to produce recurring price competition and margin pressure. On the cost side, a 2024 third-quarter report noted that four of seven key raw materials, including pork, chicken, soybean oil and rapeseed oil, rose in price.

Conversely, a 2024 brokerage analysis expected stable soybean and tuna input costs to support margins, so the direction of raw-material costs has shifted over time.

On exports, a June 2024 brokerage note argued that core products used as ingredients in gimbap and other processed foods could benefit as export markets expand, though export share remains small relative to total sales.

Compared with major food peers such as CJ Cheiljedang, Dongwon Industries and Otoki, Sajodaerim's mix includes livestock and overseas fishing, which sets it apart. Channel power of discount stores and convenience stores also affects pricing negotiations.

Because cost structure drives earnings direction, the stability of raw-material prices is a central variable for the sector.

06

Outlook

In a 2024 third-quarter report, a company official attributed higher overall costs to the consolidation of acquired companies and said operating performance was expected to improve going forward.

However, third-quarter 2026 operating income fell to KRW 6.35 billion, so whether operating improvement appears is the next checkpoint. On exports, a 2024 plan described supplying three types of frozen gimbap to H Mart in the United States, with Korean-style ready meals to follow in the second half.

Premium fish-paste products were also reported as being prepared for supply in the second half of 2024. Product category expansion was reported to include tteokbokki, hot dogs and tube-type red pepper paste. In the starch-sugar business, the Korean market share of the acquired unit was estimated at around 25%.

The remaining acquisition payment of KRW 54 billion is scheduled to be paid over three years, so cash outflows should be tracked alongside operating cash flow. The 2026 annual report will disclose fourth-quarter and full-year results and year-end debt structure.

Whether export plans and acquired-unit contributions show up in earnings can be tested in quarterly results from the first quarter of 2027 onward.

08

Bull factors

Room for export growth

Export share remains small, leaving room for improvement in business metrics. The export share of OEM, pollock and cod-related product sales was 9.36% as of December 2024, and exports were about KRW 106 billion in 2023. A 2024 report cited US distribution of frozen gimbap and ready meals as a growth path.

Strong domestic shares in key products

Fish-paste products, cooking oil and fish cakes had market shares between 27.3% and 42.44% as of September 2025. A 2024 brokerage view linked these items to K-food demand because they are ingredients in gimbap and other processed foods.

Profitability of acquired units

A 2024 report said profitability at the acquired unit expanded substantially after acquisition. Consolidated 2025 sales rose to KRW 3.5 trillion, which supports the scale argument. Whether acquired assets translate into core profit can be tracked through the operating margin trend.

09

Bear factors

Earnings decline and swing to losses

Attributable net loss was KRW 78.6 billion in 2025, and third-quarter 2026 operating income fell 79.9% year on year to KRW 6.35 billion. Two consecutive quarterly declines in operating income show that the first-quarter rebound has not been sustained.

Financial leverage burden

The debt-to-equity ratio rose from 77.7% in 2023 to 174.7% in 2025, with total liabilities of KRW 1.39 trillion at the end of 2025. A 2024 report noted short-term borrowings and current portion of long-term debt of about KRW 353 billion due within a year, close to twice cash and equivalents of KRW 187.8 billion.

Cost and fixed-expense pressure

A 2024 third-quarter report said several key raw-material prices rose and labor costs in selling and administrative expenses increased sharply. Because fixed costs such as logistics also grew, any failure to pass cost increases through to prices could compress margins further.

10

Risk factors

Financial and liquidity

With borrowings and the debt ratio elevated, lower operating income raises the relative burden of interest and repayment. Operating cash flow fell to KRW 62.7 billion in 2025 from KRW 153 billion in 2024. Weaker cash generation could increase reliance on borrowing.

Raw materials and cost structure

Food manufacturing costs move with pork, soybean and rapeseed oil prices. A 2024 report showed that several key raw materials rose in price. If price increases lag behind cost increases, operating margin could keep declining.

Acquisition integration and scope

Consolidating acquired subsidiaries expanded sales, but the scope now spans food manufacturing, starch and sugar, food-service distribution and livestock. Foodist was reported to have sales of about KRW 1 trillion, and profit gaps across subsidiaries can increase volatility in consolidated earnings.

What to watch next

  1. December 31, 2026 (fiscal year-end)

    Check year-end debt structure and short-term repayment amounts. How the remaining KRW 54 billion acquisition payment, scheduled over three years, is reflected in borrowings is the next point to confirm.

  2. March 31, 2027 (annual report filing deadline)

    The 2026 annual report will disclose fourth-quarter and full-year operating margin and attributable net income. Compare with the 2.7% operating margin in 2025 to see whether margins recover and how fourth-quarter 2026 results affect full-year profit.

  3. May 15, 2027 (first-quarter report filing deadline)

    Check whether first-quarter 2027 operating income recovers to the KRW 23.8 billion level of the first quarter of 2026 and whether attributable net income moves out of loss. Also watch whether export plans and acquired-unit contributions appear in quarterly results.

12

Overall view

Sajodaerim posted its largest sales year in 2025 at KRW 3.5 trillion, yet operating income of KRW 94.7 billion and an attributable net loss of KRW 78.6 billion show earnings went backward.

After operating income recovered to KRW 23.8 billion in the first quarter of 2026, it declined in the second and third quarters, and attributable losses have persisted for two quarters.

The debt-to-equity ratio reached 174.7% and operating cash flow fell to KRW 62.7 billion, so many financial indicators fall on the weaker side.

Positive factors include room for export growth, strong domestic shares in fish-paste products, cooking oil and fish cakes, and the profitability expansion reported at an acquired unit. Negative factors include raw-material and labor cost pressure, high borrowings, and cumulative net losses over the last four quarters.

The next checkpoints are the fourth-quarter and full-year operating margin in the 2026 annual report, and whether operating income recovers in the first quarter of 2027.
