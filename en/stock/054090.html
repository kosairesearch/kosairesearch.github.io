Report overview

ESS parts push a turnaround; leverage remains the test

Samjin LND is shifting its mix from OA finishers toward ESS and UPS power-infrastructure parts. Operating profit turned positive in Q1 2026, but net losses over the last four quarters totaling KRW 6.39 billion and a 217.3% debt ratio at end-2025 remain open issues.

  3. 3

02

Business structure

Samjin LND Company, founded in 1987, started as a precision mold and injection maker for display and mobile parts and now runs parts, product and other segments.

Parts operations generated KRW 19.86 billion in Q1 2026, or 55.3% of revenue, while the product segment, centered on OA finishers, contributed KRW 15.59 billion (43.4%) and other operations KRW 440 million (1.3%).

Key parts include ESS and UPS busbar assemblies, ESS top covers, UPS middle assemblies and gaskets for secondary battery production. The OA finisher business has supplied Konica Minolta on an OEM and ODM basis since 2010.

Four commercial printer options ordered in August 2025 are being readied for mass production at the Vietnam subsidiary, with revenue contribution expected from the second half of 2026.

By segment, OA held 59.9% of 2025 consolidated revenue and ESS, UPS and gasket parts 38.5%, before parts crossed the majority mark in Q1 2026. Production sites span Korea, Vietnam (VINA), Mexico and the United States (ELEMEK).

Parts utilization was 82.9% in Q1 2026, with Vietnam at 87.5%, the Korean headquarters at 69.0% and ELEMEK at 50.7%. The company cites nine process stations, 46 inspection channels and 100% inspection in busbar assembly as differentiators. It also says it has worked with a major battery customer for more than 28 years.

Unlike large component makers, the company concentrates on narrow niches such as OA finishers and battery and power structural parts.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual sales fell from KRW 256.5 billion in 2022 to KRW 212.7 billion in 2023 (down 17.1%), KRW 173.2 billion in 2024 (down 18.6%) and KRW 136.3 billion in 2025 (down 21.3%), a three-year decline from 2023 through 2025. Sales were largest in 2022 and smallest in 2025 within the 2022 to 2025 period.

Operating losses widened from KRW 1.52 billion in 2022 to KRW 14.4 billion in 2023, then narrowed to KRW 8.94 billion in 2024 and KRW 1.97 billion in 2025. The company has posted operating losses for four consecutive years from 2022 to 2025.

Operating margin was -0.6% in 2022, -6.8% in 2023, -5.2% in 2024 and -1.4% in 2025. Net losses attributable to controlling shareholders were KRW 1.61 billion in 2022, KRW 22.9 billion in 2023, KRW 21.7 billion in 2024 and KRW 10.6 billion in 2025.

In 2023 and 2024 the net loss was far larger than the operating loss, which points to a heavy burden from non-operating items. Restructuring ran alongside this period.

The company sold a China-based LCD-related unit in 2024 and later decided to halt part of the injection business at a US subsidiary, exiting lower-margin activities. Operating cash flow moved from negative KRW 4.81 billion in 2022 to KRW 3.87 billion in 2023, KRW 884 million in 2024 and KRW 11.7 billion in 2025.

On a quarterly basis, Q3 2025 showed revenue of KRW 36.4 billion, operating profit of KRW 933 million and controlling-shareholder net income of KRW 2.71 billion.

Q4 2025 revenue fell 20.6% quarter on quarter to KRW 28.9 billion, with an operating loss of KRW 818 million and a controlling-shareholder net loss of KRW 2.47 billion.

Q1 2026 revenue rose 24.1% quarter on quarter to KRW 35.9 billion, with operating profit of KRW 679 million and controlling-shareholder net income of KRW 3.85 billion. Q2 2026 revenue was KRW 40.4 billion, operating profit KRW 1.37 billion and a controlling-shareholder net loss of KRW 667 million.

Q3 2026 revenue reached KRW 42.9 billion, up 17.8% year on year, while operating profit was KRW 905 million, down 3.0% year on year, and the controlling-shareholder net loss widened to KRW 7.1 billion.

The company attributed the Q1 2026 gross margin rise from 3.6% to 11.5% and a 22.4% year-on-year drop in SG&A to cost improvements and production efficiency.

05

Industry analysis

End demand is driven by AI data center expansion, which is lifting spending on high-capacity power equipment. The company judges that the power connection component market is growing alongside investment in AI data centers, ESS and UPS systems.

Data center UPS and ESS run around the clock, so part reliability is cited as a key factor. On batteries, the CEO said the mix of EV and ESS volumes was roughly 9 to 1 in the past and is now close to 5 to 5 as EV demand slowed.

With Chinese suppliers excluded from the US market, local production by Korean cell makers is expanding, which the company sees as widening supply opportunities for Korean parts makers. The same interview noted that prices may fall if long-term supply opportunities continue.

In the electronics components group, Samsung Electro-Mechanics, LG Innotek and Isupetasys are among listed peers, but Samjin LND's revenue concentrates on OA finishers and battery and power structural parts.

The company says gasket production can be largely automated, which eases response to demands for local production abroad. Hydrogen storage vessels and dual-arm robotic assembly are still in development and demonstration, so their revenue contribution is not yet clear.

06

Outlook

The company describes 2026 as the first year of a renewed growth phase after a margin recovery.

Revenue from the Vietnam-made commercial printer options is expected from the second half of 2026, and the company's own estimate for this business is about KRW 3.5 billion in the second half of 2026 and about KRW 13.6 billion in 2027.

According to industry reports, busbar assembly exports to the Americas are set to begin in earnest in the fourth quarter of 2026. On batteries, local production by Korean cell makers in the United States is moving forward, and additional orders from major battery customers are being pursued in the second half.

The CEO projected that ESS and UPS-related revenue could roughly double within three years. In hydrogen, the company completed key performance tests on its Type 4 high-pressure storage solution in February 2026 and targeted European certification within the first half of 2026.

Whether that certification is obtained remains the dividing line for this business moving into revenue. On capital, a share consolidation approved at the extraordinary shareholders' meeting on December 22, 2026 is scheduled to take effect on January 7, 2027, reducing outstanding shares from 24,959,232 to 4,991,846.

The company says the aim is to maintain an appropriate share float, stabilize the share price and raise corporate value. The share buyback trust signed in June 2026 was terminated early in September, and reports put the shares acquired at 871,721.

08

Bull factors

Power-infrastructure parts now a majority

Parts operations generated KRW 19.86 billion in Q1 2026, or 55.3% of revenue. OA products accounted for 59.9% of 2025 revenue, so the mix has begun to change. The company says parts will gradually grow larger through higher ESS volumes and entry into US projects. Expanding power-equipment investment in data centers is cited as the demand base.

Quarterly profit turn and cost improvement

Operating profit turned positive at KRW 679 million in Q1 2026 and reached KRW 1.37 billion in Q2, up 101.7% from the prior quarter. The company says its Q1 gross margin rose from 3.6% to 11.5% year on year and SG&A fell 22.4% year on year. It attributes the gains to cost structure improvement, production efficiency and product mix changes.

Insider stake growth and capital housekeeping

Reports from August 2026 put the stake of CEO Myung-jong Lee and related parties at 24.64%, up from 23.31% in February, and the CEO has continued open-market purchases. The 2026 regular shareholders' meeting approved transfers to retained earnings that laid the groundwork for clearing accumulated deficits. Share buybacks and the share consolidation are aimed at stabilizing the share price and managing the float.

09

Bear factors

Continued net losses and softer operating profit

In Q3 2026 the controlling-shareholder net loss was KRW 7.1 billion, a swing into loss year on year, while operating profit fell 3.0% year on year to KRW 905 million.

Operating profit and controlling-shareholder net income have moved in opposite directions across quarters, so the link from operating gains to net income is not yet established.

Borrowing burden and cash generation

Total liabilities were KRW 82.3 billion at end-2025, and the debt ratio rose sharply from 119.1% in 2022 to 217.3%. Press reports put the debt ratio at 240.1% at end-June 2026, with interest-bearing debt of KRW 57.1 billion well above cash of KRW 16.3 billion.

The same reports show first-half 2026 operating cash flow of KRW 3.1 billion, down from KRW 6.3 billion a year earlier.

Parts revenue down during the transition

First-half 2026 parts revenue, including ESS and UPS components, was KRW 29.6 billion, below KRW 34.6 billion a year earlier. Product revenue rose from KRW 30.9 billion to KRW 45.9 billion over the same period and carried the overall recovery. Press coverage reads that new supply will take time to convert into higher revenue.

10

Risk factors

Demand mix and pricing

The CEO described a shift in battery volumes from EVs toward ESS. Exclusion of Chinese suppliers in the US is widening opportunities for Korean parts makers, but the same interview said prices may fall as long-term supply continues. The pace of EV demand recovery and supply pricing terms are key variables for the revenue mix.

Liquidity

Press reports put the current ratio at 85.5% at end-June 2026, meaning short-term liabilities exceeded current assets. If interest-bearing debt continues to exceed cash, financing costs and refinancing needs could weigh on results.

The effect of the share consolidation and buybacks on the capital structure will show up in future periodic reports.

Execution of new businesses

The CEO said hydrogen vessel certification is costly for each product, so the company plans to certify one product first and then develop to customer specifications. The European certification target was the first half of 2026. Dual-arm robotic assembly is being demonstrated within a national R and D project. Delays in certification or mass production could lengthen payback periods for these businesses.

Overall view

Samjin LND recorded operating losses from 2022 through 2025, and sales fell for three straight years from 2023. Sales have risen for three consecutive quarters from the Q4 2025 low through Q3 2026, and operating profit turned positive in Q1 2026.

Parts operations reached 55.3% of Q1 2026 revenue, a majority, as the mix moves toward power-infrastructure components. On the other side, the controlling-shareholder net loss widened to KRW 7.1 billion in Q3 2026, a swing into loss year on year, and the four-quarter total is KRW 6.39 billion.

The debt ratio stood at 217.3% at end-2025, and press reports of debt exceeding cash keep financial burden a major variable. Strengths are ESS and UPS demand and cost improvement, while weaknesses are persistent net losses and leverage.

The next checkpoints are the extraordinary shareholders' meeting on December 22, 2026, the share consolidation effective on January 7, 2027, and the operating margin and controlling-shareholder net income in the 2026 annual business report.
