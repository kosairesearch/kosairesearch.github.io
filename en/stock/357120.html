02

Business structure

Koramco Life Infra Reit is a KOSPI-listed REIT managed by Koramco Asset Trust that listed in August 2020 as Asia's first gas-station-based REIT.

Its holdings span HD Hyundai Oilbank gas station sites nationwide alongside large-format appliance stores operated by Samsung Electronics and LG Electronics, Coupang fulfillment and last-mile logistics centers, and drive-through outlets of McDonald's and Paul Bassett.

As of November 2025, gas stations (energy) made up roughly 60% of the portfolio, logistics centers 28%, office and retail 5% each, and mobility and hotel assets 1% each. The company holds preferred-share stakes in prime office assets such as the Majestar City Tower in Seocho and the DF Tower near Gangnam Station.

Its core strategy is a 'capital recycling' model that sells legacy gas stations to fund special dividends and reinvests proceeds into growth assets such as logistics and hotels.

In 2026 the company is proceeding with the sale of 19 additional gas stations spread across Incheon, Gyeonggi, Daejeon, Chungbuk, Daegu, Gyeongbuk and Gyeongnam, with Cushman & Wakefield Korea serving as sale advisor.

The Jaedong gas station site in Jongno-gu is being developed into a small-to-midsize hotel with one basement and 14 above-ground floors totaling 91 rooms. Mirae Asset Global Investments is the largest shareholder, reporting a 27.53% stake as of end-March 2026.

Unlike single-sector specialists such as Shinhan Alpha REIT and ESR Kendall Square REIT, Koramco Life Infra Reit differentiates itself by running a multi-sector portfolio spanning energy, logistics, office, retail and hotel assets.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual revenue moved from KRW 34.4 billion in 2022 to KRW 31.5 billion in 2023, KRW 48.5 billion in 2024 and KRW 38.6 billion in 2025, falling 8.4% year over year in 2023, rising 54.0% in 2024 and falling 20.5% again in 2025. Among the four years, 2024 recorded the largest revenue and 2023 the smallest.

Operating income followed KRW 23.6 billion, KRW 18.1 billion, KRW 35.6 billion and KRW 24.2 billion over the same period, surging 96.7% year over year in 2024 before falling 32.2% in 2025. The operating margin slipped from 68.6% in 2022 to 57.6% in 2023, climbed to 73.5% in 2024, and eased back to 62.7% in 2025.

Net income attributable to controlling interests rose from KRW 11.5 billion in 2022 to KRW 10.7 billion in 2023 and KRW 23.8 billion in 2024, then fell 71.9% year over year to KRW 6.67 billion in 2025, the smallest of the four years.

Net income including non-controlling interests moved in step, also registering KRW 6.67 billion in 2025. Operating cash flow declined every year, from KRW 24 billion in 2022 to KRW 17.1 billion in 2023, KRW 8.05 billion in 2024 and KRW 3.42 billion in 2025, contracting more sharply than reported earnings.

The debt-to-equity ratio ranged from the mid-150% to mid-170% band, at 175.1% in 2022, 176.2% in 2023, 155.0% in 2024 and 168.1% in 2025. The sum of net income attributable to controlling interests over the last four quarters (1Q-4Q 2025) was KRW 6.67 billion, matching the full-year 2025 figure.

05

Industry analysis

The oil refining sector, the core tenant group for Koramco Life Infra Reit, is an oligopoly dominated by SK Energy (SK Innovation), S-OIL, GS Caltex and HD Hyundai Oilbank.

In the first quarter of 2025 S-OIL posted a loss, while operating income at SK Innovation's petroleum division and HD Hyundai Oilbank fell 90% year over year and GS Caltex's fell 72.1%, reflecting broad weakness in the refining business.

Such swings directly affect gas-station tenants' ability to pay rent, and concerns about longer-term demand erosion from the shift to electric vehicles are also cited.

Within Korea's listed REIT sector, Koramco Life Infra Reit stands out from single-sector specialists such as Shinhan Alpha REIT and ESR Kendall Square REIT by operating a mixed portfolio spanning energy, logistics, office, retail and hotel assets.

Its capital-recycling strategy, which channels asset-sale gains into special dividends, has delivered a dividend ratio exceeding its 6.2% target over the past three years.

However, the ongoing gas-station sales are structurally lowering the energy share of the portfolio, making the profitability and stability of the logistics, office and hotel assets into which proceeds are reinvested a key variable for future performance.

06

Outlook

As of October 2026 the company is pursuing the sale of 19 gas stations nationwide, located across Incheon, Gyeonggi, Daejeon, Chungbuk, Daegu, Gyeongbuk and Gyeongnam. Proceeds are planned for investment in new growth assets.

The Jaedong gas station site in Jongno-gu is being developed into a hotel with one basement and 14 above-ground floors totaling 91 rooms, at a construction cost of about KRW 20 billion, targeting an October 2027 opening. That development was funded by a KRW 17 billion convertible bond issued in February 2026.

In September 2026 the company issued new eighth and ninth series convertible bonds worth KRW 24 billion and KRW 10 billion for liquidity purposes, while also retiring and canceling part of its existing first, third and fourth series bonds, restructuring its capital base.

Including the new issuance, the number of shares potentially issuable upon conversion equals 10.20% of total shares outstanding. The company's next regular disclosure, the third-quarter 2026 report, is legally due by November 16, 2026 under the Capital Markets Act.

Bull factors

Portfolio Upgrade via Capital Recycling

The company sells gas stations to fund special dividends from the gains and reinvests the proceeds into higher-return assets such as hotels and logistics centers. In 2026 it is accelerating this portfolio shift by pursuing the sale of 19 additional gas stations nationwide.

Over the past three years this strategy has delivered a dividend ratio exceeding its 6.2% target, demonstrating execution capability.

Lease Structure Sustaining High Operating Margins

The disclosed operating margin ranged from the 60% to 70% level between 2022 and 2025, reflecting the stable profitability typical of real estate leasing. It peaked at 73.5% in 2024, the highest of the four years. The diversified tenant base is seen as a factor that reduces dependence on any single sector.

New Growth Driver via Hotel Development

A hotel development project at the Jaedong site in Jongno-gu is underway, targeting 91 rooms and completion by October 2027. Together with existing hotel assets, this expands the hotel sector's weight within the portfolio over time.

Funding the development through a combination of convertible bonds and internal cash also avoided an immediate new share offering.

Bear factors

Weakening Profit and Cash Flow

2025 net income attributable to controlling interests fell 71.9% year over year to KRW 6.67 billion, the smallest among the four years. Operating cash flow also declined every year, from KRW 24 billion in 2022 to KRW 3.42 billion in 2025, contracting more sharply than earnings. If one-off gains from asset sales diminish, earnings volatility could increase going forward.

Sensitivity to Refiner Tenant Conditions

Energy (gas station) assets form the largest share of the portfolio, with tenants concentrated among the oligopoly refiners SK Energy, S-OIL, GS Caltex and HD Hyundai Oilbank.

In the first quarter of 2025, S-OIL posted a loss and operating income at SK Innovation's petroleum division and HD Hyundai Oilbank fell 90% year over year, reflecting weak refining conditions. Concerns about longer-term declines in gas-station demand from the shift to electric vehicles are also cited.

Dilution Pressure from Ongoing CB Issuance

Multiple series of convertible bonds have been issued and repaid in 2026, and including September's new issuance, the number of shares issuable upon conversion reaches 10.20% of total shares outstanding.

As CB issuance for liquidity purposes continues, existing shareholders' stakes could be diluted if conversion requests accelerate. The conversion price was set at KRW 4,092 per share, meaning conversion incentives could shift depending on share price movements.

10

Risk factors

Financial Structure and Leverage

The debt-to-equity ratio has stayed in the 155% to 176% range between 2022 and 2025, reaching 168.1% at the end of 2025. Funding relies substantially on convertible bond issuance, making interest terms and repayment/conversion schedule management important. Liquidity pressure could rise if put-option exercise windows for multiple CB series overlap.

Tenant Concentration Risk

Energy assets represent the largest portfolio share, with tenants concentrated among a small number of refiners. Refining-sector conditions or group-level business restructuring could potentially affect rent collection. This risk persists until the ongoing gas-station sales are completed.

Development Execution Risk

New development projects such as the Jaedong hotel are exposed to typical development risks including construction cost changes, permitting and schedule delays. The targeted completion of October 2027 could be subject to change. If post-completion occupancy and returns fall short of expectations, the investment benefit could be limited.

Overall view

Koramco Life Infra Reit continues to reshape its portfolio through a capital-recycling strategy centered on gas-station sales and hotel/logistics asset repositioning. 2025 net income attributable to controlling interests fell 71.9% year over year to KRW 6.67 billion, the smallest of the four years, while operating cash flow also contracted for a fourth consecutive year.

The operating margin, however, has stayed in the mid-60% to 70% range, preserving the profitability structure typical of leasing businesses. The repeated issuance and early retirement of multiple convertible bond series in 2026 has simultaneously expanded the funding base and potential dilution, a point worth watching.

The completion timing and realized gains or losses from ongoing projects, including the additional 19-gas-station sale and the Jaedong hotel development, will shape the direction of results in coming quarters.

Tenant concentration in the refining sector and cyclical swings in that industry remain factors to monitor until the portfolio restructuring is complete.
