02

Business structure

Koramco Life Infra Reit is a KOSPI-listed REIT managed by KORAMCO REITs Management and Trust, and at its 2020 listing its entire portfolio consisted of gas station-related assets.

Through a capital-recycling strategy, the company has since selectively sold existing gas stations and used the proceeds to diversify into logistics centers, office preferred shares, retail and hotel assets.

Based on a November 2025 disclosure, the assets-under-management mix stood at gas stations 60%, logistics centers 28%, offices 5%, retail 5%, mobility 1% and hotels 1%.

Major tenants include directly-operated HD Hyundai Oilbank gas stations, large-format Samsung Electronics and LG Electronics appliance stores, Coupang fulfillment and last-mile logistics centers, and drive-through outlets of McDonald's and Paul Bassett.

The largest shareholder is Mirae Asset Global Investments, holding a 27.53% stake as of March 31, 2026, with Koramco Asset Management, KORAMCO REITs Management and Trust, and Hyundai Oilbank also among major shareholders.

As part of its asset-reshuffling strategy, the company began a sale process for 19 gas stations nationwide on October 14, 2026, with a bidding notice issued and acquisition proposals to be accepted through November 19.

The properties for sale include four each in Incheon and Gyeonggi, four in Daejeon and North Chungcheong combined, four in Daegu and North Gyeongsang combined, and three in South Gyeongsang, with Cushman & Wakefield Korea advising the sale.

In a related move, the company has also begun demolishing a former gas station site in Jaedong, Jongno-gu, to directly develop a small-to-mid-size hotel.

Competitively, unlike sector-focused peers such as Shinhan Alpha REIT (office) or ESR Kendall Square REIT (logistics), the company maintains a portfolio diversified across multiple sectors and regions.

04

Earnings analysis

On a consolidated basis, revenue fell 8.4% from KRW 34.4 billion in 2022 to KRW 31.5 billion in 2023, then rose 54.0% to KRW 48.5 billion in 2024 before falling 20.5% to KRW 38.6 billion in 2025. Among 2022-2025, 2024 was the largest revenue year and 2023 the smallest.

Operating profit moved similarly, from KRW 23.6 billion in 2022 to KRW 18.1 billion in 2023 (-23.1%), KRW 35.6 billion in 2024 (+96.7%), and KRW 24.2 billion in 2025 (-32.2%). The operating margin was 68.6% in 2022, 57.6% in 2023, 73.5% in 2024 and 62.7% in 2025, peaking in 2024 before easing in 2025.

Controlling-interest net profit rose from KRW 11.5 billion in 2022 to KRW 10.7 billion in 2023 (-7.0%) and KRW 23.8 billion in 2024 (+121.2%), before falling sharply to KRW 6.67 billion in 2025 (-71.9%), making 2025 the smallest year of the 2022-2025 period.

Net profit including non-controlling interests matched the controlling-interest figure in every year, as there was no minority interest, recording KRW 11.5 billion, KRW 10.7 billion, KRW 23.8 billion and KRW 6.67 billion from 2022 to 2025.

The pronounced profit increase in 2024 and sharp decline in 2025 are interpreted as reflecting the REIT-specific pattern of varying disposal-gain scale from gas station and other asset sales, which amplifies year-to-year earnings volatility.

Operating cash flow declined for four straight years, from KRW 24 billion in 2022 to KRW 17.1 billion in 2023, KRW 8.05 billion in 2024 and KRW 3.42 billion in 2025, indicating a slowing cash-generation trend separate from the profit swings.

The debt-to-equity ratio moved within a 150-180% range, at 175.1% in 2022, 176.2% in 2023, 155.0% in 2024 and 168.1% in 2025, with total equity of KRW 480.5 billion (all attributable to controlling interests) and total liabilities of KRW 807.7 billion at the end of 2025.

Industry analysis

According to the Korea Association of REITs' June 2026 tally, the average occupancy rate across 13 listed REITs including D&D Platform REIT, Shinhan Seobu T&D REIT and Shinhan Alpha REIT stood at 97.6% excluding the hotel sector, indicating overall tenancy stability remains intact across the sector.

However, as of July 14, 2026, the combined market capitalization of 23 listed REITs was KRW 8.2833 trillion, markedly down from KRW 10.2848 trillion reported at the end of April 2026 (then across 25 names), even accounting for the difference in the number of listed names.

At the same point in time, 15 of the 23 names yielded above 8% and 11 above 10%, with Koramco Life Infra Reit at 8.23%, placing it alongside Shinhan Alpha REIT, Lotte Reit, Daishin Value REIT and Shinhan Seobu T&D REIT in the 6-10% rental-income-based dividend group.

At the other end sit names such as SK Reit, Hanwha REIT and SamsungFN REIT, which trade at comparatively lower yields on the back of large-group sponsorship and assets, reflecting a market premium for sponsor stability.

Interest rates remain a key variable for refinancing costs, and Samsung Securities projected in a late-2025 report that average funding rates for domestic-asset-focused listed REITs would trend down from their peak.

On the tax front, the government specified in its January 2026 "2026 Economic Growth Strategy" a push for preferential separate taxation on listed REIT dividend income, and the Korea Association of REITs has requested a 9% separate tax rate be applied to dividend income up to KRW 20 million in the tax reform plan.

Unlike sector-focused office or logistics specialist REITs, Koramco Life Infra Reit's diversification across gas stations, logistics, retail and hotels is both a point of competitive differentiation and a source of criticism that its investment direction lacks a clear identity.

Elevated Leverage and Execution Uncertainty in Asset Sales

The debt-to-equity ratio moved in a 155-176% range from 2022 to 2025, indicating leverage is not low.

The 19-gas-station sale pursued in October 2026 also carries execution uncertainty, as pricing and timing could shift depending on gas-station-specific deal variables such as soil contamination, existing lease arrangements and demolition costs.

Risk factors

Interest Rate and Financial Risk

With the debt-to-equity ratio sustained at 150-180%, interest expense could vary depending on refinancing rates when secured loans mature. If anticipated rate declines do not materialize, this could pressure distributable dividend resources.

Asset Disposal Execution Risk

The sale of 19 gas stations, with bidding closing in November 2026, could see its final sale price and completion affected by soil remediation costs, existing lease contracts and permitting conditions. Delays or prices below expectations could affect the timeline for securing funds for new growth-asset investment.

Funding and Dilution Risk

Following the March 2026 issuance of a KRW 12 billion private-placement convertible bond, the company submitted a letter of intent in April for a KRW 119.3 billion equity investment in a new REIT, leaving open the possibility of further funding needs.

Should the convertible bond be converted into shares or additional capital raised, dilution of existing shareholders cannot be ruled out.

Overall view

Koramco Life Infra Reit began as a single-asset gas station REIT at listing and has since broadened its portfolio into logistics, retail, office and hotel assets under a capital-recycling strategy, continuing this reshuffling with the pursuit of a 19-gas-station sale in October 2026.

Disclosed results show revenue and profit surged in 2024 before revenue, operating profit and controlling-interest net profit all fell by double digits or more in 2025, with operating cash flow also declining for four consecutive years.

This volatility stems from the REIT-specific structure in which disposal-gain scale from asset sales varies year to year, illustrating that profit levels are difficult to gauge from rental income alone.

The debt-to-equity ratio has been managed in the 150-180% range, alongside additional funding and investment moves such as convertible bond issuance and the pursuit of a new REIT equity investment.

On pricing metrics, the price-to-book ratio has traded below 1x and the dividend yield ranks in the upper-middle tier among domestic-asset-focused REITs, though the earnings volatility warrants caution in interpreting simple comparisons.

The outcome of the November gas station sale bidding and the confirmed figures in the next regular business report are likely to be key checkpoints for assessing the portfolio's and earnings' future direction.
