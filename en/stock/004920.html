Citech: Annual Turnaround Meets Three Straight Quarterly Operating Losses

Citech returned to annual operating profit in 2025, but it has booked operating losses in each quarter from the first quarter of 2026 through the third, so when its AI healthcare and PA businesses begin to lift earnings is the next point to track.

02

Business structure

Citech, founded in 1967, is an information and communications company whose main businesses are manufacturing kiosks, including unmanned automatic certificate issuance machines, and premium network audio.

Its kiosks are sold mainly to public-sector customers such as government offices and schools as well as service companies, and the company says its accumulated track record helps win new orders. The audio business is export-driven, supplying products under its own ROSE (HiFi ROSE) brand to 40 countries.

However, according to wisereport data, the export share of certificate-issuance machines and library systems and other products was 22.34% in 2024, which indicates a sizable reliance on domestic demand.

In 2025 the company absorbed MOD, bringing a hospital network and medical IT infrastructure in-house while pursuing lower overlapping costs and operating efficiency. In the same year its new public address (PA) business began generating early revenue from public agencies, transport facilities and large complexes.

The company is also pursuing a merger with KEVIC to internalize next-generation IP broadcasting technology. Its medium-to-long-term core is an AI healthcare platform that combines medical infrastructure with AI, aimed at in-hospital patient monitoring and medical data analysis.

Competitive dynamics differ by business: kiosks compete mainly for domestic public and service demand, while audio competes on brand in the global premium audio market. Compared with component-supplier peers in the same electronics and parts sector, its structure is centered on finished products and services.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual revenue peaked at KRW 57.1 billion in 2023, up 0.6% from the prior year, then fell 16.9% to KRW 47.4 billion in 2024 and 12.6% to KRW 41.4 billion in 2025, a decline across 2024 and 2025.

The company said 2025 revenue rose 22.8% excluding discontinued operations, which points in the opposite direction from the reported decline, so the two bases should be read separately. Operating margin moved from 1.0% in 2022 to -12.5% in 2023, then recovered to -4.3% in 2024 and 4.8% in 2025.

The 2023 operating loss of KRW 7.11 billion was a swing into deficit, while the 2024 loss of KRW 2.05 billion reflected a narrower deficit. For 2025's operating profit of KRW 2 billion, the company cited merger synergies from MOD, early revenue from the new PA unit and steady growth in its core businesses.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders reached KRW 14.6 billion that year, well above operating profit, and the company attributed the gap mainly to higher financial instrument valuation gains, equity-method investment gains and a lower tax expense from recognizing deferred tax assets.

Quarterly, the fourth quarter of 2025 combined operating profit of KRW 1.61 billion with controlling-shareholder net income of KRW 14.2 billion, suggesting non-operating items weighed most heavily in that quarter.

Revenue rebounded to KRW 6.59 billion in the first quarter of 2026 from KRW 42.32 million in the prior quarter, yet the company returned to an operating loss of KRW 1.55 billion.

Revenue then was KRW 5.1 billion in the second quarter and KRW 6.41 billion in the third, with operating losses of KRW 1.7 billion and KRW 1.67 billion, extending the losses to three straight quarters. Third-quarter revenue fell 46.8% from a year earlier.

Controlling-shareholder net income swung from KRW 6.92 billion in the first quarter to net losses of KRW 3.09 billion and KRW 4.06 billion in the second and third quarters, two consecutive quarters of losses.

The debt-to-equity ratio rose to 73.4% at the end of 2025 from 67.6% in 2024, while operating cash flow improved to KRW 3.16 billion from KRW 1.49 billion.

05

Industry analysis

The kiosk business depends on public demand from government offices and schools and on capital spending by service companies, so it is shaped by budget execution and investment cycles. The company expects expanded public infrastructure investment to accelerate growth in its PA business.

In broadcasting infrastructure, the shift from analog to IP-based systems is under way, and the company says that transition demand is spreading across the public and private sectors.

The audio business is export-oriented and can benefit directly from growth in the global premium audio market, but it is also exposed to currency moves and overseas economic swings. In healthcare, voice and hearing-based AI technology is presented as the core engine.

Citech said it placed first among 22 teams in the CLIP (Cadenza Lyric Intelligibility Prediction) Challenge at the 2026 IEEE ICASSP, and it announced that it presented the technology at the ICASSP 2026 conference in May.

Whether the competition win and conference presentation will translate into hospital-related revenue is not yet visible in reported results.

Large component suppliers in the same electronics and parts sector are built around parts supply and concentrated customer relationships, whereas Citech's weight sits in finished products and services, so its growth path differs. The position of each business in its industry cycle should be read separately for kiosks, audio and PA.

06

Outlook

The company has framed 2026 as a year to pursue both sound management and top-line growth, setting a goal of substantially exceeding the prior year's results. The key test is how quickly it can narrow operating losses that have run through the third quarter.

Management plans to cut unnecessary costs through organizational integration and operating efficiency after the mergers. The AI healthcare platform is designed to combine proprietary AI algorithms with existing IT hardware, and the company identifies its hospital network and medical field data as core assets.

The company says the kiosk unit will launch next-generation unmanned solutions, while the audio unit plans new products and wider exports. A shift toward next-generation IP broadcasting through the planned KEVIC merger is also included in its 2026 core strategy.

On the capital side, the share count was reduced from 5,217,987 to 5,117,815 shares, with the change listed on November 13, 2026. An extraordinary shareholders' meeting on December 21, 2026 will consider an amendment to the articles of incorporation to change the registered head office location.

The next points to confirm are whether fourth-quarter 2026 results end the three-quarter run of operating losses and how much AI healthcare revenue appears in the income statement.

08

Bull factors

Annual profitability recovery

Operating margin moved from 1.0% in 2022 and -12.5% in 2023 to 4.8% in 2025, shifting the direction from deficit to profit. Operating cash flow also rose to KRW 3.16 billion in 2025 from KRW 1.49 billion in 2024. The recovery in annual earnings shown in reported results is a positive factor.

Merger synergies and early PA revenue

The company says that after the absorption of MOD, overlapping costs were cut and operations became more efficient at the same time. The new PA unit has started generating early revenue from public agencies, transport facilities and large complexes.

The company also expects growth to accelerate alongside expanding public infrastructure investment, which is cited as a bullish argument.

AI competition win and conference presentation

The company said it placed first among 22 teams in the CLIP Challenge at the 2026 IEEE ICASSP. It says the core algorithm was developed in-house and that it is extending voice and hearing-based technology to vital-sign analysis. Whether this technical validation will translate into actual revenue has not yet been confirmed.

09

Bear factors

Quarterly operating losses persist

Operating losses of KRW 1.55 billion, KRW 1.7 billion and KRW 1.67 billion in the first through third quarters of 2026 mark three straight quarters of deficits. Third-quarter revenue of KRW 6.41 billion fell 46.8% from a year earlier.

The gap between the company's goal of substantially exceeding the prior year and the current quarterly trend is a bearish factor.

Profit reliant on non-operating items and falling revenue

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 14.6 billion in 2025 was well above operating profit of KRW 2 billion, and the factors the company cited were financial instrument valuation gains, equity-method investment gains and deferred tax asset recognition.

Revenue has declined for two consecutive years through 2025, and the 22.8% increase the company cited excludes discontinued operations. The quality of earnings and the revenue base both need checking, which is a bearish argument.

Rising leverage and unproven new business

The debt-to-equity ratio rose to 73.4% at the end of 2025 from 67.6% in 2024. With controlling-shareholder net losses continuing for two straight quarters, the pace of building equity could be constrained.

The AI healthcare business remains at the technology validation stage, and no contribution to reported results has yet been confirmed.

10

Risk factors

Earnings volatility and reliance on non-operating items

The 2025 swing to profit relied heavily on non-operating items. In 2026, quarterly results showed mismatched signs, with a first-quarter net profit alongside an operating loss. Large swings make it hard to judge the trend from quarterly results alone, which is a risk in itself.

Concentration and dependence on public demand

Kiosks depend on public demand from government offices and schools, while audio depends on overseas exports. Budget timing and currency moves could disrupt the revenue flow. The new PA business also relies on public infrastructure investment, so it shares the same type of exposure.

New-business execution and financial burden

The AI healthcare business is still at a stage that requires leveraging the hospital network and expanding the technology. Merger integration costs and new-business investment could weigh on near-term earnings. With the debt-to-equity ratio up to 73.4%, future funding conditions are another variable to watch.

Overall view

Citech swung to profit in 2025 with annual operating profit of KRW 2 billion and net income attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 14.6 billion, after losses in 2023. Operating margin moved from 1.0% in 2022 and -12.5% in 2023 to -4.3% in 2024 and 4.8% in 2025.

However, 2025 net income relied heavily on non-operating items and tax effects, and revenue has declined for two consecutive years. In 2026, operating losses have run for three straight quarters through the third quarter, and controlling-shareholder net losses have continued for two quarters.

Bullish factors include the annual earnings recovery, synergies from the MOD merger, early PA revenue and technical validation through first place in the CLIP Challenge at ICASSP 2026.

Bearish factors include deteriorating quarterly results, falling revenue, a debt-to-equity ratio that rose to 73.4% at the end of 2025, and no confirmed contribution from the AI healthcare business to reported results.

The next points to confirm are the extraordinary shareholders' meeting on December 21, 2026, fourth-quarter 2026 results and the 2026 annual business report due by the end of March 2027.
