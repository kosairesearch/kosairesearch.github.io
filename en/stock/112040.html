Wemade Back in the Black, but Pipeline and China Remain the Tests

Wemade returned to operating profit of KRW 26.4 billion in the third quarter of 2026, but revenue from existing live games is shrinking, so the timing of new titles and China licensing are the next checkpoints.

02

Business structure

Wemade develops and operates games centered on MMORPGs, and its core franchises are MIR4 and Night Crow. Both titles have been served in global markets, and Night Crow received a Chinese foreign-game approval in June 2026 and is preparing a local launch.

Revenue comes from three main sources: sales from its own live games, licensing of the Legend of Mir 2 and 3 IP to a Chinese partner, and a token economy tied to the WEMIX blockchain ecosystem.

In the third quarter of 2026, Chinese licensing revenue from Legend of Mir 2 and 3 was recognized, lifting quarterly revenue, while sales from existing live games declined. The pipeline includes about 20 titles, among them Night Crow W, Mir5, Project TAL, a new Hundred Note IP title, Project IL and Project MO.

Project TAL is presented as the first title aimed at console platforms. The affiliate Wemade Max, which handles development of part of the pipeline, reported third-quarter 2026 revenue of KRW 29.9 billion and an operating loss of KRW 13.5 billion.

Because affiliate results flow into consolidated figures, its losses should be weighed alongside the parent's numbers. Among domestic peers, Wemade competes for global MMORPG players with Netmarble, NC, Pearl Abyss and Krafton.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual revenue rose from KRW 463.5 billion in 2022 to KRW 605.3 billion in 2023 (up 30.6% year on year) and KRW 711.9 billion in 2024 (up 17.6%), then fell to KRW 614 billion in 2025 (down 13.7%). Among 2022 to 2025, 2024 was the largest year for revenue and 2022 the smallest.

Operating losses were KRW 84.9 billion in 2022 and KRW 110.4 billion in 2023, with losses widening in 2023. The company returned to operating profit of KRW 7.06 billion in 2024, and operating profit rose 51.2% to KRW 10.7 billion in 2025.

The operating margin moved from -18.3% in 2022 and -18.2% in 2023 to 1.0% in 2024 and 1.7% in 2025. Net income attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 188.4 billion in 2024, while total net income for the same year was KRW 181.6 billion, and the gap reflects non-controlling interests.

The 2024 year-on-year increase in attributable net income is shown as 2,918,963,114.9%, but the 2023 base was about KRW 0.01 million, so that growth rate carries limited meaning. In 2025, attributable net income turned into a loss of KRW 18.6 billion, and total net loss was KRW 27 billion.

Operating cash flow went from -KRW 37 billion in 2022, -KRW 10.3 billion in 2023 and -KRW 80.2 billion in 2024 to KRW 64.3 billion in 2025. The debt ratio fell from 167.6% in 2022 and 253.7% in 2023 to 125.5% in 2024 and 114.8% in 2025.

At the end of 2025, total liabilities were KRW 933 billion, total equity was KRW 812.7 billion, and equity attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 406.8 billion.

Quarterly revenue was KRW 163.6 billion in 3Q 2025 and KRW 191.7 billion in 4Q 2025 (up 17.2% quarter on quarter), with operating profit of KRW 26.3 billion and KRW 24.3 billion (down 7.8%). Revenue fell 20.0% to KRW 153.3 billion in 1Q 2026, and operating profit fell 65.0% to KRW 8.49 billion.

In 2Q 2026, revenue dropped 29.4% to KRW 108.3 billion and the company posted an operating loss of KRW 21 billion. In 3Q 2026, revenue was KRW 146 billion (up 34.9% quarter on quarter, down 10.8% year on year) and operating profit was KRW 26.4 billion (up 0.3% year on year).

The company attributed the third-quarter revenue increase to recognition of Chinese licensing revenue from Legend of Mir 2 and 3, and whether that revenue recurs can be gauged only from later quarterly filings.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 15.2 billion in 3Q 2026, down 75.5% from KRW 62.1 billion a year earlier.

05

Industry analysis

MMORPGs have long been regarded as a genre with a broad global player base and the potential for long-running hits, and Wemade has global hit experience in the genre through MIR4 and Night Crow. China is different, because launch timing depends on whether a publishing approval, known as a hao, is issued.

Night Crow received a Chinese foreign-game approval in June 2026, while at its 1Q 2026 earnings call in May 2026 the company said it continues efforts to obtain MIR4's approval and has completed development so it can launch quickly once approved.

The blockchain features differentiate the overseas versions, but play-to-earn structures are not permitted in Korea or China, so those versions omit them.

By genre, the company is preparing titles across casual action, RPG, extraction, shooter and subculture through its studio affiliates, and it aims for a portfolio not concentrated in one genre. It is also expanding to consoles, and Project TAL is described as the first console effort by the team behind Night Crow.

In the same quarter, NC reported an operating profit turnaround, and press headlines attributed it to Lineage Classic and casual titles.

Until new titles arrive, Wemade is defending profit by cutting costs against declining sales from existing live games, so the commercial reception of new releases will shape its position in the sector.

Outlook

The most specific schedule the company has given is a Q2 2027 launch target for Night Crow W, a new title aimed at global markets that Wemade is developing and preparing to launch together with affiliates including Mad Engine.

At its 1Q 2026 earnings call in May 2026, the company named a new Night Crow IP title with a Q4 2026 target as its nearest earnings catalyst.

In its November 2026 announcement, the company gave a Q2 2027 launch target for Night Crow W, so the relationship between the two timelines should be tracked in future disclosures.

In China, Night Crow received a foreign-game approval in June 2026 and is preparing for launch, while MIR4 continues efforts to obtain approval. Licensing revenue from Legend of Mir 2 and 3 was reflected in third-quarter results, and whether it continues after the fourth quarter is a key variable.

The new-title pipeline includes Mir5, Project TAL, a new Hundred Note IP title, Project IL and Project MO among about 20 titles. A central question is how far new titles and licensing income can offset the decline in sales from existing live games.

The operating margin turned positive in the third quarter of 2026, but quarterly swings remain large, as the second-quarter operating loss showed.

08

Bull factors

Third-quarter turnaround and cost efficiency

Operating profit in the third quarter of 2026 was KRW 26.4 billion, reversing a KRW 21 billion operating loss in the second quarter. Revenue fell 10.8% year on year, yet operating profit rose 0.3%, reflecting lower personnel and advertising costs. The company describes this as offsetting declining sales from existing live games through cost control.

China licensing and franchise assets

Chinese licensing revenue from Legend of Mir 2 and 3 lifted third-quarter revenue 34.9% quarter on quarter. Night Crow received a Chinese foreign-game approval in June 2026, and MIR4 is also preparing for China. Licensing income from established franchises can generate revenue without a new launch.

Pipeline and platform expansion

The company says it is preparing about 20 titles, including Mir5, Project TAL and a new Hundred Note IP title. Night Crow W targets a Q2 2027 launch, and Project TAL is presented as the first console-targeted title. Broadening into casual action, RPG and shooter genres reduces reliance on a single genre.

Bear factors

Declining sales from existing games

Third-quarter 2026 revenue was KRW 146 billion, down 10.8% year on year, and 2025 annual revenue fell 13.7% to KRW 614 billion. The third-quarter rise depended on licensing revenue recognition, so it does not show existing game sales recovering at the same pace. The decline in existing live games remains the main weakness.

Earnings volatility and loss history

Operating losses persisted in 2022 and 2023, and attributable net income turned into a KRW 18.6 billion loss in 2025. Quarterly losses have recurred, including a KRW 29.9 billion attributable net loss in 4Q 2025 and a KRW 21 billion operating loss in 2Q 2026.

With KRW 17.5 billion in attributable net income over the last four quarters, the track record of profitability is still short.

Schedule shifts and affiliate losses

At its May 2026 earnings call the company pointed to a Night Crow IP title with a Q4 2026 target, while in November it presented a Q2 2027 target for Night Crow W. Affiliate Wemade Max reported third-quarter 2026 revenue of KRW 29.9 billion and an operating loss of KRW 13.5 billion. Until new titles launch, affiliate losses and falling core sales could persist together.

Risk factors

Recurrence of licensing revenue

Licensing revenue from Legend of Mir 2 and 3, the main driver of the third-quarter turnaround, can vary sharply by quarter depending on when it is recognized. Whether a similar scale continues after the fourth quarter will become clearer only as later disclosures accumulate.

The reported figures show that a drop in licensing income could return the company to an operating loss, as in the second quarter.

New-title timing and performance

The Q2 2027 target for Night Crow W is still ahead, and about 20 titles have not launched. Press reports said MIR M Global did not achieve notable results in Korea or globally. A delayed launch or weak performance would make it hard to offset the decline in existing game sales.

Balance sheet and affiliate exposure

At the end of 2025, total liabilities were KRW 933 billion against total equity of KRW 812.7 billion, and the debt ratio was 114.8%. The ratio has fallen from 167.6% in 2022, but the liability base remains large.

Equity attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 406.8 billion, and a wider operating loss at affiliate Wemade Max could weigh on consolidated results and financial burden.

Overall view

Wemade's operating profit in the third quarter of 2026 was KRW 26.4 billion, turning from a second-quarter operating loss into a profit. However, revenue fell 10.8% year on year, and the direct driver of the turnaround was Chinese licensing revenue from Legend of Mir 2 and 3.

On an annual basis, operating results moved from the 2023 operating loss to a 2024 profit, and 2025 operating profit was KRW 10.7 billion. Attributable net income swung from KRW 188.4 billion in 2024 to a KRW 18.6 billion loss in 2025, showing high volatility.

The debt ratio declined from 167.6% in 2022 to 114.8% in 2025, but total liabilities remain KRW 933 billion.

Valuation can be read against attributable net income of KRW 17.5 billion over the last four quarters and equity attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 406.8 billion, and dividends are effectively absent.

The next checkpoints are the Q2 2027 target for Night Crow W, the timing of Chinese approvals and launches, and confirmed full-year results in the 2026 annual report. Bullish factors are licensing income and cost efficiency, while bearish factors are declining sales from existing games and earnings volatility.
