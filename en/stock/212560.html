Report overview

Electrified Parts Growth, Robotics Still Early

02

Business structure

Neooto is a maker of precision driveline parts for automobiles, centered on gears for transmissions and reducers as well as motor shafts. Growth has been driven by rising supply of core drive parts for hybrid (HEV) and electric vehicles (EV).

Its main customer appears to be the Hyundai Motor Group, where mass production of gears and motor shafts for the EV3, EV4 and EV5 electric models has ramped up.

On the production side, the Yesan No. 4 plant received its completion approval in early February 2026, completing preparations for a dedicated electrified production base; the new plant was announced in 2024 with a planned investment of KRW 41.3 billion.

The largest shareholder, Kim Seon-hyeon and seven related parties, held 54.55% as of October 22, 2026. The company is pursuing a comprehensive share exchange to make the unlisted Auto Industry, a company on the largest shareholder's side, a wholly owned subsidiary, with completion targeted for February 2027.

The structure would split the process so the affiliate handles forging and primary machining while Neooto handles precision gear cutting and assembly, unifying production, purchasing and investment; the company targets annual cost savings of about KRW 5 billion and investment savings of KRW 52.5 billion over five years.

In new business, in October 2026 it began physically assembling core drive parts, including a reducer for a planetary-exploration rover, under a national research project, and it is pursuing precision reducers and actuators for humanoid robots based on its experience mass-producing automotive transmission reducers. Revenue remains weighted toward automotive driveline parts, while robot parts are still at the assembly stage.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual revenue rose from KRW 164 billion in 2022 to KRW 198.2 billion in 2023 (up 20.8% year-on-year), KRW 218.6 billion in 2024 (up 10.3%) and KRW 232.1 billion in 2025 (up 6.2%), growing for three consecutive years from 2023 through 2025, with 2025 the largest year of 2022-2025.

The slowdown in growth from 20.8% to 10.3% and then 6.2% shows that the pace of top-line expansion has been decelerating.

Operating income went from KRW 7.97 billion in 2022 to KRW 10.4 billion in 2023 (up 30.3%), KRW 12.6 billion in 2024 (up 21.8%) and KRW 13.7 billion in 2025 (up 8.1%), also rising for three consecutive years, while the operating margin improved from 4.9% to 5.9%.

Margin gains have been modest relative to revenue growth, suggesting profit growth has come mainly from production efficiency and cost control.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders rose from KRW 7.85 billion in 2022 to KRW 11.9 billion in 2023 (up 51.9%) and KRW 17.7 billion in 2024 (up 48.6%), making 2024 the largest year, before falling 16.0% to KRW 14.9 billion in 2025.

According to FnGuide's consolidated figures, financial income dropped from KRW 6.7 billion in 2024 to KRW 2.4 billion in 2025, which appears to explain why net income fell even as operating income grew.

Operating cash flow ran at KRW 26.5 billion in 2022, KRW 20.2 billion in 2023, KRW 30.1 billion in 2024 and KRW 25.3 billion in 2025.

On a quarterly basis, the operating income of KRW 2.34 billion in the fourth quarter of 2025, down 35.5% from the prior quarter, was the lowest of the last five quarters on a reported basis, and it recovered to KRW 3.66 billion in the first quarter of 2026 on revenue of KRW 60.4 billion.

In the second quarter of 2026, revenue reached KRW 63.4 billion and operating income KRW 4.11 billion, the highest of the five quarters, while third-quarter operating income of KRW 3.63 billion was down 0.2% year-on-year.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders swung from KRW 4.33 billion in the first quarter of 2026 to KRW 2.34 billion in the second and KRW 2.69 billion in the third, a volatility that suggests non-operating items and taxes have a larger effect than operating income does.

Cumulative revenue for January-September 2026 was KRW 182.6 billion, up 3.4% year-on-year according to the company, and net income attributable to controlling shareholders over the last four quarters totaled KRW 11.5 billion.

05

Industry analysis

The end market is in a transition phase in which hybrid and electric vehicles are growing side by side. The expansion of Hyundai Motor Group's hybrid and EV lineups supports supply of automatic transmission gears for next-generation hybrids and gears and motor shafts for the EV3, EV4 and EV5 models.

The company describes its growth as electrified parts volume layered onto a stable base of demand for conventional combustion-engine parts. Driveline parts orders track automakers' production plans, so shifts in the powertrain mix translate directly into changes in parts composition.

In competition, larger suppliers such as Hyundai Mobis and automaker-affiliated parts makers are the main rivals, and Neooto emphasizes its transmission and reducer machining know-how along with flexible production lines that can make multiple models without additional equipment.

In robotics, demand for precision reducers for humanoid robots is still at an early stage, and the company stresses that its automotive precision machining can be applied to this market.

The company's logic is that bringing forging through assembly under one roof through the share exchange would reduce reliance on outside suppliers and lower costs. How parts prices and volume mix change during the electrification shift remains a medium-term variable.

06

Outlook

The most concrete quantitative targets the company has given are annual cost savings of about KRW 5 billion and investment savings of KRW 52.5 billion over five years after the share exchange is completed.

The exchange is targeted for completion in February 2027, and once done it would create an integrated production system running from forging to final assembly. The Yesan No. 4 plant received its completion approval in early February 2026, finishing preparations for a dedicated electrified production base.

In a November 2025 announcement, the company said it was installing equipment and improving line efficiency to prepare for mass production of a new extended-range EV (EREV) program.

It is also strengthening its response for parts used on Hyundai Motor Group's EM platform, which covers large EV models such as the Genesis lineup, so sales on that platform could directly affect volumes.

The company has set up a robotics team under its research institute to secure core technologies such as robot drive modules. It began assembling a rover reducer for planetary exploration in October 2026 and is pursuing commercialization of precision reducers and actuators for humanoid robots.

The company says this will position it as a specialist in robot and mobility parts. However, the timing at which robot parts appear as revenue in reported results has not yet shown up, and how the existing electrified parts volumes and the robotics business will share equipment and staff is also worth watching.

08

Bull factors

Electrified parts demand supports top-line growth

Revenue has risen for three consecutive years from 2023 through 2025 as supply of core drive parts for hybrids and EVs expanded, and cumulative revenue for January-September 2026 was up 3.4% year-on-year.

Mass production of motor shafts and gears for the EV3, EV4 and EV5 models for Hyundai Motor Group is cited as volume support. Completion of the dedicated electrified No. 4 plant has also added production capacity.

Gradual improvement in operating margin

The operating margin rose from 4.9% in 2022 to 5.9% in 2025, and operating income also grew for three consecutive years through 2025.

The company has explained that its flexible production lines and a composite-dimension automated inspection system, which it introduced as an industry first, helped lower defect rates and maintain profitability.

Second-quarter 2026 operating income of KRW 4.11 billion is the highest of the last five quarters on a reported basis.

Robot reducers as an attempt to broaden the business

In October 2026, under a national research project, the company began physically assembling core drive parts, including a reducer for a planetary-exploration rover. It plans to extend its automotive transmission reducer mass-production experience into precision reducers and actuators for humanoid robots. The company has said that establishing this business could reduce its dependence on automakers.

09

Bear factors

2025 net income decline and lower financial income

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 14.9 billion in 2025, down 16.0% year-on-year. FnGuide's consolidated figures suggest the drop was driven by financial income falling from KRW 6.7 billion in 2024 to KRW 2.4 billion in 2025. The result shows net income can swing with non-operating items even when operating income rises.

Slowing revenue growth and quarterly volatility

Revenue growth slowed from 20.8% in 2023 to 10.3% in 2024 and 6.2% in 2025. Third-quarter 2026 revenue of KRW 58.8 billion fell 7.2% from the prior quarter, and operating income of KRW 3.63 billion was down 0.2% year-on-year.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 2.69 billion in the third quarter was down 31.2% year-on-year.

Dependence on Hyundai Motor Group volume and related-party structure

Because the main customer appears to be Hyundai Motor Group, results could depend heavily on electrified model volumes and automaker sales flow. The share exchange absorbing an unlisted company on the largest shareholder's side will require scrutiny of the exchange ratio and the transaction structure afterward. The debt-to-equity ratio reached 74.9% in 2024, so financial burden also deserves attention.

10

Risk factors

Customer concentration and pace of electrification

If results are tied to the electrified model volumes of Hyundai Motor Group, slower sales of those models or a change in the pace of electrification could feed directly into revenue growth. Which of hybrid or EV demand grows faster also changes the parts mix.

Execution of the share exchange and integration benefits

If the comprehensive share exchange targeted for February 2027 is delayed or its terms change, the timing of the target annual cost savings of about KRW 5 billion and five-year investment savings of KRW 52.5 billion would also slip. Whether the integrated production system performs as expected is the key question.

Execution risk in robotics commercialization

The planetary-rover reducer is still at the national research project stage, and humanoid-robot reducers are at the commercialization push stage. Without mass-production results and customer wins, the contribution to earnings relative to investment could be delayed. Whether automotive precision machining meets robot-level precision requirements is also yet to be proven.

Overall view

Neooto is a driveline parts maker that has grown both top line and profitability, with revenue and operating income rising for three consecutive years.

Operating income rose in 2025, but net income attributable to controlling shareholders fell 16.0% as financial income declined, so earnings volatility remains an open issue.

In 2026, operating income recovered to KRW 3.66 billion in the first quarter and KRW 4.11 billion in the second, and cumulative revenue through September rose 3.4% to KRW 182.6 billion.

Strengths include expanding supply of HEV and EV parts, completion of the Yesan No. 4 plant and the cost-savings targets of the share exchange. Weaknesses include slowing revenue growth, large quarterly swings in net income, and dependence on Hyundai Motor Group volumes alongside a related-party transaction structure.

The robot reducer business is still at the assembly stage under a national research project, and when it will appear in reported results is not yet visible.

The next checkpoints are completion of the share exchange targeted for February 2027, fourth-quarter and full-year 2026 preliminary results, and the 2026 annual business report.
