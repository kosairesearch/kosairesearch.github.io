02

Business structure

Moatech is a small precision motor maker founded in 1989 and classified under the electronics and components sector on KOSDAQ. Its core products are stepping motors (STEPPINGMOTOR) and flow-control actuators (ACTUATOR), while rental income and staffing services contribute small shares of revenue.

According to a company summary cited by WiseReport, the automotive segment is growing production as the customer base diversifies. The actuators are fitted to both combustion-engine and eco-friendly vehicles and are described as steadily sold because they are linked to carbon emission allowance regulation.

According to IB Tomato, domestic sales accounted for 84.5% of revenue from April 2025 to March 2026, and exports rose 34.5% to KRW 6.6 billion from KRW 4.9 billion a year earlier.

Most of the export increase came from purchased-for-resale products, while exports of the core stepping motors were KRW 3.3 billion, similar to the prior period. R&D spending in the fiscal year ended March 2025 was KRW 163.6 million, or 5.19% of sales (FnGuide).

IB Tomato also reported that the cost of sales ratio reached 89.7% over the same period, which it said has slowed a recovery in core profitability.

In competition, listed sector peers such as Samsung Electro-Mechanics and LG Innotek are large components companies with broader business scope, while Moatech is a mid-sized specialist in motors and actuators.

The foreign ownership ratio was 79.85% as of September 14, 2026 (WiseReport), and an overseas financial information site lists MINEBEA MITSUMI as the largest holder.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual results show sales growth and a narrowing loss moving in the same direction.

Sales fell from KRW 32 billion in the fiscal year ended March 2023 to KRW 30.6 billion in the fiscal year ended March 2024, a 4.3% decline, then rose to KRW 31.5 billion in the fiscal year ended March 2025, up 3.0%, and to KRW 42.6 billion in the fiscal year ended March 2026, up 35.4%.

The fiscal year ended March 2026 is the largest year in the March 2023 to March 2026 span. The operating loss was KRW 2.26 billion, KRW 2.17 billion, and KRW 3.16 billion in the fiscal years ended March 2023, 2024 and 2025, before narrowing to KRW 1.69 billion in the fiscal year ended March 2026.

The operating margin was -7.1%, -7.1%, -10.0% and -4.0% across the four years, showing narrowing in the latest year.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders turned to a profit of KRW 56.48 million in the fiscal year ended March 2024, moved back to a loss of KRW 1.98 billion in the fiscal year ended March 2025, and narrowed to a loss of KRW 666 million in the fiscal year ended March 2026.

Net income including non-controlling interests was a loss of KRW 655 million in the fiscal year ended March 2026. Operating cash flow moved from KRW 736 million in the fiscal year ended March 2024 to an outflow of KRW 2.84 billion in the fiscal year ended March 2026.

IB Tomato reported that receivables rose from KRW 4.9 billion to KRW 7.9 billion and inventories from KRW 3.4 billion to KRW 5.5 billion over the same period, and it attributed part of the cash drain to working capital expansion.

Quarterly sales fell from KRW 10.5 billion in the June 2025 quarter to KRW 9.93 billion in the December 2025 quarter, then rose to KRW 12.1 billion in the March 2026 quarter and KRW 14.4 billion in the June 2026 quarter, marking two straight quarters of growth.

The June 2026 quarter operating loss of KRW 130 million is the smallest of the five consecutive loss quarters since the June 2025 quarter. The combined net loss attributable to controlling shareholders over the last four quarters, from the September 2025 quarter to the June 2026 quarter, was KRW 597 million. No one-off profit or loss items were identified in the disclosed figures used for this report.

08

Bull factors

Sustained revenue growth

Sales in the fiscal year ended March 2026 rose 35.4% to KRW 42.6 billion, and June 2026 quarter sales reached KRW 14.4 billion, up 37.1% from a year earlier. Customer diversification in the automotive segment and steady sales of flow-control actuators are cited as the background. Two consecutive quarters of growth suggest the trend may not be a one-off.

Narrowing loss trend

The operating margin improved from -10.0% in the fiscal year ended March 2025 to -4.0% in the fiscal year ended March 2026. The June 2026 quarter operating loss of KRW 130 million compares with KRW 436 million a year earlier and is the smallest of the five loss quarters. Losses are narrowing while sales expand, which points to room for margin improvement.

Low leverage and shareholder return steps

The debt ratio was 12.7% at March 2026, and total equity was KRW 68.9 billion. The company decided to cancel repurchased treasury shares, and reports cite 5,126,656 common shares as the target. Shareholder return steps alongside a low debt ratio are a point to check for the capital structure.

Bear factors

Prolonged operating losses and weaker cash flow

An operating loss has been recorded in every fiscal year from March 2023 through March 2026, and IB Tomato reported five consecutive fiscal years of operating losses.

Operating cash flow deteriorated from KRW 736 million in the fiscal year ended March 2024 to an outflow of KRW 2.84 billion in the fiscal year ended March 2026. Receivables and inventories grew together, leaving the core business short of cash generation.

High cost ratio and fragile earnings structure

According to IB Tomato, the cost of sales ratio reached 89.7% from April 2025 to March 2026. The analysis said gross profit did not cover selling and administrative expenses, which kept operating losses in place.

The operating margin narrowed to -4.0% in the fiscal year ended March 2026, but it remains some distance from break-even.

Caution on sales durability and a disclosure gap

IB Tomato reported that some market participants are cautious on whether sales growth can be sustained. Results for the July to September 2026 period have not yet been disclosed in a periodic report.

The company was designated as an investment-caution stock in June 2024, which requires further attention to disclosure and trading conditions.

Risk factors

Cash flow and working capital risk

As sales grew, receivables and inventories increased together, and operating cash flow widened to an outflow of KRW 2.84 billion in the fiscal year ended March 2026. If working capital keeps expanding, funding needs could increase. The debt ratio is low at 12.7%, but continued cash outflows could still affect the capital structure.

Customer concentration and sector risk

Sales depend heavily on automotive customers, and the domestic share of revenue is high at 84.5%. Changes in vehicle production or in orders from specific customers could directly affect sales. If end demand slows, the sales growth trend could weaken and cost pressures could come back into focus.

Disclosure and trading designation risk

The company was designated as an investment-caution stock in June 2024. The free float ratio of 20.83% is low, which can amplify trading volatility in a small-cap stock. Until results for the July to September 2026 period are confirmed in the periodic report, uncertainty from the disclosure gap remains.

Overall view

Moatech grew sales for two consecutive fiscal years to KRW 42.6 billion in the year ended March 2026, and recorded sales of KRW 14.4 billion and an operating loss of KRW 130 million in the June 2026 quarter.

The operating margin improved to -4.0% in the fiscal year ended March 2026, but the operating losses that began in the March 2023 fiscal year have not yet ended. Operating cash flow worsened to an outflow of KRW 2.84 billion, and a high cost of sales ratio continues to constrain profitability.

On the balance sheet, a debt ratio of 12.7% and total equity of KRW 68.9 billion provide headroom. With the price-earnings ratio not calculable and the price-to-book ratio well below 1x, valuation reflects both asset value and the loss structure.

The decision to cancel treasury shares is a factual shareholder return step. The next checkpoints are the operating loss scale and sales durability in the half-year report covering July to September 2026, and whether the annual report shows a return to profit.
