02

Business structure

Moatech is a small precision motor maker founded in 1989 and listed on KOSDAQ in 1997. Its core product is the stepping motor, used in home appliances, office equipment, automobiles and telecommunications.

According to the company profile on WiseReport, the automotive segment accounts for more than 80% of sales, and automotive products include actuators for aerodynamic control that are linked to carbon emission regulation.

The same profile says the customer base in automotive is diversifying, which has raised production volumes. By region, sales are concentrated domestically: IB Tomato reported in June 2026 that domestic sales made up 84.5% of revenue for the year from April 2025 to March 2026.

Domestic stepping motor sales rose from KRW 25.8 billion to KRW 35.2 billion, and sales of non-stepping-motor items that the company buys on consignment and resells rose from KRW 1.3 billion to KRW 2.9 billion. Two consolidated subsidiaries are engaged in real estate leasing.

In competition, low-priced Chinese products are cited as a source of margin pressure. The largest shareholder is MINEBEA MITSUMI, with a 79.17% stake as of June 30, 2026.

04

Earnings analysis

On a reported annual basis, sales were KRW 32 billion for the fiscal year ended March 2023, KRW 30.6 billion for the year ended March 2024 (down 4.3% year on year), KRW 31.5 billion for the year ended March 2025 (up 3.0%), and KRW 42.6 billion for the year ended March 2026 (up 35.4%).

Sales have grown in both the years ended March 2025 and March 2026, following the low point in the year ended March 2024, and the year ended March 2026 is the largest year in the period from March 2023 to March 2026.

Operating losses were KRW 2.26 billion, KRW 2.17 billion (losses continued, narrowing), KRW 3.16 billion (losses continued, widening) and KRW 1.69 billion (losses continued, narrowing) across the same four years, making four consecutive years of operating losses. Operating margins were -7.1%, -7.1%, -10.0% and -4.0%.

According to IB Tomato, the cost of sales ratio for April 2025 to March 2026 was 89.7%, and gross profit of KRW 4.4 billion did not cover selling and administrative expenses of KRW 6.1 billion. Consignment-produced goods accounted for KRW 18.6 billion of cost of sales, close to half.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 56.48 million in the year ended March 2024, a swing to profit, then a loss of KRW 1.98 billion in the year ended March 2025, and a loss of KRW 666 million in the year ended March 2026, which was a narrower loss.

Quarterly sales rose from KRW 10.1 billion in the third quarter of 2025 and KRW 9.93 billion in the fourth quarter, to KRW 12.1 billion, KRW 14.4 billion and KRW 15.9 billion in the first three quarters of 2026, and the third-quarter figure was up 56.9% from a year earlier.

The operating loss narrowed from KRW 555 million in the third quarter of 2025 to KRW 130 million in the second quarter of 2026, then widened to KRW 367 million in the third quarter of 2026.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders turned to KRW 581 million in the third quarter of 2026, even as the operating loss widened, so non-operating items need to be checked. Over the last four quarters, net income attributable to controlling shareholders totaled KRW 175 million.

Operating cash flow moved from an inflow of KRW 736 million in the year ended March 2024 to outflows of KRW 753 million and KRW 2.84 billion in the following two years, while receivables rose from KRW 4.9 billion to KRW 7.9 billion and inventory from KRW 3.4 billion to KRW 5.5 billion, according to IB Tomato.

05

Industry analysis

Based on the business commentary in the WiseReport company profile, market growth drivers include rising demand for automotive electronics from eco-friendly vehicles and autonomous driving, AI-based robotics, and premium and IoT-linked home appliances.

Stepping motors are precision positioning parts and sit close to this end demand. Even so, the company's profitability depends on its cost structure, and a cost of sales ratio of 89.7% for the year ended March 2026 reflects both low automotive supplier margins and a high share of consignment purchasing.

IB Tomato reported that competition from low-priced Chinese products is pressuring pricing across product lines. In size, Moatech is classified as a mid-sized company by employee count and is a small supplier concentrated in one product family compared with large automotive electronics makers.

The company says its aerodynamic control actuators sell steadily under carbon emission rules, but no market share or competitor volume data was available for this product.

Other applications such as home appliances and office equipment account for a smaller share of sales, so changes in automotive demand have a large effect on results. The upside drivers are automotive electrification and new uses such as robots, and the downside drivers are price competition and a thin margin structure.

06

Outlook

Among the company's disclosed plans, the one most directly tied to shareholder value is the treasury share cancellation. In April 2026 the company disclosed that it would cancel about KRW 25.2 billion of treasury shares, or 5,126,656 common shares, which reduces the share count rather than paying cash dividends.

On operations, sales rose for three consecutive quarters from the first quarter to the third quarter of 2026, reaching KRW 15.9 billion in the third quarter.

If that trend holds, sales growth can continue, but turning the operating loss into a profit depends on lowering the cost of sales ratio, which was 89.7% on a reported basis and depends on how much consignment-purchased product is in the mix. Working capital is another variable.

Available liquidity at the end of March 2026 was KRW 38.8 billion, combining cash equivalents of KRW 14.6 billion and short-term financial instruments of KRW 24.2 billion, and total equity was KRW 68.9 billion. The debt ratio rose to 12.7% in the year ended March 2026 but remains low.

Whether the expansion in automotive electronics, robotics and home appliance demand translates into Moatech's results needs to be checked against reported figures. It also needs to be determined whether the third-quarter 2026 turn to profit came from non-operating items or from core operations. No new order or capacity expansion plans have been confirmed.

The cost of sales ratio was 89.7% for April 2025 to March 2026, and gross profit of KRW 4.4 billion did not cover selling and administrative expenses of KRW 6.1 billion. Consignment-purchased product accounted for KRW 18.6 billion of cost of sales, close to half. Price competition from low-priced Chinese products is also cited as a margin burden.

Cash flow and working capital burden

Part of this reflects working capital tied to higher sales, but failing to generate cash from core operations remains a burden.

Recurring losses and quarterly volatility

The operating loss persisted for four consecutive fiscal years from the year ended March 2023 to the year ended March 2026. The operating loss widened to KRW 367 million in the third quarter of 2026 from KRW 130 million in the second quarter.

The third-quarter profit at the attributable level has not yet been explained by core operations, so the durability of quarterly profit is uncertain. The company was designated as an investment-caution stock in June 2024.

Risk factors

Customer and product concentration

According to the WiseReport profile, the automotive segment accounts for more than 80% of sales, so results are highly exposed to vehicle production and changes in automotive parts demand. The core product is a single stepping motor family, so changes in its price or volume feed directly into total sales. The company says it is diversifying its automotive customers, but customer-level shares are not disclosed.

Largest shareholder stake and free float

The largest shareholder, MINEBEA MITSUMI, holds 79.17%, which limits the number of shares available to trade on the market. This structure can affect share price swings and trading liquidity. Cancelling treasury shares reduces the total share count, which also changes the free float.

Price competition and cost volatility

IB Tomato reported that competition from low-priced Chinese products is weighing on margins. Consignment-purchased goods account for nearly half of cost of sales, so margins can shift with purchase terms and supply prices. A structure in which working capital grows with sales also adds pressure on cash flow.

Overall view

Moatech's reported results show sales and profit moving in different directions. Sales for the year ended March 2026 reached KRW 42.6 billion, up for two straight fiscal years, and quarterly sales rose for three consecutive quarters.

Operating losses, however, have persisted for four consecutive fiscal years, and the third-quarter 2026 loss widened from the prior quarter. The turn to KRW 581 million in attributable net income in the third quarter is a positive sign, but the role of non-operating items is still unclear.

On costs, the 89.7% cost of sales ratio and the share of consignment-purchased goods constrain margins. Operating cash outflows widened alongside rising working capital. On the balance sheet, total equity of KRW 68.9 billion and available liquidity of about KRW 38.8 billion provide a cushion.

The 79.17% stake held by the largest shareholder and the decision to cancel treasury shares are factors affecting the shareholder structure and share count.

The next checkpoints are the direction of quarterly operating losses and whether operating cash flow improves, with automotive parts demand and the cost ratio likely to shape this path.
