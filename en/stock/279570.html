Business loans lift profit; asset quality and fee income remain open issues

02

Business structure

Kbank was established in January 2016 as the first internet-only bank in Korea and returned to an annual profit in 2021, after which it has kept improving profitability. Customers reached 16.88 million at the end of the third quarter of 2026, up about 1.91 million from a year earlier.

Deposits stood at KRW 29.39 trillion and loans at KRW 20.58 trillion over the same period. Business loans rose 89% year on year, and their share of total loans widened to 17.7%. More than half of the loan growth this year came from business lending.

In sole-proprietor lending, Kbank accounted for 33.1% of the net increase across the Korean banking sector through the third quarter of 2026. Its guaranteed loan product, launched in 2022, has cumulatively supplied more than KRW 5 trillion, with KRW 2.1 trillion supplied in the first three quarters of 2026 alone.

Non-interest income depends heavily on gains from loan sales, and net fee income remains in deficit. The main comparison points in competition are fellow internet bank KakaoBank and bank units of the financial holding groups.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual operating profit fell 82.1% from KRW 91.9 billion in 2022 to KRW 16.5 billion in 2023, the smallest year in the 2022 to 2025 window. It then rose 707.6% to KRW 133 billion in 2024, the largest year in the window, before declining 11.9% to KRW 117.1 billion in 2025.

Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 83.6 billion in 2022, KRW 12.8 billion in 2023 (down 84.7%), KRW 128.1 billion in 2024 (up 899.5%), and KRW 112.6 billion in 2025 (down 12.1%).

Operating cash flow was -KRW 513.8 billion in 2023, a net outflow, before recovering to KRW 1.85 trillion in 2024 and KRW 616.7 billion in 2025. The debt-to-equity ratio (total liabilities divided by total equity) rose from 825.0% in 2022 to 1,462.4% in 2024 and eased to 1,367.2% in 2025.

At year-end 2025, total equity of KRW 2.17 trillion was entirely attributable to controlling shareholders, against total liabilities of KRW 29.7 trillion.

Quarterly operating profit fell from KRW 20.7 billion in the third quarter of 2025 to KRW 11 billion in the fourth quarter (down 46.9% from the prior quarter), the lowest of the five quarters, then recovered to KRW 32.4 billion in the first quarter of 2026, KRW 27 billion in the second, and KRW 44 billion in the third.

Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 33.2 billion in the first quarter of 2026, KRW 26.9 billion in the second, and KRW 42.1 billion in the third, and the last four quarters combined total KRW 111.3 billion.

Third-quarter 2026 interest income rose 16.7% year on year to KRW 130.1 billion, while non-interest income of KRW 26.9 billion, up 28.3%, included gains on loan sales and securities-related income.

Cumulatively through the third quarter, non-interest income fell 46.8% to KRW 50.2 billion, and higher credit-loss provisions pulled cumulative net profit to KRW 102.1 billion, down 1.3% from a year earlier.

05

Industry analysis

The broad industry trend is a continued household loan management stance alongside a push to make business lending the new growth axis. Kbank alone accounted for 33.1% of the net increase in sole-proprietor lending across the Korean banking sector, showing how far its lending reach has extended.

Deposits were about 3% lower than a year earlier, while funding costs rose, so the deposit side still carries pressure.

At the end of the third quarter, the delinquency ratio stood at 0.62% and the fixed-below-standard loan ratio at 0.60%, both up from the prior quarter, and the credit cost ratio rose 0.4 percentage points to 1.12%.

In household lending, the NPL ratio climbed from 0.54% to 0.63% compared with the same point a year earlier, and delinquency on mid-to-low credit loans rose from 1.89% to 2.00%.

Business loan delinquency, by contrast, fell from 0.62% to 0.55%, which suggests the deterioration is concentrated in household and mid-to-low credit segments. NPL coverage declined from 276.76% to 263.21%.

Shinhan Financial Group, a large peer in the same sector, reported that its cumulative third-quarter interest income rose 7.9% year on year to KRW 9.35 trillion.

06

Outlook

Proceeds from the listing will be used for entry into the SME market, stronger technology differentiation, platform business infrastructure, and new businesses such as digital assets.

Kbank plans to win retail customers through a partnership with Musinsa, and to launch a credit loan service co-underwritten with Naver Pay within 2026. In digital assets, it is pursuing stablecoin-based cross-border remittance and payment infrastructure cooperation centered on Thailand and the UAE.

The company has also outlined building alternative investment products, including virtual assets and gold, alongside stocks and bonds. Capital raised through the listing is to be used to expand its deposit and loan product lines.

Lock-ups were set at one year for the largest shareholder and at three and six months for half each of the major financial investor shares, so the three- and six-month tranches were structured to release in June and September 2026.

The largest shareholder's one-year lock-up runs from the March 2026 listing and therefore expires in early March 2027. Whether the new businesses contribute to reported results will be visible in the 2026 annual report and subsequent quarterly filings.

08

Bull factors

Loan growth led by business lending

The loan balance passed KRW 20 trillion for the first time, reaching KRW 20.58 trillion, and business loans rose 89% year on year. Business lending accounted for more than half of this year's loan growth and 17.7% of total loans.

Kbank's 33.1% share of the sector's net sole-proprietor lending increase is cited as evidence that the growth base has widened.

Interest income and margin improvement

Third-quarter 2026 interest income rose 16.7% year on year to KRW 130.1 billion. Cumulative net interest margin reached 1.60%, up 0.22 percentage points from 1.38% a year earlier. Interest income improved even as funding costs rose, helped by lower interest paid on customer deposits.

Quarterly profit recovery and added capital

Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders reached KRW 42.1 billion in the third quarter of 2026, up 119.2% year on year, after dipping to KRW 26.9 billion in the second quarter. The March 2026 KOSPI listing added capital, with proceeds planned for SME lending and platform and digital asset businesses. The last four quarters combined total KRW 111.3 billion.

09

Bear factors

Lower non-interest income and fee deficit

Cumulative non-interest income fell 46.8% to KRW 50.2 billion from KRW 94.4 billion a year earlier. Most of the decline came from smaller gains on loan sales, which dropped 40.3% to KRW 50.1 billion from KRW 84 billion.

Net fee income remained in deficit at about KRW 2.3 billion, and the quarterly fee deficit widened from about KRW 0.4 billion to KRW 1.4 billion.

Asset quality indicators weaken

The delinquency ratio rose to 0.62% at the end of the third quarter, up 0.02 percentage points from the prior quarter, and the credit cost ratio rose 0.4 percentage points to 1.12%.

The household NPL ratio climbed from 0.54% to 0.63% against the same point a year earlier, and delinquency on mid-to-low credit loans rose to 2.00%.

Deposit decline and shareholder supply overhang

Deposits at the end of the third quarter were about 3% lower than a year earlier, while funding costs rose. Lock-up releases after the listing could bring shares to market in stages. Before the listing, market commentary cited Upbit dependence and the mix of institutional lock-up commitments as burdens.

10

Risk factors

Credit risk

Delinquency on mid-to-low credit loans and the household NPL ratio both rose, which could raise credit costs further. Loan-loss provisions increased but did not keep pace with NPL growth, so coverage declined. Lower business loan delinquency offers a partial buffer.

Earnings mix and policy risk

Because non-interest income leans on gains from loan sales, changes in sale conditions could widen earnings swings. The shift toward business lending must be read against the ongoing household loan management stance.

Market commentary also cited a KRW 110 billion compensation burden linked to the largest shareholder's related business.

Supply overhang and concentration

Lock-up release schedules leave a staged supply overhang after the listing. Dependence on Upbit was raised as a burden during the IPO process. With deposits lower than a year earlier, diversifying the funding base remains a task.

12

Overall view

Kbank's annual operating profit peaked at KRW 133 billion in 2024, fell to KRW 117.1 billion in 2025, and quarterly profit has recovered through 2026. The recovery rests mainly on higher interest income from business and sole-proprietor lending.

However, cumulative non-interest income fell 46.8%, net fee income remains in deficit, and smaller gains on loan sales are the main reason. Asset quality indicators weakened from the prior quarter, with the delinquency ratio at 0.62% and the credit cost ratio at 1.12%, and coverage declined.

On the shareholder side, the March 2026 listing added capital, and the lock-up release in early March 2027 is a remaining supply variable. Deposits fell year on year while funding costs rose, which bears on how durable interest income growth will be.

The next checkpoints are the full-year operating profit and debt-to-equity ratio in the 2026 annual report, and the delinquency trend of the credit loan service scheduled to launch within 2026.
