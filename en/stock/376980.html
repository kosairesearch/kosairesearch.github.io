Report overview

AX Growth Rises as the Core Recruiting Business Lags

Wanted Lab is widening its revenue base toward enterprise AX services, but a third-quarter 2026 operating loss and a weaker recruiting core remain the key items to watch.

  2. 2

  3. 3

02

Business structure

Wanted Lab is an AI-based HR tech company whose core is the Wanted recruiting platform, which links job seekers and employers. The company says the platform connects about 4 million job seekers and 40,000 employers.

Its recruiting business added a flat-fee subscription model in 2025 alongside the earlier per-hire fee model, and under the subscription revenue is recognized over the contract period.

The new business centers on AX (AI transformation) services built around Enoia, an enterprise AI agent platform, covering change-management programs, AI agent development and enterprise platform construction.

The company says more than 1,200 agents are running on Enoia, including about 200 used internally, and that affiliates of two large corporate groups have adopted it. Its education business runs the AX Champion program for AI talent and AI training for public institutions and universities.

A ZDNet Korea analysis of the quarterly report shows AI-related recruiting fell from 78.13% of total revenue in 2024 to 60.71% in the third quarter of 2026, while the 'other' category, which includes financial investment income, rose to 18.21%.

The investment arm runs a KRW 90 billion AX investment business through its subsidiary Wanted Lab Partners. On the client side, the company says Palantir, Upstage and Hyundai Autoever also recruit talent through Wanted.

Overseas, the company is working with Japanese IT talent platform LAPRAS, yet Wanted Japan's cumulative revenue for the first three quarters of 2026 was KRW 35.99 million, or 0.12% of the total.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual revenue peaked at KRW 50.3 billion in 2022, then fell to KRW 39.7 billion in 2023, down 21.1% year on year, and to KRW 36.7 billion in 2024, down 7.4%. It recovered to KRW 38.2 billion in 2025, up 3.9%. Among 2022 to 2025, 2022 was the largest year and 2024 the smallest.

Operating profit dropped from KRW 8.95 billion in 2022 to KRW 1.57 billion in 2023, down 82.4%, and the company posted an operating loss of KRW 818 million in 2024, a turn into loss. Operating profit of KRW 1.02 billion in 2025 was a turn back to profit.

Operating margin declined from 17.8% in 2022 to 4.0% in 2023, -2.2% in 2024 and 2.7% in 2025. The company attributed the 2024 loss to continued investment in its overseas subsidiary Wanted Lab Japan and its investment subsidiary Wanted Lab Partners, which generated subsidiary-level operating losses.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 9.44 billion in 2022, KRW 1.4 billion in 2023 (down 85.1%), a net loss of KRW 1.23 billion in 2024, and KRW 1.28 billion in 2025, a turn back to profit. Net income including non-controlling interests was KRW 1.28 billion in 2025.

Operating cash flow fell from KRW 12.5 billion in 2022 to KRW 2.58 billion in 2025, so cash generation remains below the 2022 level even after the return to profit. The debt ratio rose from 32.4% in 2022 to 59.8% in 2025, when total liabilities were KRW 23 billion and total equity KRW 38.5 billion.

Quarterly revenue was KRW 9.66 billion in the third quarter of 2025, KRW 10.9 billion in the fourth quarter (up 13.0% quarter on quarter), KRW 8.9 billion in the first quarter of 2026 (down 18.5%), KRW 11.7 billion in the second quarter (up 31.8%) and KRW 9.92 billion in the third quarter (down 15.4%).

Operating profit was KRW 1.17 billion in the fourth quarter of 2025, an operating loss of KRW 983 million in the first quarter of 2026, operating profit of KRW 1.22 billion in the second quarter and an operating loss of KRW 131 million in the third quarter.

The second-quarter profit came alongside a rebound in revenue, while in the third quarter revenue rose 2.8% year on year but operating expenses rose 10.2%, according to ddaily's analysis of the filing.

Fair-value losses and disposal losses on financial assets of KRW 1.216 billion were charged to operating expenses in the third quarter.

For the first nine months, financial investment operations earned KRW 746 million in operating profit, which offset KRW 643 million of operating losses in domestic and overseas paid job placement. Net income attributable to controlling shareholders over the last four quarters totaled KRW 1.06 billion.

05

Industry analysis

The center of hiring demand is shifting toward AI talent. According to the company, AX-related job postings on the Wanted platform rose 88% year on year in the third quarter of 2026 and accounted for 36% of all postings.

Postings for forward-deployed AI engineers (FDE) rose to 14.5 times the prior-year level for January to September 2026.

Government support is also expanding, with a report in August 2026 stating that this year's government AI budget is KRW 9.9 trillion, of which about KRW 3.8 trillion is allocated to AI talent development and industrial AX support.

Overall hiring activity, however, has moved with corporate caution: the company said in its 2024 results that conservative hiring sentiment persisted, and a ZDNet Korea analysis pointed to a slowdown in startups as a warning sign for collections in the core business.

On competition, Wanted Lab presents its mix of an AI-matching recruiting platform and an enterprise agent platform as differentiated, but in the enterprise AI transformation market it competes for the same clients as large IT services companies. Overseas, it is targeting the Japanese HR market, which the company says is about 20 times the size of Korea's.

06

Outlook

In its second-quarter 2026 results announcement in August 2026, the company said it aims to raise the share of global and AX businesses to about 50% of total revenue by the fourth quarter of 2026.

The AX order backlog grew to KRW 8 billion at the end of the third quarter of 2026, and the company said new orders came in across all three AX business areas. It also plans to shift its recruiting business toward an 'AI search firm' model in which AI agents run the full hiring process.

In education, AI-related revenue rose 18% quarter on quarter in the second quarter of 2026, and public institutions such as the Korea Tourism Organization and the Korea International Trade Association, along with several universities, adopted Wanted's AI training.

Enoia has been deployed at affiliates of two large corporate groups. ZDNet Korea, however, pointed out that Enoia's standalone revenue is not separately disclosed, which makes its AX contribution hard to isolate from outside.

The company says it is strengthening its AX business in line with the direction of government AI budgets. Whether the 50% target is met can be checked against segment revenue and backlog changes in the 2026 annual report. How far the recruiting core can offset AX-related costs will likely be a key variable for earnings after 2027.

08

Bull factors

Rising AX revenue and order backlog

According to the company, third-quarter 2026 AX revenue reached KRW 2.47 billion, up 37% from the prior quarter, and accounted for 29% of revenue excluding investment income. The backlog stands at KRW 8 billion, and more than 1,200 agents run on Enoia.

On the quarterly report basis, AX business revenue for January to September 2026 was KRW 6.44 billion, or 21.1% of cumulative consolidated revenue.

Growing demand for AI talent hiring

AX-related job postings on the Wanted platform rose 88% year on year in the third quarter of 2026 and made up 36% of all postings. The company says AI-native talent accounted for 40% of total hires in September. Palantir, Upstage and Hyundai Autoever are also recruiting talent through Wanted. The government's KRW 9.9 trillion AI budget for 2026 is cited as a supporting backdrop for this demand.

Return to profit and cost efficiency

Operating profit of KRW 1.02 billion in 2025 marked a turn back to profit, and the second quarter of 2026 returned to operating profit of KRW 1.22 billion. Revenue for that quarter rose 31.8% from the prior quarter to KRW 11.7 billion.

The company says deploying about 200 internal AI agents cut third-quarter 2026 costs 4.4% from the prior quarter.

09

Bear factors

Contraction in the core recruiting business

A ZDNet Korea analysis of the quarterly report found that third-quarter 2026 standalone core revenue was KRW 8.51 billion, down 11.2% year on year. AI-related recruiting fell from 78.13% of total revenue in 2024 to 60.71% in the third quarter of 2026. Although the company stresses AX growth, the analysis argues that the decline in recruiting revenue was larger.

Third-quarter loss and fixed cost pressure

Third-quarter 2026 operating expenses rose 10.2% year on year, and fair-value and disposal losses on financial assets of KRW 1.22 billion were charged to operating expenses. Right-of-use asset depreciation reached KRW 1.86 billion for January to September 2026, up 75.5% year on year, adding to fixed costs.

Wanted Japan's cumulative net result for the first three quarters of 2026 swung from a profit a year earlier to a loss of KRW 156.2 million.

Reliance on investments and asset quality

According to a ZDNet Korea analysis, investment assets, combining short- and long-term investment securities and equity-method investments, equal 39.6% of total assets. The KRW 18 billion in investment securities is entirely Level 3 assets without an active market price.

The allowance for doubtful accounts rose to 31.49% of receivables at the end of the third quarter of 2026 from 19.37% at the end of 2025. Short-term deposits fell from KRW 13 billion at the end of 2025 to KRW 6 billion.

10

Risk factors

Investment asset valuation risk

Investment assets make up a large share of total assets, and some are unlisted assets valued on internal estimates. Fair-value and disposal losses on financial assets flow into operating expenses, increasing quarterly earnings volatility.

Continued capital calls to venture investment funds could enlarge both the asset base and the valuation burden. Whether valuation gains or losses mask the core business's results should be separated quarter by quarter.

How the AX metrics are compiled

The company's AX revenue share is 29% excluding investment revenue, while the quarterly-report basis gives 21.1% for January to September 2026, so the two measures differ. ZDNet Korea notes that Enoia's standalone revenue is not separately disclosed.

The same analysis argues that government-supported career education and freelance matching revenue are included in the AX total.

Hiring cycle and receivable collection

Recruiting revenue is sensitive to corporate hiring sentiment and the startup environment. At the end of the third quarter of 2026, receivables overdue by more than one year totaled KRW 470 million, or 19.6% of the receivable balance.

Overseas revenue is still small, with Wanted Japan's cumulative revenue for the first three quarters of 2026 at KRW 35.99 million, so its contribution to earnings remains limited until it scales.

What to watch next

  1. Late March 2027 (the 2025 annual results were announced on March 23, 2026)

    The 2026 annual report will show fourth-quarter 2026 results and full-year operating margin. The same filing will show how close the AX and global share came to the company's 50% target.

  2. As of December 31, 2026 (fiscal year-end)

    Check whether the KRW 8 billion AX order backlog from the end of the third quarter of 2026 was maintained or grew by year-end, using IR materials and the annual report. The pace at which backlog converts into revenue indicates the quality of the AX business.

  3. Late March 2027 (annual report notes)

    Review the valuation of Level 3 investment securities, the full-year total of fair-value and disposal gains or losses on financial assets, and the share of investment assets in total assets. The key question is whether these items can be read separately from core operating results.

  4. Around the 2027 annual general meeting (last year's notice was issued on March 23, 2026)

    Check whether the KRW 998 million in share buybacks made in the first three quarters of 2026 lead to cancellation and whether a shareholder return policy is disclosed. The absence of a cancellation plan was raised in a third-quarter 2026 analysis.

12

Overall view

Wanted Lab is widening its business from a recruiting platform into an AI transformation (AX) services company.

It returned to profit in 2025 with operating profit of KRW 1.02 billion and posted operating profit of KRW 1.22 billion in the second quarter of 2026, but the third quarter swung to an operating loss of KRW 131 million.

AX revenue rose to KRW 2.47 billion in the third quarter and the order backlog reached KRW 8 billion, while the growth in AX-related job postings is cited as evidence of demand.

On the other side, the decline in standalone core revenue, the falling share of AI-related recruiting, investment assets equal to 39.6% of total assets, and a higher allowance for doubtful accounts at the end of the third quarter are open issues for the core business and asset quality.

Operating profit moved from a loss in 2024 to a profit in 2025 but remains below the 2022 level, and operating cash flow of KRW 2.58 billion in 2025 is also lower than in 2022. Net income attributable to controlling shareholders over the last four quarters totaled KRW 1.06 billion, a thin base.

The next checkpoint is how the AX and global revenue share and recruiting segment revenue appear in the 2026 annual report.
