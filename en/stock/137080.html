Narae Nanotech: Display Coater Turnaround Meets New Businesses

Narae Nanotech narrowed its operating loss in 2025 and kept operating profit positive through the first three quarters of 2026. Net earnings over the same period, however, were lifted substantially by one-off gains from a land sale, so operating and net results need to be read separately.

02

Business structure

Narae Nanotech was founded in 1995 as a maker of display manufacturing equipment and listed on KOSDAQ in 2022. Its core product is coater equipment used in LCD and OLED panel processes, with photoresist coaters as the main line.

Building on this coating technology, the company is extending into lithium-ion electrode coating and drying equipment, semiconductor panel-level packaging (PLP) equipment, and perovskite solar cell coating and drying equipment.

In the first quarter of 2026, display made up KRW 14.62 billion of KRW 17.3 billion in revenue, batteries KRW 1.9 billion, semiconductors KRW 62 million and new businesses KRW 11 million.

Display remains the dominant revenue source, but on an order-backlog basis new businesses such as semiconductors and batteries accounted for 55% at the end of the third quarter of 2026, according to a company official. Exports were KRW 15.53 billion, or 89.9% of first-quarter revenue.

The largest customer, Customer A, accounted for 55% of that quarter's revenue and Customer B for 24.07%. On the display side, the company's customers include panel makers in Korea and China's BOE and HKC, which are expanding OLED and LCD capacity.

In batteries, the company points to its proprietary xenon lamp drying technology as a differentiator. The company says it supplies PLP coating and drying equipment on an exclusive basis to leading overseas firms and is pursuing development of Through Glass Via (TGV) equipment.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual revenue fell from KRW 88 billion in 2022 to KRW 52.9 billion in 2023 (down 39.9% year on year) and KRW 40.4 billion in 2024 (down 23.6%), then rebounded to KRW 54.5 billion in 2025 (up 34.8%). Among 2022 to 2025, 2022 was the largest year and 2024 the smallest.

Operating profit of KRW 1.96 billion in 2022 turned into an operating loss of KRW 15 billion in 2023, and the loss widened to KRW 22.1 billion in 2024. In 2025 the operating loss narrowed to KRW 8.84 billion, and the company recorded losses for three consecutive years from 2023 through 2025.

The company attributed the narrower 2025 loss to higher orders and sales and lower fixed costs. Controlling-shareholder net income swung to KRW 40 billion in 2025 from a loss of KRW 17.1 billion in 2024, and the gain was driven more by non-operating income from the sale of a principal asset than by operations.

The land was transferred for about KRW 105.3 billion between late 2025 and early 2026. Operating cash flow went from negative figures in 2022 through 2024 to positive KRW 13.7 billion in 2025, and the debt ratio fell from 126.9% in 2024 to 70.8% in 2025.

Quarterly operating results moved from an operating loss of KRW 2.05 billion in the third quarter of 2025 to operating profit of KRW 356 million in the fourth quarter, then KRW 660 million, KRW 3 billion and KRW 649 million in the first three quarters of 2026.

Revenue went from KRW 15.8 billion in the fourth quarter of 2025 and KRW 20.7 billion in the second quarter of 2026 to KRW 13.4 billion in the third quarter.

The trailing four-quarter controlling-shareholder net income total of KRW 64.8 billion includes land-related gains in the fourth quarter of 2025 (KRW 49.9 billion) and the first quarter of 2026 (KRW 13.2 billion), so it should be read apart from the operating trend.

05

Industry analysis

Demand for the company's main display equipment is tied to investment in IT OLED and the expansion of automotive displays. In China, BOE is adding OLED capacity in Chengdu and HKC is expanding LCD capacity in Sichuan, which supports a steady flow of equipment orders.

On the semiconductor side, the market backdrop is demand for AI accelerators and high-bandwidth memory (HBM) that continues to outpace supply. The company is scaling its PLP coating and drying equipment in line with this trend, but semiconductors were still only 0.36% of first-quarter 2026 revenue.

Batteries face the question of when the market will recover, and the CEO has said that xenon lamp rapid drying equipment would add to results once that recovery arrives. Perovskite solar coating and drying equipment has been cited in connection with exports to the United States.

Competitively, the company is moving coating technology from displays into semiconductors, solar and batteries, so both its existing equipment competitiveness and the speed of entry into new fields are relevant.

Because display still accounts for 84.63% of revenue, the business remains sensitive to panel makers' capital spending cycles. The 89.9% export share and customer concentration should be read in the same context.

06

Outlook

In a July 26, 2026 interview, the CEO said display equipment results would be joined by semiconductor and solar equipment contributions, and he expected 2026 growth of more than 50% over the prior year.

Cumulative revenue for the first three quarters of 2026 was reported at KRW 51.4 billion and operating profit at KRW 4.3 billion. Reaching growth of more than 50% would require fourth-quarter 2026 revenue to rise considerably above the third-quarter level, so fourth-quarter results are the key check on that outlook.

As of the first-quarter 2026 report, total order volume was KRW 209.23 billion and the order backlog was KRW 38.18 billion.

On September 22, 2026 the company disclosed a confirmed display manufacturing equipment supply contract worth KRW 6.04 billion, with a contract period ending December 14, 2026, and the disclosure notes that the end date was changed by mutual agreement.

A semiconductor manufacturing equipment contract worth KRW 4.30 billion was signed in January 2026. The PLP business is supplied exclusively to overseas firms, and TGV equipment development and a Through Silicon Via (TSV) equipment launch are planned.

For batteries, the CEO said talks were under way in the second half of 2026 to supply rapid drying equipment to a global battery maker. Whether those talks produce an order by the end of 2026 is the next checkpoint for the new-business expansion.

Bull factors

Higher share of new-business orders

According to a company official, new businesses such as semiconductors and batteries accounted for 55% of the order backlog at the end of the third quarter of 2026, diversifying a display-centered structure. The company says it supplies PLP equipment exclusively to leading overseas firms. A September 2026 display supply contract adds to the order flow.

Operating profit held positive

Operating profit turned positive in the fourth quarter of 2025 and stayed positive in each of the first three quarters of 2026. The 2025 operating loss narrowed to KRW 8.84 billion, and the shift to profit in 2026 is the basis for the improvement. Cumulative operating profit for the first three quarters of 2026 was reported at KRW 4.3 billion.

Stronger balance sheet

The debt ratio fell from 126.9% in 2024 to 70.8% in 2025, and total equity at the end of 2025 was KRW 116.3 billion. Operating cash flow turned positive at KRW 13.7 billion in 2025. Reports say land sale proceeds also strengthened liquidity.

09

Bear factors

Quality of net earnings

Net income of KRW 40 billion in 2025, both total and controlling-shareholder, was affected by non-operating income from the land sale. In the third quarter of 2026, operating profit of KRW 649 million was accompanied by a controlling-shareholder net loss of KRW 1.01 billion. Net income figures alone make it hard to judge underlying operating strength.

Customer and export concentration

In the first quarter of 2026, the largest customer, Customer A, accounted for 55% of revenue and exports for 89.9%. Display made up 84.63% of revenue, leaving the company sensitive to panel makers' capital spending. A change in orders from one or two customers could move quarterly results sharply.

Quarterly volatility

Third-quarter 2026 revenue of KRW 13.4 billion fell 35.2% from the prior quarter. Operating losses ran from 2023 through 2025, and profit so far covers only the first three quarters of 2026. Whether the profit trend continues will need confirmation in upcoming periodic reports.

10

Risk factors

Customer and export concentration risk

A 55% share for the largest customer and 89.9% exports mean results could depend heavily on one customer's capex timing or overseas policy. Delays in Chinese panel capacity expansion could reduce display orders. How quickly the KRW 38.18 billion order backlog converts into quarterly revenue is the key variable.

One-off gains and interpretation

Controlling-shareholder net income of KRW 49.9 billion in the fourth quarter of 2025 reflects large non-operating gains from the land sale. Such gains are unlikely to recur, so they must be separated from operating results. The third-quarter 2026 net loss, despite operating profit, signals high volatility in net earnings.

Timing of new-business commercialization

TGV equipment, xenon lamp rapid drying equipment for batteries and TSV equipment are reported to be at development or negotiation stages, so the timing of revenue contribution is uncertain. The CEO linked battery results to a market recovery after 2026, which points to 2027 and later. Near-term contribution may be limited.

12

Overall view

Narae Nanotech posted operating losses from 2023 through 2025 but narrowed the loss in 2025, and it has shown operating profit across the first three quarters of 2026.

A positive factor is that the strategy of extending display coating technology into semiconductor, battery and solar equipment is showing up in the order backlog. On the other side, display makes up 84.63% of revenue, the largest customer accounts for 55% and exports for 89.9%, so concentration risk is high.

Net earnings in 2025 and controlling-shareholder net income in the fourth quarter of 2025 and first quarter of 2026 were substantially lifted by non-operating gains from the land sale, so they should be read apart from operating results.

The third quarter of 2026 showed operating profit alongside a controlling-shareholder net loss of KRW 1.01 billion, which also highlights earnings volatility.

In July 2026 the CEO expected 2026 revenue to grow by more than 50% over 2025, but the fourth-quarter results and the 2026 annual report needed to support that have not yet been released.

The next checkpoints are the display equipment contract whose period ends on December 14, 2026, the outcome of battery equipment supply talks in the second half of 2026, and the filing of the 2026 annual business report by March 31, 2027.
