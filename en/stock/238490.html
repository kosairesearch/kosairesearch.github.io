Hims: Five Loss-Making Quarters Put Its OLED Equipment Base Under Review

Hims is built around OLED process equipment, and with losses in each of the last five reported quarters, its next annual report is the key test of whether operating losses narrow.

  3. 3

  4. 4

02

Business structure

Hims was founded in 1999 and listed on KOSDAQ in July 2017, and it is headquartered in Incheon. Its core business is OLED process equipment, centered on divided metal mask tools used in OLED deposition, and a 2020 technical report described mask stretchers and mask inspection systems among its main products.

An Alphasquare sales breakdown also shows OLED equipment products as the largest revenue category, with parts and services and non-OLED equipment making up the rest.

Non-OLED products include blue-filter inspection machines for smartphone cameras, pellicle inspection equipment for semiconductors, blood-glucose test sheet inspectors and vision modules.

The 2020 report also described an OLED mask stretcher supply relationship with Samsung Display, a description tied to that date rather than the present. WiseReport says the company is diversifying into semiconductor back-end inspection equipment and micro LED-related tools.

A March 2026 disclosure described a semiconductor equipment supply contract worth KRW 7.49 billion with a domestic counterparty, running from March 17 to May 31, 2026, equal to 12.2% of recent sales as stated in the filing.

WiseReport also notes that competition has intensified as Chinese manufacturers enter the market and their share of small and mid-sized OLED panels grows. Revenue depends heavily on the capital spending plans of display makers, so order timing drives sizable quarterly swings.

04

Earnings analysis

On a filed basis, sales rose from KRW 31.5 billion in 2022 to KRW 50.1 billion in 2023, an increase of 58.9%, and reached KRW 61.3 billion in 2024, up 22.3% and the largest year in 2022–2025. Sales then fell 3.3% to KRW 59.3 billion in 2025.

Operating results moved from an operating loss of KRW 13.3 billion in 2022 (operating margin -42.1%) to an operating profit of KRW 5.69 billion in 2023 (operating margin 11.3%), a swing back to profit.

In 2024 operating profit shrank 97.0% to KRW 169 million, a 0.3% margin, and in 2025 the company returned to an operating loss of KRW 7.53 billion, a -12.7% margin.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders turned to KRW 5.6 billion in 2023 before falling 51.6% to KRW 2.71 billion in 2024, and in 2025 it became a net loss of KRW 12.5 billion. Total net loss including non-controlling interests was also KRW 12.5 billion in 2025.

The debt-to-equity ratio rose from 22.6% in 2024 to 53.3% in 2025, with total liabilities of KRW 31 billion and total equity of KRW 58.2 billion at the end of 2025. Operating cash flow was positive at KRW 11.7 billion in 2023 and KRW 7.58 billion in 2024, then turned to an outflow of KRW 14.5 billion in 2025.

Quarterly, the third quarter of 2025 opened with sales of KRW 17 billion and an operating loss of KRW 249 million, and the fourth quarter widened the operating loss to KRW 4.69 billion with a net loss attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 11.2 billion.

In the first quarter of 2026 sales rose 5.2% to KRW 18.2 billion and the operating loss narrowed to KRW 1.85 billion, but second-quarter sales fell 40.4% to KRW 10.8 billion with an operating loss of KRW 3.36 billion.

Third-quarter 2026 sales were KRW 11 billion, up 1.7% from the prior quarter but down 35.2% year on year, and the operating loss widened to KRW 7.28 billion. Net losses attributable to controlling shareholders over the last four quarters total KRW 21.8 billion.

WiseReport noted that sales in the first half of 2025 on a separate basis fell 21.5% and operating profit turned to a loss, attributing the trend to intensifying competition from Chinese makers.

05

Industry analysis

Hims has a heavy OLED equipment weighting, so it is directly exposed to display capital spending cycles. A 2020 technical report attributed equipment market growth to OLED capacity expansions by Samsung Display, LG Display and Chinese-region makers.

WiseReport later pointed to Chinese manufacturers entering the market and rising share in small and mid-sized OLED panels as sources of intensified competition.

In an Alphasquare comparison on an annual basis against the display equipment and parts sector, Hims' sales of KRW 59.2 billion fell short of the sector average of KRW 509.9 billion.

The same comparison showed an operating margin of -12.71% against a sector average of -4.69%, and a debt ratio of 53.33% well below the sector average.

On the semiconductor side, the March 2026 contract of KRW 7.49 billion is the one confirmed order of that kind, and by disclosed value it is a partial share of total sales. Semiconductor back-end inspection equipment and micro LED-related tools are the stated routes to broaden the portfolio.

Compared with other semiconductor-classified listed companies, Hims has a larger display equipment weighting, so its business mix differs. The main swing factors for the sector are the direction of mid-sized OLED investment and the competitive landscape with China, and both connect directly to Hims' order flow.

Against that, the third quarter of 2026 showed sales of KRW 11 billion and an operating loss of KRW 7.28 billion, so the cost structure and order flow need to be read together. The one confirmed order in 2026 is the March contract worth KRW 7.49 billion, which ran through May 31, 2026.

Whether further order disclosures follow that contract is the next point to watch. The company also needs to show that its semiconductor back-end inspection and micro LED-related tools turn into revenue.

Demand for its core OLED tools can shift with the Chinese competitive landscape and mid-sized OLED investment direction. On the financial side, the 2025 operating cash outflow of KRW 14.5 billion and the 53.3% debt ratio need to be tracked through the 2026 annual report. Whether the fourth quarter of 2026 returns to profit is not yet confirmed by any regular filing.

Bull factors

Trading below net asset value

The market capitalization sits below that net asset base, and an Alphasquare comparison also shows a price-to-book ratio below the sector average. A large asset base could support a recovery if operations return to profit.

Track record of a 2023 return to profit

The company moved from an operating loss of KRW 13.3 billion in 2022 to an operating profit of KRW 5.69 billion in 2023. The operating margin that year was 11.3%. It is a past case in which an order recovery turned the equipment business back to positive earnings.

Expansion attempts into semiconductor equipment

A March 2026 contract worth KRW 7.49 billion equals 12.2% of recent sales as stated in the filing. The company is developing semiconductor back-end inspection tools and micro LED-related equipment. Those lines are cited as a way to reduce dependence on OLED demand alone.

Bear factors

Five consecutive loss-making quarters

Operating losses widened from KRW 249 million in the third quarter of 2025 to KRW 7.28 billion in the third quarter of 2026. Net loss attributable to controlling shareholders in the third quarter of 2026 was KRW 6.87 billion, after a profit in the same quarter of the prior year. The four-quarter net loss total of KRW 21.8 billion equals about 37% of equity at the end of 2025.

Chinese competition and pressure in OLED

WiseReport described intensifying competition as Chinese manufacturers entered the market and mid-sized OLED share rose. Separate-basis sales in the first half of 2025 fell 21.5%. Because core revenue is tied to OLED investment, competition can hit pricing and orders at the same time.

Weaker cash flow and rising debt

Operating cash flow swung from KRW 7.58 billion in 2024 to an outflow of KRW 14.5 billion in 2025. The debt ratio rose from 22.6% to 53.3%, with total liabilities of KRW 31 billion. Third-quarter 2026 sales fell 35.2% year on year, which weakens the capacity to generate cash.

Financial liquidity risk

Operating cash flow was an outflow of KRW 14.5 billion in 2025, and the debt ratio is 53.3%. Total liabilities at the end of 2025 were KRW 31 billion. If losses continue, the pace of cash burn and borrowing capacity become the next items to check.

Quarterly earnings volatility

Second-quarter 2026 sales fell 40.4% from the prior quarter, and third-quarter sales fell 35.2% year on year. Because quarterly revenue moves with order timing, earnings visibility is limited. The operating loss has ranged from KRW 249 million to KRW 7.28 billion across the last five quarters.

Overall view

Hims is built around OLED process equipment, and its filed results moved from a profit in 2023 to losses in 2025. Sales peaked at KRW 61.3 billion in 2024, the largest year in the 2022–2025 period, but operating profit was only KRW 169 million that year.

In 2025 the company recorded an operating loss of KRW 7.53 billion and a net loss attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 12.5 billion. Losses have run for five consecutive quarters since the third quarter of 2025, and the four-quarter net loss total is KRW 21.8 billion.

On the financial side, year-end 2025 total equity was KRW 58.2 billion, total liabilities KRW 31 billion, and the debt ratio 53.3%, with operating cash flow at an outflow of KRW 14.5 billion.

The share price sits at a discount to net asset value and to the sector average, but with losses no earnings-based multiple can be calculated. Strengths include the size of the asset base, the 2023 return to profit, and the attempt to expand into semiconductor equipment.

Weaknesses include continuing quarterly losses, pressure in OLED equipment from Chinese competition, and weaker cash flow. The next checkpoints are the fourth-quarter 2026 results, the 2026 annual report, and the first-quarter 2027 report, to see whether the operating loss narrows.
