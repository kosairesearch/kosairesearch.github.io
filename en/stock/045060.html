02

Business structure

Okong is a KOSDAQ-listed chemical company that makes adhesives and adhesive tapes. It listed on KOSDAQ in August 2000, and its business centers on manufacturing and selling adhesives. Its main product lines are water-based adhesives, hot-melt adhesives, solvent-based adhesives, and OPP adhesive tapes.

Demand comes mainly from construction and interior markets and from packaging, and the downturn in domestic construction affects the company's results through the related industries.

In the first quarter of 2026, growth in e-commerce lifted demand for packaging tapes, and wider interior-market activity lifted demand for DIY adhesives, which the company cited as the reasons for the improvement. The company emphasizes eco-friendly adhesive development and keeps obtaining environmental certifications.

On the automotive parts side, it obtained IATF16949 certification to widen its capacity to serve the mobility industry. Its business summary presents the shift toward high-performance products as a source of competitiveness.

Based on the parent-only financial statements disclosed in March 2026, 2025 revenue was KRW 55.3 billion and operating profit was KRW 1.42 billion. The gap between consolidated and parent-only results reflects subsidiary earnings included in consolidation.

Among listed chemical companies, its business structure and scale differ sharply from large petrochemical groups such as LG Chem and Lotte Chemical. Product-line revenue shares are an item to verify in the business report's segment information.

04

Earnings analysis

On the basis of the disclosed annual results, revenue fell from KRW 170.4 billion in 2022 to KRW 158.4 billion in 2023, a decrease of 7.0% year on year, recovered to KRW 163 billion in 2024, an increase of 2.9%, and then declined to KRW 160.1 billion in 2025, a decrease of 1.8%.

Among 2022 to 2025, 2022 was the largest year for revenue and 2023 the smallest.

Operating profit rose from KRW 8.61 billion in 2022 to KRW 10.2 billion in 2023, an increase of 18.3%, and then fell for two consecutive years, to KRW 8.64 billion in 2024, a decrease of 15.1%, and to KRW 5.62 billion in 2025, a decrease of 34.9%.

The operating margin followed the same path, at 5.1% in 2022, 6.4% in 2023, 5.3% in 2024, and 3.5% in 2025. Net income attributable to controlling shareholders went from KRW 8.88 billion in 2023, up 18.8%, to KRW 7.94 billion in 2024, down 10.6%, and to KRW 5.58 billion in 2025, down 29.8%.

Net income including non-controlling interests was KRW 5.62 billion in 2025, a separate figure from the controlling-shareholder number. The company attributed the 2025 operating profit decline to a downturn in domestic construction and price volatility caused by overheating in the domestic supply market.

Quarterly operating profit was KRW 1.76 billion in the third quarter of 2025, KRW 1.69 billion in the fourth quarter, and KRW 1.63 billion in the first quarter of 2026, a narrow range, before reaching KRW 4.94 billion in the second quarter of 2026, up 203.5% from the prior quarter.

Revenue in the third quarter of 2026 was KRW 39 billion, down 13.9% from the prior quarter, and the company posted an operating loss of KRW 300 million, a swing into loss.

Over the last four quarters, from the fourth quarter of 2025 to the third quarter of 2026, net income attributable to controlling shareholders totaled KRW 7.37 billion.

Industry analysis

Demand for adhesives spans construction and interiors, packaging, and automotive parts, so it is not tied to the cycle of any single industry. The company itself cited the downturn in domestic construction as a reason for the 2025 decline.

Over the same period, price volatility caused by overheating in the domestic supply market was cited as a factor pressuring margins. In the first quarter of 2026, by contrast, growth in e-commerce lifted demand for packaging tapes, and wider interior-market activity lifted demand for DIY adhesives.

On the automotive side, the company widened its capacity to serve the mobility industry by obtaining IATF16949 certification. The shift toward high-performance adhesives for electronics and electrical uses is also presented as a business direction.

Among sector peers, large chemical groups such as Kumho Petro Chemical and Hanwha Solutions have different business structures, which makes direct comparison difficult.

Against adhesive-focused small and mid-sized chemical companies, the relevant benchmarks are the pace of customer diversification and the status of certifications. The cyclical position depends on whether construction orders and consumer packaging demand move in the same direction.
