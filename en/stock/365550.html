Interest Costs Weigh as Supply Crunch Builds

02

Business structure

ESR Kendall Square REIT is Korea's first listed logistics-focused REIT, having listed on the KOSPI in December 2020. The parent of its asset management company, Kendall Square REIT Management, is ESR Kendall Square, an affiliate of ESR Group, Asia's largest logistics real estate platform.

The portfolio currently comprises 21 logistics centers with a combined acquisition cost of KRW 2.6802 trillion, while the most recent appraised value is estimated at KRW 3.0306 trillion, implying an increase of more than 13% over acquisition cost.

This acquisition cost ranks second among Korea's 25 listed REITs, behind SK Reit. Analysis has pointed to Coupang accounting for more than half of the tenant base, with the remainder filled by domestic and international third-party logistics (3PL) operators.

Portfolio occupancy has been reported to be maintained above 98%.

Rather than acquiring one large asset at a time, the company has pursued steady accumulation of small-to-mid-sized properties, and in 2025 it deployed KRW 134.1 billion raised through an equity issuance toward the Cheonan-1 logistics center acquisition and debt repayment.

Compared with diversified peers such as SK Reit and Lotte Reit, the company is viewed as a pure-play logistics REIT.

04

Earnings analysis

ESR Kendall Square REIT's revenue rose for a third consecutive year, from KRW 49 billion in 2022 to KRW 53 billion in 2023 (up 8.1% year on year), KRW 56.9 billion in 2024 (up 7.4%), and KRW 59.6 billion in 2025 (up 4.7%).

Operating profit climbed from KRW 22.5 billion in 2022 to KRW 24.2 billion in 2023 (up 7.3%) and KRW 26.6 billion in 2024 (up 9.7%), before declining to KRW 25.9 billion in 2025 (down 2.6%) — 2024 was the peak year for operating profit over 2022-2025, while 2022 was the smallest.

The operating margin rose from 46.0% in 2022 to 46.7% in 2024 before slipping to 43.4% in 2025. By contrast, net profit attributable to owners fell from KRW 12.8 billion in 2022 to KRW 11.9 billion in 2023 (down 7.1%) and KRW 3.6 billion in 2024 (down 69.6%), before swinging to a net loss of KRW 3.44 billion in 2025.

Net income including non-controlling interests followed the same pattern, also posting a net loss of KRW 3.44 billion in 2025.

The growth in revenue and operating profit reflects rental income from newly acquired assets, while the deterioration in net profit is attributable to higher interest expense from increased borrowings offsetting the gain in operating profit.

On the balance sheet, the debt-to-equity ratio rose every year, from 98.6% in 2022 to 109.9% in 2023, 132.8% in 2024, and 143.4% in 2025, with total equity of KRW 1.17 trillion (entirely attributable to owners) and total liabilities of KRW 1.67 trillion at the end of 2025.

Operating cash flow fell sharply from KRW 36.5 billion in 2023 to KRW 7.71 billion in 2024, recovering modestly to KRW 8.84 billion in 2025.

Over the last four quarters (Q3 2025 through Q2 2026), combined net profit attributable to owners totaled KRW 629 million, suggesting results are hovering near breakeven on an annualized basis despite quarter-to-quarter volatility.

05

Industry analysis

The Seoul capital area logistics market is shifting from the oversupply phase of the early-to-mid 2020s toward a period of supply contraction.

As of the first quarter of 2026, the vacancy rate for ambient logistics facilities in the Seoul capital area stood at about 10.0%, sharply lower than a year earlier, while cold-storage vacancy remained elevated at 46.5% over the same period.

According to JLL Korea, the overall Seoul capital area logistics vacancy rate was 15.5% in the second quarter of 2026, with new supply scarcity becoming more pronounced even as regional trends diverged.

Gyeonggi Province's implementation of an ordinance restricting the proliferation of logistics warehouses in October 2025 has also tightened approvals for large, high-spec logistics centers, further structuring the supply contraction.

Market observers have suggested that if the sharp supply decline seen in 2025-2026 continues, rent increase pressure could become visible from 2027 onward, concentrated in prime logistics assets.

Against this backdrop, ESR Kendall Square REIT's portfolio is concentrated in ambient logistics centers in the Seoul capital area, a segment viewed as resolving vacancy more quickly than other categories.

Cold-storage vacancy, however, illustrates a broader market polarization, and REITs or funds that have not pursued an ambient, Seoul-centric strategy carry a different sensitivity to this dynamic.

06

Outlook

In March 2026, the company stated it would operate its capital policy without a further equity issuance in 2026, and disclosed plans to pursue the divestment of two to three assets in the second half of the year, with proceeds directed toward shareholder returns.

This has reportedly included consideration of special dividends among other distribution methods.

The KRW 134.1 billion raised through the first-half 2025 equity issuance was used for the Cheonan-1 logistics center acquisition and the repayment of a revolving credit facility (RCF), leaving additional borrowing capacity in reserve.

Meanwhile, as of the end of February 2026, borrowings and interest maturing within one year totaled KRW 684.8 billion, more than tripling from three months earlier.

Interest expense in the same quarter reportedly reached KRW 17.4 billion, exceeding operating profit of KRW 15.6 billion, highlighting refinancing conditions tied to the interest rate environment as a factor that could affect future dividend capacity.

That said, a substantial portion of outstanding borrowings has reportedly been converted to a floating-rate structure, meaning interest costs could fall quickly if market rates decline.

On the leasing side, past lease renewals have achieved double-digit rent increases, and renewal terms with major tenants such as Coupang are seen as a key variable shaping future profitability.

Should the logistics market's supply contraction persist, the company's negotiating position in lease terms could strengthen, though this remains a market forecast rather than a confirmed outcome.

08

Bull factors

Potential Industry Turn from a Supply Cliff

New logistics center supply in the Seoul capital area has fallen sharply in 2025-2026, compounded by tighter approval regulations in Gyeonggi Province that are increasing the scarcity of large, high-spec assets.

Market observers suggest this supply gap could translate into rent increase pressure on prime assets from 2027 onward. With a portfolio concentrated in ambient, Seoul capital area assets, ESR Kendall Square REIT is seen as relatively well positioned for this shift.

Appraised Asset Value Reportedly Above Book Cost

The most recent appraised value of the 21 logistics centers held is estimated to exceed total acquisition cost by more than 13%. This suggests the locations and quality of the portfolio's assets are being favorably assessed by the market. Portfolio occupancy has also been reported to be maintained above 98%, a point worth noting on asset quality.

No-Dilution Capital Policy and Shareholder Return Plan

The company has stated it will run its capital policy without a further equity issuance in 2026. Instead, it disclosed plans to pursue two to three asset sales in the second half of the year, with proceeds directed toward shareholder returns including potential special dividends. This can be read as an attempt to improve the balance sheet without further dilution.

Bear factors

A Quarter Where Interest Expense Outpaced Operating Profit

Consolidated interest expense reportedly totaled KRW 17.4 billion between December 2025 and February 2026, exceeding operating profit of KRW 15.6 billion over the same period. The debt-to-equity ratio has also risen every year, from 98.6% in 2022 to 143.4% in 2025, expanding financial leverage. This is cited as a key factor behind the swing to a KRW 3.44 billion net loss attributable to owners in 2025.

Large Near-Term Debt Maturities Requiring Refinancing

As of the end of February 2026, borrowings and interest maturing within one year totaled KRW 684.8 billion, more than tripling over three months. If a high-interest-rate environment persists, refinancing terms could add further pressure on dividend capacity and financial stability.

The high share of borrowings converted to floating rates is favorable if rates fall, but carries the opposite risk if rates rise again.

Recent Earnings Capacity Hovering Near Breakeven

Combined net profit attributable to owners over the last four quarters (Q3 2025 through Q2 2026) totaled just KRW 629 million, leaving results hovering near breakeven on an annualized basis. Quarter-to-quarter volatility is significant, making it difficult to judge direction from a single quarter's results. The broader logistics market's unresolved vacancy issues, particularly in cold storage, also remain a variable.

10

Risk factors

Interest Rate and Refinancing Risk

With short-term debt maturities having grown rapidly, a renewed deterioration in the interest rate environment could add to interest expense burdens. A high share of floating-rate debt means profit and loss sensitivity to rate direction is significant. Unfavorable refinancing terms could also affect dividend funding.

Tenant Concentration Risk

With analysis having pointed to Coupang accounting for more than half of the tenant base, the renewal terms or departure of a single major tenant could materially affect results. Rental income trends can shift depending on the outcome of negotiations for leases coming up for renewal. While the tenant base includes diversified 3PL operators, reliance on a core tenant remains relatively high.

Execution Risk on Asset Sales and Capital Raising

The company's stated plan to divest two to three assets in the second half remains unconfirmed in execution. Delays or less-than-expected terms on any sale could affect the shareholder return plan.

The possibility that a further equity issuance could again be needed to fund future asset acquisitions, depending on market conditions, cannot be ruled out.

Overall view

ESR Kendall Square REIT sits at a juncture where positive factors — three consecutive years of revenue growth and a rise in the appraised value of its held assets — coexist with the negative factor of a 2025 swing to a net loss driven by rising interest expense from expanded borrowings.

On the operating side, a potential industry turn from a sharp pullback in Seoul capital area logistics supply has been discussed, while on the financial side, a concrete task remains in refinancing near-term debt maturities.

The company has stated it will avoid further equity issuance in 2026 and instead pursue shareholder returns through asset sales, but execution of this plan remains to be confirmed.

With combined net profit attributable to owners over the last four quarters hovering near breakeven, the direction of earnings capacity is a variable that needs reconfirmation through upcoming quarterly results and regular reports.

With multiple variables — lease renewal terms, the interest rate environment, and asset sale execution — moving simultaneously, a sequential check of each event's outcome is warranted rather than a one-sided conclusion.
