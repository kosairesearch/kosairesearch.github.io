Logistics REIT: Assets Expand, Profit Wavers

ESR Kendall Square REIT has steadily added logistics centers across the Seoul metropolitan area and Busan, lifting revenue for four straight years, but net profit attributable to owners swung to a loss in 2025, widening the gap between top-line growth and bottom-line results.

02

Business structure

ESR Kendall Square REIT is Korea's first listed REIT dedicated purely to logistics real estate, having listed on the KOSPI in December 2020.

The asset management company, Kendall Square REIT Management, is owned by ESR Kendall Square, which is itself an affiliate of ESR Group, Asia's largest logistics real estate platform.

According to analysis as of November 2025, the company held a total of 21 logistics centers across the Seoul metropolitan area and Busan, with combined floor area of 418,145 square meters and a total acquisition cost of KRW 2,680.2 billion.

That ranked as the second-largest acquisition value among Korea's 25 listed REITs, behind only SK Reit. The portfolio is concentrated in ambient-temperature warehouses, with major tenants including Coupang and other e-commerce and third-party logistics (3PL) operators.

Analysis from Daishin Securities as of November 2025 put occupancy at around 97% with a weighted average lease expiry (WALE) of 3.5 years.

The company has expanded through a 'capital recycling' strategy, acquiring assets developed or held by its sponsor ESR Kendall Square while selling appreciated legacy assets for reinvestment. In the first half of 2025, it added a Cheonan logistics center funded by a rights offering, expanding its operating asset base.

Each new asset addition has also generated economies of scale, such as reduced per-asset insurance and management costs.

04

Earnings analysis

Revenue rose for four straight years, from KRW 49 billion in 2022 to KRW 53 billion in 2023 (up 8.1% year on year), KRW 56.9 billion in 2024 (up 7.4%), and KRW 59.6 billion in 2025 (up 4.7%), with 2025 the largest year and 2022 the smallest within this span.

Operating profit grew from KRW 22.5 billion in 2022 to KRW 24.2 billion in 2023 (up 7.3%) and KRW 26.6 billion in 2024 (up 9.7%, the largest year in the period), before declining 2.6% to KRW 25.9 billion in 2025.

The operating margin stood at 46.0% in 2022, 45.7% in 2023, 46.7% in 2024, and 43.4% in 2025, edging lower in the most recent year.

By contrast, net income attributable to owners fell sharply and out of step with revenue and operating profit growth, dropping from KRW 12.8 billion in 2022 to KRW 11.9 billion in 2023 (down 7.1%) and KRW 3.6 billion in 2024 (down 69.6%), before turning into a KRW 3.44 billion net loss in 2025, a swing from profit to loss versus the prior year.

The fact that bottom-line profit swung far more sharply than the mild decline in operating profit suggests that items below the operating line have been weighing meaningfully on results.

Over the same period, the debt ratio rose steadily from 98.6% in 2022 to 109.9% in 2023, 132.8% in 2024, and 143.4% in 2025, reflecting continued leverage expansion.

Operating cash flow rose from KRW 26 billion in 2022 to KRW 36.5 billion in 2023, then fell sharply to KRW 7.71 billion in 2024 and KRW 8.84 billion in 2025, a notable decline in cash generation relative to operating profit since 2024.

At year-end 2025, total equity stood at KRW 1.17 trillion (entirely attributable to owners) against total liabilities of KRW 1.67 trillion.

In the most recent four quarters beyond the disclosed annual reports, from the third quarter of 2025 through the second quarter of 2026, net income attributable to owners totaled KRW 629 million, a modest improvement that moved closer to breakeven after the 2025 annual loss.

05

Industry analysis

Korea's logistics real estate market is in a transition period, moving past the oversupply that followed a surge of new completions in 2024 and gradually finding a new supply-demand balance.

According to Cushman & Wakefield's '2026 Korea Logistics Market Outlook' report, planned new supply in the Seoul metropolitan area is expected to fall to 453,000 square meters in 2026, less than half of 2025's 1,055,000 square meters, with the number of new projects expected to drop to just seven.

The decline is most pronounced among large prime facilities exceeding roughly 165,000 square meters, and Gyeonggi Province's new ordinance restricting indiscriminate warehouse development has also contributed to the slowdown in large-scale permitting.

The market has not fully normalized, however, as metropolitan-area vacancy remains elevated and cold-storage facilities continue to face the effects of prior oversupply; Alsquare Research Center data put the cold-storage vacancy rate at 39.2% as of the third quarter of 2025.

By contrast, well-located, automation-equipped ambient-temperature mega-warehouses are seeing vacancies fall quickly, driving a widening bifurcation across the market.

CBRE Korea projected that 2026 supply of prime Seoul-area logistics facilities would fall to its lowest level in a decade, and noted that average nominal ambient rent for prime metropolitan facilities rose 2.4% year on year as of end-2025.

Within this environment, ESR Kendall Square REIT's ambient-temperature, metropolitan-focused portfolio and stable tenant base anchored by Coupang position it in a relatively favored asset segment compared with the oversupplied cold-storage category.

06

Outlook

The company completed a rights offering of approximately KRW 134.1 billion in the first half of 2025, using the proceeds to acquire the Cheonan logistics center entirely with equity capital, and had indicated plans to add further assets by year-end 2025 without additional equity raises, relying instead on the borrowing capacity freed up by the offering.

In a shareholder letter released in March 2026, the company stated it does not plan further rights offerings during the year, and instead outlined plans to prepare two to three asset disposals in the second half of 2026, with proceeds to be directed toward shareholder returns.

The company also indicated it would prioritize non-dilutive financing going forward. This marks a potential shift in capital policy emphasis, from the prior approach of combining equity raises and debt to grow scale, toward sharing gains from asset recycling with shareholders.

On the industry side, as new metropolitan-area supply is expected to decline structurally from 2026 onward, how lease terms are renewed on the company's existing assets at upcoming renewal dates stands out as a variable that could affect future earnings.

At the same time, with the debt ratio having risen to 143.4%, the impact of interest rate conditions and refinancing terms on net income bears continued monitoring.

The fact that the trailing four-quarter sum of net income attributable to owners turned modestly positive after the 2025 annual net loss offers one reference point for gauging whether earnings have passed a trough.

The upcoming third-quarter 2026 report, the next regular periodic disclosure, should provide further detail on changes in rental income and the profit structure.

08

Bull factors

Potential Beneficiary of Structural Supply Decline

Amid expectations that new supply of logistics centers in the Seoul metropolitan area will fall to its lowest level in 10 years by 2026, existing REITs that already hold quality ambient-temperature assets are positioned in an environment where new competitive pressure eases.

Stricter permit standards in Gyeonggi Province are also raising entry barriers for large new projects. This represents a change in the business environment that could positively affect the negotiating power of existing assets in lease renewals.

Stable Tenant Base and High Occupancy

The portfolio's major tenants are e-commerce and 3PL companies including Coupang, with occupancy rate recorded at around 97% as of the November 2025 analysis. The CPI-linked rent structure partially defends the downside of rental income during periods of inflation. However, the high dependence on specific tenants is also a point that warrants attention.

Shift Toward Non-Dilutive Growth

The company has announced its policy to use funds secured from asset sales for shareholder returns in 2026 without additional rights offerings. This represents a capital policy direction that alleviates concerns over equity dilution that arose from multiple past rights offerings. The capital recycling strategy utilizing the sponsor's asset pipeline also continues.

09

Bear factors

Widening Earnings Volatility

Even during 2023-2024 when revenue and operating profit were growing, net income attributable to controlling shareholders decreased by 7.1% and 69.6% respectively, and ultimately turned to a loss in 2025.

The widening gap between operating indicators and the bottom line suggests that the influence of non-operating factors is growing. The sum of the most recent four quarters also amounted to only KRW 0.63 billion, making it difficult to confirm a clear profit rebound yet.

Rising Leverage and Interest Cost Burden

The debt ratio has steadily risen from 98.6% in 2022 to 143.4% in 2025, expanding financial leverage. If interest rates or refinancing conditions change unfavorably, the burden of interest expenses on net income could increase. This risk grows as the proportion of asset acquisitions funded through borrowing increases.

Cash Generation Lagging Operating Profit

Operating cash flow decreased sharply from KRW 36.5 billion in 2023 to KRW 7.71 billion in 2024 and KRW 8.84 billion in 2025, becoming markedly lower than operating profit (KRW 25.9-26.6 billion) during the same period. If this gap persists, it could pose a burden on securing funds for dividends. The specific cause of this discrepancy needs to be confirmed in future regular reports.

10

Risk factors

Interest Rate and Refinancing Risk

Under the elevated debt ratio, interest rate conditions at the time of future borrowing refinancing could directly affect interest expenses and net income. If the interest rate cut trend unfolds differently than expected, the burden of capital procurement costs could increase. This is a variable that could affect both dividend funding and the capacity for additional asset acquisition.

Tenant Concentration Risk

The portfolio's rental income relies substantially on a small number of large tenants such as Coupang. Changes in a specific tenant's leasing strategy or lease renewal terms have a relatively large impact on overall rental income. During periods when lease renewals are concentrated, volatility in lease terms could increase.

Structural Bifurcation in the Logistics Property Market

Cold-chain logistics centers remain under the influence of oversupply, showing a vacancy rate as high as 39.2% as of Q3 2025. The polarization trend continues, with widening gaps in asset value and lease terms between ambient and cold-chain facilities, and between the Seoul metropolitan area and non-metropolitan areas.

Even though the portfolio is ambient-temperature focused, it is not entirely free from changes in overall market sentiment or transaction conditions.

Overall view

ESR Kendall Square REIT, one of Korea's largest logistics-focused REITs, has steadily grown revenue from 2022 through 2025, but net income attributable to owners contracted repeatedly over the same period before turning into a loss in 2025, widening the gap between top-line growth and bottom-line results.

A financial structure with the debt ratio rising to 143.4% and operating cash flow running well below operating profit are points that warrant continued attention regarding both interest costs and the funds available for dividends.

On the industry side, new supply in the Seoul metropolitan area is expected to decline structurally in 2026, even as cold-storage oversupply remains unresolved and market bifurcation persists.

The company has signaled a shift in capital policy by committing to fund shareholder returns through asset disposals rather than further rights offerings in 2026.

The modest return to positive territory in the trailing four-quarter sum of net income attributable to owners offers one reference point for assessing whether earnings have passed a trough, though it is too early to call this a clear trend.

The next periodic report, the outcome of planned second-half asset sales, and upcoming dividend resolution disclosures should provide the key information needed for further assessment.
