02

Business structure

Dream Security is an information security company founded in 1998 that listed on KOSDAQ in 2017 through a merger with a special purpose acquisition vehicle.

It runs PKI-based authentication, wired and wireless security, electronic document security solutions and system integration work, and reports three segments: IT security, defense and rental.

Its flagship products include Magic ZTA, which implements a zero-trust model, Magic MFA for multi-factor authentication, and Magic DBPlus and Magic IAM, which apply post-quantum cryptography. A corporate information site puts the rental segment at about 67% of revenue, making it the core of the revenue mix.

Rental demand is tied to IT equipment, elevated work platforms, industrial equipment and instrumentation demand from 5G and IoT deployment. The defense segment drove expansion with KRW 23.5 billion of first-half 2026 revenue. Consolidated subsidiaries number 15 companies, including Korea Rental, DigiCAP and Seedcore.

The largest shareholder group led by Beom Jin-gyu and five others holds 41.81% as of October 2, 2026. In August 2026 the company also disclosed a KRW 10 billion investment in another company.

In IT security, the core business competes with PKI and authentication vendors, and it partly overlaps with Ahnlab in security software.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual revenue rose from KRW 224.2 billion in 2022 to KRW 232.7 billion in 2023 (up 3.8%), KRW 268.8 billion in 2024 (up 15.5%) and KRW 318.9 billion in 2025 (up 18.6%), and grew for three consecutive years from 2023 through 2025. 2025 was the largest year of the 2022 to 2025 period.

Operating profit peaked at KRW 33.3 billion in 2023 (up 31.8%) from KRW 25.2 billion in 2022, then fell 22.9% to KRW 25.6 billion in 2024 and 12.3% to KRW 22.5 billion in 2025, a two-year decline. Revenue grew while profit shrank, and the operating margin moved from 14.3% in 2023 to 9.5% in 2024 and 7.1% in 2025.

The company attributed 2025 changes to higher sales from stronger competitiveness and to a business environment that included a high exchange rate. R&D expense booked in selling and administrative costs rose to KRW 5.66 billion in 2025 from a year earlier.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders rose to KRW 22.5 billion in 2023, then fell 42.3% to KRW 13 billion in 2024 and 36.2% to KRW 8.28 billion in 2025.

Total net income including non-controlling interests was KRW 7.39 billion in 2025, a different figure from net income attributable to controlling shareholders. Operating cash flow was KRW 20.7 billion in 2023, -KRW 3 billion in 2024 and -KRW 14.8 billion in 2025.

By quarter, revenue was KRW 75.1 billion with operating profit of KRW 4 billion in the third quarter of 2025, and KRW 94.7 billion with KRW 10.1 billion in the fourth quarter (up 152.2% from the prior quarter).

Revenue peaked at KRW 108.4 billion in the first quarter of 2026, while operating profit hit its low of KRW 3.76 billion (down 62.7% from the prior quarter).

In the first-half disclosure the company cited added research hiring and investment as the reason for weaker profitability, and an analysis points to one-off costs such as performance bonus provisions in the first quarter.

Second-quarter 2026 revenue was KRW 108.3 billion with operating profit of KRW 8.16 billion (up 117.0%). Third-quarter revenue was KRW 103.2 billion (down 4.7% from the prior quarter and up 37.4% year on year), with operating profit of KRW 9.84 billion (up 146.2% year on year).

05

Industry analysis

Public and financial-sector demand for quantum-resistant communications and security authentication is expected to expand. The company positions post-quantum cryptography solutions and zero-trust models as its next-generation core products and plans to extend AI security offerings.

In the near term it aims to consolidate a dominant position in Korea's post-quantum security market. The company also credits a strong defense industry for its first-half results.

The rental segment depends on IT equipment, industrial equipment and instrumentation demand, so it is exposed to drivers different from the security market cycle. Competition in the sector takes place among PKI and authentication vendors, and its business overlaps with security software players such as Ahnlab.

Public market-share data and a direct profitability comparison with competitors are not included in this comparison. The pace of growth in next-generation security markets and the company's commercial track record will determine its position in the industry.

Outlook

The company expects growth to continue in the second half of 2026. It judges that its defense and next-generation security businesses have entered a growth phase. It also plans to strengthen its growth base by pursuing cost efficiency and better profitability.

It intends to secure medium- to long-term growth drivers through synergies among group affiliates. The board decided on a convertible bond issuance on July 22, 2026, and on August 25, 2026 the company disclosed a KRW 10 billion investment in another company.

How these two moves affect the capital and shareholding structure will be confirmed in the detailed disclosures. Because one-off costs were recorded in the first quarter of 2026, whether the cost structure stabilizes in later quarters is the key question.

Third-quarter operating profit rose 146.2% year on year while net income attributable to controlling shareholders fell, and the source of that gap will be visible in the income statement details of the next periodic report.

Whether commercial results build up in post-quantum cryptography and zero-trust markets is central to the medium-term outlook.

Bull factors

Rapid growth in defense

Defense revenue reached KRW 23.5 billion in the first half of 2026, up more than 190% from a year earlier, and the company cited it as a key driver of top-line growth. The company judges that its defense business has entered a growth phase.

As defense makes up a larger share of revenue, its contribution to growth also rises, so whether this segment keeps growing in the coming quarters is the point to watch.

Next-generation security lineup

The company positions Magic ZTA, which implements zero trust, and post-quantum-based Magic MFA, Magic DBPlus and Magic IAM as its flagship products. It has set a goal of securing the top spot in Korea's post-quantum security market.

The expected growth in public and financial-sector demand for quantum-resistant communications and authentication forms the backdrop for this lineup.

Quarterly operating profit recovery

Operating profit recovered from a first-quarter 2026 low of KRW 3.76 billion to KRW 8.16 billion in the second quarter and KRW 9.84 billion in the third, rising for two consecutive quarters. Third-quarter operating profit was up 146.2% year on year. The company said higher sales and selling and administrative cost efficiency were reflected in the increase.

Bear factors

Downtrend in operating margin

The operating margin declined from 14.3% in 2023 to 9.5% in 2024 and 7.1% in 2025. Operating profit fell for two consecutive years in 2024 and 2025, and margins did not keep pace with revenue growth. The company attributed weaker profitability to added research hiring and investment.

Financial burden and cash flow

Total liabilities at the end of 2025 were KRW 421.2 billion, and the debt-to-equity ratio was 281.0%. Operating cash flow was -KRW 3 billion in 2024 and -KRW 14.8 billion in 2025, showing continued net outflows. Private bond maturities recur, so debt management and repayment are key to financial stability.

Quality of quarterly earnings

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders over the last four quarters totals KRW 11.2 billion, a thinner base than at the annual peak.

Risk factors

Exchange-rate pressure

The company cited changes in the business environment, including a high exchange rate, as a factor behind 2025 results. If the exchange rate stays high, it could keep pressure on profitability through costs and operating expenses.

With rental making up a large share of revenue, changes in equipment-related costs could feed through to results. If the exchange rate does not turn, the pace at which revenue growth converts into profit recovery could slow.

Delayed investment recovery

On August 25, 2026, the company disclosed a KRW 10 billion investment in another company. It plans to secure medium- to long-term growth drivers through synergies among group affiliates.

With 15 consolidated subsidiaries, if collaboration among affiliates does not translate into earnings, recovery of the invested capital could be delayed. In that case, the investment burden could remain a factor that thins the earnings base.

Dilution and concentration

A convertible bond issuance was decided on July 22, 2026, and conversion right exercises disclosed in April 2026 equaled about 2.3% and 2.5% of outstanding shares. Rental accounts for about 67% of revenue, so the business leans heavily on one segment. If the timing of defense orders shifts, growth could change as well.

Overall view

Dream Security showed both top-line growth and a quarterly profit recovery in the first half of 2026, with revenue of KRW 216.7 billion and operating profit of KRW 11.9 billion. Defense revenue of KRW 23.5 billion and the large weight of the rental segment are the two visible growth pillars.

The operating margin, however, fell from 14.3% in 2023 to 7.1% in 2025, and net income attributable to controlling shareholders has declined for two years since its 2023 peak. Operating cash flow was -KRW 14.8 billion in 2025, and total liabilities of KRW 421.2 billion exceed total equity of KRW 149.9 billion.

Operating profit rose in the third quarter of 2026, but net income attributable to controlling shareholders fell, so earnings quality still needs confirmation. With market capitalization above controlling-shareholder equity, the durability of profit recovery is the central variable for valuation.

Strengths center on growth businesses such as defense and post-quantum security, while weaknesses are falling profitability and financial burden.

The next checkpoints are the operating margin in the 2026 annual report, the quarterly earnings trend in the first-quarter 2027 report, and how the private bond maturing in June 2027 is handled.
