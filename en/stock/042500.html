02

Business structure

RingNet, founded in April 2000, is an IT infrastructure company whose core is NI business covering design, equipment supply, installation and maintenance of computer networks, alongside video conferencing, storage systems and IP telephony.

A 2022 technical analysis report described the company as specializing in network and system integration while expanding into cloud, data centers and smart workspaces.

Its customers fall into public institutions, general enterprises and educational institutions, and it provides equipment supply, construction and maintenance for devices such as routers, switches and servers.

Sales run through both direct sales and indirect sales via partner companies; the same report noted that direct sales carry higher profitability, while partner-channel revenue is smaller but recurring.

In 2026 the company expanded its former system sales organization into a newly created System Business Division and set a target of 15% or more growth to address demand for data center, network and system infrastructure.

Competitively, RingNet is classed as a comprehensive ICT company combining NI, SI and maintenance, and within the IT and software sector it is compared with Samsung SDS, LG CNS, Hyundai Autoever and Daou Technology.

A project-centered structure of equipment supply and construction can concentrate revenue recognition in particular quarters and widen quarterly earnings swings. As of end-August 2026, the largest shareholder and related parties held 31.24%, and the company had 280 employees. Segment revenue mix and customer concentration should be checked in the notes to the periodic reports.

04

Earnings analysis

On an annual basis, revenue went from KRW 204 billion in 2022 to KRW 217.7 billion in 2023 (up 6.7% year on year), the largest year in the period, before falling to KRW 163.9 billion in 2024 (down 24.7%), the smallest year, and recovering to KRW 183.1 billion in 2025 (up 11.7%).

Operating profit peaked at KRW 16.3 billion in 2023 (up 50.3%), the largest year in the period, then fell to KRW 13.7 billion in 2024 (down 16.3%) and edged up to KRW 14.3 billion in 2025 (up 4.5%). Even as revenue fell 24.7% in 2024, the operating margin rose to 8.4% from 7.5% in 2023, and it was 7.8% in 2025.

Gross profit, per fnguide financial data, declined from KRW 30.4 billion in 2023 to KRW 27.5 billion in 2024, a smaller drop than the revenue decline, which helps explain the margin gain.

Profit attributable to owners of the parent rose from KRW 10.1 billion in 2022 to KRW 12.8 billion in 2023 (up 27.2%) and KRW 12.9 billion in 2024 (up 0.5%), then fell to KRW 10.9 billion in 2025 (down 15.2%); 2024 was the largest year and 2022 the smallest.

The gap between operating profit of KRW 14.3 billion and parent-owned profit of KRW 10.9 billion in 2025 reflects non-operating items and taxes, with financial income of about KRW 4.3 billion in 2025 among the contributors.

Quarterly revenue moved from KRW 43.7 billion in the third quarter of 2025 to KRW 79.1 billion in the fourth quarter (up 81.1% from the prior quarter), with operating profit rising from KRW 4.96 billion to KRW 6.3 billion (up 27.0%).

Revenue then fell to KRW 37.5 billion in the first quarter of 2026 (down 52.6%) with operating profit of KRW 2.82 billion (down 55.2%), recovered to KRW 58 billion in the second quarter (up 54.7%), but operating profit dropped to KRW 621 million (down 78.0%).

Third-quarter 2026 revenue of KRW 56.6 billion rose 29.6% year on year, while operating profit of KRW 4.59 billion fell 7.4% from a year earlier, though it rose 638.9% from the prior quarter.

For the first quarter of 2026, Kiwoom-style monitoring service Wisereport tallied year-on-year gains of 45.2% in revenue, 193.8% in operating profit and 34.1% in net income, citing demand for AI transformation and more complex ICT infrastructure.

Parent-owned net income over the last four quarters (fourth quarter 2025 to third quarter 2026) totaled KRW 10.4 billion, and third-quarter 2026 parent-owned net income of KRW 4.38 billion rose 15.5% year on year.

Operating cash flow swung from -KRW 22.5 billion in 2022 to KRW 50.6 billion in 2023, KRW 7.18 billion in 2024 and -KRW 9.34 billion in 2025. The debt ratio at end-2025 was 41.3%, down from 92.9% in 2022, and all KRW 119 billion of equity is attributable to owners of the parent. Whether one-off costs affected quarterly profit is an item to check in the notes to the quarterly reports.

Industry analysis

RingNet's end-market demand comes from IT infrastructure investment by enterprises and public institutions, and AI investment has lifted that demand in 2026.

The company attributed the creation of its System Business Division to rising demand for data center, network and system infrastructure driven by expanding AI and semiconductor investment.

Wisereport likewise cited growing ICT infrastructure complexity from AI transformation demand as a factor in first-quarter 2026 results.

In April 2026 the company said it expects to benefit once a government GPU infrastructure program of about KRW 2 trillion gets fully underway, but that is the company's own expectation and the timing depends on outside factors.

On the cycle, revenue fell 24.7% in 2024, which signals sensitivity to infrastructure investment cycles. Quarterly revenue edged down from KRW 58 billion in the second quarter of 2026 to KRW 56.6 billion in the third, so whether growth continues will be shown by coming quarters.

Competitively, RingNet is classed as a comprehensive ICT company covering NI, SI and maintenance, but its scale is much smaller than large SI groups in the same sector. Listed peers in the IT and software sector include NAVER, Samsung SDS, LG CNS and Hyundai Autoever, with differing scale and business scope.

A smaller company is likely to see wider earnings swings when industry conditions change, which is a structural feature to keep in mind.

Risk factors

Business cycle

Revenue fell 24.7% to KRW 163.9 billion in 2024 and recovered in 2025, showing that revenue can swing sharply with IT infrastructure investment cycles. Results reflect the gap between years with large infrastructure projects and years without them. If policy programs or AI demand slow, achieving the growth target could become harder.

Cash flow and working capital

Operating cash flow was -KRW 9.34 billion in 2025, diverging from operating profit. Changes in working-capital items such as receivables and inventory can affect how quickly cash comes in.

In a business centered on equipment supply and construction, cash flow can vary by quarter depending on when payments are collected.

Uncertainty in policy program execution

The government GPU program is one the company expects to benefit from, but the timing of execution and RingNet's order volume are not yet tied to any filing. If the policy schedule slips, the path to the 2026 growth target could also be delayed. The lag between expectations for a specific program and its appearance in profit is itself a risk.
