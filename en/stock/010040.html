Korea Refractories: Testing Earnings Strength in a Refractory Oligopoly

02

Business structure

Korea Refractories was founded in 1974 and combines the manufacture and sale of refractory products with the design and construction of industrial furnaces. Refractories line the inner walls of high-temperature equipment and come in shaped brick products and unshaped products such as mortars and castables.

According to CNews reporting on its 2023 annual report, refractory sales were KRW 193.5 billion, or 47.01% of revenue, and the industrial furnace and kiln construction segment accounted for most of the remainder.

The construction business is won project by project, such as building new furnaces or repairing existing ones, so quarterly revenue can move with plant operating schedules.

The refractory segment depends on product volume and pricing, while the construction segment depends on project progress and site costs, so the two earn profits in different ways.

The domestic market is widely described as an oligopoly, and an equity information site's company profile says the company, together with POSCO Future M and Chosun Refractories, meets about 80% of domestic demand.

Its main customers are operators of high-temperature facilities in steelmaking, nonferrous metals, incineration and power generation.

An April 2026 earnings comment from a financial data provider said competition among refractory makers has intensified, with Chinese products improving in technology and domestic firms setting up overseas subsidiaries, driving both price and quality competition.

As product quality converges, price has become the central competitive variable and a source of margin pressure. The structure therefore combines stable demand in a concentrated market with margin pressure from price competition.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual revenue moved from KRW 358.8 billion in 2022 to KRW 411.8 billion in 2023 (up 14.8% year on year), KRW 416 billion in 2024 (up 1.0%) and KRW 406.3 billion in 2025 (down 2.3%). Among 2022 to 2025, 2024 was the largest year by revenue and 2022 the smallest.

Operating profit of KRW 10.5 billion in 2022 turned into an operating loss of KRW 7.54 billion in 2023, then returned to a profit of KRW 8.59 billion in 2024. Operating margins were 2.9% in 2022, -1.8% in 2023, 2.1% in 2024 and 0.7% in 2025.

In 2025 operating profit fell 67.7% to KRW 2.78 billion, and net income attributable to controlling shareholders swung from a KRW 5.51 billion profit in 2024 to a net loss of KRW 21.8 billion.

The 2023 controlling-shareholder net loss of KRW 14.7 billion and the 2025 loss show that net results swung more widely than operating profit. Operating cash flow stayed positive at KRW 10.2 billion in 2023, a loss year, and was KRW 7.72 billion in 2025.

The debt-to-equity ratio rose from 48.0% in 2022 to 69.3% in 2024 before easing to 58.1% in 2025, when total equity was KRW 174.1 billion and total liabilities KRW 101.1 billion.

By quarter, third-quarter 2025 revenue was KRW 103.3 billion with operating profit of KRW 4.41 billion; fourth-quarter revenue fell 10.5% quarter on quarter to KRW 92.4 billion and operating profit fell 35.7% to KRW 2.84 billion.

In the first quarter of 2026 revenue was KRW 88.4 billion, down 4.3% quarter on quarter, and an operating loss of KRW 310 million turned the result into a loss. In the second quarter revenue rose 17.7% to KRW 104.1 billion and operating profit returned to KRW 1.82 billion.

Third-quarter 2026 revenue was KRW 101.6 billion, down 2.4% quarter on quarter and down 1.6% year on year, yet operating profit was KRW 96.17 million, down 94.7% quarter on quarter and 97.8% year on year.

Controlling-shareholder net losses over the latest four quarters, from the fourth quarter of 2025 to the third quarter of 2026, total KRW 597 million, as profits in the second and third quarters of 2026 nearly offset losses in the fourth quarter of 2025 and first quarter of 2026. How much one-off items affected quarterly results cannot be judged from the disclosed figures alone.

Industry analysis

Refractories line high-temperature equipment used in steelmaking, nonferrous metals, cement and incineration, so demand is tied to the operating rates and investment cycles of those downstream plants. Steel output and downstream facility investment plans are the key variables for the company's sales.

The domestic market is widely described as an oligopoly, yet an April 2026 earnings comment from a financial data provider said competition among refractory makers has intensified.

Chinese products have improved in technology and domestic firms have set up local subsidiaries, bringing price and quality competition together. An oligopoly protects the demand base, but once product quality converges, price becomes the central competitive variable and squeezes margins.

Korea Refractories combines a product-sales refractory segment with a construction segment for furnace work, so its earnings pattern differs from that of a pure refractory maker. Construction profits depend on project progress and site costs, so margins can differ even at similar revenue levels.

Changes in raw material and energy costs also feed directly into margins. Third-quarter 2026 revenue holding above KRW 100 billion suggests the demand base has not collapsed, but operating profit falling below KRW 100 million in the same quarter shows weaker capacity to absorb costs. The next task is to track the intensity of price competition alongside the progress of construction projects.

Outlook

On a disclosed-results basis, the direction of 2026 earnings splits by quarter. After an operating loss of KRW 310 million in the first quarter of 2026, operating profit returned to KRW 1.82 billion in the second quarter, but third-quarter operating profit fell to KRW 96.17 million.

Whether the recovery was a one-off rebound in the second quarter or a base that carries into later quarters is the starting point for the next read.

The 2026 annual report will confirm full-year results including the fourth quarter, and it will show the direction of annual profitability against the 0.7% operating margin in 2025. Fourth-quarter revenue may move from the third-quarter level of KRW 101.6 billion depending on when construction projects are completed.

Strengths include the second-quarter return to profit, revenue holding above KRW 100 billion, and a small cumulative loss of KRW 597 million over the latest four quarters.

Weaknesses include the sharp drop in third-quarter operating profit, intensifying price competition, and the 2025 swing of controlling-shareholder net income into a loss.

Medium-term capacity expansion and new product timing should be checked in the business descriptions of the periodic reports and in order-related disclosures. Whether these two trends point the same way will serve as the benchmark for reading the next quarters.

Bull factors

Return to profit in Q2 2026 and steady revenue

Controlling-shareholder net losses over the latest four quarters total KRW 597 million, a limited cumulative burden.

Concentrated market and domestic demand base

A company profile on the equity information site alphasquare describes Korea Refractories as one of three firms that together meet about 80% of domestic refractory demand. Operators of high-temperature facilities are its main customers, so continued repair and replacement demand can sustain the sales base. The concentrated structure is cited as a factor that eases pressure from new entrants.

Improving leverage and positive operating cash flow

Earnings volatility and loss history

On a quarterly basis, controlling-shareholder net losses appeared in the fourth quarter of 2025 and the first quarter of 2026.

Risk factors

Demand and capex cycle

Refractory demand depends on the operating rates and investment timing of downstream plants in steel, nonferrous metals and incineration. If downstream facility investment declines, both refractory sales and construction orders can shrink together, so the two segments may move in the same direction. This is a factor that can be read alongside the 2.3% decline in 2025 revenue.

Expansion and new product execution

Medium-term capacity expansion and new product timing remain items to be checked in periodic report business descriptions and order-related disclosures. If capacity additions or new product launches fall behind plan, the timing of gains in sales volume and pricing may slip and slow the margin recovery.

Fixed costs and upfront investment tied to expansion may be recognized first, and if sales results do not follow, the earnings burden could last longer. Whether new products can earn price differentiation in a market where quality has converged also depends on how execution plays out.

Order volatility and capital cushion

The construction segment books revenue project by project, so quarterly sales and profit can swing during gaps in orders. Quarterly swings have been large, with an operating loss of KRW 310 million in the first quarter of 2026 followed by operating profit in the second quarter.

Total liabilities of KRW 101.1 billion against equity of KRW 174.1 billion at the end of 2025 is a limited burden, but if losses persist, the pace of equity erosion becomes an important variable.

Overall view

Korea Refractories makes refractory products and also builds industrial furnaces, and it operates within a domestic refractory market where a few producers hold an oligopoly.

Annual revenue since 2022 has ranged between KRW 358.8 billion and KRW 416 billion, reaching KRW 406.3 billion in 2025, when the operating margin stayed low at 0.7%. In 2025 operating profit fell 67.7% to KRW 2.78 billion and the company swung to a controlling-shareholder net loss of KRW 21.8 billion.

In 2026 the second quarter returned to operating profit of KRW 1.82 billion, but third-quarter operating profit dropped to KRW 96.17 million. Controlling-shareholder net losses over the latest four quarters total KRW 597 million, so the cumulative burden is limited.

The price-to-book ratio sits below 1x, but earnings alternating between profit and loss make PER hard to apply.

Strengths include the oligopoly structure, the lower debt-to-equity ratio and continued positive operating cash flow, while weaknesses include intensifying price competition and the sharp third-quarter profit drop.

The next checkpoints are the operating margin in the 2026 annual report and whether the first-quarter 2027 quarterly report shows a profit.
