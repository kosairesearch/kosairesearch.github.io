Toilet-Part Maker Narrowing Losses; Revenue Recovery Is the Test

02

Business structure

Watoscoreaco has produced fittings and piping materials for toilets, urinals and washbasins since its founding in 1973. Its core products are toilet tank fittings, fill valves and flush valves.

An industry profile on a job portal puts toilet fittings at 49% of sales and washbasin and trap products at 11%, though that data carries no reference date. Manufacturing accounted for 99% of sales in the second quarter of 2026, with leasing making up the remaining 1%.

Its main customers are sanitary-ware makers, and some products reach consumers through distributors. The company says it has supplied Daelim B&Co, Kyelim Ceramic and ISD Dongseo under long-term contracts of more than 20 years.

An older industry article, however, says it supplies major makers other than Daelim B&Co, so the customer mix differs by source. The company produces both in-house developed and ODM products.

It cites more than 100 domestic and overseas industrial property rights and highlights water-saving products with environmental certification. Raw material is mainly plastic, processed through mold making and injection molding.

Because it supplies components rather than finished fixtures, demand tracks construction and remodeling activity and the production volumes of its main customers.

04

Earnings analysis

On a reported basis, revenue fell from KRW 19.5 billion in 2022 to KRW 17.7 billion in 2023 (down 9.4% year on year), KRW 17.5 billion in 2024 (down 1.0%) and KRW 14.1 billion in 2025 (down 19.3%).

Revenue has declined for three consecutive years from 2023 through 2025, with 2022 the largest year and 2025 the smallest in the period. Operating profit of KRW 137 million in 2022 turned into an operating loss of KRW 90.31 million in 2023, then KRW 247 million in 2024 and KRW 4.32 billion in 2025.

The operating margin slid from 0.7% in 2022 to -0.5% in 2023, -1.4% in 2024 and -30.6% in 2025. Revenue shrinking while operating losses widened is the core pattern, and the filings do not split the cost items that drove it.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders fell from KRW 5.31 billion in 2022 to KRW 2.67 billion in 2023 (down 49.8%) and KRW 2.07 billion in 2024 (down 22.5%), then turned into a net loss of KRW 2.74 billion in 2025. Total net income matched the controlling-shareholder figure in every year from 2022 to 2025.

The gap between the 2025 operating loss and net loss is consistent with KRW 2.44 billion of non-operating income, KRW 1.77 billion of non-operating expenses and a KRW 900 million tax benefit, based on a job-portal financial table drawn from NICE data.

Operating cash flow moved from positive amounts of KRW 3.12 billion in 2022, KRW 2.4 billion in 2023 and KRW 4.49 billion in 2024 to negative KRW 519 million in 2025.

Quarterly revenue rose from KRW 3.33 billion in the second quarter of 2025 to KRW 3.58 billion in the third quarter (up 7.5%), then eased to KRW 3.48 billion in the fourth quarter and KRW 3.02 billion in the first quarter of 2026, before reaching KRW 3.4 billion in the second quarter, up 12.6% from the prior quarter.

The operating loss grew to KRW 2.64 billion in the fourth quarter of 2025 before narrowing to KRW 834 million in the first quarter of 2026 and KRW 480 million in the second.

The net loss attributable to controlling shareholders totals KRW 3.18 billion over the latest four quarters, with the fourth quarter of 2025 (KRW 2.85 billion) accounting for most of it.

Whether the fourth-quarter widening reflects one-off items or the operating structure cannot be separated from the reported figures alone.

05

Industry analysis

The toilet-parts market is structured around a small group of large sanitary-ware makers. One industry article said four companies hold more than 50% of the domestic sanitary-ware market, but that report is nearly a decade old and should not be read as the current structure.

A sales platform's company profile says the firm holds a 70% to 80% share of the domestic core toilet-component market; that figure reads more like a self-description than a market tally.

Demand comes from residential construction and remodeling, and public facilities such as government offices, schools and subway stations are also listed as applications. Upgrading bathroom culture and growth in interior and remodeling work are cited as demand drivers.

In the construction-products sector of 21 companies, the average operating margin in 2024 was -0.09%, while Watoscoreaco's -1.41% ranked 16th in the sector. Plastic raw material prices and construction activity appear to affect profitability together.

The company is classified under chemicals, but its product mix and scale differ sharply from large petrochemical listings such as LG Chem and Lotte Chemical, so direct comparison is limited. Environmental certification for water-saving products is cited as a differentiator.

Because end demand is tied to the construction cycle, housing starts and remodeling activity set the direction of the sector.

06

Outlook

On March 27, 2026, the company filed a corporate value-up plan. It targets the top domestic share in toilet components and says it will respond to new products and structural change. The plan also states that it will keep developing products so it can benefit when construction activity recovers.

The company intends to concentrate on its established sanitary-ware parts business to keep sales stable. In the same plan, it notes that it qualifies as a high-dividend company under the Restriction of Special Taxation Act, and it cited a 25% payout ratio for 2025.

On February 13, 2026, it announced a year-end cash dividend on common shares. In quarterly terms, second-quarter 2026 revenue of KRW 3.4 billion rose 12.6% from the prior quarter, but the KRW 480 million operating loss in the same quarter extends a run of five consecutive quarterly operating losses.

The plan's core is protecting the sales base, with earnings recovery presented as a later step. The first checkpoint is whether revenue keeps rebounding and whether operating losses keep narrowing in the 2026 third-quarter report.

With almost no borrowings and a high equity share at the end of 2025, the company has room to fund its plans internally. Since operating cash flow turned negative in 2025, the sustainability of dividend and investment funding is also worth tracking.

Debt-light balance sheet

That leaves room on the balance sheet during the loss period, although equity has been declining and that cushion shrinks if losses continue.

Quarterly losses narrowing

The operating loss narrowed from KRW 2.64 billion in the fourth quarter of 2025 to KRW 834 million in the first quarter of 2026 and KRW 480 million in the second. Second-quarter 2026 revenue of KRW 3.4 billion rose 12.6% from the prior quarter.

Revenue recovery and narrower losses moving in the same direction is a constructive sign, though the company has still posted operating losses for five consecutive quarters.

Explicit shareholder-return stance

On February 13, 2026, the company announced a year-end cash dividend on common shares. Its March 27, 2026 value-up plan identifies it as a high-dividend company under the Restriction of Special Taxation Act and cites a 25% payout ratio for 2025. Stating a dividend policy publicly during a loss period is seen as a point in favor for shareholders.

Bear factors

Three straight years of revenue decline

Revenue fell to KRW 17.7 billion in 2023, KRW 17.5 billion in 2024 and KRW 14.1 billion in 2025, a three-year decline. The 2025 drop of 19.3% was the steepest in the period. Against KRW 19.5 billion in 2022, the 2025 base is substantially smaller, and the shrinking sales base coincides with wider operating losses.

Continued quarterly losses and weaker cash flow

The company has posted operating losses for five consecutive quarters from the second quarter of 2025 through the second quarter of 2026, and the net loss attributable to controlling shareholders totals KRW 3.18 billion over the latest four quarters.

The fourth quarter of 2025 was the weakest, with an operating loss of KRW 2.64 billion. Operating cash flow turned negative at KRW 519 million in 2025.

Dependence on construction and product concentration

Residential construction and remodeling are the main application markets, so a slowdown in construction translates directly into weaker demand. Toilet fittings are reported at 49% of sales, which means heavy reliance on a single product group.

The company itself says it expects to benefit when construction activity recovers, acknowledging the link to the cycle.

Risk factors

Product and customer concentration

Sales are concentrated in toilet fittings, and key customers are limited to sanitary-ware makers. If those customers cut production or change sourcing, revenue could move quickly. The company cites long-term supply contracts, but the terms and volumes are not disclosed in detail.

Its stated plan to respond to new products and structural change is the way it aims to broaden this concentrated base.

Raw materials and production

Its main raw material is plastic, and products are made through mold making, injection molding and rubber and plastic processing. Higher raw material prices or processing costs could pressure margins. With the operating margin at -30.6% in 2025, cost pressure could add to losses. How much of any cost increase can be passed on to customers is a point to confirm in future filings.

Dividend sustainability and small-cap liquidity

The company announced a year-end cash dividend in February 2026, even though it recorded a net loss in 2025. Operating cash flow turned negative in 2025, so the funding behind dividends is a point to monitor. As a small-cap stock, it may show greater trading illiquidity and price volatility than large caps. Whether the dividend policy holds through the loss period is the key variable for shareholders.

Overall view

Watoscoreaco has a business concentrated in toilet fittings. On a reported basis, revenue has declined for three consecutive years from 2023, and operating losses have continued for the same three years.

The 2025 operating loss widened to KRW 4.32 billion, but the net loss attributable to controlling shareholders was smaller at KRW 2.74 billion. In the second quarter of 2026, revenue rose and the operating loss narrowed from the prior quarter, yet the company has posted operating losses for five consecutive quarters.

On the financial side, the 6.9% debt ratio and low borrowings are strengths. Declining equity and a negative operating cash flow in 2025 are weaknesses. The company's value-up plan centers on holding the top domestic share in toilet components and concentrating on its core business.

Because construction demand and product concentration are high, the business direction depends heavily on the sector cycle. The next checkpoints are revenue and operating loss in the 2026 third-quarter report, and whether annual operating profit turns positive.
