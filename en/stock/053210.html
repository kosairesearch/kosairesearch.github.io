Skylife: Profit Recovery Amid a Shrinking Satellite Base

KT Skylife returned to profit in 2025 at both the operating and controlling-shareholder net levels, and quarterly operating profit has risen for two straight quarters since the first-quarter 2026 low. Declining satellite subscribers and three consecutive years of falling sales remain unresolved.

  1. 1

    Annual sales fell to KRW 984.2 billion in 2025, a third straight annual decline starting in 2023, and the smallest year in the 2022–2025 span.

  2. 2

    Operating profit swung from an operating loss of KRW 1.08 billion in 2024 to KRW 23 billion in 2025, with an operating margin of 2.3%.

  3. 3

    Operating profit bottomed at KRW 5.94 billion in the first quarter of 2026, then rose to KRW 11.1 billion and KRW 15.6 billion in the second and third quarters, though the third quarter was down 5.5% year on year.

  4. 4

02

Business structure

KT Skylife operates a satellite broadcasting platform and, on a consolidated basis, three business lines: broadcasting, telecom (internet and mobile), and platform services (home-shopping carriage fees, T-commerce and advertising).

Its GTS bundle combines satellite TV with internet TV, and ipit TV, an IPTV product launched in July 2025, is becoming the company's new growth engine in wired services.

In the first-quarter 2026 results briefing disclosed on May 8, 2026, the company said ipit TV added a net 60,000 subscribers in the quarter alone, reaching 185,000 retained subscribers. The same briefing showed internet subscribers up by a net 33,000 to 632,000, and total retained subscribers of about 4.28 million.

On a standalone basis, telecom revenue growth of KRW 6.6 billion outpaced the KRW 3.3 billion decline in broadcasting revenue, according to the company. Satellite and GTS subscribers, however, are still falling, so the broadcasting base is shrinking.

Subsidiary kt ENA handles channel content and video sales, and the company said its advertising revenue market share rose from 6.7% to 7.2% year on year.

Subsidiary kt HCN is winding down its home-appliance installment business, and that wind-down along with lower ENA video sales reduced other revenue in the first quarter. The platform segment's first-quarter revenue slipped slightly under weak advertising and home-shopping markets.

The pay-TV market is contested by IPTV operators and cable operators, so differentiating satellite-based products is the key to retaining subscribers.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual sales fell from KRW 1.03 trillion in 2022 to KRW 1.03 trillion in 2023 (down 0.8%), KRW 1.02 trillion in 2024 (down 0.3%), and KRW 984.2 billion in 2025 (down 3.8%), a three-year consecutive decline from 2023 through 2025. The largest year in the 2022–2025 span was 2022, and the smallest was 2025.

Operating profit dropped from KRW 63.2 billion in 2022 to KRW 14.1 billion in 2023 (down 77.7%), turned into an operating loss of KRW 1.08 billion in 2024, and returned to profit at KRW 23 billion in 2025.

Operating margin was 6.1% in 2022, 1.4% in 2023, -0.1% in 2024 and 2.3% in 2025, recovering in 2025 but still below the 2022 level.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders went from KRW 22.4 billion in 2022 to net losses of KRW 97.9 billion in 2023 and KRW 135.9 billion in 2024, then returned to profit at KRW 6.94 billion in 2025. Total net income including non-controlling interests, at KRW 1.47 billion in 2025, is a different figure.

For the widening loss in 2024, Meritz Securities pointed to impairment losses related to HCN (a January 2025 report). Operating cash flow fell from KRW 176.4 billion in 2022 to KRW 207.2 billion in 2023, KRW 162.3 billion in 2024 and KRW 99.9 billion in 2025.

The debt ratio declined from 80.4% in 2024 to 73.3% in 2025, and total equity at the end of 2025 was KRW 563.2 billion. Quarterly, operating profit of KRW 16.5 billion in the third quarter of 2025 was followed by an operating loss of KRW 11.6 billion in the fourth quarter.

The first quarter of 2026 at KRW 5.94 billion, a return to profit, was the low point, followed by KRW 11.1 billion in the second quarter (up 87.0% from the prior quarter) and KRW 15.6 billion in the third (up 40.4%).

Third-quarter operating profit was still down 5.5% year on year, while net income attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 17.1 billion rose 28.2% year on year.

According to the company, the 16.2% year-on-year rise in first-quarter operating profit reflected lower advertising sales costs, lower home-appliance installment costs and lower amortization of intangible assets after adjustments to subsidiary content capex, while the cost of acquiring ipit TV subscribers and higher network usage fees weighed on profit.

05

Industry analysis

In pay TV, satellite broadcasting faces structural pressure as its subscriber base shrinks against IPTV and cable operators.

In an April 2026 report, Meritz Securities estimated that net broadcast subscriber losses would continue, with subscribers falling from 3.671 million in 2025 to 3.663 million in 2026 and 3.656 million in 2027.

Advertising and home-shopping markets are also weak; the company cited a slump in broadcasting and home-shopping markets as a drag on platform revenue in the first quarter of 2026. The same report expected broadcast advertising to rebound from the second quarter on sports events such as the North American World Cup.

By contrast, kt ENA's advertising market share rose from 6.7% to 7.2% year on year, which the company presented as a sign of stronger channel competitiveness.

In competition, telecom IPTV services from SK Telecom, KT and LG Uplus are growing wired markets through bundles, while Skylife responds with satellite TV combined with internet. It also competes for wired subscribers with cable operators such as LG HelloVision.

DB Securities said in a September 2026 report that growth in internet-based TV subscribers was insufficient to offset declines in satellite and GTS subscribers.

The industry is better described as in transition than in recovery, and whether internet-bundled products can fill the gap left by shrinking satellite subscribers will shape the company's position.

06

Outlook

On the conference call for its first-quarter 2026 results, the company said it would strengthen profitability through quality rather than quantity of growth. For the second quarter, it said it would focus on stable market settlement and profitability management rather than aggressive subscriber acquisition.

It also outlined plans to slow the decline in broadcast subscribers while finding new growth momentum through new businesses. In an April 2026 report, Meritz Securities estimated consolidated operating profit of KRW 411 billion for 2026 (up 78.8% year on year) and KRW 441 billion for 2027 (up 7.3%).

The same estimate saw the 2026 operating margin recovering to 4.1%. However, third-quarter 2026 operating profit of KRW 15.6 billion was down 5.5% year on year, which suggests that cost efficiency gains are not appearing evenly across quarters.

DB Securities projected third-quarter 2026 operating profit at KRW 10.1 billion in September 2026, while the disclosed figure was KRW 15.6 billion, so there was a gap between brokerage estimates and reported results. The gap depends on the pace of new subscriber inflows and marketing costs.

The next checkpoints are whether fourth-quarter 2026 operating profit holds near the third-quarter level and the full-year operating margin in the 2026 annual report. The company has not yet disclosed specific revenue scale or timing for its new businesses, so their contribution will be visible only in later filings.

08

Bull factors

Quarterly profit recovery

Operating profit bottomed at KRW 5.94 billion in the first quarter of 2026 and then rose to KRW 11.1 billion and KRW 15.6 billion over the next two quarters. Net income attributable to controlling shareholders followed a similar path, reaching KRW 17.1 billion in the third quarter.

Following the return to operating profit of KRW 23 billion in 2025, the company's move out of the loss phase is the core bullish argument.

ipit TV and internet bundle growth

ipit TV added a net 60,000 subscribers in the first quarter of 2026, reaching 185,000 retained subscribers. Internet subscribers rose by a net 33,000 to 632,000. According to the company, new TV-plus-internet subscribers totaled 128,000, up more than 47% from 87,000 a year earlier. Wired products are partly filling the gap left by declining broadcast subscribers.

Cost efficiency and content competitiveness

First-quarter operating profit growth was supported by lower advertising sales costs, lower home-appliance installment costs and lower intangible amortization.

Subsidiary kt ENA's advertising market share rose from 6.7% to 7.2%, and its channel ranking among viewers aged 20 to 49 in the Seoul metropolitan area improved from 11th to 7th, according to the company. Brokerage reports also pointed to the dividend appeal.

09

Bear factors

Pace of broadcast subscriber decline

DB Securities said in a September 2026 report that the decline in satellite and GTS subscribers exceeds the growth in internet-based TV subscribers. Meritz Securities also estimated in April 2026 that net broadcast subscriber losses would continue, reducing the subscriber base each year.

Falling broadcast service revenue, combined with weak advertising and home-shopping markets, limits sales growth potential.

Three years of falling sales and thin margins

Annual sales fell for three straight years from 2023 through 2025, reaching KRW 984.2 billion in 2025, the smallest in the 2022–2025 span. The 2025 operating margin was a thin 2.3%, and total net income was KRW 1.47 billion.

With the four-quarter total of net income attributable to controlling shareholders at KRW 6.15 billion, small changes in earnings produce large swings.

Uneven quarterly results

The company swung to an operating loss of KRW 11.6 billion in the fourth quarter of 2025, and third-quarter 2026 operating profit was down 5.5% year on year. DB Securities cited marketing costs and subscriber losses as factors weighing on third-quarter results. Its report also noted that dividend appeal is high but that revenue growth drivers are limited.

10

Risk factors

Subscriber mix risk

If net additions from ipit TV fail to offset declines in satellite and GTS subscribers, sales recovery could be delayed. Marketing costs to win subscribers and higher network usage fees slow the pace of operating profit recovery. Whether profit growth since the second quarter of 2026 can absorb these costs is the central question.

Subsidiary and intangible asset risk

Meritz Securities pointed to impairment losses related to HCN as a factor widening the 2024 loss. Subsidiary content investment and intangible amortization are items that can move consolidated earnings significantly.

Lower amortization helped the first-quarter recovery, so a renewed increase in content investment could pressure profit again.

Advertising and home-shopping market risk

The platform segment's revenue slipped in the first quarter of 2026 as broadcasting and home-shopping markets weakened. The advertising rebound rests on a forecast tied to sports events, so its timing and size are uncertain. Other revenue is also shrinking because of the home-appliance installment wind-down and lower ENA video sales.

12

Overall view

Skylife's sales have declined since 2023, yet the company returned both operating profit and net income attributable to controlling shareholders to profit in 2025.

In 2026, operating profit and controlling-shareholder net income have each risen for two straight quarters since the first-quarter low, making the direction of recovery clearer.

However, third-quarter operating profit was down year on year, and the four-quarter net income total of KRW 6.15 billion is small, so earnings volatility remains high.

On subscribers, ipit TV and internet bundles are driving net additions, but brokerage analysis suggests satellite and GTS losses are faster, limiting sales recovery.

Bullish factors are cost efficiency and growth in wired products, while bearish factors are broadcast subscriber declines, weak advertising markets and subsidiary amortization risk. On valuation, the stock combines a low price-to-book level with high dividend appeal on a small earnings base.

The items to watch are fourth-quarter 2026 results, the full-year operating margin in the 2026 annual report, and the net subscriber additions in the first quarter of 2027. This report is for informational purposes and does not include a buy or sell opinion.
