02

Business structure

Daesan F&B is centered on pork processing and food ingredient distribution. After acquiring Daesan Pork in 2021, the company restructured itself around meat processing (cited above). In 2023 it spun off its pizza business into Mr. Pizza, which it now holds as an equity stake.

The business is split between franchise and restaurant operations in the food service unit and pork processing in the pork unit, with facilities being moved to Daejeong-dong to expand scale. On the cost side, it contracts directly with many farms and uses four slaughterhouses.

Quality certifications include HACCP and antibiotic-free livestock handling, and it obtained the Hanwoo-style 'Hanton' brand approval in September 2025. In the first half of 2026, distribution-channel sales were KRW 23.7 billion, or 42.2% of total revenue of KRW 56.2 billion.

Online B2B platform sales were KRW 2.9 billion over the same period, 12.3% of distribution revenue. Its wholly owned subsidiaries are FMpresh and DS Oilbank, and as of March 2026 it held stakes in Mr. Pizza (5.57%) and other investees. Compared with listed food peers such as Harim and CJ Cheiljedang, its capital base is small.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual revenue went from KRW 150 billion in 2022 to KRW 128.8 trillion in 2023 (up 85,759.2% year on year), fell to KRW 102.9 billion in 2024 (down 99.9%), and was KRW 110.9 billion in 2025 (up 7.8%). Among 2022 to 2025, 2023 was the largest year and 2024 the smallest.

Operating results moved from an operating loss of KRW 5.37 billion in 2022 and KRW 634.9 billion in 2023 (losses widened) to KRW 821 million in 2024 (losses narrowed), then to operating profit of KRW 4.55 billion in 2025, a turn to profit.

Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 8.32 billion in 2025, also a turn to profit; net profit including non-controlling interests was the same KRW 8.32 billion. Operating margin went from -3.6% in 2022, -0.5% in 2023 and -0.8% in 2024 to 4.1% in 2025.

The company cited a stronger core operating activity and an improved profit structure from disposal of property, plant and equipment as the main drivers of the 2025 result. Because the 2025 profit includes that disposal effect, whether it repeats will be gauged from the operating margin in the next annual report.

The debt ratio fell from 260.9% in 2024 to 53.9% in 2025, with year-end 2025 total equity of KRW 24.3 billion and total liabilities of KRW 13.1 billion. Operating cash flow moved from -KRW 356 million in 2024 to KRW 4.88 billion in 2025.

Quarterly revenue fell from KRW 28.9 billion in Q3 2025 to KRW 25.3 billion in Q1 2026 (down 7.3% quarter on quarter), then rose to KRW 30.8 billion in Q2 (up 21.5%) and KRW 34.2 billion in Q3 (up 10.9%), two straight quarterly gains.

Operating profit peaked at KRW 1.35 billion in Q1 2026 and then fell to KRW 977 million in Q2 and KRW 740 million in Q3, two straight declines; Q3 was down 32.4% year on year.

Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 662 million in Q3 2026, down 81.4% year on year, and the four quarters from Q4 2025 to Q3 2026 total KRW 6.99 billion. Press reports put Q1 2026 operating cash flow at negative KRW 1.5 billion, a larger outflow than a year earlier.

05

Industry analysis

Pork processing is seasonal, with raw-material purchasing timed ahead of selling periods. According to the company, a pattern has repeated in which it secures inventory in the first quarter and sells it through the second and third quarters.

Because of this pattern, the cash outflow from higher inventory in Q1 2026 was about KRW 1 billion. Seasonality amplifies quarter-to-quarter swings, and advance raw-material purchases feed into working-capital strain.

Judging where the business sits in the industry cycle requires external indicators such as hog prices and feed costs. The company has been strengthening a domestic-pork-based business. Listed food-sector peers include Harim, CJ Cheiljedang and Dongwon Industries.

Daesan F&B is close to a single meat-processing axis and has a smaller scale and capital base, so its business breadth is narrower than theirs. Its stated differentiator is same-day production and same-day delivery of ordered cuts. It has also widened its distribution channels to B2B online platforms such as Meatbox and Topmeat.

06

Outlook

The company plans to keep its existing meat-processing production base while expanding B2B online sales, with the aim of sustaining an operating margin of roughly 4% or higher. It intends to keep expanding online channels to secure a steadier revenue base.

A medium-term item is entry into smart pig farming, using AI-based temperature and ventilation control and building-type pig housing to extend the business to the rearing stage. Specific investment size and start timing have not yet been disclosed and will come in later filings.

Given equity of KRW 24.3 billion, the effect of rearing-facility investment on the balance sheet is a point to check in the annual report.

On the shareholder side, CEO Kim Sang-wook bought about KRW 100 million of shares from November 11, 2026, raising his personal stake to 2.35% and the largest shareholder group's stake to 24.48%. The company said it is reviewing a shareholder-return measure.

It attributed the Q1 operating cash outflow to its business structure and said it will focus on core competitiveness and profitability. Analysts argue that stable cash flow is needed to restore corporate value after the listing was maintained.

The next checkpoint is the 2026 annual report, where the annual operating margin and operating cash flow will be confirmed.

08

Bull factors

Return to profit and recovering earnings base

Operating profit turned positive in 2025 at KRW 4.55 billion, and the company reported first-half 2026 operating profit of KRW 2.33 billion, about 51% of the full-year 2025 figure. The operating margin moved from -0.8% in 2024 to 4.1% in 2025, which supports the bull case on direction. Compared with the loss-making 2023 and 2024, the earnings structure looks different.

Broader distribution channels

Online B2B platform sales reached KRW 2.9 billion in the first half of 2026, 12.3% of distribution-channel revenue. The company said it will expand online channels to keep its operating margin at about 4% or higher.

Same-day production and delivery and HACCP and antibiotic-free certifications are presented as the basis of customer trust.

Controlling shareholder commitment and balance sheet repair

CEO Kim Sang-wook bought about KRW 100 million of shares, lifting the largest shareholder group's stake to 24.48%. According to the company, the consolidated debt ratio fell from 220.5% at end-2024 to 36.3% at end-June 2026.

The July 2026 decision to keep the listing resumed trading and removed the stock from the administrative-issue list, which reduces shareholder uncertainty.

09

Bear factors

Cash flow deficit and working-capital strain

Press-reported operating cash outflow in Q1 2026 was negative KRW 1.5 billion, larger than the negative KRW 1.1 billion a year earlier. Rising receivables, falling payables and higher prepayments all added to the outflow. Cash and equivalents fell from KRW 1.5 billion at end-2025 to KRW 200 million at end-Q1 2026. This contrasts with the positive full-year 2025 operating cash flow.

Slowing quarterly earnings

Revenue rose in Q2 and Q3 2026, yet operating profit fell from KRW 1.35 billion in Q1 to KRW 740 million in Q3, two straight declines. Q3 2026 net profit attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 662 million, down 81.4% year on year, so revenue growth has not translated into profit growth.

Q3 operating profit was down 32.4% year on year. Defending profit during seasonally weaker selling periods remains a challenge.

Listing history and investment-stake uncertainty

The company's trading was previously suspended after a disclaimer of opinion on its 2023 annual audit. Trading resumed after the exchange decided to keep the listing in July 2026. A July 2026 press report said most of its investee companies had recorded net losses. Results from investment stakes can move independently of core operating profit, which remains an uncertainty.

10

Risk factors

Accounting and governance

The company has a history of prolonged trading suspensions tied to accounting issues and a substantive listing review. A January 2025 rights offering changed the largest shareholder to Daesan Holdings Company, and a re-audit in February 2025 yielded a clean opinion.

A new accounting-expert auditor was appointed at the March 2026 general meeting, and whether this restores long-term confidence will be shown by future audit outcomes.

Raw-material purchasing and seasonality

Because of the pattern of building inventory in Q1 and selling it through Q2 and Q3, quarterly cash flow swings widely. If hog-price volatility coincides with advance raw-material purchases, working capital could grow larger. Whether cash continues to get tied up during revenue growth is a point to monitor.

Expansion and capital capacity

Entry into smart pig farming extends the business from processing and distribution into rearing, which could bring facility-investment burdens. Specific investment size and timing have not been disclosed.

Given year-end 2025 total equity of KRW 24.3 billion, the effect of funding sources and borrowing on the balance sheet needs to be considered together.

Overall view

Daesan F&B is centered on pork processing and food distribution, and its 2025 return to operating profit changed the earnings direction. The annual operating margin rose to 4.1% in 2025, and the debt ratio fell to 53.9%.

On the other hand, quarterly operating profit in 2026 peaked at KRW 1.35 billion in Q1 and then fell for two straight quarters, and Q1 2026 operating cash flow was negative.

The July 2026 listing decision resumed trading, and the CEO's share purchase and larger controlling stake followed, but its accounting history and investment-stake results still need monitoring.
