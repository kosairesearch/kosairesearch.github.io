Pamtek: Sales Recovery Meets Continued Losses

Pamtek's sales rose 39.8% year on year in the third quarter of 2026, but an operating loss of KRW 3.93 billion extended a third straight quarter of losses, so the timing of profit conversion from its semiconductor equipment business is the key checkpoint.

  1. 1

    Third-quarter 2026 sales reached KRW 10.8 billion, up 39.8% year on year, with camera module equipment revenue on a standalone basis rising 123.9% and leading the expansion.

  2. 2

    The operating loss widened to KRW 3.93 billion in the same quarter, marking a third consecutive quarter of operating losses since the first quarter of 2026.

  3. 3

    Semiconductor equipment grew sharply, with standalone first-half 2026 sales up about 406% year on year and nine-month cumulative semiconductor equipment sales up 102.8%.

  4. 4

  5. 5

02

Business structure

Pamtek is a developer and manufacturer of automation equipment applied in camera modules, semiconductors and smart factories, with local subsidiaries in Vietnam and China.

Its core business is camera module equipment, and it supplies high-spec lineups such as multi-camera and 3D sensor solutions, while in semiconductors it supplies wafer transfer robots among other products.

The business mix is diversifying: on a standalone basis, the semiconductor share rose from 6.7% to 18.7% and the mobility and other share rose from 18.1% to 21.7%. Camera module revenue in the first half of 2026 was KRW 7.73 billion, up 44.9% year on year.

Semiconductor revenue on a standalone basis was KRW 2.43 billion in the first half of 2026, up about 406% year on year, driven by Peltier-based test equipment for AI chip inspection, wafer handling robots and wafer sorters.

Its subsidiary TIS, a robot and semiconductor equipment maker acquired recently, has its results consolidated, and TIS supplied 8-inch wafer handling robots and sorting equipment for power semiconductors in the first half of 2026.

On the customer side, Pamtek signed a KRW 3.12 billion inspection equipment supply contract with LG Electronics in June 2026, running from June 8 to December 31, 2026.

In semiconductors, it began direct trading with a large domestic integrated chip maker in November 2026 and is co-developing handlers and inspection equipment.

Camera module equipment results depend on customer investment cycles, while semiconductor equipment must pass supplier registration and co-development before moving into volume supply.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual sales fell from KRW 101.9 billion in 2022 to KRW 96.1 billion in 2023 (down 5.6% year on year), KRW 44.4 billion in 2024 (down 53.9%) and KRW 38.7 billion in 2025 (down 12.7%). Sales declined for three consecutive years from 2023 through 2025; 2022 was the largest year and 2025 the smallest within 2022 to 2025.

Consolidated sales fell in 2025, but gross profit rose from KRW 9.99 billion in 2024 to KRW 11.35 billion in 2025. The company attributed the weakness to a shift toward more conservative equipment investment amid a global economic slowdown and weaker consumption from inflation.

Operating profit fell from KRW 24.6 billion in 2022 to KRW 10.8 billion in 2023 (down 55.9%), turned to an operating loss of KRW 3.73 billion in 2024, and the loss widened to KRW 4.13 billion in 2025. The operating margin went from 24.1% in 2022 to 11.3% in 2023, -8.4% in 2024 and -10.7% in 2025.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders went from KRW 16.9 billion in 2022 to KRW 7.51 billion in 2023 (down 55.5%), then to a net loss of KRW 107 million in 2024 and a net loss of KRW 6.16 billion in 2025.

Net income including non-controlling interests, which was a net loss of KRW 5.41 billion in 2025, is a different figure from the controlling-shareholder figure.

Quarterly, the fourth quarter of 2025 posted an operating profit of KRW 1.76 billion, a swing to profit, but the first quarter of 2026 returned to an operating loss of KRW 3.01 billion on sales of KRW 5.14 billion, down 77.3% from the prior quarter.

The company pointed to upfront costs for new equipment development and customer support. Second-quarter 2026 sales rose 84.0% quarter on quarter to KRW 9.46 billion, and the operating loss narrowed to KRW 2.68 billion.

The company judged the rise in material cost ratio from rush orders and upfront production as temporary and planned to normalize the cost ratio in the second half. Third-quarter 2026 sales were KRW 10.8 billion, up 39.8% year on year, but the operating loss of KRW 3.93 billion widened from a year earlier.

Combined net losses attributable to controlling shareholders over the last four quarters total KRW 10 billion. The main challenge is that the sales recovery has not yet translated into better profit.

05

Industry analysis

In camera module equipment, FnGuide's company summary attributes revenue growth to wider adoption of high-spec optical components and advancing autonomous driving technology in automotive camera modules.

On the semiconductor side, the same summary points to rising demand for high-performance chips driven by the acceleration of the AI industry. In packaging, Pamtek showed automation and test temperature control equipment for semiconductor packaging at KPCA SHOW 2026 as it expands its semiconductor equipment business.

FnGuide named the start of HBM4 mass production as a new growth driver, but none of Pamtek's disclosed major orders identified so far involves HBM-related equipment. The downstream capex cycle turned conservative in 2025, and in 2026 a camera module recovery and semiconductor equipment expansion are overlapping.

Semiconductor equipment sales roughly doubled from KRW 4.1 billion in 2024 to KRW 8.5 billion in 2025 on a standalone basis, though the absolute scale remains small.

Koh Young Technology, a sector peer focused on inspection equipment, overlaps with Pamtek in inspection, but Pamtek's earnings base rests on camera module equipment.

Extending into back-end semiconductor equipment changes the business structure, and since its revenue share is still low, customer adoption in volume production will determine its position in the industry.

06

Outlook

In a June 2026 report, Hana Securities noted that Pamtek presented 2026 sales guidance of KRW 65 billion at a 5% operating margin and 2027 sales of KRW 100 billion at a 10 to 15% operating margin. The same report noted that the timing of quarterly profit expansion is not yet clear.

Nine-month cumulative 2026 sales reached KRW 25.37 billion, up 57.7% year on year. Compared with the KRW 65 billion guidance, reaching the target requires a large step-up in fourth-quarter sales.

The company said it expects fourth-quarter results to improve as orders secured and deferred semiconductor equipment shipments are recognized, and that growth should continue in 2027. Standalone backlog at the end of the third quarter stood at KRW 35.9 billion, and secured volume will be recognized sequentially.

The Peltier-based memory test handler is under equipment validation, and the company plans to pursue supply to a 2027 mass-production line based on the results. Using the technology of its subsidiary TIS, the company plans to expand semiconductor equipment while growing its camera module business in parallel.

The LG Electronics supply contract runs through December 31, 2026, so follow-on orders after that date are the next checkpoint. The fourth convertible bond series, worth KRW 10 billion, was disclosed on July 2, 2026, with conversion rights starting from July 10, 2027.

08

Bull factors

Rapid growth in semiconductor equipment sales

Standalone semiconductor sales in the first half of 2026 rose about 406% year on year, and cumulative nine-month semiconductor equipment sales reached KRW 3.15 billion, up 102.8%. The company said it completed supplier registration with a large integrated chip maker and began direct trading. Wafer transfer robot expansion relies on the WTR and EFEM technology of its subsidiary TIS.

Sales recovery with two straight quarters of growth

Third-quarter 2026 sales rose 13.7% from the prior quarter, and sales have grown for two consecutive quarters since the first-quarter low of 2026. Standalone camera module equipment sales jumped 123.9% year on year in the third quarter. Cumulative nine-month sales rose 57.7% year on year.

Order backlog and planned cost-ratio normalization

Standalone order backlog at the end of the third quarter of 2026 stood at KRW 35.9 billion. The company judged the rise in material cost ratio as temporary and planned to normalize it in the second half.

Consolidated gross profit rose from KRW 9.99 billion in 2024 to KRW 11.35 billion in 2025, showing that the cost structure can shift relative to sales.

09

Bear factors

Three straight quarters of operating losses with widening loss

Pamtek has posted operating losses for three consecutive quarters from the first quarter to the third quarter of 2026. The third-quarter operating loss of KRW 3.93 billion was larger than a year earlier. Rising material costs were cited as a loss factor in the first half, yet losses widened again in the third quarter. Combined net losses attributable to controlling shareholders over the last four quarters total KRW 10 billion.

Gap between guidance and quarterly results

Hana Securities cited the KRW 65 billion 2026 sales guidance but also noted that the timing of quarterly profit expansion is unclear. Against nine-month cumulative sales of KRW 25.37 billion, reaching the guidance requires a large increase in the fourth quarter.

The same report said a clearer valuation estimate would become possible once semiconductor customers adopt the products in mass-production lines.

Camera module dependence and conservative capex

In 2025 the company attributed weaker results to conservative equipment investment amid a global slowdown and softer consumption from inflation, which produced an annual operating loss of KRW 4.13 billion. Camera module equipment remains the core of earnings and is sensitive to changes in customer investment. The largest shareholder group held 44.25% as of July 2025, a concentrated ownership structure.

10

Risk factors

Business concentration and downstream capex cycle

Camera module equipment remains the center of earnings, so quarterly sales can swing widely with customer investment timing. Semiconductor equipment sales reached KRW 8.5 billion in 2025, but the absolute scale is still modest.

Until the new businesses grow to a larger share, earnings remain tied to the investment cycle of the existing business.

Cost ratios and upfront production costs

Rush-order handling and upfront production raised the material cost ratio. The company called this temporary, but normalization in the second half has not yet been confirmed in results. Upfront costs for new equipment development and customer support kept operating results in the red. Development costs in new semiconductor businesses continue to weigh on quarterly profit and loss.

Listing procedures and financing burden

In June 2026 the company applied to suspend a delisting decision effective June 8 until a final ruling in the related lawsuit. The stock was designated an investment-warning issue the same month. In July 2026 a KRW 10 billion fourth convertible bond series was disclosed with a conversion price of KRW 1,524. Conversions would add dilution pressure on per-share value.

12

Overall view

Pamtek is in a phase where sales are recovering, led by camera module equipment. Third-quarter 2026 sales were KRW 10.8 billion, up 39.8% year on year, and nine-month cumulative sales rose 57.7%.

Semiconductor equipment and wafer handling through subsidiary TIS are expanding, and standalone backlog at the end of the third quarter was KRW 35.9 billion. On the other side, operating losses have continued for three straight quarters from the first quarter of 2026, and the loss widened in the third quarter.

The 2025 operating margin was -10.7%, and net losses attributable to controlling shareholders have continued for two years since 2024. How the gap between the KRW 65 billion 2026 sales guidance cited by Hana Securities and nine-month sales is closed in the fourth quarter is the key question.

Procedures around the delisting decision and financing events such as the convertible bond are also ongoing. The next checkpoints are the annual operating margin and fourth-quarter results in the 2026 annual report, and whether the 2027 mass-production supply materializes.
