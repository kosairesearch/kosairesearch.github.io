US Extra-High-Voltage Orders Drive Earnings Recovery

02

Business structure

Hyosung Heavy Industries runs two segments, Heavy Industries and Construction. Heavy Industries generated KRW 4.15 trillion of 2025 revenue, more than half of the KRW 5.97 trillion total, according to the segment table in an April 2026 report by Eugene Investment & Securities.

Its core products are extra-high-voltage transformers and extra-high-voltage circuit breakers. The Memphis plant is the only facility in the United States able to design and produce 765kV transformers, and the company says it has held the top share of the US 765kV transformer market since the early 2010s.

Customers are expanding from US utilities to US technology companies building AI data centers. In Europe, the company signed a long-term supply contract with a German transmission operator covering extra-high-voltage transformers and reactors.

In circuit breakers, a single contract with a large US utility was reported as the largest of its kind. The Construction segment is smaller in revenue, and its profit slowed temporarily in the third quarter of 2026 as site construction costs rose at some projects.

The company describes itself as one of the four global leaders in power equipment, and listed sector peers include LS ELECTRIC and HD Hyundai Electric, which compete with it for large transformer orders.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual results show revenue and profit growing together. Revenue rose from KRW 3.51 trillion in 2022 to KRW 4.3 trillion in 2023 (up 22.5%), KRW 4.89 trillion in 2024 (up 13.8%) and KRW 5.97 trillion in 2025 (up 21.9%), and it has grown for three consecutive years from 2023 through 2025.

Operating profit went from KRW 143.2 billion in 2022 to KRW 257.8 billion in 2023, KRW 362.5 billion in 2024 and KRW 747 billion in 2025, a 106.1% increase in 2025. Operating margin rose each year, from 4.1% in 2022 to 6.0%, 7.4% and 12.5% in 2025.

Because operating profit grew far faster than revenue in 2025, margin improvement rather than volume growth appears to be the main driver. Net income attributable to controlling shareholders rose from KRW 29.1 billion in 2022 to KRW 519.9 billion in 2025, up 133.5% in the latest year.

Net income including non-controlling interests was KRW 502.8 billion in 2025, below the controlling figure, which suggests non-controlling interests were a loss in that year.

The balance sheet also improved: the debt-to-equity ratio fell from 325.4% in 2022 to 190.3% in 2025, with total equity of KRW 2.49 trillion and total liabilities of KRW 4.74 trillion at the end of 2025.

Operating cash flow was -KRW 67.1 billion in 2022 and then KRW 454.7 billion, KRW 412.1 billion and KRW 493 billion in 2023 through 2025. Quarterly, operating profit was KRW 219.8 billion in the third quarter of 2025 and KRW 260.5 billion in the fourth quarter, up 18.5% from the prior quarter.

It bottomed at KRW 152.3 billion in the first quarter of 2026, down 41.5% from the prior quarter, then rebounded to KRW 264.3 billion in the second quarter, up 73.5%.

In the third quarter of 2026, operating profit was KRW 243.5 billion, down 7.9% from the prior quarter but up 10.8% from a year earlier, on revenue of KRW 1.74 trillion.

Controlling shareholders' net income reached KRW 219.6 billion in that quarter, up 46.2% from a year earlier, and the last four quarters total KRW 654.5 billion. The first-quarter trough came even as new orders hit a quarterly record, because orders convert to revenue with a lag.

05

Industry analysis

The power equipment industry is seeing growing demand from AI data centers and from the replacement of aging transmission and distribution networks at the same time. About 70% of large US transformers are reported to be more than 25 years old, which points to replacement demand building over time.

The US Department of Energy has said roughly 90% of the country's electricity passes through large transformers.

The US transformer market is expected to grow at an average annual rate of about 7.7%, from KRW 17.8 trillion (USD 12.2 billion) in 2024 to KRW 37.5 trillion (USD 25.7 billion) in 2034, according to industry estimates. Renewable energy expansion and EV charging infrastructure are also cited as demand drivers.

In cycle terms, orders are rising ahead of revenue, and the growth in backlog in 2026 reflects that stage. Hyosung Heavy Industries says it has held the top share of the US 765kV transformer market since the early 2010s, and 765kV designs reduce transmission losses compared with 345kV or 500kV.

The company also says it became the first Korean supplier to deliver a full 765kV transformer and 800kV circuit breaker package to a US grid operator. Listed peers in the same sector include LS ELECTRIC and HD Hyundai Electric, which compete for large transformer orders and capacity investment. The company's differentiation rests on combining 765kV design capability with a US production base.

06

Outlook

For 2026, the company's guidance, as summarized by LS Securities in a February 2026 report, calls for Heavy Industries orders up at least 10% from a year earlier, revenue up 15%, and an operating margin in the upper mid-teens.

First-quarter 2026 new orders were KRW 4.17 trillion, more than double the prior-year level, and third-quarter new orders were KRW 3.23 trillion, up 122% from a year earlier. A September 2026 report said the company raised its full-year new order target from KRW 8.4 trillion to KRW 12 trillion, a 42.9% increase.

The same report said new orders through August 2026 reached about KRW 9.3 trillion, already above the full-year 2025 total. The company said in its third-quarter materials that some deferred volumes are expected to be recognized in fourth-quarter revenue.

On capacity, the company plans to invest USD 157 million (about KRW 230 billion) at its Memphis extra-high-voltage transformer plant to lift capacity by more than 50% by 2028, a plan announced in November 2025, which would make Memphis the largest production base in the United States.

In circuit breakers, the company is serving North American demand, including a single contract with a large US utility reported as the largest of its kind.

The chairman has described the power infrastructure demand as a supercycle not seen in 60 years and has set a goal of reaching the global top three within five years.

The next points to confirm are whether deferred volumes were recognized in fourth-quarter 2026 results and how close full-year orders came to the KRW 12 trillion target.

08

Bull factors

US 765kV position and backlog

Hyosung Heavy Industries runs the only US plant able to design and produce 765kV extra-high-voltage transformers, and says it has supplied nearly half of the 765kV transformers installed on the US grid. Cumulative backlog reached KRW 18.9 trillion at the end of the third quarter of 2026, a record. A larger backlog widens visibility on future revenue.

Improved earnings profile

Operating margin rose from 4.1% in 2022 to 12.5% in 2025, and 2025 operating profit of KRW 747 billion was up 106.1% from a year earlier. Over the same period the debt-to-equity ratio fell from 325.4% to 190.3%.

Third-quarter 2026 operating profit of KRW 243.5 billion, up 10.8% from a year earlier, shows the recovery continuing at the quarterly level.

Structural power demand

Industry estimates project the US transformer market to grow about 7.7% a year, from USD 12.2 billion in 2024 to USD 25.7 billion in 2034. Demand drivers cited include AI data center expansion, replacement of aging grids and higher renewable penetration. The chairman has called this a supercycle and stated a goal of reaching the global top three within five years.

Bear factors

Quarterly earnings volatility

Operating profit fell 41.5% from the prior quarter to KRW 152.3 billion in the first quarter of 2026, and controlling shareholders' net income dropped 50.1% to KRW 87.3 billion. Operating profit also declined from the prior quarter in the first and third quarters of 2026.

Revenue growth can coexist with quarterly swings driven by construction costs and the timing of revenue recognition, which is the core of the bear case.

Construction costs and order selectivity

The Construction segment's profit slowed temporarily in the third quarter of 2026 as site costs rose at some projects. The company has said it will keep selective, profit-focused ordering, which could limit how fast order volume grows.

With the full-year order target raised to KRW 12 trillion, the quality of orders will be tested as that target is pursued.

Valuation burden

The price-to-book ratio sits in a range carrying a large premium over net asset value, and the dividend yield is low. In such a range, an earnings growth shortfall would make valuation strain more visible.

Controlling-shareholder equity was KRW 2.35 trillion at the end of 2025, so the premium has to be supported by continued earnings growth.

10

Risk factors

Revenue recognition timing

Large extra-high-voltage projects can shift revenue between quarters depending on installation and delivery timing. The company said some deferred volumes are expected to be recognized in fourth-quarter revenue. Longer deferrals could make quarterly margins more volatile and slow the conversion of backlog into revenue.

US policy and trade environment

A large share of the business relies on US production and US customers, so changes in US trade policy, tariffs or grid investment budgets could affect order flow. With about KRW 230 billion going into the Memphis expansion through 2028, a shift in policy could also change when the capacity benefits arrive. The high concentration in the US market enlarges this risk.

Raw material costs and pass-through

Power equipment costs are sensitive to raw materials such as copper and electrical steel, as well as to exchange rates. The Construction segment has already faced higher site costs in the third quarter of 2026.

If raw material increases cannot be passed through under fixed-price contracts, margins could come under pressure.

Overall view

Hyosung Heavy Industries recorded annual increases in revenue, operating profit and controlling shareholders' net income in each year from 2023 through 2025, and its operating margin rose from 4.1% to 12.5%.

The recovery rests on its share of the US 765kV extra-high-voltage transformer market and its local production base at Memphis. A backlog of KRW 18.9 trillion at the end of the third quarter of 2026 widens visibility on future revenue.

Quarterly results have recovered for two consecutive quarters since the first-quarter 2026 trough, but they remain volatile with project timing and construction costs.

The debt-to-equity ratio fell to 190.3% in 2025, yet the price-to-book ratio sits in a premium range, so whether earnings growth can support that premium is the central question. The bull case rests on demand structure and market position, and the bear case on quarterly volatility and valuation strain.

The next points to confirm are whether the KRW 12 trillion full-year order target is reached, the preliminary 2026 fourth-quarter results and the 2026 annual report. This report is provided for information only and does not include a buy or sell opinion.
