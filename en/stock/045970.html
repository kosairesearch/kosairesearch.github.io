02

Business structure

CoAsia runs two businesses: electronic component manufacturing, centered on smartphone camera modules and optical lenses, and system semiconductors.

Based on FnGuide's 2024 breakdown, camera/lens modules accounted for 74.71% of revenue, LED for 16.58%, system semiconductors for 9.01%, and technology-business finance for 1.41%.

Camera modules are focused on high-resolution products for Galaxy smartphones and optical lens production, and the company is widening its lineup with automotive and home-appliance LEDs.

System semiconductors are handled by subsidiary CoAsia SEMI, which the company says applies its packaging technology to domestic and overseas AI chip development projects and is discussing collaboration with global HPC companies.

In June 2025 it started a national PIM semiconductor project with Rebellions, a domestic AI fabless company. A technology analysis report from January 2022 described Samsung Electronics-related sales as the driver of growth in 2020.

Cumulative system semiconductor revenue through Q3 2026 rose 227%, and its share of total revenue increased from 14% to 36%. An industry insider called CoAsia one of the few domestic companies combining foundry design solutions with optical module technology. Camera module earnings, however, depend heavily on smartphone demand and orders from major customers.

04

Earnings analysis

On a DART-disclosed basis, annual revenue went from KRW 459.7 billion in 2022 to KRW 377.6 billion in 2023 (down 17.9% year on year) and KRW 357.8 billion in 2024 (down 5.2%), before rising to KRW 375.1 billion in 2025 (up 4.8%). 2022 was the largest year and 2024 the smallest within 2022 to 2025.

Operating losses were KRW 22.6 billion in 2022, KRW 25.5 billion in 2023 and KRW 39.6 billion in 2024, widening before narrowing to KRW 2.24 billion in 2025. The company posted operating losses in each year from 2022 to 2025, and operating margins were -4.9%, -6.8%, -11.1% and -0.6%.

Net loss attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 27.2 billion in 2022, KRW 26.4 billion in 2023, KRW 46.9 billion in 2024 and KRW 14 billion in 2025.

Net loss including non-controlling interests, which is a different figure, was KRW 30.6 billion, KRW 40.1 billion, KRW 63.5 billion and KRW 19.2 billion in the same years.

Operating cash flow was KRW 26.3 billion in 2022 and KRW 958 million in 2023, turned negative at -KRW 39.3 billion in 2024, and recovered to KRW 13 billion in 2025. At end-2025, total equity was KRW 121.8 billion, of which controlling shareholders held KRW 77.5 billion, and total liabilities were KRW 238.6 billion.

The debt ratio rose from 151.5% in 2022 to 195.9% in 2025. Quarterly revenue moved from KRW 90.7 billion in Q3 2025 to KRW 81.9 billion in Q4 2025 (down 9.7% quarter on quarter), then KRW 121.8 billion in Q1 2026 (up 48.7%) and KRW 132.1 billion in Q2 2026 (up 8.4%).

Q3 2026 revenue was KRW 114.9 billion, down 13.0% from the prior quarter but up 26.6% from a year earlier.

Quarterly operating results went from a KRW 1.18 billion loss in Q3 2025 and a KRW 6.58 billion loss in Q4 2025 to a KRW 2.03 billion loss in Q1 2026 and a KRW 3.04 billion loss in Q2 2026, before Q3 2026 turned to a KRW 3.1 billion operating profit.

In August 2025 the company attributed its first-half 2025 return to operating profit to growth in new system semiconductor projects and an improved product mix in electronic components.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders moved from KRW 2.23 billion in Q1 2026 and KRW 368 million in Q2 2026 to a KRW 4.94 billion loss in Q3 2026. The four quarters from Q4 2025 to Q3 2026 total a net loss attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 6.63 billion.

According to press coverage of the Q3 report, cumulative operating loss for January to September 2026 was KRW 2.0 billion.

05

Industry analysis

CoAsia's business rests on two demand drivers: smartphone camera module demand for set components, and AI and HPC semiconductor demand. A October 2025 press report argued that high-resolution camera modules and automotive LEDs have high technology barriers and can form a stable earnings base.

The same report noted that global LED demand for high-value products is rising under energy-efficiency regulation and green policies. For the system semiconductor side, the end-market is AI data centers.

In an August 2025 announcement, the company said it expects to complete product development and verification by end-2026 and to supply large volumes to AI data centers in Korea and abroad. A related press headline dated 2 November 2026 refers to mass shipment of a leading-edge big-die chip for HPC customers.

On competition, an industry insider rated CoAsia as one of the few domestic companies combining foundry design solutions with optical module technology.

Cumulative system semiconductor revenue share was 14% through Q3 2025 and 36% through Q3 2026, so the mix of smartphone set-component demand and AI chip demand in earnings is shifting. Competitive intensity in AI chips and whether large customers adopt the products are points to check in upcoming disclosures.

Outlook

The main dated item the company has disclosed is a target of end-2026 for completing development and verification of its AI semiconductor products (based on the August 2025 announcement). In the same announcement, the company said it expects to supply large volumes to AI data centers in Korea and abroad.

System semiconductor subsidiary CoAsia SEMI is applying its packaging technology to domestic and overseas AI chip development projects and continuing discussions with global HPC companies. A press headline dated 2 November 2026 refers to mass shipment of a leading-edge big-die chip for HPC customers.

Based on press coverage of the Q3 2026 report, consolidated revenue for January to September 2026 was KRW 368.8 billion, up 26% from KRW 293.2 billion a year earlier, and cumulative system semiconductor revenue rose 227%.

Electronic component manufacturing, centered on smartphone camera modules, saw Q3 2026 revenue fall 13.0% from the prior quarter. The next checkpoint is therefore whether Q4 2026 revenue keeps the system semiconductor expansion going while offsetting the decline in electronic components.

Given that full-year 2025 results were disclosed on 13 February 2026, Q4 2026 preliminary results are expected around mid-February 2027. The 2026 annual business report is due within 90 days of fiscal year-end, by 31 March 2027, and will formalize the Q4 and full-year 2026 results.

Risk factors

Customer and business concentration

The camera module business depends on smartphone demand and orders from major customers. A January 2022 technology analysis report described Samsung Electronics-related sales as the driver of earnings. A change in customer mix could cause large quarterly swings in the electronic components segment.

Financial structure and liquidity

The debt ratio at end-2025 was 195.9%, with total liabilities of KRW 238.6 billion. Operating cash flow swung from -KRW 39.3 billion in 2024 to KRW 13 billion in 2025.

Whether quarterly operating profit carries into full-year profit, and whether net income turns positive on a sustained basis, will become clearer from results after Q4 2026.

Commercialization uncertainty

The target for completing development and verification of AI semiconductor products is end-2026. Large-scale mass production and supply come after verification, and customer adoption and production timing could slip beyond plans.

The timing of commercialization for the national PIM project with a domestic AI fabless company is not part of the company's stated schedule.

Overall view

CoAsia recorded operating losses in each year from 2022 to 2025, but the operating margin narrowed from -4.9% to -0.6%. Q3 2026 turned to an operating profit of KRW 3.1 billion, while net loss attributable to controlling shareholders in the same quarter was KRW 4.94 billion.

The business mix is shifting from camera modules toward system semiconductors, and cumulative revenue for January to September 2026 rose 26% year on year. On the financial side, the debt ratio was 195.9% at end-2025, and operating cash flow turned negative in 2024 before recovering in 2025.

Price-to-book sits around 1x, and the price-to-earnings ratio is not calculated because of net losses over the last four quarters. Bullish factors are system semiconductor growth and AI chip projects, while bearish factors are cumulative net losses, high leverage and dependence on smartphone camera modules.

The next checkpoints are the end-2026 target for AI chip development and verification, preliminary full-year 2026 results around mid-February 2027, and the 31 March 2027 deadline for the annual business report.
