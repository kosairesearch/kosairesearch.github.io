02

Business structure

Samyoung M-Tek was founded in 1997 as a maker of structural metal products, centered on structural cast steel. Its lines include marine engine structures, plant equipment, structural steel and wind power components, organized into a Materials division and an E&C division.

Main bearing supports (MBS) are the flagship marine engine part, and KB Securities described the MBS franchise as a top-tier global supplier in a September 2025 report. Plant equipment includes the Pallet Car for mining plants, which the same report called a core cash cow.

Structural steel covers bridge bearings and structural steel products. The same report noted that revenue from nuclear equipment such as pumps and valves was minimal. Revenue mix has shifted over time.

Marine engine structures were 62% of sales in 2011, and by 2017 the mix was 33% marine engine, 35% plant, 30% structural and 2% wind. The company holds 100% of Dalian Samyoung Doosan Metal Products in China, and as of March 31, 2026 the chairman held 1.3 million shares.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual revenue went from KRW 90.7 billion in 2022 to KRW 95.7 billion in 2023 (up 5.6% year on year), KRW 118 billion in 2024 (up 23.3%) and KRW 118.5 billion in 2025 (up 0.4%). Revenue has risen for three consecutive years from 2023 through 2025, and 2025 is the largest year in the 2022 to 2025 span.

Most of the expansion came in 2024, while 2025 growth slowed to 0.4%. Operating results moved from an operating loss of KRW 3.18 billion in 2022 to operating profit of KRW 5.99 billion in 2023, then KRW 10.3 billion in 2024 (up 71.8%) and KRW 10.2 billion in 2025 (down 0.8%).

The operating margin rose from -3.5% in 2022 to 6.3% in 2023, 8.7% in 2024 and 8.6% in 2025. Profit attributable to controlling shareholders turned from a net loss of KRW 8.23 billion in 2022 to a profit of KRW 6.03 billion in 2023, then KRW 5.98 billion in 2024 (down 0.9%) and KRW 12.9 billion in 2025 (up 115.8%).

Net profit including non-controlling interests was KRW 13.1 billion in 2025, a separate figure from the controlling-shareholder profit. At end-2025, total equity was KRW 242.8 billion, of which KRW 85 billion belonged to controlling shareholders, and the debt-to-equity ratio rose from 56.1% in 2024 to 124.1%.

Quarterly revenue went from KRW 29.5 billion in Q3 2025 and KRW 33 billion in Q4 2025 to KRW 130.9 billion in Q1 2026, then KRW 131.4 billion in Q2 and KRW 136 billion in Q3.

Operating profit over the same quarters was KRW 1.5 billion, KRW 5.61 billion, KRW 3.18 billion and KRW 3.72 billion, leaving quarterly operating margins in the range of roughly 2% to 4%.

Q3 2026 operating profit rose 9.0% year on year, yet the company posted a net loss attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 1.01 billion, so non-operating items and income tax need to be checked in the quarterly report.

In September 2025 KB Securities forecast 2025 revenue of KRW 121.5 billion and operating profit of KRW 12.5 billion, against disclosed results of KRW 118.5 billion and KRW 10.2 billion.

05

Industry analysis

Demand for marine engine structures moves with shipbuilding orders. A 2017 analysis tied the company's earnings volatility to its end markets, and it described how the marine structures market stagnated after the shipbuilding downturn that began around 2010.

In September 2025, KB Securities said the MASGA project and rising global vessel orders would feed into earnings and share-price momentum, and that the company could become an essential supply chain member for the MBS business of Korea's three major shipbuilders.

The same report expected steady growth for Pallet Car in the mining plant segment. Nuclear-related equipment contributes little revenue today, but KB Securities saw potential benefits once nuclear projects progress.

Doosan Enerbility, in the same machinery and equipment sector, operates in nuclear and power generation equipment, so it is the natural reference for the nuclear theme. Stock price history shows that the shipbuilding supplier theme has repeatedly driven sharp moves in the shares.

Positioning-wise, the company is described as the largest domestic supplier of large marine engine parts and a top-tier global MBS supplier.

Risk factors

Profitability

Even with quarterly revenue above KRW 130 billion, operating margins have stayed at roughly 2% to 4%. Whether the cost structure or product mix has changed needs to be checked in the quarterly report details.

Operating profit and controlling-shareholder profit moving in opposite directions also complicates the read on profitability.

Balance sheet and shipbuilding dependence

The debt-to-equity ratio rose to 124.1% at end-2025. Demand for the core marine engine structures follows shipbuilding orders, so a downturn in that cycle could weaken the revenue base. Customer concentration among the major shipbuilders and the durability of orders are items to check in the annual report.

Theme-driven share price volatility

According to a stock-history site, shares rose 26.6% on September 4, 2025 on a nuclear theme tied to a NuScale SMR contract, and 14.4% on July 31, 2026 on strength in the shipbuilding equipment theme. Moves like these can occur regardless of earnings, which adds to share price swings. How the stock reacts around earnings releases is worth watching alongside the fundamentals.

Overall view

Samyoung M-Tek's revenue and operating profit have grown together since 2023, and profit attributable to controlling shareholders reached KRW 12.9 billion in 2025, up 115.8%.

Quarterly revenue jumped to the KRW 130 billion range in 2026, but operating margins fell to roughly 2% to 4%, and Q3 2026 turned to a net loss attributable to controlling shareholders.

Bullish points include the marine engine structures backlog, four consecutive quarters of revenue growth, and the Pallet Car and nuclear-related business base.
