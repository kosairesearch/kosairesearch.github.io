Vitzrocell: operating profit up, quarterly net profit down

02

Business structure

Vitzrocell is a specialist maker of lithium primary (non-rechargeable) batteries, with its production base in Dangjin, Chungcheongnam-do.

In the first nine months of 2025, lithium primary battery products accounted for 91.7% of sales, and end-market exposure split into smart grid 55.5%, defense 19.6%, petrochemical 19.8% and other 5.1%, according to an independent Korean IR research report.

By product type in 2025, Bobbin-type civilian batteries made up 51.2%, Wound-type military batteries 7.2% and high-temperature batteries 17.5%, per press reports.

Bobbin cells for smart meters form the base of the business, ampoule and thermal batteries serve defense customers, and high-temperature cells target oil and gas drilling.

The high-temperature line was strengthened in 2025 by the acquisition of Innova Power Solutions, a Canadian battery pack maker for oil and gas drilling, which expanded North American reach. Customers named in announced contracts include Badger Meter, Xylem and Pietro Fiorentini.

In high-spec products the market remains dominated by three advanced producers, while Chinese players such as EVE Energy are gaining share in low-priced civilian segments.

The company is also developing lithium-ion anode materials, ultra-thin film batteries, lithium foil and lithium metal recycling, and opened a lithium metal research center at its Dangjin headquarters in 2024.

Its key raw material, lithium metal at about 100 tons a year, is currently sourced entirely from China, which makes supply structurally fragile.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual sales rose from KRW 141 billion in 2022 to KRW 176.2 billion in 2023 (up 25.0% year on year), KRW 210.8 billion in 2024 (up 19.6%) and KRW 243.1 billion in 2025 (up 15.3%). Sales have grown for three consecutive years from 2023 through 2025, and 2025 was the largest year of the 2022 to 2025 period.

Operating profit climbed from KRW 28.8 billion in 2022 to KRW 69.3 billion in 2025, and the operating margin moved from 20.4% to 21.4%, then 24.6%, and reached 28.5% in 2025. In 2025 operating profit grew 33.4% against 15.3% sales growth, so margin expansion accounted for most of the earnings gain.

Profit attributable to controlling shareholders rose from KRW 23.1 billion in 2022 to KRW 56.9 billion in 2025, but its 11.1% growth in 2025 trailed operating profit growth.

At the end of 2025, total equity was KRW 330.5 billion, all of it attributable to controlling shareholders, total liabilities were KRW 40.3 billion, and the liabilities-to-equity ratio fell from 27.7% in 2022 to 12.2%. Operating cash flow grew from KRW 26.5 billion in 2022 to KRW 63.6 billion in 2025.

Quarterly sales moved from KRW 55.3 billion in the third quarter of 2025 to KRW 77.3 billion in the fourth quarter (up 39.6% quarter on quarter), and operating profit rose from KRW 16.7 billion to KRW 21.3 billion.

In 2026, sales were KRW 68.3 billion in the first quarter (down 11.7% quarter on quarter), KRW 76.2 billion in the second (up 11.6%) and KRW 70.3 billion in the third (down 7.7% quarter on quarter, up 27.1% year on year).

Operating profit was KRW 20.3 billion, KRW 18.8 billion and KRW 21.1 billion in the first three quarters of 2026, with the third quarter up 25.9% year on year. Samsung Securities said the second quarter included one-off costs of about KRW 4 billion, mainly staff incentives.

Profit attributable to controlling shareholders peaked at KRW 20.2 billion in the first quarter of 2026, then fell to KRW 17.3 billion in the second and KRW 5.64 billion in the third, down 64.4% year on year. Over the last four reported quarters it totaled KRW 61.1 billion.

The gap between rising operating profit and sharply lower controlling-shareholder profit should be traced through non-operating items and tax in the periodic reports.

05

Industry analysis

An independent Korean IR research report from February 2026 expects the global lithium primary battery market to grow at a compound annual rate of 8.3% from 2024 to 2030. The same report cites more than 1.6 billion smart meters installed worldwide and industrial automation penetration of 61% as growth drivers.

A market research citation in the report puts the adoption of lithium primary batteries in oil and gas monitoring systems up 26%.

In defense, military spending has structurally risen since the war in Ukraine began in 2022, and the report estimates that the US defense sector alone uses more than 92 million lithium primary batteries a year.

For high-temperature drilling environments, lithium thionyl chloride high-temperature cells are described as effectively the only viable option.

In competition, the high-spec segment remains dominated by three advanced producers, while Chinese players such as EVE Energy are expanding share in low-priced civilian segments. Japanese makers Hitachi Maxell and Toshiba also produce lithium primary batteries, but their global share is limited.

The company says it holds the number one position in smart meter primary batteries in the US and the number two position in Europe. Domestic battery makers have been weighed down by a weak market, while Vitzrocell has been reported as growing steadily.

Analysts cite higher crude oil prices and Middle East geopolitical risk as factors lifting demand for high-temperature cells in drilling and for defense products.

06

Outlook

An independent Korean IR research report lists company targets of KRW 600 billion in sales and KRW 160 billion in operating profit for 2030, with 2024 results (sales of KRW 210.8 billion and operating profit of KRW 51.9 billion) as the baseline. The same targets include new businesses reaching 11% of sales by 2030.

The company is pursuing secondary battery materials and next-generation lithium foil alongside its core business. The supply contract with Badger Meter runs through December 31, 2028, supporting North American smart meter volumes.

The company targets in-house production of 50 tons of lithium metal a year within two to three years, which the report says would cut dependence on China from 100% to 50%.

In a September 2026 report, Shinhan Investment Corp. expected existing core products such as high-temperature batteries to keep growing sales above 20%, and said the company formalized medium-term capacity expansion through 2031 to prepare for new businesses in self-destructing drone batteries and AI data center applications.

In July 2026, Samsung Securities estimated 2026 sales at KRW 321.6 billion (up 32% year on year) and operating profit at KRW 98 billion, and saw the consolidation of Innova and a fire at a North American competitor as factors shaping second-half results.

In the same September report, Shinhan said concerns over second-half profitability had grown because of a fire at a battery storage warehouse and currency volatility, but judged that the fire had not disrupted production schedules. Any gap between these estimates and reported results can be checked in the 2026 annual preliminary results.

Bull factors

High-margin mix in high-temperature and defense

High-temperature and defense products are classified as high-margin, and as their share grew, the operating margin reached 28.5% in 2025. Shinhan Investment Corp. said high-temperature battery sales rose 95.5% in the first quarter of 2026.

Analysts read Middle East tensions and higher crude oil prices as lifting demand for high-temperature cells used in drilling.

Long-term contracts and North American footprint

The company signed a three-year supply contract with Badger Meter running from January 2026 to December 2028, valued at about KRW 43.6 billion. Shinhan Investment Corp. said the Innova combination strengthened its North American high-temperature battery competitiveness.

The company describes its position as number one in the US and number two in Europe for smart meter primary batteries.

Financial capacity and cash generation

Total liabilities at the end of 2025 were KRW 40.3 billion, and the liabilities-to-equity ratio was a low 12.2%. This cash-generating capacity could fund investment in secondary battery materials and new businesses.

Bear factors

Sharp drop in third-quarter net profit

Profit attributable to controlling shareholders fell to KRW 5.64 billion in the third quarter of 2026, down 67.4% from the prior quarter. Operating profit rose to KRW 21.1 billion, so it is necessary to confirm how the composition of earnings changed. Samsung Securities said about KRW 4 billion of one-off costs were reflected in the second quarter of 2026.

China dependence in raw materials and price competition

All of the roughly 100 tons of lithium metal used each year is currently imported from China. Chinese producers are expanding share in low-priced civilian segments, which creates price competition pressure. The target of 50 tons of in-house metal production is set for completion within two to three years.

Recent downward revisions to target prices

In July 2026, Samsung Securities lowered its target price to 43,000 won and cited a decline in the average price-to-earnings ratio of its peer group.

In September 2026, Shinhan Investment Corp. lowered its target to 45,000 won and said the stock had entered a lower-volatility phase after falling more than 50% from its May high. Both firms kept buy ratings.

Risk factors

Raw materials and supply chain

All of the roughly 100 tons of lithium metal used each year comes from China. If Chinese suppliers raise prices or restrict exports, costs and production schedules could be disrupted.

The company has set up a research center in Dangjin targeting in-house production of 50 tons a year within two to three years, a project still in progress.

Production disruption and currency

Shinhan Investment Corp. said a fire at a battery storage warehouse and currency volatility heightened concerns over second-half profitability. The same report judged that the fire had not disrupted production schedules. Press reports say exports exceed 80% of sales, so currency movements feed directly into results.

Geopolitics and shifts in defense demand

Samsung Securities linked a mid-2026 share price correction to Middle East ceasefire talks. Defense and drilling demand could ease as geopolitical tensions ease, which could reverse earnings estimates.

New products for self-destructing drones and AI data centers are still at a stage where their sales contribution must be confirmed.

Overall view

From 2022 to 2025, Vitzrocell's sales, operating profit and profit attributable to controlling shareholders all rose, and the operating margin climbed from 20.4% to 28.5%, so the change in business structure is visible in the numbers.

A rising share of high-margin products in high-temperature batteries and defense is cited as the driver of the margin gain.

On the other hand, in the third quarter of 2026 operating profit rose 25.9% year on year, yet profit attributable to controlling shareholders fell 64.4% to KRW 5.64 billion, making the gap between operating profit and net profit the largest item to verify.

Samsung Securities (July 2026, target 43,000 won) and Shinhan Investment Corp. (September 2026, target 45,000 won) both lowered their targets, and both kept buy ratings.

Weak-side factors include dependence on Chinese lithium metal, a battery storage warehouse fire and currency volatility, and shifts in defense demand as geopolitical tensions ease.

Strong-side factors include long-term supply contracts such as the Badger Meter deal, expansion of high-temperature batteries in North America after the Innova acquisition, and financial capacity reflected in a 12.2% liabilities-to-equity ratio.

New businesses in secondary battery materials and products for self-destructing drones and AI data centers are still at the stage of confirming their sales contribution. The next checkpoints are the annual preliminary results around late January 2027 and the 2026 annual report due by the end of March 2027.
