Wonbiogen: Profit Rebound Meets Capacity Expansion

Wonbiogen, a maker of advanced wound dressings and medical-device cosmetics, posted consecutive growth in revenue, operating profit and controlling-interest net income through 2025 and extended double-digit year-over-year gains in the second quarter of 2026, while completing a KRW 13.0 billion production facility in October 2026 ahead of year-end mass production.

02

Business structure

Wonbiogen was founded in 2006 and listed on KOSDAQ in 2021 through a merger with Kyobo No.8 Special Purpose Acquisition Company, operating as a specialist in medical-grade wound dressings and cosmetics.

Its core products are polyurethane foam dressings and hydrocolloid dressings, supplied to domestic and overseas partners on an ODM basis.

According to the most recent disclosed product mix, polyurethane foam dressings accounted for 48.13% of revenue, cosmetics 29.76%, hydrocolloid dressings 9.83%, household goods 2.55%, and other items 9.73%.

In the 2025 breakdown by domestic sales versus exports, cosmetics had the highest export share at 32.52%, followed by other dressings at 17.61%, polyurethane foam dressings at 9.97%, and hydrocolloid dressings at 4.46%. Its consolidated subsidiary J&Cos, a cosmetics manufacturer, is 33.34%-owned by Wonbiogen.

Management is centered on CEO Kim Won-il, who also serves as head of research and holds multiple research credentials including hand-burn dressings and biosensor-based wound monitoring.

In 2026 the company is carrying out government-funded R&D projects related to squalane, organogel, and cylindrical reverse micelle technologies to expand its core material platform.

The founder and an affiliated holder together own 32.76% of shares, with treasury stock at 4.07%, leaving a relatively narrow free float typical of a small-cap name.

04

Earnings analysis

Wonbiogen's annual revenue rose from KRW 26.7 billion in 2022 to KRW 29.6 billion in 2023 (up 10.7% YoY), KRW 30.4 billion in 2024 (up 2.8%), and KRW 35.2 billion in 2025 (up 15.8%), marking three consecutive years of growth from 2023 through 2025.

Operating profit climbed from KRW 5.15 billion in 2022 to KRW 7.12 billion in 2023 (up 38.3%), dipped to KRW 6.41 billion in 2024 (down 10.1%), then recovered to KRW 7.93 billion in 2025 (up 23.7%).

Controlling-interest net income rose from KRW 3.27 billion in 2022 to KRW 5.5 billion in 2023 (up 68.0%), KRW 5.69 billion in 2024 (up 3.5%), and KRW 6.62 billion in 2025 (up 16.3%), also a three-year consecutive increase.

The operating margin moved from 19.3% in 2022 to 24.1% in 2023, eased to 21.0% in 2024, then improved again to 22.5% in 2025. The company attributed the 2025 improvement to higher sales of polyurethane foam dressings and expansion of its cosmetics business.

On a quarterly basis, revenue grew from KRW 8.4 billion in Q2 2025 to KRW 9.22 billion in Q3 2025 and KRW 9.37 billion in Q4 2025, dipped 1.0% quarter-on-quarter to KRW 9.28 billion in Q1 2026, then rose 7.0% quarter-on-quarter and 18.3% year-over-year to KRW 9.93 billion in Q2 2026.

Operating profit and controlling-interest net income bottomed in Q4 2025 and rose for two consecutive quarters into Q1-Q2 2026, with Q2 2026 operating profit reaching KRW 2.78 billion (up 31.3% QoQ, 46.9% YoY) and controlling-interest net income reaching KRW 2.99 billion (up 78.8% QoQ, 86.0% YoY).

The sum of controlling-interest net income over the trailing four quarters (Q3 2025-Q2 2026) was KRW 8.42 billion, while operating cash flow stayed close to operating profit levels at KRW 8.85 billion in 2024 and KRW 8.48 billion in 2025.

05

Industry analysis

The advanced wound-care and medical-device cosmetics segment that Wonbiogen operates in sits at the intersection of moist-wound-healing demand and derma-cosmetics growth, a relatively narrow niche.

Overall Korean cosmetics exports rose 11.9% year-over-year in 2025 and are forecast to grow 14.5% in 2026, suggesting the broader K-beauty end market remains in a moderate growth phase.

However, cosmetics exports to China fell roughly 18% in 2025 and are projected to decline by a reduced but still negative rate of about 7% in 2026, leaving China exposure a continuing variable for companies with exposure there.

Wonbiogen has reportedly been diversifying its export destinations beyond China into Japan, Indonesia and Turkiye, while pursuing new buyers to capture rising demand for trouble-care patches in the U.S. and China.

On the production side, the cosmetics utilization rate rose sharply from 47.86% in 2023 and 37.55% in 2024 to 71.76% in 2025, indicating improving equipment utilization.

In terms of competitive positioning, the company differentiates itself from large-cap pharmaceutical and biotech issuers by building a focused position in wound-dressing ODM and medical-device cosmetics rather than competing head-on in broader pharma.

06

Outlook

Wonbiogen announced on October 19, 2026 that it had completed a total of KRW 13.0 billion in production and R&D infrastructure and was preparing for operation.

The company said new factory construction, acquisition of an adjacent plant, equipment installation and expanded sales bases were in their final stage, with equipment setup to be completed in November and full-scale production to begin in December.

The investment aims to strengthen capacity in its core wound-dressing business while securing mass-production capability for medical-device cosmetics and hydrocolloid products such as trouble spot patches.

Management said the goal is to complete a production system spanning from R&D to mass manufacturing, improving cost competitiveness and profitability.

On the R&D front, the company is carrying out government-funded projects in 2026 related to squalane, organogel and cylindrical reverse micelle technologies, extending its new-material platform.

The company has also continued brand visibility activities, including showcasing medical-device products under its own "MEDISORB" brand.

Because the new facility's actual start of operations is scheduled for December 2026, whether the investment contributes to revenue and margins will need to be confirmed in results from the fourth quarter of 2026 onward.

08

Bull factors

Entering a Profit Recovery Phase

Operating profit in Q2 2026 came to KRW 2.78 billion, up 31.3% quarter-on-quarter and 46.9% year-on-year. Net income attributable to controlling shareholders also rose 86.0% year-on-year to KRW 2.99 billion, extending the two-quarter consecutive increase since bottoming out in Q4 2025.

On an annual basis, both revenue and net income attributable to controlling shareholders increased for three consecutive years from 2023 to 2025, confirming the continuity of growth. The operating margin also rebounded from 21.0% in 2024 to 22.5% in 2025, accompanying profitability improvement.

Capacity Expansion Nearing Completion

In October 2026, the KRW 13 billion production and R&D infrastructure build-out was completed, with equipment installation scheduled for November and mass production for December.

This expansion aims to strengthen wound dressing production capacity as well as secure mass-production capability for medical device cosmetics and hydrocolloid products.

With the cosmetics segment's utilization rate already rising sharply from 37.55% in 2024 to 71.76% in 2025, the addition of new equipment leaves room for further cost competitiveness improvement. The company stated its policy to complete the system from R&D to mass production through this investment.

Improving Cosmetics Export Mix

In 2025, the export share of cosmetics-segment revenue stood at 32.52%, far higher than polyurethane foam dressing (9.97%) or hydrocolloid dressing (4.46%). Overall K-beauty exports are expected to grow 11.9% in 2025 and 14.5% in 2026, indicating that the downstream market itself is in a phase of moderate growth.

The company is diversifying its export destinations beyond China to Japan, Indonesia, and Turkey, which is expected to reduce dependence on any single country. It is also understood to be developing new clients in response to growing demand for trouble-care patches in the US and China.

Bear factors

Signs of Slowing Quarterly Growth

Revenue in Q1 2026 was KRW 9.28 billion, down 1.0% quarter-on-quarter, temporarily breaking the streak of consecutive quarterly growth. Although it rebounded again in Q2, the wide fluctuation in quarterly growth rates warrants checking the consistency of growth on a quarter-by-quarter basis.

There was also a case in 2024 where annual operating profit declined 10.1% year-on-year, showing that profit can slow depending on changes in cost structure or product mix.

Headwinds in China Cosmetics Exports

Korea's cosmetics exports to China fell sharply (about -18%) in 2025 and the decline is not expected to fully reverse in 2026 either (estimated at about -7%).

Although Wonbio diversifying its exports to multiple countries including China, product lines with exposure to China could still be affected by demand fluctuations in that market.

The lack of a clear timeline for the industry-wide recovery of exports to China is a variable that could affect the pace of export-mix expansion.

Volatility Tied to Small-Cap Structure

With the largest shareholder and related parties holding 32.76% and treasury shares at 4.07%, the free float is relatively limited, which could lead to relatively greater share price volatility from supply-demand changes.

At the March 2026 annual general meeting, an agenda item on approving the director compensation ceiling failed to pass due to insufficient voting quorum after excluding votes from interested parties, highlighting the need for caution regarding governance-related agenda items.

Given the characteristics of a stock with a small market capitalization, it may also be affected by noise such as short-term trading recommendations from informal media outlets.

Risk factors

Export and Foreign Exchange Risk

With cosmetics-segment revenue's export share reaching 32.52%, the company is exposed to exchange rate fluctuations and changes in importing countries' regulatory and tariff policies.

Amid an industry-wide decline in cosmetics exports to China, product items with exposure to China cannot rule out the possibility of further demand slowdown.

The pace of obtaining approvals and building distribution networks in newly mentioned export destinations such as Japan, Indonesia, and Turkey is also a variable that could affect performance.

New Facility Ramp-Up Risk

The new KRW 13 billion production facility targets completion of equipment installation in November 2026 and mass production in December, so it is necessary to confirm whether the actual start-up timing and initial yield/utilization rate proceed as planned.

In the early stages of the equipment investment, fixed costs such as depreciation may increase, potentially weighing on margins in the short term. If the utilization rate fails to rise as expected, the effect of expanded cosmetics-segment production capacity could be delayed.

Governance and Liquidity Risk

Combined, the stakes of the largest shareholder, related parties, and treasury shares account for a substantial portion of issued shares, limiting the free float, which could affect trading liquidity and share price volatility.

As seen in the case at the March 2026 annual general meeting where the director compensation ceiling agenda failed to pass due to insufficient voting quorum, the process of handling governance-related agenda items also warrants monitoring.

Given the characteristics of a small-cap stock, investors should be cautious as target prices or trading recommendations of unclear basis may circulate through channels other than official disclosures.

Overall view

Wonbiogen posted three consecutive years of growth in both revenue and controlling-interest net income on a disclosed basis from 2022 through 2025, and extended that recovery into Q2 2026 with operating profit and controlling-interest net income rising 46.9% and 86.0% year-over-year, respectively, following a Q4 2025 trough.

In October 2026 the company completed a KRW 13.0 billion production and R&D facility ahead of planned mass production in December, leaving the facility's actual contribution as the next item to confirm.

A growing export share in cosmetics and a moderately growing K-beauty end market are favorable factors, but slowing China exports, volatility in quarter-to-quarter growth rates, and a limited free float are variables that warrant equal consideration.

On valuation, the company trades at an earnings multiple below the industry average and a price-to-book ratio near 1x, while maintaining a consistent cash dividend policy. The next quarterly disclosure and news on the new facility's ramp-up will be key variables for gauging the forward earnings trajectory.
