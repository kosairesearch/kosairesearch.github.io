Report overview

Shinsung ST: North American ESS Plant and the Margin Recovery Test

Shinsung ST's Q3 2026 revenue rose 131.9% year on year to KRW 68.6 billion and operating income turned positive, but net losses attributable to controlling shareholders remained in the quarter. The pace at which the US Kentucky subsidiary moves into mass production and margins recover is the key point to watch.

02

Business structure

Shinsung ST is a secondary battery parts specialist whose main products are busbars, which carry high-voltage current between battery cells, and battery module cases.

Since 2025 it has been building a mass production line for heat sinks, the water-cooled thermal management parts used in ESS, and completed that line at the end of 2025.

Secondary battery-related products accounted for about 71% of revenue in 2025, and Samsung Securities projected in an April 2026 report that the share would rise above 80% in 2026. The share varies across reports because of differences in segment classification.

According to a eToday report, secondary battery segment sales were KRW 47.0 billion, or 93.4%, of KRW 50.2 billion in consolidated revenue in Q1 2026. The remaining business is EV camera modules and automotive electronic parts made at the Vietnam subsidiary, which accounted for 28.2% of revenue as of Q3 2025.

Production sites include the Changwon headquarters and plants in Gumi and Haman in Korea, plus overseas sites in Nanjing China, Vietnam, Poland and Kentucky in the US.

LG Energy Solution is the largest customer, and Mirae Asset Securities flagged dependence on a single customer as a pressure point in a February 2026 report. The company said in its 2023 IPO filing that its mold technology and R&D capability had given it an edge in winning orders. The largest shareholder is Shinsung Delta Tech, which held 25.91% as of June 30, 2026.

04

Earnings analysis

On a DART-disclosed basis, annual revenue rose from KRW 106.5 billion in 2022 to KRW 124.7 billion in 2023 (up 17.1%) and KRW 127 billion in 2024 (up 1.9%), then fell to KRW 116.9 billion in 2025 (down 8.0%). Among 2022 to 2025, 2024 was the largest year.

Operating income climbed from KRW 7.89 billion in 2022 to KRW 8.81 billion in 2024, then dropped 74.2% to KRW 2.27 billion in 2025. The operating margin went from 7.4% in 2022, 6.7% in 2023 and 6.9% in 2024 to 1.9% in 2025.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders fell from KRW 9.29 billion in 2022 to KRW 554 million in 2025, and declined for three consecutive years from 2023 to 2025. Total net income, including non-controlling interests, was disclosed at the same amounts for each year.

Samsung Securities attributed the 2025 margin decline first to lower LG Energy Solution volumes from China after US tariffs, then to higher costs from shifting production to Poland and Korea from Q3, and to early fixed-cost burdens from the delayed US plant in Q4.

On a quarterly basis, Q3 2025 revenue was KRW 29.6 billion with operating income of KRW 1.38 billion. Q4 2025 revenue rose 14.7% quarter on quarter to KRW 34 billion, but the company posted an operating loss of KRW 2.25 billion.

Q1 2026 revenue rose 48.0% quarter on quarter to KRW 50.2 billion, with an operating loss of KRW 542 million that narrowed. Q2 2026 revenue was KRW 59.9 billion and operating income turned positive at KRW 1.67 billion. Q3 2026 revenue was KRW 68.6 billion, up 14.6% quarter on quarter and 131.9% year on year.

Operating income that quarter was KRW 3.28 billion, but the net loss attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 2.47 billion, so operating and shareholder net results moved in different directions.

The net loss attributable to controlling shareholders over the last four quarters, from Q4 2025 to Q3 2026, totaled KRW 3.71 billion.

05

Industry analysis

ESS demand is rising alongside expansion of AI data centers. According to eToday and Paxetv reports, the US is a market where data center demand is surging, and Korea's three major battery makers are increasing North American investment.

Hana Securities noted in a June 2024 report that Korean battery makers were converting EV lines to ESS production. The same report put Korean makers' global ESS battery share at 10% in 2023. The secondary battery industry went through a temporary demand lull, known as a chasm, around 2025.

Tariffs also changed production footprints. Paxetv reported that the company dropped plans to expand Korean plants and shifted toward US production because of tariff concerns. The tariff impact on Chinese output showed up directly in 2025 results.

On product mix, Samsung Securities viewed the broadening of the lineup into higher value-added products such as heat sinks and TPA modules as positive. In cycle terms, the company sits in a phase of rising ESS demand, but it also needs its core secondary battery business to return to normal utilization.

Direct margin comparisons with other listed electronic components companies are limited because customer structures and product mixes differ.

06

Outlook

The biggest item to verify is the move of the US Kentucky subsidiary into mass production. The subsidiary was established in January 2025 and has annual capacity of KRW 230 billion. As of the end of Q1 2026, KRW 29.1 billion had been invested to expand US production capacity.

At an August 2026 investor briefing, the company said it targeted operation within Q3 and described trial runs and mass production preparation on a schedule agreed with customers.

The company said it maintains its existing performance guidance based on contracts with customers and its medium- to long-term production plan. It also noted that delivery volumes in the auto and battery parts business can vary with customers' actual order volumes.

Mirae Asset Securities projected 2026 annual revenue of KRW 140 billion in a February 2026 report and saw KRW 230 billion to KRW 240 billion a year for 2027 to 2029. The same report cited an LG Energy Solution North American ESS order of KRW 1.1 trillion through 2030 as a basis.

Revenue across the first three quarters of 2026 has already exceeded that annual estimate in total. The same report flagged that limited room for additional borrowing could be an execution risk during the North American ramp-up.

The 2026 annual report will show when the Kentucky subsidiary actually started operating, the scale of its fixed-cost burden, and the full-year 2026 operating margin.

08

Bull factors

Revenue growth and North American capacity

Q3 2026 revenue reached KRW 68.6 billion, up 131.9% year on year, and revenue has risen for four consecutive quarters since Q4 2025. The US Kentucky subsidiary was designed with annual capacity of KRW 230 billion, and KRW 29.1 billion had been invested by the end of Q1 2026.

The bullish case is that this capacity, tied to North American ESS orders from LG Energy Solution, broadens the revenue base.

Large order backlog cited by analysts

Yuhwa Securities said in a January 2026 report that the order backlog topped KRW 7.2 trillion and suggested KRW 1 trillion in revenue could be reached in 2027. Mirae Asset Securities cited a KRW 1.1 trillion North American ESS order from LG Energy Solution through 2030 in its February 2026 report. Both brokers' outlooks point the same way in treating the order base as the foundation for growth.

Early signs of margin recovery

The operating margin fell to 1.9% in 2025, but operating income turned positive at KRW 1.67 billion in Q2 2026 and rose to KRW 3.28 billion in Q3.

Samsung Securities said in an April 2026 report that the 2025 margin hit reflected tariff and US plant delay factors and that gradual margin improvement could begin this year.

09

Bear factors

Prolonged margin damage

The 2025 operating margin of 1.9% fell well short of the roughly 7% average Samsung Securities cited for 2021 to 2024.

The same report expected margin recovery to remain difficult in 2026 because of early fixed-cost burdens from the heat sink ramp and sluggish results from shifting production of existing secondary battery products. The Q2 and Q3 2026 operating profits offer early counterevidence, but full-year confirmation is still pending.

Continued net losses for shareholders

Operating income was positive in Q3 2026, but the net loss attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 2.47 billion. The four-quarter total of net losses attributable to controlling shareholders reached KRW 3.71 billion, and that profit fell from KRW 9.29 billion in 2022 to KRW 554 million in 2025.

While earnings-based valuation metrics remain unavailable, interpretation leans more heavily on asset-side measures.

Customer concentration and funding limits

Heavy reliance on LG Energy Solution, the largest customer, means its order changes flow directly into results. Mirae Asset Securities flagged limited room for additional borrowing as an execution risk during the North American ramp-up in its February 2026 report.

The company itself said delivery volumes in its auto and battery parts business can vary with customers' order volumes.

10

Risk factors

Customer concentration

LG Energy Solution is the largest customer, and its North American ESS orders and the allocation of volumes across production sites determine results. In 2025, tariffs on China sharply reduced volumes for this customer from the Chinese plant. A structure reliant on one customer can magnify earnings swings when tariffs or policy changes occur.

US plant ramp-up and fixed costs

The US subsidiary has annual capacity of KRW 230 billion, and KRW 29.1 billion had been invested by the end of Q1 2026. If the shift to mass production slips, initial fixed costs could weigh on margins for longer. The operating loss of KRW 542 million in Q1 2026 reflects this burden during the ramp.

Overseas guarantees and borrowing

According to a 2025 disclosure report, the company approved a EUR 7.3 million guarantee, about KRW 12.6 billion, on borrowing by its Polish subsidiary for facility investment. At the time that equaled 12.3% of its KRW 102.6 billion in equity. Mirae Asset Securities flagged limited capacity for additional borrowing.

Overseas guarantees and borrowing capacity will interact with North American investment as a financial burden variable.

12

Overall view

Shinsung ST's disclosed results show revenue recovery and margin recovery moving in different directions. Revenue has risen for four consecutive quarters from Q4 2025 through Q3 2026, and Q3 2026 revenue of KRW 68.6 billion was up 131.9% year on year.

Operating income turned positive in Q2 2026 and reached KRW 3.28 billion in Q3, but the same quarter still carried a net loss attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 2.47 billion, and the last four quarters total KRW 3.71 billion in losses.

The 2025 operating margin of 1.9% is far below the roughly 7% average for 2021 to 2024 that Samsung Securities cited. On the bullish side, the US Kentucky subsidiary's annual capacity of KRW 230 billion and North American ESS volumes from LG Energy Solution are cited as grounds for recovery.

On the bearish side, dependence on a single customer, fixed costs at the US plant and limited borrowing capacity are flagged. Broker views also diverge.

Yuhwa Securities suggested in a January 2026 report that KRW 1 trillion in 2027 revenue was achievable, while Samsung Securities said in an April 2026 report that margin recovery in 2026 would be difficult.

The next checkpoints are the status of the Kentucky subsidiary in the 2026 annual report and the operating margin in Q1 2027.
