02

Business structure

KleanNara is a household-goods company founded in 1966 that runs both a feminine-care business and a paper business. Its feminine-care products are sold through online and offline channels under brands such as Sunsuhanmyeon and Dearskin.

In early 2026 the company said it was reviewing an expansion of its mid- to low-priced sanitary-pad lineup. In June 2026 it was selected as a supplier for the government's public sanitary-pad pilot, with Sunsuhanmyeon Zero Medium (2P) as the supplied product.

The company says this product is made to the same quality standards and specifications as the version sold on the market, using a 100% natural cotton cover. Production takes place at the Cheongju plant, where the company says 1,200 pads are produced per hour.

The paper business includes white board, and the company cited lower production volume tied to a weaker white-board market as the main cause of the 2025 operating loss.

Customers are divided between government public procurement and general consumers, and public-sector volume goes to public facilities in 12 pilot regions nationwide. The domestic hygiene-products market is dominated by three large companies, and all three took part in the public sanitary-pad bid. Money Today reported that KleanNara was ranked first in the bid's qualification review.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual revenue fell from KRW 606.5 billion in 2022 to KRW 514.9 billion in 2023, a 15.1% decline, then recovered to KRW 537 billion in 2024, up 4.3%, before dropping to KRW 508.2 billion in 2025, down 5.4%. Operating profit of KRW 3.75 billion in 2022 turned into an operating loss of KRW 18.9 billion in 2023.

The loss narrowed to KRW 902 million in 2024 and widened to KRW 22.6 billion in 2025. Operating margin moved from 0.6% in 2022 to -3.7% in 2023, -0.2% in 2024 and -4.5% in 2025.

Net losses attributable to controlling shareholders were KRW 2.91 billion in 2022, KRW 30.8 billion in 2023, KRW 22.1 billion in 2024 and KRW 47.5 billion in 2025. Net loss including non-controlling interests was reported at the same amounts in each year.

Operating cash flow was KRW 34.2 billion positive in 2023, and minus KRW 5.36 billion, minus KRW 5.64 billion and minus KRW 2.13 billion in 2022, 2024 and 2025. At the end of 2025 total liabilities were KRW 466.6 billion and total equity was KRW 170.7 billion, with the debt ratio at 273.4%, up from 173.9% in 2022.

Quarterly, the operating loss went from KRW 3.25 billion in the third quarter of 2025 to KRW 9.34 billion in the fourth quarter, narrowed to KRW 3.97 billion in the first quarter of 2026, and turned into an operating profit of KRW 620 million in the second quarter.

In the third quarter of 2026 revenue was KRW 135.1 billion, up 2.9% year on year, while the operating loss widened to KRW 7.2 billion. Seoul Economic TV reported that cost of sales rose 7% year on year to KRW 120.7 billion in the same quarter, and NEWS1 reported that gross margin fell 3.44 percentage points.

Both reports put the cumulative operating loss for January to September 2026 at KRW 10.5 billion, smaller than a year earlier, even as the third-quarter loss grew.

05

Industry analysis

The sanitary-pad market is dominated by three large domestic hygiene-products companies, and all three took part in the 2026 public sanitary-pad bid.

Money Today reported that low-priced sanitary pads have been launched voluntarily in the distribution channel, and that the government program is expected to intensify price competition. In response, the company said in early 2026 that it was reviewing an expansion of its mid- to low-priced sanitary-pad lineup.

On the input-cost side, the company cited higher raw-material prices linked to the US-Iran conflict as a key point in its bid discussions. In the paper business, the company attributed the 2025 losses to a weaker white-board market, and cost-of-sales pressure continued in the third quarter of 2026.

The two businesses face different market conditions, so their earnings may move in different directions. The company promoted its Sunsuhanmyeon brand as the product supplied to the public program, stressing the brand exposure.

Because public-sector volume was taken on with reduced margins, the company's ability to defend profitability under price pressure is a measure to watch.

06

Outlook

The public sanitary-pad program, known as Modu Saengliday in Korean, began in July 2026 in 12 pilot regions nationwide. The company plans to supply 5.4 million packs within 2026. Because each pack contains two medium pads, the company said it will introduce automated packaging equipment soon.

The company said it took part with reduced margins and does not expect public-sector volume to reduce sales of its existing products. Nationwide delivery will use the existing logistics network, with parcel service for low-volume locations.

Expansion of the mid- to low-priced sanitary-pad lineup is still under review, and no specific launch timing has been given. On costs, the company said it will focus on improving its cost structure and production efficiency to restore profitability.

Seoul Economic TV reported that the debt ratio rose to 297% at the end of September 2026, and Herald Business also covered borrowing from the owner family. The scale of borrowing and its interest burden are therefore a point to track for future earnings.

Once the 2026 annual report discloses full-year 2026 operating margin and the debt ratio, the effect of the public program and cost measures can be checked in figures.

Risk factors

Input-cost risk

Higher raw-material prices can lift the cost-of-sales ratio and widen losses. The company cited the rise in raw-material prices tied to the US-Iran conflict as a background to its bid discussions. Reports also said cost of sales rose 7% year on year in the third quarter of 2026.

If public-sector volume, where cost pass-through is harder, grows as a share of sales, profitability risk could increase.

Financial structure and borrowing risk

At the end of 2025, total liabilities were KRW 466.6 billion and total equity was KRW 170.7 billion, so debt exceeded twice equity. A 297% debt ratio at the end of September 2026 was reported. If losses continue, equity will shrink further and financial room will narrow.

A structure that relies on borrowed funds could face rising pressure from interest rates and repayment schedules.

Public-program and pricing risk

The public sanitary-pad program is still at the pilot stage, so whether volume and duration continue into a full program is not yet settled. Reports said low-priced pads are being launched in the distribution channel, intensifying price competition.

In a market dominated by three large companies, a mid- to low-priced lineup could weigh on profitability. Because public volume carries reduced margins, margin pressure may persist.
