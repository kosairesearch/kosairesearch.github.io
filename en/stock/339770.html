Kyochon F&B: Profit Recovery Meets Cost and Overseas Tests

Operating profit rose to KRW 35 billion in 2025 and grew for three straight quarters through Q3 2026, but raw material costs absorbed in the first half left nine-month operating profit below the prior year on a preliminary basis.

02

Business structure

Kyochon F&B operates a chicken franchise business centered on Kyochon Chicken and is a food and beverage company. As of the end of June 2026, the company had 1,368 domestic stores and 77 overseas stores. Its own ordering app has 7.78 million members, and app orders account for 12% of sales.

Overseas, the business is recovering as stores in existing markets such as China and the United States return to normal operations and new openings increase, improving the profit of overseas subsidiaries.

The company plans a first Japan opening in the first half of 2027, entry into new countries, and more stores in China and Southeast Asia.

The core of the new business is the buckwheat restaurant brand Memil Danpyeon, which opened its third store in Gwanggyo, Suwon in June 2026 and a fourth store in Pangyo in September, and infrastructure for its franchise business is under construction.

The new business segment also includes supply of liquor and eco-friendly packaging, and higher supply volumes contributed to third-quarter sales.

According to the value-up plan announced on March 31, 2026, global and new business accounted for 5.5% of sales in 2025, and the target is to raise that share to 10% or more by 2028.

In Korea, seasonal peaks such as the summer Boknal period and year-end demand are supported by new menu launches and star and sports marketing.

Increases in raw material prices have been absorbed by the head office rather than passed on to franchisees, so the head office's profitability is directly exposed to cost changes.

04

Earnings analysis

On the basis of the regular report disclosures, annual sales went from KRW 517.5 billion in 2022 to KRW 445 billion in 2023 (down 14.0%), then rose to KRW 480.8 billion in 2024 (up 8.1%) and KRW 517.4 billion in 2025 (up 7.6%), so sales have grown for two consecutive years from 2024 to 2025.

Within 2022-2025, 2022 was the largest sales year and 2023 the smallest. Operating profit moved from KRW 8.84 billion in 2022 to KRW 24.8 billion in 2023 (up 181.0%), KRW 15.4 billion in 2024 (down 37.8%) and KRW 35 billion in 2025 (up 127.0%), with 2025 the largest year.

The operating margin was 1.7% in 2022, 5.6% in 2023, 3.2% in 2024 and 6.8% in 2025, dipping in 2024 before recovering in 2025. Profit attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 5.34 billion in 2022, KRW 14.3 billion in 2023, KRW 2.17 billion in 2024 (down 84.9%) and KRW 17.6 billion in 2025 (up 708.3%).

Net income including non-controlling interests was KRW 17.2 billion in 2025, a different figure from profit attributable to controlling shareholders.

Quarterly operating profit fell from KRW 11.3 billion in Q3 2025 to KRW 3.77 billion in Q4 2025 (down 66.5% from the prior quarter), and Q4 2025 showed a net loss attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 1.36 billion, a turn to loss.

Operating profit then rose for three consecutive quarters, to KRW 5.29 billion in Q1 2026, KRW 7.85 billion in Q2 2026 and KRW 12.3 billion in Q3 2026. The first-half squeeze came from raw materials.

According to company statements, the head office absorbed raw material price increases linked to a prolonged avian influenza outbreak and the Middle East situation rather than passing them to franchisees, and delivery and other selling expenses also rose.

On a preliminary press basis, the Q3 2026 operating margin was about 8.5%, roughly in line with the prior year, and the company attributed the profit increase to better results at its overseas subsidiaries and affiliates.

Cumulative operating profit for the first three quarters of 2026 was KRW 25.4 billion on a preliminary basis, below the same period last year. Cash generation supports the picture, with operating cash flow rising from KRW 4.52 billion in 2022 to KRW 39.3 billion in 2025.

The debt-to-equity ratio rose from 70.2% in 2022 to 100.1% in 2025, with total liabilities of KRW 188.9 billion and total equity of KRW 188.6 billion at the end of 2025.

05

Industry analysis

Chicken franchising is a business whose results are sensitive to raw material prices and seasonal demand.

In the first half of 2026, a prolonged avian influenza outbreak combined with the Middle East situation pushed up raw material and partial-cut costs, and Kyochon kept those increases at the head office instead of passing them to franchisees.

That made the cost structure the main swing factor for the head office's operating margin. By contrast, the pattern in which the summer Boknal season and the year-end peak lift quarterly sales is common across the industry, and in Q3 2026 Kyochon leaned on new menu launches and sports marketing during that window.

In cycle terms, operating profit recovered sharply in 2025, was pressed again by first-half cost burdens in 2026, and turned upward in the third quarter. Set against 1,368 domestic stores, the 77 overseas stores show that international expansion is still at an early stage.

Overseas stores in China and the United States are going through a return to normal operations, and overseas subsidiary results are improving as local operations normalize. The new business, Memil Danpyeon, is a restaurant franchise model in which infrastructure building and store expansion are proceeding together.

In chicken competition, the share of demand captured depends on how effectively new menus and marketing frequency pull in customers, and Kyochon continues to rely on those tools.

06

Outlook

The company's medium-term plan is the value-up plan announced on March 31, 2026. It targets sales growth of 8% or more per year through 2028, opening 100 new stores each in Korea and overseas, and raising the share of global and new business in sales from 5.5% in 2025 to 10% or more in 2028.

The profitability target is to lift the operating margin from 6.8% in 2025 to about 10% in 2028, through cost reduction, efficiency gains in selling, general and administrative expenses and logistics, and growth in global exports and royalty income.

On shareholder returns, the company plans to implement a high-dividend policy and to buy back part of its treasury shares for employee compensation and cancellation. The company says it has maintained an average payout ratio of 40% or more while raising total dividends through 2022-2025.

Near-term, the company will continue new-product promotions and marketing timed for the year-end peak in Q4 2026, and it is focusing on stabilizing raw material supply and strengthening franchise competitiveness.

Overseas, a first Japan opening is planned for the first half of 2027, alongside entry into new countries and more stores in China and Southeast Asia. In the new business, the tasks are building franchise infrastructure for Memil Danpyeon and developing new restaurant formats.

Whether the 2028 targets are met can be tracked through the annual operating margin and the trend in the number of overseas stores.

Bull factors

Overseas store growth and Japan entry

As stores in China and the United States returned to normal operations, overseas subsidiary profit improved in the third quarter. A first Japan opening in the first half of 2027 and entry into new countries are in progress, which gives room for the base of 77 overseas stores as of June 2026 to grow. The company has set a target of raising the share of global and new business in sales to 10% or more by 2028.

Earnings recovery and three straight quarterly gains

Operating profit in 2025 was KRW 35 billion, up 127.0%, with an operating margin of 6.8%. Operating profit has risen for three consecutive quarters since Q1 2026, and Q3 operating profit of KRW 12.3 billion was up 9.6% year on year.

According to press reports, operating profit growth outpaced sales growth in the third quarter, so profitability improved slightly.

Shareholder returns and value-up plan

The company has targeted an operating margin of about 10% by 2028, citing cost reduction, logistics efficiency and higher royalty income. It has maintained an average payout ratio of 40% or more and has announced plans to buy back and cancel part of its treasury shares. If earnings recovery continues, room for shareholder returns could also grow as the targets are pursued.

Bear factors

Raw material burden and lower cumulative profit

In the first half of 2026, the head office absorbed raw material price increases and selling expenses such as delivery costs rose. This raw material burden and the increase in selling expenses are the backdrop to cumulative operating profit being lower than in the same period last year.

If cost pressure returns, the head office's margin could come under pressure quickly, which is a bearish factor.

Volatility of quarterly profit

Q4 2025 operating profit was KRW 3.77 billion, down 66.5% from the prior quarter, and profit attributable to controlling shareholders turned to a net loss of KRW 1.36 billion. Q3 2026 profit attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 7 billion was also 8.1% below the prior-year quarter. Because the annual recovery has not appeared evenly across quarters, the predictability of earnings is lower.

Rising debt and financial burden

The debt-to-equity ratio rose from 70.2% in 2022 to 100.1% in 2025. Total liabilities at the end of 2025 were KRW 188.9 billion, close to total equity of KRW 188.6 billion. If overseas expansion and new business investment increase, the financial burden could grow, which is a bearish factor.

Risk factors

Raw material prices

If avian influenza persists and Middle East tensions keep raw material prices elevated, the head office's burden grows. Whether costs are passed to franchisees determines how head office margins and franchisee profitability diverge. The first half of 2026 showed cost pressure holding back operating profit, so the same structure could recur.

Overseas and new business profitability

Press reports point to remaining tasks in securing profitability overseas, building a stable operating system for new brands, and managing franchisee burdens. Memil Danpyeon opened its fourth store in September 2026 and its franchise infrastructure is still being built. The share of earnings contributed by the new business is difficult to gauge from disclosed figures alone.

Financial structure and earnings volatility

The debt-to-equity ratio exceeded 100%, and profit attributable to controlling shareholders swung from KRW 2.17 billion in 2024 to KRW 17.6 billion in 2025. A net loss was recorded in Q4 2025. With a thin earnings base, results could be shaken by external shocks such as cost increases or higher selling expenses.

Overall view

Kyochon F&B's results have been recovering since the 2023 low. Operating profit in 2025 rose 127.0% to KRW 35 billion, and it has grown for three straight quarters through Q3 2026.

However, cumulative operating profit for the first three quarters of 2026 was below the prior year on a preliminary basis because of first-half raw material pressure.

Bullish factors include the recovery in overseas stores, the planned Japan entry, the 10% operating margin target for 2028, and the maintained payout ratio.

Bearish factors include margin volatility from the cost pass-through structure, the swing to a net loss in Q4 2025 that shows quarterly earnings instability, and the financial burden as the debt-to-equity ratio rose to 100.1%. The new business, Memil Danpyeon, is still early, so its contribution is hard to gauge.

The next points to confirm are the preliminary Q4 2026 results, the annual operating margin in the 2026 annual report, and the performance of the Japan openings.
