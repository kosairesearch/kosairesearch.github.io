02

Business structure

AK Holdings is the business holding company of the AK group, a mixed holding structure that combines dividend income and trademark royalties from affiliates with some directly operated businesses.

According to Business Post, its stakes at end-2024 were 60.78% in the chemical affiliate, 50.41% in Jeju Air, 60.19% in AK PLAZA, 57.14% in the real estate affiliate and 46.32% in the consumer-goods affiliate.

The chemical affiliate supplies industrial chemicals such as plasticizers, phthalic anhydride, lubricant base oils and specialty resins, and the same report put its 2024 consolidated revenue at KRW 1.64 trillion with operating profit of KRW 15.3 billion.

Jeju Air is a low-cost carrier serving domestic and short-haul international routes, where jet fuel costs and the exchange rate drive earnings.

AK PLAZA runs department stores in Suwon, Bundang, Pyeongtaek and Wonju; according to a June 2026 press report, its 2025 revenue was KRW 271.7 billion, down 7% year on year, with an operating loss of KRW 6.1 billion.

AK Holdings sold its consumer-goods affiliate stake, with the proceeds of KRW 317.1 billion arriving in March 2026, and about KRW 151 billion of that went to repay existing share-backed loans.

In June 2026 the IT affiliate AKIS became the largest shareholder of AK PLAZA, so the ownership chain became AK Holdings, then AKIS, then AK PLAZA, and AK Holdings completed AKIS's consolidation as a subsidiary.

Because consolidated results reflect subsidiary performance in proportion to ownership, the holding company's figures move with businesses in aviation, chemicals and retail that follow different cycles.

04

Earnings analysis

On a reported basis, consolidated revenue rose from KRW 3.79 trillion in 2022 to KRW 4.48 trillion in 2023 (up 18.3% year on year), edged up to KRW 4.49 trillion in 2024 (up 0.2%), and fell to KRW 3.24 trillion in 2025 (down 27.9%). Among 2022 to 2025, 2024 was the largest year and 2025 the smallest.

Operating profit swung from a loss of KRW 21.8 billion in 2022 to a profit of KRW 279.1 billion in 2023, then fell 53.3% to KRW 130.3 billion in 2024 before turning to a loss of KRW 147.9 billion in 2025. The operating margin moved from -0.6% in 2022 to 6.2% in 2023, 2.9% in 2024 and -4.6% in 2025.

Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders turned from a loss of KRW 119 billion in 2022 to a profit of KRW 130.4 billion in 2023, then a loss of KRW 29.1 billion in 2024 and a larger loss of KRW 119.3 billion in 2025, which marks two consecutive loss years.

Total net loss in 2025 was KRW 174.7 billion, and the gap with the controlling-shareholder figure reflects non-controlling interests. A June 2026 press report said Jeju Air and the chemical affiliate both swung to operating losses in 2025, which points to subsidiary downturns as the main drag.

Quarterly revenue bottomed at KRW 389.4 billion in the fourth quarter of 2025, down 59.6% from the prior quarter, and then rose to KRW 937.7 billion in the first quarter of 2026, KRW 1.03 trillion in the second and KRW 1.05 trillion in the third, marking three straight quarters of growth.

Operating profit moved from a loss of KRW 40.3 billion in the fourth quarter of 2025 to a profit of KRW 67.2 billion in the first quarter of 2026, back to a loss of KRW 931 million in the second quarter, and then to a profit of KRW 61.9 billion in the third quarter.

Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders totaled KRW 163.1 billion over the last four reported quarters. Operating cash flow declined from KRW 366.5 billion in 2022 to KRW 527.3 billion in 2023, KRW 195.7 billion in 2024 and KRW 46.4 billion in 2025.

05

Industry analysis

AK Holdings' industry exposure differs by affiliate. The aviation business depends on international demand, the exchange rate and jet fuel prices, and Jeju Air was reported to have swung to an operating loss in 2025.

The petrochemical business is driven by crude oil prices, product spreads and Chinese demand, and the chemical affiliate's swing to an operating loss in 2025 weighed heavily on results.

After the chemical merger, AK Holdings' stake in the chemical affiliate rose to 62.23%, and the group framed chemicals as a route to growth. In retail, the press has reported that the group's distribution business lost competitiveness around the COVID-19 period, and AK PLAZA had not returned to profit since 2020.

Its smaller, neighborhood-focused stores make it more sensitive to local consumption than large department store rivals. Listed holding companies such as GS Holdings, LS, Doosan and CJ are commonly cited peers, but AK Holdings' mix of aviation, chemicals and retail makes a single-industry comparison difficult.

Korea IR Council analysts said the rights offerings, payment guarantees and collateral provided for AK PLAZA had acted as a discount factor for AK Holdings. The same analysts expected no further support for the time being after the capital increase.

The aviation and chemical cycles are therefore at a stage where a recovery must be confirmed after the swing to losses, while retail is in a phase of structural reorganization.

06

Outlook

No full-year 2026 earnings target from the company was confirmed in the sources, so no outlook figures are given. The confirmed plans are centered on affiliates.

A Korea IR Council report noted that the chemical affiliate decided in September 2024 to invest about KRW 96.7 billion in a TPC mass-production facility for aramid raw materials.

Business Post reported that the same affiliate set medium-term targets of KRW 4 trillion in revenue and KRW 300 billion in operating profit by 2030. In June 2026 AK Holdings disclosed that AKIS would take over AK PLAZA shares, and a company official said there were no current plans to sell AK PLAZA.

The stated purpose of bringing AKIS into the group is to combine its AI and data capabilities with AK PLAZA's offline retail operations.

On the financing side, AK Holdings continues to pledge 5.3 million shares of the chemical affiliate as collateral to extend a KRW 31.1 billion borrowing of AK PLAZA from a Korean securities finance lender. Debt-related obligations therefore remain with the holding company.

The stake in AKIS was acquired for KRW 43.3 billion, and together with a KRW 56.5 billion loan to AKIS, the holding company put roughly KRW 99.8 billion into AKIS in a short period. The next periodic report is the 2026 annual report, which is due around the end of March 2027 under the statutory deadline. Whether the operating margin and debt ratio move away from their 2025 levels is the next check point.

08

Bull factors

Improving quarterly earnings flow

Revenue rose for three straight quarters, reaching KRW 1.05 trillion in the third quarter of 2026. Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders was positive in all three quarters of 2026, moving away from the fourth-quarter 2025 loss. Quarterly swings remain large, however, so the durability of this trend has yet to be tested.

Restructuring aims to reduce burdens

Proceeds from the consumer-goods affiliate stake sale arrived in March 2026, and about KRW 151 billion was used to repay share-backed loans. In June 2026 AK PLAZA shares moved under AKIS, which some observers read as easing the holding company's direct burden. The company said the move is part of a restructuring and that it has no current plan to sell AK PLAZA.

Low price-to-book multiple

The price-to-book ratio is far below 1x, which means market value sits low relative to book value. Total equity of KRW 1.12 trillion, including non-controlling interests, forms the asset base. Because affiliate stakes are part of that base, a recovery in subsidiary earnings could change how the asset value is read.

09

Bear factors

Two straight loss years and a wider loss

The controlling-shareholder net loss widened from KRW 29.1 billion in 2024 to KRW 119.3 billion in 2025, and the total net loss reached KRW 174.7 billion. Operating profit swung to a loss of KRW 147.9 billion in 2025, and the operating margin fell to -4.6%. Profitable quarters in 2026 have not yet translated into a full-year recovery.

Weaker balance sheet and lower cash flow

The debt ratio climbed from 290.3% in 2022 to 432.2% in 2025, with total liabilities of KRW 4.85 trillion. Operating cash flow dropped from KRW 366.5 billion to KRW 46.4 billion over the same period. Lower cash generation has raised reliance on borrowing and collateral.

Subsidiary weakness and support needs

A June 2026 press report said AK PLAZA posted a 2025 operating loss of KRW 6.1 billion and that its accumulated deficit grew to about KRW 168 billion. The holding company keeps pledging 5.3 million chemical affiliate shares as collateral for AK PLAZA's borrowing.

Cumulative support, including a KRW 60.1 billion rights offering in December 2024, constrains the holding company's financial room.

10

Risk factors

Affiliate cycle risk

Aviation earnings depend on jet fuel prices, the exchange rate and international demand, while chemicals are exposed to crude prices, product spreads and Chinese demand. Jeju Air and the chemical affiliate both swung to operating losses in 2025, showing that two cycles can weaken together.

Because consolidated results pass through subsidiary performance in proportion to ownership, a weak affiliate feeds directly into holding-company earnings.

Financial and liquidity risk

A debt ratio of 432.2% and total liabilities of KRW 4.85 trillion indicate high leverage. Operating cash flow of KRW 46.4 billion in 2025 narrows room for debt repayment. Affiliate borrowings backed by shares of the chemical affiliate mean changes in collateral value or loan terms could add pressure.

Governance and intragroup transaction risk

AK Holdings paid KRW 43.3 billion to acquire AKIS and lent KRW 56.5 billion to AKIS at an annual rate of 4.6%. The June 2026 transfer of AK PLAZA shares is an intragroup deal, so how costs and benefits are shared with shareholders remains a point to verify. The holding company also keeps the practical financial responsibility for affiliate support.

12

Overall view

AK Holdings' reported results show losses attributable to controlling shareholders in both 2024 and 2025, and the debt ratio at end-2025 was 432.2%, up from 290.3% in 2022.

On the other side, revenue rose in each of the first three quarters of 2026 and net profit attributable to controlling shareholders was positive in all three. The second quarter still had an operating loss, and the gap between operating and net results is worth noting when reading the quarterly trend.

The restructuring, including the AK PLAZA transfer to AKIS and the sale of the consumer-goods affiliate stake, has been completed in part, yet support obligations and share-backed borrowing still sit with the holding company.

The price-to-book ratio is far below 1x, but earnings swing with affiliate cycles, so a multiple alone gives limited guidance. Bullish factors are the quarterly profit flow and eased burdens from restructuring, while bearish factors are full-year losses, lower operating cash flow and affiliate weakness. The 2026 annual report and the first-quarter 2027 report will show how these forces settle.
