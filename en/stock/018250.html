Report overview

Bigger Sales, Thinner Profit at Aekyung Industrial

Third-quarter 2026 sales hit a record for that quarter, yet the company swung to an operating loss of KRW 3.3 billion, and annual operating profit has fallen two years in a row since its 2023 peak.

02

Business structure

Aekyung Industrial runs household goods and cosmetics side by side, and the two businesses share its sales.

Of third-quarter 2026 sales of KRW 190.4 billion, the household goods business contributed KRW 127.8 billion, up 11.5% year on year, and the cosmetics business KRW 58.7 billion, up 13.9% (company release, November 2, 2026). Press reports put the cosmetics share of third-quarter 2026 sales at about 31%.

Reports differ on the 2025 full-year cosmetics share, citing 32% in an August 2026 news report and 33% in a company history summary. Household goods growth was led by personal care products such as hair care and body care, with Kerasys and Showermate cited as key brands.

The cosmetics lineup centers on AGE20'S and LUNA. Overseas sales made up 42% of second-quarter 2026 revenue, up 7 percentage points from a year earlier. In the United States, Kerasys products entered 390 Walmart stores and online channels, and in Japan the AGE20'S Signature Fact sold well during Qoo10 Mega Wari.

In China, AGE20'S has recovered after the business structure was reorganized, but intensifying competition in the Chinese cosmetics market remains a profit challenge. Since July 1, 2026, Taekwang Group has held practical control, placing Aekyung Industrial within its consumer goods portfolio.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual earnings have trended downward since a peak in 2023. Sales rose from KRW 610.4 billion in 2022 to KRW 668.9 billion in 2023 (up 9.6%), then KRW 679.1 billion in 2024 (up 1.5%), which was the largest year of 2022 to 2025. Sales fell 3.6% to KRW 654.5 billion in 2025.

Operating profit climbed from KRW 39 billion in 2022 to KRW 61.9 billion in 2023 (up 58.7%), the peak, then dropped 24.4% to KRW 46.8 billion in 2024 and 54.8% to KRW 21.1 billion in 2025, two straight years of decline. The operating margin moved from 6.4% in 2022 to 9.3% in 2023, 6.9% in 2024 and 3.2% in 2025.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders went from KRW 48.6 billion in 2023 (up 191.4%) to KRW 42.5 billion in 2024 (down 12.6%) and KRW 15.2 billion in 2025 (down 64.2%). Net income including non-controlling interests was KRW 15.1 billion in 2025, a separate figure from the controlling-shareholder number.

Operating cash flow rose from KRW 24.8 billion in 2024 to KRW 41 billion in 2025, diverging from the drop in operating profit. Quarterly, operating profit of KRW 7.3 billion in the third quarter of 2025 gave way to an operating loss of KRW 3.35 billion in the fourth quarter.

The loss narrowed to KRW 1.59 billion in the first quarter of 2026, and the second quarter returned to operating profit of KRW 4.53 billion. The third quarter swung back to an operating loss of KRW 3.3 billion, with a net loss attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 9.82 billion.

Second-quarter 2026 sales of KRW 194.2 billion were the highest for any quarter according to the company's release on August 12, 2026. The company attributed the third-quarter loss to higher brand investment, including global ambassador appointments and experiential marketing. Net income attributable to controlling shareholders over the last four quarters totaled KRW 3.14 billion.

05

Industry analysis

For both businesses, overseas channel expansion has become the main variable for growth. In China, competition in cosmetics has intensified, and the company reorganized its business structure and online and offline distribution, bringing AGE20'S sales back to recovery.

In the United States, Kerasys entered Walmart stores and online channels, while AGE20'S and LUNA entered Olive Young stores. In Japan, online sales grew, and the signature fact product sold well during Qoo10 Mega Wari. Domestically, sales grew mainly through digital and health and beauty (H&B) channels.

The household goods segment centers on personal care, including hair and body care, and home care, so it is larger than cosmetics in the company's sales mix. That makes its profit structure different from cosmetics-focused listed peers such as Amorepacific and Cosmax.

LG H&H, which also runs both household goods and cosmetics, is a more direct comparison on distribution channels and brand investment. The cosmetics business is shifting toward a higher overseas share, while household goods is expanding its sales through domestic and overseas distribution.

06

Outlook

The company has set a target of raising the cosmetics share of sales above 50% by 2028 (August 2026 news report). Given that the cosmetics share was around 32% in 2025, the target requires a fast shift in mix.

The company said it will continue market-specific strategies in the second half, pursuing entry into major global distribution channels and new markets.

For medium- to long-term brand competitiveness, it plans to keep investing in brand activity, including global ambassador appointments, pop-up stores and experiential marketing. In the short term, that spending is weighing on the operating margin, as the third-quarter 2026 operating loss shows.

In household goods, new products include a collaboration line with a character brand, cooling body washes and baking soda capsule detergent. The 2026 annual report will show the full-year trend in brand spending and operating margin.

The next checkpoint is whether the operating margin in the 2026 annual report improves from 3.2% in 2025. Whether the cosmetics share moves toward the 2028 target will also be a yearly point to track.

Continued top-line growth

Both household goods and cosmetics posted double-digit growth. The overseas sales share rose to 42% in the second quarter of 2026.

Possible payoff from brand investment

In China, AGE20'S sales have recovered after the business structure was reorganized, and in Japan its signature fact product sold well during Qoo10 Mega Wari. In the United States, Kerasys entered 390 Walmart stores and online channels. Global ambassador appointments and experiential marketing are linked to this channel expansion.

Balance-sheet room and group backing

Total liabilities at the end of 2025 were KRW 98.7 billion against KRW 399.1 billion of total equity, a debt-to-equity ratio of 24.7%. Operating cash flow rose to KRW 41 billion in 2025 from KRW 24.8 billion in 2024.

Press reports say the industry is watching whether Taekwang Group, which has held practical control since July 1, 2026, can speed up global brand building and distribution.

Bear factors

Eroding profitability

The operating margin fell to 3.2% in 2025 from 9.3% in 2023. The third-quarter 2026 operating loss of KRW 3.3 billion compares with an operating profit of KRW 7.3 billion a year earlier, and the controlling-shareholder net loss in that quarter was KRW 9.82 billion. Net income attributable to controlling shareholders over the last four quarters totaled KRW 3.14 billion.

Weight of brand spending

The company attributed the third-quarter loss to higher costs from global ambassador appointments, experiential marketing and expanded pop-up stores. Even with double-digit sales growth, marketing costs have been offsetting the gains. The operating margin fell from 6.9% in 2024 to 3.2% in 2025.

China competition and reliance on overseas sales

Press reports cite intensifying competition in China's cosmetics market as a profit challenge that predates the change in ownership. AGE20'S recovery follows a business restructuring, so its durability needs more time to show.

With the cosmetics share at around 32% to 33%, the pace of overseas expansion will determine how much support it gives to earnings.

Risk factors

Persistent marketing costs

If brand spending grows faster than sales, operating results could stay in loss territory. Quarterly swings are large, with an operating loss of KRW 1.59 billion in the first quarter of 2026 and KRW 3.3 billion in the third.

The second-quarter 2026 profit shows that results can change from quarter to quarter depending on the size of that spending.

China restructuring and channel changes

The business structure and online and offline distribution in China are being reorganized. Whether the recovery marks a sustained trend or a short-term gain remains to be seen. Changes in the Chinese business feed directly into overall cosmetics sales.

Ownership change and strategy execution

Since Taekwang Group gained practical control on July 1, 2026, portfolio expansion and global brand building have been advancing together. Press reports describe the acquisition's effect on profitability as a test still to be passed. Cost structures may shift for a while as strategy changes are implemented.

Overall view

Aekyung Industrial is a company whose sales are growing while its profitability has weakened. Annual sales were largest in 2024 at KRW 679.1 billion, but operating profit fell from its 2023 peak of KRW 61.9 billion to KRW 46.8 billion in 2024 and KRW 21.1 billion in 2025, two straight years of decline.

Third-quarter 2026 sales of KRW 190.4 billion were the highest for a third quarter, yet the company posted an operating loss of KRW 3.3 billion, which it attributed to higher brand investment. Overseas sales at 42% of second-quarter revenue and double-digit growth in both businesses are strengths.

On the other hand, net income attributable to controlling shareholders over the last four quarters totaled only KRW 3.14 billion, a thin earnings base. A debt-to-equity ratio of 24.7% and total equity of KRW 399.1 billion show financial room.

With Taekwang Group holding practical control since July 1, 2026, the direction of global brand building is drawing attention. The next checkpoints are whether the operating margin in the 2026 annual report improves from 3.2% in 2025 and whether the cosmetics share moves toward the 50% target for 2028.
