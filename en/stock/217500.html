02

Business structure

Russell refurbishes and resells used semiconductor deposition equipment and also runs an unmanned automation business that includes unmanned transport vehicles. According to FnGuide company data, 2024 revenue consisted of semiconductor equipment at 62% and unmanned automation systems at 38%.

The unmanned automation business is run through a consolidated subsidiary. Another tally, based on 2026 second-quarter revenue, shows semiconductor equipment at 90% and automation equipment at 10%, so the segment mix differs by measurement period.

Refurbished equipment is bought used, reconditioned and resold, so results are sensitive to customers' facility investment and the supply of used tools.

The unmanned automation unit has completed commercialization of its forklift product lineup and is developing mobile robot technology, according to company monitoring data. Media reports said the subsidiary signed a contract with Hyundai Motor to supply 40 unmanned forklifts to KIA's dedicated PBV plant in Hwaseong.

Consolidated results include subsidiary profit and loss, and non-controlling interests create a gap between net income and profit attributable to controlling shareholders.

Listed peers in the same KOSDAQ machinery and equipment sector include SFA, People & Technology and Doosan Robotics, but their business mix differs from a used-equipment refurbisher, so direct comparison is limited. Results reflect two drivers, the semiconductor cycle and unmanned automation orders.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual revenue rose from KRW 41.9 billion in 2022 to KRW 44.7 billion in 2023, up 6.6% year on year, then fell 16.2% to KRW 37.5 billion in 2024 and 27.8% to KRW 27.1 billion in 2025.

Among the years 2022 to 2025, 2023 was the largest and 2025 the smallest, and revenue declined two years in a row from 2024 through 2025.

Operating profit fell from KRW 4.5 billion in 2022 to KRW 1.87 billion in 2023, a 58.4% decline, recovered to KRW 2.04 billion in 2024, up 9.2%, and then turned into an operating loss of KRW 5.17 billion in 2025. The operating margin moved from 10.7% in 2022 to 4.2% in 2023, 5.5% in 2024 and -19.1% in 2025.

In 2024 the margin rose even though revenue fell, which points to a recovery driven by profitability rather than volume. Profit attributable to controlling shareholders fell to KRW 1.51 billion in 2023, then rose to KRW 4.2 billion in 2024, before turning into a loss of KRW 5.08 billion in 2025.

Operating cash flow was -KRW 6.62 billion in 2022, KRW 347 million in 2023, KRW 2.32 billion in 2024 and -KRW 3.83 billion in 2025, with large swings between years.

The debt-to-equity ratio declined from 41.3% in 2022 to 22.4% in 2025, with total liabilities of KRW 12 billion and total equity of KRW 53.4 billion at the end of 2025.

On a quarterly basis, third-quarter 2025 revenue was KRW 3.09 billion with an operating loss of KRW 811 million, and fourth-quarter revenue jumped to KRW 13.2 billion, up 326.0% from the prior quarter, while the operating loss widened to KRW 2.41 billion.

What drove the fourth-quarter revenue spike, including whether it reflected order recognition timing, still needs confirmation. First-quarter 2026 revenue was KRW 4.98 billion, down 62.2% quarter on quarter, with an operating loss of KRW 349 million.

Second-quarter revenue rose to KRW 11.2 billion, up 125.3%, and operating profit of KRW 161 million turned the quarter positive.

Third-quarter revenue was KRW 9.84 billion, down 12.2% from the prior quarter, with operating profit of KRW 103 million, down 36.3%, and an attributable net loss of KRW 1.14 billion that moved back into the red. Attributable net losses over the last four quarters totaled KRW 2.41 billion.

05

Industry analysis

Russell operates in refurbished semiconductor equipment, a market that tends to move with new tool investment cycles. The FnGuide company overview says that rising AI and HBM demand is positive for the semiconductor recovery and for growth in the used-equipment market.

The same source says the company is expanding into new business areas as smart factories spread. The company cited delayed new orders as a cause of the 2025 revenue decline, so whether semiconductor equipment demand converts into actual orders is the key point to watch.

In terms of cycle position, revenue fell from 2024 and rebounded in 2026 quarters, but the size of the quarterly rebounds has varied, so calling it a sustained trend would be premature.

Among competitors, Russell focuses on reconditioning used tools rather than manufacturing new ones, so its demand can move differently from new equipment investment.

The unmanned automation unit ties into demand for unmanned material handling in factories and logistics, and a report described an order from an automaker's dedicated electric-vehicle plant.

Doosan Robotics and Robotis are also in the robotics space within the same sector, but their business mix differs, so direct comparison of earnings levels is limited. The 2026 industry picture depends on whether semiconductor facility spending turns into new order disclosures.

Bull factors

Quarterly profit turn and revenue rebound

Third-quarter operating profit of KRW 103 million also compares favorably with the operating loss a year earlier. The shift from operating losses to profit on a quarterly basis is the main bullish point.

Expanding unmanned automation orders

Media reports said the subsidiary signed a contract with Hyundai Motor to supply 40 unmanned forklifts to KIA's PBV plant in Hwaseong. The forklift lineup is commercialized and mobile robot technology is in development.

Hyundai Motor Group's robotics and PBV investment could become a demand base for this business, which is a bullish factor.

Lower debt and financial room

The debt-to-equity ratio has declined since 2022, and total equity far exceeds total liabilities. A lighter debt burden gives the company more capacity to absorb loss-making periods. Financial capacity does not, however, guarantee an earnings recovery.

Bear factors

Two years of falling revenue and a return to loss

Annual revenue fell from KRW 44.7 billion in 2023 to KRW 37.5 billion in 2024 and KRW 27.1 billion in 2025. The company cited delayed new orders and higher costs compressing margins for the 2025 operating loss of KRW 5.17 billion. The operating margin has not returned to its 2022 level of 10.7%, which is a bearish point.

Losses returning on a quarterly basis

In the third quarter of 2026, the attributable net loss of KRW 1.14 billion moved back into the red after a profitable second quarter. Operating profit did not translate into a profit attributable to controlling shareholders, which is a bearish point.

Cash flow deficit and quarterly volatility

Operating cash flow was -KRW 3.83 billion in 2025. Quarterly revenue swung from KRW 13.2 billion in the fourth quarter of 2025 to KRW 4.98 billion in the first quarter of 2026, down 62.2% quarter on quarter. Because revenue is hard to predict, the uncertainty around earnings estimates is large.

Risk factors

Semiconductor facility investment cycle

A third-party tally shows semiconductor equipment accounted for 90% of second-quarter 2026 revenue. The company cited delayed new orders for the 2025 revenue decline, a risk that could recur.

A pullback in facility investment could delay orders again, and used-equipment demand may not move in step with new equipment spending.

Subsidiary order execution risk

The reported contract for 40 unmanned forklifts is the main order in the unmanned automation unit, but its value and revenue timing remain to be confirmed through future disclosures. As the subsidiary's share of revenue grows, its profit swings feed directly into consolidated results. Delays in delivery or acceptance would also push revenue recognition further out.

Financial and cash flow volatility

Operating cash flow was negative in 2022 at -KRW 6.62 billion and again in 2025 at -KRW 3.83 billion. Quarterly revenue moved sharply, including a 62.2% decline in the first quarter of 2026. Such swings could make it harder to sustain dividends and facility investment on a steady basis.
