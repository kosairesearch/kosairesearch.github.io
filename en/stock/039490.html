Report overview

KIWOOM Securities: Trading Volume Recovery and IB Growth as Key Swing Factors

KIWOOM Securities' 3Q 2026 sales rose sharply from a year earlier, but lower domestic trading volume pushed operating profit and profit attributable to owners down from the prior quarter, so the earnings direction depends on market trading volume versus IB fee growth.

  1. 1

    Sales in 3Q 2026 reached KRW 6.93 trillion, up 105.7% from a year earlier, while operating profit of KRW 406.1 billion fell 0.7%.

  2. 2

    Full-year 2025 operating profit was KRW 1.49 trillion, rising for two consecutive years from the 2023 low.

  3. 3

    Brokerage commission income fell 42.9% from the prior quarter to KRW 286.9 billion, and press reports attributed the decline mainly to lower domestic daily trading value.

  4. 4

    IB fee income reached KRW 96.8 billion in 3Q 2026, up 62.4% year on year, and grew from KRW 93.9 billion in 2023 to KRW 277.0 billion in 2025.

  5. 5

    Weak spots were trading-related income and costs: investment and S&T income fell 43.7% from the prior quarter, while SG&A rose 63.2% year on year.

02

Business structure

KIWOOM Securities began as a retail brokerage focused on individual investors and has expanded into corporate finance (IB) and investment businesses.

Brokerage commission income in 3Q 2026 was KRW 286.9 billion, up 30.8% year on year, including stock commissions of KRW 244.7 billion and derivatives commissions of KRW 42.2 billion. Press reports said the company's domestic retail market share stayed at 25.0% in the third quarter, the same level as the prior quarter.

IB fee income was KRW 96.8 billion, up 62.4% year on year, and structured and project financing fees made up the largest share at KRW 73.8 billion. Reported drivers included real estate development participation, acquisition financing arrangement, and bond underwriting.

Investment and dividend income was KRW 140.2 billion, up 90.0% year on year but down 43.7% from the prior quarter, as equity valuation losses on proprietary investments weighed. Net interest income was KRW 221.7 billion, of which credit-loan interest was KRW 123.5 billion.

Reported client assets under management were KRW 27.1 trillion. Because brokerage, IB and investment income respond to different market variables, weakness in one segment can be partly offset by another. Competition for retail customers with large securities firms is the central variable for the business.

04

Earnings analysis

The annual filings show revenue and profit rising together. Revenue went from KRW 8.94 trillion in 2022 to KRW 9.54 trillion in 2023 (up 6.7%), KRW 11.3 trillion in 2024 (up 18.2%) and KRW 17.1 trillion in 2025 (up 51.8%), rising for three consecutive years from 2023 through 2025.

Among 2022 to 2025, 2025 was the largest year and 2022 the smallest. Operating profit fell from KRW 656.4 billion in 2022 to KRW 564.7 billion in 2023 (down 14.0%), then rose to KRW 1.1 trillion in 2024 (up 94.5%) and KRW 1.49 trillion in 2025 (up 35.5%), increasing for two consecutive years.

Profit attributable to owners of the parent bottomed at KRW 436.3 billion in 2023, then reached KRW 835 billion in 2024 and KRW 1.11 trillion in 2025, also up for two consecutive years. Net income including non-controlling interests, a separate figure, was KRW 1.11 trillion in 2025.

The operating margin was 7.3% in 2022, 5.9% in 2023, 9.7% in 2024 and 8.7% in 2025, so in 2025 revenue growth outpaced operating profit growth and the margin declined from 2024.

Over the same period the debt-to-equity ratio rose from 888.8% in 2024 to 1,107.5% in 2025, with total equity of KRW 6.72 trillion and total liabilities of KRW 74.5 trillion at year-end 2025. Operating cash flow was negative KRW 6.4 trillion in 2025, with the outflow widening every year since 2022.

Quarterly figures moved more sharply. Revenue rose from KRW 3.37 trillion in 3Q 2025 to KRW 5.59 trillion in 4Q 2025, KRW 9.4 trillion in 1Q 2026 and KRW 16.1 trillion in 2Q 2026, then fell 56.9% to KRW 6.93 trillion in 3Q 2026.

Operating profit peaked at KRW 788.9 billion in 2Q 2026 and fell 48.5% to KRW 406.1 billion in 3Q 2026. Profit attributable to owners dropped 73.8% from KRW 680.1 billion in 2Q 2026 to KRW 177.9 billion in 3Q 2026, and the last four quarters total KRW 1.58 trillion.

Press reports tied the 3Q decline mainly to lower commissions as domestic trading value shrank.

Press reports also noted about KRW 100 billion of non-operating income from fund holdings in 2Q 2026 that did not recur in 3Q, and fund and partnership losses of about KRW 72.9 billion in 3Q that exceeded subsidiary profits of about KRW 25.4 billion.

05

Industry analysis

Korea's securities industry is structurally exposed to market cycles because commission income is directly linked to trading value.

Domestic average daily trading value fell to KRW 75.5 trillion in 3Q 2026, and KIWOOM's average daily domestic stock brokerage volume dropped from KRW 36.3 trillion in 2Q 2026 to KRW 21.5 trillion in 3Q 2026. Domestic stock commissions fell 47% from the prior quarter over the same period.

That shows how sharply a retail-focused broker's fee income responds to a smaller trading market. Press reports said the company's domestic retail market share held at 25.0%, the same level as the prior quarter, so share held even as the market shrank.

Overseas stock daily brokerage also fell from KRW 2.1 trillion to KRW 1.2 trillion, according to press reports. On the IB side, large real estate PF and acquisition financing deals and bond underwriting supported results.

Reported 3Q 2026 deals included a KRW 150 billion private convertible bond subscription for Hyundai E&C and a KRW 133.1 billion rights offering underwritten for TLB. The company competes with large securities firms for retail customers, so its earnings capacity moves with short-term market swings.

06

Outlook

On the 3Q 2026 earnings call, the company said a domestic market correction and lower trading value weighed on brokerage and investment income. The biggest variable going forward is whether trading value recovers to 2Q 2026 levels.

In the IB pipeline, structured and PF fees rose from KRW 65.8 billion in 2Q 2026 to KRW 73.8 billion in 3Q 2026, and M&A fees rose from KRW 3.2 billion to KRW 10.0 billion. Credit-loan balances reached KRW 5.7 trillion at the end of September 2026, above the 3Q average of KRW 5.1 trillion.

A larger credit book widens interest income, but it also increases the impact of collateral value swings on earnings when markets move sharply. Separate non-operating income swung from a KRW 99.2 billion gain in 2Q 2026 to a KRW 5.1 billion loss in 3Q 2026.

Press reports said shareholder returns remain centered on cash dividends. Future earnings therefore rest on two pillars: domestic trading value recovery and sustained IB deal flow.

08

Bull factors

Shift in IB fee structure

IB fees rose from KRW 93.9 billion in 2023 to KRW 277.0 billion in 2025, according to Herald Business. In 3Q 2026 they reached KRW 96.8 billion, up 62.4% year on year, with structured and PF fees making up KRW 73.8 billion. Diversifying away from brokerage dependence is cited as the main bullish point.

Annual earnings recovery

Operating profit rose two years in a row after the 2023 low of KRW 564.7 billion, reaching KRW 1.1 trillion in 2024 and KRW 1.49 trillion in 2025. Profit attributable to owners reached KRW 1.11 trillion in 2025, well above the 2023 low. The operating margin, however, slipped to 8.7% in 2025 from 9.7% in 2024.

Sensitivity of fees to trading recovery

Domestic average daily trading value fell to KRW 75.5 trillion in 3Q 2026, but stock commissions track trading volume closely in both directions. The steady 25.0% domestic retail share is cited as the basis for benefit from a recovery. Domestic stock commissions were KRW 316.5 billion in 2Q 2026.

Bear factors

Sharp drop in brokerage income

Brokerage commission income in 3Q 2026 fell 42.9% from the prior quarter to KRW 286.9 billion, and stock commissions dropped 45.9% to KRW 244.7 billion. Average daily domestic brokerage volume fell from KRW 36.3 trillion in 2Q 2026 to KRW 21.5 trillion in 3Q 2026. The uncertain timing of any trading recovery is the main bearish point.

Volatility in investment and non-operating income

Investment and dividend income was KRW 140.2 billion in 3Q 2026, down 43.7% from 2Q, as equity valuation losses on proprietary holdings weighed. Reported fund and partnership losses of about KRW 72.9 billion exceeded subsidiary profits of about KRW 25.4 billion, a net drag of about KRW 47.5 billion. The roughly KRW 100 billion non-operating gain booked in 2Q 2026 did not recur.

Costs rising faster than income

SG&A expenses reached KRW 259.4 billion in 3Q 2026, up 63.2% year on year, and the expense ratio rose from 29.7% to 36.4%. Separate-basis operating profit fell 34.3% from the prior quarter. Costs rising faster than income is cited as a bearish factor.

Risk factors

Brokerage and investment income swing sharply with domestic trading value and volatility. Domestic daily trading value fell to KRW 75.5 trillion in 3Q 2026, and commissions dropped 42.9% from the prior quarter.

Revenue swinging from KRW 9.4 trillion in 1Q 2026 to KRW 16.1 trillion in 2Q and back to KRW 6.93 trillion in 3Q also shows that sensitivity.

Balance sheet and leverage

At year-end 2025, total liabilities were KRW 74.5 trillion against total equity of KRW 6.72 trillion, and the debt-to-equity ratio rose from 888.8% in 2024 to 1,107.5% in 2025. Operating cash flow went from negative KRW 2.0 trillion in 2022 to negative KRW 6.4 trillion in 2025. Changes in the debt structure and cash flow should be tracked together.

Credit and investment exposure

The credit-loan balance reached KRW 5.7 trillion at the end of September 2026, above the 3Q average of KRW 5.1 trillion. A larger credit book raises the effect of collateral value declines on earnings during sharp market drops.

Separate non-operating income also swung from a KRW 99.2 billion gain in 2Q 2026 to a KRW 5.1 billion loss in 3Q 2026.

Overall view

KIWOOM Securities' full-year 2025 filings show revenue, operating profit and profit attributable to owners all rising. In 3Q 2026, however, lower domestic trading value cut brokerage commissions and investment income, pushing operating profit and profit attributable to owners well below the prior quarter.

IB fee income rose 62.4% year on year to KRW 96.8 billion in the same quarter, which is cited as evidence of revenue diversification. Bullish points are IB fee growth, a steady 25.0% retail share, and the earnings recovery through 2025.

Bearish points are dependence on trading value, rising SG&A, volatility in investment and non-operating income, and a higher debt-to-equity ratio alongside a deeper negative operating cash flow. Earnings direction depends on whether domestic trading value recovers and whether IB fees hold near the 3Q 2026 level.
