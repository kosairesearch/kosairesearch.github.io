02

Business structure

Samsung Publishing is a publisher centered on children's and juvenile books, and its 2026 second-quarter sales mix, as compiled by an equity data service, was 93% publishing and 7% leasing.

The company's profile describes its products as sold mainly through online and offline bookstores and book sections of social commerce platforms, and says it holds a leading position in the juvenile trade book market.

The company says it has secured direct distribution channels to consumers, which it uses to reflect changes in demand quickly while keeping price competitiveness. In its 2025 results commentary, the company described a differentiation strategy amid concerns about a shrinking market.

The leasing business is a smaller secondary segment relative to publishing. Press reports indicate that equity-method results tied to Artbox also affect the company's profit. In juvenile educational content, the company competes with firms built on character intellectual property and digital learning services.

Listed peers in the same sector include The Pinkfong Company. Because sales run through bookstores and online channels, the fixed book price system directly affects its pricing environment.

04

Earnings analysis

On a disclosed basis, sales fell from KRW 51.7 billion in 2022 to KRW 41.8 billion in 2023 (down 19.1% year on year), KRW 40.6 billion in 2024 (down 3.0%), and KRW 40.3 billion in 2025 (down 0.7%), declining for three consecutive years from 2023.

The steepest drop came in 2023, and the declines in the following two years were milder.

The company posted an operating loss of KRW 901 million in 2022, an operating loss of KRW 1.31 billion in 2023, an operating loss of KRW 181 million in 2024, and an operating loss of KRW 795 million in 2025, a loss in each of the four years.

The operating margin was -1.7% in 2022, -3.1% in 2023, -0.4% in 2024, and -2.0% in 2025, with 2024 showing the smallest loss ratio.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders fell from KRW 16.4 billion in 2022 to KRW 8.75 billion in 2023 (down 46.8%), recovered to KRW 10.9 billion in 2024 (up 24.0%), and dropped to KRW 4.05 billion in 2025 (down 62.7%).

The pattern of operating losses alongside positive net income persisted, and the company attributed the 2025 deterioration in profit to lower income from equity-method results related to Artbox.

Quarterly, operating income of KRW 79.86 million in the third quarter of 2025 turned into an operating loss of KRW 295 million in the fourth quarter of 2025, and the company has reported losses in four consecutive quarters through the third quarter of 2026, with operating losses of KRW 198 million, KRW 214 million, and KRW 100 million in the first through third quarters of 2026.

Quarterly sales rose to KRW 10.9 billion in the fourth quarter of 2025, eased to KRW 10.7 billion in the first quarter of 2026 and KRW 10 billion in the second quarter, and recovered to KRW 10.6 billion in the third quarter (up 6.9% year on year).

Operating cash flow fell from KRW 19.7 billion in 2022 to KRW 1.95 billion in 2023, KRW 3.06 billion in 2024, and KRW 1.88 billion in 2025, remaining positive each year.

At the end of 2025, total equity was KRW 164 billion and total liabilities were KRW 30.7 billion, and the debt ratio moved from 20.4% in 2022 to 18.7% in 2025.

05

Industry analysis

Demand in the juvenile publishing market is directly tied to the number of births. The company's own results commentary cites the low birth rate as a primary reason the market is shrinking. Combined with reduced consumption after the fixed book price system took effect, publishing and leasing sales both declined.

On distribution, bookstores and the book sections of social commerce platforms are close to consumers, but unit prices and discount levels are shaped by the system. In cycle terms, the demand contraction stems from demographic structure, so an economic recovery alone is unlikely to restore sales.

The company is presented as having strong brand recognition in juvenile trade books, but it competes for the same age group's spending with digital learning content and character IP businesses. The Pinkfong Company, a listed peer in the same sector, is built around content IP and offers a useful comparison point.

Whether the company's differentiation strategy can offset the shrinking customer base will determine its position in the industry.

06

Outlook

Sales in the third quarter of 2026 were KRW 10.6 billion, up 6.9% year on year, which marks a sign of sales recovery among recent quarters.

The operating loss in the same quarter was KRW 100 million, narrower than the prior quarter, but losses have continued for four straight quarters, so whether the company returns to operating profit is the key watch point.

Since the operating loss began in the fourth quarter of 2025 at KRW 295 million, quarterly losses have narrowed from the KRW 200 million range to the KRW 100 million range.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders stayed positive at KRW 1.98 billion, KRW 3.74 billion, and KRW 1.73 billion in the first through third quarters of 2026 despite operating losses, and whether this came from non-operating items such as equity-method results can be verified only through the composition in periodic reports.

Public materials offer limited numerical detail on the specifics of the differentiation strategy or the timing of new products. With leasing at 7% of sales, the overall sales trajectory depends largely on publishing.

External factors such as the birth population and the fixed book price system are unlikely to change quickly, which limits the upside to sales. Operating cash flow remaining positive every year shows that the core business has not lost all cash generation.

The fourth quarter of 2026 is the final quarter of the current loss streak, and full-year results will be confirmed in the periodic report after year-end closing.
