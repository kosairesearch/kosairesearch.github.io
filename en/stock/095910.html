02

Business structure

S-EnergyCO is a first-generation Korean solar specialist that combines PV module manufacturing, EPC construction of solar plants, and operation and maintenance under one business structure.

The affiliate list in the March 31, 2026 quarterly report includes 22 subsidiaries operating solar power plants, placing power generation at the center of the group structure. Orders come mainly from state-owned power generators, and the company said its major orders for 2026 exceeded KRW 30 billion.

Representative projects include the Gasi-ri solar project in Jeju (about KRW 12 billion) and the definitive contract for the Kamei repowering project in Japan (about KRW 7.2 billion).

Two projects contracted in September 2025, Lee S-Energy 2 (about KRW 8.28 billion) and CS Energy (about KRW 8.18 billion), were 45% to 51% complete as of March 31, 2026.

In a project for Korea Western Power, the company secured an 88.09% share of a total project cost of about KRW 12.5 billion and handles equipment supply and core work.

On the product side, it is developing high-efficiency N-type and HJT modules, and an independent research firm cited the company's HJT KS certification as evidence of technological edge.

Overseas, it signed a MOU in May 2026 for supplying 20 MW of HJT modules in the Middle East, with a first 3 MW phase in progress, and it is pursuing grid-scale ESS projects through its Japanese base. Its subsidiary S-Fuel Cell runs hydrogen fuel cell operations, and S-Power runs O&M. In modules, it competes on price with low-cost products from supply chains concentrated in China.

04

Earnings analysis

On a filed basis, revenue fell from KRW 208.7 billion in 2022 to KRW 169.9 billion in 2023 (down 18.6% year on year), KRW 147.8 billion in 2024 (down 13.0%), and KRW 102.4 billion in 2025 (down 30.7%). Among 2022 to 2025, 2022 was the largest year and 2025 the smallest.

The operating result moved from an operating loss of KRW 8.91 billion in 2022 to an operating profit of KRW 3.14 billion in 2023, a turnaround to profit, then to an operating loss of KRW 14.4 billion in 2024, a return to loss, and widened to an operating loss of KRW 18.7 billion in 2025.

The operating margin was -4.3% in 2022, 1.8% in 2023, -9.7% in 2024 and -18.2% in 2025. The company cited EPC permitting delays and a decline in hydrogen fuel cell downstream demand and government support as reasons for the 2025 revenue drop.

The same disclosure said inventory valuation losses at a subsidiary produced a gross loss, and allowances for doubtful receivables at a subsidiary were also reflected.

These items could be read as non-recurring, but two consecutive years of losses in 2024 and 2025 suggest that profitability has not yet clearly recovered.

Attributable net losses were KRW 23.4 billion in 2022, KRW 5.36 billion in 2023, KRW 24.6 billion in 2024 and KRW 13.5 billion in 2025, four straight years of losses. Including non-controlling interests, the 2025 net loss was KRW 28.7 billion, larger than the attributable figure.

The debt ratio rose from 206.0% in 2022 to 240.9% in 2024 and 243.6% in 2025. Operating cash flow was KRW 16.6 billion positive in 2024 but turned to a negative KRW 11.2 billion in 2025.

Quarterly revenue peaked at KRW 28.2 billion in the fourth quarter of 2025 and then declined for three consecutive quarters, to KRW 26.4 billion, KRW 24.2 billion and KRW 22.3 billion in the first three quarters of 2026.

The operating loss narrowed from KRW 8.37 billion in the fourth quarter of 2025 to KRW 6 billion and KRW 4.34 billion in the first two quarters of 2026, then widened again to KRW 4.57 billion in the third quarter.

05

Industry analysis

The global PV module market is concentrated in China, where supply exceeds demand, and the resulting price declines are weighing on the profitability of module makers in Korea. S-EnergyCO itself cites this structural pressure as a background to its weak results.

At home, the government is pursuing a solar localization strategy, and policies such as a production tax credit for domestic cell makers are being discussed. These policies are viewed as a positive factor for domestic module producers.

The company cited EPC permitting delays as a reason for the 2025 revenue decline, which connects to the pace of permits for domestic power projects. The hydrogen fuel cell segment was affected by a shrinking downstream demand base as government support was reduced.

Public orders from state-owned power generators provide stability in orders, but schedules can shift with policy and permitting. Diversification into offshore-durable modules, colored BIPV and combined solar-thermal modules connects with demand from expanding zero-energy building mandates.

Compared with domestic solar-related stocks grouped in the same theme, such as SK eternix, the company is presented as a first-generation player with a value chain spanning module manufacturing, plant construction and operation.

06

Outlook

The company has said it aims to convert secured orders into profit and cash collection. To do so, it has reinforced its EPC risk management staff and set up stage-by-stage reviews of contract terms, input costs, progress and receivables collection.

On cash flow, it has described aligning billing and collection schedules with equipment procurement and construction spending.

For the projects contracted in September 2025, Lee S-Energy 2 (about KRW 8.28 billion), CS Energy (about KRW 8.18 billion) and Dongshin Solar (about KRW 4.30 billion) were 45.6% to 50.8% complete as of March 31, 2026, and the quarter in which this construction revenue is recognized is a key variable for upcoming results.

In new businesses, a KRW 12.9 billion national R&D project is developing offshore-durable solar modules, with a roadmap that starts with small-scale field trials with the Korea Rural Community Corporation, moves to stage-by-stage trials at Saemangeum inland water, and then to commercialization.

In buildings, the company is targeting public buildings and multi-family housing, and it is co-developing colored PV modules with a materials firm and combined solar-thermal modules with a heating and cooling equipment maker.

Overseas, follow-on volumes under the 20 MW HJT Middle East MOU and grid-scale ESS through the Japanese base are presented as growth pillars. On shareholder returns, the company decided on July 6, 2026 to cancel all 860,275 treasury shares it held. A rights offering in March 2026 was reported as completed, strengthening financial soundness.

Bull factors

Order accumulation and utility references

The company said its cumulative major orders for 2026 exceeded KRW 30 billion, and its state-owned utility customers are building a track record that analysts cite as a strength.

In a Korea Western Power project, it secured an 88.09% share of a total cost of about KRW 12.5 billion and took on core work, following completion of the roughly KRW 41.1 billion Iwonho floating solar project. How much of these orders is recognized as revenue after 2026 is the next point to confirm.

Localization policy and technology certification

The government is pursuing policies to strengthen a domestic localization ecosystem, including a production tax credit for domestic cell makers, which is viewed as a positive for domestic module producers.

An analyst at the independent research firm Valuefinder cited the company as the first in Korea to obtain KS certification for HJT modules. In May 2026 the company signed a MOU to supply 20 MW of HJT modules in the Middle East, and a first 3 MW phase is underway.

Narrower losses and shareholder-return steps

The operating loss narrowed to KRW 4.34 billion in the second quarter of 2026 from KRW 6 billion in the first quarter. A rights offering in March 2026 was reported as completed, bolstering financial soundness, and in July 2026 the company decided to cancel all 860,275 treasury shares it held.

Third-quarter revenue of KRW 22.3 billion rose 1.1% year on year, so the year-on-year revenue decline has paused.

Bear factors

Four straight years of attributable losses

Attributable net losses have continued for four straight years from 2022 to 2025 and for five consecutive quarters through the third quarter of 2026.

The cumulative attributable net loss over the last four quarters is KRW 22 billion, which is sizable relative to the KRW 34.8 billion of attributable equity at the end of 2025. The third-quarter 2026 operating loss of KRW 4.57 billion was larger than the KRW 4.53 billion recorded a year earlier.

Revenue decline and permitting delays

Revenue declined for three straight years from 2023 to 2025 and reached KRW 102.4 billion in 2025. The company cited EPC permitting delays and lower downstream demand and government support for hydrogen fuel cells as the reasons.

Although third-quarter 2026 revenue rose year on year, it has fallen for three consecutive quarters since the fourth-quarter 2025 peak, so a recovery trend is not yet established.

Financial burden and cash flow reversal

Total liabilities at the end of 2025 were KRW 161.5 billion, exceeding total equity of KRW 66.3 billion, and the debt ratio stood at 243.6%. Operating cash flow swung from KRW 16.6 billion positive in 2024 to a negative KRW 11.2 billion in 2025. Price pressure from supply chains concentrated in China is also weighing on module margins.

Risk factors

Listing status and trading conditions

The stock was designated as a managed issue on August 13, 2026 for trading below the price threshold, and the exchange disclosure noted that delisting could follow if that condition persists for a set period. Trading was halted from October 6, 2026 for a share consolidation and resumed on October 28.

Because the share count has changed, volume and liquidity measures must be read against a different base than before.

Profitability and cash flow

Operating cash flow was a negative KRW 11.2 billion in 2025, and the debt ratio rose from 206.0% in 2022 to 243.6% in 2025. The operating loss widened again in the third quarter of 2026, so the loss pattern has not broken.

Inventory valuation losses and allowances for doubtful receivables reflected in 2025 results are factors that slow the pace of a profitability recovery.

Policy dependence and governance

Growth drivers such as localization policy and overseas MOUs depend on policy timing and the progress of partner projects. Projects ordered by state-owned utilities have also seen revenue fall after permitting delays in the past.

A disclosure showed that on April 3, 2026, the representative director transferred 400,000 shares to a related party, Hong Moon-gyo, so changes in the ownership structure merit attention.

Overall view

S-EnergyCO's filed results show revenue declines and a loss pattern since 2022, with operating losses recorded for five consecutive quarters through the third quarter of 2026. Third-quarter 2026 revenue of KRW 22.3 billion rose 1.1% year on year, yet the operating loss widened to KRW 4.57 billion.

Positive factors include cumulative major orders for 2026 surpassing KRW 30 billion, localization policy and HJT module certification, the Middle East MOU, and the cancellation of treasury shares.

Negative factors include four straight years of attributable net losses, an attributable net loss of KRW 22 billion over the last four quarters, a debt ratio of 243.6%, a swing to negative operating cash flow in 2025, and the managed-issue designation.

These two sides pull in opposite directions, and the key test for results is whether orders convert into revenue and cash collection. That process will appear in numbers in the 2026 annual report and the 2027 quarterly reports.

Valuation sits at a discount to book value, but because losses are reducing equity, the trend in book value must be read alongside it. This report does not present an investment opinion or target price, and its figures are based on DART periodic report values and confirmed news.
