Hyundai-Rotem: K2 execution and the timing of new export orders

Hyundai-Rotem's 2025 operating profit of KRW 1.01 trillion was the largest of 2022 to 2025, yet net profit attributable to controlling shareholders has fallen for three straight quarters in 2026, and the timing of the next large export contracts, including Poland's third and Peru's, is the key variable.

02

Business structure

Hyundai-Rotem runs a rail rolling stock segment (Rail Solutions), a defense segment and a plant business, and is listed in the aerospace and defense sector.

The core of its defense business is the K2 tank: the second execution contract with Poland covers 180 tanks, made up of 116 export-type K2GF units for 2026 to 2027 and 64 Polish-configured K2PL units for 2028 to 2030.

Of the 64 K2PL, 61 are to be assembled locally at the Bumar-Łabędy plant in Poland, and the other three are produced in Korea. Customers are national government procurement bodies such as the Polish Armament Agency and the Peruvian Army, so the timing and size of contracts depend on government decisions.

New orders in the second quarter of 2026 totaled KRW 2.5 trillion, including a Ho Chi Minh City electric train project (KRW 499.1 billion) and a Moroccan refinery project (KRW 787.2 billion), according to Hyundai Motor Securities' August 2026 report, which also said the order backlog rose slightly to KRW 30.4 trillion.

In Poland, Hanwha Aerospace's K9 self-propelled howitzers are also being procured alongside the K2, so the two Korean land systems compete in the same export market. Leopard 2A8 and KF51 Panther are cited as rival tanks in Europe. Defense profitability depends on when initial risk-cost provisions are reversed.

Daol Investment & Securities attributed the first-quarter 2026 defense operating margin of 27.2%, down 1.9 percentage points year on year, to EC2 work being executed at smoothed estimated costs.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual revenue rose from KRW 3.16 trillion in 2022 to KRW 3.59 trillion in 2023 (up 13.4% year on year), KRW 4.38 trillion in 2024 (up 22.0%) and KRW 5.84 trillion in 2025 (up 33.4%), and has increased for three straight years from 2023 to 2025.

Operating profit expanded from KRW 147.5 billion in 2022 to KRW 1.01 trillion in 2025, and 2025 was the largest year of 2022 to 2025. The operating margin moved from 4.7% in 2022 to 5.9%, 10.4% and 17.2% in 2025, so profitability improved alongside revenue growth.

In 2023 operating profit rose 42.4% to KRW 210 billion while net profit attributable to controlling shareholders fell 18.6% to KRW 161 billion, so the two moved in opposite directions.

Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders then rose to KRW 406.9 billion in 2024 (up 152.7%) and KRW 769.9 billion in 2025 (up 89.2%), two consecutive years of growth. Total net profit in 2025 was KRW 770.5 billion, nearly identical to the controlling-shareholder figure.

Operating cash flow fell to KRW 142.5 billion in 2024 and recovered to KRW 904.3 billion in 2025. Over the same period the debt-to-equity ratio rose from 163.1% in 2024 to 206.4% in 2025, and total liabilities at end-2025 were KRW 6.28 trillion.

On a quarterly basis, first-quarter 2026 revenue of KRW 1.46 trillion, down 10.3% from the prior quarter, was the low point, followed by KRW 1.61 trillion in the second quarter and KRW 1.62 trillion in the third, two straight quarters of growth.

Q3 2026 operating profit of KRW 247.1 billion rose 6.3% from the prior quarter but fell 11.0% from a year earlier. Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders peaked at KRW 222.4 billion in Q4 2025 and fell for three straight quarters to KRW 176.6 billion in Q3 2026.

The first-quarter 2026 defense operating margin of 27.2% reflected EC2 work being executed at smoothed estimated costs, and Daol Investment & Securities expected a reversal toward the end of EC2 (April 2026).

05

Industry analysis

Demand in the defense market rests on a supply-demand imbalance in the global tank market. Korea Investment & Securities said in an October 2026 report that, given this imbalance, the probability of contracts being signed remains high, while the timing of signing is uncertain.

In Europe, demand for tanks and armored vehicles from countries bordering Russia has been cited, and Romania is seen as the likely next buyer after Poland.

In June 2026 the Polish government reaffirmed that its plan to acquire 1,000 K2 tanks would proceed as originally agreed, after concerns about a reduction in K-defense exports followed a change of government.

In the competitive field, a Hana Securities report from April 2026 pointed to Leopard 2A8 being only at the early stage of deliveries and KF51 Panther still in development, and assessed Hyundai-Rotem's chances of winning orders as still high.

Romania has not included its main battle tank program in SAFE loan eligibility, leaving a funding-side variable. In land systems, Hanwha Aerospace's K9 competes in the same Polish market. In rail, overseas electric train projects are the main source of order visibility.

06

Outlook

Deliveries under Poland's second execution contract are proceeding on schedule. The company planned to deliver 58 of its 116 K2GF units in 2026, that delivery program has been completed, and a further 58 K2GF units are planned for 2027.

Production under the second execution contract is scheduled to finish by 2030, and the 61 K2PL units assembled locally will be built at the Bumar-Łabędy plant. A third execution contract (EC3) for 210 K2PL tanks is being pursued, and if signed by year-end, delivery would be set for 2026 to 2034.

If the third contract is completed, the number of K2 tanks deployed in Poland would rise to 570. Hyundai Motor Securities said in an August 2026 report that the timing of major orders such as Peru, Iraq and Poland EC3 determines medium-term earnings growth.

In December 2025 the company signed a master supply agreement with Peru for 54 K2 tanks and 141 K808 wheeled armored vehicles, and many expect the execution contract to exceed KRW 3 trillion.

However, Hana Securities said in April 2026 that the timing of Peru's execution contract had been pushed back in view of the presidential election and the start of the new government.

Romania is reportedly discussing 216 K2 tanks with 76 support vehicles, a package worth more than KRW 11 trillion according to local media.

Poland's 2027 defense budget bill did not include K2 funding, but the final budget vote on November 20 or a supplementary budget could still include it, according to Financial News.

Overall view

Hyundai-Rotem's disclosed results show revenue and operating profit rising together from 2022 to 2025. Operating profit reached KRW 1.01 trillion in 2025, with an operating margin of 17.2%, the largest year of the period.

However, net profit attributable to controlling shareholders declined for three straight quarters in 2026 through Q3, and Q3 2026 operating profit was down 11.0% from a year earlier. The trailing four-quarter total of KRW 785.8 billion exceeds the 2025 full-year figure for controlling-shareholder net profit.

The growth drivers are the delivery and local production under Poland's second execution contract, the third Polish contract for 210 tanks, and Peru's execution contract.

Korea Investment & Securities and Hyundai Motor Securities both lowered estimates or target prices in 2026 reports, citing uncertainty over order timing. The near-term checkpoints are whether Poland's 2027 budget includes K2 funding after the November 20 vote.
