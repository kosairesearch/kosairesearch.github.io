02

Business structure

FarmStory, founded in 1991 and part of the EZ Holdings group, operates compound feed and meat processing as its two core businesses. As of the first half of 2026, revenue was composed of feed at 49.6%, meat processing at 41.6%, poultry at 7.3%, and other at 1.6%.

The largest shareholder is EZ Holdings, a holding company that has vertically integrated the feed, livestock, and meat processing value chain, with a 48.72% stake. The company produces and sells compound feed for swine, poultry, and cattle, while also handling carcass and cut meat products and chicken-related items.

The hog integration business is run through subsidiary Farmsworld, in which FarmStory holds a 90% stake; Farmsworld posted operating losses in 2022–2023 amid higher feed costs before turning profitable in 2024.

Farmsworld's net income recovery was delayed by heavy borrowings and interest expense until a KRW 50 billion rights offering in September 2025, in which FarmStory contributed KRW 45 billion pro rata, resolved its full capital impairment.

In the domestic compound feed market, the company competes with Farmsco, Sunjin, Koryo Industrial, Hanil Feed, and Daeju Industrial, and ranks among the sector's larger players. The meat processing segment's performance is closely tied to slaughter and hog sales volumes and the trajectory of hog prices.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual revenue rose from KRW 1.43 trillion in 2022 to KRW 1.5 trillion in 2023 (up 5.2% YoY), then fell to KRW 1.45 trillion in 2024 (down 3.1% YoY) before edging up to KRW 1.46 trillion in 2025 (up 0.1% YoY), with 2023 the largest and 2022 the smallest of the four years.

Operating profit was KRW 31.5 billion in 2022, KRW 26.2 billion in 2023 (down 16.6% YoY), KRW 40.8 billion in 2024 (up 55.4% YoY), and KRW 35 billion in 2025 (down 14.2% YoY), making 2024 the largest and 2023 the smallest.

Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders swung from a loss of KRW 7.55 billion in 2022 to a profit of KRW 10 billion in 2023, then fell to KRW 3.89 billion in 2024 (down 61.2% YoY) before surging to KRW 30.1 billion in 2025 (up 674.3% YoY).

The operating margin fluctuated across the period at 2.2% in 2022, 1.7% in 2023, 2.8% in 2024, and 2.4% in 2025.

On a quarterly basis, operating profit improved from KRW 15.9 billion in Q2 2025 to KRW 19.3 billion in Q3 2025 (up 21.7% QoQ), then swung to an operating loss of KRW 3.93 billion in Q4 2025, before turning profitable again at KRW 8.69 billion in Q1 2026 and expanding to KRW 17.6 billion in Q2 2026 (up 102.9% QoQ, up 11.0% YoY).

Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders followed a similar path, posting losses of KRW 3.37 billion in Q4 2025 and KRW 2.03 billion in Q1 2026 (loss narrowing) before turning to a profit of KRW 11.3 billion in Q2 2026 (down 35.7% YoY), with the trailing four quarters (Q3 2025–Q2 2026) totaling KRW 16.8 billion.

One media report analyzing cumulative nine-month 2025 results (as of January 29, 2026) noted that despite lower operating profit, expanded financial income from foreign-exchange gains and fair-value gains on financial instruments drove the net profit increase, indicating that non-operating factors have meaningfully influenced net income swings.

05

Industry analysis

The Korea Rural Economic Institute's 'Agricultural Outlook 2026' projected domestic hog wholesale prices at KRW 5,500–5,700 per kg, below the 2025 level of KRW 5,763 per kg, citing increased slaughter volumes as the main driver of expanded supply.

At the same time, pork imports are forecast to rise 1.6% YoY to about 457,000 tonnes, suggesting downward price pressure on both domestic and imported pork. Conversely, international import prices for feed grains have reportedly been revised upward multiple times through 2026, keeping cost pressure elevated.

Under this structure, the key variables are whether the feed segment can pass through rising costs via pricing, and whether the meat processing segment can defend profitability as hog prices soften.

Korea's compound feed market has been described as facing slowing growth and declining production volumes, shifting the competitive focus from volume expansion toward cost efficiency and price-linked margin protection.

FarmStory forms a listed compound-feed peer group alongside Farmsco, Sunjin, Koryo Industrial, Hanil Feed, and Daeju Industrial, and has been assessed as trading at a price-to-book ratio broadly similar to that peer group.

06

Outlook

FarmStory disclosed a 2026 corporate value-up plan on April 1, 2026, setting goals of strengthening profitability-focused management and financial soundness, with action items including cost competitiveness, productivity improvement, stabilizing its borrowing structure, and maintaining a sustainable dividend policy.

In that disclosure, the company stated it qualifies as a high-dividend company under the Restriction of Special Taxation Act, with a 2025 dividend payout ratio of 36.73%.

The Korea Investors Service Research Center of the Korea IR Service published a report on September 2, 2026, projecting 2026 revenue of KRW 1,454.5 billion (down 0.1% YoY) and operating profit of KRW 39 billion (up 11.4% YoY).

The same report expected feed-segment sales to decline in the first half on lower domestic production and sales volumes, partly offset by second-half price increases, while meat-processing revenue was projected to dip slightly on lower slaughter and hog sales volumes even as profitability improves, led by Farmsworld.

Farmsworld's operating profit rose 81.9% YoY to KRW 3.7 billion in 2025, and it posted a net profit of KRW 10.2 billion (11.6% net margin) in the first half of 2026, reflecting reduced interest burden after the rights offering.

However, the report flagged valuation gains and losses on biological assets from the hog-raising business as a factor that increases quarterly earnings volatility.

08

Bull factors

Earnings Stability in the Feed Segment

An analysis noted that between 2023 and 2025, even as revenue declined, the decline in operating profit was relatively limited, attributed to the feed business's price-linked structure cushioning profit swings relative to top-line changes.

This is cited as a factor supporting relatively steady cash generation despite grain-price volatility. Operating cash flow improved from negative KRW 79.6 billion in 2022 to KRW 28.3 billion in 2024 and KRW 77.4 billion in 2025, contributing to a stronger balance sheet.

Farmsworld's Earnings Normalization

Subsidiary Farmsworld resolved full capital impairment through a KRW 50 billion rights offering in September 2025, reducing borrowings from KRW 149.4 billion to KRW 103.9 billion and turning total equity positive at KRW 27.8 billion.

Its net profit reached KRW 10.2 billion with an 11.6% net margin in the first half of 2026, reflecting a lighter interest burden. This feeds through to margin-improvement potential in FarmStory's consolidated meat-processing and hog businesses.

Expanded Dividends and Improved Balance Sheet

The 2025 year-end dividend was set at KRW 100 per share, four times the KRW 25 level of 2023–2024, and the company's 2026 corporate value-up plan explicitly commits to maintaining a sustainable dividend policy.

The debt-to-equity ratio fell from 291.3% in 2022 to 186.6% in 2025, alongside improving financial soundness metrics. The sharp rise in 2025 net profit attributable to controlling shareholders to KRW 30.1 billion also supported greater capacity for shareholder returns.

09

Bear factors

Quarterly Earnings Volatility

Quarterly results swung sharply, with an operating loss of KRW 3.93 billion and a net loss of KRW 3.37 billion attributable to controlling shareholders in Q4 2025, followed by a continued net loss in Q1 2026.

Valuation gains and losses on biological assets from the hog business are cited as a factor amplifying this volatility. Because these are unrealized valuation items rather than realized gains, they reduce the predictability of quarterly results.

Pressure on Meat Processing from Softer Hog Prices

The Korea Rural Economic Institute projected domestic hog wholesale prices in 2026 to stay at KRW 5,500–5,700 per kg, below 2025 levels, due to rising supply. Pork imports are also expected to increase year-on-year, which could weigh on both revenue and margins in the meat processing and hog businesses.

Conversely, feed grain import costs face upward pressure, raising the possibility of a simultaneous cost squeeze.

Net Income Dependence on Non-Operating Factors

One media report analyzing cumulative nine-month 2025 results noted that even as operating profit declined year-on-year, net profit rose on expanded foreign-exchange gains and fair-value gains on financial instruments.

Such financial income items can swing significantly with exchange rates and market conditions, and the report flagged the possibility that these gains could reverse into losses if conditions change. Whether core operating profit improves remains a more important variable for assessing the quality of net income.

10

Risk factors

Short-Term Debt Repayment Burden

One media report noted that short-term borrowings due within one year account for 91% of total debt, describing a structure under significant repayment and refinancing pressure.

In April 2026, FarmStory disclosed an extension of a debt guarantee with NongHyup Bank through April 2027, underscoring continued reliance on affiliate credit support. Should interest rates or refinancing terms worsen, interest expense burden could increase again.

Raw Material and Currency Volatility

Feed grain import prices in dollar terms have reportedly been on an upward trend, which could sustain cost pressure. Won-dollar exchange rate movements directly affect not only import costs but also net profit through foreign-exchange and valuation gains or losses. If price increases fail to offset rising costs, the stability of feed-segment profit could weaken.

Biological Asset Valuation Volatility

Fluctuations in the net fair value of consumable biological assets from the hog business are cited as a factor that can swing quarterly earnings significantly, independent of actual cash flow.

Valuation gains and losses can alternate depending on hog price trends, making it difficult to draw firm trend conclusions from any single quarter's results. This is flagged as a point investors should weigh carefully when interpreting quarterly figures.

12

Overall view

FarmStory showed an earnings recovery in 2025, with revenue of KRW 1.46 trillion and net profit attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 30.1 billion, underpinned by the feed segment's price-linked cost structure, yet quarterly volatility persists, as seen in the Q4 2025 operating loss and the Q1 2026 net loss.

The resolution of capital impairment and the return to profit at subsidiary Farmsworld are cited as qualitative improvements to consolidated earnings, but the impact of biological-asset valuation swings and financial income on net profit also warrants attention.

Dividends rose sharply to KRW 100 per share for fiscal 2025, and the company has committed to maintaining a sustainable dividend policy into 2026, though the high proportion of short-term debt in its financial structure still bears watching.

The 2026 industry backdrop features both softer hog prices and rising feed grain costs, making it important to track whether feed pricing power and Farmsworld-led meat-processing profitability improvements can offset these pressures.

Readers should review upcoming quarterly disclosures, dividend decisions, and hog price and grain cost trends before forming any investment judgment.
