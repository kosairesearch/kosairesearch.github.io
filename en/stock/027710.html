02

Business structure

FarmStory is a livestock specialist founded in 1991 whose structure centers on compound feed, with carcass, pork, and chicken businesses alongside. As of March 2026, the sales mix was compound feed 49%, carcass and related 24%, pork sales 11%, chicken and related 7%, and other 8%.

With compound feed close to half of sales, the feed business forms the core of the revenue base.

The company describes itself on its corporate introduction page as the number one laying-hen feed maker in the industry, and its history section records a monthly record of 65,795 tons of laying-hen feed sold in November 2023.

Because the company itself published this material, it has limits as an independent market statistic. In slaughter operations, the company recorded 672,534 head in 2024 for a 3.53% share, broadly in line with 3.61% in 2022 and 3.51% in 2023.

Since feed and meat together make up sales, pork and Korean beef prices and grain raw material costs are the main variables for earnings. In 2025, the company cited higher pork and Korean beef prices as a driver of sales growth.

Competitive comparison is mainly against listed compound feed makers, and one equity research report benchmarked valuation against a peer group that includes Farmsco. The same report also noted that the return to profit at a subsidiary coincided with the earnings rebound. The structure combines feed-business stability with sensitivity to meat prices.

04

Earnings analysis

Based on DART periodic reports, annual revenue rose from KRW 1.43 trillion in 2022 to KRW 1.5 trillion in 2023 (up 5.2% year on year), then moved to KRW 1.45 trillion in 2024 (down 3.1%) and KRW 1.46 trillion in 2025 (up 0.1%).

The company cited higher pork and Korean beef prices as the main driver of 2025 sales growth. Operating profit was KRW 31.5 billion in 2022, KRW 26.2 billion in 2023 (down 16.6%), KRW 40.8 billion in 2024 (up 55.4%), and KRW 35 billion in 2025 (down 14.2%).

Operating margin was 2.2%, 1.7%, 2.8%, and 2.4% for 2022 through 2025, a thin margin structure relative to sales size. Within 2022 to 2025, 2024 was the largest year for operating profit and 2023 the smallest.

Parent net income moved from a KRW 7.55 billion net loss in 2022 to KRW 10 billion in 2023, a return to profit, then KRW 3.89 billion in 2024 (down 61.2%) and KRW 30.1 billion in 2025 (up 674.3%).

Total net income for 2025 was KRW 30.7 billion, and it differs from parent net income because it includes non-controlling interests.

Cumulative foreign exchange gains for the first nine months of 2025 were KRW 9.4 billion, more than six times the KRW 1.5 billion in the same period of 2024, and because this item moves with exchange rates it carries a largely one-off character.

By quarter, operating profit of KRW 19.3 billion in 3Q 2025 was followed by an operating loss of KRW 3.93 billion in 4Q 2025, a swing into loss. Operating profit of KRW 8.69 billion in 1Q 2026 was a return to profit from the prior quarter, and it recovered to KRW 17.6 billion in 2Q 2026 (up 102.9% quarter on quarter).

Operating profit in 3Q 2026 was KRW 5.78 billion, down 67.2% from the prior quarter and down 70.1% year on year. Revenue in the same quarter reached KRW 369 billion, up 2.1% year on year, so top-line growth coexisted with a sharp margin decline.

Operating cash flow went from -KRW 79.6 billion in 2022 and -KRW 13.9 billion in 2023 to KRW 28.3 billion in 2024 and KRW 77.4 billion in 2025.

05

Industry analysis

The domestic compound feed market faces slowing growth and declining domestic output. FarmStory's own explanation of its 1Q 2026 results noted that slower growth in the compound feed market and lower domestic production led to a slight decline in feed division sales.

The same explanation said cost reduction and efficiency gains improved gross profit and operating profit. On the meat side, the government launched on-site inspections of livestock market disruption in response to unstable pork and egg prices.

The Ministry of Agriculture, Food and Rural Affairs designated the inventory of pork hind-leg meat at the top six large meat processors as an inspection target, and FarmStory and Farmsco were both included. Suspicions of extra payment demands in egg distribution were also added to the inspection scope.

FarmStory also appears on the list of nine companies sanctioned by the Korea Fair Trade Commission for price collusion. On the cycle, feed demand is slowing gradually while meat prices lack a clear direction, which suggests a phase in which earnings volatility can widen in both segments.

Because Farmsco was placed under the same inspection, the regulatory environment is a shared industry variable. Whether the company's claim of leading the laying-hen feed segment connects with egg price trends can be checked through changes in the sales mix in the next periodic report.

Outlook

One equity research report projected 2026 revenue at KRW 1.45 trillion (down 0.1% year on year) and operating profit at KRW 39 billion (up 11.4%). The report was published in early September 2026, before the 3Q 2026 results were disclosed on November 16.

Given disclosed operating profit of KRW 8.69 billion in 1Q 2026, KRW 17.6 billion in 2Q 2026, and KRW 5.78 billion in 3Q 2026, whether the projection is reached depends on 4Q operating profit.

Revenue in 3Q 2026 rose 2.1% year on year to KRW 369 billion, but operating profit fell 70.1%, so whether the margin assumed in the projection holds is the central question.

In the feed business, slower growth in the compound feed market was cited as a cause of lower sales, so whether cost reductions offset that revenue pressure is the checkpoint.

The 2025 fiscal year year-end dividend was decided at a board meeting in February 2026 with an increase, and at the next dividend decision it will be visible how earnings recovery and dividend policy connect.

The return to profit at a subsidiary was named as one factor in the 2026 earnings rebound, so whether that continues into 4Q is under review. When the 2026 annual report comes out, the full-year operating margin relative to 2.4% in 2025 will be the next checkpoint.

08

Bull factors

Quarterly profit rebound and consecutive revenue growth

Operating profit turned positive in 1Q 2026 at KRW 8.69 billion and rose to KRW 17.6 billion in 2Q 2026, up 102.9% from the prior quarter. Revenue also grew in two consecutive quarters, reaching KRW 363 billion in 2Q 2026 and KRW 369 billion in 3Q 2026.

Operating profit recovered within two quarters of the KRW 3.93 billion operating loss in 4Q 2025, which is cited as evidence of earnings resilience.

Feed-centered earnings stability and a larger dividend

One equity research report cited limited swings in operating profit relative to sales movements as evidence of earnings stability. Based on that stability, the year-end dividend for fiscal 2025 was raised substantially from 2023 and 2024, and the board decided it in February 2026. A business structure with compound feed close to half of sales is seen as providing a buffer for profits.

Bottom of historical valuation band and dividend level

Since 2021 the price-to-book ratio has moved between 0.3x and 2.5x, and one report assessed the current level as sitting at the bottom of that band on 2026 estimated earnings. Parent net income of KRW 30.1 billion in 2025 shows an earnings recovery after the 2022 net loss.

09

Bear factors

Sharp drop in 3Q operating profit

Revenue in 3Q 2026 rose to KRW 369 billion, but the margin fell sharply. Together with the KRW 3.93 billion operating loss in 4Q 2025, the pattern of sharp quarterly swings is repeating.

Possible reliance on foreign exchange gains

An explanation has been offered that much of the 2025 net income improvement was supported by higher foreign exchange gains.

Cumulative FX gains for the first nine months of 2025 rose to KRW 9.4 billion from KRW 1.5 billion in the same period of 2024, and commentary notes this item is highly variable with market conditions. If the exchange rate direction reverses, the contribution from non-operating income could shrink.

Debt burden and slowing feed market

Total liabilities at the end of 2025 were KRW 630.9 billion against total equity of KRW 338.2 billion, with a debt ratio of 186.6%. In 1Q 2026, slower growth in the compound feed market and lower domestic output led to a slight decline in feed division sales. With top-line growth in the core business limited, the debt load could amplify earnings swings.

10

Risk factors

Policy and regulation

The Ministry of Agriculture, Food and Rural Affairs named FarmStory among six large meat processors whose pork hind-leg inventories are under inspection.

FarmStory is also on the list of nine companies sanctioned for price collusion by the Korea Fair Trade Commission, and exclusion from policy fund support is under review. Business conditions could change depending on the outcome of institutional reforms.

Raw materials and livestock prices

The company has explained that changes in pork and Korean beef prices drive sales. Egg price instability and suspicions of extra payment demands in distribution are also under review. Whether pricing can be defended when prices fall will need to be checked through future gross profit trends.

Exchange rates and non-operating items

Foreign exchange gains grew during a period of falling exchange rates. In 3Q 2026, parent net income of KRW 19.7 billion was well above operating profit of KRW 5.78 billion, which makes the reliance of net income on non-operating items a central question. If the exchange rate reverses or non-operating income shrinks, net income could fall below operating profit.

Overall view

FarmStory is a livestock company centered on compound feed that maintains revenue in the KRW 1.4 trillion range, and annual earnings recovered from a 2022 net loss to parent net income of KRW 30.1 billion in 2025.

However, commentary notes that much of 2025 profit was influenced by foreign exchange gains and livestock prices, so annual figures alone make it hard to judge earnings capacity.

Quarterly results in 2026 rebounded to KRW 17.6 billion operating profit in 2Q before falling to KRW 5.78 billion in 3Q, confirming volatility. Strength factors include earnings stability from the feed business, a larger dividend, and a price-to-book ratio at the bottom of its historical band.

Weakness factors include quarterly earnings swings, possible reliance on FX gains, a 186.6% debt ratio, and inclusion in government inspections and collusion sanctions. 4Q 2026 results will be confirmed in the 2026 annual report at the end of March 2027, and whether they match the external projection of KRW 39 billion operating profit is the next checkpoint.
