Earnings Recovering, but Operating Cash Outflow Widening at Han Kook Capital

Operating profit rose in both 2024 and 2025 and profit attributable to owners has grown for three straight years, yet the operating cash outflow has deepened every year since 2022, so earnings and cash flow are moving in different directions.

  1. 1

    Operating profit reached KRW 127.9 billion in 2025, up 32.1% year on year, the largest year in the 2022 to 2025 period.

  2. 2

    Profit attributable to owners was KRW 100.5 billion in 2025, extending a three-year growth run that began in 2023, and net income including non-controlling interests was reported at the same amount.

  3. 3

    Operating cash flow moved from KRW -77 billion in 2022 to KRW -529.3 billion in 2025, a widening deficit.

  4. 4

    The debt ratio bottomed at 537.3% in 2023 and climbed to 562.1% in 2024 and 583.7% in 2025, when total liabilities stood at KRW 4.4 trillion.

  5. 5

    Operating profit of KRW 29.7 billion in the third quarter of 2026 fell 1.7% year on year, and profit attributable to owners of KRW 25.3 billion fell 4.1%, so quarterly profit growth has stalled.

02

Business structure

Han Kook Capital was founded in November 1989 as a specialized credit finance company and is listed on KOSDAQ under code 023760. The company's website groups its products into four areas: leasing, installment finance and rental, personal finance, corporate finance, and auto finance.

Its auto finance offerings include installment loans for used passenger cars, auto leasing, and loans for used commercial vehicles and construction equipment.

Its personal lending includes auto-collateral loans, business real estate collateral loans, the M+ loan product and unsecured loans for military personnel and civilian military staff. Corporate finance covers business loans, project financing loans and factoring.

The largest shareholder is a mutual aid association for military personnel, which a shareholder data site listed at 80.41% as of September 2025. This shareholder base and the military-oriented loan lineup point to a customer base concentrated in one group.

In auto finance, captive captive-affiliated and independent capital companies have led the market, and specialty card companies and banks have entered auto installment lending.

According to a report in the Korean press (Etoday), card companies and banks gained share quickly by offering lower interest rates and benefiting from exclusion from DSR regulation. Segment revenue and asset mix should be compared on a consistent basis once confirmed from the filings.

04

Earnings analysis

Operating profit, based on filed annual reports, fell from KRW 88.9 billion in 2022 to KRW 82.1 billion in 2023, a 7.6% decline, and then rose 17.9% to KRW 96.9 billion in 2024 and 32.1% to KRW 127.9 billion in 2025.

Operating profit has grown in two consecutive years, 2024 and 2025, and 2025 is the largest year of the 2022 to 2025 period, while 2023 is the smallest.

Profit attributable to owners rose from KRW 65.1 billion in 2022 to KRW 66.2 billion in 2023 (up 1.7%), KRW 81.1 billion in 2024 (up 22.6%) and KRW 100.5 billion in 2025 (up 23.9%), a three-year growth run starting in 2023.

Net income including non-controlling interests was filed at the same amounts each year, so no gap between the two measures appears in this period. Operating profit growth in 2025 of 32.1% outpaced the 23.9% growth in attributable profit, so the improvement was larger at the operating line.

On a quarterly basis, operating profit was KRW 30.2 billion in the third quarter of 2025, KRW 36.6 billion in the fourth quarter (up 21.1% from the prior quarter), KRW 29.1 billion in the first quarter of 2026 (down 20.4%), KRW 38.7 billion in the second quarter (up 33.0%) and KRW 29.7 billion in the third quarter (down 23.3% quarter on quarter and down 1.7% year on year).

Profit attributable to owners over the same five quarters was KRW 26.4 billion, KRW 27.9 billion, KRW 23.4 billion, KRW 26 billion and KRW 25.3 billion, and the last four quarters total KRW 102.6 billion.

The second quarter of 2026 was the highest operating profit quarter in the last five, but it fell back in the third quarter, so the quarterly direction is not clear.

Operating cash flow was KRW -77 billion in 2022, KRW -279.8 billion in 2023, KRW -502.5 billion in 2024 and KRW -529.3 billion in 2025, a deficit that deepened each year. The reason profit growth and a widening operating cash deficit occurred together should be checked against the line-item detail in the filing body. The debt ratio fell from 659.5% in 2022 to 537.3% in 2023, then rose to 562.1% in 2024 and 583.7% in 2025.

05

Industry analysis

According to a report in the Korean press (Etoday), capital companies' auto installment financing assets reached KRW 33.9 trillion at the end of 2024, up KRW 860.7 billion from the prior year.

The same report said capital-industry auto installment assets have kept rising since 2022, with growth in the used-car market cited as a driver.

Over the same period, the eight specialty card companies' auto installment assets fell by KRW 167.8 billion to KRW 9.5 trillion, the second consecutive annual decline, and a drop in new-car sales was cited as the reason.

Domestic used-car registrations were 2.54 million in 2024, down only 0.5%, which the report described as a relatively resilient market given lower new-car sales.

Non-performing loans for the capital industry, meaning substandard and worse loans, rose by KRW 1.11 trillion to KRW 3.8 trillion at the end of 2024, and some analysts link the increase to losses in real estate project financing.

The same report noted that capital firms have leaned toward auto finance, which they have long offered and which is seen as a safer asset, rather than expanding project finance to protect asset quality.

For Han Kook Capital, the key question is how its combined auto, corporate and personal finance mix responds to this shift in the industry.

Outlook

The starting point for the outlook is the quarterly profit trend in 2026. Operating profit moved from KRW 29.1 billion in the first quarter to KRW 38.7 billion in the second and then KRW 29.7 billion in the third.

Profit attributable to owners of KRW 25.3 billion in the third quarter fell 4.1% year on year, so quarterly profit growth has paused. The last four quarters total KRW 102.6 billion, slightly above the KRW 100.5 billion reported for full-year 2025.

Auto installment balances at capital companies are still growing, but continued low-rate competition from banks and card companies could pressure lending spreads.

The next regular report is the 2026 annual report, where the key items are how full-year operating profit and operating cash flow compare with 2025 and where the debt ratio moves from 583.7%.

Whether this discount narrows is cited as depending on the earnings trend and asset-quality indicators.

Operating cash flow went from KRW -77 billion in 2022 to KRW -529.3 billion in 2025, with the deficit larger every year. The widening cash deficit alongside rising profit is cited as the main bear point. The cause of the gap cannot be pinned down until the line-item detail in the filing body is checked.

Debt ratio climbing again

The debt ratio fell to 537.3% in 2023 but then rose to 562.1% in 2024 and 583.7% in 2025. Total liabilities of KRW 4.4 trillion are large relative to equity of KRW 754.1 billion, a highly leveraged structure. A shift in funding or interest-rate conditions could add pressure to earnings, which is the bear argument.

Signs of slowing quarterly profit

Operating profit dropped back after reaching KRW 38.7 billion in the second quarter, so the quarterly pattern has not clearly improved. Whether annual growth carries into quarterly results is the central bear question.

Risk factors

Asset quality and project finance losses

Substandard and worse loans across the capital industry rose by KRW 1.11 trillion to KRW 3.8 trillion at the end of 2024. Some analysts link the increase to real estate project financing losses.

If industry asset quality deteriorates, credit costs could erode profit, and the company's own trend should be checked in the filing body.

Interest rates and competition

Banks and specialty card companies entered auto installment lending with lower rates and benefit from exclusion from DSR regulation. Card-company auto installment assets have fallen two years in a row, but a shift in competition could pressure capital companies' lending rates and spreads. Changes in funding costs also feed directly into margins.

Concentrated ownership and float

The largest shareholder is a military personnel mutual aid association, shown at 80.41% in a shareholder data site as of September 2025. When a single holder controls most of the shares, the tradable float is limited and price swings can be larger. Changes in ownership structure and the free float can be checked through filings.

Overall view

Han Kook Capital's operating profit rose in 2024 and 2025 to KRW 127.9 billion, and profit attributable to owners has grown for three straight years to KRW 100.5 billion.

However, the operating cash outflow deepened every year from KRW -77 billion in 2022 to KRW -529.3 billion in 2025, and the debt ratio rose again from 537.3% in 2023 to 583.7% in 2025.

Quarterly results in 2026 have been mixed, at KRW 29.1 billion, KRW 38.7 billion and KRW 29.7 billion in operating profit across the first three quarters, and third-quarter attributable profit fell 4.1% year on year.

The share price trades well below book value, and how that discount is read depends on whether cash flow and asset-quality indicators move in the same direction as earnings. The industry shows rising substandard loans from project finance losses alongside growing auto installment balances. The key check is how annual cash flow and the debt ratio change in the 2026 annual report.
