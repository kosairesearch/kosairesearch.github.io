Chobi: Fertilizer Profits Swing Between Gains and Losses

02

Business structure

Chobi is a KOSPI-listed chemical company whose main business is manufacturing and selling fertilizer.

The company runs a process capable of producing eco-friendly custom fertilizers based on scientific soil testing, as well as slow-release fertilizers, and it produces compound fertilizers in more than 100 product items for sale through Nonghyup and local dealers.

In Q2 2026, fertilizer products made up 88% of revenue, other items 8%, and traded fertilizer goods 4%, so self-produced fertilizer accounts for most sales. Because raw materials are largely imported, the company has weak international competitiveness and is heavily exposed to swings in raw material prices.

Sales run through Nonghyup contracts and dealers, and company summary material attributes weaker profitability to market liberalization and bidding competition within Nonghyup.

The same material lists expanding Nonghyup contracts, cutting costs, developing higher-value products and promotion to raise market share as priorities.

Product count descriptions differ by source: a 2023 brokerage report described more than 280 fertilizer products, while the current company profile cites about 100 items.

Unlike petrochemical and battery-materials peers such as LG Chem and Hanwha Solutions, Chobi is a specialist focused on fertilizer for agricultural markets. At the annual general meeting on March 26, 2026, shareholders approved the 71st fiscal year financial statements as proposed.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual revenue fell from KRW 148.6 billion in 2022 to KRW 114.7 billion in 2023, KRW 102.4 billion in 2024 and KRW 99.7 billion in 2025. Revenue has declined for three consecutive years from 2023 through 2025, and 2022 was the largest year of the 2022–2025 span.

Operating profit went from KRW 11.9 billion in 2022 to an operating loss of KRW 5.58 billion in 2023, which turned back into a profit of KRW 4.83 billion in 2024, and then fell 20.9% to KRW 3.82 billion in 2025. The operating margin moved from 8.0% in 2022 to -4.9% in 2023, 4.7% in 2024 and 3.8% in 2025.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders went from KRW 8.64 billion in 2022 to a net loss of KRW 9.04 billion in 2023, KRW 640 million in 2024 and KRW 1.97 billion in 2025, a 208.3% increase.

A factor behind the 2023 weakness is visible in the first quarter of that year, when lower sales, lower product selling prices and higher raw material costs combined to produce an operating loss.

Operating cash flow ran at KRW -13.7 billion in 2022, KRW 4.8 billion in 2023, KRW -507 million in 2024 and KRW 4.03 billion in 2025. For the first nine months of 2025 on a standalone basis, revenue fell 5.7%, operating profit fell 13.4% and net income rose 18.0%, so operating and bottom-line trends diverged.

Quarterly figures show Q3 2025 revenue of KRW 11.3 billion with an operating loss of KRW 2.62 billion, followed by Q4 2025 revenue of KRW 14.1 billion and an operating loss of KRW 712 million.

Q1 2026 revenue reached KRW 43.6 billion with operating profit of KRW 4.73 billion, and Q2 2026 revenue was KRW 46.4 billion with operating profit of KRW 3.84 billion. Q3 2026 revenue then dropped to KRW 9.14 billion with an operating loss of KRW 2.94 billion.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders for the last four quarters totals KRW 3.71 billion. The product, merchandise and other breakdown behind the Q1 2026 surge and the Q3 2026 drop should be checked in the annual report notes.

05

Industry analysis

According to company summary material, competition over prices and bids has intensified in fertilizer since market liberalization. When price competition in Nonghyup bidding pushes down selling prices, operating margins come under pressure.

On the raw material side, high import dependence exposes the company to exchange rates and global commodity prices. As in the first quarter of 2023, falling selling prices combined with rising input costs can quickly turn profit into loss.

Crop prices and farmers' purchasing power are common drivers of fertilizer demand, though volume indicators for demand were not part of the material reviewed here. Judging the industry cycle requires external indicators such as fertilizer price indices and domestic sales volumes.

Among chemical peers, LG Chem, Hanwha Solutions and Lotte Chemical focus on petrochemicals and battery materials, so their inputs and customers differ from Chobi's. Comparisons with the industry are therefore more meaningful against agricultural material companies.

A 13.4% cumulative decline in operating profit through the first nine months of 2025 shows that price pressure is feeding directly into profitability. Whether the Q1 2026 revenue surge and the Q3 2026 drop reflect demand or the timing of bid volumes is a point to clarify.

That plan appears in material covering the first nine months of 2025, so actual results must be checked in subsequent quarterly figures and margins.

The effect of cost cuts is most directly visible in the operating margin trend, against the 3.8% reached in 2025. Bear factors

Losses Widened Again in Q3

Declining Revenue and Thin Margins

Annual revenue fell from KRW 148.6 billion in 2022 to KRW 99.7 billion in 2025, declining for three consecutive years from 2023. The operating margin was 3.8% in 2025, a thin level. The company posted an operating loss of KRW 5.58 billion in 2023, so earnings capacity has not been stable even on an annual basis. The debt-to-equity ratio of 125.4% is also elevated.

Price Competition and Cost Pressure

Company summary material says that market liberalization and bidding competition within Nonghyup have weakened profitability. Heavy reliance on imported raw materials leaves the company exposed to cost swings.

In the first quarter of 2023, lower selling prices and higher raw material costs together produced an operating loss of KRW 780 million. In a structure where selling prices are hard to raise, rising costs can erode profit quickly.

10

Risk factors

Raw Material Prices and Exchange Rates

Because raw materials are imported, global commodity prices and exchange-rate moves flow directly into costs. If selling prices cannot be raised when costs rise, operating margins fall quickly. The first-quarter 2023 loss is an example where lower selling prices and higher raw material costs acted together. The effect of the cost-cutting plan depends on how raw material prices move.

Distribution Channels and Bidding Competition

Sales depend on Nonghyup contracts and dealers, and company summary material describes price competition in Nonghyup bidding as pressuring profitability. A change in contract terms with one channel can swing quarterly revenue sharply.

The gap between Q1 2026 revenue of KRW 43.6 billion and Q3 2026 revenue of KRW 9.14 billion illustrates that sensitivity. Whether expanded Nonghyup contracts reduce this volatility remains an open question.

Quarterly Volatility and Financial Burden

At the end of 2025, total liabilities of KRW 68.6 billion exceeded total equity of KRW 54.7 billion. The debt-to-equity ratio moved from 111.5% in 2022 to 126.8% in 2023, 127.7% in 2024 and 125.4% in 2025, staying at an elevated level. Operating cash flow was KRW -507 million in 2024 and KRW -13.7 billion in 2022. With quarterly profit swinging and debt burden high, the capacity to absorb shocks is limited.
