Ray: Whether Operating Profit Can Hold Is the Key Question

02

Business structure

Ray, founded in 2004 and listed on KOSDAQ in 2019, makes digital dental imaging diagnostic equipment and treatment solutions. A 2025 securities report put the 2024 revenue mix at 52% for digital treatment solutions and 48% for digital diagnostic systems.

The share of digital treatment solutions grew from 7% in 2017 to 66% in 2023, making it the main growth driver. Core products include dental CBCT and panoramic digital X-ray systems, 3D printers and milling machines.

According to a company profile, Ray runs sales offices in 13 countries including the United States, Japan and Australia, and a 2025 report cited a dealer network of around 70. The regional mix has shifted sharply.

Edaily reported that China accounted for about 3% of 2025 revenue and that US sales rose more than 200% year on year. Competition from low-priced Chinese equipment makers has intensified and weighs on profitability.

Edaily also reported that a Middle East sales gap in the first quarter of 2026 was offset by a focus on advanced markets. WiseReport attributes revenue growth partly to rising demand from dental CAD/CAM and wider 3D printing adoption.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual revenue rose from KRW 129 billion in 2022 to KRW 145.9 billion in 2023, an increase of 13.1%, then fell to KRW 79.8 billion in 2024, a decrease of 45.3%. Revenue recovered to KRW 111.3 billion in 2025, up 39.4% year on year. Among 2022 to 2025, 2023 was the largest year and 2024 the smallest.

Operating profit fell from KRW 16.2 billion in 2022 to KRW 6.12 billion in 2023, a decrease of 62.2%, and then turned into an operating loss of KRW 44.3 billion in 2024. The 2025 operating loss narrowed to KRW 9.79 billion, and operating losses were recorded in both 2024 and 2025.

The operating margin moved from 12.6% in 2022 to 4.2% in 2023, -55.4% in 2024 and -8.8% in 2025. Shareholder net income swung to a net loss of KRW 60.4 billion in 2024 before returning to a net profit of KRW 3.14 billion in 2025. The 2025 net profit of KRW 467 million includes non-controlling interests.

Digital Today reported that the 2025 improvement reflected better profitability and gains from investment asset disposals and valuations. Quarterly, the operating profit of KRW 184 million in the third quarter of 2025 became an operating loss of KRW 8.34 billion in the fourth quarter.

Operating profit of KRW 5.65 billion in the second quarter of 2026 was followed by a third-quarter operating loss of KRW 606 million, and third-quarter revenue of KRW 25.2 billion fell 26.2% from the prior quarter and 16.9% from a year earlier.

Shareholder net losses over the last four quarters totaled KRW 18.9 billion. The debt ratio eased from 174.4% in 2024 to 157.0% in 2025, with total liabilities of KRW 117.4 billion and total equity of KRW 74.8 billion at year-end 2025.

05

Industry analysis

Demand from the dental sector is tied to the shift toward digital imaging and treatment. WiseReport cites growth in the dental CAD/CAM market and wider 3D printing adoption as revenue drivers.

In China, Edaily reported that weak consumer demand and a shrinking dental market led Chinese equipment makers to cut prices sharply, reshaping competition. Against that backdrop Ray has reduced its dependence on China and expanded in advanced markets such as the United States and Europe.

WiseReport also reported that costs from expanding sales offices and launching new products weighed on profitability in the first quarter of 2026. Component cost pressure from memory and storage prices is a possible factor. Edaily reported that a Middle East sales gap affected revenue in the first quarter of 2026.

Domestic dental imaging makers are named as competitors, and overseas sales depend largely on distribution structure and product differentiation. The industry therefore combines demand growth with intense price competition.

06

Outlook

After the May 2026 disclosure, a company representative said that once revenue expands in earnest from the second quarter, operating leverage from fixed costs would gradually improve profitability (Edaily, Nate).

Operating profit of KRW 5.65 billion in the second quarter of 2026 fit that direction, but the third quarter returned to an operating loss of KRW 606 million as revenue declined, so whether fixed-cost leverage works consistently is the next thing to watch.

In a late-2025 Edaily report, the company expected revenue above KRW 130 billion in 2026, about 20% growth on the prior year, and said US sales would keep growing rapidly.

Quarterly revenue in 2026 has ranged between KRW 25.2 billion and KRW 34.1 billion, so the gap to that target is the point to track through year-end.

For South America, Ray has completed the setup of a Brazilian subsidiary and is going through local product certification, and the company expects revenue contribution after certification. With China at about 3% of revenue, how the overseas mix develops is also worth tracking. The regional revenue and cost ratios in the 2026 annual report will serve as the test of this outlook.

Bull factors

Narrowing losses and a return to quarterly profit

The operating loss narrowed from KRW 44.3 billion in 2024 to KRW 9.79 billion in 2025. Operating profit of KRW 5.65 billion in the second quarter of 2026 marked a return to quarterly profit. The operating margin improved from -55.4% in 2024 to -8.8% in 2025, which points to a change in the business.

Overseas growth led by the United States

Edaily reported that US sales rose more than 200% in 2025 year on year. Revenue exposure to China fell to about 3%, reducing dependence on one country. Advanced-market focus helped defend first-quarter 2026 results despite the Middle East gap.

Improving balance sheet and cash flow

The debt ratio fell from 174.4% in 2024 to 157.0% in 2025. Operating cash flow improved from KRW 205 million in 2024 to KRW 12.8 billion in 2025. Shareholder net income returned to KRW 3.14 billion in 2025.

09

Bear factors

Losses returned in the third quarter of 2026

Third-quarter 2026 revenue fell to KRW 25.2 billion, down 26.2% from the prior quarter and 16.9% from a year earlier. An operating loss of KRW 606 million and a shareholder net loss of KRW 14.7 billion returned. The failure of second-quarter profit to persist into the third quarter is the main bearish point.

Price competition from low-cost Chinese equipment

Edaily reported that Chinese equipment makers cut prices excessively, rapidly changing the competitive landscape. That weighed on profitability and the broader revenue structure. If price competition persists, the pace of margin recovery could be constrained.

Expansion costs and accumulated losses

WiseReport noted that expanding global sales offices and higher new-product launch costs weighed on first-quarter 2026 profitability. The 2024 operating loss of KRW 44.3 billion remains a large legacy burden.

Risk factors

Shortfall against 2026 revenue target

The company expects 2026 revenue above KRW 130 billion and continued rapid growth in US sales. If quarterly revenue runs below that path, revenue would need to expand sharply in the fourth quarter to close the gap. Slower US growth or delayed Brazilian product certification could make the target difficult to reach. In that case, the timing of profit improvement from fixed-cost leverage could also slip.

Regional concentration and geopolitical risk

A Middle East sales gap affected revenue in the first quarter of 2026. US sales have grown sharply, so changes in distribution in that market could affect total revenue. South American sales depend on a subsidiary and local product certification that are still in progress.

Financial leverage

Total liabilities at year-end 2025 were KRW 117.4 billion, above total equity of KRW 74.8 billion. The debt ratio of 157.0% is lower than 174.4% in 2024 but still high. Operating cash flow turned positive in 2025, and its continuation is needed to ease the financial burden.

Overall view

In 2025 Ray's revenue rose 39.4%, its operating loss narrowed and shareholder net income returned to profit. The overseas mix has shifted toward the United States, and China fell to about 3% of revenue. The debt ratio eased from 174.4% in 2024 to 157.0% in 2025, and operating cash flow turned positive.

On the other side, third-quarter 2026 revenue fell 26.2% from the prior quarter and operating and shareholder net losses reappeared. Shareholder net losses over the last four quarters totaled KRW 18.9 billion, and whether profitability has stabilized is not yet confirmed.

Price competition from low-cost Chinese equipment and costs of expanding sales weigh on profitability. The company expects fixed-cost leverage to improve profitability and has projected 2026 revenue above KRW 130 billion.

The next checkpoints are the preliminary fiscal 2026 results, the operating profit outcome in the 2026 annual report, and the trend in the debt ratio.
