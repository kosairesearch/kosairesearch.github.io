02

Business structure

Sangboco was founded in 1977 as Sangbo Chemical Industrial, a coating film specialist, and listed on KOSDAQ in 2007.

Its core business is window films and protective films, including heat-blocking films for vehicles and buildings and paint protection film (PPF) for car exteriors, which account for 82% of consolidated gross sales.

Automotive and architectural window films raise energy efficiency by blocking infrared and ultraviolet light, while PPF protects vehicles from external impact and contamination.

By its own description, the company's segments are display films, window films, media films and new materials, producing CD optical films, quantum dot films, transparent electrode films and graphene barrier films.

Display optical films have grown in demand as displays have become larger and higher resolution and high-performance mobile devices have spread. On the new materials side, the company is developing barrier film technology to extend applications to high-barrier products such as flexible OLED.

Its subsidiary Sangbo Shinjae Materials-related holding Sangsin Gwaji (Suzhou) is wholly owned, and its stake in Kovan Chemical rose from 51% to 68.5% in March 2024. In display optical films the company faces low-priced Chinese competitors, while in automotive and building window films it competes with domestic makers.

Earnings analysis

Annual revenue went from KRW 67.4 billion in 2022 to KRW 66.2 billion in 2023 (down 1.9% year on year), KRW 53.8 billion in 2024 (down 18.6%) and KRW 50.1 billion in 2025 (down 6.9%), declining for three consecutive years from 2023 through 2025.

Of the 2022–2025 period, 2022 was the largest year by revenue and 2025 the smallest. Operating profit was KRW 2.54 billion in 2022 and KRW 2.25 billion in 2023 (down 11.3%), before an operating loss of KRW 8.03 billion in 2024 turned the result to a loss, and the operating loss widened to KRW 8.91 billion in 2025.

The operating margin moved from 3.8% in 2022 and 3.4% in 2023 to -14.9% in 2024 and -17.8% in 2025.

Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders fell from KRW 8.79 billion in 2022 to KRW 438 million in 2023 (down 95.0%), then posted losses of KRW 10.8 billion in 2024 and KRW 12.9 billion in 2025, two consecutive loss years.

Net loss including non-controlling interests was KRW 10.9 billion in 2024 and KRW 13.1 billion in 2025. Operating cash flow went from KRW 614 million in 2022 to KRW 7.78 billion in 2023 and KRW 433 million in 2024, then turned to KRW -7.18 billion in 2025.

By quarter, revenue went from KRW 12.7 billion in the third quarter of 2025 to KRW 10 billion in the fourth quarter (down 21.3% quarter on quarter), KRW 8.89 billion in the first quarter of 2026, KRW 10.2 billion in the second quarter (up 15.0%) and KRW 6.48 billion in the third quarter (down 36.6% quarter on quarter and down 49.0% year on year).

Operating losses were KRW 2.12 billion, KRW 4.65 billion, KRW 3.39 billion, KRW 2.57 billion and KRW 4.45 billion across the five quarters from the third quarter of 2025 through the third quarter of 2026, making five straight loss quarters.

The operating loss narrowed in the second quarter of 2026 but widened again in the third as revenue dropped sharply. Net loss attributable to controlling shareholders totaled KRW 20.2 billion over the last four reported quarters.

The company's commentary attributes the decline to weaker global economic conditions and lower market demand in its window and protective film segment.

The disclosed tables do not separate one-off items, and the fourth-quarter 2025 controlling-shareholder net loss of KRW 7.43 billion exceeded the operating loss of KRW 4.65 billion, a gap that the tables do not explain in detail.

Industry analysis

Demand for display optical films has grown as TVs have gotten larger and mobile devices have spread, but low-priced Chinese competitors have intensified competition in that segment.

Demand for automotive and architectural window films is cited as being supported by tighter energy-efficiency regulation and the spread of electric vehicles.

The company's first-half 2026 commentary, however, attributes its revenue decline in the window and protective film segment to weaker global economic conditions and lower market demand.

The broader market showed a similar pressure: KOSDAQ-listed companies on a December fiscal year swung to a KRW 1.3 trillion loss in the third quarter of 2026. This contrasts with KOSPI, where semiconductor strength drove profits.

Whether Sangboco's sales decline is steeper than that of window film peers can only be judged by comparing disclosures for the same period. In window films the company competes with domestic makers such as Advanced Nano Products, and in display films it faces Chinese price competition.

Bull factors

A period of narrowing losses

The operating loss narrowed from KRW 3.39 billion in the first quarter of 2026 to KRW 2.57 billion in the second. If that narrowing extends across more quarters, it would indicate cost control working. The third-quarter loss widened again to KRW 4.45 billion, so it is too early to call a trend.

Expanding high-barrier film applications

The company is developing barrier film technology to extend applications to high-barrier products such as flexible OLED. If the applications widen into flexible display materials, the business mix could shift away from conventional optical films. The timing of any revenue contribution still needs to be confirmed in disclosures.

Low multiple against net assets and an asset base

The price-to-book ratio is below 1x, so the stock trades at a low multiple of book value. Total equity at the end of 2025 was KRW 43.3 billion, all of it attributable to controlling shareholders. If signs of earnings recovery appear, the interpretation anchored on net assets could broaden.

Bear factors

Sales decline and a sharp third-quarter drop

Revenue fell from KRW 67.4 billion in 2022 to KRW 50.1 billion in 2025, declining for three consecutive years. Third-quarter 2026 revenue of KRW 6.48 billion was down 49.0% from a year earlier. If demand in the core segment does not recover, the revenue base could narrow further.

Accumulating losses and a heavier financial load

The operating loss widened from KRW 8.03 billion in 2024 to KRW 8.91 billion in 2025, and losses have continued for five straight quarters. The debt-to-equity ratio rose from 70.9% in 2022 to 117.7% in 2025. Operating cash flow turned to KRW -7.18 billion in 2025.

Concentration in the core film segment

Heat-blocking films for vehicles and buildings and paint protection film for car exteriors account for 82% of consolidated gross sales. A decline in demand for this segment moves the company's total revenue. With new businesses still a small share, diversification has limited effect so far.

Risk factors

Demand and industry risk

The company's window and protective film segment has faced weaker global economic conditions and lower market demand. Lower utilization would raise the fixed-cost burden and could widen operating losses. If vehicle and building demand weakens further, the loss-making period could lengthen.

Financial structure risk

Total liabilities at the end of 2025 were KRW 51 billion, above total equity of KRW 43.3 billion. The debt-to-equity ratio rose from 70.9% in 2022 to 117.7% in 2025, and operating cash flow turned negative. If losses continue, borrowing or capital raising could become necessary.

Uncertainty over commercialization of new businesses

Barrier film development is at a stage of extending applications to high-barrier products such as flexible OLED. The timing of revenue from the new materials business has not yet appeared in disclosed figures. If the technology does not reach commercial scale, it cannot offset weakness in the core segment.

Operating profit turned into a loss of KRW 8.03 billion in 2024 and the loss widened to KRW 8.91 billion in 2025.
