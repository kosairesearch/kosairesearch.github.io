Losses Narrowing Amid Ad and Carriage-Fee Uncertainty

02

Business structure

YTN operates a news channel business and was listed on KOSDAQ in September 2001. The first-half 2026 figures on a standalone basis showed that growth in broadcasting and advertising revenue within the channel-usage business was the main reason revenue rose slightly.

Its revenue structure centers on channel usage fees and advertising, so both sides are sensitive to the external market. Beyond broadcasting, Seoul Tower media façade and IP businesses are cited as additional revenue sources, and the 2025 year-end awards recognized higher Seoul Tower business revenue.

On the ad side, the company won a new sponsorship for crypto price displays from the exchange Dunamu, which was also honored. Viewership data show that YTN ranked first among pay-TV channels in 2025 with an annual rating of 0.914% per Nielsen Korea, and fifth among all channels including terrestrial networks.

On ownership, Eugene Group secured a 30.95% stake previously held by Korea Racing Authority and KEPCO KDN for KRW 319.9 billion, which is the central variable in governance. Eugene Group has also framed its media business as a platform beyond advertising and subscription fees, extending into data, commerce, and events.

Competitively, YTN shares the ad and carriage-fee pool with peers such as SBS and CJ ENM, so it faces the same industry variables.

04

Earnings analysis

On a reported annual basis, revenue fell from KRW 152.2 billion in 2022 to KRW 130.5 billion in 2023 (down 14.3% year on year), making 2023 the smallest year in the 2022–2025 span.

Revenue then rose to KRW 131.2 billion in 2024 (up 0.5%) and KRW 134.2 billion in 2025 (up 2.3%), growing two consecutive years from 2024 through 2025.

Operating income went from KRW 5.31 billion in 2022 to an operating loss of KRW 9.3 billion in 2023, widened to an operating loss of KRW 26.7 billion in 2024, and narrowed to an operating loss of KRW 13.6 billion in 2025. The operating margin moved from 3.5% in 2022 to -7.1% in 2023, -20.3% in 2024, and -10.1% in 2025.

The company attributed the smaller 2025 loss to lower bad-debt provision expenses. Controlling-shareholder net losses ran for three consecutive years from 2023 through 2025, with a loss of KRW 14.2 billion in 2025.

Operating cash flow was KRW 5.76 billion in 2022, then negative at KRW -1.13 billion in 2023, KRW -2.46 billion in 2024, and KRW -4.7 billion in 2025.

Quarterly, Q4 2025 revenue reached KRW 43.6 billion with an operating profit of KRW 1.07 billion, but Q1 2026 revenue fell 38.4% quarter on quarter to KRW 26.9 billion and the operating loss returned to KRW 7.05 billion.

Q2 2026 narrowed the operating loss to KRW 826 million and turned the controlling-shareholder result to a profit of KRW 558 million. Q3 2026 revenue was KRW 33.1 billion, up 3.2% year on year, but the operating loss of KRW 3.3 billion was wider than the KRW 2.04 billion loss a year earlier.

Cumulative controlling-shareholder net losses over the last four quarters total KRW 9.81 billion. A first-half 2026 standalone tally showed the operating loss down 37.6%, which points to large quarter-to-quarter swings.

05

Industry analysis

The biggest industry variable is that broadcast advertising and pay-TV have entered a downturn together. A media regulator review released in May 2026 found that total broadcasting revenue declined for two consecutive years and that broadcast ad revenue kept falling.

The same review found that pay-TV subscriber counts and revenue growth stagnated in 2024 after 2023. Pay-TV lost about 150,000 subscribers over two years, and viewing is being reshaped around OTT services. In this environment, disputes over channel usage fees and home-shopping transmission fees may become more likely.

YTN is still negotiating with LG HelloVision and CJ ENM over how a new fee calculation standard should apply, without reaching agreement. Financial News analyzed in October 2026 that cost cuts have limits as a way to improve profitability in pay-TV.

On viewership, YTN ranked first among pay-TV channels in 2025, so its audience metrics are comparatively resilient among news channels. Still, ad pricing and ad structure are tied to the broader decline in broadcast advertising, so a viewership lead does not translate directly into higher ad revenue.

Among listed peers in the same industry, SBS and CJ ENM also rely on broadcast advertising and pay-TV revenue, so they share the same industry variables.

06

Outlook

The company's own outlook starts with the New Year message at the 2026 kickoff ceremony. Acting CEO Jeong Jae-hoon said that slimming and simplifying the organization and expanding new revenue sources such as Seoul Tower would lead to a return to profit before long.

In the first-half 2026 standalone tally, revenue rose 2.1% and the operating loss fell 37.6%. Lower personnel-related costs contributed to the narrower loss.

However, the Q3 2026 operating loss of KRW 3.3 billion was wider than a year earlier, and operating losses have continued for three straight quarters, so the timing of a return to profit is not yet visible.

On the shareholder side, Eugene Group unveiled a media vision at a press briefing in July 2026 and announced new investment of more than KRW 2 trillion over the next ten years. That plan covers AI transformation, content funds, and global platforms at the group level.

The group has said it will treat YTN as a long-term growth axis rather than a short-term investment. Meanwhile, the high-price acquisition dispute, concerns over broadcasting independence, and the lawsuit seeking to revoke approval remain burdens.

The first checkpoint is whether the operating margin in the 2026 annual report improves from -10.1% in 2025. The next checkpoint after that is whether the operating loss trend continues in Q1 2027 results.

Bull factors

Narrowing losses and a profitable quarter

Q4 2025 turned to an operating profit of KRW 1.07 billion, and Q2 2026 posted a controlling-shareholder profit of KRW 558 million. The company said lower bad-debt provisions drove the smaller loss.

Viewership and new revenue sources

Per Nielsen Korea, YTN's 2025 annual rating of 0.914% ranked first among pay-TV channels. Seoul Tower media façade and IP businesses are cited as new revenue sources and were honored in the 2025 year-end awards. A first-half 2026 tally also showed higher broadcasting and advertising revenue in the channel-usage business.

Long-term commitment from the controlling group

Eugene Group has said it intends to make YTN a long-term growth axis rather than a short-term investment. At group level it announced plans for more than KRW 2 trillion in new investment over ten years. The debt ratio fell to 51.9% at end-2025 from 57.6% in 2024.

Bear factors

Persistent losses and cash outflow

The company posted losses for three consecutive years from 2023 through 2025, with a 2025 operating margin of -10.1%. Operating cash flow has been negative since 2023, at KRW -4.7 billion in 2025. The Q3 2026 operating loss of KRW 3.3 billion was wider than the year-earlier loss of KRW 2.04 billion.

Joint slowdown in advertising and pay-TV

According to a media regulator review, total broadcasting revenue declined for two consecutive years. Pay-TV lost roughly 150,000 subscribers over two years. Financial News analyzed that profitability gains relying on cost cuts have limits.

Governance and carriage-fee negotiation risk

The group's stake acquisition drew a high-price acquisition dispute and concerns over broadcasting independence, and a suit seeking to revoke approval remains a burden. Channel fee negotiations, including YTN's, have not yet reached agreement.

10

Risk factors

Regulation and governance

Concerns over the high-price stake acquisition by Eugene Group and over broadcasting independence have been raised, and a suit seeking to revoke approval remains a burden. Programming and the direction of the controlling shareholder could affect management stability. Until the lawsuit and regulatory decisions are resolved, the scope of governance change remains open.

Ad and pay-TV downturn

Broadcast ad revenue is still declining and pay-TV subscribers continue to leave. If channel fee disputes widen, channel usage revenue could be affected. Even with a viewership lead, revenue recovery is hard to achieve if the ad structure does not change.

Loss structure and cash flow

Operating losses have persisted for three consecutive years from 2023, and operating cash flow remains negative. The company explained that the 2025 loss reduction mainly reflected lower bad-debt provisions.

Whether operating profit turns positive as provision effects fade will show whether the core business is improving.

Overall view

YTN's 2025 operating loss narrowed to KRW 13.6 billion from 2024, showing a smaller loss, but operating losses have continued for three consecutive years since 2023. In 2026 quarterly results remained volatile, with a Q1 operating loss of KRW 7.05 billion and a Q3 operating loss of KRW 3.3 billion.

Q2 2026 turned to a controlling-shareholder profit of KRW 558 million, but Q3 returned to a loss. On the positive side are the narrowing loss trend, first place among pay-TV channels in 2025 viewership, new revenue sources such as Seoul Tower, a lower debt ratio, and the controlling group's long-term investment plan.

On the negative side are the joint decline in broadcast advertising and pay-TV subscribers, unresolved channel fee negotiations, continued negative operating cash flow, and disputes and litigation over governance. Based on the reported figures, the two sides carry similar weight.

The next checkpoints are the 2026 annual report, Q1 2027 results, the outcome of carriage-fee negotiations, and the progress of the lawsuit seeking to revoke approval.
