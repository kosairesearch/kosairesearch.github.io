02

Business structure

Nara Space Technology runs the full chain of small satellite work, from designing and building low-Earth-orbit micro-satellites to operating them and analyzing satellite imagery.

Its business has three pillars: satellite manufacturing (hardware sales), satellite data sales, and AI-based satellite imagery analysis solutions.

According to an external summary of 2025 results, revenue was made up of satellite platform products at KRW 7.41 billion (56%), satellite platform services at KRW 3.60 billion (27%), satellite operations at KRW 1.16 billion, satellite imagery at KRW 770 million, and other revenue at KRW 300 million.

Data businesses such as imagery and operations still account for a small share, and manufacturing and services make up most of sales.

Key customers include state research institutes such as the Korea Aerospace Research Institute and the Korea Astronomy and Space Science Institute, as well as defense companies such as Hanwha Systems.

In 2026, disclosed contracts included a space-semiconductor verification payload and satellite design service with the Electronics and Telecommunications Research Institute (January disclosure, KRW 2.05 billion), development of a small greenhouse gas observation satellite with Hanwha Systems (April disclosure, KRW 2.93 billion), and a space verification satellite development service (June disclosure, KRW 6.11 billion).

Through its space verification satellite business, the company is also widening its test-bed offering, which flies customers' components and sensors in actual orbit. At its December 2025 listing, the company presented a plan to operate 84 satellites by 2031.

In peer comparisons of space-related listed stocks, it is often grouped with Innospace and other space-related names. The satellite-imagery-based data and financial analysis services remain in beta operation, according to a company official.

04

Earnings analysis

On a disclosed basis, revenue for 2025 was KRW 13.2 billion, up 207.9% from the prior year. The company attributed the increase to a rise in manufacturing orders for satellites.

The operating loss for the same year was KRW 5.46 billion, wider than the KRW 4.43 billion loss in 2024, and the operating margin improved from -103.1% to -41.3%, but the company has posted losses for two consecutive years, from 2024 through 2025.

The net loss attributable to controlling shareholders narrowed from KRW 10.7 billion in 2024 to KRW 7.68 billion in 2025, and the company said fair-value changes in derivatives tied to convertible redeemable preferred shares affected net earnings.

Because the operating loss grew, the narrower net loss is hard to read as an improvement in core operations. Operating cash flow worsened from -KRW 2.95 billion in 2024 to -KRW 3.53 billion in 2025. At the end of 2025, total equity was KRW 47.6 billion and total liabilities were KRW 7.7 billion, a debt ratio of 16.2%.

Quarterly, revenue was KRW 1.87 billion in the fourth quarter of 2025 with an operating loss of KRW 1.62 billion, then fell to KRW 413 million in the first quarter of 2026 (down 77.9% from the prior quarter), its low point.

Revenue then rose to KRW 1.95 billion in the second quarter and KRW 3.45 billion in the third quarter, two consecutive quarterly increases. Operating losses were KRW 3.03 billion, KRW 3.67 billion and KRW 3.22 billion in the first, second and third quarters of 2026.

The net loss attributable to controlling shareholders over the latest four quarters totaled KRW 11.1 billion.

The company attributed the weaker first-half 2026 revenue and higher selling and administrative costs to most new contracts being signed after April, which delayed revenue recognition, according to media reports.

05

Industry analysis

The satellite industry is shifting from developing one large satellite over a long period toward operating several smaller, cheaper micro-satellites with shorter development cycles. This shift is behind Nara Space Technology's push to expand operations and data businesses alongside manufacturing.

Domestic demand centers on state research institutes and defense companies. Many of the 2026 orders were demonstration and mission-type projects, such as space component verification, greenhouse gas observation and satellites for defense simulation.

The company is trying to build revenue at several points in the value chain, including ground-station operations, imagery processing solutions and space verification satellites. In terms of cycle position, the company is in an early commercialization stage, building mass-production capacity after its listing.

The company has said mass production should lower cost ratios and lead to a return to profit. The 2025 cost of revenue ratio was 75.7%, up from 45.7% the year before, which is explained by satellite production and prototype costs (per an external summary).

In competition, it is often compared with domestic satellite manufacturers such as Satrec Initiative and with launch-vehicle developer Innospace as space-related listed stocks. The company points to its record of in-orbit performance verification as the basis of its competitiveness in orders.

06

Outlook

Orders confirmed in 2026 have been rising. Disclosures covered contracts of KRW 2.05 billion in January, KRW 2.93 billion in April and KRW 6.11 billion in June, and additional projects with the National Security Research Institute and the Agency for Defense Development were signed in March and April (media reports).

The order backlog reached KRW 28.7 billion at the end of the first half of 2026, up 62% from KRW 17.6 billion at the end of 2025 (September 2026 media report). Another source puts the backlog at KRW 28.6 billion as of the same date, so the figures differ slightly.

How quickly that backlog converts into revenue is the largest variable. The greenhouse gas observation satellite contract runs through December 26, 2026, and the KRW 6.11 billion space verification satellite development service runs through July 31, 2030.

At its December 2025 listing, the company stated a target of returning to profit in 2026, yet media reports said the first-half 2026 operating loss widened to KRW 6.7 billion from the year-earlier period.

An October 2026 report from the Korea IR Association, as reported in the media, projected 2026 revenue of about KRW 15 billion and an operating loss of about KRW 9.3 billion.

The methane observation satellite NarSha was scheduled for launch in the second half of 2026 according to a February 2026 report, and any change to that schedule will be visible through disclosures and media coverage.

In February 2026, a DB Investment researcher said the number of operating satellites is expected to rise steeply from 2028.

08

Bull factors

Rapid backlog growth

The order backlog at the end of the first half of 2026 was larger than at the end of 2025, increasing the volume that can convert to revenue going forward (September 2026 media report).

Major contracts disclosed over the same period were a KRW 2.05 billion service with the Electronics and Telecommunications Research Institute in January, a KRW 2.93 billion greenhouse gas observation satellite development with Hanwha Systems in April, and a KRW 6.11 billion space verification satellite development service in June.

Because the verification satellite contract runs through July 31, 2030, it is cited as a factor that widens the long-term revenue base.

Quarterly revenue recovery

Revenue for 2025 reached KRW 13.2 billion, up 207.9% from the prior year. Quarterly revenue, which fell to KRW 413 million in the first quarter of 2026, rose to KRW 1.95 billion in the second quarter and KRW 3.45 billion in the third, two consecutive increases.

Cumulative revenue for the first nine months of 2025 was KRW 11.37 billion, above the prior full-year total, according to materials presented at a pre-listing briefing.

Financial capacity and expansion base

Media reports said cash and equivalents at the end of the first half of 2026 were KRW 28.2 billion, with net debt at -KRW 27.3 billion, meaning the company holds more cash than interest-bearing debt. Total liabilities were KRW 7.7 billion and total equity KRW 47.6 billion at the end of 2025.

The company said listing proceeds would fund prototype development and mass production of its second-generation optical satellite and mass production of environmental monitoring satellites. Satellite imagery data and space verification services are being prepared as new revenue sources.

09

Bear factors

Persistent losses and wider operating loss

The company posted losses in both 2024 and 2025, and operating losses have continued for four consecutive quarters from the fourth quarter of 2025 through the third quarter of 2026. Operating losses were KRW 3.03 billion in the first quarter of 2026, KRW 3.67 billion in the second and KRW 3.22 billion in the third.

The first-half 2026 operating loss widened to KRW 6.7 billion from KRW 2.9 billion a year earlier (media report). The operating losses have carried through to net losses attributable to controlling shareholders, so the accumulated deficit is growing.

Delayed revenue recognition from orders

Revenue in the first half of 2026 was KRW 2.4 billion, down 68.7% from KRW 7.5 billion a year earlier (media report). Over the same period, selling and administrative expenses rose from KRW 3.7 billion to KRW 6.3 billion, adding to the loss as sales fell.

The company said most new contracts were signed after April, so the first half ended before major contracts began recognizing revenue. The main bear argument is that the backlog has built up without yet translating into revenue for an extended period.

New businesses still in early stages

New businesses such as satellite imagery analysis, financial-related data and 6G satellite communications are still at an early stage with minimal revenue.

The company added a semi-advisory business and investment-related purposes to its articles of incorporation at its shareholders' meeting, but related revenue has not yet appeared. Data services remain in beta operation, so the timing of customer acquisition and monetization is not fixed. If the new businesses do not become meaningful revenue sources, reliance on manufacturing orders will continue.

10

Risk factors

Order-to-revenue conversion and cost ratio

If the order backlog converts to revenue more slowly, the narrowing of losses will be delayed. The 2025 cost of revenue ratio rose to 75.7% from 45.7% a year earlier, as satellite production and testing costs are reflected in cost of sales.

Analysts note that even as manufacturing orders grow, cost control during testing and delivery determines profitability. If the cost ratio stays high, operating losses could persist even as revenue grows.

Cost structure and cash burn

Selling and administrative expenses are rising on R&D and headcount expansion. The larger cash outflow from operating activities bears on how fast cash is consumed. The pace at which listing proceeds are deployed and the size of further investment will shape financial capacity.

Even with a net cash position in the first half of 2026, continued losses will make the use of funds more important.

Customer concentration and dependence on policy and launch schedules

An external summary states that more than 90% of revenue is concentrated in a few public and defense customers, including the Korea Aerospace Research Institute, the Korea Astronomy and Space Science Institute and Hanwha Systems.

Orders and revenue recognition could both shift with the timing of state research and defense budget execution. If launch schedules such as the methane observation satellite slip, the start of satellite-operation-based data services will also be delayed.

Overall view

Nara Space Technology raised 2025 revenue to KRW 13.2 billion, up 207.9%, but its operating loss widened to KRW 5.46 billion, and it has posted losses for two consecutive years from 2024.

Orders rose quickly in 2026, lifting the order backlog to KRW 28.7 billion at the end of the first half, and revenue rose for two consecutive quarters in the second and third quarters of 2026.

On the other side, operating losses have continued for four consecutive quarters from the fourth quarter of 2025 through the third quarter of 2026, and the net loss attributable to controlling shareholders over the latest four quarters totals KRW 11.1 billion.

Bull points are the expansion of orders, the revenue recovery trend and a net cash position, while bear points are persistent losses, delayed conversion of orders into revenue and new businesses still at an early stage.

The company's 2026 target of returning to profit, set at its listing, and the operating loss projection in an external report cited in October 2026 point in different directions and merit attention.

The next checkpoints are the end of the greenhouse gas observation satellite contract on December 26, 2026 and the filing of the 2026 annual business report by March 31, 2027. Checking revenue recognized and the trend in operating loss at those two points will show how quickly the order backlog is turning into revenue.
