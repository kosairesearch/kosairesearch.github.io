Motrex: Quarterly Rebound Versus Annual Decline

Motrex's quarterly revenue and profit attributable to controlling shareholders rose sharply in 2026, but annual operating profit and controlling-shareholder net income fell through 2025 and margins narrowed.

02

Business structure

Motrex is an automotive electronics company founded in 2001, centered on in-vehicle infotainment (IVI) systems and AVN and AV products.

According to a February 2024 analysis by the Korea Investor Relations Council, for the first nine months of 2023 the electronics business accounted for 70.3% of revenue and the heavy-equipment business for 29.7%. Within electronics, AVN made up 41.8% and AV 34.9%.

The electronics line is widening into dash cams, in-cabin air purifiers and rear-seat entertainment. Overseas sales are a defining feature: exports accounted for 94.2% of electronics revenue in 2022, and 89.6% of total revenue in the first nine months of 2025.

Hyundai Motor and KIA were the main customers, but a five-year IVI supply contract with Stellantis, reported at KRW 98.7 billion, broadened the customer base, with revenue recognized from the fourth quarter of 2025 and ramping from the first quarter of 2026.

The construction-equipment business runs through a subsidiary, Jinjin Construction Robot, whose flagship product is the concrete pump truck; North American revenue for the first nine months of 2025 rose 27.8% to KRW 76.3 billion.

Interior materials are handled by a subsidiary, Motrex EFM, which booked KRW 123.4 billion in revenue for the first nine months of 2025. Other units include MTR, active in PBV and SDV vehicles, and Motrex EV, active in EV charging infrastructure.

Competition in IVI and infotainment comes from global electronics suppliers and domestic auto parts makers, and Motrex has grown mainly by supplying overseas assembly plants of automakers.

04

Earnings analysis

On a reported annual basis, revenue fell from KRW 568.5 billion in 2022 to KRW 531 billion in 2023, a 6.6% decline, then rose to KRW 586.9 billion in 2024 (up 10.5%) and KRW 702.3 billion in 2025 (up 19.7%).

Revenue has grown for two consecutive years, from 2024 through 2025, and 2025 was the largest year in the 2022 to 2025 span. Operating profit moved the other way: KRW 58.8 billion in 2022, KRW 53.2 billion in 2023 (down 9.6%), KRW 43.6 billion in 2024 (down 18.0%) and KRW 41.8 billion in 2025 (down 4.2%).

It has declined for three consecutive years from 2023 to 2025, and 2022 was the largest year in the span. The operating margin narrowed from 10.3% in 2022 to 10.0%, 7.4% and 6.0% in 2025.

Profit attributable to controlling shareholders fell from KRW 38.5 billion in 2022 to KRW 31.6 billion in 2023 (down 17.9%), KRW 19.3 billion in 2024 (down 38.8%) and KRW 7.4 billion in 2025 (down 61.7%). Net income including non-controlling interests was KRW 12.5 billion in 2025.

Reports on the 2025 disclosure say the company cited higher sales from concrete pump trucks and interior materials, while the net income decline was attributed to a base effect from a tax refund claim in the prior year.

On a quarterly basis, the third quarter of 2025 showed revenue of KRW 167.5 billion and operating profit of KRW 8.07 billion. The fourth quarter brought revenue of KRW 168 billion (up 0.3%) but operating profit fell to KRW 1.17 billion (down 85.5%).

The first quarter of 2026 recovered to revenue of KRW 179.8 billion and operating profit of KRW 11.7 billion, and the second quarter reached KRW 186.4 billion and KRW 18.3 billion.

Third-quarter 2026 revenue jumped to KRW 279.5 billion (up 49.9% from the prior quarter and 66.8% from a year earlier), yet operating profit was KRW 10 billion (down 45.3% from the prior quarter), so profit did not keep pace with sales.

Profit attributable to controlling shareholders moved from KRW 2.71 billion in the third quarter of 2025 to a net loss of KRW 7.38 billion in the fourth quarter, then KRW 6.02 billion, KRW 16.2 billion and KRW 18 billion through the third quarter of 2026.

Since that third-quarter profit exceeded operating profit, non-operating items contributed. Over the latest four quarters, profit attributable to controlling shareholders totaled KRW 32.9 billion, a clear recovery against the KRW 7.4 billion recorded for full-year 2025.

Operating cash flow was KRW 45 billion in 2022, KRW 54.4 billion in 2023, KRW 22 billion in 2024 and KRW 56.6 billion in 2025, bottoming in 2024 before recovering.

Press reports from the first quarter of 2026 linked the gains to higher Stellantis supply and Vietnam and domestic sales, with lower quality costs and better product mix. Reports on the second quarter of 2026 cited growth in Mexico, Latin America, Stellantis and Russia/CIS, along with AV and AVN demand.

05

Industry analysis

Motrex's end market is automaker production and sales volume. Electronics parts are supplied over several years after being adopted on a vehicle platform, so securing volume determines earnings.

A February 2024 analysis by the Korea Investor Relations Council reported that Indian vehicle sales rose from 2.43 million units in 2020 to 4.1 million in 2023, an average annual gain of 19.1%, and that the Indian unit's revenue grew at an average annual rate of 27.0% from 2020 to 2022.

The same report described the Vietnam unit as weak, with KRW 600 million in revenue and a KRW 100 million operating loss in the first nine months of 2023. In 2026, press reports credited growth in Mexico, Latin America, Stellantis and Russia/CIS for sales, so revenue depends on regional automaker demand.

The construction-equipment business moves with North American construction machinery markets, and the subsidiary reported growth there.

Among listed peers in auto parts, Hyundai Mobis, Hanon Systems and Hyundai WIA have close ties to large automakers, while Motrex combines IVI and AVN expertise with a heavy-equipment business. Adding interior materials through Motrex EFM is presented as a way to expand into the vehicle cabin.

Volume diversification across automaker regions and the sustainability of the Stellantis program are the main variables to watch.

06

Outlook

Disclosed results through the third quarter of 2026 show revenue rising for four consecutive quarters, while operating profit varied sharply from quarter to quarter.

The Stellantis IVI supply contract, reported at KRW 98.7 billion over five years, began contributing revenue in the fourth quarter of 2025 and ramped up from the first quarter of 2026. Whether this volume sustains revenue beyond 2027 is the key point to verify.

In the cabin segment, Motrex EFM agreed in 2026 to acquire Duol, widening its interior-materials business and seeking synergies with the electronics line, according to August 2026 reports. How the acquisition shows up in consolidated sales and margins should be confirmed in upcoming periodic filings.

The new-business units remain small: MTR booked KRW 7 billion and Motrex EV KRW 1.5 billion in revenue for the first nine months of 2025. No formal full-year guidance from the company was identified in the materials gathered.

The outlook therefore rests on three things: the trend in disclosed results, the durability of Stellantis volume, and the effect of the interior-materials acquisition.

The 2026 annual report will show whether the operating margin recovers from 6.0% in 2025, and whether the debt load eases and operating cash flow holds near the KRW 56.6 billion level of 2025.

Bull factors

Overseas volume and consecutive revenue gains

Revenue has risen for four straight quarters from the fourth quarter of 2025 through the third quarter of 2026. Press reports tie this to the Stellantis IVI supply ramping from early 2026, alongside higher sales in Mexico, Latin America and Russia/CIS. Bulls point to the fact that growth is spread across several regions rather than a single customer.

Quarterly recovery in controlling-shareholder profit

Profit attributable to controlling shareholders turned positive in the first quarter of 2026 and then rose in both the second and third quarters. The latest four quarters total KRW 32.9 billion, well above the full-year 2025 figure. Bulls see the recovery as visible quarter by quarter.

Broader footprint in interiors and heavy equipment

The interior-materials subsidiary Motrex EFM booked KRW 123.4 billion in revenue for the first nine months of 2025, and the Duol acquisition extends its scope. The construction robot subsidiary's North American revenue rose 27.8% over the same period. Bulls argue the revenue base is diversifying beyond electronics.

Bear factors

Operating profit down for three straight years

Operating profit fell from KRW 53.2 billion in 2023 to KRW 43.6 billion in 2024 and KRW 41.8 billion in 2025, a third straight annual decline. The operating margin dropped to 6.0% in 2025 from 10.3% in 2022.

Bears note that third-quarter 2026 revenue rose to KRW 279.5 billion while operating profit fell to KRW 10 billion from the prior quarter.

Heavy debt burden

Total liabilities at the end of 2025 were KRW 634.6 billion against total equity of KRW 290.3 billion, leaving the debt-to-equity ratio at 218.6%. That is down from 242.8% in 2024 but above 188.3% in 2022. Bears point to the sensitivity of financing costs and earnings to interest and currency moves.

Volatile quarterly earnings

Operating profit collapsed to KRW 1.17 billion in the fourth quarter of 2025, and controlling-shareholder profit swung to a KRW 7.38 billion net loss. Operating profit then moved from KRW 18.3 billion in the second quarter of 2026 to KRW 10 billion in the third. Bears argue that quarter-to-quarter swings make it hard to judge the trend from a few periods.

Risk factors

Customer and regional concentration

Exports accounted for 89.6% of revenue in the first nine months of 2025. With the Stellantis contract reported as a key driver, exposure to a single customer and to overseas demand has grown. A change in local production plans or lower sales could move quarterly revenue quickly.

Balance sheet and cash generation

Total liabilities were KRW 634.6 billion at the end of 2025, with a debt-to-equity ratio of 218.6%. Operating cash flow fell to KRW 22 billion in 2024 before recovering to KRW 56.6 billion in 2025. If cash generation does not hold steady, borrowing costs and investment capacity could be constrained.

Integration of acquisitions and new businesses

The acquisition of Duol expands the interior-materials business, but the integration cost and the timing of profit contribution are not yet clear from public reporting. The PBV and SDV unit MTR and the EV charging unit Motrex EV are still small, so their profit contribution could vary with execution. Early-stage costs in new businesses could offset gains in the core electronics line.

Overall view

Motrex's revenue has risen for four straight quarters through 2026, and profit attributable to controlling shareholders has recovered quarter by quarter. The Stellantis IVI supply and higher sales in Mexico, Latin America and other overseas markets are reported as the main drivers.

On an annual basis, however, operating profit and controlling-shareholder net income declined for three consecutive years from 2023 to 2025, and the operating margin fell to 6.0% in 2025.

Third-quarter 2026 revenue rose to KRW 279.5 billion, but operating profit slipped to KRW 10 billion, so the link between sales and profit remains weak. A debt-to-equity ratio of 218.6% and total liabilities of KRW 634.6 billion remain a financial burden. Strengths and weaknesses coexist.

The next checkpoints are the operating margin in the 2026 annual business report, whether Stellantis volume holds in the first-quarter 2027 preliminary results, and the contribution from the interior-materials acquisition.
