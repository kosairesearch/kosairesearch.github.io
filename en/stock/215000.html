Golfzon: Overseas Growth vs. Domestic Slowdown in Profit Recovery

Golfzon's overseas business is holding up revenue as domestic screen-golf sales weaken, but operating profit fell for three straight years from 2023 to 2025, so margin recovery is the key variable.

02

Business structure

Golfzon supplies screen-golf systems and hardware to franchise stores and also earns round revenue each time a customer plays a round on its system. The business is split into franchise, non-franchise, the GDR golf-practice direct-operation business, and overseas operations.

Domestic sales centre on new-product sales to franchise stores, and in the third quarter of 2026 upgrade sales of the TwoVision NX Plus lifted screen-golf revenue. By contrast, the non-franchise and GDR businesses have kept declining as new golf participation slows and leisure demand diversifies.

Overseas operations are centred on the United States, Japan and China, and the global store count rose by 673 from 3,534 in the first quarter of 2025 to 4,207 in the first quarter of 2026. In the US, the company is expanding TwoVision NX supply to sports pubs, indoor entertainment venues and recreation stores.

Golfzon was also named the official indoor golf simulator partner of the US Open and US Women's Open together with the United States Golf Association (USGA). According to Eugene Investment & Securities' March 2026 report, the overseas share of 2025 annual revenue topped 20% for the first time, at 22.8%.

In Korea, intensifying competition and diversifying leisure demand have slowed growth in new store openings, which is cited as a structural challenge. Earnings rest mainly on domestic franchise sales and overseas sales, while the GDR direct-operated golf-practice business continues to be restructured.

04

Earnings analysis

In the annual disclosures, revenue rose from KRW 617.5 billion in 2022 to KRW 685.1 billion in 2023, a 10.9% increase, before falling 9.5% to KRW 620 billion in 2024 and 22.1% to KRW 483.3 billion in 2025.

Operating profit declined three years in a row, from KRW 148.7 billion in 2022 to KRW 114.5 billion, KRW 95.8 billion and KRW 68.1 billion in 2025, and the operating margin narrowed from 24.1% to 14.1%. Profit fell faster than revenue over this period.

Profit attributable to controlling shareholders dropped from KRW 113.7 billion in 2022 to KRW 25.6 billion in 2025. In 2025, that figure exceeded net income including non-controlling interests of KRW 12.9 billion, which means non-controlling interests absorbed a loss.

The debt-to-equity ratio fell from 58.0% in 2022 to 40.7% in 2025, with total equity at KRW 438.9 billion and total liabilities at KRW 178.6 billion at the end of 2025. Operating cash flow was lowest in 2023 at KRW 11.5 billion and recovered to KRW 95.4 billion in 2025.

On a quarterly basis, the fourth quarter of 2025 posted revenue of KRW 111.5 billion and operating profit of KRW 6.24 billion, and a net loss attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 22.1 billion.

Press coverage attributed the gap between operating and pre-tax results in that quarter to one-off costs from closing some US direct-operated stores and asset impairment losses.

In the first quarter of 2026, operating profit rose 125.2% from the prior quarter to KRW 14.1 billion, and profit attributable to controlling shareholders turned positive at KRW 15.9 billion. Operating profit fell to KRW 6.62 billion in the second quarter of 2026.

In the third quarter, revenue rose 4.3% year on year to KRW 126 billion, but operating profit fell 34.4% to KRW 10 billion and the controlling-shareholder result returned to a net loss of KRW 1.24 billion.

The company attributed the third-quarter profit decline to a higher cost-of-sales ratio from domestic sales promotion policies and higher operating costs from expansion overseas.

05

Industry analysis

NH Investment & Securities estimated in a January 2026 report that domestic screen-golf rounds fell about 5% to roughly 92 million in 2025. The same report expected new system sales to fall to around 900 units, about half the prior-year level.

Industry coverage points to slowing growth in the domestic golf market and cost pressure as factors weighing on earnings. Golfzon has described the domestic market as normalizing after COVID-19. Domestic demand is being pressured by slower inflows of new golfers and more diversified leisure demand at the same time.

Overseas markets are viewed as having more room to grow. NH Investment & Securities projected North American revenue would rise 72.9% to KRW 92.2 billion in 2026, but overseas sales fell 3.7% year on year to KRW 23.5 billion in the third quarter of 2026, so short-term trends are mixed.

On competition, the company has cited intensifying competition in the domestic screen-golf market as a reason for lower sales. In the US, the company says demand for TwoVision NX continues to grow.

06

Outlook

Park Jong-seon of Eugene Investment & Securities said in a November 2, 2026 report that overseas new-product sales and store-operation revenue should rise as the fourth-quarter peak season begins.

The same report expected 2026 annual revenue of KRW 461.7 billion and operating profit of KRW 41.6 billion, down 4.5% and 38.8% from the prior year.

Lee Byung-hwa of Shinhan Investment Corp. estimated in an October 23, 2026 report that the overseas share of 2026 annual revenue would reach 25.8%, up 3 percentage points from 2025. The same report expected a full recovery in domestic operations to come in 2027.

On products, upgrade sales of the new TwoVision NX Plus to franchise stores lifted third-quarter revenue. Overseas store expansion is also continuing, with 4,641 stores at the end of the third quarter of 2026, up 20.3% year on year, which is cited as a base for future revenue recovery.

However, the same quarter's overseas sales decline was attributed to lower product sales in Japan and the US and restructuring of direct-operated stores in China. The next checkpoints are whether the fourth-quarter peak season lifts overseas revenue and whether the decline in domestic franchise sales narrows.

08

Bull factors

Overseas mix rising, store base expanding

The overseas store count rose 20.3% year on year to 4,641 at the end of the third quarter of 2026. Shinhan Investment Corp. estimated the overseas share would reach 25.8% for 2026, up 3 percentage points from 2025.

Quarterly revenue turns up

Third-quarter 2026 revenue rose 4.3% year on year to KRW 126 billion, which press reports described as the first year-on-year increase in 11 quarters. The company attributed the increase to upgrade sales of the TwoVision NX Plus to franchise stores. Revenue also rose 24.5% from the previous quarter. Profit, however, did not follow the same path.

Fourth-quarter peak season outlook

Park Jong-seon of Eugene Investment & Securities said in a November 2, 2026 report that overseas new-product sales and store-operation revenue should rise as the fourth-quarter peak season begins. The same report pointed to overseas operations as a possible axis of earnings recovery. Whether store expansion and new-product sales move together is the point to watch.

09

Bear factors

Operating profit down three straight years

Operating profit fell from KRW 148.7 billion in 2022 to KRW 68.1 billion in 2025, and the operating margin narrowed from 24.1% to 14.1% over the same period. Third-quarter 2026 operating profit also fell 34.4% year on year to KRW 10 billion. A higher cost-of-sales ratio and rising overseas sales and marketing costs weighed on profitability.

Domestic franchise and GDR sales weak

In the first quarter of 2026, franchise sales fell 19.6% year on year, non-franchise sales fell 20.4%, and GDR sales plunged 38.1%. Second-quarter 2026 revenue fell 16.4% year on year to KRW 101.2 billion. Slower inflows of new golfers and exhaustion of replacement demand for new products are cited as causes.

Overseas sales dip, lawsuit pending

Third-quarter 2026 overseas sales fell 3.7% year on year to KRW 23.5 billion, reflecting lower product sales in Japan and the US and restructuring of direct-operated stores in China. A damages lawsuit by victims of a personal data leak is also ongoing. Whether overseas sales and marketing costs continue to weigh on profit is the central question.

10

Risk factors

Legal exposure (personal data)

Press reports said a law firm filed the suit on June 29, 2026 on behalf of data-leak victims, with 23 initial plaintiffs seeking KRW 200,000 each. The company did not provide a specific public response. If the number of claimants grows, litigation costs and whether provisions are booked could become earnings variables.

Overseas expansion costs and direct stores

Press coverage linked the fourth-quarter 2025 gap between operating and pre-tax results to one-off costs from closing some US direct-operated stores and asset impairments. In the third quarter of 2026, personnel, marketing and service costs rose as sales and marketing in the US and China were strengthened. If costs grow faster than revenue as stores expand, margin improvement will be limited.

Domestic demand structure

Domestic screen-golf rounds were estimated to fall about 5% in 2025, and new system sales were expected to roughly halve. Slower inflows of new golfers and more diversified leisure demand have delayed a recovery in domestic franchise and non-franchise sales. Shinhan Investment Corp. expects a full domestic recovery in 2027.

12

Overall view

Golfzon's disclosed results show revenue and operating profit declining since peaking in 2023. Operating profit in 2025 was KRW 68.1 billion, below KRW 148.7 billion in 2022, and profit attributable to controlling shareholders has fallen for three straight years from 2023.

In the third quarter of 2026, upgrade sales of the TwoVision NX Plus lifted revenue to a first year-on-year increase in 11 quarters, but operating profit fell 34.4% to KRW 10 billion.

The overseas business shows a clear expansion trend in both revenue mix and store count, although overseas sales declined in the third quarter. Domestic operations are expected to recover in 2027 amid slower inflows of new golfers. The data-leak lawsuit and overseas marketing costs remain open uncertainties.

On the strength side, the rising overseas mix and the quarterly revenue rebound stand out; on the weakness side, the lower operating margin and falling domestic franchise and GDR sales stand out.

The next checkpoints are the operating margin and overseas revenue trend in the 2026 preliminary fourth-quarter results and the 2026 annual report.
