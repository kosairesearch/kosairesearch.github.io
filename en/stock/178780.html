02

Business structure

Ilwoul GML was founded in 2006 as a metal mold developer and manufacturer and listed on KOSDAQ in 2015. After its largest shareholder changed to Ilwoul in 2023, the company refocused on buying and selling seasonal appliances such as electric and hot mats.

Its business segment is currently reported as a single merchandise distribution segment, with electric mats, hot mats and electric fans as the main products. The sale of its Vietnamese subsidiary's stake in 2025 ended the manufacturing business, so product supply now relies on production by the controlling shareholder.

A May 2025 press report said goods sold through the US unit are produced by the parent and exported. The overseas base is ILWOUL GML CORP., a US unit established in August 2023 with a capital contribution of USD 7 million.

Sales channels span online marketplaces such as Amazon, Walmart and eBay, along with the Korean-American grocer H-Mart and the Korean online marketplace Hanpoom. A December 2025 report said some days exceed USD 10,000 in daily sales, and that additional tie-ups with Ultari Mall and Miss USA were planned at the time.

In Europe, a January 2025 report said the company had obtained UL and CE certification and was pursuing listings with large supermarket chains. The company stresses product quality and competitive pricing as its selling points abroad. As of September 2026, the largest shareholder and related parties held 43.74% of the shares.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual sales moved from KRW 13.1 billion in 2022 to KRW 28.8 billion in 2023 (up 119.6% year on year) and KRW 45.5 billion in 2024 (up 58.0%), before falling to KRW 33.9 billion in 2025 (down 25.5%). Among 2022 to 2025, 2024 was the largest year and 2022 the smallest.

Operating results were an operating loss of KRW 3.19 billion in 2022 and an operating loss of KRW 851 million in 2023, before turning to an operating profit of KRW 4.69 billion in 2024 and then falling 56.5% to KRW 2.04 billion in 2025.

The operating margin went from -24.3% in 2022 to -3.0% in 2023, 10.3% in 2024 and 6.0% in 2025. Profit attributable to owners of the parent was a loss of KRW 4.08 billion in 2022 and a loss of KRW 6.58 billion in 2023, turned to a profit of KRW 7.48 billion in 2024, and fell 29.4% to KRW 5.29 billion in 2025.

Net income including non-controlling interests was reported at the same amounts in each year. A May 2025 press report attributed the 2024 swing to profit to the shift of the main business from manufacturing to distributing electric and hot mats.

On a quarterly basis, sales in Q3 2025 were KRW 3.27 billion with an operating loss of KRW 47.27 million, and Q4 2025 sales jumped to KRW 26.9 billion (up 721.6% from the prior quarter) with an operating profit of KRW 3.37 billion.

Since the electric mat business is described as slow in the first half, the Q4 concentration reads as a winter-weighted pattern.

Sales fell to KRW 1.65 billion in Q1 2026 (down 93.9% from the prior quarter) and KRW 483 million in Q2 (down 70.8%), then rose to KRW 3.98 billion in Q3 (up 724.5% from the prior quarter and up 21.6% year on year).

Operating losses were KRW 309 million in Q1 2026, KRW 1.16 billion in Q2 and KRW 1.21 billion in Q3, three straight quarters of losses, while profit attributable to owners of the parent was a net loss of KRW 903 million in Q2 and KRW 1.11 billion in Q3, two straight quarters of losses.

The last four quarters (Q4 2025 to Q3 2026) total KRW 2.98 billion in profit attributable to owners of the parent, and the three quarters other than Q4 2025 together were a net loss.

A securities information site cited the 2025 sale of the Vietnamese subsidiary's stake and seasonal sales declines as factors in the weaker first half of 2026.

05

Industry analysis

Electric and hot mats are seasonal appliances whose sales concentrate in winter demand. A securities information site's company overview describes the electric mat market as roughly KRW 1 trillion and the hot mat market as about KRW 600 billion, and characterizes both as growing.

However, the figures rest on a summary of company overview data rather than an independent market study. Abroad, the company relies on US online marketplaces as its main channel, and a May 2025 press report said Amazon sales were to shift to the fulfillment by Amazon (FBA) model from June 2025.

The same report said delivery times that had taken seven days would fall to within two days, and it highlighted logistics as an improvement. The US online channel is a contest on price and product quality, and the company emphasizes its quality and price competitiveness.

Because production sits with the parent, unit costs and supply stability depend on the parent's production capacity and export logistics. Export-import conditions and currency movements can affect costs and margins.

Since 2023 the company has focused on seasonal appliance distribution and has exited manufacturing, so its scope is narrower and centered on electric mats, hot mats and fans. Among listed consumer and distribution companies, its sales base is on the small side. As overseas sales grow, channel concentration and exposure to local policy changes also rise.

A January 2025 report said offline listings at Costco and Walmart were being pursued for the second half of that year. A December 2025 report said tie-ups with Ultari Mall and Miss USA were planned, which would broaden distribution channels.

The company set stable dividends and mid-to-long-term growth as its management goals, and cash dividend items passed at the March 27, 2026 general shareholders meeting. In May 2026, the CEO Lee Kwang-mo bought 8,000 shares on the open market, lifting his stake to 6.8%, according to a disclosure.

The KRW 3.0 billion third-party private placement disclosed in August 2026 is to raise operating funds, and all new shares are to be locked up for one year. The new shares were priced at KRW 1,981 each, with payment on September 23, 2026 and a listing date set for October 21, 2026.

The company said it is also pursuing European markets alongside the US, presenting channel diversification as the direction. The next checkpoint is how the operating margin in the 2026 annual report compares with the 6.0% recorded in 2025.

Next, the Q1 2027 quarterly report will be compared against the KRW 309 million operating loss in Q1 2026, and the lock-up on the third-party placement shares is due to end around September 2027.

Bull factors

Operating margin turned from losses to profit

The operating margin moved from -24.3% in 2022 to 10.3% in 2024, turning from losses to profit, and stayed positive at 6.0% in 2025. The 2024 turn was reported as coinciding with the shift of the main business to electric and hot mat distribution. The margin recovery is the clearest indicator of the restructuring's effect so far.

Low leverage and positive operating cash flow

The debt ratio fell from 89.4% in 2022 to 15.2% in 2025, with total liabilities of KRW 6.37 billion and total equity of KRW 41.9 billion at the end of 2025. Operating cash flow was negative in 2022 and 2023, then was KRW 3.18 billion in 2024 and KRW 3.02 billion in 2025, positive in both years. A stronger balance sheet reduces the pressure of raising additional funds.

US online channels and early European expansion

The channel mix expanded to include Amazon, Walmart, eBay and a Korean online marketplace, and a December 2025 report said some days exceed USD 10,000 in daily sales. A January 2025 report said the company had obtained UL and CE certification and was pursuing European supermarket listings. Having several overseas routes in place reduces dependence on any single channel.

Bear factors

Operating losses continued through 2026

Operating losses were KRW 309 million in Q1 2026, KRW 1.16 billion in Q2 and KRW 1.21 billion in Q3, three straight quarters of losses. Q3 sales were KRW 3.98 billion, up 21.6% year on year, yet the operating loss widened from a year earlier. Profit attributable to owners of the parent also turned to losses in Q2 and Q3 2026, two straight quarters.

Seasonal sales and an annual decline

Annual sales for 2025 fell 25.5% to KRW 33.9 billion, and quarterly sales dropped from KRW 26.9 billion in Q4 2025 to KRW 1.65 billion in Q1 2026 and KRW 483 million in Q2. Because sales concentrate in particular quarters, quarterly results swing widely. Sales rebounded in Q3 2026, but the full-year direction for 2026 remains to be seen.

Dilution and convertible bond balance

The third-party placement disclosed in August 2026 increases the share count, which lowers the ownership stake of existing shareholders. The KRW 10 billion convertible bonds issued in May 2023 had a balance of about KRW 2.4 billion as of a 2025 report, and conversions raised concerns about potential selling pressure. These potential shares are a factor in any future increase in the share count.

10

Risk factors

Dependence on the controlling shareholder

After exiting manufacturing, product supply depends on production by the largest shareholder, and a 2025 press report said US sales volume also comes from parent production. As of September 2026, the largest shareholder and related parties held 43.74%, so control is concentrated.

Terms of related-party transactions and changes in the parent's capacity flow directly into the company's results.

Listing history and past financial strain

At the end of 2024, the KOSDAQ committee's one-year improvement period ended and the exchange began the process to decide on delisting. Trading later resumed, but the company had long loss periods in the past, which is background to any judgment of financial stability. Whether profitability holds after the 2024 turn is the key test for putting this history behind it.

Currency and overseas sales environment

As sales through Amazon and other online channels and exports grow, exposure to currency moves and local distribution policy changes rises. Because the company sells products it sources from its parent, unit costs and margins may be sensitive to exchange rates and logistics costs. Changes in channel fees or platform rules could alter sales terms.

Overall view

Ilwoul GML moved from metal mold making to seasonal appliance distribution after 2023, and it is going through a period of large earnings swings. On the disclosed results, operating profit turned positive in 2024 and Q4 2025 rebounded, but operating losses were disclosed for three straight quarters from Q1 to Q3 2026.

The balance sheet looks light, with a debt ratio of 15.2% and total equity of KRW 41.9 billion at the end of 2025, and operating cash flow was positive in both 2024 and 2025. Strengths include the overseas online channel expansion, a stable dividend policy and low leverage.

Weaknesses include continuing quarterly losses, seasonal and declining annual sales, and dilution from the third-party placement and convertible bonds. The reliance on the largest shareholder for supply and the 2024 listing-maintenance process are also factors to weigh.

Annual sales fell from KRW 45.5 billion in 2024 to KRW 33.9 billion in 2025, but Q3 2026 sales rose year on year, so the directional signals are mixed. The next checkpoints are the operating margin in the 2026 annual report, operating results in the Q1 2027 quarterly report and the lock-up expiry around September 2027.
