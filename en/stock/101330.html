02

Business structure

Mobase was founded in 1999 to manufacture and sell metal molds, and it now operates several business segments including mobile phone parts, automotive parts, and sewing and embroidery machines.

The company entered automotive electronics in earnest in 2019 through the acquisition of an automotive electronic parts company, and automotive parts have since become one of its core businesses. Mobile phone components are shrinking in revenue as that market matures.

Automotive parts are benefiting from rising demand for electronics and electrification components, but cost-reduction pressure makes it difficult to sustain profitability.

The company says it is building a low-cost, high-quality production system through new technology development and process automation to strengthen its trading relationship with Samsung Electronics.

Demand for automotive parts depends on vehicle production volumes, so the car production cycle drives revenue swings in this segment. As the shift to electric vehicles reduces demand for internal combustion engine parts, how far the product mix moves toward electrification is the key to the structural change.

On September 3, 2026, the company agreed to sell its entire stake in an unlisted subsidiary, Mobase Sunstar, and said it would concentrate resources on core businesses. Revenue shares by segment and by customer should be checked in the segment information of the regular report.

Competitive differentiation rests on production automation and the cooperative relationship with Samsung Electronics. Market share comparisons with peers cannot be built from disclosure line items alone.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual revenue fell from KRW 1,326.9 billion in 2022 to KRW 1,262.8 billion in 2023 (down 4.8% year on year), then rose for two consecutive years, to KRW 1,301.7 billion in 2024 (up 3.1% year on year) and KRW 1,371.3 billion in 2025 (up 5.3% year on year). 2025 was the largest year of 2022-2025 and 2023 was the smallest.

Operating profit was highest in 2022 at KRW 70.6 billion and lowest in 2023 at KRW 40.3 billion (down 43.0% year on year). It recovered to KRW 53.3 billion in 2024 (up 32.4% year on year) and then declined again in 2025 to KRW 41.8 billion (down 21.6% year on year).

The operating margin fell from 5.3% in 2022 to 3.2% in 2023, 4.1% in 2024 and 3.0% in 2025, and the margin declined in 2025 even though revenue grew. The company cited higher cost of sales as the reason for the decline in operating profit in 2025.

Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 18.8 billion in 2022, KRW 15.9 billion in 2023, KRW 27.9 billion in 2024 (the largest year in the period) and KRW 11.7 billion in 2025 (the smallest year).

Net income for the same year, 2025, was KRW 21.2 billion, which differs from net profit attributable to controlling shareholders because it includes non-controlling interests.

On a quarterly basis, revenue was KRW 321.6 billion and operating profit KRW 15.9 billion in the third quarter of 2025, followed by KRW 359.5 billion and KRW 9.15 billion in the fourth quarter of 2025, KRW 331.1 billion and KRW 9.95 billion in the first quarter of 2026, KRW 332.8 billion and KRW 8.35 billion in the second quarter of 2026, and KRW 313.0 billion and KRW 5.59 billion in the third quarter of 2026.

Operating profit fell for two consecutive quarters, the second and third quarters, after the first quarter of 2026, and third-quarter revenue was down 2.7% from the same period a year earlier.

Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 11.4 billion in the third quarter of 2025, KRW 0.29 billion in the fourth quarter, KRW 13.2 billion in the first quarter of 2026 and KRW 5.62 billion in the second quarter, before swinging to a net loss of KRW 8.45 billion in the third quarter.

The sum of net profit attributable to controlling shareholders over the latest four quarters is KRW 10.7 billion. The gap between operating profit and net profit attributable to controlling shareholders widens every quarter, so non-operating items and income taxes must be considered together to read the earnings trend.

Operating cash flow was KRW 97.2 billion in 2022, KRW 52.0 billion in 2023, KRW 90.2 billion in 2024 and KRW 105.0 billion in 2025, with 2025 the largest.

05

Industry analysis

Demand for automotive electronic parts depends on vehicle production volumes. As the shift to electric vehicles reduces demand for internal combustion engine parts, demand for higher-value electronics and electrification components is rising, so the mix of parts is changing.

Mobile phone components are shrinking as their market matures, so whether growth in automotive electronics can offset that decline will determine the direction of the business as a whole.

On costs, increases in raw material and component prices can take time to pass through to customer pricing, so cost of sales pressure can show up in margins first. The 3.0% operating margin in 2025, despite higher revenue, is consistent with this cost pressure.

Relative positioning rests on the trading relationship with Samsung Electronics and production automation. Market share and profitability gaps against peers are harder to compare using disclosed figures alone. Because customer concentration is high, production volume changes at key customers flow directly into results.

Two variables will shape the sector outlook: the pace of vehicle electrification and the restructuring of the mobile phone component market.

The September 2026 sale of its entire stake in an unlisted subsidiary was presented as part of a strategy to focus on core businesses, and its effect on results will appear in the quarterly reports for the period.

The 2026 annual report, the next regular filing, will confirm fourth-quarter operating profit and net income attributable to owners of the parent, and the first checkpoint is whether the declines and the net loss seen through the third quarter continued into the fourth quarter.

The first quarter 2027 quarterly report will show whether the operating margin recovers to the 3.0% level seen in 2025. Expanding the revenue share of electronics and electrification components is a condition for profit recovery. Whether the treasury share cancellation proceeds as planned is a separate check on shareholder return.

08

Bull factors

Rising demand for electronics and electrification parts

Demand for higher-value electronics and electrification components is rising, which aligns the automotive parts business with the shift to electric vehicles. The automotive electronics push that began with the 2019 acquisition has been reinforced through the Samsung Electronics relationship and production upgrades.

If automotive parts can offset the decline in mobile phone components, the business mix could become more stable.

Shareholder return plans and a stronger balance sheet

The company plans to cancel part of its treasury shares during 2026 and 2027 and to operate its dividend policy in a stable manner. The debt-to-equity ratio fell from 143.6% in 2022 to 133.1% in 2025, and operating cash flow of KRW 105 billion in 2025 was the highest of the four years. As long as cash generation holds, the funding base for the return plan appears to be in place.

Larger revenue base in 2025

Revenue reached KRW 1.37 trillion in 2025, the largest year of the 2022–2025 period, and grew for two consecutive years from 2024. With a larger revenue base, profit size could change meaningfully if profitability recovers.

Operating profit rose to KRW 9.95 billion in the first quarter of 2026, showing that cost management did produce results at one point.

Bear factors

Operating profit decline continues

Operating profit in the third quarter of 2026 was KRW 5.59 billion, down 64.9% year on year. It has declined for two consecutive quarters since the first quarter of 2026, and the decline has been steep enough to question the pace of any recovery in earnings power.

Swing to a net loss attributable to owners

The third quarter of 2026 produced a net loss attributable to owners of the parent of KRW 8.45 billion, after a profit of KRW 5.62 billion in the prior quarter. Net income attributable to owners was only KRW 294 million in the fourth quarter of 2025.

The four-quarter sum of KRW 10.7 billion reflects large quarterly swings, which limits how much weight recent earnings can carry.

Mobile phone parts shrinking and cost pressure

Mobile phone components are shrinking in revenue as their market matures. In 2025, even as revenue grew, the company cited higher cost of sales as a factor behind the decline in operating profit. If raw material and component price increases take time to reach customer pricing, cost pressure can hit margins first.

10

Risk factors

Cost structure

The company cited higher cost of sales as a factor in the 2025 decline in operating profit. When raw material and component prices rise, the lag before customer pricing catches up can make quarterly profit volatile.

The fall in the operating margin from 5.3% in 2022 to 3.0% in 2025 reads as the cumulative result of this structural pressure.

Customer concentration

The company presents its trading relationship with Samsung Electronics as central to its production system. When business depends heavily on a single customer, changes in that customer's volumes flow straight into results. In segments tied to customer product cycles, such as mobile phone components, order swings can be large.

Portfolio restructuring and earnings gap

The September 2026 sale of the entire stake in an unlisted subsidiary is changing the business mix. Because the gap between operating profit and net income attributable to owners widens from quarter to quarter, swings in non-operating items make results harder to interpret. The profit and loss effects of the sale and restructuring must be checked in the regular reports.

Overall view

Mobase's 2025 revenue of KRW 1.37 trillion was the largest of the 2022–2025 period, yet operating profit of KRW 41.8 billion fell short of the KRW 70.6 billion recorded in 2022, so revenue and profit moved in different directions.

Quarterly revenue has been running in the KRW 310 billion to KRW 330 billion range in 2026, but operating profit has declined for two consecutive quarters, and the third quarter brought a net loss attributable to owners of KRW 8.45 billion.

Strengths include rising demand for electronics and electrification parts, shareholder return plans covering treasury share cancellation and dividends, and a lower debt-to-equity ratio. Weaknesses include the declining operating profit, the swing to a net loss, the maturing mobile phone parts market and cost pressure.

These two sides deserve roughly equal weight, and neither alone explains the trend. The next checkpoints are the fourth-quarter results to be confirmed in the 2026 annual report and the operating margin in the first quarter 2027 report.

Progress on the treasury share cancellation and the performance of core businesses after the unlisted subsidiary sale should also be tracked.
