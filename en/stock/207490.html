LED core shrinks as losses persist

02

Business structure

Apex Intec was founded in November 1997 and listed on KONEX in November 2018 as an LED lighting company. Its main products are outdoor lights such as streetlights, floodlights, security lights and tunnel lights, along with indoor luminaires, landscape lighting, explosion-proof fixtures and solar streetlights.

The business is organized into three areas: LED lighting devices, solar power generation equipment, and IoT-based remote access devices. A technology evaluation report dated April 2024 described the LED lighting segment as the driver of total revenue.

The renewable energy segment is expanding into solar power equipment and energy storage systems. The IoT remote access devices, which include leakage monitoring functions, are meant to widen market share in electrical safety.

Its main customers are government and public institutions through the public procurement (B2G) channel, and it holds several excellent public procurement product certifications. On competition, the company emphasizes technology differentiation through a range of certifications.

The company has subsidiaries in the Philippines and Hong Kong and is pursuing wider overseas sales. The largest shareholder is CEO Kim Kwon-jin, who held 91.72% as of December 2023.

03

Recent trends

According to DART-based financials, revenue in 2025 was KRW 20,015.84 million, down 19.86% from KRW 24,976.62 million in 2024. Revenue in 2023 was KRW 25,452.72 million, so the decline over two years was KRW 5,436.88 million.

The 2025 operating loss was KRW 522.43 million, close to the KRW 546.18 million loss in 2024, and the operating margin was -2.61%. The 2025 net loss was KRW 441.94 million, following a KRW 591.86 million net loss in 2024, marking a second straight year of losses.

Operating cash flow was positive at KRW 191.58 million in 2025 but lower than KRW 875.47 million in 2024. Cash operating profit was negative at KRW 1,038.04 million in 2025, while investing cash outflows reached KRW 5,271.73 million. Total assets rose 36.29% to KRW 31,486.90 million at the end of 2025.

As of the close on October 2, 2026, the share price was 6,400 won, the 52-week range was 5,440 to 7,800 won, and market capitalization was KRW 23.0 billion. Returns were -3.03% over three months, -5.88% over six months and -3.18% over twelve months.

The unfaithful disclosure case concerns a delayed disclosure of a trading halt with a customer, with the triggering date of September 26, 2024 and a disclosure on September 18, 2026, and carries 2.0 penalty points.

04

Outlook

Future revenue trends will depend on how far LED replacement demand supports the core business. A forecast cited in the April 2024 technology evaluation report, based on TrendForce data, expects replacement demand to keep rising as LED lights installed between 2014 and 2016 reach the end of their life from 2023.

The renewable energy segment is set to expand around solar streetlights, solar power equipment and energy storage systems. The IoT remote access devices are positioned to gain share as electrical safety management shifts toward a platform with private participation.

Stronger online sales and wellbeing lighting promotion are also presented as routes to revenue recovery. Whether new business revenue grows large enough to offset the core decline is the key point to track.

On the financial side, the debt dependence ratio of 52.53% and any improvement in the current ratio will determine investment capacity. When third-quarter 2026 results are disclosed, they will show whether 2025 was a continuation of the trend or a turning point.

The accumulation of disclosure compliance history after the unfaithful disclosure designation is also a point to watch.

05

Bull factors

Structural base from LED replacement demand

LED lights installed between 2014 and 2016 are reaching the end of their life from 2023, which is expected to lift replacement demand. An April 2024 technology evaluation report citing this outlook noted that replacement demand continues to grow.

The company has launched human-centric wellbeing lighting with circadian and IoT functions to differentiate its products.

Solar and IoT businesses widening the scope

The company is expanding its renewable energy business through solar power equipment, solar streetlights and energy storage systems. Its IoT-based leakage monitoring remote access devices are aimed at gaining share as electrical safety management moves toward private participation.

Contribution from these new businesses is not yet clearly visible in reported figures as total revenue has declined.

Public procurement channel and a slightly smaller loss

Government and public institutions form the main sales channel through public procurement, and the company holds several excellent public procurement product certifications. The 2025 operating loss of KRW 522.43 million was slightly smaller than KRW 546.18 million in 2024.

Operating cash flow stayed positive at KRW 191.58 million in 2025, though lower than KRW 875.47 million in 2024.

06

Bear factors

Revenue declining for two consecutive years

Revenue fell 1.87% to KRW 24,976.62 million in 2024 and then fell 19.86% to KRW 20,015.84 million in 2025. Compared with KRW 25,452.72 million in 2023, revenue shrank by KRW 5,436.88 million over two years. The operating margin worsened to -2.61% in 2025 from -2.19% in 2024.

High borrowing dependence and weak cash generation

The debt ratio stood at 261.12% at the end of 2025, and the borrowings dependence ratio rose to 52.53% from 48.15% in 2024. The current ratio of around 0.59 indicates limited short-term liquidity headroom.

Cash operating profit was negative at KRW 1,038.04 million, while investing activities caused outflows of KRW 5,271.73 million.

Unfaithful disclosure designation and disclosure credibility

The Korea Exchange designated Apex Intec as an unfaithful disclosure company for disclosing a customer trading halt late. The triggering date was September 26, 2024, and the disclosure was made on September 18, 2026. The penalty was 2.0 points, and the designation date was recorded as October 20, 2026. Disclosure compliance history is cited as a weakness in terms of company credibility.

07

Risk factors

Macro

Changes in government budgets and public procurement timing feed directly into revenue for a company that depends heavily on public channels. In a rising interest rate environment, interest costs could weigh more on a company with a borrowings dependence ratio of 52.53%. A slowdown in construction and facility investment could also weaken demand for lighting and solar equipment.

Competition over price and certification in LED products could delay any recovery in profitability. The operating margin of -2.61% remains far from breakeven. If new businesses fail to offset the decline in core revenue, the loss structure will persist. A debt ratio of 261.12% and a current ratio near 0.59 may also affect the terms of any additional financing.

An average three-month trading volume of only 132 shares may constrain order execution and price stability. Being a KONEX small-cap with market capitalization of KRW 23.0 billion also limits trading liquidity. Given the unfaithful disclosure designation, further measures related to disclosure compliance could follow.

08

Overall view

Based on 2025 figures, Apex Intec shows a weak core business, with revenue declining and operating and net losses recorded for a second consecutive year. The operating loss narrowed slightly that year and operating cash flow stayed positive, but cash operating profit was negative.

On the financial side, a debt ratio of 261.12%, a borrowings dependence ratio of 52.53% and a current ratio around 0.59 remain burdens. Supportive factors include LED replacement demand, the solar and IoT businesses, and public procurement certifications.

Offsetting weaknesses are the unfaithful disclosure designation disclosed in September 2026 and liquidity with an average three-month volume of 132 shares. The share price was 6,400 won at the close on October 2, 2026, placing it in the lower-middle part of its 52-week range.

According to the Hankyung market page, no analyst consensus was shown. Going forward, the pace of core business recovery and changes in the financial structure remain the key variables.
