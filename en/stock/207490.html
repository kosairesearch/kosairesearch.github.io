Report overview

Procurement Reliance and a Loss-Making Base

Apex Intec is an LED lighting maker with heavy reliance on public procurement, and operating losses in 2024-2025 now coincide with a designation as an unfaithful disclosure corporation in October 2026.

02

Business structure

Apex Intec was established in November 1997 and listed on KONEX in November 2018, with LED lighting and solar power generation equipment as its main businesses. Its official industry classification is manufacture of light bulbs and lighting devices.

It sits in the machinery and equipment group, but its business differs materially from larger peers in that group such as Doosan Enerbility and Doosan Bobcat.

According to a corporate information site (reference date not stated), outdoor lighting (street, security and tunnel lights) accounts for 42% of sales, solar power systems 18%, indoor lighting 10%, special lighting such as solar street lights 10%, and other items 20%. Sales lean heavily on public procurement.

According to the disclosure, sales to the procurement agency were KRW 17.411 billion, or 68.40% of the latest fiscal-year sales of KRW 25.453 billion.

On the product side, an IoT/ICT-based continuous leakage-monitoring system for road lighting was designated an innovation product by the procurement agency in December 2022, and the company received a commendation from the Korea Electrical Safety Corporation in December 2023.

The company is developing a self-sufficient energy platform that combines building-integrated photovoltaics (BIPV) with energy storage systems (ESS). Overseas, it has pursued sales network expansion through subsidiaries in the Philippines and Hong Kong.

As of end-December 2023, the largest shareholder was CEO Kwon Jin Kim with a 91.72% stake. Market-share data for domestic LED lighting competitors could not be confirmed in this research, so the competitive picture is described only qualitatively.

03

Recent trends

Revenue for 2025 was KRW 20.016 billion, down 19.9% from the prior year. The operating loss for the same year was KRW 522 million.

On an annual basis, 2023 showed revenue of KRW 25.453 billion and operating profit of KRW 144 million, while 2024 turned to revenue of KRW 24.977 billion and an operating loss of KRW 546 million. Net loss was KRW 592 million in 2024 and KRW 442 million in 2025. The balance sheet is relatively leveraged.

At the end of 2025 the debt ratio was 261.12%, the equity ratio 27.69% and borrowing dependence 52.53%. Investing cash flow that year was an outflow of KRW 5.272 billion, and non-current assets rose sharply. Disclosure issues compounded this.

On September 18, 2026 the company disclosed that its bidding eligibility for public agencies had been restricted by a procurement agency sanction, with the suspension date recorded as October 4, 2024.

The company stated that, after seeking an injunction and contesting the matter in an administrative lawsuit, the sanction was lifted through the Liberation Day special pardon of 2025.

Because of the late disclosure, it received a designation notice on October 1, 2026 and was designated an unfaithful disclosure corporation on October 20, 2026, with 2.0 penalty points. The 2025 revenue decline overlaps with the sanction period, but the disclosures alone do not establish causation.

On the share price, the 52-week range is KRW 5,440 to 7,800, with changes of -3.03% over three months and -3.18% over twelve months. As of October 27, 2026 the price is KRW 6,400 and trading value is only KRW 1,015,830. Quarterly and half-year results for 2026 could not be confirmed in this research.

04

Outlook

The outlook depends on how quickly the procurement channel normalizes and on whether new businesses translate into revenue. A technical analysis report cited market researcher TrendForce in forecasting that replacement demand would keep rising from 2023 as LED lights installed in 2014-2016 reach end of life.

The same report noted that restrictions on non-compliant fluorescent lamps apply from 2028. This demand backdrop is favorable for street-light and security-light replacement markets, but whether it translates into share gains for the company is a separate question.

The company is described as accelerating its push into global markets centered on smart lighting and renewable energy solutions. However, the revenue contribution of the BIPV/ESS platform and overseas subsidiaries is not quantified in the filings reviewed.

The company moved from an operating profit in 2023 to operating losses in 2024-2025, so recovery in sales scale and an improved cost structure are preconditions for a return to profit.

Items to monitor are quarterly revenue and operating results in 2026, changes in the procurement share of sales, any accumulation of disclosure penalty points, and the borrowing burden. The thin trading volume on KONEX should always be considered when interpreting share price moves.

05

Bull factors

Procurement Credentials and Innovation Product Record

Beginning with KS certification in 2010, the company has accumulated excellent-product, innovation-product and new-product (NEP) certifications. In December 2022 its IoT-based road-lighting leakage-monitoring system was designated an innovation product by the procurement agency.

In public procurement, a certification record underpins bidding competitiveness. With the bidding restriction lifted, resumption of trade in this channel could affect the revenue structure.

LED Replacement Demand and Regulation

TrendForce forecast that LED lights installed in 2014-2016 reach end of life from 2023, so replacement demand keeps rising. Rules on non-compliant fluorescent lamps also apply from 2028. For a company whose largest sales share is outdoor lighting, a larger replacement market is a favorable backdrop. Market expansion does not by itself mean improved results for this company.

BIPV/ESS New Business and Overseas Bases

The company is developing a self-sufficient energy platform combining BIPV and ESS, pursuing climate response and ESG management together. It also has subsidiaries in the Philippines and Hong Kong, giving it a base for overseas sales.

Broadening from lighting alone into energy solutions opens a path to reduce procurement dependence. The actual revenue contribution of this segment needs to be confirmed in later filings.

06

Bear factors

Falling Sales and Persistent Operating Losses

Revenue in 2025 fell 19.9% year on year to KRW 20.016 billion. Operating losses persisted for two consecutive years, at KRW 546 million in 2024 and KRW 522 million in 2025. The slim profit of 2023 did not carry forward, which points to a weak profitability base. Until 2026 results are confirmed, it is hard to judge whether a recovery is under way.

Heavy Debt Burden

At the end of 2025 the debt ratio was 261.12%, borrowing dependence 52.53% and the quick ratio 45.93%. Investing outflows have exceeded the cash generated from operations. Greater reliance on outside funding while loss-making can add financing costs that further pressure profitability. The uncertain timing of returns on the investments is another burden.

Disclosure Credibility and Customer Concentration

The procurement agency accounted for 68.40% of sales in the latest fiscal year cited. The late disclosure of this suspension led to designation as an unfaithful disclosure corporation with 2.0 penalty points.

That a sanction on such a concentrated sales channel became public almost two years later raises questions about the disclosure process. Accumulated penalty points could lead to further measures.

07

Risk factors

Macro

Borrowing dependence is high at 52.53%, so interest-rate levels have a large effect on financing costs. With sales concentrated in public procurement, the company is sensitive to the pace of government and municipal budget execution and to procurement policy changes.

A slowdown in construction and facility investment also affects demand for lighting and solar installations. Specific sensitivities were not confirmed in filings.

The sanction by the procurement agency cited violations of direct-production requirements and specification mismatches, among other grounds. A recurrence of similar violations could trigger new bidding restrictions.

The 2.0 penalty points imposed on October 20, 2026 count in full toward the cumulative total for the past year. Further late disclosures could lead to follow-up measures under KONEX market rules.

Listed shares total 3.6 million and market capitalization is KRW 23.0 billion. As the CEO held 91.72% at the end of December 2023, the free float is small. Trading value on October 27, 2026 was only KRW 1,015,830, so even small orders can move quotes.

Because the controlling shareholder's decisions carry heavy weight, governance should be reviewed from a minority shareholder's perspective.

08

Overall view

Apex Intec is a small KONEX manufacturer centered on LED lighting and expanding into solar power and ESS. Procurement certifications, an innovation-product record and the structural backdrop of LED replacement demand are favorable elements of its operating environment.

On the other side, operating losses in 2024-2025, a debt ratio of 261.12% at the end of 2025 and concentrated procurement sales are burdens on both finances and business.

The bidding restriction disclosed on September 18, 2026 and the designation as an unfaithful disclosure corporation on October 20 added uncertainty about information disclosure.

A market capitalization of KRW 23.0 billion, a CEO stake of 91.72% (as of end-December 2023) and very small trading value show the limits of price formation. Confirmation of 2026 quarterly and half-year results and of the new businesses' revenue contribution would strengthen the basis for assessment.
