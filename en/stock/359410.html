Backlog Grows, Earnings Base Still Under Test

02

Business structure

Youngkwang traces its roots to Youngkwang Gongjakso, founded in 1962, and became an incorporated company in 1999. It now runs two segments, shipbuilding and plant, covering design, fabrication and assembly in-house.

The shipbuilding segment supplies modular engine-room units, hull blocks and fuel gas supply systems (FGSS) for vessels. The plant segment makes heat exchangers and pressure vessels as well as skids and plant modules that combine piping, structures and instrumentation.

Per the IB Tomato report, plant accounted for 21.60 percent of 2025 sales, and the shipbuilding segment generated KRW 88.7 billion of the KRW 113.1 billion total.

Per a Prime Economy report, only three domestic companies, including Youngkwang, are integrated into large shipyard supply chains for engine-room modules, and the report says Youngkwang handled about 127 engine-room units, roughly 40 percent of vessels built by Korea's three largest shipyards from 2023 to 2025.

Customers include HD Hyundai Heavy Industries, Samsung Heavy Industries and Hanwha Ocean among domestic shipbuilders, and several domestic EPC contractors on the plant side. The company operates five factories across two regions, one of them leased. New businesses include FGSS production and carbon capture, utilization and storage equipment.

03

Recent trends

Revenue in 2025 was KRW 113.1 billion, down from KRW 140.4 billion in 2024, and operating profit fell to KRW 12.3 billion from KRW 13.9 billion.

Net income rose to KRW 10.3 billion on a standalone basis from KRW 9.6 billion in 2024, but per a report in E-Today, pre-tax profit declined and the net income gain mainly reflected a corporate tax reversal.

The operating margin rose from 2.7 percent in 2023 to about 10.9 percent in 2025, according to a Prime Economy report.

In the first half of 2026, revenue was KRW 59.1 billion and operating profit KRW 5.4 billion, keeping the company in the black, with operating profit up 9.6 percent from the first half of 2025 per IB Tomato. Backlog rose from KRW 196.3 billion at the end of 2025 to KRW 202.2 billion at the end of June 2026.

Contract assets fell from KRW 29.7 billion to KRW 10.8 billion in 2025, a change that reflects collection of amounts already recognized as revenue.

The debt ratio fell from 456.1 percent at the end of 2025 to 242.2 percent at the end of the first half of 2026, helped by conversion of redeemable convertible preferred shares.

After listing on November 2, 2026 at a KRW 17,000 offering price, the first-day close was KRW 21,500 (+26.47 percent), with an intraday high of KRW 37,400. A later reported down-day close was KRW 15,630 (-27.30 percent), and reports did not identify a company disclosure or clear negative news behind that move. The share price stood at KRW 9,760 on November 11, 2026, down 1.61 percent from the prior session.

04

Outlook

Revenue visibility rests on a backlog of KRW 202.2 billion and an order for engine-room modules for six 200K-class LNG carriers, with work running from October 13, 2026 to February 14, 2028.

The company said it will spend KRW 20 billion of the roughly KRW 31.5 billion net offering proceeds on land for an integrated plant in Geoje and Goseong and plans to more than double production capacity.

According to the company, consolidating five factories would widen the range of products it can take orders for and reduce logistics costs between sites. New opportunities cited include power infrastructure and data center power equipment in North America and nuclear and SMR markets in Europe.

A planned factory in Bulgaria depends on a contract for on-site assembly and installation of nuclear power plant modules, so the timeline could slip depending on contract progress.

On the industry side, a report said the combined backlog of Korea's three major shipbuilders reached KRW 153 trillion at the end of June 2026, the highest since 2016. The Korea Chamber of Commerce and Industry forecast that Korean shipbuilding exports would rise 8.6 percent in 2026 to USD 33.92 billion.

On earnings, the points to watch are cost control in the plant segment and easing customer concentration. Plant share rose to 32.34 percent of first-half 2026 sales, so how plant profitability shows up in full-year 2026 results is the key item to track.

05

Bull factors

Backlog and engine-room module share

Backlog at the end of June 2026 was KRW 202.2 billion, up from KRW 196.3 billion at the end of 2025 and about 1.8 times 2025 revenue. An engine-room module order for six 200K-class LNG carriers announced in October 2026 runs from October 13, 2026 to February 14, 2028.

Per a Prime Economy report, Youngkwang handled engine-room units for roughly 40 percent of vessels built by Korea's three largest shipyards from 2023 to 2025. The report characterizes this order structure as raising earnings visibility.

Margin recovery and balance sheet repair

Per a Prime Economy report, the operating margin rose from 2.7 percent in 2023 to about 10.9 percent in 2025. The debt ratio at the end of the first half of 2026 was 242.2 percent, down from 456.1 percent at the end of 2025, helped by conversion of redeemable convertible preferred shares.

The company plans to put KRW 20 billion of roughly KRW 31.5 billion in net proceeds toward consolidating its production base. The company presents this financial capacity as the foundation for new facility investment.

Expansion into green vessels and energy infrastructure

Youngkwang has started producing FGSS for vessels and plans to widen supply of green-vessel equipment as well as hydrogen and carbon capture facilities. North American power infrastructure, data center power generation equipment, and European nuclear and SMR markets are cited as targets for new orders.

The Korea Chamber of Commerce and Industry forecasts that Korean shipbuilding exports will rise 8.6 percent in 2026. This industry backdrop is cited as a supportive setting for the company's diversification.

06

Bear factors

Revenue and profit both fell in 2025

Revenue in 2025 was KRW 113.1 billion, down from KRW 140.4 billion in 2024, and operating profit fell to KRW 12.3 billion from KRW 13.9 billion. Per an E-Today report, pre-tax profit also declined, and the net income gain mainly reflected a tax reversal.

Because made-to-order products are recognized by construction progress, period-to-period results can swing. Whether this profit base is durable remains a matter for subsequent reported results.

Weak profitability in the plant segment

The plant segment recorded KRW 24.4 billion in 2025 sales and KRW 0.4 billion in operating profit. Cost burdens on some contracts ran higher than expected and reduced profit by about KRW 2 billion. Plant share rose to 32.34 percent of first-half 2026 sales, but the segment has yet to show repeatable profit. Until plant margins are shown to be repeatable, earnings continue to rest mainly on the shipbuilding segment.

Customer concentration and post-listing volatility

Two customers each above 10 percent of 2025 revenue generated KRW 73.4 billion, and three customers together generated KRW 82.8 billion, more than 70 percent of total sales.

The shares rose intraday to KRW 37,400 on the first listing day, then fell to a reported close of KRW 15,630, and stood at KRW 9,760 on November 11, 2026. Reports did not identify a company disclosure or clear negative news behind these swings. Price movement appears to have been driven by supply and demand factors rather than reported earnings.

07

Risk factors

Macro

Shipbuilding demand follows a cyclical pattern driven by owner ordering and newbuild prices. Even with high backlogs at the major yards, slower ordering or lower newbuild prices could reduce equipment orders.

Exchange rate and interest rate moves can affect overseas order pricing and costs, which remain sources of margin variability.

Business and contract

The Bulgarian production entity depends on a contract for on-site assembly and installation of nuclear power plant modules, so a delayed contract would delay its setup. In the plant segment, cost overruns on certain contracts have already reached profit, making contract-level cost control important.

Consolidating several factories may bring one-time costs from relocating a leased facility and reconfiguring production lines.

Market and liquidity

Shares swung widely after listing, and reports did not identify a clear cause for the sharp declines. The company listed 9,033,863 shares, and reports put the stake held by CEO Heo Cheol-gi and related parties at 61.88 percent. Changes in the float after listing could therefore affect trading supply and price volatility.

08

Overall view

Youngkwang has secured revenue visibility from a KRW 202.2 billion backlog and an engine-room module order for LNG carriers. Operating profit fell to KRW 12.3 billion in 2025, but the operating margin rose to about 10.9 percent, and the company stayed profitable in the first half of 2026.

Bullish factors are its position as one of few domestic engine-room module suppliers, order flow linked to the shipbuilding cycle, and the planned capacity expansion from factory consolidation.

Bearish factors are weak plant-segment profitability, customer concentration with three customers above 70 percent of sales, and large post-listing share price swings. Projects tied to contracts, such as the Bulgarian entity, may shift in timing, leaving uncertainty over the pace of expansion.

Since listing, the share price has moved between the offering price and the current level, driven mainly by supply and demand factors. This view is a qualitative summary for informational purposes.
