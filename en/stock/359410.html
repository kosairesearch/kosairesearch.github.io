02

Business structure

Youngkwang, founded in 1999, is a shipbuilding and plant module specialist that performs the entire process from design through fabrication and assembly.

The business is split into a shipbuilding and offshore segment and a plant segment, and in 2025 the shipbuilding segment alone generated KRW 887 million in revenue and KRW 119 million in operating profit, accounting for most of the company's results.

In shipbuilding, the company manufactures and supplies engine room units installed in ship machinery spaces, hull blocks, and marine fuel gas supply systems in modular form.

The plant segment produces skids, process equipment such as pressure vessels and heat exchangers, and plant modules, and in 2025 it generated KRW 24.4 billion in revenue but only KRW 400 million in operating profit.

Press reports state that Youngkwang handled the engine room units for 127 of the 315 ships built by Korea's three large shipyards from 2023 through 2025.

Press reports also say the company holds ASME and European PED certifications, allowing in-house production of process equipment for high-temperature, high-pressure, and cryogenic service.

Among listed peers in the sector, HD-Hyundai Marine Engine, Hanwha Engine, and STX Engine supply marine engines and equipment, while Youngkwang emphasizes its integrated module capability from design through delivery.

Its customer base is concentrated: in 2025 two customers each accounted for more than 10% of sales, totaling about KRW 73.4 billion, and three such customers together made up more than 70% of revenue.

New businesses under development include data center power equipment, nuclear and SMR equipment, fuel cells, and hydrogen production and carbon capture facilities.

03

Recent trends

Youngkwang listed on the KOSDAQ market on November 2, 2026 at an IPO price of KRW 17,000. Cumulative third-quarter 2026 revenue was KRW 31.7 billion, up 38.1% year on year. Operating profit for the same period was KRW 2.77 billion, up only 1.7% year on year, which lagged the revenue growth by a wide margin.

Net income was KRW 900 million, down 63.2% year on year, so operating profit and net income moved in opposite directions. The drivers behind each line item can be traced in the detailed income statement and third-quarter report notes.

Revenue for 2025 was KRW 113.1 billion, down from KRW 140.3 billion in 2024, and operating profit was KRW 12.3 billion, for an operating margin of 10.9%.

Analysis reported in the press attributes the 2025 net income improvement mainly to a tax benefit from a reversal of income tax expense rather than to stronger core operating profit. The shares rose 26.47% on the first day to close at KRW 21,500 but fell intraday to KRW 16,210 the next day.

According to press reports, the stock closed at KRW 10,100 on November 6, 2026, below the IPO price, and touched an intraday low of KRW 9,500, a new 52-week low.

As of 14:23 KST on November 18, 2026, the share price stood at KRW 6,780, down 17.62% from the previous close, with a market capitalization of KRW 61.4 billion and trading value of KRW 4.4 billion.

04

Outlook

The key variables for future results are the order flow in the shipbuilding segment and cost management in the plant segment. Commentary in the press describes the shipbuilding industry as being in a profit recovery trend, and Youngkwang's shipbuilding segment recorded KRW 11.9 billion in operating profit in 2025.

Still, if vessel construction schedules are shortened or delayed, revenue and profitability can swing temporarily, and CEO Hur Cheol-ki pointed to seasonal reductions in working days in the third and fourth quarters.

Cost overruns on some contracts reduced profit by about KRW 2 billion, which makes contract-level cost control important.

The company is expanding into data center power equipment, nuclear and SMR equipment, fuel cells, and hydrogen facilities, but company remarks reported in the press indicate that concrete revenue has not yet become visible.

The planned Bulgarian production subsidiary depends on contracts for on-site assembly and installation of nuclear power plant construction modules, so timing could slip depending on contract progress.

Of the roughly KRW 31.5 billion raised in the IPO, KRW 20 billion is allocated to securing land for an integrated production plant in Geoje and Goseong, and the company has stated a plan to more than double production capacity.

Given that cumulative operating profit grew only 1.7% through the third quarter of 2026, whether higher revenue translates into sustained margins is the main point to monitor.

The debt-to-equity ratio fell from 981% in 2024 to 456% in 2025 and 242% in June 2026, but it remains high, and the quick ratio is 98.38%, below 100%. The next checkpoints are full-year results in the 2026 annual business report and changes in free float and trading supply after the listing.

05

Bull factors

Sizable order backlog

Press reports put the order backlog at the end of the first half of 2026 at KRW 202.2 billion, about 1.8 times 2025 annual revenue of KRW 113.1 billion.

Reports also say the company handled engine room units for 127 of the 315 ships built by Korea's three large shipyards from 2023 through 2025, which points to depth in its customer base. This supports revenue visibility for as long as the shipbuilding cycle continues.

Improving profitability

The operating margin rose from 2.7% in 2023 to 9.9% in 2024 and 10.9% in 2025. Press reports attribute the improvement to cost control and selective acceptance of higher-value orders. The trend is consistent with a reduction in low-margin order volume.

New businesses and capacity expansion

The company is pursuing new businesses in data center power equipment, nuclear and SMR, fuel cells, and hydrogen facilities, and has described opportunities in North American power infrastructure and European nuclear and SMR markets.

About KRW 20 billion of the roughly KRW 31.5 billion IPO proceeds is earmarked for an integrated plant site in Geoje and Goseong to expand capacity. The planned Bulgarian subsidiary was announced as a base for supplying key equipment within Europe.

06

Bear factors

Revenue growth, slower profit growth

Cumulative third-quarter 2026 revenue rose 38.1% year on year, but operating profit grew only 1.7%. Net income of KRW 900 million fell 63.2% year on year, so revenue growth did not carry through to the bottom line.

Analysis of 2025 also found that the net income improvement came mainly from a tax benefit rather than from expansion of core operating profit.

Customer and project concentration

In 2025, two customers that each accounted for more than 10% of sales generated about KRW 73.4 billion, and three such customers together made up more than 70% of revenue. If vessel schedules slip, gaps in block volumes and the timing of percentage-of-completion revenue recognition can make results volatile.

Revenue fell from KRW 140.3 billion in 2024 to KRW 113.1 billion in 2025, which press reports attribute partly to the reduction of low-margin orders and gaps in block volumes.

Post-listing supply and balance sheet

Since listing, the share price has traded below the IPO price of KRW 17,000, and commentary notes that limited free float in the early period of trading can amplify volatility. The debt-to-equity ratio fell to 242% in June 2026 but remains high, and the quick ratio stands at 98.38%.

The plant segment's 2025 revenue of KRW 24.4 billion and operating profit of KRW 400 million reflect weak profitability in that business.

07

Risk factors

Macro

On November 2, 2026, when the domestic market opened weaker after a sell-off in US technology stocks, Youngkwang drew early buying interest, showing its share movements have at times diverged from the broader market.

Macro variables such as interest rates and exchange rates can indirectly affect shipbuilding order sentiment and overseas customer demand. This risk is more about market-wide conditions than company-specific factors.

Industry

Shipbuilding and plant revenue fluctuates by quarter depending on the ordering cycle and vessel construction schedules. CEO Hur Cheol-ki noted that working days decline in the third and fourth quarters. Because customer concentration is high, changes in orders from a single customer can have a large effect on results.

Listing and liquidity

The share price fell below the IPO price within days of listing, with wide intraday swings. Foreign ownership exhaustion was reported at 0.85% as of November 9, 2026. Free float and trading supply remain sources of volatility.

08

Overall view

Youngkwang is a shipbuilding module specialist whose strengths include an operating margin that rose from 2.7% in 2023 to 10.9% in 2025 and an order backlog of KRW 202.2 billion at the end of the first half of 2026.

On the other hand, cumulative third-quarter 2026 revenue rose 38.1%, while operating profit grew only 1.7% and net income fell 63.2%. Customer concentration, low profitability in the plant segment, and a still-high debt ratio remain weaknesses in the business and balance sheet.

New businesses in data centers, SMR, fuel cells, and hydrogen are expanding but have not yet produced visible revenue, and a reported order of about KRW 30 billion has not been confirmed in disclosures.

The share price surged on the first trading day, then fell sharply, and stood at KRW 6,780 on November 18, 2026, well below the IPO price of KRW 17,000. Press data from November 9, 2026 put the PER at 6.28 times versus 7.68 times for the sector, but the multiple changes with the price, so readings differ by date.

The items to monitor are full-year results in the 2026 annual report, progress on the Bulgarian subsidiary and new-business contracts, and changes in free float after the listing.
