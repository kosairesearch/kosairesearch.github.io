Youngkwang's Debut: Over 2x Intraday, +26% at the Close

02

Business structure

Youngkwang began in 1962 as Youngkwang Machine Works and designs and builds large equipment and modules for the shipbuilding, offshore and plant industries. The company was incorporated on June 16, 1999, and is headquartered in Ulju-gun, Ulsan.

In shipbuilding and offshore, it supplies engine room units, hull blocks and fuel gas supply systems. In plant equipment, it supplies items such as pressure vessels and heat exchangers. Its plant products also include chemical process equipment and skids.

Its integrated production system keeps piping, steel structure, welding, instrumentation and assembly in-house. In LNG fuel gas supply systems, it covers design, fabrication, assembly, onboard installation and commissioning, and it supplies the system as a package.

The business runs on a made-to-order basis rather than mass production. Shipbuilding units follow the construction schedules of major domestic shipyards, while plant equipment is built to the specifications of global EPC contractors.

The company is expanding into ultra-high-voltage transformer enclosures, data center power equipment, nuclear and small modular reactor (SMR) components, and hydrogen and carbon capture equipment.

Paid-in capital is KRW 4.528 billion and the company has 192 employees. After the offering, three major shareholders including CEO Heo Cheol-ki (31.36%) hold 61.88% of issued shares. No official revenue breakdown by segment was confirmed in this research, so the segment mix is described only qualitatively.

The competitive picture covers other domestic and overseas ship equipment and plant module makers, and it is also shaped by the industry structure in which Chinese yards take a growing share of orders. According to a report citing Clarksons Research data, Chinese yards captured 75% of global newbuilding orders in 2026.

Recent trends

On a standalone basis, Youngkwang's 2025 revenue was KRW 113.133 billion, operating profit KRW 12.296 billion and net profit KRW 10.327 billion. The reported 2025 operating margin was about 10.9%. In the first half of 2026, revenue was KRW 59.1 billion and operating profit KRW 5.4 billion.

Net profit for the same period was KRW 3.8 billion. First-half operating profit was below half of the full-year 2025 figure, so any full-year margin comparison should account for seasonality and the timing of revenue recognition.

The order backlog rose from KRW 196.3 billion at the end of 2025 to KRW 202.2 billion at the end of the first half of 2026. That backlog is well above full-year 2025 revenue. Institutional book-building drew a 1000.07-to-1 ratio, and the offer price was fixed at KRW 17,000, the top of the range. The offering comprised 2.26 million shares and raised KRW 38.42 billion.

On November 2, 2026, the stock opened at KRW 21,350, 25.59% above the offer price. It rose intraday to KRW 37,400, more than double the offer price. It closed at KRW 21,500, up 26.47% from the offer price. Based on the latest market data, market capitalization is KRW 194.7 billion and trading value is KRW 1.2 trillion. The expected market capitalization at the offer price was about KRW 153.9 billion.

Outlook

The company plans to use the IPO proceeds for consolidating production sites, expanding facilities and R&D. These include securing an integrated production base in Geoje and Goseong, building painting and drying facilities for transformer enclosures, and introducing laser welding equipment.

It also plans to build a production base in Bulgaria and expand demonstration infrastructure for eco-friendly fuel supply equipment to better serve overseas customers.

If the investments proceed as planned, production efficiency and operating margin could improve, but timing and results have not yet been confirmed in filings.

Reports state that global newbuilding contracts in 2026 reached USD 205.1 billion, above the previous record. NH Investment & Securities in May 2026 raised its estimate for the combined annual orders of large Korean shipbuilders from USD 45.8 billion to USD 49.9 billion.

According to a Shinyoung Securities report, the newbuilding price index stood at 187.20 as of October 30. Because Youngkwang is a supplier to shipyards, the lag and magnitude with which such conditions reach its earnings depend on individual contracts.

Customers' capital spending, vessel construction schedules and project progress are cited as key variables for future results. Based on Clarksons data, Korean yards hold 59% of LNG carrier orders and 65% of very large ammonia and LPG carrier orders, defending their lead in gas carriers.

On the other hand, Chinese steel plate price talks and partial strikes at domestic shipyards were reported as burdens on costs and production schedules.

New areas such as nuclear and SMR components and data center power equipment are the direction the company has stated, but no confirmed figure exists for their revenue contribution.

Market assessment may shift as early post-listing trading flows settle and quarterly results are released, so the first quarterly report and order disclosures are the points to check.

Integrated production and business expansion

The company says that performing design, fabrication and assembly in-house has shortened production time and secured cost competitiveness.

Based on technology for high-temperature, high-pressure and cryogenic conditions and on international certifications such as ASME and PED, it is expanding into nuclear and SMR components and data center power equipment. The IPO proceeds are planned for an integrated production base and automation equipment. If realized, those investments leave room for better production efficiency.
