IT-Chem: Losses, Plant Build-Out and Audit Overhang

02

Business structure

IT-Chem is a fine chemicals company founded in 2005 that develops and manufactures active pharmaceutical ingredients (APIs), intermediates and OLED materials on a contract development and manufacturing (CDMO) basis, built on its synthesis technology. The company listed on KOSDAQ on August 7, 2025.

According to briefing material from July 2025, pharmaceutical ingredients accounted for 61% of 2024 standalone revenue of KRW 48.8 billion (KRW 29.8 billion), while display and electronic materials made up 39% (KRW 19 billion).

A JobKorea company profile (as of March 2026) classifies sales differently, as pharmaceutical intermediates 36%, OLED 22%, trading goods 20%, APIs 16% and other 6%, so the two breakdowns are not directly comparable. The main product is an intermediate for a diabetes drug, built on production experience with BCBB.

The company says it received technology transfer for oligopeptide and oligonucleotide APIs and succeeded in joint process development and mass production.

On the display side it produces OLED materials and heavy water (D2O), and it expects its domestic heavy-water recycling technology to replace about 22% of domestic demand. Its customers include several pharmaceutical companies and four first-tier vendors of domestic display materials.

In pharmaceuticals it is a mid-sized player with much smaller capacity than large CDMOs such as Samsung Biologics, while in display materials it competes against larger materials suppliers for supply positions.

Its Goesan plant in Chungbuk has a completed electronic materials building, and a pharmaceutical building is planned.

04

Earnings analysis

On a DART-disclosed annual basis, revenue rose from KRW 49.1 billion in 2024 to KRW 62 billion in 2025, up 26.2% year on year. Over the same period operating profit of KRW 3.59 billion turned into an operating loss of KRW 1.85 billion, and the operating margin fell from 7.3% to -3.0%.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders moved from KRW 11.5 billion to a net loss of KRW 14.3 billion. Net income including non-controlling interests went from KRW 11.4 billion to a net loss of KRW 14.3 billion, and the two measures should be read separately.

The company attributed the 2025 swing to higher order intake in pharmaceutical and display materials, which lifted sales, and to higher fixed and depreciation costs from the new plant along with losses from affiliates. Revenue grew but profitability fell because build-out costs were recognized ahead of output.

The balance sheet also shifted. At the end of 2025 total liabilities were KRW 115.7 billion and total equity was KRW 65.3 billion, all of it attributable to controlling shareholders. Operating cash flow was -KRW 2.13 billion in 2024 and -KRW 1.75 billion in 2025, an outflow in both years.

Quarterly, the third quarter of 2025 showed sales of KRW 27.2 billion and an operating loss of KRW 1.21 billion, and the fourth quarter of 2025 returned to operating profit of KRW 169 million. The first quarter of 2026 recorded sales of KRW 12.7 billion and an operating loss of KRW 1.72 billion.

The second quarter reached sales of KRW 15.1 billion, up 18.8% from the prior quarter, with an operating loss of KRW 2.77 billion.

The third quarter of 2026 brought sales of KRW 7.8 billion, down 48.3% from the prior quarter and 71.3% year on year, and an operating loss of KRW 3.16 billion, extending the losses to three consecutive quarters.

Net losses attributable to controlling shareholders were KRW 3.57 billion in the third quarter of 2025, KRW 8.4 billion in the fourth quarter, KRW 4.3 billion in the first quarter of 2026, KRW 1.46 billion in the second quarter and KRW 6.72 billion in the third quarter, totaling KRW 20.9 billion over the last four quarters.

The fourth-quarter 2025 gap between operating profit and a larger net loss points to below-the-operating-line items, and the detail should be checked in the next regular report.

05

Industry analysis

IT-Chem serves two demand pools, pharmaceuticals and displays. In pharmaceuticals, demand for oral small-molecule peptide therapeutics and high-value APIs is rising alongside the expansion of obesity and diabetes treatments, according to a February 2026 report by NewsTopKorea.

The company is building a dedicated GMP plant for next-generation drugs to align with that trend. In displays, the company positions its OLED materials as key inputs that improve luminous efficiency and lifetime.

According to a June 2025 Hankyung report, OLED materials produced first revenue of KRW 5.4 billion in 2024, and the company projected KRW 10 billion for 2025.

The display business counts four first-tier domestic vendors as customers, and the company cites an early investment from the venture arm of a leading global OLED materials maker as evidence of its technology. Localizing heavy-water recycling is presented as a way to reduce reliance on imports.

Against that, the pharmaceutical side is smaller than large CDMOs in capacity and track record, which can weigh on large contract competitions. A limited customer base in display materials means that changes in a single customer's orders can have an outsized effect on revenue.

Across the cycle, OLED materials track display investment, while APIs track pharmaceutical production schedules.

06

Outlook

The baseline for the company's outlook is the figures presented at listing. According to a June 2025 report, the company projected 2025 revenue of KRW 77 billion and operating profit of KRW 8.5 billion, while the DART-disclosed results were revenue of KRW 62 billion and an operating loss of KRW 1.85 billion.

Fixed costs recognized before the new capacity was running are cited as a factor behind the gap. As of a July 2025 investor briefing, the build-out schedule targets completion of construction in 2026, followed by commissioning and full operation of the plant in 2027.

The main BCBB production line is planned to rise from 12 units to 25, and capacity from 126,720 kg to 346,368 kg.

The third-quarter 2025 report shows KRW 38.1 billion of facility investment in progress and a planned KRW 50 billion of further investment, including KRW 20 billion for building construction and KRW 30 billion for pharmaceutical production equipment.

The KRW 40 billion convertible bond issued in February 2026 is earmarked entirely for facilities, with KRW 20 billion each to be spent from 2026 through 2027. The bond includes a call option on up to 30% of the shares from 12 months after issuance, which the structure is described as a way to manage dilution.

On the audit timetable, the company said it expects to complete the 2026 fiscal-year audit around the end of March 2027. After the improvement period ends in April 2027, the Korea Exchange will decide whether the listing can be maintained, and the timing of any resumption of trading will follow that process.

08

Bull factors

Demand for pharmaceutical intermediates

Reports point to rising demand for oral small-molecule peptide therapeutics and high-value APIs as obesity and diabetes treatments expand. Disclosed 2025 revenue rose 26.2% year on year to KRW 62 billion. The fourth quarter of 2025 returned to operating profit of KRW 169 million.

Pharmaceutical intermediates and APIs together form a large share of revenue, which is the base that would benefit from that demand.

Capacity expansion if the plant ramps

As of the July 2025 investor briefing, the main BCBB production line was planned to rise from 12 units to 25, lifting capacity from 126,720 kg to 346,368 kg. The company said it expects high utilization at completion. Its argument is that once the plant runs, fixed costs can be spread over higher output. Full operation is targeted for 2027.

Funding and strategic partners

In the KRW 40 billion convertible bond issued in February 2026, Timefolio Asset Management committed KRW 25 billion as anchor investor, Life Asset Management invested KRW 5 billion, and HY24, a French hydrogen infrastructure fund manager, put in KRW 10 billion as a strategic investor.

HY24 said the investment marks the start of a heavy-water and hydrogen infrastructure partnership with the company. Because the full amount goes to facilities, part of the plant funding is in place.

Bear factors

Falling sales and wider losses

Third-quarter 2026 revenue of KRW 7.8 billion fell 48.3% from the prior quarter and 71.3% year on year. The operating loss of KRW 3.16 billion extended a run of losses that started in the first quarter of 2026. The net loss attributable to controlling shareholders widened to KRW 6.72 billion.

Across the last four quarters the total net loss attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 20.9 billion. Fixed costs remain in place while revenue has declined.

Higher leverage and operating cash outflows

The debt-to-equity ratio rose from 134.3% in 2024 to 177.3% in 2025. Total liabilities at the end of 2025 were KRW 115.7 billion, above total equity of KRW 65.3 billion. Operating cash flow was -KRW 2.13 billion in 2024 and -KRW 1.75 billion in 2025.

While the company is loss-making and not generating operating cash, reliance on borrowing and convertible bonds rises.

IPO proceeds used up and thin cash

A News Tomato report said cash and cash equivalents of KRW 6.3 billion and short-term financial instruments of KRW 8.1 billion, about KRW 14.4 billion in total, were held at the end of the third quarter of 2025.

The third-quarter report listed KRW 38.1 billion of facility investment in progress and a planned KRW 50 billion of further investment. The same report said KRW 27.4 billion of the KRW 33.2 billion IPO proceeds had been used. Cash on hand is smaller than the planned investment.

Risk factors

Regulation and listing status

A disclaimer of opinion on the 2025 audit triggered delisting criteria, and after an objection the Korea Exchange granted an improvement period through April 10, 2027. The company expects to complete the 2026 audit around the end of March 2027.

Its half-year report filed on August 28, 2026 also carried an auditor's review disclaimer. The clean voluntary-audit opinion the company announced is separate from the exchange's decision. The listing decision after the improvement period will determine when trading can resume and the company's listing status.

Financing and share dilution

The KRW 40 billion convertible bond issued in February 2026 is scheduled to be spent at KRW 20 billion a year over 2026 and 2027, and it includes a call option on up to 30% of the shares from 12 months after issuance. If conversion or the call option is exercised, existing shareholders' stakes could be diluted.

The share count and capital structure will depend on whether further financing is raised beyond the bond. The timing and form of any further raise have not been determined.

Build-out execution and GMP approval

The pharmaceutical building is still to be built, with a target of full operation in 2027 after construction is finished in 2026. Production of APIs for commercial sale depends on GMP certification. Delays in design, permits or equipment installation would extend the period of fixed-cost burden.

If utilization falls short of target, the time needed to recover the build-out investment through sales would also lengthen.

Overall view

IT-Chem's 2025 revenue rose 26.2% year on year, but the swing to an operating loss and a net loss attributable to controlling shareholders sharply weakened profitability. Losses have continued for three consecutive quarters through the third quarter of 2026, and third-quarter revenue fell to KRW 7.8 billion.

Strengths include demand for pharmaceutical intermediates and OLED materials, a larger capacity base if the Goesan plant ramps, and strategic investors. Weaknesses include a debt-to-equity ratio of 177.3%, negative operating cash flow, IPO proceeds largely spent, and a disclaimer of opinion with trading suspension.

The points to track are the 2026 audit outcome, the listing decision after April 2027, the start of operations at the pharmaceutical building, and the deployment of the convertible bond proceeds. How these factors feed into results and the timing of trading resumption will determine the company's direction.
