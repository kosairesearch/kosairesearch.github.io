02

Business structure

AUTO& (353590) is a mobility lifestyle platform company that supplies products and services centered on the automotive aftermarket. The company is focused on its mobility lifestyle platform business, which offers products and services to people using mobility spaces.

Its core is the product and service platform segment, where it is strengthening an aftermarket-centered portfolio. The structure relies on operating official membership malls for Hyundai Motor and Kia, where it develops and supplies genuine-style optional parts and accessories ahead of new model launches.

On the product side, a standard 100W charging cable began supply for some Hyundai and Kia models in January 2026, and a smart info panel linked to the vehicle OS launched at Kia Shop in April 2026. These products are sold through the SPACE ZONE platform, which began generating sales in 2025.

Domestic sales made up at least 97% of each main revenue category as of December 2025. R&D spending ran at roughly 4% of sales from 2022 to 2025. The company positions its customization products as a barrier to entry against competitors.

Unlike large auto parts makers that depend on OEM supply contracts, AUTO& sits closer to an aftermarket channel tied to automaker brand memberships. Its revenue is therefore tied to new-model cycles and domestic auto sales.

In August 2026, it was selected as the lead operator of the 2026 AX One-Stop Voucher program run by the Ministry of Science and ICT.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual sales rose from KRW 47.8 billion in 2022 to KRW 60.4 billion in 2023, up 26.2% year on year, then fell 5.2% to KRW 57.2 billion in 2024 before recovering 3.3% to KRW 59.1 billion in 2025. Within 2022 to 2025, 2023 was the largest year and 2022 the smallest.

Operating results moved from an operating loss of KRW 3.81 billion in 2022 and KRW 2.8 billion in 2023, which narrowed the loss, to an operating loss of KRW 3.3 billion in 2024, which widened it, and then to operating profit of KRW 752 million in 2025, a swing to profit.

The operating margin improved from -8.0% in 2022, -4.6% in 2023 and -5.8% in 2024 to 1.3% in 2025. The company attributed the turn to profit to efficiency measures focused on profitability and cost reduction.

Yet the controlling-shareholder net loss was KRW 2.41 billion in 2022, KRW 1.12 billion in 2023, KRW 5.49 billion in 2024 and KRW 5.19 billion in 2025, so losses continued in 2025.

Operating profit turning positive while the net loss remained means losses persisted below the operating line, and the line items are for the full periodic report to clarify. Operating cash flow turned to KRW -366 million in 2025 from KRW 3.07 billion in 2024.

Quarterly, sales were KRW 14.7 billion with an operating loss of KRW 347 million in the third quarter of 2025, then KRW 15 billion with operating profit of KRW 1.14 billion in the fourth quarter, a swing to profit.

The first quarter of 2026 reverted to an operating loss of KRW 1.33 billion on sales of KRW 11.1 billion, down 26.1% from the prior quarter.

The second quarter of 2026 showed sales of KRW 13.3 billion and an operating loss of KRW 1.11 billion, and the third quarter showed sales of KRW 11.2 billion, down 23.3% year on year, with an operating loss of KRW 866 million. The loss narrowed but has continued for three straight quarters.

The controlling-shareholder net loss has persisted for five consecutive quarters from the third quarter of 2025 through the third quarter of 2026, totaling KRW 5.95 billion over the last four quarters.

05

Industry analysis

Demand in the aftermarket is shaped by new-model launch cycles and automaker sales trends. The company is pursuing a shift toward a mobility platform business built on software-defined vehicles (SDV).

A 2024 report described plans to develop in-vehicle electronics that collect customer or vehicle data, along with an integrated application to manage that data, with automakers. The business structure has also been described as sensitive to Hyundai Motor Group's direction.

The official membership channel is a strength, but it also means exposure to automakers' sales strategies and accessory policies. R&D spending held at roughly 4% of sales, indicating steady investment in new product development.

Large listed auto parts makers such as Hyundai Mobis, Hanon Systems and HL Mando operate with OEM supply models, so their customers and profit models differ from AUTO&'s. With domestic sales making up at least 97% of main revenue categories, the company is highly sensitive to domestic automaker sales.

In the new business areas, in-vehicle control systems and software appear to be at an early stage of revenue scale. The sector outlook depends on new-car sales trends and changes in automakers' accessory policies.

In competition, the official channel serves as a barrier to entry, while the speed of monetizing new businesses will determine differentiation.

The first half of 2026 combined declining sales with continued operating losses. Third-quarter 2026 sales of KRW 11.2 billion were down 23.3% from a year earlier, and the operating loss widened versus the same period last year.

Positives include the operating profit in the fourth quarter of 2025 and a narrowing of losses since the second quarter of 2026. The next checkpoint is whether the operating margin stays positive in the 2026 annual report. Visibility on the scale and timing of profit from new businesses also remains open.

The mobility data platform plan depends heavily on collaboration with automakers, so progress there is a key variable.

Automaker-linked channel and new products

The company operates official membership platforms for Hyundai and Kia, giving it a supply structure geared to new-model launches. The January 2026 100W charging cable and the April 2026 smart info panel at Kia Shop broadened the product lineup.

The company has also said it plans to expand the SPACE ZONE platform into in-vehicle control and software areas.

Third-quarter 2026 sales of KRW 11.2 billion were down 23.3% from a year earlier. The third-quarter operating loss widened compared with the same period last year.

Rising financial burden

The debt ratio rose from 84.7% in 2022 to 194.7% in 2025, with total liabilities of KRW 36.2 billion exceeding total equity of KRW 18.6 billion. The current ratio declined from 149.9% in 2023 to 144.4% in 2024 and 127% in 2025. Operating cash flow turned to KRW -366 million in 2025.

Automaker dependence and new-business uncertainty

The business is sensitive to changes in Hyundai Motor Group's strategy, which is a bearish factor. The mobility data platform depends on collaboration with automakers, so its timing and monetization remain uncertain. One assessment noted that declining sales and wider operating losses have weakened results.

Risk factors

Automaker group dependence

Revenue centers on official membership malls and new-model accessories, so sales could shift with automaker sales strategies, accessory policies and launch schedules. Third-quarter 2026 sales down 23.3% year on year may partly reflect this structure. The pace at which new businesses reduce channel dependence will determine how this risk plays out.

Financial structure and cash flow

A debt ratio of 194.7% and total liabilities of KRW 36.2 billion point to a heavy borrowing burden relative to equity. The current ratio falling to 127% in 2025 makes short-term liquidity a point to monitor.

Operating cash flow of KRW -366 million in 2025 shows a weak link between the operating turn to profit and cash generation.

Timing of new-business monetization

The company has said it plans to expand SPACE ZONE into in-vehicle control and software, but its revenue scale cannot yet be confirmed from disclosures. The mobility data platform plan announced in a 2024 report also depends on automaker collaboration.

The addition of high-pressure gas sales and comprehensive travel to the business purpose signals broader scope, but their profitability has not yet appeared.
