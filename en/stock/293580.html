Third-Quarter Loss Puts Capital Plan in Focus

02

Business structure

NAU IB Capital is a venture capital firm operating under the new technology business finance license, earning management fees and investment returns by forming and managing funds that invest in companies.

According to Alphasquare, operating revenue from management fees and equity-method income accounts for 72.04% of the revenue mix, while other operating income such as interest and advisory fees makes up 27.96%.

Management fees are charged on committed capital in the early stage of a partnership and on average invested balance afterward, so the size of AUM largely sets the earnings base. The company's 2026 value-up plan puts AUM at KRW 2.07 trillion across 35 funds, about double the KRW 1 trillion level in 2022.

Its core areas are parts, materials and equipment, M&A, agri-food and venture and SME investment, and it has been described as strong in M&A and buyouts. The Busan Innovation Climate Tech Fund 1, formed in May 2026, is listed at KRW 60 billion.

Following the free capital reduction, paid-in capital fell from KRW 47.9 billion to KRW 12.3 billion, and the company was re-registered with the Financial Services Commission as a specialized new technology business finance company, a shift reported as reducing lending activity and concentrating resources on investment and fund management.

Shareholders classified as largest shareholder and related parties hold 65.11% as of October 19, 2026, and the free float ratio is 34.89%. Among 17 venture capital companies in the same industry group, its market capitalization and revenue rank sixth on 2025 annual figures.

04

Earnings analysis

On disclosed annual figures, revenue rose from KRW 22.3 billion in 2022 to KRW 26.7 billion in 2023 (up 19.5% year on year) and KRW 31.4 billion in 2024 (up 17.7%), then fell to KRW 29.2 billion in 2025 (down 7.2%). Among 2022 to 2025, 2024 was the largest year for revenue and 2022 was the smallest.

Operating profit fell from KRW 11.1 billion in 2022 to KRW 2.46 billion in 2023 (down 77.9%), recovered to KRW 9.01 billion in 2024 (up 267.0%), and reached KRW 7.3 billion in 2025 (down 19.0%). The operating margin was 49.7% in 2022, 9.2% in 2023, 28.7% in 2024 and 25.0% in 2025.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 8.3 billion in 2022, KRW 1.94 billion in 2023, KRW 7.93 billion in 2024 and KRW 5.78 billion in 2025.

Quarterly, the third quarter of 2025 showed revenue of KRW 10.1 billion, operating profit of KRW 6.62 billion and net income attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 5.24 billion.

The fourth quarter of 2025 swung to revenue of KRW 6.67 billion (down 34.2% from the prior quarter), an operating loss of KRW 5.46 billion and a net loss attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 4.43 billion.

The first quarter of 2026 returned to operating profit of KRW 312 million, and the second quarter rose to KRW 5.06 billion (up 1,521.1% from the prior quarter).

The third quarter of 2026 reported revenue of KRW 4.34 billion (down 50.9% from the prior quarter and down 57.2% year on year), an operating loss of KRW 2.36 billion and a net loss attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 1.87 billion.

The net loss attributable to controlling shareholders over the last four quarters totals KRW 2.05 billion.

Reporting on the third-quarter filing submitted on November 16, 2026, the press attributed the swing to a loss mainly from investment-related results, with equity-method net income moving from a profit of KRW 1.01 billion a year earlier to a loss of KRW 3.71 billion.

At year-end 2025, total equity was KRW 104.5 billion, of which KRW 90.1 billion belonged to controlling shareholders, total liabilities were KRW 65.1 billion, and the debt-to-equity ratio was 62.3%.

Operating cash flow was KRW -10.5 billion in 2022, KRW 9.51 billion in 2023, KRW 138 million in 2024 and KRW -14.6 billion in 2025.

05

Industry analysis

Revenue at NAU IB Capital depends heavily on the size of new fund formations and selection in government-backed co-investment programs.

Reports say the company was chosen as the M&A-track general partner of a fund supporting the recovery of the secondary market run by the Korea Development Bank, securing KRW 18 billion. Reports also say it passed document screening for a regional innovation venture fund co-investment program as part of a consortium.

The inheritance-related M&A segment, created by a new government framework, is cited as another area of demand for co-investment. On Alphasquare's 2025 annual data, its operating margin of 25.04% sits below the venture capital industry average of 34.42% and ranks 13th within the group.

Over the same data set, its debt-to-equity ratio of 62.31% is above the industry average of 33.51%, and its net margin of 19.84% trails the average of 29.20%. The gap in profitability is consistent with a management-fee-based revenue structure combined with volatile investment gains.

Industry-wide sector data for the current cycle was not compiled here, so the comparison is limited to the company's own disclosures and the single data set above.

On 2025 annual figures, its market capitalization and revenue rank in the upper group among 17 venture capital companies, while profitability and dividend yield sit below the group average.

06

Outlook

The value-up plan disclosed on November 16, 2026 covers 2027 through 2028. Its core elements are a 4-for-1 share consolidation to secure an appropriate free float and maintaining a shareholder return ratio of at least 30% based on net income.

The company disclosed that the number of issued shares would fall from about 94.93 million to about 23.73 million after the capital reduction.

Roughly KRW 35.6 billion of capital reduction gains will be booked to capital surplus, and the company plans to transfer them to retained earnings and prioritize them as a dividend source from 2027. This proposal is on the agenda for the second extraordinary shareholders' meeting on December 29, 2026.

On the dividend record, the 2025 payout ratio was 36.1% and the dividend amount was KRW 2.08 billion, up 10.2% from 2024. The company also plans to expand regular investor briefings and strengthen publicity toward major institutional investors and the press.

The company expects management fees to rise to about KRW 18 billion in 2026, which is its own estimate and will be confirmed only in the 2026 annual report. Whether the equity-method losses behind the third-quarter swing continue into later quarters cannot be determined from the disclosures available.

The next items to confirm are therefore whether the shareholder return plan is carried out and whether the growth in management fees reaches operating profit.

08

Bull factors

Expanding management fee base

According to the value-up plan, AUM stands at KRW 2.07 trillion across 35 funds, roughly double the KRW 1 trillion level in 2022. The company estimates management fees will rise from KRW 10.5 billion in 2022 to about KRW 18 billion in 2026. It describes this as a fixed-income base independent of market volatility. This is the company's own estimate, not a confirmed result.

Shareholder return framework

The company's plan targets a shareholder return ratio of at least 30% based on net income through 2028. It intends to move capital reduction gains into retained earnings and use them as a priority dividend source from 2027. The 2025 payout ratio was 36.1%, and the dividend amount rose 10.2% from 2024 to KRW 2.08 billion.

Capital restructuring and earnings recovery record

Paid-in capital is set to fall from KRW 47.9 billion to KRW 12.3 billion, with about KRW 35.6 billion booked as capital surplus. Because the reduction is free, cash does not leave the company and total equity does not drop directly.

Operating profit also recovered from KRW 2.46 billion in 2023 to KRW 9.01 billion in 2024, an increase of 267.0%.

09

Bear factors

Volatility in investment-related gains

The third quarter of 2026 posted an operating loss of KRW 2.36 billion and a net loss attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 1.87 billion. The press pointed to equity-method results swinging from a profit to a loss as the main cause.

The net loss attributable to controlling shareholders over the last four quarters totals KRW 2.05 billion. Investment results can offset gains from a growing management fee base.

Lower revenue and profit, weaker cash flow

Revenue in 2025 fell 7.2% to KRW 29.2 billion, and operating profit fell 19.0% to KRW 7.3 billion. Operating cash flow was negative in both 2022 (KRW -10.5 billion) and 2025 (KRW -14.6 billion). The debt-to-equity ratio rose from 50.2% in 2024 to 62.3% in 2025.

Ownership structure and trading liquidity

Shareholders classified as largest shareholder and related parties hold 65.11%, leaving a free float of 34.89% as of October 19, 2026. Press reports from July 2026 said the company acquired a 2.5% stake in a pharmaceutical group affiliate, and the ownership reshuffle was cited as a source of uncertainty. The reduced share count after the capital reduction also changes the trading structure.

Risk factors

Earnings volatility

Quarterly losses may recur depending on investment results and fund valuations. Net losses attributable to controlling shareholders appeared in both the fourth quarter of 2025 and the third quarter of 2026. A drop in operating revenue or larger valuation losses could offset the benefit of a growing fee base.

Policy execution risk

The value-up plan targets a shareholder return ratio of at least 30% through 2028, which may be hard to meet if profits fall. Moving capital reduction gains into retained earnings requires approval at the extraordinary shareholders' meeting on December 29, 2026.

Changes in capital requirements or operating rules for specialized new technology business finance companies could also affect the business.

Governance and liquidity

With 65.11% held by the largest shareholder and related parties and a free float of 34.89%, trading liquidity may be limited. Foreign ownership is also reported at a low level. Transactions involving major shareholders, such as stake acquisitions, can affect the share price, so ownership disclosures warrant attention.

12

Overall view

NAU IB Capital has built a structure in which AUM has grown to KRW 2.07 trillion and management fees are estimated by the company to rise from KRW 10.5 billion in 2022 to about KRW 18 billion in 2026. Operating profit recovered to KRW 9.01 billion in 2024.

On the other hand, revenue and operating profit fell in 2025, and in the third quarter of 2026 equity-method results deteriorated, producing an operating loss of KRW 2.36 billion and a net loss attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 1.87 billion.

The net loss attributable to controlling shareholders over the last four quarters totals KRW 2.05 billion, so no earnings-based price-to-earnings ratio is calculated.

The capital structure is being reshaped by a 4-for-1 free capital reduction that cuts paid-in capital to KRW 12.3 billion and books the gains to capital surplus. A plan to keep the shareholder return ratio at 30% or higher on net income through 2028 has also been presented.

Strengths include the management fee base and the shareholder return plan, while weaknesses include investment gain volatility, negative operating cash flow and limited liquidity from the high largest-shareholder stake.

The next items to confirm are the outcome of the December 29, 2026 extraordinary meeting, the full-year operating margin in the 2026 annual report, and the equity-method trend in the first quarter of 2027.
