02

Business structure

Hwashin is a KOSPI-listed auto parts maker specializing in chassis and body parts, with Hyundai Motor Group affiliates as its core customers. Samsung Securities assessed that Hwashin holds a 57% share of chassis parts sourced within the Hyundai Motor Group, making it the top supplier in that segment.

Its main products include Member-type and Arm-type parts, CTBA, modules, fuel tanks and panels, supplied either as modules or individual components.

Production is organized as modules or individual parts, with overseas subsidiaries operating in the United States and China among other key locations to maintain a global manufacturing footprint.

The company recently established a new subsidiary in Pune, India to expand production capacity, and completed a dedicated EV plant in Georgia, USA that is phasing in chassis part deliveries.

Building on its existing chassis and body technology, Hwashin has entered EV battery-case production, competing for orders against Donghee Auto, Sungwoo Hitech and Webasto.

Within the broader Hyundai Motor Group supply network, Hyundai Mobis, HL Mando, SL, Hyundai WIA, Sungwoo Hitech and Myoung Shin Industry operate in overlapping or complementary chassis, body and module part segments.

More recently, the possibility of entering robot body-module production based on its chassis design and materials technology has been raised as a further avenue for business expansion.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual revenue rose from KRW 1.69 trillion in 2022 to KRW 1.8 trillion in 2023 (up 6.7%), fell to KRW 1.71 trillion in 2024 (down 5.0%), then rose to KRW 1.96 trillion in 2025 (up 14.6%), the largest figure among 2022-2025.

Operating profit declined from KRW 87.4 billion in 2022 to KRW 83.7 billion in 2023 (down 4.2%) and KRW 65.3 billion in 2024 (down 22.0%), then surged to KRW 102.4 billion in 2025 (up 56.9%), the largest of the four years, with 2024 the smallest.

Net profit attributable to controlling interests rose from KRW 74.2 billion in 2022 to KRW 77.3 billion in 2023 (up 4.1%), fell sharply to KRW 53.6 billion in 2024 (down 30.7%), then recovered to KRW 66.7 billion in 2025 (up 24.5%), with 2023 the largest and 2024 the smallest.

The operating margin slipped from 5.2% in 2022 to 4.6% in 2023 and 3.8% in 2024, before rebounding to 5.2% in 2025, back to the 2022 level.

On a quarterly basis, revenue of KRW 512.4 billion and operating profit of KRW 33.9 billion peaked in 4Q25, before falling for two consecutive quarters in 1Q26 (revenue KRW 505.6 billion, operating profit KRW 17 billion) and 2Q26 (revenue KRW 498.4 billion, operating profit KRW 5.8 billion). 2Q26 operating profit fell 78.1% year on year and 65.8% quarter on quarter, a notable profitability decline.

Net profit attributable to controlling interests was just KRW 250 million in 2Q25, then rose to KRW 18 billion in 3Q25 (up 7,086.4% quarter on quarter), KRW 19.4 billion in 4Q25 and KRW 28.3 billion in 1Q26, before falling to KRW 7.03 billion in 2Q26 (down 75.2% quarter on quarter, though up 2,712.4% year on year).

The sum of attributable net profit over the last four quarters (3Q25-2Q26) was KRW 72.7 billion. 2025 operating cash flow fell sharply to KRW 42 billion from KRW 136.9 billion in 2024, and the debt-to-equity ratio rose to 172.9% from 155.4% a year earlier.

05

Industry analysis

In the first half of 2026, KIA sold roughly 1.63 million units globally, a record first-half figure, while Hyundai Motor sold about 1.97 million units, falling below 2 million in a first half for the first time in four years.

As a result, Hyundai Mobis saw the KIA-related share of its first-half consolidated revenue (38.2%) exceed the Hyundai Motor-related share (36.3%) for the first time since such disclosure began.

Hyundai WIA's KIA-related revenue rose 15.0% to about KRW 2.15 trillion in the first half, while its Hyundai Motor-related revenue fell 18.2% to about KRW 353.9 billion, showing how customer-level production gaps flow directly into supplier revenue.

Such shifts in demand allocation within the customer base affect the Hyundai Motor Group supply chain broadly, suggesting Hwashin's quarterly revenue can also swing with new-model launches and plant ramp-up timing.

In 2025, many domestic auto parts makers posted top-line growth; Hwashin's consolidated revenue rose from KRW 1.71 trillion to KRW 1.96 trillion, placing it among the trillion-won revenue group, while its standalone revenue grew 18.5% from KRW 940.6 billion to KRW 1.11 trillion, surpassing one trillion won for the first time.

In the shift toward electrification, Hwashin competes against Donghee Auto, Sungwoo Hitech and Webasto for battery-case and battery-pack supply orders.

Over the medium to long term, the Hyundai Motor Group's expanding robotics investment is cited as a potential new business opportunity for parts makers with chassis and body technology.

Outlook

The Georgia, USA plant, which began operations in the second half of 2025, has been expected to take time to reach breakeven given its initial fixed-cost and depreciation burden. Samsung Securities noted in its 1Q26 earnings review report that the new-plant depreciation burden is expected to gradually ease.

Electrification (battery case) revenue grew from KRW 20 billion in 2024 to KRW 80 billion in 2025, and Samsung Securities projected in a 2026 report that it could expand to around KRW 170 billion in 2026.

The new Pune, India subsidiary and the phased ramp-up of deliveries from the Georgia plant are contributing to supply-chain diversification and expanded production capacity.

The robot body-module business was presented by Samsung Securities as a new venture leveraging chassis technology, but Daol Investment & Securities noted in a June 2026 company visit report that concrete business details had not yet been confirmed.

The Hyundai Motor Group's expanding robotics investment is cited as the backdrop for such new-business expectations. The pace of Hwashin's revenue growth and the scale of its quarterly swings are likely to continue depending on automakers' production plans and new-model launch schedules.

08

Bull factors

New-Business Expansion (Battery Case, Robotics)

Hwashin grew battery-case revenue from KRW 20 billion in 2024 to KRW 80 billion in 2025, building on its chassis technology. Samsung Securities assessed that this technology could extend into robot body modules. The Hyundai Motor Group's expanding robotics investment underpins such new-business expectations.

Hyundai Motor Group New Plants and New Models

The Georgia, USA plant began operating in the second half of 2025 to serve Hyundai-Kia's new-plant production volumes. The newly established Pune, India subsidiary is also contributing to expanded production capacity.

Hyundai-Kia's full SUV lineup production and strong North American eco-friendly vehicle and Genesis sales underpinned the 2025 earnings improvement.

2025 Profit Rebound and Margin Recovery

The operating margin fell to 3.8% in 2024 before rebounding to 5.2% in 2025, back to the 2022 level. Net profit attributable to controlling interests also rose 24.5% from KRW 53.6 billion in 2024 to KRW 66.7 billion in 2025. The sum of attributable net profit over the last four quarters was KRW 72.7 billion.

09

Bear factors

Profit Momentum Cooling in 2026

Revenue and operating profit both fell for two consecutive quarters in 1Q26 and 2Q26 after peaking in 4Q25. 2Q26 operating profit of KRW 5.8 billion was down 78.1% year on year. Quarterly net profit attributable to controlling interests also fell 75.2% quarter on quarter to KRW 7.03 billion.

New-Plant Start-Up Cost Burden

The Georgia plant has been expected to take time to reach breakeven given its initial fixed-cost and depreciation burden. This has been cited as one factor behind the quarterly operating margin decline seen in 2026. Profit volatility could persist until the new plant's utilization rate rises sufficiently.

Uncertainty Around the Robotics New Business

The robot body-module business has not yet been confirmed through any specific order or contract. Daol Investment & Securities raised the possibility that even if an order materializes, margins could be similar to existing parts businesses.

Since expectations alone have previously moved the share price, confirmation of when the business actually materializes is needed.

10

Risk factors

Customer Concentration Risk

Hwashin's revenue is heavily dependent on Hyundai Motor Group affiliates. Automakers' production plans or weak sales of specific models directly affect Hwashin's quarterly results.

As seen in the recent divergence between Hyundai Motor and KIA sales trends, shifts in demand allocation within the customer base are also a risk factor.

Foreign Exchange and Trade Policy Risk

With a sizable share of production and sales in the United States, movements in the won-dollar exchange rate and U.S. import tariff policy affect profitability. Costs related to the Georgia plant's operation are also linked to currency movements. Changes in the trade environment can be a source of quarterly margin volatility.

Financial and Cash Flow Risk

2025 operating cash flow fell sharply to KRW 42 billion from KRW 136.9 billion in 2024. The debt-to-equity ratio rose to 172.9% from the prior year. With new-plant and new-business investment ongoing, the pace of cash-generation recovery warrants monitoring.

12

Overall view

Hwashin holds a leading share of chassis parts supplied within the Hyundai Motor Group and confirmed an earnings rebound with operating profit up 56.9% in 2025. However, revenue and operating profit have both declined for two consecutive quarters in 2026, with 2Q26 operating profit down 78.1% year on year.

Strong battery-case revenue growth and the possibility of entering robot body-module production are cited as new-business hopes, but the robotics venture remains unconfirmed in concrete terms.

The Georgia plant's initial cost burden, high customer concentration in the Hyundai Motor Group, and the decline in operating cash flow alongside a rising debt-to-equity ratio are factors that warrant balanced attention.

Upcoming quarterly reports and new-business disclosures will be important for confirming whether the profit rebound is sustainable and whether the new businesses materialize concretely.
