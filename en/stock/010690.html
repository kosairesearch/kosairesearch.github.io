02

Business structure

Hwashin is an auto parts specialist mainly producing chassis and body components, supplying suspension-module-related parts such as arms, modules, frames, oil pans, and brackets.

Based on 2Q 2026 sales, the business mix consists of Chassis Parts 61%, other products 20%, Body Parts 10%, merchandise 6%, and Electrification Parts 3%, with chassis accounting for the overwhelming share.

Its major customers are Hyundai Motor Group affiliates, with a large portion of output supplied to Hyundai Motor and Kia. Hwashin holds exclusive chassis supply status to Hyundai Motor and Kia in both the United States and India, and has also supplied parts for Kia's North American strategic pickup truck models.

In 2023, the company signed a battery pack case supply contract with Hyundai Mobis worth KRW 939.9 billion for the next-generation eM electric vehicle platform, expanding into electrification parts, a deal cited by brokerages as evidence of backlog growth.

Production sites are spread across its domestic headquarters plus the United States, India, China, and Brazil, exposing the business to regional currency and local automaker production swings.

Headcount stood at about 1,051 as of end-June 2026, with production centered on its headquarters in Yeongcheon, North Gyeongsang Province.

Competitively, Hwashin competes with companies such as Sungwoo Hitech in the chassis and battery case segments, and must continually defend its supplier status under automakers' diversified sourcing policies.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual revenue rose from KRW 1.69 trillion in 2022 to KRW 1.8 trillion in 2023 (up 6.7% year-on-year), fell to KRW 1.71 trillion in 2024 (down 5.0%), and then climbed to KRW 1.96 trillion in 2025 (up 14.6%), the largest figure of the 2022-2025 period.

Operating profit declined for two straight years from KRW 87.4 billion in 2022 to KRW 83.7 billion in 2023 (down 4.2%) and KRW 65.3 billion in 2024 (down 22.0%), before rebounding to KRW 102.4 billion in 2025 (up 56.9%), the period's peak.

Controlling-interest net income rose from KRW 74.2 billion in 2022 to KRW 77.3 billion in 2023 (up 4.1%), fell to KRW 53.6 billion in 2024 (down 30.7%), and recovered to KRW 66.7 billion in 2025 (up 24.5%), with 2023 the largest and 2024 the smallest in the period.

Net income including non-controlling interests matched the controlling-interest figure in every year disclosed, indicating minimal non-controlling interest impact.

The operating margin slipped from 5.2% in 2022 to 4.6% in 2023 and 3.8% in 2024 before recovering to 5.2% in 2025, reflecting both eased cost pressure and the revenue rebound.

On a quarterly basis, revenue rose from KRW 490.8 billion in 2Q 2025 to KRW 485.9 billion in 3Q (down 1.0%) and peaked at KRW 512.4 billion in 4Q (up 5.5%), before declining for two straight quarters to KRW 505.6 billion in 1Q 2026 (down 1.3%) and KRW 498.4 billion in 2Q 2026 (down 1.4% sequentially, though up 1.5% year-on-year).

Operating profit followed a similar path, falling for two consecutive quarters from KRW 33.9 billion in 4Q 2025 to KRW 17 billion in 1Q 2026 (down 49.9%) and KRW 5.8 billion in 2Q 2026 (down 65.8% sequentially and 78.1% year-on-year), a slowdown Samsung Securities attributed mainly to depreciation costs at the new U.S. plant.

Controlling-interest net income moved from KRW 250 million in 2Q 2025 to KRW 18 billion in 3Q, KRW 19.4 billion in 4Q, and KRW 28.3 billion in 1Q 2026 before falling to KRW 7.03 billion in 2Q 2026, a figure up 2,712.4% versus the low 2Q 2025 base, diverging from the operating profit trend due to the low base and non-operating item swings. 2025 operating cash flow dropped sharply to KRW 42 billion from KRW 136.9 billion in 2024, which appears linked to heavier working-capital needs amid expanded overseas capital investment.

05

Industry analysis

Hwashin's performance is closely tied to Hyundai Motor and Kia's global production volumes and new model launch schedules, making it sensitive to the OEMs' production cycles.

As of cumulative 3Q 2022, regional sales mix was reported at roughly 36.6% domestic, 29.7% US, 19.4% India, and 10% Brazil, indicating a substantial overseas production footprint.

In the electrification parts segment, Hwashin competes with firms such as Sungwoo Hitech for battery pack case supply positions, as automakers continue diversifying their parts sourcing.

Even as EV demand growth has slowed relative to earlier expectations, continued hybrid and EV production has sustained demand for battery-system-related parts. The chassis segment has been cited as benefiting from rising average selling prices tied to automakers' expanding SUV and larger-vehicle mix.

In North America, the ramp-up of new Hyundai and Kia production facilities is a key variable for parts suppliers' revenue growth, while India's expanding local production capacity is viewed as a mid-to-long-term demand base.

However, like the broader auto parts industry, Hwashin remains exposed to macro variables such as US tariff policy, currency fluctuations, and raw material prices.

Given that operating profit declined for two consecutive quarters in early 2026 due to initial new-plant cost burdens, the key question going forward is when this cost pressure eases and whether growth in battery case and chassis volumes offsets it.

The 2025 year-end dividend decision was disclosed in March 2026, so continuity of dividend policy is another item to confirm at the next settlement.

Bull factors

2025 Earnings Rebound and Margin Recovery

2025 revenue reached KRW 1.96 trillion, the largest in 2022-2025, and operating profit of KRW 102.4 billion was also the period's peak. The operating margin recovered from 3.8% in 2024 to 5.2% in 2025, back to 2022 levels. This reflects both revenue recovery and eased cost pressure, indicating an improved earnings trajectory.

Exclusive Chassis Supply Status in the US and India

Hwashin holds exclusive chassis supply status to Hyundai Motor and Kia in both the US and India. As long as the Hyundai Motor Group's new plant operations and model launches continue, this exclusive supply structure can serve as a stable revenue base. India in particular is cited as a market where local production capacity expansion is underway.

Battery Case and New Business Expansion Potential

Hwashin expanded into electrification parts by signing a KRW 939.9 billion battery pack case supply contract with Hyundai Mobis for the eM platform in 2023.

Brokerages have also raised the possibility of entry into new areas such as robot body modules, leveraging chassis and materials technology, though this remains at the outlook stage pending confirmed orders.

Bear factors

Two Consecutive Quarters of Operating Profit Decline in 1H 2026

Operating profit fell for two consecutive quarters, from a peak of KRW 33.9 billion in 4Q 2025 to KRW 17 billion in 1Q 2026 and KRW 5.8 billion in 2Q 2026. Revenue also edged down over the same period from KRW 512.4 billion to KRW 505.6 billion and KRW 498.4 billion. Initial cost burdens from new plant operations are cited as the main background for this slowdown.

Weakened Cash Generation

2025 operating cash flow dropped sharply to KRW 42 billion from KRW 136.9 billion in 2024. The debt-to-equity ratio also rose again from 155.4% in 2024 to 172.9% in 2025. With overseas capital investment ongoing, financial capacity bears monitoring.

High Earnings Volatility

Hwashin's operating profit swung widely, declining for two straight years from KRW 87.4 billion in 2022 to KRW 65.3 billion in 2024 before rebounding in 2025. Quarterly controlling-interest net income also fluctuated sharply, from KRW 250 million in 2Q 2025 to KRW 28.3 billion in 1Q 2026.

Given its high fixed-cost production structure, profit is highly sensitive to changes in automaker order volumes.

Risk factors

Customer Concentration Risk

A substantial portion of Hwashin's revenue is concentrated in Hyundai Motor Group affiliates. Changes in Hyundai Motor and Kia's production plans or delays in new model launches could directly affect Hwashin's revenue and profit. With limited customer diversification, this dependency structure warrants ongoing monitoring.

New Capital Investment Cost Burden

Depreciation costs tied to the new US plant's operation were cited as the main cause of weak 1Q 2026 operating profit. With overseas plant investment ongoing, low initial utilization rates could pressure profit through fixed-cost burdens. The sharp decline in 2025 operating cash flow is also linked to this dynamic.

External Variables Such as FX and Tariffs

With production bases in the US, India, China, and Brazil, Hwashin is exposed to currency fluctuations. Changes in US tariff policy, which affect the broader auto parts industry, could also be a variable. Regional volatility in downstream automaker demand is another factor that could affect performance.

Overall view

Its exclusive chassis supply status to Hyundai Motor and Kia in the US and India, along with potential expansion into battery cases and new businesses, are cited as mid-to-long-term growth factors, though new ventures such as robot body modules remain at the outlook stage pending confirmed orders.
