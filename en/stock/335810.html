02

Business structure

Precision Biosensor is an in-vitro diagnostics company that supplies analyzers and reagent cartridges to hospitals and testing laboratories. Its business is divided into immunodiagnostics and clinical chemistry diagnostics.

Immunodiagnostic products are used across cardiovascular disease, infectious disease, hormones and cancer. Clinical chemistry products, launched in 2021, became the main business, accounting for more than 70% of total revenue from 2023 according to a report in early 2025.

The clinical chemistry line covers products for humans and animals, and the veterinary analyzers and cartridges are sold in overseas markets including Europe and Asia.

Its US subsidiary handles rapid test kits for respiratory infections such as COVID-19 and influenza, and its influenza rapid test received full FDA approval in January 2026.

Peers such as Celltrion and Samsung Biologics center on biopharmaceutical manufacturing, whereas this company sells diagnostic devices and consumables.

In North America, a planned entry based on a 2022 long-term veterinary clinical chemistry supply contract stalled after a partner was involved in an acquisition, and the company has sought new partners while shifting existing contract volumes to Europe, Asia and the Middle East. Its largest shareholder changed from iSens to Kwangdong Pharmaceutical in 2024.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual revenue was KRW 20.5 billion in 2022, KRW 20.4 billion in 2023 (down 0.3% year on year), KRW 19.1 billion in 2024 (down 6.2%) and KRW 19.9 billion in 2025 (up 4.1%). Among 2022 to 2025, 2022 was the largest year and 2024 the smallest.

Operating losses over the same period were KRW 4.5 billion in 2022, KRW 4.81 billion in 2023, KRW 5.37 billion in 2024 and KRW 5.38 billion in 2025, with operating margins of -22.0%, -23.6%, -28.0% and -27.0%. The company recorded losses for four consecutive years from 2022 through 2025.

The net loss attributable to owners of the parent narrowed from KRW 6.81 billion in 2022 to KRW 4.49 billion in 2023, then widened to KRW 7.31 billion in 2024 and KRW 10 billion in 2025.

The 2025 operating loss was little changed from 2024, so the larger 2025 net loss is best read against the impairment of tangible and intangible assets that the company cited as the main driver. The debt-to-equity ratio fell from 102.5% in 2024 to 62.0% in 2025.

Total equity at the end of 2025 was KRW 32.8 billion, all of it attributable to owners of the parent, and total liabilities were KRW 20.3 billion. Operating cash flow was negative in every year, at -KRW 2.58 billion in 2022, -KRW 1.95 billion in 2023, -KRW 1.44 billion in 2024 and -KRW 1.51 billion in 2025.

By quarter, the third quarter of 2025 showed revenue of KRW 5.12 billion and an operating loss of KRW 1.03 billion, and the fourth quarter widened the operating loss to KRW 1.4 billion on revenue of KRW 5.29 billion.

Revenue in the first quarter of 2026 was KRW 7.03 billion, up 32.8% from the prior quarter, and operating profit of KRW 17.46 million turned the result positive. In the second quarter, revenue fell 11.6% to KRW 6.22 billion and the operating loss of KRW 1.21 billion returned.

The third quarter of 2026 recorded revenue of KRW 7.02 billion and operating profit of KRW 412 million.

Net profit attributable to owners of the parent was KRW 133 million in the first quarter of 2026, followed by a net loss of KRW 1.17 billion in the second quarter and KRW 519 million in the third quarter, which means two consecutive quarters of losses.

In an interview reported in late August 2026, the CEO said first-half 2026 revenue grew 39% year on year and the operating loss narrowed.

05

Industry analysis

The in-vitro diagnostics market has been normalizing since the COVID-19 demand surge ended. The company said the 2023 end of the pandemic weighed on domestic diagnostics makers and that it responded by expanding its clinical chemistry products.

The company also said demand for point-of-care testing solutions, which deliver results on site, is growing in global markets; this is the company's own view, and no independent market-size data was used here.

In North America, a report said that after a partner's acquisition, competitor products came to be sold on the basis of analyzers already installed at customer sites. In Europe, sales are also reportedly based on analyzers already in place.

In immunodiagnostics, FDA clearances are widening the company's room to enter the US market. Celltrion and Samsung Biologics center on drug manufacturing, whereas this company sells diagnostic devices and consumables to hospitals and testing laboratories.

Demand for diagnostic devices can move with hospital testing volumes, care settings and infectious disease activity, so it may respond differently from biopharmaceutical makers. The clinical chemistry line has expanded sales channels in Korea, Europe and Asia, while the North American channel is being restructured.

Product approvals and partner contracts therefore appear to explain revenue direction more than the broader sector cycle.

Outlook

The CEO has said the company aims to make 2026 the first year of a return to profit, and has described efforts to reorganize the business and improve the cost structure after the change in largest shareholder.

In the US, the influenza rapid test received full FDA approval in January 2026, and in May 2026 the company expanded a supply agreement with Sekisui for a combo rapid test. The veterinary clinical chemistry analyzer Atlyze V launched in Japan in June 2026.

In immunodiagnostics, three thyroid function point-of-care tests received European IVDR certification in June 2026, and in early August 2026 the company obtained domestic sales certification for its HbA1c immunodiagnostic product.

The North American veterinary clinical chemistry channel is being restructured after a partner change, so whether a new partner contract is signed is a key variable for revenue. A 2025 report said the company was moving existing contract volumes to Europe, Asia and the Middle East.

The second-quarter 2026 results were released on August 14, 2026, and third-quarter 2026 results were filed in a quarterly report in mid-November 2026.

Disclosures about repurchasing convertible bonds before maturity were filed on February 19, May 8, May 15 and August 18, 2026, so changes in the capital structure are ongoing.

The next checkpoints are whether operating profit stays positive across the full year in the 2026 annual report, and whether the US supply agreements and new product approvals show up in quarterly revenue.

Bear factors

Recurring quarterly losses

The operating loss in the second quarter of 2026 was KRW 1.21 billion, so profitability lasted only one quarter. The net loss attributable to owners of the parent was KRW 1.17 billion in the second quarter and KRW 519 million in the third, meaning two consecutive quarters of losses.

The four-quarter total of KRW 5.35 billion equals about 16% of total equity at the end of 2025. The gap between operating profit and net profit is the main bearish point.

Weak execution of the North American contract

A long-term veterinary clinical chemistry supply contract signed in July 2022 was valued at about USD 91.33 million, but a 2025 report said supply recorded zero revenue in 2024 after a corrected disclosure. A partner's acquisition stalled the North American entry, and the existing contract was moved to other regions. The gap between contract value and actual execution weakens confidence in revenue forecasts.

Impairment charges and negative operating cash flow

The 2025 net loss of KRW 10 billion was attributed in disclosures to the recognition of impairment losses on tangible and intangible assets. Total liabilities at the end of 2025 were KRW 20.3 billion, lower than in 2024, but operating cash flow was still negative at -KRW 1.51 billion in 2025.

An impairment reflects a change in the accounting view of asset value, so whether those assets later generate income is the central bearish question.

Risk factors

Product and regional concentration

A 2025 report said clinical chemistry products accounted for more than 70% of total revenue from 2023. Revenue depends heavily on one product line and a few partner contracts, so a disruption in one channel can move quarterly results sharply. The 11.6% quarter-on-quarter revenue decline in the second quarter of 2026 is consistent with that volatility.

Largest shareholder stake and free float

Kwangdong Pharmaceutical's stake stood at 50.23% in the February 9, 2026 disclosure, after new shares were issued in the merger on February 4. With the largest holder close to a majority, the free float may be limited.

Business reorganization under the group and future strategic direction after the change in control are further variables.

US rapid test supply execution

In May 2026, the company expanded a supply agreement with Sekisui in the US for Combo rapid tests. How much of this contract volume will show up in quarterly revenue has not yet appeared in reported results.

Respiratory infection rapid tests see demand shift with the intensity of outbreaks, so contract volumes may not translate into orders as expected. In that case, the US channel may be limited in its ability to serve as a pillar of revenue recovery.
