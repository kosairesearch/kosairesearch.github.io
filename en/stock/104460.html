02

Business structure

Dypnf is a powder conveying system specialist listed on KOSDAQ in December 2009, building equipment that moves powdered raw materials pneumatically. Its core pneumatic conveying system (PCS) business accounts for most of its operating profit according to securities analysts.

The plant EPC business covers design through construction for petrochemical and secondary battery materials facilities. The environmental business supplies flue gas desulfurization equipment and electrostatic precipitators.

Its secondary battery materials work sits in the E&R (engineering and innovation) division, which entered the market through a 2020 order tied to a cathode materials group. In a Q3 2023 report, the E&R division's revenue share had risen to 30%, or about KRW 30.5 billion.

Its main customers are petrochemical and energy companies and battery materials makers. In 2023 it signed a KRW 156 billion supply contract with Sambak LF&T that ran for about two and a half years through June 2026. In overseas comparisons, Germany's Coperion and Zeppelin are cited as peers. The press describes the company as a leading domestic powder conveying system specialist.

04

Earnings analysis

On a disclosed annual basis, revenue rose from KRW 113.1 billion in 2022 to KRW 144.1 billion in 2023, KRW 281 billion in 2024 and KRW 396.3 billion in 2025, with growth of 95.1% in 2024 being the steepest.

The company attributed the 2024 expansion to the smooth progress of large projects, including the Baltic and Sambak LF&T projects. Operating profit moved from an operating loss of KRW 11.8 billion in 2022 to KRW 8.33 billion in 2023, then KRW 24.6 billion in 2024 and KRW 28.6 billion in 2025.

The operating margin was -10.4% in 2022, 5.8% in 2023 and 8.8% in 2024, before easing to 7.2% in 2025. In 2025 revenue grew 41.0% but operating profit grew only 16.2%, so profit did not keep pace with sales.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders turned positive at KRW 5.7 billion in 2023, then reached KRW 18.2 billion in 2024 and KRW 25.4 billion in 2025.

A securities analyst noted that a KRW 10.2 billion bad-debt charge was recognized in 2024 when designated external audits began, which affected that year's earnings. Quarterly results turned weaker from late 2025.

Q3 2025 revenue was KRW 108.5 billion with operating profit of KRW 11.1 billion, and Q4 2025 revenue fell to KRW 86.3 billion with an operating loss of KRW 9.72 billion.

Q1 2026 returned to operating profit of KRW 3.57 billion on revenue of KRW 60.7 billion, but Q2 2026 revenue was KRW 36.8 billion with operating profit of KRW 1.08 billion.

Q3 2026 revenue was KRW 33.8 billion, down 68.9% year on year, and operating profit was KRW 2.87 billion, down 74.1% year on year, with a net loss attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 2.45 billion. Over the last four quarters, that net loss attributable to controlling shareholders totaled KRW 3.07 billion.

Operating cash flow fell from KRW 47.2 billion in 2023 to KRW 7.77 billion in 2024 and turned negative at KRW -24.5 billion in 2025. Project-based revenue is recognized unevenly, depending on client investment timing, completion rates and cost estimate revisions, which analysts cite as a reason for quarterly swings.

05

Industry analysis

In petrochemical plants, a securities analyst found that intensifying global competition in petrochemical capacity lifted Dypnf's PCS sales.

The same analysis viewed expanding naphtha capacity from COTC technology advances and capacity races in high-margin specialty products such as epoxy, wetting agents and adhesives as tailwinds.

Another view holds that if refining margins recover with economic expansion, petrochemical plant construction demand and profits could rise sharply.

On the other side, in 2024 the company said investment in traditional chemical industries had slowed and that it would pursue growth in secondary batteries and specialty chemicals.

In batteries, reports around 2023 pointed to rising inquiries for battery material equipment, and the E&R division's rise to a 30% revenue share reflects that trend. On November 4, 2026, the press linked the day's share price jump to hopes for a recovery in powder conveying plant orders.

Germany's Coperion and Zeppelin are cited as peers. Because project orders take time to reach reported revenue, the timing of equipment investment cycles and revenue recognition can diverge.

06

Outlook

The most recent large order confirmed is an EPC contract signed on October 26, 2026 to build a silicon anode material plant. The contract value is KRW 47.96 billion, equal to 12.10% of 2025 revenue, with a term from October 26, 2026 to August 31, 2027.

Payment is tied to completion progress and includes a down payment and advance payment. The company said the final amount may change depending on actual-cost settlement and changes in scope.

At the end of Q1 2025, the order backlog stood at KRW 403.4 billion, comprising KRW 182.8 billion of PCS and KRW 175.5 billion of EPC, and a securities analyst expected more than 90% of it to be recognized as revenue over about three years on a completion basis.

In its value-up plan disclosed on March 31, 2026, the company set targets of stronger overseas strategic market orders, new market entry through strategic alliances, higher profitability through cost competitiveness and operating efficiency, and continued shareholder returns.

Its implementation measures include strengthening price and technology competitiveness, cutting fixed costs, and raising operating profit to secure dividend funds. The 2025 payout ratio was 26.1%, with total dividends of KRW 6.63 billion.

On November 4, 2026, the board resolved to cancel 1,047,603 treasury shares and said it will pursue a KRW 9.5 billion shareholder return package combining their KRW 7.5 billion book value with a planned KRW 2 billion buyback under a trust contract with NH Investment & Securities, with any additional shares also to be fully cancelled.

With operating profit at KRW 2.87 billion in Q3 2026, the next checkpoint is when new orders begin converting into quarterly revenue.

Bull factors

Expanded shareholder return plan

The value-up plan disclosed on March 31, 2026 set continued shareholder returns as a goal. The company disclosed a 2025 payout ratio of 26.1% and designated itself a high-dividend company. On November 4, 2026, the board resolved to cancel treasury shares and disclosed a plan to buy back another KRW 2 billion.

Lower leverage and equity base

The debt ratio fell from 150.2% in 2024 to 64.8% in 2025. Total equity at year-end 2025 was KRW 149.8 billion against total liabilities of KRW 97.1 billion. Net income attributable to controlling shareholders rose for a second straight year to KRW 25.4 billion in 2025.

Bear factors

Quarterly revenue down for four straight quarters

Quarterly revenue has declined for four consecutive quarters from Q4 2025 through Q3 2026. Q3 2026 revenue was KRW 33.8 billion, down 68.9% year on year, and operating profit was KRW 2.87 billion, down 74.1%. Net loss attributable to controlling shareholders turned negative at KRW 2.45 billion in the same quarter.

Operating cash flow turned negative

Operating cash flow fell from KRW 7.77 billion in 2024 to negative KRW 24.5 billion in 2025. Commentators note that for project businesses, unbilled contract balances and provisions matter more than order value. Cash generation lagged profit growth in 2025, a point that still needs confirmation.

Slower chemical investment and bad-debt burden

In 2024 the company said investment in traditional chemical industries had slowed. A securities analyst pointed to a KRW 10.2 billion bad-debt charge recognized in 2024 when designated audits began. Competitive gaps versus peers such as Coperion and Zeppelin are hard to gauge from public disclosures alone.

Risk factors

Quarterly earnings volatility

Project revenue swings from quarter to quarter with client investment timing, completion progress and cost estimate revisions. The Q4 2025 operating loss and Q2 2026 operating profit of KRW 1.08 billion reflect that pattern.

Press analysis says the durability of any rebound depends on advance payments from new contracts and on whether cost ratios normalize.

Contract value and schedule changes

The company said the KRW 47.96 billion EPC contract value may change with actual-cost settlement and scope changes. The end date can also move by mutual agreement. Revenue concentration in a few large contracts means delays or settlement changes can show up directly in quarterly results.

Industry cycle and financial burden

Petrochemical equipment investment follows a cycle tied to refining margins and economic conditions. The company has cited uncertainty in chemical and battery markets. The 2025 operating cash flow deficit and the 2024 bad-debt charge are key items to monitor for financial strain.

Overall view

From a loss in 2022, Dypnf's revenue, operating profit and net income attributable to controlling shareholders all rose through 2025, and its debt ratio fell to 64.8%. However, operating cash flow turned negative in 2025, and quarterly revenue has declined for four consecutive quarters from Q4 2025 through Q3 2026.

Q3 2026 revenue was KRW 33.8 billion and operating profit was KRW 2.87 billion, both below the prior-year quarter. On the positive side are the KRW 47.96 billion EPC contract signed in October 2026 and a shareholder return push that includes treasury share cancellation.

On the negative side are quarterly swings in project revenue, the pace of collections, the bad-debt charge recognized in 2024, and the petrochemical investment cycle.

Because these signals point in different directions, the next checkpoints are how fast new orders convert into revenue, the direction of operating cash flow, and the preliminary 2026 annual results.

The 2026 annual report is the next regular filing, and it will show how the annual results and the quarterly trend connected.
