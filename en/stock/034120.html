Report overview

SBS: Ad Recovery Signs Versus a Thin Earnings Base

SBS returned to operating profit in 2025 with KRW 18.2 billion, yet net income attributable to controlling shareholders has declined for three straight years and totaled only KRW 463 million over the last four quarters. Whether TV ad sales recover is the key variable for earnings.

  1. 1

    Operating profit in 2025 was KRW 18.2 billion, turning positive from an operating loss of KRW 19.2 billion in 2024, while net income attributable to controlling shareholders fell to KRW 8.03 billion.

  2. 2

    Third-quarter 2026 sales rose 6.6% year on year to KRW 254.9 billion, but operating profit fell 22.2% to KRW 10.2 billion, so the top line and profit moved in opposite directions.

  3. 3

    After an operating loss of KRW 17.6 billion in the first quarter of 2026, operating profit returned to KRW 7.79 billion in the second quarter, showing large quarterly swings.

  4. 4

  5. 5

02

Business structure

SBS relies mainly on terrestrial TV advertising and supplies self-produced dramas and variety shows to domestic and overseas platforms, adding licensing and distribution income. Ad revenue follows ratings and programming schedules, so structural changes in the ad market feed directly into earnings.

Business-segment income includes content licensing and online distribution, including video-on-demand supply to the domestic OTT service Wavve and new-media revenue from YouTube. Expanded licensing contracts with overseas OTT platforms are also cited as a source of content income.

A December 2025 DB Financial Investment report said licensing income from partners such as Netflix, together with cost efficiency in selling and administrative expenses, has supported profits.

A July 2025 KB Securities report judged that the connected subsidiaries, which are heavily exposed to the ad cycle, would hover around breakeven. A May 2026 Daishin Securities report noted that TV ad and business-segment revenue both declined in the first quarter of 2026.

Competition for ad budgets is shared with other broadcasters and online media, so how quickly ad sales recover relative to ratings is the central issue. A late-May 2026 report argued that content competitiveness is helping SBS defend its share against weaker market conditions.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual sales fell from KRW 1.17 trillion in 2022 to KRW 996.8 billion in 2023, a 15.1% decline, then recovered to KRW 1.05 trillion in 2024 with a 5.0% increase before slipping to KRW 1.01 trillion in 2025, down 3.6%. Among 2022 to 2025, 2022 was the largest year and 2023 the smallest.

Operating profit dropped from KRW 185.6 billion in 2022 to KRW 58.3 billion in 2023, a 68.6% decline. It then swung to an operating loss of KRW 19.2 billion in 2024 and back to operating profit of KRW 18.2 billion in 2025. The operating margin moved from 15.8% in 2022 to 5.9% in 2023, -1.8% in 2024 and 1.8% in 2025.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders went from KRW 152.5 billion in 2022 to KRW 46.3 billion in 2023, a 69.6% decline, then KRW 33.8 billion in 2024, down 26.9%, and KRW 8.03 billion in 2025, down 76.3%, a decline recorded for three consecutive years from 2023 to 2025.

Net income including non-controlling interests was KRW 7.93 billion in 2025 and should be read separately from the controlling-shareholder figure. Operating profit turned positive in 2025 while attributable net income still fell, so items below operating profit need to be checked.

Operating cash flow went from negative KRW 55.8 billion in 2022 to KRW 63.4 billion in 2023, KRW 8.34 billion in 2024 and KRW 64.8 billion in 2025.

On a quarterly basis, after sales of KRW 239.2 billion and operating profit of KRW 13.1 billion in the third quarter of 2025, the fourth quarter brought sales of KRW 294.7 billion, up 23.2% from the prior quarter, but operating profit of KRW 4.98 billion, down 61.9%, and a controlling-shareholder net loss of KRW 1.14 billion.

The first quarter of 2026 showed sales of KRW 190.4 billion, an operating loss of KRW 17.6 billion and a controlling-shareholder net loss of KRW 13.9 billion, with the loss widening.

The second quarter of 2026 returned to operating profit of KRW 7.79 billion on sales of KRW 285.4 billion, up 49.9% from the prior quarter.

The third quarter of 2026 posted sales of KRW 254.9 billion, down 10.7% from the prior quarter but up 6.6% year on year, with operating profit of KRW 10.2 billion, down 22.2% year on year, and controlling-shareholder net income of KRW 8.96 billion, down 13.7% year on year. Over the last four quarters, controlling-shareholder net income totaled KRW 463 million.

05

Industry analysis

The domestic broadcast ad market is under pressure from media fragmentation and intensifying competition for advertising budgets. A 2025 business summary noted that TV and radio revenue fell as ad competition intensified.

Weak domestic demand and a prolonged slump in broadcast advertising were cited as factors in the first quarter of 2026. A May 2026 Daishin Securities report said TV ad sales had contracted more than ratings declines alone would imply, and that the pullback from media fragmentation was unavoidable but excessive.

In the same period, average ratings for major titles fell 4.1 percentage points to 6.3 percent, and the drama episode count dropped by two to 22. On the cycle, the view is that the ad slump has been prolonged, with a possible rebound after the fourth quarter.

On regulation, a DB Financial Investment report and a May 2026 report both pointed to broadcast media regulatory easing as a condition for ad revenue growth. A weaker media-sector stock market and changes in valuation benchmarks also shaped broker target-setting methods.

Relative to peers, content competitiveness and expanding overseas OTT contracts are cited as defenses against weak market conditions. CJ ENM, in the same broadcasting and media sector, appears as a comparison point in some broker valuation tables.

Outlook

Fourth-quarter 2026 results are due after January 2027, when full-year 2026 profit and the fourth-quarter ad trend can be checked. Looking at the first quarter of 2026, a May 2026 Daishin Securities analysis expected TV ad sales to rebound from the fourth quarter.

Around the same period, several reports lowered 2026 operating profit estimates and presented a steeper recovery in 2027, which means a profit rebound depends on an ad recovery and continued cost control.

Easing of ad regulation is cited as a precondition for top-line growth after 2026, so the pace of policy steps directly matters for earnings. Expanded overseas OTT licensing contracts are seen as a complementary source of content income.

In July 2025, KB Securities said earnings swings would be large depending on whether the unofficial restrictions on Korean content in China are lifted, with considerable uncertainty around that issue.

The 2026 annual business report must be filed by March 31, 2027, and the full-year operating margin and the direction of controlling-shareholder net income are the next checkpoints. Drama scheduling and content sales announced by the company can also serve as leading indicators for ad revenue.

Cost control and profit turnaround

A May 2026 report assessed that selling and administrative costs fell year on year, and cost control has continued. The move from an operating loss in 2024 to operating profit in 2025 came alongside a cost-structure improvement. The second quarter of 2026 returned to operating profit of KRW 7.79 billion, a quarterly sign of recovery.

Regulatory easing and content strength

In a December 2025 report, DB Financial Investment said 2026 ad top-line growth could be confirmed as broadcast media regulation eases. A late-May 2026 report also cited growth expectations from easing ad rules. Content strength and expanding overseas OTT contracts are seen as a path to a higher recoupment rate.

Bear factors

Prolonged broadcast ad weakness

According to a May 2026 Daishin Securities report, standalone broadcast ad revenue fell 10.4% year on year to KRW 50.1 billion. The drama episode count was cut and average ratings for major titles dropped 4.1 percentage points. If ad demand does not recover, the profit trend could weaken again.

Earnings volatility and thin cumulative profit

Third-quarter 2026 operating profit fell 22.2% year on year, and controlling-shareholder net income dropped 13.7%. Net income attributable to controlling shareholders totaled only KRW 463 million over the last four quarters, so single quarterly losses can erase much of the cumulative profit. The first quarter of 2026 saw a controlling-shareholder net loss of KRW 13.9 billion, which widened the loss.

External variables and lower estimates

In July 2025, KB Securities noted that earnings swings would be large depending on whether restrictions on Korean content in China are lifted, and said that uncertainty is high. A May 2026 report lowered its 2026 operating profit estimate. Subsidiaries that are sensitive to the ad cycle and hover near breakeven add to exposure to external variables.

Risk factors

Ad market and policy risk

Broadcast advertising faces structural pressure from media fragmentation and intensifying competition, as the decline in TV and radio revenue in 2025 shows. If easing of ad regulation is delayed, the timing of a top-line recovery could also slip. If policy timing diverges from earnings expectations, estimate revisions could repeat.

Content performance and ratings risk

When drama and variety ratings slip, ad rates and licensing income tend to weaken together. Average ratings for major titles fell 4.1 percentage points to 6.3 percent in the first quarter of 2026. Expanding overseas OTT contracts partly offsets this, but the size and timing of those deals depend on partner decisions.

Subsidiary earnings and external uncertainty

A 2025 KB Securities analysis said connected subsidiaries are affected by the ad cycle and hover around breakeven. Whether restrictions on Korean content in China are lifted is cited as an external variable that could widen earnings swings. With a thin earnings base, subsidiary results or external issues could amplify quarterly volatility.
