02

Business structure

LB Investment is a venture capital firm founded in 1996 as LG Venture Capital, renamed in 2008, and listed on KOSDAQ in March 2023. Its fee income has two parts: management fees tied to assets under management (AUM), and performance fees tied to investment exits.

Mirae Asset Securities Research reported in June 2025 that management fees were KRW 2.7 billion and performance fees KRW 2.2 billion in 1Q 2025. The same report said annual fee income fell 41% to KRW 13.2 billion in 2024, mainly because several funds extended their terms or were liquidated.

The firm mostly runs blind funds, and in 2025 it formed the KRW 303 billion LB Next Future Fund, taking AUM above KRW 1.5 trillion. Its LPs include policy and public-sector institutions such as the national pension and mutual aid funds.

The investment approach is to find early-stage startups and then support growth through follow-on investment. For 2026, the CEO said in a January interview that the planned mix is about 40 to 50% deep tech, 20% bio and healthcare, 20 to 30% services and content, and roughly 10% global.

On exits, eight portfolio companies listed by IPO in 2025, and four of them were reported to have passed KRW 1 trillion in market value after listing. Domestic VCs also face pressure as major domestic LPs increase commitments to overseas VC funds, which raises the bar on global capability.

04

Earnings analysis

On a reported basis, annual revenue rose from KRW 18.5 billion in 2022 to KRW 27.6 billion in 2023 (up 48.9% year on year) and KRW 30.3 billion in 2024 (up 9.7%), then fell to KRW 26.4 billion in 2025 (down 12.7%). The largest revenue year in the 2022 to 2025 window was 2024, and the smallest was 2022.

Operating profit went from KRW 4.77 billion in 2022 to KRW 8.46 billion in 2023 (up 77.6%) and KRW 10.9 billion in 2024 (up 28.2%), then fell to KRW 9.67 billion in 2025 (down 10.9%).

The operating margin rose from 25.7% in 2022 to 36.6% in 2025, and in 2025 it improved from 35.9% to 36.6% even as revenue and operating profit both declined.

Net income attributable to owners of the parent went from KRW 3.87 billion in 2022 to KRW 6.42 billion in 2023 (up 65.8%), KRW 8.58 billion in 2024 (up 33.6%) and KRW 12.4 billion in 2025 (up 44.9%), rising for three consecutive years from 2023 through 2025.

The 2025 gain came even as operating profit fell, and the company attributed it to lower deferred tax expense after a tax-rate recalculation. Net income including non-controlling interests matched the owners' figure in every year from 2022 to 2025. Quarterly results are volatile.

Revenue fell 25.2% quarter on quarter to KRW 6.09 billion in 4Q 2025, jumped 115.3% to KRW 13.1 billion in 1Q 2026, then dropped 34.9% to KRW 8.54 billion in 2Q and 34.9% to KRW 5.56 billion in 3Q.

Operating profit fell from KRW 5.28 billion in 3Q 2025 to KRW 720 million in 4Q 2025 (down 86.4%), then recorded KRW 2.11 billion in 1Q 2026, KRW 3.44 billion in 2Q and KRW 1.76 billion in 3Q.

In 4Q 2025, operating profit fell while net income attributable to owners rose 4.5% quarter on quarter to KRW 4.86 billion, and the effect of non-operating items cannot be separated from the reported figures alone. Net income attributable to owners totaled KRW 11 billion over the last four quarters.

Operating cash flow was -KRW 6.31 billion in 2022, KRW 1.62 billion in 2023, -KRW 2.56 billion in 2024 and KRW 5.64 billion in 2025. Management fees accrue steadily with AUM, while performance fees depend on exit timing, which makes quarterly revenue swing widely.

05

Industry analysis

Venture capital earnings run on two tracks. Management fees tied to AUM are relatively stable, while performance fees from exits follow the investment cycle.

Mirae Asset Securities Research said in a June 2025 report that venture investing has long holding periods and high volatility, so share prices react strongly to portfolio company events and results. The same report noted that annual fee income fell 41% in 2024 as several funds extended terms or were liquidated.

IPOs remain the main exit channel, and the company said it took eight portfolio companies public in 2025. On policy, the CEO expected expanded policy funding in 2026, including the National Growth Fund.

In June 2026, the firm was selected as a mid-sized manager for a government-backed secondary fund aimed at supporting exit markets. Domestically, major LPs are increasing commitments to overseas VC funds, which pushes local managers to build global capability.

On peer comparison, the only quantitative marker is the company's own statement that none of its funds has fallen short of its target internal rate of return, a claim made by the company and not independently verified.

06

Outlook

The company said it plans to raise 2026 investment by more than 30% from the prior year, using KRW 200 billion as the base for deployment. Planned allocation is about 40 to 50% deep tech (AI, robotics, semiconductors and materials), 20% bio and healthcare, 20 to 30% services and content, and roughly 10% global.

By stage, it plans about 30% early-stage, 30 to 40% growth and 30 to 40% late-stage and structured investment. It is also forming a combined secondary and M&A fund.

Its third secondary fund targets more than KRW 200 billion, and an August 2026 report said the firm was selected by a church pension fund as a manager eligible for up to KRW 20 billion in commitments.

The same report said it was also picked as a blind-fund manager by a private school pension fund, securing another KRW 20 billion. The final size and LP mix will be set only after fundraising closes. The company said its LB technology finance fund is in the exit stage and that a multiple of at least 3x is expected.

It also said all funds currently under management are generating performance fees, which supports a stable GP commitment. The 2026 annual report will show the fee income mix and investment deployment results.

08

Bull factors

Plans to expand fund scale

The company plans to raise 2026 investment by more than 30% from the prior year. It is forming a combined secondary and M&A fund, with the third secondary fund targeting more than KRW 200 billion. Reports said it was selected as a manager by two pension funds. The bull case rests on management fees, which accrue steadily with AUM.

Exit activity and performance fees

Eight portfolio companies listed by IPO in 2025, and four were reported to exceed KRW 1 trillion in market value after listing. In a January 2026 interview, the CEO said all funds under management are generating performance fees. Media reported that 2025 exit results were at a record level. If exits continue, performance fee inflows could support earnings.

Financial capacity and shareholder returns

The debt-to-equity ratio was 6.9% at the end of 2025, down from 9.3% in 2022. The company has held dividends at the same level for three years since listing, which is cited as a shareholder-return strength.

09

Bear factors

Quarterly profit slowdown in 2026

Revenue peaked at KRW 13.1 billion in 1Q 2026, then fell to KRW 8.54 billion in 2Q and KRW 5.56 billion in 3Q. Operating profit in 3Q 2026 was KRW 1.76 billion, down 66.6% year on year, and net income attributable to owners fell 62.6%.

Dependence on fund terms and liquidations

Mirae Asset Securities Research noted that annual fee income fell 41% to KRW 13.2 billion in 2024, attributing it to fund term extensions and liquidations. Operating profit also fell 10.9% in 2025. Because performance fees depend on exit timing, quarterly income can swing sharply.

Exit markets and overseas competition

A brokerage report pointed out that venture investing has long cycles and that share prices react strongly to portfolio company developments. As major domestic LPs shift commitments toward overseas VC funds, domestic managers face demands for global capability. The company plans roughly 10% of investment overseas.

10

Risk factors

Dependence on exit markets

Performance fees and IPO exits depend on stock market conditions and listing timelines. If portfolio companies list later or are valued lower, the recognition of performance fees may be delayed. Whether the 2025 listing pace continues is a key variable.

Single-fund exit results

The LB Technology Finance Fund is in its exit stage, and the company expects a multiple of at least 3x to be achievable. If that target is missed, the performance fees generated by the fund could be smaller than planned.

Final exit proceeds from individual funds are fixed only at liquidation, so they may differ from interim estimates. If exit results from a single fund concentrate in one quarter, quarterly earnings swings could widen.

Reliance on policy and LP capital

Expanded policy funding and commitments from pension and mutual aid funds underpin the fund-scale plan. Much of the 2026 plan relies on policy capital and the success of secondary fund formation.

If final fund sizes fall short of targets, investment plans could shrink, and competition for LP capital from overseas VC funds adds pressure.

12

Overall view

On reported figures, LB Investment's net income attributable to owners rose for three consecutive years from 2023 through 2025, and its operating margin climbed from 25.7% in 2022 to 36.6% in 2025.

The 2025 net income gain, however, reflects lower deferred tax expense after a tax-rate recalculation, so it should be separated from operating-level strength. In 2026, revenue has declined for two quarters since the 1Q peak, and 3Q operating profit fell 66.6% year on year.

Bullish factors include the plan to expand fund scale, the company's statement that all funds under management are generating performance fees, a low debt-to-equity ratio of 6.9%, and sustained dividends.

Bearish factors include reliance on fund terms and liquidations for fee income, quarterly swings tied to exit markets and IPOs, and competition for LP capital from overseas VC funds. The share price sits below 1x book value, which is low relative to net assets, but views on how durable earnings will be can differ.
