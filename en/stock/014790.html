Report overview

Operating Profit Recovers; Shareholder Net Profit Is the Test

HL D&I Halla's operating profit rose 37.8% to KRW 79.8 billion in 2025 and grew for two straight quarters into 2026, while net profit attributable to controlling shareholders fell in both 2024 and 2025, so whether the operating recovery reaches shareholders remains the next test.

  1. 1

    Operating profit reached KRW 79.8 billion in 2025, up 37.8% from the prior year, on sales of KRW 1.74 trillion, up 10.3%. The 4.6% operating margin is the highest of the 2022–2025 period.

  2. 2

    Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders peaked at KRW 30.7 billion in 2023, then fell to KRW 21.4 billion in 2024 and KRW 15.2 billion in 2025, moving opposite to operating profit.

  3. 3

    The order backlog at end-2025 was reported at KRW 6.23 trillion, described as a record, and a company official said it was in the mid-KRW 6 trillion range at end-September 2026. New orders in 2025 totaled KRW 2.60 trillion.

  4. 4

    Own-development sales rose 116.2% to KRW 106.8 billion in the first half of 2026 and reached 12.9% of total sales, according to a Datanews analysis of the half-year report.

  5. 5

    Hanwha Investment & Securities presented a KRW 5,500 target price in a September 28, 2026 report, and Hyundai Motor Securities presented KRW 4,200 in a May 28, 2026 coverage report.

02

Business structure

HL D&I Halla is a listed construction and building materials company ranked 28th in the construction capability ranking, according to a BizWatch report from November 2026. Its business splits into contract construction for client-ordered projects and own-development projects that it builds and sells as housing.

At end-2025, the contract backlog was about KRW 4.27 trillion, the largest share, while the own-development backlog was KRW 617.1 billion. The infrastructure backlog stood at KRW 1.35 trillion, up 78.6% from the prior year's KRW 754 billion.

New orders in 2025 totaled KRW 2.60 trillion, split into KRW 1.74 trillion for development and building and KRW 858.2 billion for infrastructure. Its housing brand is EFETE, and the Ichon Buldeok-yeok and Ulsan Taehwagang EFETE projects were reported at roughly KRW 500 billion and KRW 200 billion in scale.

Own-development sales rose 116.2% to KRW 106.8 billion in the first half of 2026 and reached 12.9% of total sales.

On the public side, it won the Korea Expressway Corporation's Jecheon–Yeongwol Expressway Section 1 (KRW 155.1 billion, 75% stake) and the Saemangeum New Port breakwater extension (KRW 52.8 billion) in 2026.

Civil works accounted for KRW 231.6 billion, or 61.5%, of the KRW 376.4 billion in first-half 2026 new orders, according to a Datanews analysis of the half-year report.

On the private side, the company cites its record of building Hyundai department store complexes and the KRW 453.1 billion The Hyundai Busan project won in November 2025.

The company says it is shifting from a construction-contract model toward development-led solutions spanning planning, financing, construction and post-completion management.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual sales rose from KRW 1.47 trillion in 2022 to KRW 1.57 trillion in 2023 (up 6.8%) and KRW 1.58 trillion in 2024 (up 0.4%), before reaching KRW 1.74 trillion in 2025 (up 10.3%), a third straight year of growth.

Operating profit fell from KRW 52.6 billion in 2022 to KRW 50.7 billion in 2023 (down 3.7%), then rose to KRW 57.9 billion in 2024 (up 14.3%) and KRW 79.8 billion in 2025 (up 37.8%), two straight years of gains.

The operating margin moved from 3.6% in 2022 and 3.2% in 2023 to 3.7% in 2024 and 4.6% in 2025, the highest of the period. A BizWatch-cited report from February 2026 linked the 2025 gain to higher sales together with lower cost ratios.

Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders rose from KRW 25.1 billion in 2022 to a peak of KRW 30.7 billion in 2023 (up 22.2%), then fell to KRW 21.4 billion in 2024 (down 30.2%) and KRW 15.2 billion in 2025 (down 29.0%), two straight years of declines.

Net profit including non-controlling interests is listed in the filings at the same annual figures. Because operating profit rose 37.8% in 2025 while attributable net profit fell 29.0%, the items between operating profit and net profit are the key point to check.

The debt ratio fell from 290.3% in 2022 to 239.6% in 2025, with total equity of KRW 572.3 billion and total liabilities of KRW 1.37 trillion at end-2025. Operating cash flow moved from -KRW 1.63 billion in 2024 to KRW 75.5 billion in 2025.

Quarterly, sales in the fourth quarter of 2025 rose 11.8% to KRW 533.2 billion, but operating profit fell 20.3% to KRW 20.3 billion and attributable net profit fell 95.0% to KRW 144 million. In the first quarter of 2026, sales fell 27.9% to KRW 384.6 billion and operating profit fell 6.6% to KRW 19 billion.

Operating profit then rose 39.7% to KRW 26.5 billion in the second quarter and 13.6% to KRW 30.1 billion in the third, two straight quarterly gains. Third-quarter attributable net profit was KRW 10.4 billion, up 262.8% from a year earlier, and the four quarters from Q4 2025 to Q3 2026 total KRW 27.7 billion.

05

Industry analysis

Construction companies are narrowing their order intake under a combination of high interest rates and rising raw material costs, according to a February 2026 Segye Ilbo report. A June 2026 Newsis report cited cost pressure on building materials tied to the Middle East conflict alongside a weak property market.

Industry commentary holds that builders are prioritizing profitability over expansion, according to a July 2026 Global Economic report. Pace of the sales market recovery and construction cost trends are the variables cited as most important for HL D&I Halla's earnings.

On the public side, the company is widening its order base in roads, railways and power facilities, according to Datanews. A February 2026 report by The Bell noted that new orders have held at roughly KRW 2.6 trillion for two consecutive years.

The company competes with other builders for housing and civil works orders and differentiates itself through profitability-focused selective bidding.

On own-development cost ratios, IBK Securities analyst Cho Jung-hyun put the own-sale cost ratio at about 76.4% and said expanding own projects would be positive for company-wide profitability, per a June 2026 Newsis report.

In terms of position, the backlog and infrastructure orders are building up as a strength, while sales results and cost ratios are the next variables.

Outlook

The company says it will strategically adjust the share of high-margin own-development projects to secure a stable operating margin, per a Newsis report from August 2026.

On orders, a KRW 678.3 billion Gwangju Jikdong residential contract was disclosed in July 2026, and a KRW 253.9 billion Cheongju Songjeol 4 District contract signed on October 27, 2026 covers a 90% stake, with construction over 35 months.

That contract pays on a progress-payment basis with no down payment or advance payment. IBK Securities estimated own-development sales would expand to KRW 251.7 billion in 2026 and KRW 279.0 billion in 2027, per a June 2026 Newsis report.

Hyundai Motor Securities raised its target price, citing expected earnings gains from the expansion of own-development projects, while Hanwha Investment & Securities pointed to second-half sales from new civil works sites and steady housing margins.

The third-quarter results slightly beat the securities-industry consensus of KRW 465.8 billion in sales and KRW 29.8 billion in operating profit, BizWatch reported.

On sales, the Gyeonghuigung EFETE project, described as the first EFETE sale inside Seoul's Four Gates, was reported to be scheduled for sale in November 2026.

Analyst Song Yu-rim of Hanwha Investment & Securities said the company needs additional own-development projects and non-housing orders for medium-term growth, per a November 2026 BizWatch report.

The company previously signaled new projects including the Seodaemun Station mixed-use complex, the Yongin Yeoksam regional housing project and the Namgu-ro station redevelopment, according to a February 2026 report by The Bell.

08

Bull factors

Continuity of the operating profit recovery

Operating profit rose 37.8% to KRW 79.8 billion in 2025, and first-half 2026 operating profit reached KRW 45.5 billion, up 34.1% from a year earlier, according to a Newsis report from August 2026. Third-quarter 2026 operating profit rose 18.1% to KRW 30.1 billion, with a margin reported at 6.4%.

Two straight quarterly gains after the first-quarter low are cited as evidence that the profitability improvement is not a one-off.

Record order backlog

The order backlog at end-2025 was reported at KRW 6.23 trillion, described as a record by The Bell in February 2026. The company said in the third quarter of 2026 that the backlog remained in the mid-KRW 6 trillion range.

Large contracts disclosed in the second half of 2026 include the KRW 678.3 billion Gwangju Jikdong project and the KRW 253.9 billion Cheongju Songjeol 4 District project. A thick backlog is the core of the bullish case that the future sales base is wider.

Own-development growth and cost ratios

Own-development sales rose 116.2% to KRW 106.8 billion in the first half of 2026, according to Datanews. IBK Securities analyst Cho Jung-hyun put the own-sale cost ratio at about 76.4% and estimated own-development sales would reach KRW 251.7 billion in 2026 and KRW 279.0 billion in 2027, per a June 2026 Newsis report.

The securities-industry argument is that a higher own-development share could broaden the company-wide profitability gain.

09

Bear factors

Shrinking net profit attributable to controlling shareholders

Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders fell from KRW 30.7 billion in 2023 to KRW 21.4 billion in 2024 (down 30.2%) and KRW 15.2 billion in 2025 (down 29.0%), two straight years of declines.

It dropped to KRW 144 million in the fourth quarter of 2025 (down 95.0%), and the last four quarters total KRW 27.7 billion. The bearish argument is that if operating gains do not carry through to shareholder earnings, the recovery will feel limited.

Thin margins and a high debt ratio

The operating margin improved to 4.6% in 2025 but remains below 5% on an annual basis. Total liabilities were KRW 1.37 trillion at end-2025, with a debt ratio of 239.6%.

Operating cash flow was -KRW 1.63 billion in 2024 and recovered to KRW 75.5 billion in 2025, but the stability of cash generation in a downturn still needs confirmation.

Shrinking own-development backlog and sales dependence

The own-development backlog fell 17.8% from KRW 750.4 billion in 2024 to KRW 617.1 billion in 2025, according to The Bell. Housing sales are recognized as projects are sold, so a slower sales market recovery could slow the expansion of own-development revenue.

Civil works made up 61.5% of first-half 2026 new orders, leaving a correspondingly smaller share for other segments.

10

Risk factors

Sales market and project financing

A slower sales market recovery could delay own-development revenue recognition and cash collection. The Cheongju Songjeol 4 District contract pays on a progress-payment basis with no down payment, and it can be canceled if the project financing loan agreement is not signed within 12 months of the contract date. Financing conditions could shift construction start dates for large sites.

Materials and construction cost inflation

A June 2026 Newsis report cited cost pressure on building materials tied to the Middle East conflict. The company names cost-ratio management and selective bidding as key drivers of its earnings recovery, so rising construction costs could slow the pace of margin improvement. Cost ratio trends are a metric to watch in upcoming periodic reports and quarterly results.

Reliance on public civil works and earnings gap

Civil works made up 61.5% of first-half 2026 new orders, raising dependence on public-sector orders. If the timing of civil project revenue and housing margins diverge, quarterly earnings volatility could increase.

The wide gap between operating profit and attributable net profit also remains a risk tied to non-operating items.

Overall view

HL D&I Halla's recent results show a clear direction of operating profit recovery. Operating profit rose 37.8% to KRW 79.8 billion in 2025, and it grew in the second and third quarters of 2026 after the first-quarter low.

The order backlog was reported at KRW 6.23 trillion at end-2025, described as a record, and large projects including Gwangju Jikdong and Cheongju Songjeol 4 District were disclosed in 2026.

However, net profit attributable to controlling shareholders fell in both 2024 and 2025, and the gap with operating profit persists, so whether the operating recovery reaches shareholders is the central question.

Own-development sales rose 116.2% in the first half of 2026 and gained share, but the pace of the sales market recovery and cost ratio trends will determine the outcome.

Securities firms cite own-development expansion and civil works revenue as grounds for improvement, and whether those forecasts hold will be visible in the 2026 annual report and 2027 quarterly results.
