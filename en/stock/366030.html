02

Business structure

Nine & Company, founded in 2006, is a plus-size women's apparel company. Its flagship brand 09WOMEN (formerly Gonggu Woman) is supported by ACTIRABLE, WENEED, ZEROLINE and JOGUN SHOP, with categories spanning daily women's wear, functional fitness wear, underwear and men's casual wear.

The company runs a direct-to-consumer model in which it handles planning, production and distribution in-house.

In September 2026 it announced a reorganization that split out beauty, media and entertainment, and professional investment into three wholly owned subsidiaries, leaving the parent to focus on fashion and an integrated lifestyle platform.

It has started building ixandco.com, a platform linking subsidiary beauty products and AI content, aimed at women in their 20s to 40s. The fashion side plans a new brand covering all body types and a marketplace for outside brands.

Peers in the same sector such as Handsome, F&F, LF and Shinsegae International run brand-portfolio businesses, while Youngone and Hansae are production-led, so the closest comparison is a small, body-specific D2C operator. Segment revenue shares should be checked against the segment disclosures in the annual report.

04

Earnings analysis

Revenue on a DART-filed basis fell from KRW 58.7 billion in 2022 to KRW 53 billion in 2023, KRW 49.9 billion in 2024 and KRW 45 billion in 2025, a decline of 9.7% in 2023, 5.9% in 2024 and 9.7% in 2025. 2022 was the largest year in the 2022–2025 span and 2025 the smallest.

Operating profit fell from KRW 12.7 billion in 2022 to KRW 10.5 billion in 2023, KRW 8.1 billion in 2024 and KRW 6.8 billion in 2025, with declines of 17.0%, 22.9% and 16.0% in those years, so operating profit has declined for three consecutive years.

The operating margin moved from 21.6% in 2022 to 19.8%, 16.2% and 15.1%, with operating profit falling faster than revenue.

Profit attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 11.3 billion in 2022, KRW 10.1 billion in 2023, KRW 7.86 billion in 2024 and KRW 7.45 billion in 2025, while net income including non-controlling interests was KRW 7.4 billion in 2025, a different figure.

Operating cash flow fell from KRW 10.5 billion in 2022 to KRW 6.4 billion in 2025. At the end of 2025, total liabilities were KRW 5.93 billion, total equity was KRW 82.4 billion, equity attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 82.8 billion, and the debt ratio was 7.2%.

On a quarterly basis, revenue was KRW 9.93 billion in Q3 2025 and KRW 12.8 billion in Q4 2025, up 29.2% from the prior quarter, before falling 35.3% to KRW 8.3 billion in Q1 2026.

Q2 2026 revenue of KRW 14.1 billion was the highest of the disclosed quarters, with operating profit of KRW 1.89 billion and profit attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 8.02 billion.

Q3 2026 revenue was KRW 10 billion, up 1.0% year on year, but the quarter produced an operating loss of KRW 2.54 billion and a net loss attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 2.09 billion.

In Q1 and Q2 2026, profit attributable to controlling shareholders far exceeded operating profit, so sales and operating profit alone do not explain the profit trend. The non-operating items and one-off factors behind that gap should be checked against the detailed income statement in the 2026 annual report.

05

Industry analysis

Plus-size women's apparel depends on size-specific product planning and fit, which gives the category a customer base distinct from general women's wear, and the company itself positions body-fit clothing at the center of its business.

Because the customer base is tied to specific body types, growth depends more on repeat purchases and channel efficiency than on broad market expansion.

Among sector peers, Handsome, F&F, LF and Shinsegae International have grown through multi-brand portfolios, while Youngone, Hansae and Hyosung TNC run production- and fabric-led models. Nine & Company's in-house D2C structure can protect margins but also carries a heavier fixed-cost burden.

External cycle data was not used here, so the company's own revenue path serves as the proxy: sales declined from 2023 through 2025, then moved up and down quarterly, reaching KRW 12.8 billion in Q4 2025 and KRW 14.1 billion in Q2 2026.

Whether this reflects recovering demand or the effect of new launches and promotions still needs to be separated. Demand in the category is sensitive to price and to competition in online channels, so repeat purchase rates and average order value in the core customer base should be read alongside the revenue trend.

06

Outlook

The company's stated direction is to become a lifestyle group built on fashion.

In its September 2026 reorganization it plans to split out beauty, media and entertainment, and professional investment into wholly owned subsidiaries, while the parent concentrates on strengthening fashion and running an integrated platform.

The CEO has described the aim as building a structure in which several businesses reinforce one another around fashion, similar to Musinsa. On the fashion side, the company plans to upgrade 09WOMEN and launch a new brand covering all body types, but no launch timing or scale has been disclosed.

A marketplace for outside brands is still at the concept stage. The ixandco.com platform has started construction and targets women in their 20s to 40s with fashion, beauty and content in one place.

On the positive side, adding commissions from outside brands could diversify revenue, and beauty and content subsidiaries could reduce dependence on apparel.

On the negative side, new subsidiaries and platform development front-load staff and marketing costs, so it needs to be confirmed whether the third-quarter operating loss is linked to these investments.

The next checkpoints are segment revenue and operating profit in the 2026 annual report, the launch schedule for the new brand, and the go-live timing of ixandco.com. Those three items will show whether the restructuring is translating into reported results.

08

Bull factors

A low-leverage balance sheet

Total liabilities were KRW 5.93 billion and total equity was KRW 82.4 billion at the end of 2025, with a debt ratio of 7.2%. The debt ratio stayed in a low range of 6.6%, 5.2%, 8.5% and 7.2% across 2022 to 2025.

Limited reliance on borrowing gives the company room during the restructuring, even though one loss quarter has occurred.

A rebound in quarterly revenue

Q4 2025 revenue of KRW 12.8 billion rose 29.2% from the prior quarter. Q2 2026 revenue of KRW 14.1 billion was the highest among the disclosed quarters, with operating profit of KRW 1.89 billion and profit attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 8.02 billion.

Revenue returning to higher levels after the Q1 2026 drop can be read as a sign that some product appeal is holding. The quarter-to-quarter swings are large, so it is too early to treat this as a trend.

Options from broader business scope

The company has created three wholly owned subsidiaries for beauty, media and entertainment, and professional investment. A marketplace for outside brands and the ixandco.com platform are also under development. The rationale is to spread revenue sources beyond a single apparel line. If subsidiary results become visible, the revenue mix could shift away from apparel over time.

Bear factors

Three consecutive years of declines

Revenue fell from KRW 58.7 billion in 2022 to KRW 45 billion in 2025, and operating profit fell from KRW 12.7 billion to KRW 6.8 billion. Both declined for three consecutive years from 2023 through 2025. The operating margin also dropped from 21.6% to 15.1%. With profitability falling alongside sales, any recovery has to show up in quarterly results.

A return to losses in Q3 2026

Q3 2026 revenue of KRW 10 billion rose only 1.0% year on year, yet the quarter produced an operating loss of KRW 2.54 billion and a net loss attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 2.09 billion. Losses appearing while sales were flat put cost structure under scrutiny.

The gains of the first two quarters of 2026 were reversed in the third quarter, so the durability of profit has not yet been shown.

Slowing cash generation

Operating cash flow fell from KRW 10.5 billion in 2022 to KRW 10.2 billion in 2023, KRW 9.87 billion in 2024 and KRW 6.4 billion in 2025. Cash generation has weakened along with profit.

Setting up new subsidiaries and building the platform may consume cash in the same period, so the balance between investment spending and cash generation matters.

Risk factors

Ownership and management change

The largest shareholder changed to CCGI in June 2026, and the company changed its name from Gonggu Woman to Nine & Company around the same time. Because the business is being restructured under new ownership, the strategy's direction and pace have not yet settled. Changes in management can shift business priorities and cost allocation at the same time.

Quarterly earnings volatility

Operating profit moved from KRW 839 million in Q1 2026 to KRW 1.89 billion in Q2 and an operating loss of KRW 2.54 billion in Q3. Profit attributable to controlling shareholders swung from KRW 4.67 billion in Q1 and KRW 8.02 billion in Q2 to a net loss of KRW 2.09 billion in Q3.

Swings this large reduce confidence in annual profit estimates, and the causes of the gap between operating and attributable profit need to be confirmed in the annual report.

Execution risk in expansion

Beauty, media and entertainment, and professional investment differ from plus-size apparel in customer base and operating model. Whether synergies between the subsidiaries will translate into revenue has yet to be tested.

It also remains to be seen whether new brands and the marketplace draw on existing customers or bring in new ones. If headcount and marketing spending rise before revenue, a return to profitability could be delayed.

Overall view

Nine & Company's revenue fell from KRW 58.7 billion in 2022 to KRW 45 billion in 2025, its operating margin declined from 21.6% to 15.1%, and it swung to an operating loss in the third quarter of 2026. Its balance sheet is light, with a debt ratio of 7.2% and total equity of KRW 82.4 billion.

There are signs of recovery, including Q2 2026 revenue of KRW 14.1 billion and profit attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 8.02 billion, but in Q3 revenue rose only 1.0% and the quarter turned into a loss.

The four-quarter total of profit attributable to controlling shareholders is KRW 13.1 billion, yet quarterly swings are large enough that it is hard to treat as an annual run rate.

The September 2026 split into beauty, media and investment subsidiaries and the ixandco.com platform are attempts to change a fashion-centered structure, and their costs may appear first.

On the strength side are the balance sheet and the quarterly revenue rebound; on the weakness side are three years of declines, the Q3 loss and weaker cash generation.

The next checkpoints are segment results and non-operating items in the 2026 annual report, the first post-split revenue mix in the Q1 2027 quarterly report, and the launch timing of new brands and the platform.
