02

Business structure

Tascom is a KONEX-listed maker of in-vitro diagnostic medical devices. It is described as a specialist in in-vitro diagnostic devices, and although the market groups it under electronics and components, its actual business is medical devices.

Its flagship product is SimplexTAS, a portable point-of-care testing device. In an April 2024 interview, the CEO said the device was being exported to hospitals and pharmacies in 20 countries. A user applies blood drawn from a fingertip, selects the test, and gets a result in about 13 minutes.

The coverage at that time cited 17 test items, and the company planned to expand them to 40. Adoption by prominent European football clubs such as Bayern Munich was also reported.

The present Tascom was formed through a merger of Yido Bio and the former Tascom. Yido Bio had been developing a stem-cell therapy for type 1 diabetes. As of 2021 reporting, pen needles and syringes accounted for 58% of sales and glucose test strips and similar items for 42%.

The CEO then said most glucose meters were sourced from i-SENS, a leading player in the field, and resold. That reliance on distributed products helps explain the volatility in later results.

03

Recent trends

At its annual general meeting on March 26, 2026, Tascom approved the financial statements for its 12th fiscal period. Standalone revenue for 2025 was KRW 2.311 billion. The operating loss was KRW 1.767 billion and the net loss was KRW 1.699 billion.

Total assets were KRW 2.154 billion against total liabilities of KRW 3.334 billion, which means full capital impairment.

For the 2024 comparison, FnGuide shows revenue of KRW 6.5 billion, an operating loss of KRW 1.5 billion and a net loss of KRW 1.7 billion. Revenue in 2025 was well below that level. One factor was the board decision to halt pen needle and syringe sales after the sales contract with Becton Dickinson Korea ended. That line made up KRW 2.031 billion of first-half 2024 revenue of KRW 3.392 billion.

Fundraising continued. A rights issue filing described the sale of 740,740 common shares at KRW 2,700 each, raising about KRW 2.0 billion. The scheduled payment date was January 22, 2026. No disclosure on 2026 quarterly results or the post-issue capital position could be confirmed, so neither is reflected here.

The shares closed at KRW 3,900 on October 27, 2026, unchanged from the prior session, for a market capitalization of KRW 22.6 billion. The 52-week range runs from KRW 1,800 to KRW 5,280. Average three-month volume was only 22 shares, so trading is extremely thin.

Outlook

No disclosure on 2026 quarterly results could be confirmed, so the outlook is qualitative only. The first variable is rebuilding the revenue base. Higher SimplexTAS sales and more test items are the central levers, and the company has stated a plan to expand test items from 17 to 40.

Domestic approval is another variable. According to the company's online shop notice, the product is sold with certification in Europe, while Korean MFDS certification is in progress. The timing of that approval could not be confirmed.

The second variable is the capital structure. The company was in full capital impairment at the end of 2025, and the effect of the KRW 2.0 billion rights issue scheduled for payment in January 2026 must be checked in quarterly filings. Repeated rights issues or convertible bonds would dilute existing shareholders.

Third, the shareholder base is shifting. On April 30, 2026, a KAI-affiliated unicorn investment partnership (No. 8) reported an increase of 490,000 shares. Earlier, on April 9, Smilegate Investment reported a reduction of 257,069 shares. These are facts about ownership transfers and do not by themselves indicate a price direction.

Finally, a move to KOSDAQ remains a long-term task. The company once stated a goal of completing a transfer listing to KOSDAQ by 2023. As of October 2026 the stock still trades on KONEX. No disclosure on clinical progress for the cell-therapy pipeline could be confirmed.

05

Bull factors

Differentiated point-of-care technology and overseas channels

SimplexTAS has a built-in centrifuge that removes the need for add-on equipment, and the company describes it as the first POCT to eliminate blood-cell interference. Management cited exports to 20 countries. Adoption by European professional sports clubs suggests uses beyond hospitals. These figures rely on press coverage quoting the company.

Narrower losses in 2024

FnGuide reports that in fiscal 2024, revenue fell but gross profit improved, and the operating loss narrowed by 62.7%. This shows that a better margin structure is achievable. On business metrics, there is room for further margin improvement. The operating loss widened again in 2025, however.

Continued funding from existing investors

The rights issue filing lists a KRW 300 million third convertible bond subscribed by KAI-Tion Unicorn Partnership No. 7. In April 2026, the affiliated No. 8 partnership reported adding 490,000 shares.

The fact that existing investors have taken part in funding is one clue to the company's ability to secure working capital. It reflects financing history rather than business performance.

06

Bear factors

Loss of a core product line and a revenue slump

Pen needles and syringes were the largest contributor to first-half 2024 revenue, but sales were halted when the contract ended. Standalone revenue for 2025 was KRW 2.311 billion, well below the KRW 6.5 billion FnGuide shows for 2024. How much SimplexTAS can fill the gap has not been confirmed. The episode exposed the fragility of a business reliant on distributed products.

Full capital impairment and accumulated losses

On a 2025 standalone basis, total assets of KRW 2.154 billion against total liabilities of KRW 3.334 billion mean full capital impairment. Total equity was negative KRW 1.180 billion. Per FnGuide, the 2024 debt ratio had already exceeded 800%. As long as operating losses continue, the burden of recapitalization may recur.

Low liquidity and a delayed listing-transfer goal

Average three-month volume is about 22 shares, so even small orders can move the price. KONEX also limits information access and ease of trading. The company has said it intends to move to KOSDAQ, yet it remained on KONEX as of October 2026. Meeting transfer-listing requirements will be difficult while losses persist.

07

Risk factors

Macro

The company says it exports to 20 countries. A small medical-device maker with a large export share is exposed to exchange rates, overseas demand and the purchasing capacity of distribution partners. Whether sales are concentrated in particular regions or customers has not been confirmed. Its reliance on external borrowing also makes it sensitive to changes in financing conditions.

Regulatory and business

According to the company notice, Korean MFDS certification is still in progress. A delay would push back domestic sales expansion. The plan to add test items also requires development and approval for each item. Whether the cell-therapy program has entered clinical stages has not been confirmed, so uncertainty is high.

Financial and dilution

The rights issue filing described 740,740 shares being issued at KRW 2,700 each. The same filing lists a short-term loan of KRW 877.6 million from MiCo BioMed USA and a KRW 300 million convertible bond. Further share or convertible issuance could dilute existing shareholders. Funding for repaying maturing borrowings also warrants monitoring.

08

Overall view

Tascom is a KONEX-listed in-vitro diagnostics company with point-of-care technology and overseas channels but a fragile financial base. For 2025 on a standalone basis, it reported revenue of KRW 2.311 billion and an operating loss of KRW 1.767 billion, and it was in full capital impairment.

The halt of pen needle and syringe sales, which carried a large revenue share in the first half of 2024, appears to be linked to the revenue decline.

On the bullish side are SimplexTAS exports, plans to add test items, the narrower losses of 2024 and continued participation by existing investors. On the bearish side are the sharp revenue drop, capital impairment, thin trading liquidity and potential dilution from repeated financing.

No disclosure on 2026 quarterly results or the capital position after the rights issue could be confirmed, so neither is reflected. Readers should first check later quarterly filings for any revenue recovery, the outcome of capital raising and progress on MFDS certification.
