02

Business structure

Pyung HWA Industrial is an automotive parts maker founded in 1950 under the predecessor Pyung Hwa Rubber Industrial. It absorbed the vibration-control specialist Fabco in 2014 and absorbed Seoil through a no-capital-increase merger on August 1, 2023.

Its main products are vibration-control parts, hoses and air suspension components, supplied to major domestic automakers. Vibration-control parts are rubber components that absorb vibration, and hoses are rubber tubes that carry fluids.

Overseas, the company serves local markets through subsidiaries in the United States, China and India. One analysis report (2024 ver.) puts the product mix at 53.5% vibration-control, 41.0% hoses, 3.4% air suspension and 2.0% special vehicle parts, so two product groups account for most sales.

A 2019 first-quarter securities filing showed the Hyundai Motor group at 49% of hose product sales. That data is from 2019, so current customer shares may differ. R&D spending in 2024 equaled 2.09% of revenue. DN Automotive is named as a competitor in the same analysis.

The business is directly exposed to the auto industry and depends on heavy capital investment and long-standing ties with automakers.

04

Earnings analysis

On a disclosed basis, revenue went from KRW 519.9 billion in 2022 to KRW 564 billion in 2023 (up 8.5% year on year), KRW 588.4 billion in 2024 (up 4.3%) and KRW 626.1 billion in 2025 (up 6.4%), rising for three consecutive years from 2023 to 2025. Of the 2022 to 2025 period, 2025 was the largest year.

Operating profit rose from KRW 5.73 billion in 2022 to KRW 9.94 billion in 2023 (up 73.3%) and KRW 14.1 billion in 2024 (up 41.5%), then fell to KRW 13.3 billion in 2025 (down 5.1%). The operating margin climbed from 1.1% in 2022 to 2.4% in 2024 before easing to 2.1% in 2025.

Revenue has grown into the 600 billion won range, but the margin remains in the low single digits. Attributable net income bottomed at KRW 3.63 billion in 2023 (down 40.3%), then rose to KRW 3.83 billion in 2024 and KRW 15.7 billion in 2025 (up 309.7%), growing for two consecutive years.

In 2025 operating profit declined while attributable net income rose sharply, so the two measures moved in opposite directions. Net income equals attributable net income in each of the four years on the disclosed basis, so no non-controlling interest gap appears.

Operating cash flow was KRW 19.2 billion in 2022 and KRW 29.3 billion in 2023, turned to a net outflow of KRW 2.99 billion in 2024, and recovered to KRW 19 billion in 2025. The liabilities-to-equity ratio fell from 471.1% in 2022 and 482.0% in 2023 to 312.1% in 2025.

At end-2025, total equity was KRW 62.1 billion and total liabilities were KRW 193.9 billion.

Quarterly attributable net income peaked at KRW 9.12 billion in the fourth quarter of 2025, then fell to KRW 8.49 billion in the first quarter of 2026, KRW 7.38 billion in the second quarter and KRW 851 million in the third quarter, declining for three consecutive quarters.

Third-quarter 2026 revenue was KRW 158.2 billion, down 0.8% year on year, and operating profit was KRW 1.67 billion, down 8.2%. Attributable net income for the last four quarters totals KRW 25.8 billion.

Reports indicate operating and net losses persisted from 2018 to 2020, so the profit period from 2022 onward reads as a recovery from that stretch.

05

Industry analysis

Pyung HWA Industrial operates in the auto parts sector, which is directly shaped by automaker production and demand. A securities filing describes the business as directly affected by the auto industry and notes that auto parts require heavy capital investment and continuous joint development with vehicle makers.

On the industry backdrop, a company profile from the corporate monitoring service describes automakers expanding global sourcing and pressing for price cuts, making profitability harder to secure.

The same profile says tighter environmental and safety rules are lifting demand for electrical components, and that wider adoption of hybrid and electric vehicles is expected to sharply increase related parts demand.

The company's vibration-control and hose lines are used in vehicle vibration and fluid systems, so how electrification affects volumes in these lines can be checked once product-level sales are disclosed. Listed peers in the sector include Hyundai Mobis, Hanon Systems, DN Automotive, HL Mando and Seoyon E-Hwa.

At a market capitalization of KRW 42.5 billion, the company is small relative to these peers. Its focus on vibration-control parts and hoses gives it a different demand exposure from multi-product parts makers.

Third-quarter 2026 revenue fell slightly year on year, and whether that reflects softer vehicle demand combined with pricing pressure is a point to watch.

Bull factors

Earnings recovery

Sustained revenue growth

Revenue rose from KRW 519.9 billion in 2022 to KRW 626.1 billion in 2025, growing for three consecutive years. The corporate monitoring profile cites tighter rules and hybrid and electric vehicle expansion as drivers of electrical parts demand. This demand backdrop supports the top-line growth case.

Balance sheet improvement

The liabilities-to-equity ratio fell from 482.0% in 2023 to 312.1% in 2025. Operating cash flow of KRW 19 billion in 2025 widens room to reduce reliance on borrowing. The overseas structure through the US, China and India subsidiaries remains a path for expansion.

Bear factors

Quarterly profit slowdown in 2026

Attributable net income was KRW 851 million in the third quarter of 2026, down 66.6% year on year. It has declined for three consecutive quarters since the fourth-quarter 2025 peak. The last four quarters total KRW 25.8 billion, so the slowdown is visible in the recent figures.

Thin margins and price pressure

The operating margin slipped to 2.1% in 2025 from 2.4% in 2024. Automakers' expanding global sourcing and pressure for price cuts limit room to widen margins, which forms the core of the bear case. Revenue growth has translated into a low share of operating profit.

Liabilities against equity and cash flow swings

Total liabilities at end-2025 were KRW 193.9 billion, more than three times total equity of KRW 62.1 billion. Operating cash flow turned to a KRW 2.99 billion net outflow in 2024, showing volatility. When financial burden coincides with earnings swings, the buffer is limited, which is a bear factor.

Risk factors

Overseas subsidiary performance gap

If sales at the US, China and India subsidiaries fail to offset changes in domestic automaker volumes, group revenue will remain more tightly tied to domestic production plans. The pace at which overseas units win local customers and the stability of their operations could shape the range of revenue growth. Deterioration in overseas subsidiary earnings could also limit the extent of any consolidated profit recovery.

Product mix shift from electrification

A product lineup centered on vibration-control parts and hoses may see its demand mix change during the shift to electric vehicles. Moving the portfolio toward electrical components requires equipment and R&D investment, which could weigh on near-term margins. The earnings path will depend on the pace of the transition and the scale of that investment.

Return to past loss range

Reports indicate operating and net losses persisted from 2018 to 2020. If the profit recovery since 2022 cannot be sustained within automaker volumes and production plans, the company could return to the loss range of that earlier period.

In a business that requires heavy capital investment, a sales decline could accelerate a slide back into operating losses.

Overall view

Pyung HWA Industrial's revenue has grown for three consecutive years since 2022, reaching KRW 626.1 billion in 2025, and attributable net income rose 309.7% to KRW 15.7 billion in 2025, a recovery in earnings.

However, the operating margin slipped from 2.4% in 2024 to 2.1% in 2025, and the thin-margin structure remains. Quarterly trends softened in 2026. Third-quarter attributable net income fell to KRW 851 million and has declined for three consecutive quarters.

The liabilities-to-equity ratio fell to 312.1% at end-2025 from 482.0% in 2023, but total liabilities of KRW 193.9 billion still exceed total equity of KRW 62.1 billion. On the industry side, demand growth for electrical and electrification-related parts coexists with automaker price-cut pressure.

The fact that market capitalization sits below book net assets is a starting point for valuation interpretation. On the regular-report calendar, the fourth-quarter results in the 2026 annual report and the first-quarter 2027 results are the next checkpoints.
