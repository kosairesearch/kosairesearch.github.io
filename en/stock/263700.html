02

Business structure

Carelabs is a digital healthcare company centered on mobile healthcare and beauty-care platforms. Founded in 2012 and listed on KOSDAQ in 2018, it owns subsidiaries including the telehealth reservation platform Goodoc and the cosmetic-procedure information platform Babitalk.

Goodoc connects users with hospital reservations and remote care, while Babitalk is an app for sharing cosmetic procedure information. The company's business units are platforms, digital advertising marketing, and CRM software solutions for medical institutions, which it says work together to create synergies.

As of Q1 2019, the revenue mix was 32% platforms, 37% healthcare digital marketing, 14% healthcare solutions and 16% social network. The largest shareholder changed to a new holding company as of the January 2023 report, and the company subsequently pursued management efficiency measures.

At its March 27, 2026 shareholder meeting, the company said it would make senior care the first year of full-scale new business, and it added business purposes such as health functional foods, cosmetics and quasi-drugs manufacturing and distribution to its articles of incorporation. It has also said it plans to link data accumulated by Goodoc and Babitalk with AI.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual revenue fell from KRW 87.6 billion in 2022 to KRW 81.8 billion in 2023 (down 6.6% year on year), rebounded to KRW 83.2 billion in 2024 (up 1.7%), and declined to KRW 81.7 billion in 2025 (down 1.8%). Among 2022–2025, 2022 was the largest year by revenue and 2025 the smallest.

Operating loss widened from KRW 6.38 billion in 2022 to KRW 11.6 billion in 2023, then narrowed to KRW 4.46 billion in 2024 and KRW 2.88 billion in 2025. The operating margin moved from -7.3% in 2022 to -14.2% in 2023, then improved to -5.4% in 2024 and -3.5% in 2025.

Attributable net loss shrank from KRW 26.1 billion in 2022 to KRW 24.5 billion in 2023 and KRW 20.3 billion in 2024, and the company swung to attributable net profit of KRW 19.2 billion in 2025. Net profit including non-controlling interests, at KRW 19.5 billion, is a separate figure from attributable net profit.

The company's March 2026 disclosure attributed the 2025 swing to management efficiency, which reduced losses, and to higher non-operating income from an asset disposal.

The gap between an operating loss of KRW 2.88 billion and attributable net profit of KRW 19.2 billion suggests non-operating items, not core earnings recovery, drove the swing.

Quarterly, revenue of KRW 21.2 billion and an operating loss of KRW 587 million in Q3 2025 were followed by a Q4 2025 operating loss of KRW 5.71 billion on revenue of KRW 14.9 billion (down 29.3% from the prior quarter).

Q1 2026 revenue of KRW 20.5 billion (up 37.4% quarter on quarter) produced operating profit of KRW 675 million, but Q2 turned to an operating loss of KRW 723 million on revenue of KRW 17.4 billion.

Q3 2026 revenue of KRW 18.6 billion (down 11.9% year on year) came with a wider operating loss of KRW 3.56 billion, and attributable net losses over the latest four quarters total KRW 7.61 billion. One research site put first-half 2026 revenue down 16.9% year on year.

It attributed weakness to lower demand for high-priced procedures at Babitalk amid an economic slowdown, and to labor supply imbalances in elder and nursing care.

05

Industry analysis

Carelabs' core businesses are Goodoc, a telehealth reservation platform, and Babitalk, a cosmetic-procedure information platform. Demand for remote medical reservation services rises and falls with the broader demand for non-face-to-face care.

Growth in non-face-to-face medical demand was cited as a driver of strong results in the past, but revenue has since declined from KRW 87.6 billion in 2022 to KRW 81.7 billion in 2025.

On the beauty side, an economic slowdown has reduced demand for high-priced procedures, which has been identified as a pressure point for Babitalk. In elder and nursing care, poor working conditions and institutional limits have deepened labor supply imbalances.

Senior care was shaped in a September 2023 interview, when the CEO said the company was preparing a care-town business aimed at active seniors who are not eligible for long-term care benefits. The same interview set a target of opening a first site in 2025, with the site still under review.

After being acquired by a Wonik group entity, the company pursued management efficiency and sold some business units. In terms of cycle, the business appears to be in a phase of seeking profitability recovery after the boost from non-face-to-face demand faded.

Competition in healthcare platforms centers on winning users against other platforms, and the company is positioning its data and AI services as a differentiator.

Outlook

The company's medium-term direction centers on combining senior care and other new businesses with data from its existing platforms.

At its March 2026 shareholder meeting it declared senior care the first year of full-scale new business, and it added manufacturing and distribution purposes to its articles of incorporation.

Whether the first-site target set in a September 2023 interview, for 2025, has actually been pursued should be checked against upcoming annual and shareholder meeting materials.

In the near term, Q4 2026 results will shape the direction of full-year operating results, since the Q3 2026 operating loss widened to KRW 3.56 billion. The first checkpoint is how 2026 annual revenue compares with KRW 81.7 billion in 2025 in the FY2026 annual report.

It is also necessary to check whether operating results extend the losses recorded from 2022 through 2025. The timing of revenue contributions from AI-linked data services and senior care has not yet been presented in company disclosures.

With the debt-to-equity ratio down to 43.9% in 2025, the financial burden of new-business investment is smaller than in 2024. However, because the 2025 profit swing relied on non-operating items, whether the earnings structure shifts toward core operations is the key point to confirm.

2025 profit swing and capital base

Total equity at end-2025 was KRW 74.6 billion and total liabilities KRW 32.8 billion. The retained capital base can be read as a factor in assessing capacity for new-business spending.

Senior care and data-linked plans

The amendment to the articles of incorporation provides a basis for manufacturing and distributing products in new areas such as health functional foods. Plans to link data from Goodoc and Babitalk with AI are read as an expansion built on existing platform assets.

Continued revenue decline

Quality of the profit swing

Whether core profit recovery continues must be confirmed from 2026 full-year operating results.

Risk factors

Core profitability

The operating margin has improved but remains negative, and operating losses continued in Q2 and Q3 2026. If costs are not reduced while revenue declines, losses could persist. The FY2026 annual report's operating results will show the extent of this.

New business execution

Senior care was presented in a September 2023 interview with a target of opening a first site in 2025. Labor supply imbalances in elder and nursing care could constrain execution. The investment scale and revenue model of the new business are items to confirm in periodic reports.

Earnings quality and ownership

The 2025 profit swing relied heavily on non-operating items, so a similar effect may not recur. The largest shareholder changed as of the January 2023 report, and ownership shifts could affect management direction. It is necessary to check how much of the profit came from one-time asset disposal gains.

Overall view

Carelabs recorded operating losses from 2022 through 2025, but the size of its operating loss and its operating margin have improved since 2023. The 2025 swing to attributable net profit of KRW 19.2 billion was driven by higher non-operating income and should be read separately from any core profit recovery.

In 2026, a Q1 operating profit was followed by operating losses in Q2 and Q3, with the Q3 loss widening to KRW 3.56 billion. Revenue in 2025 was KRW 81.7 billion, below 2022, and Q3 2026 revenue fell 11.9% year on year.

Financially, the debt-to-equity ratio was 43.9% and total equity KRW 74.6 billion at end-2025, a lighter burden than in 2024. The senior care business and the added business purposes signal direction, but the timing of revenue contributions has not yet been presented.

The next checkpoints are full-year operating results and the Q4 loss in the FY2026 annual report, and whether the new businesses contribute revenue.
