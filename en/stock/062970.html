02

Business structure

Korea Advanced Materials was founded in 1999 in Gwangju as an optical communications equipment maker. Built on planar lightwave circuit (PLC) core technology, it makes optical power splitters and arrayed waveguide gratings (AWG) and supplies them to domestic telecom operators and global markets.

The company is widening the use of PLC chips and AWG products by mounting them in data-center transceivers across several industries, and it is expanding its lineup with optical receiver modules and AWG chips for transceivers inside data centers.

The product mix centers on AWG, portable optical testers and optical line (OSP) modules, and as of December 2025 export shares were 99.75% for AWG, 93.19% for portable optical testers and 88.53% for OSP modules, per the WiseReport company monitor.

Its customer base covers three major domestic telecom operators and global markets, and according to the same monitor as of April 2026, evaluation of DEMUX AWG for a new overseas customer has been completed and mass production preparations are under way.

On the competitive side, new manufacturers entering the data-center AWG market have weakened price competitiveness. The company is shortening development cycles for high-end product families to keep supplying customers.

In quantum security, it launched the post-quantum cryptography (PQC) orchestration platform Quantum Safe and is focusing on commercializing quantum key linkage equipment.

It also received silicon photonics-based temperature sensor technology from the Korea Photonics Technology Institute, broadening its core technology base. The business is moving from optical components toward security software and sensor technology.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual revenue fell from KRW 21.3 billion in 2022 to KRW 14.4 billion in 2023 (down 32.3% year on year), KRW 6.5 billion in 2024 (down 54.9%) and KRW 3.58 billion in 2025 (down 45.0%), declining for three consecutive years from 2023. 2022 was the largest year and 2025 the smallest within 2022 to 2025.

Operating profit of KRW 827 million in 2022 turned into an operating loss of KRW 3.02 billion in 2023, widened to KRW 4.8 billion in 2024, and narrowed to KRW 3.42 billion in 2025.

Operating margin was 3.9% in 2022, -21.0% in 2023, -73.7% in 2024 and -95.4% in 2025, so in 2025 the loss shrank in absolute terms but margin worsened because revenue fell faster.

The company attributed the 2025 revenue decline to intensifying market competition and to provisions and disposal losses on previously produced products.

Losses were also affected by a derivative valuation loss on convertible bonds after the share price rose in early 2025, which the company said is a non-cash accounting loss.

Net loss attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 4.03 billion in 2023, KRW 14.3 billion in 2024 and KRW 12.8 billion in 2025, three consecutive years of losses. Operating cash flow was KRW 1.52 billion in 2022 and negative in 2023 (-KRW 3.08 billion), 2024 (-KRW 2.95 billion) and 2025 (-KRW 1.57 billion).

Quarterly revenue was KRW 855 million in Q3 2025 and KRW 641 million in Q4 2025 (down 25.1% from the prior quarter), the low point, followed by KRW 1.06 billion in Q1 2026, KRW 1.19 billion in Q2 2026 and KRW 2.11 billion in Q3 2026, rising for three straight quarters.

Operating losses were recorded in five straight quarters, reaching KRW 2.11 billion in Q3 2026.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders turned to KRW 1.48 billion in Q2 2026 before a net loss of KRW 3.35 billion in Q3 2026 returned the company to the red, and the four-quarter attributable net loss from Q4 2025 through Q3 2026 totals KRW 3.52 billion.

Revenue recovery is visible, but whether it translates into better profitability has not yet shown up in the Q3 2026 results.

05

Industry analysis

Domestic optical communication stocks have moved together on expectations of expanding AI data-center infrastructure investment, showing market attention to demand for optical components.

News coverage has also raised expectations for continued demand for transmission network upgrades as 5G standalone (SA) deployment and rising data traffic coincide. The company is widening its product family with optical receiver modules for data-center transceivers and AWG chips in line with this trend.

However, a WiseReport company monitor noted as of April 2026 that new manufacturers entering the data-center AWG market have weakened price competitiveness.

In quantum security, the PQC platform and quantum key linkage equipment are presented as core business lines, and shipments of quantum cryptography communication products to overseas customers are rising, according to the same source.

News reports described the company's quantum cryptography equipment and PQC commercialization as building technical entry barriers. On the cycle, revenue has risen for three straight quarters from the fourth-quarter 2025 low, but on an annual basis it remains far below the KRW 21.3 billion recorded in 2022.

In optical component business structure, TAIHAN Fiberoptics is a comparable peer, and the company's competitiveness rests on its PLC core technology and AWG supply track record. Funding capacity and the pace of scaling production remain constraints on market expansion.

06

Outlook

The confirmed business plan includes KRW 8.9 billion in facility funds for replacing AWG core manufacturing and packaging lines and introducing quantum cryptography equipment such as exposure systems.

According to a Korean media analysis, the investment is aimed at restoring unit-price competitiveness and consolidating a first-mover position in public-sector and overseas quantum security markets. The same analysis cited an internal funding plan that projects year-end 2026 cash at KRW 22.74 billion.

Evaluation of DEMUX AWG for a new overseas customer, with mass production preparations, and rising shipments of quantum cryptography communication products were reported by the WiseReport company monitor as of April 2026.

The official launch of Quantum Safe and the transfer of silicon photonics temperature sensor technology are part of plans to broaden the product family into quantum security and sensors.

News coverage has raised expectations that 5G SA deployment and rising data traffic will lead to demand for transmission network upgrades. However, data-center AWG faces weaker price competitiveness, and the company is responding by shortening development cycles for high-end product families.

The next checkpoints are whether revenue growth seen in Q3 2026 continues in later quarters and whether DEMUX AWG mass production appears in the revenue mix. Whether facility investment and fundraising translate into a smaller operating loss can be checked in the profit structure of the 2026 annual report.

Annual revenue fell from KRW 21.3 billion in 2022 to KRW 3.58 billion in 2025, and 2025 revenue was down 45.0% year on year. A Korean media analysis described 2022 as the peak and said 2025 revenue fell 83.2% over that span.

Three straight years of declines from 2023 show that the quarterly rebound has not yet shown up in annual results.

Quarterly losses and volatility

Operating losses have run for five straight quarters from Q3 2025 through Q3 2026, widening to KRW 2.11 billion in Q3 2026. Net income attributable to controlling shareholders turned positive at KRW 1.48 billion in Q2 2026 but returned to a loss of KRW 3.35 billion in Q3. The four-quarter attributable net loss from Q4 2025 through Q3 2026 totals KRW 3.52 billion.

Competition and funding pressure

New manufacturers entering data-center AWG have weakened price competitiveness, and the company attributed its 2025 revenue decline to intensifying competition. Operating cash flow was negative in 2023 (-KRW 3.08 billion), 2024 (-KRW 2.95 billion) and 2025 (-KRW 1.57 billion).

A Korean media analysis said the interest coverage ratio of -6.01 times meant operating activities alone could not cover financing costs, and it also raised the risk of designation as a managed stock.

10

Risk factors

Product concentration and pricing

Revenue is concentrated in optical components such as AWG, portable optical testers and OSP modules, whose export shares are high, close to 90% for several products. The company is exposed to overseas orders and currency swings that affect profitability.

With new entrants weakening price competitiveness in data-center AWG, unit price declines could further pressure operating margin.

Financial and liquidity

Operating cash outflows persisted from 2023 through 2025. A Korean media analysis put the interest coverage ratio at -6.01 times and cited the risk of designation as a managed stock after three years of operating losses and cash outflows.

If the KRW 8.9 billion facility investment and funding plans are not executed as scheduled, financial pressure could increase.

Governance and dilution

In September 2024 the largest shareholder changed to DeepMind Platform and the company name changed to Korea Advanced Materials, altering its governance. A derivative valuation loss on convertible bonds added to the 2025 net loss, and accounting results can swing with the share price. If planned rights offerings proceed, the increased share count would dilute per-share metrics.

Overall view

Korea Advanced Materials is an optical components maker whose annual revenue fell from KRW 21.3 billion in 2022 to KRW 3.58 billion in 2025, with three consecutive years of losses from 2023. Revenue rose over the first three quarters of 2026, to KRW 1.06 billion in Q1, KRW 1.19 billion in Q2 and KRW 2.11 billion in Q3.

However, the Q3 2026 operating loss widened to KRW 2.11 billion and the net loss attributable to controlling shareholders returned at KRW 3.35 billion. The Q2 2026 swing to attributable net income of KRW 1.48 billion lasted only one quarter.

The debt ratio fell to 41.5% at the end of 2025 from 477.1% in 2024, but a Korean media analysis attributes much of the change to equity effects such as convertible bond conversions.

Bullish factors include the revenue recovery, the Quantum Safe launch and overseas DEMUX AWG mass production preparations, while bearish factors include weaker AWG pricing, negative operating cash flow and the risk of designation as a managed stock.

Valuation reads through the absence of a PER and a market value at a premium to net assets, and the interpretation changes considerably depending on whether profitability turns.

The next checkpoints are the 2026 preliminary annual results expected in early February 2027, the 2026 annual report due by the end of March 2027, and the Q1 2027 results expected in mid-May 2027.
