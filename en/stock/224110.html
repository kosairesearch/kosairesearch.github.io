Business structure

Atec Mobility was spun off in 2015 from Atec's transportation card solutions division and later relisted on KOSDAQ as a mobility-focused company. Its core products are transport-related hardware such as in-vehicle payment terminals, ticket vending machines, bus driver terminals and taxi payment terminals.

Beyond hardware manufacturing, the company adds software development and maintenance services to offer integrated ITS (intelligent transport systems) solutions. Some data sources classify the main business segment as mobility alone based on second-quarter 2026 revenue.

Its customers are centered on public transport buyers, including multiple domestic bus operators under transit card system contracts. In 2026, contracts recorded included an upgrade of station automation equipment on the Suin-Bundang Line for Korea Rail Network Authority and supplies to Incheon bus terminals.

The company argues that its edge lies in offering a full package of hardware, software and maintenance rather than terminal production alone. An industry observer in a press report described it as a rare company that can supply terminals together with software and maintenance.

A 2024 report said its portfolio had also extended to automation devices such as shared bicycle terminals and unmanned ordering kiosks. In late 2025, the company said it was focusing on autonomous driving technology and data-driven traffic optimization to address mobility-as-a-service (MaaS).

Replacement demand for transit terminals and the expansion into unmanned automation equipment read as the two pillars of the business.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual revenue rose from KRW 150.5 billion in 2022 to a peak of KRW 201 billion in 2023, a 33.6% increase, then fell to KRW 104.2 billion in 2024 (down 48.2%) and KRW 64.3 billion in 2025 (down 38.3%), two consecutive annual declines.

Operating profit was largest in 2023 at KRW 15.4 billion, up 135.4%, before falling to KRW 7.87 billion in 2024 and KRW 6.17 billion in 2025.

The operating margin moved from 4.3% in 2022 to 7.6% in 2023, 7.6% in 2024 and 9.6% in 2025, so the ratio improved even as sales shrank, although the figures alone do not pin down the cause.

A 2024 press report attributed the 2023 growth to subsidiaries acquired in 2022 being consolidated for a full year and to rising orders for replacing aging transit card terminals.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders went from KRW 4.31 billion in 2022 to KRW 6.44 billion in 2023 and KRW 10.8 billion in 2024, then fell 93.7% to KRW 685 million in 2025.

Total net income including non-controlling interests was also KRW 685 million in 2025, and the company cited changes tied to the disposal of subsidiary investment shares as the main driver.

In 2024, total net income of KRW 19.7 billion exceeded the controlling-shareholder figure of KRW 10.8 billion, and the gap reflects non-controlling interests. Operating cash flow fell from KRW 15.4 billion in 2022 and KRW 18.2 billion in 2023 to KRW 2.41 billion in 2024, then turned to -KRW 2.4 billion in 2025.

The debt ratio at the end of 2025 was 37.1%, down from 67.9% in 2022 and 79.2% in 2023, with total liabilities of KRW 25.8 billion and total equity of KRW 69.5 billion.

Quarterly results show operating profit of KRW 4.36 billion on revenue of KRW 23.1 billion in the third quarter of 2025, followed by a fourth-quarter operating loss of KRW 35.32 million on revenue of KRW 18.1 billion.

The first quarter of 2026 returned to operating profit of KRW 69.85 million on revenue of KRW 12 billion.

The second quarter had revenue of KRW 11.4 billion and operating profit of KRW 500 million, and the third quarter had revenue of KRW 15.9 billion and operating profit of KRW 1.86 billion, so operating profit rose for two consecutive quarters.

Third-quarter revenue was still down 31.0% and operating profit down 57.4% from a year earlier, and net losses attributable to controlling shareholders over the latest four quarters total KRW 1.49 billion.

A 2026 analysis noted that vehicle card issuer sales rose in the first half but higher cost burdens weighed on profitability.

Industry analysis

Demand for transit terminals depends on policy investment and the replacement of aging equipment. A 2024 press report said transit card terminals installed in the mid-to-late 2000s are aging and being replaced in sequence.

In October 2023, Research Alum analyzed that the government's mobility innovation roadmap and the promotion of the autonomous driving industry could benefit Atec Mobility.

Industry trends cited by the company include stricter environmental regulation and expanding eco-friendly policies that support public transport and smart transport infrastructure investment. The company also describes recurring maintenance and replacement demand as intelligent transport systems spread.

This structure offers steady demand, but it also means revenue can swing sharply depending on when public orders land.

Revenue fell from KRW 201 billion in 2023 to KRW 64.3 billion in 2025, and quarterly revenue moved from KRW 12 billion in the first quarter of 2026 to KRW 11.4 billion in the second and KRW 15.9 billion in the third.

Five quarters of data are too short to say whether the cycle has bottomed, so the direction needs confirmation in further quarters. On competitive positioning, the company points to its ability to supply hardware, software and maintenance together as its differentiator.

Autonomous driving and ITS expansion are cited as long-term themes, but their revenue contribution is not yet separable in the disclosed figures. Whether industry growth turns into reported earnings will show up in how quickly awarded contracts are recognized as revenue.

Public transport contracts continue

Continued public-sector orders could cushion revenue during a trough. How quickly these contracts convert into revenue is the key to recovery.

Shareholder returns and insider share purchases

Bear factors

Revenue down for a second year, still lower in Q3

Revenue fell for two straight years, to KRW 104.2 billion in 2024 and KRW 64.3 billion in 2025. A press report based on the quarterly filing said cumulative revenue for January to September 2026 fell 14.8% from a year earlier.

Third-quarter 2026 revenue of KRW 15.9 billion was also 31.0% lower than the prior-year quarter. Quarterly revenue rose from KRW 12 billion in the first quarter to KRW 15.9 billion in the third, but it has not returned to the prior-year level.

Loss quarters and accumulated net losses

The fourth quarter of 2025 swung to an operating loss of KRW 35.32 million. Net losses attributable to controlling shareholders over the latest four quarters total KRW 1.49 billion. Operating cash flow also turned from KRW 2.41 billion in 2024 to -KRW 2.4 billion in 2025.

Operating profit rose for two quarters, but whether net income and cash flow are improving in the same direction needs more quarters to confirm.

Cost burden and swings from disposal gains

An analysis of the first half of 2026 said vehicle card issuer sales rose, but higher cost burdens cut operating profit and weakened profitability. The disposal of subsidiary investment shares, cited as the main driver of the 2025 net income decline, should be separated from operating activity.

Non-controlling interests also shape how net income is read, as the gap between KRW 19.7 billion in 2024 total net income and KRW 10.8 billion attributable to controlling shareholders shows.

Risk factors

Revenue swings tied to order timing

Revenue has moved sharply with public orders and the timing of contract recognition. The fall from KRW 201 billion in 2023 to KRW 64.3 billion in 2025 is the clearest example. Quarterly revenue in 2026 also varied widely, at KRW 12 billion, KRW 11.4 billion and KRW 15.9 billion. If large contracts cluster in particular quarters, the direction of quarterly results can change quickly.

One-off gains and subsidiary stake disposals

The large decline in 2025 net income, both total and attributable to controlling shareholders, was attributed mainly to changes from disposing subsidiary investment shares. Such disposal results can obscure the profitability of core operations.

The gap between total net income of KRW 19.7 billion and controlling-shareholder net income of KRW 10.8 billion in 2024 shows how non-controlling interests complicate reading earnings.

Share liquidity and ownership concentration

As of November 3, 2026, related parties of the largest shareholder held 34.31%, and the free float ratio was 62.09%. Foreign ownership was 3.48%, a low level. In a shareholder base concentrated in a few holders, the share price can respond sharply to changes in supply and demand.

Overall view

Atec Mobility combines a past trend of two consecutive annual declines in revenue and operating profit after the 2023 peak with a recent trend in which quarterly operating profit turned positive in 2026 and then rose for two consecutive quarters.

In 2025 revenue was KRW 64.3 billion, operating profit KRW 6.17 billion and net income attributable to controlling shareholders KRW 685 million, and the operating margin of 9.6% was above the 4.3% of 2022.

On the other side, third-quarter 2026 revenue of KRW 15.9 billion was down 31.0% from a year earlier, and net losses attributable to controlling shareholders over the latest four quarters total KRW 1.49 billion.

Bullish factors include the recovery in quarterly operating profit, a growing set of public transport contracts, a debt ratio that eased to 37.1%, and shareholder-related moves such as treasury share cancellation and insider share purchases.

Bearish factors include two years of revenue declines, loss-making quarters and a swing to negative operating cash flow, and a net income structure that depends heavily on cost burdens and disposal gains.

On valuation, no PER is calculated, so asset and shareholder return measures such as net assets and dividends are the main references. The next checkpoints are annual revenue and fourth-quarter operating profit in the 2026 annual report and whether revenue rebounds in the first-quarter 2027 report.
