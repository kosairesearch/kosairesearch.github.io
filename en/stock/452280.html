02

Business structure

Hansun Engineering, founded in 2012, makes instrumentation fittings and valves and supplies its S-LOK brand products to petrochemical, shipbuilding and offshore, defense, semiconductor, hydrogen, energy storage and aerospace customers.

Its components control fluid flow and measure pressure and temperature inside industrial plants, and the company says they are designed for high-pressure, high-temperature, cryogenic and ultra-high-purity gas service.

After its KOSDAQ listing in 2023, hydrogen fuel cells emerged as a second growth axis alongside the traditional instrumentation business.

The main example is plumbing modules supplied for solid oxide fuel cell (SOFC) systems of U.S. company Bloom Energy, and a Korea Economic Daily report in early November 2026 described the company as a first-tier supplier to Bloom Energy.

Ultra-high-purity fittings and valves for semiconductor equipment makers in Korea and abroad are also a growing line. In its first-quarter 2026 results commentary, the company linked revenue growth to surging demand for fittings, valves and modules used in hydrogen fuel cells.

Demand from oil and gas and shipbuilding customers continues as well. The company emphasizes a high-mix, low-volume production system that lets it respond flexibly to shifts in demand. It has also secured overseas vendor registrations with PETRONAS, ADNOC and NIOC, and a CRN certification in Canada.

04

Earnings analysis

Based on filed results, revenue rose from KRW 41 billion in 2022 to KRW 47.8 billion in 2023 (up 16.6% year on year), KRW 48.5 billion in 2024 (up 1.7%) and KRW 63.2 billion in 2025 (up 30.2%), with growth in each year from 2023 to 2025.

Operating profit fell from KRW 7.17 billion in 2022 to KRW 6.19 billion in 2023 (down 13.6%) and KRW 4.92 billion in 2024 (down 20.5%), then rose to KRW 9.75 billion in 2025 (up 98.1%). The operating margin declined from 17.5% in 2022 and 13.0% in 2023 to 10.1% in 2024, then recovered to 15.4% in 2025.

The company said higher sales in its core and new business segments eased the burden of fixed costs and improved profitability. Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders reached KRW 8.94 billion in 2025, up 114.9%, and net profit including non-controlling interests was also KRW 8.94 billion.

Operating cash flow was KRW 1.43 billion in 2025, down from KRW 2.2 billion in 2024, so cash generation lagged the profit increase.

In the first quarter of 2026, revenue was KRW 22.4 billion and operating profit was KRW 6.21 billion, an operating margin of 27.7%, but the company posted a net loss attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 5.13 billion.

The non-operating items that separated the operating profit from the net loss should be checked in the periodic report notes.

In the second quarter, revenue was KRW 23.6 billion, operating profit was KRW 5.6 billion (down 9.7% from the prior quarter), and net profit attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 2.03 billion, a return to profit.

Third-quarter revenue of KRW 21.4 billion fell 9.5% from the prior quarter but rose 32.2% year on year, and operating profit of KRW 3.76 billion fell 32.8% from the prior quarter. Over the last four quarters (Q4 2025 to Q3 2026), the combined net loss attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 688 million.

05

Industry analysis

Demand from end markets runs along two lines. One is the fuel-cell market, driven by AI expansion and rising data-center power needs, as U.S. company Bloom Energy expands solid oxide fuel cell (SOFC) distributed power projects that also lift demand for fittings and valves.

The other is the established base of oil and gas, shipbuilding and offshore, and semiconductor customers, which Korea Investment & Securities said in a May 2026 report continues to hold up well.

The same report said the market's central focus is the revaluation of Hansun Engineering as part of the Bloom Energy value chain. Korea Investment & Securities also reported that Bloom Energy raised its 2026 revenue growth guidance to about 80% at its first-quarter results.

A shipbuilding upcycle is cited as having broadened the order base. In competition, the company differentiates on high-mix, low-volume production and overseas certifications within the instrumentation fittings and valves category.

However, as sales concentrate on Bloom Energy, the pace of investment by downstream customers has a larger effect on results. Its ultra-high-purity supply to semiconductor customers is expanding, but its revenue share is not confirmed in filings. In cycle terms, the fuel-cell investment upturn overlaps with steady demand from established industries.

06

Outlook

The company's 2026 annual revenue guidance is KRW 72 billion, which Korea Investment & Securities cited in a May 2026 report, adding that completion of facility investment would lift total capacity to about KRW 80 billion.

A report in the same period carried a headline citing capacity expansion of up to KRW 150 billion, so the figures differ across reports.

Simply adding filed revenue for the first three quarters of 2026 gives KRW 67.4 billion, meaning roughly KRW 4.6 billion more would be needed in the fourth quarter to reach the guidance.

The new plant is located in Nakseong Industrial Complex in Busan, and a May 2026 report said construction was nearly complete and the plant was in mass-production testing.

The company plans to start full operation of the new plant within 2026, and it intends to use the existing plant for capacity expansion and profitability improvement after the move. The plant is fully automated for internal logistics, which the company expects to support profitability.

In an early November 2026 Korea Economic Daily interview, CEO Lee Je-hun said the company expects additional orders from Bloom Energy and is preparing a contract with a major U.S. technology company, and he expected earnings to keep rising in 2027 while the company reviews further equipment investment.

These are management statements, so the timing and signing of contracts should be confirmed through disclosures.

Bull factors

Expanding demand within the Bloom Energy value chain

Korea Investment & Securities reported that Bloom Energy raised its revenue growth guidance to about 80%. The company is described as a first-tier supplier to Bloom Energy, and its CEO said additional orders and a contract with a major U.S. technology company are in progress. A 27.7% operating margin in the first quarter of 2026 shows that this demand has reached profit.

Recovery in operating margin

The operating margin fell from 17.5% in 2022 to 10.1% in 2024 and recovered to 15.4% in 2025. Operating profit rose 98.1% to KRW 9.75 billion in 2025, and the company attributed this to easing fixed-cost pressure.

Operating profit reached KRW 6.21 billion in the first quarter of 2026, the highest quarterly level in the filed series.

Preparation for capacity expansion

A May 2026 report said the new Nakseong plant in Busan had completed construction and was in mass-production testing, and the company plans full operation within 2026. The plant's fully automated internal logistics is expected to support profitability. The company's 2026 revenue guidance is KRW 72 billion.

09

Bear factors

Dependence on a single key customer

Korea Investment & Securities identified higher deliveries to Bloom Energy as the main driver of recent growth. The more sales depend on one downstream customer, the more quarterly results can swing with its investment pace and contract timing.

The company is broadening its customer base into semiconductors, oil and gas, and shipbuilding, but the revenue share of each segment is not confirmed in filings.

Volatility in quarterly profit

The company posted a net loss attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 5.13 billion in the first quarter of 2026. It returned to profit of KRW 2.03 billion in the second quarter, but third-quarter operating profit of KRW 3.76 billion fell 32.8% from the prior quarter.

Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders was only KRW 126 million in the fourth quarter of 2025, and the combined net loss over the last four quarters is KRW 688 million.

Balance sheet and cash flow

The debt-to-equity ratio rose from 34.0% in 2024 to 105.7% in 2025, and total liabilities of KRW 72.2 billion exceeded total equity of KRW 68.3 billion at the end of 2025. Operating cash flow fell to KRW 1.43 billion in 2025 even as net profit rose. With quarterly profit uneven in 2026, the higher debt burden needs to be watched.

Risk factors

Customer and order concentration

If investment schedules at Bloom Energy or other downstream customers slip, delivery timing and revenue recognition could be delayed as well. Additional orders and a contract with a major U.S. technology company are at the management-statement stage, and signing and size have not been confirmed. Concentration risk remains until diversification progress is visible.

New plant start-up and expansion timing

A May 2026 report said the new plant was in mass-production testing, and full operation is planned within 2026. A delay would push the effect on the KRW 72 billion guidance and capacity expansion into the following year. Reported capacity figures also differ between KRW 80 billion and KRW 150 billion, so the basis needs checking.

Capital structure and convertible bonds

In May 2026 the company decided to acquire and retire KRW 6.3 billion of its third series of private convertible bonds. Some analysts say overhang concerns have eased, but total liabilities exceed total equity, and further fundraising or convertible-related gains and losses could affect quarterly results. The composition of non-operating items should be checked in the periodic reports.

Overall view

Hansun Engineering is shifting from instrumentation fittings and valves toward supplying plumbing modules for hydrogen fuel cells.

Its filed 2025 results show revenue of KRW 63.2 billion, operating profit of KRW 9.75 billion and an operating margin of 15.4%, a profit recovery, while the debt-to-equity ratio rose to 105.7%.

The operating margin was 27.7% in the first quarter of 2026, but the same quarter showed a net loss attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 5.13 billion, and operating profit fell in the third quarter.

Filed revenue for the first three quarters of 2026 totals KRW 67.4 billion against the company's KRW 72 billion guidance. Demand growth within the Bloom Energy value chain is a positive factor, while dependence on a key customer and the new plant's start-up timing remain negatives.

The items to confirm are the new plant's start of full operation by the end of 2026, whether additional Bloom Energy orders are signed, and the full-year results and non-operating items in the 2026 annual report.
