Report overview

Revenue Growth Ahead of Profit Recovery; Acquisition Effects in Focus

After swinging to a net loss in 2025, ADforus's revenue has risen for four straight quarters through Q3 2026 and operating profit has stayed in the black, but the contribution of the recent acquisition and the durability of profits have not yet been confirmed in periodic filings.

    Full-year 2025 filed results show revenue of KRW 30.6 billion (down 3.6% year on year), operating profit of KRW 2.42 billion (down 20.7%), and a controlling-shareholder net loss of KRW 2.07 billion, a swing into loss.

  2. 2

  3. 3

02

Business structure

ADforus was founded in 2014 and runs an advertising platform business built on its own data platform, focused on performance marketing that is optimized around metrics such as clicks and conversions.

Its main business lines are advertising platforms, e-commerce and advertising agency services, and it currently operates six platforms, including CubeX.

CubeX, launched in July 2025, uses AI machine learning models to analyze traffic, and the company says platform profitability improved by more than 250% after the AI upgrade.

Its client base started with gaming and has expanded to e-commerce, travel, finance and lifestyle, and at the time of its listing the company said long-term contracts account for more than 70% of its business.

Overseas distribution relies mainly on Google Ad Exchange under its Google Certified Publishing Partner (GCPP) status, through which the company said it served ads in more than 30 countries at the time of listing.

It later signed a contract with Pangle, the ad platform run by ByteDance, and joined the ecosystem of Nasdaq-listed AppLovin. In June 2026 the company acquired a brand strategy firm, which reportedly posted 2025 revenue of KRW 35.8 billion and operating profit of KRW 2 billion.

After the deal, the company said it is integrating brand strategy consulting, creative planning, AI orchestration, data analysis and media execution into a single business structure.

Competitively, the company presents its differentiation as a platform-based model that generates revenue from data and technology while reducing reliance on manpower. On the other hand, the concentration of clients in consumption-sensitive sectors such as e-commerce and travel is a structural weakness.

04

Earnings analysis

On a full-year basis, filed results show a broad decline from 2024 to 2025. Revenue fell 3.6% to KRW 30.6 billion from KRW 31.7 billion, operating profit dropped 20.7% to KRW 2.42 billion from KRW 3.05 billion, and the operating margin narrowed from 9.6% to 7.9%.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders swung from KRW 3.11 billion to a net loss of KRW 2.07 billion, while net income including non-controlling interests moved from KRW 3.19 billion to a net loss of KRW 1.92 billion.

The company attributed the operating profit decline to listing-related accounting and legal costs and other expenses, and said a one-off cost of KRW 4.55 billion tied to the SPAC merger listing pushed net income into the red.

The debt-to-equity ratio rose modestly from 24.0% to 26.0%; total equity at year-end 2025 was KRW 37.8 billion, of which KRW 35.6 billion belonged to controlling shareholders, and total liabilities were KRW 9.82 billion. Operating cash flow declined from KRW 3.27 billion to KRW 2.38 billion.

The quarterly trend runs in a different direction from the annual figures.

Revenue rose from KRW 6.48 billion in Q3 2025 to KRW 6.85 billion in Q4 2025, KRW 7.82 billion in Q1 2026, KRW 12.5 billion in Q2 2026 and KRW 18.6 billion in Q3 2026, with the increase continuing for four consecutive quarters from Q4 2025 through Q3 2026; Q3 2026 revenue was up 186.6% year on year.

Operating profit went from KRW 318 million in Q3 2025 to an operating loss of KRW 43.84 million in Q4 2025, then turned to a profit of KRW 676 million in Q1 2026, fell 31.5% to KRW 463 million in Q2 2026 and rose to KRW 1.25 billion in Q3 2026.

Press coverage attributed the Q2 decline to one-off costs, including M&A fees, incurred during the acquisition of the brand strategy firm.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders moved from a loss of KRW 4.11 billion in Q3 2025 to a profit of KRW 457 million in Q4 2025, then KRW 905 million, KRW 416 million and KRW 557 million in the next three quarters, and the four-quarter total from Q4 2025 to Q3 2026 is KRW 2.34 billion.

Because quarterly profit remains small, a single quarter of one-off costs can move controlling-shareholder net income sharply.

05

Industry analysis

Performance marketing is advertising whose spending is tied to measurable results such as clicks and conversions. In a July 2025 analysis, the independent research firm Research Alum described this market as having strong growth characteristics even during economic downturns.

The same analysis named partnerships with big tech, AI and data-driven business models as key challenges for advertising platforms. It introduced ADforus as one of the few domestic companies with this business model and noted high revenue and operating profit per employee within the industry.

The same analysis also pointed out that its main clients are in e-commerce and travel, sectors sensitive to domestic consumption. If consumer conditions weigh on those sectors, advertising budgets can shift accordingly.

On the travel side, the company expects the market for travel-platform advertising to grow as major Korean travel agencies expand their online business.

Overseas, the analyst firm ValueFinder reported that overseas sales reached KRW 1.5 billion in the first half of 2026, up 130% year on year, a level that already exceeded full-year 2025 overseas sales of KRW 1.3 billion.

Even so, that overseas figure remains a small share of consolidated revenue of KRW 20.3 billion in the first half of 2026.

In competition, ADforus does not try to match large platform operators on scale; it positions itself by optimizing ad delivery against advertisers' performance metrics using its own data and AI platforms.

The industry backdrop includes favorable trends such as AI adoption and big tech partnerships, while demand could still fluctuate with domestic consumer conditions and changes in platform policies.

06

Outlook

In its August 2026 earnings announcement, the company said the effects of the June 2026 acquisition and expanded global partnerships would be fully reflected in the second half of the year.

Results from the acquired firm began to show up in second-quarter consolidated figures, and the filings alone do not split out the drivers of the Q3 revenue increase.

The remaining 48% stake carries a purchase obligation only if the three-year average operating profit for 2026 to 2028 stays at or above KRW 1 billion and no annual loss occurs.

Under this structure, a sliding price applies if the three-year average operating profit reaches KRW 3.5 billion or more, and the total consideration for the remaining stake is capped at KRW 8.6 billion, according to a report in E-Today.

On shareholder returns, a KRW 3 billion buyback trust contract was signed in August 2026, and if fully executed, the treasury share ratio could rise to 35.5%. The company said it is reviewing measures to maximize shareholder value, including cancellation of its treasury shares.

In July 2026 the largest shareholder and senior executives bought 32,467 shares on the open market, equal to 0.63% of shares issued, with the CEO, CTO and COO each buying more than 10,000 shares.

Abroad, the company plans to expand its share of overseas markets using its Google partnership, its Pangle agreement and its place in the AppLovin ecosystem. On the product side, the AI upgrade of CubeX is presented as the core driver of platform profitability.

Beyond these directional statements, no specific numerical targets for upcoming quarters were confirmed in the sources reviewed.

Bull factors

Sustained quarterly revenue growth and operating profit

Filed results show revenue rising for four consecutive quarters from Q4 2025 through Q3 2026, with Q3 2026 revenue of KRW 18.6 billion up 186.6% year on year.

Operating profit stayed positive in each quarter of 2026, at KRW 676 million, KRW 463 million and KRW 1.25 billion, and press coverage tied the Q2 decline to one-off acquisition costs.

Shareholder returns and insider buying

As of the end of the first half of 2026, treasury shares stood at 22.2% of shares issued, and a KRW 3 billion buyback trust contract was signed in August 2026. In July 2026 the largest shareholder and senior executives bought 32,467 shares on the open market, equal to 0.63% of shares issued.

A cash dividend has also been paid since the listing, so shareholder returns are being pursued through several channels.

Acquisition and overseas sales growth

The brand strategy firm acquired in June 2026 reportedly posted 2025 revenue of KRW 35.8 billion and operating profit of KRW 2 billion, and integration across brand strategy and media execution is under way. An analyst report put overseas sales at KRW 1.5 billion in the first half of 2026, up 130% year on year.

The Google publishing partner status, the Pangle agreement and the AppLovin ecosystem membership are cited as the base for overseas channel expansion.

Bear factors

Annual decline and swing to loss in 2025

Full-year 2025 revenue fell 3.6% to KRW 30.6 billion, operating profit dropped 20.7% to KRW 2.42 billion, and the operating margin narrowed from 9.6% to 7.9%. Net income attributable to controlling shareholders swung to a loss of KRW 2.07 billion.

The company attributed the result to listing-related costs and a one-off expense of KRW 4.55 billion tied to the SPAC merger.

Volatility in quarterly profit

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders swung from a loss of KRW 4.11 billion in Q3 2025 to profits in the following quarters, but fell 54.0% quarter on quarter to KRW 416 million in Q2 2026.

The four-quarter total of KRW 2.34 billion is small, so acquisition costs or other one-off items can move reported results considerably. Operating profit also fell 31.5% in Q2 2026.

Client concentration in consumer-sensitive sectors

A 2025 analysis by Research Alum also flagged that the main clients are in consumption-sensitive sectors such as e-commerce and travel. If consumer spending slows, advertising budgets in those sectors could shrink and affect results directly.

Overseas sales remain a limited share of consolidated revenue, so dependence on the domestic advertising market is still high.

10

Risk factors

Client sector concentration

Clients are concentrated in consumption-sensitive sectors such as e-commerce and travel. If consumer sentiment weakens or budgets in those sectors shrink, revenue across several platforms could fall at once. A high share of long-term contracts offers a buffer, but changes in terms at renewal need separate monitoring.

Acquisition integration and earn-out structure

Following the acquisition, the company is integrating brand strategy, creative work, data and media execution into one structure. The obligation to buy the remaining 48% depends on three-year average operating profit for 2026 to 2028 and on whether an annual loss occurs. If integration is delayed, improvement in consolidated profit could also be delayed.

Dependence on external platform partners

Overseas distribution depends on the policies and contract terms of external platforms, including Google Ad Exchange, ByteDance's Pangle and the AppLovin ecosystem. If their ad policies or fee structures change, delivery conditions and profitability could shift.

The company presents its own data platform as a way to reduce this reliance, but overseas revenue is still a small share.

Overall view

ADforus recorded declining revenue and operating profit and a swing to a net loss on a full-year 2025 basis, but quarterly revenue has risen for four consecutive quarters since Q4 2025, and operating profit has stayed positive from Q1 through Q3 2026.

The June 2026 acquisition is widening the business structure, and the remaining 48% stake carries a purchase obligation only if the three-year average operating profit threshold is met.

On shareholder returns, treasury shares are high, a buyback trust contract has been signed, and insiders have bought shares on the open market.

Against that, client revenue is concentrated in consumption-sensitive e-commerce and travel, and the four-quarter total of KRW 2.34 billion in net income attributable to controlling shareholders is small enough that one-off costs can move results.

Valuation reads through the price-to-book relationship and the size of recent earnings, and how consistently profit recovers quarter by quarter is the key variable.

The next checkpoints are the Q4 2026 preliminary results, the consolidated effect of the acquired firm in the 2026 annual business report, and the acquisition's effect in the Q1 2027 quarterly report.
