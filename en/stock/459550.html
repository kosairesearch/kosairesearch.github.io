02

Business structure

ALT is a mobile device and media equipment maker established in September 2017, which listed on KOSDAQ on November 17, 2025 through a merger with IBKS No. 21 SPAC.

The company centers its mobile business on kids' phones and senior phones under its own MIVE brand, and said at the 2025 listing that it counts the three major telecom carriers among its customers.

The press described ALT as the third-largest domestic mobile device maker, a ranking drawn from media reporting rather than an official aggregate.

The business is organized into three areas: mobile, media, and AI and services (SVC), with the media area including set-top boxes for IPTV, OTT sticks, and AI speaker-type set-top boxes. Data devices in the product lineup include an AI care gateway, care robots, and LTE routers.

At the November 2025 listing, the company said it had been selected as a supplier of next-generation AI set-top boxes for a large domestic company and was developing a new product applying a Korean large language model.

Its care robot business is built on human-robot interaction technology secured through a national research project. Press reports in the first half of 2026 said ALT completed a US patent registration for a technology linking smartphones and set-top boxes to send danger alerts.

Production is based on an OEM and ODM system. At the listing, the company said it had set up subsidiaries in the United States and Japan and was in supply talks with local partners. Without segment revenue figures, the structure is mobile devices at the center with set-top boxes as the next pillar.

The company argues that the US kids' phone market has no dominant brand, which gives customized feature lineups a first-mover opportunity.

04

Earnings analysis

In 2024, ALT reported revenue of KRW 127.6 billion, operating profit of KRW 15.3 billion, and net income attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 12.2 billion, with an operating margin of 12.0%. Press reports described that year's results as a record.

In 2025, revenue fell 11.5% to KRW 112.9 billion, operating profit fell 41.4% to KRW 8.97 billion, and the operating margin declined to 7.9%. Net income attributable to controlling shareholders fell 58.5% to KRW 5.08 billion. Net income including non-controlling interests for 2025 was also KRW 5.08 billion.

The company attributed the 2025 changes to weaker sales after a customer halted operations, together with higher manufacturing costs from a strong-dollar exchange rate and rising prices for key components such as memory chips. Operating cash flow fell from KRW 20.6 billion in 2024 to KRW 2.42 billion in 2025.

The debt-to-equity ratio dropped from 77.2% in 2024 to 29.3% in 2025, and total equity at year-end 2025 was KRW 48.3 billion, all of it attributable to controlling shareholders.

On a quarterly basis, Q4 2025 revenue was KRW 24 billion, operating profit was KRW 1.62 billion, and net income attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 383 million.

Q1 2026 revenue was KRW 18.1 billion, down 24.4% from the prior quarter, and the company swung to an operating loss of KRW 788 million and a net loss attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 1.69 billion.

Q2 2026 revenue was KRW 8.29 billion, down 54.3% from the prior quarter, with an operating loss of KRW 789 million.

In Q3 2026, revenue rose 114.2% from the prior quarter to KRW 17.8 billion and operating profit of KRW 787 million turned the result positive, while net income attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 578 million, down 29.3% from the prior quarter.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders over the last four quarters (Q4 2025 to Q3 2026) totaled KRW 94.49 million.

05

Industry analysis

ALT's results appear sensitive to demand for finished handsets and to component cost conditions. For 2025, the company cited weaker sales after a customer halted operations, along with a strong-dollar exchange rate and rising prices for components such as memory chips.

Because finished-device makers feel exchange rate and component price changes directly, these cost factors also read as industry-wide pressures. On kids' phones, the press reported that the US kids' phone market is expected to grow at an annual rate of more than 10%.

The company said the market has no dominant brand, which it argues gives it a first-mover opening. In set-top boxes, supply to IPTV operators has led to selection as a supplier of next-generation AI set-top boxes for a large domestic company.

ALT falls under electronic components in industry classification, but unlike listed peers such as Samsung Electro-Mechanics and LG Innotek, which focus on components, its business centers on finished devices and set-top boxes.

The gap in market capitalization is very large, so numerical comparisons with major peers in the same sector are limited. Domestically, the press describes ALT as the third-largest mobile device maker, but its overseas market share has not yet been presented in numbers.

Shrinking profitability and quarterly losses

The operating margin fell from 12.0% in 2024 to 7.9% in 2025. Operating losses continued in Q1 and Q2 2026, and net income attributable to controlling shareholders over the last four quarters totaled only KRW 94.49 million. The return to profit so far covers a single quarter, Q3 2026.

Exchange rate and component cost pressure

The company said higher manufacturing costs came from a strong-dollar exchange rate and rising prices for components such as memory chips. For a maker of finished devices, component cost increases may not pass through to selling prices right away, which can add pressure to margins. Operating cash flow also fell to KRW 2.42 billion in 2025 from KRW 20.6 billion in 2024.

Risk factors

Customer concentration

With three major telecom carriers as key customers, a halt at any one of them flows directly into revenue. The company said weaker sales in 2025 came from a customer halting operations. How far overseas customers grow as a share of the base is the next point to watch for this risk.

Exchange rate and component prices

The company cited a strong-dollar exchange rate and higher memory and other component prices as factors raising manufacturing costs. These cost factors coincide with the decline in the operating margin in 2025. The speed at which cost changes are reflected in selling prices is a condition for recovering profitability.

Ownership concentration and small-cap trading

Right after the November 2025 listing, BDC Partners reported holding 13.21% of ALT, and Crossroad No. 6 private equity partnership reported a new 8.52% stake. The reason for these reports was the listing, and the stated purpose was passive investment. As a small-cap KOSDAQ stock, trading volume and price movements can be large.
