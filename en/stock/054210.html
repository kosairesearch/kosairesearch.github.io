Report overview

Battery Pack Pivot Tests Earnings Inflection

E-Lantec has layered new EV, ESS, and robotics battery pack businesses onto its established Samsung Electronics, Samsung SDI, and KT&G-centered mobile and e-cigarette operations, entering a phase of wider quarter-to-quarter earnings swings.

  1. 1

    2025 consolidated revenue of KRW 753.2 billion and operating profit of KRW 43.2 billion marked a recovery from 2024's weak results.

  2. 2

    After operating profit of KRW 17.5 billion in Q1 2026, both revenue and profit declined in Q2, exposing earnings volatility tied to new business ramp-up.

  3. 3

    An 8GWh EV battery pack project supplying China's Chery Automobile via LG Energy Solution's 46-series cells has been added as a new growth pillar.

  4. 4

    A new Yongin plant, built with over KRW 90 billion in investment after withdrawing from China, is expanding as the production base for mid-to-large battery packs.

  5. 5

02

Business structure

Founded in 1978, E-Lantec is an electronic parts specialist that produces battery packs, mobile phone cases, and chargers supplied mainly to Samsung Electronics and Samsung SDI, which together account for roughly 60% of consolidated revenue.

E-cigarette devices supplied to KT&G add about 26% of sales, meaning mobile products and e-cigarette devices together make up the bulk of revenue.

Battery pack R&D and mid-to-large production are handled at the Korean headquarters, while Vietnam and India subsidiaries produce mobile and e-mobility battery packs, with a combined nine assembly lines (two in Korea, four in Vietnam, three in India) delivering monthly capacity of 4.36 million packs.

The company grew from phone-case manufacturing into e-cigarette devices, home ESS, and e-bike battery packs, and has now extended for the first time into electric-vehicle battery pack production.

In mobility, it supplies electric rickshaw battery packs to Honda, with potential to extend its portable power solution format into various industrial and consumer products.

New growth areas include EV battery packs, ESS battery management systems (BMS), and mid-to-large battery packs for robotics, including a confirmed joint development with a robotics customer.

To build mid-to-large pack capacity, the company withdrew from its China plant and constructed a new facility in Yongin, Gyeonggi Province, which will serve as its domestic production hub for mid-to-large battery packs.

Lithium-ion (polymer) cells are sourced from Samsung SDI and LG Energy Solution and combined with protection circuit modules (PCM), making raw-material supply a direct driver of results.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual results peaked in 2022 with revenue of KRW 997.9 billion and operating profit of KRW 79.8 billion, then entered a downcycle through 2023 (revenue KRW 751.2 billion, operating profit KRW 30.4 billion) and 2024 (revenue KRW 556.0 billion, operating profit KRW 16.1 billion, net profit of just KRW 0.35 billion).

In 2025, results rebounded sharply to revenue of KRW 753.2 billion, operating profit of KRW 43.2 billion, and owners' net profit of KRW 26.7 billion, with the operating margin improving from 2.9% to 5.7%, a clear profit recovery.

On a quarterly basis, Q2 2025 posted an operating profit of KRW 7.5 billion but still recorded an owners' net loss of KRW 1.9 billion, before turning into a clear rebound in Q3 (operating profit KRW 15.4 billion, net profit KRW 11.5 billion) and Q4 (operating profit KRW 20.9 billion, net profit KRW 22.9 billion).

Q1 2026 delivered the strongest recent quarterly profitability with revenue of KRW 235.2 billion, operating profit of KRW 17.5 billion, and net profit of KRW 14.9 billion, but Q2 2026 revenue fell to KRW 213.5 billion with operating profit down to KRW 7.1 billion and net profit at KRW 3.2 billion, reigniting quarter-to-quarter volatility.

Per company IR materials, the Q2 2026 operating margin came in at 3.33%; revenue declined from Q1, yet operating profit still more than doubled year over year.

The same materials showed first-half 2026 revenue of KRW 249.5 billion for the mobile segment (phone cases, battery packs, chargers), underscoring that mobile remains the core of revenue.

Summed over the most recent four quarters (Q3 2025 through Q2 2026), owners' net profit reached roughly KRW 52.5 billion, a larger figure than full-year 2025, though whether the Q2 slowdown persists into coming quarters remains to be seen.

The debt ratio fell from 133.4% in 2022 to 102.7% in 2024 before rising again to 118.6% in 2025, reflecting financial-structure shifts tied to new-business investment.

05

Industry analysis

The mobile parts and e-cigarette device markets are already mature, with revenue stability hinging on relationships with a handful of large customers such as Samsung Electronics and KT&G.

Still, the European Union's move toward mandating user-replaceable battery designs for smartphones sold in the region is cited as a factor that could boost demand for finished battery packs.

By contrast, the EV, ESS, and robotics mid-to-large battery pack markets are at an early growth stage, where securing partnerships with global automakers and battery cell makers is the key competitive factor.

Domestically, the state-run power exchange's third round of ESS central contract market bidding, sized at roughly KRW 1 trillion, is intensifying order competition across the supply chain including the three major battery makers.

Competitor Hanjung NCS has also completed a North American plant to expand its ESS and EV businesses, reflecting a broader simultaneous push by domestic battery pack and module makers into mid-to-large markets.

A common industry backdrop is the growing importance of ESS as power infrastructure amid AI data center expansion and renewable energy adoption.

E-Lantec has accumulated technical experience in mid-to-large battery management through its power-tool battery pack supply to global tool makers via Samsung SDI, but remains a relative latecomer in terms of large-scale capital investment dedicated to EV and ESS.

06

Outlook

The company had targeted mass production of EV battery packs by the end of the first half of 2026, saying production would initially center on a single model supplied through LG Energy Solution to Chery Automobile in China, with customers and applied models to be expanded gradually thereafter.

Company IR materials reaffirmed plans to begin EV battery pack mass production in the second half and to expand cooperation with battery cell makers to grow volumes and win new projects.

The same materials attributed the Q2 operating profit slowdown partly to new-business investment and preparation for the Yongin plant's ramp-up, while expressing expectations for second-half growth.

In ESS, the company has a track record of supplying home ESS units to the European market since last May, and whether this reference base translates into additional customers is a point to watch.

Development of a mid-to-large battery pack with a robotics customer has been confirmed to be underway, though it remains unconfirmed whether this will lead to mass production or a supply contract.

The Yongin plant, built as a domestic production base after the company withdrew from China, will be a key indicator of how far mid-to-large battery pack capacity expands as utilization rises.

The outcome of the third round of Korea's ESS central contract market bidding is also seen as a variable that could indirectly affect downstream demand.

Bull factors

Diversification into mid-to-large battery packs

The company is broadening into EV, ESS, and robotics battery packs, moving away from a structure centered solely on mobile and e-cigarette products. An 8GWh Chery Automobile supply project using LG Energy Solution's 46-series cells could serve as an entry point into global automaker supply chains.

A track record of supplying home ESS units to Europe and joint development with a robotics customer are also cited as additional growth drivers.

Earnings recovery from the 2024 trough

Results that had fallen to revenue of KRW 556.0 billion and operating profit of KRW 16.1 billion in 2024 jumped to revenue of KRW 753.2 billion and operating profit of KRW 43.2 billion in 2025.

Net profit exceeded KRW 10 billion in both Q3 and Q4 2025, marking a clear turnaround, which continued into Q1 2026 with operating profit of KRW 17.5 billion.

Reshoring of domestic production base

By withdrawing from its China plant and investing over KRW 90 billion in a new Yongin facility, the company has brought mid-to-large battery pack production onshore. This is cited as a factor reducing exposure to geopolitical risk and tariff variables. As the new plant's operations expand, production capacity constraints on the EV and ESS businesses could ease.

09

Bear factors

Widening quarter-to-quarter earnings volatility

In Q2 2025, the company posted an owners' net loss of KRW 1.9 billion despite a positive operating profit, and in Q2 2026 revenue, operating profit, and net profit all declined from Q1. Profit and loss appear more unstable than in the core business during the early investment and ramp-up phase of new businesses. Whether this volatility persists for additional quarters remains to be confirmed.

Concentration in core customers

Samsung Electronics and Samsung SDI together account for roughly 60% of revenue, with KT&G e-cigarette devices adding about 26%, leaving high dependence on a small number of large customers. With the mobile and e-cigarette markets already mature, further growth from these segments may be limited.

Until the new businesses are firmly established, any change in relationships with existing customers could directly affect results.

Uncertainty over new-business ramp-up and profitability

EV battery pack production has started centered on a single model, with customer and model expansion not yet realized. Robotics battery packs remain at the joint-development stage with a customer, with no certainty that this will lead to mass production or a supply contract.

Overseas new-business expansion such as entry into the North American ESS market also remains at an early stage.

Risk factors

Customer and revenue concentration risk

High revenue dependence on three customers—Samsung Electronics, Samsung SDI, and KT&G—means changes in their order volumes or strategies could directly affect results. With the mobile and e-cigarette markets already mature, revenue growth potential from the existing business may be limited.

New-business execution risk

EV, ESS, and robotics battery packs are all at an early stage, and delays in mass-production schedules or failure to expand the customer base could postpone expected growth. Quarterly earnings volatility may persist until the mid-to-large battery pack business stabilizes its profitability.

Overseas production and raw-material supply risk

Mobile battery packs are produced at Vietnam and India subsidiaries, exposing the company to currency fluctuations and changes in local production conditions.

Lithium-ion cells, the core raw material for battery packs, are sourced from a small number of suppliers such as Samsung SDI and LG Energy Solution, making cell supply conditions a factor that can affect results.

Overall view

E-Lantec is at a transition point, layering new EV, ESS, and robotics mid-to-large battery pack growth pillars onto its stable mobile and e-cigarette business centered on Samsung Electronics, Samsung SDI, and KT&G.

Having passed its 2024 trough, it recovered to revenue of KRW 753.2 billion and operating profit of KRW 43.2 billion in 2025, with profit improvement continuing through Q3-Q4 2025 and Q1 2026, before revenue and profit both declined in Q2, exposing early-stage new-business volatility.

The new-business pipeline—including EV battery packs for Chery Automobile, ESS BMS supply to Europe, and joint robotics battery pack development—is diversified but largely at an early stage, making execution on mass production and customer expansion a key variable.

Expansion of the new Yongin plant's operations and trends in Korea's ESS central contract market could serve as indicators of future production capacity and downstream demand.

At the same time, the structure in which a large share of revenue depends on a small number of major customers remains unchanged, meaning the stability of the existing business and the pace at which new businesses stabilize profitability will jointly shape the future earnings path.

Further confirmation of coming quarterly results and new-business progress is warranted before drawing conclusions.
