02

Business structure

ShinwonConstruction, founded in 1983 and listed on KOSDAQ in 1994, is a mid-sized construction company that runs housing, public works, industrial and semiconductor plants, and urban redevelopment together.

Its housing business is split between the redevelopment-focused brand Achimdosi and the premium residential brand Upperhouse, which press reports credit with supporting steadier revenue on end-user demand.

The company's profile states that it is actively pursuing public works such as civil engineering, gas facility and landscaping projects to build a stable long-term earnings base.

In industrial plants it has built experience in cleanrooms, specialty gas facilities and logistics centers, and its 2024 public works orders of KRW 206.6 billion were described as standing out among peers.

In redevelopment it won the Gasoro housing projects in Wonjong-dong, Bucheon (disclosed November 2025, KRW 53.5 billion) and Geongeon-dong, Ansan in succession. Construction order backlog was reported at KRW 733 billion at the end of Q3 2025, up from KRW 385 billion at the end of Q3 2016.

Its construction capability ranking rose from 210th in 2021 to 115th in 2024 and 93rd in the 2025 assessment published in July 2025, its first entry into the top 100.

Compared with large builders such as Samsung C&T, Hyundai Engineering & Construction and DL E&C, the company is smaller, and it differentiates by combining public works, redevelopment and plant projects. The exact revenue mix by segment is a point to confirm in the next annual report.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual revenue rose from KRW 188.2 billion in 2022 to KRW 309.8 billion in 2023 (up 64.6% year on year), then fell for two years to KRW 259.8 billion in 2024 (down 16.1%) and KRW 211.3 billion in 2025 (down 18.7%).

Operating profit moved from KRW 2.03 billion in 2022 to KRW 3.13 billion in 2023 (up 53.9%) and KRW 15 billion in 2024 (up 379.9%), the largest year of 2022 to 2025, before falling to KRW 8.6 billion in 2025 (down 42.7%).

In 2024 operating profit grew even as revenue declined, and press reports credited cost reductions and a larger share of public works and plant projects, with the cost of sales ratio reported as falling from 95.8 percent to 88.9 percent.

For 2025, the company attributed the drop to fewer new projects amid a slowdown in construction activity. The operating margin was 1.1 percent in 2022 and 1.0 percent in 2023, rose to 5.8 percent in 2024 and eased to 4.1 percent in 2025.

Attributable net income peaked at KRW 8.25 billion in 2023 (up 85.9%), then fell to KRW 5.53 billion in 2024 (down 33.0%) and KRW 4.86 billion in 2025 (down 12.1%). Net income, which includes non-controlling interests, is disclosed at the same amounts as attributable net income in every year from 2022 to 2025.

Operating cash flow fell from KRW 12.9 billion in 2024 to KRW 3.41 billion in 2025.

On a quarterly basis, Q3 2025 revenue was KRW 53.5 billion with operating profit of KRW 5.88 billion, then Q4 2025 revenue rose to KRW 67.7 billion but the company posted an operating loss of KRW 996 million and an attributable net loss of KRW 4.78 billion.

Q1 2026 revenue was KRW 48.8 billion with operating profit of KRW 3.33 billion, returning to profit, followed by Q2 revenue of KRW 65.5 billion with operating profit of KRW 4.39 billion and Q3 revenue of KRW 67.7 billion with operating profit of KRW 4.62 billion.

Q3 2026 operating profit was down 21.4 percent from a year earlier. The cause of the Q4 2025 loss and the size of quarterly swings are points to confirm in the next annual report's income statement details.

05

Industry analysis

ShinwonConstruction's end markets fall into three groups: private housing sales, public procurement and industrial facility investment.

The company attributed the 2025 decline in revenue and operating profit to fewer new projects amid a slowdown in construction activity, which shows how sensitive new private orders are to the cycle. Public works, plants and redevelopment follow different procurement paths, according to the company.

Redevelopment projects depend on urban renewal policy, and the November 2025 Wonjong-dong order and the Geongeon-dong project are examples. Semiconductor and industrial plant orders tie into large industrial investment, as shown by the FAB.3 change order in 2026 and the expansion contract disclosed in October 2026.

The construction capability ranking improved from 210th in 2021 to 93rd in 2025, which raises its standing among mid-sized builders, but its revenue remains small next to large builders such as Samsung C&T, Hyundai Engineering & Construction and DL E&C.

Large builders put more weight on overseas plants and large redevelopment projects, while ShinwonConstruction focuses on domestic public, redevelopment and plant work. Contract disclosures continued through 2026, which is a flow to track, though the cycle position of private housing sales is not assessed here.

Bull factors

Continuity of contract disclosures

Contracts continued through 2026, including the KRW 42.2 billion Icheon logistics center in January, the FAB.3 project worth KRW 21.5 billion after a change order in September, and a KRW 37.5 billion expansion contract disclosed in October.

Construction order backlog was reported at KRW 733 billion at the end of Q3 2025, up from KRW 385 billion at the end of Q3 2016. Public works and redevelopment projects with different clients are spread across several procurement channels.

Earnings recovery trend

After an operating loss of KRW 996 million in Q4 2025, operating profit returned to positive at KRW 3.33 billion in Q1 2026, then rose to KRW 4.39 billion in Q2 and KRW 4.62 billion in Q3.

Press reports cited improved cumulative operating profit for January to September 2026 compared with the same period a year earlier. The operating margin reached 5.8 percent in 2024, which shows that cost ratios can improve.

Stronger financial structure

The debt-to-equity ratio fell from 142.1 percent in 2022 and 151.3 percent in 2023 to 104.0 percent in 2024 and 98.1 percent in 2025. Total equity at the end of 2025 was KRW 112.1 billion and total liabilities were KRW 110 billion.

Equity attributable to controlling shareholders matched total equity at KRW 112.1 billion, so no non-controlling interest is recorded.

Bear factors

Revenue down for two consecutive years

Revenue fell from KRW 309.8 billion in 2023 to KRW 259.8 billion in 2024 and KRW 211.3 billion in 2025, a two-year decline. The company attributed the 2025 drop to fewer new projects amid a slowdown in construction activity. Operating cash flow also fell sharply to KRW 3.41 billion in 2025 from KRW 12.9 billion in 2024.

Volatility in quarterly profit

In Q4 2025 the company posted an operating loss of KRW 996 million and an attributable net loss of KRW 4.78 billion. Attributable net income for the last four quarters totals KRW 6.54 billion, so a single loss quarter moves the total sharply. Q3 2026 operating profit was still down 21.4 percent from a year earlier, so the recovery has been uneven.

Limits to profitability

The operating margin was 1.1 percent in 2022 and 1.0 percent in 2023, rose to 5.8 percent in 2024 and eased to 4.1 percent in 2025. Operating profit fell to KRW 8.6 billion in 2025 from KRW 15 billion in 2024. With revenue in the KRW 200 billion range, profit is highly sensitive to changes in cost ratios.

Risk factors

Concentration in large contracts

The KRW 42.2 billion logistics center contract signed in January 2026 equals about 16 percent of the prior year's revenue. When a few large contracts drive quarterly results, delays or changes in scope can move revenue sharply. The disclosure notes that contract amounts may change under the construction contract terms.

Delay in redevelopment construction start

The Wonjong-dong redevelopment project has a planned 30-month construction period counted from the start date, but the start date has not been fixed. A later start would push back revenue recognition and cash collection. Payments are made on a two-month progress-billing basis, so the pace of work matters for cash flow.

Financial position and loan recovery

In April 2026 the company lent KRW 11.1 billion for land deposits in the Cheonan Unseong district project, equal to 9.93 percent of equity. The loan runs to April 10, 2027, and recovery timing could shift if the project slows. Operating cash flow of KRW 3.41 billion in 2025 is also worth reading alongside these funding commitments.

Overall view

ShinwonConstruction has widened its business from housing into public works, plants and redevelopment, and its annual results turned down in 2025 after the 2024 operating profit peak.

Revenue peaked at KRW 309.8 billion in 2023 and then fell for two years, and the operating margin was 4.1 percent in 2025, below 5.8 percent in 2024.

The company posted an operating loss of KRW 996 million and an attributable net loss of KRW 4.78 billion in Q4 2025, but operating profit returned to positive in Q1 2026 and rose in Q2 and Q3.

Contracts disclosed in 2026 include a KRW 42.2 billion logistics center, a KRW 21.5 billion FAB.3 project after a change order and a KRW 37.5 billion expansion contract, and the order backlog was KRW 733 billion at the end of Q3 2025.

Supporting factors are the contract flow and a stronger balance sheet, while the weakening factors are the two-year revenue decline, quarterly profit swings and a thin margin.

This summary does not offer an investment view, and the next points to check are the operating margin in the 2026 annual report, the Wonjong-dong construction start and the repayment of the Cheonan-related loan.
