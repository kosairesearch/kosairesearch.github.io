02

Business structure

Rainbow Robotics is a robotics platform company founded by researchers who took part in developing the KAIST humanoid HUBO. Its core business is collaborative robots, extended to dual-arm mobile robots, mobile robots and quadrupedal walking robots.

In the first nine months of 2025, consolidated sales were KRW 21.1 billion, and robot segment sales were KRW 17.6 billion, or 83% of the total.

Service revenue rose from KRW 810 million in 2024 to KRW 9.09 billion in 2025, reflecting a larger share of project-type work such as robot installation, operation and maintenance.

Most cobot sales run through roughly 45 system integration partners, managed by region and industry to limit competition, and the company also pursues direct sales in food and beverage and service robots with its in-house 'MIX' beverage system.

Samsung Electronics is a core customer: after exercising a call option in 2024, its stake rose to 35.0% and it became the largest shareholder. Samsung-related sales were KRW 10.4 billion in 2025, about 30% of annual sales, and KRW 5.68 billion in the first half of 2026, or 26.6% of the total.

The product mix and division of roles between the two companies have not been disclosed. In space, the company signed a KRW 7.83 billion contract with the Korea Astronomy and Space Science Institute to develop optical surveillance systems. In cobots, it competes with domestic listed peers such as Doosan Robotics and Robotis.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual revenue was KRW 13.6 billion in 2022, KRW 15.3 billion in 2023 (up 12.1% year on year), KRW 19.3 billion in 2024 (up 26.8%) and KRW 34.1 billion in 2025 (up 76.4%), rising for three consecutive years from 2023 through 2025. Among 2022 to 2025, 2025 was the largest year for revenue.

Operating profit of KRW 1.3 billion in 2022 turned into an operating loss of KRW 44.6 billion in 2023, and the loss narrowed to KRW 2.98 billion in 2024 and KRW 2.48 billion in 2025. The operating margin moved from 9.6% in 2022 to -292.2% in 2023, -15.4% in 2024 and -7.3% in 2025.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders went from KRW 5.78 billion in 2022 to a net loss of KRW 895 million in 2023, returned to a profit of KRW 2.14 billion in 2024, and fell 33.4% to KRW 1.42 billion in 2025.

Operating cash flow went from KRW 1.17 billion in 2022 to -KRW 9.29 billion in 2025, a widening outflow. Quarterly, Q4 2025 revenue was KRW 13 billion with operating profit of KRW 1.7 billion, turning positive, and net income attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 2.71 billion.

Q1 2026 revenue was KRW 9.06 billion, down 30.4% from the prior quarter, with an operating loss of KRW 1.57 billion as the company returned to losses. Q3 2026 revenue of KRW 13.9 billion rose 30.1% year on year, but the operating loss of KRW 1.77 billion was larger than a year earlier.

The net loss attributable to controlling shareholders over the last four quarters totaled KRW 159 million. On the drivers, Samsung Electronics volume lifted sales, with cumulative cobot sales to Samsung Electronics through Q3 2025 of KRW 6.88 billion.

Selling and administrative expenses, however, were KRW 11.1 billion in the first nine months of 2025, or 53% of sales, and inventory more than doubled from KRW 7.16 billion to KRW 15.52 billion in 2025.

05

Industry analysis

Demand for domestic collaborative robots is rising on structural shifts such as a shrinking working-age population, skilled labor shortages and higher labor costs, which have expanded demand for automation. This is the most direct backdrop to Rainbow Robotics' revenue growth.

Humanoids, by contrast, remain in the development and mass-production validation stage. The market is watching whether humanoid development and production validation translate into reported earnings. Robot partnerships or prototype unveilings do not equal mass-production orders.

Cobot sales are already booked as revenue, but the timing of full-scale humanoid production is still unconfirmed. Samsung Electronics plans to build a humanoid mass-production system and a robot data factory in Gumi, North Gyeongsang Province.

In competition, Doosan Robotics, a cobot specialist, is the closest direct comparison, while Robotis operates in education and research robots and components. Being part of the Samsung group gives Rainbow Robotics a customer and capital advantage.

Because the division of roles with Samsung has not been disclosed, how far that advantage translates into cobot share must be checked against reported results.

06

Outlook

Three confirmed items frame the company's near-term plans. First, Samsung Electronics plans a humanoid mass-production system in Gumi, and Rainbow Robotics has said it will announce confirmed details through official channels such as regulatory filings.

Second, the optical surveillance contract with the Korea Astronomy and Space Science Institute runs from September 2, 2024 to October 31, 2027, and the company expects the contract to lift the share of space business in revenue as it is recognized.

Third, cobot sales run through roughly 45 system integration partners alongside direct-sales efforts. Samsung-related sales rose from KRW 10.4 billion in 2025 to KRW 5.68 billion in the first half of 2026, showing that Samsung-related transactions are turning into revenue.

Because the division of roles with Samsung Electronics has not been disclosed, the products and sites behind Samsung-related sales are difficult to confirm from outside. The rise in inventory and receivables is a variable that could weigh on working capital during the shift to mass production. The next checkpoint is the annual operating profit and operating cash flow in the 2026 annual report.

Bull factors

Three years of top-line growth

Revenue rose to KRW 15.3 billion in 2023, KRW 19.3 billion in 2024 and KRW 34.1 billion in 2025, three straight years of growth. Sales then rose for two consecutive quarters to KRW 12.3 billion in Q2 2026 and KRW 13.9 billion in Q3 2026, with Q3 up 30.1% year on year. Analysts point to automation demand driven by structural labor shortages as the growth backdrop.

Samsung customer base and ownership

Samsung Electronics is the largest shareholder with a 35% stake. Samsung-related sales were KRW 10.4 billion in 2025, about 30% of annual revenue, and reached 26.6% of sales in the first half of 2026. Whether Samsung's planned Gumi humanoid production expands the business is the next point to watch.

Improving profit trajectory

The operating margin improved from -292.2% in 2023 to -7.3% in 2025. Q4 2025 turned positive, with operating profit of KRW 1.7 billion and net income attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 2.71 billion. The Q3 2026 operating loss of KRW 1.77 billion was smaller than the prior quarter's loss.

Bear factors

Quarterly losses persist

The company has posted an operating loss for three straight quarters from Q1 to Q3 2026, and the Q3 loss of KRW 1.77 billion was larger than a year earlier. The Q3 net loss attributable to controlling shareholders widened to KRW 1.89 billion. Q4 results are the next point to check for how the cumulative net loss develops.

Cash flow and working-capital strain

Operating cash flow shifted from an outflow of KRW 468 million in 2023 to an outflow of KRW 9.29 billion in 2025. The larger inventory tied up more working capital, which coincides with the wider cash outflow. Loss allowances on receivables also rose from KRW 1.43 billion to KRW 4.28 billion as receivables grew.

Customer concentration and fixed-cost burden

Selling and administrative expenses for the first nine months of 2025 reached KRW 11.1 billion, or 53% of sales. Samsung-related sales made up a high 26.6% of sales in the first half of 2026, while the future division of roles between the two companies has not been disclosed in detail.

Risk factors

Customer concentration risk

Samsung Electronics is both the largest shareholder and a key customer, so transaction volume and terms could concentrate in a single client. Because the division of roles and purchasing structure are not public, the durability of the business is hard to gauge from outside. A slowdown in Samsung orders could widen swings in revenue.

Valuation and share-price volatility

PER cannot be calculated because the last four quarters show net losses, and the PBR sits in a range with a large premium over book value. The stock's 52-week beta of 1.30 indicates notable price volatility. If commercialization expectations are not confirmed in earnings, pressure on the multiple could grow.

Working capital and mass-production risk

Inventory burdens and reserve provisions could weigh on reported results as the company shifts toward mass production. The timing at which humanoid development and production validation affect actual earnings may differ. If cash outflows continue, the improvement in profit could be delayed.

Overall view

Rainbow Robotics sits at a crossroads where top-line growth and profitability recovery are moving in different directions. The operating loss narrowed from KRW 44.6 billion in 2023 to KRW 2.48 billion in 2025, and Q4 2025 showed a turn to operating profit of KRW 1.7 billion.

However, operating losses have persisted for three straight quarters from Q1 to Q3 2026, and the Q3 loss was larger than a year earlier. Revenue rose for three consecutive years to KRW 34.1 billion in 2025 and rose for two straight quarters to KRW 13.9 billion in Q3 2026.

Samsung-related sales climbing to KRW 10.4 billion in 2025 and KRW 5.68 billion in the first half of 2026 provide concrete evidence of the customer base. On the other side, operating cash outflow widened to KRW 9.29 billion in 2025 and inventory more than doubled.

With the PBR in a range with a large premium over book value, the pace of profit recovery is the key variable for interpreting the share price. The next checkpoints are the full-year operating profit in the 2026 annual report and the Samsung-related sales share and cash flow in the Q1 2027 quarterly report.
