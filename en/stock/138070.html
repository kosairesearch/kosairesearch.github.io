Quarterly Profit Recovers, but Full-Year Loss Remains a Challenge

02

Business structure

Sinjin SM is a machinery parts processor founded in 2001 that specializes in standard plates cut to fixed dimensions. The company has been known for a technology that cuts plates and finishes their faces at the same time, which shortens the manufacturing process.

A 2011 press report said the company sold about 4,200 standard plate specifications and consumables such as circular saw blades and brackets through roughly 30 distributors in Korea and abroad, and cited a 76% market share in the standard plate segment that same year.

Because that share figure dates from 2011, it cannot be read as the current position. Standard plates are core components of machine structures, so the same report described demand as spanning nearly every industry, including automotive, electronics, chemicals and textiles.

In the first half of 2026, consolidated revenue of KRW 32.2 billion comprised KRW 25.3 billion of product sales and KRW 5.9 billion of merchandise sales. Merchandise sales more than doubled from KRW 2.8 billion a year earlier, while product sales rose 9.1% year on year, according to press coverage.

The company attributed the gains to higher selling prices for its products and a larger share of aluminum sales. Overseas, the company entered Japan in 2009 and then expanded into Asia, including Singapore, and in 2025 it established subsidiaries in Taiwan in March and Malaysia in May.

Its subsidiary Shinjin Materials completed liquidation in December 2025.

For new businesses, the company is pursuing precision finished parts and metal processed parts for electric vehicles (EV), and in June 2026 it decided on a new facility investment of KRW 8.15 billion at an idle site at its Jeongnam plant in Hwaseong, Gyeonggi Province.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual revenue went from KRW 66.3 billion in 2022 to KRW 59.7 billion in 2023 (down 10.0% year on year), KRW 54.6 billion in 2024 (down 8.5%), and KRW 54.4 billion in 2025 (down 0.3%), a decline in each of the three years from 2023 to 2025. 2022 was the largest year of the 2022 to 2025 period and 2025 the smallest.

Operating profit fell from KRW 6.72 billion in 2022 to KRW 3.95 billion in 2023 (down 41.1%) and KRW 1.3 billion in 2024 (down 67.0%), and in 2025 the company swung to an operating loss of KRW 383 million. The operating margin went from 10.1% in 2022 to 6.6% in 2023, 2.4% in 2024 and -0.7% in 2025.

Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders went from KRW 4.34 billion in 2022 to KRW 4.01 billion in 2023 (down 7.7%) and KRW 774 million in 2024 (down 80.7%), before turning into a controlling-shareholder net loss of KRW 379 million in 2025.

Total net profit including non-controlling interests was a net loss of KRW 412 million in 2025. The company attributed the 2025 declines in revenue and profit to a slowdown in its business environment, among other factors. The quarterly pattern has reversed direction.

The operating loss was KRW 27.64 million in Q3 2025 and KRW 449 million in Q4 2025, and then operating profit of KRW 446 million in Q1 2026 marked a return to profit. Operating profit reached KRW 1.2 billion in Q2 2026, up 169.8% from the prior quarter, and KRW 1.16 billion in Q3 2026, down 3.8% from the prior quarter.

Revenue rose for three consecutive quarters, from KRW 15.2 billion in Q1 2026 to KRW 17 billion in Q2 and KRW 17.4 billion in Q3.

Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 341 million in Q1 2026, KRW 1.13 billion in Q2 and KRW 862 million in Q3, and the total for the last four quarters (Q4 2025 to Q3 2026) was KRW 2.1 billion.

Operating cash flow declined from KRW 8.28 billion in 2022 to KRW 6.16 billion in 2023, KRW 6.56 billion in 2024 and KRW 4.19 billion in 2025. Over the same period the debt ratio fell from 44.3% to 32.7%.

At the end of 2025, total equity was KRW 77.7 billion, of which KRW 76.7 billion was attributable to controlling shareholders, and total liabilities were KRW 25.4 billion.

Industry analysis

A 2011 press report said standard plates are used across the machinery industry, so business-cycle swings are limited and revenue is relatively stable. That description rests on 2011 data, so today's demand structure cannot be read directly from it.

The company cited a slowdown in its business environment as a cause of the 2025 revenue and profit decline. Revenue rose for three consecutive quarters in 2026, and in the first half the company attributed the increase to higher selling prices and a larger share of aluminum sales.

On the demand side, after the government set out measures on battery thermal runaway on October 26, 2026, press coverage highlighted the stock as linked to flame-retardant materials for thermal runaway. That coverage concerns a market theme rather than any earnings contribution.

The EV metal processed parts and precision finished parts that the company is pursuing are new businesses tied to demand for electric vehicles and precision components. The company's proprietary technology for cutting and face-finishing in one process was highlighted as a differentiator in the 2011 report.

The 76% market share cited in that report is also a 2011 figure and offers limited evidence of competitive position today. Taken together, the company's exposure runs through capital spending and production across machinery, automotive and electronics, and recent results reflect both selling prices and product mix.
