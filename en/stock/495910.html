02

Business structure

EST designs and manufactures electrostatic chucks (ESC), components that hold wafers and display glass substrates flat using electrostatic force during processing. ESCs are known as core process parts that prevent substrates from moving or warping and keep temperature uniform.

The business is split between display ESCs and semiconductor ESCs. In display, EST supplies products for large-area OLED processes and plans to keep display ESC capacity at about KRW 25 billion a year.

In semiconductors, the business grew mainly by refurbishing used chucks to restore their performance, and it is now widening into new-product supply. Semiconductor ESC capacity is planned to rise from about KRW 20 billion a year to about KRW 50 billion by the second quarter of 2027.

On customer qualification, EST supplied 61 products to 22 potential customers for testing and received approval for 21 products from nine customers, a 95.5% approval rate as reported. The ESC market was first established by Japanese companies, and EST is challenging that position with domestic original technology.

Next-generation products include ESCs for glass core substrates used in semiconductor packaging and for OLEDoS, and EST has co-developed dedicated ESCs with Wonik IPS and others.

Company-wide, the 2028 sales target is KRW 70 billion, and semiconductor revenue is projected to grow from KRW 9.6 billion in 2026 to KRW 37.8 billion in 2028.

Recent trends

According to DART filings, first-three-quarter 2026 sales were KRW 6.76 billion, up 4.3% year on year. Operating loss for the same period was KRW 1.85 billion, a continued loss but narrower than a year earlier. Net loss was KRW 2.49 billion, also a continued loss that narrowed year on year.

Because sales growth was only 4.3%, the filings alone do not clearly isolate what drove the narrower losses. The share price closed at KRW 9,890 on the first trading day, November 4, 2026, below the KRW 12,400 offer price.

It had reached KRW 20,700 intraday before reversing, and one report said the decline came without any disclosure or clearly negative news. On November 5 the stock fell 23.66% to KRW 7,550, and by the November 6 close it was KRW 6,560, with market capitalization of KRW 91.5 billion.

Before listing, the expected market capitalization range had been KRW 147.5 billion to KRW 172.5 billion. As of November 25, 2026, the share price was KRW 3,260 (down 7.65% from the prior day), market capitalization was KRW 45.4 billion, and trading value was KRW 600 million.

04

Outlook

The next checkpoints are mainly operating indicators. The first is whether semiconductor ESC capacity actually expands to about KRW 50 billion a year by the second quarter of 2027.

The second is customer approval growth, where the company expects cumulative approved products to reach 57 by 2028 if qualification tests proceed as planned. The third is whether new orders materialize.

The CEO has said capacity expansion does not translate directly into higher sales, and that reaching a normal utilization rate takes time after equipment is installed.

EST is taking a cautious approach with Samsung Electronics, while SK hynix has been introduced to the company through its sales network, and actual transactions would require further development and evaluation. ESCs for glass core substrates target entry with a domestic customer identified as S.

For the etch-process ESC co-developed with Applied Materials, the company expects mass-production orders by June 2027. Fourth-quarter 2026 results have not yet been disclosed, and the share of semiconductor revenue in the next periodic report will show how the mix is changing.

The 2028 company-wide sales target of KRW 70 billion and the semiconductor target of KRW 37.8 billion are presented as contingent on approved product counts and customer orders.

05

Bull factors

Semiconductor business widening from refurbishment to new products

EST holds mass-production approvals for 21 products from nine customers in its semiconductor refurbishment business. Of 22 products that completed evaluation, 21 were approved, a 95.5% approval rate as reported.

The company plans to expand supply of approved products and win additional approvals to lift semiconductor sales to about KRW 37.8 billion in 2028. Adding new-product supply broadens the portfolio beyond refurbished chucks, which is cited as a positive factor.

Plan to expand semiconductor ESC capacity

Semiconductor ESC capacity is targeted to rise from about KRW 20 billion a year to about KRW 50 billion by the second quarter of 2027. IPO proceeds are earmarked for production facilities and technology development, including precision manufacturing and quality inspection equipment.

Display ESC capacity is to stay at about KRW 25 billion, so investment tilts toward semiconductors. If the facility build-out proceeds on schedule, supply capacity becomes the foundation for scaling the business.

Narrower losses and a next-generation substrate pipeline

According to DART filings, both operating loss and net loss for the first three quarters of 2026 narrowed year on year. ESCs for glass core substrates are in joint development and mass-production verification with domestic and overseas equipment makers.

OLEDoS ESCs have completed mass-production product development and are moving into customer evaluation and follow-on supply. Glass substrates for semiconductor packaging and ultra-high-resolution displays are presented as medium- to long-term growth axes.

06

Bear factors

Sales growth of only 4.3%

According to DART filings, an operating loss of KRW 1.85 billion and a net loss of KRW 2.49 billion continued in the first three quarters of 2026, and no turn to profit is shown on a year-to-date basis.

Reaching the semiconductor sales path from KRW 9.6 billion in 2026 to KRW 37.8 billion in 2028 requires a sharp acceleration in growth. If growth falls short of that path, fixed costs could weigh more heavily on profitability.

Order conversion lagging behind capacity

Management has said capacity expansion does not directly lead to higher sales and that customer orders and production staff are also needed. EST is approaching Samsung Electronics cautiously, and SK hynix has so far been introduced to the company through its sales network.

Actual transactions require further product development and evaluation to meet customer requirements. Because equipment is added before utilization rises, fixed costs could affect profitability until the plants reach normal operating levels.

Post-listing declines and small-cap liquidity

On November 5, the share price fell 23.66% to KRW 7,550, and reports said no specific disclosure or clearly negative news was identified during the decline. As of November 25, 2026, the share price was KRW 3,260, market capitalization was KRW 45.4 billion, and trading value was KRW 600 million.

In a thinly traded small cap, a limited number of trades can amplify price swings, which works as a negative factor. The wide gap from the KRW 12,400 offer price is also a psychological burden for market participants.

07

Risk factors

Macro

EST's business is tied to the semiconductor capex cycle and to the expansion schedules of its customers. If large chipmakers delay investment, approval and order timing could slip with them. Swings in the memory market directly affect the timing of demand for equipment and components.

Business and customer

The customer approval process is long, and revenue follows only when approved products receive recurring supply. Nine approving customers out of 22 potential customers means concentration and gaps in approvals could drive earnings volatility.

In a market first established by Japanese companies, EST still has to keep demonstrating technological differentiation.

Liquidity and volatility

Since listing, the share price has been highly volatile, and trading value is around KRW 600 million. At a market capitalization of KRW 45.4 billion, large trades can have an outsized effect on price. Reports describe sharp declines and rallies without disclosures, so price moves can diverge from fundamentals.

08

Overall view

According to DART filings, EST's first-three-quarter 2026 sales rose 4.3% year on year, and both operating and net losses narrowed. Its semiconductor ESC business is widening from refurbishment toward new-product supply, with 21 products reported as approved by nine customers.

Capacity is planned to reach about KRW 50 billion by the second quarter of 2027, so equipment and customer base must develop together. On the other hand, losses continue on a year-to-date basis, and with sales growth of only 4.3%, a turn to profitability is not yet visible in the filed figures.

Since listing, the share price has traded well below the offer price with sharp swings, and as of November 25, 2026, market capitalization was KRW 45.4 billion and trading value was KRW 600 million.

Because customer approvals and additional orders underpin capacity expansion, the bullish and bearish factors rest on the same set of indicators.
