02

Business structure

Aniplus is a distributor that brings Japanese animation content to domestic broadcast, OTT and theatrical channels, and operates Raftel, the largest animation-focused OTT platform in Korea.

Its broadcast segment transmits content through the cable and IPTV channels 'Aniplus' and 'Animax,' and on July 1, 2026 the company absorbed subsidiary Animax Broadcasting Korea, bringing both channels under one corporate entity.

In theatrical distribution, the company holds exclusive Korean rights to popular Japanese anime IP such as 'Demon Slayer' and 'Attack on Titan,' with box-office success feeding into merchandise sales.

Through subsidiaries, it also runs character goods and merchandise distribution as well as an exhibition business (Media & Art), expanding content distribution across multiple channels.

Its competitive landscape partly overlaps with comprehensive OTT platforms such as Netflix and Tving and the global anime-focused platform Crunchyroll, though Raftel leans on anime specialization and a domestic fan base as differentiators.

Following the merger, the company laid out a strategy to build an integrated value chain spanning IP rights acquisition, broadcast transmission and OTT streaming. It has also stated plans to move beyond IP distribution into original production, expanded online stores and a new portal service.

Given this structure, quarterly results tend to swing significantly depending on whether a major theatrical release succeeds.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual revenue rose for three consecutive years, from KRW 58.1 billion in 2022 to KRW 111.3 billion in 2023 (up 91.5% year-on-year), KRW 131 billion in 2024 (up 17.6%) and KRW 150.3 billion in 2025 (up 14.8%).

Operating profit, however, grew from KRW 9.63 billion in 2022 to KRW 22.9 billion in 2023 (up 137.5%) and KRW 25.1 billion in 2024 (up 10.0%) before declining 9.8% to KRW 22.7 billion in 2025.

Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders likewise peaked at KRW 15.6 billion in 2024 before falling 22.8% to KRW 12.1 billion in 2025. The operating margin has trended lower, from 20.5% in 2023 to 19.2% in 2024 and 15.1% in 2025.

On a quarterly basis, Q3 2025 revenue jumped 94.7% quarter-on-quarter to KRW 55.7 billion and operating profit surged 437.8% to KRW 16.4 billion, a result tied to the theatrical success of 'Demon Slayer: Infinity Castle.' Revenue then fell 34.3% to KRW 36.6 billion in Q4 2025, with the company swinging to an operating loss of KRW 2.05 billion and a net loss attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 5.41 billion.

Q1 2026 returned to profit with operating profit of KRW 2.42 billion and controlling-shareholder net profit of KRW 1.71 billion, while Q2 2026 revenue rose 12.9% year-on-year to KRW 32.3 billion and operating profit rose 7.3% to KRW 3.28 billion, even as controlling-shareholder net profit fell 56.3% quarter-on-quarter and 55.7% year-on-year to KRW 750 million.

The sum of controlling-shareholder net profit over the last four quarters (Q3 2025 through Q2 2026) was KRW 8.71 billion, suggesting that underlying quarterly profitability is relatively modest once the one-time Q3 2025 box-office boost is excluded.

Meanwhile, the debt ratio climbed steadily from 75.8% in 2022 to 111.7% in 2025, even as operating cash flow improved markedly from KRW 21.2 billion in 2022 to KRW 85.6 billion in 2025.

05

Industry analysis

Korea's animation content market shows steady demand for Japanese anime IP, with the box-office performance of theatrical releases significantly affecting the results of related companies.

'Demon Slayer: Infinity Castle' drew roughly 5.81 million cumulative admissions during its Korean theatrical run, becoming the top-grossing Japanese film ever released domestically, while its worldwide box office reached 117.9 billion yen (about KRW 1.1 trillion), making it the first Japanese film to surpass 100 billion yen globally.

Such large-IP hits tend to extend revenue streams beyond theaters into OTT releases, re-releases and merchandise sales.

In the OTT competitive landscape, comprehensive platforms such as Netflix and Tving coexist with globally focused anime platforms, and Raftel maintains its positioning as a dedicated animation platform.

In 2025, Aniplus drove a dual box-office success through 'Attack on Titan' theatrical films and 'Demon Slayer: Infinity Castle,' with Aniplus and Animax each holding exclusive rights to one of the two titles.

Given the nature of the industry, securing a lineup of major new IP titles is a key variable separating competitive positioning among players.

06

Outlook

Having completed the absorption merger of Animax on July 1, 2026, Aniplus has set a strategic direction of building an integrated value chain spanning IP rights acquisition, broadcast channel operations and OTT streaming.

The company stated it plans to move beyond IP distribution into original production, along with expanding its online store and launching a new portal service.

'Demon Slayer: Infinity Castle' was released on major OTT platforms including Netflix, Tving and Raftel on July 29, 2026, and a special one-year anniversary theatrical screening co-presented with CJ ENM took place on August 26, 2026, marking the first wide release by the merged entity.

These OTT release and re-release effects could potentially show up in third-quarter 2026 results, though specific figures are not yet available since no regulatory filing has been issued for that period. The existing broadcast channels 'Aniplus' and 'Animax' continue to operate as before following the merger.

While the company has stated an intent to strengthen its competitive position domestically and internationally through the merged entity, detailed schedules for new title lineups or original production projects have not yet been disclosed in full.

08

Bull factors

Exclusive Rights to Major Japanese Anime IP

Aniplus and its merged subsidiary Animax each hold exclusive domestic distribution rights to proven hit Japanese anime IPs such as 'Demon Slayer' and 'Attack on Titan.' 'Demon Slayer: Infinity Castle' drew approximately 5.81 million moviegoers domestically, ranking as the all-time number-one Japanese film at the Korean box office, with global box office revenue reaching JPY 117.9 billion.

Such large-scale IPs have a revenue structure that extends beyond theatrical earnings to OTT releases, re-releases, and merchandise sales.

Value Chain Integration Through the Merger

The absorption-type merger of Animax, completed on July 1, 2026, established an integrated system spanning IP sourcing, broadcasting, theatrical distribution, and OTT distribution.

The company stated that this merger would serve as an opportunity to enhance management efficiency, fully launch its in-house production business, and newly launch an online store and portal service. Operating as a single corporate entity while maintaining two broadcast channels may contribute to cost efficiency.

Improving Cash Generation

Operating cash flow surged significantly from KRW 21.2 billion in 2022 to KRW 85.6 billion in 2025, showing a clear improvement in cash-generating capacity relative to earnings volatility. This appears related to a business structure in which revenue from major box-office hits flows in as cash relatively quickly. Steady cash generation could serve as a funding source for securing new IP or investing in in-house production.

Bear factors

Widening Quarter-to-Quarter Earnings Volatility

Quarterly results show significant volatility, with revenue and operating profit surging 94.7% and 437.8% quarter-on-quarter, respectively, in Q3 2025, before swinging to an operating loss of KRW 2.05 billion in Q4.

Even after returning to profitability in Q1 2026, net income attributable to controlling shareholders in Q2 fell 56.3% quarter-on-quarter. A structure in which performance fluctuates sharply depending on the release timing of major theatrical distribution titles makes it difficult to identify quarterly trends.

Weakening Profitability Metrics

The operating margin declined from 20.5% in 2023 to 15.1% in 2025, with operating profit and net income attributable to controlling shareholders in 2025 falling 9.8% and 22.8% year-on-year, respectively.

The fact that the profit growth trend that continued through 2024 reversed in 2025 suggests a need to examine changes in cost structure or business mix.

Rising Debt Ratio Trend

The debt-to-equity ratio rose steadily each year, from 75.8% in 2022 to 97.4% in 2023, 106.4% in 2024, and 111.7% in 2025. As of the end of 2025, total liabilities stood at KRW 143.2 billion, exceeding total equity of KRW 128.2 billion.

How the company manages its financial leverage level amid the merger and new business expansion could be a key point to watch.

Risk factors

IP Licensing Dependency Risk

The company's core revenue source is based on exclusive domestic distribution rights granted by Japanese anime IP holders. If competition for securing lineups of new IP intensifies or the terms for renewing licenses for major IPs become unfavorable, this could affect the revenue structure.

A structure in which performance is heavily dependent on the box-office success of specific blockbuster titles indicates a high concentration of licensing.

Capital Raising and Dilution Risk

The company has a history of raising mezzanine funding, such as convertible bonds, during its growth process, and the possibility of further equity dilution in the course of raising funds for new business investments or acquisitions in the future cannot be ruled out. The rising trend in the debt-to-equity ratio is also a point to watch from a financial structure perspective.

Risk from Expanding Competing Platforms

If comprehensive OTT platforms such as Netflix and TVING, as well as global anime-specialized platforms, expand their investment in anime content, this could put competitive pressure on Laftel's content exclusivity and subscriber base.

If competition for content distribution rights intensifies among platforms, the cost of securing distribution rights could also rise.

Overall view

Aniplus saw revenue grow for three consecutive years from 2022 through 2025, while operating profit and controlling-shareholder net profit peaked in 2024 and declined in 2025, marking a break in the earnings growth trend.

Quarterly results swung sharply, surging in Q3 2025 on the box-office strength of 'Demon Slayer: Infinity Castle,' turning to a loss in Q4 2025, returning to profit in Q1 2026, and then showing slower profit growth in Q2 2026.

The absorption merger with Animax, completed on July 1, 2026, aims to build an integrated system spanning broadcast, OTT and IP distribution, alongside plans for original production and new platform business expansion.

The debt ratio has risen steadily, yet operating cash flow improved to KRW 85.6 billion in 2025, indicating stronger cash generation.

Given a business structure heavily dependent on the box-office performance of major theatrical releases, tracking post-merger earnings trends through the next quarterly filing will be an important point to watch.

Readers should weigh this earnings volatility and the evolving financial structure in forming their own investment judgment.
