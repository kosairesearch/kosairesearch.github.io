Revenue Grew, but Earnings Turned Volatile

Aniplus grew annual revenue to KRW 150.3 billion in 2025, but quarterly profit has swung between profit and loss since the fourth quarter of 2025, and its debt ratio has risen.

02

Business structure

Aniplus runs an anime-focused TV channel and builds content distribution and merchandising businesses on top of it. The company said its domestic reach was about 23.09 million households nationwide based on 2019 data, across cable, IPTV and satellite pay-TV.

Overseas, it said about 14 million households in Singapore, Malaysia, Thailand, Indonesia, the Philippines, Vietnam and Hong Kong received its channel as of 2020. Animation is the core business: its nine-month 2024 animation revenue was KRW 76.3 billion, up 48.4% year on year, according to press reports.

The merger with subsidiary Animax was completed on July 1, 2026, and both broadcast channels continue to operate. Press reports say the Animax acquisition gave Aniplus a share of close to 80% of domestic imported sub-culture licenses.

The company describes the combination as linking rights acquisition, broadcasting and the Laftel OTT platform. Customers are pay-TV platforms and domestic and overseas OTT operators. Drama-focused Studio Dragon and character-IP-focused The Pinkfong Company have different business structures. Segment revenue shares are outside the scope of the disclosed figures in this report.

04

Earnings analysis

Aniplus' annual results show revenue expansion alongside uneven profit. Revenue went from KRW 58.1 billion in 2022 to KRW 111.3 billion in 2023 (up 91.5%), KRW 131 billion in 2024 (up 17.6%) and KRW 150.3 billion in 2025 (up 14.8%), rising three years in a row from 2023 through 2025.

Operating income rose from KRW 9.63 billion in 2022 to KRW 22.9 billion in 2023 (up 137.5%), peaked at KRW 25.1 billion in 2024, and fell 9.8% to KRW 22.7 billion in 2025. The operating margin was 16.6%, 20.5%, 19.2% and 15.1% from 2022 to 2025, lowest in 2025.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 4.02 billion, KRW 8.91 billion, KRW 15.6 billion and KRW 12.1 billion from 2022 to 2025, peaking in 2024 and falling 22.8% in 2025.

Total net income including non-controlling interests was KRW 14 billion in 2025; the gap from the controlling-shareholder figure reflects non-controlling interests. IB Tomato reported in March 2026 that cost burdens lowered the operating margin and that the Animax merger was intended to remove duplicated costs.

Quarterly figures show sharper swings. After KRW 55.7 billion in revenue and KRW 16.4 billion in operating income in the third quarter of 2025, the fourth quarter brought revenue of KRW 36.6 billion, an operating loss of KRW 2.05 billion and a controlling-shareholder net loss of KRW 5.41 billion.

The first quarter of 2026 returned to operating profit of KRW 2.42 billion on revenue of KRW 29.6 billion. The second quarter posted revenue of KRW 32.3 billion, operating income of KRW 3.28 billion and controlling-shareholder net income of KRW 750 million.

The third quarter of 2026 recorded revenue of KRW 30.3 billion (down 45.6% year on year), operating income of KRW 1.84 billion (down 88.8%) and a controlling-shareholder net loss of KRW 1.46 billion.

At the end of 2025, total equity was KRW 128.2 billion, of which controlling-shareholder equity was KRW 119.6 billion, and total liabilities were KRW 143.2 billion.

05

Industry analysis

Demand for animation IP distribution moves with the expansion of domestic and overseas OTT operators, which the company cites as a driver of its results. Aniplus combines subscription-based channel revenue with newer distribution windows such as OTT, so shifts in both demand streams affect results at the same time.

Press reports say the Animax acquisition gave Aniplus a high share of domestic imported sub-culture licenses, which can strengthen its position in distribution negotiations. License acquisition costs are booked as cost of sales, which is one reason the 2025 operating margin of 15.1% is worth tracking.

Among peers, drama-focused Studio Dragon's earnings depend on production volume and scheduling, while character-IP-focused The Pinkfong Company leans more on licensing of toys and educational content. CJ ENM is a large broadcasting and content producer and a useful benchmark for scale.

Aniplus' differentiation lies in holding an anime channel, licenses and the Laftel OTT platform together, but its scale is much smaller than large media groups. Quarterly revenue fell from KRW 55.7 billion in the third quarter of 2025 to KRW 30.3 billion in the third quarter of 2026.

06

Outlook

The company said it plans to start full-scale self-produced content after the merger and to expand its online store and launch a portal service. The stated aim of the merger is to integrate the core animation businesses and raise operating efficiency.

Growing self-production could reduce reliance on purchased licenses, but production costs and upfront investment would likely rise first. Revenue and profitability of the new businesses are not yet reflected in disclosed results.

The third quarter of 2026 showed revenue of KRW 30.3 billion, operating income of KRW 1.84 billion and a controlling-shareholder net loss of KRW 1.46 billion, so how far merger effects reach quarterly results is still unproven.

With a combined controlling-shareholder net loss of KRW 4.41 billion over the last four quarters, the direction of profit recovery needs to be confirmed quarter by quarter. The next checkpoint is the operating margin and debt ratio in the 2026 annual report, which will also include the fourth quarter of 2026.

No specific launch schedule for the online store or portal service has been confirmed, so the timing of their effect on results depends on when the company discloses them.

08

Bull factors

Three straight years of revenue growth

Revenue rose for three consecutive years from 2023 through 2025, reaching KRW 150.3 billion in 2025. The company cites growing animation demand from domestic and overseas OTT operators. Press reports on a share of close to 80% of domestic imported sub-culture licenses point to a broader IP base.

Rising operating cash flow

Operating cash flow rose from KRW 21.2 billion in 2022 to KRW 85.6 billion in 2025, increasing every year. Cash generation strengthened even though operating income declined after its 2024 peak, so working capital movements should be read alongside it. This is a positive factual data point separate from the profit level.

Integrated value chain after the merger

The July 1, 2026 merger with Animax links IP acquisition, broadcasting and the Laftel OTT platform within one corporate entity. The company plans self-production, online store expansion and a portal service. Reducing duplicated costs through the merger is presented as a basis for margin recovery.

09

Bear factors

Slower quarterly results in 2026

Third-quarter 2026 revenue was KRW 30.3 billion, down 45.6% year on year, and operating income was KRW 1.84 billion, down 88.8%. The company swung to a controlling-shareholder net loss of KRW 1.46 billion versus a year earlier. Profit has not held steady since the fourth-quarter 2025 operating loss.

Higher debt burden

The debt ratio rose every year, from 75.8% in 2022 to 111.7% in 2025. Total liabilities of KRW 143.2 billion exceed total equity of KRW 128.2 billion at the end of 2025. With operating income lower in 2025, rising debt could constrain financial flexibility.

Cost pressure and thinner margins

The operating margin fell from 19.2% in 2024 to 15.1% in 2025. IB Tomato reported that cost burdens pulled the margin down. If license costs grow faster than revenue, margin pressure could persist, and whether the merger changed the cost structure needs to be confirmed in the next annual report.

10

Risk factors

Channel and licence dependence

Reach depends on pay-TV platforms such as cable, IPTV and satellite. The domestic and overseas household figures are older benchmarks, dating from 2019 and 2020. Declines in pay-TV subscriptions or shifts in platform scheduling could directly affect channel revenue. Intensifying competition for licenses could also raise acquisition costs.

Financial leverage

At the end of 2025, total liabilities were KRW 143.2 billion against total equity of KRW 128.2 billion. The debt ratio of 111.7% is higher than 75.8% in 2022. Operating cash flow grew, but how much goes to debt repayment and license investment needs to be tracked. Borrowing burden in a period of lower profit could reduce financial flexibility.

Execution of new businesses

The company plans to scale self-production, expand its online store and launch a portal service. These new businesses need upfront investment and staff, and monetization takes time. Whether they create synergies with the channel business or first raise costs is not yet visible in disclosed results. Clear disclosure of new-business results will be needed to judge them quarter by quarter.

Overall view

Aniplus grew revenue from KRW 58.1 billion in 2022 to KRW 150.3 billion in 2025, rising three years in a row from 2023 through 2025. Operating income, however, peaked at KRW 25.1 billion in 2024 and fell to KRW 22.7 billion in 2025, with the operating margin at 15.1%.

Quarterly results in 2026 have been mixed: a return to operating profit in the first quarter was followed by third-quarter revenue down 45.6% year on year. The debt ratio rose to 111.7%, while operating cash flow grew to KRW 85.6 billion.

The Animax merger completed in July 2026 links IP, broadcasting and OTT within one entity, and self-production and new businesses are planned. Strengths include revenue growth and rising cash flow; weaknesses include quarterly earnings volatility and financial leverage.

The next checkpoints are the operating margin and debt ratio in the 2026 annual report and first-quarter 2027 results.
