Whether automotive chip profits can hold

02

Business structure

Telechips designs automotive system-on-chip products, and its core business is in-vehicle infotainment (IVI) semiconductors that integrate information and entertainment functions inside a vehicle.

According to Pinpoint News, IVI sales in the third quarter of 2026 rose 4.0% from a year earlier and accounted for the largest share of total revenue at 65.1%.

Cockpit-related revenue, including digital instrument clusters and head-up displays (HUD), rose 52.1% over the same period and is becoming a central pillar of product diversification. Royalty revenue, which includes R&D service revenue, grew 266.3%.

SoC development service revenue from a contract with a global customer began to be recognized in the fourth quarter of 2025. The company disclosed in October 2025 an SoC development service order worth KRW 77.1 billion (about USD 54.89 million), with the contract running from October 1, 2025 to January 31, 2028.

By region, third-quarter 2026 sales in China rose 19.2% and in Mexico 78.3%, while sales in other regions rose 200.8%. The export ratio exceeded 60% in the second quarter of 2025, and the company is widening its customer base in Japan, China, Thailand and India.

On competition, analysts view the question as whether technology and automaker relationships built in IVI can carry expansion into digital cockpit and HUD products.

Demand for automotive chips is shaped by automakers' electronic system upgrades and wider in-vehicle displays, and the company has said it will diversify beyond a car-infotainment-centered structure.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual revenue went from KRW 150.4 billion in 2022 to KRW 191.1 billion in 2023 (up 27.1% year on year), KRW 186.6 billion in 2024 (down 2.3%) and KRW 192.8 billion in 2025 (up 3.3%), making 2025 the largest year of 2022–2025.

Operating profit was KRW 9.18 billion in 2022 and KRW 16.8 billion in 2023 (up 82.8%), then fell to KRW 4.87 billion in 2024 (down 70.9%) before turning into an operating loss of KRW 6.19 billion in 2025.

The operating margin was 6.1% in 2022, 8.8% in 2023, 2.6% in 2024 and -3.2% in 2025, so margins turned negative in 2025 even as revenue grew.

Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 45.9 billion in 2022 and KRW 62.6 billion in 2023 (up 36.5%), then turned into a net loss of KRW 38.6 billion in 2024 and widened to a net loss of KRW 61.6 billion in 2025.

Net income including non-controlling interests followed the same path, with net losses of KRW 38.6 billion in 2024 and KRW 61.6 billion in 2025. The debt-to-equity ratio at the end of 2025 was 157.1%, up from 82.8% in 2022, 52.2% in 2023 and 87.3% in 2024.

Operating cash flow moved from an inflow of KRW 5.75 billion in 2024 to an outflow of KRW 9.14 billion in 2025.

On a quarterly basis, an operating loss of KRW 2.72 billion in the third quarter of 2025 turned into operating profit of KRW 2.68 billion in the fourth quarter, and the first quarter of 2026 peaked with revenue of KRW 66.1 billion and operating profit of KRW 6.12 billion.

Operating profit then fell to KRW 3.18 billion in the second quarter (down 48.1% quarter on quarter) and KRW 492 million in the third (down 84.5%), a decline across two consecutive quarters.

Press coverage attributed the second-quarter revenue dip to lower SoC development service revenue, partly offset by existing products such as IVI.

At the net profit line, the fourth quarter of 2025 showed a net loss attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 50.8 billion, followed by KRW 944 million of net profit in the first quarter of 2026, a KRW 4.1 billion loss in the second and KRW 1.63 billion of profit in the third, with the last four quarters totaling a net loss of KRW 52.3 billion.

The company said a weaker exchange rate weighed on profitability in the third quarter.

Cumulative preliminary revenue for January to September 2026 was KRW 188.58 billion and operating profit KRW 9.79 billion, turning from an operating loss of KRW 8.87 billion in the same period of 2025 (preliminary figures disclosed on November 12, 2026).

05

Industry analysis

Demand for automotive chips is growing as electric and autonomous vehicles spread. A company profile summary on WiseReport states that the automotive semiconductor market is expected to grow at an annual rate of 9.7%.

Digital cockpit and HUD products are described as areas where applications are widening, as in-vehicle displays and driving-information projection expand. Electrification and premiumization of vehicles are cited as the background for HUD demand.

Digital Daily reported that the spread of in-vehicle infotainment, autonomous driving and software-defined vehicles is lifting chip demand, and that Telechips is accelerating its customer base expansion.

On the cycle, sales growth slowed in 2024 as the front-end market weakened and R&D spending rose for new chip development; Eugene Investment & Securities attributed the decline in operating profit to these factors in an August 2024 report.

From late 2025, SoC development services and new product revenue were added on top of that base. The company itself cited the weaker exchange rate as a burden on profitability in the third quarter of 2026.

On overseas customers, sales in China, Mexico and other regions are rising as the company moves away from a domestically concentrated structure. Its position relative to peers reads as an expansion from an IVI-centered business into cockpit, HUD, royalty and development service revenue.

Outlook

In its first-quarter 2026 results release, the company said it would strengthen its global customer base and expand eco-partnerships to build further growth momentum.

In its second-quarter 2026 release, it outlined plans to widen its global customer base for existing products, expand mass production of new projects and strengthen synergies between SoC development services and existing product businesses in the second half.

Eugene Investment & Securities expected in an August 2026 report that revenue growth would continue in the third quarter, reflecting development service revenue.

The same firm, in a report covered in November 2026, estimated fourth-quarter 2026 consolidated revenue at KRW 59.6 billion and operating profit at KRW 3.2 billion. A November 2025 report expected the new Dolphin3 product, supplied to major European automakers through Continental, to be fully reflected in 2026 results.

The SoC development service contract runs until January 31, 2028, so the structure of development service revenue after that date is a medium-term variable. On overseas sales, rising revenue from China and Mexico and a widening customer base in Japan, Thailand and India are the main threads for 2026.

The company's guidance is framed around directions such as customer expansion, mass production and synergies rather than quantified targets, so progress will be visible through quarterly results.

Bull factors

Growth beyond IVI

Cockpit and HUD-related revenue rose 52.1% in the third quarter of 2026, and IVI sales grew 4.0%. Royalty revenue jumped 266.3%, showing that R&D service and licensing income are growing together. Sales in China rose 19.2% and in Mexico 78.3%, which analysts read as a sign that the overseas customer base is widening.

Revenue visibility from a service contract

The SoC development service contract is set to run from October 1, 2025 to January 31, 2028, with a contract value of about USD 54.89 million.

Because the company disclosed an order worth KRW 77.1 billion in October 2025, the period during which development service revenue is recognized can be traced through disclosures.

When service revenue fell in the second quarter of 2026, existing products filled the gap, which reads as a sign that the business base has broadened.

Outlook for sustained quarterly profit

In a report covered in November 2026, Eugene Investment & Securities estimated fourth-quarter 2026 revenue at KRW 59.6 billion and operating profit at KRW 3.2 billion. The same firm's August 2026 report expected revenue growth to continue on development service revenue. The operating margin improved to 0.8% in the third quarter of 2026 from a negative 5.4% a year earlier.

Bear factors

Limits to the pace of margin recovery

Third-quarter 2026 operating profit of KRW 492 million fell far short of a KRW 4.2 billion market estimate, and the pace of margin recovery was judged below expectations. The operating margin was only 0.8%.

Pinpoint News noted that how far revenue growth translates into actual profit improvement still needs confirmation.

Volatility in development service revenue

SoC development service revenue declined quarter on quarter in the second quarter of 2026, and existing products such as IVI filled the gap. Operating profit moved from KRW 6.12 billion in the first quarter to KRW 3.18 billion in the second and KRW 492 million in the third.

Whether existing product sales can replace development service revenue when it falls will remain the key variable for quarterly profit.

Instability of the swing to net profit

Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 944 million in the first quarter of 2026, turned to a KRW 4.1 billion loss in the second quarter and returned to a KRW 1.63 billion profit in the third.

The fourth quarter of 2025 had a large net loss attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 50.8 billion. Press coverage reported that operating-level improvement did not carry through to the bottom line.

Risk factors

Product and customer concentration

IVI accounted for 65.1% of third-quarter 2026 revenue, so a single product line carries a high share. Because key customers are global automakers and automotive electronics suppliers, delays in vehicle production or model adoption could quickly affect revenue. Delays in mass production of new products also remain a possibility.

Exchange rate and export exposure

The company said a weaker exchange rate weighed on profitability in the third quarter. The export ratio exceeded 60% in the second quarter of 2025, and as overseas sales grow, currency moves may feed more directly into profit. Shifts in exchange-rate direction from quarter to quarter also add to the difficulty of reading results.

Balance sheet and contract timing

At the end of 2025 the debt-to-equity ratio was 157.1%, with total liabilities of KRW 224.6 billion against total equity of KRW 143 billion. Operating cash flow was an outflow of KRW 9.14 billion in 2025.

Because the SoC development service contract ends on January 31, 2028, how revenue is sustained after that date is a medium-term risk.

12

Overall view

Telechips' results read as a shift from an operating-loss structure toward quarterly profitability. Based on disclosed figures, revenue peaked at KRW 66.1 billion and operating profit at KRW 6.12 billion in the first quarter of 2026, and operating profit fell in the second and third quarters.

The revenue base has widened across IVI, cockpit and HUD, royalty and SoC development services, but third-quarter operating profit fell well short of market expectations.

Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders swung from a first-quarter profit to a second-quarter loss of KRW 4.1 billion and a third-quarter profit of KRW 1.63 billion, so how far operating improvement reaches the bottom line remains the open question.

On the balance sheet, the debt-to-equity ratio stood at 157.1% at the end of 2025, with total liabilities of KRW 224.6 billion against total equity of KRW 143 billion, and this structure may shift with earnings.

Supportive factors include the SoC development contract running to January 31, 2028 and growth in overseas customers, while the weaker exchange rate and concentration in one product line weigh against it.

The next checkpoints are the fourth-quarter 2026 preliminary results and the 2026 annual report, which will indicate whether quarterly profitability is sustained.
