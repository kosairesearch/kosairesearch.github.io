Neosem: Profit rebound tests a memory-test equipment recovery

02

Business structure

Neosem, founded in 2002, makes back-end semiconductor test equipment and listed on KOSDAQ in 2018 through a SPAC merger. Its products fall into SSD testers, burn-in testers and CXL memory test equipment.

According to a summary of the 2025 business report, SSD testers generated KRW 45 billion (about 70%) and burn-in testers KRW 12.8 billion (about 20%), with the rest coming from merchandise and services.

The burn-in division had been a loss-making unit averaging KRW 5 to 6 billion in annual sales over the previous decade, but the company said it recorded KRW 16.7 billion in sales in 2024 for the first time. The company says its BX series burn-in tester is applied to high-bandwidth memory (HBM) reliability verification.

Customers include both of Korea's leading memory makers, and in May 2026 the company signed a low-frequency test equipment (CLT) interface board supply contract with Samsung Electronics. In the same month its U.S. subsidiary NEOSEM TECHNOLOGY INC. signed a KRW 11.5 billion semiconductor test equipment supply contract.

On the competitive side, a Japanese rival's exit from the SSD business is cited as a factor, and the company claims it is the only remaining supplier of fifth-generation SSD test equipment. The company said it has kept R&D spending at about 3% of sales over the last three years.

SSD testers and burn-in testers account for most of sales in the business report, so earnings move sharply with memory investment flows.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual sales rose from KRW 74.7 billion in 2022 to KRW 100.9 billion in 2023 (up 35.2% year on year) and KRW 105.2 billion in 2024 (up 4.3%), then fell to KRW 63.9 billion in 2025 (down 39.2%). The largest year among 2022 to 2025 was 2024 and the smallest was 2025.

Operating profit was KRW 8.36 billion in 2022, KRW 8.09 billion in 2023 (down 3.3%), KRW 16.5 billion in 2024 (up 103.6%) and KRW 4.06 billion in 2025 (down 75.4%). The operating margin was 11.2% in 2022, 8.0% in 2023, 15.6% in 2024 and 6.3% in 2025, peaking in 2024 before dropping sharply in 2025.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 10 billion, KRW 8.28 billion, KRW 19.2 billion and KRW 5.34 billion for 2022 through 2025, and net income including non-controlling interests was disclosed at the same amounts.

The company attributed the 2025 decline to lower orders tied to investment concentrated in specific chip segments and higher R&D costs for next-generation test equipment. The debt ratio fell from 110.0% in 2022 to 22.6% in 2023 and 22.4% in 2024 before rising to 30.1% in 2025.

Year-end 2025 total equity was KRW 119.2 billion, all of it attributable to controlling shareholders, and total liabilities were KRW 35.9 billion. Operating cash flow was KRW 3.74 billion in 2025, the smallest of the four years.

Quarterly, the company moved from an operating loss of KRW 481 million in Q3 2025 to operating profit of KRW 2.52 billion in Q4 2025, a turnaround to profit. Sales were KRW 14.5 billion in Q1 2026 (down 35.7% from the prior quarter) and operating profit KRW 598 million (down 76.2%), marking the trough.

Q2 2026 sales then reached KRW 36 billion with operating profit of KRW 11.4 billion (up 1,808.6% quarter on quarter), and Q3 2026 sales reached KRW 50.7 billion (up 40.9% quarter on quarter and up 398.6% year on year) with operating profit of KRW 19.2 billion, a turnaround from the year-earlier loss.

Q3 2026 operating profit of KRW 19.2 billion is above the full-year 2024 operating profit of KRW 16.5 billion. Net income attributable to controlling shareholders for the last four quarters totaled KRW 25 billion.

A company earnings comment attributes the sales increase to expanding AI server and data center infrastructure, which has raised demand for high-performance memory and SSDs and therefore for test equipment.

05

Industry analysis

End demand comes from expanding AI server and data center investment. This is raising volumes of high-performance memory and SSDs, which increases the importance of test equipment that verifies quality before shipment.

A TechWorld article reported that as HBM advances, testing becomes harder and demand for burn-in testers rises. In CXL, the company said it developed the world's first CXL 1.0 test equipment in 2022, and it sees 2026 as the point when the CXL 3.1 tester market opens in earnest, targeting about a 0.5% share.

On the competitive side, a Japanese rival's exit from the SSD business is cited as a favorable factor for Neosem. At the same time, reports say Samsung Electronics is quality-testing a similar tool from another maker as HBM test equipment, so competition in burn-in equipment is becoming visible.

If memory makers cut capital spending, test equipment orders can adjust as well, so the position in the industry cycle feeds directly into earnings. The company claims a monopoly position in fifth-generation SSD testers, but results for sixth-generation products will depend on customer qualification progress.

As a back-end equipment supplier to Korea's two leading memory makers, Neosem's demand tracks the investment direction of its largest memory customers.

06

Outlook

The starting point for the company's outlook is its 2025 statements. At the time, the company said that new SSD and CXL test equipment launches and sales growth in burn-in test equipment would set a record for 2026, and it projected burn-in division sales above KRW 10 billion.

In the same reporting, the company said it had completed development of PCIe 6th-generation SSD test equipment and was running demonstration tests with major customers. Contracts announced or reported in 2026 are consistent with that direction.

In April 2026 the U.S. subsidiary reportedly signed a KRW 4.7 billion Gen6 SSD tester supply contract, and in May 2026 a CIB supply contract with Samsung Electronics and a KRW 11.5 billion SSD tester contract followed.

According to reports, a KRW 8.6 billion supply of CLT memory testers to Samsung Electronics is scheduled to be delivered to its Onyang Campus by the end of 2026. The company has set a target of a 0.5% share of the CXL 3.1 market.

For burn-in, the key point to watch is how widely the BX equipment is applied to HBM verification. This outlook rests on company statements and contract disclosures, and full-year confirmed figures, including Q4 2026, will come from the 2026 business report. The next checkpoint is whether the quarterly recovery through Q3 2026 carries into full-year results.

08

Bull factors

Broad CXL and SSD tester lineup

The company says it was the first to commercialize CXL 1.1 and 2.0 test equipment, and in May 2026 its U.S. subsidiary signed a KRW 11.5 billion SSD tester supply contract. After a Japanese rival exited the SSD business, the company says it remains the sole supplier of fifth-generation SSD test equipment.

Its PCIe 6th-generation test equipment is in demonstration testing with major customers. A lineup tied to AI server memory testing demand is cited as the main bullish argument.

Quarterly profit recovery in 2026

Operating profit rose from a KRW 598 million low in Q1 2026 to KRW 11.4 billion in Q2 and KRW 19.2 billion in Q3, increasing for two consecutive quarters. Q3 operating profit turned to a profit from a loss in the year-earlier quarter.

A company earnings comment explains that expanding AI server and data center infrastructure lifted demand for high-performance memory and SSDs and so raised test equipment sales. Q3 2026 operating profit of KRW 19.2 billion is above the full-year 2024 operating profit of KRW 16.5 billion.

Customer references including Samsung

In May 2026 the company said it signed an interface board supply contract with Samsung Electronics. According to reports, the CLT memory tester supply is worth KRW 8.6 billion and is scheduled for delivery to the Onyang Campus by the end of 2026.

The company says its BX burn-in tester is applied in actual HBM reliability verification. A 2026 report also said its application in production lines is expanding.

09

Bear factors

2025 earnings drop and cycle dependence

2025 sales were KRW 63.9 billion, down 39.2% year on year, and operating profit fell 75.4% to KRW 4.06 billion. The company cited lower orders tied to investment concentrated in specific chip segments. Operating cash flow of KRW 3.74 billion was the smallest of the four disclosed years. Quarterly swings are also large, as Q1 2026 sales fell 35.7% from the prior quarter.

Competition and customer qualification risk

Reports say Samsung Electronics is quality-testing a similar tool from another maker as HBM test equipment. In a 2025 report, the company said its BX tool was not designed exclusively for HBM and it was considering developing a dedicated HBM burn-in tool.

Burn-in equipment goes through long customer qualification periods, so any delay in moving to mass production also pushes back revenue timing. Revenue recognition for new products can shift depending on customer qualification results.

Concentrated ownership and earnings volatility

CEO Yeom Dong-hyun was the largest shareholder with 41.65% as of March 31, 2026. A high stake held by the largest shareholder can limit the shares traded in the market, making the share price more sensitive to supply and demand.

Earnings have also swung sharply over short periods, with Q1 2026 operating profit at KRW 598 million. Annual net income attributable to controlling shareholders fell from KRW 19.2 billion in 2024 to KRW 5.34 billion in 2025.

Risk factors

Industry and investment cycle

Earnings are sensitive to the direction of memory makers' capital spending. The company attributed the 2025 decline to lower orders tied to investment concentrated in specific chip segments. If the same pattern repeats, quarterly sales could fall again. Test equipment order timing may slip during periods of industry slowdown.

Customer concentration and revenue timing

Because contracts are large, changes in customer schedules pass directly into quarterly results. Q1 2026 sales falling 35.7% from the prior quarter is one example. If the U.S. subsidiary contracts or Samsung supply schedules slip, the quarterly recovery could be delayed. Revenue recognition may move between quarters depending on delivery completion and acceptance criteria.

Technology transitions and R&D burden

The company said it has kept R&D spending at about 3% of sales over the last three years. In 2025, higher R&D costs for next-generation test equipment were cited as a factor behind the earnings decline.

If customer qualification of next-generation products such as PCIe 6th generation and CXL 3.1 is delayed, development costs could hit earnings first. A slower product generation cycle than rivals could also affect market share.

Overall view

Neosem is a back-end semiconductor equipment maker whose core products are SSD testers, burn-in testers and CXL memory test equipment. After a 2025 earnings trough, its quarterly results have risen for two consecutive quarters in 2026.

Q3 2026 sales of KRW 50.7 billion and operating profit of KRW 19.2 billion are the largest of the last five disclosed quarters.

Bullish factors include SSD, CXL and HBM test demand tied to AI server growth, the 2026 contracts with Samsung Electronics and the U.S. subsidiary, and a Japanese rival's exit from the SSD business.

Bearish factors include earnings volatility shown by 2025 sales of KRW 63.9 billion and a 6.3% operating margin, dependence on the memory investment cycle, Samsung's testing of a rival's similar tool, and concentrated ownership by the largest shareholder.

Both sets of factors carry comparable weight, and the company's outlook rests on contracts and management statements. Confirmed full-year results will come with the 2026 business report.

The next checkpoints are delivery of the Samsung CLT supply by the end of December 2026 and the operating margin in the 2026 business report due by the end of March 2027. This report is provided for information only and does not include a buy or sell opinion.
