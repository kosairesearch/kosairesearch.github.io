02

Business structure

Hansol Logistics is a comprehensive logistics company founded in 1973, and its core business is 3PL (third-party logistics), in which it runs transport, handling and warehousing on behalf of clients.

In 2022, international logistics accounted for about 60% of revenue, warehousing and delivery (W&D) about 20%, and container and truck transport about 10% each, according to a March 2023 brokerage analysis.

International logistics covers sea and air freight forwarding and rail transport across Asia, the Americas and Europe. Its customers fall into three groups: the Hansol Group, mainly Hansol Paper; the Samsung Group, including logistics for Samsung SDI's China unit; and external third-party clients.

The 3PL share of revenue rose from 54.1% in 2017 to 62.3% in 2022, while Hansol-affiliated 2PL revenue grew at an annual rate of 18.6% over the same period.

Even so, group-affiliate sales reached KRW 210.9 billion in 2023 according to a press report on that year's results, about 30% of total sales, with Hansol Paper accounting for more than 85% of intragroup sales. Secondary battery logistics revenue was roughly KRW 300 billion in 2022.

The company has recently extended its China battery-container rental, collection, cleaning and storage service to the United States, and said in October 2026 it would build a battery-cell logistics hub in Indiana.

The Indian subsidiary runs a nationwide network linking Delhi, Chennai and Pune and handles automotive parts, electronics and chemicals logistics.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual revenue peaked at KRW 1.02 trillion in 2022 and then fell to KRW 726.5 billion in 2023 (down 28.4%), KRW 687 billion in 2024 (down 5.4%) and KRW 674.6 billion in 2025 (down 1.8%), declining for three consecutive years from 2023.

A 2022 press report attributed part of that year's strength to a one-off rise in freight rates during the global logistics disruption. The 2023 decline was linked to weaker freight rates and heavy reliance on internal group and Samsung volumes, which the company could not fully offset.

Operating profit was KRW 36.3 billion in 2022, KRW 26.3 billion in 2023 (down 27.8%) and KRW 16.8 billion in 2024 (down 36.1%), before rising to KRW 20.7 billion in 2025 (up 23.3%).

The operating margin was 3.6% in 2022, 3.6% in 2023, 2.4% in 2024 and 3.1% in 2025, recovering from the 2024 low but still below the 2022 level.

Profit attributable to controlling shareholders went from KRW 24.2 billion in 2022 and KRW 18.5 billion in 2023 to KRW 8.68 billion in 2024 (down 53.2%), then KRW 15.8 billion in 2025 (up 82.5%).

Total net income including non-controlling interests was KRW 8.67 billion in 2024 and KRW 15.8 billion in 2025, a different figure from profit attributable to controlling shareholders. Operating cash flow was KRW 28.4 billion in 2022, KRW 42 billion in 2023, KRW 20.2 billion in 2024 and KRW 32 billion in 2025.

The debt-to-equity ratio fell from 167.8% in 2022, 107.0% in 2023 and 106.0% in 2024 to 82.0% in 2025, when total equity was KRW 143.8 billion (KRW 143.6 billion attributable to controlling shareholders) and total liabilities were KRW 118 billion.

On a quarterly basis, Q4 2025 was the low point: revenue fell from KRW 169.5 billion in Q3 2025 to KRW 152.9 billion (down 9.8% quarter on quarter), and operating profit fell from KRW 6.11 billion to KRW 2.48 billion (down 59.4%).

Revenue then rose for three straight quarters to KRW 164.9 billion in Q1 2026, KRW 182.8 billion in Q2 and KRW 190.2 billion in Q3, and operating profit followed with KRW 4.5 billion, KRW 6.12 billion and KRW 7.64 billion.

Profit attributable to controlling shareholders reached KRW 5.72 billion in Q3 2026, up 61.1% year on year, and the last four quarters total KRW 13.9 billion.

05

Industry analysis

Korea's logistics industry posted revenue of KRW 154.8 trillion in 2021, up 38.0% from the prior year, as freight rates surged after the pandemic. In 2023, weaker shipping conditions and concerns over lower global cargo volumes slowed growth.

Forwarding, which is exposed to ocean freight rates, is estimated at 20–30% of Hansol Logistics' revenue, so freight swings affect results in a limited but meaningful way.

Given a press report that the 2022 result benefited from a one-off rise in freight rates, ocean freight and cargo volumes remain key variables for earnings. On the demand side, the secondary battery and electric vehicle supply chains are the main drivers.

Battery production moves materials, parts and finished goods across several sites, which raises the importance of container management and storage alongside transport. In India, the company's account of demand points to global manufacturers diversifying production bases.

Compared with large peers such as CJ Logistics, HANJIN Logistics and Hyundai Glovis, Hansol Logistics is shifting its business axis from group-affiliate volume toward external 3PL clients. Domestic transport is sensitive to economic conditions and cargo volumes, so the industry cycle affects its results.

06

Outlook

The corporate value-up plan disclosed in March 2026 sets out diversifying the business portfolio, expanding into fast-growing overseas markets and strengthening digital operations.

Its execution items include setting up a U.S. subsidiary to expand overseas sales, investing in trade-hub infrastructure, expanding investment in digital logistics systems and cutting logistics costs.

On shareholder returns, the company targets maintaining a payout ratio around 35%, with dividends and interim dividends funded from 10–40% of free cash flow (FCF). The payout ratio for 2025 was presented as 35.5%.

A July 2026 press report said the Indian subsidiary expects 2026 revenue of KRW 61.7 billion and is set to pass KRW 60 billion for the first time. The Indiana battery-cell logistics hub was introduced as the start of a more active battery supply-chain business.

About KRW 200 billion of new contracts were reported for 2025 in a press report from early 2026. In November 2026 the company said it had introduced 30-odd AX (AI transformation) projects proposed by business units, cutting manual work time in some tasks by 40%.

Its credit ratings are A- from Korea Ratings and A2- on commercial paper from NICE Investors Service. Because the U.S. subsidiary and facility investments must be funded alongside the 35% payout target, how free cash flow covers both is the next point to track.

Bull factors

A turn in quarterly profit

After the Q4 2025 low, operating profit rose for three straight quarters to KRW 4.5 billion in Q1 2026, KRW 6.12 billion in Q2 and KRW 7.64 billion in Q3. Profit attributable to controlling shareholders grew from KRW 615 million to KRW 5.72 billion over the same span, and the last four quarters total KRW 13.9 billion. The annual operating margin also recovered from 2.4% in 2024 to 3.1% in 2025.

Stronger balance sheet

The debt-to-equity ratio fell from 167.8% in 2022 to 82.0% in 2025, with total equity of KRW 143.8 billion at end-2025. Operating cash flow rose to KRW 32 billion in 2025 from KRW 20.2 billion in 2024. Its Korea Ratings credit rating of A- gives an external measure of financial stability.

Overseas expansion and a shareholder return baseline

New U.S. subsidiary plans and the Indiana battery hub widen the overseas footprint. The Indian subsidiary expects 2026 revenue of KRW 61.7 billion, which would be its first year above KRW 60 billion. The company has stated a target of maintaining a payout ratio around 35%.

Bear factors

Three years of falling revenue

Annual revenue fell to KRW 726.5 billion in 2023, KRW 687 billion in 2024 and KRW 674.6 billion in 2025. The operating margin of 3.1% in 2025 remains below the 3.6% recorded in 2022. Operating profit of KRW 20.7 billion in 2025 is just over half the KRW 36.3 billion of 2022.

Reliance on group volume

Group-affiliate sales were KRW 210.9 billion in 2023 according to a press report, about 30% of total sales. Hansol Paper accounted for more than 85% of intragroup sales. A drop in group cargo volume would feed directly into results.

Sensitivity to freight rates and shipping cycles

Analysts linked the 2023 operating profit decline of 27.8% to weaker freight rates that the company could not offset. Profit attributable to controlling shareholders fell 53.2% in 2024 to KRW 8.68 billion. Forwarding exposed to ocean freight is estimated at 20–30% of revenue.

Risk factors

Customer concentration

Intragroup volume from Hansol Paper is large, so changes in group production plans would quickly affect cargo volume. Group-affiliate sales were about 30% of total sales in 2023. How far external 3PL clients reduce this concentration is the key variable.

Freight rates and the economic cycle

Ocean freight and cargo volumes are a main cause of earnings swings. The 2022 result was boosted by a one-off freight increase, while falling freight rates contributed to the 2023 decline. Domestic transport is also sensitive to economic conditions and cargo volumes.

Competing claims on cash for investment and dividends

The company is pursuing a U.S. subsidiary, facility investment and digital system spending while targeting a payout ratio around 35%. Because dividends are funded from 10–40% of free cash flow, a larger investment program could reduce dividend capacity. Operating cash flow was KRW 32 billion in 2025.

12

Overall view

Hansol Logistics' revenue fell from KRW 1.02 trillion in 2022 to KRW 674.6 billion in 2025, a three-year decline, but quarterly results have improved since the Q4 2025 low.

Revenue, operating profit and profit attributable to controlling shareholders rose for three straight quarters through Q3 2026, with Q3 operating profit at KRW 7.64 billion. The debt-to-equity ratio fell from 167.8% in 2022 to 82.0% in 2025, which indicates a stronger balance sheet.

On the other side, group-affiliate volume remains large, and earnings still move with ocean freight rates and the economic cycle. Overseas growth drivers include the Indian subsidiary, battery logistics extended from China to the United States, and the planned U.S. subsidiary.

The company targets a payout ratio around 35%, but overseas investment and dividends must be funded from the same free cash flow. This report presents bullish and bearish factors side by side and does not include an investment rating or target price.
