Report overview

Shipping Carries Profit, Distribution Carries Sales

Hyundai Glovis posted record quarterly revenue and operating profit in 3Q 2026. Shipping contributed more than 40% of operating profit, while a recovery in distribution margins remains unconfirmed.

  1. 1

    Revenue in 3Q 2026 reached KRW 9.05 trillion, up 23.1% year on year, and operating profit reached KRW 566.5 billion, up 8.1%. Both are quarterly records, but the 6.3% operating margin is below a year earlier.

  2. 2

    Shipping accounted for 20.0% of consolidated revenue but 40.2% of third-quarter operating profit. PCTC revenue rose 38.5% to KRW 1.37 trillion, making it the core of growth.

  3. 3

02

Business structure

Hyundai Glovis reports its business in three segments: Logistics, Shipping and Distribution. In 3Q 2026, Distribution was the largest segment by revenue at KRW 4.29 trillion, about 47.4% of consolidated revenue. Shipping was 20.0% of consolidated revenue but 40.2% of operating profit.

Segment operating margins were 12.6% for Shipping, 7.6% for Logistics and 2.7% for Distribution. Logistics revenue was KRW 2.95 trillion; overseas logistics rose 22.2% to KRW 2.46 trillion while domestic logistics was flat at KRW 488.7 billion, so growth came mainly from overseas.

Distribution growth came from CKD exports to emerging-market assembly plants, which rose 22% to KRW 3.72 trillion. Within Shipping, PCTC revenue rose 38.5% to KRW 1.37 trillion, and the average operating fleet was 106 vessels in 3Q, including chartered ships added beyond owned vessels.

The customer base is widening from Hyundai Motor and Kia group volumes toward external brand volumes. According to company data, non-affiliated PCTC revenue share reached 52% in 2019, overtaking the affiliated share for the first time. Listed peers in shipping and logistics include HMM, Pan Ocean and CJ Logistics.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual revenue fell from KRW 27 trillion in 2022 to KRW 25.7 trillion in 2023 (down 4.8% YoY), then rose to KRW 28.4 trillion in 2024 (up 10.6%) and KRW 29.6 trillion in 2025 (up 4.1%), an increase in two consecutive years. 2025 was the largest year of the 2022 to 2025 period.

Operating profit bottomed in 2023 at KRW 1.55 trillion (down 13.6%), then rose to KRW 1.75 trillion in 2024 and KRW 2.07 trillion in 2025, an increase in two consecutive years. The operating margin moved from 6.7% in 2022 and 6.1% in 2023 to 6.2% in 2024 and 7.0% in 2025, the highest of the four years.

Owners' net profit recovered from KRW 1.06 trillion in 2023 to KRW 1.73 trillion in 2025 (up 58.5% year on year). Net profit including non-controlling interests was KRW 1.73 trillion in 2025, nearly identical to owners' net profit.

Operating cash flow rose from KRW 1.56 trillion in 2022 to KRW 2.5 trillion in 2025, and the debt-to-equity ratio fell from 101.7% to 78.9%. Quarterly revenue rose for four consecutive quarters from KRW 7.36 trillion in 3Q 2025 to KRW 9.05 trillion in 3Q 2026.

Operating profit slipped to KRW 508.3 billion in 4Q 2025 (down 3.0% QoQ) and KRW 495.1 billion in 2Q 2026 (down 5.1%) before jumping to KRW 566.5 billion in 3Q 2026 (up 14.4% QoQ). The company said 2Q 2026 absorbed a one-time cost from higher fuel prices tied to the war.

In 3Q, about KRW 70 billion of fuel surcharges was reflected, which the company said lifted shipping operating profit by roughly KRW 100 billion QoQ. Other gains and losses in 3Q were a KRW 122.5 billion gain, improving from a year earlier, while financial and equity-method results were losses. Owners' net profit bottomed at KRW 340.3 billion in 1Q 2026 and rose in 2Q and 3Q to KRW 574.8 billion.

05

Industry analysis

Shipping demand is tied to growth in Chinese automobile exports. Hyundai Glovis projected that Chinese auto exports could approach 12 million units in 2026, up from about 7 million in 2025. Press reports say its Chinese finished-vehicle seaborne volume rose from about 260,000 units in 2023 to about 510,000 in 2025.

Its share of Chinese auto seaborne exports was given as roughly 12% to 13%. Export volume is growing faster than available vessel capacity, which the company says limits its ability to absorb all incremental demand. Expanding the fleet is therefore key to whether share can rise.

A tight PCTC supply market has made its early-secured large newbuild vessels a competitive asset. The company judged that faster export growth limits the risk of a PCTC oversupply in 2028 to 2029.

In containers, freight conditions were favorable, but new container ship supply and geopolitical risk could raise volatility. On the Red Sea route, PCTC transits through the Bab el-Mandeb Strait reached 144 in 3Q 2026, the highest since late 2023, as some carriers return.

Hyundai Glovis is considering a return to the route from the first half of 2027. EU restrictions on Chinese EVs and US Trade Representative rules on car carriers remain long-term variables. Competitors HMM and Pan Ocean are similarly exposed to freight-rate cycles.

06

Outlook

The company expects continued seaborne demand from the Far East, driven by strong Chinese auto exports. As Chinese customers expand overseas production and sales, they increasingly request inland transport, rail and KD packing alongside ocean freight.

The company aims to turn this into long-term supply chain management customers rather than short-term high-freight business. A September 2026 report cited company annual revenue guidance of KRW 31 trillion, while brokerage estimates put 2026 revenue above KRW 33 trillion.

The same report described a 2030 revenue target of KRW 40 trillion, supported by new businesses such as logistics automation and used battery handling. At the 3Q 2026 results, the CEO said the quarter gave reason to believe the 2030 roadmap presented at the 2024 investor day could be largely realized.

The 2026 annual results will be confirmed in the annual report due by late March 2027, when actual results can be compared with the KRW 31 trillion guidance. The company said halving European-bound volume would affect total non-affiliated volume by about 3%. If European regulation accelerates local production of Chinese EVs, this volume mix could change.

08

Bull factors

A shift in shipping profit structure

Shipping was 20.0% of consolidated revenue but 40.2% of 3Q operating profit, with a 12.6% segment margin. PCTC revenue rose 38.5% to KRW 1.37 trillion, and the operating fleet expanded to 106 vessels. The company attributes the change in profit structure mainly to growth in high-freight non-affiliated volume.

Chinese export growth and tight vessel capacity

The company projected Chinese auto exports could approach 12 million units in 2026. Its share of Chinese seaborne auto exports is about 12% to 13%, and it says limited vessel capacity keeps it from absorbing all incremental demand. If fleet expansion lifts that share, shipping has room to grow.

Earnings recovery and balance sheet strength

Owners' net profit rose 58.5% to KRW 1.73 trillion in 2025, and the debt-to-equity ratio fell from 101.7% in 2022 to 78.9% in 2025. Operating cash flow reached KRW 2.5 trillion in 2025, higher than in 2022, indicating stronger cash generation.

The company's 2030 target of KRW 40 trillion in revenue includes new businesses such as logistics automation and used battery handling.

09

Bear factors

Weaker profitability in Distribution

Distribution revenue rose 21.5% to KRW 4.29 trillion, yet operating profit fell 18.7% to KRW 115.3 billion. The 2.7% segment margin trails Shipping at 12.6% and Logistics at 7.6% by a wide gap.

The company cites weaker non-ferrous metal prices and a stronger won for lower trading profitability, which caps company-wide margin gains.

Shipping margin compression and fuel and freight sensitivity

Shipping's operating margin was 12.6% in 3Q, down from 14.8% a year earlier. Profit did not grow as fast as revenue, and the company says higher fuel costs were offset through surcharges. If fuel prices and freight rates swing again, margins could come under pressure.

Part of 3Q profit came from non-operating items

Other gains of KRW 122.5 billion helped 3Q pre-tax profit, while financial results and equity-method results worsened. About KRW 70 billion of fuel surcharge recovery was also reflected. Whether these factors persist will need to be checked in later filed results.

10

Risk factors

New business execution and targets

The company targets KRW 40 trillion in revenue by 2030 and plans to grow new businesses such as logistics automation and used battery handling to reach it. This target assumes new businesses add earnings on top of the current shipping and overseas logistics base.

If monetization is delayed or investment burdens grow, the revenue and profit mix at the target date could differ from the plan. Progress toward the 2030 target should be checked against the contribution from new businesses.

Policy, regulation and routing

EU import restrictions on Chinese EVs and expanded local production are long-term variables. The company says Chinese-origin exports are about 25% of non-affiliated volume, with about a quarter of that bound for Europe, and that halving European volume would affect the total by about 3%.

US Trade Representative rules on car carriers and Red Sea routing limits could also affect fleet operations and costs.

Group volume dependence and supply structure

Non-affiliated volume has offset reduced Hyundai Motor and Kia sales, which is a positive. However, greater reliance on external volume raises exposure to market swings.

The company sees new container capacity and geopolitical risk as sources of volatility, and considers 2028 to 2029 PCTC oversupply risk limited, a view that still needs confirmation.

  5. By 2030

12

Overall view

Hyundai Glovis recorded record quarterly results in 3Q 2026, with revenue of KRW 9.05 trillion and operating profit of KRW 566.5 billion. Shipping is the center of growth: it was 20.0% of consolidated revenue but contributed 40.2% of operating profit, and PCTC revenue rose 38.5%.

Logistics also grew, led by overseas operations. Distribution revenue rose 21.5%, but operating profit fell 18.7% to a 2.7% margin, and consolidated operating margin of 6.3% was below a year earlier.

On an annual basis, 2025 operating profit was KRW 2.07 trillion, owners' net profit was KRW 1.73 trillion and the debt-to-equity ratio was 78.9%, so both earnings and balance sheet improved.

Expanding Chinese auto exports and tight vessel capacity are positives, while freight and fuel volatility, EU regulation and Distribution profitability remain negatives.

The next checkpoints are the 4Q 2026 preliminary results, the 2026 annual report against the KRW 31 trillion revenue guidance, and the direction of shipping margins.
