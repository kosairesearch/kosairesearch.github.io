02

Business structure

KS Industry is a shipbuilding equipment maker specializing in marine cranes, offshore plant cranes, deck machinery, material handling equipment, and LPG/LNG tanks. Its main customers are large domestic shipbuilders including the HD Hyundai group, Samsung Heavy Industries, and Hanwha Ocean.

In 2025, on a consolidated basis, ship component manufacturing accounted for the largest share of revenue at 64.44%, followed by cosmetics manufacturing and sales at 21.68% and equipment sales and repair at 13.88%. The cosmetics segment is handled by a consolidated subsidiary, S&C Cos & Trading, established in 2018.

The company states it has delivered more than 9,000 marine cranes cumulatively and has set a target of raising its domestic market share from roughly 30% currently to over 40% by 2027.

It also cites a track record of being the first in Korea to localize offshore plant crane production as part of its competitive positioning.

More recently, it established a Marine Robotics Institute combining crane design and manufacturing know-how with AI technology, moving into the Ulsan Info Industry Promotion Agency's shipbuilding-marine high-tech town, and appointed a new president to head its shipbuilding-marine business.

The company has also pursued diversification outside its core business, including a US distribution agreement for its 'FireHunter' fire-safety product, and relocated its headquarters from Gwangyang, South Jeolla Province to Haman County, South Gyeongsang Province in February 2025.

04

Earnings analysis

2025 consolidated revenue rose sharply to KRW 39.4 billion from KRW 24.7 billion a year earlier, but the operating loss widened to KRW 4.6 billion from KRW 0.7 billion, and the net loss attributable to owners deepened to KRW 7.3 billion from KRW 5.1 billion.

The revenue increase was attributed to higher sales at the consolidated subsidiary and expansion of the marine crane business, but cost-of-sales and SG&A ratios both rose, worsening profitability.

The operating margin deteriorated from -3.0% in 2024 to -11.7% in 2025, consistent with an industry assessment that profitability has suffered from rising raw material costs and competition.

By quarter, revenue edged up from KRW 11.2 billion in Q2 2025 to KRW 12.0 billion in Q3 before dropping sharply to KRW 7.2 billion in Q4, while the operating loss narrowed from KRW 1.5 billion in Q2 to KRW 0.4 billion in Q4.

Losses continued into 2026, with Q1 revenue of KRW 10.4 billion and an operating loss of KRW 0.8 billion, followed by Q2 revenue of KRW 9.5 billion and an operating loss of KRW 0.8 billion.

Notably, despite the Q2 2026 operating loss, net income attributable to owners swung to a profit of KRW 2.5 billion, likely driven by non-operating items whose details require confirmation from further disclosures.

Over the trailing four quarters (Q3 2025–Q2 2026), the combined net loss attributable to owners was about KRW 2.0 billion, with high quarter-to-quarter volatility but no resolution of the underlying operating loss trend.

On the balance sheet, the debt ratio steadily declined from 391.9% in 2022 to 178.7% in 2023, 78.7% in 2024, and 74.8% in 2025—a change attributable more to repeated capital raises via rights offerings and convertible bonds than to earnings improvement.

Operating cash flow swung from an inflow of KRW 6.6 billion in 2024 to an outflow of KRW 3.7 billion in 2025, indicating weakened cash generation from core operations.

05

Industry analysis

KS Industry's performance is directly tied to the broader shipbuilding cycle and order flow from large domestic shipbuilders.

In recent years, Korea's three major shipbuilders have maintained strong order momentum centered on high-value vessels such as LNG carriers and containerships, which could translate into greater demand for ship equipment such as cranes and deck machinery.

However, the company itself has cited deteriorating profitability from rising raw material costs and competition, suggesting that steel plate price volatility and competition among equipment makers remain pressure points on margins.

In the marine crane segment, the company says it has secured roughly a 30% domestic market share based on a cumulative delivery track record of more than 9,000 units. Its history of being the first in Korea to localize offshore plant crane production is also cited as part of its competitive positioning.

More broadly, there is a trend across the industry of equipment makers moving beyond traditional manufacturing into AI-based smart maintenance, repair, and overhaul (MRO) services, and KS Industry is participating in this trend through its Marine Robotics Institute.

That said, given its small scale and unstable financial structure, the company is simultaneously pursuing diversification into cosmetics and fire safety, leaving investors to watch how it prioritizes resources between strengthening its core shipbuilding equipment business and these new ventures.

06

Outlook

In the near term, the company secured liquidity through a KRW 6.0 billion third-party allotment rights offering decided on September 11, 2026 and completed on October 1, 2026, which also resulted in a change of largest shareholder to E-medison.

However, an injunction petition seeking to block the new share issuance was filed in court on September 17, 2026, and the company has stated it is negotiating with the petitioner to withdraw the suit, meaning uncertainty over the ownership structure has not been fully resolved.

The company has indicated a planned listing date of October 20 for the new shares, and management reshuffles including new executive appointments are expected under the E-medison regime.

On the business side, the company has set a goal of expanding marine crane orders from large domestic shipbuilders to raise its market share to over 40% by 2027. It is also pursuing AI-based MRO solution development through its Marine Robotics Institute as a new growth driver beyond traditional equipment manufacturing.

Diversification continues through the US market entry of its 'FireHunter' fire-safety product.

Financially, since the company is currently designated as an administrative-issue stock by the Korea Exchange, whether it can continuously meet the market capitalization requirement remains a key variable directly tied to its continued listing.

If diversification succeeds, it could reduce the company's dependence on the shipbuilding cycle.

Balance Sheet Improvement via Capital Raises

The debt ratio has steadily fallen from 391.9% in 2022 to 74.8% in 2025, driven by repeated capital raises via rights offerings and convertible bond issuances, including a fresh capital inflow from the new largest shareholder in October 2026. External financing has eased the company's immediate liquidity pressures.

Administrative-Issue Designation and Listing Risk

Risk factors

A new rights offering aimed at boosting market cap is underway, but litigation over the share issuance could delay the timeline.

Governance and Litigation Risk

Business and Industry Risk

Overall view

KS Industry expanded its top line to KRW 39.4 billion in 2025 revenue, but continued to post losses, with an operating loss of KRW 4.6 billion and a net loss of KRW 7.3 billion.

Losses persisted through the first and second quarters of 2026, and Q2 saw net income swing to a profit despite an operating loss, reflecting significant quarterly volatility.

The company is pursuing diversified growth initiatives, including expanding marine crane market share, an AI-based marine robotics venture, and overseas expansion of its fire-safety products.

At the same time, uncertainty around governance and continued listing is substantial, given the administrative-issue designation from a market cap shortfall, repeated changes of largest shareholder within a short period, and an injunction lawsuit over a new share issuance.

The balance sheet has nominally improved, with the debt ratio falling through repeated rights offerings and convertible bond issuances, but this reflects reliance on external financing rather than a recovery in earnings power.

Investors should monitor multiple financial and non-financial variables together, including whether operating performance turns profitable, whether the administrative-issue designation is resolved, and the outcome of the pending injunction lawsuit.
