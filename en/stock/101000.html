02

Business structure

KS Industry, founded in 2004 and listed on KOSDAQ in 2009, manufactures marine cranes for ships. Its main products are marine cranes, offshore plant cranes, deck machinery, material handling equipment, and LPG and LNG tanks, and it also sells and repairs related equipment.

The company says it has delivered more than 9,000 marine cranes cumulatively. It aims to raise its share of the domestic marine crane market from about 30% to more than 40% by 2027. Its customers are mainly large domestic shipbuilders.

A news report from the first half of 2026 described a KRW 7.86 billion contract for hose handling cranes and three related units. The company says the subsidiaries focus on battery materials and cosmetics. Its marine robotics research center in Ulsan has been confirmed. The market share figure is the company's own statement and may differ from independent estimates.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual revenue rose from KRW 74.4 billion in 2022 to KRW 86.4 billion in 2023 (up 16.0% year on year), fell to KRW 24.7 billion in 2024 (down 71.4%), and recovered to KRW 39.4 billion in 2025 (up 59.8%). The largest year among 2022 to 2025 was 2023, and the smallest was 2024.

The company attributed the 2025 revenue increase to higher subsidiary revenue and marine crane revenue.

Operating loss of KRW 14.6 billion in 2022 turned into operating profit of KRW 10.9 billion in 2023, then into an operating loss of KRW 749 million in 2024, and widened to an operating loss of KRW 4.62 billion in 2025. Operating margins were -19.6%, 12.6%, -3.0% and -11.7%.

The company cited the deduction of prior-year discontinued operations gains as a factor in the 2025 operating loss change.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders was a net loss of KRW 13.9 billion in 2022, a profit of KRW 3.68 billion in 2023, a net loss of KRW 5.13 billion in 2024 and a net loss of KRW 7.26 billion in 2025, with losses in both 2024 and 2025.

Operating cash flow went from -KRW 10.4 billion in 2022 to KRW 3.76 billion in 2023 and KRW 6.59 billion in 2024, then turned to -KRW 3.67 billion in 2025. At end-2025, total equity was KRW 35 billion and total liabilities KRW 26.2 billion, and the debt ratio fell from 391.9% in 2022 to 74.8%.

Quarterly revenue went from KRW 12 billion in Q3 2025 to KRW 7.18 billion in Q4 2025 (down 40.4% quarter on quarter), then KRW 10.4 billion in Q1 2026, KRW 9.46 billion in Q2 and KRW 11.3 billion in Q3.

Operating results moved from losses in Q3 2025 (KRW 1.01 billion), Q4 2025 (KRW 376 million), Q1 2026 (KRW 794 million) and Q2 2026 (KRW 806 million) to an operating profit of KRW 345 million in Q3 2026.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders was a profit of KRW 2.52 billion in Q2 2026 but a net loss of KRW 5.31 billion in Q3 2026, and the four-quarter total of net losses is KRW 6.07 billion.

Operating profit and net losses moved in opposite directions in some quarters, so non-operating items and finance costs need to be reviewed alongside operating results.

05

Industry analysis

The marine crane business is directly tied to shipbuilding orders. The company expects order intake to keep expanding, led by large domestic shipbuilders. It also stressed that the 2026 order was a bulk order after about two years without one.

Demand for new cranes moves with shipbuilding orders, so the company says it is increasing aftermarket parts and service revenue to reduce that volatility. The 30% domestic share is the company's own figure, with a target of more than 40% by 2027.

Its strengths, as presented by the company, are its cumulative deliveries and its record with large shipbuilders. Other KOSDAQ machinery and equipment companies include Doosan Enerbility, HD Construction Equipment and Hyundai Elevator, but the business structures and scale differ, so direct comparisons are limited.

Its planned unmanned offshore cranes, AI-based MRO agents and teleoperation overlap in direction with robotics-listed companies such as Rainbow Robotics, Doosan Robotics and Robotis. The battery materials and cosmetics subsidiaries operate in areas unrelated to shipbuilding and could add earnings volatility.

Whether shipbuilding orders continue and how fast new businesses reach commercialization are the key variables for its position in the industry.

06

Outlook

The most specific target the company has given is to raise its domestic marine crane share to more than 40% by 2027. On orders, a KRW 7.86 billion contract was reported, and the company expects order intake to expand led by large domestic shipbuilders.

In new businesses, the marine robotics research center move-in has been confirmed, and the company plans to accelerate commercialization of unmanned offshore cranes, AI-based MRO agents and teleoperation.

On management, the board approved a managing director appointment on 13 January 2026 with the stated aim of restoring normal management. On 23 October 2026 the board appointed a new CEO, and the company now has two co-CEOs.

At the extraordinary shareholders meeting on 8 October 2026, all four director appointments were approved. On funding, the company bought KRW 2.5 billion of its 15th-series convertible bonds off-market on 30 October 2026, leaving KRW 3.6 billion outstanding.

On 14 April 2026 it decided to lend KRW 10 billion to a cosmetics company, running until 13 April 2027. The points to confirm are whether the share target is met and when new business revenue shows up in results.

08

Bull factors

Order recovery and a share target

The company expects orders to continue from large domestic shipbuilders and plans to raise its domestic marine crane share to more than 40% by 2027. The KRW 7.86 billion contract reported in the first half of 2026 was described as a bulk order after about two years.

The company says its installed base of more than 9,000 cranes could support parts and service revenue. Whether these orders convert into reported results is the next point to confirm.

Operating profit turnarounds and a stronger balance sheet

Operating profit turned positive in 2023 at KRW 10.9 billion, and it turned positive again in Q3 2026 at KRW 345 million. The debt burden has fallen sharply, so financial stability is better than in 2022, and whether this level holds is the next point to confirm.

The operating margin, at -11.7% in 2025, has room to improve, and whether profitable quarters continue is the point to confirm.

Parts, service and new-business diversification

The company says a growing installed base should raise the share of parts and aftermarket service, which could reduce the effect of order swings. Its marine robotics research center, unmanned offshore cranes, AI-based MRO agents and teleoperation are presented as new business pillars.

Battery materials and cosmetics subsidiaries are also cited as diversification steps. New businesses are not yet separated in the reported results.

Bear factors

Two straight years of losses and weaker cash generation

Losses in 2024 and 2025 were consecutive, and the 2025 operating loss of KRW 4.62 billion was wider than the KRW 749 million loss in 2024. Operating cash flow fell from KRW 6.59 billion in 2024 to -KRW 3.67 billion in 2025.

Revenue rose 59.8% in 2025 while the operating loss widened, which points to a need to examine the cost and expense structure.

Accumulated quarterly net losses

Net losses attributable to controlling shareholders total KRW 6.07 billion over the last four quarters. Operating profit in Q3 2026 turned positive, but the net loss attributable to controlling shareholders widened to KRW 5.31 billion.

The Q2 2026 profit of KRW 2.52 billion came during an operating loss quarter, so non-operating factors need to be checked.

Reliance on external funding and dilution

In April 2026 the company lent KRW 10 billion to a cosmetics company at 5% interest, equal to 28.57% of equity. A 19th-series convertible bond was reported with a potential dilution rate of 49.05%. With operating cash flow negative again, continued reliance on loans and convertible bonds is a weakness.

10

Risk factors

Governance and management stability

The largest shareholder changed in January 2026 to a growth investment fund, from the previous holder. A court injunction case against the new share issuance was filed on 17 September 2026, and the company said it is responding through legal procedures and in talks to withdraw the suit.

The company has previously been designated as an unfaithful disclosure company. With co-CEOs and a changed shareholder base, consistency of management direction is a key risk.

Listing maintenance requirements

The exchange set a KRW 15 billion minimum market capitalization for continued KOSDAQ listing from 2026, reported to rise to KRW 20 billion in the second half. A share price below KRW 1,000 is classed as a penny stock, and the company decided on a 4-for-1 reverse stock split in June 2026. If the market cap stays below the threshold, listing requirements are directly affected.

Funding and dilution

Past rights offerings were withdrawn and payments delayed, leading to a designation as an unfaithful disclosure company, which has repeatedly slowed funding. A June 2026 report also described a strategy of concentrating a KRW 10 billion-plus investment in one listed company as risky.

The potential dilution rate of 49.05% and the KRW 3.6 billion balance of the 15th-series bonds will affect shareholder value depending on how they are handled.

12

Overall view

KS Industry is a marine crane maker that posted an operating profit turnaround in 2023 and again in Q3 2026. Against that, losses in 2024 and 2025 were consecutive, operating cash flow turned negative in 2025, and net losses attributable to controlling shareholders total KRW 6.07 billion over the last four quarters.

The debt ratio fell from 391.9% in 2022 to 74.8% in 2025. On orders, bulk shipbuilder demand and the 40% share target are presented as strengths, while new businesses and parts and service expansion could change the earnings structure.

On governance, changes in the largest shareholder, the change in CEO, a share issuance lawsuit and an unfaithful disclosure history leave management stability as a weakness. Tighter listing requirements and convertible bond dilution are variables that could directly affect shareholder value.

The next points to confirm are the operating margin and operating cash flow in the 2026 annual report, the loan maturity on 13 April 2027, and the convertible bond maturity on 27 November 2027. This report contains no investment opinion, rating or target price.
