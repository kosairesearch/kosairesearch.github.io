02

Business structure

KS Industry is a shipbuilding and offshore equipment maker whose core product is marine deck cranes. The company was founded in 2004 and listed on KOSDAQ in 2009 as a specialized marine crane manufacturer.

Its main products are marine cranes installed on container ships, bulk carriers, LNG carriers and LPG carriers, along with large cranes for offshore plants such as FPSOs and drillships, and it also manufactures and supplies deck machinery and LPG/LNG tanks while handling sales and repair of ship equipment such as motors and reducers.

The company has said it is pursuing localization of high-value-added ship types and offshore equipment while focusing on technology-intensive products that meet international conventions and classification society certification standards across countries.

Its main customers are large domestic shipbuilders including HD Hyundai Heavy Industries and Samsung Heavy Industries, reflecting an indirect-export structure in which it supplies equipment to shipyards.

In 2026 the company also attempted diversification: in April, KS Industry, itself a KOSDAQ-listed company, paid KRW 10 billion into a third-party allotment capital raise of KOSPI-listed Inscobee (now renamed Freet) to take control and become its largest shareholder, aiming to enter the mobile virtual network operator business.

However, neither the revenue contribution of the core shipbuilding equipment business nor the current status of that equity stake can be clearly confirmed from periodic filings alone, leaving the direction of the business portfolio fluid.

Over the course of 2026 the company also went through multiple changes in its controlling shareholder, adding uncertainty around management stability.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual revenue rose from KRW 74.4 billion in 2022 to KRW 86.4 billion in 2023, up 16.0% year over year, before falling 71.4% to KRW 24.7 billion in 2024 and then rising 59.8% to KRW 39.4 billion in 2025. Among 2022-2025, revenue was largest in 2023 and smallest in 2024.

Operating profit swung from an operating loss of KRW 14.6 billion in 2022 to an operating profit of KRW 10.9 billion in 2023, before turning to an operating loss of KRW 749 million in 2024 and widening further to an operating loss of KRW 4.62 billion in 2025 as losses persisted.

Controlling-interest net income followed a similar path, from a net loss of KRW 13.9 billion in 2022 to a net profit of KRW 3.68 billion in 2023, before swinging to a net loss of KRW 5.13 billion in 2024 and widening to a net loss of KRW 7.26 billion in 2025.

Both operating profit and controlling-interest net income posted losses for two consecutive years from 2024 through 2025.

On a quarterly basis, the company recorded operating losses for five consecutive quarters, from an operating loss of KRW 1.48 billion in the second quarter of 2025 through an operating loss of KRW 806 million in the second quarter of 2026.

Controlling-interest net income, however, swung from a net loss of KRW 1.03 billion in the first quarter of 2026 to a net profit of KRW 2.52 billion in the second quarter of 2026, turning positive both quarter-on-quarter and year-on-year; because this turnaround occurred while operating losses persisted, it may have been influenced by non-operating or non-recurring items.

The sum of controlling-interest net losses over the trailing four quarters (third quarter of 2025 through second quarter of 2026) was KRW 1.98 billion, underscoring high quarter-to-quarter volatility.

The debt-to-equity ratio fell sharply from 391.9% in 2022 to 74.8% in 2025, but operating cash flow reversed to negative KRW 3.67 billion in 2025 from positive KRW 6.59 billion a year earlier.

05

Industry analysis

The global shipbuilding sector has maintained an order boom through 2026. According to KOTRA, global ship orders rose 57% year over year in the January-September 2026 period, and domestic ship exports grew 11% to a cumulative USD 21.5 billion over January-August.

Korean shipbuilders have continued to prioritize high-value-added ship types over low-price volume competition, with the average order value per ship for Korean yards running at about 1.8 times that of China as of September.

According to Clarksons Research data, Korean shipbuilders' order volume in January-August 2026 rose 54% year over year to 9.38 million CGT, while China's order volume over the same period reached 45.39 million CGT, or 76% of the global total, highlighting China's dominance in volume terms.

Ahead of discussions on the International Maritime Organization's carbon-neutral framework, demand for green equipment such as alternative-fuel propulsion systems is also expanding.

Because rising ship orders tend to lift demand for equipment such as engines, piping, valves, electrical systems and navigation management systems, the broader order upturn could be a favorable backdrop for equipment makers like KS Industry.

However, given the large volume gap with China, domestic equipment makers increasingly need to pursue niche positioning built on green and digital technology rather than compete on volume alone, and the benefit of rising orders takes time to flow through to actual revenue given the company's indirect-export structure.

06

Outlook

The company said it signed an order worth KRW 7.86 billion for H-series marine hose handling cranes and three other items from a major domestic shipbuilder, and emphasized that this was its first large order win in roughly two years.

According to FnGuide data, consolidated revenue in the first quarter of 2026 rose year over year while both the operating loss and net loss narrowed, which was attributed to higher sales to major shipbuilding customers including HD Hyundai Heavy Industries and Samsung Heavy Industries in the ship-component manufacturing segment.

On the governance front, however, the controlling shareholder changed multiple times during 2026, accompanied by repeated management disruptions and litigation; the former controlling shareholder ELM System filed a series of lawsuits, including requests to inspect company books, injunctions against new share issuance and suspension of voting rights, and suspension of director and auditor duties, most of which were dismissed.

In October, an injunction lawsuit was filed against the new share issuance after the third-party allotment recipient was changed from Sangsaeng No.1 Partnership to Emedison, creating uncertainty around the share listing originally scheduled for October 20.

Emedison, the new controlling shareholder reported to be in a state of complete capital impairment, paid in KRW 6 billion, but the timeline for additional promised capital raises and convertible bond payments has repeatedly slipped, raising questions about its funding capacity.

The company remains designated as an administrative-issue stock on the Korea Exchange, so investors should monitor whether conditions for lifting that designation are met alongside the stabilization of the new management structure.

With the global shipbuilding cycle remaining favorable, additional order wins will likely be key to a turnaround in the core business.

Bull factors

Q2 2026 Net Income Turned Positive

Controlling-interest net income turned to a profit of KRW 2.52 billion in the second quarter of 2026, positive both quarter-on-quarter and year-on-year.

After bottoming at KRW 7.18 billion in revenue in the fourth quarter of 2025, revenue recovered to KRW 10.4 billion and KRW 9.46 billion in the first and second quarters of 2026, respectively. The operating loss itself has fluctuated quarter to quarter, so whether the net profit trend continues remains to be confirmed.

Favorable Shipbuilding Order Cycle

Global ship orders rose 57% year over year in January-September 2026, extending the shipbuilding order boom. Because rising ship orders tend to lift demand for engines, piping and electrical equipment, this could create a favorable environment for shipbuilding equipment makers such as KS Industry. The company said it secured a large order worth KRW 7.86 billion from a major domestic shipbuilder.

Improved Leverage Ratios

The debt-to-equity ratio fell sharply from 391.9% in 2022 to 74.8% in 2025. Through 2026, the company has repeatedly pursued third-party allotment capital raises to shore up liquidity. However, these fundraisings have come with increases in share count, which should be weighed alongside the deleveraging.

Bear factors

Five Straight Quarters of Operating Losses

Operating losses continued for five consecutive quarters from the second quarter of 2025 through the second quarter of 2026. The annual operating loss for 2025 also widened to KRW 4.62 billion from a year earlier, marking a second consecutive year of operating losses in 2024-2025.

Even as net income turned positive in the second quarter of 2026, the operating loss itself, at KRW 806 million, persisted both quarter-on-quarter and year-on-year.

Repeated Ownership Changes and Litigation

The controlling shareholder changed from ELM System to KSI No.1 Growth Investment Partnership and then to Emedison over the course of 2026.

The former controlling shareholder, ELM System, repeatedly filed lawsuits including requests to inspect company books and injunctions against new share issuance, and a fresh injunction lawsuit over share issuance was filed again in October. Such governance disputes risk management gaps and delayed decision-making.

Dilutive, Recurring Capital Raises

The company pursued multiple third-party allotment capital raises and convertible bond issuances in 2026 alone. The structure of the fundraising itself was fluid, as seen when the capital raise recipient shifted from Sangsaeng No.1 Partnership to Emedison. Repeated new share issuance can lead to dilution for existing shareholders.

Risk factors

Governance and Listing Eligibility Risk

The company is currently designated as an administrative-issue stock on the Korea Exchange. An injunction lawsuit was filed against the new share issuance tied to the capital raise completed on October 1, raising the possibility that the share listing scheduled for October 20 could be deferred.

The new controlling shareholder, Emedison, is reported to be an unlisted company in a state of complete capital impairment, raising questions about its ability to fund additional promised capital commitments.

Financial Health Risk

Operating losses have continued for five straight quarters from the second quarter of 2025 through the second quarter of 2026. Operating cash flow swung back to negative KRW 3.67 billion in 2025 from positive KRW 6.59 billion a year earlier.

A structure reliant on repeated external fundraising could become a financial burden if underlying profitability does not recover.

Industry and Competitive Risk

China accounted for 76% of global ship orders by volume in January-August 2026, underscoring a pronounced tilt toward China. Given the indirect-export structure of supplying major domestic shipbuilders, the benefit of rising orders takes time to flow through to revenue, and bargaining power may be limited.

With a large volume gap versus China, technological differentiation rather than price competition is increasingly required.

Overall view

KS Industry has shown significant volatility in both earnings and governance through 2026.

Annual revenue rose 59.8% year over year in 2025, but the operating loss widened to KRW 4.62 billion, extending operating losses for a second consecutive year in 2024-2025, and operating losses continued for five straight quarters through the second quarter of 2026.

Still, controlling-interest net income turning to a profit of KRW 2.52 billion in the second quarter of 2026 illustrates the quarter-to-quarter volatility in results.

At the same time, the controlling shareholder changed twice during 2026, the company remains designated as an administrative-issue stock, and litigation over new share issuance has added to governance risk.

The upstream global shipbuilding industry remains favorable, with an order boom and a shift toward high-value ship types keeping the door open for greater equipment demand.

However, both the timing at which this industry tailwind translates into actual orders and earnings improvement, and whether governance stabilizes, remain variables to confirm going forward.
