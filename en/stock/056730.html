Quarterly Profit Turn Amid Sales Decline, Management-Stock Warning Persists

CNT85 posted operating profit in the first two quarters of 2026 after an operating loss in 2025, but sales have fallen for three straight quarters and market capitalization has stayed below KRW 20 billion, prompting a management-stock warning.

02

Business structure

CNT85 is an integrated engineering company that operates in three segments: plant, filter press, and construction. Based on second-quarter 2026 sales, the plant segment accounted for 72%, construction for 20%, and filter presses for 9%.

The plant business covers incineration and power generation facilities, sludge recycling systems, air pollution control equipment, wastewater treatment facilities, and production process equipment for secondary batteries, and it has expanded into specialized areas such as surface treatment equipment for aerospace and defense.

Filter presses are used in secondary battery recycling processes, and a filter press contract for a large corporate customer's battery recycling line was reported as under production.

Disclosures in 2022 showed supply contracts worth KRW 7.1 billion with a multinational company and KRW 3.2 billion with a secondary battery materials company.

Overseas, the company won a turnkey contract worth KRW 20 billion from the Maldives Ministry of Environment for an energy-recovery waste incineration facility, which was reported to be in its final construction stage in 2023.

After a 2020 court-supervised reorganization, its largest shareholder became an affiliate of Shinan Group, and the company later merged two affiliated companies to reshape its business units. Trading resumed on March 30, 2023, after roughly four years of suspension.

Customers identified in filings include overseas government clients, multinational companies, and secondary battery-related manufacturers, and contract sizes range from several billion to KRW 20 billion won.

The company competes with mid-sized plant engineering and environmental equipment makers, and its revenue scale is far smaller than that of large listed construction and engineering groups.

04

Earnings analysis

Based on annual figures disclosed in regular reports, sales rose from KRW 32.1 billion in 2022 to KRW 50.2 billion in 2023 (up 56.4% year on year) and KRW 67 billion in 2024 (up 33.6%), making 2024 the largest year in the 2022 to 2025 period. Sales fell 29.1% to KRW 47.5 billion in 2025.

Operating profit was KRW 1.53 billion in 2022 and KRW 881 million in 2023 (down 42.3%), before the company swung to an operating loss of KRW 1.41 billion in 2024. The 2025 operating loss widened to KRW 11.3 billion.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders peaked at KRW 8.37 billion in 2023 (up 507.9%), but turned into losses of KRW 1.35 billion in 2024 and KRW 12.7 billion in 2025, which means two consecutive loss years from 2024 to 2025.

Net income including non-controlling interests was disclosed at the same amounts for 2023 to 2025. The operating margin moved from 4.8% in 2022 to 1.8% in 2023, -2.1% in 2024, and -23.7% in 2025.

The company attributed the 2025 decline mainly to lower orders from a construction slowdown and a larger allowance for doubtful receivables.

At the end of 2025, total equity was KRW 58.5 billion, all attributable to controlling shareholders, and total liabilities were KRW 26.6 billion, lifting the debt-to-equity ratio from 25.9% in 2024 to 45.6% in 2025.

Operating cash flow went from KRW 11.4 billion in 2023 to negative KRW 11.1 billion in 2024 and negative KRW 8.3 billion in 2025.

On a quarterly basis, sales peaked at KRW 19.6 billion in the fourth quarter of 2025 (up 40.6% from the prior quarter), when the operating loss reached KRW 8.94 billion and the net loss attributable to controlling shareholders reached KRW 11.5 billion.

Sales were KRW 14.2 billion in the first quarter of 2026 (down 27.7% quarter on quarter), and the company posted an operating profit of KRW 173 million. The second quarter brought sales of KRW 9.99 billion (down 29.6%) and an operating profit of KRW 533 million (up 208.5%).

In the third quarter, sales fell to KRW 4.76 billion (down 52.3% quarter on quarter and down 65.9% year on year), and the company returned to an operating loss of KRW 967 million. Net income attributable to controlling shareholders in the third quarter was KRW 1.11 billion, a swing to profit from a year earlier.

Over the last four quarters, from the fourth quarter of 2025 to the third quarter of 2026, the net loss attributable to controlling shareholders totaled KRW 9.22 billion.

05

Industry analysis

The company attributed its 2025 sales decline mainly to lower orders caused by a slowdown in the construction market. A 2025 first-quarter analysis also pointed to fewer orders from weaker private investment and a slump in manufacturing as factors behind the sales drop.

Plant and filter press equipment depends on private capital spending and factory operating levels, so these two indicators are key variables for the order flow.

Since late 2022, orders for secondary battery recycling equipment have continued, making battery materials and recycling demand one pillar of the company's diversification. In environmental plants, demand for incineration, wastewater, and air pollution control equipment spans public and private projects.

Sales have declined since peaking at KRW 19.6 billion in the fourth quarter of 2025, so how quickly the order backlog converts into revenue will determine whether the industry cycle turns for the company.

Its business model centers on turnkey specialized equipment contracts sized from several billion won to about KRW 20 billion, which differs in method and scale from large construction companies.

Compared with larger listed peers such as Samsung E&A and DL E&C, its revenue scale is much smaller, and it competes mainly with specialized equipment suppliers within the sector.

06

Outlook

In an August 2026 disclosure, the company said it decided to borrow KRW 10 billion in short-term funds from financial institutions to finance new business investment and operating capital.

A supply contract worth KRW 5.32 billion (16.59% of sales), disclosed as corrected on June 30, 2026, had its contract end date revised to October 15, 2026. Another contract worth KRW 4.61 billion (9.19% of sales), corrected on September 15, 2026, had its end date revised to October 31, 2026.

The share consolidation raised the par value from KRW 500 to KRW 2,500 and reduced outstanding shares from about 74.1 million to about 14.8 million, taking effect on April 28, 2026. The company cited stabilizing the share price through an appropriate free float and enhancing corporate value as the purpose.

With third-quarter 2026 sales falling to KRW 4.76 billion, the size of the order backlog that will support revenue from the fourth quarter of 2026 onward is the next point to watch. Fourth-quarter 2026 results will first be disclosed with the full-year figures in the 2026 annual report.

It will also be important to see which new business the KRW 10 billion borrowing funds and when that business starts contributing to sales and operating profit.

With the management-stock warning in place, whether the market-capitalization threshold is met will depend on share price movements, and exchange disclosures will mark the next decision point.

Bull factors

Quarterly profit turnaround

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 1.11 billion in the third quarter of 2026, a swing to profit from a year earlier. Compared with the net loss attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 11.5 billion in the fourth quarter of 2025, the swing in quarterly results has narrowed considerably.

The ratio fell sharply from 146.1% in 2022 to 48.8% in 2023 and was 25.9% in 2024. All of the KRW 58.5 billion in equity was disclosed as attributable to controlling shareholders.

Supply contract disclosures in 2026

Trading was suspended around the share consolidation in April 2026 before resuming. The stock also has a history of a trading suspension in 2019 that ended with trading resuming in March 2023.

Risk factors

Order and cycle risk

The 2025 results reflected lower orders as construction slowed and private investment weakened. With sales still declining through 2026, a slower conversion of the order backlog into revenue could delay any recovery from the fourth quarter of 2026.

Because individual contracts range from several billion to about KRW 20 billion, a gap between projects could have a large effect on revenue.

Financing and liquidity risk

Operating cash flow was negative KRW 8.3 billion in 2025, and a KRW 10 billion short-term borrowing was decided in August 2026. The debt-to-equity ratio rose from 25.9% in 2024 to 45.6% in 2025.

The repayment timing of the short-term borrowing and the company's ability to generate operating cash are the next points to check.

Listing and trading risk

The Korea Exchange has said the stock could be designated for management if market capitalization stays below KRW 20 billion for 30 consecutive trading days. Although the share consolidation reduced the number of shares, whether the market-capitalization threshold is met depends on the share price. Given the past trading suspensions, the stock may be sensitive to changes in the trading environment.

Overall view

CNT85 recorded an operating loss of KRW 11.3 billion in 2025, its second consecutive year of losses, but posted operating profit in the first and second quarters of 2026, showing an improvement in quarterly results.

However, sales fell for three consecutive quarters from a peak of KRW 19.6 billion in the fourth quarter of 2025 to KRW 4.76 billion in the third quarter of 2026, and the company returned to an operating loss of KRW 967 million in the third quarter.

On the financial side, total equity was KRW 58.5 billion and total liabilities were KRW 26.6 billion at the end of 2025, with the debt-to-equity ratio rising from 25.9% in 2024 to 45.6% in 2025, and a KRW 10 billion short-term borrowing was decided in August 2026.

Market capitalization stayed below KRW 20 billion for 25 consecutive trading days as of November 12, 2026, prompting a management-stock warning.

Strengths include the quarterly turn to profit, a low debt load relative to equity, and supply contract disclosures in 2026, while weaknesses include the consecutive sales declines, two years of annual losses with negative operating cash flow, and the management-stock warning.

Weighing both sides equally, the next checkpoints are the Korea Exchange's designation decision and the annual results in the 2026 annual report.
