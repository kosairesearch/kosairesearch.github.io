02

Business structure

Asia Pacific Satellite lnc runs two divisions: a communications division that develops and produces satellite communication terminals, and a satellite division that manufactures satellite bodies and payloads.

The communications division accounted for 64% of 2025 sales and the satellite division for 36%, according to Korea IR Consulting's March 23, 2026 report.

In the third quarter of 2026, satellite-division revenue reached KRW 5.1 billion, up 53% year on year, and communications-division revenue rose 77% to KRW 3.6 billion, according to the company's November 13, 2026 announcement. Satellite therefore made up about 59% of third-quarter revenue and communications about 41%.

The communications business supplies terminals to Thuraya- and Inmarsat-linked customers.

On November 12, 2026, the company disclosed a contract with Space42 of the UAE to develop and mass-produce the next-generation satellite smartphone Thuraya Two, worth US$10.96 million (about KRW 14.7 billion), or roughly 30% of recent annual sales.

The company says it designs the system-on-chip at the core of its satellite terminals and can take products through full development and mass production. The satellite division's revenue is growing from development work on Korea's KPS navigation payload and the Baekdu system capability enhancement Phase 2 program.

Analysts describe the division as entering an early stage of shifting from development-led projects to mass-production manufacturing. In satellites, the company competes with Satrec Initiative in small satellite manufacturing and export, Hanwha Systems in payloads, and Korea Aerospace Industries in system integration.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual revenue fell from KRW 53.6 billion in 2022 to KRW 49.4 billion in 2023 (down 7.8% year on year), then rose to KRW 58.6 billion in 2024 (up 18.6%), the largest year in the 2022-2025 span. Revenue was smallest in 2025 at KRW 47.8 billion (down 18.5%).

Operating profit rose to KRW 10.1 billion in 2023 (up 124.1%), fell to KRW 7.3 billion in 2024 (down 27.5%), and turned into an operating loss of KRW 4.3 billion in 2025. Operating margin moved from 20.4% in 2023 to 12.5% in 2024 and -9.0% in 2025.

Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 10.5 billion in 2023 and KRW 10.4 billion in 2024, then turned into a net loss of KRW 2.77 billion in 2025. Net income including non-controlling interests was also a net loss of KRW 2.77 billion in 2025.

Korea IR Consulting noted that the satellite payload business was development-led, with R&D and labor costs incurred before revenue, which limited profitability, and that about 30 staff were added ahead of 2026 satellite work, raising fixed costs.

Equity at the end of 2025 was KRW 103.9 billion, liabilities were KRW 22.4 billion, and the debt ratio fell from 52.0% in 2023 to 21.6% in 2025. Operating cash flow went from KRW 28.3 billion in 2023 to -KRW 7.11 billion in 2024 and -KRW 4.46 billion in 2025.

By quarter, the third quarter of 2025 had revenue of KRW 5.36 billion and an operating loss of KRW 2.38 billion. The fourth quarter of 2025 jumped to KRW 15.9 billion (up 196.1% from the prior quarter) with operating profit of KRW 739 million.

The first quarter of 2026 fell to KRW 4.07 billion (down 74.4%) with an operating loss of KRW 2.56 billion. Revenue recovered to KRW 6.76 billion in the second quarter and KRW 8.69 billion in the third quarter (up 62.2% year on year), while operating losses narrowed to KRW 2.32 billion and KRW 1.48 billion.

However, the net loss attributable to controlling shareholders widened to KRW 1.29 billion, KRW 1.5 billion and KRW 2.42 billion across the first three quarters of 2026. The last four quarters' controlling-shareholder net loss totals KRW 4 billion.

Revenue timing follows development and delivery milestones, which explains the large quarter-to-quarter swings. Why operating and net loss trends diverge will be visible in the line items of the quarterly reports.

05

Industry analysis

The space industry is entering a phase in which government space programs are scaling up. An iM Securities analyst expected Korea's lunar exploration Phase 2 program, aimed at launching a lunar lander in 2032, to ramp up from 2026.

The same firm saw orders for the Cheollian-5 weather satellite gaining momentum as development moves forward. Small military reconnaissance satellite programs were also cited as favorable for the company, based on a January 2026 report, and the timing of tenders depends on government schedules.

In terminals, Korea IR Consulting expects new development demand for the few makers with in-house SoC design to persist over the medium term. Securities analysts have highlighted that the company's SoC design through full mass production helped it gain a position in Middle Eastern government communications.

In terms of cycle, the satellite division is moving from completed development toward mass-production manufacturing. Domestic satellite peers named in the same research include Satrec Initiative, Hanwha Systems and Korea Aerospace Industries.

On the customer side, supply to satellite operators such as Thuraya and Inmarsat is the core structure. Terminal demand follows commercial and specialized communications needs, which differ from the government-tender pattern of the satellite division.

Outlook

Management said at its third-quarter 2026 results that it will focus on sustained revenue growth and earnings improvement based on stable execution of existing projects and expanding new orders. The only firm new order is the UAE contract, which runs for up to 16 months from its effective date.

The next-generation product Thuraya Two is scheduled for launch in the fourth quarter of 2027. In March 2026, Korea IR Consulting estimated 2026 revenue at KRW 63.1 billion and operating profit at KRW 8.7 billion, a V-shaped recovery.

Its satellite-division revenue estimate was KRW 22.0 billion, of which about KRW 14.6 billion came from the existing backlog, while the rest depends on new lunar lander and Cheollian-5 orders. The same report said the estimate hinges on whether satellite-division orders are delayed.

Quarterly revenue in the first three quarters of 2026 was KRW 4.07 billion, KRW 6.76 billion and KRW 8.69 billion, so reaching the KRW 63.1 billion estimate would require a large fourth quarter. iM Securities described a lunar lander design to be completed by 2028, a validation vehicle launch in 2031 and a landing targeted for 2032.

The same firm also flagged likely orders for 40 small constellation satellites. The 2026 annual report, the next regular filing, will show annual revenue recognition and the order backlog, which will indicate how far these expectations are being realized.

Bull factors

Wider overseas terminal orders

The UAE contract with Space42, disclosed on November 12, 2026, is worth KRW 14.7 billion, roughly 30% of recent annual sales. The counterparty has executed similar contracts with the company over the last three years.

The company describes the Thuraya Two work as carrying its terminal experience into the next generation. The contract runs for up to 16 months from its effective date.

Shift from development to mass production

Fourth-quarter 2025 revenue rose to KRW 15.9 billion (up 196.1% from the prior quarter), with operating profit of KRW 739 million that turned the quarter profitable. Revenue increased in the second and third quarters of 2026.

Satellite-division revenue reached KRW 5.1 billion in the third quarter of 2026, up 53% year on year. Korea IR Consulting described the division as entering the early phase of moving from development-led work to mass production.

Government space program pipeline

iM Securities expected orders for lunar exploration Phase 2 and Cheollian-5 to materialize during 2026, according to a January 2026 report. Payload-computer development for Inmarsat ISat3 and a very-low-orbit small SAR test satellite has started to contribute to revenue.

The company's AIS payload work in small military reconnaissance satellites was cited as supporting order continuity.

Bear factors

Operating losses persist

Operating losses have been recorded in each of the first three quarters of 2026. The third-quarter operating loss of KRW 1.48 billion narrowed from a year earlier, but the only profitable quarter since the third quarter of 2025 was the fourth quarter of 2025. Operating margin was -9.0% in 2025. Narrower losses are not the same as a return to profit.

Wider controlling-shareholder net losses

The controlling-shareholder net loss widened each quarter in 2026, to KRW 1.29 billion, KRW 1.5 billion and KRW 2.42 billion. The four-quarter total is KRW 4 billion.

Operating losses narrowed while net losses widened, so the two measures point in different directions, and the reasons require checking the detailed income-statement lines.

Dependence on order timing and cash flow

Operating cash flow was -KRW 7.11 billion in 2024 and -KRW 4.46 billion in 2025. Korea IR Consulting noted that its 2026 revenue estimate relies on new satellite-division orders being recognized in the year.

Government projects are recognized as development and delivery progress after contracting, so order delays could shift the estimate.

10

Risk factors

Timing of government orders

Government space projects such as Cheollian-5 and lunar exploration Phase 2 recognize revenue in development and delivery stages after orders and contracts. If new orders for these programs arrive later than expected, satellite-division revenue and the timing of earnings recovery could slip. Part of the 2026 estimate depends on those new orders.

Overseas customers, FX and component sourcing

Profitability in the communications business can be affected by overseas customer orders, component procurement and exchange rates. Thuraya Two will be supplied through outsourced production. As overseas orders grow, FX moves and outsourced production costs will weigh more on margins.

Volatility of the space theme

Space and satellite communications stocks are noted for wide price swings driven by policy direction and changes in the global industry. Analysts point out that price moves unaccompanied by earnings improvement or new-business disclosures are harder to sustain. Whether earnings and order disclosures come through together is the key to this risk.

Overall view

Asia Pacific Satellite lnc is organized into a communications division for terminals and a satellite division for spacecraft and payloads.

After an operating loss of KRW 4.3 billion in 2025, revenue rose in the second and third quarters of 2026, but operating losses have persisted for three straight quarters from the first quarter to the third quarter of 2026.

Bullish factors include the KRW 14.7 billion UAE contract with Space42, the satellite division's shift from development to mass production, and government pipelines such as lunar exploration Phase 2 and Cheollian-5.

Bearish factors include the controlling-shareholder net loss widening to KRW 2.42 billion in the third quarter of 2026, a four-quarter total of KRW 4 billion, negative operating cash flow in 2024 and 2025, and reliance on the timing of government orders.

With losses over the last four quarters, the PER cannot be calculated, so valuation is compared against the historical PER band and the premium to book value. The next checkpoints are the 2026 annual report due by the end of March 2027 and any new order disclosures within 2026.

At those points, whether operating losses narrow and how fast the satellite division's backlog converts to revenue will determine which side of the debate gains ground.
