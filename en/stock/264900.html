02

Business structure

Crown Confectionery manufactures and sells biscuits, candy, chocolate, snacks and pies. The company was spun off from CROWNHAITAI Holdings in 2017 and relisted on the KOSPI in April of that year. The parent and four related parties hold 61.68% of common shares, and the free float ratio was 34.27% as of April 2026.

Its strengths are long-running brands and new product development, supplied through direct sales offices and distributors across Korea. Reliance on large discount stores and convenience store chains has grown. The company has said that higher selling expenses have limited profitability even as sales rose.

Global brands entering the market and intensifying competition have pushed up selling costs. The company is widening its reach into wellness products and health functional foods and keeps launching functional new products.

Overseas, it exports Saeukdalkom, Ha-Im, Jolly Pong, Kookdas and C-Corn mainly to Australia, the United States and China. Exports totaled KRW 13.5 billion in the first half of 2026, and overseas sales accounted for 6.2% of revenue.

By comparison, Orion's overseas sales share is in the 70% range and Lotte Wellfood's is in the 30% range, so Crown's overseas share is low among listed peers. Investment in the Asan plant began in 2022, and KRW 70 billion was spent including line relocation before completion in April 2024.

04

Earnings analysis

On a disclosed annual basis, sales rose from KRW 413.6 billion in 2022 to KRW 434.7 billion in 2023 (up 5.1% year on year), KRW 438.1 billion in 2024 (up 0.8%), and KRW 444.8 billion in 2025 (up 1.5%). Sales increased for three consecutive years from 2023 to 2025, and 2025 was the largest year of the 2022-2025 period.

Operating profit peaked at KRW 28.4 billion in 2023 (up 61.7% year on year) and then fell to KRW 21 billion in 2024 (down 26.0%) and KRW 15.3 billion in 2025 (down 27.5%), a two-year consecutive decline. Operating margin declined from 6.5% in 2023 to 4.8% in 2024 and 3.4% in 2025.

Sales grew while margins narrowed, a pattern that analysts generally attribute to higher selling expenses combined with cost pressure. Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders fell for three consecutive years, from KRW 27.1 billion in 2022 to KRW 23.5 billion, KRW 16.8 billion and KRW 12.3 billion in 2025.

Net profit including non-controlling interests matches these figures for each year from 2022 to 2025.

At the end of 2025, total equity was KRW 227.5 billion, all attributable to controlling shareholders, and total liabilities were KRW 127.4 billion, with the debt-to-equity ratio falling from 71.7% in 2022 to 56.0% in 2025.

Operating cash flow was KRW 24.6 billion in 2022, KRW 19.9 billion in 2023, KRW 33.6 billion in 2024 and KRW 21.2 billion in 2025, positive in every year.

Quarterly sales moved from KRW 108.5 billion in the third quarter of 2025 and KRW 112.5 billion in the fourth quarter to KRW 108.4 billion, KRW 110.7 billion and KRW 106.2 billion in the first three quarters of 2026.

Operating profit peaked at KRW 4.04 billion in the first quarter of 2026 and then declined for two consecutive quarters, to KRW 3.53 billion and KRW 1.85 billion. Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders over the last four quarters totaled KRW 9.32 billion.

Per press reports, cumulative sales for the first three quarters of 2026 were KRW 325.2 billion, down 2.2% year on year, and cumulative operating profit was KRW 9.4 billion, down 30.5%.

05

Industry analysis

The domestic snack market is one in which distribution channels hold growing bargaining power. Per the company's earnings commentary, reliance on large discount stores and convenience store chains has risen, and global brands entering the market have lifted selling expenses.

On the cost side, higher prices for naphtha and other key inputs, along with imported raw materials, have added to cost pressure. Press reports on the cost of sales ratio rising to 70.6% in the third quarter of 2026 point in the same direction.

Within the same group, Haetae raised prices on key products from October 2026, but the effect was not reflected in third-quarter results. How far such price increases offset cost pressure is likely to become visible in the group's fourth-quarter results.

Demand at the front end is weakening, as cumulative sales for the first three quarters of 2026 fell 2.2% year on year. Compared with Orion, whose overseas share is in the 70% range, and Lotte Wellfood, in the 30% range, Crown occupies a low position on overseas exposure.

Press reports also say the parent CROWNHAITAI Holdings depends on domestic sales for more than 90% of revenue, so the group as a whole is centered on the domestic market. New products tied to wellness and health functional food trends are a key axis of competition.

08

Bull factors

Balance sheet has strengthened

The debt-to-equity ratio fell from 71.7% in 2022 to 56.0% in 2025, and total equity expanded to KRW 227.5 billion at the end of 2025. Total liabilities stood at KRW 127.4 billion, so debt burden relative to equity has eased. Operating cash flow stayed positive in every year from 2022 to 2025. These figures provide some financial buffer while cost pressure persists.

Room for margin recovery from cost cuts

The company has said it is pursuing cost reductions against the burden of raw materials such as naphtha.

Operating profit of KRW 4.04 billion in the first quarter of 2026 was the highest among recent quarters, and net profit attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 3.42 billion was also the highest in that period. If the cost ratio stabilizes, there is room for operating margin to recover from 3.4% in 2025. That room, however, depends on the path of raw material prices.

Scope to expand exports

Exports were KRW 13.5 billion in the first half of 2026, and overseas sales accounted for 6.2% of revenue. Key export products include Saeukdalkom, Ha-Im, Jolly Pong, Kookdas and C-Corn, shipped mainly to Australia, the United States and China.

The Asan plant, completed in April 2024, is cited as a base for higher production capacity and efficiency. Given that Orion's overseas share is in the 70% range, the room for expansion remains wide.

Bear factors

Sales growth has slowed and turned negative

Sales grew only 0.8% in 2024 and 1.5% in 2025, and third-quarter 2026 sales of KRW 106.2 billion fell 2.1% year on year. Cumulative sales for the first three quarters of 2026 declined 2.2% year on year, per press reports.

Quarterly sales slipped from KRW 108.4 billion in the first quarter of 2026 to KRW 106.2 billion in the third. With top-line growth stalled, passing cost increases through to selling prices may be difficult.

Rising cost ratio and falling operating margin

Operating margin fell from 6.5% in 2023 to 3.4% in 2025. Press reports say the cost of sales ratio rose to 70.6% in the third quarter of 2026 from 69.5% a year earlier. The company pointed to higher prices for naphtha and other key inputs, as well as imported raw materials, as the source of cost pressure. The accumulation of raw material burden through the third quarter of 2026 is a weakness.

Distribution structure and selling expense burden

Reliance on large discount stores and convenience store chains has grown, and global brands entering the market have raised selling expenses. Operating profit fell 27.5% to KRW 15.3 billion in 2025. Cumulative operating profit for the first three quarters of 2026 declined 30.5% year on year, per press reports. If distribution partners' bargaining power strengthens further, promotional costs could rise.

Risk factors

Raw material price risk

Raw material costs, including naphtha-based inputs and imported materials, largely determine the company's cost base. The cost of sales ratio rose to 70.6% in the third quarter of 2026. How much of the increase in input prices can be passed on to selling prices will shape the direction of operating margin. The timing of when cost-reduction benefits appear is also uncertain.

Distribution and competition risk

Growing reliance on large discount stores and convenience store chains is strengthening the bargaining power of distribution partners. Global brands entering the domestic market are pushing up selling expenses. If selling expenses keep rising while sales stall, the decline in operating margin could continue.

Liquidity and overseas expansion risk

The parent and related parties hold 61.68% of shares, and the free float ratio was 34.27% as of April 2026, which limits the number of shares available to trade. With overseas sales at 6.2%, it remains uncertain whether export growth will become large enough to change results. If overseas expansion is delayed, the domestic-dependent structure will persist.

Overall view

Crown Confectionery's sales rose from KRW 413.6 billion in 2022 to KRW 444.8 billion in 2025, while operating profit fell from KRW 17.6 billion to KRW 15.3 billion over the same period.

On the balance sheet, the debt-to-equity ratio fell from 71.7% to 56.0% and total equity reached KRW 227.5 billion, widening the financial buffer. Operating cash flow was positive in every year from 2022 to 2025.

On the other side, sales have declined through the third quarter of 2026, and higher naphtha and other raw material costs pushed up the cost of sales ratio. Operating profit peaked at KRW 4.04 billion in the first quarter of 2026 and has since fallen for two consecutive quarters.

Strengths include financial capacity, room from cost reductions and the potential for overseas growth, while weaknesses include stalled sales, a higher cost ratio and selling expense pressure from the distribution structure.

The next checkpoints are the operating margin in the 2026 annual report and quarterly results for the first quarter of 2027.
