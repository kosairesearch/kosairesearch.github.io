Revenue rebounds, losses persist; turnaround is the test

02

Business structure

CrowdWorks was founded in 2017 as an AI training data specialist and listed on KOSDAQ on August 31, 2023.

The company organizes its business around three pillars, DATA, AI and education, starting from data labeling and expanding after generative AI into AI-ready data construction, custom model development and model evaluation.

Its data business runs on a crowdsourcing model that draws on a large pool of workers, and it presents full-scale inspection as its quality edge. According to a May 16, 2026 Digital Daily report based on the DART quarterly filing, first-quarter 2026 data revenue was KRW 1.277 billion, up 2.5% year on year.

Within that, LLM data revenue reached KRW 1.028 billion, up 49.9% and the main driver of growth, while AI data revenue was KRW 249 million, down 55.5%. AI business revenue was KRW 561 million, down 0.5% year on year. Government-led projects are also growing in weight.

In a national foundation model project led by the Ministry of Science and ICT, the company supplies core data for a 300B reasoning model led by Motif Technologies.

In May 2026 it also said it would take the lead on data construction in a KRW 40.3 billion shipbuilding AI project with UNIST and HD Hyundai Heavy Industries. The education business is run by its subsidiary, CrowdAcademy.

Competition is broad: a February 2022 ZDNet report counted about 700 AI training data platform companies in Korea.

04

Earnings analysis

In annual results from the regulatory filings, revenue rose from KRW 11.9 billion in 2022 to KRW 24 billion in 2023, a 101.6% increase, before falling to KRW 12 billion in 2024 (down 49.9%) and KRW 10.5 billion in 2025 (down 12.7%).

Operating losses were KRW 6.31 billion in 2022, KRW 1.79 billion in 2023, KRW 11.7 billion in 2024 and KRW 13.4 billion in 2025, a four-year run of losses.

Losses narrowed in 2023 but widened again in 2024 and 2025 as revenue declined, and the operating margin moved from -53.0% in 2022 to -7.4% in 2023, then -97.6% in 2024 and -127.5% in 2025.

Net losses attributable to controlling shareholders were KRW 13.5 billion, KRW 9.58 billion, KRW 8.82 billion and KRW 21.2 billion for 2022 through 2025, while total net loss including non-controlling interests was KRW 21.7 billion in 2025; the two measures differ and should be read separately.

In the February 2026 disclosure on the 2025 change, the company attributed the revenue decline to slow domestic AI adoption and a smaller government-funded education program, and cited development spending for its AI solutions business as a cost driver.

Quarterly revenue moved from KRW 2.35 billion in the third quarter of 2025 to KRW 4.24 billion in the fourth quarter (up 80.1% quarter on quarter), then KRW 2.85 billion in the first quarter of 2026 (down 32.8%), KRW 2.35 billion in the second quarter and KRW 2.48 billion in the third quarter (up 5.5% year on year).

Operating losses over the same five quarters were KRW 3.01 billion, KRW 4.58 billion, KRW 2.16 billion, KRW 3.59 billion and KRW 3.33 billion.

In the first quarter of 2026, based on DART data reported by Digital Daily, commission fees rose 204.2% and lifted costs, but revenue growth outpaced cost growth, so the loss ratio improved.

Total equity at the end of 2025 was KRW 16.8 billion and total liabilities KRW 23.8 billion, and operating cash flow was -KRW 11 billion in 2025 versus -KRW 4.88 billion in 2022.

05

Industry analysis

Demand for AI training data in Korea rises as AI models grow more sophisticated. A February 2022 ZDNet report counted about 700 AI training data platform companies in the domestic market.

In January 2022, the Electronic Times reported that new competitors keep appearing as CrowdWorks rose to the top of the data labeling industry, citing a comparatively low barrier to entry.

Downstream demand depends on how fast companies adopt AI, and the company's own explanation for the 2025 revenue decline, slow domestic AI adoption, shows how closely the business tracks that pace.

Government projects have become a rebound lever: a May 22, 2026 Money Today headline said national project awards had reached seven times the prior year's level.

Physical AI is emerging as a new demand source; in May 2026 the company set up a physical AI data lab inside the firm to research data from humanoid robots and remote manipulation equipment.

Securing manufacturing data is another response to the cycle, and the company linked its stake purchase in a stainless steel maker to the importance of manufacturing data collection. Direction varies by business line: AI data revenue fell 55.5% in the first quarter of 2026 while LLM data revenue rose 49.9%.

Compared with large IT services companies in the same sector, the scale of revenue and financial capacity differ widely, which is a structural feature of the company.

06

Outlook

The company has pointed to three directions: physical AI data, manufacturing AI data, and evaluation data for enterprise LLMs and agents. It is pursuing work that evaluates agent execution and turns robot movements into training data.

In the KRW 40.3 billion shipbuilding AI project, the company refines and builds training data from design drawings, work instructions, site video and sensor data. In the national foundation model project it is the dedicated supplier of core data for the 300B reasoning model.

Capital raising continued in 2026, with third-party allotment rights offerings of roughly KRW 4 billion in January and KRW 5 billion in February, followed by a small public offering of about KRW 1 billion in May.

The KRW 12 billion purchase of a 4.8% stake was approved by the board on August 6, 2026, with the transfer scheduled for August 10, 2026. The deal involves joint management participation, and the company said it is internally reviewing ways to generate revenue through collaboration with the stainless steel maker.

On liquidity, cash and cash equivalents were about KRW 7.5 billion at the end of the first quarter of 2026, and the stake payment equaled 71% of year-end 2025 total equity. The next checkpoint is the 2026 annual business report.

Against 2025 revenue of KRW 10.5 billion and an operating loss of KRW 13.4 billion, the key question is how far the 2026 operating margin and controlling-shareholder net loss narrow.

08

Bull factors

Expansion into physical AI and national projects

The company supplies core data for a national foundation model project and takes the lead on data construction in a KRW 40.3 billion shipbuilding AI project. It set up a physical AI data lab in May 2026. A Money Today report said national project awards had reached seven times the prior year's level.

LLM data growth and a narrower first-quarter loss ratio

First-quarter 2026 revenue of KRW 2.85 billion rose 50% year on year, with LLM data revenue up 49.9%. The operating loss narrowed 22% to KRW 2.16 billion, and the operating loss ratio improved from -145.9% to -75.8%.

Manufacturing data access and a stronger capital base

In August 2026 the company disclosed the KRW 12 billion purchase of a 4.8% stake in a stainless steel maker to secure manufacturing data and join joint management. Third-party allotment rights offerings in early 2026 added capital, and the debt-to-equity ratio fell from 313.3% in 2022 to 141.7% in 2025.

Bear factors

Persistent losses and wider losses

The 2025 operating loss of KRW 13.4 billion was larger than the KRW 6.31 billion in 2022. Controlling-shareholder net loss widened to KRW 21.2 billion in 2025 from KRW 8.82 billion in 2024.

Controlling-shareholder net losses totaled KRW 18.6 billion over the last four quarters, with operating losses in five straight quarters.

Falling revenue and wider cash outflows

Revenue peaked at KRW 24 billion in 2023 and then fell to KRW 12 billion in 2024 and KRW 10.5 billion in 2025. Operating cash flow was -KRW 11 billion in 2025, a larger outflow than the -KRW 4.88 billion of 2022. Cash and cash equivalents were about KRW 7.5 billion at the end of the first quarter of 2026.

Large cash outlay and liquidity pressure

The KRW 12 billion stake purchase equals 71% of year-end 2025 total equity. According to an eToday report, the market is watching its effect on liquidity, and accumulated deficit reached KRW 32.9 billion.

10

Risk factors

Liquidity and funding

After a large stake purchase, working capital management has become more demanding with cash and equivalents at about KRW 7.5 billion. Several rights offerings in 2026 show repeated fundraising, and further capital raises could dilute existing shareholders.

Governance and management change

In February 2026 the largest shareholder changed from a co-founder's holding to a new investment fund, and the CEO was replaced by Kim Kwang-il. The business continuity and execution speed of the strategy remain uncertain during this transition.

Demand and competition

The disclosed reasons for the 2025 revenue decline were slow domestic AI adoption and a smaller government-funded education program. The market had about 700 platform companies in 2022 and a comparatively low barrier to entry, so new competitors keep emerging.

12

Overall view

CrowdWorks's revenue peaked at KRW 24 billion in 2023 and then declined, and first-quarter 2026 revenue rose 50% year on year, a sign of top-line recovery.

Yet operating losses ran for four consecutive years from 2022 to 2025, with a 2025 operating margin of -127.5%, and have continued for five straight quarters since the third quarter of 2025, so a shift in the earnings structure is still an open task.

On the business side, LLM data, national projects and physical AI data are presented as new pillars, while AI data revenue fell 55.5% in the same period.

On the financial side, the debt-to-equity ratio fell from 313.3% in 2022 to 141.7% in 2025, but rights offerings continued in 2026 and the KRW 12 billion stake purchase in August 2026 equaled 71% of year-end 2025 total equity.

On valuation, the absence of an earnings-based multiple leaves book value and asset structure as the main points of comparison. The next checkpoint is the 2026 annual business report, where the operating margin and the size of the controlling-shareholder net loss will show whether the issues raised here are resolving.
