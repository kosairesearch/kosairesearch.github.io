Report overview

Printer losses meet a litho-equipment restructuring

Aroot has recorded operating losses for five straight quarters from the third quarter of 2025 through the third quarter of 2026, with the operating loss widening to KRW 11.6 billion in the latest quarter. At the same time, the company is absorbing its semiconductor equipment subsidiary NSRC to bring litho equipment business in-house.

02

Business structure

Aroot is a KOSDAQ-listed electronics and components company headquartered in Osan, Gyeonggi Province, with businesses spanning mini printers, semiconductor equipment, electrical equipment, waste treatment and distribution.

Based on 2Q 2026 sales, the mix was 41% mini printers, 32% semiconductor equipment, 17% electrical equipment, 6% waste treatment and 3% distribution.

The mini printer business centers on POS printers, mobile printers and label printers, and in the December 2025 consolidated breakdown the export share was 67.25% for POS printers and 96.78% for mobile printers.

The company estimates its domestic mini printer market share at about 30% and its overseas share at about 3%. Sales run through overseas specialist distributors and multinational system integrators.

The semiconductor equipment business has been run by subsidiary NSRC, which since 2024 has provided litho equipment installation and operation technical services to A, a large Chinese chipmaker.

The electrical equipment unit makes power supply and power conversion equipment, and waste recycling sits in subsidiary Aroot Eco. R&D spending equaled 4.42% of consolidated sales in the year ended December 2025.

Among competitors, overseas customer structure is the key variable in mini printers, and compared with large components makers such as Samsung Electro-Mechanics and LG Innotek, Aroot's revenue scale is small.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual revenue fell from KRW 64.9 billion in 2022 to KRW 62.2 billion in 2023, down 4.1% year on year, then rose to KRW 68.6 billion in 2024, up 10.2%, before dropping to KRW 50.6 billion in 2025, down 26.2%, making 2025 the smallest year of the 2022 to 2025 period.

Operating profit of KRW 347 million in 2022 turned into an operating loss of KRW 7.07 billion in 2023, widened to an operating loss of KRW 20.8 billion in 2024, and narrowed to an operating loss of KRW 10.9 billion in 2025.

The operating margin moved from 0.5% in 2022 to -11.4% in 2023, -30.3% in 2024 and -21.6% in 2025.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 6.24 billion in 2022, turned into a net loss of KRW 12.9 billion in 2023, widened to a net loss of KRW 45 billion in 2024, and narrowed to a net loss of KRW 16.9 billion in 2025.

Net income including non-controlling interests, a different measure, was a net loss of KRW 45.3 billion in 2024 and KRW 18.5 billion in 2025. In 2024 the net loss of KRW 45.3 billion exceeded the operating loss of KRW 20.8 billion, which points to a heavier burden from non-operating items that year.

Operating cash flow was negative in each of the four years, at -KRW 31.6 billion in 2022, -KRW 18.7 billion in 2023, -KRW 9.25 billion in 2024 and -KRW 10.8 billion in 2025.

The debt ratio rose from 26.3% in 2023 to 82.4% in 2024 and stood at 82.2% at the end of 2025, when total equity was KRW 105.2 billion, of which KRW 99.5 billion belonged to controlling shareholders.

Quarterly revenue went from KRW 14.4 billion in both the third and fourth quarters of 2025 to KRW 13.5 billion in the first quarter of 2026, rose to KRW 16.3 billion in the second quarter, and fell to KRW 8.47 billion in the third quarter, down 48.1% from the prior quarter.

Quarterly operating losses went from KRW 691 million in the third quarter of 2025 to KRW 4.69 billion in the fourth quarter, then KRW 3.05 billion in the first quarter of 2026 and KRW 3.36 billion in the second quarter, before widening to KRW 11.6 billion in the third quarter.

In the third quarter of 2026 the controlling-shareholder net loss was only KRW 953 million, far smaller than the operating loss, so the weight of non-operating items that quarter is a point to confirm in the periodic report's income breakdown.

Over the latest four quarters, from the fourth quarter of 2025 through the third quarter of 2026, the controlling-shareholder net losses totaled KRW 12.7 billion.

05

Industry analysis

The mini printer market is driven mainly by commercial demand such as POS, and the company's own estimate puts its domestic market share at about 30% and its overseas share at about 3%.

Because the product mix is weighted toward exports, order flow from overseas distributors tends to show up quickly in quarterly revenue. In semiconductor equipment, NSRC performs ArF litho equipment installation and operation technical services for A, a large Chinese chipmaker, at fabs where the equipment is applied.

Reports say this engagement was won in June 2026 through a specialist agent for refurbished equipment in China, and ArF tools are front-end equipment that enables very fine circuit patterns. The company expects large new supply contracts as demand for power semiconductors used in AI data centers grows from 2026.

The electrical equipment unit is linked to the same demand theme, but no public filing yet quantifies its contract size. In litho equipment, the company has said it will expand investment in higher-value tools after the merger.

The best evidence for the cycle position is the trend in printer revenue and the count of litho equipment orders, and no public figure shows the contribution of litho orders by value.

Among electronics and components companies, large players such as Samsung Electro-Mechanics and LG Innotek have far broader scale and customer bases, so Aroot sits in a smaller position centered on niche equipment and printers.

Its strengths are an overseas printer distribution network and litho technical staff, while its weaknesses are revenue volatility and customer concentration.

Outlook

Rather than giving numerical annual revenue or profit guidance, the company has pointed to expanding litho equipment orders and increasing investment after the merger.

For the NSRC absorption, objections to the merger could be filed from November 10 through November 24, 2026, and creditor objections are scheduled from November 25 through December 31, 2026.

The merger is set to take effect on January 1, 2027, with registration expected on January 15, 2027, and Aroot will take over NSRC's employment relationships as of the effective date.

The corporate value enhancement plan was approved by the board on October 22, 2026, and targets a price-to-book ratio of at least 0.50 times by 2029.

The same plan says the company will pursue shareholder returns of 10% to 20% of normalized net income attributable to controlling shareholders when financial conditions allow. If the KRW 24.7 billion recovered from the investment exit is used to strengthen the balance sheet, there may be room to manage the debt ratio.

On the capital side, the company completed a capital reduction in April 2026, a material-fact report on a rights offering was amended in August 2026, and the new shares from a third-party allotment are recorded with a listing date of December 15, 2026.

In November 2026 the de facto controlling shareholder, Choi Jeong-im, reported increases in common shares through an off-market purchase and on-market purchases, bringing holdings to 348,322 shares, or 7.97%, as of November 10, 2026.

The next periodic report, the 2026 annual report, must be filed by March 31, 2027, and it will show the fourth-quarter 2026 results and the full-year operating margin.

08

Bull factors

Building an in-house litho equipment base

According to reports published in late October 2026, NSRC won an engagement in June 2026 to provide ArF litho equipment installation and operation technical services to A, a large Chinese chipmaker. NSRC has supplied litho technical services to the same customer since 2024.

After the absorption, Aroot plans to run the business directly and increase investment to pursue higher-value litho equipment markets. Whether follow-on orders come in is a point for future disclosures.

Expected demand from AI data center power chips

The company has said it expects large new supply contracts as demand for power semiconductors used in AI data centers rises from 2026. The electrical equipment business, which makes power supply and power conversion equipment, accounted for 17% of 2Q 2026 sales.

The company also said it will pool sales capacity to pursue additional orders. Whether this expectation turns into revenue can be checked through supply contract disclosures and the electrical equipment sales trend.

Factors widening financial room

Reports in early September 2026 said the company recovered KRW 24.7 billion from an investment exit and realized an additional KRW 9.2 billion in profit above the principal. The company plans to use the funds to strengthen its financial structure and to fund new businesses, including stablecoin-related projects.

The corporate value enhancement plan approved on October 22, 2026 includes shareholder returns of 10% to 20% of normalized net income attributable to controlling shareholders. That return plan is conditional on financial conditions.

Bear factors

Continued losses and falling sales

The company has posted losses for five consecutive quarters from the third quarter of 2025 through the third quarter of 2026, and controlling-shareholder net losses over the latest four quarters total KRW 12.7 billion.

Third-quarter 2026 revenue was KRW 8.47 billion, down 41.2% from a year earlier, and the operating loss widened to KRW 11.6 billion. The fact that the operating loss grew while revenue fell makes the cost and fixed-cost structure a point to confirm in the next periodic report.

Deteriorating financial indicators

The debt ratio rose from 26.3% in 2023 to 82.4% in 2024 and stood at 82.2% at the end of 2025. Operating cash flow was negative in every year shown, at -KRW 31.6 billion in 2022, -KRW 18.7 billion in 2023, -KRW 9.25 billion in 2024 and -KRW 10.8 billion in 2025.

Total liabilities at the end of 2025 were KRW 86.5 billion and total equity was KRW 105.2 billion. Without a return to positive operating cash flow, funding needs could grow, and that is cited as a bearish factor.

Dilution and changes in the shareholder structure

A capital reduction was completed on April 30, 2026, sharply reducing the number of common shares, with the reduction ratio listed at 83.33% for both the major shareholder and minority shareholders.

In August 2026 an amended material-fact report on a rights offering was filed, and the new shares from a third-party allotment are recorded with a listing date of December 15, 2026. If capital raising continues, dilution of existing shareholders could persist, and that is a bearish factor.

Risk factors

Business and customer concentration

The litho equipment technical services have been centered on a single customer, A, since 2024. Printer sales, with a heavy overseas share, can also swing with the structure of export distributors.

Whether the third-quarter 2026 revenue decline reflects a one-time factor or a structural one cannot be separated with the figures publicly available.

Funding and dilution

The debt ratio stood at 82.2% at the end of 2025, up sharply from 26.3% in 2023. With operating cash flow negative and rights offerings and capital reductions occurring in succession, dilution of shareholdings is a risk factor.

Whether the KRW 24.7 billion recovered from the investment exit covers operating losses or goes into litho equipment investment after the merger is a point to confirm.

Merger execution and overseas regulation

The merger process runs through objection filings through November 24, 2026, creditor objections through December 31, 2026, and an effective date of January 1, 2027. Whether expanded litho investment after the merger translates into actual orders remains uncertain.

Overseas semiconductor equipment trade is subject to policy variables such as export controls, which could reach revenue from Chinese customers directly.

12

Overall view

Aroot combines a mini printer business with semiconductor equipment and electrical equipment, and operating losses narrowed in 2025 after three consecutive loss years from 2023 through 2025.

However, the operating loss widened again in the third quarter of 2026, to KRW 11.6 billion on revenue of KRW 8.47 billion, down 41.2% year on year.

Bullish factors include direct operation of litho equipment after the NSRC absorption, the company's expectation for AI data center power chip demand, the KRW 24.7 billion recovered from an investment exit, and a conditional shareholder return plan.

Bearish factors include five straight quarters of losses, a debt ratio of 82.2% with negative operating cash flow, and share structure changes from rights offerings and a capital reduction. Public figures are not yet enough to settle either side.

The next checkpoints are the merger timetable around January 1, 2027 and the operating margin in the 2026 annual report.
