02

Business structure

SGC Energy is an eco-friendly integrated energy company with power generation, construction and real estate, and glass segments. Power generation segment sales reached KRW 2.47 billion in Q3 2026, up 18.7% from a year earlier, according to company-announced segment figures released on October 22, 2026.

The segment passed through higher fuel costs by linking steam sale prices to them, and steam sales rose 16.8% to KRW 1.25 billion in the quarter. The average system marginal price, the wholesale electricity benchmark, rose 13% from the prior quarter to KRW 133 per kWh.

Renewable energy certificates (REC) and carbon emission allowances add to segment revenue. Q3 REC supply was 1.18 million RECs, and cumulative supply for 2026 reached 3.5 million, up 17% year on year.

The construction and real estate segment, run mainly through the subsidiary SGC E&C, saw Q3 sales fall 18.4% to KRW 2.68 billion as Middle East instability delayed some plant projects.

The glass segment posted Q3 sales of KRW 729 million-scale revenue of KRW 72.9 billion with an operating loss of KRW 5.3 billion, and the company is expanding overseas distribution for its Glasslock brand.

Power segment results move with the SMP and the fuel procurement structure, and segment operating profit rose 147.8% to KRW 33.2 billion in Q3.

04

Earnings analysis

On a reported basis, annual sales rose from KRW 2.82 trillion in 2022 to KRW 3.02 trillion in 2023, up 7.1%, before falling 22.1% to KRW 2.35 trillion in 2024 and recovering 4.5% to KRW 2.46 trillion in 2025. Operating profit was largest in 2022 at KRW 209.4 billion and smallest in 2025 at KRW 95.8 billion.

It fell 49.0% to KRW 106.8 billion in 2023, rebounded 77.5% to KRW 189.6 billion in 2024, and fell 49.5% in 2025. The operating margin moved from 7.4% in 2022 to 3.5% in 2023, 8.1% in 2024 and 3.9% in 2025.

Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 112.8 billion in 2022, KRW 42.1 billion in 2023 and KRW 65 billion in 2024, before a net loss of KRW 61.4 billion in 2025 that marked a swing into loss. Net loss for 2025 was KRW 94.8 billion on a total basis including non-controlling interests.

Operating cash flow was KRW 345.7 billion in 2022, -KRW 33.3 billion in 2023, KRW 94.1 billion in 2024 and -KRW 32.7 billion in 2025.

On a quarterly basis, operating profit was KRW 25.4 billion in Q3 2025, KRW 18.8 billion in Q4 2025, KRW 35.7 billion in Q1 2026, KRW 44.9 billion in Q2 2026 and KRW 39.9 billion in Q3 2026.

Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders turned positive in Q1 2026 at KRW 3.17 billion, then reached KRW 8.02 billion in Q2 and KRW 8.73 billion in Q3.

According to the company's own announcement on October 22, 2026, nine-month operating profit reached KRW 120.5 billion, up 56.5%, or about 85% of the initial full-year guidance of KRW 142 billion.

05

Industry analysis

Profitability in the power sector depends on oil and LNG price trends. In an early May 2026 report, Hana Securities noted that domestic LNG import prices often track the Japan Crude Cocktail benchmark, creating a lag of about five to six months before costs are reflected.

The same report described the oil price surge as a relatively favorable environment for generators. Plants that use direct-import LNG, bituminous coal, or biomass could benefit from higher SMP, the report said.

In a May 22, 2026 report, Hana Securities classified SGC Energy as a Top Pick, citing low exposure to bituminous coal and factors such as higher SMP, higher emission allowance prices and expanded REC issuance. SK GAS was also named a top pick in the same coverage.

On the other side, Hana Securities flagged that concentrated maintenance at global LNG production facilities in June and July could raise spot price volatility.

The same firm also pointed to a possible long-term margin squeeze for generators from competitive bidding price caps under the government's first renewable energy basic plan. The company said the average carbon allowance price in Q3 2026 was KRW 27,549 per ton, up 40% from the prior quarter.

The construction segment's exposure to project delays has pushed its results in a different direction from the power segment.

06

Outlook

The company set an initial full-year operating profit guidance of KRW 142 billion and said nine-month operating profit reached about 85% of that target. For Q4, it expects continued gains in the power segment as seasonal electricity demand lifts the SMP.

The company also expects carbon allowance prices to remain strong at around KRW 30,000 per ton in Q4, and plans to carry surplus allowances into 2027 for profit recognition.

In the AI data center business, the 60MW Gunsan project began construction in October 2026, with commercial operation targeted for 2028 and a 240MW commercial operation goal for 2030.

In September 2026 the board approved a KRW 86.9 billion investment as a limited partner in a private equity partnership, with SGC Partners, Korea Investment & Securities and Harvest Equity Partners as co-general partners.

For the 240MW phase, the company has already filed for a power grid impact assessment, aims to pass final review by September 2028, and plans to start construction in 2029. The company also plans to build LNG power plants later to provide integrated power infrastructure for the data centers.

A second-phase business plan is slated for announcement in the first half of 2027. These projects are still before construction, so their effect on reported earnings is likely to come after 2028.

08

Bull factors

Improving power segment profitability

Power segment operating profit rose 147.8% to KRW 33.2 billion in Q3. Linking steam sale prices to fuel costs and a higher SMP lifted profit. The average carbon allowance price in Q3 was also sharply higher than a year earlier.

On-site AI data center push

The company presents an on-site model that supplies power from its own plants directly to data centers as a new growth axis. The board approved a KRW 86.9 billion investment in September 2026, and the 240MW project has completed its grid impact assessment filing. The 60MW Gunsan project entered construction in October 2026.

Quarterly earnings recovery

Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders turned positive in Q1 2026, then rose to KRW 8.02 billion in Q2 and KRW 8.73 billion in Q3. According to the company's announcement, nine-month operating profit of KRW 120.5 billion reached about 85% of the initial guidance.

Bear factors

Weak construction and glass segments

Construction and real estate sales fell 18.4% to KRW 2.68 billion in Q3 as Middle East instability delayed plant projects. The glass segment widened its operating loss to KRW 5.3 billion in Q3. Consolidated sales have declined for three consecutive quarters since Q1 2026.

Rising financial burden

The debt-to-equity ratio rose to 339.2% in 2025 from 286.9% in 2024, and total liabilities at the end of 2025 stood at KRW 2.58 trillion. Operating cash flow in 2025 was -KRW 32.7 billion. Funding needs for the KRW 86.9 billion investment and the 240MW project could add to this burden.

Policy and price volatility

Hana Securities noted that competitive bidding price caps under the first renewable energy basic plan could narrow generator margins over the long term. Spot LNG price volatility could rise during the June-July period of concentrated maintenance at global LNG facilities. Power segment results depend heavily on SMP trends.

10

Risk factors

Policy and regulation

The shift to competitive bidding under the renewable energy basic plan could compress generator margins. Revenue tied to carbon allowances and RECs could change with regulatory revisions. Power segment sales depend on the SMP and the fuel cost pass-through structure.

Financing and capital needs

With the debt-to-equity ratio at 339.2%, full-scale AI data center development could raise additional funding needs. Total liabilities at the end of 2025 were KRW 2.58 trillion. Whether the KRW 86.9 billion investment and the project finance structure operate smoothly is a key point to monitor.

Execution and geopolitics

The 240MW project must proceed in line with final grid impact review and the 2029 construction start target. The construction segment faces delay risks from Middle East instability. The glass segment needs to show that overseas distribution expansion translates into results.

12

Overall view

SGC Energy's Q3 2026 operating profit reached KRW 39.9 billion, supported by better profitability in the power generation segment. Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders stayed positive from Q1 through Q3 2026, and nine-month operating profit reached about 85% of the initial full-year guidance.

On the other hand, the KRW 61.4 billion net loss attributable to controlling shareholders in 2025 and the KRW 23.8 billion cumulative net loss over the last four quarters show that the pace of the earnings recovery still needs confirmation.

Construction and real estate sales fell 18.4%, and the glass segment remains loss-making, so consolidated results depend heavily on power generation. The debt-to-equity ratio rose to 339.2%, and how the KRW 86.9 billion investment and AI data center funding affect the capital structure is a key variable.

The Gunsan 60MW construction and the 240MW grid review schedule show when the on-site business could start to affect results. The next checkpoints are the confirmed full-year results and debt ratio in the 2026 annual report, and the second-phase plan due in the first half of 2027.
