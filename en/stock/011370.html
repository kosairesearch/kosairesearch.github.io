Seohan: Order Growth Amid Quarterly Earnings Swings

Seohan's 2025 operating profit rose to KRW 62.6 billion and order intake is expanding in 2026, but revenue decline and swings in quarterly net profit remain the key tensions.

  1. 1

    Operating profit in 2025 was KRW 62.6 billion, up 136.4% from the prior year, and the operating margin rose to 9.7%, above the 8.2% recorded in 2022.

  2. 2

    Operating profit reached KRW 24.2 billion in the third quarter of 2026, rising for three straight quarters from the fourth-quarter 2025 low and up 148.1% from a year earlier.

  3. 3

    Order intake in 2025 reached KRW 1.6 trillion, the largest in the company's history, and management set a 2026 order target of KRW 2.2 trillion.

  4. 4

    Revenue fell 13.9% to KRW 645.1 billion in 2025, and attributable net income turned to a loss of KRW 5 billion in the fourth quarter of 2025 before returning to profit in the second quarter of 2026.

  5. 5

02

Business structure

Seohan is a Daegu-based construction company founded in 1971 that built its base in housing and expanded into public infrastructure such as railways and roads, as well as urban redevelopment. Its business is divided into building construction, civil engineering, sales-linked construction and other.

Per the FnGuide data, sales-linked construction accounted for 49.1 percent of revenue in its own classification, and it focuses on public-sector contracts. In the third quarter of 2024, building construction made up 58.19 percent of total revenue, the largest share.

Order intake in 2025 reached KRW 1.6 trillion, the largest in company history, with KRW 208 billion from urban rail and infrastructure, KRW 592.6 billion from private contracts and KRW 395.6 billion from self-developed projects.

In housing, Seohan is building on its regional housing-cooperative experience in Daegu to expand into the Seoul metropolitan area and the Honam region. On August 31, 2026, it signed a KRW 164.9 billion mixed-use construction contract with the Yangsan Central regional housing cooperative in Gwangju.

A October 2026 report said it also won a KRW 129.6 billion AI data center project in Daechi-dong, Gangnam, a case described as unusual because a mid-sized regional builder took on the shell and facilities together in a market led by large contractors.

By the 2026 construction capability ranking it ranks 49th nationally and second among Daegu builders. Compared with large general contractors such as Hyundai Engineering & Construction and DL E&C, its scale is smaller, but it is widening a regional order base into a nationwide one.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual revenue moved from KRW 730 billion in 2022 to KRW 621.7 billion in 2023, KRW 749.4 billion in 2024 and KRW 645.1 billion in 2025. 2024 was the largest year in the period and 2023 the smallest. Revenue fell 14.8 percent in 2023, rose 20.5 percent in 2024 and fell 13.9 percent in 2025.

Operating profit dropped from KRW 59.8 billion in 2022 to KRW 24.2 billion in 2023, a decline of 59.5 percent, then rose to KRW 26.5 billion in 2024 and KRW 62.6 billion in 2025. The company attributed the 2025 improvement mainly to better cost ratios at project sites and a profit-focused approach to operations.

The operating margin went from 8.2 percent in 2022 to 3.9 percent in 2023 and 3.5 percent in 2024, then rose to 9.7 percent in 2025, above the 2022 level. Higher revenue share from sales-linked construction and cost reductions in building construction appear to have lifted gross profit.

Attributable net income fell from KRW 39.4 billion in 2022 to KRW 8.54 billion in 2023, a 78.3 percent decline, then rose to KRW 19.3 billion in 2024 and KRW 28.9 billion in 2025, with growth in each of the last two years. Net income including non-controlling interests was reported at the same amounts for each year.

Operating cash flow moved from KRW -167.2 billion in 2022, KRW -7.35 billion in 2023 and KRW -85.7 billion in 2024 to KRW 123.6 billion in 2025, turning positive. The debt ratio declined from 164.4 percent in 2022 and 181.8 percent in 2023 to 163.9 percent in 2024 and 129.1 percent in 2025.

Total equity at the end of 2025 was KRW 536.8 billion, all of it attributable to controlling shareholders, against total liabilities of KRW 692.8 billion.

On a quarterly basis, revenue was KRW 138.7 billion in the third quarter of 2025, KRW 155.2 billion in the fourth quarter (up 11.9 percent from the prior quarter), KRW 105.3 billion in the first quarter of 2026 (down 32.2 percent), KRW 147.6 billion in the second quarter (up 40.2 percent) and KRW 153.5 billion in the third quarter (up 4.0 percent quarter on quarter and 10.7 percent year on year).

Operating profit bottomed at KRW 2.5 billion in the fourth quarter of 2025, down 74.4 percent from the prior quarter, then rose to KRW 6.23 billion in the first quarter of 2026, KRW 22.3 billion in the second and KRW 24.2 billion in the third, three straight quarterly increases.

Attributable net income swung to a loss of KRW 5 billion in the fourth quarter of 2025, a loss of KRW 858 million in the first quarter of 2026 with a narrower loss, then a profit of KRW 19.5 billion in the second quarter.

The third quarter came in at KRW 11.6 billion, down 40.6 percent quarter on quarter but up 111.9 percent year on year. Attributable net income over the most recent four quarters totaled KRW 25.2 billion.

Operating profit kept rising through the third quarter while attributable net income declined after the second quarter, so the gap between operating and net results needs to be read together in the next regular filing.

05

Industry analysis

The domestic construction industry has been described as in a prolonged downturn as tighter safety rules and rising construction costs compound each other.

FnGuide attributed Seohan's sales declines to government real estate regulation, smaller SOC budgets and public-sector debt concerns, which limited new orders across its housing-linked, building and civil works. The first-half 2026 consolidated revenue decline is consistent with that backdrop.

Seohan is nonetheless diversifying into SOC, urban rail, tourist lodging and T/K and BTL project formats to adapt to changes in how public work is procured. Its order base is also moving from Daegu toward the Seoul metropolitan area and the Honam region.

The data center market has been led by large contractors, so a mid-sized regional builder taking on shell and facilities together was described as unusual, and facility construction capability is becoming a key competitive factor.

In the Ministry of Land, Infrastructure and Transport's 2026 mutual cooperation evaluation, Seohan ranked first among large Daegu and Gyeongbuk contractors for the tenth straight year, and before the 2025 Chuseok and 2026 Lunar New Year holidays it paid about KRW 56.7 billion in advance to subcontractors.

In the 2025 safety assessment it was the only Daegu builder rated Very Excellent. It is also taking part in an NPL restructuring of a distressed project financing site in Daegu.

Compared with large general contractors, its scale and overseas experience are more limited, so it reads as a mid-sized builder balancing regional and out-of-region projects.

06

Outlook

The company set a 2026 order target of KRW 2.2 trillion and emphasized business diversification and out-of-region expansion. Because that target is well above the KRW 1.6 trillion booked in 2025, the result will be confirmed by the 2026 order tally expected in early 2027.

Its housing supply plan covers about 2,277 units in 2026, including 510 units at Seohan EduCentum in Pyeongtaek Godeok, 512 units at Seohan Edaum in Namyangju Jinjeop 2, 462 units in Gimpo Singok and a planned 793 units in Ulsan Hwajeong.

The data center project covers about 4,949 square meters, with five basement levels and four above ground, a 27-month construction period from groundbreaking, and a contract period running from November 30, 2026 to February 28, 2029.

The Gwangju Yangsan Central mixed-use project is a 39-story, five-basement development with 406 apartment units and a 50-month construction period, with a groundbreaking target of December 2026.

These two projects add to the order backlog, but revenue recognition will follow groundbreaking and construction progress from quarter to quarter. Since first-quarter 2026 revenue marked the low point, the key question is how far revenue recovers from the fourth quarter of 2026 onward.

On infrastructure, the weight of railway and road work and T/K and BTL projects will shape funding structure and profitability. Safety and subcontractor management remain emphasized in competition for orders.

The next checkpoints are the annual revenue and operating margin in the 2026 annual business report, the order backlog it discloses, whether the Gwangju project breaks ground, and how the data center work progresses after the contract period begins.

Risk factors

Order Execution and Construction Timing

The Gwangju mixed-use project targets a December 2026 groundbreaking, and the data center contract period begins November 30, 2026. Delays would push back the point at which backlog converts to revenue. With construction periods of 27 to 50 months, cost and schedule control will drive margins.

Housing Sales and Regional Property Conditions

The 2026 sales plan covers about 2,277 units, including projects by regional housing cooperatives and urban development schemes. Schedules for housing-cooperative projects can shift with member recruitment and permits.

Returns on the NPL restructuring of distressed project financing sites in Daegu will depend on local property conditions.

Cost Ratios and Policy Variables

The 2025 operating profit improvement was mainly attributed to better cost ratios at project sites. Tighter safety rules could raise on-site costs. Because the company made early payments of about KRW 56.7 billion to subcontractors ahead of holidays, collection pace and liquidity management are also points to monitor.
