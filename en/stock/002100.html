Kyungnong: Seasonal Earnings and Quarterly Swings

Kyungnong's results depend heavily on farming-season sales, and after the first-quarter 2026 peak, profit fell sharply in the second and third quarters, making quarterly swings the defining feature.

02

Business structure

Kyungnong makes and sells chemical crop protection products, including fungicides, insecticides and herbicides, and crop protection is one of its two main revenue pillars.

Its fertilizer business operates through a subsidiary, and a company results comment dated September 3, 2026 said fertilizer sales expanded on rising demand for slow-release fertilizers as the rural population ages.

The same comment said the crop protection segment held up on concentrated demand during the farming season and a stable Nonghyup distribution channel.

The NH cooperative channel is a large share of sales, and a supply contract disclosed on February 6, 2026 with NH Agricultural Products Marketing Corporation totaled KRW 132.7 billion, equal to 40.82% of recent sales as disclosed.

Its flagship product Dysen M-45 still anchors the lineup, while products registered within the last decade have started to rise in the sales rankings. A 2025 industry analysis by Youngnong Jaerye News reported Captain at about KRW 33 billion and the mite control product Mitking at about KRW 6 billion in sales.

The same analysis noted Kyungnong has nine products above KRW 5 billion, fewer than the major players, and that shared products such as Dysen M-45 leave a concentration question open. The market is an oligopoly in which the leader Farmhannong is followed by NH Chemical, Dongbang Agro and Hankook Samgong.

In the first half of 2025 the market shares were Farmhannong 28.2%, NH Chemical 18.4% and Kyungnong 15.9%. Pesticide demand follows pest outbreaks and planting seasons, so quarterly sales tend to cluster in particular months.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual revenue went from KRW 361.6 billion in 2022 to KRW 347.4 billion in 2023, down 3.9% year on year, and then to KRW 325.2 billion in 2024, down 6.4%, before recovering to KRW 339.2 billion in 2025, up 4.3%. Among 2022 to 2025, 2022 was the largest year and 2024 the smallest.

Operating profit fell from KRW 41 billion in 2022 to KRW 25.2 billion in 2023, down 38.5%, the low point of the period, and then rose to KRW 26.7 billion in 2024, up 6.0%, and KRW 28.7 billion in 2025, up 7.4%, for two consecutive years of growth.

The operating margin slid from 11.3% in 2022 to 7.3% in 2023 and then edged back to 8.2% in 2024 and 8.5% in 2025, still below the 2022 level.

Profit attributable to controlling shareholders fell from KRW 30.9 billion in 2022 to KRW 15.1 billion in 2023, down 51.0%, and then recovered to KRW 17.2 billion in 2024, up 13.7%, and KRW 18.6 billion in 2025, up 8.4%.

Net income including non-controlling interests was KRW 19.6 billion in 2025, a different figure from profit attributable to controlling shareholders.

The debt-to-equity ratio moved from 72.2% in 2022 to 67.1% in 2023, 75.5% in 2024 and 68.7% in 2025, and at year-end 2025 total equity was KRW 288.4 billion, of which KRW 263.5 billion belonged to controlling shareholders, against total liabilities of KRW 198.1 billion.

Operating cash flow rose from KRW 14.4 billion in 2022 to KRW 27 billion in 2024 before turning to -KRW 7.73 billion in 2025.

On a quarterly basis, the third quarter of 2025 had revenue of KRW 45.5 billion and an operating loss of KRW 4.39 billion, and the fourth quarter had revenue of KRW 42.8 billion and an operating loss of KRW 15 billion.

The first quarter of 2026 posted revenue of KRW 153.5 billion and operating profit of KRW 32.3 billion, a swing back to profit.

The second quarter brought revenue of KRW 122 billion and operating profit of KRW 13.5 billion, and the third quarter revenue fell 67.3% from the prior quarter to KRW 39.9 billion, with an operating loss of KRW 6.46 billion.

Profit attributable to controlling shareholders over the last four quarters totaled KRW 14 billion, and the concentration of farming-season demand in the first quarter appears to be what amplifies the quarterly swings.

05

Industry analysis

According to Youngnong Jaerye News, total domestic pesticide sales reached KRW 2.0895 trillion in 2025, up 4.0% from the year before. The same analysis counted 26 products with sales above KRW 10 billion, totaling KRW 526.7 billion, and noted that newer active-ingredient products filled much of the top ranks.

Sales in the first half of 2024 were reported to have declined. Kyungnong's market share edged up from 15.2% in the first half of 2024 to 15.9% in the first half of 2025, while Farmhannong held the top position at 28.2%.

The gap also shows in the count of large products, with Farmhannong at 25, NH Chemical at 12, Dongbang Agro at 11, Hankook Samgong at 10 and Kyungnong at 9 products above KRW 5 billion. Kyungnong sits in the top three by share but has a less concentrated product base than the largest players.

Demand follows pest outbreaks and planting calendars, and it runs through the NH purchasing structure, so price competition among suppliers and channel bargaining power affect results. The fertilizer business adds a separate demand driver tied to slow-release products for an aging farm population.

Because weather and pest pressure swing annual demand, the cycle position is better read from market size and share trends over several years than from a single figure.

Bull factors

Stable NH channel and large supply contract

A supply contract with the NH cooperative channel disclosed on February 6, 2026 totaled KRW 132.7 billion, equal to 40.82% of recent sales as disclosed. A company results comment said the secured NH distribution channel supported steady crop protection results. A sales channel backed by contracts of this size adds visibility during the season when demand concentrates.

A second demand driver in fertilizer

A company results comment dated September 3, 2026 said fertilizer sales expanded on rising demand for slow-release products as the rural population ages. Crop protection depends on pest outbreaks and planting calendars, while fertilizer draws on a different demand factor, so the two can partly offset seasonal swings.

The segment-level revenue split is limited in the public record, so the contribution can only be read directionally.

Slight share gain and newer registrations

Kyungnong's market share rose from 15.2% in the first half of 2024 to 15.9% in the first half of 2025. Total domestic pesticide sales grew 4.0% in 2025, and products registered within the last decade have climbed the sales rankings. As newer products carry a larger share of sales, dependence on a single flagship item would fall.

Bear factors

Sharp quarterly profit swings

Third-quarter 2026 revenue fell 67.3% from the prior quarter and 12.3% from a year earlier, to KRW 39.9 billion. The quarter posted an operating loss of KRW 6.46 billion and a net loss attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 4.15 billion. A thin revenue base outside the farming season leaves quarterly results highly sensitive to seasonality.

Operating cash flow turned negative

Operating cash flow fell from KRW 27 billion in 2024 to -KRW 7.73 billion in 2025. Operating profit rose to KRW 28.7 billion over the same year, yet cash generation did not follow, a point to examine in working capital. The debt-to-equity ratio eased to 68.7% in 2025 from 75.5% in 2024, but it should be read alongside cash flow.

Fewer large products than leaders

Kyungnong has nine products above KRW 5 billion in sales, compared with 25 for the market leader. Shared products that overlap with peers were also flagged as a task. With heavy reliance on the NH channel, a thinner product base can weaken bargaining power relative to larger suppliers.

Risk factors

Weather and pest risk

Pesticide demand shifts from year to year with pest pressure and weather. Because sales cluster in the farming season, a shift in timing can move quarterly results sharply. The first-quarter 2026 peak followed by a third-quarter loss shows seasonality flowing directly into reported earnings.

Financial flow and cash generation

At year-end 2025, total liabilities were KRW 198.1 billion against total equity of KRW 288.4 billion. Operating cash flow was negative at -KRW 7.73 billion in 2025.

With profit attributable to controlling shareholders over the last four quarters at KRW 14 billion, a thin earnings base combined with rising working capital could narrow financial room.

Channel concentration, regulation and inputs

A high share of sales through the NH channel means changes in channel terms or contract volume feed directly into revenue. Crop protection is exposed to safety regulation and raw material costs, so tighter rules or input price increases could pressure margins. These effects would appear in cost ratios and operating margin in the 2026 annual report.

12

Overall view

Kyungnong combines crop protection and fertilizer in one agricultural inputs business, and its reliance on the NH cooperative channel is pronounced.

Annual operating profit and profit attributable to controlling shareholders have risen for two consecutive years from 2024 to 2025 after the 2023 low, and the operating margin recovered from 7.3% in 2023 to 8.5% in 2025.

Quarterly results in 2026, however, have been volatile, with a first-quarter swing to profit, a decline in the second quarter and a third-quarter operating loss. Operating cash flow turned negative in 2025, and profit attributable to controlling shareholders over the last four quarters totaled KRW 14 billion.

The valuation sits within Kyungnong's own annual PER range, while the price-to-book ratio is below its annual lows. The bull points are the NH channel, large contracts, fertilizer demand and a slight share gain, while the bear points are quarterly volatility, weaker cash flow and a thin large-product base. The next checkpoints are the 2026 annual report and farming-season results in 2027.
