02

Business structure

Seoul Guarantee Insurance Company is Korea's only full-line guarantee insurer, handling performance guarantees, receivables guarantees, financial guarantees and housing and real estate guarantees.

As of the end of March 2026, total guarantee balances stood at KRW 485 trillion, with performance guarantees at 29%, personal guarantees at 14%, licensing guarantees at 6%, personal finance at 12%, MCI mortgage-related at 5% and jeonse deposit protection at 1%.

Based on 2025 data, the company held a 23.9% share of total guarantee balances, rising to 55% within the private guarantee market.

By industry, financial and insurance services accounted for 23%, manufacturing 18%, real estate and leasing 9% and construction 6%, which the company's supporters describe as broad diversification.

Earnings come from two sources: underwriting profit from the core guarantee business and investment income from a bond-centered portfolio.

At the end of March 2026, safe assets such as cash, deposits, government and special bonds made up 34% of invested assets, corporate and financial bonds 26% and beneficiary certificates 18%, while risk assets were 25%.

Jeonse-related products previously ran high loss ratios, but retroactive premium settlements are reflected each year and affect underwriting profitability.

Competition comes from public-sector guarantee bodies such as Korea Housing & Urban Guarantee Corporation, mutual aid associations, banks and the Korea Trade Insurance Corporation, each serving its own segments. The company says premiums from Saitdol, a mid-interest-rate personal finance product, are growing.

04

Earnings analysis

Full-year 2025 insurance revenue was KRW 2.18 trillion, up 4.2% from KRW 2.09 trillion in 2024. Operating profit rose from KRW 279.7 billion to KRW 375.7 billion, up 34.3%, and profit attributable to controlling shareholders rose from KRW 213.1 billion to KRW 268.4 billion, up 25.9%.

Net profit including non-controlling interests moved from KRW 213.3 billion to KRW 268.4 billion. Operating profit grew faster than insurance revenue, and improved underwriting profit is cited as the main reason.

The company said 2025 underwriting profit was KRW 251.1 billion, up 71.7% from the prior year, and that Q4 2025 underwriting profit rose 117.0%. A credit rating report noted that the loss ratio fell from 79.1% in 2024 to 76.4% in 2025.

The debt-to-equity ratio edged up from 80.3% in 2024 to 81.1% in 2025, and operating cash flow rose from KRW 315 billion to KRW 387.2 billion.

By quarter, insurance revenue went from KRW 530.9 billion in Q3 2025 to KRW 554.6 billion in Q4 2025, peaked at KRW 652.4 billion in Q1 2026, then fell to KRW 545.7 billion in Q2 and KRW 547.2 billion in Q3 2026.

Operating profit peaked at KRW 182.9 billion in Q4 2025 and then declined for three consecutive quarters, to KRW 93.5 billion in Q1 2026, KRW 87 billion in Q2 and KRW 39.1 billion in Q3 2026.

In Q3 2026, one-off performance-guarantee claim payments reduced underwriting profit to KRW 9.8 billion, according to iM Securities.

Profit attributable to controlling shareholders fell from KRW 121.7 billion in Q4 2025 to KRW 39 billion in Q3 2026, and the last four quarters (Q4 2025 to Q3 2026) total KRW 284.2 billion.

05

Industry analysis

The guarantee insurance market had total guarantee balances of KRW 485 trillion at the end of March 2026, with performance guarantees the largest category.

Seoul Guarantee Insurance held a 23.9% share of total guarantee balances in 2025 and 55% of the private guarantee market, which gives it a large position in the private segment. Guarantee demand is tied to construction, real estate and financial conditions, so it moves with the economic cycle.

According to brokerage research, the original earned loss ratio fell from a low of 32% in 2021 to a high of 74.7% in 2024 and then to 53.0% in Q1 2026.

A credit rating report explained that when economic weakness coincides with the end of policy support, the loss ratio rises and subrogation recovery rates fall, which weighs on profitability. iM Securities assessed that profit sensitivity to the economy is somewhat higher than at general insurers, because claims rise in downturns and are recovered through subrogation in upturns.

Retroactive premium settlements on jeonse-related products lifted underwriting profit in 2026. On capital quality, the weighted non-performing asset ratio was 0.15% at the end of March 2026, below the non-life insurance industry average of 0.24% at the end of 2025. The rating report cites this as evidence of strong asset quality.

Outlook

In its 2026 corporate value enhancement plan, the company set targets of at least KRW 200 billion in annual shareholder returns and a payout ratio of 50% or more. It said it would keep a minimum dividend floor while carrying out quarterly dividends.

The same plan listed core business growth, profitability improvement, higher investment returns and a global strategy upgrade as 2026 priorities. According to the company, premiums on Saitdol products and jeonse-related products grew in the first half of 2026, widening underwriting profit.

On November 16, 2026, iM Securities said in a report that the key to future earnings improvement is retroactive premium settlements, and that underwriting profit could recover as jeonse guarantee settlement premiums improve from 2027.

The same brokerage estimated standalone net profit at KRW 315.2 billion for 2026, KRW 390 billion for 2027 and KRW 414 billion for 2028; these are broker estimates, not disclosed results.

LS Securities said in a May 2026 report that the pace of profitability recovery is slow but the earnings improvement trend continues. The next checkpoints are operating profit for Q4 2026 and the full-year loss ratio trend.

The public fund redemption fund is scheduled to be liquidated at the end of 2027, so the timing of the Korea Deposit Insurance Corporation's stake sales is also an item to track.

08

Bull factors

Recovery in underwriting profit

Underwriting profit in Q1 2026 was KRW 62.5 billion, up sharply from KRW 2.7 billion a year earlier, helped by retroactive premium settlements on jeonse-related products. The original earned loss ratio fell from a high of 74.7% in 2024 to 53.0% in Q1 2026.

LS Securities said that the cumulative effect of tighter underwriting and premium rate adjustments should keep underwriting profit improving.

Shareholder return policy

The company targets annual shareholder returns of at least KRW 200 billion and a payout ratio of 50% or more. Its 2025 shareholder return ratio was 76%, and in 2026 it carried out its first quarterly dividend since listing.

The 2025 dividend payout ratio was 76%, down from 95% in 2024, while total dividends stayed around KRW 200 billion.

Capital strength and asset quality

The K-ICS ratio was 395.1% at the end of March 2026, which is among the highest in the industry. A credit rating report cites a risk-asset share of 25% and a weighted non-performing asset ratio of 0.15% as evidence of strong asset quality. The report attributes the capital cushion to conservative investment management.

09

Bear factors

Three straight quarters of lower operating profit

Operating profit peaked at KRW 182.9 billion in Q4 2025 and then fell to KRW 93.5 billion in Q1 2026, KRW 87 billion in Q2 and KRW 39.1 billion in Q3 2026. In Q3, one-off performance-guarantee claims were paid, which iM Securities said cut underwriting profit to KRW 9.8 billion. Profit attributable to controlling shareholders fell 37.0% year on year to KRW 39 billion in Q3 2026.

Economic sensitivity and loss ratio swings

Guarantee insurance claims rise when defaults increase during economic downturns. A credit rating report noted that weaker economic conditions and the end of policy support pushed up the loss ratio and lowered subrogation recovery rates, reducing profitability compared with the past. Profit volatility is therefore a structural weakness.

Possible stake sales by the largest shareholder

The Korea Deposit Insurance Corporation holds 79.56% of the shares, and the public fund redemption fund is set to be liquidated at the end of 2027.

That raises the possibility of stake sales reaching the market. iM Securities judged that, given the public-fund recovery objective and the current dividend level, sales large enough to shock the market in the short term are limited.

10

Risk factors

Loss ratio and economic cycle

Guarantee claims rise during economic downturns, and a renewed rise in the loss ratio could reverse the recovery in underwriting profit. The loss ratio peaked at 74.7% in 2024. Subrogation recoveries increase in upturns, but the timing depends on the economic cycle.

Volatility of retroactive premium settlements

Retroactive premium settlements on jeonse-related products vary widely by period and can cause one-off swings in underwriting profit.

Underwriting profit reached KRW 62.5 billion in Q1 2026 on settlements, but fell to KRW 9.8 billion in Q3 after one-off performance-guarantee claims. iM Securities views settlement premiums as the key driver of future improvement.

Capital ratio and interest-rate sensitivity

The K-ICS ratio was 395.1% at the end of March 2026, but it has drifted lower in a gradual trend. Based on year-end 2025 data, a 100bp rise in interest rates would lower the K-ICS ratio to 375.4%, and a 100bp fall would raise it to 404.6%. Interest-rate moves therefore affect both capital ratios and investment income.

Overall view

Seoul Guarantee Insurance Company is Korea's only full-line guarantee insurer, and its 2025 disclosed results show higher insurance revenue, operating profit and profit attributable to controlling shareholders.

Underwriting profit recovered in 2026 on retroactive jeonse-related premium settlements, but operating profit has fallen for three consecutive quarters since peaking in Q4 2025. One-off performance-guarantee claims weighed on Q3 2026.

The company has set targets of at least KRW 200 billion in annual shareholder returns and a payout ratio of 50% or more, and has introduced quarterly dividends. Financial soundness is supported by a K-ICS ratio of 395.1% and a low risk-asset share.

Earnings remain sensitive to the economy and to settlement premiums, and the timing of stake sales by the largest shareholder remains a variable. Broker forecasts point to a 2026 profit recovery, but these are estimates rather than disclosed results. The next checkpoints are Q4 2026 operating profit and the operating profit mix in the 2026 annual report.
