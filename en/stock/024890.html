Profit Turn Stalls as Quarterly Losses and Debt Weigh

Daewon Chemical returned to annual operating profit in 2025, but quarterly losses have continued through 2026, leaving debt repayment and the delisting-risk status as the core issues to monitor.

  1. 1

    After operating losses in 2022 through 2024, the company posted an operating profit of KRW 871 million in 2025, and profit attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 3.5 billion.

  2. 2

  3. 3

  4. 4

02

Business structure

Daewon Chemical, listed on KOSPI since 1997, is a specialty maker of synthetic leather. Built on its synthetic leather base, the company is moving into automotive interior materials and eco-friendly PU materials as higher-value products, while expanding its North American automotive supply chain business.

After taking over a plant in Jincheon, it is also pursuing LPMC composite materials and molded parts, which began generating new revenue in the first quarter of 2026.

In December 2023 the company decided to halt its wallpaper business, whose profitability had kept deteriorating, and in 2024 it liquidated its U.S. and Shanghai subsidiaries. Its production structure is being reorganized to link an Asian network in Vietnam and Indonesia with a production base in Mexico.

The core of the existing business is synthetic leather supplied to global sportswear brands, while the Mexico subsidiary KODEC S. de R.L. de C.V. handles the response to North American automotive supply chains.

KODEC's 2025 revenue was KRW 29.9 billion, up 133.5% from KRW 12.8 billion a year earlier, and its net loss narrowed from KRW 4.7 billion to KRW 1.4 billion. The company says the Mexico plant entered full operation from the second half of 2024.

According to press reports, KODEC's first-quarter 2026 revenue was about KRW 11.4 billion and its net loss narrowed to about KRW 180 million, approaching break-even.

In August 2026 the company sold the Jincheon plant, which it had received from an affiliate through a debt-for-asset transfer, for KRW 11.0 billion, recording a book loss of about KRW 3.2 billion.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual revenue fell from KRW 146.8 billion in 2022 to KRW 122.2 billion in 2023, a 16.8% decline, making 2023 the smallest year in the 2022–2025 period.

Revenue then rose 3.1% to KRW 125.9 billion in 2024 and 0.4% to KRW 126.4 billion in 2025, which is two consecutive years of growth after 2023, and 2022 was the largest year in the period. Press reports linked the revenue decline to the halt of the wallpaper business.

Operating results moved from an operating loss of KRW 7.4 billion in 2022 (operating margin -5.0%), KRW 6.44 billion in 2023 (operating margin -5.3%) and KRW 6.11 billion in 2024 (operating margin -4.9%) to an operating profit of KRW 871 million in 2025 (operating margin 0.7%), a swing from losses to profit.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders moved from a net loss of KRW 9.63 billion in 2022, to a loss widened to KRW 9.75 billion in 2023 and KRW 12.1 billion in 2024, before turning to a profit of KRW 3.5 billion in 2025. Net income including non-controlling interests was KRW 3.52 billion in 2025.

Press reports attributed the gap between 2025 net income and operating profit to KRW 7.73 billion of discontinued operations income recognized in the 2025 closing, a one-off item.

Operating cash flow was negative in 2022 through 2024, at KRW -6.79 billion, KRW -298 million and KRW -13.7 billion, before turning to KRW 3.54 billion in 2025.

The debt ratio rose from 109.6% in 2022 to 263.9% in 2024 and eased to 232.9% in 2025, when total liabilities were KRW 103.8 billion against total equity of KRW 44.6 billion.

On a quarterly basis, revenue peaked at KRW 35 billion in the first quarter of 2026 and then declined for two consecutive quarters to KRW 34.6 billion and KRW 33.9 billion.

Operating profit of KRW 493 million in the first quarter of 2026 followed an operating loss in the fourth quarter of 2025, but operating losses of KRW 370 million and KRW 765 million followed in the second and third quarters, two consecutive loss quarters.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders has been negative for three consecutive quarters since the first quarter of 2026, with a net loss of KRW 7.8 billion in the third quarter. Interest expense in the first half of 2026 exceeded KRW 2.4 billion, larger than first-half operating profit.

05

Industry analysis

Synthetic leather, Daewon Chemical's core product, is a material group sensitive to raw material prices and exchange rates. The company cited rising raw material prices and currency pressure as reasons for exiting its wallpaper business, which suggests cost swings directly affect margins.

On the demand side, growing requirements for carbon reduction and PVC substitutes have pushed the company toward eco-friendly PU and bio-based synthetic leather.

In automotive, local production and stable component and materials supply chains have become key competitive factors in the North American vehicle market, and the company is building its Mexico base as the center of its North American automotive materials business.

Large chemical peers in the same sector, including LG Chem, Lotte Chemical, Hanwha Solutions, Kolon Industries and SKC, have far larger petrochemical and materials portfolios, so Daewon Chemical's scale and product mix differ substantially.

It is more comparable to a small or mid-sized materials company focused on specific applications such as synthetic leather and automotive interiors. Its earnings swings can therefore differ in pattern from those of large petrochemical companies.

06

Outlook

The company views strengthening its North American supply chain response and raising the share of higher-value products such as automotive interiors and eco-friendly PU as the main drivers of margin improvement.

Order intake reached KRW 164.1 billion in 2025, up from KRW 132.0 billion in 2024, and first-quarter 2026 orders of KRW 47.8 billion rose 29.3% from a year earlier.

The company plans to develop its Mexico subsidiary into a core base for North American automotive supply chains and to expand into North American vehicle manufacturers and component markets.

It has said it expects commercialization and revenue from its LPMC composite materials and molded parts business once mass production starts in 2027–2028. On the financial side, the company expects the Osan land revaluation to strengthen its capital base and improve its debt ratio.

The board resolved on October 20, 2026 to cancel all treasury shares, with a scheduled cancellation date of November 9, 2026, which the filing said could change depending on coordination with authorities; the cancellation would reduce issued shares to 8,149,412.

The next checkpoints are whether the 2026 annual report shows the operating results after four quarters of losses, how the debt maturity profile changes, and whether revenue growth at the Mexico subsidiary translates into quarterly profit improvement.

08

Bull factors

Wider capital base from asset revaluation

The Osan plant land was revalued from KRW 36.2 billion to KRW 91.0 billion, generating a revaluation gain of KRW 54.7 billion. The company said it expects the step to strengthen its financial structure and improve its debt ratio. The debt ratio fell to 232.9% in 2025 from 263.9% in 2024. Whether the capital increase translates into lower leverage will show in future financial statements.

Mexico subsidiary growth and narrower losses

KODEC, the Mexico subsidiary, grew 2025 revenue to KRW 29.9 billion, up 133.5%, and narrowed its net loss from KRW 4.7 billion to KRW 1.4 billion. Press reports said the improvement continued in the first quarter of 2026, with revenue of about KRW 11.4 billion and a net loss of about KRW 180 million.

The company says fixed cost absorption is starting to show. Growing demand for local production in North American automotive supply chains is cited as room for growth.

Order growth and a timeline for new businesses

Order intake rose from KRW 132.0 billion in 2024 to KRW 164.1 billion in 2025, and first-quarter 2026 orders reached KRW 47.8 billion, up 29.3% from a year earlier. The LPMC composite and molded parts business began generating new revenue in the first quarter of 2026 after the Jincheon plant takeover.

The company expects commercialization and revenue growth around 2027–2028, when partner mass production is expected to begin. Whether orders convert into quarterly revenue is the point to watch.

Bear factors

Quarterly losses continue through 2026

Operating losses continued in the second and third quarters of 2026, and net losses attributable to controlling shareholders have persisted for three consecutive quarters since the first quarter of 2026. The third-quarter net loss widened to KRW 7.8 billion from the prior quarter.

The four quarters from the fourth quarter of 2025 through the third quarter of 2026 total a net loss of KRW 1.78 billion. Revenue has also declined for two consecutive quarters after peaking in the first quarter of 2026, adding pressure on profit recovery.

Short-term debt and financing cost burden

Of total borrowings of KRW 76.3 billion in the first half of 2026 on a consolidated basis, KRW 75.7 billion falls due within one year, against cash of about KRW 8.0 billion. Operating cash flow turned negative at KRW -5.1 billion in the first half.

First-half interest expense exceeded KRW 2.4 billion, well above first-half operating profit of about KRW 120 million. Press reports say the company is repaying high-interest private fund borrowings with asset sale proceeds to lower financing costs.

Listing requirements and weaker market confidence

Even after the share consolidation, market capitalization did not recover to the KRW 30 billion threshold, and the shares were designated as administrative issues in August 2026, with trading suspended from August 13 and resumed on August 14.

The company has filed a motion against the exchange for a provisional injunction on the designation. In June 2026 the shares were also designated as investment caution stocks due to concentrated selling from a small number of accounts.

Ownership changes after the consolidation are also part of the picture, with the largest shareholder group holding 46.9% of common shares.

10

Risk factors

Listing continuity risk

The designation was triggered by a market capitalization shortfall, and the company is pursuing legal remedies through a provisional injunction. The outcome could change its listing status and trading conditions. If market capitalization does not recover to the threshold, the process could lead toward delisting.

Treasury share cancellation and asset revaluation do not by themselves raise market capitalization, so they need separate monitoring.

Debt maturity and liquidity risk

Short-term maturities of KRW 75.7 billion compare with cash of about KRW 8.0 billion, a wide gap. Operating cash flow also turned negative in the first half of 2026. Press reports say financial institutions have become more reluctant to roll over maturities since the designation. Whether asset sales and repayments close this gap is the key point to monitor.

Overseas production, currency and cost risk

Growth in the North American automotive business depends heavily on utilization at the Mexico subsidiary and on customer demand. Raw material prices and exchange rates affected profitability to the point of prompting the exit from the wallpaper business.

The 2025 operating margin of 0.7% leaves little room for cost swings. Restructuring and liquidation of overseas units may bring recurring one-time costs.

12

Overall view

Daewon Chemical returned to annual profitability in 2025, with operating profit of KRW 871 million and net income attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 3.5 billion.

Press reports, however, say the profit included one-off items such as discontinued operations income, and quarterly losses have continued for three consecutive quarters in 2026.

On the financial side, the Osan land revaluation broadened the capital base, but short-term debt well exceeds cash, making liquidity management the central task. The Mexico subsidiary is showing improvement in both revenue and losses and is being developed as a pillar of the North American automotive business.

Order growth and the 2027–2028 mass production plans for LPMC are cited as growth drivers but are not yet fully visible in quarterly results. The administrative-issue designation and the injunction process remain variables that will determine the listing outcome.

The next points to track are the 2026 annual report, the 2027 quarterly and half-year reports, and the progress of the administrative-issue procedure.
