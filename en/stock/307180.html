Report overview

Back in Profit, Still Leveraged, Testing Physical AI

IL posted operating profit in the second and third quarters of 2026 on its existing automotive-electronics and ITS businesses, but with a debt ratio of 567.1% it still has to show how fast its humanoid and physical AI ventures can turn into revenue.

  1. 1

    Annual revenue rose from KRW 83.5 billion in 2024 to KRW 102.9 billion in 2025, two straight years of increase from 2024 to 2025. Operating profit of KRW 5.04 billion in 2024 turned to an operating loss of KRW 8.5 billion in 2025.

  2. 2

    Third-quarter 2026 revenue was KRW 33.4 billion (up 14.4% year on year) and operating profit was KRW 2.14 billion (up 83.0% year on year). Operating profit was nonetheless 22.6% lower than in the prior quarter.

  3. 3

    At the end of 2025 the debt ratio was 567.1%, total equity KRW 23.2 billion and total liabilities KRW 131.5 billion. Operating cash flow was also negative in each of 2023-2025, which makes the balance sheet the key variable.

  4. 4

    In May 2026 IL signed a first supply contract for its ILBOT humanoid worth about KRW 3 billion. In September 2026 it booked its first physical AI data contract at KRW 220 million, which is still small in scale.

  5. 5

    On May 29, 2026 the board approved a KRW 17.2 billion convertible bond issue. Because it carries a conversion price reset clause, both dilution and repayment burden need to be watched.

02

Business structure

IL was founded in 2008 to develop high-efficiency LED lighting and listed on KOSDAQ in 2019 through a SPAC merger. Its core technology is the silicon lens, which it developed as the world's first. In 2024 it acquired IL Mobility and invested in IL Celion, vertically integrating the whole lamp assembly process.

The chain runs from automotive lamp PCBs through silicon lenses to plastic cover injection molding, but initial investment and integration costs led to an operating loss.

Through its merger with Itronics, IL gained capabilities in ITS, image recognition, vehicle communication and traffic infrastructure, and has built a base in the domestic hi-pass and smart traffic system market. A segment revenue breakdown is not available in the filings used here.

The Bell reported, however, that in the first nine months of 2025 its legacy lighting lines, such as security and street lights, generated KRW 67.6 billion, or 81% of cumulative revenue of KRW 83.5 billion. On customers, the company said in 2025 that IL Celion had won new customers including Porsche and Tesla.

The new businesses are the ILBOT humanoid, developed on a Korea exclusive supply partnership and technical cooperation with China's AgiBot, and an operating data platform.

The same machinery and equipment sector includes robot-related listed companies such as Rainbow Robotics, Doosan Robotics and Robotis, but IL's revenue is centered on automotive lamps and ITS, so its business structure differs.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual revenue fell from KRW 49.2 billion in 2022 to KRW 39.1 billion in 2023 (down 20.5%), then rose to KRW 83.5 billion in 2024 (up 113.7%) and KRW 102.9 billion in 2025 (up 23.2%), two straight years of increase. 2025 was the largest year of 2022-2025, and the company itself described 2025 consolidated revenue as its highest since its founding.

The operating loss widened from KRW 744 million in 2022 to KRW 16.7 billion in 2023, turned to an operating profit of KRW 5.04 billion in 2024, then swung back to an operating loss of KRW 8.5 billion in 2025. The operating margin fell from 6.0% in 2024 to -8.3% in 2025.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders went from KRW 432 million in 2024 to a net loss of KRW 11.1 billion in 2025, while the net loss including non-controlling interests, a separate figure, was KRW 10.8 billion.

In the third quarter of 2025 revenue was KRW 29.2 billion with operating profit of KRW 1.17 billion, but the fourth quarter brought revenue of KRW 19.3 billion (down 33.8% quarter on quarter) and an operating loss of KRW 9.31 billion.

The company said it changed part of its revenue recognition standard during the external audit, and industry observers attribute the loss to the new auditor applying a conservative asset impairment review.

In 2026, the first quarter showed revenue of KRW 25.2 billion, operating profit of KRW 154 million and a controlling-shareholder net loss of KRW 592 million; the second quarter showed revenue of KRW 24.9 billion and operating profit of KRW 2.76 billion; the third quarter showed revenue of KRW 33.4 billion (up 34.2% quarter on quarter) and operating profit of KRW 2.14 billion (down 22.6% quarter on quarter).

Revenue rose in the third quarter but profit fell from the second quarter, so the quarterly operating margin declined.

The debt ratio at the end of 2025 was 567.1%, operating cash flow in 2025 was still negative at KRW 1.12 billion, and the controlling-shareholder net loss over the last four quarters totals KRW 10.9 billion.

05

Industry analysis

IL's end markets are construction lighting, automotive lamps and electronics, public traffic infrastructure (ITS) and robot data, and each follows its own cycle.

Construction lighting is sensitive to construction activity: in the first half of 2025, weak construction conditions cut construction lighting revenue and the company posted a half-year operating loss.

Automotive lamp demand moves with new-vehicle adoption and carmaker production volumes, and subsidiaries such as IL Celion serve it. ITS and hi-pass depend on public tenders such as those of the Korea Expressway Corporation, so quarterly revenue can swing with order timing.

In humanoids, press reports describe AgiBot as the world's top humanoid shipper, and IL holds a Korea supply partnership with it.

Listed robot peers in the same sector include Rainbow Robotics, Doosan Robotics, Robotis and Yuilrobotics, and IL positions itself as different because it operates robots on its own production lines to build data.

The humanoid market is still at an early stage, so new-business revenue is a small share of IL's consolidated sales today.

06

Outlook

The company said it expects meaningful revenue from its physical AI business starting in the third quarter of 2026. In existing businesses, it won a KRW 10.7 billion multi-lane hi-pass project in a consortium with Lotte Innovate, due for completion by year-end.

It also won a hi-pass replacement project of about KRW 13.8 billion from the Korea Expressway Corporation, with installation due within the year. In construction lighting it signed a roughly KRW 3.6 billion 2026 lighting unit-price contract with POSCO E&C, running through May 2027.

In new businesses there is a roughly KRW 3 billion contract to supply ILBOT to a Korean company identified as H, and in September 2026 it signed a KRW 220 million humanoid and physical AI data supply contract with an auto parts maker.

More than half of that contract was priced as training data rather than robot hardware, which is the revenue model the company is promoting, and on October 26, 2026 it began operating its data platform PHYDATUM.

In a June 23, 2026 report, the independent research house GL Research estimated 2026 humanoid segment revenue of KRW 10.9 billion and operating profit of KRW 2.18 billion and set a target price of KRW 12,000.

In a September 2026 interview, CEO Song Seong-geun said there are currently no plans for additional convertible bonds or a rights offering.

08

Bull factors

Profit recovery in existing businesses

Operating profit grew from KRW 154 million in the first quarter of 2026 to KRW 2.76 billion in the second and KRW 2.14 billion in the third. Third-quarter operating profit rose 83.0% year on year. The company said second-quarter operating profit was the highest for any second quarter. It attributes this to profitability in automotive electronics and ITS and to efficiency measures.

Order visibility from public and construction projects

Hi-pass projects worth KRW 10.7 billion (May 2026) and about KRW 13.8 billion (July 2026) and a KRW 3.6 billion lighting contract with POSCO E&C (June 2026) have been confirmed. Both hi-pass projects are scheduled for completion within the year, so they could feed fourth-quarter 2026 revenue.

The lighting unit-price contract runs to May 2027. The company says the order base in legacy businesses funds its investment in new ventures.

A data-centered revenue model for new ventures

IL aims to sell humanoids not just as hardware but linked to operating data and subscription-type services. In its September 2026 contract, more than half of the amount was priced as data.

The company has secured more than 430,000 humanoid operating data records and aims to build a platform of more than 5 million this year. The AgiBot partnership underpins the product supply side.

09

Bear factors

High leverage and negative cash flow

Total liabilities at the end of 2025 were KRW 131.5 billion, more than five times total equity of KRW 23.2 billion, for a debt ratio of 567.1%. Operating cash flow stayed negative: -KRW 5.21 billion in 2023, -KRW 3.98 billion in 2024 and -KRW 1.12 billion in 2025.

The Bell noted that most of the debt on a standalone basis is current liabilities due within a year. Even if earnings stay positive, debt repayment and interest costs remain.

Small scale and uncertainty of new-business revenue

The new-business contracts identified are a first supply deal of about KRW 3 billion and a KRW 220 million data contract. That is small against 2025 revenue of KRW 102.9 billion.

GL Research's 2026 humanoid revenue estimate of KRW 10.9 billion is only an estimate, and the actual timing of recognition has not been confirmed. Quarterly results may remain driven more by existing-business orders than by the new ventures for some time.

Dilution pressure from the convertible bond

The convertible bond approved in May 2026 is KRW 17.2 billion in size, with a conversion price of KRW 8,467, resetting of up to 80% allowed, and adjustments every seven months. The number of common shares to be issued on conversion is 2,031,416.

If the share price stays below the conversion price, a reset could increase the number of shares issued on conversion. With a 0% coupon there is no interest burden, but the five-year redemption obligation remains.

10

Risk factors

Listing status and accounting

In March 2026 the company disclosed that, at the internal closing stage, pre-tax loss from continuing operations had exceeded 50% of equity in two of the last three fiscal years, creating grounds for designation as an administrative issue.

The company described this as an advance notice under accounting rules, not a situation in which a material problem has arisen in operations. The final designation outcome after the audit and any follow-up procedure could not be confirmed in this research.

Because large losses were booked during the audit process in the fourth quarter of 2025, the annual audit result needs continued monitoring.

Order and revenue volatility

ITS and hi-pass depend on public tender timing, and construction lighting on the construction cycle. Quarterly revenue ranged widely, from KRW 19.3 billion in the fourth quarter of 2025 to KRW 33.4 billion in the third quarter of 2026.

If contract signing and revenue recognition do not line up, quarterly results can swing. In the third quarter, revenue grew but operating profit fell from the prior quarter.

Partner and new-business dependence

ILBOT was developed on a partnership and technical cooperation with China's AgiBot. A change in supply or cooperation terms could affect the new-business plan. Press reports say AgiBot began its Hong Kong IPO process in July 2026 and is preparing to list within the year. The listing timetable and partnership terms are external variables that IL cannot control.

12

Overall view

IL's revenue rose for two straight years from 2024 to 2025, reaching KRW 102.9 billion in 2025, but the company swung to an operating loss of KRW 8.5 billion that year.

In 2026 earnings are recovering, with operating profit of KRW 154 million in the first quarter, KRW 2.76 billion in the second and KRW 2.14 billion in the third, and third-quarter revenue grew to KRW 33.4 billion.

Hi-pass and lighting orders support the revenue base of existing businesses, while humanoid and data contracts show the new ventures have entered an early revenue stage.

On the other side, the 567.1% debt ratio, negative operating cash flow, potential dilution from the KRW 17.2 billion convertible bond and the history of administrative-issue disclosures are financial burdens.

The new-business contracts are still small, so existing businesses drive consolidated results, and in the third quarter operating profit fell from the prior quarter even as revenue grew.

The points to check next are fourth-quarter 2026 hi-pass revenue recognition, whether more new-business contracts follow, and the 2026 annual audit result and listing requirements. This report is for information only, and any investment judgment is left to the reader.
