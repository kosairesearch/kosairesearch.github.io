Report overview

Woori Technology: Nuclear Deliveries Must Prove a Turnaround

Woori Technology, Incorporation, effectively the sole domestic supplier of nuclear plant instrumentation and control systems (MMIS), grew revenue to KRW 87.1 billion in 2025 but posted an operating loss of KRW 5.49 billion due to start-up costs in new businesses. The central test is whether Shin-Hanul Unit 3 main-equipment deliveries, which began in 2026, turn into a recovery in operating profit.

02

Business structure

Woori Technology, Incorporation, founded in 1993, is an instrumentation and control specialist that in 2010 became the world's fourth developer of its own distributed control system (DCS) for nuclear MMIS.

It has supplied the system to major domestic plants, including Shin-Hanul Units 1 and 2, Saeul Units 3 and 4, and Shin-Hanul Units 3 and 4.

Beyond its core nuclear-focused control systems, the company spans defense, infrastructure such as railway platform screen doors (PSD), offshore wind, resource recycling and smart farming, and it has 11 subsidiaries.

According to a Korea IR Service report from April 2024, the 2023 revenue mix was systems (nuclear) 38%, defense 33%, infrastructure (rail) 23% and other 6%. In the 2026 half-year report, the defense segment's revenue of KRW 19.66 billion exceeded the control systems segment's KRW 14.65 billion.

The resource recycling segment, by contrast, booked first-half revenue of KRW 1.12 billion with an operating loss of KRW 4.56 billion, and the offshore wind segment recorded no revenue. Key customers are Korea Hydro & Nuclear Power (KHNP) and main-equipment maker Doosan Enerbility.

In August 2025 the company signed an exclusive MMIS DCS supply MOU with Doosan Enerbility and also holds a long-term supply agreement with it. The company has effectively no domestic competitor in non-safety-class MMIS, but in export projects it must compete with large global instrumentation and control vendors.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual revenue rose for three consecutive years, from KRW 49.5 billion in 2022 to KRW 63.2 billion in 2023, KRW 71.3 billion in 2024 and KRW 87.1 billion in 2025. 2025 was the largest revenue year of the 2022-2025 period, and the Korea Credit News described it as record revenue.

Profits moved the other way over the same span. Operating profit fell 91.7% from KRW 6.68 billion in 2023 to KRW 554 million in 2024, then swung to an operating loss of KRW 5.49 billion in 2025. In its disclosure, the company attributed the 2025 loss mainly to higher start-up costs in subsidiaries' new businesses.

Controlling-interest net profit turned positive at KRW 792 million in 2025, but net loss including non-controlling interests was KRW 4.1 billion. This indicates that losses are concentrated in subsidiaries where outside shareholders hold stakes.

On a quarterly basis, revenue fell 31.7% from KRW 24.8 billion in Q4 2025 to KRW 17 billion in Q1 2026, and the operating loss widened to KRW 6.74 billion in that quarter. Q2 2026 revenue of KRW 19.8 billion was up 16.7% from the previous quarter but down 12.4% year on year.

The Q2 operating loss of KRW 1.13 billion was smaller than in Q1 but larger than a year earlier. According to the half-year report, first-half gross profit fell from KRW 8.99 billion to KRW 6.81 billion, while SG&A expenses rose from KRW 12.65 billion to KRW 14.68 billion.

The Q1 2026 controlling-interest net profit of KRW 28.9 billion came from financial income rather than operations; derivative valuation gains alone totaled KRW 29.36 billion in the first half.

05

Industry analysis

The domestic nuclear market is entering a phase of renewed new-build activity. On June 17, 2026, KHNP selected Yeongdeok in North Gyeongsang Province as the candidate site for two new large reactors and Gijang in Busan for the i-SMR. It was the first new nuclear site selection since 2011.

The 11th Basic Plan for Electricity Supply and Demand targets completion of two 1.4GW large units in 2037 and 2038 and one SMR in 2035. The 12th Basic Plan's steering committee provisionally projects peak demand of 138.2GW in 2040, 37% above the 100.9GW recorded in 2025.

In July 2026, the Minister of Climate, Energy and Environment said whether to add new reactors and SMRs would be decided through public deliberation and reflected in the 12th Basic Plan. Because reactors take many years to build, however, there is a multi-year lag between policy decisions and MMIS revenue.

In offshore wind, the 390MW Sinan Ui project, with a total cost of KRW 3.4 trillion and EPC by Hyundai Engineering & Construction and Hanwha Ocean, is advancing as the first large-scale complex. Woori Technology entered this market by securing the country's only offshore wind foundation installation vessel.

06

Outlook

In a March 2026 interview with Dealsite, CFO Jeon Dae-young projected 2026 consolidated revenue of KRW 160 billion, operating profit of KRW 30 billion and net profit of KRW 20 billion. The nuclear segment's targets are revenue of KRW 75 billion and operating profit of KRW 32 billion.

According to the company, its order backlog stood at KRW 100.4 billion at the end of Q1 2026, a large share of it tied to the MMIS DCS it supplies exclusively in Korea.

The company says main-equipment deliveries of the MMIS DCS for Shin-Hanul Unit 3 began in 2026, with related revenue to be recognized in stages from that year. In May 2026 it signed a KRW 9.24 billion contract for data acquisition systems at Hanul Units 1 and 2.

In August 2026 it followed with a KRW 8.5 billion contract related to Kori Units 3 and 4, and in September it signed a virtualized DCS (digital twin) development contract with Doosan Enerbility for Shin-Hanul Units 3 and 4.

Proceeds from the KRW 40 billion convertible bond are slated for deployment of KRW 21 billion in 2026, KRW 15 billion in 2027 and KRW 4 billion from 2028 onward.

In offshore wind, the company plans to deploy its foundation installation vessel Nuribaram No. 1 first on the Sinan Ui project and later on its own 88MW Aphae project.

08

Bull factors

Exclusive MMIS and the start of main-equipment revenue

Woori Technology effectively supplies domestic nuclear MMIS DCS on its own, and plants maintain an adopted control system for decades. The order backlog of KRW 100.4 billion at the end of Q1 2026 exceeds full-year 2025 revenue.

Shin-Hanul Unit 3 main-equipment deliveries, which began in 2026, are high-margin new-build volume, leaving substantial room for operating margin improvement once revenue recognition ramps up.

Expanding new-build and SMR policy

KHNP's June 2026 selection of Yeongdeok and Gijang as new-build and i-SMR candidate sites made the 11th Basic Plan's volumes more concrete. The government is reviewing additional reactors for the 12th Basic Plan, and the Korean Nuclear Society has proposed two to four more large units and two SMRs.

The company participates in a national project to develop an MMIS safety-system platform for the i-SMR, positioning it as a candidate supplier for next-generation reactor designs.

Upgrade demand from operating plants

In 2026, upgrade contracts for operating plants have continued, including KRW 9.24 billion for Hanul Units 1 and 2 and KRW 8.5 billion for Kori Units 3 and 4. The vendor-managed inventory (VMI) agreement signed with KHNP in August 2026 provides a framework for long-term parts supply.

Replacement demand from aging plants arises independently of new-build schedules and can help fill revenue gaps between large projects.

09

Bear factors

New-business losses eroding the core

In the first half of 2026, the resource recycling segment's operating loss of KRW 4.56 billion was nearly unchanged from a year earlier, and the offshore wind segment posted losses with no revenue.

The company had signaled that new businesses would reach breakeven in 2026, but half-year results showed no such improvement. Even if nuclear generates profit, continued subsidiary losses would delay a recovery in consolidated operating profit.

Thin cash and repeated financing

Consolidated cash and cash equivalents fell to KRW 4.47 billion at the end of June 2026, less than half the level at the start of the year. Operating cash flow was -KRW 18.8 billion in 2025 and -KRW 8.52 billion in the first half of 2026.

Starting in July 2025, the company issued convertible bonds three times over roughly a year, raising KRW 72.8 billion in total. The longer core cash generation is delayed, the heavier the reliance on external funding becomes.

Gap between guidance and results

First-half revenue was about 23% of the KRW 160 billion annual target, and the company posted an operating loss over the same period. First-half revenue in the core control systems segment fell 17.2% year on year, and export sales dropped from KRW 7.68 billion to KRW 3.12 billion.

Catching up to the target would require a heavy concentration of main-equipment revenue recognition in the second half.

10

Risk factors

Policy and schedule

Whether to add new reactors and SMRs will be decided in the 12th Basic Plan after public deliberation, and the scale could fall short of expectations. New reactors take a long time to build, creating a wide lag before policy decisions translate into MMIS revenue. Any delay in Shin-Hanul Units 3 and 4 construction would push back main-equipment revenue recognition as well.

Balance sheet and dilution

Outstanding convertible bonds totaled KRW 72.15 billion as of September 2026, with 13,168,491 convertible shares equal to 7.62% of shares outstanding. The KRW 40 billion bond becomes convertible from August 13, 2027, and investors gain a put option from August 13, 2028.

Depending on the share price, this could become potential selling supply or a repayment burden, and the largest shareholder group's stake is relatively low at 8.54%.

Accounting volatility

Derivatives tied to the conversion feature of the convertible bonds generate valuation gains and losses with the share price, moving quarterly net results sharply regardless of operations.

The gap between the Q1 2026 controlling-interest net profit of KRW 28.9 billion and the Q2 controlling-interest net loss of KRW 18.3 billion illustrates this. This is why operating results and operating cash flow need to be tracked separately from net income.

12

Overall view

Woori Technology, Incorporation is the exclusive domestic supplier in nuclear MMIS DCS, a field with high entry barriers, and its revenue grew for three consecutive years from 2023 to 2025.

Over the same period, however, its operating margin fell from 9.7% to -6.3%, with losses stemming from subsidiaries' new businesses rather than nuclear.

For 2026, Shin-Hanul Unit 3 main-equipment deliveries, a KRW 100.4 billion order backlog and upgrade contracts for operating plants are cited as grounds for a revenue recovery. On the other side are thin cash reserves, repeated convertible bond financing and first-half results well short of company targets.

Because quarterly net income is driven by derivative valuation swings, performance is more accurately judged by operating results and operating cash flow.

The next checkpoints are the scale of main-equipment revenue recognized in the Q3 2026 report and the nuclear capacity included in the 12th Basic Plan at the end of 2026. This report is for informational purposes only and contains no buy or sell recommendation.
