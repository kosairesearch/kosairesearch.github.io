02

Business structure

Woori Technology, Incorporation designs, builds and installs instrumentation and control systems (MMIS) for nuclear power plants, and it is a KOSDAQ-listed specialist. The company has been at the forefront of localizing nuclear I&C technology that foreign suppliers once monopolized.

It says it holds a monopoly position in domestic MMIS DCS (distributed control system) supply, and a 2023 supply contract for Shin Hanul Units 3 and 4 DCS controllers alone was worth about KRW 54 billion. Multi-purpose research reactor supply contracts followed in 2024 and early 2025.

Non-nuclear businesses include rail control systems (including PSD supply for the Tel-Aviv Green Line in Israel), defense-related control systems, and defense subsidiary Woori DS.

Eco-friendly projects such as offshore wind, low-temperature pyrolysis oil equipment and incinerator ash recycling are also under way, and their early investment costs are cited as a factor weighing on 2025 profitability.

On the nuclear side, the company says it completed development of a DCS for SMR test use, laying groundwork for entry into the SMR market. Its customers are mainly domestic nuclear plant operators, while overseas nuclear projects involve complex contract structures and local licensing and political risks.

The revenue mix by segment can be checked in the segment information of the periodic report. Competition is characterized by few domestic MMIS suppliers, while overseas local conditions and contract structures remain variables.

04

Earnings analysis

On a DART-filed basis, annual revenue rose from KRW 49.5 billion in 2022 to KRW 63.2 billion in 2023, KRW 71.3 billion in 2024 and KRW 87.1 billion in 2025, growing for three consecutive years from 2023 and reaching its largest level of the 2022 to 2025 span in 2025.

Operating profit went from KRW 4.8 billion in 2022 to KRW 6.68 billion in 2023 (up 39.1% YoY) and then fell to KRW 554 million in 2024 (down 91.7%), before an operating loss of KRW 5.49 billion in 2025 turned the year into a loss.

The operating margin slid from 9.7% in 2022 and 10.6% in 2023 to 0.8% in 2024 and -6.3% in 2025, so sales growth did not carry through to profit. An earnings summary from a securities data provider attributes the pressure to initial investment costs in new green energy projects and rising costs in existing businesses.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders went from KRW 5.03 billion in 2023 to a net loss of KRW 3.57 billion in 2024, then returned to profit at KRW 792 million in 2025, while net income including non-controlling interests was a net loss of KRW 4.4 billion in 2024 and KRW 4.1 billion in 2025.

At year-end 2025, total liabilities stood at KRW 214.1 billion and total equity at KRW 124.4 billion, of which controlling shareholders' equity was KRW 125.8 billion, and the debt-to-equity ratio climbed from 69.7% in 2022 to 172.1% in 2025.

Operating cash flow was KRW 1.57 billion in 2022, KRW 3.71 billion in 2023 and KRW 3.94 billion in 2024, then turned to -KRW 18.8 billion in 2025.

Quarterly revenue ran at KRW 21.2 billion in Q3 2025, KRW 24.8 billion in Q4 2025, KRW 17 billion in Q1 2026, KRW 19.8 billion in Q2 2026 and KRW 16.3 billion in Q3 2026, with Q3 2026 down 23.1% from a year earlier.

Operating losses were recorded in each of the last five quarters, ranging from KRW 650 million in Q3 2025 to KRW 6.74 billion in Q1 2026 and KRW 2.72 billion in Q3 2026.

Controlling shareholders recorded a net profit of KRW 28.9 billion in Q1 2026 but net losses of KRW 18.3 billion in Q2 and KRW 22.7 billion in Q3 2026, and the last four quarters from Q4 2025 to Q3 2026 total a net loss of KRW 27.5 billion attributable to controlling shareholders.

The Q1 2026 profit runs opposite to the operating loss, so one-off or non-operating items are a point to check in that quarter's periodic report.

05

Industry analysis

The nuclear instrumentation and control market that Woori Technology, Incorporation serves combines new reactor construction orders with demand for upgrades at existing plants.

When an existing reactor receives approval for continued operation, aging equipment must be replaced, and converting analog control systems to digital creates steady demand. The company expects additional orders at plants whose equipment upgrade timing has arrived, such as Units 3 and 4 at Gori.

Overseas exports of Korean-standard reactors could open chances for domestic I&C supply chains to expand abroad, but overseas projects involve complex contract structures and local licensing and political risks that make them more uncertain than domestic orders.

Nuclear projects can see costs rise from design changes to meet safety standards, licensing delays and higher material prices, and margins can deteriorate even as revenue grows, which is not rare among small engineering companies.

The company emphasizes its record as the domestic sole supplier of MMIS DCS and says that record underpins expected orders for new plants and upgrades.

Policy expectations for nuclear power have been a major driver of the share price, but market reports in October 2026 pointed out the need to separate policy hopes from signed contracts. Non-nuclear fields such as defense and rail are being expanded to reduce dependence on the nuclear policy cycle.

Outlook

In a May 21, 2026 announcement, the company said its order backlog at end-Q1 2026 reached KRW 100.4 billion, up about 12% from a year earlier. It stated that deliveries of MMIS DCS main units worth more than KRW 50 billion would start in 2026, with nuclear segment growth expected as a result.

DCS main-unit deliveries for Shin Hanul Units 3 and 4 are scheduled in two batches through 2027. Main-unit deliveries under the multi-purpose research reactor contracts signed in 2024 and early 2025 were announced for the second half of 2026.

In May 2026, a KRW 9.2 billion supply contract for PMS at Hanul Units 1 and 2 was disclosed. The KRW 40 billion convertible bond decided in August 2026 is mostly earmarked for nuclear projects, and conversion requests can be made from August 13, 2027 through July 13, 2031.

If fully converted, about 3.73 million new shares would be issued, equal to 2.11% of total shares outstanding. In SMR, the company has completed development of a DCS for SMR test use and plans to spend part of the funds on i-SMR R&D.

Revenue from orders is recognized depending on inspection and progress, so how much quarterly revenue and operating profit reflect deliveries once they ramp up is the next point to check.

Upgrade Demand at Aging Plants

Plants that receive continued-operation approval need to convert aging control systems to digital. The company expects more orders at plants such as Gori Units 3 and 4, where upgrade timing has arrived. A KRW 9.2 billion PMS contract for Hanul Units 1 and 2 in May 2026 fits this trend.

Upgrade demand at existing plants can recur as long as those reactors keep running, which broadens the order base.

Room to Expand into SMR, Overseas and Non-Nuclear

Growth in Korean-standard reactor exports could open overseas chances for domestic I&C supply.
