02

Business structure

Youngone (111770) is a manufacturer that supplies functional and outdoor apparel to overseas brands on an OEM (original equipment manufacturing) basis, and it serves as the core affiliate of its group.

Key customers are The North Face, Patagonia, Engelbert Strauss, lululemon and Arc'teryx, and DB Financial Investment described Arc'teryx, with a mid-teens share of 2025 sales, as one of a stable top five.

In a February 2026 report, DB Financial Investment said these customers were still expanding orders by double digits. The business is split between the OEM segment and the bike subsidiary SCOTT.

OEM segment sales in the third quarter of 2026 were KRW 886.9 billion, up 4% in US dollar terms and 7% in won terms (KB Securities tally). SCOTT sales in the same quarter were KRW 290.8 billion, down 11.8% year on year.

Production bases were estimated by Kiwoom Securities in July 2025 at roughly 70% Bangladesh, 20% Vietnam and 10% other countries. KB Securities noted that the US share of sales is lower than peers and that margin terms in contracts are a key variable.

In global competition, OEM peers such as Taiwan's Eclat and Makalot are the reference group, and domestic listed apparel OEM Hansae is a comparison point. The company points to functional apparel production capability and customer diversification as its base. In April 2026 NH Investment & Securities said new outdoor and sports brand customers support OEM growth.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual sales moved from KRW 3.91 trillion in 2022 to KRW 3.6 trillion in 2023 (down 7.8% year on year) and KRW 3.52 trillion in 2024 (down 2.4%), before rising to KRW 4.06 trillion in 2025 (up 15.5%). Among 2022 to 2025, 2025 was the largest year for sales and 2024 the smallest.

Operating profit fell from KRW 823 billion in 2022 to KRW 637.1 billion in 2023 (down 22.6%) and KRW 315.6 billion in 2024 (down 50.5%), then rose to KRW 514.4 billion in 2025 (up 63.0%). The operating margin went from 21.0% in 2022 to 17.7% in 2023, 9.0% in 2024 and 12.7% in 2025.

Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders was largest in 2022 at KRW 674.5 billion and smallest in 2024 at KRW 427.1 billion, and reached KRW 492.3 billion in 2025 (up 15.3%).

Net income including non-controlling interests was KRW 426.3 billion in 2025, a different figure from controlling-shareholder profit, and the controlling share exceeded total net income that year.

The debt-to-equity ratio declined from 44.9% in 2022 to 39.3% in 2025, with total liabilities of KRW 1.61 trillion at the end of 2025. Operating cash flow fell from KRW 624 billion in 2024 to KRW 374.9 billion in 2025.

Quarterly sales went from KRW 1.2 trillion in the third quarter of 2025 to KRW 1.01 trillion in the fourth quarter (down 16.2% quarter on quarter) and KRW 895.8 billion in the first quarter of 2026 (down 11.2%), then recovered to KRW 1.23 trillion in the second quarter (up 37.5%) and KRW 1.22 trillion in the third quarter, which was up only 1.0% year on year.

Quarterly operating profit bottomed at KRW 105.2 billion in the fourth quarter of 2025 and then rose for three straight quarters to KRW 120.4 billion, KRW 175.6 billion and KRW 199 billion.

Part of the first-quarter improvement came from a smaller SCOTT loss: per KB Securities, SCOTT posted an operating loss of KRW 2.3 billion in the first quarter of 2026 versus KRW 28.1 billion a year earlier, and the OEM operating margin was estimated at 20.8%, up 1.1 percentage points.

Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders, however, fell to KRW 66.9 billion in the third quarter of 2026, down 57.3% from the prior quarter and 59.5% year on year, moving against operating profit.

The four quarters from the fourth quarter of 2025 through the third quarter of 2026 total KRW 492.4 billion in controlling-shareholder net profit.

Industry analysis

Global apparel OEM makers as a group shrank after COVID-19, but the company said it kept long-term partnerships with global brands through geographically spread production and reliable delivery, according to a July 2026 press report.

In April 2026 NH Investment & Securities said customer diversification and functional apparel production capability were being maintained even as the global fashion industry slowed. Another analysis said higher inventory would likely lead to higher shipments in the first half.

On demand, Hanwha Investment & Securities noted in a May 2025 report that China's outdoor market grew 23% in 2024, and it cited expanding China businesses at key customers including The North Face, Arc'teryx and lululemon.

On tariffs, Kiwoom Securities in July 2025 pointed to a flagged additional US tariff rate of 37% on Bangladesh and said share price volatility could rise depending on whether tariff negotiations progressed.

On competition, NH Investment & Securities in a March 2025 report cited 27% sales growth for global peers Eclat and Makalot in January 2025 as a benchmark for the company. In a May 2026 report, Daishin Securities said concern about cost increases from the war was already largely reflected in the share price. OEM demand depends on customer inventory and order flow, so quarterly sales swings tend to be large.

06

Outlook

In a November 17, 2026 report, KB Securities said fourth-quarter sales expectations needed to be lowered. Its reasons were that OEM inventory at the end of the third quarter rose 0.4% and that it expects the won-dollar exchange rate to fall 7.3% in the fourth quarter.

Daishin Securities lowered its 2026 to 2027 earnings estimates to reflect exchange rate moves, but judged that the estimate adjustment from the exchange rate decline had run its course. In a February 2026 report, DB Financial Investment expected double-digit order growth from key customers to continue.

In a May 2026 report, KB Securities said profit levels were rising as new customers were added. In the same report, it expected SCOTT's annual losses to keep narrowing as discounting shrinks. Daishin Securities expected the dividend payout ratio for 2026 to 2027 to rise from 25% to 30%.

The company has said it will keep the separate-statement dividend payout near 50% of net income. Three points are worth tracking: whether the exchange rate matches the estimate assumptions, whether SCOTT's losses keep narrowing, and whether OEM inventory growth turns into higher shipments. The fourth-quarter 2026 results and the 2026 annual report will be the next regular disclosures to check.

Bull factors

Key customer orders expanding

In a February 2026 report, DB Financial Investment said The North Face, Patagonia, Engelbert Strauss, lululemon and Arc'teryx were still expanding orders by double digits. It also cited operating leverage from higher production share. KB Securities named a surge in key customer orders as a reason first-quarter 2026 results beat expectations.

SCOTT losses narrowing

SCOTT's operating loss narrowed to KRW 2.3 billion in the first quarter of 2026 from KRW 28.1 billion in the first quarter of 2025. SCOTT's third-quarter 2026 operating loss was KRW 1.6 billion, KRW 18.6 billion smaller than a year earlier (KB Securities tally). KB Securities pointed to reduced discounting and inventory normalization as profitability drivers.

Lower US sales exposure than peers

KB Securities noted that the company's US sales share is lower than peers and that contract margin terms are a key variable. OEM segment sales grew 4% in US dollar terms in the third quarter of 2026 (KB Securities tally). Relatively smaller exposure to any single tariff-hit market is cited as a supporting factor.

Bear factors

Sales growth stalled

Third-quarter 2026 sales of KRW 1.22 trillion grew only 1.0% year on year and fell short of market expectations (KB Securities). KB Securities cited a high base from the 2025 surge at Arc'teryx and slowing September sales growth. Sales did not hold the second-quarter level of KRW 1.23 trillion in the third quarter.

Exchange rate and fourth-quarter outlook

KB Securities lowered its fourth-quarter earnings expectations, reflecting an expected 7.3% fall in the won-dollar rate and third-quarter OEM inventory levels. Daishin Securities also cut 2026 to 2027 estimates for exchange rate moves.

KB Securities noted that OEM operating margin fell 1.8 percentage points year on year amid concern over a stronger won.

Weak net profit and industry slowdown

Controlling-shareholder net profit fell to KRW 66.9 billion in the third quarter of 2026, down 57.3% quarter on quarter and 59.5% year on year. Operating profit rose, but the gap between operating and net profit needs to be checked in the next disclosure.

The global fashion industry has been described as slowing, which NH Investment & Securities cited in April 2026 as a sales variable.

Risk factors

Trade and tariffs

US tariff policy directly affects tax rates in production countries. In July 2025 Kiwoom Securities cited a flagged additional US rate of 37% on Bangladesh and said volatility could rise while tariff talks remain unresolved. KB Securities noted the lower US share, but tariff policy changes remain a variable.

Exchange rate

A stronger won reduces won-translated earnings from dollar-denominated sales. Daishin Securities lowered its 2026 to 2027 estimates to reflect exchange rate moves. KB Securities noted that OEM operating margin fell 1.8 percentage points amid concern over a stronger won.

Customer and SCOTT concentration

DB Financial Investment put Arc'teryx at a mid-teens share of sales, so order changes at one customer can have a large effect on results. SCOTT is working through excess electric bicycle inventory and still posted an operating loss of KRW 1.6 billion in the third quarter of 2026. If SCOTT losses widen again, they could offset OEM profit gains.

Overall view

Youngone's operating profit recovered to KRW 514.4 billion in 2025 from the 2024 low, and it rose for three straight quarters from the first quarter through the third quarter of 2026. Supporting factors are double-digit order growth from key customers, narrowing losses at SCOTT and a relatively lower US sales share.

Offsetting factors are a flat top line, with third-quarter 2026 sales up only 1.0% year on year, a lower fourth-quarter outlook tied to the won-dollar rate, and a sharp fall in controlling-shareholder net profit. Broker views also differ.

KB Securities cut its target price to KRW 100,000, while Daishin Securities described the valuation range as attractive after a recent pullback. The expected rise in dividend payout is a shareholder-return point to check.

Whether profit gains continue depends on whether SCOTT's loss narrowing and OEM shipment flow both hold. The next regular filing is the 2026 annual report, which will disclose fourth-quarter results and the annual operating margin.
