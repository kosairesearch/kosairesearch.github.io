Ultracapacitor orders are the swing factor for a turnaround

02

Business structure

LS Materials was spun off in 2021 from the ultracapacitor division of LS Mtron and runs two businesses, ultracapacitors (UC) and aluminum parts. In Q3 2023, according to contemporary press reports, UC accounted for 26% of consolidated revenue and aluminum parts and other products for 74%.

A March 2026 Sisa Journal report said more than 80% of current revenue comes from an aluminum subsidiary, while UC is seen as the core growth axis over the medium term. A company analysis note from around 2024 described the aluminum business as anchored by supply to LS Group affiliates and Hyundai Motor Group.

The company says it is the global leader in large-format UC products and focuses on mid-to-large products. In September 2024 it announced a data-center power solutions collaboration with Vertiv.

In electric-vehicle parts, it formed the HAIMK joint venture with Austria's HAI, an aluminum extrusion firm, investing KRW 67.5 billion, and the company cited higher fixed costs from the HAIMK plant as a factor in the 2025 loss.

UC is classified in the secondary battery sector, but it is a power-storage device rather than a battery cell, so its economics differ from LG Energy Solution or Samsung SDI. The company says larger follow-on UC shipments start in 2027.

04

Earnings analysis

On a DART-filed basis, annual revenue fell 15.5% from KRW 162 billion in 2022 to KRW 136.9 billion in 2023, then rose 3.8% to KRW 142.1 billion in 2024 and 8.1% to KRW 153.6 billion in 2025.

Operating profit fell from KRW 14.5 billion in 2022 (8.9% margin) to KRW 13.6 billion in 2023 (9.9% margin), then dropped to KRW 6.2 billion in 2024 (4.4% margin, down 54.3%).

In 2025 the company swung to an operating loss of KRW 142 million (-0.1% margin), and it attributed this to higher fixed costs from the HAIMK plant under construction and lower sales of high-margin UC products.

Profit attributable to controlling shareholders peaked at KRW 12.2 billion in 2023 (up 44.6%), fell to KRW 5.67 billion in 2024 (down 53.3%), and reached KRW 435 million in 2025 (down 92.3%).

Net loss including non-controlling interests was KRW 1.01 billion in 2025, so the gap with the positive controlling-shareholder figure reflects losses booked on the non-controlling side.

At end-2025, total equity was KRW 206.1 billion, of which KRW 185.1 billion belonged to controlling shareholders, and total liabilities were KRW 68.5 billion. The debt ratio rose from 22.2% in 2024 to 33.2% in 2025, and operating cash flow moved from KRW -10.9 billion in 2024 to KRW 5.64 billion in 2025.

Quarterly revenue rose from KRW 38.5 billion in Q3 2025 to KRW 40.6 billion in Q4 2025, with Q4 turning to an operating profit of KRW 1.11 billion.

Q1 2026 revenue of KRW 43.1 billion came with an operating loss of KRW 1.15 billion, Q2 2026 revenue of KRW 51.3 billion came with an operating profit of KRW 533 million, and Q3 2026 revenue of KRW 56.9 billion came with an operating loss of KRW 347 million.

Profit attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 101 million in Q3 2026, down 84.8% from the prior quarter, and totaled KRW 1.29 billion over the last four quarters. Revenue has risen for four straight quarters, but that growth has not yet carried into a higher operating margin.

05

Industry analysis

Ultracapacitors are power devices that require high instantaneous output and long life. The company projects that the market for UC in AI data-center power stabilization will reach about KRW 1 trillion by 2027, a company-side estimate rather than an independent market study.

In a March 2026 report, Meritz Securities said the company's main growth directions are AI data centers, grid and electric vehicles, and that its EV parts business is delayed more than expected while UC supply for AI data centers and grids should start within 2026.

In a mid-2025 report, Kiwoom Securities pointed to European grid build-outs and a recovery in global wind installations as drivers of higher UC shipments and average selling prices, and it did not present an investment rating or target price.

Coverage describes the hydrogen fuel-cell UC market as at an early stage, so additional orders could follow as customers expand power-system deliveries.

The aluminum business depends on automotive lightweighting demand, and 2025 aluminum sales rose on that demand and on aluminum module deliveries for solid oxide fuel cells. UC capacity expansion was delayed by site selection as of Q2 2024.

Within the secondary battery sector, the company sells UC and aluminum parts rather than cathode or electrolyte materials, so its customers and cycle differ from those peers. Its position as the global leader in large-format UC rests on company statements.

Affiliates across the LS Group have also posted strong results on rising demand for power grids and energy infrastructure.

06

Outlook

In a March 2026 report, the company said it aims to reach KRW 600 billion in revenue by 2030 through its existing businesses and expanded UC supply for AI data centers. The first step toward that goal is the UC contract with a US hydrogen fuel-cell company.

According to coverage, an initial supply deal of about KRW 7 billion signed in July 2026 starts shipments in November 2026, and a further supply contract of about KRW 69 billion brought cumulative contracts with that customer to about KRW 76 billion.

That cumulative amount is about 2.6 times the 2025 standalone revenue of roughly KRW 29 billion. The company expects cumulative orders to reach about five times 2025 standalone revenue if additional contracts are signed within 2026. It plans to ramp larger follow-on shipments from 2027.

In AI data centers, its representative said in March 2026 that it is negotiating supply with global server makers following the September 2024 Vertiv collaboration.

The EV-parts joint venture HAIMK was expected at the 2023 listing to generate about KRW 200 billion in revenue in 2027, while Meritz Securities said in March 2026 that this business is delayed more than expected, so the gap between those figures is a key checkpoint.

The KRW 600 billion 2030 target is large relative to current revenue, so it depends on expanding UC volumes. The 2026 annual report will show how much UC revenue and orders are reflected.

08

Bull factors

US customer orders broaden the revenue base

Cumulative UC contracts with a US hydrogen fuel-cell customer were reported at about KRW 76 billion, roughly 2.6 times 2025 standalone revenue of about KRW 29 billion. The company is pursuing additional contracts within 2026 and plans larger shipments from 2027.

If orders convert into revenue, a new axis would be added to a structure that is currently dominated by aluminum.

Four quarters of revenue growth and a Q2 profit swing

Revenue rose for four consecutive quarters from Q4 2025 through Q3 2026, and Q2 2026 swung to an operating profit of KRW 533 million. Q3 2026 revenue grew 47.7% year on year. Even a brief stretch in which revenue growth reached operating profit is a positive signal.

AI data-center and grid expansion with a 2030 target

The company projects a market of about KRW 1 trillion for UC in AI data-center power stabilization by 2027 and has set a KRW 600 billion revenue target for 2030. Its claimed global leadership in large-format UC and the 2024 Vertiv collaboration are cited as grounds for this direction. The target is a company projection, so it will be tested by how orders convert into reported revenue.

09

Bear factors

Operating losses recur quarter after quarter

After the 2025 operating loss of KRW 142 million, quarterly operating losses recurred, at KRW 1.15 billion in Q1 2026 and KRW 347 million in Q3 2026. The Q3 loss was larger than a year earlier. Whether the fixed-cost burden from the HAIMK plant, which the company cited, eases remains an open issue.

A thin base of profit for controlling shareholders

Profit attributable to controlling shareholders fell from KRW 12.2 billion in 2023 to KRW 435 million in 2025, and totaled KRW 1.29 billion over the last four quarters. The Q3 2026 figure of KRW 101 million was down 84.8% from the prior quarter. Revenue has grown, but profit has not kept pace.

Customer concentration and delays in EV parts

Cumulative contracts of about KRW 76 billion come from a single US customer, so customer concentration is high. Meritz Securities said in a March 2026 report that the HAIMK EV-parts joint venture is delayed more than expected. A company analysis note from around 2024 also described UC capacity expansion as delayed during site selection.

10

Risk factors

Customer concentration and contract execution

A contract with a single U.S. customer could account for a significant portion of UC orders, so any delay in contract performance or in signing additional contracts could push back earnings expectations.

The UC market for hydrogen fuel cells is reported to be at an early stage of growth, and volumes vary with the scale of the customer's power system supply. The key risk is whether large-scale shipments after 2027 proceed as planned.

Business structure and fixed costs

The aluminum parts business, which accounts for most of revenue, is highly dependent on subsidiaries and relies on demand for automotive lightweighting.

The fixed-cost burden of the joint venture plant was cited as the cause of the 2025 loss, and if the utilization rate is low, the same cost structure could erode profit further. The timing of capacity expansion and the start of joint venture operations could increase earnings volatility.

Balance sheet and valuation

The debt-to-equity ratio rose from 22.2% in 2024 to 33.2% in 2025, and operating cash flow was negative in 2024. The price-to-book ratio is in a range with a large premium over net assets, so if earnings expectations are not met, the share price could swing widely. Whether quarterly returns to profitability are repeated will determine the size of this risk.

12

Overall view

On a DART-filed basis, revenue has risen since 2023, when it bottomed, but operating profit fell from KRW 14.5 billion in 2022 to an operating loss of KRW 142 million in 2025, so margin recovery remains the open issue.

Quarterly revenue has risen for four straight quarters in 2026 and Q2 swung to an operating profit, but Q3 returned to an operating loss of KRW 347 million.

The largest variable is the UC contract with a US hydrogen fuel-cell customer, with cumulative contracts of about KRW 76 billion reported and initial supply slated from November 2026. Whether those orders lead to large shipments in 2027 and to the KRW 600 billion 2030 revenue target is the central medium-term fact.

On the other side, the customer base is concentrated in one buyer, the EV-parts joint venture is delayed, and the profit base for controlling shareholders is thin at KRW 1.29 billion over four quarters.

The price-to-book ratio sits well above book value, so the gap between growth expectations and the earnings base is the issue for valuation.

The next checkpoints are the start of initial shipments in late November 2026, additional contracts by December 2026, Q4 2026 provisional results in early 2027, and the 2026 annual report in March 2027.
