02

Business structure

Founded in 2018, Finger Story is an integrated story-content business focused on distributing webtoons, web novels, and digital comics online.

The company operates 'Mootoon', a male-oriented martial-arts and action webtoon platform, and 'Qutoon', a female-oriented romance and BL platform, together offering roughly 30,000 content titles.

Revenue composition is skewed heavily toward the webtoon, web novel, and digital comics segment at about 98%, with film and other revenue each making up roughly 1%. The revenue model is built on content payment (coin-based) charges and advertising income.

At the end of 2025, Finger Story brought cosmetics maker Swanicoco into its consolidated group, expanding beyond content into a new business line. Swanicoco sells low-irritant skincare products formulated with plant-derived technology through its own online store and pharmacy distribution channels, among others.

On the content side, the company is pursuing secondary licensing revenue through self-produced intellectual property and copyright ownership, with potential IP value-chain expansion tied to the broader trend of webtoon-to-screen adaptations.

Competitively, Finger Story is classified alongside similarly-sized domestic webtoon and web-novel platforms such as Toomics, Contentsfirst (Tappytoon), Kidari Studio (Bomtoon), and Mr. Blue, and the company listed on KOSDAQ in December 2022 through a merger with Yuanta No. 7 Special Purpose Acquisition Company.

04

Earnings analysis

On an annual basis, revenue rose from KRW 17.4 billion in 2022 to KRW 18.4 billion in 2023, the largest figure in the 2022–2025 period, before falling for two consecutive years to KRW 18.4 billion in 2024 (down 0.4%) and KRW 17.3 billion in 2025 (down 5.7%), making 2025 the smallest year.

Operating profit climbed from KRW 987 million in 2022 to KRW 1.38 billion in 2023 (+40.1%) and KRW 1.51 billion in 2024 (+9.0%), the largest year in the period, before dropping 43.8% to KRW 847 million in 2025, the smallest year.

The operating margin improved from 5.7% in 2022 to 8.2% in 2024 before easing back to 4.9% in 2025 as profitability weakened again.

In contrast, net profit attributable to controlling shareholders swung from a net loss of KRW 3.61 billion in 2022 to a profit of KRW 1.73 billion in 2023, then KRW 1.68 billion in 2024 (-3.0%), before jumping 109.3% to KRW 3.51 billion in 2025; the company explained that financial asset valuation gains and convertible bond derivative valuation gains contributed to the increase in net profit.

Net income including non-controlling interests matched the controlling-shareholder figure in each of these years, indicating minority interests had little impact.

On a quarterly basis, revenue and operating profit rose sequentially through the third quarter of 2025 (revenue KRW 4.38 billion, +4.1%; operating profit KRW 217 million, +29.2%), but the company posted operating losses of KRW 108 million and KRW 345 million in the first and second quarters of 2026 respectively, marking two consecutive loss-making quarters.

Second-quarter 2026 revenue of KRW 4.77 billion rose 13.3% year-on-year but fell 4.5% quarter-on-quarter, while net profit attributable to controlling shareholders swung from a KRW 9.49 million loss in the prior quarter to a KRW 606 million profit, though that was down 84.5% from the same quarter a year earlier.

At the end of 2025, total equity stood at KRW 31.1 billion (controlling interest also KRW 31.1 billion) and total liabilities at KRW 19.9 billion, pushing the debt-to-equity ratio up to 63.8%, while operating cash flow rose from KRW 1.18 billion in 2022 to KRW 2.46 billion in 2023 before declining to KRW 2.37 billion in 2024 and KRW 1.66 billion in 2025.

05

Industry analysis

South Korea's webtoon and web-novel platform industry is led by large operators that dominate traffic and the intellectual-property value chain, while smaller specialized platforms like Finger Story target genre-specific niches such as martial arts/action and romance/BL.

Finger Story is classified alongside similarly sized peers with comparable business models, including Toomics, Contentsfirst (Tappytoon), Kidari Studio (Bomtoon), and Mr. Blue.

These smaller platforms share similar revenue structures built on subscription/payment-based charges and advertising income, while simultaneously bearing significant content production and marketing cost burdens.

Across the industry, the expansion of IP into screen adaptations and secondary licensing has gained attention as an additional revenue source, and Finger Story is likewise pursuing secondary licensing revenue through self-produced IP and copyright ownership.

However, the webtoon and web-novel content business has relatively low entry barriers, and continued traffic concentration toward large platforms makes it difficult for smaller operators to defend profitability.

Beyond content, Finger Story diversified its portfolio by adding cosmetics manufacturing and sales (Swanicoco) to its consolidated scope, which reduces exposure to content-industry cycles while simultaneously increasing business complexity.

06

Outlook

Looking ahead, the first fact to confirm is Finger Story's cumulative results for the first three quarters of 2026.

According to an October 9, 2026 news report, nine-month cumulative revenue fell 6.7% year-on-year, operating profit dropped 43.5%, and net income rose 322.3%; these are preliminary figures that have not yet been disclosed via a periodic report and may differ from the confirmed results.

The company carried out a 5-for-1 reverse stock split in April 2026 and subsequently issued new shares through a third-party allotment capital increase, raising the total number of common shares outstanding.

On the capital front, the company is reviewing a plan to either retire or retain/dispose of the 2,532 treasury shares it held as of the end of the first half of 2026, with shareholder-meeting approval under the revised Commercial Act set to be a key factor.

On the business side, secondary licensing revenue from self-produced IP and copyright ownership in the content segment, along with the consolidated contribution of newly added cosmetics subsidiary Swanicoco, are expected to become more visible in upcoming quarterly reports.

However, having posted operating losses in both the first and second quarters of 2026, whether content production and marketing cost burdens ease remains a precondition for any profitability recovery.

On governance, given that the largest shareholder changed from Swanicoco to Global Tax Free in July 2025 and then from Global Tax Free to Moon Yang-geun and three others in December 2025, further management changes or shareholding disclosures warrant continued monitoring.

08

Bull factors

Signs of Renewed Revenue Growth

First-quarter 2026 revenue of KRW 4.99 billion and second-quarter revenue of KRW 4.77 billion both showed year-on-year growth, with the second quarter up 13.3%.

Quarterly revenue climbing from the low-to-mid KRW 4 billion range in the second and third quarters of 2025 to the high KRW 4 billion range in 2026 could be read as a signal of recovering content consumption. That said, this revenue recovery has not yet translated into an operating profit.

Potential IP Licensing and Screen-Adaptation Value-Chain Expansion

Finger Story is pursuing secondary licensing revenue through self-produced IP and copyright ownership, with IP value-chain expansion tied to the webtoon-to-screen adaptation trend cited as a potential catalyst.

With roughly 30,000 content titles across its martial-arts/action-focused Mootoon and romance/BL-focused Qutoon platforms, the company's underlying content pool is relatively deep. Whether these assets translate into actual monetization will need to be confirmed through future disclosures.

Revenue Diversification Through Business Expansion

By consolidating cosmetics maker Swanicoco as a subsidiary at the end of 2025, Finger Story moved away from a single-business structure centered solely on content.

Swanicoco sells low-irritant skincare products formulated with plant-derived technology through its own online store and pharmacy channels, among others, adding a revenue source decoupled from the content industry cycle.

However, as consolidation is still in an early stage, the cosmetics segment's specific revenue contribution has not yet been fully disclosed in quarterly reports.

Bear factors

Two Consecutive Quarters of Operating Losses

Finger Story posted operating losses of KRW 108 million and KRW 345 million in the first and second quarters of 2026, respectively, breaking the profitable streak that had continued through 2024.

Content production and marketing cost burdens offset revenue growth, and the annual operating margin fell sharply to 4.9% in 2025 from 8.2% the prior year. A structural cost burden that prevents revenue recovery from translating into profit remains a near-term challenge.

Governance Uncertainty from Frequent Changes in Largest Shareholder

Finger Story's largest shareholder changed from Swanicoco to Global Tax Free in July 2025, then from Global Tax Free to Moon Yang-geun and three others in December 2025. Governance changing more than twice within less than a year could be a source of uncertainty for the continuity of management strategy.

The company, however, explained this as a move to secure a mid-to-long-term growth foundation through management participation and unification of the business operating entity.

Changing Financial Structure from Expanded Consolidation Scope

Following the Swanicoco consolidation, the debt-to-equity ratio rose sharply to 63.8% at the end of 2025 from 41.0% a year earlier.

Cosmetics manufacturing and distribution has a different cost structure and working-capital profile than the content business, requiring care in interpreting the consolidated financial statements.

There is not yet enough quarterly data to assess how the profitability and cash flow of the newly added business interact with the existing content business.

Risk factors

Core Business Profitability

Content production and marketing cost burdens drove operating losses in both the first and second quarters of 2026, and if this trend continues into the third quarter and beyond, it could weigh on full-year results. Even as revenue grew year-on-year, cost growth appears to have outpaced it. Confirmation of the cost structure through the next periodic report is needed.

Governance and Largest-Shareholder Changes

Over the past year, the largest shareholder moved from Swanicoco to Global Tax Free and then to Moon Yang-geun and three others. Frequent governance changes can reduce predictability in management strategy continuity and key decision-making. Continued monitoring of any further shareholding disclosures is warranted.

Consolidated Financial Structure and Debt Ratio

The debt-to-equity ratio rose sharply to 63.8% at the end of 2025, coinciding with the expanded consolidation scope from the Swanicoco subsidiary addition. The financial health of the cosmetics segment and its effect on Finger Story's consolidated financial structure warrants continued attention.

Whether the rise in the debt ratio is a temporary or structural shift needs to be confirmed through subsequent quarterly trends.

Overall view

Finger Story showed signs of quarterly revenue recovery on a year-on-year basis in 2026, but content production and marketing cost burdens drove operating losses in both the first and second quarters.

The sharp increase in 2025 net profit attributable to controlling shareholders was driven largely by non-operating factors such as financial asset and convertible bond derivative valuation gains rather than core operating performance, so earnings quality should be assessed alongside the operating margin.

Over the past year, the largest shareholder changed three times — from Swanicoco to Global Tax Free to Moon Yang-geun and three others — rapidly reshaping governance, while the consolidation of cosmetics maker Swanicoco as a subsidiary pushed the debt-to-equity ratio up to 63.8%.

Preliminary nine-month figures reported on October 9, 2026 indicated simultaneous declines in revenue and operating profit alongside a sharp rise in net income, though these have not yet been confirmed through a periodic report.

Going forward, key points to monitor include whether operating losses persist in the third-quarter periodic report, how capital-increase proceeds are used, the treasury share disposal plan, and whether the ownership structure stabilizes.
