Three Straight Quarters of Operating Losses as Finger Story Reshapes Its Capital

Revenue fell for two straight years from 2024 to 2025, and operating losses have run for three consecutive quarters through the third quarter of 2026, while a bonus issue and convertible bond clean-up are reshaping the share count and capital structure.

02

Business structure

Finger Story was founded in 2018 and listed on KOSDAQ in December 2022 as a webtoon and web novel company. Its core platforms are Mootoon, focused on wuxia webtoons, and Qtoon, focused on romance and fantasy titles, where it produces its own IP and also distributes third-party works.

According to company materials, revenue is based on online content sales and advertising agency income. The company is pursuing secondary rights income from owned IP, and the Company profile tracker of WiseReport describes IP value-chain expansion as expected as webtoons move into adaptation.

Earlier, it reportedly supplied IP to domestic companies such as NAVER and Kakao for distribution on their overseas platforms. In December 2025 its subsidiary Swanicoco became a consolidated subsidiary, selling low-irritation cosmetics through various distribution channels.

The largest shareholder changed to Moon Yang-keun from Global Tax Free in December 2025. Segment revenue weights are to be gauged from the segment information in the annual business report.

04

Earnings analysis

On a disclosed annual basis, revenue rose from KRW 17.4 billion in 2022 to KRW 18.4 billion in 2023 (up 5.8% year on year), the peak year, then edged down to KRW 18.4 billion in 2024 (down 0.4%) and fell to KRW 17.3 billion in 2025 (down 5.7%). Revenue has fallen for two consecutive years, from 2024 through 2025.

The largest year in the 2022 to 2025 window was 2023, and the smallest was 2025. Operating profit rose from KRW 987 million in 2022 to KRW 1.38 billion in 2023 (up 40.1%) and KRW 1.51 billion in 2024 (up 9.0%), then dropped to KRW 847 million in 2025 (down 43.8%).

The operating margin slid from 8.2% in 2024 to 4.9% in 2025, so falling sales and falling profit coincided. Net income tells a different story.

Controlling shareholders' net income reached KRW 3.51 billion in 2025, up 109.3%, and the company said gains on financial assets and on convertible bond derivatives helped lift it.

In 2022 the company posted a controlling-shareholder net loss of KRW 3.61 billion, which turned into a profit of KRW 1.73 billion in 2023, followed by KRW 1.68 billion in 2024 (down 3.0%).

The debt-to-equity ratio rose from 32.6% in 2022 and 41.0% in 2024 to 63.8% in 2025, when total liabilities stood at KRW 19.9 billion and total equity at KRW 31.1 billion. The rise coincides with the fourth private convertible bond issued on November 18, 2025 with a face value of KRW 8.5 billion.

Operating cash flow was KRW 1.18 billion in 2022, KRW 2.46 billion in 2023, KRW 2.37 billion in 2024 and KRW 1.66 billion in 2025. On a quarterly basis, operating profit of KRW 217 million in the third quarter of 2025 turned into an operating loss of KRW 108 million in the first quarter of 2026.

The second quarter brought a KRW 345 million operating loss, which widened the loss, and the third quarter a KRW 288 million loss, which narrowed it. Three consecutive quarters of operating losses have now been recorded from the first quarter to the third quarter of 2026.

WiseReport's company summary for the first half of 2026 described an operating loss compared with a profit a year earlier, and attributed the drag to content production and marketing costs.

Third-quarter controlling-shareholder net income was KRW 509 million, up 339.8% year on year, yet a profit alongside an operating loss means non-operating items need to be examined. The sum of controlling-shareholder net income over the last four quarters is KRW 59.46 million.

05

Industry analysis

The webtoon industry is structured around large domestic platforms that control distribution, and smaller players typically carve out niches through genre focus and IP ownership.

Finger Story runs genre-specific platforms in wuxia and romance while also supplying its own IP to domestic platforms such as NAVER and Kakao for distribution. Platform fee structures and negotiating terms with those external channels therefore shape profitability.

WiseReport frames the webtoon-to-adaptation trend as an opportunity for IP value-chain expansion, though that reading mirrors the company's own strategy. Scale differences versus peers are clear.

Compared with the KOSDAQ IT services sector average in FnGuide's 2025 annual comparison, Finger Story's revenue and operating profit are small. The market backdrop is also volatile, and a August 2026 news headline reported that 49 KOSDAQ companies were clustered near the KRW 20 billion market capitalization threshold.

Webtoon production carries fixed costs for authors and titles, so revenue growth does not translate easily into profit. Third-quarter 2026 revenue rose 9.4% year on year, a sign of top-line recovery, but with operating losses continuing, cost structure is the key variable. Peers such as NAVER and Kakao operate at a far larger scale, so direct margin comparison is limited.

The company said the bonus issue would help it strengthen content competitiveness on its core platforms and move faster on new growth drivers. A bonus issue does not change total equity, so the capital-side change concentrates on share count and the adjustment of per-share figures.

Whether revenue recovery is sustained will be verified through full-year revenue in the 2026 annual business report, which includes the fourth quarter. The direction of content production and marketing spending, the main drivers of the losses, will show up in the cost lines of that report.

Swanicoco's contribution is not yet separated in the disclosed results and should be read within consolidated figures.

Risk factors

Business risk: production costs and IP acquisition

Because cost is concentrated in producing its own IP and securing copyrights, operating profit may not rise in step with revenue. The 2026 quarterly losses have been linked to production and marketing costs.

Changes in the share of distribution through external platforms and in fee terms could directly affect profitability.

Capital and dilution risk

The remaining KRW 2.5 billion of convertible bonds can convert into shares during the conversion window from November 18, 2026 to October 18, 2030. Together with the June 2026 third-party allotment, the August 2026 listing and about 1.71 million bonus shares, the share count rises sharply.

Per-share figures are adjusted by the share count change, so comparisons with past numbers need to account for it.

Regulatory and market risk: administrative-issue standard

Under KOSDAQ listing rules, a company is designated as administrative issue if common-stock market capitalization stays below KRW 20 billion for 30 consecutive trading days. The company disclosed on July 30, 2026 that it had been below the threshold for 25 trading days.

An August 2026 news headline counted 49 KOSDAQ companies clustered near that threshold. Market capitalization movements therefore bear directly on listing maintenance.

Overall view

Finger Story operates the Mootoon and Qtoon platforms and is a KOSDAQ-listed webtoon and web novel company.

On disclosed annual figures, revenue peaked at KRW 18.4 billion in 2023 and fell to KRW 17.3 billion in 2025, with declines in both 2024 and 2025, while the operating margin dropped from 8.2% in 2024 to 4.9% in 2025.

Controlling-shareholder net income of KRW 3.51 billion in 2025 was attributed by the company to valuation gains on non-operating items. Operating losses have run for three consecutive quarters from the first quarter to the third quarter of 2026.

Third-quarter 2026 revenue rose 9.4% year on year, an early sign of top-line recovery, but the profit side has not yet shown improvement in the quarterly results. On the capital side, a shrinking convertible bond balance, a third-party allotment and a bonus issue are reshaping share count and debt structure together.

Administrative-issue concerns tied to the KRW 20 billion market capitalization threshold are linked to the rising share count. Strengths include capital clean-up and shareholder-return steps, while weaknesses include continued operating losses and two years of falling revenue.

The next checkpoints are the November 25, 2026 bonus issue record date and the 2026 annual business report due by the end of March 2027, where the full-year 2026 operating margin is the central item.
