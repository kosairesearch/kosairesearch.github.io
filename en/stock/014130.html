HAN Express: Profit Turn Tested by 2030 Growth Plan

HAN Express swung to an operating loss in 2025, and its operating profit recovered to KRW 3.07 billion in the third quarter of 2026, though a net-income recovery is not yet confirmed. Its value-up plan disclosed on August 14, 2026 targets a return to operating profit in 2026 and KRW 900 billion in revenue by 2030.

02

Business structure

HAN Express is a logistics company whose main businesses are road freight transport, international freight forwarding and 3PL (third-party logistics). The company says it holds top-tier competitiveness in specialty cargo such as refrigerated goods, chemical products and toxic materials.

Its operating segments include domestic transport, international logistics, distribution logistics, warehousing and services. It has three overseas subsidiaries in China, Vietnam and Singapore and three domestic subsidiaries.

A cold-chain logistics center was completed in 2022, and a US subsidiary was set up in Georgia the same year to expand infrastructure along the southeastern US coast.

Before the spin-off from Hanwha Group, it held an exclusive position in inland container transport for Hanwha Chemical's exports, and that trading relationship has continued since.

Drawing on 47 years of specialty cargo capability, the company plans to build semiconductor specialty chemicals and defense logistics into strategic businesses. Its less profitable fulfillment subsidiary has completed liquidation procedures.

The domestic logistics market has grown more competitive, with many small and mid-sized players weighing on profitability. As of June 2026, the largest shareholder group led by Lee Seok-hwan and four related parties held 48.01%, and in January 2026 Lee bought 18,608 shares on the open market.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual revenue fell from KRW 858.1 billion in 2022 to KRW 668.4 billion in 2023 (down 22.1% year on year), rose to KRW 712.3 billion in 2024 (up 6.6%), and declined to KRW 629.8 billion in 2025 (down 11.6%). Among 2022 to 2025, 2022 was the largest year and 2025 the smallest.

Operating profit went from KRW 19.9 billion in 2022 to KRW 8.72 billion in 2023 (down 56.1%) and KRW 3.37 billion in 2024 (down 61.4%), and the company swung to an operating loss of KRW 3.62 billion in 2025. The operating margin slid from 2.3% in 2022 to 0.5% in 2024 and -0.6% in 2025.

The company attributed the 2025 deterioration mainly to lower profitability at its domestic and overseas subsidiaries.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 8.54 billion in 2022, a loss of KRW 15.6 billion in 2023 after a turn to loss, a profit of KRW 17.2 billion in 2024 after a turn to profit, and a loss of KRW 13.8 billion in 2025.

In 2024 net income was well above operating profit, which suggests non-operating items had a large effect; the detailed cause should be checked in the notes to the business report.

Operating cash flow was KRW 10.7 billion in 2022, KRW 30.4 billion in 2023 and KRW 16.2 billion in 2024, before turning to -KRW 3.38 billion in 2025. The debt ratio rose to 365.3% in 2025 from 289.6% in 2024.

At the end of 2025, total equity was KRW 86.2 billion, of which KRW 82.3 billion was attributable to controlling shareholders, and total liabilities were KRW 315 billion.

On a quarterly basis, revenue was KRW 163.4 billion in the third quarter of 2025 with operating profit of KRW 665 million; in the fourth quarter it fell to KRW 151.1 billion (down 7.5% from the prior quarter) with an operating loss of KRW 3.22 billion.

In the first quarter of 2026 revenue was KRW 154.5 billion with an operating loss of KRW 2.18 billion, and in the second quarter revenue was KRW 153.4 billion with operating profit of KRW 1.14 billion, a turn to profit.

In the third quarter of 2026 revenue reached KRW 170.2 billion (up 11.0% from the prior quarter and 4.2% year on year) and operating profit reached KRW 3.07 billion (up 168.1% from the prior quarter and 361.8% year on year).

The parent-attributable net loss was KRW 4.05 billion in the first quarter of 2026, and the total over the last four quarters was KRW 10.1 billion. Whether the operating turn to profit carries through to net income will become clear in the next periodic reports.

05

Industry analysis

In the trucking and warehousing segment that HAN Express belongs to, the domestic logistics market has grown more competitive, and many small and mid-sized players keep profitability under pressure.

A securities information site's comment on the first quarter of 2026 also pointed to this intensifying competition as a factor weighing on profitability. On the demand side, the company attributed its lower profitability to a slowdown in key end-markets and higher cost burdens.

The macro backdrop includes high prices and rising interest rates that lifted logistics costs, while e-commerce growth and demand for customized services are accelerating automation and geopolitical risk is pressuring results.

In 2022, the shift to non-face-to-face consumption and surging e-commerce use lifted cargo volumes, and industry revenue broadly rose, according to an analysis. Results since 2025 overlap with a period in which the business slowed and profitability eroded.

The semiconductor specialty chemicals and defense logistics the company aims to grow draw on the same capability in handling chemical and hazardous specialty cargo.

Larger peers in the same sector, Hyundai Glovis, CJ Logistics and HANJIN Logistics, have a much broader business scope, so HAN Express competes in the niche of specialty cargo transport. The direction of the cycle can be gauged from specialty cargo volumes and the operating rates of major customers.

06

Outlook

On August 14, 2026, following board review and approval, the company disclosed a value-up plan. Its core is to shrink low-margin and non-core businesses, optimize hub operations, and raise productivity through process standardization and digital transformation, with the goal of returning to operating profit in 2026.

The plan targets 10.3% annual growth from 2026 to 2030 and KRW 900 billion in revenue by 2030. The company intends to build semiconductor specialty chemicals and defense logistics into core strategic businesses and plans to make a US local hub investment in 2027.

For shareholder returns, it set a target of 20% of consolidated parent-attributable net income from 2027, with flexible use of dividends and share buybacks and cancellations. In July 2026 it cancelled 182,117 treasury shares and decided to acquire an additional 400,000 shares, about 3.3% of issued shares.

It has also completed liquidation procedures for the fulfillment subsidiary, which it says will continue to improve the financial structure. For capital efficiency, the plan presents a target of a price-to-book ratio of 1.0x or higher by 2030.

The next checkpoint is whether the company returns to operating profit for 2026, which can be checked in the full-year provisional results expected in early 2027 and the business report due by late March 2027. Progress toward the 2030 revenue target can be gauged from annual revenue growth disclosed each year.

Bull factors

Quarterly operating profit recovery

Operating profit turned positive at KRW 1.14 billion in the second quarter of 2026 and rose to KRW 3.07 billion in the third quarter. The third-quarter figure was up 168.1% from the prior quarter and 361.8% year on year, showing the direction of the change in the numbers.

Whether the planned reduction of low-margin businesses and hub optimization sustain this trend will become visible in later quarterly results.

Shareholder return policy and buybacks

The company set a target of returning 20% of consolidated parent-attributable net income to shareholders from 2027. In July 2026 it cancelled 182,117 treasury shares and decided to acquire 400,000 more. In January 2026, Lee Seok-hwan, a major shareholder, was disclosed as having bought 18,608 shares on the open market.

Business restructuring and new strategic areas

The fulfillment subsidiary has completed liquidation, and non-core businesses are being reduced. The company plans to grow semiconductor specialty chemicals and defense logistics as strategic businesses, resting on 47 years of specialty cargo capability. The company says a US hub investment in 2027 would broaden its North American customer base.

Bear factors

Continued losses at the net level

After an operating loss of KRW 3.62 billion and a parent-attributable net loss of KRW 13.8 billion in 2025, the first quarter of 2026 also recorded a parent-attributable net loss of KRW 4.05 billion. The parent-attributable net loss over the last four quarters totaled KRW 10.1 billion. If operating profit does not translate into net profit, the basis for the shareholder return target weakens.

Debt burden and weaker cash generation

The debt ratio rose to 365.3% at the end of 2025 from 289.6% in 2024, and total liabilities stand at KRW 315 billion. Operating cash flow fell from KRW 30.4 billion in 2023 to KRW 16.2 billion in 2024 and to -KRW 3.38 billion in 2025, weakening cash generation. Funding pressure could rise if the 2027 US hub investment and shareholder returns proceed at the same time.

Industry slowdown and competitive pressure

The domestic logistics market has grown more competitive, with many small and mid-sized players pressuring margins. The company points to a slowdown in key end-markets and higher cost burdens as factors lowering profitability.

High prices and rising interest rates have lifted logistics costs, which may limit the effect of cost reduction.

Risk factors

Target achievement risk

The 2030 revenue target of KRW 900 billion and 10.3% annual growth assume a large expansion from 2025 revenue of KRW 629.8 billion. If the semiconductor and defense logistics efforts and the US hub investment do not run as planned, the target path could slip. Revenue could also decline first while low-margin businesses are reduced.

Financial and liquidity risk

A debt ratio of 365.3% and total liabilities of KRW 315 billion could translate into higher financing costs. With operating cash flow turning negative in 2025, simultaneous investment and treasury share purchases would increase funding needs. Rising interest rates would add to this burden.

Governance and disclosure risk

The largest shareholder group holds 48.01%, leaving a free float ratio of 50.31%. In February 2026, reports said the company faced a warning of designation as an insincere disclosure filer after disclosing a late event at an investee company. The timeliness of disclosures should be assessed alongside the reliability of future results.

Overall view

HAN Express's revenue fell from KRW 858.1 billion in 2022 to KRW 629.8 billion in 2025, and over the same period operating profit turned into an operating loss of KRW 3.62 billion in 2025.

The return to profit in the second quarter of 2026 and operating profit of KRW 3.07 billion in the third quarter are signs of recovery, but the parent-attributable net loss of KRW 4.05 billion in the first quarter of 2026 and the KRW 10.1 billion total over the last four quarters remain open issues.

The financial structure carries a heavy burden, with a debt ratio of 365.3% and total liabilities of KRW 315 billion at the end of 2025, and operating cash flow turned negative at -KRW 3.38 billion in 2025.

The company has presented a plan for an operating profit in 2026, KRW 900 billion in revenue by 2030, and shareholder returns of 20% of parent-attributable net income from 2027.

Positive factors include the quarterly operating profit recovery, treasury share cancellation and additional buybacks, and open-market purchases by a major shareholder.

Negative factors include intensifying competition in the domestic logistics market, a slowdown in key end-markets, and how far accumulated net losses will erode the capital base.

Both sets of factors still need to be verified in reported figures, and the next checkpoint is whether the company returns to full-year operating profit, which will be shown in the provisional full-year results in early 2027 and the business report due in late March 2027.
