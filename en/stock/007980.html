02

Business structure

TP (007980) is a KOSPI-listed apparel OEM (original equipment manufacturing) company that produces and supplies goods against orders from overseas apparel brands. Its major customers are North American apparel brands, so changes in customer supply-chain policy can alter order flows.

The company has maintained long-term trading relationships with global customers and secured stable order volumes, which credit rating agencies have analyzed as the basis of solid profitability.

Because order volume drives revenue, customer ordering timing and inventory policy become the key variables for quarterly results.

Apparel companies continue to run inventories conservatively, but the low share of inventory relative to sales in the apparel industry is cited as a reason why a sharp revenue decline is considered limited.

In June 2025 the company added a business purpose covering consignment support services for subsidiaries, including planning, accounting and IT. Headcount at the end of June 2026 was 237, down from 252 at the end of December 2025.

In the same apparel and fashion sector, Youngone and Hansae are frequently cited as the main listed comparison points in the OEM competitive landscape. In OEM businesses, the ability to secure customers and production efficiency are regarded as factors that determine long-term competitiveness.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual revenue fell from KRW 1.08 trillion in 2022 to KRW 920.2 billion in 2023 (down 15.2% year on year), recovered to KRW 1.06 trillion in 2024 (up 15.6%), and eased to KRW 1.03 trillion in 2025 (down 3.3%).

Operating profit dropped from KRW 68.4 billion in 2022 to KRW 40.6 billion in 2023 (down 40.7%), the low point, and then rose to KRW 48.9 billion in 2024 (up 20.6%) and KRW 62 billion in 2025 (up 26.7%), increasing for two consecutive years.

The operating margin was 6.3% in 2022, 4.4% in 2023, 4.6% in 2024 and 6.0% in 2025, reflecting a 2023 combination of lower sales and margin pressure followed by recovery after 2024.

Profit attributable to owners of the parent fell from KRW 25.1 billion in 2022 to KRW 6.05 billion in 2023 (down 75.9%), then rose to KRW 22.2 billion in 2024 (up 266.5%) and KRW 38.2 billion in 2025 (up 72.5%), making 2025 the largest year in the period.

Net income including non-controlling interests was KRW 38 billion in 2025, which differs from profit attributable to owners of the parent, and the gap reflects the share attributable to non-controlling interests.

Operating cash flow was KRW 56.5 billion in 2022 and KRW 49 billion in 2023, fell to KRW 16.2 billion in 2024, the low point, and recovered to KRW 52 billion in 2025.

On a quarterly basis, revenue of KRW 303.4 billion and operating profit of KRW 25.5 billion in the third quarter of 2025 gave way to fourth-quarter revenue of KRW 210.2 billion (down 30.7% from the prior quarter) and operating profit of KRW 9.86 billion (down 61.4%).

Revenue then rebounded to KRW 248.9 billion in the first quarter of 2026 (up 18.4%) with operating profit of KRW 14.7 billion (up 48.7%), but second-quarter operating profit of KRW 14 billion (down 4.5%) and third-quarter operating profit of KRW 6.69 billion (down 52.2% from the prior quarter and down 73.8% year on year) marked two consecutive quarterly declines.

Over the same period, profit attributable to owners of the parent was KRW 12.5 billion in the first quarter of 2026 and KRW 15.9 billion in the third quarter, well above operating profit, so the two measures moved in different directions, which suggests non-operating or one-off items may have played a role. The sum of profit attributable to owners of the parent over the last four quarters was KRW 33 billion.

Industry analysis

The OEM apparel and textile industry is structurally sensitive to order flows from overseas apparel brands and to tariff policy. Apparel companies continue to run inventories conservatively, so the environment in which orders can change within a short period appears likely to persist.

However, the low share of inventory relative to sales in the apparel industry is cited as a factor that limits the possibility of a sharp decline in TP's revenue. TP's revenue hit a low of KRW 920.2 billion in 2023, recovered to KRW 1.06 trillion in 2024, and eased to KRW 1.03 trillion in 2025.

Revenue rose for three consecutive quarters from the first quarter to the third quarter of 2026, but third-quarter 2026 revenue was down 0.5% year on year. A credit rating agency put the average operating margin for 2022 to 2024 at 5.1%, and the 2025 operating margin of 6.0% is above that average.

In cycle terms, the company is in a recovery phase after the 2023 low, but its orders could fluctuate again if customer inventory policy or tariffs change.

Compared with large listed OEM peers such as Youngone and Hansae, TP's high share of North American brand customers is discussed both as a distinguishing feature and as a concentration risk.

Broadening the customer base is a common challenge in the industry, and progress on customer diversification is a useful indicator of structural change.

Improving financial structure

Bear factors

Profit attributable to owners of the parent of KRW 15.9 billion rose sharply from the prior quarter but was down 16.9% year on year, so operating profit and net profit moved in different directions.

North American customer concentration and tariffs

Because major customers are North American apparel brands, there are concerns that orders could change under customer supply-chain policies if the US imposes tariffs. Apparel companies are maintaining conservative inventory operations, which leaves room for order volumes to shift in a short period.

Leverage and volatile cash flow

Operating cash flow moved widely, from KRW 56.5 billion in 2022 and KRW 49 billion in 2023 down to KRW 16.2 billion in 2024 before recovering to KRW 52 billion in 2025.

Risk factors

Customer concentration and tariffs

Because major customers are North American apparel brands, tariffs and changes in customer supply-chain policy could shift order volumes. If customers keep inventories conservative, orders may fall and revenue could be affected.

Some views, however, hold that the possibility of a revenue decline is limited given the low inventory share in the fashion industry.

Financial leverage

Total liabilities of KRW 481.8 billion at year-end 2025 exceed total equity of KRW 284.9 billion, so interest rate changes and debt maturity management matter. Operating cash flow fell to KRW 16.2 billion in 2024, showing how the debt burden can weigh on cash generation. Although the debt ratio improved, the absolute size of debt remains large.

Quarterly earnings volatility

Profit attributable to owners of the parent moved sharply, from KRW 491 million in the fourth quarter of 2025 to KRW 12.5 billion in the first quarter of 2026. In some quarters operating profit and net profit moved in different directions, so non-operating or one-off items may have contributed.

Overall view

TP recovered from the 2023 operating profit low of KRW 40.6 billion to KRW 62 billion in 2025, and its debt ratio fell from 258.2% in 2022 to 169.1% in 2025. Profit attributable to owners of the parent reached KRW 38.2 billion in 2025, the largest year in the period.

Strengths include the annual earnings recovery, improved financial structure, and shareholder returns through share cancellation and trust purchases.

Weaknesses include concentration in North American customers and tariff policy, financial leverage with total liabilities above total equity, and wide swings in quarterly profit. The two sets of factors carry similar weight, so it is difficult to reach a one-sided conclusion.

The next checkpoint is the full-year operating margin and operating cash flow in the 2026 annual report, which has a statutory filing deadline at the end of March 2027.
