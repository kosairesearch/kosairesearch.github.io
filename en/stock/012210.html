02

Business structure

Sammi Metal Products is a metal die-forging specialist founded in 1977 that uses large hammer-forging lines to make core parts for automotive, construction machinery, shipbuilding and defense applications.

Its main products are high-strength large forged parts such as front axles for commercial vehicles and spindles and bosses for construction machinery, and the company says it is the exclusive domestic supplier of large forged products.

According to Hana Securities' report dated November 10, 2026, the first-half 2026 sales mix was automotive parts 45%, construction-machinery parts 26%, marine engine parts 15% and defense parts 4%.

Automotive customers include Hyundai Motor, while construction-machinery customers include Caterpillar and other global equipment makers, and marine engine parts are connecting rods for medium-speed engines supplied to two engine makers. Defense supply to Hanwha Aerospace has been reported.

The core new business is nuclear turbine blades, and the company has said it is the only domestic maker mass-producing them. It signed a large nuclear turbine blade supply contract with Doosan Enerbility on December 29, 2025 and a KRW 10.8 billion blade contract with customer D on March 31, 2026.

In gas turbines, it signed a supply contract with the U.S. gas-turbine solutions company PSM in the fourth quarter of 2025. Kumkang group acquired the company in September 2022, and it listed on KOSDAQ through a merger with IBKS No. 22 SPAC on December 29, 2025.

04

Earnings analysis

On a disclosed basis, revenue rose from KRW 70.8 billion in 2022 to KRW 76.8 billion in 2023, the largest year in the period, then fell 0.5% to KRW 76.4 billion in 2024 and 3.5% to KRW 73.8 billion in 2025, declining for two consecutive years from 2024 to 2025. Profitability improved while sales contracted.

The operating result swung from an operating loss of KRW 1.49 billion in 2022 to an operating profit of KRW 5.96 billion in 2023, and the operating margin ran at -2.1% in 2022, 7.8% in 2023, 5.8% in 2024 and 7.9% in 2025.

Operating profit fell 25.3% to KRW 4.45 billion in 2024 and then rose 30.5% to KRW 5.81 billion in 2025, which the company attributed to cost reduction and better production efficiency.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders jumped to KRW 3.46 billion in 2024, up 268.6%, then fell 51.2% to KRW 1.69 billion in 2025. The company cited a one-off merger cost of KRW 2.92 billion booked as non-operating expense during the SPAC merger.

The debt ratio declined from 195.1% in 2022 to 103.6% in 2025, and press reports linked part of the improvement to a June 2024 land revaluation that raised land valuation by KRW 13.7 billion. Operating cash flow was KRW 5.36 billion positive in 2024 but -KRW 1.79 billion in 2025, despite the higher operating profit.

On a quarterly basis, revenue in the fourth quarter of 2025 was KRW 16 billion, down 8.8% from the prior quarter, and the company swung to a net loss attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 869 million.

Revenue recovered to KRW 22.3 billion in the second quarter of 2026, up 21.9% quarter on quarter, with operating profit of KRW 2.61 billion, up 62.7%, and it was the largest quarterly revenue among the last five quarters.

Third-quarter 2026 revenue of KRW 19.1 billion fell 14.1% quarter on quarter but rose 8.9% year on year. Third-quarter operating profit of KRW 901 million fell 65.4% quarter on quarter and 2.6% year on year, while net income attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 1.55 billion rose 138.0% year on year.

That divergence suggests non-operating items contributed meaningfully. Net income attributable to controlling shareholders rose for two consecutive quarters after the first-quarter 2026 low, from the second quarter through the third quarter.

The sum of net income attributable to controlling shareholders over the last four quarters, from the fourth quarter of 2025 through the third quarter of 2026, was KRW 3.21 billion.

05

Industry analysis

Demand for the company comes from established segments such as commercial vehicles and construction machinery and from newer segments such as marine engines, nuclear power, defense and data-center power equipment.

A January 2026 press report said large hammer-forging lines require heavy upfront investment and high technical barriers, which makes entry by new competitors difficult, and it described the company as operating one of the largest hammer-forging facilities in Korea.

In nuclear power, an NH Investment & Securities analyst said in a late January 2026 report that the maintenance cycle of major domestic reactors is creating demand for replacement turbine blades.

In marine engines, the company focuses on connecting rods for medium-speed diesel engines, and Hana Securities has highlighted its push into eco-friendly marine engines and gas-turbine and nuclear applications.

In data centers, demand for large standby generator engine parts is rising, and the company has reportedly supplied crankshafts for large generator engines mainly to North America. Automotive parts still account for the largest share, so the business remains exposed to commercial-vehicle production cycles.

Within the metals sector, the company differs from steel producers because its focus is die-forged components. The company also stresses a one-stop process covering in-house die design, forging, heat treatment and machining.

06

Outlook

Hana Securities, in a report dated November 10, 2026, estimated 2026 revenue at KRW 81.1 billion, up 10.0% year on year, and operating profit at KRW 8.6 billion, a 10.6% operating margin, and projected 2027 revenue above KRW 100 billion. These are broker estimates, not company guidance.

In 2025 reports the company cited a 2025 revenue target of KRW 80 billion and a medium-term target of KRW 100 billion, and 2025 revenue of KRW 73.8 billion came in below the annual target. Hana Securities said volume production under both the Doosan Enerbility contract and the customer D contract is expected from 2027.

For marine engine connecting rods, capacity expansion is scheduled to be completed in the second quarter of 2027, and the effect is expected to show up in earnings from 2027.

Supply of HiMSEN-related products and finished-unit deliveries will expand, and new engine parts for data-center power infrastructure are being developed at the expansion plant.

On the funding side, the company decided on June 2, 2026 to issue a KRW 30 billion fourth private convertible bond with a 0% coupon, maturing on June 10, 2031, and a conversion price of KRW 13,043 per share.

The company said the proceeds will fund development and supply expansion in high-value products such as nuclear, medium-speed engines and AI data-center power infrastructure. The connecting-rod processing plant investment of KRW 8.99 billion has an investment period through the end of 2026.

08

Bull factors

Expansion into high-value nuclear and gas-turbine blades

The company signed a nuclear turbine blade contract with Doosan Enerbility in December 2025 and a KRW 10.8 billion contract with customer D on March 31, 2026. Hana Securities estimates that both contracts will begin volume supply in 2027, adding more than KRW 10 billion in sales growth.

The company has said it is the only domestic maker mass-producing nuclear turbine blades. If these new businesses reach sales, dependence on automotive parts could fall.

Operating profit recovery and a stronger balance sheet

Operating profit turned positive in 2023 at KRW 5.96 billion and rose to KRW 5.81 billion in 2025, while the operating margin improved from -2.1% in 2022 to 7.9% in 2025. The company credited cost reduction and better production efficiency.

Second-quarter 2026 operating profit of KRW 2.61 billion was up 62.7% from the prior quarter. The debt ratio also fell from 195.1% in 2022 to 103.6% in 2025, lowering financial strain.

Connecting-rod expansion and engine-part demand

Orders for connecting rods for small and mid-sized engines are solid, and capacity expansion is scheduled to finish in the second quarter of 2027. Hana Securities said development of new L35/44G engine parts for data-center power infrastructure is progressing well.

Hana Securities estimates 2026 revenue of KRW 81.1 billion and operating profit of KRW 8.6 billion, with 2027 revenue above KRW 100 billion. If these broker estimates materialize, both sales growth and profit growth would appear together.

09

Bear factors

Stagnant revenue and large quarterly swings

Revenue peaked at KRW 76.8 billion in 2023 and then fell for two consecutive years in 2024 and 2025. Third-quarter 2026 revenue of KRW 19.1 billion rose 8.9% year on year but fell 14.1% from the prior quarter.

Operating profit of KRW 901 million in the same quarter dropped 65.4% from the prior quarter and 2.6% year on year. Because quarterly profit swings are large, judging the trend requires several quarters of data.

One-off costs and a gap with cash flow

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders fell 51.2% to KRW 1.69 billion in 2025, and the company said a one-off SPAC merger cost of KRW 2.92 billion was booked as non-operating expense. Operating cash flow swung from KRW 5.36 billion in 2024 to -KRW 1.79 billion in 2025.

In a year when operating profit rose, cash flow was negative, so converting profit into cash remains an open issue.

Dependence on automotive and a high debt load

According to Hana Securities, automotive parts were 45% of first-half 2026 sales, the largest share, followed by construction-machinery parts at 26%. Those two segments make up the largest part of sales, so the company is exposed to commercial-vehicle and construction-equipment demand.

Total liabilities of KRW 96.1 billion exceeded equity of KRW 92.7 billion at the end of 2025, leaving a debt ratio of 103.6%. The June 2026 convertible bond also adds potential dilution if it is converted.

10

Risk factors

Customer and cycle concentration

Automotive and construction-machinery parts make up most of the sales, so commercial-vehicle and construction-equipment production cycles strongly influence results. Key customers are concentrated among vehicle makers and global equipment makers, so order changes from a single customer pass through to sales. The third-quarter 2026 sales decline from the prior quarter is one example of this quarterly demand variation.

New-business timing and capacity delays

Volume production of nuclear and gas-turbine blades is expected from 2027, and that timing depends on contract execution and qualification procedures. Capacity expansion for connecting rods is scheduled to finish in the second quarter of 2027.

The company has said the investment amount may change with permitting, construction progress and the business environment. If new-business sales are delayed, capacity costs could be recognized before the related revenue.

Financial leverage and convertible bond dilution

Total liabilities of KRW 96.1 billion exceeded equity of KRW 92.7 billion at the end of 2025, so financial leverage remains high. The KRW 30 billion convertible bond issued in June 2026 carries a 0% coupon, but its conversion price of KRW 13,043 has no refixing clause, so conversion would dilute shareholders.

With operating cash flow negative in 2025, funding for facility investment depends in part on external financing.

12

Overall view

Sammi Metal Products has shown an improving earnings structure since returning to operating profit in 2023, with operating profit rising and the debt ratio falling. However, revenue declined for two consecutive years in 2024 and 2025, and 2025 net income was affected by a one-off merger cost.

Second-quarter 2026 revenue recovered to KRW 22.3 billion and operating profit to KRW 2.61 billion, but third-quarter operating profit fell year on year while net income attributable to controlling shareholders rose, so the two measures moved in different directions.

On the business side, broker estimates expect nuclear and gas-turbine blades and the connecting-rod expansion to show up in results from 2027. On the other side, automotive parts remain the largest share, liabilities exceed equity, and the convertible bond adds dilution risk.

The market capitalization is above book equity, so the pace of earnings growth and the timing of new-business sales are the key variables for interpreting the valuation. The next checkpoints are the operating margin in the 2026 annual report and whether volume production of new businesses begins in 2027.
