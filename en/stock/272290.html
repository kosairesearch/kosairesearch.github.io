02

Business structure

INNOX Advanced Materials was spun off from INNOX in 2017 and develops, manufactures and sells IT materials, including OLED materials for displays, circuit materials and semiconductor packaging materials.

As of the third quarter of 2025, display OLED materials accounted for 68.3% of revenue, circuit materials for 23.5%, and semiconductor packaging materials for 8.2%. The OLED line supplies TV panels and mobile OLED panels, and films for foldable devices have been a driver of growth within it.

Circuit materials serve flexible printed circuit boards (FPCB) used in smartphones, tablets and laptops, and the company describes itself as keeping the number one share in this field.

Semiconductor packaging materials centre on film-type products that bond chips to substrates, and their revenue share rose to 9.9% in the first quarter of 2026, according to a June 2026 press report. That share had climbed for five consecutive quarters from 6.6% in the first quarter of 2025.

Samsung Electronics is the identified customer for this segment, and its LPDDR DAF product entered mass production in March 2026.

Through its lithium subsidiary, the group also runs a secondary-battery materials business, and a June 2026 report described a first lithium hydroxide supply achieved together with LX International.

04

Earnings analysis

On a reported basis, annual revenue went from KRW 489.4 billion in 2022 to KRW 387 billion in 2023, a 20.9% decline, then rose 9.3% to KRW 422.8 billion in 2024 and 4.0% to KRW 439.6 billion in 2025, a second straight year of growth. Revenue was largest in 2022 and smallest in 2023.

Operating profit fell from KRW 97.1 billion in 2022 to KRW 42.2 billion in 2023, a 56.6% drop and the low point of the period, then recovered 105.9% to KRW 86.8 billion in 2024 before slipping 5.6% to KRW 81.9 billion in 2025.

The operating margin was 19.8% in 2022, 10.9% in 2023, 20.5% in 2024 and 18.6% in 2025, recovering in 2024 after the 2023 dip and easing again in 2025.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 85.4 billion in 2022, KRW 32.8 billion in 2023 (down 61.6%), KRW 70.7 billion in 2024 (up 115.8%) and KRW 58 billion in 2025 (down 17.9%).

Net income including non-controlling interests over the same years was KRW 70.1 billion in 2024 and KRW 55.9 billion in 2025, which differs from the attributable figure. A company briefing for the third quarter of 2025 cited a diversified product mix and lithium-related losses as factors in operating profit.

Quarterly revenue went from KRW 112.7 billion in Q3 2025 to KRW 102.4 billion in Q4 2025 (down 9.2% quarter on quarter) and KRW 95.1 billion in Q1 2026 (down 7.1%), then rebounded to KRW 108.7 billion in Q2 2026 (up 14.2%) before reaching KRW 103.6 billion in Q3 2026 (down 4.6% quarter on quarter and 8.0% year on year).

Quarterly operating profit was KRW 20.6 billion in Q3 2025, KRW 12.7 billion in Q4 2025 (down 38.5%) and KRW 16.9 billion in Q1 2026 (up 33.2%). Q1 2026 was the quarterly peak, and operating profit then fell for two consecutive quarters to KRW 10.4 billion in Q2 2026 and KRW 7.31 billion in Q3 2026.

DB Securities pointed to lower Q1 2026 operating profit year on year, citing weaker OLED material sales on soft TV demand, a circuit-board revenue drop after a product was not adopted, and lithium subsidiary amortization.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 17.7 billion in Q3 2025, KRW 7.43 billion in Q4 2025, KRW 15.6 billion in Q1 2026 and KRW 19.7 billion in Q2 2026, before a controlling-shareholder net loss of KRW 14 billion in Q3 2026.

Over the last four quarters, controlling-shareholder net income totalled KRW 28.7 billion. A third-quarter 2026 press report suggested that non-operating items such as financial gains or losses and taxes may have affected quarterly net results.

For Q4 2025, a securities firm's review pointed to one-off costs and inventory adjustments, per a February 2026 report.

05

Industry analysis

OLED materials are sensitive to the demand cycle for TV panels. In a June 2026 report, DB Securities cited weak TV demand as the reason for lower OLED material sales. On the mobile side, new businesses such as foldable-device films were cited by brokers in 2025 as support for earnings.

Demand in semiconductor packaging is tied to rising FCBGA demand, and DB Securities said tighter supply of ABF, a build-up layer raw material, is raising the need for substitute materials.

Against this backdrop, the company is developing low-dielectric films for domestic substrate makers, building on its bonding-sheet and build-up film references. Circuit materials saw lower circuit-board revenue, but brokers expected new earnings momentum from semiconductor materials and circuit boards.

On the cycle, OLED is in a phase of weak TV demand, while semiconductor materials are in an early mass-production phase with customer expansion underway.

On competitive position, the company has stressed its number one standing in FPCB materials, yet its semiconductor packaging share stayed below 10% in the first quarter of 2026.

Compared with listed electronics component and substrate-material peers, its heavy reliance on display materials means display-cycle changes flow directly into its results. In lithium, supply progress has been reported, but amortization in that unit was cited as a drag on Q1 2026 operating profit.

Dated new-business milestones

If these milestones are confirmed, the timing of new-business contribution to earnings can be tracked, which is the core of the bull argument.

Overall view

INNOX Advanced Materials still derives 68.3% of revenue from display OLED materials as of the third quarter of 2025, while its semiconductor packaging share rose to 9.9% in the first quarter of 2026, broadening the business mix.

Operating profit fell from KRW 97.1 billion in 2022 to KRW 42.2 billion in 2023, recovered to KRW 86.8 billion in 2024, and stood at KRW 81.9 billion in 2025, with controlling-shareholder net income of KRW 58 billion.

In 2026, quarterly operating profit peaked in Q1 at KRW 16.9 billion and then declined for two consecutive quarters, and Q3 swung to a controlling-shareholder net loss of KRW 14 billion.

On the balance sheet, the debt ratio rose from 22.0% in 2022 to 108.3% in 2025, and subsidiary earnings and borrowing burdens are being discussed together. On the bull side are expanding semiconductor packaging mass production, the low-dielectric film timeline and the 2028 breakeven target.

On the bear side are weak TV demand, falling quarterly profit and a larger leverage burden. A price-to-book ratio below 1x is a factual comparison against book equity and does not by itself answer whether earnings will recover.

The next checkpoints are the Q4 2026 preliminary results, the full-year operating margin in the 2026 annual business report, and the start of first mass production of low-dielectric films.
