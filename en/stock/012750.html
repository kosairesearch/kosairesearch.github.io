02

Business structure

S-1 is a leading security services company in Korea, with Japan's SECOM as its largest shareholder, and one aggregator data set indicates a high share of revenue from related parties. The company reports two segments, Security and Infrastructure.

In 2025, Security generated KRW 1,385.4 billion, or 47.96% of consolidated revenue, while Infrastructure generated KRW 1,489.3 billion, or 51.53%.

Security covers physical security linked to dispatch personnel and control centers, along with digital and mobile security services, and has long been the core revenue source. Infrastructure comprises real estate services (building management), integrated security, and security system integration (SI).

Building management revenue was KRW 827.4 billion in 2025, which a media tally places at roughly 28% to 31% of total revenue. Security SI is project-driven, so quarterly revenue moves with construction timing, and one aggregator data set shows a 7.2% decline in 2025.

Security services run on long-term contracts that generate recurring cash, and most revenue is recognized over time, which tends to limit volatility. The competitive base includes more than 100 dispatch facilities nationwide and integrated control centers in Suwon and Daegu.

The company was ranked first in the 2026 K-BPI security service category, and it is widening AI-based products such as SVMS video analytics, the BlueScan smart building service, the Ansim24 unmanned store security solution, and an AI door cam developed with Samsung Electronics.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual revenue rose from KRW 2.47 trillion in 2022 to KRW 2.62 trillion in 2023 (up 6.2% year on year), KRW 2.8 trillion in 2024 (up 7.0%), and KRW 2.89 trillion in 2025 (up 3.0%), marking three straight years of growth from 2023 through 2025, with 2025 the largest year in the 2022–2025 span.

Operating profit went from KRW 204.1 billion in 2022 to KRW 212.6 billion in 2023 (up 4.1%) and KRW 209.2 billion in 2024 (down 1.6%), then reached KRW 234.6 billion in 2025 (up 12.1%), the largest year in the span.

The operating margin moved from 8.3% in 2022 and 8.1% in 2023 to 7.5% in 2024 before recovering to 8.1% in 2025. Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders peaked at KRW 189.5 billion in 2023 (up 25.5%), fell to KRW 176.7 billion in 2024 (down 6.7%), and reached KRW 178.6 billion in 2025 (up 1.0%).

One aggregator attributes the gap between 2025 operating profit growth and net profit growth to higher income tax expense, and net profit including non-controlling interests was also KRW 178.6 billion in 2025.

The debt-to-equity ratio, measured as total liabilities divided by total equity, was 36.3% in 2022, 36.4% in 2023, 39.9% in 2024, and 38.5% in 2025, with total equity of KRW 1.76 trillion at the end of 2025.

Operating cash flow was KRW 277.4 billion in 2022, then KRW 368.4 billion, KRW 376.6 billion, and KRW 368.6 billion in 2025, staying above KRW 360 billion since 2023.

In quarterly terms, fourth-quarter 2025 revenue rose 12.2% from the prior quarter to KRW 783.9 billion, while operating profit fell 10.1% to KRW 55.8 billion.

First-quarter 2026 operating profit was KRW 20.7 billion, down 62.8% from the prior quarter, which the company said reflected a one-off retirement benefit charge tied to a wage lawsuit.

Operating profit then rose to KRW 64.4 billion in the second quarter of 2026 and KRW 65.4 billion in the third, two consecutive quarterly increases, while third-quarter revenue of KRW 716.7 billion grew 2.6% year on year.

Controlling-shareholder net profit for the last four quarters, from the fourth quarter of 2025 through the third quarter of 2026, totaled KRW 154 billion.

05

Industry analysis

The company argues that the security market is shifting from response after incidents toward AI-based detection and prediction. In a January 2026 survey of 27,207 customers, 83% of respondents said AI-based real-time risk detection is needed, up from 58% in the prior-year survey.

The number of unmanned stores was estimated at more than 10,000 in 2025, up from about 2,250 in 2020, and crimes targeting them rose from 3,514 cases in 2021 to 10,847 in 2023. Unmanned store operators cited detection only after an incident and the burden of constant monitoring as their main difficulties.

Industrial accident deaths rose 8.4% year on year to 1,120 as of June 2025, according to Ministry of Employment and Labor statistics that the company cited. In security SI, project timing depends on construction and facility investment cycles, so Infrastructure revenue can swing from quarter to quarter.

The Security segment, by contrast, rests on long-term service contracts, which tends to dampen exposure to those cycles. One aggregator's cost analysis says labor is the main cost, so wage conditions and staffing supply affect margins.

Competitively, the company points to its first-place ranking in the 2026 K-BPI security service category, its dispatch network, and a solution set that combines physical security with AI video security.

Outlook

In its 2026 value-up plan disclosed on March 19, 2026, the company said it would expand into AI- and robot-based products and services while keeping the 2026 dividend payout ratio at 50% to 60%.

In infrastructure, it plans to widen building management into integrated facility management (IFM) that adds business support, and to deepen design and construction capability in security SI. Over the medium term, it plans M&A and strategic fund investments to secure future technologies.

The payout ratio for the prior year was 60.6%, and the prior-year dividend amount rose 18.5% from the year before. The tender offer, decided on October 14, 2026 for about KRW 450 billion, was undersubscribed, and the company acquired 1,304,797 shares for KRW 129.17 billion, with settlement on November 5, 2026.

Treasury shares reached 14.39% of issued shares after the purchase. The company said funds not used in the tender will go toward shareholder returns, with details to be set by a future board resolution.

On the first-quarter one-off, the company said core business fundamentals were unaffected, citing steady security growth and new building-management contracts.

The next checkpoints are the fourth-quarter 2026 provisional results and the 2026 annual report, including how building management and security SI revenue trend.

Bull factors

Recurring revenue base

The Security segment runs on long-term contracts that bring in cash each month, and most of its revenue is recognized over time. That structure has been associated with lower earnings volatility through economic cycles. Revenue rose for three straight years from 2023 through 2025, which shows the base has held up. The operating margin recovered to 8.1% in 2025 from 7.5% in 2024.

Building management as a growth pillar

Building management revenue rose from KRW 703.5 billion in 2023 to KRW 771.7 billion in 2024 and KRW 827.4 billion in 2025, according to a media tally. Quarterly revenue of KRW 220.7 billion in the second quarter of 2026 and KRW 222.9 billion in the third kept the segment above KRW 220 billion for two quarters.

The company plans to widen the business into integrated facility management that adds business support services.

Payout policy and treasury share purchase

The company disclosed on March 19, 2026 that it plans to keep the 2026 dividend payout ratio at 50% to 60%. Through a tender offer, the company acquired 1,304,797 shares for KRW 129.17 billion, lifting treasury shares to 14.39% of issued shares. Funds not used in the tender are to be directed to shareholder returns.

Bear factors

Q1 2026 profit trough and lawsuit costs

First-quarter 2026 operating profit was KRW 20.7 billion, down 62.4% from a year earlier, and controlling-shareholder net profit was KRW 21.5 billion, down 40.4% from the prior quarter. The company attributed the drop to retirement benefit costs tied to a wage lawsuit, recognized as a one-off. Whether further costs follow from the case's outcome remains a variable for coming quarters.

Cumulative profit decline and SI volatility

Cumulative operating profit for the first three quarters of 2026 was KRW 150.5 billion, down 15.78% from a year earlier. Security SI is project-driven, so quarterly revenue swings with construction timing, and one aggregator data set shows Security SI revenue fell 7.2% in 2025. Whether that revenue recovers after the fourth quarter of 2026 is the next point to watch.

Slowing net profit growth

Operating profit rose 12.1% in 2025, but net profit attributable to controlling shareholders grew only 1.0%. One aggregator attributes the gap to higher income tax expense.

Controlling-shareholder net profit was KRW 176.7 billion in 2024, below KRW 189.5 billion in 2023, and in 2025 it has not returned to the 2023 level. Whether operating gains carry through to net profit is the open question.

Risk factors

One-off costs and litigation

Retirement benefit costs tied to a wage lawsuit were recognized as a one-off in the first quarter of 2026. The company says business fundamentals are unaffected, but further costs depending on the case's outcome remain possible.

If similar charges recur, quarterly operating margin could come under pressure, as the first-quarter 2026 trough shows.

Parent and group dependence; treasury share funding

SECOM, a Japanese company, is the largest shareholder, and one aggregator data set puts related-party sales at roughly 35% of consolidated revenue. A high share of intragroup dealings makes contract terms and renewals more consequential for results.

The tender offer was undersubscribed, and the use of the unspent funds will be set by a future board resolution.

Project-based SI and labor-intensive costs

Security SI and new building-management contracts depend on construction and facility investment cycles. Security services are staffed and dispatch-based, so wage increases and labor supply feed directly into costs.

One aggregator reports a slight improvement in the cost ratio in 2025, but wage pressure could reverse that gain.

Overall view

S-1 grew revenue for three straight years from 2023 through 2025, and 2025 operating profit of KRW 234.6 billion was the largest year in the 2022–2025 span. The business mixes a long-contract Security segment with an Infrastructure segment led by building management, at nearly equal revenue shares.

In the first quarter of 2026, retirement benefit costs tied to a wage lawsuit reduced operating profit to KRW 20.7 billion, but profit rose for two consecutive quarters afterward to KRW 64.4 billion and KRW 65.4 billion. Controlling-shareholder net profit for the last four quarters totaled KRW 154 billion.

On shareholder returns, a 50% to 60% payout plan and a 2026 treasury share tender offer both moved forward.

Strengths include the recurring revenue base, building management growth, and expanded shareholder returns, while weaknesses include the possible recurrence of one-off costs, project volatility in security SI, and controlling-shareholder net profit growth of just 1.0% in 2025.

The next checkpoints are the fourth-quarter 2026 provisional results and the 2026 annual report, with operating margin and building management and security SI revenue trends at the center.
