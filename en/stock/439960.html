Report overview

A Rehab-Robot Story Shaken by a Police Probe

Cosmo Robotics has a business base built on FDA and CE clearances and a pediatric rehabilitation lineup, but a police probe into alleged 2025 revenue inflation and weak first-half results are testing both credibility and how the market values the company.

02

Business structure

Cosmo Robotics was founded in 2016 by CEO Oh Joo-young under the name Exo Atlet Asia and develops and manufactures wearable robots for rehabilitation and assistance. Its core product is an exoskeleton robot for rehabilitation and walking assistance of patients with lower-limb paralysis.

According to press reports around listing, adult products made up about 51% of revenue and pediatric and adolescent products about 44%. The lineup spans medical and daily-use robots for infants, adolescents, adults and seniors, as well as the COSaver industrial robot.

The Bambini Kids ground-walking robot for infants is distinctive for its eight motors, including an ankle motor. The company said it passed the FDA 510(k) review, which requires clinical and safety data, whereas some domestic peers remain at a notification stage.

Overseas, it runs subsidiaries in five countries, including the US, Japan, Russia, Europe and China, and works with local partners in 13 countries. However, the US entity accounted for only about 10% of total 2025 revenue.

At listing, the company said only six companies worldwide, including Ekso Bionics, hold both FDA and CE clearance. In Korea, Angel Robotics was cited as the market-share leader in wearable robots.

Listed robot companies such as Doosan Robotics, Rainbow Robotics and Robotis focus mainly on collaborative, service and industrial robots, so their business areas differ from medical rehabilitation. Before listing, the largest shareholder was Cosmo & Company (21.04%), and founder Oh and related parties held 11.82%.

03

Recent trends

Based on reported figures, cumulative first-half 2026 revenue was KRW 1.24 billion, down 63.4% from a year earlier. The operating loss was KRW 3.32 billion, with losses continuing and widening. The net loss was KRW 7.22 billion, still a loss but smaller than a year earlier.

When preparing the listing, the company projected 2026 full-year revenue of KRW 12.9 billion, an operating loss of KRW 5.9 billion and a net loss of KRW 5.2 billion. First-half revenue of KRW 1.24 billion is below 10% of that full-year projection.

On a consolidated basis, 2025 revenue was KRW 8.863 billion and the operating loss was KRW 8.151 billion. Police suspect that a roughly KRW 400 million domestic sale in March 2025 and a roughly KRW 2 billion sale by the European subsidiary in December 2025 were not genuine transactions.

In a shareholder notice on September 30, 2026, the company said the revenue in question was legitimately generated and that it would cooperate with the authorities. The share price has been highly volatile. The stock listed on May 11, 2026 at an IPO price of KRW 6,000 and closed at KRW 24,000 on its first day.

The 52-week high is KRW 68,500. The stock closed at KRW 11,390 on September 28, then fell 30.3% to KRW 7,940 on September 29, when the search was reported. On October 15 it dropped to KRW 5,150 intraday, a 52-week low.

As of October 27, 2026, the price is KRW 5,140, market capitalization is KRW 169.1 billion and trading value is KRW 2.6 billion.

04

Outlook

The first variable for the outlook is the outcome of the investigation. According to press reports, further action by the Korea Exchange is possible depending on the results. The company said it would thoroughly review its financial and accounting management systems.

On the business side, certification and home-use entry are central. The company said it plans to complete US FDA clearance for the infant model BAM-K by the second quarter of 2027. It is also pursuing FDA and CE clearance for the adolescent model BAM-T during 2026.

The company itself acknowledged that delays in these clearances could disrupt its revenue targets. In Korea, a home rental service for the Kinder series of children's walking-assist robots has operated since August 2026.

The company set targets of an operating profit of KRW 6.114 billion in 2027, marking a return to profit, and revenue of KRW 37.313 billion and operating profit of KRW 9.367 billion in 2028.

Because first-half 2026 results fell well short of the projections made at listing, those targets are likely to depend on the timing of certifications and the pace of sales growth.

Room for improvement in business metrics such as operating margin depends on how much localization of core components lowers the cost ratio.

05

Bull factors

Certification base and product lineup

Holding both US FDA and European CE certification among Korean wearable-robot companies creates a barrier to entry for overseas sales. The infant model Bambini Kids won an innovation award at CES 2026. A clinical trial has also begun at Asan Medical Center in Seoul. A lineup spanning adults to infants provides a basis for cross-selling across products.

Domestic insurance coverage and home rental

As the government expands national health insurance coverage for robotic gait rehabilitation for patients aged 18 and under, the cost burden for patients using the adolescent Bambini Teens Pro is reported to ease. A rental service extending hospital-centered gait training to homes has also operated since August 2026. It offers a path to widen revenue from hospital sales into service-type income.

Growth room in the US home-use market

CEO Oh explained that once FDA home-use clearance is obtained, the US health insurance authority can provide buyers with subsidies of up to 80%. Because the US entity's share of revenue was low in 2025, clearance could change the revenue mix. The company also said it is improving its cost of sales ratio through localization of core components.

06

Bear factors

Probe into alleged revenue inflation

The roughly KRW 3.5 billion under review equals about 39.5% of 2025 consolidated revenue of KRW 8.863 billion. Police are also investigating whether the inflated financial statements were presented to investors during the IPO.

The allegation has not been established, but if confirmed it could affect trust in the financial statements and trigger exchange measures.

Weak first-half results

Based on reported figures, first-half 2026 revenue fell 63.4% to KRW 1.24 billion and the operating loss widened to KRW 3.32 billion. The net loss reached KRW 7.22 billion. The gap from the full-year projection made at listing is large, so the pace of second-half revenue recovery is a key issue.

Cash collection and working-capital strain

According to pre-listing press reports, the receivables collection period lengthened from about ten days in the past to more than 130 days. Operating cash flow has been negative for four consecutive years since 2022.

The company itself said additional external funding could become unavoidable if certifications are delayed or demand weakens.

07

Risk factors

Legal and regulatory

Depending on the outcome of the investigation, the accuracy of the registration statement and financial information at listing could become an issue. Attention has also turned to due diligence during the IPO. A notice of designation as an investment-warning stock was disclosed on May 14, 2026. Exchange measures or litigation would depend on how the investigation proceeds.

Share supply

On May 13, 2026, conversion of the second convertible bond led to the issuance of 330,675 new shares, equal to 1.03% of previously outstanding shares. Part of the institutional allocation is subject to lock-ups of up to six months, so tradable supply may increase as they expire. Lock-up releases on shares held by financial investors could also affect supply and demand.

Macro and market

The share price has repeatedly swung sharply across recent trading days, making it sensitive to shifts in sentiment toward robot-themed stocks overall. A change in US medical insurance subsidy policy or a delay in clearance reviews would undermine the premise behind overseas revenue projections. Whether US pediatric home-use products sell as planned determines whether the earnings outlook is realized.

Overall view
