Histeel: Mixed Signals as Pipe Maker Tests a Fragile Turnaround

Histeel swung back to operating profit for two straight quarters in 2026, but the lingering effects of two consecutive years of losses in 2024-2025 and a sluggish construction market still test the durability of its earnings recovery.

02

Business structure

Histeel is a dedicated steel pipe manufacturer established in 2003 through a spin-off of the pipe business from Hanil Steel.

Its Dangjin plant operates a production system spanning ERW to SAW pipes, producing everything from thin-wall and small-diameter pipes to heavy-walled pipes up to 60 inches in diameter capable of transporting oil and gas.

Since 2022, its Haman plant in South Gyeongsang Province has been mass-producing the earthquake-resistant large rectangular steel pipe "Hi-Square" using proprietary technology, extending the business into construction and civil engineering structures.

Hi-Square builds on 1-Seam forming technology developed in 2017 for sizes up to 1,000mm square—a world first at the time—and the company obtained Japanese Ministry of Land, Infrastructure, Transport and Tourism certification in 2018, which it uses to target Japan's strict seismic-resistant construction market.

Histeel is also expanding the share of higher value-added products such as high-manganese steel pipes, targeting demand from subsea pipelines, offshore plants and high-rise building reinforcement. Its subsidiaries include the US sales entity HISTEEL PIPE&TUBE INC and an affiliate handling pipe heat treatment.

Ownership by the largest shareholder and related parties stood at 53.47% as of the last recorded change in December 2024, pointing to relatively stable governance.

Domestic competitors in the steel pipe industry include SeAH Steel, Nexteel, and other pipe makers, with Histeel seeking differentiation through its large-diameter rectangular pipe and cryogenic high-manganese pipe product lines.

Earnings analysis

Consolidated revenue fell for two straight years, from KRW 316.4 billion in 2022 to KRW 256.7 billion in 2023 (down 18.9% year-on-year) and KRW 246.3 billion in 2024 (down 4.0%), before rebounding to KRW 256.6 billion in 2025 (up 4.2%).

Over the same period, operating profit declined from KRW 16.9 billion in 2022 to KRW 3.39 billion in 2023 (down 79.9%) and KRW 912 million in 2024 (down 73.1%), before turning to an operating loss of KRW 3.32 billion in 2025.

The operating margin fell from 5.3% in 2022 to 1.3% in 2023 and 0.4% in 2024, before slipping into negative territory at -1.3% in 2025.

Controlling-interest net income went from KRW 11.5 billion in 2022 to KRW 1.31 billion in 2023 (down 88.6%), then turned to a net loss of KRW 1.1 billion in 2024, with the loss widening to KRW 4.58 billion in 2025. This marked two consecutive years of losses from 2024 through 2025.

On a quarterly basis, the company posted an operating loss of KRW 3.36 billion in Q3 2025 (a swing to loss from the prior quarter) and an operating loss of KRW 2.17 billion in Q4 2025 (loss continuing but narrowing), before turning to an operating profit of KRW 1.77 billion in Q1 2026, followed by an operating profit of KRW 3.23 billion in Q2 2026, up 83.0% quarter-on-quarter and 81.3% year-on-year.

Controlling-interest net income likewise turned positive at KRW 2.22 billion in Q1 2026 and rose 48.3% quarter-on-quarter to KRW 3.3 billion in Q2 2026, swinging from a net loss of KRW 546 million in the year-earlier quarter.

Q2 2026 revenue came in at KRW 81.3 billion, up 48.0% quarter-on-quarter and 38.1% year-on-year.

However, combined controlling-interest net income over the last four quarters (Q3 2025-Q2 2026) totaled only KRW 903 million, suggesting the 2026 first-half profit largely offset the heavy losses of the second half of 2025, while operating cash flow fell from KRW 19.8 billion in 2023 to KRW 3.67 billion in 2024 before turning negative at -KRW 3.21 billion in 2025, reflecting earnings volatility in cash generation as well.

Industry analysis

Domestic hot-rolled steel distribution prices bottomed out in the low KRW 800,000-per-ton range in early 2026, before gradually recovering as anti-dumping measures against Chinese and Japanese imports expanded the share of domestically produced steel.

In the second half, prices climbed to the low KRW 900,000-per-ton range on the back of stronger global steel prices, but have traded sideways without a clear further rebound since mid-September.

In the downstream construction sector, prolonged weakness has intensified competition for sales volume in domestically oriented products such as piping steel pipe, adding downward pressure on prices.

By contrast, exports to the United States grew despite high tariffs, driven by expanding energy and infrastructure investment; steel pipe exports surged 40.53% year-on-year in September 2026 and continued to rise 30.8% in the first 20 days of that month.

In September 2026, the US Department of Commerce finalized a weighted-average dumping margin of 0.00% for Histeel in its annual administrative review of heavy-walled rectangular welded carbon steel pipe, relieving the cash-deposit burden on that specific product.

However, it is noted that this ruling applies only to that specific product category, and other US steel tariffs or trade measures may still apply separately.

Domestic competitors in the steel pipe industry include SeAH Steel, Nexteel, and other pipe makers, with industry observers noting that order flow tends to concentrate among companies with clear new growth drivers such as offshore wind and energy infrastructure.

Outlook

In May 2026, Histeel signed a supply contract worth roughly USD 25 million (about KRW 37.6 billion) for piping steel pipe through its US subsidiary, HISTEEL PIPE&TUBE INC, covering the period from May 20 to December 31, 2026.

The contract size equals about 14.65% of recent annual revenue, and is expected to help strengthen the company's overseas revenue base through its US subsidiary.

Possessing high-manganese steel pipe technology that withstands cryogenic conditions, Histeel has stated it could potentially participate in an Alaska LNG development project should one move forward.

The Haman plant's earthquake-resistant large rectangular steel pipe, Hi-Square, is designed to meet Japan's strict seismic design standards, and the company is pursuing expanded deliveries into the Japanese construction market, with management stating it is working to expand business with major Japanese construction firms.

Domestically, the pace of construction-sector recovery remains a swing factor, leaving the timing of a full-scale pickup in demand for civil engineering and construction-use rectangular pipe uncertain.

For hot-rolled steel prices, a key variable going into the fourth quarter is whether supply reductions from maintenance at major production facilities translate into a distribution price rebound that would ease input cost pressure.

Having followed two consecutive years of losses through 2025 with back-to-back profitable quarters in the first half of 2026, the next checkpoint is whether the recovery carries through the second half to deliver a full-year swing back to profit.

Bull factors

US Anti-Dumping Risk Eased

In September 2026, the US Department of Commerce finalized a zero-percent dumping margin for Histeel's heavy-walled rectangular welded carbon steel pipe, removing the cash-deposit burden on that product.

Even amid high tariffs, US-bound steel pipe exports have risen sharply year-on-year, suggesting a widening export channel. That said, the ruling is limited to a specific product category and applies separately from other US steel tariff measures.

First-Half 2026 Earnings Recovery

Both operating profit and controlling-interest net income swung back to the black in Q1 and Q2 of 2026, with Q2 revenue reaching KRW 81.3 billion, up sharply both quarter-on-quarter and year-on-year. This confirms a shift away from the heavy operating losses seen in the third and fourth quarters of 2025.

Specialized Product Portfolio

Through higher value-added product lines such as the Hi-Square earthquake-resistant rectangular pipe from the Haman plant and cryogenic-grade high-manganese steel pipe, the company is expanding into diverse downstream markets including construction, energy, and LNG. Its Japanese seismic certification serves as a foothold for pursuing overseas construction markets.

Bear factors

Widening Earnings Volatility

The company posted operating losses for two consecutive quarters in Q3 and Q4 2025, and also recorded two consecutive years of annual losses from 2024 through 2025.

Despite the first-half 2026 profit, combined controlling-interest net income over the last four quarters totaled just KRW 903 million, indicating the earnings base is not yet firmly established.

Construction Cycle and Raw Material Price Swings

Prolonged weakness in domestic construction has intensified price competition for products such as piping steel pipe sold domestically. Hot-rolled steel prices, the key raw material, have fluctuated between the KRW 800,000 and 900,000-per-ton range in 2026, keeping input cost pressure a key driver of margins.

Deteriorating Cash Flow

Operating cash flow fell from KRW 19.8 billion in 2023 to KRW 3.67 billion in 2024, then turned negative at -KRW 3.21 billion in 2025. Earnings volatility has thus also shown up in cash generation, potentially straining financial flexibility.

Risk factors

Trade and Tariff Risk

US product-specific tariff policies, including Section 232 steel tariffs, can change frequently, and the zero-percent anti-dumping ruling does not replace other applicable tariff measures. Trade policy shifts in other major export markets, such as the EU, also remain a potential variable.

Raw Material Prices and Foreign Exchange

Fluctuations in raw material prices such as hot-rolled steel and currency movements directly affect export profitability. The supply contract with the US subsidiary is also denominated in dollars, meaning exchange rate movements could affect profit and loss.

Downstream Demand Cycle

If the recovery of domestic construction and civil engineering activity is delayed, domestic sales of structural and piping steel pipe could slow. Demand from other downstream sectors such as shipbuilding and energy is also tied to global economic conditions, which could add to earnings volatility.

Overall view

Histeel's earnings base steadily weakened after 2022, leading to two consecutive years of losses in 2024-2025, but the company turned to operating and controlling-interest net profit in both Q1 and Q2 of 2026.

Q2 2026 revenue reached KRW 81.3 billion, up both quarter-on-quarter and year-on-year, showing a recovery in sales scale as well.

However, combined controlling-interest net income over the last four quarters totaled only KRW 903 million, meaning the first-half 2026 profit largely offset the losses of the second half of 2025.

A zero-percent anti-dumping margin ruling from the US Department of Commerce in September 2026 eased some uncertainty around the US export channel, and a supply contract through its US subsidiary is also underway.

On the other hand, a weak domestic construction market, volatility in hot-rolled steel prices, and a swing to negative operating cash flow remain factors testing the sustainability of the earnings recovery.

The next quarterly report, execution of the US-bound supply contract, and raw material price trends will likely be key indicators for gauging the direction of future results.
