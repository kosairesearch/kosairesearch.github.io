02

Business structure

Histeel was spun off in 2003 from the steel pipe division of a predecessor steel company and is headquartered in Dangjin, South Chungcheong Province.

Its main products are piping steel pipe, pressure piping steel pipe, structural steel pipe and oil-transport pipe, which serve piping, building structures and energy transport. A 2023 broker report showed the 2022 revenue mix at 89.4% product, 6.9% merchandise and 3.7% services, and that breakdown applies only to 2022.

Exports were reported at 42% of total revenue in 2024. North American supply runs through the subsidiary HISTEEL PIPE&TUBE INC. Histeel is classified as a mid-sized company, so its benchmarks include large steel and pipe makers such as SeAH Steel, Hyundai Steel Company and Dongkuk Steel Mill.

Company descriptions also mention diversification into heat treatment and gas station leasing. A September 2025 press report said a gift transfer was completed, the third generation took over management, and an executive vice president became the largest shareholder.

Earnings analysis

Annual revenue fell from KRW 316.4 billion in 2022 to KRW 256.7 billion in 2023 (down 18.9% year on year) and KRW 246.3 billion in 2024 (down 4.0%), then rebounded to KRW 256.6 billion in 2025 (up 4.2%). 2022 was the largest year and 2024 the smallest within 2022 to 2025.

Operating profit dropped from KRW 16.9 billion in 2022 to KRW 3.39 billion in 2023 (down 79.9%) and KRW 912 million in 2024 (down 73.1%), and turned into an operating loss of KRW 3.32 billion in 2025, a swing from profit to loss.

The operating margin moved from 5.3% in 2022 to 1.3% in 2023, 0.4% in 2024 and -1.3% in 2025. Revenue grew in 2025, but profitability did not improve, so the sales gain did not carry through to earnings.

Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders fell from KRW 11.5 billion in 2022 to KRW 1.31 billion in 2023 (down 88.6%), turned into a net loss of KRW 1.1 billion in 2024, and widened to a net loss of KRW 4.58 billion in 2025. The company has recorded net losses for two consecutive years, 2024 and 2025.

Net profit including non-controlling interests moved in the same direction, at a net loss of KRW 1.1 billion in 2024 and KRW 4.58 billion in 2025. Operating cash flow rose from KRW 516 million in 2022 to KRW 19.8 billion in 2023, eased to KRW 3.67 billion in 2024, and turned negative at -KRW 3.21 billion in 2025.

The debt ratio stayed in the low 80% range after 96.3% in 2022, reaching 81.8% in 2025, when total equity was KRW 143.7 billion and total liabilities KRW 117.6 billion.

Quarterly revenue climbed from KRW 54 billion in 3Q 2025 to KRW 73.2 billion in 4Q 2025 (up 35.5% quarter on quarter), dipped to KRW 54.9 billion in 1Q 2026 (down 25.0%), and then rose to KRW 81.3 billion in 2Q 2026 (up 48.0%) and KRW 83.2 billion in 3Q 2026 (up 2.4%), marking two consecutive quarters of growth.

Quarterly operating results went from losses of KRW 3.36 billion in 3Q 2025 and KRW 2.17 billion in 4Q 2025 to an operating profit of KRW 1.77 billion in 1Q 2026.

After KRW 3.23 billion in 2Q 2026 (up 83.0% quarter on quarter), 3Q 2026 returned to an operating loss of KRW 218 million, though the loss was much smaller than the KRW 3.36 billion a year earlier.

Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 2.22 billion in 1Q 2026 and KRW 3.3 billion in 2Q 2026, then a loss of KRW 2.88 billion in 3Q 2026.

The 3Q 2026 net loss is much larger than the operating loss, which suggests non-operating items may have played a role, and the breakdown should be checked in the quarterly report.

Industry analysis

Steel pipe demand tracks oil and gas transmission projects, construction structures and plant piping, all linked to investment and infrastructure orders. With exports at 42% of revenue in 2024, Histeel's results are directly affected by overseas demand, especially in the United States.

A September 2025 press report pointed to expectations that U.S. steel pipe demand would grow. Raw material prices such as hot-rolled coil and the exchange rate affect pricing and remain key variables for profitability.

Large domestic steel makers including SeAH Steel, Hyundai Steel Company and Dongkuk Steel Mill serve as benchmarks for scale and cost. As a mid-sized company, Histeel's competitiveness depends on diversifying sales channels and export customers. Its 1Q 2026 results were described as benefiting from Asian export sales.

Where the industry sits in its cycle is hard to pin down, so whether U.S. demand holds and whether export destinations widen are the points to watch.

Risk factors

Export and trade risk

With exports at 42% of revenue in 2024, results are directly exposed to overseas demand and changes in trade policy. If U.S. steel pipe demand does not grow as expected, utilization in the North American channel could weaken. Exchange rate swings also affect export pricing and margins. If exports are concentrated in one market, the impact could be focused.

New business execution risk

The plan to expand into heat treatment and gas station leasing aims to build revenue sources beyond steel pipe. Because these businesses' earnings contribution is not yet visible in reported figures, initial investment and operating costs could weigh on core profitability.

If equipment and staff are split toward new businesses during an operating loss period, capacity for export and North American supply recovery could shrink. If the new businesses fail to earn as expected, the benefit of structural improvement could also be delayed.

Governance and subsidiary transactions

A September 2025 press report said a gift transfer was completed and the third generation took over management. Because control changed hands through succession, the direction of decision-making deserves attention.

The KRW 37.6 billion supply contract with a subsidiary, disclosed in May 2026, makes contract terms and transaction structure important. The share of related-party transactions and their conditions should be checked in quarterly reports.
