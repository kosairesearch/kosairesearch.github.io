02

Business structure

YM was founded in 1971 and listed on KOSDAQ in 1994 as a fastener specialist. The company makes forged products such as bolts, screws and rivets for automobiles, electronics and construction, supplying about 10,000 items to customers at home and abroad.

It operates a single business, producing and selling fasteners and related parts. As of December 2025, products accounted for 95.71% of sales, with merchandise and service revenue making up the rest. In bolt and screw product sales over the same period, exports were 60.26% and domestic sales 39.74%.

On the customer side, YM is strengthening ties with Hyundai Motor and pursuing wider global parts orders. Raw materials come mainly from domestic special steel suppliers, and the parts are made by cold forging. Automotive fasteners require materials technology, tooling, heat treatment and surface treatment.

Overseas, YM holds a 100% stake in YM (THAILAND), which handles automotive bolts and construction screws. Competition is shaped by a shrinking domestic auto production base and intensifying global rivals.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual revenue rose from KRW 146.3 billion in 2022 to KRW 166.3 billion in 2023 (up 13.6% year on year), KRW 172.2 billion in 2024 (up 3.6%) and KRW 177.1 billion in 2025 (up 2.9%), marking three straight years of growth from 2023 through 2025, with 2025 the largest year of 2022 to 2025.

Operating profit was KRW 4.63 billion in 2022, KRW 4.16 billion in 2023 (down 10.2%), KRW 6.11 billion in 2024 (up 46.7%) and KRW 3.99 billion in 2025 (down 34.7%), with 2024 the largest year and 2025 the smallest.

Operating margin ran at 3.2%, 2.5%, 3.5% and 2.3% from 2022 to 2025, so profitability moved within a narrow band even as sales grew.

Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 2.49 billion in 2022, KRW 947 million in 2023 (down 61.9%), KRW 5.19 billion in 2024 (up 448.5%) and KRW 2.5 billion in 2025 (down 51.8%). Net income including non-controlling interests was reported at the same levels.

The debt ratio fell from 236.6% in 2022 to 178.9% in 2024 before edging up to 182.1% in 2025, when total equity was KRW 52.7 billion and total liabilities KRW 95.9 billion. Operating cash flow was KRW 9.85 billion in 2022, -KRW 3.27 million in 2023, KRW 10.5 billion in 2024 and KRW 4.85 billion in 2025.

Quarterly revenue went from KRW 44.5 billion in the third quarter of 2025 to KRW 43 billion in the fourth quarter, KRW 42.7 billion in the first quarter of 2026, KRW 45.8 billion in the second quarter, and KRW 41.5 billion in the third quarter of 2026, which was down 6.7% year on year.

Quarterly operating profit went from KRW 1.94 billion in the third quarter of 2025 to KRW 524 million, KRW 1.44 billion and KRW 279 million, then turned into an operating loss of KRW 503 million in the third quarter of 2026.

Controlling-shareholder results swung from a KRW 2.39 billion profit in the third quarter of 2025 to a KRW 108 million loss in the fourth quarter, a KRW 1.14 billion profit in the first quarter of 2026, and losses of KRW 526 million in the second quarter and KRW 4.32 billion in the third quarter of 2026, which is two consecutive loss quarters.

Net losses over the last four quarters total KRW 3.81 billion. Cost pressure from higher raw material and utility prices has been cited as a factor behind these swings.

Industry analysis

Company profile data classifies YM under KOSDAQ metals and FICS automotive parts. Most revenue comes from automotive parts, so demand depends on vehicle production volumes and the pace of electrification.

Earnings commentary notes that sales grew despite a weak downstream industry, while domestic auto production declined and global competition intensified. One analysis links the recovery in domestic and export sales to improved semiconductor supply, which normalized automobile output and eased pent-up demand.

Development of new fasteners for electric-vehicle modules, including motors, inverters, reducers and batteries, is under way. Commentary also expects these products to grow with the electric-vehicle industry as automakers develop eco-friendly models.

On the technology side, cold forging and materials, tooling and heat-treatment know-how act as entry barriers for smaller rivals. Raw materials depend on domestic special steel suppliers, so raw material price moves pass through to cost quickly.

Export exposure of 60.26% also ties results to overseas vehicle demand and exchange rates. Companies grouped under the chemical sector differ in business structure and products, so direct comparison with sector averages is limited.

Bull factors

Export-heavy sales base

Export sales made up 60.26% of bolt and screw product sales as of December 2025, so the company is directly exposed to overseas auto demand. A 100% stake in the Thai subsidiary provides an overseas production and sales base. A wider overseas customer base is cited as a partial offset to weaker domestic auto output.

New fasteners for electrified modules

New fasteners for motors, inverters, reducers and batteries are in development. Commentary links this to automaker eco-friendly vehicle programs and expects growth alongside the electric-vehicle industry. Broadening the portfolio into electrification parts could change the price structure of automotive fasteners.

Recent swing back to profit

The first quarter of 2026 showed operating profit of KRW 1.44 billion and a controlling-shareholder net profit of KRW 1.14 billion after a swing to profit. Operating profit reached KRW 6.11 billion in 2024, the largest year of 2022 to 2025. The quick rebound in past quarters is cited as a basis for recovery if cost pressure eases.

Bear factors

Consecutive losses in 2026

The third quarter of 2026 recorded an operating loss of KRW 503 million and a controlling-shareholder net loss of KRW 4.32 billion. Losses followed the second quarter, making two consecutive loss quarters, and the last four quarters total a KRW 3.81 billion net loss. Third-quarter revenue fell 6.7% year on year, so sales and profit weakened together.

Cost pressure and thin margins

Analysts cite higher raw material and utility prices as a source of cost pressure that limits profitability. Operating margin was 3.2% in 2022 and 2.3% in 2025, staying low even as sales grew. If cost pass-through is difficult, higher sales may not translate into larger profit.

Domestic auto output and competition

Commentary points to a shrinking domestic auto output base, limited domestic demand and intensifying global competition. Because much of revenue depends on vehicle production, weaker downstream demand quickly affects order volume and utilization. The 51.8% drop in 2025 controlling-shareholder profit can be read in this context.

Risk factors

Dependence on automotive customers

Revenue concentrates in automotive parts, so results can swing with production plans and specific customer relationships. Deepening ties with Hyundai Motor are under way, but high customer concentration means model-level output changes flow directly into sales. The timing of volume production for new electrified fasteners also depends on customer schedules.

Raw material and cost swings

Raw materials come from domestic special steel suppliers, so steel prices and utility costs pass straight through to cost. Cost pressure is cited behind the swings between profit and loss in quarterly results in 2026.

If cost increases cannot be passed on to selling prices, operating margin can fall further, as it did to 2.3% in 2025.

Balance sheet and cash flow

Total liabilities of KRW 95.9 billion exceeded equity of KRW 52.7 billion at the end of 2025, with a debt ratio of 182.1%. Operating cash flow was nearly zero in 2023 at -KRW 3.27 million and fell to KRW 4.85 billion in 2025. If loss quarters continue, borrowing costs and room for capital spending could be constrained.

Overall view

YM makes automotive fasteners as its core business. Revenue rose from KRW 146.3 billion in 2022 to KRW 177.1 billion in 2025, but operating margin stayed in a 2.3% to 3.5% range.

Controlling-shareholder net profit fell 51.8% to KRW 2.5 billion in 2025, and quarterly results in 2026 swung between profit and loss before two consecutive loss quarters in the second and third quarters.

Third-quarter 2026 revenue of KRW 41.5 billion was down 6.7% year on year, and the last four quarters total a KRW 3.81 billion controlling-shareholder net loss.

Strengths include an export-heavy sales base, with exports at 60.26% of bolt and screw product sales as of December 2025, a Thai production base and new fasteners for electrified modules.

Weaknesses include raw material and utility cost pressure, shrinking domestic auto output and a balance sheet where liabilities exceed equity. The debt ratio fell from 236.6% in 2022 to 182.1% in 2025 but remains high relative to equity.
