Report overview

Yooshin shifts from condo sales to engineering services and EPC

02

Business structure

Yooshin was founded in 1966 to engage in construction engineering and became the first civil engineering company in its industry to list on KOSDAQ, in 2002. Its core is engineering services such as design and supervision, and its construction management work spans roads, transport, structures and water resources.

Service revenue rose from KRW 288.9 billion in 2023 to KRW 337.8 billion in 2024, according to a 2025 IB Tomato report, and that segment's operating margin has stayed in the low single digits because of competitive bidding and labor costs.

The condominium sales business has wound down, with operating profit of KRW 15.4 billion in 2023 and almost none in 2024. In October 2024 the company acquired 100% of a plant construction firm with waste incineration and power plant technology, and completed an absorption merger in February 2025 to enter EPC work.

In April 2025 it won a KRW 39.5 billion waste treatment facility construction contract, with a disclosed term ending May 29, 2026. In February 2026 it disclosed a KRW 91.14 billion construction management contract for the Bataan-Cavite connection bridge, with a term ending February 10, 2033. Clients are mainly public agencies, and the largest shareholder and related parties hold 46.67% of shares.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual revenue rose from KRW 304 billion in 2022 to KRW 341.1 billion in 2023 (up 12.2%), then eased to KRW 339.6 billion in 2024 (down 0.4%) before reaching KRW 385.5 billion in 2025 (up 13.5%). The largest year of 2022–2025 was 2025 and the smallest was 2022.

Operating profit went from KRW 20.9 billion in 2022 to KRW 18.3 billion in 2023 (down 12.4%) and KRW 5.81 billion in 2024 (down 68.3%), then recovered to KRW 9.24 billion in 2025 (up 59.0%). The operating margin fell from 6.9% in 2022 to 5.4% in 2023 and 1.7% in 2024, and was 2.4% in 2025.

The 2024 drop is linked to the end of condominium sales profit, while service revenue kept growing but its margin stayed in the 1% range.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders rose from KRW 18.3 billion in 2022 to KRW 20.1 billion in 2023 (up 9.7%) and KRW 24.4 billion in 2024 (up 21.5%), then fell to KRW 10.5 billion in 2025 (down 56.9%), with 2024 the largest year of the four.

Net income including non-controlling interests is disclosed at the same yearly amounts, so the two should be read carefully. At the end of 2025, total equity was KRW 155.1 billion and total liabilities were KRW 184.1 billion, lifting the debt ratio from 86.6% in 2023 to 118.7%.

Operating cash flow went from KRW 164 million in 2024 to KRW 39 billion in 2025. On a quarterly basis, Q4 2025 revenue was KRW 111.3 billion (up 14.4% from the prior quarter) with operating profit of KRW 4.5 billion (up 225.1%).

Q1 2026 revenue was KRW 108.6 billion (down 2.4%) and the company posted an operating loss of KRW 764 million. Q2 2026 revenue was KRW 112 billion (up 3.1%) with operating profit of KRW 6.79 billion, turning profitable.

Q3 2026 revenue of KRW 124.9 billion (up 11.5% from the prior quarter and 28.3% year on year) marked a second straight quarterly increase after Q1, yet operating profit of KRW 3.85 billion fell 43.3% from Q2.

Controlling-shareholder net income was KRW 5.12 billion in Q2 and KRW 2.37 billion in Q3, and the last four quarters total KRW 12.7 billion.

A market data aggregator reports first-half 2026 standalone revenue up 24.7%, operating profit up 79.1% and net income up 31.9%, but that is a standalone basis and may not match the disclosed quarterly figures.

05

Industry analysis

Engineering services orders depend heavily on public procurement outcomes. Reports said that if a public bidding ban is confirmed, orders for public projects could be disrupted. The service segment works through competitive bidding, and labor costs keep its margin low.

Sales-related profit shrank as condominium projects wound down, but reports expected revenue growth to continue on wider service orders and EPC sales. Korea Ratings said the end of condominium sales reduced profit and that EPC work could bring greater financial volatility in its early stage.

On the other side, the agency said cash exceeding borrowings and steady service orders should support growth. Large construction peers in the same sector are mainly general contractors focused on construction, while Yooshin is centered on design, supervision and construction management, so its EPC share is still small.

A 2025 report said EPC work had begun generating construction revenue but was still at an early stage with a small profit contribution. The first-half 2026 standalone improvement was attributed to expansion in construction management and the start of waste incineration and plant projects.

06

Outlook

Backlog reportedly rose from KRW 498.3 billion at the end of 2023 to KRW 726.7 billion at the end of March 2025, according to a 2025 report. A 2025 report also said the company had secured about KRW 100 billion of work volume on a contract basis, including an SRF power project, as of the first quarter.

The waste treatment construction order won in April 2025 is an EPC contract with a disclosed term ending May 29, 2026. The Philippine bridge construction management contract disclosed in February 2026 runs to February 2033 and forms a long-term base for service revenue.

Whether the waste incineration and plant projects cited for the first-half 2026 improvement convert into profit is the next point to watch. A 2025 report said working-capital needs and early project costs from EPC expansion weighed on cash flow. No annual company guidance has been disclosed.

Whether the operating margin in the next annual report rises above the 2.4% recorded in 2025, and how the 118.7% debt ratio moves, are the key variables.

Bull factors

Backlog and new contracts build up

A 2025 report put backlog at KRW 498.3 billion at the end of 2023 and KRW 726.7 billion at the end of March 2025. Service orders expanded and new EPC revenue began to appear, according to that report.

In February 2026 the company disclosed an additional KRW 91.14 billion construction management contract in the Philippines.

Return to quarterly profit and first-half improvement in 2026

Operating profit returned to the black in Q2 2026 at KRW 6.79 billion, and revenue rose for two straight quarters from Q2 into Q3. A market data aggregator reports first-half 2026 standalone operating profit up 79.1%. Expansion in road, transport and water projects within construction management is cited as a driver.

A financial cushion

Total equity stood at KRW 155.1 billion at the end of 2025. Low borrowing carried over from earlier years and cash exceeding debt are cited as the basis of financial stability. A 2025 report said debt of KRW 39.2 billion was held against KRW 69.6 billion of cash and financial instruments.

09

Bear factors

Low margins and a profit decline

The operating margin fell from 6.9% in 2022 to 1.7% in 2024 and was 2.4% in 2025. Service work runs through competitive bidding and carries labor costs, which keeps the margin in the 1% range according to a 2025 report. Net income attributable to controlling shareholders fell 56.9% in 2025 to KRW 10.5 billion.

Public bidding restriction risk

According to 2025 reporting, the company received a three-month public bidding restriction in April over a rule violation tied to a national rail agency project, and an administrative appeal was rejected in July.

The company filed an administrative lawsuit and a request to suspend enforcement, and reports said confirmed restrictions could disrupt public project orders.

Volatility in the early EPC stage

A 2025 report described EPC work as still at an early stage with a small profit contribution. A credit rating analysis said financial volatility could increase during the early EPC phase. The company posted an operating loss of KRW 764 million in Q1 2026, and Q3 operating profit fell 43.3% from Q2.

Risk factors

Client concentration risk

Service revenue depends on the timing and bidding results of public and overseas clients. The Philippine bridge project's counterparty is a public works department, and any schedule change on the client side could shift the timing of revenue recognition. Even with long contracts, concentration on a few clients remains a risk factor.

Working capital and balance sheet

The debt ratio rose from 86.6% in 2023 to 118.7% in 2025. Operating cash flow reached KRW 39 billion in 2025 but has swung widely from year to year.

Quarterly volatility and float

Quarterly operating profit in 2026 ranged from an operating loss of KRW 764 million in Q1 to KRW 6.79 billion in Q2 and KRW 3.85 billion in Q3, a wide swing. Controlling-shareholder net income fell from KRW 5.12 billion in Q2 to KRW 2.37 billion in Q3. Related parties hold 46.67% of shares, and the free float ratio is 50.67%.

12

Overall view

Yooshin's 2026 results show quarterly revenue rising while operating profit swings between profit and loss. Revenue of KRW 385.5 billion and operating profit of KRW 9.24 billion (a 2.4% margin) in 2025 serve as the reference point for the shift toward services and EPC.

The return to operating profit in Q2 2026 and the first-half standalone improvement read as positive signals, but Q3 operating profit fell from Q2. Supporting factors include a growing backlog, the Philippine bridge management contract and cash exceeding borrowings.

Offsetting factors include a low operating margin, public bidding restriction risk and financial volatility in the early EPC phase. The debt ratio rose to 118.7% in 2025 from 86.6% in 2023, and the last four quarters of controlling-shareholder net income total KRW 12.7 billion.

The next checkpoints are the operating margin in the 2026 annual report and whether profitability holds in Q1 2027.
