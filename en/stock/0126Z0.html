02

Business structure

Samsung Epis Holdings is a newly created holding company spun off from Samsung Biologics; it manages its stakes in subsidiaries and makes new investments. By Q2 2026 revenue, biosimilars account for 100% of the business.

Its core subsidiary, Samsung Bioepis, sells 11 biosimilars for autoimmune, oncology, ophthalmology and rare diseases, and is developing seven follow-on biosimilars, including SB27. The follow-on products reference Keytruda, Dupixent, Tremfya, Taltz, Enhertu, Entyvio and Ocrevus.

In March 2026 it agreed to co-develop and commercialize up to five biosimilars with Sandoz, including an Entyvio biosimilar, and in October 2026 it signed a deal with Teva covering Fasenra and Ilaris biosimilars.

Its subsidiary Epis NexLab is building a platform that turns peptides, the base material of obesity drugs, into long-acting formulations. Shinyoung Securities said the company ranks first in biosimilar market share in Europe and second in the US.

The largest shareholder is Samsung C&T with 43.06%, and Samsung Electronics holds 31.22%. Celltrion, which handles development, manufacturing and sales itself, is the domestic peer most often used for comparison.

04

Earnings analysis

On a reported basis, 2025 revenue was KRW 251.7 billion with an operating loss of KRW 63.6 billion, an operating margin of -25.3%. Because the figure covers only the two months after incorporation (November–December 2025), it is hard to compare directly with later quarters.

Profit attributable to owners and net profit for 2025 were both reported at KRW 85.3 billion, and operating cash flow was -KRW 103.7 billion. Hana Securities treated separately the purchase price allocation (PPA) amortization booked since the acquisition of Biogen's stake in Samsung Bioepis.

That amortization makes consolidated operating profit lower than the subsidiary's standalone figure. In Q1 2026, revenue was KRW 453.9 billion and operating profit KRW 90.5 billion, a margin of about 20%, with profit attributable to owners of KRW 99.5 billion.

In Q2, revenue was KRW 391.9 billion (down 13.7% quarter on quarter) and operating profit KRW 58.7 billion (down 35.1%), taking the margin to about 15%. Profit attributable to owners fell 13.7% in the same quarter to KRW 85.8 billion.

Hana Securities cited the absence of milestones and the deferral of some sales into Q3. Part of the Q2 decline may therefore be temporary, but the Q3 results are needed to confirm it. The debt-to-equity ratio was 30.6% at end-2025, so the balance sheet is not heavily burdened.

05

Industry analysis

The biosimilar market follows patent expiries: each time a blockbuster originator drug loses protection, new products enter. Keytruda's composition-of-matter patent expires in 2028 in Korea and 2029 in the US.

Keytruda's 2025 global sales were roughly KRW 46 trillion, the largest market among the company's follow-on targets. Given the size of that market, competition among global biosimilar developers is intensifying.

Daishin Securities described a model in which new biosimilar launches offset price erosion on existing products. Prices of older products fall over time across the industry, so sustaining profits requires a steady stream of launches.

Hana Securities expected direct sales of SB12 and SB16 in Europe and a private-label strategy in the US to ease market entry. Unlike Celltrion, which sells directly, the company combines partner sales with direct sales. As a result, its margin structure differs by region.

06

Outlook

The company said it will disclose its January–September 2026 results on October 28, 2026. Hana Securities estimated about KRW 7.6 billion of additional milestone income in Q3 and, together with sales carried over from Q2, Q3 revenue of KRW 489.3 billion.

Hana expected the Q3 milestone to come from the extension of Biogen's European rights to SB2, a Remicade biosimilar. Hana forecast 2026 revenue of KRW 1.87 trillion and operating profit of KRW 405.2 billion.

Daishin Securities forecast 2026 revenue of KRW 1.85 trillion and operating profit of KRW 449.0 billion for the subsidiary Samsung Bioepis, up 10.5% and 19.4% year on year. The company aims to expand its biosimilar portfolio from the current 11 products to 20 by 2030. Samsung Bioepis plans to move at least one new drug candidate into clinical trials every year starting in 2027.

08

Bull factors

Deferred sales and milestones may land in Q3

Part of the Q2 drop in operating profit came from timing, as some sales slipped into the next quarter. Hana Securities estimated Q3 revenue at KRW 489.3 billion and saw a strong chance that growth continues into Q4. If that happens, the quarterly operating margin could move back toward the Q1 level.

First Keytruda biosimilar filing in Korea

SB27 ran a Phase 1 trial in 163 patients across four countries from 2024, and its Phase 3 trial enrolled 555 patients in 14 countries. The filing covers 16 indications, including melanoma, non-small cell lung cancer and head and neck cancer.

Being early in the biosimilar race for the world's largest drug by sales gives it a possible medium-to-long-term revenue source.

Global partners and balance-sheet capacity

The company signs commercial partners such as Sandoz and Teva early in development to prepare for entry once patents expire. The strategy is to secure sales partners from the start of development so it can move quickly after patent expiry. A debt-to-equity ratio of 30.6% at end-2025 leaves room to fund higher R&D spending.

09

Bear factors

Volatile quarterly earnings

Q2 2026 operating profit fell 35.1% quarter on quarter, showing how much quarterly profit swings with the timing of milestones and shipments. Q2 revenue came in 12.52% below market expectations. If the deferred sales fail to appear in Q3 as expected, doubts about earnings visibility could grow.

Margin pressure from drug R&D

Hana Securities said lower margins are unavoidable once full-scale new drug development begins. The plan to put at least one candidate into the clinic each year means R&D spending will keep rising. It will take time before any value from new drugs shows up in earnings.

Holdco discount and share supply-demand

Samsung Securities pointed to a holding-company discount and supply-demand uncertainty. Foreign ownership stood at 5.27% of the limit as of October 8, 2026. On some days the shares fell sharply with no company disclosure or negative news.

10

Risk factors

Competition and pricing

Several companies are developing biosimilars of blockbusters such as Keytruda at the same time, so price competition could intensify after launch. Since the model relies on new launches to offset price erosion on existing products, launch delays could shrink profits. Launch timing and price trends for existing products need to be tracked together.

Accounting and profit structure

PPA amortization from the acquisition of Biogen's stake keeps weighing on consolidated profit. As a result, profit looks different on a consolidated basis than on the subsidiary's standalone basis. Readers comparing results should first check which basis is being used.

Regulation and patents

Even after approval, a biosimilar can launch only once the originator's patents expire. Originators can delay launches through patent litigation or additional patents. When SB27 starts generating revenue depends on the outcome of the Korean regulatory review and on patent timelines in major markets.

Overall view

Samsung Epis Holdings is a holding company that relies on Samsung Bioepis's biosimilar business for earnings while expanding into new drugs and platform technology.

Its reported results run from an operating loss of KRW 63.6 billion over two months of 2025 to operating profit of KRW 90.5 billion in Q1 2026 and KRW 58.7 billion in Q2 2026.

Deferred sales and the absence of milestones have been offered as reasons for the Q2 decline, but the Q3 results on October 28, 2026 are needed to confirm whether it was temporary. The bull case rests on the first Keytruda biosimilar filing in Korea, a global partner network and low leverage.

The bear case centers on volatile quarterly earnings, margin pressure from drug R&D, and the holding-company discount and share supply-demand uncertainty. This report is for information only and contains no buy or sell opinion or target price.
