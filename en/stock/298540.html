02

Business structure

THE Nature Holdings runs a license-based apparel business covering outerwear, travel bags and accessories. It introduced the National Geographic license in 2013, expanded into a outdoor fashion portfolio through the 2022 acquisition of Barrel and the 2024 stake in Deus ex Machina.

National Geographic Apparel is the core, accounting for about 80% of company sales in the first three quarters of 2025, when its sales fell 6.5% year on year to KRW 246.7 billion. Domestic sales of the brand fell 12.1% in that period while overseas sales grew.

The company also holds domestic distribution rights for Mark Gonzales, Deus ex Machina and Brompton London, but the two core brands, National Geographic and Barrel, make up about 95% of sales, so their performance drives the group.

Barrel, a water-sports apparel subsidiary, was reported to have weakened in the first three quarters of 2025.

Overseas, the company expanded China stores mainly as company-operated outlets, and the Shanghai entity posted net losses of KRW 7.0 billion in 2023, KRW 4.7 billion in 2024 and KRW 9.5 billion for the first nine months of 2025, prompting localization and distribution changes.

Listed peers in the sector include Youngone, F&F, Handsome, Shinsegae International and LF. As domestic fashion demand polarizes across brands, companies with a high domestic sales mix feel the impact most directly.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual sales rose from KRW 497.9 billion in 2022 to KRW 548.4 billion in 2023, a 10.2% increase, before falling 5.8% to KRW 516.9 billion in 2024 and 7.6% to KRW 477.5 billion in 2025.

Operating profit fell for three consecutive years, from KRW 90.8 billion in 2022 to KRW 67 billion in 2023 (down 26.3%), KRW 30.1 billion in 2024 (down 55.1%) and KRW 6.97 billion in 2025 (down 76.8%), with 2022 the largest year of the period.

The operating margin declined from 18.2% in 2022 to 12.2%, 5.8% and 1.5% in 2025. Profit attributable to owners of the parent fell from KRW 68.6 billion in 2022 to KRW 6.81 billion in 2025, and net income including non-controlling interests was KRW 1.02 billion in 2025, a different measure.

Based on press reports for the first nine months of 2025, sales fell 4.7% to KRW 318.9 billion while operating profit dropped 79% to KRW 2.4 billion, showing how fixed selling and administrative costs amplify the impact of lower sales.

Quarterly, an operating loss of KRW 6.74 billion in Q3 2025 turned into operating profit of KRW 4.59 billion in Q4 2025, but operating losses of KRW 877 million in Q1 2026, KRW 1.79 billion in Q2 2026 and KRW 6.47 billion in Q3 2026 followed, three consecutive loss quarters.

Sales peaked at KRW 158.6 billion in Q4 2025 and then declined for three consecutive quarters to KRW 112.8 billion, KRW 95.6 billion and KRW 78.3 billion, with Q3 2026 down 16.2% year on year.

In Q3 2026 the operating loss narrowed year on year while the loss attributable to owners widened from KRW 7.8 billion to KRW 9.44 billion, so the operating and net lines moved in different directions. The combined loss attributable to owners over the last four quarters was KRW 1.75 billion.

05

Industry analysis

The domestic fashion market is in a phase of weaker consumer sentiment, reduced purchasing power under high interest rates and widening gaps between brands, as cited in securities commentary on the company's results. Because domestic sales make up a large share of the company, it is directly exposed to this slowdown.

A 2024 brokerage report pointed to intensifying competition among outdoor brands alongside weak consumption as a factor behind lower profitability. In terms of cycle position, the key question is whether domestic consumption shows signs of recovery, and the company is trying to offset this through overseas channels.

Overseas, company-operated stores have been a cost burden: at the end of Q3 2025, 14 of the 17 National Geographic stores in China were directly operated, and the Shanghai entity's early store investment rose sharply. Investment has since declined, but without cash recovery from operations, net losses have continued.

Against peers, the company's distinguishing feature and its weakness is high dependence on a single licensed brand, National Geographic.

Listed peers such as F&F, LF, Handsome, Shinsegae International and Youngone operate multi-brand portfolios, while this company's results remain closely tied to one brand's performance.

06

Outlook

In its 2026 corporate value enhancement plan, the company set targets of 10% year-on-year sales growth and an operating profit in the double-digit range. It also pledged to maintain dividends at the prior-year level or raise them based on performance.

Execution measures include revenue-focused operations, more efficient production forecasting to manage inventory, and continued investment alongside dividends.

Q3 2026 sales fell 16.2% year on year, moving opposite to the growth direction set in the plan, so whether the full-year targets are reached is the next checkpoint.

For its core brand, the company said it would strengthen National Geographic Apparel fall-winter sales and expand Brompton bicycles and Barrel distribution in China, with Q4 2026 focused on brand performance recovery.

Overseas, it is expanding channels through the Disney Adventure cruise ship and National Geographic headquarters experience center listings and wholesale supply in the United States. Store changes in 2026 included closing a Hong Kong store in April, opening Taiwan's 12th store in April and new China stores in May.

On licensing, the company said a favorable outcome in the Mark Gonzales IP dispute secured a stable base for the brand. On costs, it plans continued marketing efficiency gains, cost structure improvement and operating streamlining.

08

Bull factors

Insider buying and a maintained shareholder return policy

Executives and related parties bought about KRW 320 million of shares in October 2026, which the company framed as an extension of its 2026 value enhancement plan. The CEO added 1,000 shares through open-market purchases on November 3 and 4, 2026, bringing his holding to 3,312,094 shares. These purchases are facts that support the bull case, but they do not guarantee a share price direction.

The Q4 2025 return to profit

In Q4 2025 the company posted operating profit of KRW 4.59 billion after an operating loss in the prior quarter, and profit attributable to owners of KRW 8.39 billion. Sales that quarter reached KRW 158.6 billion, the highest of the last five quarters.

Losses resumed from Q1 2026, but the quarter shows the business can return to profit on a quarterly basis. The company said it is focusing on brand performance recovery in Q4 2026.

Diversifying overseas distribution channels

The company is widening its global distribution through US wholesale supply and listings on the Disney Adventure cruise ship and at the National Geographic headquarters experience center. It opened its 12th store in Taiwan in April 2026 and new China stores in May 2026.

Mark Gonzales sales rose 172% year on year in the first three quarters of 2025, though the absolute amount of KRW 11.6 billion keeps its contribution limited so far.

Bear factors

Three years of profit decline and three loss quarters

The operating margin fell from 18.2% in 2022 to 1.5% in 2025, and operating profit declined for three consecutive years. Operating losses have continued from Q1 to Q3 2026, with a Q3 2026 loss of KRW 6.47 billion.

A market data aggregator reported first-half 2026 sales down 7.6% year on year and both operating and net income turned to losses. The combined loss attributable to owners over the last four quarters was KRW 1.75 billion, so the pace of recovery is not yet clear.

Single-brand concentration and weakening domestic demand

National Geographic accounted for about 80% of company sales in the first three quarters of 2025, and the two core brands made up 95%. Domestic sales of the group fell 12.1% over the same period.

Analysts cite weaker consumer sentiment, high interest rates and widening gaps between brands in the domestic market, which weighs on companies with a high domestic sales mix. When one brand weakens, overall results move with it, which is the bear case.

Cumulative net losses at the China subsidiary

Press reports said the Shanghai entity posted net losses of KRW 7.0 billion in 2023, KRW 4.7 billion in 2024 and KRW 9.5 billion for the first nine months of 2025, three consecutive years of losses.

High company-operated store ratios drove early investment, and the investment did not come back through operating cash flow. The company said it is shifting strategy through localization and distribution changes, but the results of that shift are not yet visible in reported figures.

10

Risk factors

Fixed-cost structure amplifying sales declines

Apparel companies carry high fixed selling and administrative costs such as labor, rent and marketing, so lower sales quickly weigh on margins. In the first three quarters of 2025, sales fell 4.7% while operating profit dropped 79%, illustrating the effect.

Quarterly sales kept declining through 2026 under the same structure, and the key risk is how much cost improvement offsets this before sales recover.

Dependence on licensed brands and management change

The core business rests on licensed brands such as National Geographic, so license terms or IP disputes affect results directly. The company said a favorable outcome in the Mark Gonzales IP dispute secured stable brand operations.

In 2026 a new CEO, Hyun Jong-hyuk, and a new CFO, Kim Jin-young, took over management and finance, and board changes occurred several times. How strategy evolves under the new management is the next item to watch.

Mismatch between inventory and demand forecasts

The company has made a more efficient production forecasting system a core execution item for inventory management. If seasonal demand forecasts miss, inventory builds and discounting can compress margins.

Sales peaked at KRW 158.6 billion in Q4 2025 and then declined for three consecutive quarters in 2026, which shows how much forecast accuracy matters. Tracking how quarterly inventory moves connect to the operating margin is the next step.

Overall view

Operating losses have continued for three consecutive quarters from Q1 to Q3 2026, and Q3 sales fell 16.2% year on year. Bullish points include insider share purchases, the return to operating profit in Q4 2025, wider overseas distribution and a plan to keep dividends at the prior-year level.
