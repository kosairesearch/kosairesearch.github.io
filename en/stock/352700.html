Air-Filter Slump and Workwear Build-Out Weigh on Earnings

Falling air-filter sales and the early costs of the new workwear business have kept Cntus in the red for five straight quarters since the third quarter of 2025.

02

Business structure

Cntus (352700) makes air filters used in products such as air purifiers and face masks, and is listed on KOSDAQ in the chemicals sector. It runs five domestic production centers and one in Vietnam, operating through a group structure that includes five subsidiaries and sub-subsidiaries.

The business has two pillars: the established air-filter manufacturing and the newer workwear business. The air-filter business is business-to-business, with results driven by orders from customers such as home-appliance makers.

In its 2025 explanation, the company said customers cut air-filter orders amid global tariff issues. The global air-filter market is growing on rising concern about air pollution and health, but filter revenue has declined as mask demand fell after the endemic phase, according to one analysis.

The workwear business started with an exclusive domestic distribution deal with five leading Japanese workwear brands and a network of company-run stores. Through Aer, its own-brand subsidiary, the company is also targeting business-to-consumer sales.

As of the March 2026 shareholder meeting it operated five directly run stores, and more openings followed. Established players already hold much of the workwear market, so whether the in-house brand can win a place is the open question. The domestic workwear market is estimated at roughly KRW 1.5 trillion.

04

Earnings analysis

On a reported basis, revenue fell from KRW 141.6 billion in 2022 to KRW 78 billion in 2023 (down 44.9% year on year), KRW 73 billion in 2024 (down 6.5%), and KRW 67.2 billion in 2025 (down 8.0%).

Revenue declined for three consecutive years from 2023 through 2025, with 2022 the largest year and 2025 the smallest in the 2022–2025 span.

Operating profit of KRW 26.7 billion in 2022 turned into an operating loss of KRW 744 million in 2023, returned to a KRW 1.4 billion profit in 2024, and became an operating loss of KRW 5.75 billion in 2025. The operating margin moved from 18.9% in 2022 to -1.0% in 2023, 1.9% in 2024 and -8.6% in 2025.

Attributable net income fell from KRW 19.5 billion in 2022 to KRW 3.31 billion in 2023 (down 83.0%) and KRW 1.97 billion in 2024 (down 40.3%), before turning into an attributable net loss of KRW 7.82 billion in 2025.

The company attributed the 2025 loss mainly to about KRW 11 billion of non-recurring charges, including goodwill amortization of KRW 5.8 billion, a KRW 2.0 billion loss from the liquidation of a SPAC investment, and KRW 3.1 billion of impairment on idle assets.

Its statement that net profit would have been about KRW 3.2 billion excluding these items is a company explanation and is separate from the reported figures.

By quarter, revenue rose from KRW 13.2 billion in the third quarter of 2025 to KRW 17.2 billion in the fourth quarter (up 30.4% from the prior quarter), but the operating loss widened to KRW 4.14 billion and the attributable net loss to KRW 10.5 billion.

In the first quarter of 2026, revenue was KRW 15.8 billion (down 8.3%), the operating loss narrowed to KRW 364 million, and attributable net income swung to KRW 1.64 billion. The second quarter brought revenue of KRW 16.3 billion, an operating loss of KRW 1.99 billion and an attributable net loss of KRW 2.42 billion.

In the third quarter of 2026, revenue was KRW 14.3 billion (down 12.3% from the prior quarter, up 8.6% year on year), with an operating loss of KRW 860 million and an attributable net loss of KRW 2.61 billion. The attributable net loss over the most recent four quarters totals KRW 13.9 billion.

05

Industry analysis

Air filters, Cntus's core product, have a medium-term demand base supported by growing concern about air pollution and health. At the same time, analysts note that filter revenue has declined since mask demand fell after the endemic phase. In 2025 the company blamed customers cutting orders amid global tariff issues.

In the near term, order timing and external factors such as tariffs therefore appear to drive results more than end demand. According to IB Tomato's July 2026 report, the workwear market grew after heatwaves and the Serious Accidents Punishment Act.

Cntus is a late entrant in this market, where established players already hold strong positions, so it must differentiate through its own brand and distribution channels. The company is trying to stand out by moving beyond business-to-business sales toward business-to-consumer.

Compared with larger chemical and materials companies in the same sector, such as SK Chemicals and Hanwha Solutions, Cntus is a small-cap by market value. Its model also requires running two capabilities at once: filter material technology and workwear distribution.

Whether a workwear brand built on filter material technology gains traction will largely determine the outcome of the business shift. On the filter side, demand depends on order recovery from customers and on utilization, so both should be tracked together.

06

Outlook

At its March 2026 shareholder meeting, the company said it would diversify along two lines: its existing manufacturing business and a new platform business. The workwear business is in the stage of adding company-run stores and deploying dedicated marketing staff.

According to a July 2026 report, the company plans to expand directly operated stores during 2026 and pursue nationwide franchising in 2027. The same report said the company targets the first product from its own filter-material-based workwear brand by the end of 2027.

The company explained that the first-quarter 2026 operating loss reflected market-entry costs including six new store openings. For the second half, it plans to broaden seasonal functional lineups and diversify sales channels.

On the technology side, the company's overview says it secured annual capacity of 1 million square meters through a supercritical flash-spinning nonwoven line, with medical packaging and water-treatment filters slated for commercialization from 2026.

Commercialization progress should be tracked through the quarterly results that follow. Because workwear spending comes before revenue, the balance between costs and sales in 2027 is the key checkpoint.

The narrower third-quarter 2026 operating loss can be read as an improvement signal, but the wider attributable net loss should be watched alongside it.

Bull factors

Room for a Recovery in the Core Filter Business

The company blamed customers cutting orders amid tariff issues for the 2025 revenue decline. Analysts point to a long-term demand base for air filters as air-pollution and health concerns grow. If orders normalize, the operating margin has room to move out of negative territory.

The narrower third-quarter 2026 operating loss versus a year earlier can also be read as a signal in that direction.

Workwear Market Growth and an In-House Brand Strategy

The domestic workwear market is estimated at roughly KRW 1.5 trillion, and reports say demand grew after heatwaves and the Serious Accidents Punishment Act. The company secured a product base through an exclusive domestic distribution deal with five leading Japanese workwear brands.

If expansion of company-run stores and the 2027 franchise plan proceed as scheduled, the business could scale up. An in-house brand built on filter material technology could become a point of differentiation if it succeeds.

Shareholder Returns and the CEO's Share Purchases

A cash dividend funded by tax-exempt retained earnings was approved at the March 2026 shareholder meeting. In November 2025 the company disclosed a decision to buy back KRW 2 billion of its own shares.

CEO Ha Choon-wook raised his stake through open-market purchases in January and March 2026, and his stake stood at 42.01% in the March disclosure. Some observers point to shareholder-return capacity given the sizable asset base.

Bear factors

Five Straight Quarters of Operating Losses and Accumulated Net Losses

The company has posted an operating loss in each of the five quarters from the third quarter of 2025 through the third quarter of 2026. In the third quarter of 2026, the operating loss narrowed from a year earlier, but the attributable net loss widened. Whether the first-quarter 2026 profit is one-off or sustainable remains to be seen.

Quality of the Profit Swing and the Burden of the New Business

Reports tie the first-quarter 2026 attributable profit to higher financial income rather than core operations. Over the same period, revenue at the Aer subsidiary fell to KRW 1.7 billion from KRW 3.6 billion a year earlier. Early costs such as new store openings come before revenue in the workwear business. Core earnings may take longer to absorb the cost of the new business.

Three Years of Falling Revenue and Non-Recurring Charges

Revenue fell from KRW 141.6 billion in 2022 to KRW 67.2 billion in 2025, declining for three consecutive years from 2023. The 2025 results absorbed about KRW 11 billion of non-recurring charges, including goodwill amortization, SPAC liquidation and impairment of idle assets.

The operating margin dropped from 18.9% in 2022 to -8.6% in 2025. If core earnings do not recover, similar charges could recur.

Risk factors

Business Transition Risk

The workwear business requires upfront investment in company-run stores and an in-house brand launch. The market is dominated by established players, so building distribution and brand recognition may take time. A delay to the targeted late-2027 first product launch would prolong the cost burden. Running filter and workwear operations at the same time also adds management complexity.

Impairment and Goodwill Risk

The 2025 results included KRW 5.8 billion of goodwill amortization and KRW 3.1 billion of impairment on idle assets. Further impairments of goodwill or tangible assets could again move net assets and net income.

Total equity was KRW 150.1 billion at the end of 2025, but its quality depends on the likelihood of further impairments. The company's adjusted net profit excludes these charges, so it should be kept separate from reported figures.

External Demand and Small-Cap Liquidity Risk

Filter revenue has been sensitive to tariff issues and to the timing of customer orders. Some analysts argue the filter demand base weakened after mask demand normalized. As a small-cap with a free float of about 44.30%, the stock can show sizable price swings.

Foreign ownership is low at about 1.50%, which shapes how the stock responds to institutional and foreign flows.

Overall view

Cntus is expanding its workwear business while its air-filter revenue has declined, from KRW 141.6 billion in 2022 to KRW 67.2 billion in 2025.

The company posted an operating loss of KRW 5.75 billion and an attributable net loss of KRW 7.82 billion in 2025, which it linked mainly to about KRW 11 billion of non-recurring charges. Operating losses have continued for five straight quarters since the third quarter of 2025.

The third quarter of 2026 showed a narrower operating loss but a wider attributable net loss. Reports tie the first-quarter 2026 attributable profit to higher financial income, which limits its value as evidence of a core recovery.

The domestic workwear market is estimated at roughly KRW 1.5 trillion, and the company targets more company-run stores, a franchise push in 2027 and its first in-house brand product by late 2027.

Positive factors include the long-term filter demand base, workwear market growth, shareholder returns and the CEO's share purchases. Negative factors include accumulated losses, the quality of the recent profit swing and the possibility of further impairments.

The next checkpoints are the fourth-quarter and full-year 2026 preliminary results, the 2026 annual report, and the first-quarter 2027 results.
