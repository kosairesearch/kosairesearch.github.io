Quarterly profit recovery, but memory-cycle dependence remains the test

02

Business structure

Exicon was founded in 2001 and listed on KOSDAQ in 2015. Its core business is final-test equipment for back-end semiconductor production, which screens completed chips and modules for normal operation before shipment.

Its products fall into memory module testers, SSD testers and next-generation CXL testers, and its SSD tester is presented as the number one product by domestic market share. Its major customers are Samsung Electronics and SK hynix.

According to Daily Invest, the revenue mix as of the second quarter of 2024 was 31.6% memory testers (including burn-in testers), 54.1% SSD testers and 14.3% SoC testers.

The chambered low-frequency memory tester (CLT), first launched in 2025, is a domestic replacement for aging foreign equipment, and reports say its hourly throughput is more than 20 times that of existing equipment.

A technology report estimated that 10 CLT units were supplied in 2025 and that the count would rise to 15 to 20 units in 2026, although these are estimates.

In non-memory testing, Exicon completed development of a CMOS image sensor (CIS) tester in 2023, and in 2024 it passed mass-production verification at a leading domestic chipmaker. In SSD testing, it supplies PCIe Gen5 testers and is developing PCIe Gen6 and CXL 3.1 testers.

Its only consolidated subsidiary is Exicon Japan Corporation, a semiconductor equipment research services company.

In competition, global players such as Advantest of Japan, Teradyne of the United States and Cohu lead the market, and domestic equipment's price and technology competitiveness is cited as a point of differentiation.

04

Earnings analysis

On a DART-filed basis, annual revenue fell from KRW 91.2 billion in 2022 to KRW 82.3 billion in 2023 (down 9.7% year on year) and then to KRW 31.6 billion in 2024 (down 61.6%), before recovering to KRW 66 billion in 2025 (up 108.9%). Among 2022 to 2025, 2022 was the largest year and 2024 the smallest.

Operating profit fell from KRW 10.3 billion in 2022 to KRW 1.46 billion in 2023 (down 85.7%), then turned into an operating loss of KRW 15.9 billion in 2024 before returning to an operating profit of KRW 80.12 million in 2025. The operating margin was 11.2% in 2022, 1.8% in 2023, -50.3% in 2024 and 0.1% in 2025.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 15.2 billion in 2022 and KRW 4.89 billion in 2023 (down 67.9%). It became a net loss of KRW 1.36 billion in 2024 and returned to net income of KRW 8.96 billion in 2025.

Net income including non-controlling interests is a separate figure and was KRW 8.96 billion in 2025. At the end of 2025, total equity was KRW 195.7 billion, all attributable to controlling shareholders, total liabilities were KRW 28.8 billion, and the debt ratio was 14.7%.

Operating cash flow was -KRW 2.54 billion in 2022, KRW 21.2 billion in 2023, -KRW 14.9 billion in 2024 and -KRW 4.03 billion in 2025, with wide swings between years.

Quarterly results moved from revenue of KRW 11.4 billion and an operating loss of KRW 2.03 billion in the third quarter of 2025 to revenue of KRW 45.1 billion in the fourth quarter (up 295.5% from the prior quarter) and operating profit of KRW 10.7 billion.

Yuanta Securities, forecasting a strong fourth quarter of 2025, noted that most revenue is recognized late in the process, when product installation is completed. Revenue then fell to KRW 9.81 billion in the first quarter of 2026, with an operating loss of KRW 1.97 billion, which marked the low point.

In the second quarter, revenue recovered to KRW 31.5 billion with operating profit of KRW 4.13 billion. In the third quarter, revenue reached KRW 50.5 billion (up 60.4% from the prior quarter and up 342.4% year on year), with operating profit of KRW 15.1 billion, a turn to profit from a year earlier.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders rose for two consecutive quarters after the first-quarter low of KRW 935 million, reaching KRW 7.59 billion in the second quarter and KRW 18.4 billion in the third.

Over the last four quarters (fourth quarter 2025 to third quarter 2026), net income attributable to controlling shareholders totaled KRW 40 billion.

05

Industry analysis

Demand for memory testers is generally described by brokers as driven by new fab construction, replacement of existing fabs and process changes. Yuanta Securities expected replacement demand for aging Advantest equipment in Japan to keep CLT revenue going in 2026.

At the same time, because testers are strongly affected by the memory cycle, commentary from BusinessPost points out that results can fall sharply during memory downturns.

On the SSD side, expanding AI data center investment and inference workloads are seen increasing demand for high-performance, high-capacity SSDs and the testers that serve them.

TechnoValue expects PCIe 6.0 support to become more widespread alongside AMD's Venice chip, scheduled for late 2026, and Intel's Diamond Rapids in 2027.

Exicon is developing PCIe Gen6 SSD testers and CXL 3.1 testers, and the company's strengths are cited as the ability to extend into HBM and GDDR testing by replacing only the board. In the global tester market, Advantest, Teradyne and Cohu together hold more than half of the share.

Exicon has built a domestic-substitution position centered on memory and SSD testers and has expanded into non-memory CIS testers. Diversifying the customer base to include SK hynix as well as overseas customers in the Americas, Taiwan and China is cited as a remaining challenge.

06

Outlook

The visible drivers for the outlook are orders and the mass production of new products. BusinessPost reported that in March 2026 Exicon agreed to supply CLT and SSD testers worth about KRW 30.2 billion to Samsung Electronics' Onyang facility.

In May 2026 a CLT interface board contract worth KRW 9.68 billion followed, and in June a semiconductor test equipment contract worth KRW 12.07 billion. In July, a CLT and SSD tester supply contract worth KRW 49.85 billion was signed.

The next checkpoint is how these contracts show up in third-quarter revenue and then in revenue recognition in later quarters. The same report cited a forecast that Exicon would surpass KRW 100 billion in annual revenue for the first time in 2026, but this is a media forecast and not a figure from company disclosures.

AMD's next-generation chip, scheduled for late 2026, and Intel's product launch in 2027 are external timelines that will shape demand for PCIe 6.0 SSD testers and CXL testers.

Expansion into HBM and GDDR testing is repeatedly described in broker reports, including Yuanta Securities', as contingent on passing customer qualification tests.

After quarterly profit rose for two consecutive quarters from the first-quarter 2026 low, the key question is whether revenue recognition continues in the fourth quarter of 2026 and in 2027. The clearest checkpoint is the 2026 annual report, which will confirm full-year revenue and operating cash flow.

Bull factors

A turn in quarterly profit

Operating profit rose from KRW 4.13 billion in the second quarter of 2026 to KRW 15.1 billion in the third, and net income attributable to controlling shareholders rose for two consecutive quarters. Third-quarter net income of KRW 18.4 billion compares with a net loss of KRW 760 million in the third quarter of 2025. Over the last four quarters, the total was KRW 40 billion, extending the turn to profit seen in 2025.

New CLT tester and large customer contracts

The CLT, a chambered low-frequency memory tester first launched in 2025, is presented as a domestic substitute for aging foreign equipment. A technology report estimated 10 units supplied in 2025 and 15 to 20 units in 2026.

According to BusinessPost, CLT and SSD tester supply contracts with Samsung Electronics followed one another from March to July 2026.

Demand for next-generation interfaces

TechnoValue expects PCIe 6.0 support to gain traction alongside AMD's chip launch in late 2026 and Intel's product in 2027. Exicon is developing PCIe Gen6 SSD testers and CXL 3.1 testers. The ability to extend into HBM and GDDR testing by replacing only the board is also cited as a strength.

Bear factors

Revenue volatility and loss history

Annual revenue fell from KRW 91.2 billion in 2022 to KRW 31.6 billion in 2024, a decline of 61.6%, and the company posted an operating loss of KRW 15.9 billion and a net loss attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 1.36 billion that year. The operating margin for 2025 was only 0.1%.

Quarterly revenue is also volatile, falling to KRW 9.81 billion in the first quarter of 2026, down 78.3% from the prior quarter.

Memory cycle and customer concentration

Major customers are concentrated in Samsung Electronics and SK hynix, so results depend heavily on the memory investment plans of those two companies. BusinessPost commentary notes that results could fall sharply in memory downturns. Winning overseas customers and expanding non-memory test products are cited as ways to stabilize results.

Cash flow and competitive landscape

Operating cash flow was -KRW 14.9 billion in 2024 and -KRW 4.03 billion in 2025, negative in both years. In the global tester market, Advantest, Teradyne and Cohu together hold more than half of the share, so the pace of expansion for domestic equipment is the key issue.

Maintaining cost competitiveness and strengthening engineering support at overseas sites are also cited as challenges.

10

Risk factors

Order concentration and revenue recognition timing

Revenue from large equipment is mostly recognized when installation is complete, so quarterly results can diverge from contract signing dates. Revenue rose 295.5% in the fourth quarter of 2025 and then fell 78.3% in the first quarter of 2026, which illustrates the pattern.

Because individual contracts are large, a gap in orders in a single quarter feeds directly into reported results.

Memory investment cycle

If memory makers cut capital spending, demand for testers can fall quickly. In 2024, a brokerage noted that customers were focusing investment on high-bandwidth memory and taking a conservative stance on other back-end investment. That shows how a shift in memory investment priorities can affect tester orders.

Technology generation shifts and cash flow

When the market shifts to next-generation interfaces such as PCIe 6.0 and CXL 3.1, development and mass-production schedules must line up. If development is delayed, it becomes hard to offset falling demand for existing products.

Negative operating cash flow in 2024 and 2025 also needs to be watched alongside the burden of development investment.

Overall view

Exicon is a memory test equipment maker whose annual revenue fell from KRW 91.2 billion in 2022 to KRW 31.6 billion in 2024 before recovering to KRW 66 billion in 2025.

Operating profit and net income attributable to controlling shareholders turned positive in 2025, and third-quarter 2026 operating profit of KRW 15.1 billion was the largest among the last five disclosed quarters.

Growth is centered on CLT and SSD testers, and press reports describe a run of supply contracts with Samsung Electronics from March to July 2026.

On the other side, revenue remains volatile, the 2024 operating loss and negative operating cash flow in 2024 and 2025 remain on record, and structural risk from memory market conditions and customer concentration persists.

In the market, global leaders hold more than half of the share, and demand for next-generation interfaces such as PCIe 6.0 and CXL is tied to the late-2026 AMD launch and Intel's 2027 product.

The next checkpoints are full-year results and cash flow in the 2026 annual report, demand for next-generation SSD testers, and the timing of revenue recognition on large customer contracts.
