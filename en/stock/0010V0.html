02

Business structure

Founded in 1980 and renamed to its current corporate name in 2010, JPI Healthcare is a digital X-ray imaging solutions company whose core business is the X-ray grid, a key component that removes scattered radiation to improve image quality.

According to the company, its grid business holds roughly a 40% global market share and supplies multiple customers, including some of the world's largest medical imaging device makers.

Beyond grids, the company has expanded into finished imaging equipment including digital radiography (DR) systems, the low-dose, site-specific CBCT system 'DeteCT,' the digital tomosynthesis system 'StriXion,' and the diagnostic X-ray system 'DRE Duo.' More than 80% of revenue comes from exports, supported by local subsidiaries in the United States and Japan and a sales network spanning more than 70 countries.

At the time of its IPO, grid products accounted for more than 60% of total revenue, indicating relatively high reliance on a single product category, while finished medical imaging equipment sales were described as still in an early stage.

The largest shareholder is CEO Kim Jin-kook, whose combined stake with his spouse and children as related parties exceeds 30%, with a financial investor holding a second-largest stake. The company listed on KOSDAQ on August 21, 2025, with Kiwoom Securities serving as lead underwriter.

In finished equipment, the company partially competes with larger global medical device makers, while in its core grid component business it operates in an oligopolistic structure with few domestic or international producers.

04

Earnings analysis

Full-year 2025 revenue came to KRW 48.5 billion, up 7.6% from KRW 45.1 billion in 2024, while operating profit rose 23.4% to KRW 4.86 billion from KRW 3.94 billion. The operating margin improved from 8.7% in 2024 to 10.0% in 2025.

However, net profit attributable to owners declined 12.9% to KRW 5.26 billion in 2025 from KRW 6.04 billion in 2024, moving in the opposite direction from operating profit, likely reflecting non-operating items or changes related to non-controlling interests.

Net income including non-controlling interests also fell to KRW 4.84 billion in 2025 from KRW 5.55 billion in 2024.

On a quarterly basis, after bottoming in Q3 2025 with revenue of KRW 11.9 billion, operating profit of KRW 660 million, and owners' net profit of KRW 1.25 billion, the company rebounded in Q4 2025 with revenue of KRW 12.3 billion (up 3.6% QoQ), operating profit of KRW 1.42 billion (up 115.7% QoQ), and owners' net profit of KRW 1.72 billion (up 37.0% QoQ).

In Q1 2026, revenue fell 12.5% QoQ to KRW 10.8 billion and operating profit dropped 67.8% to KRW 458 million, though owners' net profit still rose slightly by 1.5% to KRW 1.74 billion.

Q2 2026 showed a clearer improvement, with revenue of KRW 12 billion (up 11.2% QoQ), operating profit of KRW 2.15 billion (up 369.0% QoQ), and owners' net profit of KRW 2.32 billion (up 32.9% QoQ).

As a result, owners' net profit rose for three consecutive quarters from Q4 2025 through Q2 2026 following the Q3 2025 trough, and the sum of owners' net profit over the trailing four quarters (Q3 2025–Q2 2026) totaled KRW 7.04 billion.

On the balance sheet, the debt-to-equity ratio fell sharply to 30.7% at the end of 2025 from 59.2% a year earlier, while operating cash flow edged down from KRW 3.9 billion in 2024 to KRW 3.22 billion in 2025.

05

Industry analysis

The X-ray grid is a key consumable component that determines image quality in digital X-ray imaging, and the industry has an oligopolistic structure with only a small number of producers worldwide.

The global medical imaging equipment market is understood to be growing steadily, driven by replacement demand for aging equipment and expanding healthcare infrastructure in emerging markets.

A company representative cited the global X-ray security screening market, projected to grow at an average annual rate of 8.9% to reach about USD 6.25 billion by 2030, as a new growth avenue beyond its traditional medical grid business.

This aligns with a strategy to extend the application of its grid technology from medical use into security and baggage inspection as well as industrial non-destructive testing.

In finished equipment such as DR, CBCT, and tomosynthesis systems, the company competes partly with larger medical device makers from the United States, Europe, and Japan, while maintaining a relatively dominant position in the grid component segment.

Growing demand for diagnostic imaging equipment in emerging healthcare markets such as the Middle East and Southeast Asia is cited as a growth opportunity for the finished-equipment business.

However, the medical device industry also faces country-specific regulatory approval processes and a relatively high share of public procurement, which can make quarterly revenue recognition uneven. Within the KOSDAQ medical and precision equipment sector, the company is classified as a relatively small-cap stock.

Outlook

In 2026, the company has participated in a series of international exhibitions, including WHX 2026 in Dubai, ECR 2026 in Vienna, and KIMES 2026 in Seoul, in an effort to secure new buyers across the Middle East, Europe, and the domestic market.

At the Middle East exhibition in particular, the company showcased its finished-equipment lineup, including the low-dose CBCT system 'DeteCT,' the digital tomosynthesis system 'StriXion,' and the diagnostic X-ray system 'DRE Duo,' aiming to strengthen local partnerships.

In January 2026, 'StriXion' was designated as a first-batch innovation product by the Public Procurement Service, providing a foothold for domestic public-sector sales.

A significant portion of IPO proceeds was disclosed as earmarked for expanding grid production capacity and building an integrated factory for next-generation smart X-ray imaging equipment.

The company has stated plans to broaden the application of its grid technology into security and baggage screening as well as industrial non-destructive testing, signaling an intent to reduce reliance on the grid as a single product category.

With the earnings recovery continuing through the second quarter of 2026, a key point to watch is whether this product and market diversification strategy translates into actual revenue contribution in coming quarters.

However, the timing and scale at which overseas exhibition activity and buyer meetings convert into firm supply contracts have not yet been specifically disclosed.

08

Bull factors

World's No.1 Position in X-ray Grids

According to the company, it maintains roughly a 40% global market share in X-ray grids, a core component, supplying multiple customers including some of the world's largest medical imaging device makers.

The oligopolistic structure of the industry makes new entry difficult, keeping competitive intensity relatively low. With exports accounting for more than 80% of revenue, dependence on any single country's demand is also limited.

Quarterly Earnings Recovery

Net profit attributable to owners rose for three consecutive quarters from Q4 2025 through Q2 2026, following a trough in Q3 2025. Profitability improvement was particularly pronounced in Q2 2026, when operating profit jumped 369.0% from the prior quarter.

The trailing four-quarter total of owners' net profit reaching KRW 7.04 billion also indicates a gradually building earnings base.

Diversification into Finished Products and New Markets

The company is expanding beyond its reliance on grids into finished products such as DR systems, CBCT, and digital tomosynthesis.

The designation of StriXion as a Public Procurement Service innovation product in January 2026 opened a domestic public-sector sales channel, while exhibitions in the Middle East and Europe are being used to develop new overseas sales channels.

A strategy to extend grid technology applications into security and industrial non-destructive testing is also cited as an additional growth avenue.

Bear factors

Widening Quarterly Volatility

In Q1 2026, revenue fell 12.5% and operating profit dropped 67.8% from the prior quarter, illustrating considerable quarter-to-quarter swings in results. This suggests that revenue recognition timing can be uneven across quarters given the nature of public procurement and export contracts. Confirming whether the direction of quarterly results remains consistent going forward will be important.

Divergence Between Owners' Net Profit and Operating Profit

While full-year 2025 operating profit rose 23.4%, net profit attributable to owners declined 12.9%, moving in the opposite direction. Net income including non-controlling interests also fell compared with 2024, suggesting that non-operating items or changes in ownership structure affected the bottom line.

Whether operating profit improvement fully translates into growth in profit attributable to shareholders warrants continued monitoring.

Governance-Related Controversy

Reports that the controlling family received a sizable dividend payout following the FY2025 year-end dividend decision drew mixed market reactions regarding the appropriateness of a dividend at a stock price below the IPO level in the company's early period as a listed firm.

The company has explained that it has long maintained a policy of paying dividends when profitable, but additional shareholder return measures or share buyback plans remain undecided.

The shareholding trend of the second-largest shareholder, a financial investor, is also worth monitoring from a supply-demand perspective.

Risk factors

Customer and Product Concentration Risk

According to data at the time of the IPO, grid products accounted for more than 60% of total revenue, indicating relatively high reliance on a single product category.

As the finished-equipment business was described as still in an early revenue stage, fluctuations in grid demand have a relatively large impact on overall results. If supply to a particular major customer represents a high share, changes in that customer's procurement policy could affect results.

Foreign Exchange and Export Risk

With more than 80% of revenue generated from exports, results can be sensitive to currency fluctuations such as the KRW-USD rate. Changes in medical device approval or tariff policies in major export markets such as the United States and Japan are also potential variables. Profit and loss volatility stemming from the operation of overseas subsidiaries is another factor to monitor.

Early-Listing Supply-Demand Risk

At the time of listing, the second-largest shareholder, a financial investor, held a substantial stake, and the proportion of institutional lock-up commitments was relatively low, which has been cited as a potential factor for sell pressure around lock-up expiration dates.

Governance controversy surrounding the first-year dividend decision is another factor that could affect investor sentiment. Disclosures related to changes in free-floating shares warrant continued monitoring.

Overall view

JPI Healthcare posted higher revenue and operating profit in 2025 compared with the prior year on the strength of its position as the world's leading X-ray grid supplier, and net profit attributable to owners rose for three consecutive quarters from a Q3 2025 trough through Q2 2026.

However, over the same period, owners' net profit moved in the opposite direction from operating profit on an annual basis, declining, and quarterly volatility was also evident, with both revenue and operating profit falling in Q1 2026 compared with the prior quarter.

The expansion of finished-product lines, the Public Procurement Service innovation product designation, and participation in Middle East and European exhibitions appear aimed at reducing reliance on the grid as a single product, though concrete order results have not yet been disclosed.

The scale of dividends received by the controlling family following the first-year year-end dividend decision drew controversy, raising governance-related concerns as well.

The financial structure showed signs of stabilization, with the debt-to-equity ratio falling sharply to 30.7% at the end of 2025 from the prior year.

Investors may wish to continue monitoring the direction of future quarterly results, the revenue contribution of new products and markets, and disclosures related to dividends and shareholding.
