Unick: Earnings Recovery Tested by Robotics Push

Unick's 2025 sales and operating profit both rose, but in the third quarter of 2026 operating profit fell and the company swung to a net loss attributable to controlling shareholders, making the robotics push the next test.

02

Business structure

Unick was founded in 1971 as an auto parts specialist and is a mid-sized company that developed Korea's first automotive clock.

Its core products are control parts such as solenoid valves used in transmissions and engines, which regulate fluid flow inside vehicles, and its key technology is opening and closing valves based on electrical signals.

According to the Butler data service, drive-system parts made up 51% of sales, electronics 28% and control parts 21% in the second quarter of 2026. Because drive parts account for just over half of sales, demand for drivetrain components has the largest influence on revenue.

Customers include Hyundai Motor Group and other domestic and overseas automakers, and existing customers such as Hyundai Motor and Hyundai Mobis are cited as entry points for robotics demand. Production runs through domestic plants and a Chinese consolidated subsidiary in Qingdao that makes auto parts.

Within the cumulative results for the first nine months of 2025, the improvement was attributed to higher sales in Korea and better profitability in China.

The company points to its experience developing future-mobility parts, including hydrogen fuel supply systems, and says it handles the full product process in-house, from equipment design to prototyping. Its R&D spending in 2024 was 3.82% of consolidated revenue. Against rivals, the company cites its mass-production and quality-control experience as its differentiator.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual revenue rose from KRW 284.1 billion in 2022 to KRW 329 billion in 2023 (up 15.8%), KRW 374.8 billion in 2024 (up 13.9%) and KRW 430.2 billion in 2025 (up 14.8%), growing for three consecutive years from 2023 through 2025.

Operating profit went from KRW 5.94 billion in 2022 to KRW 9.34 billion in 2023 (up 57.4%), eased to KRW 8.35 billion in 2024 (down 10.6%) and reached KRW 14.4 billion in 2025 (up 72.8%), the largest year of the 2022 to 2025 period. The operating margin was 2.1% in 2022, 2.8% in 2023, 2.2% in 2024 and 3.4% in 2025.

In 2025 operating profit growth of 72.8% far outpaced revenue growth of 14.8%, lifting the margin from 2.2% to 3.4%. Net income attributable to controlling shareholders rose from KRW 3.55 billion in 2022 to KRW 10.8 billion in 2025, growing for three consecutive years from 2023, and climbed 39.9% in 2025.

Net income including non-controlling interests was reported at the same amounts in the same years. The company attributed the 2025 improvement to wider application on new vehicle models and higher demand from major customers.

The debt-to-equity ratio was 116.6% in 2022, 114.2% in 2023, 115.2% in 2024 and 119.9% in 2025, the highest of the four years, with total liabilities of KRW 163.4 billion and total equity of KRW 136.3 billion at the end of 2025.

Operating cash flow went from KRW 4.65 billion in 2022 to KRW 13 billion in 2023, KRW 12.1 billion in 2024 and KRW 19.9 billion in 2025.

On a quarterly basis, revenue rose from KRW 109.7 billion in the third quarter of 2025 to KRW 121.4 billion in the second quarter of 2026, then fell 6.8% to KRW 113.2 billion in the third quarter, up 3.2% from a year earlier while operating profit fell 45.5%, which narrowed the margin.

Operating profit peaked at KRW 5.08 billion in the first quarter of 2026, up 347.2% from the prior quarter, then declined to KRW 4.87 billion and KRW 2.48 billion, two straight quarterly declines.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 4.75 billion in the first quarter of 2026 and KRW 4.62 billion in the second, before a net loss of KRW 796 million in the third quarter. The last four quarters together totaled KRW 9.46 billion.

05

Industry analysis

Domestic automaker production depends heavily on Hyundai Motor Group, so parts makers' sales track the group's output closely. According to NICE, Hyundai Motor Group's domestic production reached 2.6 million units in the first nine months of 2025, up about 2% from a year earlier.

The same agency placed its 2026 earnings outlook for auto parts makers in the lowest category, 'deterioration'. Tariff policy is pushing automaker production toward the United States, which could raise sales volatility for parts makers with domestic bases.

Hyundai Motor Group plans to raise utilization at its US plant and expand local parts sourcing from 60% in 2025 to 80% by 2030. As a result, parts makers may face structural pressures such as lower export volumes to the US, fiercer competition for new model awards and pressure to cut domestic procurement prices.

Electrification investment is expected to be largely completed around 2025, easing the burden, but low initial utilization of electrified facilities is seen as a constraint on recovering earnings power.

Across the industry, OEM sales accounted for 68.5% of parts makers' sales in 2024, well above the 26.7% export share, so automaker production flows drive revenue.

Unick's position in the value chain, whether closer to second- or third-tier suppliers with heavy domestic dependence, shapes how sensitive it is to these shifts. The company's overseas production share is not directly disclosed in its filings, and its Chinese consolidated subsidiary offers the main clue.

Outlook

The robotics business moved into a fuller phase with the July 2026 change to the articles of incorporation and the creation of a new business unit. The company is currently notifying existing customers of its plan to enter robot parts and checking demand.

Equipment investment is under review depending on the pace of the business, but its size and timing have not been set.

An official said the company would use existing relationships with Hyundai Motor and Hyundai Mobis to find robotics customers and mentioned that visible results such as contracts could come around year-end.

Actuators, which account for 30 to 40% of robot cost, require high precision and durability, and the company has pointed to applying its vehicle drivetrain and valve control technology. The company says it has brought equipment design and prototyping in-house and continues R&D spending.

On ownership, founder Chairman Ahn Yeong-gu continued open-market purchases in September 2026, while Ahn Jae-beom has become sole CEO and a share transfer is proceeding in parallel.

On November 10, 2026, the board decided to dispose of 1,799 common shares to reward an in-house suggestion scheme and contests, with a disposal period set for November 11, 2026.

The next checkpoints are whether a year-end contract materializes, any disclosure on equipment investment, and the 2026 annual results expected in early 2027.

08

Bull factors

Profit recovery and sales growth in 2025

Operating profit rose 72.8% to KRW 14.4 billion in 2025 on revenue of KRW 430.2 billion, up 14.8%. The company credited wider application on new vehicle models and higher demand from major customers.

The operating margin reached 3.4%, the highest of the 2022 to 2025 period, so the profit recovery came with margin gains.

Expansion into robot actuators

In July 2026 robot parts manufacturing and sales were added to the articles of incorporation, and a new business unit was set up internally. The company aims to secure the actuator market, which accounts for 30 to 40% of robot cost.

The company says its automotive precision control and solenoid valve technology are technically related because both turn electrical signals into motion.

Founder stake increase and second-generation management

Founder Chairman Ahn Yeong-gu bought 70,000 common shares on the open market between September 16 and 22, 2026, raising his stake to 20.65%. He bought another 30,000 shares between September 23 and 29 of the same year.

Ahn Jae-beom has become sole CEO, and a share transfer from the chairman is reported to be proceeding in parallel.

Bear factors

Third-quarter profit decline and swing to loss

Operating profit in the third quarter of 2026 was KRW 2.48 billion, down 45.5% year on year, and has declined for two straight quarters. The company posted a net loss attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 796 million after a profit in the second quarter. Revenue rose 3.2% from a year earlier over the same period, so sales and profit moved in opposite directions.

Weak industry outlook and production relocation

A Korean credit rating agency placed its 2026 outlook for auto parts makers in the lowest category, 'deterioration'. It said tariff-driven relocation of automaker production to the United States could raise sales volatility for parts makers with domestic bases.

It also pointed out that low initial utilization of electrified facilities constrains recovery in earnings power.

Rising leverage and thin margins

The debt-to-equity ratio rose from 114.2% in 2023 to 119.9% in 2025, with total liabilities of KRW 163.4 billion at the end of 2025. The operating margin stayed at 3.4% in 2025, leaving little cushion relative to sales.

Because the size and timing of robotics equipment investment are not set, new business spending could add to financial pressure.

Risk factors

Customer concentration risk

One analysis says Hyundai Motor Group produces about 80% of domestic automaker output, and parts makers' sales track the group's production closely. Because the group is expanding localization in the US, the allocation of domestic volumes could change.

With a high reliance on automaker volumes, changes in the group's production plans could pass directly into the company's results.

Execution risk in the new business

The company said at the time of reporting that robotics had not yet reached a concrete contract stage. The scale and timing of equipment investment are also undecided. Because actuators require high precision and durability, customer qualification and the shift to mass production could take time.

Financial and policy risk

The debt-to-equity ratio rose to 119.9% in 2025 from 2023, but operating cash flow grew to KRW 19.9 billion in 2025, which shows some capacity to service obligations. Policy variables such as tariffs and reduced electric vehicle subsidies could shake demand, according to a rating agency's assessment. Because profits are sensitive to margin changes, policy shifts could show up quickly in quarterly results.

Overall view

Unick's 2025 revenue of KRW 430.2 billion and operating profit of KRW 14.4 billion both grew, and operating cash flow expanded to KRW 19.9 billion.

However, operating profit has declined for two straight quarters after peaking in the first quarter of 2026, and the third quarter brought a net loss attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 796 million.

The last four quarters' combined net income attributable to controlling shareholders totaled KRW 9.46 billion. The auto parts backdrop still carries tariff and production relocation pressure, as shown by a rating agency placing its 2026 outlook in the lowest category.

The debt-to-equity ratio rose to 119.9% in 2025 from 2023. In new business, the company is preparing robot actuators, but contracts and the scale of equipment investment are not yet set.

Bullish factors include the 2025 earnings recovery, the founder's share purchases and the technical link to robotics, while bearish factors include the third-quarter profit decline, the industry slowdown and financial pressure.

The next checkpoints are the preliminary fourth-quarter 2026 results and the operating margin in the 2026 annual business report.
