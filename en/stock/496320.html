02

Business structure

Bonsystems is a robot-component maker based in Changwon, South Gyeongsang Province, founded in 2015. Its incorporation date is June 2015, and its head office is in Uichang-gu, Changwon. It develops and produces reducers and actuators based on cycloidal gearing in-house.

Its core product is an integrated actuator that combines a reducer, motor, encoder and driver in a single unit. The company built its slim reducers and actuators on a pinless cycloidal tooth profile that transmits power without roller pins.

It says the design cuts volume by up to 70% compared with conventional involute tooth profiles. Its lineup runs from cycloidal reducers to micro actuators, smart actuators and robot modules. An 18mm-class ultra-small actuator aimed at humanoid robot hands has completed development. It also offers customized design and robot hardware ODM services.

The revenue split by segment could not be confirmed in this research. The company said sales tied to reducers, actuators and robot modules grew about 47-fold from 2023. Its chief executive described robot reducers as a key drive component with high reliance on overseas supply.

Domestic peers such as Hyundai-affiliated Hyzen R&M and Samhyun also make actuators and reducers. Compared with listed robotics names in the same sector, such as Rainbow Robotics, Doosan Robotics and Robotis, Bonsystems is an early-stage company with far smaller revenue.

04

Outlook

The near-term question is whether the mass-production setup actually comes online. The company plans to complete its second plant in the second half of 2026 and expand its reducer and actuator production base.

It also aims to finish installing actuator manufacturing equipment within the year to build a mass-production facility. Even if the equipment starts running, the pace at which operating losses narrow depends on whether gross margin improves.

After the defense unmanned-system trial, the company plans to widen application to various unmanned platforms and seek new business opportunities. Whether actual orders or supply contracts are disclosed after the trial is the item to watch.

The company has targeted a transfer to KOSDAQ during 2026-2027. KONEX-listed companies can use a fast-track transfer program that eases some listing requirements. Continued losses and the balance sheet could be assessed in the transfer review.

The company's own view is that the humanoid industry's focus is shifting from prototype demonstrations to field deployment and mass-producibility. If that shift continues, component demand could grow, but timing would depend on customers' production schedules.

Room for operating-margin improvement depends on scale and fixed-cost absorption, which is a statement about business metrics and not a view on the share price.

Bull factors

Fast revenue growth and rising exports

Revenue for 2025 rose 93% from the prior year. The company said its full-year 2025 revenue was about 2.6 times the 2023 level. It also said first-quarter 2026 exports exceeded full-year 2025 exports by more than double. If top-line growth continues, fixed-cost burden could be spread over a larger base.

Funding and production build-out

In June 2026 the company completed a KRW 14.5 billion capital increase with institutions including NH Investment & Securities and IBK Securities. Over the past three years it has attracted a total of KRW 24.5 billion from domestic venture investors.

A second plant planned for the second half would broaden its production base. Total equity turning positive is also an improvement in financial stability.

Wider applications: humanoids and defense

The company completed a sea trial with its actuator applied on a global defense group's unmanned surface vessel. It said the actuator ran stably even under wave and hull-motion conditions at sea. It has also finished developing an ultra-small actuator for humanoid robot hands. Diversified end markets could reduce dependence on any single market.

Risk factors

Macro and industry

The humanoid and service robot markets are still at an early stage of mass production, and delays in customers' production schedules could push back component demand. Core drive components are an area with high overseas reliance, so price and performance competition with foreign rivals is unavoidable.

Weaker rates or investor sentiment could affect funding conditions and share-price volatility across the robot theme.

Regulatory and market

One report expects financial investors' main exit to occur after a KOSDAQ transfer. The transfer review could focus on losses and capital structure, and the timetable could change. Share supply from existing investors after a transfer, and market-warning measures, could also drive price swings.

Execution and financial

If the second plant and mass-production equipment do not start on schedule, or if yield and quality problems arise, revenue recognition and the cost structure could suffer. As the humanoid industry shifts its focus to mass-producibility, proving quality and reliability becomes more important.

With capital increases in both February and June 2026, any further issuance could dilute existing shareholders. Collaboration with global customers rests on company statements, and contract size and terms could not be confirmed from public sources.
