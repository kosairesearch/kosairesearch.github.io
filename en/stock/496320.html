02

Business structure

Bonsystems is a robot drive-system company founded in June 2015 and based in Changwon, and it listed on the KONEX market on September 8, 2025. Its core product is the cycloidal reducer, and it adds actuators and robot drive modules to offer a Full-Stack structure that handles design, machining and testing in-house.

Its business divides into supply of reducers, actuators and drive modules, plus made-to-order work such as rotor sail and prototype production. The BCSA series anchors its lineup, and a NICE Investors Service technical report dated November 27, 2025 described the BCSA V4 launch as completing its actuator range.

In drive modules, it has developed in-wheel modules for AGV and AMR use and a hybrid drive unit for four-legged and four-wheeled platforms. NICE Investors Service projected the global robot and automation actuator market to grow from about USD 24.4 billion in 2024 to USD 87.7 billion in 2034, roughly 13.65% a year.

The domestic robot supply chain consists of finished robot makers, system integrators and core component suppliers for drive systems, sensors and controllers, and Bonsystems sits in the core component layer.

It competes with domestic reducer and actuator makers, and it positions high-torque, thin-profile and lightweight technology as its differentiators. Recent press reports say Bonsystems is moving to build in-house production capacity for core drive modules for humanoid robots.

The company is reported to be expanding production capacity for robot reducers and actuators with a second plant targeted for completion in the second half of 2026.

Recent trends

Revenue for 2025 was KRW 6,447,527 thousand, up 93.17% from the prior year. The operating loss for the same year was KRW 1,614,545 thousand, much wider than the KRW 730,785 thousand loss in 2024. The net loss was KRW 1,909,800 thousand, and the operating loss equaled 25.04% of revenue, compared with 21.9% in 2024.

Operating cash flow from ordinary activities showed an outflow of KRW 2,262,893 thousand. As of year-end 2025, the equity ratio stood at 36.95% and the borrowings dependency ratio at 50.77%.

A disclosure dated January 28, 2026 showed the company resolved to acquire land and buildings in Changwon for KRW 1,618,000 thousand, with the purpose of securing factory land to expand existing operations.

On February 12, 2026, the company decided on a third-party rights offering to raise KRW 496,121,380 for operating funds, at an issue price of KRW 31,060 per share.

Recent press reports say the company confirmed a KRW 11.5 billion investment round led in part by a fund associated with IBK Securities, which implied a valuation of about KRW 90 billion.

At the extraordinary shareholder meeting held on September 30, 2026, all four agenda items, including charter amendments and the election of an executive director, were approved. On the same day, the company granted stock options for 30,000 common shares to three employees at an exercise price of KRW 50,000 per share.

The closing price on October 7, 2026 was KRW 1,023,000, and as of 02:10 KST on November 11, 2026 the current price was KRW 1,176,000 with trading value at zero.

04

Outlook

The outlook for Bonsystems can be framed around two axes: top-line growth on one side, and profitability and financial burden on the other.

On the top line, revenue rose 93.17% from the prior year, and NICE Investors Service cited the recovery of reducer sales in 2024 and the recognition of rotor sail revenue as growth drivers.

NICE Investors Service said the spread of humanoid and AMR robots, the rise of physical AI and domestic component policies are combining to strengthen medium-term growth momentum. The same report noted that the company is still in an early growth phase with limited profitability.

On profitability, the operating loss ratio rose from 21.9% in 2024 to 25.04% in 2025, so revenue growth has not yet narrowed the operating loss. On production, completion of the humanoid drive module facility and the second plant will determine how far capacity can expand.

On capital, the 36.95% equity ratio and the investment proceeds have widened financial room, while the 50.77% borrowings dependency ratio and the operating cash outflow remain burdens. Share count increases from the rights offering and stock option exercises could dilute existing shareholders.

The market growth rate for actuators is an outside estimate and is not a metric directly tied to the company's revenue. The next checkpoints are the completion timing of the second plant and whether the operating loss trend changes in upcoming quarterly results.

Bull factors

Full-Stack Drive Technology

NICE Investors Service assessed that Bonsystems has a structure that allows it to design and build reducers, actuators and drive modules in an integrated way. The report describes in-house design, machining and testing as a basis for quality and delivery competitiveness.

Multiple domestic and US patents, along with analysis and verification infrastructure, are cited as support for technical reliability. The structure is read as a foundation for expanding beyond a single component supplier into a robot system development partner.

Domestic Component Localization

NICE Investors Service projected the global robot and automation actuator market to grow about 13.65% a year through 2034. Demand for high-performance humanoid, quadruped and AMR robots is rising, and finished robot makers are seeking to reduce reliance on overseas parts for cost and supply stability.

As a domestic core component supplier, the company is cited as a possible beneficiary of localization policy. The external forecast is an institutional estimate and is not directly tied to the company's results.

Revenue Growth and Capital Build-Up

Revenue for 2025 rose 93.17% from the prior year, confirming top-line expansion. A February 2026 rights offering and the KRW 11.5 billion investment round strengthened the capital base.

The January 2026 land and building acquisition secured factory land for expansion, and in-house production for humanoid drive modules is under way. A broader production base could support further growth in revenue.

Bear factors

Persistent Operating Losses

The 2025 operating loss of KRW 1,614,545 thousand widened from KRW 730,785 thousand in 2024. The operating loss ratio also rose to 25.04% from 21.9% in 2024. The net loss of KRW 1,909,800 thousand shows that revenue growth has not yet reached the bottom line. A timeline for returning to profit is not yet visible.

Cash Outflow and Financial Burden

Operating cash flow from ordinary activities in 2025 was an outflow of KRW 2,262,893 thousand. The borrowings dependency ratio stood at 50.77%, a relatively high reliance on external funding. The land acquisition and rights offering in 2026 also reflect a structure that depends on outside financing and capital raising. The equity ratio at year-end 2025 was 36.95%.

Share Price Gap and Dilution

The February 2026 third-party rights offering was priced at KRW 31,060 per share, and the October 7, 2026 closing price was KRW 1,023,000. Price movement over the same period was very large. Stock options for 30,000 shares granted in September 2026 increased potential share count.

Because earnings are negative, earnings-based valuation metrics are hard to compute, so price formation can vary with how market participants interpret the company.

Risk factors

Macro

Recent press reports say government bond yields rose close to their highest levels in 24 years, keeping rate caution in place. Higher rates raise funding costs and could add to interest burdens for a company with high borrowing dependence.

Because the company relies on investment rounds and rights offerings, it is exposed to market interest rates and capital market sentiment.

Industry and Policy

Growth in the robot market depends on the timing of mass production for new platforms such as humanoid and AMR robots. If mass-production schedules of finished robot makers slip, component demand could also be delayed.

Because policy support is focused on localization, changes in the policy environment could affect business conditions.

Execution

Whether construction of the second plant and the humanoid drive module facility proceeds on schedule determines how far capacity can expand. Delays in facility investment or rising costs could push back the timing of narrower losses. Given heavy reliance on capital raising, the terms of future funding also act as an execution risk.

08

Overall view

Bonsystems is a robot drive-system company that has internalized reducers, actuators and drive modules, and it benefits from humanoid and AMR demand and domestic component localization as strengths.

On the other hand, the 2025 operating loss widened to KRW 1,614,545 thousand and the operating loss ratio rose to 25.04%, so its profit model has not yet been proven. In 2026, disclosures on land acquisition, a rights offering and investment proceeds have continued, and production capacity is being built out.

However, the 50.77% borrowings dependency ratio and the operating cash outflow show that the financial burden remains. The share price has moved sharply between the February 2026 issue price of KRW 31,060 and the current price of KRW 1,176,000, and how market participants interpret that gap can differ.
