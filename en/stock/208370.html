Body Composition Devices Grow, Quarterly Swings Persist

02

Business structure

SELVAS Healthcare operates U.S. subsidiary HIMS, Inc. and Chinese subsidiary Beijing SELVAS Healthcare as wholly owned units to support overseas sales, alongside its medical diagnostic devices such as body composition analyzers and automatic blood pressure monitors and its assistive technology products for the visually impaired.

Product mix shows body composition analyzers at 38.61% of revenue, Braille note-takers at 28.91%, automatic blood pressure monitors at 10.46%, audio book readers at 5.06%, and other items at 16.96%.

Export exposure is heavy, with body composition analyzers and Braille note-takers deriving the large majority of their sales from overseas markets. The body composition analyzer business has grown further in 2026; the company said this product line's share of total revenue had risen to about 60%.

The parent company is SELVAS AI, an AI voice-recognition and healthcare firm; SELVAS AI held a 38.12% stake as of June 30, 2026. SELVAS AI disposed of part of its holding in November 2025, reducing its stake from 40.29% to 38.08%.

Within the group, affiliate Mediana is preparing to launch new patient monitoring and automated CPR devices, forming another pillar of the group's hardware business alongside SELVAS Healthcare.

Competitively, the company faces global specialists in body composition analysis and domestic and overseas niche players in visual assistive technology.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual revenue rose from KRW 27.7 billion in 2022 to KRW 29.8 billion in 2023 (up 7.7% year on year) and KRW 31.7 billion in 2024 (up 6.3%), the largest of the four disclosed years, before falling to KRW 30.8 billion in 2025 (down 2.8%).

Operating profit increased from KRW 2.32 billion in 2022 to KRW 3.35 billion in 2023 (up 44.3%), slipped to KRW 3.27 billion in 2024 (down 2.2%), then swung to an operating loss of KRW 162 million in 2025. The operating margin fell from 8.4% in 2022 and 11.2% in 2023 and 10.3% in 2024 to -0.5% in 2025.

The company attributed the decline in operating profit to higher costs from new R&D investment and an increase in bad debt expenses.

By contrast, net income attributable to controlling shareholders rose from KRW 2.65 billion in 2022 to KRW 5.17 billion in 2023 (up 95.1%, the largest of the four years), fell to KRW 3.15 billion in 2024 (down 39.0%), then recovered to KRW 3.89 billion in 2025 (up 23.4%).

On a quarterly basis, the operating result swung from a loss of KRW 1.03 billion in the second quarter of 2025 to a profit of KRW 541 million in the third quarter, back to a loss of KRW 21.9 million in the fourth quarter, then to a profit of KRW 669 million in the first quarter of 2026, before falling 44.3% quarter on quarter (turning profitable year on year) to KRW 372 million in the second quarter of 2026.

Quarterly net income attributable to controlling shareholders swung far more sharply, from a loss of KRW 456 million in the second quarter of 2025 to a profit of KRW 509 million in the third quarter, KRW 3.64 billion in the fourth quarter (up 615.6% quarter on quarter), and KRW 13.2 billion in the first quarter of 2026 (up 263.0%), before reversing to a loss of KRW 8.68 billion in the second quarter of 2026, a loss that widened from the year-earlier quarter.

The fact that quarterly net income swings far exceeded swings in operating profit suggests non-operating items were the main driver of quarterly results.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders over the most recent four quarters (third quarter of 2025 through second quarter of 2026) totaled KRW 8.7 billion, while operating cash flow rose from KRW 50.81 million in 2022 to KRW 3.47 billion in 2023 and KRW 4.5 billion in 2024 before falling to KRW 305 million in 2025.

05

Industry analysis

Observers note that medical device market growth is expected amid aging demographics and the spread of digital healthcare, with assistive devices for the visually impaired also projected to see double-digit annual growth as welfare policy expands and AI/ICT features are adopted.

Demand for body composition analyzers has been broadening beyond hospitals, and the company has strengthened cooperation with domestic and global fitness distribution channels to expand both the domestic and overseas markets.

The assistive technology segment, centered on Braille note-takers and audio book readers, tends to see steady demand tied to overseas welfare budgets, including in the United States.

At the parent group level, the AI medical and healthcare segment has become a core pillar, accounting for about 85% of SELVAS AI's consolidated revenue as of the third quarter of 2025.

Group affiliate Mediana has benefited from a recovery in demand for and rising exports of patient monitoring devices (PMD) and automated external defibrillators (AED), according to the company.

Within the KOSDAQ medical and precision device sector, SELVAS Healthcare is a small-cap name; one outlet reported in November 2025 that the stock is better classified as a theme-driven small/mid-cap medical device stock than a market leader.

The same report noted that no brokerage consensus or target price had been published for the stock, meaning no clear mid- to long-term valuation benchmark existed at the time.

06

Outlook

At the KIMES exhibition held in Seoul in 2026, the company focused on showcasing hospital-grade and wellness-grade products. At the event, the company's head of sales said body composition analyzer revenue had grown 150% the previous year and set a goal of expanding total revenue by more than 35% year on year in 2026.

The business mix, once centered on blood pressure monitors, has shifted rapidly, with body composition analyzers now accounting for about 60% of total revenue, the company said.

The company said it is preparing to launch new medical devices, including a stroke diagnostic guidance device, in addition to its existing body composition analyzer and blood pressure monitor businesses.

It also laid out plans to combine parent SELVAS AI's AI technology and cloud systems to expand into a data- and AI-driven platform business. Separately, the company said it is participating in a second-phase ARPA-H project worth KRW 12.7 billion, extending its R&D into emergency medical care.

However, the timing and scale at which these new projects and products will translate into disclosed revenue and profit have not yet been confirmed in financial statements.

Bull factors

Revenue Mix Shifting Toward Body Composition Devices

The body composition analyzer business now accounts for about 60% of total revenue, and the company said this product's revenue grew 150% the previous year.

A product mix skewed toward exports supports further overseas expansion, and cooperation with domestic and global fitness distribution channels offers an additional growth path. Broadening demand from hospital to wellness markets is also a positive factor.

Low Leverage and a Stable Balance Sheet

The debt ratio has stayed low at 11.0% in 2023, 10.1% in 2024, and 11.1% in 2025. Total equity stood at KRW 71.1 billion at the end of 2025, keeping the balance sheet relatively solid despite the operating loss. Operating cash flow also remained positive at KRW 305 million in 2025.

R&D Projects and a New Product Pipeline

Participation in a second-phase ARPA-H project worth KRW 12.7 billion and preparations to launch new medical devices including a stroke diagnostic guidance device point to potential medium-term product expansion. The company is also pursuing integration with parent SELVAS AI's AI and cloud technology.

Bear factors

2025 Swing to an Operating Loss

Operating profit swung to a loss of KRW 162 million in 2025, and the operating margin fell to -0.5%. The company attributed this to higher costs from new R&D investment and an increase in bad debt expenses.

Revenue itself fell from KRW 31.7 billion in 2024 to KRW 30.8 billion in 2025, marking a pause in top-line growth.

Large Quarter-to-Quarter Earnings Swings

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders reversed sharply from KRW 13.2 billion in the first quarter of 2026 to a loss of KRW 8.68 billion in the second quarter, with quarterly swings far exceeding those in operating profit. This suggests non-operating items are driving quarterly results and lowers the predictability of earnings.

Changes in the Controlling Shareholder's Stake

Controlling shareholder SELVAS AI disposed of part of its holding in November 2025, reducing its stake from 40.29% to 38.08%. Changes in the group's ownership policy remain a factor that could affect trading supply and demand.

Risk factors

Profitability

If new R&D investment continues, the resulting cost burden could delay a recovery in the operating margin. Volatility in one-off items such as bad debt expenses also makes quarterly results harder to predict.

Currency and Export Dependence

Exports account for a large majority of sales for flagship products such as body composition analyzers and Braille note-takers, leaving results sensitive to currency swings and changes in overseas customer demand. The cost structure tied to operating U.S. and Chinese subsidiaries is also a variable.

Ownership and Trading Supply/Demand

A history of stake disposals by the controlling shareholder combined with theme-driven trading flows could amplify share price volatility. Limited formal brokerage coverage can also create information asymmetry for investors.

Overall view

SELVAS Healthcare is a medical device company built around body composition analyzers and visual assistive technology; in 2025 its revenue fell to KRW 30.8 billion and it swung to an operating loss of KRW 162 million, yet net income attributable to controlling shareholders rose to KRW 3.89 billion.

In 2026, operating profit alternated between gains and losses quarter to quarter, and controlling-shareholder net income swung sharply from KRW 13.2 billion in the first quarter to a loss of KRW 8.68 billion in the second.

Business restructuring continues, with body composition analyzers gaining revenue share and export exposure rising, alongside new R&D and product initiatives such as the ARPA-H project and the stroke diagnostic guidance device.

The balance sheet remains stable with a debt ratio in the low teens, but the operating margin, which fell to -0.5% in 2025, has continued to swing between quarterly recovery and renewed weakness.

Changes in controlling shareholder SELVAS AI's stake and limited brokerage coverage are factors worth watching from a trading supply and demand perspective.

The next quarterly and annual reports will be the key points to check for a stable recovery in the operating margin and the revenue contribution of new initiatives.
