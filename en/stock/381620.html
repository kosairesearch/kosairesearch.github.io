02

Business structure

Zenix Robotics, founded in 2010 and based in Cheonan, Chungcheongnam-do, designs, builds and installs automated material handling systems (AMHS) for semiconductor, display, automotive and secondary battery plants. Its core products are the Stocker, which stores and moves FOUPs carrying wafers, and AGVs and AMRs.

The product line serves clean environments such as semiconductor and display processes, and general logistics such as automotive production lines and battery assembly.

Per reports on the 2026 half-year report, first-half 2026 sales by product were about KRW 3.27 billion for Stocker, about KRW 1.68 billion for AGV/AMR, about KRW 3.24 billion for Job Change and about KRW 370 million for other equipment. On an amount basis, Stocker and Job Change are of similar scale.

On customers, reports said supply of automation products to Hyundai Motor's Savannah plant in Georgia, USA was completed in April 2025. Talks on Stocker supply for Samsung Electronics' Taylor, Texas fab are reported, and the company is said to hold more than half of Samsung Electronics' Stocker share.

The business has relied on made-to-order, customized projects, and the company says it plans to shift toward an AGV/AMR brand, standardization and a mass-production lineup. Valueline's company overview notes that the company localized the first laser-guided AGV in Korea. Hyundai Movex is a listed peer in logistics automation.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual sales rose from KRW 61.3 billion in 2023 to KRW 63.5 billion in 2024, a 3.5% increase, then fell to KRW 22.7 billion in 2025, a 64.3% decline. Among 2023 to 2025, 2024 was the largest year and 2025 the smallest.

Operating profit was KRW 3.19 billion in 2023 and KRW 4.31 billion in 2024, up 35.0%, before the company swung to an operating loss of KRW 12.3 billion in 2025. Operating margin moved from 5.2% in 2023 and 6.8% in 2024 to -54.3% in 2025.

Attributable net profit was KRW 3.13 billion in 2023 and KRW 6.05 billion in 2024, up 93.0%, before turning into an attributable net loss of KRW 7.32 billion in 2025. Net income including non-controlling interests was the same as attributable net profit in 2023 and 2024, and a net loss of KRW 7.32 billion in 2025.

The company attributed the 2025 decline to lower sales from fewer orders and higher R&D expenses. The debt-to-equity ratio was 192.3% in 2023, 40.6% in 2024 and 51.0% in 2025.

Quarterly sales were KRW 8.2 billion in Q3 2025 and KRW 1.89 billion in Q4 2025, a 77.0% drop quarter on quarter, and then rose for three straight quarters to KRW 4.32 billion in Q1 2026, KRW 4.48 billion in Q2 2026 and KRW 6.16 billion in Q3 2026.

The operating loss was KRW 3.79 billion in Q3 2025 and KRW 4.07 billion in Q4 2025, then narrowed to KRW 3.11 billion, KRW 2.53 billion and KRW 2.17 billion over the next three quarters, and the company has recorded losses for five consecutive quarters since Q3 2025.

The attributable net loss widened to KRW 2 billion in Q3 2026 from the prior quarter but narrowed year on year. Per reports on the half-year report, gross profit turned to a surplus of about KRW 200 million in Q2 2026.

05

Industry analysis

The logistics automation market that Zenix Robotics serves rests on two pillars: clean-process automation for semiconductors and displays, and general production lines for automotive and secondary batteries.

The core products, Stockers and AGVs/AMRs, depend on fab expansion and equipment replacement demand in semiconductors. A November 2025 report said easing concerns over investment delays could accelerate US investment in manufacturing, semiconductors and robotics infrastructure.

For Hyundai Motor, the company says follow-on supply tied to further expansion is under discussion. For Samsung Electronics, Stocker supply talks for the Taylor, Texas fab and an Austin plant have been reported. The scale and timing of follow-on supply are under NDA, so no figures are available.

The company differentiates by offering both Stockers and AGV/AMR, but a direct comparison with larger logistics automation players cannot be confirmed from disclosures. Hyundai Movex is a listed peer in logistics automation.

Because much of the business is project-based, revenue depends on order timing and on installation and inspection schedules, and the company has said revenue recognition follows design, build, installation and acceptance, which creates a lag between backlog and sales.

Outlook

In reports on the 2026 half-year report, the company said the backlog stood at about KRW 32 billion and that the Q2 gross profit surplus supports faster revenue conversion in the second half. However, operating cash flow was an outflow in the first half, cash and equivalents declined and short-term borrowings rose.

Whether backlog turns into sales and better operating cash flow is therefore the central check. The new facility investment disclosed on November 11, 2026, equal to 10.49% of equity, aims to expand production capacity and facilities, running from November 11, 2026 to May 31, 2027.

Whether capacity actually rises after completion can be checked against operating results and equipment utilization in 2027. Product strategy focuses on AGV/AMR branding, standardization and a mass-production lineup.

Follow-on supply tied to further Hyundai Motor expansion and Stocker talks for Samsung Electronics' Texas fab remain at the discussion stage. New roadmaps such as port logistics automation and an AI-based control platform have been announced, but no disclosure confirms when they will contribute revenue. The 2026 annual report will summarize annual order conversion and the operating loss trend.

If follow-on supply to Hyundai Motor or the Samsung Electronics talks are delayed or fall through, the pace of backlog conversion could slow.

Financial and cash-flow

The 2025 net loss was KRW 7.32 billion and the attributable net loss over the last four quarters totaled KRW 6.89 billion. Operating cash flow was an outflow in the first half of 2026 and short-term borrowings increased. Share price volatility and liquidity

Overall view

Zenix Robotics recorded sales of KRW 22.7 billion and an operating loss of KRW 12.3 billion in 2025 after swinging to deficit, and in 2026 quarterly sales have risen while the operating loss has narrowed.

Strengths include a backlog of about KRW 32 billion per reports on the 2026 half-year report, a Q2 gross profit surplus, and talks on follow-on supply with Hyundai Motor and Samsung Electronics.

Weaknesses include the 64.3% sales decline and -54.3% operating margin in 2025, an attributable net loss of KRW 6.89 billion over the last four quarters, and the first-half 2026 operating cash outflow with higher short-term borrowings. The two sides coexist with comparable weight.

The next checks are how fast backlog converts into sales, whether narrowing losses turn into profit, and whether cash flow improves. The 2026 annual report will fix full-year results and the scale of backlog conversion, and the May 31, 2027 end of the investment period will show whether capacity has actually expanded.

Whether the AGV/AMR standardization and mass-production strategy improves profitability is another point to watch.
