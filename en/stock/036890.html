02

Business structure

Jinsung TEC was founded in 1975 and makes undercarriage parts for construction equipment, mainly rollers and idlers. It has been listed on KOSDAQ since 2000. According to FnGuide's breakdown, construction-equipment parts accounted for 96.39% of sales at year-end 2024 and fuel-cell parts for 3.61%.

The fuel-cell parts are consumable pressure plates and manifolds supplied to a domestic fuel-cell maker, and that segment grew from KRW 8.8 billion in 2019 to KRW 17 billion in 2023. Caterpillar is the largest customer, at an average 57.6% of sales in 2019 to 2023.

In 2021 Caterpillar accounted for 60.8% of sales, Hitachi 13.7% and a Korean construction-equipment maker 13.4%, so those three made up 88%. A 2025 report said Caterpillar's share now approaches 70% of sales. Production sites include Korea, the U.S. through JINSUNG AMERICA with a Georgia site, China and Thailand.

The 2022 capacity was KRW 200 billion in Korea, KRW 150 billion in China and KRW 100 billion in the U.S. Thailand is a four-plant project with cumulative investment of KRW 120 billion, and according to a 2024 report, the first plant was completed. The company holds a global market share of about 10%, ranked sixth.

Its 2024 export share was 35.7% on FnGuide's breakdown. Its Q1 2026 company commentary said sales of roller-type products increased.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual results peaked in 2022, declined through 2024 and recovered in 2025. Sales of KRW 541 billion in 2022 were the largest in the filed period, with operating profit of KRW 66.3 billion and an operating margin of 12.3%. A 2024 report described 2022 sales as the company's highest since its founding.

Sales fell to KRW 485.1 billion in 2023 (down 10.3%), operating profit to KRW 39.6 billion (down 40.3%) and the margin to 8.2%. In 2024 sales were KRW 390.5 billion (down 19.5%), operating profit KRW 24.4 billion (down 38.3%) and the margin 6.3%, the lowest in the filed period.

A 2024 brokerage view cited a construction-market slowdown and Caterpillar's pricing pressure as margin drags. In 2025 sales recovered to KRW 463.8 billion (up 18.8%), operating profit to KRW 47.9 billion (up 96.2%) and the margin to 10.3%.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 32.6 billion (up 55.7%), and total net income including non-controlling interests was also KRW 32.6 billion.

Operating cash flow went from KRW 33.3 billion in 2022 to KRW 87.6 billion in 2023, then KRW 48.7 billion in 2024 and KRW 21.7 billion in 2025, the lowest in the filed period even as profit rose. The debt ratio fell from 92.2% in 2022 to 72.8% in 2025, and total equity at year-end 2025 was KRW 297.5 billion.

Quarterly results moved from sales of KRW 118.3 billion and operating profit of KRW 12.7 billion in Q3 2025 to sales of KRW 136.4 billion and operating profit of KRW 12.9 billion in Q1 2026.

The Q1 2026 company commentary attributed the gain to lower interest rates and expanding North American infrastructure investment, which lifted construction-equipment demand. Q2 2026 sales of KRW 152.8 billion were the highest of the filed quarters, but operating profit fell 11.3% to KRW 11.5 billion.

Q1 2026 was the peak for operating profit, followed by two consecutive quarterly declines in Q2 and Q3 2026. Q2 net income attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 34 billion well exceeded operating profit, so the size and content of non-operating items should be checked in the filings.

Q3 2026 sales of KRW 141.5 billion rose 19.6% year on year, but operating profit was KRW 5.14 billion, down 59.4%, and the company swung to a net loss attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 13.4 billion.

The sum of net income attributable to controlling shareholders over the last four quarters (Q4 2025 to Q3 2026) is KRW 43.2 billion.

05

Industry analysis

Jinsung TEC's end market is construction equipment such as excavators, and demand is linked to interest rates and North American infrastructure investment, as the Q1 2026 commentary explained.

After the 2016 U.S. presidential election, sales of the U.S. subsidiary JINSUNG AMERICA rose from KRW 62.4 billion in 2016 to KRW 103.7 billion in 2018 as Caterpillar's U.S. sales grew, and a 2024 report cited this as a precedent for policy-driven gains.

The same report projected Caterpillar's group sales would rise from USD 65 billion in 2025 to USD 69.6 billion in 2026. The analysis assumes that customer earnings cycles move in step with Jinsung TEC's sales. The company holds a global share of about 10%, ranked sixth.

The cycle has moved from a construction slowdown in 2024 to higher sales in 2025 and in Q1 and Q2 2026. On diversification, a 2025 report said the company also supplies Hitachi and Doosan Bobcat.

A 2024 report said the Thai plant could capture demand that is shifting away from Chinese suppliers, using new products such as rubber tracks. Policy variables include the scale of U.S. infrastructure spending and the direction of interest rates. How much these variables move North American roller demand will show up in future quarterly sales.

Bull factors

Recovery tied to Caterpillar's cycle

Sales in 2025 rose 18.8% to KRW 463.8 billion, and Q2 2026 sales of KRW 152.8 billion were the highest of the filed quarters. The Q1 2026 commentary linked roller product sales growth to the recovery in North American construction-equipment demand. Q3 2026 sales of KRW 141.5 billion were below Q2 but above the prior-year quarter.

Thai production base and new products

Thailand is a four-plant base with cumulative investment of KRW 120 billion, and according to a 2024 report the first plant was completed. A 2025 report said company-wide sales could expand once the Thai plant ramps up. Rubber-track products were also cited as a way to capture demand that is shifting away from Chinese suppliers.

Balance-sheet repair and margin recovery

The debt ratio fell from 92.2% in 2022 to 72.8% in 2025, and total equity at year-end 2025 was KRW 297.5 billion. The operating margin recovered from 6.3% in 2024 to 10.3% in 2025. Bulls argue this financial capacity can cushion a weaker customer cycle.

Bear factors

Q3 profit collapse and swing to loss

Q3 2026 operating profit was KRW 5.14 billion, down 59.4% year on year, and the company swung to a net loss attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 13.4 billion. Sales rose 19.6% in the same quarter, so profitability fell sharply. The size and content of non-operating items will be visible in the periodic filing.

Caterpillar concentration and price pressure

Caterpillar averaged 57.6% of sales in 2019 to 2023, and a 2025 report said its share approaches 70%. A 2024 brokerage report cited Caterpillar's price pressure as a drag on margins. Because price negotiations run on a six-month cycle, the customer's bargaining power can feed into results quickly.

Weaker cash generation

Operating cash flow fell from KRW 33.3 billion in 2022 to KRW 21.7 billion in 2025, the lowest in the filed period. Cash generation diverged from the 2025 rise in operating profit. The reasons sit in the cash flow statement and working-capital lines of the periodic filing.

10

Risk factors

Customer concentration

High exposure to Caterpillar means its volume and pricing changes flow directly into sales and profit. The 2024 margin decline showed this structure at work. The pace of customer diversification is the next point to track.

Policy and cycle swings

Interest rates and North American infrastructure policy shape the direction of demand. There is a precedent of sharp subsidiary sales growth after the 2016 U.S. election, but demand can fall if policy expectations reverse. If the construction-equipment cycle turns down again, a sales decline similar to 2024 is possible.

Overseas production and expansion execution

If the Thai plant does not reach planned utilization or new-product production on schedule, the profit contribution from the expansion could be delayed. A large overseas footprint also means exchange-rate moves affect won-denominated results. Volatility in non-operating items, such as the Q3 2026 losses, is another variable to watch.
