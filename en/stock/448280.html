02

Business structure

Ecoeye, founded in 2005 and listed on KOSDAQ in 2023, is a greenhouse gas reduction specialist. Its core model is to carry out emission reduction projects, have the credits issued by bodies such as UNFCCC, Verra and Gold Standard, and then sell them in domestic and overseas markets.

Main project types include high-efficiency cookstove distribution, prevention of methane leakage from PNG, and mangrove reforestation, with hydropower, biomass power and waste refrigerant recovery being added.

In the regulated market, UNFCCC-certified credits are converted into KOC and i-KOC units and sold to domestic allocation-covered companies, while in the voluntary market credits are issued and sold through Verra and Gold Standard.

Since 2025 the company has used its trading membership at the European Energy Exchange (EEX) to pursue EU Allowance (EUA) trading in earnest. From 2026 it has sold credits meeting CORSIA standards to airlines, and the company cites this as a driver of second-quarter 2026 revenue growth.

In competition, Ecoeye accounted for 49.63% of overseas external-project certifications from 2021 to 2025, which ranks it first in Korea. It distributes more than 64 million emission-reduction products across 14 countries.

Registration to credit issuance takes at least 3.6 years, so project management capability acts as an entry barrier. Credit sales remain the core of revenue, with environmental consulting and EU allowance trading as complements.

04

Earnings analysis

On a DART-filed annual basis, revenue rose from KRW 60.1 billion in 2022 to KRW 64.8 billion in 2023 (up 7.8% year on year), then dropped to KRW 25.8 billion in 2024 (down 60.2%) before recovering to KRW 30.9 billion in 2025 (up 20.0%). Across 2022 to 2025, 2023 was the largest year and 2024 the smallest.

Operating profit went from KRW 20 billion in 2022 to KRW 18.2 billion in 2023 (down 9.0%), KRW 199 million in 2024 (down 98.9%) and KRW 953 million in 2025 (up 379.4%). The operating margin fell from 33.3% in 2022 to 28.1% in 2023, 0.8% in 2024 and 3.1% in 2025.

The 2024 drop was attributed to lower carbon credit prices and delays in certification approvals.

Profit attributable to controlling shareholders peaked at KRW 15.7 billion in 2023 and then fell to KRW 2.25 billion in 2024 (down 85.6%) and KRW 1.78 billion in 2025 (down 20.8%), declining for two consecutive years from 2024 to 2025.

Net income including non-controlling interests was reported at the same amounts as controlling-shareholder profit in each year from 2022 to 2025. Operating cash flow was negative every year: -KRW 7.6 billion in 2022, -KRW 1.3 billion in 2023, -KRW 28.2 billion in 2024 and -KRW 7.76 billion in 2025.

The debt-to-equity ratio at year-end 2025 was 14.1%, and all KRW 125.3 billion of total equity belonged to controlling shareholders.

On a quarterly basis, revenue fell 31.1% to KRW 8.74 billion in the fourth quarter of 2025 and then to KRW 5.38 billion in the first quarter of 2026, before jumping 229.7% to KRW 17.8 billion in the second quarter.

The company said second-quarter operating profit of KRW 6.16 billion reflected the full start of CORSIA-related credit sales and a larger share of higher-margin greenhouse gas reduction revenue.

Third-quarter 2026 revenue of KRW 16 billion was 10.1% lower than the prior quarter but 25.7% higher than a year earlier, and operating profit of KRW 4.98 billion was 19.2% lower quarter on quarter but up 134.1% year on year. Profit attributable to controlling shareholders over the most recent four quarters totaled KRW 5.79 billion.

05

Industry analysis

Korea's emissions trading scheme entered its fourth phase, covering 2026 to 2030. In December 2024 the government confirmed a basic plan to expand the auctioned share of allowances step by step from the current level of around 10%.

Because a larger auctioned share affects allowance demand and prices, this policy is the central variable for the sector. In a 2025 report, however, domestic allowance prices were still weak, reflecting softer demand and continued free allocation.

Shipping has been brought into the EU Emissions Trading System since 2024, which raised demand for EU Allowances and is cited as a downstream driver. On the aviation side, demand for CORSIA-eligible credits opened up, and Ecoeye is regarded as having entered this market early.

The expanded application of the EU Carbon Border Adjustment Mechanism (CBAM) is also cited as a factor that increases reduction demand. On competition, the company's first-place share of overseas external-project certifications in Korea is a strength.

Reduction projects take a long time from registration to issuance, which makes it hard for new entrants to catch up, and the same structure slows how quickly supply expands, adding some visibility to earnings.

In sum, the policy direction and demand outlook are favorable, but earnings swing widely depending on credit prices and the speed of certification.

06

Outlook

In a voluntary disclosure on April 21, 2026, the company set out a plan to enhance corporate value. Under the plan, it will proceed with the UNFCCC approval process and complete validation during 2026.

In 2027, after government approval, it intends to issue and sell i-KOC units, shifting its structure from a voluntary-market focus toward the regulated market. The same plan says profitability indicators will be improved from 2027, and that a stable dividend policy will be set within the range of distributable profit.

The company also stated that from 2026 it will secure CORSIA-compliant credits and pursue sales to airlines and other buyers. At its second-quarter 2026 results announcement, CEO Hyun Sung-wan said the company is also expanding supply of EU Allowances (EUA).

An April 2025 press report had suggested that earnings could grow sharply from the second half of 2026, but that view rested on assumptions made at the time and is hard to compare directly with current results.

The next checkpoint is the annual figures in the 2026 business report, to be filed in 2027, and whether operating margins improve from 2027 as the disclosure plan describes. If government approval in 2027 is delayed, the timing of i-KOC sales could slip as well, so the approval progress also needs to be tracked.

08

Bull factors

Revenue base widens with CORSIA and EUA sales

Second-quarter 2026 revenue was KRW 17.8 billion, up 229.7% from the prior quarter, and third-quarter revenue of KRW 16 billion rose 25.7% year on year. The company attributes the growth to full-scale CORSIA credit sales and expanded EUA supply. Operating profit was KRW 6.16 billion in the second quarter and KRW 4.98 billion in the third, both profitable.

Shift toward the domestic regulated market

The government confirmed a plan in December 2024 to expand the auctioned allowance share, and the fourth phase runs from 2026 to 2030. The company plans to issue and sell i-KOC units in the domestic regulated market after government approval in 2027. If regulated-market sales materialize, the revenue mix could be less concentrated in the voluntary market.

Credit inventory and overseas project base

An April 2025 press report said the company held roughly 10 million tCO2eq of allowances. A June 2025 securities report put the volume awaiting UN approval at about 20 million tonnes. Its share of overseas external-project certifications from 2021 to 2025, at 49.63%, is also cited as evidence of the project base.

Bear factors

Thin earnings cushion in recent years

The operating margin was 0.8% in 2024 and 3.1% in 2025, and profit attributable to controlling shareholders declined for two consecutive years from 2024 to 2025. Operating cash flow was negative in every year from 2022 to 2025. A return to profit has been confirmed, but the low margins of recent years are not yet clearly recovered.

High volatility in quarterly results

In the first quarter of 2026 the company posted an operating loss of KRW 3.71 billion and a loss attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 3.54 billion. Third-quarter 2026 revenue fell 10.1% and operating profit fell 19.2% from the prior quarter.

Profit attributable to controlling shareholders over the last four quarters totaled KRW 5.79 billion, with results shifting sharply from quarter to quarter.

Policy delays and a high multiple

Certification approval delays were cited as a cause of the 2024 earnings collapse, and both the 2026 UNFCCC approval and the 2027 i-KOC issuance depend on government timing. The multiple sits above the industry average while the earnings base is small.

Because profitability improvement is framed as a 2027-onward plan, current quarterly results offer limited evidence for it.

10

Risk factors

Policy and price risk

Allowance prices move with demand and free-allocation policy, and a 2025 report pointed to continued price weakness. The timing and scale of the auctioned share increase could change with policy. If sale prices fall, revenue and profit could decline together, as they did in 2024.

Certification and project risk

Delays in UN approval, validation or government sign-off would push back the volume and timing of saleable credits. Overseas projects span several countries, including Vietnam, Cambodia and Myanmar, so country-specific regulation and on-the-ground operations carry risk.

Because reduction projects take years to issue credits, whether current approvals translate into future revenue will take time to show.

Ownership and liquidity risk

Major shareholders and related parties hold 69.25% of the company, and the free float ratio is 29.12% (as of October 27, 2026). Foreign ownership is low, at about 0.54%. With limited tradable shares, the share price can react sharply to earnings releases or policy news.

12

Overall view

Ecoeye's earnings peaked in 2023, bottomed in 2024, and in 2026 have recovered mainly on CORSIA-related sales. Operating margins of 0.8% in 2024 and 3.1% in 2025 show how thin the earnings cushion was.

Operating profit of KRW 6.16 billion in the second quarter and KRW 4.98 billion in the third returned the company to profit, but profit attributable to controlling shareholders over the last four quarters totals only KRW 5.79 billion.

On the positive side, CORSIA sales, expanded EU Allowance supply and the domestic auctioned-share policy broaden the revenue base. On the negative side, weak allowance prices and UN approval delays have depressed earnings before, and third-quarter 2026 revenue and operating profit both fell from the prior quarter.

On the balance sheet, the debt-to-equity ratio was 14.1% at year-end 2025, a modest level. Valuation sits at multiples above the industry average against a small earnings base, so whether profitability improvements from 2027 materialize is the benchmark.

The next checkpoints are the 2026 business report, first-quarter 2027 results, and whether i-KOC sales begin after government approval.
