Report overview

AI server pivot starts as four straight years of operating losses weigh

Nexa Dynamics is widening into AI servers, K-beauty and content commerce as its display equipment sales shrink, but operating losses that have run since 2022 and its reliance on convertible bonds remain unresolved.

02

Business structure

Nexa Dynamics' core business is its Smart Solutions segment, which supplies display manufacturing equipment such as bonding systems and logistics automation. The company says it is extending that base into integrated solutions that add AI-based process control and unmanned automation.

Equipment sales for 2025 were KRW 12.04 billion, and the export share rose from 29.7% to 97.2%. Equipment exports grew from KRW 2.54 billion in 2024 to about KRW 11.7 billion in 2025, according to a company announcement reported in April 2026.

The company says it maintains relationships with major display customers including CSOT, BOE, HKC, Sharp and Foxconn. Its cosmetics distribution business started in the third quarter of 2025 and generated about KRW 900 million of sales in 2025.

In the first half of 2026, K-beauty sales were about KRW 700 million, or 45.5% of total sales, and 96% of that was exports. Content commerce began with a main investment in a drama production that secured an equity stake in the drama's intellectual property, and it remains at an early stage.

In September 2026 the company said it would move forward with AI GPU server manufacturing, a finished-product market where overseas makers such as Supermicro, Dell and HPE dominate. The business mix is shifting from equipment alone toward equipment, K-beauty and AI servers operating side by side.

04

Earnings analysis

On a DART-filed basis, annual revenue went from KRW 32.9 billion in 2022 to KRW 16.2 billion in 2023 (down 50.9% year on year) and KRW 8.57 billion in 2024 (down 47.0%), before rising to KRW 13 billion in 2025 (up 51.9%). The largest year in 2022 to 2025 was 2022, and the smallest was 2024.

The company attributed the 2025 revenue increase to the stable execution of newly won contracts. Operating losses, however, widened to KRW 9.46 billion in 2025 from KRW 8.4 billion in 2024, and the company cited a higher cost ratio and rising selling, general and administrative expenses.

The operating margin was -0.5% in 2022, -52.9% in 2023, -98.1% in 2024 and -72.7% in 2025. The operating loss jumped from KRW 159 million in 2022 to KRW 8.56 billion in 2023, eased to KRW 8.4 billion in 2024 and widened again to KRW 9.46 billion in 2025.

Controlling-shareholder net income turned from a profit of KRW 783 million in 2022 to a net loss of KRW 6.42 billion in 2023, and that loss widened to KRW 12.1 billion in 2025. Net loss including non-controlling interests was KRW 14.2 billion in 2025.

Operating cash flow moved from a KRW 381 million inflow in 2022 to -KRW 8.1 billion in 2025, and a press report said cash and cash equivalents fell from KRW 10.16 billion to KRW 4.34 billion during 2025.

Quarterly revenue fell 66.8% from KRW 1.76 billion in 4Q 2025 to KRW 584 million in 1Q 2026, then recovered to KRW 865 million in 2Q and KRW 813 million in 3Q 2026, which was down 32.0% year on year.

Operating loss in 3Q 2026 was KRW 1.11 billion, narrower than KRW 2.81 billion in 3Q 2025, while the controlling-shareholder net loss widened to KRW 3.27 billion from KRW 3.21 billion. The controlling-shareholder net loss over the last four quarters totaled KRW 11.4 billion.

A press report said outsourced processing costs reached KRW 5.8 billion in the first three quarters of 2025, adding to cost-of-sales pressure. With revenue still below 2025 levels, cost of sales and selling and administrative expenses are shaping the bottom line.

05

Industry analysis

The core end market for Nexa Dynamics is capital spending by display panel makers. The company said its exports rose about 4.6 times in 2025 even as domestic display equipment investment slowed, which indicates that overseas panel customers now drive much of its orders.

Domestic spending has been selectively executed, and the domestic market has weakened. A February 2026 press report said a prolonged display industry downturn has left upstream equipment makers without a clear path to a earnings rebound.

The same report noted that the company is recognized for its bonding technology, which precisely joins chips and circuits. Equipment sales in the first half of 2026 were about KRW 600 million, sharply below the 2025 annual level of roughly KRW 12 billion.

In AI servers, Korea has strength in core components such as HBM, DDR memory and enterprise SSDs, but finished GPU servers that combine them are dominated by overseas makers including Supermicro, Dell and HPE. The company describes domestic dependence on foreign suppliers in AI servers as 100%.

Its exchange classification is automobiles, but the business description centers on display manufacturing equipment and AI server hardware, so auto-parts cycles do not directly drive its earnings. Both of its business lines depend on customer investment cycles, which defines its position in the industry.

06

Outlook

The most concrete checkpoint is the AI GPU server business. The company said in September 2026 that the full payment for its KRW 13 billion rights offering had been made.

The proceeds will fund a dedicated GPU server line of 3,000 square meters on the second floor of its plant in the Siheung National Industrial Complex in Gyeonggi Province, made up of 2,000 square meters of operations space and 1,000 square meters of production area.

The line will house GPU server assembly and quality test equipment. Trial production is planned for 4Q 2026, full mass production starts in 2027, and about 60 initial technical staff are to be deployed. Early demand is to be secured through collaboration with DC Korea and the DCP group.

The company also plans to supply its in-house servers to an urban edge AI data center in the Seoul metropolitan area and a hyperscale data center in Dangjin, South Chungcheong Province. The company said it is pursuing an additional KRW 30 billion rights offering in parallel.

A press report put a supply contract with a Japanese company for AI servers and components at about KRW 1.6 billion, equal to 12.2% of 2025 revenue.

In content commerce, the company has opened the door to expanding from K-beauty into K-fashion and K-food, with emphasis on medium-term brand assets rather than short-term sales. The equipment business depends on whether domestic panel investment recovers and on new orders from overseas customers.

The next checkpoints are the start of trial production in 4Q 2026, the timing and terms of the KRW 30 billion rights offering, and the scale of AI server orders around the 2027 mass-production start.

08

Bull factors

Funding secured for the AI server line

A KRW 13 billion rights offering was fully paid in September 2026, securing funds for a dedicated GPU server line. Trial production is planned for 4Q 2026 and mass production for 2027. A supply contract with a Japanese company for AI servers and components, worth about KRW 1.6 billion, was also reported.

The company said it will work with DC Korea and the DCP group to supply initial volumes to domestic data centers.

Overseas demand anchoring equipment sales

According to the company, equipment exports rose from KRW 2.54 billion in 2024 to about KRW 11.7 billion in 2025, and the export share of equipment sales increased from 29.7% to 97.2%.

The company said it maintains relationships with major customers including CSOT, BOE, HKC, Sharp and Foxconn and expects additional orders. Overseas customers are holding up sales while domestic display investment has slowed.

Narrowing quarterly operating loss

The operating loss in 3Q 2026 was KRW 1.11 billion, narrower than KRW 2.81 billion in 3Q 2025. Revenue in the same quarter was KRW 813 million, down 32.0% year on year, so the loss narrowed even as sales fell. Whether the cost structure keeps this trend going is the next quarterly checkpoint.

Bear factors

Four straight years of operating losses

Operating losses ran from KRW 159 million in 2022 to KRW 8.56 billion in 2023, KRW 8.4 billion in 2024 and KRW 9.46 billion in 2025. The company posted losses for four consecutive years from 2022 through 2025, and the 2025 loss widened.

The 2025 net loss including non-controlling interests was KRW 14.2 billion, and the controlling-shareholder net loss was KRW 12.1 billion. Controlling-shareholder losses have continued for three consecutive years from 2023 through 2025.

Rising debt and cash outflow

The debt ratio rose from 72.1% in 2022 to 173.8% in 2025, and total liabilities were KRW 37 billion at end-2025. Operating cash flow swung from a KRW 381 million inflow in 2022 to -KRW 8.1 billion in 2025. A press report said cash and cash equivalents fell from KRW 10.16 billion to KRW 4.34 billion during 2025. Another report put the current ratio at 42% as of 3Q 2025.

Governance and disclosure issues

At a September 2026 extraordinary shareholders' meeting, nine agenda items were approved, including four inside directors. In the same period, the largest shareholder changed to a 28.71% holder.

In April 2026 the company issued KRW 13 billion of convertible bonds and decided to acquire 100% of a non-listed company for KRW 20 billion. The Korea Exchange reviewed possible designation as an insincere-disclosure company, assigned 4.0 penalty points, and reportedly deferred the designation under certain conditions. A lawsuit challenging the validity of a convertible bond issuance is also on record as of 21 September 2026.

Risk factors

Financing and liquidity

Operating cash outflow widened to -KRW 8.1 billion in 2025, and financing inflows have repeatedly covered the gap. Operating losses have continued in every quarter from 3Q 2025 through 3Q 2026. If the additional KRW 30 billion rights offering goes ahead, dilution for existing shareholders could increase. Conversions of convertible bonds would add further dilution.

Governance and disclosure

The largest shareholder changed in September 2026, and the CEO was replaced in the same period. A press report said the company has issued KRW 48.11 billion of convertible bonds across seven tranches in recent years, creating potential dilution on conversion. The deferred insincere-disclosure designation and a lawsuit over bond issuance validity also remain on record.

Concentration and new-business execution

Equipment sales fell from roughly KRW 12 billion in 2025 to about KRW 600 million in the first half of 2026. If the slowdown in domestic display investment persists, the revenue gap could widen. AI server mass production is not until 2027, and content commerce is at an early stage with limited proven results. Because 96% of K-beauty sales are exports, that business is sensitive to overseas demand.

12

Overall view

Nexa Dynamics has posted operating losses since 2022 while widening its business from equipment into AI servers, K-beauty and content commerce. Revenue rose 51.9% to KRW 13 billion in 2025, yet the operating loss widened to KRW 9.46 billion.

Equipment sales fell from roughly KRW 12 billion in 2025 to about KRW 600 million in the first half of 2026, so whether new businesses can fill that gap is the central question.

The 3Q 2026 operating loss narrowed to KRW 1.11 billion, but the controlling-shareholder net loss widened to KRW 3.27 billion, so the profit trend is mixed.

Positive factors include the GPU server line planned with the KRW 13 billion rights offering, a 97.2% export share of equipment, and a reported supply contract with a Japanese company worth about KRW 1.6 billion.

Negative factors include a 173.8% debt ratio, operating cash outflow of -KRW 8.1 billion, repeated convertible bond issuance, and governance and disclosure issues following the September 2026 change in the largest shareholder.

Any recovery will show up as a smaller operating loss and better cash flow appearing together. The next checkpoints are the start of trial production in 4Q 2026, the timing of the KRW 30 billion rights offering, and the 2026 annual report.
