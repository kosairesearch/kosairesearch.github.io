Shrinking Parallel-Import Core as Autonomous Driving Expands

02

Business structure

PonyLink's business is organized around three areas: parallel imports of luxury goods, IT solutions, and mobility (autonomous driving). Of the KRW 10.2 billion in revenue for the first quarter of 2026, the overseas fashion segment accounted for 79.74% and the IT segment for 20.01% (quarterly report basis).

The overseas fashion segment imports and distributes luxury brands including Prada and Gucci, 43 brands in total, and overseas luxury goods sales in 2025 were about KRW 32.9 billion.

Direct sales, meaning orders placed by buyers, made up 95.37% of 2025 revenue, while the remaining 4.63% came through indirect channels that run via subsidiaries of mobile carriers.

The IT segment supplies wireless-internet messaging and gateway solutions, application solutions, and maintenance services to mobile operators and related companies.

In 2025, application solutions in the IT segment came to about KRW 3.5 billion and maintenance and operation to about KRW 3.3 billion (quarterly report basis). The IT segment is small in revenue share but is tied to mobile carrier channels, so its customer base differs from the luxury segment.

To explain its edge in luxury, the company says direct purchasing from overseas branches lowers import costs, and that it has distribution through Lotte and Hyundai department store outlets, outlet malls and online shops. Parallel importing carries inventory risk and exposure to exchange-rate swings.

The mobility segment mainly runs autonomous driving services and is still at an early commercial stage. Because overseas fashion makes up most of revenue, changes in luxury spending and the exchange rate pass through quickly to results. The high share of direct sales also means order flows move with individual trading partners.

04

Earnings analysis

On a disclosed basis, revenue fell from KRW 95.5 billion in 2022 to KRW 71.5 billion in 2023 (down 25.2% year on year), KRW 61.3 billion in 2024 (down 14.2%), and KRW 44.9 billion in 2025 (down 26.8%).

Revenue has declined for three consecutive years from 2023 through 2025, with 2022 the largest year and 2025 the smallest in the 2022 to 2025 period. For 2025, the company attributed the revenue decline to a weak consumer economy and a rise in the exchange rate.

The operating loss grew from KRW 1.2 billion in 2022 to KRW 9.33 billion in 2023 and KRW 26.2 billion in 2024, then narrowed to KRW 21.1 billion in 2025. The operating margin moved from -1.3% in 2022 to -13.1% in 2023, -42.7% in 2024 and -47.0% in 2025, so the loss rate rose even as revenue shrank.

Net loss attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 19.3 billion in 2022, KRW 10.5 billion in 2023 and KRW 38.4 billion in 2024, before turning to net income of KRW 15.8 billion in 2025. Net income including non-controlling interests followed the same path, at KRW 15.8 billion for 2025.

The company cited a rise in the value of its equity investments as the reason for the turn to profit. Because the operating loss continued in 2025, this profit reads as coming from non-operating gains rather than a recovery in core earnings. The quarterly path is more volatile.

Revenue was KRW 11 billion in the third quarter of 2025, KRW 13.4 billion in the fourth quarter (up 21.6% from the prior quarter), KRW 10.2 billion in the first quarter of 2026 (down 24.2%), KRW 12 billion in the second quarter (up 17.7%) and KRW 10.9 billion in the third quarter (down 9.3% quarter on quarter and down 1.6% year on year).

The operating loss has continued for five straight quarters from the third quarter of 2025 through the third quarter of 2026, and narrowed to KRW 3.4 billion in the latest quarter.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders swung from a loss of KRW 33.3 billion in the third quarter of 2025 and KRW 61.4 billion in the fourth quarter, to a profit of KRW 8.11 billion in the first quarter of 2026, then to a loss of KRW 58.7 billion in the second quarter and KRW 9.11 billion in the third quarter.

The second-quarter 2026 controlling-shareholder net loss of KRW 58.7 billion is far larger than the KRW 4.42 billion operating loss, so the non-operating items behind it are worth checking in the quarterly report notes. The combined controlling-shareholder net loss for the last four quarters is KRW 121.1 billion.

At the end of 2025, total equity was KRW 216.1 billion, total liabilities KRW 71.6 billion and the debt ratio 33.1%. Operating cash flow was negative in all four years, at -KRW 17.3 billion in 2022, -KRW 19.5 billion in 2023, -KRW 4.29 billion in 2024 and -KRW 10.1 billion in 2025.

05

Industry analysis

Luxury parallel-import distribution is a business whose revenue moves sharply with overseas brands' pricing policies, the exchange rate and domestic consumer sentiment.

The company attributed its 2025 revenue decline to a weak consumer economy and a higher exchange rate, and a higher exchange rate raises import costs and pressures margins.

Listed peers in the same sector, such as Shinsegae, Lotte Shopping and Hyundai Department Store, share consumer spending through department store and retail networks, and PonyLink differs from them in distribution scale and brand access.

The overseas fashion segment accounted for 79.74% of revenue in the first quarter of 2026, so the business is concentrated in one segment.

In a sector comparison published by a securities information service, the average operating margin of the apparel and fashion accessories industry was positive, while PonyLink's operating margin of -47.0% for 2025 sat far below that average.

The IT segment depends on mobile carrier channels, so its revenue can move with carrier investment cycles and procurement decisions.

The domestic autonomous driving market is at the stage of demonstrations and trial operations, and the timing of commercialization and the revenue model have not been fixed in disclosures. Results from the new business should therefore be judged separately from the cash generation of the existing business.

The existing luxury distribution business is in a contraction phase, while the new business is in an early investment phase, so the two operate on different timelines.

06

Outlook

The company's next periodic report is the 2026 annual report, which will be the first official confirmation of full-year results and fourth-quarter profit and loss.

In the autonomous driving business, disclosures describe plans from the second half of 2025 to widen demonstration zones to Gangnam, Sangam, Pangyo and Dongtan, and Osiria in Busan, and to strengthen operations and control capabilities.

According to the first-quarter 2026 quarterly report, PonyLink plans to set up an operations hub in Seongnam, Gyeonggi together with its China-based autonomous driving partner, and to start autonomous driving business in Korea after the corporate entity is established.

The same report describes a collaboration structure with Kyung Hee University and Inha University on AI agent-based robot control and location recognition. The second development stage was planned for February to July 2026, aimed at autonomous patrol and charging functions.

Reports for the first half of 2025 said that cash used in investing activities rose to KRW 25.1 billion from KRW 12.6 billion a year earlier. With new-business investment increasing while the operating loss continues, the gap between funding sources and operating cash generation remains a point to track.

Based on disclosed results, any recovery in revenue depends on luxury distribution volumes and the exchange-rate trend. Quarterly revenue in 2026 has moved in the low KRW 10 billion range, which shows the floor level of the existing business.

The next checkpoints are the operating profit and loss and operating cash flow in the 2026 annual report, and the profit and loss trend in the first-quarter 2027 quarterly report.

Bull factors

Narrowing operating losses

The operating loss narrowed from KRW 26.2 billion in 2024 to KRW 21.1 billion in 2025, and to KRW 3.4 billion in the third quarter of 2026. Quarterly operating losses have come down from the KRW 4 billion range in recent quarters, which can be read as a sign that the cost base is being adjusted. Whether this trend holds has to be confirmed on a full-year basis in the 2026 annual report.

Wider autonomous driving demonstrations

After receiving a temporary operating permit for robotaxis, trial operations in Gangnam, Seoul were reported, and disclosures describe widening demonstration zones to Sangam, Pangyo and Dongtan, and Osiria in Busan.

The quarterly report also includes plans for an operations hub in Seongnam to start autonomous driving business in Korea. This gives the company room to build a new revenue source alongside the existing business.

Equity base and return to profit

Total equity at the end of 2025 was KRW 216.1 billion and total liabilities KRW 71.6 billion, giving a debt ratio of 33.1%. Net income attributable to controlling shareholders turned positive at KRW 15.8 billion in 2025. A sizable equity base is cited as a factor that gives room to absorb losses and fund new business investment.

Bear factors

Revenue down three years running

Revenue fell from KRW 95.5 billion in 2022 to KRW 44.9 billion in 2025, declining for three consecutive years from 2023. The company cites a weak consumer economy and a higher exchange rate. Quarterly revenue in 2026 has stayed in the low KRW 10 billion range, with no clear sign of recovery yet.

Persistent operating losses and negative cash flow

The operating loss has continued for four consecutive years from 2022, and the operating margin worsened to -47.0% in 2025 from -42.7% in 2024. Operating cash flow was negative in all four years from 2022 to 2025. Reports also said investing outflows increased in the first half of 2025.

Profit driven by non-operating gains

The KRW 15.8 billion net income attributable to controlling shareholders in 2025 is one the company linked to a rise in the value of its equity investments. Controlling-shareholder net losses were KRW 33.3 billion in the third quarter of 2025 and KRW 61.4 billion in the fourth quarter.

The second-quarter 2026 net loss of KRW 58.7 billion is far larger than the KRW 4.42 billion operating loss, showing high volatility in non-operating results.

Risk factors

Financial and cash flow risk

The combined controlling-shareholder net loss for the last four quarters is KRW 121.1 billion, and operating cash flow has been negative for four consecutive years. If operating cash does not cover losses while new-business investment rises, reliance on external funding increases. Total liabilities were KRW 71.6 billion at the end of 2025, but funding terms need to be checked going forward.

Autonomous driving commercialization risk

The autonomous driving business remains at the level of temporary permits and demonstrations, and the timing of commercialization and the revenue model have not been fixed in disclosures. Because the business depends on the regulatory environment and a partner's technology, schedules could slip.

The risk grows if investment burdens rise while the existing luxury business is not generating enough cash to fund them.

Distribution and exchange-rate risk

The overseas fashion segment accounted for 79.74% of first-quarter 2026 revenue, so exposure to luxury spending and the exchange rate is high. A higher exchange rate raises import costs and compresses margins, and a consumer slowdown could extend revenue declines. Direct sales made up 95.37% of 2025 revenue, so order flows depend heavily on individual trading partners.

Overall view

On a disclosed basis, PonyLink's revenue fell from KRW 95.5 billion in 2022 to KRW 44.9 billion in 2025, and its operating loss has continued for four consecutive years from 2022.

The 2025 turn to net income of KRW 15.8 billion attributable to controlling shareholders reflects non-operating gains, which the company linked to a rise in the value of its equity investments, while the operating loss continued.

The operating loss narrowed to KRW 3.4 billion in the third quarter of 2026, but losses have persisted for five straight quarters, and the combined controlling-shareholder net loss for the last four quarters is KRW 121.1 billion.

Financially, total equity at the end of 2025 was KRW 216.1 billion and the debt ratio 33.1%, but operating cash flow was negative in all four years.

Bullish points include the narrowing operating loss and wider autonomous driving demonstrations, while bearish points include falling revenue, negative cash flow and large swings in quarterly profit and loss. The timing of monetization for the autonomous driving business has not yet been fixed in disclosures.
