Report overview

Acquisitions lift operating profit, but net losses test earnings quality

Sungho Electronics has lifted operating profit and cut its debt ratio after acquiring semiconductor and optical-communications subsidiaries, yet net losses in the second and third quarters of 2026 make earnings quality and capital structure the next checkpoints.

02

Business structure

Sungho Electronics started as an electronic components maker known for film capacitors and power supplies, and since late 2025 it has been reshaping its portfolio by acquiring semiconductor and optical-communications subsidiaries.

Its optical-alignment subsidiary, consolidated in February 2026 after an 87.5% stake was acquired for about KRW 280 billion, makes equipment tied to optical transceivers and CPO (co-packaged optics) alignment.

Three acquired subsidiaries recorded combined first-half 2026 revenue of KRW 34.63 billion and operating profit of KRW 8.59 billion, equal to 22.7% of consolidated revenue and 85.1% of consolidated operating profit.

Headquarters operations, including the film capacitor and power supply production units, posted first-half revenue of KRW 76.45 billion and operating profit of KRW 180 million. Because the headquarters contributed little operating profit, group earnings now depend heavily on the acquired subsidiaries.

In March 2026 the company decided to buy an 11.12% stake in Global Technology, an electronic-parts processing company, for about KRW 30 billion in cash.

Major customers of the optical-alignment subsidiary include Optolink and Lumentum, and a February 2026 securities report expected deliveries to widen to Jabil, Broadcom, Marvell and Accton. Press coverage described an expanding equipment business aimed at AI data-center and optical-communications markets.

The debt ratio stood at 172% at the end of the third quarter, and the capital structure is changing alongside the acquisitions.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual revenue rose from KRW 153.6 billion in 2022 to KRW 208.1 billion in 2023 (up 35.5%), then slipped to KRW 207.3 billion in 2024 (down 0.4%) before reaching KRW 231.6 billion in 2025 (up 11.7%), the largest year of 2022 to 2025.

Operating profit went from KRW 2.05 billion in 2022 to KRW 25.9 billion in 2023 (up 1,164.0%), the largest year in the period, then fell to KRW 6.31 billion in 2024 (down 75.6%) and recovered to KRW 7.55 billion in 2025 (up 19.6%).

The operating margin was 1.3% in 2022, 12.4% in 2023, 3.0% in 2024 and 3.3% in 2025, so the 2023 level did not carry forward.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders moved from a net loss of KRW 4.19 billion in 2022 to a turnaround to profit of KRW 17.6 billion in 2023, then KRW 8.08 billion in 2024 (down 54.1%) and KRW 91 billion in 2025 (up 1,026.2%).

Total net income in 2025 was also KRW 91 billion, far above operating profit, so the contribution of non-operating items needs checking in the notes. The debt ratio declined from 179.7% in 2022 to 128.8% in 2025.

At the end of 2025, total equity was KRW 254 billion, of which KRW 250.8 billion belonged to controlling shareholders, and total liabilities were KRW 327.1 billion. Operating cash flow was KRW 39.4 billion in 2022, KRW 4.54 billion in 2023, KRW 25.2 billion in 2024 and KRW 20.1 billion in 2025.

Quarterly revenue went from KRW 55.6 billion with operating profit of KRW 540 million in the third quarter of 2025 to KRW 60.2 billion with KRW 4.09 billion in the fourth quarter (up 8.2% and 657.7% quarter on quarter).

In the first quarter of 2026 revenue rose 9.7% to KRW 66 billion while operating profit fell 21.7% to KRW 3.2 billion. The second quarter recovered to KRW 85.2 billion in revenue, up 29.2%, and KRW 6.89 billion in operating profit, up 115.1%.

Third-quarter revenue was KRW 79.7 billion, down 6.4% quarter on quarter but up 43.4% year on year, and operating profit was KRW 4.33 billion, down 37.1% quarter on quarter.

The company put the third-quarter operating margin at 5.4% and said it absorbed one-off M&A costs of KRW 1.1 billion, or KRW 5.1 billion cumulatively.

Controlling-shareholder results swing widely: net income of KRW 2.02 billion in the third quarter of 2025 and KRW 85.9 billion in the fourth quarter gave way to a net loss of KRW 46.1 billion in the second quarter of 2026 and KRW 47.1 billion in the third quarter, two consecutive loss-making quarters.

Because the third quarter posted operating profit yet a larger controlling-shareholder net loss, the non-operating items behind that gap need checking in the quarterly report.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders over the last four quarters totals KRW 312.1 billion, and the first-quarter 2026 figure of KRW 319.5 billion carries most of that sum.

05

Industry analysis

The optical-communications and semiconductor equipment area that Sungho Electronics has expanded into is tied to rising AI data-center investment.

A February 2026 securities report said orders for optical-communications equipment were concentrated in the first half of 2024, and that orders tied to the 1.6T generation transition began to build from late 2025.

The same report said the company had finished preparing for volume CPO equipment orders in 2025 and expected orders and new revenue to expand from 2026. Generational transitions can raise both unit prices and volumes, so optical-communications equipment is becoming the main driver of results.

If the transition is delayed, however, order gaps could emerge. Customers are concentrated among optical transceiver and networking equipment makers, so large-customer order flows pass through to results quickly.

The core film capacitor and power supply business moves with electronic component demand, and headquarters operating profit of KRW 180 million in the first half of 2026 shows that this business contributes little to profit.

Through the acquisition of its optical-alignment subsidiary, the company entered optical-communications equipment together with alignment technology and customers. Press coverage described the equipment business in this area as expanding toward AI data-center and optical-communications markets.

The business now spans electronic components and semiconductor and optical-communications equipment, and the stage of the optical-communications cycle, which is in a generational transition, is the key variable.

Outlook

In its November 2026 statement the company described the third quarter as the earnings low, and said results and balance-sheet improvement would accelerate from the fourth quarter of 2026, when the acquired businesses begin to show up fully in profit.

The company said one-off M&A costs are shrinking each quarter, and the cumulative amount through the third quarter was KRW 5.1 billion. In an August 2026 briefing on first-half results, the company said the 0% interest rate on its 20th convertible bond would reduce annual interest expense by about KRW 4.1 billion.

On the securities side, Daishin Securities projected operating profit of KRW 30 billion for 2026 and KRW 206.5 billion for 2027 in a June 2026 report.

Cumulative operating profit through the third quarter of 2026 was KRW 14.4 billion, about half of that 2026 projection, so whether fourth-quarter results follow that path is the next checkpoint.

Controlling-shareholder net income of KRW 319.5 billion in the first quarter of 2026 means that 2027 earnings could differ widely depending on whether non-operating items recur. In optical communications, expansion of 1.6T and CPO equipment customers is the key variable for future results.

Fourth-quarter 2026 preliminary results are expected in early 2027, and the 2026 annual report is due by the end of March 2027, where full-year operating margin, debt ratio and operating cash flow will be checked.

08

Bull factors

Operating profit recovers after acquisitions

Third-quarter 2026 operating profit was KRW 4.33 billion, up 703.0% year on year, and the operating margin rose to 5.4% from about 1% in the same quarter of 2025.

Combined operating profit of the three acquired subsidiaries, KRW 4.9 billion in the quarter, exceeded consolidated operating profit and drove the improvement. In the first half of 2026 those three companies accounted for 85.1% of consolidated operating profit.

Lower debt ratio and interest savings

The debt ratio fell from 223% at the end of the first half of 2026 to 172% at the end of the third quarter. The company said it repaid borrowings with convertible bond proceeds and that partial exercise of its 14th series of warrant bonds added equity.

It also said the 0% coupon on its 20th convertible bond would cut annual interest expense by about KRW 4.1 billion.

AI optical-equipment demand and customer expansion

The optical-alignment subsidiary makes optical transceiver and CPO alignment equipment and is widening its equipment business aimed at AI data-center and optical-communications markets. A February 2026 securities report expected the 1.6T generational transition and CPO equipment orders to pick up from 2026. The same report said additional deliveries to Jabil, Broadcom, Marvell and Accton were expected.

Bear factors

Two consecutive quarters of controlling-shareholder net losses

The controlling-shareholder net loss was KRW 46.1 billion in the second quarter and KRW 47.1 billion in the third quarter of 2026, two consecutive loss-making quarters with the third-quarter loss larger than the second.

Third-quarter operating profit was a positive KRW 4.33 billion, so the gap between the operating line and the bottom line is large. The size of the non-operating items behind that gap needs checking in the quarterly report.

Weak headquarters profitability and reliance on subsidiaries

In the first half of 2026 the headquarters, including the film capacitor and power supply production units, generated revenue of KRW 76.45 billion and operating profit of KRW 180 million.

Over the same period the three acquired subsidiaries accounted for 85.1% of consolidated operating profit, so profit is concentrated in those businesses. A downturn at the subsidiaries could shift group profitability quickly.

M&A costs and integration burden

Cumulative M&A-related costs, including advisory fees, due diligence and subsidiary restructuring, reached KRW 5.1 billion through the third quarter. The company said these one-off costs are shrinking each quarter, but they still reduce operating profit. Bringing several subsidiaries into the group in a short period has also raised the integration burden.

Risk factors

Dilution and capital structure

A June 2026 Daishin Securities report put the dilutive share count from the 16th, 17th and 18th convertible bonds and the 19th warrant bonds at 42,416,459 shares. The debt ratio was 223% at the end of the first half of 2026, and the company put its debt ratio including mezzanine instruments at 171.5%. Conversions or exercises would increase the share count and could lower per-share metrics.

Whether one-off gains and losses recur

Controlling-shareholder net income of KRW 319.5 billion in the first quarter of 2026 and KRW 85.9 billion in the fourth quarter of 2025 are unusually large for single quarters, and results turned to losses from the second quarter.

Whether these items are one-off or could recur must be checked in the notes to the periodic reports. The wide swings make quarterly results difficult to interpret.

Timing of the generational transition and customer concentration

Optical-equipment revenue is likely to depend heavily on the timing of the 1.6T and CPO transition. If customer expansion lags the February 2026 securities outlook, the earnings estimates could be hard to reach.

With customers concentrated among large optical-communications companies, the business may be sensitive to order changes from individual customers.

Overall view

Sungho Electronics has seen operating profit recover and its debt ratio fall after acquiring semiconductor and optical-communications subsidiaries, but controlling-shareholder net losses in the second and third quarters of 2026 mean earnings quality still needs confirming.

Annual operating profit in 2025 was KRW 7.55 billion, below KRW 25.9 billion in 2023, yet net income attributable to controlling shareholders rose to KRW 91 billion in 2025, so the earnings mix has changed.

Third-quarter operating profit was KRW 4.33 billion, up 703.0% year on year, with an operating margin of 5.4%, and three acquired subsidiaries accounted for 85.1% of consolidated operating profit in the first half.

Headquarters operating profit of KRW 180 million in the first half leaves group profit highly dependent on those subsidiaries. The debt ratio fell from 223% in the first half to 172% in the third quarter, but 42,416,459 dilutive shares as of June 2026 keep capital-structure changes under review.

Positive factors are subsidiary contributions, lower interest costs and the generational shift in AI optical equipment, while negative factors are quarterly net losses, M&A one-off costs and reliance on subsidiaries.
