02

Business structure

DRB Industrial is a manufacturer of industrial rubber products, with conveyor belts and crawlers (rubber tracks) as its core product lines. Rubber tracks are described as gaining preference over steel tracks because they produce less noise and vibration and protect road surfaces.

The company has also developed a rubber-based seismic isolation system for earthquake damage reduction, extending its reach into civil engineering and construction. Steel mills and cement plants, where conveyor belts face high temperatures, are cited as key end-use sites.

Caterpillar, a construction equipment maker, and Deere & Company, an agricultural machinery brand, are named as customers. Production bases are located in Qingdao in China, Vietnam and Russia, and the company operates overseas subsidiaries and offices in five countries including the United States and Japan.

In November 2025 the Korea Fair Trade Commission selected the company as a 'Dealer Partnership Company', citing an average dealer transaction period of 21 years and 5 months and a 94.5% share of dealers on long-term contracts of five years or more. Long-standing dealer relationships point to a stable sales base.

On competition, the CEO said in a March 2024 interview that the company ranked around second globally in rubber tracks and named Japan's Bridgestone as the leader. The company is also expanding into robotics and healthcare, and in 2024 it opened a research center with KAIST after investing about KRW 5 billion.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual sales fell from KRW 390.4 billion in 2022 to KRW 357.8 billion in 2023 (down 8.4%) and KRW 337 billion in 2024 (down 5.8%), then rose 8.2% to KRW 364.6 billion in 2025. Of 2022 to 2025, 2022 was the largest year for sales and 2024 the smallest.

Operating profit went from KRW 8.08 billion in 2022 to KRW 13.7 billion in 2023 (up 69.4%), dropped to KRW 9.59 billion in 2024 (down 30.0%), and reached KRW 18.6 billion in 2025 (up 93.7%), the largest year in the 2022 to 2025 period.

The company attributed the 2025 operating profit gain to higher sales, cost reduction and lower selling and administrative expenses. The operating margin was 2.1% in 2022, 3.8% in 2023, 2.8% in 2024 and 5.1% in 2025, the highest of the four years.

Net profit was KRW 9.79 billion in 2025, down from KRW 9.93 billion in 2024, so the net profit line did not keep pace with the operating gain.

Profit attributable to controlling shareholders rose from KRW 7.79 billion in 2022 to KRW 9.58 billion in 2023 (up 22.9%) and KRW 10.9 billion in 2024 (up 13.7%), then came to KRW 10.7 billion in 2025 (down 2.1%); 2024 was the largest year.

In 2025 the non-controlling interest was negative, so attributable profit exceeded total net profit. Operating cash flow was -KRW 19.8 billion in 2022, KRW 42 billion in 2023, KRW 13.2 billion in 2024 and -KRW 5.13 billion in 2025, with large swings year to year.

At the end of 2025, total equity was KRW 239 billion, of which KRW 237.4 billion belonged to controlling shareholders, and total liabilities were KRW 116.3 billion.

Quarterly, sales reached KRW 99.4 billion in the fourth quarter of 2025 with operating profit of KRW 7.07 billion; in the first quarter of 2026 sales were KRW 95.4 billion (down 4.0% quarter on quarter), operating profit KRW 7.78 billion and profit attributable to controlling shareholders KRW 10.4 billion.

In that first quarter, attributable profit exceeded operating profit, which suggests non-operating items had a large effect. Sales were KRW 103.5 billion in the second quarter and KRW 116.9 billion in the third quarter of 2026, rising for two consecutive quarters.

However, third-quarter operating profit of KRW 2.71 billion a year earlier turned into an operating loss of KRW 542 million.

The third-quarter 2026 net loss attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 11.8 billion is larger than the operating loss, so the non-operating breakdown in the quarterly report is needed to explain the gap.

The sum of profit attributable to controlling shareholders over the last four quarters (fourth quarter 2025 to third quarter 2026) is KRW 10.1 billion, and it includes the third-quarter 2026 loss.

Industry analysis

In the rubber track market, rubber-based products are gaining preference over steel tracks because of their lower noise and vibration. In a March 2024 interview, the CEO said the company was around second globally and named Bridgestone of Japan as the leader.

A 2025 analysis said rubber track supply for original equipment rose and improved results. The customer base spans construction and agricultural machinery makers, so demand is tied to production of that equipment and to construction and farming conditions.

Conveyor belt demand is linked to operating rates in heavy industries such as steel and cement.

Petrochemical companies in the same chemical sector, such as LG Chem and Lotte Chemical, are heavily exposed to feedstock prices and refining margins, while DRB Industrial's rubber belt and crawler business has a different exposure.

Production bases in China, Vietnam, Russia and the United States mean regional demand and exchange rates both matter. Third-quarter 2026 sales rose 31.9% year on year, yet the same quarter posted an operating loss, so whether top-line growth converts into profit is the industry-level question for this company.

Bull factors

Recovery in operating profit

Operating profit reached KRW 18.6 billion in 2025, up 93.7% and the largest year of 2022 to 2025. The company cited higher sales, cost reduction and lower selling and administrative expenses. The operating margin of 5.1% in 2025 was the highest of the four years. Whether this improvement continues in the next periodic report is the item to check.

Stronger balance sheet

The debt-to-equity ratio fell from 74.9% in 2022 to 48.7% in 2025. Total equity rose to KRW 239 billion at the end of 2025, while total liabilities fell to KRW 116.3 billion from the prior year-end. Equity growth alongside lower liabilities reduces reliance on external funding.

Rubber track position and dealer base

The company described itself as around second globally in rubber tracks and has Caterpillar and Deere & Company among its customers. The Fair Trade Commission praised its long-term dealer relationships in 2025. A customer and dealer base could underpin the profit recovery, which is the bullish case.

Bear factors

Third-quarter operating loss

The operating loss in the third quarter of 2026 was KRW 542 million, compared with operating profit of KRW 2.71 billion a year earlier. The net loss attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 11.8 billion, far larger than the operating loss.

Sales rose 31.9% year on year, yet the quarter produced no profit, so the cost structure and non-operating items are the next items to examine.

Volatile operating cash flow

Operating cash flow was -KRW 19.8 billion in 2022 and -KRW 5.13 billion in 2025, while it reached KRW 42 billion in 2023 and KRW 13.2 billion in 2024. Cash generation did not track profit steadily in some years. Whether cash flow moves in step with profit from 2026 onward is the item to check.

Thin margins and earnings quality

The operating margin stayed in a range of 2.1% to 5.1% over 2022 to 2025. In the first quarter of 2026, profit attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 10.4 billion exceeded operating profit of KRW 7.78 billion, which shows reliance on non-operating items. When profit includes items outside core operations, the earnings trend is harder to read.

Risk factors

Demand cycle

Demand for rubber tracks and conveyor belts is tied to production of construction and agricultural machinery and to equipment investment in steel and cement. A slowdown in those sectors could weigh on sales growth.

The drivers of third-quarter 2026 sales growth can be separated using the business segment discussion in the quarterly report.

Overseas production and FX

Production bases in China, Vietnam and Russia mean regional policy and exchange rate moves can affect costs and profits. With overseas subsidiaries in several countries, currency translation gains and losses can appear in non-operating items. Regional plant utilization and currency effects can be checked in the business report.

Earnings volatility

Quarterly profit has swung widely, with a KRW 11.8 billion net loss attributable to controlling shareholders in the third quarter of 2026 and KRW 10.4 billion of profit in the first quarter. When non-operating items heavily drive profit, the earnings trend becomes harder to judge. The four-quarter total of KRW 10.1 billion also reflects this volatility.

Overall view

However, in the third quarter of 2026 sales rose to KRW 116.9 billion while the company posted an operating loss of KRW 542 million and a net loss attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 11.8 billion, which exposed a gap between top-line growth and profitability.
