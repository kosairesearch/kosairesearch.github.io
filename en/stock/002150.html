Record Orders, Uneven Earnings at Dohwa Engineering

02

Business structure

Dohwa Engineering, founded in 1957, is one of Korea's leading integrated engineering firms, offering design, supervision and project management across social infrastructure (SOC) such as water and waterworks, transport, energy and urban development.

As of December 2025, engineering accounted for 98.5% of revenue, while power sales made up 1.35% and ESS 0.15%, so diversification remains small. Within engineering, design is the largest segment, followed by construction management (supervision).

A news report citing the H1 2024 quarterly filing put design at KRW 186.1 billion (66.51%), supervision at KRW 65.5 billion (23.41%), O&M at KRW 11.3 billion (4.06%) and EPC at KRW 5.8 billion (2.08%). These segment figures are as of H1 2024 and the current mix may differ.

Its clients are mainly public bodies, and recent order listings include water utility work for Gwangju City in Gyeonggi Province, Incheon Urban Corporation and the Chungcheongnam-do Construction Headquarters.

Overseas, it acquired a Polish rail engineering firm in 2023 as its European base and runs subsidiaries in eight countries, including Vietnam, Indonesia, India, Poland, Colombia, Peru, the United States and Japan.

Water and water-resources work is described as a major contributor to order value, sales and operating profit. Private investment projects and overseas work are cited as drivers of order diversification. Competition centers on design and supervision among domestic engineering firms.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual revenue went from KRW 555.8 billion in 2022 to KRW 575 billion in 2023 (up 3.4% year on year), KRW 582.8 billion in 2024 (up 1.4%) and KRW 698.5 billion in 2025 (up 19.9%). Revenue has risen three years in a row from 2023 through 2025, and 2025 is the largest year of the 2022–2025 period.

Operating profit was KRW 11.9 billion in 2022 and KRW 21.9 billion in 2023 (up 83.4%), then turned into an operating loss of KRW 13.4 billion in 2024 before returning to a profit of KRW 30.1 billion in 2025. The operating margin ran at 2.1% in 2022, 3.8% in 2023, -2.3% in 2024 and 4.3% in 2025.

Reports tied the 2024 operating loss to EPC project provisions booked ahead of time, a case where revenue grew but cost pressure weighed on profit. In H1 2024, supervision revenue rose 31% year on year and a mix shift with lower cost ratios was cited as a support.

Profit attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 1.64 billion in 2022, KRW 20.7 billion in 2023 (up 1,163.2%), a net loss of KRW 5.28 billion in 2024 and KRW 7.33 billion in 2025, a return to profit.

Net profit including non-controlling interests was KRW 6.32 billion in 2025, which differs from the controlling-shareholder figure, so the two should be read separately.

Operating cash flow was KRW 23 billion in 2022, KRW 29.1 billion in 2023, KRW 21.4 billion in 2024 and KRW 12.9 billion in 2025, declining for two straight years.

The debt ratio rose from 95.5% in 2022 to 187.8% in 2025, and at year-end 2025 total liabilities were KRW 473.2 billion against total equity of KRW 252 billion.

On a quarterly basis, revenue was KRW 180.7 billion in 3Q 2025, KRW 193.4 billion in 4Q 2025 (up 7.0% from the prior quarter), KRW 155.5 billion in 1Q 2026 (down 19.6%), KRW 169.6 billion in 2Q 2026 (up 9.1%) and KRW 152 billion in 3Q 2026 (down 10.4% from the prior quarter and down 15.9% year on year).

Operating profit was KRW 7.81 billion in 3Q 2025 and KRW 14.1 billion in 4Q 2025, swung to an operating loss of KRW 2.39 billion in 1Q 2026, then posted KRW 4.49 billion in 2Q 2026 and KRW 13.6 billion in 3Q 2026 (up 74.6% year on year).

In 3Q 2026 revenue fell while operating profit rose sharply and net profit attributable to controlling shareholders turned into a loss, so below-the-line items need to be read alongside operating profit.

Profit attributable to controlling shareholders over the last four quarters (4Q 2025 to 3Q 2026) totaled KRW 832 million.

05

Industry analysis

Order intake in engineering depends on domestic SOC spending and the flow of overseas development projects. In a 2025 industry tally, Dohwa ranked first with KRW 963.1 billion on a preliminary basis, up 24.4% from KRW 774.2 billion in 2024.

In the same tally, a domestic rival ranked third with KRW 620.6 billion, and eight firms won more than KRW 300 billion. Dohwa's order intake moved from KRW 818.4 billion in 2022 to KRW 880 billion in 2023 and KRW 774.2 billion in 2024, and reports noted that domestic rivals had narrowed the gap.

First-half 2025 orders reached KRW 507.8 billion, up 48.3% year on year, with four Polish rail projects worth about KRW 100 billion cited as a main driver. Overseas bidding meant competing against Anglo-American firms that pushed back with objections.

EPC orders were reported at about KRW 60 billion in 2025, a level that was read as a retreat from EPC. The domestic base is still traditional design and supervision work, while overseas rail and PMC work is positioned as the growth axis.

06

Outlook

The company set a 2026 order target of KRW 1 trillion, according to a January 2026 report, which is above its preliminary 2025 order intake of KRW 963.1 billion. Its transport division set a 2026 order target of KRW 250 billion and a 2030 target of KRW 600 billion.

Order backlog at year-end 2025 was KRW 1.8438 trillion, split 66% domestic and 34% overseas, down from an overseas share of 38% at the end of 2023.

Overseas, the company said it is concentrating its efforts on large projects such as the Polish Central Communication Port (CPK) airport PMC and a Lima to Ica rail PMO in Peru. Work in Ulaanbaatar Metro Line 1 in Mongolia as PMC and Lima Metro Line 2 supervision in Peru is already under way.

In October 2026 it signed an agreement with a Korean power plant engineering company to pursue overseas LNG combined-cycle, hydro, pumped-storage and renewable projects together. The agreement is at a foundation stage, and turning it into awarded projects remains the task.

The company has also outlined expansion into higher-value areas such as renewable EPC, O&M, investment development and PMC. The next points to check are how close 2026 order intake comes to the KRW 1 trillion target and whether the Polish CPK PMC award materializes.

08

Bull factors

Larger order intake and backlog

Order intake in 2025 was KRW 963.1 billion on a preliminary basis, the highest since the company's founding by its own account. Backlog at year-end 2025 was KRW 1.8438 trillion, with the overseas share down from 38% at the end of 2023 to 34%. The 2026 order target is set at KRW 1 trillion.

Recovery in operating profit

Operating profit swung from a KRW 13.4 billion operating loss in 2024 to KRW 30.1 billion in 2025, with a 4.3% operating margin. Operating profit was positive in both 2Q and 3Q 2026. The 3Q 2026 figure of KRW 13.6 billion was up 74.6% year on year.

Overseas rail, PMC and partnerships

Cumulative Polish high-speed rail awards reached about KRW 200 billion, and in June 2025 the company won about KRW 100 billion across a Polish rail project and four others.

It also won a site-evaluation job for a new nuclear plant in Uganda and signed an overseas co-bidding agreement with a power engineering firm in October 2026. Its network of subsidiaries in eight countries supports a push into higher-value work such as PMC.

09

Bear factors

Fragile continuity in net profit

Profit attributable to controlling shareholders swung to a KRW 7.33 billion profit in 2025 after a KRW 5.28 billion loss in 2024, but the last four quarters total only KRW 832 million. Controlling-shareholder losses occurred in both 1Q and 3Q 2026.

If operating gains repeatedly fail to carry through to net profit, the quality of earnings needs closer scrutiny.

Higher leverage and slower cash flow

The debt ratio climbed from 95.5% in 2022 to 187.8% in 2025, with total liabilities of KRW 473.2 billion at year-end 2025 exceeding total equity of KRW 252 billion. Operating cash flow fell from KRW 29.1 billion in 2023 to KRW 12.9 billion in 2025. Cash generation has not kept pace with the growth in orders, which adds to the burden.

Quarterly revenue swings and cost provisions

Revenue fell 19.6% from the prior quarter in 1Q 2026 and 15.9% year on year in 3Q 2026. In 2024, EPC project provisions were booked early and produced an operating loss. Overseas bidding faces competition and objections from Anglo-American rivals, so order timing and revenue recognition may not line up.

10

Risk factors

Project costs and provisions

When provisions are booked all at once in EPC and construction-related projects, quarterly profit can swing sharply. Early booking of such provisions was the backdrop to the 2024 operating loss. Higher materials and labor costs could also pressure margins in fee-based design work.

Overseas policy, currency and competition

Overseas work accounts for 34% of backlog at year-end 2025. Projects in Poland, Peru and Indonesia are exposed to local policy, currency moves and bidding calendars. A co-bidding agreement only adds value if it converts into actual awards, and until then the effect remains limited.

Balance sheet and credit profile

With the debt ratio at 187.8% and operating cash flow trending lower, the balance sheet has less room for error. The company's website lists its credit rating as A2+. A change in interest rates or borrowing conditions would affect interest costs and financial flexibility.

12

Overall view

Dohwa Engineering grew its 2025 revenue to KRW 698.5 billion and returned operating profit to KRW 30.1 billion, and its preliminary 2025 order intake of KRW 963.1 billion together with a KRW 1 trillion 2026 target signals a broader order base.

Still, profit attributable to controlling shareholders totaled only KRW 832 million over the last four quarters and turned to a loss in 3Q 2026, so earnings continuity is not yet established.

The debt ratio rose from 95.5% in 2022 to 187.8% in 2025, and operating cash flow has declined since 2023, which makes financial capacity a point to watch closely.

Overseas work, including Polish rail and PMC, is becoming a growth axis, but 34% of backlog sits abroad and brings policy, currency and competitive variables. The bullish factors are the scale of orders and the recovery in operating profit, while the bearish factors are net profit volatility and rising leverage.

The next checkpoints are 2026 full-year order intake, the annual net profit and cash flow in the 2026 annual report, and whether profit holds in 1Q 2027.
