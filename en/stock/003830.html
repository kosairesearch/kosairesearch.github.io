02

Business structure

Daehan Synthetic Fibrer is a textile and petrochemical affiliate of the Taekwang Group whose business has long centered on commodity fibers. The company says it intends to move away from that structure and raise the weight of industrial and advanced materials.

The centerpiece is the liquid crystal polymer (LCP) fiber 'ACEPAR', which is spun in a liquid crystal state and, according to the company, combines high tensile strength, dimensional stability and abrasion resistance.

Its low moisture absorption and low dielectric constant point to high-value uses in electrical and electronics, semiconductors and medical applications, alongside industrial ropes, cables and composites.

The company plans to run ACEPAR alongside the para-aramid fiber 'ACEPARA' made by Taekwang Industrial, the group's sister company, so that it can offer product lines by customer use. It also plans to extend the lightweight, high-strength material into premium bags, outdoor goods and performance apparel.

In 2026 the company took part in the TECHTEXTIL industrial textile fair in Germany and Seoul Premium Textile, and in August it opened a business lounge at COEX in Seoul to discuss cooperation with customers.

Because the production system was built after performance verification with major domestic customers, whether sales growth translates into revenue is the key test of this business shift.

Among listed peers in the fiber and advanced materials space, HS Hyosung Advanced Materials and Kolon Industries are the names most often compared with the sector.

04

Earnings analysis

On a disclosed basis, annual revenue fell from KRW 150.9 billion in 2022 to KRW 134.4 billion in 2023, KRW 123.8 billion in 2024 and KRW 108.2 billion in 2025, a three-year decline. The 2025 drop was 12.6% year on year, with 2022 the largest year and 2025 the smallest in the 2022 to 2025 range.

Operating profit fell from KRW 6.38 billion in 2022 to KRW 2.39 billion in 2023, a 62.5% decline, and turned into a KRW 1.54 billion operating loss in 2024. The 2025 operating loss widened to KRW 7.26 billion. The operating margin slid from 4.2% in 2022 to 1.8% in 2023, -1.2% in 2024 and -6.7% in 2025.

The change-in-results disclosure filed in February 2026 cited a sales decline from domestic and global economic slowdown and deteriorating profitability from external headwinds, and it also noted higher equity-method income from an improved performance at an associate.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders moved from a KRW 2.49 billion net loss in 2024 to a KRW 9.61 billion profit in 2025, so the 2025 profit arose alongside a larger operating loss.

On a quarterly basis, revenue bottomed at KRW 25.4 billion in Q4 2025 and then rose to KRW 26.5 billion, KRW 27.7 billion and KRW 28.3 billion in Q1 to Q3 2026, the third consecutive quarterly increase.

Operating losses moved in the opposite direction, from KRW 642 million in Q1 2026 to KRW 1.14 billion in Q2 and KRW 3.47 billion in Q3. Controlling-shareholder net income swung from a KRW 11 billion profit in Q1 2026 to KRW 2.05 billion in Q2, down 81.5%, and then to a KRW 2.26 billion net loss in Q3.

Because operating and net results diverged this sharply, the influence of non-operating items and equity-method income is a point to verify in the quarterly filings.

05

Industry analysis

Demand across the fiber and materials space has been weak in commodity products. The company's 2026 filing attributed its sales decline to domestic and global economic slowdown, which points to softer demand in that segment.

The company says demand for lightweight, high-performance materials is rising across global industry and is restructuring its portfolio from commodity fibers toward high-value industrial fibers. That market view comes from the company, so the pace of demand growth will need to be judged from future sales.

Electrical and electronics, semiconductor and medical applications, which the company cites as LCP end markets, typically involve longer customer qualification and tighter quality standards than bulk commodity products.

The company presents its completed mass-production system after performance verification with major domestic customers as the basis for early market entry.

In super-fiber lineups, ACEPAR is positioned alongside the para-aramid product of its group sister Taekwang Industrial for industrial rope, cable and composite demand.

Among listed fiber and advanced materials peers, HS Hyosung Advanced Materials and Kolon Industries are the names most often compared, and on disclosed revenue Daehan Synthetic Fibrer is the smaller company.

Because commodity fiber earnings are sensitive to the business cycle, whether three straight years of revenue decline reflect a cyclical downturn or a structural change will become clearer in upcoming annual results.

06

Outlook

The company said it would start full-scale ACEPAR sales in the second half of 2026. Industrial rope, cable and composite customers are the first targets, with later expansion into electrical and electronics, semiconductors, medical uses and consumer goods such as premium bags, outdoor products and apparel.

The strategy pairs the product with the para-aramid 'ACEPARA' from sister company Taekwang Industrial to widen the options available by customer application. Marketing moved from the TECHTEXTIL fair in April 2026 to Seoul Premium Textile in June and a COEX business lounge in August.

The company intends to keep reshaping its portfolio from commodity fibers toward high-value industrial fibers. The first signals that this shift is reaching profitability would be changes in product mix and the direction of the operating margin.

With the Q3 2026 operating loss widening to KRW 3.47 billion, how much early ACEPAR sales narrow that loss is the next item to track. In the 2026 annual report, the key checks are how the operating structure and the weight of equity-method income compare with 2025.

Bull factors

LCP commercial production completed

On August 31, 2026, the company said it had completed a commercial production system for the LCP fiber ACEPAR. It plans to extend use from industrial ropes, cables and composites into semiconductors, medical and consumer bags and apparel.

As the share of commodity fibers declines, a change in product mix could become a path to profitability recovery, which is the core bull argument.

Thick equity and a low debt ratio

Total equity at the end of 2025 was KRW 711 billion against total liabilities of KRW 126.3 billion, a debt ratio of 17.8%. The debt ratio moved within a 17.2% to 20.0% range over 2022 to 2025.

Operating cash flow was negative at KRW 4.9 billion in 2025, but the financial burden is low, leaving relatively more room to fund the business shift.

Revenue up for three straight quarters

Revenue bottomed at KRW 25.4 billion in Q4 2025 and then rose to KRW 26.5 billion in Q1 2026, KRW 27.7 billion in Q2 and KRW 28.3 billion in Q3. Q3 2026 revenue was up 4.7% from a year earlier.

Controlling-shareholder net income posted a KRW 11 billion profit in Q1 2026, and the last four quarters total KRW 10.6 billion.

Bear factors

Five straight quarters of operating losses

Operating losses have run for five consecutive quarters from Q3 2025 through Q3 2026. The Q3 2026 operating loss widened to KRW 3.47 billion from the prior quarter. The annual operating margin fell from 4.2% in 2022 to -6.7% in 2025.

Revenue down three years in a row

Revenue fell from KRW 150.9 billion in 2022 to KRW 108.2 billion in 2025, with a 12.6% decline in 2025. The February 2026 change-in-results disclosure cited domestic and global economic slowdown. If demand for commodity fibers does not recover, it may take time for new products to offset the revenue decline.

Net income swings back to losses

Controlling-shareholder net income fell 81.5% in Q2 2026 to KRW 2.05 billion after a KRW 11 billion profit in Q1, then swung to a KRW 2.26 billion net loss in Q3, a switch to loss from a year earlier. The 2025 profit was linked in the filing to higher equity-method income from an associate.

If net income depends on non-operating items while the core business loses money, earnings volatility will stay high.

10

Risk factors

New product market ramp-up

ACEPAR sales began after performance verification with major domestic customers, but the pace of expansion into overseas customers and consumer markets is not yet visible in disclosed figures. If customer qualification takes longer, the second-half 2026 sales plan may reach revenue later than expected. If pricing and volume in new markets fall short, narrowing the operating loss could be delayed.

Group affiliate product linkage

ACEPAR is planned to be sold alongside ACEPARA, the para-aramid fiber made by sister company Taekwang Industrial, to widen the options offered by customer application.

Because this pairing depends on the group affiliate's production and supply, changes in Taekwang Industrial's volumes or product mix could alter the breadth of the joint lineup. If the combined lineup does not take shape as planned, the company's pitch to industrial rope, cable and composite customers could weaken.

Operating cash flow and financial buffer

Operating cash flow was negative at KRW 4.9 billion in 2025. If operating cash outflows continue while the core business posts losses, the financial cushion built in total equity could be drawn down faster to fund transition costs.

A total equity base well above total liabilities does not by itself offset continuing cash outflows. The burden of covering transition costs from financial capacity could grow.

Overall view

Daehan Synthetic Fibrer is in the early stage of shifting its business from commodity fibers toward high-value LCP fiber. The completion of the ACEPAR commercial production system announced on August 31, 2026 is the central milestone in this transition.

On a disclosed basis, revenue has fallen for three consecutive years since 2022, and operating losses have widened since 2024. Revenue rose for three straight quarters from Q1 to Q3 2026, but operating losses have run for five consecutive quarters.

Controlling-shareholder net income swung sharply from quarter to quarter after the return to profit in 2025. On the financial side, total equity is far larger than total liabilities, which provides a buffer.

The next checkpoints are Q4 2026 revenue, the operating structure in the 2026 annual report, and operating results in Q1 2027.
