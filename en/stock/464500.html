Report overview

Revenue Is Growing, but Costs and Customer Concentration Remain the Issues

Iron Device has posted operating losses for five straight quarters from the third quarter of 2025 through the third quarter of 2026, while revenue remains concentrated in one smart power amplifier product and one customer, Samsung Electronics.

02

Business structure

Iron Device is a fabless company founded in May 2008 that designs and sells mixed-signal SoCs, including audio amplifier chips and smart power amplifiers. Wafer processing is outsourced to external foundries, and analysts describe partnerships with DB HiTek and Samsung Foundry as part of this model.

Smart power amplifiers are the core of revenue. In 2024, they accounted for KRW 6.3 billion of KRW 8.4 billion in sales, or 75%, with government projects making up the remaining 25%. The share rose to 81.0% in 2025 and 91.9% in the first half of 2026.

The company said its flagship predictive smart power amplifier, SMA1305, passed 100 million units sold in June 2026. The main customer is the smartphone division of Samsung Electronics, which press reports put at either above 90% or above 95% of total revenue.

The company has supplied its own SoCs to Samsung since late 2021, and by 2023 the chips had expanded to four models. Analysts note that very few companies in Korea design and supply smart power amplifier SoCs. The business is also widening into power semiconductors and robotics and mobility.

In June 2026 a national project for an electric-vehicle power module with an embedded drive circuit was selected, and in September 2026 a high-voltage SoC for driving film-type ceramic actuators was selected. In August 2026 the company unveiled a compact GaN power stage for humanoid robot joints.

04

Earnings analysis

On a disclosed basis, revenue was KRW 6.23 billion in 2023, KRW 8.37 billion in 2024 (up 34.3% year on year) and KRW 10.1 billion in 2025 (up 21.0% year on year), rising for two consecutive years from 2024 to 2025, with 2025 the largest year of the 2023 to 2025 period.

Operating losses were KRW 3.52 billion in 2023, KRW 4.88 billion in 2024 and KRW 5.57 billion in 2025, three consecutive years of losses from 2023 to 2025, with the loss widening each year. The operating margin was -56.4% in 2023, -58.2% in 2024 and -55.0% in 2025, staying in loss territory without a large improvement.

Net loss attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 3.93 billion in 2023, KRW 4.22 billion in 2024 and KRW 4.54 billion in 2025, and the net loss including non-controlling interests followed the same pattern.

The first-half 2026 operating loss was about KRW 2.3 billion, KRW 0.3 billion smaller than the roughly KRW 2.6 billion in the first half of 2025, according to press reports. Revenue rose by about KRW 8.4 billion, yet the loss narrowed only slightly because of cost growth.

Press reports said first-half 2026 cost of sales was about KRW 7.6 billion, more than eight times the roughly KRW 0.9 billion in the same period of 2025, with raw material and outsourcing costs rising sharply.

By quarter, revenue was KRW 3.11 billion in the third quarter of 2025 and KRW 5.06 billion in the fourth quarter (up 62.6% from the prior quarter), followed by KRW 4.7 billion in the first quarter of 2026, KRW 5.68 billion in the second quarter and KRW 4.97 billion in the third quarter.

Media reported the second-quarter 2026 revenue as the highest quarterly revenue on record. The third-quarter 2026 operating loss was KRW 1.54 billion, wider than a year earlier, and the net loss attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 2.21 billion was the largest of the last five quarters.

The four quarters from the fourth quarter of 2025 through the third quarter of 2026 add up to a net loss attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 4.97 billion. Operating cash flow was KRW -0.723 billion in 2023, KRW -2.63 billion in 2024 and KRW -9.65 billion in 2025, a widening deficit.

05

Industry analysis

The company's end markets include smartphones, automotive infotainment and voice recognition devices, and reports describe steady global demand for high-end audio SoCs. Very few companies in Korea hold this technology, which is cited as a strength.

The company is expanding into humanoid robots, future mobility and AI data centers, areas where demand for high-performance, low-power semiconductors is growing quickly.

The government has said it will provide KRW 1 trillion for a K-on-device AI semiconductor project that pairs fabless designers, foundries and device makers, and press reports said the company could benefit if it joins.

Policy emphasis on diversifying the semiconductor supply chain and strengthening the domestic system chip ecosystem also supports its strategic position. Results track customer volumes more directly than the broader cycle.

When first-quarter 2025 revenue fell to less than half of the prior-year level, the company attributed the drop to production volume adjustments for existing smartphone models and a delayed supply schedule for new smart power amplifiers.

In competitive terms, the company is described as the only domestic firm to localize and commercialize smart power amplifiers. As a fabless company it concentrates on design and relies on foundry partners for wafer production, a model common across the sector.

06

Outlook

When it listed in 2024, the company said it would diversify its customers to six by 2025, and it presented a 2026 revenue target of KRW 59.3 billion and an operating profit forecast of about KRW 13.4 billion.

First-half 2026 revenue of about KRW 10.4 billion equals 17.5% of the target, and press calculations say about KRW 48.9 billion more would be needed in the second half. Given a first-half 2026 operating loss of KRW 2.3 billion, the gap to the profit forecast is also wide.

On the product side, the SMA1305 passed 100 million units sold, and national projects for an electric-vehicle power module and a high-voltage SoC were selected in June and September 2026, widening the power semiconductor pipeline.

In April 2026 a land and building transfer worth KRW 1.40 billion was completed, increasing non-current assets, which reflects added facilities. The GaN power stage for humanoid robot joints was unveiled in August 2026, but the timing of any revenue contribution has not been disclosed. Three points are worth tracking.

First, whether fourth-quarter 2026 revenue holds near the third-quarter level and whether the operating loss narrows from KRW 1.54 billion. Second, whether the gross margin recovers from 26.6% in the first half, as shown in the 2026 annual report.

Third, whether revenue from products other than smart power amplifiers and from customers other than Samsung Electronics grows. These three items will shape the direction of 2026 full-year results.

Bull factors

Expanding revenue base

Revenue rose from KRW 6.23 billion in 2023 to KRW 10.1 billion in 2025, and first-half 2026 revenue passed full-year 2025 revenue within six months. Media reported the second-quarter 2026 revenue as the highest quarterly revenue on record.

Quarterly revenue has since held in a range of about KRW 3.11 billion to KRW 5.68 billion. Whether this trajectory continues is the next point to watch.

Technology scarcity and product traction

The company is described as the only domestic firm to localize and commercialize smart power amplifiers. The company said its predictive smart power amplifier SMA1305 passed 100 million units sold in June 2026. Supplying its own SoCs to Samsung smartphones since late 2021 is also cited as evidence of product validation.

Progress in business diversification

In June 2026 a national project for an electric-vehicle power module with an embedded drive circuit was selected, and in September 2026 a high-voltage SoC project for film-type ceramic actuators was selected. Expansion into power semiconductors and robotics is now visible in filings and press coverage.

Bear factors

Cost pressure and persistent losses

Operating losses have persisted for three consecutive years from 2023 to 2025, with an operating margin of -55.0% in 2025. The first-half 2026 gross margin fell to 26.6% from 56.0% a year earlier. Despite higher revenue, the first-half operating loss narrowed by only about KRW 0.3 billion.

Concentration in customer and product

The smart power amplifier share of revenue reached 91.9% in the first half of 2026. Reports put dependence on Samsung Electronics at between about 90% and more than 95% of revenue. This is some distance from the plan presented at the 2024 listing to diversify to six customers by 2025.

Gap versus targets and cash flow

First-half 2026 revenue equals 17.5% of the KRW 59.3 billion target for 2026. Compared with the KRW 13.4 billion operating profit forecast presented at the listing, the first-half 2026 operating loss was KRW 2.3 billion. Operating cash flow deficit widened to KRW -9.65 billion in 2025.

Risk factors

Customer concentration risk

Most revenue depends on the volume of Samsung smartphone models that adopt the company's chips. In the first quarter of 2025, revenue fell to less than half of the prior-year level after production adjustments for existing models and a delayed supply schedule for new products. Results remain tied to customer model adoption decisions.

Cost and profitability risk

Cost of sales in the first half of 2026 rose to about KRW 7.6 billion from about KRW 0.9 billion a year earlier. Press reports cite higher raw material and outsourcing costs as the cause of the margin decline. If revenue growth does not translate into profit, the loss structure could persist.

Execution and funding risk in new businesses

Expanding into power semiconductors, robotics and mobility may keep research and development staffing costs rising. At the listing, the company said a large part of the offering proceeds would go to research and development personnel costs. Costs may appear before new businesses contribute measurable revenue.

Overall view

Iron Device's revenue rose from KRW 6.23 billion in 2023 to KRW 10.1 billion in 2025, two consecutive years of growth, and first-half 2026 revenue passed full-year 2025 revenue within six months.

However, operating and net losses attributable to controlling shareholders have persisted for three consecutive years from 2023 to 2025, and the company has posted losses for five straight quarters through the third quarter of 2026.

The first-half 2026 gross margin fell to 26.6%, which press reports identify as the reason revenue growth has not reached profit. The rise of the smart power amplifier share to 91.9% and the high dependence on Samsung Electronics are read both as strengths and as risks.

Some analysts also point to a wide gap between first-half results and the 2026 targets and profit forecast presented at the listing.

On the other side, the scarcity of smart power amplifier technology, the national projects selected in 2026 and development progress in power semiconductors and robotics are cited as grounds for diversification.

The next checkpoints are the operating loss in the fourth-quarter and full-year 2026 preliminary results, the cost of sales ratio in the 2026 annual report, and the revenue share from customers other than Samsung Electronics.
