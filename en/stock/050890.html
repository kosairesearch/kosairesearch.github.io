Solid: Overseas Sales Carry the Model, Losses Test It

02

Business structure

Solid makes telecom equipment, mainly mobile repeaters and in-building distributed antenna systems (DAS), and also runs a defense business through its subsidiary, Solid's defense unit. Telecom equipment accounted for KRW 57.5 billion, or 90.3%, of Q1 2026 revenue of KRW 63.7 billion.

In Korea, the company says it is the only supplier delivering equipment to all three mobile carriers, SK Telecom, KT and LG Uplus. Its center of gravity, however, is overseas. In 2025 the revenue mix was 31.8% United States, 18.4% Europe and 13.8% Japan, so sales outside Korea exceeded 70%.

US sales rose 9.9% to KRW 93.8 billion in the same year. Q1 2026 regional sales were KRW 22.4 billion in Korea, KRW 16.9 billion in the US, KRW 13.9 billion in Europe and KRW 8.1 billion in Japan, and US plus European sales exceeded domestic sales.

The core product is in-building DAS, which improves indoor coverage in large buildings, subway lines and stadiums. Electronic Times reported that Solid holds a global DAS share of about 15.1%, ranking it third. The company is also extending into Open RAN, a next-generation hybrid DAS and AI-RAN products.

R&D spending in the telecom equipment segment was KRW 45.3 billion in 2025, up from KRW 36.6 billion a year earlier, equal to 15.4% of segment sales.

04

Earnings analysis

On the disclosed annual figures, revenue rose from KRW 279.8 billion in 2022 to KRW 321.4 billion in 2023, a 14.9% increase, and reached its high of KRW 331.1 billion in 2024, the largest year of 2022 to 2025. It then fell 10.9% to KRW 294.8 billion in 2025.

Operating profit climbed 26.8% to KRW 36.3 billion in 2023, the largest year of the period, and then declined 3.1% to KRW 35.1 billion in 2024 and 5.3% to KRW 33.3 billion in 2025, a two-year decline from 2024 through 2025.

The operating margin read 10.2% in 2022, 11.3% in 2023, 10.6% in 2024 and 11.3% in 2025, so it improved in 2025 even as revenue shrank. Company-cited headwinds in 2025 were lower domestic telecom investment and a smaller defense subsidiary business, partly offset by demand for overseas indoor coverage equipment.

Profit attributable to controlling shareholders rose from KRW 29.8 billion in 2022 to KRW 40.9 billion in 2023 and KRW 46.1 billion in 2024, the period high, before falling 21.1% to KRW 36.4 billion in 2025.

Net income including non-controlling interests is a separate figure, KRW 46.2 billion in 2024 and KRW 36.4 billion in 2025.

The debt-to-equity ratio fell from 89.8% in 2022 to 51.9% in 2025, with total equity of KRW 353.3 billion, equity attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 352.7 billion and total liabilities of KRW 183.3 billion at end-2025.

Operating cash flow went from KRW 15.1 billion in 2022 to KRW 56.9 billion in 2024 and then KRW 22.8 billion in 2025. Quarterly sales are heavily weighted to the fourth quarter.

Q4 2025 revenue reached KRW 112.9 billion, up 61.2% from Q3, and operating profit jumped to KRW 26.1 billion, which the company linked to defense projects that typically concentrate sales at year-end.

Q1 2026 swung to an operating loss of KRW 2.13 billion on revenue of KRW 63.7 billion, while profit attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 22.3 billion, a wide gap between the operating and net lines.

Q2 2026 returned to operating profit of KRW 3.54 billion on revenue of KRW 70.1 billion, but posted a net loss attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 142 million.

Q3 2026 revenue was KRW 67.8 billion, down 3.1% year on year, with operating profit of KRW 5.14 billion, up 84.0%, yet the controlling-shareholder net loss widened to KRW 9.35 billion and turned to a loss from a year earlier.

Profit attributable to controlling shareholders over the last four quarters totals KRW 39.9 billion, and KRW 27.1 billion of that came from Q4 2025.

05

Industry analysis

Domestic telecom equipment demand depends heavily on capital spending by the three mobile carriers. The company said that in Q1 2026 reduced domestic carrier investment limited the recovery of domestic sales.

Meanwhile, Electronic Times reported in May 2026 that global carriers are pacing their 5G spending, while demand for better indoor coverage in large buildings, subway lines and stadiums continues.

Industry observers also view indoor coverage upgrades in buildings, subways and public facilities as relatively steady when base-station investment slows. This structure is consistent with 2025, when overseas indoor-network equipment sales partly offset weaker domestic investment.

Open RAN is a technology that builds the 5G radio access interface and software as open standards to reduce dependence on a single equipment maker, and Solid supplies Open RAN equipment. Solid ranks third among global DAS suppliers with a share of about 15.1%.

In December 2025, Hana Securities published a report that cited domestic frequency reallocation as a potential beneficiary. The timing and scale of that reallocation are not confirmed, so its size as a benefit cannot be stated with confidence.

The balance between domestic investment and overseas indoor demand is the core reference for judging the sector.

06

Outlook

In May 2026 the company said it would expand telecom equipment sales in North America and Europe and grow new businesses such as military satellite communications and Open RAN to restore Q2 results. It also said it aimed for a rebound in the second half.

Q2 2026 did return to operating profit of KRW 3.54 billion, and Q3 operating profit rose 84.0% year on year to KRW 5.14 billion.

However, net losses attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 142 million in Q2 and KRW 9.35 billion in Q3 show that the operating-level improvement has not carried through to the net line.

Per the Q1 2026 quarterly report, the order backlog stood at USD 20.04 million in North America and USD 14.55 million in Europe. The company said it would respond to rising demand for in-building DAS, Open RAN and AI-RAN, and R&D spending was KRW 9.5 billion in Q1 2026, or 14.8% of revenue.

In defense, the shift from tactical information communication systems to military satellite communications created a gap in results, and the point at which that transition completes and revenue resumes is the checkpoint for the defense segment.

Whether global carriers keep pacing 5G spending remains a variable for the speed of overseas orders. Whether the year-end concentration of sales reappears in Q4 2026 will shape the full-year picture.

08

Bull factors

Overseas-weighted earnings base

Sales outside Korea exceeded 70% of 2025 revenue, and US sales rose 9.9% to KRW 93.8 billion. The operating margin reached 11.3% in 2025, up from 10.2% in 2022.

The margin rose even as revenue fell 10.9%, which lines up with the company's account that overseas indoor-network demand partly offset weaker domestic investment.

Expansion into Open RAN and DAS products

R&D spending in the telecom equipment segment reached KRW 45.3 billion in 2025, up from KRW 36.6 billion, and its share of sales rose to 15.4%. The company is supplying Open RAN equipment while developing next-generation hybrid DAS. Q1 2026 R&D was KRW 9.5 billion, or 14.8% of revenue.

A broader product set could spread exposure to any single country's telecom investment cycle, which is one pillar of the bullish case.

Balance sheet and overseas order backlog

The debt-to-equity ratio fell from 89.8% in 2022 to 51.9% at end-2025, with liabilities of KRW 183.3 billion and total equity of KRW 353.3 billion. The Q1 2026 quarterly report shows a backlog of USD 20.04 million in North America and USD 14.55 million in Europe.

With a larger equity base, the company could be read as having financial capacity even through loss-making quarters.

09

Bear factors

Shrinking domestic telecom capex

Reduced capex by the three domestic carriers limited the recovery of domestic sales in Q1 2026. The domestic share of sales fell from 37% to 29% in 2025. As that share shrinks, results become more sensitive to overseas order timing, exchange rates and the investment pace of foreign carriers.

Accumulating loss-making quarters

Net losses attributable to controlling shareholders in Q2 and Q3 2026 make it two consecutive loss-making quarters. Q3 operating profit rose 84.0% year on year to KRW 5.14 billion, but the controlling-shareholder net loss of KRW 9.35 billion turned a year-earlier profit into a loss. The failure of the operating recovery to reach the net line raises questions about earnings quality.

Slowing profit and cash flow

Operating profit has fallen for two consecutive years through 2025 after peaking at KRW 36.3 billion in 2023. Operating cash flow dropped from KRW 56.9 billion in 2024 to KRW 22.8 billion in 2025. Because sales concentrate in the fourth quarter, full-year results depend heavily on Q4 outcomes.

10

Risk factors

Dependence on overseas investment cycles

With more than 70% of sales overseas, earnings depend on how quickly carriers in the United States, Europe and Japan invest. If global carriers slow 5G spending, order timing can slip and quarterly sales can swing. In Q1 2025, Japanese sales fell 64.4% after a customer delayed purchase orders.

Quarterly seasonality and volatility

Because defense project revenue tends to concentrate at year-end, Q4 2025 revenue of KRW 112.9 billion far exceeded Q3's KRW 70 billion. In Q1 2026, revenue fell to KRW 63.7 billion and the company posted an operating loss. Quarterly profitability swings widely, so full-year figures alone cannot explain the quarterly pattern.

Defense transition and consolidation costs

The defense subsidiary moved from tactical information communication systems toward military satellite communications, which left a gap in its results. Start-up costs and staff costs from a newly consolidated subsidiary, plus marketing spending for new overseas equipment, weighed on Q1 2026 profitability. Until that transition is complete, it remains a source of earnings volatility.

Overall view

Solid kept its 2025 operating margin at 11.3% on a base where sales outside Korea exceed 70%. Annual operating profit peaked at KRW 36.3 billion in 2023 and then fell for two consecutive years through 2025, while profit attributable to controlling shareholders fell to KRW 36.4 billion in 2025.

Quarterly results were heavily concentrated in Q4, and after an operating loss in Q1 2026, the controlling-shareholder line posted net losses in Q2 and Q3. The return to KRW 5.14 billion in Q3 operating profit is a positive sign, but its failure to reach the net line is the central difference in this period.

On the balance sheet, the debt-to-equity ratio fell from 89.8% in 2022 to 51.9% in 2025, with total equity of KRW 353.3 billion at end-2025. The bullish factors are the overseas earnings base, product expansion in Open RAN and DAS, and the overseas order backlog.

The bearish factors are shrinking domestic telecom investment, the defense business transition and the accumulation of loss-making quarters.

The next checkpoints are the items that explain the gap between operating and controlling-shareholder net profit in the 2026 annual report, and the regional sales mix in the Q1 2027 report. This report is for information only and does not include an investment opinion.
