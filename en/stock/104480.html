02

Business structure

TK Chemical was founded in 2007 to make and sell synthetic fibers and related chemical products, and in 2008 it took over the chemical business division of a trading company.

Its core chemicals segment centers on PET resin (PET chips) for beverage and food containers, where the company cites economic efficiency and processability as the basis for steady demand. The company says its upgraded facilities now allow it to produce higher-quality PET chips to strengthen competitiveness.

It exited the polyester yarn business in 2024, with Chinese low-priced products eroding the yarn business's cost competitiveness. The construction segment recognizes revenue from apartment sales, and in 2024 delayed sales schedules pushed back revenue recognition.

In its March 2026 disclosure, the company attributed a sharp swing in results to higher construction revenue recognition and higher equity-method income from affiliates.

Because chemicals, construction and equity-method results move together inside one company, the segment mix needs to be separated when reading earnings. The largest shareholder group, led by SM Industries, held 47.89% as of September 29, 2026, and the company is an affiliate of SM Group. Its competitive set is best read against PET producers such as Lotte Chemical and SK Chemicals.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual revenue fell from KRW 753.3 billion in 2022 to KRW 416.3 billion in 2023, down 44.7% year on year, and then to KRW 339.6 billion in 2024, down 18.4%. Revenue edged up 1.8% to KRW 345.8 billion in 2025. Among 2022 to 2025, 2022 was the largest year and 2024 the smallest.

Operating profit of KRW 481 million in 2022 turned into an operating loss of KRW 32 billion in 2023, and the operating loss narrowed to KRW 1.59 billion in 2024, which continued the loss. 2025 returned to an operating profit of KRW 2.4 billion, an operating margin of 0.7%.

The company said the 2024 operating loss reflected lower sales during a roughly one-month regular overhaul at the Gumi plant and revenue recognition delayed by apartment sale schedules.

Profit attributable to controlling shareholders moved from KRW 275.2 billion in 2022 to a loss of KRW 8.92 billion in 2023 and a loss of KRW 112.1 billion in 2024, then returned to a KRW 116.5 billion profit in 2025. The 2024 net loss included a disposal loss on equity stakes sold to repay borrowings.

Because the 2025 controlling-shareholder profit is far larger than the KRW 2.4 billion operating profit, non-operating and equity-method gains appear to carry much of the result.

Operating cash flow went from KRW 11.8 billion in 2022 and outflows of KRW 51.2 billion in 2023 and KRW 29.9 billion in 2024 to an inflow of KRW 145.1 billion in 2025.

On a quarterly basis, Q4 2025 revenue was KRW 74.6 billion, down 18.9% from the prior quarter, with operating profit of KRW 1.2 billion and a controlling-shareholder net loss of KRW 10.7 billion. Q1 2026 revenue was KRW 104.8 billion with operating profit of KRW 5.62 billion.

Q2 2026 revenue reached KRW 129.5 billion, operating profit KRW 21.5 billion and controlling-shareholder profit KRW 41.1 billion. Q3 2026 revenue was KRW 104.3 billion, up 13.3% year on year, and controlling-shareholder profit was KRW 103.7 billion, while operating profit fell to KRW 3.8 billion. Controlling-shareholder profit for the latest four quarters totals KRW 141.4 billion.

05

Industry analysis

PET resin, TK Chemical's core product, is a commodity material tied to demand for beverage and food containers. Standalone revenue for the first half of 2026 rose 30.8% year on year and operating profit turned positive, while net profit fell 53.2% in the same data set.

Anti-dumping complaints against low-priced Chinese products and overseas market development are cited as drivers of the improvement.

Chinese low-priced supply eroded the cost competitiveness of the yarn business and led to its exit, so whether the same pressure carries into PET chip pricing and volumes is central to the industry read.

Mandatory use of recycled raw materials from 2026 has put the company in a position to build supply capability in recycled PET. The construction segment has been sensitive to property markets, since delayed sales schedules shifted revenue recognition in 2024.

Against large petrochemical names such as LG Chem and Lotte Chemical, the company's scale is much smaller, and its earnings swings combine PET chip cycles with construction and equity-method results. Compared with SK Chemicals, which also operates in PET, the business mix differs considerably.

06

Outlook

At its March 2025 annual shareholder meeting, the company set targets of returning to profit that year after exiting the yarn business and achieving a debt-free operation by 2026.

The profit target was met on the 2025 figures, with operating profit of KRW 2.4 billion and controlling-shareholder profit of KRW 116.5 billion. Whether the debt-free goal has been reached will need to be checked against borrowing balances in the 2026 annual report.

In the chemicals segment, the company plans to strengthen competitiveness with higher-quality PET chips produced on upgraded equipment. For construction, it expected full-scale profit improvement from apartment sales for a project started in the fourth quarter of 2024.

The company also plans to secure medium- to long-term growth through mergers and acquisitions in new businesses. Expanding recycled PET supply capacity is tied to mandatory recycled-content rules that apply from 2026.

At the March 31, 2026 annual meeting, the re-election of an inside director, Woo Oh-hyun, was approved. The December 11, 2026 extraordinary meeting will take up the election of another inside director, which is the point to watch for changes in board composition and management direction.

08

Bull factors

Direction of quarterly profit has turned

Profit attributable to controlling shareholders rose from KRW 7.31 billion in Q1 2026 to KRW 41.1 billion in Q2 and KRW 103.7 billion in Q3, two consecutive increases. Q2 2026 revenue of KRW 129.5 billion was the largest of the last five quarters on the disclosed figures. Q3 revenue grew 13.3% year on year, showing that the top-line recovery continued.

PET positioning and trade environment

Standalone revenue for the first half of 2026 rose 30.8% year on year, and anti-dumping complaints against Chinese low-priced products together with overseas market development were cited as improvement drivers.

Some assessments credit the company with moving early to meet the recycled-content mandate and secure recycled PET supply. Operating profit's positive run in the first half of 2026 aligns with the same trend.

Easing of financial burden

The debt ratio fell from 51.0% in 2022 to 20.2% in 2025, and total liabilities at the end of 2025 were KRW 248 billion. Operating cash flow moved from an outflow of KRW 29.9 billion in 2024 to an inflow of KRW 145.1 billion in 2025. The company said the 2024 sale of equity stakes produced annual interest savings of KRW 5 billion.

09

Bear factors

Thin operating base

Operating profit was KRW 2.4 billion in 2025, an operating margin of 0.7%, which is thin relative to revenue. Q3 2026 operating profit of KRW 3.8 billion fell 82.3% from the prior quarter.

The gap with the KRW 103.7 billion controlling-shareholder profit in the same quarter is the starting point of the bear argument that earnings rest on non-operating items.

Loss history and revenue declines

The company posted an operating loss of KRW 32 billion in 2023 and a controlling-shareholder net loss of KRW 112.1 billion in 2024. Revenue fell 44.7% in 2023 and 18.4% in 2024 from the prior year.

The 2025 return to profit is a single year's result, so whether it repeats needs confirmation from further quarterly results.

Recurrence of Chinese low-price pressure

The yarn business exited after Chinese low-priced products eroded its cost competitiveness, and the sale of equity stakes in that process produced a disposal loss. PET chips also compete with low-priced Chinese supply, so the effectiveness and durability of anti-dumping measures could decide earnings. If the measures weaken, pressure similar to the yarn case could return.

10

Risk factors

Earnings quality

The gap between operating profit and controlling-shareholder profit is wide, so swings in equity-method and non-operating results can move quarterly earnings sharply. In its March 2026 disclosure, the company cited higher equity-method income as a reason for the swing. Results at affiliates therefore pass directly into the company's net profit.

Chinese supply and trade policy

Whether anti-dumping complaints lead to actual duties, and whether Chinese supply capacity expands again, will drive PET chip prices and volumes. The yarn exit showed how Chinese low-priced penetration can erode a business, and the same risk could recur in chips. Shifts in trade policy add uncertainty to earnings projections.

Governance and shareholder structure

The largest shareholder group held 47.89% as of September 29, 2026, a high concentration. An inside director election is on the agenda for the December 11, 2026 extraordinary meeting, which could change board composition.

A controlling-shareholder structure is central to understanding how management direction and capital policy are decided.

12

Overall view

TK Chemical swung from operating and net losses in 2024 to positive operating profit and controlling-shareholder profit in 2025. In 2026, controlling-shareholder profit has risen for two consecutive quarters after the first-quarter low, and the latest four quarters total KRW 141.4 billion.

Bullish points include the lower debt ratio, the turn to positive operating cash flow, anti-dumping complaints against low-priced Chinese products, and positioning for recycled PET.

Bearish points include a small operating profit base, the 82.3% quarter-on-quarter drop in Q3 2026 operating profit, and reliance on non-operating and equity-method gains for net profit.

Earlier yarn exit and construction schedule effects still shape the earnings trend, and the high concentration of the largest shareholder is also a factor to weigh. The current picture is a period in which quarterly profit has recovered while the operating base remains thin.

The next checkpoints are the non-operating breakdown and borrowing balance in the 2026 annual report, along with any board changes from the December 11 extraordinary meeting.
