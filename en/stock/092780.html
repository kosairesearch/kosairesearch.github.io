DYP: North American Volume Growth Versus Quarterly Losses

Sales are rising on North American volume, but the third quarter of 2026 swung to an operating loss of KRW 951 million and a controlling-shareholder net loss of KRW 10.1 billion, leaving new-business ramp-ups as the next variable.

  1. 1

    Third-quarter 2026 sales rose 3.4% year on year to KRW 119.9 billion, yet the company posted an operating loss of KRW 951 million after an operating profit of KRW 6.74 billion a year earlier.

  2. 2

    Annual sales reached KRW 459.3 billion in 2025 and operating profit reached KRW 16.7 billion, the largest year of 2022 to 2025, while the operating margin stayed at 3.6%.

  3. 3

    Over the last four quarters (Q4 2025 to Q3 2026), the controlling-shareholder net loss totals KRW 3.49 billion, and the company has posted losses for two consecutive quarters since Q2 2026.

  4. 4

    Cost pressure came from higher North American freight costs (cumulative increase of about KRW 4.4 billion), staffing investment at the Mexico subsidiary, and about KRW 9 billion of foreign-exchange-related losses in the third quarter, most of them non-cash.

  5. 5

02

Business structure

DYP is a piston specialist founded in 1977 that manufactures, assembles and sells internal-combustion engine pistons.

According to the company profile, it operates through headquarters, two domestic and four overseas subsidiaries, and supplies Hyundai Motor, KIA, BMW and AUDI, holding a 9% share of global automotive OEM sales.

As of year-end 2025, product pistons accounted for 74.37% of sales, commodity pistons for 18.52%, eco-friendly products for 3.88% and other sales for 2.54%. Export share was 68.4% for product pistons and 53.2% for the company overall.

Overseas production bases include Mexico, Wendeng in China and Alabama in the United States, and the Mexico subsidiary handles volume for North American customers. The company acquired a hydrogen fuel-cell vehicle enclosure business in 2018 and set up an eco-friendly vehicle parts subsidiary, DYP Eco, in December 2024.

One domestic research house estimates a domestic piston market share of about 60% and a global ranking of fourth. On the customer side, volumes from major customers such as Ford and Stellantis in North America have increased, and supply to Hyundai Motor and KIA North American plants has expanded.

On the competitive side, a top-three global piston maker plans to close its Mexico plant in April 2027, and according to the company part of its supply volume will move to DYP. The business is widening from internal-combustion pistons into electric-vehicle, hydrogen and robotics components.

04

Earnings analysis

In the annual results from the regulatory filings, sales were KRW 397.9 billion in 2022, KRW 421.7 billion in 2023 (up 6.0% year on year), KRW 431 billion in 2024 (up 2.2%) and KRW 459.3 billion in 2025 (up 6.6%), rising three consecutive years from 2023 to 2025.

Within 2022 to 2025, 2025 was the largest year and 2022 the smallest. Operating profit was KRW 7.44 billion in 2022, KRW 13.3 billion in 2023 (up 78.8%), KRW 9.7 billion in 2024 (down 27.1%) and KRW 16.7 billion in 2025 (up 71.8%).

The operating margin was 1.9% in 2022, 3.2% in 2023, 2.2% in 2024 and 3.6% in 2025, so margin improvement lagged sales growth.

Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders rose from KRW 2.61 billion in 2022 to KRW 7.63 billion in 2023 (up 192.5%), peaked at KRW 8.48 billion in 2024, and fell to KRW 7.79 billion in 2025 (down 8.2%).

Net income including non-controlling interests, at KRW 8.07 billion in 2025, is a separate figure from controlling-shareholder net profit. The debt-to-equity ratio was 200.6% in 2022, 197.8% in 2023, 194.2% in 2024 and 214.8% in 2025.

At year-end 2025, total equity was KRW 147.2 billion, of which KRW 143.7 billion belonged to controlling shareholders, and total liabilities were KRW 316.2 billion. Operating cash flow fell from KRW 44.1 billion in 2023 to KRW 18.2 billion in 2024 and to KRW 2.15 billion negative in 2025.

Quarterly results show a sharper pattern. Sales went from KRW 115.9 billion in Q3 2025 to KRW 126.7 billion in Q4 2025 (up 9.3% quarter on quarter), then KRW 117.1 billion in Q1 2026 (down 7.6%) and KRW 130.3 billion in Q2 2026 (up 11.2%).

Operating profit went from KRW 6.74 billion in Q3 2025 to KRW 6.26 billion in Q4 2025, KRW 2.95 billion in Q1 2026 (down 52.9%) and KRW 453 million in Q2 2026 (down 84.6%).

In Q3 2026, sales of KRW 119.9 billion rose 3.4% year on year, but the company recorded an operating loss of KRW 951 million and a controlling-shareholder net loss of KRW 10.1 billion, swinging to a loss from a year-earlier profit.

The company said freight costs tied to North American volume rose about KRW 4.4 billion on a cumulative basis, that staffing for capacity at the Mexico subsidiary was added ahead of demand, and that about KRW 9 billion of foreign-exchange losses from the weaker won were included in the third-quarter net loss, most of them non-cash. The controlling-shareholder net loss over the last four quarters totals KRW 3.49 billion.

05

Industry analysis

Demand for DYP's core internal-combustion piston business depends on vehicle production volumes and the local production structure. One domestic research house analyzed that major global piston makers are cutting investment and supply chains are being reorganized, with North American customers asking for more supply.

The same analysis expects DYP's market share to expand on that basis. On the demand side, growth in AI data-center generator engines is cited as a new avenue for engine components.

In eco-friendly vehicles, the company has built an electric-vehicle heat-dissipation module line at its Hwaseong plant and has been selected as a supplier of hydrogen fuel-cell stack end plates.

In robotics, the company said it has completed an internal feasibility review of humanoid core components such as actuator housings and is preparing customer proposals. Its competitive position is split in two. The domestic market share of about 60% is high, while its global ranking is cited as fourth.

The planned closure of a top-three global piston maker's Mexico plant is a North American supply opportunity, but the timing and scale of volume transfers and how fast they reach earnings remain uncertain.

The CEO said that concerns about a shrinking internal-combustion market should be weighed alongside the competitiveness of the existing business and the growth potential of new businesses.

06

Outlook

The company has set a goal of growing new-business sales to KRW 100 billion. It began supplying electric-vehicle cap assemblies to Samsung SDI in July 2026 and expects full-scale mass-production revenue from Q4 2026.

Two end-plate products for Hyundai Motor's next-generation 100 kW hydrogen fuel-cell system were selected for mass production, as disclosed in a regulatory filing. The electric-vehicle heat-dissipation module line at the Hwaseong plant is part of the eco-friendly expansion.

The company said most common infrastructure investment for new businesses, including measurement and inspection equipment and plant space, is expected to be completed during 2026.

In North America, volumes from major customers such as Ford and Stellantis increased and supply to Hyundai Motor and KIA's North American plants expanded, lifting cumulative Americas sales by 26% year on year through the third quarter of 2026.

The company prioritized supply stability and local capacity to match North American volume, and said that profitability should recover once the added capacity converts to sales and logistics efficiency improves.

The next checkpoints are whether new-business mass-production revenue appears in Q4 2026 and how much North American freight and foreign-exchange items weigh on quarterly results.

Over the medium term, the volume transfer tied to the planned April 2027 closure of the Mexico plant of a competitor is a key point for North American utilization. How the scale and profitability of new businesses appear in the 2026 annual report is another point to track.

Bear factors

Two consecutive loss quarters and margin pressure

The company has posted losses for two consecutive quarters, from Q2 2026 through Q3 2026. The Q3 2026 operating loss of KRW 951 million compares with an operating profit of KRW 6.74 billion a year earlier. Accumulated net losses have weakened the earnings base. Sales grew, but freight costs and upfront investment in new businesses are pressing margins.

Currency exposure and financial leverage

The third-quarter 2026 net loss included about KRW 9 billion of foreign-exchange losses from the weaker won. The debt-to-equity ratio rose to 214.8% at year-end 2025 from 194.2% in 2024. Operating cash flow fell from KRW 44.1 billion in 2023 to KRW 2.15 billion negative in 2025.

Shrinking internal-combustion demand and customer concentration

Product pistons, the internal-combustion core, accounted for 74.37% of sales at year-end 2025. Investment cuts by global piston makers can create supply opportunities, but faster vehicle electrification would reduce core demand. Dependence on volumes from a few major North American customers is also relatively high.

Risk factors

Demand structure risk

Sales concentrated in pistons are directly exposed to shrinking internal-combustion demand. Whether new-business revenue can replace the decline in core volumes is the key question.

The CEO said concerns about a shrinking internal-combustion market should be considered alongside the competitiveness of the existing business.

Currency and freight cost risk

Won-dollar exchange rate moves swing net profit through foreign-currency translation gains and losses. The company said most of the roughly KRW 9 billion of third-quarter 2026 foreign-exchange losses were non-cash. If freight costs for North American delivery keep rising, margin recovery could be delayed.

New-business execution risk

The timing of mass-production revenue for electric-vehicle parts for Samsung SDI and hydrogen end plates for Hyundai Motor depends on the company's guidance. Humanoid components are still at the stage of customer proposals after an internal feasibility review, so orders remain some way off. If infrastructure investment is complete but revenue does not follow, the profitability burden could persist.

Overall view

DYP started as an internal-combustion piston maker and is widening into North American volume growth and into eco-friendly vehicle, hydrogen and robotics components. Annual sales rose for three consecutive years from 2023 to 2025, and 2025 was the largest year of 2022 to 2025 with operating profit of KRW 16.7 billion.

However, third-quarter 2026 sales rose while the company posted an operating loss of KRW 951 million and a controlling-shareholder net loss of KRW 10.1 billion, and it has posted losses for two consecutive quarters since Q2 2026.

The drivers include higher North American freight costs, staffing at the Mexico subsidiary and foreign-exchange losses. On the strength side are North American volume growth, mass-production timing for new businesses with Samsung SDI and Hyundai Motor, and possible volume transfers from a competitor's plant closure.

On the weakness side are an operating margin that stayed in the low single digits, a debt-to-equity ratio of 214.8% at year-end 2025 and weaker operating cash flow. A P/E ratio cannot be calculated given the last four quarters of net losses, while on a net-asset basis the share price is read as trading at a discount.

The next checkpoints are new-business mass-production revenue in Q4 2026, annual margin and cash flow in the 2026 annual report, and the volume transfer from the Mexico plant closure in April 2027.
