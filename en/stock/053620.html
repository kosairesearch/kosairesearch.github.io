Taeyang: Cost Pressure and Earnings Durability in Core Butane Gas

02

Business structure

Taeyang makes portable butane gas (fuel tubes) and aerosol products. According to Alphasquare's company page as of October 2, 2026, the butane gas (fuel tube) segment accounts for 81.42% of sales and the aerosol segment for 18.58%.

The core fuel tube business sells under the Sun Fuel and lighter gas brands and has expanded exports to more than 60 countries. A company overview describes the global butane gas market as growing on rising incomes and wider awareness of product utility.

However, on a cumulative basis through Q3 2025, the fuel tube segment faced higher cost burdens from intensifying market competition and rising raw material prices. The aerosol segment saw sales and profitability decline as utilization rates fell.

In its corporate value-up plan disclosed on March 10, 2026, the company set out profitability-focused operations, continued R&D, quality control, and product line diversification through new launches, alongside a dividend policy.

Because sales are heavily concentrated in one product family, diversification remains a key challenge. In the fuel tube market, pricing and cost competition appear to be the main variables shaping results. Segment cost ratios and customer-level sales mix can be checked in the notes to the periodic reports.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual revenue fell from KRW 174.2 billion in 2022 to KRW 157 billion in 2023 (down 9.9% year on year), KRW 152.2 billion in 2024 (down 3.0%), and KRW 140.4 billion in 2025 (down 7.8%), marking three consecutive annual declines from 2023 through 2025. Among 2022 to 2025, 2022 was the largest year and 2025 the smallest.

Operating profit fell from KRW 7.43 billion in 2022 to KRW 6.54 billion in 2023 (down 12.0%) and KRW 2.17 billion in 2024 (down 66.9%). Operating profit then rebounded to KRW 2.39 billion in 2025, up 10.4%.

The operating margin slipped from 4.3% in 2022 and 4.2% in 2023 to 1.4% in 2024 and 1.7% in 2025, a period in which falling sales and thinner margins overlapped.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders moved the other way, rising from KRW 6.58 billion in 2022 to KRW 7.31 billion in 2023 (up 11.0%), KRW 8.26 billion in 2024 (up 13.0%), and KRW 11.9 billion in 2025 (up 43.5%), with three consecutive annual increases from 2023 through 2025.

Because net income rose in 2024 even as operating profit fell sharply, non-operating items appear to have contributed materially. Net income including non-controlling interests was also KRW 11.9 billion in 2025, the same as the controlling-shareholder figure.

Operating cash flow was KRW 9.68 billion in 2025, below KRW 11.9 billion in 2022, KRW 16.4 billion in 2023, and KRW 13.7 billion in 2024.

On a quarterly basis, sales were KRW 33.6 billion in Q3 2025, KRW 36.9 billion in Q4 2025 (up 9.8% quarter on quarter), KRW 36.8 billion in Q1 2026, and KRW 44.2 billion in Q2 2026 (up 20.1%), the highest level in the window. Q3 2026 sales fell to KRW 31.7 billion, down 28.3% quarter on quarter and 5.7% year on year.

Operating profit moved from KRW 34.59 million in Q3 2025 to KRW 1.82 billion in Q4 2025, KRW 2.49 billion in Q1 2026, and KRW 3.64 billion in Q2 2026, then fell to KRW 287 million in Q3 2026.

Controlling-shareholder net income of KRW 8.62 billion in Q1 2026 sat well above that quarter's operating profit of KRW 2.49 billion, which points to non-operating or one-off items. The figure then fell to KRW 5.2 billion in Q2 and turned into a net loss of KRW 6.21 billion in Q3 2026.

Over the last four quarters (Q4 2025 through Q3 2026), net income attributable to controlling shareholders totals KRW 9.13 billion.

Industry analysis

The butane gas market is described as growing on rising incomes and wider awareness of product utility. However, on a cumulative basis through Q3 2025, the fuel tube segment saw market competition intensify into cost pressure, and rising raw material prices squeezed margins.

Whether raw material costs can be passed through to selling prices is therefore the central margin variable.

A domestic financial information site classifies Taeyang under the household goods sub-sector within lifestyle goods, and its October 2, 2026 data ranks Taeyang third by sales and second by operating margin among ten companies in that sub-sector (Alphasquare).

That classification differs from the steel and metals sector, so sector-level metal comparisons cannot be applied directly. Taeyang is also small against metals peers in both market value and sales scale. Lower utilization at the aerosol business can translate into higher fixed-cost burdens.

With 2025 sales at the lowest level in the 2022 to 2025 window, whether the revenue decline reflects sector weakness or company-specific factors needs to be checked against segment volume data.

In relative terms, the company ranks high on operating margin within its sub-sector but mid-range on sales, so the emphasis is on defending profitability rather than growth.

Because Q3 2026 sales fell to KRW 31.7 billion, the sales trajectory in 2027 quarters is also a key test.

Another item to track is whether new product launches and diversification change the fuel tube-centered revenue mix. It is also worth checking whether non-operating items continue to drive the gap between operating profit and net income.

Bull factors

Financial capacity and low leverage

Total equity at end-2025 was KRW 194.2 billion, all of it attributable to controlling shareholders. Total liabilities were KRW 28 billion, and the debt ratio fell from 21.6% in 2022 to 14.4% in 2025. A low-debt structure can serve as a buffer during raw material price swings or sector slowdowns.

Profit rebound in 2025

Operating profit rose 10.4% to KRW 2.39 billion in 2025 from KRW 2.17 billion in 2024. The operating margin edged up from 1.4% to 1.7%. Net income attributable to controlling shareholders rose 43.5% to KRW 11.9 billion, pointing to a recovery in earnings.

Earnings-linked dividend policy

In its corporate value-up plan disclosed on March 10, 2026, the company said it will pay dividends based on stable earnings while considering the domestic and external operating environment. The dividend yield ranks among the highest in its sub-sector (Alphasquare, data as of October 2, 2026). With a stated dividend policy, the follow-through against earnings trends becomes a checkable item.

Bear factors

Three years of falling sales

Revenue declined for three consecutive years from 2023 through 2025, ending at KRW 140.4 billion in 2025 versus KRW 174.2 billion in 2022. Q3 2026 sales of KRW 31.7 billion fell 5.7% year on year. Quarterly sales dropped from KRW 44.2 billion in Q2 to KRW 31.7 billion in Q3, interrupting the top-line momentum.

Cost pressure and intensifying competition

On a cumulative basis through Q3 2025, the fuel tube segment faced higher costs from intensifying competition and rising raw material prices. The operating margin stayed at 1.4% in 2024 and 1.7% in 2025, below 4.3% in 2022, which underpins the bearish case.

Swing to loss in Q3 2026

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders swung to a loss of KRW 6.21 billion in Q3 2026. Operating profit in the same quarter was only KRW 287 million, leaving a wide gap between operating and net results. With the quality and durability of earnings now less certain, the path after Q4 will be decisive.

Risk factors

Raw materials and competition

The fuel tube segment faced higher costs from rising raw material prices and intensifying competition, on a cumulative basis through Q3 2025. If raw material increases cannot be passed through to selling prices, margin pressure could persist. Limited pricing power would also slow any recovery in the operating margin.

Earnings volatility and non-operating items

Controlling-shareholder net income of KRW 8.62 billion in Q1 2026 far exceeded operating profit of KRW 2.49 billion, while Q3 2026 swung to a KRW 6.21 billion net loss on operating profit of KRW 287 million. Swings in non-operating items are driving net results sharply. Net income attributable to controlling shareholders totals KRW 9.13 billion over the last four quarters.

Small-cap liquidity and dividend durability

As a small-cap stock, Taeyang's share price can move sharply with changes in trading volume. The company's stated policy ties dividends to earnings, so a run of loss-making quarters could reduce dividend capacity. The durability of the dividend policy depends on how quickly earnings recover.
