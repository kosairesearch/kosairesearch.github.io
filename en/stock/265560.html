02

Business structure

Younghwa Tech is an automotive electronic components maker whose core products are engine-room junction boxes (UEC) and power electronics modules. Junction boxes distribute power to a vehicle's electrical systems and protect its circuits, so design and manufacturing reliability is central.

The company builds on its design and mass-production know-how in junction boxes and power electronics. Its customers are Hyundai Motor Group and General Motors (GM), and the company has said it will expand power electronics supply from electric vehicles to hydrogen vehicles.

A PCDU/HFDC supply contract with Hyundai Motor signed in February 2026 carries a contract amount of KRW 198.6 billion and runs from February 2, 2026 to December 31, 2033.

A converter supply contract with Hyundai Motor signed on November 6, 2026 carries a contract amount of KRW 81.6 billion and runs through December 31, 2036, with the products made in-house. The company describes that contract as firm rather than conditional.

In late October 2026 Younghwa Tech won an engine-room junction box award from GM for pickup and SUV applications under the T1XX-2 program, covering 2030–2036 model years and an estimated cumulative volume of about 4.89 million vehicles. Estimated revenue including related components is about KRW 264.0 billion.

Production for this award is assigned to the Monterrey plant in Mexico. Compared with larger peers in the sector such as Hyundai Mobis and HL Mando, the company has a narrower product and customer base, so changes in a single program carry relatively more weight.

04

Earnings analysis

Revenue rose from KRW 47.9 billion in 2022 to KRW 64.9 billion in 2023 (up 35.4% year on year), KRW 94.8 billion in 2024 (up 46.1%), and KRW 108.6 billion in 2025 (up 14.5%).

Operating profit grew from KRW 1.91 billion in 2022 to KRW 5.01 billion in 2023, KRW 15.4 billion in 2024 (up 206.9%), and KRW 18 billion in 2025 (up 17.2%). The operating margin moved from 4.0% in 2022 to 7.7%, 16.2% and 16.6% in 2025, staying in the mid-teens since 2024.

Profit attributable to controlling shareholders rose from KRW 4.83 billion in 2022 to KRW 19 billion in 2025, and net income for 2025 was also reported at KRW 19 billion.

Quarterly revenue went from KRW 25.6 billion in Q3 2025 to KRW 28.7 billion in Q4 2025, then dipped to KRW 24.4 billion in Q1 2026 (down 14.9% quarter on quarter). It then rose to KRW 28.1 billion in Q2 2026 and KRW 34.6 billion in Q3 2026, up 35.3% year on year.

Operating profit bottomed at KRW 3.07 billion in Q1 2026, then reached KRW 3.99 billion in Q2 and KRW 7.53 billion in Q3.

Profit attributable to controlling shareholders peaked at KRW 8.84 billion in Q4 2025, fell to KRW 7.74 billion in Q1 2026 and KRW 6.4 billion in Q2, and turned into a net loss of KRW 841 million in Q3 2026.

Why operating profit rose while the attributable result moved to a loss in the same quarter depends on non-operating items and income taxes, which require the detailed breakdown.

Cumulative revenue for January to September 2026 was KRW 87.1 billion, up 9.0%, and cumulative operating profit was KRW 14.6 billion, up 21.4%. On cash flow, operating cash flow expanded from KRW 5.2 billion in 2022 and KRW 5.09 billion in 2023 to KRW 17.5 billion in 2024 and KRW 31.7 billion in 2025.

The debt ratio fell from 44.0% in 2024 to 27.7% in 2025, with total liabilities of KRW 25.7 billion and total equity of KRW 92.7 billion at end-2025.

05

Industry analysis

The company presents the shift toward electrification, from electric vehicles to hydrogen vehicles, as the direction of its business.

Power conversion and control units for electric and hydrogen vehicles become more necessary as vehicle architectures change, and Younghwa Tech has secured a Hyundai Motor contract for commercial hydrogen vehicles in this area.

Junction boxes and power electronics modules are electrical parts tied to vehicle safety, so customer reliability validation and mass-production track records act as entry barriers.

Programs once adopted tend to supply volume for years, and the Hyundai Motor converter contract runs through 2036 while the GM junction box award covers model years through 2036.

In its September 2025 investor briefing, the company said it would widen its North American base using the Mexico plant and expand its mobility market share in the second half. Expansion of North American production can be affected by tariffs and exchange rates alongside the local production setup.

Compared with sector peers such as Hyundai Mobis, Hanon Systems, HL Mando and Hyundai WIA, the company concentrates on a narrower range of junction box and power electronics products. Contract duration and customer mix are the reference for visibility.

06

Outlook

The company projects that annual revenue at the Mexico plant will reach about KRW 75.0 billion in 2029 and exceed KRW 100.0 billion from 2030, as existing and newly won volumes are reflected in sequence. That projection covers the plant as a whole, not only the GM award.

The company points to 2030, when GM program supply starts, as the point at which growth at the local plant becomes full-scale. The Hyundai Motor converter contract began on November 6, 2026 and runs through 2036, forming a base for long-term revenue.

However, the contract amount is a total over the whole period, and how much is recognized each year will be confirmed in each periodic report. R&D spending for large-capacity LDC development is planned at KRW 400 million in 2026, KRW 1.0 billion in 2027, and about KRW 600 million a year from 2028.

Funding came from an April 2026 third-party allotment rights issue of 177,320 new shares. That financing relates to a 2026 technology development project linked to parts and materials investment.

In February 2026 the company resolved to dispose of 229,000 treasury shares to secure funds for purchasing a factory for hydrogen vehicle parts, equal to 2.14% of total issued shares. The 2025 fiscal year year-end dividend was also decided as a cash dividend per common share.

Much of this outlook rests on figures presented by the company, so North American revenue after 2030 depends on the start of GM program supply and on how customer volumes are executed.

Bull factors

Long-term contract visibility

The Hyundai Motor converter contract runs through December 31, 2036, and the PCDU/HFDC contract through December 31, 2033. The GM junction box volume spans 2030 to 2036 model years, so together these contracts form a supply base extending to 2036. The converter contract is recorded as a firm contract.

Improved earnings capacity

The operating margin rose from 4.0% in 2022 to 16.6% in 2025. Operating cash flow grew from KRW 5.2 billion in 2022 to KRW 31.7 billion in 2025. The debt ratio fell to 27.7% in 2025, widening financial room.

Expanding North American production base

The company projects Monterrey plant revenue of about KRW 75.0 billion in 2029 and above KRW 100.0 billion from 2030. The projection combines existing and newly won volumes at the plant level. GM program supply beginning in 2030 could broaden the North American revenue base.

09

Bear factors

Q3 2026 swing to net loss

Q3 2026 operating profit rose to KRW 7.53 billion, yet profit attributable to controlling shareholders was a loss of KRW 841 million. This follows a decline from KRW 7.74 billion in Q1 2026 to KRW 6.4 billion in Q2.

The cause, whether non-operating items or taxes, can only be pinned down from the detailed periodic report breakdown.

Customer concentration

Supply volume is concentrated in two customers, Hyundai Motor Group and General Motors. Volume adjustments or pricing changes in one program could affect earnings directly. Even large contract amounts are recognized in revenue across the full contract period.

North American revenue weighted to 2030 onward

GM program supply starts in 2030, so North American revenue will take time to be fully reflected. The KRW 75.0 billion for 2029 and above KRW 100.0 billion from 2030 are figures presented by the company. Actual execution depends on customer volumes and plant operations.

10

Risk factors

Customer concentration and contract terms

High exposure to Hyundai Motor Group and GM means order adjustments or unit price changes can flow directly into revenue. Contract amounts are totals over the full period and differ from annual revenue. Comparing them with annual revenue in periodic reports is necessary.

Overseas production, FX and trade

As the Monterrey plant becomes the North American hub, exposure to exchange rates, tariffs and local labor costs grows. The company's North American revenue outlook should be read as its own figures built on assumptions about those variables. These factors could widen quarterly earnings swings.

Financing and capital needs

The February 2026 treasury share disposal and the April 2026 third-party rights issue were followed by funding needs for factory purchase and large-capacity LDC development. Raising capital changes the number of shares, which affects per-share interpretation. R&D spending continuing after 2027 could weigh on earnings.

12

Overall view

Younghwa Tech saw both revenue and operating profit rise from 2022 through 2025, and its operating margin climbed from 4.0% to 16.6%. In 2026 it signed a series of long-term supply contracts with Hyundai Motor that extend to 2036, forming a contract base.

However, Q3 2026 operating profit rose 107.3% year on year while the company posted a net loss attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 841 million, leaving earnings quality as an open question.

After the Q1 2026 low, revenue and operating profit rose for two straight quarters, but profit attributable to controlling shareholders declined.

In North America, GM junction box supply begins in 2030 and the company projects Monterrey plant revenue above KRW 100.0 billion from 2030, but these are company-provided projections and not confirmed results.

Strengths include long-term contracts, a better operating margin, expanding operating cash flow and a lower debt ratio. Weaknesses include the Q3 2026 loss, customer concentration and North American revenue weighted to 2030 onward. The next checkpoints are the annual business report for fiscal 2026 and the Q1 2027 quarterly report.
