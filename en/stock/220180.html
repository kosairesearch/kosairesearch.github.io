02

Business structure

Polaris AI Handy (formerly Handysoft) is a software company founded in November 2009 that changed its name in July 2026 and declared a transition toward an AI-centered AX (AI transformation) business.

Its core product is the enterprise groupware HANDY Groupware, and the lineup extends to HANDY Mobile GW, the cloud-based collaboration suite HANDY CGW, the project record system HANDY BMS, the process modeling tool HANDY PAL, and HANDY BPM.

HANDY RMS, a records management solution, is positioned as a customized product for public agencies. The business structure centers on groupware deployment and follow-on feature supply.

A May 2026 report said the company won an AI groupware project from a veterans welfare agency and is pursuing the public AX market jointly with Polaris Office.

A September 2026 report said the company is using a web drafting system it built for KAMCO (Korea Asset Management Corporation) as a reference to expand into public, financial and large corporate markets.

A November 2026 report said the company is jointly pursuing the corporate security market with Polaris Office and plans to use its existing customer base as a new sales channel for security business. According to a market data site, the largest shareholder group holds 48.75%.

Peers in the same industry that are often compared include Daou Technology and Itcenglobal, both IT service providers serving public and corporate clients.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual revenue fell from KRW 33.1 billion in 2022 to KRW 26.1 billion in 2023, a 21.1% decline, and then rose 8.6% to KRW 28.4 billion in 2024 and 10.8% to KRW 31.4 billion in 2025, with growth in both 2024 and 2025 after the 2023 low. 2022 was the largest year in the 2022 to 2025 period.

Operating profit of KRW 465 million in 2022 turned into an operating loss of KRW 967 million in 2023, a swing into the red, followed by a KRW 1.99 billion operating loss in 2024 as losses widened and a KRW 481 million loss in 2025 as losses narrowed.

The company has posted operating losses for three consecutive years from 2023 through 2025. The operating margin moved from 1.4% in 2022 to -3.7% in 2023, -7.0% in 2024 and -1.5% in 2025, so the margin has improved since the 2024 low.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 129 million in 2022, KRW 1.71 billion in 2023 (up 1,221.0%), KRW 598 million in 2024 (down 65.0%) and KRW 817 million in 2025 (up 36.6%), with 2023 the largest year.

That profit peak came in a year with an operating loss, which points to non-operating items driving net income; the breakdown belongs in the notes to the financial statements of the annual report.

Net income including non-controlling interests was KRW 642 million in 2025, below the KRW 817 million attributable to controlling shareholders, and the gap reflects the non-controlling share.

Quarterly results show an operating profit of KRW 39.4 million in the third quarter of 2025, followed by KRW 653 million in the fourth quarter (up 1,556.3% from the prior quarter).

Revenue fell 41.5% to KRW 59.2 million... to KRW 5.92 billion in the first quarter of 2026, with an operating loss of KRW 542 million that marked a return to losses, while net income attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 497 million in the same quarter.

Revenue rose to KRW 6.59 billion in the second quarter of 2026 (up 11.3%) and to KRW 14.7 billion in the third quarter (up 123.0%), two straight quarters of growth, yet the operating loss was KRW 779 million in the second quarter and narrowed to KRW 430 million in the third.

Controlling shareholders posted net losses of KRW 449 million in the second quarter and KRW 212 million in the third. Operating cash flow moved from an inflow of KRW 4.52 billion in 2024 to an outflow of KRW 4.15 billion in 2025, so accounting profit and cash generation diverged.

Whether the third-quarter revenue jump converts into a higher operating margin is the key question for the next periodic report.

05

Industry analysis

Groupware and collaboration software form the base layer of enterprise and public-sector work environments, and competition is shifting toward combining cloud migration with generative AI features.

Alongside its 2026 name change, the company declared a shift toward an AI-centered AX business, which reads as adding AI functions to existing groupware products. In the public market, the veterans agency award and the KAMCO web drafting reference are presented as grounds for sales.

Listed peers include Daou Technology, Itcenglobal, Samsung SDS and LG CNS. Large system integrators lead in capital and project scale, while Polaris AI Handy operates a groupware-product-centered structure with roughly 100 employees.

Demand depends on corporate IT budgets and public digital transformation budgets, which follow government and corporate budget cycles more than the economic cycle. Expanding joint sales with Polaris Office within the group can be read as a group-level AX strategy.

The profitability question is how quickly AI features are adopted and how the resulting product mix shows up in the operating margin.

Even with revenue at KRW 14.7 billion in the third quarter of 2026, operating losses could recur if the cost structure does not change. Governance and group dependence

Overall view

Polaris AI Handy posted third-quarter 2026 revenue of KRW 14.7 billion, a rebound on a quarterly basis, with two straight quarters of growth since the second quarter of 2026.

Operating losses, however, have continued for three straight quarters from the first quarter of 2026, and the third-quarter loss of KRW 430 million marked a swing from an operating profit a year earlier. The 2025 operating margin of -1.5% improved from -7.0% in 2024, but a return to profitability has not yet appeared.

Operating cash flow moved from an inflow in 2024 to a KRW 4.15 billion outflow in 2025, and cumulative controlling-shareholder net income over the last four reported quarters was KRW 322 million.

On the financial side, a debt-to-equity ratio of 15.2% and total equity of KRW 78.3 billion at the end of 2025 provide a base, and market capitalization sits below total equity.

Bullish factors are the revenue recovery and the expansion in public and security business, while bearish factors are continued losses and the reversal in cash flow. The next checkpoints are the fourth-quarter revenue, the full-year operating margin and operating cash flow in the 2026 annual report.
