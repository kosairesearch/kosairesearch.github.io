02

Business structure

Milae Bioresources was founded in 1998 and started with livestock feed additive manufacturing before expanding into pet health supplements and food ingredients. The business is split into a feed segment and a food segment.

Saramin company data puts the business mix at 75.4% feed and 23.9% food, while a April 2022 report from Asia Economy said the feed segment accounted for more than 80% of revenue at the end of 2021. The two data points come from different dates, so it is difficult to determine how the mix has shifted in between.

The core of the feed segment is specialty processed raw materials (EP) made from corn, soybean and oat, along with premix feed additives.

The premix additive ML-F1, launched in 2014, is a GABA-containing stress reliever, and its export version ML-F2 has been shipped to markets including the Philippines, Bangladesh and Taiwan according to the company history.

The pet segment consists of the company's own brand Optipet along with ODM supply of health supplements for third-party brands such as Coupang's pet brand and Cargill Agri Purina's daycare product. The food segment handles edible eggs and functional ingredients.

In October 2024 the company brought the beverage maker Geonganmaeul in as a subsidiary, adding a beverage business. In July 2025 its Icheon No. 2 plant was registered as a US FDA-compliant production facility, and in September 2025 an eye-health raw material received individual functional recognition.

Customers include domestic feed manufacturers and pet product distribution channels. The company is classified as a small and mid-sized enterprise that competes on proprietary raw material technology.

04

Earnings analysis

On a disclosed annual basis, revenue fell from KRW 75.6 billion in 2022 to KRW 60 billion in 2023, a 20.7% decline, then rose to KRW 62.5 billion in 2024 (up 4.1%) and KRW 67.6 billion in 2025 (up 8.2%).

Revenue has increased for two consecutive years from 2024 to 2025, with 2022 the largest year and 2023 the smallest in the 2022–2025 period. Operating profit dropped from KRW 1.58 billion in 2022 to KRW 20.19 million in 2023, a 98.7% decline.

It recovered to KRW 184 million in 2024, up 810.2%, before the company posted an operating loss of KRW 1.44 billion in 2025, a swing into the red. The operating margin moved from 2.1% in 2022 to 0.0% in 2023, 0.3% in 2024 and -2.1% in 2025, so the margin did not improve even as revenue grew in 2024 and 2025.

Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 963 million in 2022 and KRW 441 million in 2023, a 54.2% decline, then turned to a loss of KRW 433 million in 2024 and widened to a loss of KRW 3.26 billion in 2025, which the filings describe as continued losses with an expanded deficit.

Net income including non-controlling interests was a loss of KRW 2.99 billion in 2025, which differs from the controlling-shareholder figure because of the non-controlling interest share.

On a quarterly basis, operating profit of KRW 1.24 billion in Q3 2025 turned into an operating loss of KRW 3.34 billion in Q4 2025, when the net loss attributable to controlling shareholders reached KRW 5 billion. FnGuide financial data show that selling expenses rose sharply in 2025 compared with the prior year.

Q1 2026 operating profit of KRW 439 million marked a swing to profit, followed by KRW 698 million in Q2 (up 59.1% from the prior quarter) and KRW 796 million in Q3 (up 14.1%), an increase for two consecutive quarters.

Q3 2026 revenue of KRW 20.8 billion rose 11.1% year on year, yet operating profit fell 35.7% over the same period, so higher sales did not translate into higher profit.

Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders swung to a loss of KRW 65.77 million in Q2 2026 and back to a profit of KRW 1.49 billion in Q3 2026, showing large quarter-to-quarter swings. The net loss attributable to controlling shareholders over the last four quarters totals KRW 3.08 billion. Operating cash flow turned from -KRW 966 million in 2024 to KRW 4.4 billion in 2025.

05

Industry analysis

The feed industry depends heavily on imported raw materials, so profitability is sensitive to exchange rates and input prices. The company's 2023 business report noted that most feed raw materials are imported and that prices rose sharply during a period of high exchange rates and inflation.

That environment raised demand for feed additives that lower feed costs and improve feed efficiency, and the company said it sourced and introduced such additives in Korea.

For the first half of 2026, FnGuide-linked company monitor commentary attributed the improvement to rising demand for eco-friendly and functional products, including higher-value additives such as probiotics and enzymes. Overseas exports and ODM/OEM production for Optipet were also cited.

The food segment's pricing moves with farm-gate egg prices; a 2022 press report said higher farm-gate egg prices lifted food segment sales. Pet food is named by the company as a growth driver, and it operates a dedicated pet food plant that received HACCP certification.

On competition, the company differentiates through proprietary specialty raw materials, a GABA-based additive and pet supplement ODM capabilities. Data comparing its market share or scale with key rivals in feed additives were not available for this analysis.

Reading the company's disclosed results alongside raw material and exchange-rate trends is the most direct way to place it in its industry context.

Outlook

The company has disclosed several business milestones. In July 2025 its Icheon No. 2 plant was registered as a US FDA-compliant production facility, and in September 2025 an eye-health raw material received individual functional recognition.

In December 2025 an official distributor agreement for feed enzyme solutions was signed with IFF Danisco. In March 2026 the representative director changed to Lee Jae-hwan. FnGuide-linked company monitor commentary cited Optipet's overseas exports and higher ODM/OEM production as drivers of first-half 2026 results.

In food, the company's recruitment information page describes planned development of products such as hangover relief, sarcopenia-related and low-fat protein supplements, but no launch schedule or revenue contribution has been disclosed.

The company has leaned into specialty processed raw materials and functional additives in its feed segment, and that focus coincided with higher Q3 2026 revenue. Operating profit in Q3 2026 nevertheless declined from a year earlier, so whether sales growth turns into margin recovery is the central question.

Whether the swing from a Q2 2026 net loss to a Q3 2026 net profit reflects a lasting change cannot be judged from quarterly data alone. The next checkpoint is how the 2026 annual business report presents the full-year operating margin and the net profit attributable to controlling shareholders.

Risk factors

Raw material prices and FX

The company's 2023 business report says most feed raw materials are imported. Raw material prices rose sharply during a period of high exchange rates and inflation, so the cost structure is exposed to FX and global input prices. Higher costs could push the operating margin lower again.

Concentration of the business

Most revenue comes from the feed segment, which makes the company sensitive to changes in the feed market. The food segment's pricing follows farm-gate egg prices, adding price volatility.

The revenue contribution of new businesses such as pet health supplements and food ingredients is difficult to separate in the disclosed figures.

Financial headroom and cash flow

Total liabilities rose from KRW 16.2 billion at the end of 2025 to KRW 17.7 billion at the end of March 2026 according to FnGuide. Operating cash flow was negative in Q1 2026. The equity base remains sizable, but financial headroom could narrow if the earnings recovery does not hold.
