HBL: Profit Recovery Hinges on Order Confirmation

02

Business structure

HBL was founded in Daejeon in 1997 and provides laser application systems and solutions to secondary battery, EV and semiconductor customers. The company is known for having localized high-output industrial laser technology for the first time in Korea.

Its main products are laser welding, cutting and surface-treatment equipment, supplied to leading companies in secondary batteries, automotive and semiconductors.

The business is split into three units: secondary battery equipment, automotive (including EV) equipment, and other equipment for semiconductors and electronics. The flagship battery product is the i-Scan marker, a cell traceability laser marking system used to track manufacturing history of EV batteries.

The battery line-up also includes the i-Scan welder for vision-fused high-speed welding and a laser cleaning system for removing contaminants from tabs. In automotive, HBL offers a chassis-number engraving system, 3D high-speed remote welding and bus-bar welders.

Its laser VIN marker is cited with a 92% share of the domestic automaker production-line market. Battery customers include LG Energy Solution, Samsung SDI and SK On, while automotive customers include Hyundai Motor and KIA.

Other products include laser systems for MLCC processes, PCB film cutting, and glass-substrate scribing and cutting. Sales go to China, Vietnam and Southeast Asia, European OEMs and the US. Overseas, HBL has a 100%-owned subsidiary, HBES, and a Chinese unit, CHBL Co., Ltd.

With customers concentrated in batteries and automotive, changes in those industries' capital spending feed directly into revenue.

04

Earnings analysis

On a DART-filed annual basis, revenue rose from KRW 20.6 billion in 2022 to KRW 22.7 billion in 2023 (up 10.2% year on year), fell to KRW 18.5 billion in 2024 (down 18.2%), and reached KRW 24.2 billion in 2025 (up 30.8%). 2025 was the largest year of 2022 to 2025 and 2024 the smallest.

Operating profit fell from KRW 2.38 billion in 2022 to KRW 645 million in 2023 (down 72.9%), then turned into an operating loss of KRW 2.56 billion in 2024. It returned to operating profit of KRW 1.96 billion in 2025.

The operating margin went from 11.6% in 2022 to 2.8% in 2023 and -13.8% in 2024, then recovered to 8.1% in 2025.

Profit attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 2.09 billion in 2022, turned into a net loss of KRW 5.56 billion in 2023, remained a loss in 2024 at KRW 2.11 billion with a smaller deficit, and turned to a profit of KRW 2.44 billion in 2025.

In 2023, the gap between operating profit of KRW 645 million and the net loss to controlling shareholders was large, which points to items below the operating line weighing heavily that year. For 2025, reports attributed the turnaround to wider sales in semiconductor and electronics and lower fixed costs.

Operating cash flow, however, fell to KRW 303 million in 2025 from KRW 3.89 billion in 2024, and the debt-to-equity ratio rose from 22.7% to 36.2%. At the end of 2025, total equity was KRW 30.6 billion, all of it attributable to controlling shareholders, and total liabilities were KRW 11.1 billion.

Quarterly, the company moved from an operating loss of KRW 337 million and a net loss to controlling shareholders of KRW 164 million in 2025 Q3 to revenue of KRW 10.2 billion in 2025 Q4 (up 97.1% from the prior quarter), operating profit of KRW 2.6 billion and profit to controlling shareholders of KRW 2.9 billion.

Equipment revenue swings sharply with customer inspection and delivery timing. Revenue then fell to KRW 6.33 billion in 2026 Q1, with operating profit of KRW 171 million and profit to controlling shareholders of KRW 307 million.

In 2026 Q2 revenue was KRW 4.45 billion, but operating profit rose to KRW 509 million and profit to controlling shareholders to KRW 472 million.

In 2026 Q3 revenue was KRW 4.71 billion, up 5.9% from the prior quarter and down 8.9% year on year, while operating profit of KRW 647 million and profit to controlling shareholders of KRW 550 million both turned positive from a year earlier.

Operating profit and profit to controlling shareholders have risen for two consecutive quarters from 2026 Q2 through Q3. Profit to controlling shareholders for the last four quarters totals KRW 4.23 billion.

Revenue has recovered slowly while profit has improved first, so whether that gain came from cost ratios or product mix is the key question for the next quarters.

05

Industry analysis

Sentiment in secondary-battery equipment stocks has improved on expectations that EV and energy storage markets will recover. A press report on HBL's share price cited four drivers working together: the battery recovery outlook, the glass-substrate theme, rising demand for laser equipment and short-term trading flows.

Actual order growth, however, depends on the pace of overseas battery plant investment and on customers qualifying new equipment. On the semiconductor side, shrinking and denser chip processes are seen raising demand for precision processing equipment.

Reports also said Samsung Electronics and SK hynix were working with suppliers to move HBM wafer debonding to laser methods. Samsung Electro-Mechanics has built a glass-substrate pilot line at its Sejong site and plans mass production from 2027.

HBL is described as holding a patent on glass-substrate scribing and cutting and is grouped with the same theme. Philoptics and Taesung appear in the same glass-substrate theme group. HBL has also reportedly entered the charge-discharge equipment market, which had been dominated by China.

Its position in the cycle differs by segment: the VIN marker in automotive has a high share, while battery and semiconductor equipment are sensitive to customer capex. Whether the recovery narrative converts into orders is the main yardstick for where the company sits in the cycle.

06

Outlook

The most concrete capacity change is the expansion of the third plant. A ceremony marking completion of the third plant's expansion (the CR building) was held in April 2026, and a press report said it adds more than 310% of prior production capacity.

The new facility was described as a cleanroom-type facility for large equipment. On new businesses, HBL was reported as selected to lead a KRW 11.7 billion space national project.

In secondary batteries, HBL said it is developing a 3kW-class entry-level laser system together with KNS, combining KNS automation technology. Production and testing for that system are planned at KNS's factory in Vietnam.

On quality, HBL obtained international environmental and occupational safety certification, which the company expects to strengthen its competitiveness in overseas high-tech orders.

New-plant utilization and new order volume have not yet appeared in the filed results, so whether the capacity turns into revenue will become visible in later periodic reports. For near-term outlook, the direction of quarterly revenue from 2026 Q4 onward is the main benchmark.

Bull factors

Quarterly profit has turned up

Operating profit and profit attributable to controlling shareholders have risen for two straight quarters from 2026 Q2, and both turned positive year on year in Q3.

Together with the 2025 annual turnaround to operating profit of KRW 1.96 billion and profit of KRW 2.44 billion, earnings power has moved away from the 2024 loss zone. Profit attributable to controlling shareholders for the last four quarters totals KRW 4.23 billion.

Capacity expansion and new businesses

A report said the third-plant expansion added more than 310% of prior production capacity. Entry into charge-discharge equipment runs in parallel with development of a 3kW-class entry-level laser system. Selection as lead organization of a space national project is presented as evidence of business diversification.

Domestic share and customer access

HBL's VIN marker for domestic automaker lines is cited at a 92% share, giving the automotive segment a broad demand base. With the three large battery makers and Hyundai Motor and KIA as customers, the company has wide access as long as their investment continues. Its glass-substrate scribing patent is a technology asset that can link to semiconductor packaging processes.

Bear factors

Slow revenue recovery

Q3 2026 revenue was KRW 4.71 billion, down 8.9% year on year. After the KRW 10.2 billion peak in 2025 Q4, revenue fell to KRW 6.33 billion in 2026 Q1 and KRW 4.45 billion in Q2, and the Q3 rebound was small.

Equipment revenue swings with customer inspection and delivery timing, so one quarter is not enough to define the trend.

Cash flow and balance sheet shifts

Operating cash flow fell to KRW 303 million in 2025 from KRW 3.89 billion in 2024. The debt-to-equity ratio rose from 22.7% to 36.2%. The company posted a KRW 5.56 billion net loss to controlling shareholders in 2023, and the 2025 turnaround is a single year, so the record of sustained profit is still short.

Theme-driven volatility and customer concentration

On September 29, 2026 the stock rose 16.88% in one session without a single disclosure to explain the move. An analysis holds that theme-driven gains will depend on whether order growth and earnings improvement follow through in the medium term.

With customers concentrated in three battery makers and two automakers, a single customer's investment adjustment reaches earnings directly.

Risk factors

Customer investment cycle

The company itself has said that lower demand in end markets, reduced customer investment and orders, and shifts in customer strategies could reduce demand for its products. Equipment orders also tend to bunch in the second half, which widens quarterly swings. A pullback in battery or automotive capex could push quarterly revenue down again.

Earnings volatility and cash generation

Revenue recognition depends on inspection and delivery timing, so quarterly profit can swing sharply. Operating cash flow of KRW 303 million in 2025 shows that profit did not convert to cash quickly. The pace of the rise in the debt-to-equity ratio also needs watching.

Execution risk in new plant and new businesses

New-plant utilization and new order volume have not yet appeared in the filed results. Glass-substrate volume production follows Samsung Electro-Mechanics' plan for 2027 and later, so revenue could come later than hoped. Customer qualification of new equipment can take time, which is an added risk for new products.

Overall view

HBL returned to profit in 2025, and operating profit and profit attributable to controlling shareholders rose for two straight quarters in 2026 Q2 and Q3, so earnings power is recovering.

Q3 2026 operating profit of KRW 647 million and profit attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 550 million both turned positive year on year.

Revenue, however, was still down 8.9% year on year in Q3 2026, so it is necessary to separate whether the profit gains came from revenue recovery or from cost and product mix.

The fall in 2025 operating cash flow to KRW 303 million and the rise in the debt-to-equity ratio are weaker points on the quality of the recovery.

On the bull side, the company has the third-plant expansion, new products such as charge-discharge equipment and a 3kW entry-level laser, a space national project, and a 92% share in domestic VIN markers.

On the bear side, revenue depends on customer investment cycles, the share price is prone to theme-driven swings, and the timing of new plant capacity turning into sales is uncertain.

The next checkpoints are the full-year operating margin and cash flow in the 2026 annual report, whether revenue recovers in 2027 Q1, and disclosures on new-plant utilization and new orders.
