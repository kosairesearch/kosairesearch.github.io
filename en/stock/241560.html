02

Business structure

Doosan Bobcat runs three product groups: Compact Equipment, Industrial Vehicles and Portable Power. Dollar-based sales in the third quarter of 2026 rose 15% for compact equipment, 8% for industrial vehicles and 5% for portable power, with compact equipment leading growth.

Reports attributed the compact equipment gain to shipments of volume deferred from the second quarter together with higher demand from rental companies. Industrial vehicles grew on higher sales in North America and Europe, while Portable Power benefited from stronger construction activity in North America.

North America is the core market, and Hana Securities put its share of revenue at 73.0% in a June 2026 report. The remaining regions are ALAO (Asia, Latin America and Oceania) and EMEA (Europe, the Middle East and Africa), where dollar-based sales rose 14% and 3% respectively in the third quarter of 2026.

Customers center on North American rental companies and dealers, so dealer inventory adjustments feed directly into quarterly shipments. Competition in North American compact equipment includes global construction equipment makers such as Caterpillar and Kubota.

Daol Investment & Securities noted in a February 2026 analysis that rivals also began raising prices from January 2026. The company raised prices on compact construction equipment in April 2026. Net cash at the end of the third quarter of 2026 was USD 453 million, about USD 100 million higher than the prior quarter.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual results in the periodic filings show a clear downward trend from 2023 through 2025.

Revenue fell from KRW 11,524.1 trillion in 2022 to KRW 9.99 trillion in 2023 (down 99.9% year on year), KRW 8.38 trillion in 2024 (down 16.1%) and KRW 8.26 trillion in 2025 (down 1.4%), declining for three consecutive years from 2023.

Operating profit went from KRW 1,432.3 trillion in 2022 to KRW 1.42 trillion in 2023 (down 99.9%), KRW 853.9 billion in 2024 (down 40.0%) and KRW 644.8 billion in 2025 (down 24.5%), also declining for three consecutive years.

The operating margin rose from 12.4% in 2022 to 14.2% in 2023, then fell to 10.2% in 2024 and 7.8% in 2025. The 2023 margin gain came while revenue was shrinking, so it is hard to read it as a recovery in scale.

Net profit attributable to owners of the parent was KRW 943.5 billion in 2023, KRW 552.1 billion in 2024 (down 41.5%) and KRW 378.1 billion in 2025 (down 31.5%), and net profit including non-controlling interests was reported at the same amounts for those years.

The debt-to-equity ratio declined from 84.1% in 2022 to 70.8% in 2025, with total equity of KRW 6.7 trillion and total liabilities of KRW 4.74 trillion at the end of 2025.

Operating cash flow was KRW 1.33 trillion in 2023, KRW 712.6 billion in 2024 and KRW 1.1 trillion in 2025, so cash generation rose in 2025 even as profit fell.

Quarterly revenue went from KRW 2.04 trillion in the third quarter of 2025 to KRW 2.19 trillion in the fourth quarter and KRW 2.05 trillion in the first quarter of 2026 (down 6.5% quarter on quarter).

Operating profit rose from KRW 130.5 billion in the third quarter of 2025 to KRW 136 billion in the fourth quarter and KRW 188.8 billion in the first quarter of 2026 (up 38.9%).

In the second quarter of 2026, revenue was KRW 2.18 trillion and operating profit KRW 260.4 billion (up 37.9% quarter on quarter), and press reports said one-time income from tariff refunds was booked in that period.

Third-quarter 2026 revenue was KRW 2.31 trillion (up 6.0% quarter on quarter and 13.0% year on year), and operating profit was KRW 159.2 billion (down 38.9% quarter on quarter and up 22.0% year on year).

Reports attributed the quarterly decline in operating profit to the base effect of the prior quarter's one-time gain.

Net profit attributable to owners of the parent was KRW 90.3 billion in the third quarter of 2026, up 13.1% year on year, and totaled KRW 466.6 billion over the last four quarters, from the fourth quarter of 2025 through the third quarter of 2026.

Industry analysis

North American demand for compact construction equipment and industrial vehicles moves with interest rates and housing and construction activity. In a June 2026 report, Hana Securities said dealer inventories in North America were lower than in the past because of tariff and high-interest-rate uncertainty.

In a July 2026 report, Samsung Securities viewed the decline in dealer inventory to about three months as a basis for an industry recovery. On pricing, Daol Investment & Securities said in a February 2026 analysis that rivals also began raising prices from January 2026, drawing attention to possible share gains.

On tariffs, Kiwoom Securities in a July 2026 report pointed to higher tariff burdens on Mexico-based production after changes to Section 232 tariffs. In a February 2026 report, Kiwoom Securities also expected the full launch of the Mexico plant to reduce part of the tariff burden.

The cycle reads as a recovery phase in which dealer inventory normalization and price increases coincide, but tariff and interest-rate uncertainty remain.

Doosan Bobcat competes against global construction equipment makers in North American compact equipment, and pricing and production footprint are the variables that shape its share and margins.

Outlook

The annual guidance the company presented in February 2026 called for revenue of USD 6.45 billion (up 4.3% year on year), operating profit of USD 482 million (flat, with a 7.5% operating margin).

Cumulative operating profit through the third quarter of 2026 reached USD 455 million, about 94% of the USD 482 million guidance level. At its October 29, 2026 results release, the company said demand for its main products should remain stable in the fourth quarter.

It expected compact equipment sales similar to the third quarter as deferred volumes continue to ship, and it saw solid demand holding for industrial vehicles.

In a July 2026 report, Kiwoom Securities expected solid growth in North America and Europe to continue in the second half, producing results consistent with guidance.

In a July 2026 report, Samsung Securities pointed to the inclusion of the tariff refund of USD 81 million as the main reason second-quarter operating profit far exceeded market expectations.

The next checkpoints are whether fourth-quarter compact equipment shipments hold after deferred volumes are cleared, the effect of price increases on demand, and adjustments to Mexico-based production volumes.

Under its corporate value enhancement plan, the company continues a policy of paying quarterly dividends with a minimum annual dividend level. The next periodic report is the 2026 annual report, which will show whether full-year sales and operating profit matched guidance.

Fourth-quarter 2026 results have not yet been filed in a periodic report and should be read separately from filed figures once they are.

Price increases and a changing production footprint

In a February 2026 report, Kiwoom Securities expected the full launch of the Mexico plant to reduce part of the tariff burden. Daol Investment & Securities said rivals also began raising prices from January 2026.

Financial capacity and cash generation

Net cash at the end of the third quarter of 2026 rose about USD 100 million from the prior quarter to USD 453 million. The debt-to-equity ratio fell to 70.8% at the end of 2025 from 84.1% in 2022. Operating cash flow was KRW 1.1 trillion in 2025, up from KRW 712.6 billion in 2024.

Bear factors

Three straight years of lower annual profit

Operating profit fell from KRW 1.42 trillion in 2023 to KRW 853.9 billion in 2024 and KRW 644.8 billion in 2025, declining for three consecutive years. The operating margin of 7.8% in 2025 is below the 14.2% of 2023.

Whether the 2026 quarterly recovery marks a change in the annual trend will be checked in the 2026 annual report.

Tariff and currency variables

In a July 2026 report, Kiwoom Securities pointed to higher tariff burdens on Mexico-based production after the Section 232 changes. Because sales are generated in dollars, won-denominated results are exposed to exchange rates. Hana Securities also cited tariff and high-interest-rate uncertainty as industry variables.

Base effect from one-time income

Press reports said one-time income from tariff refunds was booked in the second quarter of 2026. As a base effect, third-quarter operating profit of KRW 159.2 billion fell 38.9% from the prior quarter. The trend in core operating profit, excluding one-time items, needs further confirmation across quarters.

Risk factors

Currency

Sales are generated in dollars, but reported amounts are translated into won, so currency moves feed directly into won-based results. Net cash is also tallied in dollars, so its won-equivalent size shifts with the exchange rate. Currency losses were cited as a burden in the EMEA region.

Tariffs and trade policy

Changes to Section 232 tariffs and adjustments to Mexico-based production volumes could add cost. Tariff refunds the company received are one-time in nature, so whether they recur is uncertain. Whether price increases slow demand is also an item to monitor.

North American demand and interest rates

With North America at 73.0% of revenue, exposure to housing, construction activity and interest rates is high. Hana Securities cited high-interest-rate uncertainty in a June 2026 report. If dealer inventories rise again, shipments could be adjusted.

Overall view

Doosan Bobcat is a machinery and equipment company centered on North American compact equipment, and its third-quarter 2026 revenue of KRW 2.31 trillion and operating profit of KRW 159.2 billion rose year on year.

However, in the periodic filings, annual revenue, operating profit and net profit attributable to owners of the parent declined for three consecutive years from 2023 through 2025, and the operating margin of 7.8% in 2025 is below the 14.2% of 2023.

Quarterly operating profit recovered to KRW 188.9 billion and KRW 260.4 billion in the first two quarters of 2026 before adjusting to KRW 159.2 billion in the third quarter, and the second-quarter figure should be read with the one-time tariff refund in mind.

Bull factors include the recovery in North American compact equipment shipments, price increases, the Mexico production footprint, and financial capacity reflected in USD 453 million of net cash and a 70.8% debt-to-equity ratio.

Bear factors include the Section 232 tariff changes, currency effects on dollar-based sales, exposure to North American housing, construction and interest-rate conditions with 73.0% of revenue from the region, and the base effect of one-time income.

Cumulative operating profit of USD 455 million through the third quarter equals about 94% of the USD 482 million guidance level, a fact that can be compared against the annual target.

Kiwoom Securities presented a target price of KRW 96,000 in a July 2026 report, Samsung Securities KRW 98,000, and Shinhan Investment Corp. KRW 90,000. These are the brokers' own estimates and do not represent the opinion of this report.
