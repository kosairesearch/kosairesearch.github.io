02

Business structure

Kumyang Green Power is an Ulsan-based renewable energy EPC (engineering, procurement and construction) and electrical works company that earns revenue in three areas: plant and power station works, renewable energy construction and routine maintenance.

According to an Ulsan Daily UTV report, the share of renewable energy in sales rose from 9.0% in 2022 to 34.3% in 2025 since the company's listing.

The Bell reported that solar construction contract revenue rose from KRW 2.6 billion in 2024 to KRW 47.9 billion in 2025, with the Jeju Haeoreum solar project driving the increase. The plant segment centers on nuclear power facility works and electrical works for overseas plants.

In March 2026 the company disclosed a KRW 57.17 billion main equipment works contract with Samsung C&T for Saeul Nuclear Units 3 and 4, equal to about 24.8% of recent revenue. A correction filed in November 2026 raised the Shahin ethylene facility electrical contract with Hyundai E&C to KRW 34.12 billion.

The renewable pipeline, covering onshore and offshore wind, solar and fuel cells, is reported at about 1.1 GW. A company official said most orders come from large corporate clients.

In the nuclear segment, the company was registered as a Q-class qualified contractor for nuclear instrumentation and control maintenance in December 2025 and expected to take part in related bidding from the second quarter of 2026.

In competition, nuclear maintenance overlaps with KEPCO Plant Service & Engineering and other incumbents, while renewable EPC overlaps with large construction firms and electrical contractors.

04

Earnings analysis

On a DART periodic-report basis, annual revenue rose from KRW 230.6 billion in 2022 to KRW 238.9 billion in 2023 (up 3.6% year on year), KRW 243.2 billion in 2024 (up 1.8%) and KRW 264.8 billion in 2025 (up 8.9%).

Revenue has risen for three consecutive years from 2023 through 2025, and 2025 was the largest year of 2022 to 2025. Operating margin went from 4.6% in 2022 to 2.5% in 2023, -7.0% in 2024 and 0.2% in 2025, so the larger top line did not carry through to profit.

Operating profit of KRW 6.05 billion in 2023 fell 43.5% year on year, and 2024 turned to an operating loss of KRW 17.1 billion. A securities firm attributed the 2024 loss to delayed settlement on projects where construction costs could not be recovered.

The 2025 operating profit of KRW 427 million was a turnaround, but its small size led one analyst to see limited earnings capacity. In the quarterly series, Q4 2025 revenue of KRW 87.7 billion rose 18.6% from the prior quarter, with operating profit of KRW 4.38 billion.

The Bell noted that revenue recognized on construction progress tends to cluster at particular points in time.

Q1 2026 revenue was KRW 72.8 billion and an operating loss of KRW 8.02 billion turned the quarter negative, and a third-party analysis linked the continued gross-profit deficit to higher construction costs in the plant segment.

Q2 2026 revenue of KRW 84.2 billion and operating profit of KRW 1.8 billion returned to profit, while Q3 2026 revenue of KRW 72.2 billion and operating profit of KRW 930 million fell 48.3% from the prior quarter and 78.4% year on year.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 11.73 million in Q3 2026, down 99.8% year on year. Operating cash flow moved from minus KRW 421 million in 2023 to KRW 8.15 billion in 2024 and KRW 15.1 billion in 2025, while the debt ratio rose from 71.9% in 2024 to 92.7% in 2025.

05

Industry analysis

Expansion of renewable energy is cited as a supportive policy backdrop for Kumyang Green Power. A securities industry official said that as Middle East risks recur, renewable expansion policy is more likely to accelerate from an energy security perspective.

This is an industry view, and whether permitting, bidding and grid connection schedules keep pace with policy must be checked separately. The 1.1 GW pipeline combines wind and solar with fuel cells.

Among listed peers in the sector, KEPCO Plant Service & Engineering and KEPCO Engineering & Construction Company overlap in nuclear maintenance and engineering, Doosan Fuel Cell competes in fuel cells, and HD Hyundai Energy Solutions is an indirect competitor in solar equipment.

The company's model of combining EPC execution with power generation differentiates it from a pure contractor. In nuclear, the company completed the absorption merger of GIPS, a nuclear instrumentation and control maintenance firm, in January 2025 and moved forward with Q-class certification.

Because EPC revenue is recognized on construction progress, changes in the industry tend to show up as swings in quarterly profit. Wind projects are long-dated and require permits and large capital, so the position in the cycle is difficult to pin down.

The sector currently combines potential gains from rising renewable equipment demand with construction cost and settlement risks.

06

Outlook

In an April 2026 press release on its perpetual convertible bond issue, the company said it would start construction in sequence from the second half of 2026, beginning with the Gyeongbuk 1 wind project and followed by the Jeju village wind, Gangdong Green, Kumyang Solar and Gyeongbuk 2 wind projects.

Of the KRW 22 billion raised, KRW 6 billion is for operating funds and KRW 16 billion for acquiring interests in other companies tied to renewable development projects. The conversion price is KRW 15,447 per share, and conversion requests can be made from April 29, 2027.

For the Shahin ethylene facility electrical works, a November 9, 2026 correction raised the contract value to KRW 34.12 billion and moved the completion date from November 30, 2026 to March 31, 2027. The Saeul Units 3 and 4 main equipment works contract worth KRW 57.17 billion, disclosed in March 2026, is in progress.

On November 13, 2026, the company met Korea East-West Power and an offshore wind partner to discuss technical and financing issues for a 375 MW floating offshore wind project off Ulsan.

Management has described a structure in which power generation revenue is layered on top of electrical works, renewable EPC and routine maintenance, becoming visible from 2026.

Securities reports issued in autumn 2025 projected operating profit for 2026, but Q1 2026 posted an operating loss, so the forecast is still being tested.

Bull factors

Large nuclear and power works contracts

In March 2026 the company disclosed a KRW 57.17 billion main equipment works contract with Samsung C&T for Saeul Units 3 and 4, equal to about 24.8% of recent revenue. A November 2026 correction raised the Shahin ethylene electrical contract to KRW 34.12 billion. Contract size of this scale is cited as supporting the visibility of construction revenue.

1.1 GW pipeline and construction schedule

Wind, solar and fuel cell projects combined are reported to form a pipeline of about 1.1 GW. The company said it would begin construction on several projects in sequence from the second half of 2026, starting with Gyeongbuk 1 wind.

On November 13, 2026 it discussed issues for a 375 MW floating offshore wind project with a power generator and an offshore wind developer.

Entry into nuclear maintenance

The merger of GIPS, a nuclear instrumentation and control maintenance firm, was completed in January 2025, and the company was registered as a Q-class qualified contractor in December 2025. A company official expected new revenue from related bidding starting in the second quarter of 2026. Routine maintenance work can recur, which is cited as a factor that could stabilize the revenue mix.

Bear factors

Volatility in quarterly profit

The operating loss in Q1 2026 was KRW 8.02 billion, followed by operating profit of KRW 1.8 billion in Q2 and KRW 930 million in Q3, so the direction changed every quarter. Q3 operating profit fell 48.3% from the prior quarter, and net income attributable to controlling shareholders was only KRW 11.73 million.

The net loss attributable to controlling shareholders over the latest four quarters totals KRW 6.31 billion, and the earnings trend has not settled into one direction.

Construction cost and settlement risk

In 2024, delayed settlement on projects left construction costs unrecovered, and the company swung to an operating loss of KRW 17.1 billion. A third-party analysis linked the continued gross-profit deficit in Q1 2026 to higher construction costs in the plant segment.

Trade receivables rose from KRW 30.6 billion to KRW 43.4 billion over the same period, so collection timing on large projects could affect cash flow.

Capital structure and conversion dilution

The debt ratio rose to 92.7% at the end of 2025 from 71.9% in 2024, and total liabilities were KRW 88.7 billion. The KRW 22 billion perpetual convertible bonds carry a conversion price of KRW 15,447 per share, with conversion possible from April 29, 2027.

The shares underlying the bonds equal 10.52% of total shares outstanding, which would add to the share count if converted.

Risk factors

Policy and permitting dependence

Permitting and construction timing for power projects can shift with the policy environment, including renewable expansion and the power supply plan. If the second-half 2026 construction schedule slips, the timing of power generation revenue would also shift. Wind projects are long-dated, so schedules can vary widely.

Share price volatility

On November 5, 2026, the share price rose more than 20% intraday, though reports said no new disclosure or confirmed catalyst appeared that day. A static volatility interruption was also triggered intraday in March 2026. Short-term price moves can diverge from earnings trends, which is a risk factor in itself.

Conversion dilution and construction costs

Because conversion becomes possible from April 29, 2027, the share count could rise. Revenue is recognized on construction progress, so higher costs on large projects can move quarterly profit sharply. The two factors arrive at different times but both affect shareholder value and earnings.

Overall view

Kumyang Green Power combines renewable EPC, power generation and nuclear maintenance, and the key fact on a DART periodic-report basis is that operating profit turned positive in 2025.

Over the same period revenue rose for three consecutive years, but operating margin stayed low, moving from -7.0% in 2024 to 0.2% in 2025. Q1 2026 operating loss, Q2 profit and a decline in Q3 operating profit show that quarterly profit remains volatile.

Strengths cited include large contracts such as the KRW 57.17 billion Saeul Units 3 and 4 works and the Shahin electrical works, along with a pipeline of about 1.1 GW and a construction schedule from the second half of 2026.

Weaknesses include loss-making quarters tied to construction costs and settlement delays, a net loss attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 6.31 billion over the latest four quarters, and a higher debt ratio.

The perpetual convertible bonds are capital-type funding that eases financial burden, but the share count could rise once conversion requests begin after April 29, 2027.

The next checkpoints are the full-year operating margin in the 2026 annual report, the scheduled March 31, 2027 completion date for the Shahin works, and Q1 2027 results.
