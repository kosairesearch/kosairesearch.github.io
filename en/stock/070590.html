Report overview

2025 Turnaround Stalls, AI Pivot Still Unproven

Inticube returned to an operating profit for 2025, but it has posted losses in each of the four quarters from Q4 2025 through Q3 2026, so the earnings contribution of contact-center contract wins and the AI push are the next checkpoints.

02

Business structure

Inticube is a contact-center solutions company founded in 2003 that has extended its business from building traditional PBX-based call-center systems to grafting AI technology onto them.

Its core business is building and operating contact-center and messaging solutions, and it has experience building IPCC and AICC systems for more than 200 enterprise customers across finance, public sector, telecom and retail.

On the technology side it offers enterprise AI capabilities including natural language processing (NLP), speech recognition (STT), speech synthesis (TTS) and dialogue management, and it is known to hold the call-bot solution 'Inticube Isaac' and a freeform AI voice bot.

Revenue appears to depend on winning contact-center project awards, which explains the wide swings in quarterly revenue.

In March 2026 the largest shareholder changed from Hansol Holdings to Playbus, a special purpose company that acquired a 42.25% stake for KRW 15.0 billion in total; Playbus is reported to be a wholly owned subsidiary of Play Company.

A KRW 10.99 billion contract from Shinhan Card's telecom infrastructure upgrade, with LG Uplus as the counterparty, was disclosed on September 11, 2026 and equals about 17.0% of 2025 revenue.

The AI business runs on two tracks: in the enterprise market, existing IPCC and AICC customers are the target for AI agent deployments, while in the consumer market the company is promoting the Who Chu AI character platform.

Against large IT service peers in the same sector such as NAVER, Samsung SDS and LG CNS, Inticube is much smaller in scale and its earnings remain tied to a narrow contact-center niche.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual revenue fell from KRW 59.1 billion in 2022 to KRW 41.7 billion in 2023, a 29.5% decline, then rose 17.0% to KRW 48.8 billion in 2024 and 32.9% to KRW 64.8 billion in 2025, two straight years of growth after the 2023 low. 2025 was the largest year of 2022 to 2025.

Operating losses widened from KRW 3.97 billion in 2022 to KRW 6.03 billion in 2023, narrowed to KRW 2.98 billion in 2024, and turned into an operating profit of KRW 1.05 billion in 2025. The operating margin moved from -6.7% in 2022 and -14.5% in 2023 to -6.1% in 2024 and 1.6% in 2025.

One industry-level explanation cited for the 2025 turnaround is the spread of AI contact centers, which improved agent efficiency. Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders turned to a profit of KRW 794 million in 2025, while net profit including non-controlling interests was KRW 896 million.

At the end of 2025, total equity was KRW 13.5 billion, of which KRW 13 billion belonged to controlling shareholders, and total liabilities were KRW 19.4 billion. The debt-to-equity ratio rose from 71.1% in 2022 to 152.2% in 2024 and stood at 143.9% in 2025.

Operating cash flow was negative KRW 2.47 billion in 2022, KRW 4.13 billion in 2023, negative KRW 3.87 billion in 2024 and KRW 2.23 billion in 2025.

Quarterly, Q3 2025 revenue of KRW 14.6 billion and operating profit of KRW 346 million gave way to Q4 2025 revenue of KRW 13.8 billion and an operating loss of KRW 381 million.

Q1 2026 revenue was KRW 10.4 billion, down 24.3% from the prior quarter, with an operating loss of KRW 2.27 billion, the widest loss in the window.

Q2 2026 revenue was KRW 10.7 billion with an operating loss of KRW 1.02 billion, and Q3 2026 revenue recovered to KRW 12.5 billion, up 16.8% quarter on quarter, but still posted an operating loss of KRW 662 million.

Press coverage reported first-half 2026 revenue down about 42% year on year, with operating results moving from profit to loss. Audit reports have repeatedly flagged key audit matters on revenue recognition and asset impairment.

05

Industry analysis

The AI contact center (AICC) market is spreading among enterprises and small businesses, and demand for collaboration systems that raise agent efficiency is cited as a driver of Inticube's 2025 earnings recovery.

Its end markets include finance, the public sector, telecom and retail, all of which carry heavy customer-service volumes. The company has built IPCC and AICC systems for more than 200 enterprise customers in these sectors and has proposed converting existing clients into AI agent customers.

Contact-center build demand tends to arrive in project waves tied to corporate IT spending, which helps explain the swings in quarterly revenue, from KRW 14.6 billion in Q3 2025 to KRW 10.4 billion in Q1 2026.

Compared with large IT service peers in the same sector such as NAVER, Samsung SDS, LG CNS and Hyundai Autoever, the gap in market capitalization is wide. Its differentiation lies in combining contact-center implementation experience with speech and dialogue AI technology as a specialist provider.

In AI characters and content, securing IP is the key constraint: the company is reported to be in talks with domestic and foreign content companies, but the IP and counterparties to be applied have not been disclosed.

Combining it with the entertainment IP distribution network of the Play Company group has been cited as a route to fan-communication businesses. Ultimately, the number of voice-AI references and the pace of AI agent commercialization will shape its position in the competitive landscape.

06

Outlook

Results from the AI pivot are not yet visible in reported numbers. The Who Chu AI character platform was targeted for a September 2026 launch, but later coverage described its service launch and revenue model as still undefined.

Whether the content IP and counterparties to be applied are disclosed is the first thing to watch for the new business. The AI-native contact center is being commercialized with an AI data infrastructure partner by applying AI agents to existing contact-center customer systems.

The KRW 10.99 billion Shinhan Card project equals about 17.0% of 2025 revenue at disclosure, and how much of it is recognized as revenue in which quarters will show up in later quarterly results.

On funding, year-end 2025 cash and cash equivalents were reported at about KRW 1.9 billion, and analysts argue external financing must come first to cover R&D and working capital.

After Play Company-linked directors joined the board in March 2026, entertainment-related business purposes were added, which has prompted discussion of group-level business links.

The KOSDAQ market-cap threshold rises to KRW 30 billion from July 2027, so whether the company can keep its market cap above that line is a listing-status checkpoint. In the 2026 annual report, whether the operating margin improves from 1.6% in 2025 is the key item to check.

08

Bull factors

Turnaround and cash inflow

2025 revenue rose 32.9% to KRW 64.8 billion and operating profit turned to KRW 1.05 billion. Operating cash flow improved to an inflow of KRW 2.23 billion from an outflow in 2024. Press coverage points to a high ratio of cash generated against operating profit as a sign that earnings are backed by cash.

Large contract and existing customer base

The KRW 10.99 billion Shinhan Card telecom infrastructure contract equals about 17.0% of 2025 revenue. With IPCC and AICC build experience across more than 200 enterprise customers in finance, the public sector, telecom and retail, the company has proposed converting existing clients into AI agent customers.

AI new business and group links

The Who Chu AI character platform and commercialization of an AI-native contact center are in progress. Combining them with the entertainment IP distribution network of the Play Company group could extend the business into fan-communication services. Entertainment-related business purposes were added after the largest shareholder changed in March 2026.

09

Bear factors

Four straight quarters of losses

The company has posted operating losses in each of the four quarters from Q4 2025 through Q3 2026. Net loss attributable to controlling shareholders totaled KRW 4.25 billion over the last four quarters.

Q3 2026's operating loss of KRW 662 million compares with an operating profit of KRW 346 million a year earlier, and the Q1 2026 loss of KRW 2.27 billion was the widest in the window.

Declining revenue

Q3 2026 revenue of KRW 12.5 billion fell 14.0% from a year earlier. Q1 2026 revenue of KRW 10.4 billion was down 24.3% from the prior quarter. Press coverage reported first-half 2026 revenue down about 42% year on year.

Balance-sheet burden and governance changes

Total liabilities at the end of 2025 were KRW 19.4 billion. The largest shareholder and the CEO both changed in March 2026, and key audit matters have been repeatedly raised in audit reports.

10

Risk factors

Commercialization risk

The launch date for the AI character platform and the securing of content IP are not settled. Revenue models such as subscriptions, pay-per-use and licensing have also been reported as undefined. Until new businesses contribute revenue, earnings remain tied to contact-center contract flow.

Funding and balance-sheet risk

Press coverage put year-end 2025 cash and cash equivalents at around KRW 1.9 billion. With the debt-to-equity ratio above 140%, analysts argue the company may need external funding for R&D and working capital. The financing method and any dilution have not yet been disclosed.

Governance, accounting and trading risk

Changes in the largest shareholder and in executives have been concentrated in a short period, which has drawn criticism. Key audit matters that recur in audit reports are also on record.

On October 28, 2026, the stock was designated for investment caution because of concentrated buying by a small number of accounts, a period in which a sharp short-term rise and buying concentration appeared together.

12

Overall view

On an annual basis for 2025, Inticube reached KRW 64.8 billion in revenue and KRW 1.05 billion in operating profit, a turn back into profit.

However, the company posted losses in each of the four quarters from Q4 2025 through Q3 2026, and net loss attributable to controlling shareholders over the last four quarters totaled KRW 4.25 billion.

Its strengths are a customer base of more than 200 enterprises and the KRW 10.99 billion Shinhan Card contract disclosed in September 2026. Its weaknesses are quarterly revenue volatility, a debt-to-equity ratio above 140%, and year-end 2025 cash of roughly KRW 1.9 billion.

The AI new businesses have a stated direction, but launch timing, IP and revenue models are not yet public, so their contribution to results is hard to assess. Governance was reshaped by changes in the largest shareholder and CEO in March 2026, and recurring key audit matters are also part of the record.

The next checkpoints are the operating margin in the 2026 annual report, the Q1 2027 quarterly report, and the July 2027 change in the market-cap threshold. Positive and negative factors coexist, and this report does not offer an investment opinion.
