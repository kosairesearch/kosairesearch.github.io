HLB Global: Narrowing Losses Meet Financing and Merger Moves

02

Business structure

HLB Global is a consumer goods company that expands new product lines and sells mainly through its online direct-to-consumer storefront, using consumer data for performance marketing. Revenue is split into three segments: media commerce, food and beverages, and cosmetics.

Based on AlphaSquare's breakdown as of September 1, 2026, media commerce accounts for 56.20%, food and beverages for 32.10%, and cosmetics for 3.90%. The media commerce segment generates sales through content-linked marketing aimed at Gen MZ consumers and launches multiple brands.

The food and beverage segment is run by a fermented drink subsidiary, with a kombucha product as its flagship. Cosmetics is handled by subsidiaries including HLB Life Style and Purplish. In June 2025, the company disclosed a halt to its resource development business, narrowing its focus toward consumer goods.

In December 2024, it acquired a bio company, which broadened its portfolio at the time. Competition overlaps with food and cosmetics D2C companies that also rely on owned storefronts and content marketing, and profitability depends largely on advertising efficiency and repeat purchases. The largest shareholder is Jin Yang-gon.

04

Earnings analysis

On a reported basis, annual revenue grew from KRW 44.6 billion in 2022 to KRW 79.9 billion in 2023 (up 79.1% year on year), KRW 98.3 billion in 2024 (up 23.0%), and KRW 100.3 billion in 2025 (up 2.1%), marking three consecutive years of growth from 2023 through 2025.

Growth was strongest in 2023 and 2024 and slowed to 2.1% in 2025. Over the same period, the operating loss was KRW 14.7 billion in 2022, KRW 12.9 billion in 2023, KRW 3.67 billion in 2024, and KRW 9.88 billion in 2025, a loss in each of the four years from 2022 to 2025.

The loss narrowed sharply in 2024 before widening again in 2025. The operating margin improved from -33.1% in 2022 to -16.2% in 2023 and -3.7% in 2024, then fell back to -9.9% in 2025.

The attributable net loss was KRW 11.5 billion in 2022, KRW 22.8 billion in 2023, KRW 8.04 billion in 2024, and KRW 7.54 billion in 2025.

The net loss including non-controlling interests was KRW 15.2 billion, KRW 25.7 billion, KRW 9.01 billion, and KRW 8.91 billion in the same years, and the gap between the two figures is the share attributable to non-controlling interests.

On a quarterly basis, revenue was KRW 27.4 billion in the third quarter of 2025, KRW 21.8 billion in the fourth quarter (down 20.5% from the prior quarter), KRW 26.7 billion in the first quarter of 2026 (up 22.3%), KRW 31.2 billion in the second quarter (up 16.8%), and KRW 28.2 billion in the third quarter of 2026 (down 9.5% from the prior quarter, up 2.8% year on year).

The operating loss widened from KRW 83.74 million in the third quarter of 2025 to KRW 4.47 billion in the fourth quarter, narrowed to KRW 2.51 billion in the first quarter of 2026 and KRW 875 million in the second quarter, and widened again to KRW 2.37 billion in the third quarter, which was also a larger loss than a year earlier.

Attributable net income turned positive at KRW 1.96 billion in the fourth quarter of 2025, then returned to losses of KRW 1.44 billion in the first quarter of 2026, KRW 1.34 billion in the second quarter, and KRW 1.82 billion in the third quarter, three consecutive quarters of losses.

05

Industry analysis

HLB Global operates in a consumer goods sales model that combines owned online storefronts with content marketing to reach customers directly. In this model, customer data and advertising efficiency largely determine profitability, so losses can shift sharply with marketing spending even as sales grow.

The company's results show this pattern: third-quarter 2026 revenue fell 9.5% from the prior quarter, while the operating loss widened from KRW 875 million in the second quarter to KRW 2.37 billion. The food and beverage segment centers on fermented drinks such as kombucha, alongside cosmetics and media commerce.

Other listed companies in the consumer goods sector have different business structures, including rentals, home appliances, furniture, and large retail, so direct comparison is limited.

The company's strength lies in running product planning and sales channels in-house, while its weakness is reliance on advertising, which means revenue growth has not translated quickly into a smaller loss.

The June 2025 halt of the resource development segment can be read as a move toward a consumer-goods-focused structure. Its position within the sector should be read together with scale and profitability, and the most visible gap is that the operating margin remains negative.

08

Bull factors

Three Years of Revenue Growth

Revenue rose for three consecutive years, to KRW 79.9 billion in 2023, KRW 98.3 billion in 2024, and KRW 100.3 billion in 2025, more than double the KRW 44.6 billion recorded in 2022. First-half 2026 revenue also grew 13.3% year over year, so growth is continuing.

However, the growth rate slowed from 79.1% in 2023 to 2.1% in 2025, so the next thing to check is whether growth can be sustained rather than how fast it is.

Signs of Narrowing Losses

The operating loss for the first half of 2026 shrank 36.4% year over year, and the second-quarter 2026 operating loss of KRW 0.87 billion was narrower than the first quarter's KRW 2.51 billion.

The operating margin improved from -33.1% in 2022 to -3.7% in 2024, so there is reason to check whether the narrowing loss reflects a structural change. The 2025 operating margin fell back to -9.9%, so the sustainability of the narrowing is the key issue.

Balance Sheet and Room for Restructuring

At the end of 2025, total liabilities were KRW 41.9 billion and total equity was KRW 75.5 billion. The debt ratio rose from 43.0% in 2024 to 55.4% in 2025, but it is still lower than the 112.0% in 2023.

The discontinuation of the resource development segment in 2025 left the business structure centered on consumer goods, and the company merger decision in August 2026 could become an opportunity for business restructuring.
