02

Business structure

Osangjaiel is a KOSDAQ-listed company that runs an IT business centered on 3D design, simulation and product lifecycle management (PLM) implementation, alongside a bio business covering fruit packaging and new materials.

In the first half of 2026, PLM solutions accounted for 54.74% of consolidated revenue, system services for 33.40%, system integration (SI) for 6.50%, and packaging and new materials for 5.36%, according to a Korea IR Association report.

In that half, PLM solution revenue was KRW 31.7 billion, system services KRW 19.3 billion and packaging and new materials KRW 3.1 billion.

The company distributes Dassault Systèmes' CATIA 3D design software in Korea and provides after-sales solutions, and it runs authorized CATIA training centers in Incheon, Changwon and Daejeon.

Its customers reportedly include global battery makers, secondary battery material companies, space and defense firms, and automakers building robots. The company said investments by about 13 robot-related customers supported its 2025 results.

Its SI unit offers planning, construction and maintenance services and is expanding into AI infrastructure projects. In bio materials, the company is pursuing cosmetic and pharmaceutical raw material businesses based on its in-house JAIELITE material.

Compared with large system integrators in the same sector, the company functions more as a niche supplier focused on 3D design and PLM. Because most revenue comes from IT, demand changes in that segment feed directly into results.

04

Earnings analysis

On a disclosed annual basis, revenue rose from KRW 95.8 billion in 2022 to KRW 103.3 billion in 2023 (up 7.8% year on year), KRW 116.9 billion in 2024 (up 13.2%) and KRW 136.4 billion in 2025 (up 16.6%). Revenue has increased for three consecutive years from 2023 to 2025.

Operating profit went from KRW 3.67 billion in 2022 to KRW 6.34 billion in 2023 (up 72.8%), KRW 7.97 billion in 2024 (up 25.6%) and KRW 11 billion in 2025 (up 38.0%), also rising for three consecutive years.

The operating margin climbed from 3.8% in 2022 to 6.1%, 6.8% and 8.1% in 2025, so profitability improved faster than sales.

Attributable net profit was KRW 8.44 billion in 2022, KRW 23.9 billion in 2023 (up 182.9%), KRW 6.36 billion in 2024 (down 73.4%) and KRW 10.4 billion in 2025 (up 63.1%), a volatile path with 2023 the largest year. For 2024, the company attributed the decline in net profit to lower equity-method income.

Operating cash flow was KRW 6.13 billion in 2022, KRW 13.4 billion in 2023, KRW 15 billion in 2024 and KRW 11.1 billion in 2025, so it fell in 2025 even as operating profit rose.

On a quarterly basis, revenue reached KRW 30.8 billion in Q3 2025 and KRW 41.6 billion in Q4 2025 (up 35.3% quarter on quarter), then dropped to KRW 28.5 billion in Q1 2026 (down 31.6%).

Revenue then rose to KRW 29.4 billion in Q2 and KRW 30 billion in Q3 2026, two straight quarterly gains, though Q3 was still down 2.6% year on year. Operating profit was KRW 2.93 billion in Q3 2025, KRW 2.26 billion in Q4 2025, KRW 2.26 billion in Q1 2026, KRW 2.07 billion in Q2 and KRW 2.69 billion in Q3 2026.

Attributable net profit peaked at KRW 3.93 billion in Q2 2026 before falling to KRW 876 million in Q3. Operating profit and attributable net profit moved in different directions across quarters, suggesting non-operating items played a role.

05

Industry analysis

Demand from the downstream market is closely tied to capital spending and R&D budgets in robotics, defense and secondary batteries. The company cited expanded equipment and R&D investment in these industries as the main driver of its 2025 results.

A company official said its main customers include robot-related firms, battery makers and defense companies, and that requests for additional supply keep rising. This demand, however, moves with customers' investment timing.

Quarterly revenue fell from KRW 41.6 billion in Q4 2025 to KRW 28.5 billion in Q1 2026 before recovering to KRW 30 billion in Q3, which suggests this demand fluctuates sharply from quarter to quarter. Efforts by manufacturers to shorten product development cycles support demand for 3D design and simulation.

Among listed peers in IT and software, Samsung SDS, Hyundai Autoever and LG CNS are large system integrators with enterprise IT focus. Osangjaiel is positioned differently, centering on CATIA distribution and PLM implementation for design departments.

This focus builds a customer base in a specialized niche, but it also ties results to a narrower set of design demand.

Outlook

When it announced 2025 preliminary results, the company said demand for 3D design and simulation in the robotics industry would continue. It pointed to ongoing robot-related investment by about 13 customers as the basis. Three areas are being expanded. First, its SI unit is extending into AI infrastructure projects.

Second, the company is pursuing cosmetic and pharmaceutical raw material businesses based on JAIELITE, and is developing functional ingredients.

Third, shareholder returns: the company paid cash dividends for two consecutive years, and at the annual general meeting on March 27, 2026, shareholders approved the year-end cash dividend along with the financial statements.

The CEO bought shares on the market in June and September 2026, with the disclosed total at 71,000 shares across the two purchases. In September 2026, the CEO's September purchases of 20,000 and 21,000 shares were also reported in a major-shareholding filing.

Future direction can be gauged by whether quarterly revenue holds near the KRW 30 billion level of Q3 2026 and whether the operating margin exceeds the 8.1% of 2025. The contribution of the bio cosmetic raw material business to consolidated revenue is still small in the disclosed data.

Financial capacity and shareholder returns

Cash and financial products were about KRW 43 billion at the end of the first half of 2026. The debt ratio was 34.8% at the end of 2025. The company has paid cash dividends for two consecutive years, and the CEO increased his stake through market purchases in June and September 2026.

Bear factors

Profit slowdown in 2026 quarters

Q3 2026 revenue was KRW 30 billion, down 2.6% year on year, and operating profit of KRW 2.69 billion fell 8.4%. Attributable net profit was KRW 876 million, down 75.3% year on year. Revenue has risen for two quarters but has not returned to its year-earlier level in Q3.

Earnings volatility and equity-method effects

Attributable net profit fell 73.4% in 2024 from KRW 23.9 billion in 2023 to KRW 6.36 billion. The company attributed the 2024 decline in net profit to lower equity-method income. Large swings in annual net profit make the earnings trend hard to read as a single direction.

Limited contribution from the bio business

The bio business accounted for 5.36% of consolidated revenue in the first half of 2026. The contribution of the JAIELITE-based cosmetic and pharmaceutical raw material business to consolidated results is difficult to gauge from current disclosures. With IT dominating revenue, the diversification effect remains limited.

Risk factors

Customer and industry concentration

Revenue is linked to equipment and R&D spending in specific sectors such as defense, secondary batteries and robotics. Reduced investment by the roughly 13 customers the company cited would directly affect demand for 3D design and simulation services. A pullback in downstream investment could again cause quarterly revenue to swing.

Earnings swings and quarterly pattern

Attributable net profit fell 73.4% in 2024, and Q3 2026 attributable net profit fell 75.3% year on year. Quarterly revenue dropped from KRW 41.6 billion in Q4 2025 to KRW 28.5 billion in Q1 2026.

Operating profit and net profit moved in different directions across quarters, so the quality of earnings needs careful reading.

Largest shareholder's pledged shares and governance

The largest shareholder and related parties held 38.03% in September 2026. Filings show the largest shareholder entered loan agreements with the company and set pledges on its shares in November 2025, February 2026 and March 2026. A share-pledge structure is a factor that could affect governance if share prices move sharply.
