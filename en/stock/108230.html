Revenue rebounds, but net losses persist in recent quarters

02

Business structure

Toptec Company is a factory automation (FA) equipment maker. The parent and its subsidiaries run two main businesses, FA and product sales. In the first nine months of 2024, the FA segment accounted for 96.23% of cumulative revenue and the product sales segment for 2.74%.

In Q1 2026, FA was 89.76% of revenue, product sales 9.91% and other 0.33%, with no construction segment revenue.

The FA business covers secondary battery module and assembly equipment, smart factory logistics automation, and display and semiconductor equipment, while product sales cover nanofiber and electronic components. As of year-end 2025, exports made up 73.30% of FA segment sales versus 26.70% domestic.

R&D spending in 2025 was KRW 5.66 billion, or 3.31% of revenue. Customers are increasingly battery makers. A July 2025 report noted the company was seen as a core equipment supplier for an SK On prismatic battery pilot line.

A June 2026 report described the Indian automaker order as its first direct delivery of battery assembly lines to an overseas automaker.

The company also has a display-era history: revenue fell from KRW 1,138.4 billion in 2017 to KRW 308.8 billion in 2018, a decline reported as stemming from reduced Samsung business after a technology leak case, compounded by a display downturn.

The company presents batteries, electrified vehicle parts and smart logistics automation as its main business areas.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual revenue rose from KRW 345 billion in 2022 to KRW 601.7 billion in 2023, up 74.4% year on year, marking the peak. It then fell to KRW 473.6 billion in 2024, down 21.3%, and to KRW 171 billion in 2025, down 63.9%, a two-year consecutive decline from 2024 through 2025.

Among 2022 to 2025, 2023 was the largest year and 2025 the smallest. Operating profit grew from KRW 20.8 billion in 2022 to KRW 69 billion in 2023, up 230.8%, then fell to KRW 12.5 billion in 2024, down 81.9%.

In 2025 the company recorded an operating loss of KRW 3.14 billion, turning into a deficit, and the operating margin moved from 11.5% in 2023 to 2.6% in 2024 and -1.8% in 2025.

Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders rose to KRW 57.3 billion in 2023, up 293.0%, fell to KRW 35.5 billion in 2024, down 38.1%, and turned into a net loss of KRW 14.8 billion in 2025. Net profit including non-controlling interests, a different measure, was a net loss of KRW 20.6 billion in 2025.

Operating cash flow swung from KRW 11.9 billion in 2022 to -KRW 43.3 billion in 2023, KRW 126.9 billion in 2024 and -KRW 8.24 billion in 2025.

Quarterly revenue fell from KRW 56.2 billion in Q3 2025 to KRW 43.7 billion in Q4 2025, down 22.3% quarter on quarter, and to KRW 32.6 billion in Q1 2026, down 25.3%, making Q1 2026 the low point.

It then rose to KRW 40.8 billion in Q2 2026, up 25.2%, and KRW 82.3 billion in Q3 2026, up 101.5% quarter on quarter and 46.6% year on year, with revenue increasing for two consecutive quarters.

Operating profit was KRW 7.41 billion in Q3 2025, swung to an operating loss of KRW 3.72 billion in Q4 2025, returned to a profit of KRW 6.05 billion in Q1 2026, and went back to an operating loss of KRW 6.51 billion in Q2 2026. In Q3 2026 it was an operating profit of KRW 9.68 billion, up 30.5% year on year.

Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 12.4 billion in Q1 2026, then a net loss of KRW 2.78 billion in Q2 and KRW 3.67 billion in Q3, a two-quarter losing streak.

The Q3 2026 case, with an operating profit but a net loss attributable to controlling shareholders, means the gap between operating profit and final earnings needs to be checked in the detailed items of the periodic reports.

Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders for the last four quarters, from Q4 2025 to Q3 2026, totals KRW 5.57 billion. Set against the company's KRW 1 trillion revenue target stated in a 2024 report, 2025 revenue of KRW 171 billion shows that the recovery gap remains wide.

05

Industry analysis

Toptec Company's results depend on orders in both secondary battery and display equipment. Display equipment makers saw Q1 2026 results improve from a year earlier, and a May 2026 report said the resumption of China OLED investment and a recovery in equipment orders led to better profitability at some firms.

Toptec's revenue fell in the same period, but its operating profit turned positive. On the battery side, the effect of restructuring toward battery equipment is cited as a driver of the profitability recovery.

A July 2025 report said SK On's formal start of its first prismatic battery pilot line drew attention to Toptec as a core equipment supplier. On customer diversification, expectations for battery system assembly lines for Hyundai Mobis were also reported.

India, with roughly 4.4 million vehicles a year, is the world's third-largest auto market, and the company said it would step up additional order activity with Indian automakers and energy storage system makers.

In cycle terms, secondary battery equipment orders come in large lots, so quarterly revenue can swing sharply, as the jump to KRW 82.3 billion in Q3 2026 illustrates. Listed peers in the same machinery and equipment sector include Sunic System, Koh Young Technology and SFA Engineering.

Because the FA segment is a large share of revenue, the company is exposed to both the display and secondary battery cycles at once.

06

Outlook

The nearest confirmed milestone is the battery module and pack assembly line in Pune, India. A report says the line will be completed by late February 2027 and enter mass production from March. The contract value of about KRW 13 billion equals 7.6% of 2025 revenue of KRW 171 billion.

Backlog stood at KRW 152.8 billion at the end of Q1 2026, of which KRW 145.0 billion was in the FA segment. Compared with a July 2025 report that the first-half 2025 backlog exceeded KRW 640 billion, the balance has declined.

If backlog converts to revenue faster, quarterly sales can stay elevated, but if new orders do not follow, the balance will keep shrinking. The company presents batteries, electrified vehicle parts and smart logistics automation as its main areas.

A 2024 report said the company was developing four new products and targeting stable revenue of KRW 1 trillion, and how it narrows the gap with 2025 results is a medium-term checkpoint.

Whether the large orders expected after the SK On pilot line reported in July 2025 actually materialize is the battery-side checkpoint. The next periodic report will show whether Q4 2026 revenue holds near the KRW 82.3 billion of Q3 and how backlog changed.

A 2024 report said the company stated a 20% payout ratio policy, so any change should be checked after full-year results are confirmed.

08

Bull factors

Direct supply to an Indian automaker widens the customer base

A June 2026 report said a contract worth about KRW 13 billion to supply battery module and pack assembly equipment to an Indian automaker group was signed. It equals 7.6% of 2025 revenue and was introduced as a first direct supply to an overseas automaker.

The company said it will step up order activity with Indian automakers and energy storage makers. If customers widen beyond battery makers to automakers, dependence on a single customer group could decline.

Q3 2026 revenue and operating profit recover together

Q3 2026 revenue was KRW 82.3 billion, up 101.5% from the prior quarter and 46.6% from a year earlier. Operating profit was KRW 9.68 billion, up 30.5% year on year. Revenue has risen for two consecutive quarters from the Q1 2026 low of KRW 32.6 billion, which points to the direction of recovery. Whether the margin recovery continues will be seen in the next periodic report.

Debt reduction and a rise in cash

The debt ratio fell from 98.1% in 2023 to 48.8% in 2024 and 43.3% in 2025. Total liabilities at year-end 2025 were KRW 167.8 billion and total equity KRW 387.8 billion. A May 2026 report said cash and equivalents rose to KRW 203.2 billion in Q1 2026 while borrowings declined.

A larger financial buffer helps absorb equipment investment and working capital needs during periods of order expansion.

09

Bear factors

Profit and loss alternate from quarter to quarter

After an operating loss of KRW 3.72 billion in Q4 2025, the company posted a profit in Q1 2026, an operating loss of KRW 6.51 billion in Q2, and a profit in Q3, so the direction changed each quarter. Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders was negative in both Q2 and Q3 2026.

Over the last four quarters it totals only KRW 5.57 billion. A steady accumulation of earnings has not yet been confirmed.

A sharp fall in order backlog

A July 2025 report said first-half 2025 backlog exceeded KRW 640 billion. Backlog at the end of Q1 2026 was KRW 152.8 billion, with the FA segment accounting for KRW 145.0 billion. A smaller backlog reduces the visibility of future quarterly revenue.

Whether the Q3 2026 revenue rebound came from new orders should be checked in the backlog figures of the annual report.

Volatility from industry cycles and customer concentration

Revenue fell 21.3% in 2024 and 63.9% in 2025. The 2017 to 2018 decline, from KRW 1,138.4 billion to KRW 308.8 billion, showed how reduced dealings with one major customer can swing results.

The return to an operating loss in Q2 2026 shows how much results depend on the timing of orders from a few customers or businesses. With exports at 73.30% of FA segment sales as of year-end 2025, exchange rates and overseas order swings also matter.

Risk factors

Order concentration and backlog decline

Backlog stood at KRW 152.8 billion at the end of Q1 2026, below the level above KRW 640 billion in the first half of 2025. Most of it sits in the FA segment, so a pause in orders from a specific business could create a revenue gap.

If the Indian line is completed or enters mass production later than planned, overseas revenue recognition would also slip.

Quarterly earnings swings and continued losses

Q2 2026 posted an operating loss of KRW 6.51 billion, and net losses attributable to controlling shareholders continued in Q2 and Q3. Q3 2026 showed a net loss attributable to controlling shareholders even with an operating profit, so the impact of non-operating items or ownership structure needs to be checked. Prolonged losses could also weigh on cash flow and financial capacity.

Industry cycle and export dependence

Secondary battery equipment orders come in large lots, so quarterly results vary widely. In display equipment, the pace of recovery could shift with China OLED investment. With FA exports at 73.30% of segment sales at year-end 2025, overseas order conditions and exchange rates directly affect revenue.

Overall view

Toptec Company's revenue peaked at KRW 601.7 billion in 2023, then declined for two consecutive years in 2024 and 2025, and 2025 turned into an operating loss and a net loss attributable to controlling shareholders.

Quarterly revenue passed its low in Q1 2026 and recovered to KRW 82.3 billion in Q3, but profit and loss alternate by quarter, with an operating profit coexisting with a net loss attributable to controlling shareholders in Q3.

Backlog was KRW 152.8 billion at the end of Q1 2026, below the first-half 2025 level, and the direct supply order from an Indian automaker added a new customer base. The balance sheet has stabilized, with the debt ratio falling from 98.1% in 2023 to 43.3% in 2025.

Bullish factors are the revenue rebound, customer diversification and financial capacity, while bearish factors are the shrinking backlog, repeated quarterly losses, and dependence on industry cycles and exports.

The next checkpoints are the Pune line completion in late February 2027, the Q4 and full-year 2026 results, and the 2026 annual report due by late March 2027.

If the annual report clarifies the gap between operating profit and earnings attributable to controlling shareholders, year-end backlog and the dividend policy, the basis for assessing this thesis will be clearer.
