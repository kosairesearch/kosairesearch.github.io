02

Business structure

SFA Semicon is a KOSDAQ-listed company focused on back-end semiconductor assembly and test. It supplies packaging solutions to Samsung Electronics, Micron and IDT among other global chipmakers.

The company has expanded bumping equipment investment since completing its bumping plant and is growing bumping into a core business. Bumping forms fine protrusions on wafers or chips to connect them electrically to substrates, and demand is rising with the growth of flip-chip packaging.

By segment, memory-related products (computer peripherals and storage items) accounted for 81.2% of 2025 revenue, non-memory products such as industrial, PC and power applications for 18.1%, and other items such as modules for 0.7%.

Memory was 76.2% in 2024 and non-memory 23.0%, so the mix tilted back toward memory in 2025. Production sits in domestic plants and a Philippine subsidiary.

Per BNK Investment & Securities, the domestic non-memory bumping and test line (K2) reached utilization in the high 60% range, while the MCP-focused line (K1) remains weak. The same report noted that the Philippine plant's utilization rose from the 30% range into the 60% range.

On the customer side, single-customer dependence has been flagged as a risk. On competition, the intensifying rivalry that FnGuide's company profile cited as a cause of the 2025 deterioration remains a key variable.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual results show both revenue and profitability trending lower.

Revenue went from KRW 699.4 billion in 2022 to KRW 437.6 billion in 2023 (down 37.4% year on year), KRW 400.5 billion in 2024 (down 8.5%) and KRW 367.4 billion in 2025 (down 8.3%), with 2022 the largest year and 2025 the smallest in the 2022 to 2025 span.

Operating profit of KRW 62.9 billion in 2022 turned into an operating loss of KRW 16.7 billion in 2023, narrowed to an operating loss of KRW 31 million in 2024, and widened again to an operating loss of KRW 19.6 billion in 2025.

The operating margin moved from 9.0% in 2022 to -3.8% in 2023, -0.0% in 2024 and -5.3% in 2025. Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders turned positive at KRW 21.3 billion in 2024 but swung to a net loss of KRW 19.1 billion in 2025.

Net income including non-controlling interests was KRW 20.5 billion in 2024 and a net loss of KRW 19.1 billion in 2025, a different measure from the controlling-shareholder figure. The 2025 deterioration was attributed to weaker memory demand, slower non-memory growth, intensifying competition and higher cost burdens.

Operating cash flow fell from KRW 94.9 billion in 2022 to KRW 5.46 billion in 2025.

Quarterly revenue rose from KRW 96.5 billion in Q3 2025 to KRW 119.6 billion in Q4 2025 (up 23.9% quarter on quarter), dipped to KRW 107.3 billion in Q1 2026 (down 10.2%), then reached KRW 126.3 billion in Q2 and KRW 148.2 billion in Q3 2026 (up 17.3% quarter on quarter), marking two consecutive quarters of growth.

Over the same period, operating losses were KRW 6.3 billion in Q3 2025, KRW 590 million in Q4 2025, KRW 6.18 billion in Q1 2026, KRW 6.15 billion in Q2 and KRW 6.58 billion in Q3 2026, so the loss stayed in the KRW 6 billion range despite revenue growth.

Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders turned positive at KRW 1.19 billion in Q1 2026 but returned to net losses of KRW 5.35 billion in Q2 and KRW 9.52 billion in Q3. The detailed causes of the Q3 loss widening should be traced through the income statement items in the quarterly report.

05

Industry analysis

Back-end packaging demand is rising, led by bumping, as flip-chip-based packaging markets expand.

In a report dated September 14, 2026, Korea Investment & Securities said memory packaging volume at the Korean plant is growing sharply, and noted that beyond the DDR5 packaging outsourcing already secured, further order wins remain possible.

The same report expected packaging volumes for DDR5 memory and SSD-oriented FBGA products to increase. Back-end memory work is tied to memory cycles, so demand volatility and price pressure operate together.

FnGuide's company profile attributed the 2025 deterioration to weaker memory demand and slower non-memory growth. Intensifying competition among back-end providers has also been cited as a factor raising cost burdens and weighing on profitability.

In non-memory work, customer diversification is presented as both a challenge and the company's response. Listed back-end peers such as HanaMicron serve as comparison points in bumping and test.

The parent company, SFA, won its first domestic HBM line logistics system order in 2025, with SK hynix identified as the customer.

Outlook

In an October 28, 2026 report, Samsung Securities said the Q4 2026 setup should make test revenue meaningful, and projected a return to operating profit in 2027 and record annual results on an improving test-centered mix and higher DRAM volumes.

In a September 14, 2026 report, Korea Investment & Securities expected the Korean plant's revenue share to widen from 32% this year to 38% next year. In a February 2026 report, BNK Investment & Securities forecast a turn to profit starting Q1 2026, but the disclosed Q1 2026 operating loss was KRW 6.18 billion.

Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders turned positive while the operating line stayed in loss, so the timing of an operating-level turnaround is the key point.

A parent-company official said on November 12, 2026 that the new SFA Semicon line's full operation would bring significant earnings improvement soon. That remark is a qualitative comment from the parent and does not specify timing or amounts. The next checkpoints are the Q4 2026 preliminary results and the 2026 annual report.

Bear factors

Persistent operating losses and a wider Q3 deficit

Operating losses have run for five consecutive quarters from Q3 2025 through Q3 2026. The Q3 2026 operating loss of KRW 6.58 billion was wider than a year earlier, and the net loss attributable to controlling shareholders widened to KRW 9.52 billion.

Over the last four quarters, the cumulative controlling-shareholder net loss of KRW 14.9 billion remained negative even after the Q1 2026 profit.

Three straight years of revenue decline and a wider 2025 loss

Revenue has declined for three consecutive years from 2023 through 2025, falling to KRW 367.4 billion in 2025. The 2025 operating loss was KRW 19.6 billion and the net loss attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 19.1 billion.

FnGuide's company profile grouped weaker memory demand, slower non-memory growth, intensifying competition and cost burdens as the causes of the 2025 deterioration.

Utilization gaps and customer concentration

BNK Investment & Securities noted that the domestic K1 line, focused on MCP products, has weak utilization. The same report said a temporary deterioration in the domestic plant's product mix kept operating losses in place.

Single-customer dependence is also cited as a risk, so a delay in mix improvement could keep the domestic plant's profit structure under pressure.

Risk factors

Product and customer concentration

Public filing-based tallies show memory-related products made up 81.2% of 2025 revenue. A slowdown in memory demand could weigh on revenue, and single-customer dependence has also been raised. The company has presented non-memory customer diversification as its response.

Cost and utilization execution

The utilization gap between the Philippine subsidiary and the domestic K1 line will shape costs and profitability. Cost burdens were part of the 2025 deterioration, and if competition in back-end work intensifies, margin recovery could be delayed. If the DDR5 test setup does not start in Q4 as projected, test revenue will also be recognized later.

Ownership structure and share price volatility

Per a Wisereport tally as of September 16, 2026, the largest shareholder group holds 55.00%, leaving a free float ratio of 44.70%. The 52-week beta of 1.65 indicates above-market price swings. This structure can amplify share price moves regardless of earnings developments.

Overall view

SFA Semicon's Q3 2026 revenue reached KRW 148.2 billion, up 53.6% year on year, and has grown for two consecutive quarters. Operating losses, however, have persisted for five quarters and widened to KRW 6.58 billion in Q3 2026 from the prior-year quarter.

On the positive side, DDR5 packaging and test volume expansion and a higher Philippine plant utilization have been cited, and Samsung Securities projected a return to operating profit in 2027.

On the negative side, the last four quarters show a cumulative controlling-shareholder net loss of KRW 14.9 billion, annual revenue has declined since 2023, the 2025 operating loss was KRW 19.6 billion, and memory concentration and single-customer dependence remain.

On the financial side, the debt-to-equity ratio fell from 39.5% in 2022 to 16.2% in 2025, and total equity at year-end 2025 was KRW 472.8 billion. The next checkpoints are whether test revenue narrows the operating loss in the Q4 2026 preliminary results and the operating margin in the 2026 annual report.
