Report overview

DDR5 Outsourcing Lifts Sales; Profit Unproven

SFA Semicon's Q2 2026 revenue rose 65.9% year on year on the back of Samsung Electronics' expanded outsourcing of commodity memory back-end work, yet the company has posted five consecutive quarterly operating losses, and the key test is whether its Philippine DDR5 capacity expansion translates into actual profit.

  1. 1

    On a reported basis, annual revenue fell from KRW 699.4 billion in 2022 to KRW 367.4 billion in 2025, and the company posted operating losses for three straight years from 2023 to 2025.

  2. 2

    Quarterly revenue grew from KRW 76.1 billion in Q2 2025 to KRW 126.3 billion in Q2 2026, but an operating loss of KRW 6.15 billion persisted, so top-line growth has not yet reached the bottom line.

  3. 3

    On September 8, 2026, the company disclosed a USD 75 million (about KRW 100.7 billion) loan to its Philippine affiliate SSP, bringing total loans to the Philippines this year to KRW 130.2 billion.

  4. 4

    The Philippine unit's DDR5 test capacity is expected to rise from about KRW 10 billion per month in Q4 2026 to KRW 20 billion per month by the end of Q2 2027.

  5. 5

02

Business structure

SFA Semicon is an outsourced semiconductor assembly and test (OSAT) provider handling back-end packaging and testing, supplying packaging solutions to Samsung Electronics, Micron and others. By product, memory accounted for 84% and non-memory for 16% of Q2 2026 revenue, which means a heavy reliance on memory.

In Korea, it runs the K1 plant focused on MCP and the K2 plant for non-memory bumping and test; according to BNK Investment & Securities in February 2026, K2 utilization had climbed to the high-60% range while K1 utilization remained weak.

The main production base is the Philippine subsidiary, which provides turnkey assembly, final test and module services for server and PC products. A division of labor is taking shape: the Cheonan plant handles FBGA packaging of memory chips, while the Philippine plant handles final test and module assembly.

The Philippine unit posted 2025 revenue of KRW 223.8 billion and a net loss of KRW 22.5 billion, so it is the site that drives the direction of consolidated earnings. As a new business, the company has built two SSD module lines and is bringing them online in sequence.

The largest shareholder is SFA Engineering, holding 55.00% together with related parties as of September 16, 2026.

In the competitive landscape, HanaMicron mainly serves SK hynix volumes while SFA Semicon mainly serves Samsung Electronics, and one analysis notes that Samsung's outsourcing is growing, but it is not a winner-take-all structure.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual revenue fell from KRW 699.4 billion in 2022 to KRW 437.6 billion in 2023 (down 37.4% year on year), KRW 400.5 billion in 2024 (down 8.5%) and KRW 367.4 billion in 2025 (down 8.3%), declining for three straight years from 2023 to 2025, with 2025 the smallest year in the 2022-2025 period.

Operating profit of KRW 62.9 billion in 2022 (9.0% operating margin) swung to an operating loss of KRW 16.7 billion in 2023. The loss then nearly disappeared, narrowing to KRW 31 million in 2024, before widening again to KRW 19.6 billion in 2025 (operating margin of -5.3%).

Net income attributable to owners of the parent turned positive at KRW 21.3 billion in 2024 but swung back to a net loss of KRW 19.1 billion in 2025. A net profit in 2024 despite a roughly break-even operating result indicates a large contribution from non-operating items.

Quarterly revenue grew from KRW 76.1 billion in Q2 2025 to KRW 119.6 billion in Q4 2025, dipped to KRW 107.3 billion in Q1 2026 and rebounded to KRW 126.3 billion in Q2 2026 (up 65.9% year on year).

The operating loss narrowed to KRW 590 million in Q4 2025, which BNK Investment & Securities attributed to Philippine plant utilization rising from the 30% range to the 60% range faster than expected.

However, losses widened again to KRW 6.18 billion in Q1 2026 and KRW 6.15 billion in Q2 2026, extending the run of operating losses to five consecutive quarters from Q2 2025 through Q2 2026.

Net income attributable to owners of the parent turned positive at KRW 1.19 billion in Q1 2026, then swung to a net loss of KRW 5.35 billion in Q2, and the combined net loss for the last four quarters (Q3 2025 to Q2 2026) totals KRW 10.4 billion.

05

Industry analysis

The memory market is in a strong expansion phase. Samsung Electronics reported preliminary Q3 2026 results of KRW 195 trillion in consolidated revenue and KRW 107.4 trillion in operating profit.

The more direct variable for back-end OSAT providers is Samsung's outsourcing policy, as Samsung has decided to shift incremental commodity memory module output to outsourcing.

Accordingly, SFA Semicon is relocating DDR5 test and module assembly equipment previously used at Samsung's Onyang campus to its Philippine plant. On the cycle, one brokerage analysis notes that back-end players typically outperform in the mid-to-late stages of an upturn as utilization rises.

However, competition for outsourced volumes is under way, and Hanyang Digitech invested more than KRW 31.5 billion in Vietnam plant equipment and automation in the first half.

Because the outsourcing expansion started from a space shortage inside Samsung, it has not been confirmed whether the commodity outsourcing stance will continue after new fabs come online.

On the macro side, in September 2026 concerns about a slowdown in AI investment and a US rate hike combined to increase volatility in the Korean market.

06

Outlook

The Philippine DDR5 lines are scheduled to start phase one in November 2026 and phase two in Q2 2027. Korea Investment & Securities forecast 2027 revenue of KRW 552 billion for the Philippines and KRW 336 billion for Korea, up 45.3% and 90.6% respectively from 2026.

The same brokerage said FBGA packaging volumes for DDR5 memory and SSDs at the Korean plant will also increase. SK Securities estimated 2027 operating profit at KRW 78.5 billion, according to September 2026 reports.

By contrast, Hana Securities expects a 2026 full-year operating loss of KRW 3.3 billion, which suggests the full-year turnaround may come later than 2026.

The gap between past forecasts and actual results is also worth noting: in February 2026, BNK Investment & Securities forecast Q1 2026 operating profit of KRW 2.1 billion, but the reported Q1 2026 result was an operating loss of KRW 6.18 billion.

No official company earnings guidance has been confirmed, so assessments must rely on brokerage estimates and quarterly filings.

08

Bull factors

Direct beneficiary of Samsung's DDR5 outsourcing

In February 2026, BNK Investment & Securities expected that due to the main customer's outsourcing policy change, the Philippine plant's revenue will grow sharply, centered on profitable DDR5 final test. Quarterly revenue did rise from KRW 76.1 billion in Q2 2025 to KRW 126.3 billion in Q2 2026.

Korea Investment & Securities said additional orders are possible beyond the DDR5 outsourcing volumes already secured.

Operating leverage from rising utilization

Back-end operations carry high fixed costs, so rising utilization leaves substantial room for operating margin improvement. Q4 2025 illustrates this: as Philippine utilization climbed, the operating loss narrowed to KRW 590 million.

With Philippine DDR5 capacity planned to double by the end of Q2 2027, filled volumes could reshape the profit structure.

Low leverage and group backing

The debt-to-equity ratio declined steadily from 39.5% in 2022 to 16.2% in 2025. Total liabilities were only KRW 76.6 billion against total equity of KRW 472.8 billion at end-2025. This financial capacity allowed the company to fund large-scale expansion at the Philippine unit without external borrowing.

09

Bear factors

Operating losses persist despite sales growth

Q2 2026 revenue rose 65.9% year on year, but the operating loss of KRW 6.15 billion was little changed from KRW 6.72 billion a year earlier. The forecast turnaround in Q1 2026 also failed to materialize. With the break-even point repeatedly pushed back, the reliability of brokerage estimates is being tested.

Weaker cash generation amid heavy investment

Operating cash flow in 2025 fell to KRW 5.46 billion from KRW 53.1 billion in 2024. Meanwhile, the Philippine loan amounts to 21.3% of equity, and repayment depends on SSP's operating performance.

Loan interest is eliminated as an intercompany transaction in the consolidated statements, so the consolidated benefit shows up only through utilization and profit.

Multiple running ahead of earnings

Reported results are loss-making, yet the shares trade at a large premium to net assets. This implies the 2027 recovery estimates are largely reflected, so the multiple could swing sharply if quarterly results disappoint. Volatility is already high: on October 12, 2026, the stock moved 6.65% in a single session.

10

Risk factors

Customer concentration and outsourcing policy

A large share of revenue depends on Samsung Electronics' memory outsourcing volumes. Because the outsourcing push stemmed from Samsung's space constraints, the stance could change once new fabs come online. Capacity additions by rival OSAT providers could also affect volume allocation.

Expansion execution and yield

Equipment relocation and new line start-ups involve initial costs and a yield stabilization period. It has been noted that for overseas plant investments, start-up costs and yield stabilization determine profitability. If the phase-one start in November 2026 slips or yields are low, the turnaround could be delayed further.

Memory cycle and FX

With most revenue coming from memory, a downturn in the memory market could quickly cut utilization. Given the high fixed-cost structure, lower utilization translates directly into wider losses. The loan to the Philippine unit is denominated in US dollars, so exchange-rate moves also affect earnings.

12

Overall view

SFA Semicon sits at the center of a clear structural shift: Samsung Electronics' expanded outsourcing of commodity memory. Quarterly revenue is already recovering, and a concrete schedule for expanding Philippine DDR5 capacity has been laid out.

However, reported results show five consecutive quarterly operating losses, and the early-2026 turnaround forecast did not materialize. The large Philippine loans have concentrated part of the company's financial capacity in a single site, and 2025 operating cash flow was weak.

The shares reflect 2027 recovery estimates at a large premium to net assets, making the Q3 results and phase-one Philippine start in November 2026 the first verification points. This report is intended for informational purposes; investment decisions are left to the reader.
