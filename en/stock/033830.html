Ad Share Shrinks as Operating Losses Run Three Quarters

02

Business structure

TBC is a regional commercial broadcaster based in Daegu and North Gyeongsang, operating under a government license with a network agreement with Seoul Broadcasting System (SBS). Programming runs at roughly 70% SBS content and 30% in-house content.

To offset the loss of broadcast audience share to new media platforms, the company has expanded into performances, event services and online education. According to fnguide, the 2025 revenue mix was business revenue 44.93%, other broadcasting revenue 21.86%, TV and FM advertising 17.25% and sponsorship 15.96%.

In the same data set, TV and FM advertising was 33.59% of revenue in 2022 and business revenue 30.03%, so dependence on advertising has declined while business revenue has grown in weight.

The company's first-quarter 2025 commentary attributed weaker broadcast advertising and sponsorship income to a sluggish domestic economy and lower advertising market share, with performance-related revenue and other broadcasting income offsetting part of the decline.

Advertising revenue is the line most directly exposed to the structural pressure of falling terrestrial TV ad share. Performance and event revenue, tied to activities outside broadcasting, can add volatility to earnings.

On costs, the cost of sales ratio rose from 72.02% in 2022 to 83.05% in 2025, leaving less margin on each unit of revenue. Headcount at the end of 2025 was 113, with average tenure of 18.9 years.

Competition comes from other terrestrial and regional broadcasters competing for advertising budgets, new media platforms, and content companies in the media and entertainment sector such as CJ ENM.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual revenue rose from KRW 42.5 billion in 2022 to KRW 43.5 billion in 2023 (up 2.4%), fell to KRW 40.3 billion in 2024 (down 7.4%), and recovered to KRW 42.7 billion in 2025 (up 6.0%).

Operating profit was KRW 4.05 billion in 2022 and KRW 4.14 billion in 2023 (up 2.1%), then dropped to KRW 1.46 billion in 2024 (down 64.8%). Operating profit edged up to KRW 1.48 billion in 2025 (up 1.9%), but profitability remained at a lower level.

The operating margin was 9.5% in 2022 and 9.5% in 2023, then 3.6% in 2024 and 3.5% in 2025, and the revenue recovery in 2025 did not bring the margin back.

Profit attributable to controlling shareholders peaked at KRW 4.93 billion in 2022 and KRW 6.19 billion in 2023 (up 25.7%), then fell to KRW 3.8 billion in 2024 (down 38.6%) and KRW 3.8 billion in 2025 (down 0.1%), a decline in two consecutive years.

Net income including non-controlling interests was reported at KRW 4.93 billion in 2022, KRW 6.19 billion in 2023, and KRW 3.8 billion in both 2024 and 2025. Operating cash flow moved from negative KRW 2.08 billion in 2022 to KRW 1.71 billion in 2023, KRW 10.6 billion in 2024 and KRW 7.9 billion in 2025.

On a quarterly basis, revenue was KRW 9.63 billion in Q3 2025 and rose to KRW 13.5 billion in Q4 2025 (up 40.3% from the prior quarter), when operating profit reached KRW 1.14 billion (up 655.9%).

Revenue fell to KRW 7.37 billion in Q1 2026 (down 45.4% from the prior quarter), with an operating loss of KRW 455 million that marked a swing into deficit. In Q2 2026 revenue recovered to KRW 8.93 billion (up 21.1%), and the operating loss narrowed to KRW 268 million.

In Q3 2026 revenue was KRW 7.05 billion (down 21.0% from the prior quarter and down 26.7% year on year), and the operating loss widened to KRW 370 million, a swing into deficit against the prior-year quarter.

The company has recorded operating losses in three consecutive quarters from Q1 to Q3 2026, while profit attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 206 million, KRW 474 million and KRW 395 million in those quarters, so operating and net results moved in different directions.

Cost of sales ratios of 82.87% in 2024 and 83.05% in 2025 show that the margin squeeze has persisted. Profit attributable to controlling shareholders over the last four quarters (Q4 2025 to Q3 2026) totaled KRW 2.65 billion.

05

Industry analysis

TBC operates in a terrestrial and regional broadcasting market where new media platforms are steadily reducing broadcasters' audience share. Per fnguide, a sluggish domestic economy and a decline in terrestrial TV's advertising share explain the drop in broadcast advertising and sponsorship income.

This tracks a broader reallocation of advertising budgets across viewing platforms. The company is responding by growing non-broadcast revenue streams such as performances, event services and online education.

A larger non-broadcast share lowers exposure to ad-market swings, but performance and event income brings its own volatility tied to demand and scheduling. Relying on SBS for roughly 70% of programming ties the company's schedule and content supply to a single network partner.

Within the media and entertainment sector, SBS and CJ ENM are large players competing or cooperating in advertising and content distribution, with a scale and revenue structure very different from a regional commercial broadcaster.

The key variables for the revenue direction are a recovery in the advertising cycle and the trend in new media share. Whether the operating losses that began in Q1 2026 can be offset by advertising recovery or non-broadcast growth will be the benchmark for TBC's relative position within this industry.

At the annual general meeting held the same day, the financial statements including a cash dividend were approved. The plan's specific numerical targets are not reproduced in this report, so the direction of profitability recovery will need to be confirmed through reported results.

With operating losses running from Q1 through Q3 2026, the effectiveness of the plan will likely show up in whether operating profit turns positive.

The identified growth axes are performances, event services and online education, and the key question is how far their revenue offsets the decline in broadcast advertising.

The combination of KRW 13.5 billion in revenue and KRW 1.14 billion in operating profit in Q4 2025 shows how event-linked income can cause sharp quarterly swings in either direction. The 2026 Q3 revenue decline reflects that volatility working in the opposite direction.

Because the company has stated it will maintain its dividend payout approach, dividend levels are likely to track earnings scale. The operating margin in the next regular report will be the first checkpoint for the direction of profitability recovery.

Risk factors

Industry · ad market

New media platforms are steadily reducing broadcasters' audience share, and a weak domestic economy plus lower terrestrial TV ad share are weighing on broadcast advertising. If the ad market does not recover, declines in advertising and sponsorship income could persist.

If advertising falls faster than non-broadcast revenue grows, total revenue will decline. This balance will shape results in the quarters ahead.

Business structure · programming dependence

Roughly 70% of programming depends on the SBS network arrangement. Any change in network terms or scheduling would affect the in-house programming mix and revenue structure. Non-broadcast businesses such as performances and event services show large revenue swings depending on timing and demand. If volatility in both areas occurs at once, the range of possible outcomes widens.

Earnings volatility · shareholder returns

Quarterly results have swung sharply, from operating profit of KRW 1.14 billion in Q4 2025 to an operating loss of KRW 455 million in Q1 2026. Profit attributable to controlling shareholders has declined for two consecutive years, while the company has kept its dividend payout approach in place.

If that approach continues while earnings are weak, the dividend burden relative to profit could rise. The durability of the return policy should be read alongside the pace of earnings recovery.
