02

Business structure

KG Financial listed on KOSDAQ in December 2004 as a software development and supply company, and changed its name in March 2026 from KG Mobilians to KG Financial. The business spans electronic payments (PG and mobile phone payments), the prepaid card MobilCard, education, IT and e-learning.

Based on Butler's aggregation, electronic payments accounted for 88% of second-quarter 2026 revenue, education for 7% and IT for 6%. Because the payment segment dominates, PG transaction volume and conditions in the mobile payment market are the core variables for earnings.

Media reports say the company remains the number one player in mobile phone payments, and the company disclosed a plan to lift its share from 37% to 40% during 2026.

In July 2026 MobilCard added T-money transit card functions and expanded barcode payments, and third-quarter top-up volume exceeded KRW 200 billion with more than 750,000 members.

The new settlement-advance service lets sellers receive payments ahead of the settlement date and draws on the company's existing transaction data and risk-management capability. At the CEO Investor Day in February 2026, management declared a shift from a payment-centered structure to a finance-centered one.

Medium-term goals include obtaining virtual asset service provider (VASP) status in 2027 and building stablecoin-based payment infrastructure. As of September 1, 2026, the largest shareholder and related parties held 54.19%.

04

Earnings analysis

In the annual disclosed results, revenue went from KRW 276.1 billion in 2022 to KRW 279.6 billion in 2023, up 1.3% year on year, which marked the peak. It then fell 4.8% to KRW 266.1 billion in 2024 and 12.2% to KRW 233.8 billion in 2025, so revenue has declined for two consecutive years since 2023.

Among the 2022 to 2025 years, 2023 was the largest for revenue and 2025 the smallest. Operating profit fell from KRW 40.3 billion in 2022 to KRW 33 billion in 2023 (down 18.1%) and then to KRW 7.43 billion in 2024 (down 77.5%), before recovering to KRW 33.1 billion in 2025 (up 345.9%).

The operating margin was 14.6% in 2022, 11.8% in 2023, 2.8% in 2024 and 14.2% in 2025, so the margin climbed sharply from the 2024 trough even as 2025 revenue fell.

Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 28 billion in 2022, KRW 43.6 billion in 2023 (up 55.9%), KRW 9.72 billion in 2024 (down 77.7%) and KRW 23.2 billion in 2025 (up 139.1%).

Total net profit including non-controlling interests was KRW 22.6 billion in 2025, below the controlling-shareholder figure, which means the non-controlling interests contributed a loss in that year.

Operating cash flow was KRW -9.25 billion in 2022 and KRW -62.5 billion in 2023, then turned positive at KRW 5.89 billion in 2024 and KRW 17.2 billion in 2025. The debt-to-equity ratio rose from 49.9% in 2022 to 55.2% in 2024 and stood at 54.7% in 2025.

On a quarterly basis, revenue went from KRW 60.8 billion in the third quarter of 2025 to KRW 55.5 billion in the fourth quarter (down 8.7% quarter on quarter), then KRW 62.5 billion in the first quarter of 2026, KRW 62.2 billion in the second and KRW 71.1 billion in the third (up 14.3% quarter on quarter).

Operating profit fell from KRW 10.2 billion in the third quarter of 2025 to KRW 7.76 billion in the fourth quarter (down 23.7%) and KRW 7.01 billion in the first quarter of 2026 (down 9.7%), then rose to KRW 8.81 billion and KRW 10.4 billion.

Media coverage attributed the first-quarter 2026 decline to an early provision for bad debts linked to growth in mobile phone payment transactions.

05

Industry analysis

For KG Financial, revenue in domestic electronic payments is directly linked to PG transaction volume. According to the company, growth in credit-card PG volume in the third quarter of 2026 reflected Samsung Electronics appliance cashback events and stronger sales of the Galaxy Z Fold8 and Flip8.

Because these drivers are tied to specific event-driven demand, whether transaction flows continue after the fourth quarter will be the test for the industry picture.

On a cumulative basis through the third quarter of 2026, credit-card PG volume reached KRW 4.27 trillion, up 44.4% year on year, while mobile phone payment volume reached KRW 1.85 trillion, up 2.5%. The company is pursuing a 40% share target from a leading position in mobile phone payments.

In the prepaid card market, MobilCard's cumulative volume was KRW 576.9 billion through the third quarter, up 12.1% year on year.

The settlement-advance service addresses sellers' demand to receive settlement funds earlier, and credit and settlement risk management built on existing payment data is central to its competitiveness.

Entry into digital assets depends first on obtaining VASP status, so licensing timing and stablecoin-related rulemaking will shape the pace.

Compared with bank and securities holding companies in the same financial sector, KG Financial earns mainly from payment fees and transaction scale, so its profit swings tend to be larger. The move from KRW 7.43 billion in operating profit in 2024 to KRW 33.1 billion in 2025 illustrates that sensitivity.

Outlook

During 2026 the company disclosed plans to lift its mobile phone payment share from 37% to 40%, expand MobilCard users from 650,000 to 800,000, and grow transaction volume from KRW 680 billion to KRW 800 billion.

Over the medium term, it targets VASP status in 2027 and has set settlement-advance volume goals of KRW 500 billion for 2027 and KRW 1 trillion for 2028. According to a Korea Financial News report, a company official expects transaction volume to rise gradually in the fourth quarter of 2026 on a stable merchant base.

The settlement-advance service was reported to have passed 400 cumulative applications within a month of its full launch in July 2026. The company is also preparing a digital asset wallet link and stablecoin-based payment and settlement services.

Management said it would expand business opportunities into digital assets and other new financial services in 2027.

On near-term earnings, the points to watch are whether electronic payment volume holds its third-quarter level in the fourth quarter and whether the operating margin continues to recover from the first-quarter 2026 low.

New businesses still contribute a small share of revenue, so how quickly settlement-advance volume and MobilCard top-ups grow each quarter will indicate progress toward the medium-term targets. Whether the 2026 targets were met can be checked against the 2026 annual report.

Bear factors

High earnings volatility

Operating profit fell from KRW 40.3 billion in 2022 to KRW 7.43 billion in 2024 before recovering to KRW 33.1 billion in 2025. Revenue has declined for two consecutive years since its 2023 peak.

First-quarter 2026 operating profit of KRW 7.01 billion was down 9.7% quarter on quarter, which media linked to an early bad-debt provision.

Concentration in payments and event dependence

Electronic payments accounted for 88% of second-quarter 2026 revenue, leaving the business concentrated in one segment. The company attributed part of third-quarter PG growth to Samsung Electronics appliance cashback events and new-product sales, which are concentrated in specific periods. Whether these drivers persist after the fourth quarter remains to be seen.

Cash flow and leverage

Operating cash flow was negative at KRW -9.25 billion in 2022 and KRW -62.5 billion in 2023 before turning positive in 2024 and 2025. The debt-to-equity ratio rose from 49.9% in 2022 to 55.2% in 2024, and total liabilities at the end of 2025 were KRW 196.6 billion.

Because the period of positive operating cash flow is still short, the durability of the earnings recovery needs further confirmation.

10

Risk factors

Regulation and licensing

The digital asset business depends on obtaining VASP status in 2027. Delays in licensing or changes to stablecoin rules could push back the timing of the medium-term plan. The company is preparing a digital asset wallet link and stablecoin-based payments in parallel.

Credit and settlement risk

Media linked the first-quarter 2026 profit decline to an early bad-debt provision tied to expanding mobile phone payment transactions. Because the settlement-advance service pays sellers before the settlement date, managing counterparty credit risk becomes central. As volume grows, provisions and settlement risk could weigh more heavily on profit.

Transaction concentration and share targets

Reaching a 40% mobile phone payment share requires taking more share in the market. Cumulative mobile phone payment volume through the third quarter of 2026 grew 2.5% year on year, while credit-card PG drove most of the growth. If share gains do not translate into transaction volume, the target could be reached later than planned.

Overall view

KG Financial recorded third-quarter 2026 revenue of KRW 71.1 billion, up 17.0% year on year, and operating profit of KRW 10.4 billion, supported by growth in electronic payment volume. The 2025 operating profit of KRW 33.1 billion reflects a recovery from the 2024 trough but remains below the KRW 40.3 billion of 2022.

Revenue declined for two consecutive years after peaking in 2023, and first-quarter 2026 operating profit fell as a bad-debt provision was booked.

Factors on the positive side include growing transaction volume, early results from new businesses such as settlement-advance and MobilCard, and shareholder returns including dividends and share cancellation.

Factors on the negative side include high earnings volatility, business concentration with electronic payments at 88% of revenue, transaction gains tied to events such as Samsung Electronics cashback campaigns, and uncertainty over licensing timing for digital asset businesses.

The multiples sit below the industry average and the share price is below book value per share, but these comparisons do not guarantee that earnings will hold. The next checkpoints are the provisional fourth-quarter 2026 results, progress on targets in the 2026 annual report and whether VASP status is obtained in 2027.
