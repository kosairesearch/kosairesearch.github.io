02

Business structure

Welkeeps Hitech was founded in 1974 as an IT core-component maker and now runs three lines: semiconductor back-end packaging, power-supply components, and automotive and wireless-charging parts. Its semiconductor unit designs and packages driver ICs, with TCP and COF display driver packages as its main products.

The company lists PDP, LCD and OLED as the applications for these packages, which are mounted on thin film. The power-supply unit makes transformers and coils and has subsidiaries in Wendeng and Shandong, China.

In automotive, the company supplies electric-vehicle core parts to Hyundai and Kia and has wireless-charging modules in mass production for Hyundai Mobis.

Its main customers listed in the 2024 half-year report include DB GlobalChip, LX Semicon and BOE in semiconductors, and Molex, LG Innotek, Hansol Technics, Hyundai Mobis and Volkswagen in electronics.

In January 2024 the company bought 100% of Jewon Tech, an EV battery parts maker, for KRW 4 billion to expand its EV parts business. The company said it exited the COF business, and the press has cited a large dispute with a DB group chip-design affiliate, a long-time COF supplier, as a reason for lower revenue.

Display driver packaging volumes move with panel demand and customer orders, so shifts in the customer base feed quickly into results. At the March 2026 shareholder meeting, new business purposes such as software development, e-commerce and management consulting were added to the articles of incorporation.

Earnings analysis

On the basis of the filed annual results, revenue was KRW 20.9 billion in 2022, KRW 20.2 billion in 2023 (down 3.2% year on year), KRW 29.8 billion in 2024 (up 47.5%) and KRW 21.4 billion in 2025 (down 28.4%). 2024 was the largest year of 2022 to 2025 and 2023 the smallest.

Operating loss of KRW 160 million in 2022 turned to operating profit of KRW 293 million in 2023, a swing to profit, before returning to an operating loss of KRW 9.96 billion in 2024, a swing back to a loss.

The 2025 operating loss of KRW 2.1 billion continued the loss but was smaller, and the company has been loss-making for two consecutive years from 2024 to 2025. Operating margins were -0.8% in 2022, 1.4% in 2023, -33.4% in 2024 and -9.8% in 2025.

In 2024 revenue grew but the margin fell sharply, so higher sales did not translate into better profitability. In 2023 net profit attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 9.12 billion far exceeded the KRW 293 million operating profit, which points to a large contribution from non-operating items.

The controlling-shareholder net loss was KRW 9.83 billion in 2024 and KRW 3.24 billion in 2025, a smaller loss.

Net profit including non-controlling interests was reported as a loss of KRW 739 million in 2022, a profit of KRW 9.12 billion in 2023, a loss of KRW 9.83 billion in 2024 and a loss of KRW 3.24 billion in 2025.

The debt ratio rose from 9.0% in 2022 and 8.1% in 2023 to 51.7% in 2024 and 61.9% in 2025, with total equity of KRW 34.9 billion and total liabilities of KRW 21.6 billion at the end of 2025.

Operating cash flow was KRW 2.78 billion in 2022 and KRW 4.55 billion in 2023, then negative at KRW -3.86 billion in 2024 and KRW -1.84 billion in 2025.

Quarterly revenue fell from KRW 5.24 billion in the third quarter of 2025 to KRW 3.68 billion in the third quarter of 2026, a fourth consecutive quarterly decline, and the latest quarter was down 29.7% year on year.

The operating loss widened from KRW 580 million in the third quarter of 2025 to KRW 914 million in the fourth quarter, then ran at KRW 436 million, KRW 728 million and KRW 383 million in the first three quarters of 2026.

The fourth-quarter 2025 controlling-shareholder net loss of KRW 2.61 billion was much larger than that quarter's operating loss of KRW 914 million, which points to a sizeable non-operating component.

The first-half 2026 figures of KRW 84 million... revenue of KRW 8.4 billion and operating loss of KRW 1.2 billion match the sum of the first- and second-quarter figures.

05

Industry analysis

Display driver packaging is a back-end process whose volumes move with OLED and LCD panel output and with customer design changes. The applications the company lists are concentrated in mid-to-large displays, so panel investment flows directly into orders.

The four consecutive quarterly revenue declines through the third quarter of 2026 may be tied to these shifts in customer volumes.

The press, however, cited the legal dispute with a DB group affiliate and the COF exit as reasons for lower revenue, and filings alone do not separate industry factors from company-specific ones.

Compared with large sector players such as Samsung Electronics and SK hynix, Welkeeps Hitech is much smaller, so orders from a few customers weigh heavily on its revenue.

Its structure of combining semiconductor packaging with power-supply and automotive parts gives it two business lines that can partly offset weakness in one. On the automotive side, the company links to demand for electric-vehicle parts.

It says it supplies core EV components to Hyundai and Kia and holds wireless-charging technology as a member of the Wireless Power Consortium. The press has described the growth outlook for EV battery components positively, but the company does not report that segment's revenue separately in its filed results.

Outlook

The company reported first-half 2026 revenue of KRW 8.4 billion and an operating loss of KRW 1.2 billion in its half-year report, with revenue down 24.5% from a year earlier and the operating loss wider.

In the third quarter of 2026 the operating loss narrowed to KRW 383 million, while revenue of KRW 3.68 billion was the lowest of the last five quarters. At the regular shareholder meeting on March 30, 2026, a par-value consolidation raising par from KRW 500 to KRW 1,000 was approved.

The 1-for-2 share consolidation took effect on May 11, 2026, with the new shares listed on May 22, 2026. The same meeting added business purposes, which provides a base for new business lines.

In a March 6, 2026 filing, the company decided to commit KRW 6 billion as a limited partner in a fund investing in companies undergoing financial restructuring, with acquisition scheduled for April 15, 2026.

The company said the purpose was to secure investment returns, and the fund's target size is about KRW 10.6 billion. After the 2024 acquisition of Jewon Tech, the company told the press it would advance its EV battery component business.

The press reported the dispute with the DB group affiliate at about KRW 10 billion, and its outcome will appear in the notes to the 2026 annual report.

Bull factors

Narrowing operating losses

The operating loss narrowed from KRW 9.96 billion in 2024 to KRW 2.1 billion in 2025, and the third-quarter 2026 loss of KRW 383 million was smaller than the KRW 580 million a year earlier. The operating margin improved from -33.4% in 2024 to -9.8% in 2025. Losses continue, but their size is shrinking.

Automotive and power-supply customer base

The company says it supplies core EV components to Hyundai and Kia and has wireless-charging modules in mass production for Hyundai Mobis. Its 2024 acquisition of Jewon Tech added EV battery component production capacity.

Unlike its semiconductor packaging business, the automotive and power-supply lines are tied to vehicle-maker supply relationships.

Two business lines run in parallel

Running semiconductor packaging alongside power-supply and automotive parts spreads the effect of a single customer change across the company. The March 2026 meeting added business purposes such as software development and e-commerce, creating the corporate basis for new business reviews. The revenue contribution of each line is not separately disclosed in the filed results.

Bear factors

Four consecutive quarters of falling revenue

Quarterly revenue fell from KRW 5.24 billion in the third quarter of 2025 to KRW 3.68 billion in the third quarter of 2026, and the latest quarter was down 29.7% year on year. First-half 2026 revenue was down 24.5% from a year earlier. Because the loss narrowing has not come with a revenue recovery, this is a weakness.

Net losses and negative operating cash flow

The controlling-shareholder net loss over the last four quarters totals KRW 4.06 billion, and the fourth-quarter 2025 net loss of KRW 2.61 billion exceeded that quarter's operating loss of KRW 914 million. Operating cash flow was negative in both 2024 at KRW -3.86 billion and 2025 at KRW -1.84 billion. Operating losses and cash outflows appearing together is a burden.

Litigation and customer concentration

The press reported that the lawsuit with a DB group affiliate is about KRW 10 billion and led to lower revenue. The company exited the COF business, and its semiconductor customers are concentrated among a few names. The lawsuit outcome and a recovery in customer orders are variables that could shape the results.

Risk factors

Customer concentration

Semiconductor packaging depends on orders from a small number of customers, including DB GlobalChip, LX Semicon and BOE. A drop in one customer's volume can reduce total revenue quickly. The four consecutive quarterly declines through 2026 may connect to such volume changes.

Financial and litigation burden

The debt ratio rose from 51.7% in 2024 to 61.9% in 2025, with total liabilities of KRW 21.6 billion. The lawsuit with the DB group affiliate was reported at about KRW 10 billion, and its outcome could change the financial burden. In March 2026 the company also decided to commit KRW 6 billion to a fund for financial restructuring.

Share liquidity and market volatility

Welkeeps Hitech was designated a caution-level stock on June 29, 2026 after a sharp one-day close move, and again on July 13, 2026 under a single-account net buying criterion. Trading was suspended from May 7 to May 21, 2026 for the share consolidation. With the largest shareholder holding 49.90%, the float is limited, which can amplify price swings.

On a filed basis, 2024 revenue of KRW 29.8 billion was the largest in the period, but the company swung to an operating loss of KRW 9.96 billion, and in 2025 revenue of KRW 21.4 billion and an operating loss of KRW 2.1 billion showed a smaller loss.
