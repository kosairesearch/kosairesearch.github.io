02

Business structure

Korea Computer is an electronics manufacturing services (EMS) company that produces and sells PBA (printed board assembly) products used in display modules, along with industrial inverters.

The sales mix compiled by Alphasquare shows OLED-PBA products at 69.10%, inverters at 16.50%, LCD module parts at 9.20%, servo drives at 4.60%, others at 0.40%, and PCBs and other items at 0.20%.

Based on the 2026 Q1 quarterly report, QD-OLED PBA products for TVs and monitors were the largest line at KRW 37.6 billion (45.2%), followed by OLED PBA products for smartphones and tablets at KRW 34.3 billion (41.3%) and industrial inverters at KRW 9.8 billion (11.8%).

The main customer is Samsung Display, which receives QD-OLED PBA supply, while OLED PBA products are sold to counterparties in Vietnam.

Smartphone OLED PBA is sold under a processing-contract structure, and in its explanation of 2025 results the company cited higher sales volume tied to increased orders as a driver of profit growth.

Production assets include SMT lines, assembly lines and various test equipment, and the company is described as holding mounting technologies such as BGA and flip chip along with RF application technology.

Overseas consolidated subsidiaries are KCI VINA CO., LTD in Vietnam (EMS) and Pacom International Inc. in the United States (trading), both 100% owned. Under this structure, a drop in customer volume feeds directly into revenue, and the 33% average utilization in 2026 Q1 fits the same pattern.

The inverter line draws on a customer base separate from OLED panel volumes, which helps diversify the sales mix. Shareholder structure is covered in the risks section.

04

Earnings analysis

On an annual basis, revenue fell from KRW 423.7 billion in 2022 to KRW 371.7 billion in 2023 (down 12.3% year on year), recovered to KRW 421 billion in 2024 (up 13.3%), and then reached KRW 417.8 billion in 2025 (down 0.8%). Among 2022–2025, 2022 was the year with the largest revenue and 2023 the smallest.

Operating profit moved from KRW 18.8 billion in 2022 to KRW 23.8 billion in 2023 (up 26.4%), KRW 12.2 billion in 2024 (down 48.9%), and KRW 22.9 billion in 2025 (up 88.0%). Among 2022–2025, 2023 was the year with the largest operating profit and 2024 the smallest.

The operating margin was 4.4% in 2022, 6.4% in 2023, 2.9% in 2024 and 5.5% in 2025. In 2025 revenue slipped while operating profit rose, and the company attributed the gain to higher sales volume from increased orders, even as processing-contract sales of smartphone OLED PBA products declined.

Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 16.8 billion in 2022, KRW 21.7 billion in 2023 (up 29.4%), KRW 13.6 billion in 2024 (down 37.3%) and KRW 20.5 billion in 2025 (up 51.2%).

The debt ratio rose to 80.6% at the end of 2025 from 23.3%, 22.1% and 21.0% in 2022 to 2024, with total liabilities of KRW 132.6 billion against total equity of KRW 164.5 billion. Total liabilities at the end of 2024 were reported at KRW 31.7 billion.

Operating cash flow was KRW 9.64 billion in 2022, then KRW 28 billion in 2023, KRW 24.4 billion in 2024 and KRW 25.6 billion in 2025, staying in a KRW 24.4–28 billion range since 2023.

On a quarterly basis, revenue went from KRW 127.8 billion in 2025 Q3 to KRW 119 billion in Q4 (down 6.9% quarter on quarter), KRW 83.1 billion in 2026 Q1 (down 30.2%), KRW 77.8 billion in Q2 (down 6.4%), and KRW 99.5 billion in Q3 (up 28.0% quarter on quarter, but down 22.1% year on year).

Operating profit peaked at KRW 10.3 billion in 2025 Q4 (up 16.1%) before falling to KRW 5.1 billion in 2026 Q1 (down 50.4%) and KRW 1.2 billion in Q2 (down 76.4%), then rising to KRW 4.96 billion in Q3 (up 311.8%).

Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders fell for three consecutive quarters after the 2025 Q4 peak of KRW 10.2 billion, to KRW 5.82 billion, KRW 1.81 billion and KRW 234 million in 2026 Q3, which was down 97.1% year on year.

Operating profit rose in Q3 while controlling-shareholder net profit fell 87.1% quarter on quarter, so the gap between the two is a point to check in the quarterly report's non-operating and tax lines.

Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders for the last four quarters (2025 Q4 to 2026 Q3) totaled KRW 18.1 billion.

Industry analysis

Industry reports compiled by WiseReport describe smartphone OLED demand as growing as panel adoption expands, and they expect notebooks and TVs to extend that growth to component suppliers.

Korea Computer sits at the PBA assembly stage of this chain, so its results depend on panel shipments and how customers allocate volume. A separate aggregate puts first-half 2026 consolidated revenue down 5.9% year on year and operating profit up 69.4%, and attributes the revenue decline to changes in customer volume.

In an Alphasquare comparison of display and component companies on a 2025 annual basis, Korea Computer's operating margin was positive while the sector average was in negative territory, and its operating profit ranked 14th in the sector.

Its dividend yield ranked second in the same group, well above the sector average. That comparison covers 2025 only and the sector average is shaped by large display makers.

On the cycle, panel demand from smartphones and TVs is described as still expanding, yet the company's quarterly revenue has moved up and down through 2026. With high customer concentration, the timing of orders and volume allocation from a single customer can move short-term revenue more than end-market demand does.

Outlook

The company's next periodic report is the 2026 annual report, which will be the first disclosure of full-year 2026 and Q4 2026 results. Under the general rule, annual reports are due within 90 days of the fiscal year end, which points to late March 2027.

In 2026 Q3 operating profit rose from the prior quarter, but revenue fell year on year and net profit attributable to controlling shareholders dropped to KRW 234 million. The next checkpoints are therefore whether the operating margin holds near the 2026 Q3 level and whether revenue recovers on higher customer volume.

Average utilization across the domestic and Vietnamese plants was 33% in 2026 Q1, so whether utilization rises is the key measure for absorbing fixed costs. Quarterly interest expense has been reported as rising since 2025 Q3, so the drag from financing costs on net profit also needs to be tracked.

The composition of KRW 132.6 billion in total liabilities, including the share of short-term borrowings, can be checked in the debt schedule of the annual report. Whether smartphone OLED adoption and TV QD-OLED demand translate into revenue will show up in the product mix in each quarterly report.

Industrial inverters were KRW 9.8 billion (11.8%) of 2026 Q1 revenue, a small share, but whether they cushion revenue during downturns is worth watching. Dividend continuity will be confirmed after the annual report and the shareholder meeting resolution.

Bull factors

Operating profit recovery

Operating profit reached KRW 22.9 billion in 2025, up 88.0% from the prior year, and the operating margin rose from 2.9% in 2024 to 5.5% in 2025. The company attributed the gain to higher sales volume from increased orders. Operating profit in 2026 Q3 was KRW 4.96 billion, up 311.8% from the prior quarter. Bulls cite the rebound in quarterly profit from a low base as supporting evidence.

Expanding OLED and TV component demand

WiseReport's company monitor says smartphone OLED demand is growing as panel adoption widens and expects component suppliers to benefit as notebooks and TVs follow. QD-OLED PBA products for TVs and monitors made up 45.2% of 2026 Q1 revenue. Bulls point out that TV demand therefore connects directly to the company's revenue mix.

Low debt ratio and cash generation through 2024

The debt ratio was 23.3% in 2022, 22.1% in 2023 and 21.0% in 2024, a low level. Operating cash flow was KRW 28 billion in 2023, KRW 24.4 billion in 2024 and KRW 25.6 billion in 2025, staying in a KRW 24.4–28 billion range since 2023. Total equity at the end of 2025 was KRW 164.5 billion. Bulls cite this cash generation as room to manage the debt burden.

Bear factors

Continued revenue decline

Revenue for 2026 Q3 was KRW 99.5 billion, down 22.1% year on year. Revenue of KRW 83.1 billion in 2026 Q1 fell 30.2% from the prior quarter, and Q2 declined further to KRW 77.8 billion. WiseReport attributes the revenue decline to changes in customer volume. Compared with KRW 127.8 billion in 2025 Q3, quarterly revenue has clearly shrunk.

Three straight quarters of lower controlling-shareholder profit

Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders fell from KRW 10.2 billion in 2025 Q4 to KRW 5.82 billion in 2026 Q1, KRW 1.81 billion in Q2 and KRW 234 million in Q3. The Q3 figure was down 97.1% year on year. Operating profit rose in Q3 but did not carry through to net profit. The non-operating items and tax burden in the next quarterly report are the points to confirm.

Customer concentration and low utilization

The main customer is Samsung Display, so changes in its volumes flow directly into the company's revenue. Average utilization across the domestic and Vietnamese plants was 33% in 2026 Q1. With utilization this low, fixed costs of equipment and labor can amplify earnings swings. Bears argue that heavy reliance on one customer could delay any profit rebound if order recovery stalls.

Risk factors

Customer concentration risk

The revenue base centers on Samsung Display, and smartphone OLED PBA products are sold under a processing-contract structure. As a result, the timing of orders and volume allocation from one customer can move quarterly results sharply.

The 30.2% quarter-on-quarter revenue drop in 2026 Q1 shows how quickly volume changes reach the top line. Customer diversification can be checked in the customer revenue breakdown of the annual report.

Higher financial leverage

Total liabilities at the end of 2025 were KRW 132.6 billion against total equity of KRW 164.5 billion, a debt ratio of 80.6%. Total liabilities at the end of 2024 were reported at KRW 31.7 billion, so the balance grew sharply within one year. Google Finance data show quarterly interest expense rising since 2025 Q3. Higher interest costs make it harder for operating profit gains to reach net profit.

Shareholder structure and liquidity

As of September 17, 2026, the largest shareholder, Hanguk Holdings, held 42.11% and treasury shares accounted for 17.88%. The free float ratio is 40.01%. Foreign ownership is at a low level. With limited tradable shares, thin trading can make share price moves larger than earnings alone would suggest.

Overall view

Korea Computer's operating profit recovered to KRW 22.9 billion in 2025, up 88.0% from the prior year, but revenue and controlling-shareholder net profit have fluctuated quarter to quarter in 2026.

Revenue for 2026 Q3 was KRW 99.5 billion, down 22.1% year on year, and operating profit of KRW 4.96 billion rose from the prior quarter while controlling-shareholder net profit was only KRW 234 million.

Strengths include the operating profit recovery, growing demand for OLED and TV components, and the low debt ratio of 2022 to 2024 as financial cushion.

Weaknesses include concentration on Samsung Display, utilization of 33%, the rise in the debt ratio to 80.6% at the end of 2025, and a four-quarter controlling-shareholder net profit total of KRW 18.1 billion.

On valuation, the multiples sit below the five-year average, which partly reflects lower earnings in the denominator. The dividend yield ranks near the top of its sector, but whether the dividend continues will be known after the annual report.

The next checkpoint is the 2026 annual report, due by late March 2027, followed by the 2027 Q1 quarterly report.
