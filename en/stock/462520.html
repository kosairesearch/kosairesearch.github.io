02

Business structure

Chosun Refractories makes refractories, heat-resistant materials that shield furnaces and other equipment exposed to temperatures above 1,500 degrees Celsius. Its products are split into acidic and basic materials and subdivided by process, and they directly affect steelmakers' product quality and costs.

Reporting says about 90% of sales come from maintenance work, so results lean on replacement and repair demand at operating plants more than on new capacity. The company positions blast-furnace operating experience, customized service and a full product lineup as its three pillars of competitiveness.

Key export markets include Brazil, Turkey, Indonesia and India. Its main customers are steelmakers at home and abroad; working with POSCO, it built a system to collect and recycle spent refractories, reaching a recycling rate of about 25%.

Its headquarters is in Gwangyang, Jeollanam-do, and it also operates a plant in Pohang. As hydrogen-based steelmaking spreads, the performance required from refractories changes, and the company is preparing a next-generation refractory portfolio, involving itself from the design stage.

It competes with Chinese products, and the company says US tariffs on China weaken Chinese competitiveness and widen opportunities for Korean suppliers.

04

Earnings analysis

On a reported basis, annual revenue was KRW 279.4 billion in 2023, KRW 500.6 billion in 2024 (up 79.2% year on year) and KRW 532 billion in 2025 (up 6.3%), rising for two consecutive years from 2024 to 2025.

Operating profit fell from KRW 31.9 billion in 2023 to KRW 20 billion in 2024 (down 37.1%) and recovered to KRW 23.9 billion in 2025 (up 19.2%).

Operating margin was 11.4% in 2023, 4.0% in 2024 and 4.5% in 2025; in 2024 revenue rose 79.2% while operating profit fell 37.1%, so the sales expansion did not translate into earnings.

Attributable net income fell from KRW 23.6 billion in 2023 to KRW 5.83 billion in 2024 (down 75.3%) and KRW 2.22 billion in 2025 (down 61.8%), declining for two consecutive years from 2024 to 2025.

In 2025 operating profit rose while attributable net income fell, so items below operating profit, such as non-operating gains and losses and income tax, need to be read alongside the operating line.

Net income including non-controlling interests was KRW 3.1 billion in 2025, a different figure from attributable net income. Operating cash flow was KRW 3.08 billion in 2023, KRW 52.7 billion in 2024 and KRW 4.02 billion in 2025, a sharp drop that shows weak conversion of profit into cash.

The debt ratio was 98.4% in 2023, 138.4% in 2024 and 116.3% in 2025; at year-end 2025 total liabilities were KRW 269 billion and total equity KRW 231.3 billion, of which KRW 221.4 billion was attributable to controlling shareholders.

Quarterly operating profit went from KRW 10.2 billion in Q3 2025 to KRW 2.28 billion in Q4 (down 77.6% quarter on quarter), KRW 8.96 billion in Q1 2026 (up 293.5%), KRW 1.01 billion in Q2 (down 88.8%) and KRW 2.48 billion in Q3 2026 (up 146.5% quarter on quarter, down 75.7% year on year).

Attributable net income swung between profit and loss over the same span: a loss of KRW 1.28 billion in Q3 2025, profit of KRW 964 million in Q4, profit of KRW 7.32 billion in Q1 2026, a loss of KRW 4.81 billion in Q2 and profit of KRW 79.5 million in Q3 2026.

Attributable net income for the last four quarters totals KRW 3.56 billion, and Q3 2026 revenue of KRW 127.4 billion was down 6.0% year on year.

05

Industry analysis

Demand for refractories in Korea moves with steel output and furnace utilization, and the maintenance schedules of major equipment such as blast furnaces and converters shape sales flow.

Because the company's sales lean on maintenance work, its results are more sensitive to utilization and repair timing at existing plants than to new steel mill construction.

Steel quality is directly tied to refractory performance and cost, so customers tend to put quality and process fit ahead of price, according to the company.

Hydrogen-based steelmaking is cited as a variable that could change the conditions for refractories used with conventional blast furnaces, and the company says it is developing next-generation refractories to prepare for that shift.

A view reported in 2026 holds that US tariffs on Chinese goods weaken Chinese refractory competitiveness and widen order opportunities for Korean suppliers.

Recycling spent refractories is positioned as a way to secure raw materials and cut disposal costs at the same time, and the recycling system with POSCO is presented as one element of cost competitiveness.

However, if price competition with Chinese products and steel-sector swings act together, the size of any benefit will vary by product and market. Against other domestic and foreign refractory makers, the company stresses its blast-furnace operating experience and customized service as points of differentiation.

Outlook

The company's medium- to long-term direction centers on high-value-added products, eco-friendly materials, global market expansion and smart manufacturing. Its CEO has said the company will prioritize internal strength over outward growth, aiming to become a small but strong company.

The strategy is organized into six items, including securing core technology, a high-profit business structure and pursuing global niche markets.

In an interview reported around April 2026, the company said it was introducing an AI-based predictive maintenance system alongside replacement of aging equipment, using IoT sensors to detect equipment anomalies in advance, with plans to expand company-wide after pilot deployment.

The next-generation refractory portfolio was presented as a response to hydrogen-based steelmaking.

Quarterly revenue ran at KRW 131.9 billion in Q1 2026, KRW 126.2 billion in Q2 and KRW 127.4 billion in Q3, staying in the mid-KRW 120 billion range, while operating profit swung widely, so the question is how much the strategy shows up in quarterly results.

The next checkpoint is full-year 2026 operating margin and operating cash flow in the annual report, to be compared with 4.5% and KRW 4.02 billion for 2025.

Timing of commercialization of next-generation refractories for hydrogen-based steelmaking, and whether AI equipment management expands company-wide, are also variables for mid-term profitability.

Risk factors

Steel cycle and plant utilization

Sales are tied to utilization and repair schedules at steel plants, so a fall in steel output or a delay in customers' maintenance timing hits revenue directly. Q3 2026 revenue was down 6.0% year on year, consistent with this pattern. Investment cycles at steelmakers at home and abroad are the variable that determines order volume.

Trade environment and export market swings

US tariffs on China create an opening by weakening Chinese competition, but a change in the trade environment could affect demand and prices in export markets. Key export markets such as Brazil, Turkey, Indonesia and India are spread out, yet each market's economic swings still matter.

If the pace of hydrogen-based steelmaking differs from expectations, demand for existing products and the timing of next-generation investment could fall out of step.

Balance sheet and cash generation

The debt ratio stayed high, at 98.4% in 2023, 138.4% in 2024 and 116.3% in 2025. With operating cash flow of only KRW 4.02 billion in 2025, the capacity to fund borrowing and equipment investment at the same time may be limited.

Because quarterly operating profit swung widely in 2026, whether cash generation stays steady is the open issue.

Overall view

Chosun Refractories is a refractory maker whose sales depend on steel-plant maintenance demand, and revenue of KRW 532 billion in 2025 marked two consecutive years of growth from 2024.

However, operating margin fell from 11.4% in 2023 to 4.5% in 2025, and attributable net income declined from KRW 23.6 billion in 2023 to KRW 2.22 billion in 2025.

Q1 2026 showed a recovery, with operating profit of KRW 8.96 billion and attributable net income of KRW 7.32 billion, but a Q2 attributable net loss of KRW 4.81 billion and a 75.7% year-on-year drop in Q3 operating profit mean the durability of that recovery is still to be confirmed.
