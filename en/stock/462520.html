02

Business structure

Chosun Refractories, founded in 1947, is a refractory materials manufacturer that was spun off from its former parent (now CR Holdings) as part of a holding-company restructuring and was relisted on the KOSPI on July 28, 2023 as a new entity.

Its core business is refractories used in iron and steel making, with POSCO as its largest customer, supplying products for hot-metal runner linings, steelmaking and chemical processes, ironmaking and construction, and plate mill applications at the Pohang and Gwangyang steelworks.

As of 2023, refractory products accounted for 82.8% of total sales, with shaped refractories (26.6% of revenue) and unshaped refractories (54%) used in industrial furnace construction as the main revenue sources.

Supply contracts with POSCO are typically renewed on a semiannual or quarterly basis; in September 2025 the company signed a contract worth about KRW 74.7 billion (14.91% of 2024 revenue, covering October 2025 to September 2026), and in October 2026 it signed two contracts for the Pohang and Gwangyang plants worth approximately KRW 35.1 billion and KRW 27.9 billion respectively, together equal to 11.83% of 2025 revenue and covering October 2026 to March 2027.

Beyond refractories, the company has diversified into machine parts manufacturing, energy supply, and golf course operations, and it is also cooperating with POSCO DX, which handles robotics deployment for the POSCO group, on applying robots to ironmaking, steelmaking, and casting processes.

In terms of ownership, holding company CR Holdings is the largest shareholder with a 59.03% stake, and combined with related party Lee In-ok and other affiliated holders, total related-party ownership exceeds 80%, giving the company a stable governance structure.

Because a large share of revenue is concentrated with a single customer, the business is tied more closely to POSCO's capital expenditure and maintenance schedule than to customer diversification.

04

Earnings analysis

Chosun Refractories' consolidated revenue rose from KRW 279.4 billion in 2023 to KRW 500.6 billion in 2024, up 79.2%, and increased a further 6.3% to KRW 532 billion in 2025, marking two consecutive years of growth from 2024 through 2025.

Over the same period, operating profit fell 37.1% from KRW 31.9 billion in 2023 to KRW 20 billion in 2024, then rebounded 19.2% to KRW 23.9 billion in 2025.

The operating margin fell from 11.4% in 2023 to 4.0% in 2024 and only partially recovered to 4.5% in 2025, meaning revenue growth did not fully translate into profitability.

Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders fell 75.3% from KRW 23.6 billion in 2023 to KRW 5.83 billion in 2024, then fell a further 61.8% to KRW 2.22 billion in 2025, a two-year consecutive decline from 2024 through 2025.

Net income including non-controlling interests followed the same downward path, from KRW 24.1 billion in 2023 to KRW 6.64 billion in 2024 and KRW 3.1 billion in 2025, showing that even as operating profit rebounded in 2025, the bottom line did not improve in tandem.

Quarterly results have been notably volatile: operating profit rose 82.3% quarter on quarter to KRW 10.2 billion in the third quarter of 2025, then fell 77.6% to KRW 2.28 billion in the fourth quarter, surged 293.5% to KRW 8.96 billion in the first quarter of 2026, and then dropped 88.8% quarter on quarter (down 82.0% year on year) to KRW 1.01 billion in the second quarter of 2026.

Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders also swung repeatedly, from a net loss of KRW 1.28 billion in the third quarter of 2025 to a return to profit of KRW 964 million in the fourth quarter, a sharp jump to KRW 7.32 billion in the first quarter of 2026, and back to a net loss of KRW 4.81 billion in the second quarter of 2026.

Over the last four quarters (Q3 2025 through Q2 2026), net profit attributable to controlling shareholders totaled only KRW 2.2 billion, roughly matching the full-year 2025 figure of KRW 2.22 billion, while operating cash flow also fluctuated widely, from KRW 3.08 billion in 2023 to KRW 52.7 billion in 2024 and back down to KRW 4.02 billion in 2025.

05

Industry analysis

The refractory materials industry that Chosun Refractories belongs to is a classic downstream-dependent business, supplying essential materials to capital-intensive industries such as steel, cement, and glass that rely on high-temperature processes.

Because most of the company's revenue is tied to the operating and maintenance schedules of POSCO's Pohang and Gwangyang steelworks, Korea's domestic steel cycle and blast furnace and converter maintenance cycles are key variables for its results.

Refractories function somewhat like consumable lining components for blast furnaces and converters, so demand tends to recur as long as steelmakers maintain production.

However, because supply contracts are signed and renewed on a semiannual or quarterly basis, revenue recognition can appear uneven from quarter to quarter depending on contract timing and construction progress.

Within the same KOSPI construction materials sector, Chosun Refractories differs from cement and glass materials producers in having an unusually high dependence on a single customer, exposing it more to customer-specific risk than the sector average.

The expansion of cooperation with the POSCO group into robotics and process automation suggests a potential business relationship beyond the traditional supply arrangement.

Outlook

The two supply contracts signed with POSCO in October 2026 confirm volumes for the period from October 2026 to March 2027, equal to 6.60% and 5.24% of 2025 revenue respectively, or 11.83% combined.

This follows a renewal pattern seen in a contract signed in September 2025 worth about KRW 74.7 billion (14.91% of 2024 revenue, covering October 2025 to September 2026), indicating that the POSCO supply relationship continues to be renewed on an annual cadence.

However, both of the 2026 contracts disclose that the contract amount may increase or decrease depending on the client's circumstances, so it is premature to treat them as confirmed revenue.

Beyond refractory manufacturing, the company continues to pursue cooperation with POSCO DX on applying robotics to ironmaking, steelmaking, and casting processes, which could extend its business relationship with the POSCO group.

Given the wide swings in quarterly results through the second quarter of 2026, a key point to watch is whether future quarters return toward the level seen in the first quarter of 2026 (operating profit of KRW 8.96 billion) or continue the slowdown seen in the second quarter (KRW 1.01 billion).

Quarterly results from the third quarter of 2026 onward have not yet been disclosed in a regular report, so the direction of second-half results will need to be confirmed through future filings.

Risk factors

Customer Concentration

Because most revenue comes from a single customer, POSCO, any change in POSCO's operating rate or capital expenditure and maintenance schedule can directly affect results. Supply contract amounts are also disclosed as subject to change depending on the client's circumstances, leaving revenue exposed to variability. Limited customer diversification is a factor that bears monitoring over the medium to long term.

Earnings Quality

Even though operating profit rebounded in 2025, both net profit attributable to controlling shareholders and total net income declined for a second straight year, revealing a gap between operating performance and the bottom line.

Profit and loss also alternated quarter to quarter, making it hard to infer an annual trend from any single quarter. Operating cash flow likewise fluctuated widely, from KRW 3.08 billion in 2023 to KRW 52.7 billion in 2024 and back to KRW 4.02 billion in 2025, warranting a check on the consistency of cash generation.

Contract Term Uncertainty

Supply contracts signed with POSCO state that contract amounts may increase or decrease depending on the client's circumstances, so disclosed contract values cannot be assumed to translate directly into actual revenue.

Contract terms are also split into relatively short periods, ranging from six months to about a year, requiring renewal status to be checked each time. The potential revenue impact if a contract is not renewed or is reduced in scope warrants continued monitoring.

Overall view

Chosun Refractories is a company whose core business is supplying refractories to POSCO's Pohang and Gwangyang steelworks; revenue grew for two consecutive years from 2024 through 2025, and operating profit also rebounded in 2025.

However, net profit attributable to controlling shareholders declined for two straight years, from KRW 23.6 billion in 2023 to KRW 2.22 billion in 2025, and total net income including non-controlling interests moved in the same direction, revealing a clear gap between operating performance and the bottom line.

Through 2026, quarterly results have alternated between profit and loss, with operating profit plunging from KRW 8.96 billion in the first quarter to KRW 1.01 billion in the second quarter and net profit swinging to a loss, reducing predictability.

Supply contracts with POSCO were renewed again in October 2026, suggesting the core customer relationship remains intact, but the contracts repeatedly carry the caveat that amounts may change depending on the client's circumstances.

On the valuation side, a notable feature is that valuation metrics have swung widely from quarter to quarter as net profit alternated between gains and losses.

The next quarterly report, the year-end disclosure around late March 2027, and whether POSCO contracts are renewed thereafter will be key points to watch for gauging future performance and assessment.
