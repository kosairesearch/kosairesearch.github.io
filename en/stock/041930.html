02

Business structure

SY Dong-A was founded in 1974 as a maker of precision rubber and plastic parts for automobiles and home appliances. Although it is classified under the chemicals sector on KOSDAQ, its operations are essentially component mass production. The business runs on two pillars, automotive and appliances.

In 2025, appliances accounted for 50.61% of sales and automotive for 49.39%, while in Q1 2026 automotive rose to 52.57% and appliances fell to 47.43%.

The automotive unit supplies air intake hoses, intercooler pipes and EV battery pack gaskets to Hyundai Motor and KIA, and the appliance unit supplies door gaskets to LG Electronics and Samsung Electronics.

Overseas, the company operates manufacturing and sales subsidiaries in seven countries: the United States, China, India, Japan, Vietnam, Mexico and Russia.

Its fuel-cell subsidiary, in which the company holds a 72.5% stake, has commercialized building-use fuel cell systems, yet it recorded zero revenue in Q1 2026 and negative equity of about KRW 7.2 billion.

Robot components and defense parts are the new businesses, and the plan is to share part of the automotive production lines with robot parts to limit initial investment.

For defense, the company is preparing for the Defense Quality Management System certification and completing supplier registration with defense contractors. Analysts cite more than 50 years of high-volume production experience and quality audit capability as its main strengths.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual revenue rose from KRW 349.6 billion in 2022 to KRW 368.7 billion in 2023 (up 5.5%), KRW 383.2 billion in 2024 (up 3.9%) and KRW 403.4 billion in 2025 (up 5.3%), a rise for three straight years since 2023. 2025 was the largest revenue year in the 2022 to 2025 span, and one press report described 2025 consolidated sales as a record high.

Operating profit peaked at KRW 21.6 billion in 2023 (up 49.8%), then fell 15.0% to KRW 18.4 billion in 2024 and 6.6% to KRW 17.2 billion in 2025, a two-year decline. The operating margin slid from 5.9% in 2023 to 4.3% in 2025, so higher sales did not carry through to profitability.

Cost pressure from raw materials and exchange rates appears to explain the gap, and the company cited the same factors when describing the first half of 2026.

Net income attributable to owners was KRW 14.3 billion in 2023 (down 7.0%), KRW 14.6 billion in 2024 and KRW 27.5 billion in 2025 (up 88.9%), a two-year rise since 2023.

In 2025 that profit grew sharply even as operating profit fell, suggesting that a large share came from non-operating items, and net income including non-controlling interests was KRW 27.3 billion, a different measure.

At the end of 2025, total equity was KRW 188 billion and total liabilities were KRW 119.7 billion, and the debt ratio fell from 94.9% in 2022 to 63.7%. Quarterly revenue fell from KRW 101.7 billion in Q3 2025 to KRW 94.9 billion in Q1 2026, then recovered to KRW 98.7 billion in Q2 and KRW 102 billion in Q3 2026.

Quarterly operating profit was lowest at KRW 141 million in Q4 2025 (down 97.5% from the prior quarter), then moved to KRW 4.45 billion in Q1 2026, KRW 2.19 billion in Q2 and KRW 3.03 billion in Q3.

In Q2 2026, lower sales, a one-off charge from contingent liabilities, a weaker won and raw material costs combined to turn the result into a net loss of KRW 596 million attributable to owners, and Q3 widened that loss to KRW 5.3 billion.

05

Industry analysis

SY Dong-A's main customers are automakers and appliance manufacturers, so production volumes and global demand in those two markets drive its results. In the first half of 2026 the company said tariffs and Middle East geopolitical risk partly reduced demand.

Securities analysts also assessed that slower global demand in both automotive and appliances weighed on sales and profitability. On costs, a weaker won and higher oil prices are adding pressure at the same time.

A customer-side factor also mattered: a strike at Hyundai Motor, a major customer, delayed second-quarter shipments, and production resumed after the strike ended.

The company said that over the past three years the highest sales have typically come in July through September, which shows how shipment timing can shift results from quarter to quarter.

The robotics market is at an early stage: Hyundai Motor Group plans to begin volume production of humanoid robots from 2028, and LG Electronics is running trials of its own humanoid, which an independent research firm expects to lift demand for precision drive and cushioning rubber parts.

For defense, the company's argument rests on the view that the severe-condition design skills it uses for car parts are close to military standard requirements. Competitively, new customer wins require passing production qualification, and unit prices are being negotiated with a link to raw material costs.

06

Outlook

The company expects its core business to return to recovery in the second half of 2026. It explains that volume delayed by production disruptions at key customers in the first half will be reflected in second-half results. The end of Hyundai Motor's strike and the resumption of production also support those shipments.

On efficiency, the company is cutting process time and lead times with manufacturing automation and plans to apply robotics and IoT across all processes in a dark factory.

On pricing, it is negotiating unit price increases with major customers, and it is discussing with LG Electronics a mechanism that links unit prices to raw material costs.

As a medium-term target, the company has set KRW 600 billion in revenue for 2030, and it is also pursuing expansion and automation of its U.S. production base.

In a May 2026 report, an independent research firm estimated 2026 revenue of KRW 423.4 billion and operating profit of KRW 17.5 billion, and 2027 revenue of KRW 444.8 billion and operating profit of KRW 28.8 billion, but the first-half 2026 reported results started below that path.

In defense, DQMS certification and supplier registration remain the next steps. The next checkpoints are whether the second-half recovery shows up in numbers in the 2026 annual report, and whether the delayed volume is recognized as actual shipment revenue.

08

Bull factors

Robotics parts tied to large customers

In a May 2026 report, an independent research firm said SY Dong-A's entry into robot parts is set to accelerate, assigning a positive view.

Its basis is the planned volume production of Hyundai Motor's Atlas humanoid and LG Electronics' humanoid robot, with Hyundai Motor reportedly set to begin full-scale Atlas production from 2028.

The company is pursuing joint development with domestic and overseas robot OEMs and says it will share automotive lines with robot parts to limit upfront investment.

Second-half recovery and price negotiations

Q3 2026 operating profit of KRW 3.03 billion rose 38.5% from the prior quarter, and the company says delayed first-half shipments will be reflected in the second half.

It is negotiating with major customers to link unit prices to raw material costs, and it is raising production efficiency through manufacturing automation. If these two factors work together, the operating margin could have room to improve beyond the 4.3% recorded in 2025.

Balance sheet repair and overseas production

The debt ratio fell from 94.9% in 2022 to 63.7% in 2025, and total liabilities at the end of 2025 were KRW 119.7 billion. The company has subsidiaries in seven countries, including the United States, China and India, and emphasizes local manufacturing competitiveness.

It has set a 2030 revenue target of KRW 600 billion and is pursuing expansion and automation of its U.S. production base.

Bear factors

Quarterly losses emerged and widened

Net income attributable to owners turned to a loss of KRW 596 million in Q2 2026, and the loss widened to KRW 5.3 billion in Q3. Q3 operating profit of KRW 3.03 billion was higher than the prior quarter but 46.4% lower than a year earlier. The sum of net income attributable to owners over the last four quarters is only KRW 3.21 billion.

Financing costs and currency swings

Press coverage of Q1 2026 reported that financing costs rose to about KRW 6.7 billion, pushing the financial profit and loss line into a deficit. The company cited cost pressure from a weaker won and raw material pressure from higher oil prices as drags on results. Financial and currency swings can move net income regardless of core sales.

A revenue gap in the new businesses

The fuel-cell subsidiary recorded zero revenue in Q1 2026 and negative equity of about KRW 7.2 billion. Robotics and defense remain at prototype and certification stages, and no disclosed figure yet shows their revenue contribution. Until the new businesses appear in results, the existing business has to fill that gap.

10

Risk factors

Customer concentration and demand swings

Sales are tied to a few automakers and appliance makers, including Hyundai Motor, KIA, LG Electronics and Samsung Electronics, so a strike or volume adjustment at one customer can move quarterly results sharply. Shipments slipped in Q2 2026 because of a customer strike.

Tariffs and Middle East geopolitical risk, which partly reduced demand, belong to the same category of exposure.

Currency, raw materials and financing costs

A weaker won raises cost burdens, and higher oil prices add pressure on raw material costs. When foreign currency translation losses and financial asset valuation losses overlap, as in Q1 2026, net income can fall sharply even if the core business earns a profit. The key question is how much of raw material volatility the planned price-linkage arrangements can absorb.

New business delivery and ownership change

Robotics and defense have no disclosed revenue yet, and their timing depends on customers' volume production plans. The fuel-cell subsidiary continues to show zero revenue and negative equity.

The company said that clearing overseas subsidiary receivables during the December 2025 change in largest shareholder reduced reported profit, so whether further one-off items emerge is the next thing to check.

12

Overall view

SY Dong-A's revenue has risen for three straight years since 2023, but operating profit has declined for two straight years since 2024, so top-line growth has not carried through to earnings. In 2026, a weak first half, a swing to a net loss attributable to owners in Q2 and a wider loss in Q3 have overlapped.

However, Q3 operating profit rose from the prior quarter, and the company says delayed first-half shipments and unit price negotiations will be reflected in second-half results.

Positive factors include the robotics parts venture, defense certification preparation and expansion of the U.S. production base, while negative factors include customer concentration, currency, raw material and financing costs, and the zero revenue and negative equity at the fuel-cell subsidiary.

In May 2026, an independent research firm viewed the robotics entry positively, but the Q2 and Q3 results reported since then have diverged from that forecast path.

On valuation, the sum of earnings over the last four quarters is small, so earnings-based multiples are sensitive to results, and the share price sits low relative to net assets.

The next checkpoints are whether the second-half recovery appears in numbers in the annual report due in late March 2027, and whether the robotics and defense businesses begin to show up as revenue.
