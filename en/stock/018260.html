02

Business structure

Samsung SDS runs two segments, IT services and logistics. IT services revenue in the preliminary 3Q 2026 disclosure was KRW 1.68 trillion, up 5.3% year on year, and cloud revenue within it reached KRW 796.1 billion, up 18%. Cloud splits into Samsung Cloud Platform (SCP) and the cloud management service (MSP).

SCP grew 15% as GPU-as-a-service supply to public-sector and software customers increased. MSP rose 25%, helped by AX work in financial services, ERP projects in shipbuilding and the adoption of global AI services.

The logistics segment handles air and sea freight and posted KRW 2.09 trillion of revenue in the same quarter. Because logistics accounts for more than half of total sales, the company's top line is strongly tied to freight rates and volumes.

To strengthen its logistics business, the company acquired 100% of Woojung Air, an air cargo consolidator. On the customer side, AX and ERP projects in financial services and shipbuilding, along with public-sector GPU demand, support IT services sales.

In IT services the company competes with large domestic SI firms such as LG CNS and Hyundai Autoever, and in cloud it also competes with global cloud providers.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual revenue fell from KRW 17.2 trillion in 2022 to KRW 13.3 trillion in 2023 (down 23.0% year on year), the smallest year in the period, and then rose for two straight years to KRW 13.8 trillion in 2024 (up 4.2%) and KRW 13.9 trillion in 2025 (up 0.7%). 2022 was the largest year for revenue.

Operating profit bottomed at KRW 808.2 billion in 2023 (down 11.8%) and then rose for two straight years to KRW 911.1 billion in 2024 (up 12.7%) and KRW 957.1 billion in 2025 (up 5.0%), with 2025 the largest year in the period.

The operating margin climbed from 5.3% in 2022 to 6.1% in 2023, 6.6% in 2024 and 6.9% in 2025, so it improved even in 2023 when sales declined.

Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders fell from KRW 1.1 trillion in 2022 to KRW 693.4 billion in 2023 (down 36.9%), the smallest year, and then rose for two straight years to KRW 757 billion in 2024 and KRW 759.5 billion in 2025.

Total net income including non-controlling interests was KRW 782.7 billion in 2025, slightly below KRW 789.5 billion in 2024, while attributable profit still edged up.

At the end of 2025 total equity was KRW 10.3 trillion and total liabilities were KRW 3.19 trillion, and the debt ratio declined from 41.2% in 2022 to 31.1% in 2025.

Operating cash flow stayed above KRW 1 trillion each year, at KRW 1.29 trillion in 2022, KRW 1.46 trillion in 2023, KRW 1.24 trillion in 2024 and KRW 1.2 trillion in 2025.

On a quarterly basis, revenue rose from KRW 3.39 trillion in 3Q 2025 to KRW 3.54 trillion in 4Q 2025 (up 4.3% quarter on quarter), and the low point was KRW 3.35 trillion in 1Q 2026 (down 5.2%). Operating profit fell to KRW 78.3 billion in 1Q 2026 (down 65.4% quarter on quarter).

Kyobo Securities attributed the quarter to lower revenue, higher investment to expand external sales, and a one-off retirement cost of KRW 112 billion recognized after a change in the severance calculation basis.

Operating profit recovered to KRW 231.8 billion in 2Q 2026 (up 195.9%) and KRW 241 billion in 3Q 2026 (up 4.0%), the second straight quarterly increase. The 3Q 2026 operating margin was about 6.4%, roughly 0.5 percentage points below about 6.9% a year earlier.

Attributable net profit of KRW 176.5 billion in 3Q 2026 fell 9.6% year on year, so the operating profit recovery and the net profit trend diverged. Attributable net profit over the last four quarters totaled KRW 630.2 billion.

05

Industry analysis

Domestic IT services demand relies heavily on corporate AI transformation (AX). The MSP business grew on AX work in financial services and ERP expansion in shipbuilding. Hana Securities argued in January 2026 that cloud growth is inevitable as corporate demand for AI agents increases.

Public-sector and software customers' demand for GPU capacity is another driver of SCP growth. On when data-center revenue becomes material, one analysis placed the full recognition of GPUaaS revenue in 2029, when the Gumi data center begins operations.

On the group side, the analysis holds that data center demand from Samsung affiliates and the chance to win external customers could form the base for expansion. Logistics behaves differently: higher air and sea cargo volumes and freight rates contributed to revenue growth.

That makes logistics revenue a cyclical business tied to trade flows and freight rates. Investor interest in domestic SI stocks has risen, yet Hana Securities noted in its January 2026 report that Samsung SDS's share price return has been relatively weak.

That indicates sector expectations did not translate directly into share price returns. In IT services the company is compared with large domestic SI players such as LG CNS and Hyundai Autoever. The key variables for the sector are how fast AX demand converts into revenue and when the data centers come online.

06

Outlook

The company's medium-term plan centers on a KRW 10 trillion investment roadmap. The plan covers expanding public and external customers alongside investment in global IT services businesses.

In an October 2026 report, Kyobo Securities expected IT services revenue, led by cloud, to enter a double-digit growth phase in 4Q 2026 and 2027.

For the medium term, the Gumi AIDC (60MW, targeted for completion in 2028) and the DBO business, which targets cumulative orders of 500MW by 2031, are the key items, and the same report estimated annual revenue of about KRW 1 trillion at the 500MW scale.

In a November 2026 report, Hana Securities raised its target price to KRW 290,000 and said it added a premium reflecting faster domestic AX adoption from 2027 and the possibility of securing additional AI data center capacity.

A factor to track is whether the one-off retirement cost in 1Q 2026 recurs, which can only be confirmed in later quarters. Whether the Gumi data center meets its 2028 completion target also determines when revenue recognition begins.

The next checkpoints are whether cloud revenue sustains double-digit growth in the preliminary 4Q 2026 results and the Gumi data center schedule.

08

Bull factors

Cloud and AX demand growth

Cloud revenue rose 18% to KRW 796.1 billion on expanding AX work, and MSP grew 25%. Kyobo Securities expects cloud-led IT services revenue to enter double-digit growth in 4Q 2026 and 2027.

AI data center investment pipeline

The company announced a KRW 10 trillion investment roadmap. The Gumi AIDC is targeted at 60MW with completion in 2028. Hana Securities placed full recognition of GPUaaS revenue in 2029, when the Gumi data center starts operations. Hana also raised its target price.

Financial capacity

The debt ratio fell to 31.1% at the end of 2025 from 41.2% in 2022. Operating cash flow exceeded KRW 1 trillion every year from 2022 to 2025. Total equity at the end of 2025 was KRW 10.3 trillion, which suggests room to fund large investments from internal cash flow.

09

Bear factors

Slow profit growth relative to sales

3Q 2026 revenue rose 11.2% but operating profit rose only 3.7%, a wide gap. Profit improvement was limited relative to the increase in sales. Attributable net profit of KRW 176.5 billion fell 9.6% year on year, and the operating margin was lower than a year earlier.

Logistics weight and freight dependence

Logistics revenue rose 16% to KRW 2.09 trillion, but higher air and sea volumes and freight rates contributed to the revenue increase. Because logistics accounts for more than half of sales, a slowdown in freight rates or volumes could quickly reduce top-line growth.

Gap between sector expectations and share performance

Hana Securities noted in its January 2026 report that Samsung SDS's share price return has been relatively weak compared with the rising interest in domestic SI stocks.

That is the bear argument that cloud growth expectations may take time to reach the share price, so how long the gap persists depends on whether earnings from 2027 onward materialize.

10

Risk factors

Investment execution and timing of returns

A KRW 10 trillion investment roadmap has been announced, but data center revenue arrives with a lag. Full recognition of GPUaaS revenue is placed in 2029. If investment comes before returns, margin pressure could build in the interim.

Quarterly earnings volatility

1Q 2026 operating profit of KRW 78.3 billion fell 65.4% quarter on quarter. The recognition of a one-off retirement cost of KRW 112 billion after a change in the severance calculation basis weighed on results. When one-off costs overlap with investment to expand sales, quarterly profit can swing widely.

Dependence on group demand

One analysis holds that data center demand from Samsung affiliates and the chance to win external customers could form the base for expansion.

If group demand arrives later than expected, winning external customers becomes more important, and the pace of that effort would directly affect utilization of the Gumi data center.

12

Overall view

Samsung SDS's 3Q 2026 revenue of KRW 3.77 trillion and operating profit of KRW 241 billion both grew, but operating profit growth trailed sales growth, so margin improvement has been gradual.

On the disclosed annual figures, operating profit rose for two straight years after bottoming in 2023, and the operating margin climbed from 5.3% in 2022 to 6.9% in 2025. In 1Q 2026, operating profit fell to KRW 78.3 billion amid a one-off retirement cost and higher investment, then recovered in 2Q and 3Q.

Bullish factors include double-digit cloud revenue growth, the KRW 10 trillion investment roadmap and the Gumi AI data center plan.

Bearish factors include a logistics-heavy revenue mix, a lag before data center revenue is recognized (GPUaaS full reflection placed in 2029) and the year-on-year decline in 3Q attributable net profit.

The next checkpoints are the preliminary 4Q 2026 results, the 2026 annual report and the Gumi AIDC completion schedule in 2028.
