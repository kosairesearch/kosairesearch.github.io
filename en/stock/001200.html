02

Business structure

Eugene Investment & Securities is a mid-sized brokerage with several business divisions, including wealth management (WM), investment banking (IB), bond and derivatives trading, and proprietary investment.

The WM division has expanded its high-net-worth (HNW) client base and product lineup, lifting brokerage and product fee income, and the company cited this division's contribution in its first-half 2026 results.

The IB division handles IPO underwriting, rights offering arrangement, corporate bond underwriting and M&A advisory, and the company said it is raising the share of structured finance deals for data centers and logistics centers.

The fixed income unit handles trading and management of bonds, repos, and FX and interest rate derivatives, while the investment management unit invests its own capital in listed and unlisted equities and private equity funds.

Clients span wealthy individuals, mid-sized and small companies, and institutional investors, and because IB and bond and equity trading gains make up a large part of the mix, quarterly results can swing widely.

Equity capital passed KRW 1 trillion in August 2022 and was KRW 1.0921 trillion on a consolidated basis at the end of June 2025.

A credit rating report as of March 2024 described the IB division as having a comparative edge in convertible and bond-with-warrant underwriting for mid-sized and small companies, rights offerings and PF deals.

The same report showed the IB market share edging down from 1.9 percent in 2021 to 2022 to 1.6 percent in 2023. The largest shareholder is Yujin Group's holding company, which held 28.3 percent of common shares as of March 2024.

Earnings analysis

In the annual results, sales fell from KRW 1.82 trillion in 2022 to KRW 1.65 trillion in 2023, down 9.5 percent, then rose to KRW 1.65 trillion in 2024, up 0.3 percent, and to KRW 1.69 trillion in 2025, up 2.5 percent, so sales have increased for two consecutive years since 2023.

Among the 2022 to 2025 years, 2022 had the largest sales and 2023 the smallest. Sales scale changed little, but the earnings profile changed clearly.

Operating profit rose from KRW 18.1 billion in 2022 to KRW 27 billion in 2023, up 49.2 percent, to KRW 58.3 billion in 2024, up 115.8 percent, and to KRW 84.4 billion in 2025, up 44.7 percent, increasing for three straight years from 2023.

Operating margin moved from 1.0 percent to 1.6 percent, 3.5 percent and 5.0 percent over the same period.

Net profit attributable to owners of the parent went from KRW 15.7 billion in 2022 to KRW 30.7 billion in 2023, up 95.9 percent, KRW 49.6 billion in 2024, up 61.6 percent, and KRW 64.5 billion in 2025, up 30.1 percent. Reported net income matches the attributable figure in each year.

The company attributed the 2025 operating profit increase to higher gains from product trading and management. Operating cash flow was KRW 23.8 billion in 2022, -KRW 1.96 billion in 2023, KRW 37.1 billion in 2024 and KRW 91.6 billion in 2025. Quarterly results are volatile.

In Q3 2025, sales were KRW 272 billion and operating profit KRW 23.1 billion; in Q4 2025, sales rose to KRW 572.7 billion, up 110.6 percent from the prior quarter, while operating profit fell to KRW 11.7 billion, down 49.4 percent.

In Q1 2026, sales reached KRW 1.07 trillion and operating profit KRW 66.6 billion, up 469.8 percent from the prior quarter and roughly 11 times the year-earlier level. In Q2 2026, sales were KRW 1.5 trillion and operating profit KRW 87.6 billion, up 31.6 percent.

In Q3 2026, sales were KRW 1 trillion, down 33.2 percent from the prior quarter but up 268.3 percent from a year earlier, and operating profit was KRW 18.8 billion, down 78.6 percent from the prior quarter and down 18.8 percent from a year earlier.

Net profit attributable to owners of the parent was KRW 6.75 billion in Q4 2025, KRW 52.7 billion in Q1 2026, KRW 67.5 billion in Q2 2026 and KRW 15.7 billion in Q3 2026. The company said WM and IB income helped defend results in Q3 amid weaker equity markets.

Industry analysis

Brokerage earnings move with trading value and equity market sentiment, while trading and management gains shift with bond yields and IB deal flow. A 2026 first-quarter report described brokerage earnings as continuing to improve as a stronger equity market widened room for individual investors to trade.

The company said first-half 2026 equity investment returns were good on an active market, while its bond book was run conservatively amid concerns over rate increases and inflation. In Q3 2026, news reports said WM and IB income helped defend results despite a weaker equity market.

That pattern shows how mid-sized brokers can see profit rise quickly in rallies and drop sharply in pullbacks. Credit rating data as of March 2024 put the company's share of capital among brokers at 1.2 percent, and its share of operating net revenue at about 1.3 percent.

It competes with brokers of similar capital size, such as Shinyoung Securities, for IB fees and trading gains. A weaker property PF market is a shared issue across the IB market, and the same rating report said the company's capital adequacy ratios trailed peers somewhat as of March 2024.

Outlook

The company has outlined three priorities. First, it plans to intensify structured finance in data centers and other future core sectors as a strategic division.

Second, in IPOs it secured two lead-underwriting deals in the first half of 2026, and in Q3 it stressed expanding IPO lead-manager contracts and cross-divisional sales synergies. Third, the WM division aims to grow fee income through tailored marketing to HNW clients and rebalancing of its product portfolio.

In Q3 2026, the company said income from rights offerings and mezzanine deals increased in IB. The fixed income unit is maintaining a flexible position in an environment where rate increases remain a concern. Capital strengthening and asset quality improvement are also on the agenda.

Because the rating report rated the ratio of substandard-and-below loans to equity as high, the pace of recovery on PF loan receivables and the level of loss provisions will shape capital capacity.

Capital expansion through retained earnings has long been the company's main approach, and whether that continues will show up in equity changes in the periodic reports.

The 2026 annual report, due by March 2027, will disclose equity, net capital ratio and PF asset quality indicators, which will show how far these priorities have progressed.

Bull factors

Sharp Rise in Nine-Month Profit

According to a Money Today report, cumulative operating profit through Q3 2026 was about KRW 172.9 billion, up 137.5 percent year on year, and cumulative net profit was about KRW 135.9 billion, up roughly 135 percent. Q1 2026 operating profit of KRW 66.6 billion was roughly 11 times the year-earlier level.

The company said most divisions, including WM, equity investment and IB, outperformed the prior year in the first half of 2026. The broader earnings base is cited as a bullish factor.

Diversified Income from WM and IB

The WM division grew brokerage and product fee income by expanding its HNW client base and attracting overseas corporate clients.

The IB division broadened its revenue sources through mezzanine arrangement, acquisition finance, two IPO lead-underwriting deals in the first half, and expansion of structured finance for data centers and logistics centers. The company said income from rights offerings and mezzanine deals increased in Q3 2026.

A business mix that does not depend on one division can cushion equity market swings, which is cited as a bullish factor.

Capital Base and Underwriting Capacity

Equity capital passed KRW 1 trillion in August 2022 and was KRW 1.0921 trillion on a consolidated basis at the end of June 2025. In the brokerage industry, equity capital serves as a size indicator that shapes business eligibility and underwriting capacity.

Total equity at the end of 2025 was KRW 1.13 trillion, staying in the trillion-won range. A larger capital base is cited as an advantage for lead-managing larger deals and taking on more underwriting.

Bear factors

Sharp Q3 Drop and Quarterly Volatility

Q3 2026 operating profit was KRW 18.8 billion, down 78.6 percent from the prior quarter and down 18.8 percent from a year earlier. Attributable net profit fell to KRW 15.7 billion from KRW 67.5 billion in Q2 2026. Q4 2025 attributable net profit was only KRW 6.75 billion, so quarterly variation has been large. A weaker equity market was cited as a backdrop to the Q3 results, which is a bearish factor.

PF Asset Quality Pressure

A credit rating report showed the ratio of net substandard-and-below assets to equity at 16.3 percent as of March 2024, a level described as high. Substandard-and-below assets rose after 2022 on the property downturn and domestic PF defaults. Capital adequacy indicators were described as somewhat weaker than peers. Even with provisions in place, the burden relative to equity is cited as a bearish factor.

High Leverage Against Equity

Total liabilities at the end of 2025 were KRW 9.92 trillion, far above total equity of KRW 1.13 trillion. The debt ratio rose from 727.8 percent in 2022 to 875.7 percent in 2025. In a structure that relies heavily on borrowed funds, swings in interest rates and asset prices can move capital quickly. This is cited as a bearish factor.

Risk factors

PF and asset quality

If additional provisions build up, profit could shrink and slow capital strengthening. A credit rating report judged the ratio of net substandard-and-below loans to equity to remain at a high level as of March 2024. Another assessment warned that credit cost burdens could weaken earnings power. The PF-related disclosures in the annual report are the next checkpoint.

Equity market and trading activity

Q3 2026 results weakened as the equity market softened, and lower trading value could squeeze brokerage and IB fees together. Bond trading shifts toward defensive management when rate increases and inflation concerns rise. Equity investment gains depend on market direction, which widens quarterly swings. These are external variables that the company has limited control over.

Leverage and internal controls

In a capital structure with a debt ratio of 875.7 percent, falling asset prices can flow into equity losses quickly. In 2019, an IB division employee was found to have diverted special purpose company funds to a personal account, raising internal control concerns.

Because the largest shareholder holds a high stake in common shares, governance-related disclosures also warrant attention.

Overall view

Eugene Investment & Securities has grown its earnings base, with annual operating profit and attributable net profit rising for three straight years from 2023. Operating profit jumped in Q1 and Q2 2026, lifting cumulative profit sharply from a year earlier.

However, Q3 2026 operating profit of KRW 18.8 billion fell 78.6 percent from the prior quarter, showing how quickly quarterly profit can swing with equity markets and bond and IB results.

The capital structure carries a heavy debt load, with a debt ratio of 875.7 percent, and the ratio of net substandard-and-below loans to equity was high as of March 2024, making PF asset quality the key watch item.

Bullish factors are the diversification across WM and IB, a growing equity base and the recovery trend in earnings, while bearish factors are quarterly earnings volatility, high leverage and PF asset quality.

The share price sits well below book equity value, but the sustainability of earnings and asset quality will shape how that multiple is read. The next items to check are full-year 2026 preliminary results, the equity and PF indicators in the 2026 annual report, and the Q1 2027 quarterly report.
