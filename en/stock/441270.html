Foldable Volume Restores Operating Profit, Net Loss Remains the Test

02

Business structure

Fine M-Tec's core products are foldable display components, namely backplates and built-in hinges, and the company says it is also expanding its ESS (energy storage system) component business.

According to Hana Securities' September 17, 2026 report, 2025 segment revenue was KRW 159.8 billion for backplates, KRW 47.8 billion for secondary battery and smart components, and KRW 31.5 billion for other and intercompany items, which together match the KRW 239.1 billion disclosed revenue.

Backplates are therefore the largest revenue source. Brokers classify segments differently; Meritz Securities groups the same business under precision furniture components, so segment figures vary by source. Customers fall into two groups: established domestic foldable customers and a newer North American customer.

Yuanta Securities said its key customer expanded its foldable lineup from two models to three. The same brokerage said IT component revenue, including mobile and automotive parts, hit a record high in Q2 2026.

On competition, Hyundai Motor Securities said Fine M-Tec is absorbing part of the market share lost by rivals facing North American foldable quality issues.

The company's monthly production capacity is cited at about 1.5 million units, which Hyundai Motor Securities expects could grow by more than 30% from the current level.

The business therefore depends heavily on backplate volume and customer count, making it sensitive to model changes and quality issues at key customers.

04

Earnings analysis

On a disclosed basis, revenue rose from KRW 116.4 billion in 2022 to KRW 380 billion in 2023, a 226.6% increase, and reached KRW 382.2 billion in 2024, up 0.6%, which was the largest year of 2022 to 2025.

Revenue fell 37.4% to KRW 239.1 billion in 2025, and the company attributed the decline mainly to foldable product specification changes. It also cited higher R&D and fixed costs tied to new investment that year.

Operating profit grew from KRW 5.44 billion in 2022 to KRW 20.3 billion in 2023, a 273.6% increase, then swung to an operating loss of KRW 7.91 billion in 2024. In 2025 it returned to operating profit of KRW 1.93 billion, with operating margin moving from 4.7% in 2022 to 5.4% in 2023, -2.1% in 2024 and 0.8% in 2025.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders was a loss of KRW 7.46 billion in 2022, a profit of KRW 10.5 billion in 2023, a loss of KRW 17.2 billion in 2024, and a loss of KRW 14.9 billion in 2025, which narrowed.

Total net income including non-controlling interests was a loss of KRW 15.4 billion in 2025, and brokers cited financial costs and equity-method losses as drags.

By quarter, Q3 2025 revenue was KRW 57.5 billion with operating profit of KRW 1.54 billion, after which Q4 2025 revenue of KRW 61.5 billion, up 6.9% from the prior quarter, came with an operating loss of KRW 1.95 billion.

Q1 2026 revenue was KRW 46.1 billion, down 25.1% from the prior quarter, with an operating loss of KRW 2.2 billion. Q2 2026 revenue rose to KRW 95.2 billion, up 106.7% from the prior quarter, and operating profit turned positive at KRW 7.33 billion.

Q3 2026 revenue was KRW 144.6 billion and operating profit KRW 21.5 billion, both sharply higher year on year, and net income attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 2.51 billion.

However, the combined net loss attributable to controlling shareholders over the last four quarters is KRW 9.9 billion, and operating cash flow was negative at KRW -2.25 billion in 2024 and KRW -1.4 billion in 2025.

05

Industry analysis

The end market is foldable smartphones. In its October 27, 2026 report, Hyundai Motor Securities assumed North American foldable shipments of 6 to 6.5 million units in 2026 and 15 to 16 million units in 2027. If that holds, demand would rise sharply in 2027.

Yuanta Securities also expects new foldable launches from major global smartphone makers to gather pace in 2027. On the cycle, foldable demand has moved past the 2025 slowdown, when revenue fell on weaker sales and specification changes, into a phase of renewed growth, as reported by a May 2026 press article.

On competition, quality problems on new North American foldable models at rival makers have concentrated initial volume at Fine M-Tec, according to Hana Securities.

Hyundai Motor Securities called these quality problems short-term noise unrelated to Fine M-Tec, while citing its near-maximum utilization as evidence of improving earnings. The same report noted that few domestic companies in the value chain can supply key components to leading foldable makers at home and abroad.

Outlook

Broker estimates for full-year 2026 revenue cluster in the KRW 465 to 468 billion range. Hana Securities' September 17, 2026 report projected revenue of KRW 467.8 billion and operating profit of KRW 40.3 billion. Hyundai Motor Securities' October 27, 2026 report projected KRW 465.1 billion and KRW 44.6 billion.

Meritz Securities' October 12, 2026 report projected 2026 operating profit of KRW 44 billion and 2027 operating profit of KRW 64.3 billion. Hyundai Motor Securities projected 2027 revenue of KRW 791.8 billion and operating profit of KRW 73.9 billion.

Meritz Securities said shipments of North American customer products began in Q3 and that supply volume would be fully reflected in Q4.

Hana Securities estimated North American revenue above KRW 130 billion in the second half, with most recognized in Q4, and assumed domestic revenue in Q4 would fall to about half of Q3 on seasonality.

The company is carrying out two facility investments that began in July and October 2025, with scheduled completion on January 20 and February 27, 2027. Yuanta Securities expects earnings improvement to accelerate in 2026 as existing customers expand their foldable lineups alongside new customer entries.

The next checkpoints are whether North American volume appears in Q4 revenue, whether 2026 full-year operating margin lands within broker ranges, and whether capacity added after the facility investments converts into sales.

Bull factors

Expanding foldable volume

In its October 27, 2026 report, Hyundai Motor Securities said Fine M-Tec is absorbing part of the share lost by rivals with quality problems. Hana Securities estimated North American revenue above KRW 130 billion in the second half of 2026. Whether this volume appears in Q4 revenue is the next checkpoint.

Settling into operating profit

Operating profit turned positive at KRW 7.33 billion in Q2 2026 and rose to KRW 21.5 billion in Q3. Q3 operating profit was reported to have exceeded brokerage estimates of KRW 18.1 billion to KRW 19.1 billion.

According to material the company released on November 16, 2026, the cumulative operating margin for the first three quarters rose to 9.3% from 2.2% a year earlier.

Preparing capacity expansion

The company is carrying out two facility investments that began in July and October 2025, worth about KRW 17.5 billion and KRW 16 billion. They aim to meet expanding foldable demand and increase ultra-precision laser capacity.

According to Meritz Securities' March 11, 2026 report, completion is scheduled for January 20 and February 27, 2027. Of the KRW 35 billion convertible bond, KRW 21 billion is earmarked for facilities.

Bear factors

Net losses persist

The company posted losses in both 2024 and 2025, and the combined net loss attributable to controlling shareholders over the last four quarters is KRW 9.9 billion. Per the November 16, 2026 release, the standalone cumulative operating loss through Q3 2026 was KRW 2.1 billion and the net loss KRW 16.5 billion.

A foreign currency translation loss of about KRW 14.6 billion from the year-end exchange rate decline was booked as non-operating expense. On a consolidated basis, cumulative net income was about KRW 5.8 billion, which is also relevant.

Concentration and specification changes

Because backplates dominate revenue, specification changes in foldable products can move sales sharply. Revenue fell 37.4% in 2025, and the company cited specification changes as the main reason. Q1 2026 revenue fell 25.1% from the prior quarter, showing wide quarterly swings. Hana Securities assumed domestic Q4 revenue at about half of Q3 to reflect seasonality.

Dilution and cash burden

The KRW 35 billion 10th private convertible bond would add shares equal to 7.8% of total issued shares on conversion. A rights offering and convertible preferred share issuance in 2025 also raised the possibility of further share count growth.

Operating cash flow on a consolidated basis was a KRW 22.5 billion net outflow in Q1 2026, and KRW 23.5 billion was spent on tangible asset acquisitions. The debt ratio stood at 94.5% at the end of 2025.

Risk factors

Customer and product cycle

Foldable demand depends on launch schedules and sales results of new North American models. Brokers note that the pace of earnings improvement could vary with new customers' launch timing and sales.

Revenue is concentrated in a few customers and components, so volume changes on one model flow directly into quarterly results.

FX and non-operating items

A year-end exchange rate decline led to a foreign currency translation loss of about KRW 14.6 billion on a standalone basis, booked as non-operating expense. In 2025, financial costs and equity-method losses weighed on net income.

Even with operating profit, swings in non-operating items can determine net income attributable to controlling shareholders.

Financing and dilution

A KRW 35 billion convertible bond and past rights offerings and preferred issuance leave room for further share count growth. Total liabilities were KRW 207.7 billion at the end of 2025, with a debt ratio of 94.5%. Facility investments run into early 2027, so whether operating cash flow can cover them remains a test.

Overall view

Fine M-Tec's Q3 2026 operating profit of KRW 21.5 billion shows operating profitability taking hold. On a disclosed basis, 2025 operating profit turned positive at KRW 1.93 billion, but net loss attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 14.9 billion.

With a combined KRW 9.9 billion loss over the last four quarters, whether profit reaches shareholders is still unconfirmed.

A business concentrated in foldable backplates supports the case through North American volume and capacity expansion, but it also exposes the company to model changes and specification shifts at key customers.

Broker estimates for 2026 revenue cluster around KRW 465 to 468 billion, though operating profit estimates differ across firms. On the financial side, the KRW 35 billion convertible bond, facility investments running into early 2027, and non-operating FX losses are factors to track.
