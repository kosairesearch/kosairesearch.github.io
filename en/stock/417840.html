02

Business structure

Justem, founded in 2016, makes semiconductor humidity control equipment and focuses on nitrogen-based control of the micro-environment inside chip manufacturing processes. Its core products are the first-generation N2 LPM (Nitrogen Purge Load Port Module) and the second-generation JFS (Jet Flow Straightener).

According to the company, the first-generation product lowers cleanroom humidity from 45% to 5% or below, and JFS can push humidity down to about 1% when used alongside it.

Per company disclosure, semiconductor operations accounted for more than 90% of total revenue in the first half of 2026, with KRW 45.0 billion in semiconductor revenue. According to a Korean brokerage analysis, the revenue mix by customer is about 50% Micron, 30% SK hynix and 20% Samsung Electronics.

Because the customer base centers on memory makers, customers' capital spending plans link directly to Justem's order timing and revenue timing. The company estimates its global share of humidity control solutions at more than 80% using its own data, not independent third-party figures.

Cumulative third-quarter 2026 JFS revenue was KRW 9.3 billion, up 179% from the same period of 2025. Beyond semiconductors, the company is expanding into displays, and a February 2026 report described a KRW 7.4 billion equipment supply contract with LG Electronics.

Competition in this narrow humidity control niche appears to hinge on how widely second-generation products are adopted.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual revenue was KRW 46.1 billion in 2022 with operating profit of KRW 7.14 billion. Revenue fell 22.1% to KRW 35.9 billion in 2023, and operating profit dropped 95.6% to KRW 311 million. Revenue rose 7.7% to KRW 38.7 billion in 2024, but the company swung to an operating loss of KRW 4.61 billion.

Revenue grew 24.8% to KRW 48.3 billion in 2025, the second consecutive annual increase after the 2023 low, and 2025 was the largest revenue year of 2022 to 2025. Operating profit turned to KRW 4.63 billion in 2025, with an operating margin of 9.6%.

Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders went from KRW 6.32 billion in 2022 to a net loss of KRW 3.39 billion in 2023 and KRW 2.14 billion in 2024, which narrowed the loss, before returning to a profit of KRW 3.47 billion in 2025.

Net income including non-controlling interests was KRW 3.46 billion in 2025, slightly different from the controlling-shareholder figure. In the third quarter of 2025, revenue was KRW 12.1 billion with operating profit of KRW 545 million.

In the fourth quarter, revenue rose to KRW 14.3 billion, but operating profit fell 32.7% quarter on quarter to KRW 366 million, and net loss attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 437 million.

In the first quarter of 2026, revenue was KRW 17.7 billion and operating profit was KRW 4.07 billion, up 1,011.7% from the prior quarter, with net profit attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 4.61 billion. In the second quarter of 2026, revenue reached KRW 29.3 billion and operating profit KRW 9.28 billion.

In the third quarter, revenue was KRW 26.2 billion, down 10.6% quarter on quarter, and operating profit was KRW 7.26 billion, down 21.7% from the prior quarter but up 1,232.7% year on year.

The company attributed the margin gains to higher supply to global integrated semiconductor makers and cost reductions from scale, and said the first-half 2026 cost of sales ratio fell by up to 6 percentage points.

On the other side, the debt-to-equity ratio rose from 38.7% in 2022 to 66.6% in 2025, and operating cash flow moved from KRW -8.18 billion in 2023 to KRW 7.27 billion in 2025.

Because quarterly earnings swing with delivery timing, the starting point for reading the KRW 16 billion combined controlling-shareholder profit over the last four quarters is how much of that quarterly volatility it contains.

05

Industry analysis

As circuit linewidths shrink to the nanometer scale, cleanroom humidity has become a larger factor in yield, which raises the importance of humidity control equipment. Leading chipmakers are increasing investment in advanced process nodes, which is also expanding demand for precise control of the process environment.

Media coverage describes the 2026 chip market as an AI-driven supercycle, but equipment suppliers tend to see orders and revenue bunch into specific quarters depending on customers' capex timing.

The third-quarter 2026 revenue decline after the second-quarter peak can be read as a reflection of this delivery-timing variability.

In terms of cycle position, customers are expanding investment in advanced process nodes, and the company expects supply to continue through installed-but-unshipped lines and newly built lines at existing customers.

Its competitive position rests on its own estimate of more than 80% of the global humidity control market, a figure not verified by independent third-party data.

The first-generation N2 LPM continues to see steady demand, while the second-generation JFS volume is growing alongside advanced-node investment, so both generations support results together.

Display operations are an area where the company expects progress in the second half, but the scale of their contribution to earnings does not yet appear in disclosed figures.

06

Outlook

The company said its order backlog at the end of the third quarter of 2026 was about KRW 40 billion, with roughly 81% tied to semiconductor humidity control solutions. According to the company, this backlog is expected to be reflected in revenue in earnest starting in the fourth quarter of 2026.

The company is widening JFS adoption through installed-but-unshipped lines and newly built lines at existing customers, and expects JFS supply growth to continue in the second half. The company has cited display performance and wider adoption among global integrated chipmakers as second-half priorities.

It plans to expand capacity through construction of a third plant at Yongin 2nd Technovalley in Gyeonggi Province and has secured KRW 30 billion in semiconductor policy funding from the Korea Development Bank for facility investment.

The company bought a site of about 6,000 pyeong for the Yongin third plant in May 2025. Its medium-term targets are KRW 300 billion in revenue and KRW 2 trillion in enterprise value by 2030, to be pursued alongside new business development and M&A.

A 2025 report said the third-generation humidity control product, JDM, was being tested with a global chipmaker. The next checkpoints are whether fourth-quarter 2026 revenue reflects the backlog and when the third plant's completion schedule is disclosed.

The pace at which orders convert into revenue is the key point to confirm in the next periodic report.

Improved earnings structure

Bear factors

Customer concentration

According to a Korean brokerage analysis, Micron accounts for about 50% of revenue, making it the largest customer. A delay in one customer's capex schedule could move quarterly results sharply. Third-quarter 2026 revenue falling 10.6% from the prior quarter is one example of this sensitivity at the quarterly level.

Volatility of quarterly profit

Operating profit fell 95.6% to KRW 311 million in 2023, and the company posted an operating loss of KRW 4.61 billion in 2024. In the fourth quarter of 2025, net loss attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 437 million pushed the company back into a quarterly loss. Even after the return to profit, quarterly swings remain large, so continuity needs more verification.

Working capital and financial burden

Receivables rose from KRW 10.5 billion at the end of 2025 to KRW 20.2 billion at the end of the first half of 2026, while inventory rose from KRW 3.6 billion to KRW 12.6 billion. The debt-to-equity ratio of 66.6% at the end of 2025 is higher than 38.7% in 2022. As revenue grows, working capital rises with it, which keeps the pace of cash collection an important issue.

10

Risk factors

Customer concentration and capex cycle

Micron's roughly 50% share means a change in one customer's investment directly affects revenue. Memory capex can shift sharply with the cycle, and changes in customer schedules could delay revenue from lines not yet installed. Whether the customer base diversifies is the next point to watch.

Execution risk of large investments

The KRW 300 billion revenue target for 2030 is more than six times 2025 revenue of KRW 48.3 billion. Construction of the third plant is being funded in part by KRW 30 billion in policy funds, but if utilization and demand do not keep pace, fixed costs could weigh heavily. The pace of new business and M&A plans is another variable.

Cash flow and financial leverage

Operating cash flow was KRW -8.18 billion in 2023 and recovered to KRW 7.27 billion in 2025. Rising receivables and inventory are pressuring cash flow. Together with the debt-to-equity ratio of 66.6% at the end of 2025, the capacity to raise funds is another variable to watch.

Overall view

Justem swung from profit in 2022 to losses in 2023 and 2024, returned to profit in 2025, and has seen sharply higher quarterly revenue and operating profit through 2026.

Third-quarter 2026 revenue was KRW 26.2 billion and operating profit was KRW 7.26 billion, and combined controlling-shareholder net profit over the last four quarters was KRW 16 billion.

Its strengths are the company's self-estimated share of more than 80%, growing second-generation JFS revenue and an order backlog of about KRW 40 billion. Its weaknesses are concentration on Micron, large quarterly swings and working capital pressure from rising receivables and inventory.

The debt-to-equity ratio was 66.6% at the end of 2025, and operating cash flow recovered to KRW 7.27 billion in 2025. The 2030 revenue goal of KRW 300 billion and the third plant remain plans, and whether they are executed will be confirmed through future periodic reports.

Because the stock trades at a large premium to book value, the durability of the earnings recovery is the central point of interpretation.
