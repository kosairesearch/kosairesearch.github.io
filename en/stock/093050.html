02

Business structure

LF, separated from the LG group in 2007, is a fashion and lifestyle company that operates brands including Hazzys, DAKS, JILL STUART, Maestro, Atte and Dunst. Its core is fashion and beauty, supplemented by finance and real estate, a food division and data center operations.

In its first-quarter 2026 results, the company said growth led by mega brands Hazzys and DAKS lifted both revenue and operating profit. Demand for premium brands concentrated in department store channels was cited as a driver of the first-quarter improvement.

In the third quarter of 2026, fashion and beauty brands grew evenly, with real estate supporting the results. The finance business, which includes Koramco Asset Trust, saw revenue rise 26.4% in the third quarter of 2026 according to the company.

The food division's first-half 2026 revenue was KRW 1.823 billion, down 0.55% year on year, while pre-tax profit turned to KRW 65 million from a KRW 10 million loss a year earlier.

The data center business comprises the Busan Jangnim development, in which LF invested KRW 10 billion in August 2025 for a 21.79% stake, and the Gasan data center, which began operating in October 2025.

The Gasan facility posted a net loss in the first half of 2026, while the Busan Jangnim project has completed land ownership transfer and is in design optimization ahead of main construction. The company plans to expand overseas operations centered on Hazzys, Maestro, Atte and Dunst.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual revenue fell from KRW 1.97 trillion in 2022 to KRW 1.9 trillion in 2023 (down 3.4% year on year), recovered to KRW 1.96 trillion in 2024 (up 2.9%), and declined to KRW 1.88 trillion in 2025 (down 3.8%). Among 2022 to 2025, 2022 was the largest year and 2025 the smallest.

Operating profit fell from KRW 185.2 billion in 2022 to KRW 57.4 billion in 2023 (down 69.0%), the low point of the period. It then rose to KRW 126.1 billion in 2024 (up 119.8%) and KRW 168.1 billion in 2025 (up 33.3%), increasing for two consecutive years from 2024 through 2025.

The operating margin moved from 9.4% in 2022 to 3.0% in 2023, 6.4% in 2024 and 8.9% in 2025. In 2025, the margin rose above its 2024 level even as revenue declined, which supports the reading of a profitability recovery.

Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 152.3 billion in 2022, KRW 82.5 billion in 2023 (down 45.8%), KRW 75.5 billion in 2024 (down 8.5%) and KRW 98.9 billion in 2025 (up 31.0%).

Net income including non-controlling interests was KRW 117.5 billion in 2025 and should be read separately from attributable profit. Operating cash flow troughed at KRW 92.6 billion in 2023 and reached KRW 270.2 billion in 2025.

The debt ratio rose from 56.0% in 2022 to 63.9% in 2025, and total equity at the end of 2025 was KRW 1.85 trillion.

On a quarterly basis, operating profit bottomed at KRW 16.1 billion in the third quarter of 2025 and jumped to KRW 77.6 billion in the fourth quarter (up 381.2% from the prior quarter) as the fashion peak season took effect.

Operating profit was KRW 44.4 billion in the first quarter of 2026 (down 42.8% from the prior quarter), KRW 44.1 billion in the second quarter (down 0.7%) and KRW 51.1 billion in the third quarter (up 15.9% from the prior quarter and up 216.6% year on year).

Third-quarter 2026 revenue was KRW 429.1 billion, up 7.6% year on year, and profit attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 27.4 billion, up 20.6% year on year.

05

Industry analysis

The apparel industry is sensitive to consumer spending and department store traffic. LF's first-quarter 2026 explanation that premium demand concentrated in department store channels means that premium fashion demand reliant on those channels feeds directly into results.

Seasonality is also pronounced: fourth-quarter 2025 revenue was KRW 597.8 billion, up 50.0% from the prior quarter, and the company cited brand-specific product competitiveness for the fashion peak season.

Inventory management accuracy affects end-of-season discounting and margins, which makes the company's combination of AI-based demand forecasting and tighter inventory operations relevant. The 7.8% sequential revenue decline in the third quarter of 2026 can also be read as part of this seasonal pattern.

Listed peers in the same sector include F&F, Handsome, Shinsegae International and Youngone, and fashion companies see demand swing differently depending on their department store and online mix. LF's finance and real estate holdings spread its exposure to apparel cycles somewhat more widely.

Real estate and data center businesses follow cycles different from fashion, so whether their contribution to profit changes should be checked in the next annual report's segment commentary.

On the industry level, the bullish factors are stable premium demand and cost control, while the bearish factors are slower consumption and quarter-to-quarter volatility from seasonality.

06

Outlook

The company said it will strengthen product competitiveness tailored to core customer demand by brand during the fourth-quarter 2026 fashion peak season, and refine AI-based demand forecasting and inventory operations.

It plans to link distribution, marketing and customer service to strengthen relationships with existing customers and attract new ones. Overseas expansion will center on Hazzys, Maestro, Atte and Dunst.

In its first-quarter explanation, the company said it would strengthen brand competitiveness at home and abroad, centered on core brands including Hazzys and DAKS.

For the Gasan data center, the manager Koramco Asset Management expects most testing and operational stabilization of key equipment to be completed in the second half of 2026.

The same manager said that from 2027, higher revenue from expanded operations and accounting profitability after depreciation could allow a move to profit.

The Busan Jangnim data center is expected to begin main construction in 2027 after design optimization, with completion and operations targeted for late 2029 to early 2030.

A KRW 5 billion share buyback is scheduled from October 1 to December 30, 2026, and the holding at the time of the decision was reported as 2,801,724 shares. The 2026 annual report, the next periodic filing, must be submitted by March 31, 2027, when the 2026 annual operating margin can be compared with 8.9% in 2025.

This outlook rests on company plans and manager commentary, and realization will be confirmed through subsequent disclosed figures.

In the first quarter of 2026, growth centered on Hazzys and DAKS lifted both revenue and operating profit, according to the company. Revenue concentration in the fourth-quarter fashion peak also remains a feature of the seasonal pattern.

Diversification beyond fashion

The company reported that finance revenue rose 26.4% in the third quarter of 2026, and real estate also supported growth in that quarter. The food division's first-half 2026 revenue fell 0.55%, but its pre-tax profit moved to KRW 65 million from a prior-year loss of KRW 10 million. More revenue sources beyond fashion spread the effect of apparel cycle swings.

Shareholder returns and low book multiple

Bear factors

Revenue trend remains weak

Revenue for 2025 fell 3.8% to KRW 1.88 trillion, the smallest year among 2022 to 2025. Revenue also declined 3.4% in 2023, and the 2024 rebound was only 2.9%. Third-quarter 2026 revenue fell 7.8% from the prior quarter.

Earnings volatility

Operating profit fell 69.0% to KRW 57.4 billion in 2023, and profit attributable to controlling shareholders declined to KRW 75.5 billion in 2024. Quarterly operating profit swung from KRW 16.1 billion in the third quarter of 2025 to KRW 77.6 billion in the fourth quarter. In the first quarter of 2026 it fell 42.8% from the prior quarter to KRW 44.4 billion, indicating large swings.

Data center losses and debt burden

The Gasan data center posted a net loss in the first half of 2026. The Busan Jangnim project is expected to start main construction in 2027, with completion targeted for late 2029 to early 2030, so monetization will take time. The debt ratio rose from 56.0% in 2022 to 63.9% in 2025.

10

Risk factors

Consumer demand and department store dependence

Premium brand demand is concentrated in department store channels, so the company is directly exposed to consumption slowdowns and lower foot traffic. If premium demand cited as a first-quarter driver weakens, growth momentum from Hazzys and DAKS would slow. Revenue declines in 2023 and 2025 show how quickly shocks reach reported results.

Delays in monetizing non-fashion businesses

The Gasan data center posted a net loss in the first half of 2026, and the timing of a move to profit has been presented as 2027 or later based on the manager's outlook. The Busan Jangnim project targets main construction in 2027 and completion between late 2029 and early 2030. Depreciation and financing costs may weigh on profit during this period.

Inventory management and seasonal margin swings

Because revenue concentrates in the fourth quarter, inventory misjudgments can widen end-of-season discounts and pressure margins. The company's emphasis on AI-based demand forecasting and refined inventory operations is a response to this risk. The effect of these measures will need to be checked against results from the fourth quarter of 2026 onward.

Overall view

LF's 2025 revenue fell to KRW 1.88 trillion, but operating profit rose to KRW 168.1 billion, showing a profitability recovery. Third-quarter 2026 operating profit was KRW 51.1 billion, up 216.6% year on year, and profit attributable to controlling shareholders over the last four quarters totals KRW 118.1 billion.

Bullish factors are premium fashion growth led by Hazzys and DAKS, diversification into finance and real estate, and shareholder returns through share buybacks.

Bearish factors are revenue declines in 2023 and 2025, seasonal and volatile quarterly results, net losses at the Gasan data center and a long monetization period for the Busan Jangnim project, and a rising debt ratio.

The price-to-book ratio is well below 1x and the price-to-earnings multiple is below the sector average, so the valuation comparison is clear. This comparison does not guarantee that the earnings recovery will persist.

The next checkpoints are the operating margin in the 2026 annual report, progress at the Gasan and Busan Jangnim data centers, and the outcome of the share buyback ending December 30, 2026.
