02

Business structure

Celestra is a genome-based diagnostics company founded in July 2011 and listed on KOSDAQ in December 2020. The company has built its business around four areas: genome-based healthcare, a liquid biopsy platform, cancer diagnosis and monitoring, and early cancer detection.

Its technology detects circulating tumor cells (CTC) and cell-free DNA (cfDNA) from blood at the same time, supported by multi-omics analysis capabilities. An in-house bio big-data center accumulates large datasets for research and commercialization.

Revenue is listed as roughly 94 percent services and 6 percent medical devices by Alphasquare, a figure whose collection date is not stated. In 2023 the company carried out national R&D projects on infectious disease and epidemiological modeling.

Its former name was Clinomics, and it set up subsidiaries in the United States and United Kingdom to pursue overseas expansion. Press reports linked its rehabilitation filing to a slowdown in growth of its main business segment and a heavy fixed-cost burden.

A 2026 sale report cited the book value of its production site in Ulju County and its research center in Osong, Chungbuk at roughly KRW 9.5 billion.

The diagnostics market is contested on technology and price, while large listed peers in the biopharma sector such as Samsung Biologics and Celltrion center on contract manufacturing and biosimilars, a different structure from Celestra's service-based diagnostics model.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual results show revenue shrinking while losses persisted. Revenue fell from KRW 23.1 billion in 2022 to KRW 11.4 billion in 2023 (down 50.6% year on year), to KRW 8.5 billion in 2024 (down 25.5%), and to KRW 2.01 billion in 2025 (down 76.4%).

Revenue declined for three consecutive years from 2023 through 2025, with 2022 the largest year and 2025 the smallest in the 2022–2025 span. Operating loss widened from KRW 10.8 billion in 2022 to KRW 34.6 billion in 2023, then narrowed to KRW 28.7 billion in 2024 and KRW 7.76 billion in 2025.

The operating margin moved from -46.7% in 2022 to -303.2% in 2023, -337.7% in 2024 and -386.5% in 2025, which suggests revenue fell faster than costs could be cut.

Net loss attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 10.2 billion in 2022, KRW 42.4 billion in 2023, KRW 46.9 billion in 2024 and KRW 11.8 billion in 2025. Total net loss including non-controlling interests was KRW 47.1 billion in 2024.

The debt ratio, total liabilities divided by total equity, rose from 82.5% in 2022 and 91.0% in 2023 to 877.7% in 2024. Operating cash outflows were KRW 12 billion in 2022, KRW 10.7 billion in 2023, KRW 11.3 billion in 2024 and KRW 5.59 billion in 2025.

Press coverage tied the 2023 and 2024 expansion of losses to aggressive investments, including a hotel acquisition for about KRW 18.5 billion, a 40 percent stake in a mushroom-cultivation company and convertible bond issuance.

Quarterly operating loss went from KRW 1.87 billion in the third quarter of 2025 to KRW 168 million in the fourth quarter, then widened to KRW 254 million, KRW 447 million and KRW 854 million across the first three quarters of 2026, making five consecutive loss-making quarters.

Revenue moved from KRW 1.07 billion in the third quarter of 2025 to KRW 24.09 million in the first quarter of 2026, and third-quarter 2026 revenue was reported below KRW 300 million. Net loss attributable to controlling shareholders over the last four quarters totaled KRW 4.22 billion.

Industry analysis

Celestra operates in the genome-based diagnostics market, where demand centers on early cancer detection and liquid biopsy and depends on the spread of precision medicine and testing demand. The company's results, however, show weakening ability to convert that demand into revenue.

An August 2026 press report pointed to a rising number of biotech and healthcare companies facing disclaimers of opinion and delisting grounds. Blotter identified a prolonged industry slump and liquidity pressure as the background to the rehabilitation filing.

Larger listed peers in the sector such as Samsung Biologics and Celltrion are built around contract manufacturing and biosimilars, a revenue structure different from Celestra's service-based diagnostics model. Celestra's KRW 2.01 billion of revenue in 2025 is very small against listed peers in the same industry.

Its market capitalization ranks near the bottom of the life sciences and engineering industry group. The most direct cycle signal at present is that the company's results do not show a recovery in the industry.

Its liquid biopsy platform and big-data center are cited as differentiators, but the speed at which they translate into revenue remains the key question.

Outlook

The business plan the company has made public centers on securing funding and maintaining its listing. A sale report in the first half of 2026 said Samil PricewaterhouseCoopers was appointed as sale adviser and that a pre-approval merger and acquisition process inside the rehabilitation framework had begun.

A delisting decision was made on June 8, 2026, and the company filed an injunction application on June 9, leaving the delisting steps, including the final trading period, on hold. The Korea Exchange said it would proceed with follow-up steps once the related litigation is decided.

In August 2026 the Suwon Bankruptcy Court terminated the rehabilitation procedure after no rehabilitation plan was submitted, and the company said it would consider refiling once a new investor is confirmed.

A third-quarter 2026 report filed on November 16, 2026 showed recent-quarter revenue below KRW 300 million, which added a qualification review ground.

For the fiscal 2025 audit opinion, the company filed an objection on April 28, 2026, and the exchange said it would deliberate the matter together with the fiscal 2024 opinion ground.

The next checkpoints are the outcome of the injunction litigation, whether a new investor is confirmed, and the revenue and audit opinion in the 2026 annual report.

Bull factors

Cost cuts narrowed losses

A year-to-date analysis through the third quarter of 2025 credited improved cost ratios and cost cuts with a sharp reduction in operating loss. Full-year 2025 operating loss was KRW 7.76 billion against KRW 28.7 billion in 2024, and the third-quarter 2026 loss was narrower than a year earlier. The narrowing direction is cited as a supporting factor for the bull case.

Debt clean-up hoped for with a new investor

A first-half 2026 report noted that a new investor entering the sale process could clear existing and contingent debts and remove impaired assets. The company has said it would consider refiling for rehabilitation once an investor is confirmed. Whether this path is realized has not yet been determined.

Liquid biopsy technology and data assets

Celestra holds technology that detects circulating tumor cells and cell-free DNA from blood at the same time, along with multi-omics analysis. Its in-house bio big-data center accumulates large datasets usable for research and commercialization. A company information site lists 18 patents in total.

Bear factors

Weakening revenue base

Revenue fell from KRW 11.4 billion in 2023 and KRW 8.5 billion in 2024 to KRW 2.01 billion in 2025, a third straight annual decline. First-quarter 2026 revenue was KRW 24.09 million. Third-quarter 2026 revenue was reported below KRW 300 million, which triggered an added qualification review.

Audit opinion and listing procedures

The fiscal 2025 audit opinion was a disclaimer of opinion, and the company filed an objection on April 28, 2026. A delisting decision was made on June 8, 2026, and the procedure is on hold pending an injunction. The rehabilitation procedure was terminated in August 2026.

Limited funding routes and reports of a complaint against management

Total equity at the end of 2025 was KRW 6.22 billion against total liabilities of KRW 24.6 billion. With no new investor confirmed, funding routes remain limited.

Press reports said a complaint had been filed against the CEO over alleged breach of trust and violations of capital markets law, and that signs of fund outflows before the delisting were reported.

Risk factors

Listing status

A delisting decision is in place, and the process remains suspended until the court rules on the injunction. If the ruling goes against the company, trading conditions could change quickly. The added qualification review also leaves the exchange room to take further steps.

Liquidity and funding

Reports said the company had lost its ability to raise funds on its own, and that lenders declined to extend loan maturities. Operating losses have widened each quarter since the first quarter of 2026, which accelerates cash consumption. Without new capital, the timing of any rehabilitation refiling remains unclear.

Accounting and governance

The fiscal 2024 audit opinion was qualified, and the fiscal 2025 opinion was a disclaimer. The audit report was also delayed beyond the statutory deadline in 2026. Reports of a complaint against management and possible fund outflows add uncertainty about the reliability of financial disclosures.

Overall view

Celestra's revenue fell from KRW 23.1 billion in 2022 to KRW 2.01 billion in 2025, and its operating loss narrowed from KRW 28.7 billion in 2024 to KRW 7.76 billion in 2025.

Operating losses have widened each quarter since the first quarter of 2026, reaching KRW 854 million in the third quarter, and that quarter's revenue was reported below KRW 300 million.

The rehabilitation procedure was terminated in August 2026, and the delisting process is on hold pending the outcome of the injunction litigation. With total equity of KRW 6.22 billion and total liabilities of KRW 24.6 billion at year-end 2025, financial capacity is limited.

Supporters point to lower losses from cost cuts, the liquid biopsy technology and data assets, and the possibility of debt clean-up with a new investor. Critics point to the weakening revenue base, the disclaimer of opinion and listing procedures, and difficulty in raising funds.

The judgment on business continuity now rests largely on a single question, whether the company stays listed. The next checkpoints are the outcome of the injunction litigation, confirmation of a new investor, and the revenue and audit opinion in the 2026 annual report.
