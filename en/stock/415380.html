02

Business structure

Studio Samick is an online home-furnishing distributor that designs and develops its own bedroom and living furniture and supplies it to more than 30 online platforms.

Its main brand is Samick Furniture, supported by the premium solid-wood brand Scandia and the sleep-products brand Studio Slip, with a linen brand, Linen Anotherr, launched in June 2026.

Product sales account for almost all revenue: the cumulative share was 99.99% in the first nine months of 2024, and the 2026 Q1 quarterly report shows all sales as domestic household furniture.

Fulfillment is the core business; fulfillment revenue rose 41.7% in 2024 to KRW 21.8 billion, and Scandia sales rose 40.9% to KRW 9.5 billion. Fulfillment reached the 20% range of total sales, and the company became a Coupang fulfillment partner in September 2020.

Because it sells its own products across several platforms, it depends heavily on platform policies and fees. Its channel and product structure differs from department-store and hypermarket operators in the same sector, which makes direct comparison difficult.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual sales rose from KRW 86.6 billion in 2022 to KRW 95.4 billion in 2023 (up 10.2% year on year) and KRW 107.9 billion in 2024 (up 13.1%), then fell to KRW 95.9 billion in 2025 (down 11.1%). Of 2022 to 2025, 2024 was the largest year and 2022 the smallest.

Operating profit grew from KRW 2.4 billion in 2022 to KRW 3.99 billion in 2024 (up 43.6%) before falling to KRW 2.83 billion in 2025 (down 29.1%).

The company said in early 2025 that its 2024 results were a record, and it attributed the profit gain to lower marketing fees and higher sales of high-margin products, including premium Studio Slip items.

Operating margin was 2.8% in 2022, 2.9% in 2023, 3.7% in 2024 and 2.9% in 2025, so 2024 was the only year with a clearly higher margin. The 2022 profit drop was attributed to raw-material price increases and currency issues after the Ukraine war, along with listing-preparation costs.

Profit attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 1.93 billion in 2022, KRW 2.17 billion in 2023 (up 12.9%), KRW 3.65 billion in 2024 (up 67.8%) and KRW 2.79 billion in 2025 (down 23.5%).

Quarterly sales peaked at KRW 23.7 billion in 2025 Q4 (up 8.0% from the prior quarter) and then declined to KRW 22.6 billion, KRW 22.3 billion and KRW 21.7 billion across the first three quarters of 2026.

Operating profit in 2026 Q3 was KRW 300 million, down 45.5% year on year, and profit attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 321 million, down 49.1%. Over the last four quarters, profit attributable to controlling shareholders totaled KRW 2 billion.

Operating cash flow was positive in every year from 2022 to 2025, at KRW 3.01 billion, KRW 3.31 billion, KRW 4.59 billion and KRW 2.58 billion, while the debt ratio fell from 72.3% to 26.0%.

05

Industry analysis

Home-furnishing demand is sensitive to domestic economic conditions and construction investment.

According to a summary from FnGuide, cumulative sales for the first nine months of 2025 fell 9.0% year on year on a standalone basis, as weak domestic demand and lower construction investment dampened home-furnishing sales while raw-material and interest-rate increases limited profitability.

The pressure carried into full-year 2025 sales and the quarterly declines in 2026.

Competition is a structural variable: around the 2024 listing, a KB Securities analyst flagged intensifying competition in Korea's e-commerce market as a risk to top-line growth, while noting that product-portfolio expansion was broadening the consumer base.

The company has said its per-employee productivity is high relative to peers, citing AI chatbots for customer service and web design, though that is management's own assessment. In terms of cycle position, sales have come down from the 2024 peak, and a demand recovery has not yet appeared in quarterly results.

Raw-material and currency swings, as seen in 2022, can quickly compress profitability. Expansion into pet products and functional fabrics targets consumer spending patterns that differ from core furniture demand.

06

Outlook

The value-up plan the company filed on March 30, 2026 states that it will reach its target profit margin through stable earnings, continue growth investment, and keep dividends at a level that does not undermine shareholder value.

The company said it qualifies as a high-dividend company under the Restriction of Special Taxation Act, and its 2025 payout ratio was 41.02%. The plan also includes share buybacks and cancellations.

On growth, the company earlier outlined expansion into pet products and functional fabrics such as cooling bedding, and extending fulfillment beyond Samick Furniture to the Scandia brand. It has also said that opportunities to list on overseas platforms and expand partnerships are increasing.

On the financial side, the company continues to minimize borrowings to cut financing costs, and total liabilities at the end of 2025 were KRW 9.46 billion, lower than in 2024. The Linen Anotherr brand launched in June 2026 is the latest product addition.

The company has set no numerical sales guidance that is publicly disclosed, and its stated targets are framed around profit margin. Items to watch are whether the three-quarter decline in sales stops and whether fulfillment and Scandia revenue keep growing at a double-digit pace.

Bull factors

Record Year Already Recorded

The company said its 2024 sales of KRW 107.9 billion and operating profit of KRW 3.99 billion were a record. The gain was attributed to lower marketing fees and higher sales of high-margin products. The year also saw fulfillment and the Scandia brand grow together, which shows the earnings base can widen.

Stronger Balance Sheet

The debt ratio fell from 72.3% in 2022 to 26.0% in 2025. Total liabilities at the end of 2025 were KRW 9.46 billion, and total equity was KRW 36.3 billion. Operating cash flow was positive in each year from 2022 to 2025. Debt repayment and minimization have reduced financing costs.

Shareholder Return Policy

Its 2025 payout ratio was 41.02%. The 2026 value-up plan also includes share buybacks and cancellations.

09

Bear factors

Three Quarters of Declining Profit

Sales peaked at KRW 23.7 billion in 2025 Q4 and then declined to KRW 22.6 billion in 2026 Q1, KRW 22.3 billion in Q2 and KRW 21.7 billion in Q3. Operating profit in 2026 Q3 was KRW 300 million, down 45.5% year on year, and profit attributable to controlling shareholders fell 49.1%. The decline has continued quarter after quarter.

Weak Home-Furnishing Demand

A FnGuide summary attributes weaker home-furnishing demand to a domestic slowdown and lower construction investment. Full-year 2025 sales fell 11.1% to KRW 95.9 billion. If weak demand reduces fulfillment volume, the timing of a sales recovery could be pushed back.

Thin Margins and Rising Competition

Operating margin fell to 2.9% in 2025 from 3.7% in 2024. Around the listing, a KB Securities analyst cited intensifying e-commerce competition as a risk to growth. With thin margins, higher raw-material costs or platform fees feed directly into lower profit.

10

Risk factors

Demand and Industry

Demand for household furniture is tied to domestic economic conditions and construction investment. Cumulative sales through the first nine months of 2025 fell year on year, and quarterly sales declined through 2026. If demand does not recover, growth in fulfillment and Scandia revenue could slow.

Platform Dependence and Competition

The company supplies products to more than 30 online platforms, so earnings are sensitive to changes in platform fees or listing policies. Intensifying e-commerce competition has been a risk since the listing period. The company's disclosures do not break out how much a single platform policy change would affect total sales.

Costs and Small-Cap Volatility

In 2022, operating profit fell sharply on external factors such as rising raw-material prices and currency swings. As a small-cap stock, the company can see large trading swings and limited liquidity. If cost increases cannot be passed on to prices, margins could fall again.

Overall view

Studio Samick is an online home-furnishing distributor that supplies its own designs to multiple platforms, with product sales making up nearly all revenue and fulfillment as its growth engine.

Sales of KRW 107.9 billion and operating profit of KRW 3.99 billion in 2024 were the highest of 2022 to 2025, but 2025 sales fell to KRW 95.9 billion. Quarterly sales and operating profit have declined for three straight quarters in 2026, so profit has not yet shown a recovery.

On the balance sheet, the debt ratio fell to 26.0% by the end of 2025 and operating cash flow stayed positive. On shareholder returns, the 2025 payout ratio was 41.02% and buybacks and cancellations are part of the value-up plan.

On valuation, the price-to-book ratio is below 1x, but profit attributable to controlling shareholders over the last four quarters totaled KRW 2 billion, so the multiple should be read alongside profit recovery. Weak demand, e-commerce competition and thin operating margins are the main bearish factors.

The next checkpoints are the full-year operating margin in the 2026 annual report and the quarterly sales trend in the 2027 Q1 report.
