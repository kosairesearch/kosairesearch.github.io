Report overview

Battery-equipment orders and losses test JAS Robotics' pivot

JAS Robotics is moving from display equipment toward battery-line automation and robotics, yet its disclosed results still show revenue down for three straight years and operating losses continuing.

    Revenue fell to KRW 30.7 billion in 2025, down 51.8% year on year, and the operating loss widened to KRW 23.7 billion.

  2. 2

  3. 3

  4. 4

  5. 5

02

Business structure

JAS Robotics began its semiconductor manufacturing equipment business in 1995 and listed on KOSDAQ in 2007. In March 2026 it changed its name and declared a shift toward robot-based smart-factory solutions.

Its core is automation equipment for prismatic battery production lines, and it markets a total solution covering pilot cell lines, equipment design, logistics automation and manufacturing execution systems (MES).

In a March 2026 press report, the company said about 90% of its business is related to secondary batteries.

However, a market data site's revenue breakdown showed display equipment at 29%, the secondary battery division at 27%, semiconductor equipment and other items at 19%, bio diagnostic kit equipment at 18% and laser equipment at 7%, so segment classifications differ across sources.

Customers include overseas plants of major Korean battery makers, and the company supplies turnkey equipment for an Indian gigafactory in cooperation with US battery specialist IBC.

On the robotics side, a March 2026 report said about 250 autonomous mobile robots worth roughly KRW 30 billion were supplied to a North American battery plant at the end of 2025.

It is developing autonomous mobile manipulation robots (AMMR) with a bio company, sourcing robot arms externally and combining them at its AMR production line in Asan, South Chungcheong Province.

Competition splits between battery automation equipment makers and robotics firms, and listed robotics peers in the same sector include Doosan Robotics, Rainbow Robotics and Robotis. Related parties held 38.78% as of August 31, 2026.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual revenue went from KRW 143.9 billion in 2022 to KRW 72.1 billion in 2023 (down 49.9% year on year), KRW 63.6 billion in 2024 (down 11.7%) and KRW 30.7 billion in 2025 (down 51.8%).

Revenue has declined for three consecutive years from 2023 through 2025, with 2022 the largest year and 2025 the smallest among 2022 to 2025. Press reports attribute the decline to shrinking display equipment demand as display industry investment weakened.

Operating profit of KRW 37.5 billion in 2022 turned into an operating loss of KRW 371 million in 2023, then widened to KRW 5.78 billion in 2024 and KRW 23.7 billion in 2025. The operating margin fell from 26.0% in 2022 to -0.5% in 2023, -9.1% in 2024 and -77.2% in 2025.

A news analysis of the same period tied the wider loss to fewer equipment orders from existing downstream industries and rising upfront costs for robotics and automation projects.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders moved from KRW 27.5 billion in 2022 to a net loss of KRW 1.29 billion in 2023, KRW 5.86 billion in 2024 and KRW 24.8 billion in 2025. Net loss including non-controlling interests, a different measure, was KRW 25.4 billion in 2025.

Operating cash flow fell from KRW 28.7 billion in 2022 to -KRW 13.3 billion in 2024 and narrowed to -KRW 4.46 billion in 2025. A news report said KRW 11.0 billion of financing inflows in 2025 covered the gap, pointing to heavy reliance on external funding.

Quarterly revenue peaked at KRW 19.8 billion in Q4 2025 (up 416.6% from the prior quarter) and then declined for three consecutive quarters to KRW 6.42 billion in Q1 2026, KRW 4.04 billion in Q2 and KRW 2.79 billion in Q3.

Operating losses have run for five consecutive quarters since Q3 2025, and the Q3 2026 loss narrowed from a year earlier. Net losses attributable to controlling shareholders over the last four quarters totaled KRW 23.5 billion.

05

Industry analysis

The company's chief executive said that apart from China and Samsung SDI in Korea, few firms produce prismatic batteries at scale, and he cited this as the basis for the company's prismatic-battery equipment strength. Demand for battery equipment comes from Korean battery makers investing in overseas plants.

A May 2026 report cited an industry view that continued capacity investment in India would sustain demand for domestic automation equipment makers. The company said in June 2026 that it had supplied core welding and automation equipment to a US energy-storage project.

The display side moves the other way: weaker display industry investment reduced equipment sales, and the company had been cutting its display share since the early 2020s.

On robotics, the company argues that battery plants need autonomous mobile robots rather than ceiling-mounted logistics because battery loads weigh one to two tonnes. A securities information site also points to bio-process logistics as a long-term growth area.

Doosan Robotics, Rainbow Robotics and Robotis are listed in the same machinery sector and are the natural comparison set for the robotics business.

Outlook

The largest order marker is a KRW 33.26 billion contract for robotic automation equipment for overseas secondary-battery manufacturing, signed on September 1, 2026. The contract runs from September 1, 2026 to August 31, 2027, and the company disclosed that the end date may change by mutual agreement.

The counterparty has not been disclosed at its request, and the contract amount equals 108.48% of 2025 revenue. A KRW 5.64 billion sample-production service contract was also disclosed on September 21, 2026, running to March 31, 2028.

A March 2026 report put the order backlog at KRW 174.1 billion, but the April 2026 disclosure of the cancelled KRW 47.0 billion IBC contract falls after that figure.

The Indian project was reported as cancelled due to adjustments in local investment schedules, and a May 2026 report said talks on a replacement supply contract with a local Indian firm were in their final stage.

In bio, the chief executive said in June 2026 that a proof-of-concept for AMMR robots with a domestic bio company was nearing completion and production preparations would follow. A bio-process logistics robot was also planned for release in the second half of 2026, according to a March 2026 report.

The next checkpoints are whether quarterly revenue declines stop and whether revenue recognition from the large contracts begins, as shown in the 2027 annual and Q1 2027 quarterly filings.

08

Bull factors

Large equipment orders could add revenue

The KRW 33.26 billion contract signed on September 1, 2026 equals 108.48% of 2025 revenue and runs through August 31, 2027. A March 2026 report put the backlog at KRW 174.1 billion, several times 2025 revenue of KRW 30.7 billion.

Q3 2026 revenue was only KRW 2.79 billion, but the timing of revenue recognition on these contracts could change the quarterly revenue trend.

Robotics business aims to widen the customer base

A March 2026 report said about 250 AMRs worth roughly KRW 30 billion were supplied to a North American battery plant at the end of 2025. In June 2026 the company said an AMMR proof-of-concept with a bio company was nearing completion and production would follow.

If this business reaches customers outside batteries, the company's strategy would reduce its dependence on one industry's investment cycle.

Narrowing losses and lower cash outflow

The Q3 2026 operating loss of KRW 4.69 billion narrowed from KRW 6.25 billion a year earlier, and the net loss attributable to controlling shareholders also narrowed year on year. Operating cash outflow fell from -KRW 13.3 billion in 2024 to -KRW 4.46 billion in 2025. Whether the narrowing continues is the most direct test of the business transition.

09

Bear factors

Revenue decline has not yet reversed

Revenue fell from KRW 143.9 billion in 2022 to KRW 30.7 billion in 2025 and has declined for three straight years from 2023. Q3 2026 revenue of KRW 2.79 billion is far below the KRW 19.8 billion peak in Q4 2025.

With display equipment demand not recovering, the revenue contribution from new businesses has not yet shown up strongly in quarterly results.

Losses persist and widened in 2025

The operating loss widened to KRW 23.7 billion in 2025 from KRW 5.78 billion in 2024, and the 2025 operating margin was -77.2%. Net losses attributable to controlling shareholders over the last four quarters totaled KRW 23.5 billion. Operating losses have continued for five consecutive quarters.

Contract cancellation and disclosure credibility

The KRW 47.0 billion Indian contract cancellation was disclosed in April 2026, and in May 2026 the exchange announced a warning of possible designation as an unfaithful disclosure company due to the reversal of disclosures.

A Daily Union report said the 2025 audit report highlighted major transactions with related parties and named impairment review of new-business assets as a key audit matter.

Risk factors

Order concentration and cancellation

Most large contract counterparties are undisclosed, so customer concentration is hard to gauge from outside. The Indian KRW 47.0 billion contract was cancelled after local investment schedules shifted.

Payments are staged across advance, shipment and inspection milestones, so a gap in new orders could quickly affect the revenue trend.

Funding needs and balance-sheet strain

With operating and investing cash both outflowing, a news report said KRW 11.0 billion of 2025 financing inflows covered the shortfall. The debt-to-equity ratio rose from 13.8% in 2024 to 31.1% in 2025, and total liabilities reached KRW 42 billion. The timing and terms of funding could shape how fast the business transition proceeds.

Execution of the pivot and governance

An analysis cited upfront costs for robotics and automation rising before revenue follows. The auditor named impairment of new-business assets as a key audit matter.

Frequent ownership-change filings by executives and major shareholders, along with a history of disclosure reversals, are cited as factors weighing on governance stability.

Overall view

JAS Robotics' disclosed results show revenue falling from KRW 143.9 billion in 2022 to KRW 30.7 billion in 2025, with operating losses continuing since 2023.

In Q3 2026 revenue was KRW 2.79 billion and the operating loss was KRW 4.69 billion, narrower than a year earlier, yet revenue has fallen for three consecutive quarters and operating losses for five.

On the business side, a KRW 33.26 billion overseas battery-equipment contract from September 2026 and the robotics expansion are presented as new pillars.

Against that, the cancellation of a KRW 47.0 billion Indian order in April 2026, a warning of designation as an unfaithful disclosure company tied to disclosure reversals, and audit emphasis matters leave questions about how quickly orders will convert into earnings.

On the financial side, equity stood at KRW 135.2 billion at end-2025, but the debt-to-equity ratio rose to 31.1% and operating cash outflow continues. On valuation, the P/E cannot be calculated because of losses, and the P/B sits below 1x.

Bull factors are large orders and a narrowing loss trend; bear factors are continued revenue decline and reliance on external funding. The next checkpoints are whether revenue recovers and how the operating margin moves in the 2026 annual report and the Q1 2027 quarterly report.
