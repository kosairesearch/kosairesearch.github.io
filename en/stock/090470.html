Robotics Pivot Faces Test After Major Order Win

02

Business structure

Jasrobotics (formerly Jastech) began as a semiconductor manufacturing equipment business in 1995 and listed on KOSDAQ in 2007. The company operates five business segments -- display, semiconductor, secondary battery, bio, and logistics robots.

In the display segment it produces automated bonding equipment for TAB IC, driver IC, and PCB attachment onto panels, as well as laser-cut automation equipment.

The semiconductor segment supplies high-pressure water-jet deflashing automation equipment and plating automation systems, with a history of exports to major domestic clients and overseas markets including the Philippines, China, and Taiwan.

In secondary batteries, the company produces battery cell assembly and activation equipment as well as cap assembly welding automation, recently focusing on automation for prismatic (angular) cell production lines, which has translated into new orders.

All equipment is custom-designed and built to order based on customer specifications.

Display equipment was historically the company's largest revenue driver, and total revenue surpassed KRW 500 billion in 2017 on the back of Samsung Display's large-scale OLED investment, before shrinking as display capital spending cooled.

In response, the company renamed itself Jasrobotics in March 2026 and declared its intent to make autonomous mobile robots (AMR) and robotics automation equipment its core growth pillars, with management citing a long-term goal of KRW 1 trillion in annual revenue.

As a new business, it is developing an autonomous mobile manipulator robot (AMMR) combining an externally sourced robotic arm with its own mobile platform, targeting commercialization to replace manual labor at bio companies.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual results have swung sharply between profit and deep losses. In 2022 the company posted revenue of KRW 143.9 billion, operating profit of KRW 37.49 billion (26.0% operating margin), and owners' net income of KRW 27.55 billion, a clear profitable year.

In 2023, however, revenue fell roughly in half to KRW 72.09 billion, and the company swung to an operating loss of KRW 0.37 billion and an owners' net loss of KRW 1.29 billion.

In 2024 revenue declined further to KRW 63.63 billion, with the operating loss widening to KRW 5.78 billion and the owners' net loss to KRW 5.86 billion, while operating cash flow deteriorated to negative KRW 13.30 billion.

In 2025 revenue dropped sharply to KRW 30.66 billion and the operating loss expanded to KRW 23.67 billion (an operating margin of -77.2%), with the owners' net loss reaching KRW 24.77 billion.

Owners' equity fell from KRW 167.51 billion in 2023 to KRW 136.05 billion in 2025, while the debt ratio rose from 7.6% to 31.1% over the same period.

On a quarterly basis, revenue spiked to KRW 19.76 billion in the fourth quarter of 2025 before falling back to KRW 6.42 billion in the first quarter of 2026 and KRW 4.04 billion in the second quarter, reflecting volatility tied to project-based revenue recognition.

Operating losses in the first and second quarters of 2026 were KRW 6.93 billion and KRW 4.16 billion respectively, with owners' net losses of KRW 6.22 billion and KRW 6.79 billion, extending a run of losses across four consecutive quarters; the trailing four-quarter window (Q3 2025 through Q2 2026) showed a cumulative owners' net loss of KRW 23.77 billion, similar in magnitude to the full-year 2025 loss.

Separately, industry data indicated that first-half 2026 revenue rose 47.8% year-on-year while the operating loss and net loss narrowed by 6.0% and 7.1% respectively, suggesting some easing in the pace of losses.

05

Industry analysis

The company's end markets are split across display, semiconductor, and secondary battery equipment, each subject to different investment cycles.

In the first half of 2025, the semiconductor segment's results reportedly deteriorated as demand recovery was delayed despite rising power-efficiency requirements tied to AI, 5G, EVs, and IoT, while secondary battery and display sales also fell amid a slowing investment cycle.

In the first half of 2026, by contrast, the semiconductor equipment segment reportedly saw higher sales driven by AI, 5G, and EV-related demand, secondary battery equipment demand continued on the back of expanding EV battery factories, and the display segment shifted toward expected demand growth from rising orders for flexible and foldable displays.

In the secondary battery equipment market, continued investment in energy storage systems (ESS) and prismatic cell lines -- even as automakers moderate the pace of EV capital spending -- appears to align with the company's recent order flow.

In robotics, multiple listed companies have entered the domestic collaborative robot and autonomous mobile robot (AMR) market, with revenue scale and the pace of robotics-segment expansion varying by company.

Jasrobotics is layering a new robotics business on top of its existing semiconductor, display, and secondary battery equipment revenue base, giving it a different portfolio composition than pure-play robotics companies.

The competitive landscape mixes large equipment makers with newer robotics entrants, and the company's relative strength is generally seen as its automation equipment design and manufacturing experience across multiple industries and its broad customer base.

Outlook

The nearest confirmed event is the KRW 33.26 billion overseas secondary battery robotics automation supply contract signed September 1, 2026, running through August 31, 2027, with payments staged at 30% upfront, 40% upon shipping documents and performance bond receipt, 20% upon a performance inspection report and defect-liability bond receipt, and 10% upon a final operational inspection report.

When announcing its name change in March 2026, the company said it had signed a cumulative KRW 97.9 billion in secondary battery robotics automation contracts from December of the prior year through early February, with an order backlog of KRW 174.1 billion at that time, though this figure reflects a March 2026 disclosure and may differ from the current backlog.

On the new AMMR business, the company completed a proof-of-concept with a domestic bio company in June 2026, and its CEO stated that mass production would follow immediately after the PoC concluded, according to a June 9, 2026 ZDNet Korea interview.

Overseas, the company has referenced renewed efforts to re-enter the Indian battery market; a previously signed KRW 47 billion prismatic battery equipment supply contract with India's IBC, originally signed in July 2024, was terminated in April 2026 after repeated delays, which the company confirmed.

Management characterized the termination as an opportunity to pursue new business rather than a setback.

The company has articulated a long-term goal of reaching KRW 1 trillion in annual revenue by expanding beyond semiconductor and display equipment upgrades into secondary battery, logistics robot, and bio equipment businesses.

Bull factors

Large New Secondary Battery Robotics Order

The KRW 33.26 billion overseas secondary battery automation equipment contract signed in September 2026 equals 108.48% of full-year 2025 revenue, offering a potential demand signal after a period of stagnant sales.

The overseas counterparty suggests the company's prismatic battery automation technology may be gaining traction abroad. With the contract period running through August 2027, staged revenue recognition should continue over the coming year.

First-Half Revenue Growth and Narrowing Losses

First-half 2026 revenue reportedly rose 47.8% year-on-year, while the operating loss and net loss narrowed by 6.0% and 7.1% respectively, indicating some easing in the pace of losses. The semiconductor equipment segment is also said to have benefited from rising AI, 5G, and EV-related demand.

That said, given significant quarter-to-quarter volatility, whether this trend persists requires further confirmation.

Diversified Portfolio Plus New Robotics Business

The company operates across five business segments -- display, semiconductor, secondary battery, bio, and logistics robots -- providing some potential offset if investment weakens in any single industry.

Its new AMMR business completed a proof-of-concept with a bio company in June 2026 and is said to be preparing for mass production, potentially becoming a new revenue source. Management has articulated a long-term goal of KRW 1 trillion in annual revenue, signaling intent to keep expanding the business.

Bear factors

Structural Widening of Losses

The 2025 operating loss of KRW 23.67 billion (an operating margin of -77.2%) capped a third straight year of widening losses following the 2022 profit. As revenue fell 78.7% from KRW 143.9 billion in 2022 to KRW 30.66 billion in 2025, fixed costs and R&D spending were largely maintained, sharply eroding margins. Losses have continued into 2026, with net losses exceeding KRW 6 billion in both the first and second quarters.

High Volatility from Project-Based Revenue

Quarterly revenue has swung sharply, spiking to KRW 19.76 billion in the fourth quarter of 2025 before falling back to KRW 6.42 billion and KRW 4.04 billion in the first and second quarters of 2026, respectively.

Because large equipment contracts recognize payment in stages tied to inspection and commissioning milestones, revenue can concentrate in certain quarters while other quarters see gaps, a pattern that may recur going forward. This makes it difficult to assess the underlying trend in performance.

Rising Leverage and Liquidity Pressure

The debt ratio rose markedly from 7.6% in 2023 to 31.1% in 2025, and in June 2026 the company decided to take on an additional KRW 35 billion in short-term borrowing for working capital, pushing total short-term debt above KRW 72 billion -- equal to 25.9% of year-end 2025 equity.

This points to growing reliance on borrowed funds amid persistent losses. Operating cash flow also remained negative in both 2024 (KRW -13.30 billion) and 2025 (KRW -4.46 billion), suggesting the need for external financing could continue.

Risk factors

Contract Cancellation and Execution Risk

A KRW 47 billion prismatic battery equipment supply contract with India's IBC, originally signed in July 2024, was postponed multiple times before being terminated in April 2026.

Some new contracts also do not disclose the counterparty due to confidentiality requests, making it difficult for outside observers to verify the actual likelihood of execution and payment timing for large orders. If similar delays or cancellations recur, revenue visibility could again be undermined.

Extended Earnings Weakness

Over the trailing four quarters (Q3 2025 through Q2 2026), the cumulative owners' net loss reached KRW 23.77 billion, and no clear evidence of a turn to profit has yet been confirmed. It may take time for the new robotics and secondary battery automation business to fully offset the revenue gap left by legacy segments.

The possibility of continued losses until new orders translate into actual revenue and profit cannot be ruled out.

Capital Structure and Financing Risk

Rising leverage and repeated expansions of short-term borrowing raise the possibility of further capital-raising activity (such as rights offerings or convertible bonds), which could translate into dilution risk for existing shareholders.

Owners' equity has trended down from KRW 167.51 billion in 2023 to KRW 136.05 billion in 2025, meaning that if losses persist, the burden of managing capital adequacy could increase.

12

Overall view

Jasrobotics is in a transitional period, shifting its center of gravity from traditional display and semiconductor equipment toward robotics-based automation and secondary battery equipment.

Through 2025, revenue continued to decline and operating losses widened, increasing financial strain, while the expansion of short-term borrowing in June 2026 also pushed leverage metrics higher.

On the other hand, the large overseas order win in September 2026 and signs of first-half revenue growth with narrowing losses could be read as early results of the business transition.

That said, uncertainty remains around the execution of large contracts, as illustrated by the earlier cancellation of the India agreement, and the project-based revenue structure produces significant quarter-to-quarter volatility.

The timing of mass production for the new AMMR robot business and the pace at which recent new orders convert into actual revenue are likely to be key variables shaping future performance.

Investors should weigh upcoming quarterly results, contract execution progress, and financial health indicators together when forming a view.
