02

Business structure

Daejoo is a compound feed maker and seller founded in 1962 and listed on KOSDAQ in 1992, according to WiseReport. Most revenue comes from compound feed. Per FnGuide, the feed segment accounted for 99.43% of 2024 sales, veterinary medicines for 0.45%, and canned products for 0.12%.

Domestic sales were 99.30% of 2024 revenue, with exports at 0.70%. Subsidiary Namjeon Mulsan manufactures veterinary drugs, while the Incheon plant produces livestock feed for chickens, cattle, and pigs.

WiseReport says rising pet populations support expectations for the pet food market, and that the company is raising the revenue weight of pet food through high-quality product development and capacity expansion.

FnGuide attributes a 10.5% rise in cumulative operating profit for the first nine months of 2025 to premiumization of pet food, while cumulative revenue fell 3.7% over the same period. WiseReport describes the compound feed market as slowing, with intense competition among large players.

Per Alphasquare, 2025 revenue ranked seventh among nine listed feed-sector companies, against a sector average of KRW 451.9 billion.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual revenue rose from KRW 99.2 billion in 2022 to KRW 105 billion in 2023 (up 5.8%) and KRW 106 billion in 2024 (up 1.0%), then fell to KRW 101 billion in 2025 (down 4.8%). Among 2022 to 2025, 2024 was the largest year and 2022 the smallest.

Operating profit rose from KRW 5.53 billion in 2022 to KRW 7.68 billion in 2023 (up 39.1%), KRW 10 billion in 2024 (up 30.5%), and KRW 10.6 billion in 2025 (up 6.1%), marking three consecutive years of growth from 2023 through 2025. Operating margin widened from 5.6% in 2022 to 7.3%, 9.5%, and 10.5% in 2025.

Profit grew in 2025 even as revenue declined, which points to a change in the earnings structure, and FnGuide links this to premiumization of pet food.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders, by contrast, peaked at KRW 10.1 billion in 2023 (up 134.0% from KRW 4.34 billion in 2022), then fell to KRW 8.32 billion in 2024 (down 18.1%) and KRW 6.76 billion in 2025 (down 18.7%), two straight years of decline.

The two lines moved in opposite directions from 2024. Net income including non-controlling interests was disclosed at the same amounts as controlling-shareholder net income in each year.

Operating cash flow was KRW 6.93 billion in 2022, KRW 11.6 billion in 2023, KRW 11.3 billion in 2024, and KRW 14.9 billion in 2025, the highest of the four years.

Quarterly revenue went from KRW 24 billion in the third quarter of 2025 to KRW 26.5 billion in the fourth quarter (up 10.5% quarter on quarter), then KRW 25 billion in the first quarter of 2026, KRW 25.3 billion in the second, and KRW 23.1 billion in the third (down 8.4% quarter on quarter and 3.6% year on year).

Quarterly operating profit went from KRW 3.47 billion in the fourth quarter of 2025 to KRW 1.97 billion in the first quarter of 2026 (down 43.4%) and KRW 2.56 billion in the second quarter (up 30.2%), then swung to an operating loss of KRW 76.4 million in the third quarter, a turn into loss from a year earlier.

Revenue fell only 3.6% year on year in the third quarter, yet operating profit flipped to a loss, so margin swings were far larger than revenue swings. Controlling-shareholder net income was KRW 305 million in the third quarter, down 83.7% year on year.

The disclosed table does not break down the causes of the third-quarter operating loss, so the next annual report's line items are the point to check.

05

Industry analysis

According to WiseReport, growth in the compound feed market has slowed. Inflows of imported livestock products and reliance on imported raw materials add to earnings volatility, and intense competition among large companies adds pressure.

Because raw materials are sourced abroad, grain prices and exchange rates pass through to costs quickly. Demand for livestock feed is tied to livestock farming, so companies with a large livestock feed mix are more exposed to the livestock cycle.

A 2020 technical analysis report described Daejoo's livestock feed sales as stagnant and said the effect of the Jangham plant expansion on pet feed had been limited relative to growth potential. WiseReport says the company has since expanded capacity while raising pet food's share.

Among nine listed feed companies, Daejoo ranked seventh by 2025 revenue, a mid-to-lower position. Compared with large food companies in the same sector, its revenue base is smaller and its business is concentrated in feed. Intense competition with large feed makers may limit pricing power.

Pet food is presented as a route to offset weak demand in the core business, and whether it works will show up in the revenue mix and margin trend.

Risk factors

Concentration risk

The feed segment accounted for 99.43% of 2024 sales, so the business depends almost entirely on one segment. If livestock headcounts or feed prices shift, revenue and profit move with them. Pet food is gaining share, but the overall mix remains concentrated, which keeps exposure high.

Raw material and currency risk

WiseReport identifies reliance on imported raw materials as a driver of profit volatility. Higher grain prices or exchange-rate moves change costs, and quarterly profit can swing depending on how quickly those costs pass through to selling prices. The third-quarter 2026 operating loss should be read in this context of cost volatility.

Execution risk in the pet food shift

Expanding pet food requires high-quality product development and capacity investment. Costs often arrive before revenue during a transition, so earnings can be volatile.

A 2020 report judged that the Jangham plant expansion had limited effect on pet feed, so whether new capacity converts into sales needs to be verified.

Overall view

Daejoo is a classic feed company, with compound feed accounting for nearly all of its revenue. Operating profit and operating margin both rose from 2022 through 2025, and FnGuide attributes this partly to pet food premiumization. The balance sheet has also strengthened, with the debt-to-equity ratio down to 31.5%.

On the other hand, controlling-shareholder net income has declined for two consecutive years since its 2023 peak, and the third quarter of 2026 swung to an operating loss. The last four quarters of controlling-shareholder net income total KRW 4.81 billion.

On the industry side, slowing growth in compound feed, intense competition among large players, and reliance on imported raw materials are the weak points. Strengths include a rising pet food share, capacity expansion, and financial stability.

The next points to confirm are the fourth-quarter 2026 results in the annual report and whether operating margin recovers in later quarters.
