02

Business structure

Seoul Viosys is a compound semiconductor company that develops, manufactures and sells LED chips. According to its 2023 annual report, the company operates LED manufacturing as a single business segment and does not report separate segment revenue.

Its main product lines are UV LEDs used for sterilization, purification and deodorization, WICOP-technology LED chips made without wire bonding, visible LEDs for automotive, lighting, white goods, mobile phones and LCD backlight units, and new-business products including Micro LED, VCSEL and IR LED.

The same report said more than 85% of sales went to parent Seoul Semiconductor, which makes LED packages, with the remainder coming from in-house businesses such as UV. The structure is vertically linked: Seoul Viosys produces chips that Seoul Semiconductor packages.

The UV business is based on clean-technology developed jointly with US subsidiary SETi and is used to sterilize and purify water, air and surfaces. The company says it holds more than 4,000 proprietary patents spanning LED epitaxy, chips, modules and solutions.

Production sites are in Korea and Vietnam, and the Vietnam operation is presented as a key lever for cost reduction. New-business areas include Micro LED displays and optical communication for AI data centers. Revenue in 2023 was KRW 504 billion, and the business still centers on supplying LED chips.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual results show diverging trends for sales and profit.

Revenue went from KRW 438.8 billion in 2022 to KRW 504 billion in 2023 (up 14.9% year on year), KRW 699.2 billion in 2024 (up 38.7%) and KRW 763.8 billion in 2025 (up 9.2%), rising for three consecutive years from 2023 through 2025, with 2025 the largest year of 2022 to 2025.

Operating results moved the other way: an operating loss of KRW 62.1 billion in 2022 and KRW 72.9 billion in 2023 gave way to operating profit of KRW 3.49 billion in 2024, a turnaround to profit. Operating profit in 2025 was KRW 226 million, down 93.5% year on year, leaving an operating margin of 0.0%.

The 2024 operating margin of 0.5% was also low relative to sales. Net income attributable to controlling shareholders turned to profit at KRW 7.11 billion in 2024 but swung back to a loss of KRW 15.4 billion in 2025.

Net income including non-controlling interests was KRW 18.1 billion in 2024 and a net loss of KRW 13.6 billion in 2025, a different path from the controlling-shareholder figure.

Of total equity of KRW 169.9 billion at year-end 2025, KRW 77.8 billion belonged to controlling shareholders, so non-controlling interests make up a large share.

Operating cash flow stayed positive in all four years: KRW 65.5 billion in 2022, KRW 63.6 billion in 2023, KRW 95.4 billion in 2024 and KRW 80.3 billion in 2025.

Quarterly, an operating loss of KRW 779 million in the 2025 third quarter gave way to operating profit of KRW 15.1 billion in the fourth quarter, followed by KRW 10.4 billion in the first quarter of 2026 (down 31.2% quarter on quarter), KRW 10.2 billion in the second quarter (down 1.7%) and KRW 12.4 billion in the third quarter (up 22.0%).

The company's IR materials said EBITDA came to KRW 31 billion in the third quarter and that profitability was held despite a lower won-dollar rate. Net income attributable to controlling shareholders fell over the same stretch, so the operating recovery and the bottom-line trend diverge.

The gap between operating profit and controlling-shareholder net income reflects items below operating profit, such as non-operating gains and losses, taxes and non-controlling interests.

05

Industry analysis

Seoul Viosys sells into LED end markets including home appliances, automotive, display backlighting and lighting. The company cited growing global demand from end markets as the basis for its fourth-quarter 2026 revenue guidance.

UV LEDs are tied to rising demand for water, air and surface sterilization, which is extending their use into appliances such as dishwashers and refrigerators. Conversely, industry commentary notes that lighting is competitive because longer product life and falling prices have reduced replacement demand.

Cost competition from China and others has been cited as a threat, and the company has said Vietnam operations support cost reduction alongside profitability. The chip business is closely linked to parent Seoul Semiconductor's packaging volumes, so the parent's order flow tends to pass through to Seoul Viosys.

Differentiators cited include wire-free WICOP technology and UV chip technology. Micro LED discussions are extending from displays toward optical interconnect for AI data centers.

Unlike sector peers such as Samsung Electro-Mechanics and LG Innotek, which run broader component businesses, Seoul Viosys is a specialist LED chip company, which gives it a different competitive position.

Risk factors

Customer concentration

According to the 2023 annual report, more than 85% of sales went to Seoul Semiconductor. Volumes and utilization could shift with the parent's packaging demand and order terms. Expanding the share of self-branded and direct sales remains a structural challenge.

Cost competition and exchange rates

Cost competition from China and others has been cited as a threat, and the company says its Vietnam operations are helping lower costs. The won-dollar rate moved from about 1,483.85 in the first half of 2026 to about 1,423.38 in the third quarter. Profitability could swing with exchange-rate direction and raw material prices.

Timing of new-business commercialization

AI data center optical interconnect and Micro LED are described as being in partnership discussions and production preparation rather than meaningful revenue. No timetable has been published for conversion to mass production and sales. Until these businesses grow, earnings will be driven mainly by the LED chip business.
