SIMPAC: Profit Rebound as Ferroalloy Reliance Shrinks, Merger Litigation Continues

SIMPAC's 2025 revenue rose to KRW 1.18 trillion, but its operating margin was only 3.9%, quarterly operating profit ranged from KRW 17 billion to KRW 26.8 billion in the first three quarters of 2026, and litigation over the SIMPAC Holdings merger continues after a first-instance dismissal in September 2026.

02

Business structure

SIMPAC Inc. is a primary steel manufacturer listed on KOSPI in July 1989. Its core business was once hydraulic presses, but it shifted to ferroalloys as that line expanded.

Press reports said cheaper imported ferroalloys cut its ferroalloy sales from KRW 463.6 billion in 2022 to KRW 298.8 billion in 2023, a 35.5% decline. From May 1, 2024, the company halted ferroalloy production at its Dangjin plant and produced ferroalloys only at its Pohang plant.

Its service segment, which includes metal trading and ferrous scrap distribution, grew from KRW 6.3 billion in 2022 to KRW 37.9 billion in 2023 according to the same reporting. It also acquired a 33.3% stake in scrap distributor Bedaya. In 2024 it acquired 100% of auto-parts maker KDA for KRW 95 billion.

Its consumer arm, SIMPAC Atelier, holds exclusive domestic distribution agreements for the UK niche fragrance brand Perfumer H and the Danish cafe brand La Cabra. On governance, the company absorbed its parent SIMPAC Holdings in a merger with a effective date of December 12, 2025.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual revenue went from KRW 672.2 billion in 2022 to KRW 609.4 billion in 2023 (down 9.3%), then to KRW 785.1 billion in 2024 (up 28.8%) and KRW 1.18 trillion in 2025 (up 50.2%). After the 2023 low, revenue rose for two consecutive years from 2024 through 2025, and 2025 was the largest year of 2022 to 2025.

Operating profit fell from KRW 118.9 billion in 2022 to KRW 17.5 billion in 2023 (down 85.3%), alongside the decline in ferroalloy sales. It then recovered to KRW 24.5 billion in 2024 (up 39.7%) and KRW 46 billion in 2025 (up 87.9%).

In 2025, operating profit grew faster than revenue, lifting the operating margin from 3.1% to 3.9%.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders went from KRW 76.1 billion in 2022 to a net loss of KRW 6.46 billion in 2023, a swing into deficit, then turned to a profit of KRW 27.5 billion in 2024 and fell to KRW 20.6 billion in 2025 (down 25.0%).

Net income including non-controlling interests was KRW 22.4 billion in 2025, a different figure. Operating cash flow moved from KRW -20.8 billion in 2024 to KRW 48.4 billion in 2025.

Press reports said receivables and inventory tied up cash in the first half of 2024, with operating cash flow at a net outflow of KRW 30.4 billion.

Quarterly, revenue was KRW 300.9 billion with operating profit of KRW 9.56 billion in Q3 2025, and KRW 346.5 billion with KRW 23.6 billion in Q4 2025, up 147.1% from the prior quarter. In Q1 2026 operating profit was KRW 21.6 billion.

In Q2 2026 revenue was KRW 461.1 billion (up 46.2% from the prior quarter) and operating profit KRW 26.8 billion (up 24.1%). In Q3 2026 revenue was KRW 403.4 billion and operating profit KRW 17 billion (down 36.6% from the prior quarter, up 77.8% from a year earlier). Net income attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 16.9 billion in Q2 and KRW 7.3 billion in Q3 2026.

05

Industry analysis

The ferroalloy market has faced intensifying domestic price and volume competition as cheaper imports from Southeast Asia have grown. The company cut supply by halting Dangjin plant operations in 2024, but press reports in the first half of 2024 pointed to higher inventory and payables burdens from that adjustment.

Ferrosilicon is produced at a Pohang plant that a group affiliate, SIMPAC Industries, acquired from POSCO, according to press reports.

International ferrosilicon prices peaked at USD 4,150 per ton in 2021 and fell to a low of USD 1,095 per ton in July 2025 on oversupply, and remain in adjustment, per a October 6, 2026 report. The same report said the plant's profits depended heavily on that price cycle.

Another source cited industry sources saying Chinese production halts had lifted international prices. Ferrous scrap distribution is exposed to scrap price swings, and the auto-parts business through SIMPAC KDA is linked to demand changes in vehicle supply chains.

Because the company is diversifying away from a single ferroalloy product line, its business structure differs from that of listed steel companies centered on conventional steel products.

06

Outlook

The company's direction is visible in how it deploys capital and expands new businesses. A May 2026 press report said SIMPAC committed KRW 35.4 billion to a project fund run by private equity firm Blue Rif Equity Partners, acting as anchor LP with about 46% of the fund.

The report also noted that the fund operator's CEO is a son-in-law of SIMPAC's chairman, a related-party link. Consumer-facing new businesses are being pursued through SIMPAC Atelier.

The Pohang ferrosilicon factory site was sold to an AI data center developer, with a first sale of KRW 11.7 billion, and a second sale was reported to bring the total to about KRW 25 billion. On governance, the absorption merger of SIMPAC Holdings led to the issuance and listing of 34,212,243 new shares.

MJ Partners filed a suit seeking to void the merger board resolution and an injunction in November 2025, the injunction was dismissed in December 2025, and the appeal was rejected in February 2026. A further merger-invalidity suit was filed in May 2026.

The plaintiff reportedly challenged the merger ratio and the valuation of the unlisted holding company. The company said it would respond actively through legal procedures, and disclosed the dismissal of that suit on September 23, 2026.

08

Bull factors

Revenue expansion and diversification

Revenue reached KRW 1.18 trillion in 2025, up 50.2% from the prior year. Since 2023, when ferroalloy sales declined, the service segment has grown, and the 2024 KDA acquisition added an auto-parts business. The shift away from reliance on a single product line is showing up in the revenue base.

Earnings recovery

Operating profit rose for two consecutive years from 2024 through 2025 after the 2023 low, reaching KRW 46 billion in 2025. Q2 2026 operating profit was KRW 26.8 billion, and trailing four-quarter controlling-shareholder net income totals KRW 30 billion. The operating margin improved to 3.9% in 2025 from 3.1% in 2024.

Legal progress on merger suits

An appeal was rejected in August 2026, and in September 2026 the first-instance merger-invalidity claim was dismissed. The company said it would respond actively through legal procedures. With the merger completed, the court's refusal to grant the claims can be read as reducing uncertainty over the merger's validity.

Bear factors

Import pressure on ferroalloys

Ferroalloy sales fell 35.5% from KRW 463.6 billion in 2022 to KRW 298.8 billion in 2023. Halting Dangjin production in May 2024 reduced supply but also signaled a weaker ferroalloy revenue base. As long as cheaper imports keep growing, the timing of any recovery in ferroalloy prices and volumes is uncertain.

Thin margins and lower profit

The operating margin fell from 17.7% in 2022 to 3.9% in 2025. The decline in net income attributable to controlling shareholders moves in the same direction as this lower operating margin.

Q3 2026 operating profit was KRW 17 billion, down 36.6% from the prior quarter, showing that revenue growth has not consistently translated into profit.

Financial burden and governance disputes

The debt ratio rose from 60.9% in 2022 to 99.0% in 2025. Operating cash flow was KRW -20.8 billion in 2024, and press reports flagged working capital strain. Merger disputes with MJ Partners have been disclosed repeatedly since November 2025, keeping governance uncertainty in view.

Risk factors

Governance and litigation

Merger litigation began with an injunction request in November 2025, followed by a merger-invalidity suit in May 2026, an appeal in July 2026, rejection of that appeal in August 2026, and a first-instance dismissal in September 2026. Further challenges by the plaintiff could extend the process. The outcome could affect the merger's validity and the ownership structure.

Raw material and price swings

Ferroalloys and ferrosilicon are sensitive to import volumes and international prices. Press reports said ferrosilicon prices fell from a 2021 peak to a July 2025 low. A renewed price move could affect both inventory valuation and revenue scale.

Balance sheet and working capital

The debt ratio rose to 99.0% at end-2025 from 60.9% in 2022. Press reports described cash being tied up in receivables and inventory as sales expanded. Operating cash flow recovered to KRW 48.4 billion in 2025, and whether that continues will show up in the next periodic report.

Overall view

SIMPAC has expanded revenue to KRW 1.18 trillion in 2025 by broadening into service and auto-parts businesses while ferroalloy sales declined. However, the operating margin fell from 17.7% in 2022 to 3.9% in 2025, and net income attributable to controlling shareholders dropped 25.0% to KRW 20.6 billion in 2025.

Quarterly operating profit ranged from KRW 17 billion to KRW 26.8 billion across the first three quarters of 2026, and the trailing four-quarter controlling-shareholder net income totals KRW 30 billion.

Operating cash flow recovered from KRW -20.8 billion in 2024 to KRW 48.4 billion in 2025, but the debt ratio rose to 99.0%. The price-to-book ratio sits in a low range and the dividend yield is above the sector average, but these should be read alongside the earnings base and the merger issues.

Merger litigation has moved toward resolution with the August 2026 appeal rejection and the September 2026 first-instance dismissal, though further challenges remain possible.

Revenue expansion and earnings recovery are the strengths, while thin margins, financial burden and governance uncertainty are the weaknesses, and they carry comparable weight. The next checkpoints are the 2026 annual report, due by late March 2027, and the Q1 2027 quarterly report.
