SIMPAC: Profit Recovery Tests Whether Ferroalloy Dependence Eases

SIMPAC's revenue and operating profit both rose in 2025, signaling a profit recovery, but net income attributable to controlling shareholders fell and ferroalloy import competition and working-capital pressure remain open questions.

02

Business structure

SIMPAC was founded in 1973 as Dongsung Development Industrial and listed on the KOSPI market in 1989, separating from the Ssangyong group in 2001 before adopting its current name. The company operates press, ferroalloy, roll and trading business units.

The press segment makes and sells metal forming machinery and peripheral equipment, and company profile summaries say it is working to win global automaker customers. Ferroalloys were once the core business, but low-priced Southeast Asian imports reduced sales.

According to an IB Tomato report from April 2024, ferroalloy sales fell 35.5% from KRW 463.6 billion in 2022 to KRW 298.8 billion in 2023. The company then disclosed that it would halt ferroalloy production at its Dangjin plant from May 1, 2024, leaving the Pohang plant as the only production site.

Service businesses such as metal trading and ferrous scrap distribution have filled part of the gap, and the same report says service segment sales rose from KRW 6.3 billion in 2022 to KRW 37.9 billion in 2023.

The trading segment is responding to external changes through more sophisticated hedging and a more diversified supply chain. The auto parts segment has begun mass production of new electric vehicle items and continues to invest in equipment.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual revenue fell from KRW 672.2 billion in 2022 to KRW 609.4 billion in 2023, a 9.3% decline, then rose 28.8% to KRW 785.1 billion in 2024 and 50.2% to KRW 1.18 trillion in 2025.

Reporting attributes much of the 2023 sales decline to lower ferroalloy sales. 2025 was the largest year of the 2022 to 2025 period for revenue.

Operating profit dropped from KRW 118.9 billion in 2022 to KRW 17.5 billion in 2023, a decline of 85.3%, then rose 39.7% to KRW 24.5 billion in 2024 and 87.9% to KRW 46 billion in 2025, so profit has increased in each of the two years since the 2023 low.

The operating margin, however, was only 17.7% in 2022, 2.9% in 2023, 3.1% in 2024 and 3.9% in 2025, meaning profit has not kept pace with sales growth.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders went from KRW 76.1 billion in 2022 to a net loss of KRW 6.46 billion in 2023, a swing into the red, then returned to a profit of KRW 27.5 billion in 2024 and fell 25.0% to KRW 20.6 billion in 2025.

Net income including non-controlling interests was KRW 22.4 billion in 2025, a different figure from the attributable number. In the quarters since 2025, third-quarter 2026 revenue was KRW 403.4 billion, up 34.1% year on year, and operating profit was KRW 17 billion, up 77.8%.

Second-quarter 2026 revenue was KRW 461.1 billion with operating profit of KRW 26.8 billion and attributable net income of KRW 16.9 billion, a gap between operating and attributable profit that is large relative to other quarters. Attributable net income over the most recent four quarters totaled KRW 30 billion.

Operating cash flow was KRW 2.97 billion in 2022, KRW 6.41 billion in 2023, negative KRW 20.8 billion in 2024 and KRW 4.84 billion in 2025. Fiscal 2025 results were driven partly by business diversification, as a 2025 third-quarter summary reported nine-month revenue up 54.8% and operating profit up 36.3%.

05

Industry analysis

SIMPAC's core ferroalloy business sits in a market sensitive to steel output and import prices. According to an April 2024 IB Tomato report, sales of its core business have declined each year as low-priced ferroalloys entered Korea.

The same report described a weakening steel market that forced the company to cut part of its ferroalloy output. The company responded with production adjustments, and the reporting linked this to higher inventory and weaker cash flow.

Steel industry observers said the sales mix could change if new businesses grow in weight. The trading segment is described as managing exposure to metal price swings through hedging. The press segment makes metal forming machinery and is tied to demand from automakers and auto parts makers.

The auto parts segment is shifting toward electric vehicle components. Peers in the steel and metals sector, including Korea Zinc, Hyundai Steel Company and Poongsan, have different raw materials and product mixes, so direct comparison is only partial. SIMPAC's position in the sector cycle will depend on how far it can reduce its ferroalloy weight.

06

Outlook

Three business threads are visible. First, the trading segment is responding to external changes through more sophisticated hedging and a more diversified supply chain, and a company profile summary described first-half 2026 results as improved from the prior year.

Second, the auto parts segment has begun mass production of new electric vehicle items, so how much that volume shows up in earnings is the key question. Third, the press segment continues efforts to win global automaker customers.

On governance, the company had pursued a reverse absorption merger of its parent holding company, SIMPAC Holdings.

In February 2026 a disclosure said an appeal by MJ Partners against the injunction denial was dismissed, and in a September 23 disclosure the company said it had won a lawsuit seeking to void the merger, with the Incheon District Court dismissing the plaintiff's claim.

Whether further appeals proceed is not clear from available material. The 2026 annual business report, the next regular filing, will show how the subsidiary and segment profitability is organized.

08

Bull factors

Revenue and operating profit recovering together

Revenue reached KRW 1.18 trillion in 2025 and operating profit KRW 46 billion, up 50.2% and 87.9% year on year. Third-quarter 2026 revenue rose 34.1% year on year to KRW 403.4 billion, and operating profit rose 77.8% to KRW 17 billion. Sales and operating profit are moving in the same direction.

Growth in trading and service businesses

An April 2024 report said service segment sales rose from KRW 6.3 billion in 2022 to KRW 37.9 billion in 2023 after the company entered scrap distribution. A company profile summary says the trading segment responded flexibly to external changes through more sophisticated hedging and supply chain diversification. Moves away from the ferroalloy-centered structure are showing up in the business mix.

Auto parts and press customer base

The press segment makes and sells metal forming machinery and peripheral equipment while pursuing global automaker customers. In auto parts, mass production of new electric vehicle items has begun, and the company continues to invest in equipment. Growth in businesses tied to automaker supply chains could broaden earnings beyond ferroalloys.

Bear factors

Ferroalloy sales decline and import competition

According to an April 2024 report, ferroalloy sales fell 35.5% from KRW 463.6 billion in 2022 to KRW 298.8 billion in 2023, which was cited as the main driver of the 9.3% drop in total sales that year. The company halted production at its Dangjin plant from May 1, 2024. Continued low-priced Southeast Asian imports could limit how much the ferroalloy business recovers.

Low operating margin and falling attributable profit

The operating margin fell from 17.7% in 2022 to 3.9% in 2025. Net income attributable to controlling shareholders dropped 25.0% to KRW 20.6 billion in 2025, while net income including non-controlling interests was KRW 22.4 billion.

Third-quarter 2026 operating profit fell 36.6% from the prior quarter, and quarterly attributable profit has swung widely.

Volatile cash flow and rising leverage

Operating cash flow was negative KRW 20.8 billion in 2024 before recovering to KRW 48.4 billion in 2025, and it has swung sharply from year to year. The debt-to-equity ratio rose from 60.9% in 2022 to 99.0% in 2025. Total liabilities stood at KRW 663.5 billion and total equity at KRW 670.3 billion at the end of 2025.

10

Risk factors

Governance and legal proceedings

Legal disputes over the reverse absorption merger of SIMPAC Holdings appear to have moved past the first-instance stage after a favorable disclosure. Whether MJ Partners pursues further challenges is not clear from the material reviewed.

Reports say SIMPAC Holdings holds 52.38% of SIMPAC and that the transfer of a 33.6% stake in the holding company tied to the chairman is linked to succession. Completion of the merger and any change in control could affect financing and decision-making.

Metal price swings and hedge effectiveness

Improvement in the trading segment relies on more sophisticated hedging and a more diversified supply chain. Sharp moves in metal prices or changes in hedge counterparties and terms could reduce the effect hedging delivers.

As trading becomes a larger share of sales, price swings will weigh more heavily on quarterly results. The sharp change in operating profit between the second and third quarters of 2026 reflects this exposure.

New business ramp-up and customer wins

The auto parts segment is moving into mass production of new electric vehicle items, while the press segment aims to win global automaker customers. Delays in production schedules or orders below expectations could leave equipment investment without matching profit. Slower customer wins would also delay the goal of expanding the auto parts share of sales.

12

Overall view

SIMPAC's revenue reached KRW 1.18 trillion in 2025 and operating profit KRW 46 billion, so both grew together, but a 3.9% operating margin and attributable net income of KRW 20.6 billion, down 25.0%, show that the recovery is still thin.

Ferroalloy sales dropped sharply in 2023, and after the Dangjin plant halt the business mix has been shifting toward trading, services and auto parts.

Third-quarter 2026 revenue and operating profit both rose from the prior year, which is a positive signal, but quarterly attributable profit has swung widely, so earnings durability needs further confirmation. The debt-to-equity ratio of 99.0% and the negative operating cash flow in 2024 remain financial burdens.

The legal dispute over the merger appears to have cleared a key stage with a favorable first-instance disclosure, though follow-up steps remain. The next checkpoints are the preliminary fourth-quarter 2026 results and the 2026 annual business report.
