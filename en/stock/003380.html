Report overview

Holding company profits recover; debt and development timing are key

02

Business structure

Harim Holdings is a holding company that owns stakes in its core subsidiaries, which span shipping, animal feed, livestock and poultry processing, food manufacturing and home shopping. It was designated a large business group subject to mutual investment limits in May 2025.

The group's businesses are shipping, feed, food (poultry, hog and home meal replacement) and distribution. In the first quarter of 2026, revenue was split as follows: shipping 37.7%, feed 25.6%, food 24.5%, distribution 4.5% and the holding company 2.3%.

Operating profit was split differently: shipping 42.3%, the holding company 26.2%, feed 22.4%, distribution 6.4% and food 0.4%, making shipping the largest contributor. The shipping unit's base is considered stable because of long-term contracts with domestic and overseas cargo owners.

The feed unit earns steady profit on demand from within the group. The food unit holds a leading market position in its industry and returned to operating profit in the first quarter of 2026. In the distribution unit, first-quarter revenue rose 6.4% on demand for health supplements and beauty products.

The holding company itself depends on dividends and royalties from subsidiaries, so subsidiary results and dividend policy directly shape its assets and cash flow.

04

Earnings analysis

Based on DART periodic reports, annual revenue fell from KRW 13.8 trillion in 2022 to KRW 12.1 trillion in 2023 (down 12.4% year on year), then rose to KRW 12.3 trillion in 2024 and KRW 13.2 trillion in 2025, growing two straight years from 2024 through 2025. 2022 was the largest revenue year and 2023 the smallest.

Operating profit fell 39.7% to KRW 567.5 billion in 2023 from KRW 941.3 billion in 2022, then rose 34.9% to KRW 765.5 billion in 2024 and 15.9% to KRW 887.4 billion in 2025.

The operating margin moved from 6.8% in 2022 to 4.7% in 2023, 6.2% in 2024 and 6.7% in 2025, recovering since 2023 but not yet back to the 2022 level.

Controlling-shareholder net income dropped from KRW 244 billion in 2022 to KRW 37.1 billion in 2023 (down 84.8%) and KRW 26.4 billion in 2024 (down 28.8%), before recovering to KRW 229.7 billion in 2025 (up 771.1%).

Net income including non-controlling interests was KRW 513.8 billion in 2025; the gap from controlling-shareholder profit is the share held by outside investors in subsidiaries.

At the end of 2025 the debt ratio was 170.9%, above 162.3% in 2022, and total equity of KRW 6.28 trillion included KRW 3.23 trillion attributable to controlling shareholders. Operating cash flow fell to KRW 878.8 billion in 2024 before reaching KRW 1.14 trillion in 2025.

Quarterly revenue rose for four consecutive quarters, from KRW 3.31 trillion in the third quarter of 2025 to KRW 4.17 trillion in the third quarter of 2026, while operating profit fell 32.0% to KRW 173.5 billion in the fourth quarter of 2025 and again to KRW 227.1 billion in the third quarter of 2026, down 11.0% year on year.

Controlling-shareholder net income swung to a KRW 1.08 billion loss in the fourth quarter of 2025, turned positive at KRW 71.9 billion in the first quarter of 2026, and reached KRW 133.7 billion in the third quarter of 2026, up 176.2% year on year; the trailing four-quarter total was KRW 252.7 billion.

Operating profit fell in the third quarter of 2026 while controlling-shareholder profit rose sharply, so the items outside operating profit deserve a close look in the annual report.

According to the first-quarter 2026 IR, shipping operating profit rose 24.3% and led group results, while feed and livestock revenue rose 9.5% but operating profit was up only 0.1%.

05

Industry analysis

The shipping unit benefited from a strong bulk market and a larger LNG-led revenue base, lifting first-quarter 2026 revenue 6.5% and operating profit 24.3%, according to the company's IR materials.

KIS Rating noted that the shipping cycle weakened in 2023 and rebounded in 2024, and that long-term contract volumes support the earnings outlook.

A domestic research report said the core shipping subsidiary raised the quality of earnings by expanding its LNG and tanker mix despite bulk-market volatility (reported March 2026).

The feed and livestock unit's profitability fell in 2023 on lower hog prices and recovered in 2024 on lower grain costs and higher overseas feed sales in 2025. Analysts noted that first-quarter 2026 profitability improved as avian influenza cut chick supply and raised broiler prices.

The food unit holds a leading position in its industry, but its home meal replacement and instant noodle business, run through a subsidiary, has not covered fixed costs with its limited revenue, and operating losses have persisted.

The subsidiary's operating loss widened from KRW 29.4 billion in 2020 to KRW 109.6 billion in 2023 and KRW 146.7 billion in 2025.

A newspaper report from November 12, 2026 said that subsidiary's revenue rose from KRW 21.7 billion in 2021 to KRW 109.4 billion last year, but cumulative operating losses since 2021 reached about KRW 530 billion.

The group presents a food value chain running from grain through shipping, feed, livestock and distribution as a source of strength.

06

Outlook

Harim Industry will spin off its food business division effective December 1, 2026, and the newly created Harim Food will take over the food operations including the Dmisik brand.

The surviving Harim Industry will handle real-estate development, including the Yangjae urban high-tech logistics complex, and Harim Holdings will hold 100% of both companies after the split.

The Yangjae project carries a budget of KRW 6.8 trillion, to be funded by KRW 3.86 trillion in sales proceeds from residential units, KRW 650 billion in bank borrowings and KRW 2.3518 trillion in equity, according to a May 2026 report.

The land held for the project was appraised at KRW 1,627.5 billion in 2025, roughly four times the KRW 452.5 billion paid in May 2016. Permitting has nonetheless been delayed, and one report attributed the delay in part to controversy over preferential treatment.

KIS Rating said that since the Yangjae development entered its active phase, funding needs have become a key item to monitor. The group is pursuing 16 newbuilding vessels and 10 secondhand VLCCs to add revenue backed by long-term cargo contracts, according to the first-quarter 2026 IR.

The food unit returned to operating profit in the first quarter of 2026, so the profitability trend after the Harim Food spin-off is the next point to check.

08

Bull factors

Long-term contract base in shipping

KIS Rating said in its July 2026 report that the shipping unit's business base is secured by long-term contracts with domestic and overseas cargo owners. In the first quarter of 2026 the shipping unit accounted for 42.3% of operating profit, the largest share in the group.

The company said it plans to add long-term contract-backed revenue by acquiring 16 newbuildings and 10 secondhand VLCCs.

Asset value of the Yangjae land and other holdings

KIS Rating said that, given the 27,600-pyeong site's size, location and official land price, the property assets are worth more than their book value (July 2026). A 2026 news report put the 2025 appraised value of the Yangjae land at KRW 1,627.5 billion, about four times the KRW 452.5 billion paid in May 2016. The timing at which development income turns into cash remains the key variable for the asset value.

Food profit turnaround and shareholder return review

The food unit returned to operating profit in the first quarter of 2026, and poultry and hog sales volumes rose, according to the company.

A domestic research house cited the expansion of dividends from its core shipping subsidiary and possible treasury-share cancellation and higher dividends at the holding company as shareholder-value drivers (March 2026). It did not present an investment rating or target price.

09

Bear factors

Holding-company borrowings and guarantee burden

KIS Rating put double leverage at 162.7% at the end of March 2026 and said the guarantee limit for affiliates stood at KRW 160.5 billion. Standalone net borrowings rose from KRW 599.9 billion at the end of 2020 to KRW 1.1165 trillion at the end of March 2026.

The agency said continued capital injections into subsidiaries are increasing the holding company's borrowing burden.

Subsidiary operating losses and cumulative deficits

The operating loss at Harim Industry widened from KRW 29.4 billion in 2020 to KRW 109.6 billion in 2023 and KRW 146.7 billion in 2025, according to KIS Rating. A news report dated November 12, 2026 said cumulative operating losses since 2021 total about KRW 530 billion. The split of the subsidiary's food division on December 1, 2026 will make profit and loss by business clearer.

Earnings strength and quarterly volatility

The 2025 operating margin of 6.7% is below the 6.8% of 2022, and the debt ratio of 170.9% is above the 162.3% of 2022. Third-quarter 2026 operating profit fell 11.0% year on year to KRW 227.1 billion.

Controlling-shareholder profit swung to a KRW 1.08 billion loss in the fourth quarter of 2025, so quarterly volatility may be cited as a weakness in earnings persistence.

10

Risk factors

Holding-company debt and guarantees (financial)

As a pure holding company that relies on subsidiary dividends, the parent's cash flow reacts directly to changes in subsidiary results or dividend policy. KIS Rating cites affiliate guarantees and potential support burdens as the basis for structural subordination. Double leverage that stays at a high level is also flagged as a financial risk.

Yangjae development and permits (business)

With a project budget of KRW 6.8 trillion, any delay in permits or failure to raise funds could push back the schedule. A news report said the burden from controversy over preferential treatment has delayed permits (May 2026). The funding plan assumes KRW 3.86 trillion in sales proceeds, so its outcome depends on actual unit sales.

Ownership structure and shareholdings (shareholders)

A related shareholder company bought about 2.03 million shares on the open market from November 2025 to April 2026, raising its stake from 16.69% at the end of September 2025 to about 18.5%. Combined holdings of the family-linked entities total 24.53%, above the 21.10% held by chairman Kim Hong-guk. Analysts link the stake build-up to the second-generation succession.

12

Overall view

Harim Holdings' controlling-shareholder net income recovered to KRW 229.7 billion in 2025 from KRW 26.4 billion in 2024, and operating profit and operating cash flow have also recovered since the 2023 low.

However, the 2025 operating margin of 6.7% has not yet returned to the 6.8% of 2022, and third-quarter 2026 operating profit fell 11.0% year on year. Controlling-shareholder profit has swung between losses and profits from quarter to quarter.

The mix of shipping, feed, food and distribution gives diversification across different cycles, but the holding company relies on subsidiary dividends and double leverage stood at 162.7% at the end of March 2026.

Some assessments say the Yangjae land is worth more than its book value, but a KRW 6.8 trillion budget and permit timing remain open. The December 1, 2026 food spin-off and the 2026 annual report are the next checkpoints.

The current market capitalization sits below the book value attributable to controlling shareholders, and how that gap reads will depend on when the property value is realized and how borrowings are managed.
