Aluko: US Expansion Amid Renewed Quarterly Losses

Aluko is expanding its U.S. production base; Q3 2026 operating profit rose to KRW 13.3 billion, but the attributable net loss widened to KRW 15.5 billion.

  1. 1

  2. 2

02

Business structure

Aluko vertically integrates the aluminum value chain, from billet casting through extrusion, fabrication and installation. A domestic brokerage described it in an April 2026 report as the only domestic company with this structure.

Based on a press report on 2023 product mix, building materials accounted for 43%, display 18%, solar 8%, specialty industrial materials 8%, EV batteries 7% and others 16%.

The core building materials business includes window systems such as formwork and curtain walls, and a domestic credit rating agency (NICE) said the building materials share of revenue stays at roughly 40-50%. The new strategic products are solar module frames and EV battery cases.

Battery parts have reportedly secured more than KRW 1.5 trillion in orders from LG Energy Solution and another battery maker since 2021. Through a subsidiary, the company also runs a solar power generation business. Overseas, an EV battery parts plant in Jackson, Tennessee was completed in March 2025.

Its customers span specialty industrial materials, TV displays, mobile devices, solar, and automotive and railway parts. Its competitive edge is cited as an 8,000-ton extrusion press, among the largest in Korea.

04

Earnings analysis

On a reported basis, revenue moved from KRW 645.4 billion in 2022 to KRW 591.2 billion in 2023 (down 8.4% year on year), KRW 622.9 billion in 2024 (up 5.4%) and KRW 575.7 billion in 2025 (down 7.6%). Revenue was largest in 2022 and smallest in 2025 within the 2022-2025 range.

Operating profit was KRW 34.6 billion in 2022, KRW 37.6 billion in 2023 (up 8.8%), KRW 38.1 billion in 2024 (up 1.1%) and KRW 34.9 billion in 2025 (down 8.3%), with 2024 the largest year.

Attributable net income peaked at KRW 18.5 billion in 2023 (up 33.0%), then fell to KRW 18.2 billion in 2024 (down 2.0%) and KRW 15.5 billion in 2025 (down 14.5%), declining for two consecutive years.

Net income including non-controlling interests was KRW 15.7 billion, KRW 20.5 billion, KRW 17.7 billion and KRW 15.9 billion for 2022 through 2025; it is a different figure from attributable net income. The operating margin stayed in the 6% range, at 5.4% in 2022, 6.4% in 2023, and 6.1% in both 2024 and 2025.

NICE, a domestic credit rating agency, said EBIT as a share of revenue rose from 3.3% in 2021 to 6.1% in 2024. A slump in domestic construction was cited as the background to the 2025 revenue decline.

The debt ratio fell from 163.0% in 2022 to 125.5% in 2025; at the end of 2025, total equity was KRW 404.9 billion, of which attributable equity was KRW 326.6 billion, and total liabilities were KRW 508.1 billion.

Operating cash flow was KRW 41 billion in 2022, KRW 70.1 billion in 2023, KRW 43.7 billion in 2024 and KRW 56.8 billion in 2025.

Quarterly revenue bottomed at KRW 118 billion in Q4 2025 (down 4.4% quarter on quarter), then rose for three consecutive quarters to KRW 142.1 billion in Q1 2026, KRW 142.4 billion in Q2 and KRW 150.7 billion in Q3.

Quarterly operating profit went from KRW 2.45 billion in Q3 2025 to KRW 4.77 billion in Q4 and KRW 9.71 billion in Q1 2026, then fell to KRW 5.92 billion in Q2 (down 39.0% quarter on quarter) before reaching KRW 13.3 billion in Q3 (up 124.6% quarter on quarter and up 442.3% year on year).

Attributable results turned to a net loss of KRW 855 million in Q2 2026 and the loss widened to KRW 15.5 billion in Q3. Operating profit was positive while the attributable result was a loss, so the gap between the two, including non-operating items and taxes, is the next item to examine.

05

Industry analysis

Aluko's results rest on building materials, so they are sensitive to domestic construction activity. An industry data provider summarized that aluminum extrusion revenue and operating profit fell in the first half of 2026 on a construction slump and weaker demand from downstream industries.

In the same period, North American sales of solar module frames and ladders from its overseas sales division were gaining share on price competitiveness. Domestic steel and aluminum producers have struggled with inflows of low-priced Chinese products.

A press report described an environment in which a 50% tariff on steel and aluminum products and a 15% reciprocal tariff apply at the same time, and the company said it shifted from import dependence to local production in response.

The company highlights its structure of combining mold making, casting, extrusion, fabrication and installation into one system. NICE expects that growth in EV battery cases and solar frames will ease fixed-cost pressure and help keep the company's medium-term operating profitability at an improved level.

On cycle position, annual 2025 results declined while quarterly results in 2026 recovered, but extrusion revenue fell in the first half of 2026, so the signals are mixed.

In competition, the company is a mid-sized player that combines aluminum extrusion with downstream processing, which differs from the large integrated steel producers in the same sector.

06

Outlook

In December 2025 the company announced that it had brought forward to March 2026 the target for full operation of its Halls complex in Tennessee. The complex covers processes from melting through extrusion and fabrication, and the target is to secure annual U.S. capacity of 156,000 tons (extrusion basis) by early 2029.

Given existing capacity of about 96,000 tons, the plan nearly doubles it. In an April 2026 report, a domestic brokerage said frame deliveries were scheduled from May 2026 and that growth in revenue and profit tied to U.S. sales could be expected.

A long-term solar module frame supply contract with the U.S. unit of Hanwha Qcells, running through 2033, has been reported, with supply from the Tennessee plant starting in May 2026.

The second plant investment is about USD 107.7 million and converts an existing facility into an integrated aluminum production site, with full operation targeted by the end of 2026.

Funding has been partly scaled back: a private investment firm reportedly cut a planned KRW 70 billion investment to about KRW 30 billion, and the company shifted to raising only the funds it urgently needs, according to a press report.

NICE expects most of the roughly KRW 160 billion U.S. investment to be executed in 2026. How much U.S. business shows up in quarterly results is the item to check in the Q4 2026 provisional results and the 2026 annual report.

08

Bull factors

Expanding U.S. production base

The Tennessee complex targets annual U.S. extrusion capacity of 156,000 tons by early 2029. Against existing capacity of about 96,000 tons, the plan nearly doubles output. The start-up target was moved forward to March 2026 in a December 2025 announcement. Raising local production is the company's response to the tariff environment for steel and aluminum.

Long-term solar frame supply

A long-term supply contract for solar module frames with the U.S. unit of Hanwha Qcells, running through 2033, has been reported. Supply from the Tennessee plant was set to begin in May 2026. A company profile summary notes that North American frame sales are gaining share on price competitiveness. The contract provides a revenue base as long as U.S. solar demand continues.

Operating margin held near 6%

The reported operating margin was 6.1% in both 2024 and 2025. Quarterly operating profit reached KRW 9.71 billion in Q1 2026 and KRW 13.3 billion in Q3. Whether the fixed-cost relief holds is the next point to watch.

Bear factors

Quarterly losses in 2026

Attributable results moved to a net loss of KRW 855 million in Q2 2026, and the loss widened to KRW 15.5 billion in Q3. Operating profit was positive, so the gap between operating profit and attributable results is wide.

A company profile summary reports that operating profit fell in the first half of 2026 versus the prior year. Whether quarterly losses persist is the main bearish question.

Debt and capex burden

Total borrowings were KRW 414.3 billion at the end of September 2025, and the company bears financing costs of roughly KRW 20 billion a year. NICE expects routine capex of around KRW 50 billion a year, plus U.S. plant investment, to limit free cash flow generation in the medium term.

Part of the U.S. funding has also been scaled back. Debt and investment burdens could slow the pace of earnings recovery.

Construction slump and low-priced imports

Aluminum extrusion revenue and operating profit fell in the first half of 2026 on a construction slump and weaker demand from downstream industries. The 2025 results were also linked to a slump in domestic construction. Domestic steel and aluminum producers face pressure from low-priced Chinese imports. A large building materials share leaves the business exposed to weaker construction demand.

Risk factors

Trade and tariffs

A press report described a 50% tariff on steel and aluminum products applying together with a 15% reciprocal tariff. Local production is the company's tariff response, but start-up delays or cost increases could reduce its effect. Changes in tariff policy would also alter the economics of the local production plan.

Funding and investment execution

Part of the U.S. investment funding has come in below plan. According to a press report, a private investment firm cut its planned KRW 70 billion investment to about KRW 30 billion. The company shifted to securing only the most urgent funds. The pace of funding could affect the U.S. plant schedule.

Working capital and raw materials

NICE found that working capital burdens have grown since 2021, partly due to higher aluminum prices. The structural working capital burden persists, and fast technological substitution in downstream industries keeps capex pressure present. Whether raw material costs and working capital flow through to operating cash flow is the point to watch.

Overall view

Aluko has growth pillars in its U.S. production expansion and solar frame deliveries, but the attributable net loss widened to KRW 15.5 billion in Q3 2026. Attributable net income peaked at KRW 18.5 billion in 2023 and declined for two consecutive years through 2025, while the operating margin held in the 6% range.

On a quarterly basis, operating profit rose sharply in Q1 and Q3 2026, showing signs of recovery. NICE expects total borrowings of KRW 414.3 billion and roughly KRW 160 billion of U.S. investment to limit medium-term free cash flow.

Valuation reads differently on an earnings basis, which is affected by the small last-four-quarter total, and on an asset basis, where market capitalization sits below attributable equity.

The next checkpoints are the Q4 2026 provisional results and the 2026 annual report on non-operating items, borrowings and U.S. plant performance. Bullish and bearish factors coexist at roughly equal weight.
