02

Business structure

YMC was founded in 2008 and listed on KOSDAQ in 2012 as a display and semiconductor materials and parts specialist. Its core products are sputtering targets, backing plates and process equipment parts used in display manufacturing, and it has built its business on localising core display process materials.

Product revenue mix in the Wisereport company profile is display materials 41.14%, semiconductor and display parts 35.27%, services 17.92% and other 5.67%. The semiconductor core is SiC (silicon carbide) etch-process parts, including etch rings, cathodes and focus rings, both new-make and recycled.

Etch rings and cathodes entered mass production in 2025, and the company says their revenue contribution will expand from 2026. According to an E-Daily report, semiconductor parts sales were roughly KRW 30 billion in 2025, a still limited share of total sales.

A new SiC parts line in Dangjin, Chungnam, extended the business from reconditioning into new production. Subsidiaries include YMS, which handles equipment maintenance (100% owned), and Wicom, which serves semiconductor and display equipment (60.84% owned).

In May 2026 the company received purchase orders for three new parts, including edge rings, from a US global equipment maker, and completed initial deliveries. On 28 October 2026 it signed a basic cooperation agreement with China's JDC, a molybdenum producer, to combine raw material supply with precision processing.

Display materials compete with domestic and overseas materials suppliers, while semiconductor parts overlap with firms supplying SiC rings and focus rings. Listed peers in semiconductor materials, parts and equipment include HANMI Semiconductor, Wonik IPS and Tokai Carbon Korea.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual sales moved from KRW 235.4 billion in 2022 to KRW 204.6 billion in 2023 (down 13.1% year on year) and KRW 167.7 billion in 2024 (down 18.0%), before rising slightly to KRW 172.8 billion in 2025 (up 3.0%). 2022 was the largest year of the 2022 to 2025 span and 2024 the smallest for sales.

Operating profit fell from KRW 23.1 billion in 2022 to KRW 15.8 billion in 2023 (down 31.7%), KRW 6.9 billion in 2024 (down 56.3%) and KRW 4.57 billion in 2025 (down 33.7%), a decline for three consecutive years from 2023 to 2025.

The operating margin narrowed from 9.8% in 2022 to 7.7% in 2023, 4.1% in 2024 and 2.6% in 2025. Sales grew in 2025 while operating profit fell, so top-line growth did not carry through to profit.

Profit attributable to controlling shareholders dropped from KRW 19.4 billion in 2022 to KRW 13.7 billion in 2023, KRW 6.76 billion in 2024 and KRW 1.32 billion in 2025 (down 80.4%), while net income including non-controlling interests was KRW 1.11 billion in 2025.

Operating cash flow declined from KRW 23.2 billion in 2022 to KRW 4.16 billion in 2025. The debt ratio rose from 23.0% in 2022 to 44.7% in 2025, with total equity of KRW 124 billion at end-2025, of which KRW 114.4 billion was attributable to controlling shareholders, and total liabilities of KRW 55.4 billion.

Quarterly sales rose from KRW 41 billion in Q3 2025 to KRW 59.4 billion in Q3 2026, up 45.1% year on year in the latest quarter. Quarterly operating profit ran KRW 1.86 billion, KRW 212 million, KRW 1.1 billion, KRW 2.79 billion and KRW 1.76 billion across Q3 2025 to Q3 2026.

Q4 2025 operating profit fell 88.6% from the prior quarter, and the company posted a controlling-shareholder net loss of KRW 570 million, a swing into loss. Q1 2026 operating profit rose 419.5% from the prior quarter to KRW 1.1 billion, and Q2 2026 reached KRW 2.79 billion before Q3 2026 fell to KRW 1.76 billion.

An E-Daily report attributed the cost pressure to growth investment in the new line, and said margins could improve as utilisation and yields stabilise. Profit attributable to controlling shareholders over the last four quarters totalled KRW 446 million.

05

Industry analysis

Demand from the front end is driven by the growth of AI semiconductors, which increases etch-process steps and therefore demand for SiC recycling, according to the company. Public demand also matters.

The government plans a KRW 500 billion R&D project to localise power semiconductors, more than 90% of which are imported, with infrastructure work starting in 2026 and mass production of core devices such as 1.2kV SiC MOSFETs targeted for 2027.

Samsung Electronics is reported to re-enter SiC foundry work with mass production in 2028. Discussion of a possible semiconductor supercycle has accompanied the broader recovery in chip demand.

In cycle terms the semiconductor side reads as expanding, yet Q3 2026 sales grew far faster than operating profit, which the company's cost and utilisation trends help explain. Competitively, YMC is moving its portfolio from display materials toward semiconductor parts.

Its peers in semiconductor materials, parts and equipment include HANMI Semiconductor, Wonik IPS and Tokai Carbon Korea, and YMC is entering global equipment supply chains through SiC etch parts and recycling. Market share figures were not available in public sources.

06

Outlook

In its August 2026 provisional earnings release, the company said it would keep winning new semiconductor parts orders and widen its global customer base to sustain growth. The company says parts orders have expanded since the US equipment maker's purchase orders in May 2026.

In its corporate value-up plan published on 16 September 2026, the company set a dividend floor of at least KRW 5 billion a year and KRW 15 billion cumulatively across 2026 to 2028, and said it aims to use 30% of net income as the dividend source.

The same plan calls for cancelling at least 630,569 of its 1,261,138 treasury shares within 2026, which would reduce total issued shares by at least 3.32%.

The basic agreement with China's JDC, signed on 28 October 2026, pairs raw molybdenum supply with precision processing and global sales, and the company aims to raise cost and quality competitiveness through it.

On 29 October 2026 the company made a strategic investment of USD 1 million in Samba Solutions, a Silicon Valley dry-etch equipment startup, aiming to secure dry-etch technology for advanced packaging such as high-bandwidth memory and through-silicon vias.

The next checkpoint is whether SiC parts orders continue as the government's 2027 MOSFET production and the reported 2028 Samsung Electronics SiC production approach. The company has stated a direction of expanding semiconductor parts' share of sales, but has not published a specific target.

Bull factors

Semiconductor parts revenue growth

Semiconductor parts sales were reported at roughly KRW 30 billion in 2025, and the company expects etch-process growth to lift SiC recycling demand. The new SiC line is operating, broadening the revenue base.

Entry into a global equipment supply chain

In May 2026 the company received purchase orders from a US global equipment maker for three new parts including edge rings, and completed final quality verification and initial deliveries. The company says orders for related parts have since grown. In October 2026 it made a USD 1 million investment in a US equipment startup to secure dry-etch technology.

More concrete shareholder return plan

The company set a dividend floor of at least KRW 5 billion a year and KRW 15 billion cumulatively over 2026 to 2028, targeting 30% of net income as the dividend source. A plan to cancel at least 630,569 treasury shares within 2026 was also disclosed. The CEO and CFO's open-market purchases are cited by the company as evidence of management's commitment.

Bear factors

Diluted profitability

The operating margin fell from 9.8% in 2022 to 7.7% in 2023, 4.1% in 2024 and 2.6% in 2025. Q3 2026 sales rose 45.1% year on year, yet operating profit of KRW 1.76 billion fell 5.2%. An E-Daily report said growth investment costs in the new line are weighing on profit.

Weakening earnings base

Profit attributable to controlling shareholders fell from KRW 19.4 billion in 2022 to KRW 1.32 billion in 2025, down 80.4% in the latest year. Q4 2025 swung to a controlling-shareholder net loss of KRW 570 million, and Q3 2026 profit of KRW 9 million was down 42.3% year on year. The last four quarters total KRW 446 million.

Cash flow and financial strain

The debt ratio rose from 23.0% to 44.7% over the same period. The KRW 5 billion annual dividend floor exceeds 2025 operating cash flow, so the funding structure is a key watch point.

10

Risk factors

Concentration and customer dependence

Semiconductor parts sales were reported at roughly KRW 30 billion in 2025, a limited share of total revenue. The May 2026 purchase orders come from a single US equipment maker, so customer concentration could be high. Whether the plan to add global customers converts into orders will shape the stability of the business mix.

Raw material sourcing and geopolitics

The October 2026 cooperation with JDC rests on molybdenum raw material from China. Concentrating raw material sourcing in one country can expose the company to supply chain policy shifts and trade controls. Whether the sourcing plan delivers real cost savings still needs to be confirmed.

Dividend funding and shareholder return burden

The dividend floor of at least KRW 5 billion a year for 2026 to 2028 applies regardless of earnings. With 2025 net income at KRW 1.11 billion and operating cash flow at KRW 4.16 billion, meeting the floor will depend on earnings recovery or stronger cash generation. Continuing treasury share cancellations alongside dividends should be checked against financial capacity.

Overall view

YMC is in the middle of a shift from display materials toward semiconductor parts. Q3 2026 sales of KRW 59.4 billion rose 45.1% year on year, and sales increased for two consecutive quarters from Q2 to Q3 2026.

Operating profit in the same quarter was KRW 1.76 billion, down 5.2%, and profit attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 9 million, down 42.3%. Operating profit declined for three consecutive years from 2023 to 2025, and the operating margin narrowed from 9.8% in 2022 to 2.6% in 2025.

Bullish factors include the US equipment maker's purchase orders, the 2026 to 2028 dividend floor and treasury share cancellations. Bearish factors include diluted profitability, a weaker earnings base, and the gap between 2025 operating cash flow of KRW 4.16 billion and the dividend floor.

Next checkpoints are completion of the 2026 treasury share cancellation, the operating margin and dividend amount in the 2026 annual report due by end-March 2027, and government and Samsung Electronics SiC production schedules.
