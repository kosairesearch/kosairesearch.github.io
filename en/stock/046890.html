Auto LED mix rising as losses narrow; sustained profit is the test

02

Business structure

Seoul Semiconductor is an LED-focused optical semiconductor company, and LED accounted for 99.5% of revenue in the first quarter of 2026. Its end markets are divided into IT (TV, monitors, notebooks and mobile), automotive and general lighting.

In a November 2024 report, a Hana Securities analyst said the IT share remained above 40% and was still the largest. A February 2025 Samsung Securities estimate said the automotive LED share began to exceed 30% in 2024 and was projected to reach 35% in 2025.

Its flagship automotive technology is WICOP, a package-free LED; a Korea IR research report from around July 2026 said the company has design wins with ten of the top global automakers and is applied to more than 100 vehicle models.

The same report argued that because automotive LEDs have long production cycles and demanding qualification, early design wins are likely to lead to repeat sales. General-purpose LEDs face price competition in IT backlights and general lighting, a point repeated in brokerage reports through 2025.

Press coverage describes the company as the third-largest LED maker globally by 2023 revenue and the number one in LED backlights, and its 2024 R&D spending was KRW 87.3 billion, or 8% of sales.

A medium-term option often cited is optical interconnects for AI data centers, where analysts have discussed combining its packaging and module technology with the light-source device technology of Seoul Viosys, a separate listed company.

04

Earnings analysis

On the disclosed annual basis, revenue went from KRW 1.11 trillion in 2022 to KRW 1.03 trillion in 2023 (down 6.9% year on year), KRW 1.09 trillion in 2024 (up 5.6%), and KRW 1.01 trillion in 2025 (down 7.1%). Among 2022 to 2025, 2022 was the largest year and 2025 the smallest.

Operating results were an operating loss of KRW 33.4 billion in 2022, an operating loss of KRW 49.4 billion in 2023 (losses continued and widened), an operating profit of KRW 797 million in 2024 (a swing to profit), and an operating loss of KRW 31.5 billion in 2025 (a swing to loss).

Operating margins were -3.0% in 2022, -4.8% in 2023, 0.1% in 2024 and -3.1% in 2025, so 2024 was the only positive year.

Controlling-shareholder net results were a loss of KRW 1.1 billion in 2022, a loss of KRW 22.4 billion in 2023 (losses continued and widened), a loss of KRW 5.04 billion in 2024 (losses continued but narrowed), and a loss of KRW 51.4 billion in 2025 (losses continued and widened), which is four consecutive years of losses from 2022 to 2025.

Net income including non-controlling interests was a loss of KRW 54.3 billion, KRW 74.5 billion, KRW 1.94 billion and KRW 60 billion in the same years, a different measure from the controlling-shareholder figure.

Operating cash flow was KRW 155.7 billion in 2022, KRW 120.7 billion in 2023, KRW 59.6 billion in 2024 and KRW 87.9 billion in 2025, positive in all four years.

Quarterly, an operating loss of KRW 3.78 billion in the third quarter of 2025 was followed by an operating profit of KRW 803 million in the fourth quarter, which marked a swing to profit, and then KRW 1.78 billion, KRW 3.49 billion and KRW 4.84 billion in the first three quarters of 2026, three consecutive quarterly increases.

Daishin Securities attributed the third-quarter margin improvement to a shift away from low-margin products toward higher-profit products.

Revenue fell 10.4% in the first quarter of 2026 to KRW 238.2 billion, then rose 22.6% to KRW 292.1 billion in the second quarter and 3.5% to KRW 302.3 billion in the third, two consecutive quarterly gains.

Controlling-shareholder net income, by contrast, was KRW 4.96 billion in the first quarter and KRW 2.79 billion in the second (down 43.7%), before a controlling-shareholder net loss of KRW 15.8 billion in the third quarter.

Daishin Securities said the third-quarter net loss meant it was too early to be optimistic about a profitability recovery.

05

Industry analysis

In LED, general-purpose applications such as IT backlights and general lighting face price competition, while automotive and higher-value products are seen as the growth drivers. Daishin Securities expects IT LED demand to slow in the fourth quarter of 2026 and lowered its estimates after the third-quarter results.

In its third-quarter 2025 report, the same brokerage pointed to slowing IT sales and intensifying competition in lighting as raising fixed-cost pressure. Automotive LED sales, by contrast, have been described as growing steadily.

A Korea IR research analyst estimated automotive sales would rise from KRW 273.7 billion in 2025 to KRW 311.6 billion in 2026, up 13.8%, explaining about 62% of the company's KRW 60.8 billion projected sales increase.

The analyst argued that automotive LED demand is more sensitive to the LED area and functions per vehicle than to total car production.

On competition, analysis points to differentiation through WICOP and high-voltage optical semiconductors in mass production, where design wins can translate into repeat sales, in contrast to commodity LED pricing.

06

Outlook

The company announced via a DART filing that it would hold an online IR session on 11 November 2026 to cover its third-quarter results and its fourth-quarter revenue outlook. The clearest direction is automotive sales expansion.

A Korea IR research analyst argued that the 2026 recovery depends more on a higher share of higher-value automotive products than on a rebound in general-purpose LED demand. The same analyst projected 2026 revenue of KRW 1.07 trillion (up 6.0%) and operating profit of KRW 6.4 billion (a swing to profit).

This is an outside analyst's estimate, not company guidance. Adding disclosed operating profit for the first three quarters of 2026 gives about KRW 10.1 billion, already close to the full-year scale of that estimate, which was made when only the first quarter was available.

Daishin Securities, however, flagged slowing IT LED demand in the fourth quarter and lowered its 2026 and 2027 estimates. For automotive products, the checkpoints are whether more models adopt WICOP and whether high-voltage optical semiconductor mass-production references expand.

AI data-center optical interconnects are discussed as a medium-term option, but no revenue timing has been disclosed. The 2026 annual business report, the next regular filing, will show the full-year automotive share and operating margin.

08

Bull factors

Automotive mix expansion path

A Korea IR research analyst projected the automotive share of sales would rise from 16.5% in 2022 to 29.0% in 2026. The same report estimated 2026 automotive sales at KRW 311.6 billion. WICOP is described as having design wins with ten of the top global automakers. Long automotive production cycles are cited as the basis for repeat sales.

Operating profit recovery trend

From the fourth-quarter 2025 low of KRW 803 million, operating profit rose to KRW 1.78 billion in the first quarter of 2026, KRW 3.49 billion in the second and KRW 4.84 billion in the third, three consecutive increases. Third-quarter 2026 operating profit swung to a profit from a year earlier.

Daishin Securities attributed this to a shift toward higher-profit products. The same brokerage put the third-quarter operating margin at 1.6%.

Positive operating cash flow throughout

Operating cash flow stayed positive in every year from 2022 to 2025. Cash came in even in years with operating losses. The 2025 figure rose from the 2024 level, according to the disclosed numbers.

Bear factors

Persistent net losses

Controlling-shareholder net losses continued for four consecutive years from 2022 to 2025. The third-quarter 2026 controlling-shareholder net loss of KRW 15.8 billion was a swing to loss from a year earlier. The last four quarters total KRW 19.2 billion in controlling-shareholder net losses.

Daishin Securities said that despite an operating profit, the net loss made it too early to be optimistic about profitability.

Slowing IT and lighting demand

Daishin Securities flagged slowing IT LED demand in the fourth quarter and said weakness in IT and general lighting remained a challenge in the third quarter. Its third-quarter 2025 report also pointed to intensifying competition in lighting. The brokerage argued that when sales stay below the break-even level, fixed-cost pressure grows.

Lowered estimates and debt burden

Daishin Securities said third-quarter revenue came in slightly below its estimate of KRW 308.5 billion. The same brokerage lowered its 2026 and 2027 per-share earnings estimates. Total liabilities at the end of 2025 were KRW 476.9 billion, with a debt ratio of 76.1%. That is lower than 79.9% in 2024 but above 66.3% in 2022.

10

Risk factors

General-purpose LED pricing and demand risk

General-purpose applications such as IT backlights and general lighting face sustained price competition and margin pressure. Daishin Securities flagged slowing IT LED demand in the fourth quarter. If demand weakens further, the automotive increase may not offset declines in these lines. With high fixed costs, lower utilization could delay a recovery in operating margin.

Automotive qualification and concentration

LED accounted for 99.5% of revenue in the first quarter of 2026, so product concentration is high. Automotive products require lengthy quality verification and customer approval, which can delay the start of sales. If the projected automotive share of 29.0% in 2026 falls short, the pace of recovery could slow.

Financial burden and R&D spending

The debt ratio was 76.1% at the end of 2025, with total liabilities of KRW 476.9 billion. R&D spending in 2024 was KRW 87.3 billion, or 8% of sales. If operating improvement stalls while R&D spending stays high, the financial burden could grow.

Overall view

Seoul Semiconductor, with LED as nearly all of its business, has raised its share of higher-value automotive products and posted three consecutive quarterly increases in operating profit from the fourth quarter of 2025.

Third-quarter 2026 revenue was KRW 302.3 billion, up 17.7% from a year earlier, and operating profit of KRW 4.84 billion swung to a profit from a year earlier. The same quarter's controlling-shareholder result swung to a KRW 15.8 billion net loss, so the operating-level gains did not carry through to net income.

On an annual basis, controlling-shareholder losses continued for four consecutive years from 2022 to 2025, and 2025 saw an operating loss of KRW 31.5 billion. Positives include automotive sales expansion and positive operating cash flow in every year from 2022 to 2025.

Negatives include slowing IT and lighting demand, price competition in general-purpose LEDs, and a combined controlling-shareholder net loss of KRW 19.2 billion over the last four quarters. Daishin Securities acknowledged the improvement while lowering its estimates.

The next checkpoints are the 2026 fourth-quarter provisional results, the automotive share in the 2026 annual report, and whether controlling-shareholder net income returns to profit.
