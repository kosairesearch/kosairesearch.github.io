02

Business structure

STraffic is a transportation infrastructure company built around three business lines: road tolling and Hi-pass systems, rail signaling and interlocking equipment, and overseas automatic fare collection (AFC) solutions.

The company describes itself as the only domestic firm with an end-to-end structure covering design, equipment development, construction and maintenance in-house.

STraffic has an end-to-end business structure covering in-house development through maintenance of key equipment ranging from toll collection systems and smart tolling to intelligent transportation systems (ITS), and is described as the only domestic company with this capability.

Domestically, its main customers are public institutions such as the Korea National Railway and the Korea Expressway Corporation, and in 2026 it won a sole contract from the Korea National Railway to upgrade interlocking systems on the Gyeongbu high-speed rail line.

Overseas business is carried out through a U.S. subsidiary, supplying fare-gate and AFC equipment to major U.S. transit agencies including WMATA in Washington, BART in San Francisco, LA Metro and New York's MTA. In 2019, STraffic became the first Korean company to enter the U.S.

AFC market, winning two separate contracts worth a combined $87 million from the Washington Metropolitan Area Transit Authority (WMATA), a project regarded as the largest Korean transportation-system export to a U.S. government agency and the first such win by a Korean firm against a major U.S. transportation solutions company.

In the road segment, the company participates in multi-lane Hi-pass replacement projects through a consortium that includes Lotte Innovate.

The company has stated it plans to combine the global network secured through the acquisition of LS Electric's rail business with its own technology to grow in rail markets across Asia, the Middle East and South America.

A precise revenue breakdown by segment is not disclosed in granular detail in recent filings, but domestic road and rail public-sector work together with U.S.-centered overseas AFC business form the two main pillars of earnings.

04

Earnings analysis

STraffic's consolidated revenue rose from KRW 132.1 billion in 2022 to KRW 147.2 billion in 2023 (up 11.4% year on year) and KRW 189 billion in 2024 (up 28.5%), before falling to KRW 168.2 billion in 2025 (down 11.0%).

The 2024 revenue expansion was underpinned by stronger overseas orders: revenue growth was driven mainly by increased orders in overseas markets, with new orders rising 36.1% year on year to KRW 192.9 billion and global orders up 118.2% to KRW 81.4 billion, leaving an order backlog of KRW 174.8 billion at the end of that year.

Operating profit peaked at KRW 17.4 billion in 2023 (up 81.1% year on year) before falling for two straight years to KRW 11.3 billion in 2024 (down 34.9%) and KRW 8.96 billion in 2025 (down 20.8%), with the operating margin declining from 11.8% in 2023 to 5.3% in 2025.

Controlling-shareholder net income peaked at KRW 32 billion in 2022 and fell for three consecutive years to KRW 16.6 billion in 2023 (-48.2%), KRW 5.98 billion in 2024 (-64.0%) and KRW 4.84 billion in 2025 (-19.1%).

Quarterly results have also shown marked volatility: in Q3 2025, revenue fell 13.7% quarter on quarter to KRW 33.5 billion and the company swung to an operating loss of KRW 5.16 billion, which was attributed at the time to a decline in road and rail project orders amid a domestic economic slowdown, together with rising development and sales expenses tied to new project development and increased costs from currency fluctuations and financing.

The company then swung back to a quarterly operating profit of KRW 7.79 billion in Q4 2025 on revenue of KRW 59.3 billion (up 77.0% quarter on quarter), but revenue plunged to KRW 15.5 billion in Q1 2026 and the operating result swung back to a loss of KRW 4.27 billion; Q2 2026 revenue was KRW 18.8 billion (up 20.9% quarter on quarter but down 51.5% year on year) with an operating loss of KRW 2.71 billion, extending the loss streak to two consecutive quarters.

Controlling-shareholder net losses widened from KRW 2.4 billion in Q1 2026 to KRW 2.52 billion in Q2 2026, and the cumulative controlling-shareholder net loss over the last four quarters (Q3 2025–Q2 2026) reached KRW 1.55 billion.

Operating cash flow fell from KRW 14.2 billion in 2024 to KRW 4.04 billion in 2025, indicating cash generation has slowed alongside the profit decline.

05

Industry analysis

The digital transformation market for transportation infrastructure moves on two axes: domestic road and rail public-sector orders, and replacement demand for aging equipment at transit agencies in the United States and elsewhere.

In the U.S., transit agencies are undertaking full fare-gate replacements to curb fare evasion and strengthen passenger safety; New York's subway system is currently piloting AI-based fare-evasion prevention gates, and the MTA is pursuing an expanded next-generation Faregate rollout based on the pilot results.

STraffic's participation in the APTA 2026 TRANSform & EXPO, hosted by the American Public Transportation Association in Chicago, where it unveiled next-generation AFC solutions and smart Faregate technology, reflects an effort to respond to this demand.

The company built a track record in North America with wins at WMATA in Washington in 2019 and the AFC project in San Francisco in 2023.

Industry observers note that supplier selection for AFC systems in the U.S. is expected to continue around major sporting events, including the 2026 FIFA World Cup, the 2027 NFL Super Bowl and the 2028 Summer Olympics, driven by fare-evasion prevention and security needs.

Domestically, public infrastructure investment continues steadily through Korea Expressway Corporation's multi-lane Hi-pass expansion and aging one-toll equipment replacement and Korea National Railway's signaling upgrades, but order timing carries volatility tied to economic conditions.

In terms of competitive positioning, STraffic is regarded as the only domestic company with an end-to-end business structure spanning toll collection through maintenance; it participates in large domestic projects through consortiums while competing directly with major local transportation solutions companies in the U.S. market.

06

Outlook

Heading through 2026, STraffic has continued to win new orders at home and abroad.

In August, it won a sole contract from the Korea National Railway to upgrade interlocking systems with a total project cost of KRW 26.5 billion, an initial contract value of about KRW 4 billion, running through December 31, 2027, and covering 16 locations including Gwangmyeong, Cheonan-Asan and Ulsan stations.

In May, through a consortium that included Lotte Innovate, the company secured a KRW 3.2 billion stake (30%) in the Korea Expressway Corporation's KRW 10.7 billion 2026 multi-lane Hi-pass manufacturing and procurement project.

The company has stated its intention to use the global network secured through the LS Electric rail business acquisition to expand rail operations across Asia, the Middle East and South America.

In the U.S. market, the company unveiled next-generation AFC solutions at the APTA 2026 event in October 2026, and whether it is selected as a supplier for the New York MTA's planned expansion of next-generation Faregates is likely to be a key factor for future order wins.

On the shareholder-return front, the corporate value-up plan disclosed in March 2026 set a target price-to-book ratio of 2x by the end of 2026, including plans for an additional share buyback in the second half of 2026 out of a total KRW 15 billion buyback program, and a remaining KRW 5 billion of planned share cancellations in 2027, alongside expanded year-end dividends.

These remain targets set by the company, however, and whether they are actually achieved will depend on future quarterly results and order performance.

Long-Term Decline in Profitability

Rising development and sales expenses associated with expanding the overseas AFC business have been cited as one factor behind the profitability decline.

10

Risk factors

Earnings Volatility Inherent to Order-Based Business

Quarterly revenue and earnings can swing sharply depending on the timing of public-sector orders and billing schedules for overseas projects. The drop in revenue from KRW 59.3 billion in Q4 2025 to KRW 15.5 billion in Q1 2026 illustrates this pattern.

Delays in converting the order backlog into recognized revenue could remain a source of quarterly volatility going forward.

Foreign Exchange and Overseas Cost Risk

Currency fluctuations and rising financing costs have affected profitability as the share of overseas revenue, particularly from the U.S., has grown.

Sales and development expenses tied to pursuing new overseas projects may also continue to rise, leaving a balance between overseas expansion and profitability as an ongoing challenge.

Intensifying Competition Risk

In the U.S. market, the company competes directly against a major local transportation solutions company, and large domestic projects are structured as consortiums where the company holds only a partial stake rather than winning the full contract alone. If the company loses out in global supplier-selection competitions, its growth pace could slow.

Overall view

STraffic is a transportation infrastructure company built on road, rail and overseas AFC businesses; revenue grew through 2024 on the back of overseas order expansion, but 2025 revenue fell 11.0%, and both operating profit and controlling-shareholder net income declined for two and two consecutive years, respectively, after peaking in 2023.

In 2026, the company has recorded operating losses and controlling-shareholder net losses in both Q1 and Q2, extending the loss streak. On the other hand, its U.S.

AFC business continues to pursue new order opportunities built on relationships with major transit agencies in Washington, San Francisco, Los Angeles and New York, while domestically it is expanding into rail signaling via the Gyeongbu high-speed rail upgrade and into road tolling via the multi-lane Hi-pass project.

The company has presented a corporate value-up plan targeting a price-to-book ratio of 2x by the end of 2026, including share buybacks, cancellations and expanded dividends, though achieving this target depends on a future recovery in earnings strength.

On valuation, the stock trades at a discount to book value and a price-to-earnings comparison is difficult given net losses over the trailing four quarters.

Investors will want to watch whether the loss streak continues in the upcoming Q3 results and when new overseas and rail orders begin translating into actual revenue.
