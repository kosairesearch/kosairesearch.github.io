02

Business structure

STraffic is a transportation solutions company that builds intelligent transport systems (ITS) and transit card settlement systems across roads, rail, subways and airports.

Its road business centers on toll collection and tunnel ITS; in March 2020 it announced that it had won the Seoul Jepo Tunnel ITS project worth KRW 15.1 billion on its own, equal to 37.0% of 2018 consolidated revenue.

In its rail business, the company won in October 2024, as part of a consortium with Korail, a KRW 9.2 billion next-generation wide-area information system (KOTRIS) project that upgrades a system Samsung SDS developed about ten years earlier. The company is described as a former spin-off from Samsung SDS.

In transit cards, it followed its Seoul Metro card system work with the Korail project, which it says gives it capability spanning from card readers to upper-level settlement systems.

In January 2026 it won an AI-based ITS remote maintenance system project ordered by Korea Expressway Corporation, which includes a web-based remote management platform and AI performance diagnostics for failure prediction.

Overseas, it has taken part in automated fare collection projects in Washington, San Francisco and Los Angeles, and ITS projects in Bangladesh, Colombia and El Salvador.

The company said in October 2024 that overseas sales were close to 30% of the total, and Eugene Investment & Securities put the overseas share at 41.5% in Q3 2024. Because projects are awarded one by one from public clients, the size and timing of individual contracts strongly influence quarterly revenue.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual revenue rose from KRW 132.1 billion in 2022 to KRW 147.2 billion in 2023 (up 11.4% year on year) and KRW 189.0 billion in 2024 (up 28.5% year on year), then fell to KRW 168.2 billion in 2025 (down 11.0% year on year). Within 2022–2025, the largest year was 2024 and the smallest was 2022.

Operating profit peaked at KRW 17.4 billion in 2023, up 81.1% year on year from KRW 9.6 billion in 2022, and then declined for two consecutive years: KRW 11.3 billion in 2024 (down 34.9% year on year) and KRW 8.96 billion in 2025 (down 20.8% year on year).

The operating margin moved from 7.3% in 2022 to 11.8% in 2023, 6.0% in 2024 and 5.3% in 2025, so in 2024 the margin actually fell even though revenue grew.

Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders fell from KRW 32.0 billion in 2022 to KRW 16.6 billion in 2023 (down 48.2% year on year), KRW 5.98 billion in 2024 (down 64.0% year on year) and KRW 4.84 billion in 2025 (down 19.1% year on year), a decline for three consecutive years.

Net profit including non-controlling interests was KRW 4.95 billion in 2025, only slightly different from the net profit attributable to controlling shareholders.

Operating cash flow varied widely from year to year: KRW 5.32 billion in 2022, KRW 370 million in 2023, KRW 14.2 billion in 2024 and KRW 4.04 billion in 2025. The quarterly results started from Q3 2025 revenue of KRW 33.5 billion and an operating loss of KRW 5.16 billion.

In Q4 2025, the company returned to profit, with revenue of KRW 59.3 billion (up 77.0% quarter onGquarter), operating profit of KRW 7.79 billion and net profit attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 6.93 billion.

It then swung back to an operating loss in Q1 2026, with revenue of KRW 15.5 billion (down 73.8% quarter on quarter) and an operating loss of KRW 4.27 billion, and in Q2 2026 the loss narrowed, with revenue of KRW 18.8 billion (up 20.9% quarter on quarter) and an operating loss of KRW 2.71 billion.

In Q3 2026, revenue was KRW 17.7 billion (down 6.1% quarter on quarter and down 47.3% year on year) and the operating loss was KRW 3.69 billion, larger than in the previous quarter. The combined net loss attributable to controlling shareholders over the latest four quarters (Q4 2025 to Q3 2026) is KRW 1.11 billion.

The defining feature of results over this period is that large swings in quarterly revenue push quarterly earnings back and forth between profit and loss.

05

Industry analysis

ITS construction depends on public procurement for roads and rail, so quarterly revenue moves sharply with project schedules and budgets. The gap between KRW 59.3 billion in Q4 2025 and KRW 15.5 billion in Q1 2026 illustrates this pattern.

Public transport infrastructure is shifting toward remote and AI-assisted maintenance, as equipment scale and complexity grow and on-site inspection alone becomes less adequate.

In its January 2026 remote maintenance project, the company emphasized AI performance diagnostics for failure prediction and remaining-life analysis. It has also pointed to new areas such as autonomous-driving infrastructure and smart highways.

Technical difficulty is rising as well: building ITS inside underground roads involves radio interference and communication range limits inside tunnels, and the company said it built a test environment to secure core technology.

Transit card settlement is another area that requires integration from card readers up to upper-level settlement systems. STraffic's business is project-based, combining field equipment and systems, so the size of a single award strongly influences quarterly revenue. Overseas ITS exports extend project experience gained in Bangladesh, Colombia and El Salvador.

06

Outlook

The company's stated direction falls into three areas. First, it plans to expand AI-based ITS from the Korea Expressway Corporation remote maintenance project, pointing to autonomous-driving infrastructure, smart highways and overseas ITS exports as new opportunities.

Second, it launched an AX business division in 2026 to respond to demand for AI-based transport and infrastructure.

Third, after winning Seoul's underground expressway projects following the Jepo Tunnel award in 2020, the company said additional awards for planned deep-underground and underground road projects, including the East Trunk Underground Road, the Mandeok-Centum urban expressway and the Seongnam-Gangnam private expressway, should gain momentum.

When it won the KOTRIS project in October 2024, it said it was expanding awards in line with rising demand for system upgrades. These statements are not revenue or profit targets, so whether they translate into earnings must be checked against reported results.

The nearest reporting point is Q4 2026, and the key test is how it compares with the 2025 annual operating profit of KRW 8.96 billion. The next periodic report is the 2026 annual business report.

With losses recorded for three consecutive quarters through Q3 2026, whether Q4 2026 operating results land in profit or loss will shape the outlook.

08

Bull factors

Expansion into AI-based ITS

In January 2026 the company won an AI-based ITS remote maintenance project from Korea Expressway Corporation. It plans to combine remote control with AI performance diagnostics to raise equipment uptime and strengthen preventive maintenance. The AX business division launched in 2026 is aimed at AI demand in transport and infrastructure.

A profitable Q4 2025

In Q4 2025 the company swung to profit, with revenue of KRW 59.3 billion, operating profit of KRW 7.79 billion and attributable net income of KRW 6.93 billion. The quarter shows that profit can improve quickly when revenue rises sharply. The profit did not continue after that, so one quarter is too early to call a trend.

Improving balance sheet

The debt-to-equity ratio fell from 161.3% in 2022 to 69.1% in 2025, and total liabilities stood at KRW 70.6 billion. Of total equity of KRW 102.2 billion at the end of 2025, attributable equity accounted for KRW 101.8 billion. With lower financial burden, the company has more room to keep investing in its business.

Bear factors

Quarterly revenue declines

Q3 2026 revenue was KRW 17.7 billion, down 6.1% quarter on quarter and down 47.3% year on year. Q1 2026 revenue fell 73.8% quarter on quarter, so there are few signs of quarterly revenue recovering.

In at least one quarter, falling revenue coincided with a wider operating loss, highlighting the fragility of the earnings structure.

Declining earnings trend

Operating profit has declined for two consecutive years after peaking at KRW 17.4 billion in 2023. Attributable net income fell from KRW 32 billion in 2022 to KRW 4.84 billion in 2025, a three-year decline. The operating margin also dropped from 11.8% in 2023 to 5.3% in 2025.

Uneven cash generation

Year-to-year swings in operating cash flow were large, so cash generation lacked consistency. With losses continuing through 2026, reported results do not yet show cash generation settling into a stable pattern. Because cash tends to arrive when projects are completed, the timing adds to the volatility.

10

Risk factors

Dependence on public contracts

Revenue is booked project by project from public clients, which is why quarterly revenue ranged from KRW 15.5 billion to KRW 59.3 billion in the last five quarters. A single contract finishing or a delayed tender can move quarterly profit sharply. High reliance on large public projects keeps earnings forecasting uncertain.

Continued losses and earnings capacity

The company has posted losses for three consecutive quarters from Q1 2026 through Q3 2026, and attributable net losses over the last four quarters total KRW 1.11 billion. The operating margin was 5.3% in 2025, below 11.8% in 2023. If revenue does not recover, fixed cost burdens could keep pressuring profit.

Technical difficulty and overseas projects

ITS built inside underground roads involves high technical difficulty, including radio interference and communication range limits. The company has said overseas sales approach 30% of the total, and the Q3 2024 overseas share was put at 41.5% on a quarterly basis.

Overseas work is exposed to local procurement and policy factors, and its profitability is not separately disclosed in reported results.

12

Overall view

STraffic is a company built on public-sector ITS contracts for roads and rail, and both revenue and operating profit declined in 2025 after revenue peaked in 2024.

Operating profit has fallen for two consecutive years after peaking at KRW 17.4 billion in 2023, and attributable net income has declined for two consecutive years.

Q4 2025 swung to profit, but losses have continued for three consecutive quarters from Q1 2026 through Q3 2026, and Q3 2026 revenue fell 47.3% year on year. On the balance sheet, the debt-to-equity ratio fell from 161.3% in 2022 to 69.1% in 2025, which reduces financial burden.

The AI-based ITS award in 2026 and the launch of the AX business division show the direction of expansion as a matter of fact. At the same time, large quarter-to-quarter revenue swings and reliance on project-based awards make it hard yet to judge whether profit will hold steady.

The next checkpoints are Q4 2026 results and the 2026 annual business report, and whether full-year profit lands in the black or the red will define the recovery.
