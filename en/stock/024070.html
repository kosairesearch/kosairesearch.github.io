WISCOM: Back in the Black for 2025, Quarterly Profit Still Swings

WISCOM returned to a net profit attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 1.34 billion in 2025, after net losses in 2023 and 2024, but in the third quarter of 2026 it posted an operating profit of KRW 373 million alongside a net loss attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 568 million, so quarterly earnings have not yet settled.

02

Business structure

WISCOM, founded in 1978, makes and sells customized synthetic-resin compounds and plastic products for cable, automotive, construction, renewable-energy and industrial markets.

Its product families are based on PVC, PE, PP, PC and ABS materials and are used in power and telecom cables, electric vehicles, automotive interiors, construction, renewable-energy grids, household goods and industrial parts.

According to a nine-month 2026 summary by TipRanks, the business is organized around product manufacturing, coloring processing, goods sales and China operations, and the report lists two consolidated subsidiaries.

The company has focused on compound sales for higher-value cable materials and has said it plans to increase compound sales to overseas cable makers. Its customers are industrial demand sources such as cable, automotive and construction makers rather than end consumers.

On a disclosed basis, revenue fell from KRW 125.7 billion in 2022 to KRW 98.5 billion in 2024 before recovering to KRW 103.3 billion in 2025, and quarterly revenue was KRW 28 billion, KRW 30.8 billion and KRW 29.2 billion in Q1 to Q3 2026.

Market capitalization sits well below the chemicals industry average in an Alphasquare industry comparison, which places the company among the smaller listed chemical names. Results are closely tied to raw-material prices and cable-sector demand rather than to a single end product.

04

Earnings analysis

Revenue was highest in 2022 at KRW 125.7 billion and lowest in 2024 at KRW 98.5 billion. Revenue declined 10.6% to KRW 112.3 billion in 2023 and 12.3% to KRW 98.5 billion in 2024, before rising 4.8% to KRW 103.3 billion in 2025.

Operating profit of KRW 744 million in 2022 turned into an operating loss of KRW 2.85 billion in 2023, widened to an operating loss of KRW 8.86 billion in 2024 and narrowed to an operating loss of KRW 698 million in 2025.

The operating margin moved from 0.6% to -2.5%, -9.0% and -0.7%, and the company recorded an operating loss in each year from 2023 through 2025.

Alphasquare's industry table places the company's operating margin well below the sector average, and FnGuide summarized the 2024 third quarter as sales declines driving a wider operating loss.

Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders was a loss of KRW 7.43 billion in 2022, a loss of KRW 476 million in 2023 and a loss of KRW 23.9 billion in 2024, before turning to a profit of KRW 1.34 billion in 2025.

In 2024 the net loss was far larger than the operating loss of KRW 8.86 billion, which points to non-operating items and taxes weighing on the bottom line. Operating cash flow was KRW 2.59 billion in 2022, KRW 5.85 billion in 2023, KRW 2.69 billion in 2024 and KRW 975 million in 2025, the lowest of the four years.

Quarterly results show that Q3 2025 revenue was KRW 27.1 billion with an operating loss of KRW 728 million and a net loss attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 298 million.

Q4 2025 revenue was KRW 24.1 billion, down 10.9% from the prior quarter, with operating profit of KRW 1.1 billion and net profit attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 1.38 billion. Q1 2026 revenue of KRW 28 billion rose 16.2% from the prior quarter, but an operating loss of KRW 1.23 billion returned.

Q2 2026 revenue of KRW 30.8 billion came with operating profit of KRW 924 million and net profit attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 2.56 billion.

Q3 2026 revenue of KRW 29.2 billion fell 5.2% from the prior quarter but rose 7.7% year on year, operating profit of KRW 373 million fell 59.6% quarter on quarter, and the net loss attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 568 million widened from the prior-year period.

Because Q3 2026 showed operating profit alongside a controlling-shareholder net loss, the next annual report's income statement is the place to check how non-operating items and taxes moved the bottom line.

05

Industry analysis

WISCOM's end markets are spread across power and telecom cables, electric vehicles, automotive interiors, construction and renewable-energy grids. The company has emphasized higher-value cable materials in response to infrastructure modernization and tighter environmental requirements.

Cable demand tied to grid investment, data centers and renewable build-outs is the main variable for volume, while raw-material prices such as PVC determine whether margins can hold. The 2025 operating margin of -0.7% indicates a thin cushion between input costs and selling prices, so pass-through ability matters.

Against large petrochemical names such as LG Chem, Lotte Chemical and Kumho Petrochemical, WISCOM competes in the intermediate compound segment, working closely with cable and parts customers.

Among chemical-sector peers, its market capitalization and revenue are on the smaller side, so changes in orders from large customers weigh more heavily on results.

Expanded sales to overseas cable makers and China operations are efforts to tap export demand, but they also expose the company to local demand and currency movements.

The sector cycle position is difficult to pin down with confidence, although every quarterly revenue figure in 2026 (KRW 28 billion, KRW 30.8 billion and KRW 29.2 billion) exceeded the KRW 24.1 billion of Q4 2025, indicating gradual volume recovery.

Risk factors

Raw-material prices and pass-through

When PVC, PE and other petrochemical inputs rise, margins depend on how quickly prices can be passed on to customers. A 2025 operating margin of -0.7% indicates that the cushion between input costs and selling prices is thin, so quarterly results can swing easily with input prices.

Dependence on cable and industrial demand

The company's products are tied to demand from power and telecom cable, electric vehicle, automotive interior and construction markets. Lower orders from large cable makers or a slowdown in construction could reduce sales volume. China operations and overseas cable sales are also exposed to local demand and currency movements.

Small scale and earnings volatility

The company is a small cap whose market capitalization and revenue are well below chemical-sector averages. Because earnings are small, changes at a few customers or one-off costs can shift quarterly results sharply.

As Q3 2026 showed, an operating profit can still produce a net loss when non-operating items or taxes move, which reduces earnings predictability.
