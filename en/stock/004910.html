02

Business structure

Chokwang Paint is an industrial paint manufacturer founded in 1967. Its sales run mainly through distributors and business-to-business transactions, and press reports say industrial paints account for about 25% of revenue, followed by powder, UV and architectural paints.

A share breakdown compiled by an external information site for the second quarter of 2026 shows industrial 25%, architectural 20%, UV 17%, powder 14% and automotive repair 10%. These are third-party estimates rather than figures confirmed in periodic filings, so they are treated as reference only.

Core product lines include UV-curable coatings used in electrical and electronic materials, powder coatings, and architectural and woodworking paints, with formulation technology tailored to customer processes cited as the main competitive edge.

At INTRA 2026, held from September 30 to October 2, 2026, the company showed six themes: secondary batteries, ESS, automotive materials, displays, electrical and electronic materials, and high-performance powder coatings.

In secondary batteries it displayed separator binders and battery housing adhesives, and in ESS it showed thermal adhesives and insulating and fire-resistant coatings.

Among competitors, it faces large paint makers such as KCC, and one source ranked it fifth among domestic paint makers by sales with a 4.9% market share as of the third quarter of 2018. Its portfolio pairs a mature architectural paint market with higher-function industrial materials for electronics and batteries.

Whether the mix shifts further toward industrial materials can be checked in the segment sales disclosure of the 2026 annual report.

04

Earnings analysis

Based on the disclosed annual results, sales fell from KRW 260.5 billion in 2022 to KRW 252.8 billion in 2023 (down 2.9% year on year), KRW 248 billion in 2024 (down 1.9%), and KRW 202.7 billion in 2025 (down 18.3%).

Sales declined for three consecutive years from 2023 through 2025, with 2022 the largest year and 2025 the smallest in the 2022 to 2025 period. Operating margin was -0.6% in 2022, 1.5% in 2023 and 1.0% in 2024, before dropping to -9.6% in 2025.

Operating loss of KRW 1.62 billion in 2022 turned into operating profit of KRW 3.71 billion in 2023, but operating profit then fell 35.1% to KRW 2.4 billion in 2024 and the company returned to an operating loss of KRW 19.5 billion in 2025.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 4.76 billion in 2023 and KRW 16 billion in 2024 (up 235.7%), before a net loss of KRW 10.3 billion in 2025. In the disclosed figures, net income including non-controlling interests matches the controlling-shareholder figure in every year from 2022 to 2025.

Operating cash flow moved from -KRW 6.29 billion in 2022 to KRW 14.8 billion in 2023 and KRW 19.7 billion in 2024, then fell to KRW 3.54 billion in 2025.

Quarterly sales went from KRW 50.8 billion in Q3 2025 to KRW 49.1 billion in Q4 2025 and KRW 46.4 billion in Q1 2026, rose to KRW 59.5 billion in Q2 2026, and were KRW 52.2 billion in Q3 2026.

Operating losses ran at KRW 4.28 billion, KRW 7.54 billion, KRW 6.21 billion, KRW 2.89 billion and KRW 4.23 billion across Q3 2025 to Q3 2026.

The Q3 2026 net loss attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 7.97 billion is larger than the operating loss in the same quarter, so the non-operating items behind that gap need to be checked in the quarterly report text.

05

Industry analysis

The paint market splits into architectural, industrial, automotive and electronics end-markets whose demand patterns differ. Chokwang Paint has a high share of industrial, powder and UV coatings, so its results are tied to factory activity and capital spending in those end-markets.

An older securities analysis said its urethane and epoxy inputs are petrochemical synthetics, making the direction of crude oil important for profit. The same analysis noted a high cost-of-sales share, but because it is dated it is not used here as a current cost ratio.

Another older analysis described architectural and woodworking paints as a mature segment with operating margins of 3% to 7%, a reason the company is widening into electrical materials.

Its shipbuilding paint business runs through a joint venture, and that analysis estimated a share in the 20% range in the domestic market while noting that ship paint demand lags shipbuilder orders by one to one and a half years. KCC is cited as a large listed peer in the same sector.

One source placed the company fifth among domestic paint makers by sales as of the third quarter of 2018 with a 4.9% market share, which places it among mid-sized paint producers. In February and March 2026 it held distributor seminars in four regions to share its 2026 product lineup and coating technology training.

Risk factors

Financial and Liquidity Risk

Press reports say early redemption demands on convertible instruments increased cash outflows. Consolidated cash and cash equivalents at the end of 2025 were around KRW 9.1 billion against total liabilities of KRW 220.8 billion.

If borrowing and repayments overlap during a loss period, room for investment could shrink, so the cash flow and borrowing structure in the 2026 annual report needs checking.

Raw Material and Crude Oil Risk

Key inputs such as urethane and epoxy are petrochemical synthetics, so crude oil movements pass into cost. An older analysis credited stabilizing raw material prices for an earlier profit recovery. If input prices rise again, whether the company can pass them on to customers will largely determine its operating margin.

Ownership and Float Structure

Press reports put the owner family's stake at 37.78% and small shareholders at 38.35%. With a high controlling-holder stake, the number of freely tradable shares is limited, which can change trading patterns. The governance sub-grade in the 2026 ESG rating was B+, leaving room for improvement.
