02

Business structure

Anterogen develops and sells stem cell-based cell therapies and rare disease drugs. Its revenue structure leans heavily on products it in-licenses and sells directly. Media reports said Lemodulin merchandise sales made up 92.9% of revenue in the first half of 2025.

A data aggregator put the mix in the second quarter of 2026 at 97% merchandise sales and 3% from Cupistem and other stem cell culture products. Lemodulin, a pulmonary arterial hypertension treatment, continues to be supplied to general hospitals in Korea. Its own products have struggled to gain a foothold.

Cupistem's marketing approval was voluntarily withdrawn, and the cell therapy Quinsell had its approval deleted in June 2026 after its validity period expired. In September 2025, Taivaso failed to secure final reimbursement under Korea's national health insurance.

The most important product is Allostem for dystrophic epidermolysis bullosa, which Anterogen licensed to Japan's Ishin Pharmaceutical in 2015. Ishin ran the clinical development and obtained approval.

The pipeline includes a US Phase 2 trial in diabetic foot ulcers, a Phase 1 exploratory study in knee osteoarthritis, and the shingles candidate ANT-501. Customers are Korean hospitals and the Japanese partner, and competition comes from domestic and overseas cell therapy developers.

04

Earnings analysis

On a disclosed basis, revenue fell from KRW 6.59 billion in 2022 to KRW 6.5 billion in 2023, a 1.4% decline, then rose to KRW 6.93 billion in 2024, up 6.7%, and to KRW 7.54 billion in 2025, up 8.7%. Revenue has grown for two consecutive years from 2024 through 2025.

Among the years from 2022 to 2025, 2025 was the largest and 2023 the smallest. Operating losses were KRW 4.65 billion in 2022, KRW 5.07 billion in 2023, KRW 3.75 billion in 2024, and KRW 2.43 billion in 2025, so the company posted losses in four consecutive years, with the loss narrowing in 2024 and 2025.

The operating margin moved from -70.5% in 2022 and -78.1% in 2023 to -54.1% in 2024 and -32.3% in 2025. The loss attributable to controlling shareholders narrowed from KRW 6.84 billion in 2022 to KRW 1.37 billion in 2025, and net loss figures are identical because there are no non-controlling interests.

Past press coverage pointed to research spending and selling, general and administrative costs that are heavy relative to sales as a structural cause of losses.

Quarterly revenue bottomed at KRW 2.04 billion in the third quarter of 2025 and KRW 2 billion in the fourth quarter, down 2.0% on the prior quarter, before rising to KRW 2.12 billion, KRW 2.2 billion, and KRW 2.97 billion across the first three quarters of 2026, three consecutive quarterly increases.

The first quarter of 2026 had the largest operating loss in the window at KRW 1.1 billion, followed by a KRW 478 million loss in the second quarter and a KRW 374 million operating profit in the third quarter, which marked a swing to profit.

Profit attributable to controlling shareholders also swung to a KRW 745 million profit in the third quarter of 2026, yet the sum of net losses for the last four quarters, from the fourth quarter of 2025 through the third quarter of 2026, is KRW 793 million.

Operating cash flow was -KRW 1.02 billion in 2025, a smaller outflow than -KRW 1.99 billion in 2023. Whether the third-quarter profit persists and how much of the sales growth comes from Japan cannot yet be separated from the disclosed figures.

05

Industry analysis

Rare disease drugs serve limited patient populations, so the market is small, and Anterogen's core revenue also relies on rare medicines. In regenerative medicine, Japan has operated a conditional and expedited review system, under which Allostem went through its approval process.

Actual pricing and insurance listing terms after approval will determine the revenue scale, however. In the United States, the FDA's RMAT designation signals faster development but still needs support from Phase 2 results.

Domestic cell therapy markets have been described as difficult for products to gain a foothold even after approval, and Anterogen's Quinsell and Cupistem are cited as examples.

The industry is watching a shift toward off-the-shelf stem cell products with standardized manufacturing, and Anterogen is promoting its three-dimensional culture and cryopreservation platform.

Competition overlaps with domestic and overseas developers that hold cell therapy pipelines, and in Japan the sales capability of partner Ishin Pharmaceutical is the key variable.

Demand indicators are limited given the rare disease profile, so revenue growth depends on approvals and sales performance of individual products.

Outlook

In Japan, a July 2026 notice covered the post-approval progress of Allostem and insurance listing for the regenerative medical product Allostem Sheet, and a notice on September 30, 2026 announced collection of export payments. The company has been described as earning a royalty on sales plus a margin on product supply.

The Japanese patient population is estimated at roughly 1,000, and industry commentary in March 2026 floated export potential of about KRW 200 billion a year once full supply begins, though this is an industry estimate rather than a company disclosure.

In the United States, a September 11, 2026 notice covered a change to the US DEB clinical protocol for Allostem Sheet, and the diabetic foot ulcer program is in a US Phase 2 trial.

The Phase 1 exploratory study of the osteoarthritis candidate ANT-301 showed pain and function improvement in six patients, which the company said needs verification at an IR event on November 10, 2026. The shingles candidate ANT-501 confirmed safety in a 4-week repeat-dose non-clinical toxicity study.

In Korea, the suspension of the relevant clinical trial operations runs from November 9 to December 8, 2026.

The next checkpoints are the price and insurance terms in Japan, the pace of initial prescriptions, the timing of the US trial after the protocol change, and export revenue as reflected in the 2026 annual report.

Risk factors

Regulation and trial operations

A Ministry of Food and Drug Safety suspension followed late reporting of a clinical trial plan change and quality control violations. The suspension runs through December 8, 2026. If trial timelines shift, development schedules could slip alongside the US DEB protocol change.

Dependence on Japanese sales and pricing

Allostem's sales scale could vary widely with the actual price and insurance listing terms. The speed of early prescription uptake in Japan is also cited as a variable for earnings visibility. Because sales depend on partner Ishin Pharmaceutical, results could differ depending on its selling capability.

Uncertainty in clinical results

The Phase 1 exploratory study of ANT-301 reported pain and function improvement in six patients, but this needs verification. As the Taivaso reimbursement failure showed, clinical results can still be constrained at the commercial stage. Until US Phase 2 data for the diabetic foot ulcer program are available, a US revenue base is hard to assess.

Overall view

Anterogen's disclosed revenue rose to KRW 7.54 billion in 2025, up for two consecutive years, while operating and controlling-shareholder losses narrowed through a four-year run of losses from 2022 to 2025.

The third quarter of 2026 showed a quarterly swing to profit, with revenue of KRW 2.97 billion and operating profit of KRW 374 million. Japanese approval and the start of exports for Allostem could change the business structure, but revenue still depends on in-licensed products such as Lemodulin.

Its in-house products have struggled commercially, with one marketing approval withdrawn and another deleted, and a clinical trial suspension was imposed in November 2026.

Strengths include Japanese sales expansion, US trial progress, and a balance sheet with almost no borrowings, while weaknesses include continued annual losses, reliance on in-licensed products, and clinical and regulatory risk.

The next checkpoints are the end of the suspension on December 8, 2026, the reflection of export revenue in the 2026 annual report, and whether the profit persists in the first-quarter 2027 report.
