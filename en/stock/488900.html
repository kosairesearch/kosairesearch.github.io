02

Business structure

Vitzro Nextech runs four strategic businesses: aerospace, plasma, fusion and accelerators, centered on design and key component manufacturing. Based on 2024 results cited in a November 2025 report, segment revenue mix was aerospace 60.7%, plasma 28.2%, accelerators 5.7% and fusion 4.6%.

The same report showed the first-half 2025 mix shifting toward fusion at 40.3%, with aerospace at 23.3%, accelerators at 22.7% and plasma at 12.9%.

In aerospace, the company became the first in Korea to deliver Nuri rocket engine components, and in March 2026 it signed a combustor supply contract for next-generation launch vehicles with Hanwha Aerospace.

In fusion, it supplied all tungsten plasma-facing units for KSTAR and has supplied components to national fusion projects including ITER and KSTAR. In accelerators, the company said in a December 2025 Korean brokerage report that it had localized about 80% of key components such as klystrons.

In plasma, it is preparing to enter the nuclear decommissioning market based on radioactive waste reduction technology, and it has delivered melting equipment to Korea Hydro & Nuclear Power.

Major customers include aerospace and defense companies such as Hanwha Aerospace and national research institutes such as the Korea Aerospace Research Institute. The company was spun off from Vitzro Tech in 2016 and operates as a subsidiary of Vitzro Tech.

04

Earnings analysis

Based on DART periodic reports, revenue rose 21.1% to KRW 36.8 billion in 2025 from KRW 30.4 billion in 2024. The operating loss narrowed to KRW 7.36 billion from KRW 14.1 billion, and the operating margin improved from -46.3% to -20.0%.

The net loss attributable to controlling shareholders narrowed from KRW 15.4 billion in 2024 to KRW 6.6 billion in 2025, and the net loss including non-controlling interests moved over the same period from KRW 15.4 billion to KRW 6.6 billion.

The company posted losses in both 2024 and 2025, and its debt-to-equity ratio fell from 163.7% to 93.0%. Operating cash flow, however, worsened from negative KRW 7.21 billion in 2024 to negative KRW 8.78 billion in 2025, so the smaller loss did not translate into cash generation.

Quarterly trends are read from the first quarter of 2026 through the third quarter. Revenue was KRW 7.12 billion in the first quarter, KRW 6.67 billion in the second quarter (down 6.3% from the prior quarter) and KRW 7.05 billion in the third quarter (up 5.7%).

The operating loss narrowed from KRW 3.37 billion in the first quarter to KRW 3.12 billion in the second, then widened to KRW 3.93 billion in the third.

The net loss attributable to controlling shareholders grew from KRW 2.96 billion and KRW 2.44 billion in the first two quarters to KRW 7.19 billion in the third, which is larger than the third-quarter operating loss. The items behind that gap can be checked in the notes to the quarterly report.

Across the four quarters of 2025, the net loss attributable to controlling shareholders totaled KRW 6.6 billion, the same size as the full-year 2025 figure. The company said it would focus on competitive projects and wind down projects with weak cost structures to improve margins. It has also described rising R&D costs in its early-stage fusion business as a drag on cost improvement.

05

Industry analysis

Vitzro Nextech's core markets, aerospace and fusion, depend on government budgets and national project schedules. A 2025 report cited a forecast that the global launch vehicle manufacturing market could reach USD 18.1 billion by 2028.

The same report noted that its core aerospace and fusion businesses are driven by the public sector and carry lower profitability than private-sector work. In launch vehicles, the next-generation launcher that the government is developing as a successor to Nuri is targeted to send a lander to the Moon in 2032.

Hanwha Aerospace has been described as the systems integrator of a roughly KRW 1 trillion project developing a reusable launch vehicle based on methane engines.

In December 2025, Korea Investment & Securities said the number of domestic players designing and manufacturing liquid rocket engine core products is limited, and it expected domestic launch programs such as the Nuri upgrade to drive revenue growth in 2027.

The fusion business is centered on national projects such as ITER and KSTAR, so timing of orders is tied to project schedules.

The company is classified under electrical equipment, but its actual business is centered on aerospace and fusion, so demand sources and earnings structure differ widely from power equipment peers.

A direct comparison with sector averages is therefore less informative than tracking how launch and fusion project timing affects revenue.

06

Outlook

The largest goal the company has stated is a return to profitability in 2027. During its listing process in November 2025, the company said it would focus on competitive projects to shift its profit structure in 2027.

The targets it presented at that time were revenue of KRW 84.3 billion in 2027 and KRW 93.3 billion in 2028, with aerospace raising its revenue share to about 35%.

On the order side, the integrated wind tunnel supply contract signed on January 16, 2026 runs through October 31, 2029, with payments split by year, and the first-year payment is set at KRW 7.32 billion.

The methane engine combustion test facility design contract, valued at KRW 4.49 billion excluding VAT, runs from September 9, 2026 to May 8, 2027.

The company has said that after the next-generation launch vehicle combustor supply, it expects to take part in new projects such as small methane engines and reusable launch engines and to supply additional parts.

On facilities, the plant currently houses equipment for four business areas, so expansion is a precondition, and a company official said capital expenditure of about KRW 35 billion is needed within four years and more than KRW 70 billion over the long term.

The company is preparing a rights offering with a target timing after 2027 and plans to grow headcount to over 300 by 2027. It also plans to invest KRW 14 billion in R&D through 2030 to build an integrated research hub. Whether these targets are met will become clear from periodic reports after 2027.

Bull factors

Expanding order backlog

The integrated wind tunnel supply contract of KRW 27.2 billion excluding VAT is paid in annual installments through October 31, 2029.

In March the company signed a roughly KRW 4.5 billion combustor contract with Hanwha Aerospace, and in September it signed a KRW 4.49 billion methane engine design contract with the Korea Aerospace Research Institute. Contracts recognized as revenue over several years can improve visibility into future earnings.

Growth in fusion and launch programs

In December 2025, Korea Investment & Securities analyzed that next-generation launch vehicles and the Nuri upgrade program would contribute to revenue growth in 2027. In fusion, the company supplied all tungsten PFUs for KSTAR and said new products such as vacuum vessel ports would raise its share within projects. Fusion was the largest segment in first-half 2025 revenue at 40.3%.

Narrower operating loss and cost control

The 2025 operating loss narrowed to KRW 7.36 billion from KRW 14.1 billion, and the operating margin improved from -46.3% to -20.0%. Revenue rose 21.1% to KRW 36.8 billion in the same year. Commentary attributed the improvement to both revenue growth and cost-cutting efforts.

Bear factors

Three straight quarters of operating losses

Operating losses were KRW 3.37 billion in the first quarter of 2026, KRW 3.12 billion in the second and KRW 3.93 billion in the third, marking three consecutive quarters of losses. The third-quarter loss widened again.

The net loss attributable to controlling shareholders rose to KRW 7.19 billion in the third quarter from KRW 2.44 billion in the second. Quarterly revenue stayed between KRW 6.67 billion and KRW 7.12 billion.

Cash outflow and funding needs

Operating cash flow worsened from negative KRW 7.21 billion in 2024 to negative KRW 8.78 billion in 2025. Equity stands at KRW 59 billion, but cash generation has not kept pace with the improvement in the income statement. The planned rights offering after 2027 points to a funding need and the possibility of a larger share count.

Reliance on public orders and lower profitability

Aerospace and fusion, the company's major businesses, are driven by the public sector and tied to order timing and budgets. The same report judged these businesses to carry high external uncertainty and lower profitability than private-sector work.

The company has also said its plant mixes equipment for four business areas, which remains a production efficiency issue.

Risk factors

Order and contract terms

The company has said contract amounts and periods may change as work proceeds. Launch and fusion projects can be delayed by government schedules and budgets, which could push back revenue recognition. The next-generation launch vehicle schedule, which targets a lunar lander launch in 2032, is also a long-term variable.

Financing and funding

With operating losses and operating cash outflows continuing, the company has pointed to a rights offering with a target timing after 2027. If the funding leads to share dilution, it would weigh on the value of existing shareholders' stakes.

Capital expenditure for plant expansion is put at about KRW 35 billion within four years, so the funding need could grow.

Cost structure and R&D burden

The early-stage fusion business has seen R&D costs rise sharply, which the company has cited as a difficulty in improving its cost structure. The company said it would wind down projects with weak cost structures and seek recovery of costs already incurred. A planned KRW 14 billion R&D investment through 2030 could keep near-term cost pressure elevated.

Overall view

Vitzro Nextech narrowed its 2025 operating loss to KRW 7.36 billion on revenue of KRW 36.8 billion, up 21.1%, but operating losses have continued for three straight quarters through the third quarter of 2026.

Orders in 2026 included a KRW 27.2 billion integrated wind tunnel supply contract, a roughly KRW 4.5 billion launch vehicle combustor contract and a KRW 4.49 billion methane engine design contract. These contracts are recognized over several years and form the base for future quarterly revenue.

On the other side, operating cash outflow widened to negative KRW 8.78 billion in 2025, and a rights offering after 2027 is planned.

Bullish factors include the expanding order backlog, fusion component share and the narrower loss, while bearish factors include continued quarterly losses, cash outflows, and reliance on public orders with lower profitability.

Both sets of factors have support, and which one prevails will become clearer through future disclosures. The 2027 break-even target the company has stated can only be confirmed in periodic report figures after the 2027 annual report.

Valuation can be summarized as a stock trading at a large premium to net asset value before earnings turn positive.
