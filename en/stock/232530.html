02

Business structure

Energy Material Technology produces NCM-based precursors for cathode materials used in electric vehicle batteries and energy storage systems. According to its company overview, annual capacity is 6,120 tons, and NCM-based precursors account for 100% of revenue.

A precursor is the intermediate material before cathode active material, so cost and quality at this stage feed directly into the competitiveness of finished cathodes. The company says it reuses waste precursors and applies a metal dissolution process to lower raw material costs.

It also highlights manufacturing technology for high-capacity, high-output cathodes and eco-friendly processes. Development of next-generation precursors such as NCM424 and NCM622 is ongoing.

The domestic precursor market includes large cathode players and specialist firms, and the company positions itself as a small-to-mid specialist competing on cost and recycling. Saramin classifies the company as a small and medium enterprise.

Because revenue depends on one product, its volume and price determine results. Nickel and cobalt prices and precursor selling prices directly affect costs and margins. Standalone sales in 2025 rose 269.6% year on year, and operating profit turned positive.

03

Recent trends

Standalone sales for July–September 2026 were KRW 68.71 billion, up 78.05% from KRW 38.59 billion in April–June 2026. Operating profit in the same quarter was KRW 1.12 billion, down 76.52% from KRW 4.77 billion in April–June.

Cumulative April–September 2026 sales were KRW 107.30 billion, and cumulative operating profit was KRW 5.89 billion. The disclosure labels these as internal management figures under K-IFRS standalone statements.

Revenue rose sharply by quarter while profit fell in the same quarter, which points to a possible shift in product mix or cost burden. The disclosure does not specify the cause of the profit decline.

The 2025 annual standalone results show sales of KRW 36.40 billion, operating profit of KRW 4.87 billion, and net income of KRW 2.68 billion. In 2024 the company posted an operating loss of KRW 1.0 billion and a net loss of KRW 1.9 billion.

Equity was negative KRW 1.3 billion at the end of 2024 and turned to positive KRW 1.4 billion at the end of 2025. The share price was volatile in 2026. The 52-week range is KRW 2,960 to KRW 15,900, with the high recorded on May 8, 2026.

On June 24, 2026 the stock rose 14.29% in a day on sharply higher volume, and between June 23 and July 30 it traded between KRW 8,440 and KRW 12,990.

04

Outlook

The core question in the current figures is whether higher sales volume converts into a recovery in operating margin. Sales in July–September 2026 rose from April–June, but the operating margin is calculated to have fallen from about 12% to about 2%.

Bullish paths include the cost effect of the waste-precursor recycling process and whether next-generation precursors such as NCM424 and NCM622 reach mass production. A larger share of next-generation products would improve product mix and create room for higher operating margins.

Continued electric vehicle and ESS demand could lift precursor order volumes. On the other side, if nickel and cobalt prices pass through to selling prices with a lag, margins may come under pressure.

Because the business rests on a single product, volume changes at a major customer or slower EV demand would feed directly into results. On the balance sheet, liabilities of KRW 77.0 billion at the end of 2025 far exceed equity of KRW 1.4 billion, so the pace at which earnings rebuild the equity base matters.

The next checkpoint is whether quarterly operating margin recovers from the roughly 2% level seen in July–September.

05

Bull factors

Pace of top-line expansion

Standalone sales in July–September 2026 reached KRW 68.71 billion, up 78.05% from April–June, and cumulative April–September sales came to KRW 107.30 billion. Given the business structure, this growth appears to come from expanded NCM-based precursor output.

Standalone sales in 2025 also rose 269.6% year on year, so top-line expansion has shown up across more than one period.

Cost structure built on waste-precursor reuse

The company says its waste-precursor reuse and metal dissolution process secures a cost advantage. The company overview links this cost competitiveness and proprietary technology to improved profitability.

Manufacturing technology for high-capacity, high-output products is being developed alongside next-generation precursors such as NCM424 and NCM622.

Return to profit and end of capital impairment

Results moved from a 2024 operating loss of KRW 1.0 billion and net loss of KRW 1.9 billion to 2025 operating profit of KRW 4.87 billion and net income of KRW 2.68 billion. Equity recovered from negative KRW 1.3 billion at the end of 2024 to KRW 1.4 billion at the end of 2025.

The turnaround and equity recovery are improvements on a 2025 annual basis, and the financial cushion remains thin.

Bear factors

Sharp drop in quarterly margin

Operating profit in July–September 2026 was KRW 1.12 billion, down 76.52% from KRW 4.77 billion in April–June. Calculated from the disclosed figures, the operating margin fell from about 12% to about 2% over the same comparison.

Cumulative April–September operating profit of KRW 5.89 billion equals roughly 5.5% of cumulative sales of KRW 107.30 billion.

Heavy reliance on liabilities

At the end of 2025, total liabilities were KRW 77.0 billion against total assets of KRW 78.4 billion. Equity of KRW 1.4 billion leaves the standalone debt-to-equity ratio at 5,428.94%. While liabilities remain far larger than equity, swings in earnings feed more directly into financial stability.

Single-product concentration and share price volatility

Revenue is concentrated in a single product, with NCM-based precursors accounting for 100% of sales. The 52-week range runs from KRW 2,960 to KRW 15,900, and after a 14.29% one-day jump on June 24, 2026, the stock continued to swing.

Raw material prices and changes in EV and ESS demand could affect both earnings and the share price at the same time.

07

Risk factors

Macro

Interest rate levels and the pace of global EV adoption determine demand for battery materials, so the company is highly sensitive to external variables. Rate changes can also affect customers' capital spending plans and vehicle production schedules.

Currency movements can become a profitability variable for materials companies with significant export exposure.

Industry

Sharp moves in nickel and cobalt prices can squeeze margins during the lag before they pass through to precursor selling prices. Intense competition from large domestic and overseas cathode players would pressure both selling prices and volume.

If mass production of next-generation precursors is delayed, the timing of product mix improvement would also slip.

Financial

Equity of KRW 1.4 billion at the end of 2025 is thin relative to liabilities. The company was in capital impairment in 2024, and whether the recovery rests on lasting factors will become clearer in later financial statements. KONEX listing traits such as heavy swings in volume and share price can change financing conditions.

08

Overall view

The most important feature of recent results is a quarter in which sales grew but profit fell. Sales for July–September 2026 reached KRW 68.71 billion, up 78.05% from the prior quarter, while operating profit dropped 76.52% to KRW 1.12 billion, leaving the operating margin at roughly 2%.

The 2025 return to profit and the end of capital impairment support the view that the balance sheet is improving. Liabilities that remain far above equity, however, limit financial flexibility.

Because the business depends on a single NCM-based precursor product, it is directly exposed to raw material prices and EV and ESS demand. The share price range of KRW 2,960 to KRW 15,900 over 52 weeks points to considerable volatility.

Bullish factors are sales expansion and cost structure, while bearish factors are the quarterly margin decline and financial leverage. This is a qualitative summary for informational purposes.
