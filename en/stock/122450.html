02

Business structure

KX Innovation is the core listed company of a group built around semiconductor component manufacturing, leisure, and broadcasting. Manufacturing and leisure are the two main pillars of the group.

The manufacturing business sits mainly with its subsidiary KX HITECH, which produces trays used to transfer products in semiconductor fabrication and SSD cases, among other items.

To meet demand for high-performance, high-capacity eSSDs, it is running its Vietnamese subsidiary at full capacity and expanding production facilities.

The leisure business includes domestic golf courses such as Shinra Leisure and Paju Country Club, and is expanding overseas through Tojonomori CC in Japan, a mixed-use leisure complex in Clark, Philippines, and a golf resort in Hue Province, Vietnam.

The broadcasting business covers broadcast transmission services and channel and advertising operations. In August 2026 the group decided to merge its subsidiary M&C NetMedia in a small-scale absorption merger with a merger date of August 21, 2026.

Its security subsidiary KX NexG obtained the first domestic development certification for a next-generation PQC VPN. In 2024 the group sold Tejeibe CC and reinvested the proceeds into overseas leisure expansion.

Where it once relied partly on partners for some processes, KX HITECH has shifted toward building its own production capacity as orders have grown.

04

Earnings analysis

On a disclosed basis, revenue was KRW 326.3 billion in 2022, KRW 403.1 billion in 2023, KRW 403.8 billion in 2024, and KRW 346.1 billion in 2025, with 2024 the largest year of the 2022–2025 period.

Operating profit went from KRW 78.6 billion in 2022 to KRW 62 billion in 2023 (down 21.1%), KRW 59.7 billion in 2024 (down 3.7%), and KRW 38.4 billion in 2025 (down 35.7%), a three-year decline from 2023 through 2025. The operating margin narrowed from 24.1% in 2022 to 11.1% in 2025.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders rose 373.2% to KRW 82.6 billion in 2024, a figure that news coverage in February 2026 linked to a one-off disposal gain from Tejeibe CC, which was sold in 2024.

It then fell 75.9% to KRW 19.9 billion in 2025, while net income including non-controlling interests was KRW 25.6 billion, a different figure. Operating cash flow rose from KRW 60.2 billion in 2022 to KRW 107.4 billion in 2024 before dropping to KRW 72 billion in 2025.

Quarterly results show a sharp swing: fourth-quarter 2025 operating profit was KRW 854 million, down 95.8% from the prior quarter, and the group posted a net loss attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 5.13 billion as it turned to a loss.

First-quarter 2026 revenue was KRW 71.9 billion, down 22.3% from the prior quarter, while operating profit rose to KRW 3.81 billion and the net loss narrowed to KRW 3.63 billion.

Second-quarter 2026 revenue reached KRW 112 billion, operating profit KRW 25.2 billion, and net income attributable to controlling shareholders KRW 11.7 billion, a return to profit.

Third-quarter 2026 operating profit of KRW 21.6 billion fell 14.1% from the prior quarter but rose 7.5% year over year, while net income attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 6.7 billion fell 53.9% year over year.

The reason operating profit grew while attributable net income fell is a question for the cost and financial line items in the next annual report.

05

Industry analysis

Demand for the manufacturing business comes from expanding semiconductor and AI data center investment.

KX HITECH is running its Vietnamese subsidiary at full capacity and expanding facilities to meet demand for high-performance, high-capacity eSSDs, and its first-half 2026 operating profit rose 827% year over year, as disclosed in August 2026. That growth rate rests on a low base and should be read with that in mind.

The leisure business is highly seasonal, with the second quarter as the peak; the company cited the seasonal peak and the consolidation of Tojonomori CC, acquired in 2025, as drivers of first-half leisure profitability.

Overseas leisure projects in Clark, Philippines and Hue, Vietnam are still in development, so geographic diversification is at an early stage. The broadcasting business plays a shrinking role in group revenue, and the company said its subsidiary merger aims to improve management efficiency.

On competition, KX HITECH is moving from reliance on partners for some processes toward building its own capacity in trays and SSD cases, which points to a push to secure production volume directly.

KX's business mix differs sharply from large logistics companies in the same industry classification, so direct peer comparison of profitability is limited.

The cycle position of the manufacturing business depends on semiconductor back-end and data center investment flows, so whether demand in those flows holds up is the next point to watch.

06

Outlook

When it announced first-half 2026 results in August 2026, the company said it aims to keep raising earnings in the second half through sustained leisure performance, expansion of its global network, and maximizing synergies among affiliates. That is a statement of direction rather than a numerical annual target.

On leisure, construction has started on a mixed-use leisure complex in Clark, Philippines, described as the largest single private development of its kind in the area, with an opening targeted for the second half of 2027 at the earliest, based on a February 2026 report.

Following the acquisition of Tojonomori CC in Japan, the group is accelerating overseas portfolio expansion, and a golf resort in Hue Province, Vietnam is also under development.

On manufacturing, KX HITECH judged that production capacity must be expanded to meet accumulated orders and medium- to long-term demand, and it has moved into capital investment. The scale and completion timing of that investment were not given as figures in the material reviewed.

On the group structure, the merger of M&C NetMedia was set for a merger date of August 21, 2026. When the 2026 annual report discloses fourth-quarter results and the full-year operating margin, the comparison point will be the 11.1% margin recorded in 2025.

Contributions from new overseas leisure sites are unlikely to appear before the second half of 2027, so through the first half of 2027 the manufacturing business and existing leisure assets are likely to drive group earnings.

Bull factors

Steep Recovery at the Semiconductor Subsidiary

KX HITECH reported first-half 2026 revenue of KRW 76.2 billion and operating profit of KRW 14.8 billion, with operating profit up 827% year over year, in an August 2026 disclosure. It is running its Vietnamese subsidiary at full capacity and adding facilities to meet high-performance eSSD demand.

The fact that this subsidiary sits at the center of the group's operating profit recovery is the main bullish point.

Consecutive Quarterly Recovery Since Q2

Second-quarter 2026 revenue of KRW 112 billion rose 55.9% from the prior quarter, and third-quarter revenue of KRW 116 billion extended the increase for a second consecutive quarter. Net income attributable to controlling shareholders returned to profit at KRW 11.7 billion in the second quarter.

Third-quarter operating profit of KRW 21.6 billion was 7.5% higher than a year earlier, so the recovery continues at the operating level.

Overseas Leisure Expansion and a Stronger Balance Sheet

The debt-to-equity ratio fell to 92.6% at the end of 2025 from 196.2% in 2023. Proceeds from the Tejeibe CC sale have been used to broaden the overseas leisure portfolio, including the acquisition of Tojonomori CC in Japan.

The Clark mixed-use complex in the Philippines, with an opening targeted for the second half of 2027 at the earliest, is cited as a potential driver of leisure growth.

Bear factors

Three Years of Declining Operating Profit

Operating profit fell from KRW 78.6 billion in 2022 to KRW 38.4 billion in 2025, a decline in each of the three years from 2023 through 2025. The operating margin narrowed from 24.1% to 11.1% over the same period.

In the fourth quarter of 2025, operating profit of KRW 854 million dropped 95.8% from the prior quarter, and the company posted a net loss attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 5.13 billion.

Volatile Net Income Driven by One-Off Gains

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders reached KRW 82.6 billion in 2024, a figure that included a one-off disposal gain from Tejeibe CC, and fell 75.9% to KRW 19.9 billion in 2025. Over the last four quarters it totaled KRW 9.62 billion.

Third-quarter 2026 net income attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 6.7 billion fell 53.9% year over year, so operating recovery has not yet carried through to net income.

Complex Business Mix and Seasonality

Because the business spans manufacturing, leisure, broadcasting, and other affiliates, earnings are hard to attribute to a single source. Leisure is highly seasonal with a strong second quarter, and first-quarter 2026 revenue of KRW 71.9 billion fell 22.3% from the prior quarter.

A brokerage report from March 2026 noted that leisure and broadcasting revenue declined year over year in late 2025.

Risk factors

Dependence on End-Market Demand

Growth in the manufacturing business relies on semiconductor and AI data center investment and demand for high-performance eSSDs. A slowdown in that demand or changes in customer investment timing could affect utilization and profit at KX HITECH together. If demand weakens, the Vietnamese operations and new capacity could also add to fixed-cost pressure.

Execution Risk in Overseas Leisure

Overseas leisure projects, including the Clark complex in the Philippines, the Vietnamese golf resort, and Tojonomori CC in Japan, depend on development schedules and local approvals. Delays would push back the timing of investment recovery and of group earnings.

Because the group is funding overseas investment partly from asset sale proceeds, confirming the results of that investment is important.

Financial Load and Earnings Volatility

Total liabilities at the end of 2025 were KRW 450.1 billion, close to total equity of KRW 485.9 billion. The debt-to-equity ratio has come down from 196.2% in 2023, but financing costs can still weigh on profit.

Quarters in which operating profit and net income moved in opposite directions mean the quality of earnings needs continued review.

Overall view

In 2026, KX's quarterly revenue and operating profit have been recovering, supported by the earnings rebound at its semiconductor component subsidiary and the seasonal effect of its leisure business.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders returned to profit in the second quarter of 2026, and revenue rose for two consecutive quarters through the third quarter. On the other side, annual operating profit fell for three straight years from 2023 through 2025, and the fourth quarter of 2025 swung to a loss.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders in 2024 included a one-off disposal gain, which makes year-to-year comparisons difficult, and the last four quarters total only KRW 9.62 billion.

On the balance sheet, the debt-to-equity ratio fell from 196.2% in 2023 to 92.6% in 2025, yet total liabilities remain large. Overseas leisure and manufacturing capacity projects are likely to show up in results mainly from 2027 onward, so near-term judgments are difficult.

The next checkpoints are the full-year operating margin in the 2026 annual report, first- and second-quarter 2027 results, and the opening schedule of the Clark leisure complex.
