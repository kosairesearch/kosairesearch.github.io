02

Business structure

Bookook Steel manufactures and sells steel products from production plants in Gwangju, Iksan and Suncheon. It supplies hot-rolled and cold-rolled sheets to automotive and home-appliance makers, and steel pipes and formed products to NH and steel distributors.

Since 1977 it has been a designated POSCO hot-rolled and cold-rolled distributor, so both its raw-material sourcing and sales channels lean heavily on the POSCO supply relationship.

Major customers include KIA and Samsung Electronics, whose demand moves with the business cycles of the automotive and home-appliance industries. The sales mix is reported as concentrated in sheet products, with pipes and traded products playing a smaller role.

On competition, low-priced products from China and other emerging producers, along with the easing of tariff barriers, are cited as sources of price pressure. Its position as a POSCO designated distributor is regarded as a strength among small and mid-sized steelmakers.

04

Earnings analysis

Full-year results show falling sales alongside a weakening earnings base. Sales fell from KRW 209.7 billion in 2022 to KRW 191.4 billion in 2023 (down 8.7% year on year), KRW 181.8 billion in 2024 (down 5.0%) and KRW 179.9 billion in 2025 (down 1.0%), and declined for three consecutive years from 2023 to 2025.

Among 2022 to 2025, 2022 was the largest year for sales and 2025 the smallest. Operating profit fell from KRW 5.92 billion in 2022 to KRW 2.42 billion in 2023 (down 59.2%) and KRW 95.6 million in 2024 (down 96.0%), before rising to KRW 262 million in 2025 (up 174.4%).

The operating margin declined from 2.8% in 2022 to 1.3% in 2023, then 0.1% in both 2024 and 2025. In 2025 sales fell while operating profit rose, and the company attributed this to lower cost of sales, which more than offset a decline in shipment volume.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 7.1 billion in 2022, KRW 3.62 billion in 2023 (down 49.0%), KRW 2.33 billion in 2024 (down 35.6%) and KRW 2.15 billion in 2025 (down 7.9%), and net income including non-controlling interests followed the same path.

Operating cash flow moved from KRW 4.33 billion in 2022 to negative KRW 1.05 billion in 2023, KRW 7.25 billion in 2024 and negative KRW 9.74 billion in 2025, so cash flow turned negative in 2025 even as operating profit rose.

On a quarterly basis, the company moved from an operating loss of KRW 121 million in the third quarter of 2025 to an operating profit of KRW 232 million in the fourth quarter, and the KRW 458 million operating profit in the first quarter of 2026 was up 97.5% from the prior quarter.

Second-quarter 2026 sales of KRW 57.2 billion rose 24.3% quarter on quarter, but the company swung to an operating loss of KRW 208 million. Third-quarter sales of KRW 48.1 billion fell 15.9% from the prior quarter but rose 10.4% from a year earlier, while the operating loss widened slightly to KRW 225 million.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders peaked at KRW 708 million in the first quarter of 2026 and then fell for two straight quarters to KRW 369 million and KRW 364 million.

The second quarter still posted positive controlling-shareholder net income despite an operating loss, so the composition of non-operating items is a point to examine in the next periodic report's notes.

The last four quarters total KRW 2.11 billion of net income attributable to controlling shareholders, close to the KRW 2.15 billion recorded for full-year 2025.

05

Industry analysis

The steel industry faces two pressures at once: volatile raw-material prices and an inflow of low-priced imported products. Among the three major domestic steelmakers, POSCO posted a 4.6% separate-basis operating margin in the third quarter of 2026, Dongkuk Steel Mill about 3.2% and Hyundai Steel Company 0.3%.

The Big Four steelmakers' combined third-quarter 2026 operating profit reached KRW 1.0822 trillion, up 36.5% from a year earlier. That gain is attributed to higher selling prices and a larger share of high-margin products such as automotive sheets.

By contrast, Kisco, a smaller peer, recorded a separate-basis operating loss of about KRW 7 billion in the third quarter of 2026, and its cumulative nine-month operating loss widened to KRW 27.6 billion.

Automotive and home-appliance demand drives Bookook Steel's sales, and volumes move with the production plans of large customers such as KIA and Samsung Electronics. A prolonged domestic construction slump and global trade protectionism are cited as demand pressures across the sector.

Bookook Steel does not disclose a high-value-added product share in its figures, so a direct comparison with the industry's product mix shift is difficult. Whether the large steelmakers' third-quarter recovery carries through to small and mid-sized processors is therefore the central question for reading the sector.

Outlook

The starting point for the outlook is the third quarter of 2026. Sales of KRW 48.1 billion were higher than the KRW 43.6 billion of the third quarter of 2025, and the KRW 233 million operating loss is in the same direction as the second quarter's loss.

Fourth-quarter 2026 results have not yet been disclosed, and full-year 2026 sales and operating profit will be fixed in the 2026 annual business report, the next periodic filing.

The large steelmakers point to their ability to pass raw-material cost changes through to end-user prices as the key driver for the fourth quarter. Bookook Steel's fourth-quarter sales may move with automotive and home-appliance demand and with whether raw-material costs can be passed on to selling prices.

On shareholder returns, a common-share cash dividend was approved at the March 26, 2026 annual meeting, and a corporate value-up plan disclosed a payout ratio of 71.2%. A treasury-share trust contract was terminated in September 2025 covering 846,000 shares.

Because operating cash flow was negative at KRW 9.74 billion in 2025, changes in working capital will be an item to watch in the year-end 2026 financial statements. The next checkpoints are the operating margin in the 2026 annual business report and whether operating cash flow turns positive again.

Financial capacity and dividend approval

A cash dividend was approved at the March 26, 2026 shareholder meeting, and the corporate value-up plan included a payout ratio of 71.2%.

Third-quarter recovery at large steelmakers

The Big Four steelmakers' combined third-quarter 2026 operating profit rose 36.5% from a year earlier. Higher selling prices and a larger share of high-margin products were cited as drivers.

Whether the large-company recovery reaches small and mid-sized distributors is the benchmark for judging Bookook Steel's earnings improvement.

Shrinking earnings base

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders fell from KRW 7.1 billion in 2022 to KRW 2.15 billion in 2025. The last four quarters total KRW 2.11 billion, roughly one year's worth of earnings. It peaked at KRW 708 million in the first quarter of 2026 and then declined for two straight quarters.

Cash flow and price pressure

Risk factors

Raw-material price volatility

Raw-material prices are highly volatile, so how far costs can be passed through to selling prices determines the margin. The lower cost of sales that supported the 2025 operating profit increase could reverse as raw-material prices move. If pass-through lags, quarterly profit can swing sharply.

Concentration in supplier and customers

Since 1977 the company has been a designated POSCO hot-rolled and cold-rolled distributor, so its raw-material sourcing depends on the POSCO supply relationship. Production swings at large automotive and home-appliance customers feed directly into sales volumes.

Because it relies on a few suppliers and customers, changes in contract terms can have a large effect on results.

Demand slowdown and cash flow

A prolonged domestic construction slump and global trade protectionism are cited as factors weighing on steel demand. Because operating cash flow was negative at KRW 9.74 billion in 2025, working-capital management is important. If inventories and receivables build up during a demand slowdown, cash generation could weaken further.

Overall view

Bookook Steel's third-quarter 2026 sales of KRW 48.1 billion were higher than a year earlier, but an operating loss of KRW 225 million extended the run of loss-making quarters to two.

On the disclosed results, sales fell from KRW 209.7 billion in 2022 to KRW 179.9 billion in 2025 and declined for three consecutive years, while the operating margin dropped from 2.8% to 0.1%. Operating profit rose to KRW 262 million in 2025, and the company attributed the gain to lower cost of sales.

The balance sheet looks stable, with total equity of KRW 131.4 billion and a debt-to-equity ratio of 15.7%, and a cash dividend was approved in March 2026.

On the other side, net income attributable to controlling shareholders fell from KRW 7.1 billion in 2022 to KRW 2.15 billion in 2025, and the last four quarters total KRW 2.11 billion.

The industry shows partial recovery signs, such as the Big Four's higher third-quarter operating profit, but Kisco's nine-month operating loss widened.

Strengths include the sales recovery, financial capacity and dividends, while weaknesses include continued operating losses, a shrinking earnings base and low-priced imports. The next checkpoints are the full-year operating margin and the sign of operating cash flow in the 2026 annual business report.
