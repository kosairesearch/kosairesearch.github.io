Low-Debt Steel Distributor, Quarterly Swings Persist

Bookook Steel maintains a low debt ratio on the strength of its POSCO-designated distributor status, but operating profit has swung between gains and losses each quarter even as revenue recovered after the third quarter of 2025.

02

Business structure

Bookook Steel is a steel distribution-and-processing company that serves as a designated distributor for POSCO's hot-rolled and cold-rolled steel sheets, processing coils into sheets, pipes, and shaped steel products.

Its production plants are located in Gwangju, Iksan in North Jeolla Province, and Suncheon in South Jeolla Province.

Its main customers are automotive-related firms including KIA and appliance makers including Samsung Electronics' Gwangju plant, while it supplies pipes and formed products to NongHyup Economic Holdings and other steel distributors.

The business model involves sourcing hot-rolled and cold-rolled coils from POSCO, processing them, and reselling them, meaning profitability depends heavily on the spread between purchase and selling prices. The largest shareholder is CEO Nam Sang-gyu, who holds a 24.26% stake.

The company has a strong regional character, with the Honam region (Gwangju, South and North Jeolla) as its core operating base.

Within the KOSPI-listed steel and metals sector, it is classified as a small-to-mid-sized processing and distribution specialist, distinct from large manufacturers such as Hyundai Steel Company, Dongkuk Steel Mill, and KG Dongbusteel.

Its status as a POSCO-affiliated designated distributor acts as an entry barrier, while also tying its profitability directly to its supplier's pricing and volume policies.

04

Earnings analysis

Consolidated revenue fell for three straight years, from KRW 209.7 billion in 2022 to KRW 191.4 billion in 2023 (down 8.7% year-on-year), KRW 181.8 billion in 2024 (down 5.0%), and KRW 179.9 billion in 2025 (down 1.0%). Among 2022-2025, revenue was highest in 2022 and lowest in 2025.

Operating profit fell from KRW 5.92 billion in 2022 to KRW 2.42 billion in 2023 (down 59.2%) and KRW 95.6 million in 2024 (down 96.0%), before rebounding to KRW 262 million in 2025 (up 174.4%). The operating margin narrowed sharply, from 2.8% in 2022 to 1.3% in 2023 and just 0.1% in both 2024 and 2025.

Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders also declined for three consecutive years, from KRW 7.1 billion in 2022 to KRW 3.62 billion in 2023 (down 49.0%), KRW 2.33 billion in 2024 (down 35.6%), and KRW 2.15 billion in 2025 (down 7.9%).

On a quarterly basis, revenue rose for three consecutive quarters from a trough of KRW 43.6 billion in the third quarter of 2025 to KRW 44.7 billion in the fourth quarter, KRW 46 billion in the first quarter of 2026, and KRW 57.2 billion in the second quarter of 2026.

Operating results posted losses of KRW 382 million and KRW 121 million in the second and third quarters of 2025, respectively, before turning to a profit of KRW 232 million in the fourth quarter and rising further to KRW 458 million in the first quarter of 2026 (up 97.5% quarter-on-quarter), only to swing back to an operating loss of KRW 208 million in the second quarter of 2026.

Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders rose to KRW 708 million in the first quarter of 2026 before falling 47.9% quarter-on-quarter to KRW 369 million in the second quarter (up 25.6% year-on-year).

Operating cash flow swung from a surplus of KRW 7.25 billion in 2024 to a deficit of KRW 9.74 billion in 2025, widening the gap between booked profit and actual cash generation.

05

Industry analysis

The domestic steel industry has recently gone through a period of eroding profitability. Operating profit at 50 major domestic steelmakers fell an average of 27.7% over the preceding year, with 36 of the companies surveyed posting lower operating profit or losses.

Even large players such as Hyundai Steel Company and POSCO saw operating profit plunge by more than KRW 600 billion, showing the downturn spread to major producers as well.

Industry observers point to high raw material price volatility and a rise in low-priced Chinese steel imports as the main causes of the profitability decline.

As a POSCO-designated distributor, Bookook Steel counts large customers such as KIA and Samsung Electronics among its main clients, making its business sensitive to swings in the automotive and appliance industries. However, signs of a somewhat different mood have begun to appear entering 2026.

POSCO Holdings was projected to post an operating profit of KRW 805.6 billion in the third quarter of 2026, driven by improved profitability in its steel division. Across the industry, efforts to address low price-to-book ratios through corporate value-up initiatives have also spread.

KG Dongbusteel's board finalized a corporate value-up plan in October 2026 targeting a price-to-book ratio above 0.5 by 2029.

06

Outlook

On March 27, 2026, Bookook Steel disclosed a voluntary corporate value-up plan setting a target of maintaining a payout ratio of around 25% based on standalone net income, within the bounds of distributable profit.

This is a forward-looking policy target distinct from the 71.2% payout ratio for fiscal year 2025, which was temporarily elevated due to the small profit base.

The fiscal 2025 year-end dividend was set at KRW 80 per common share, with total dividends of roughly KRW 1.53 billion, and the dividend yield at the time of disclosure was presented as 3.8%.

Looking at quarterly trends, revenue rose for three consecutive quarters after the trough in the third quarter of 2025, but operating results swung from a profit in the first quarter of 2026 back to a loss in the second quarter, showing continued quarter-to-quarter volatility.

No specific capacity expansion or new facility investment plans from the company were confirmed through search.

As a result, the future direction of earnings is likely to depend heavily on the pace of demand recovery in downstream industries such as automobiles and appliances, as well as POSCO's pricing policy for hot-rolled and cold-rolled coil supply.

It will be necessary to check upcoming periodic reports from the third quarter of 2026 onward to see whether the revenue growth trend continues and whether operating results return to profit.

Bull factors

Low Debt Ratio and Stable Financial Structure

The debt-to-equity ratio stood at 15.7% at the end of 2025, having declined every year since 2022. Total equity of KRW 131.4 billion at the end of 2025 far exceeds total liabilities of KRW 20.7 billion. This provides a financial buffer even through a period of prolonged industry weakness.

Codified Shareholder Return Policy

The corporate value-up plan disclosed in March 2026 set a target of maintaining a payout ratio of around 25% based on standalone net income. For fiscal 2025, a cash dividend of KRW 80 per common share, totaling roughly KRW 1.53 billion, was decided.

A track record of sustaining cash dividends despite earnings volatility serves as a reference point on shareholder returns.

Sequential Revenue Recovery

Revenue rose for three consecutive quarters from the fourth quarter of 2025 through the second quarter of 2026, after bottoming at KRW 43.6 billion in the third quarter of 2025. Second-quarter 2026 revenue of KRW 57.2 billion rose 24.3% quarter-on-quarter and 22.2% year-on-year.

There was also a stretch in the first quarter of 2026 when operating profit rose 97.5% quarter-on-quarter to KRW 458 million.

Bear factors

Multi-Year Decline in Revenue and Profit

Revenue declined for three straight years from 2023 through 2025, falling from KRW 209.7 billion in 2022 to KRW 179.9 billion in 2025. Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders also fell for three consecutive years over the same period, from KRW 7.1 billion to KRW 2.15 billion. This reflects a prolonged slowdown in downstream demand and the broader industry downturn.

Extremely Thin Operating Margins

The operating margin fell from 2.8% in 2022 to just 0.1% in both 2024 and 2025. In the second quarter of 2026, the company again posted an operating loss of KRW 208 million, showing how easily quarterly profitability can swing.

At this margin level, small shifts in input costs or selling prices can readily flip results from profit to loss.

Cash Flow Volatility

Operating cash flow swung from a surplus of KRW 7.25 billion in 2024 to a deficit of KRW 9.74 billion in 2025. While booked net profit remained positive, actual cash generation deteriorated sharply. This points to a need to examine working capital burdens and changes in inventory or receivables.

10

Risk factors

Raw Material Price Pass-Through Risk

Because Bookook Steel purchases hot-rolled and cold-rolled coils from POSCO and processes them before resale, profitability hinges on the spread between purchase and selling prices. Raw material price volatility has been cited as one of the main causes of declining profitability in the sector. A change in coil supply pricing policy could directly affect margins.

Sensitivity to Downstream Industry Cycles

Main customers include automotive-related firms such as KIA and appliance makers including Samsung Electronics' Gwangju plant. Fluctuations in production and demand in the automotive and appliance industries are directly reflected in revenue. High customer concentration means a slowdown in any single industry can have a sensitive impact on results.

Inflow of Low-Priced Chinese Steel Products

Industry observers have pointed to a rise in low-priced Chinese steel imports as a key cause of profitability deterioration. As a distribution and processing company, Bookook Steel is directly exposed to this import competition pressure in setting its selling prices. Future trade measures or policy changes could affect the pricing environment.

Overall view

Bookook Steel has maintained financial soundness on the back of its stable status as a POSCO-designated distributor and a low debt ratio.

However, both revenue and net profit attributable to controlling shareholders declined for three consecutive years from 2023 through 2025, and the operating margin thinned to just 0.1% in both 2024 and 2025.

Entering 2026, quarterly revenue rose for three straight quarters after bottoming in the third quarter of 2025, but operating results continued to swing, turning to a loss in the second quarter after a profit in the first.

Operating cash flow also flipped from a surplus in 2024 to a deficit in 2025, revealing a gap between booked profit and actual cash generation. The corporate value-up plan disclosed in March 2026 set a payout ratio target of around 25%, providing a reference point for future shareholder return policy.

The upcoming third-quarter 2026 periodic report and the dividend decision expected in early 2027 will be the next milestones for gauging the direction of earnings and shareholder returns.
