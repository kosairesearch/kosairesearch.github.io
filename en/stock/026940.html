02

Business structure

Founded in 1976 and listed on the KOSPI in 2008, Bookook Steel is a primary steel manufacturing and processing company with production operations in Gwangsan-gu, Gwangju.

Its core business is the manufacture and sale of steel products such as hot- and cold-rolled sheets, with automakers and appliance makers as key customers. Large domestic buyers are supplied directly by major steelmakers including POSCO, while Bookook Steel serves as a sales agent to smaller businesses.

The company holds POSCO-designated processing and sales agent status, giving it a stable raw material supply channel. Low-priced steel imports from later-industrializing countries such as China, combined with tariff liberalization, remain an ongoing competitive factor.

On a consolidated basis, the debt ratio declined from 21.9% in 2022 to 15.7% in 2025, reflecting relatively stable financial health. Because most revenue stems from domestic steel distribution, earnings tend to track the construction, automotive, and appliance cycles in Korea.

04

Earnings analysis

Consolidated revenue in 2025 was KRW 179.91 billion, slightly down from KRW 181.82 billion in 2024, while operating profit rose sharply to KRW 262 million from KRW 96 million a year earlier. Even so, the absolute scale remained small, with operating margin stuck at roughly 0.1% in both 2024 and 2025.

Net income attributable to owners declined for four consecutive years, from roughly KRW 7.1 billion in 2022 to KRW 3.62 billion in 2023, KRW 2.33 billion in 2024, and KRW 2.15 billion in 2025.

Operating cash flow swung from an inflow of KRW 7.25 billion in 2024 to an outflow of KRW 9.74 billion in 2025, underscoring volatility in cash generation.

On a quarterly basis, Q2 2025 revenue was KRW 46.8 billion with an operating loss of KRW 382 million, followed by Q3 2025 revenue of KRW 43.6 billion with a further operating loss of KRW 121 million.

The company then returned to profit for two straight quarters, posting operating income of KRW 232 million on revenue of KRW 44.7 billion in Q4 2025 and KRW 458 million on revenue of KRW 46.0 billion in Q1 2026.

However, in Q2 2026 revenue jumped to KRW 57.2 billion yet the company swung back to an operating loss of KRW 208 million. Net income attributable to owners stayed positive in every quarter regardless of the operating result, suggesting non-operating items have played a significant role in shaping quarterly earnings.

05

Industry analysis

The small and mid-cap steel distribution sector that Bookook Steel belongs to is structurally sensitive to downstream demand from the automotive, appliance, and construction industries.

Korea's steel distribution network is bifurcated, with major steelmakers like POSCO supplying large buyers directly while designated distributors such as Bookook Steel serve smaller customers.

Across the industry, expanding production capacity and low-cost export volumes from later-industrializing countries such as China have intensified price competition in the domestic market. Tariff policy changes are a direct variable affecting the price competitiveness of imported steel products.

A slowdown in construction activity combined with moderate automotive and appliance demand has been a common pressure point, reducing shipment volumes across steel distributors.

Against this backdrop, Bookook Steel is seen as having the financial capacity to withstand a soft industry cycle, supported by a low debt ratio and a stable capital structure.

06

Outlook

On March 27, 2026, Bookook Steel disclosed a corporate value-up plan targeting a payout ratio of around 25% of standalone net income, within the bounds of distributable profit.

This follows a payout ratio of 71.2% in the prior fiscal year (2025), which met the high-dividend company criteria under Korea's Restriction of Special Taxation Act, making the trajectory of future dividend policy relative to earnings worth monitoring.

The company previously signed a KRW 2 billion treasury stock trust agreement on June 27, 2025, before terminating it on September 10, 2025, indicating it has weighed both dividends and buybacks as shareholder return tools.

With the 2026 semiannual report already filed, the next scheduled regulatory filing—the Q3 2026 report—is due by November 16, 2026 under Korea's capital markets law.

Given the recurring pattern of alternating quarterly operating profits and losses, recovery in shipment volumes and cost stabilization will likely be key to any improvement in operating margin.

The modest decline in the largest shareholder and related parties' combined stake during 2026 is also worth watching from an ownership structure standpoint.

Bull factors

Low Debt Ratio and Sound Capital Structure

The debt ratio steadily declined from 21.9% in 2022 to 15.7% in 2025, reflecting improving financial stability. This provides the company with financial flexibility to endure a soft industry cycle. Total equity has also remained stable at around KRW 130 billion with little fluctuation, indicating a solid asset base.

Commitment to a Stable Dividend Policy

In its corporate value-up plan disclosed in March 2026, the company targeted a payout ratio of around 25%, signaling an effort toward more predictable shareholder returns. In the prior fiscal year (2025), the payout ratio reached 71.2%, meeting high-dividend company criteria. A past treasury stock trust agreement also illustrates the use of buybacks as a shareholder value tool.

Distribution Position Within the POSCO Supply Chain

Its status as a POSCO-designated sales agent secures a stable raw material supply channel. Its distribution role for smaller customers fills a market space that major steelmakers do not serve directly. This position can act as a barrier that makes rapid entry by new competitors difficult.

Bear factors

Four Consecutive Years of Declining Profit

Net income attributable to owners has declined every year, from roughly KRW 7.1 billion in 2022 to KRW 2.15 billion in 2025. Operating margin also fell from 2.8% in 2022 to around 0.1% in 2024-2025, thinning profitability.

Revenue has likewise declined for four straight years, from roughly KRW 209.7 billion in 2022 to KRW 179.9 billion in 2025.

High Quarterly Earnings Volatility

After posting operating losses in Q2-Q3 2025, the company returned to profit in Q4 2025 and Q1 2026, only to swing back to an operating loss in Q2 2026 despite a sharp revenue increase. Such quarter-to-quarter swings suggest the cost structure or pricing power may not be stable.

The fact that non-operating items often determine the direction of net income also makes earnings harder to predict.

Intensifying Competition from Low-Priced Steel Imports

Expanding production capacity and low-cost export volumes from later-industrializing countries such as China are intensifying price competition in the domestic distribution market.

Tariff policy changes are a variable directly affecting the price competitiveness of imported steel, representing a risk outside the company's control. Given its domestically focused revenue structure, the company is also exposed to slowdowns in construction, automotive, and appliance demand.

Risk factors

Industry and Demand Risk

If demand weakness in downstream industries such as automotive, appliances, and construction persists, declines in shipment volumes could recur. A broader industry slowdown could weaken pricing power, constraining any improvement in operating margin.

As a small to mid-sized steel distributor, the company has relatively limited price negotiating power compared with major steelmakers.

Raw Material and Import Competition Risk

Fluctuations in raw material prices such as iron ore directly affect the cost of goods sold. Expanding inflows of low-priced imported steel, including from China, could place downward pressure on domestic distribution prices. Tariff and trade policy changes are external variables that the company cannot directly control.

Ownership Structure and Liquidity Risk

Disclosures show the combined stake of the largest shareholder and related parties edged down from 47.10% to 46.04% during 2026. As a small-cap stock, trading value and liquidity may be limited, which could increase share price volatility.

The repeated signing and termination of treasury stock trust agreements in the past is also worth monitoring in terms of consistency in shareholder return tools.
