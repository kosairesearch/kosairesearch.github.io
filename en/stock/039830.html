02

Business structure

Aurora World develops its own character IP and earns revenue from toy sales, licensing and merchandising, and royalties tied to content broadcasts.

A 2021 technical analysis report on the KOSDAQ-listed company noted that the animated series Yoohoo and Friends, broadcast in 190 countries in 20 languages from 2019, supported toy sales and royalty income.

The same report highlighted the company's exclusive toy distribution rights for externally popular IP such as Pinkfong's Baby Shark, which broadened its product lineup. Revenue is heavily weighted toward toys.

According to the Butler data aggregator, character toys accounted for 92% of second-quarter 2026 revenue, trademark royalties and similar income for 6%, and rental income for 2%. A toy-heavy mix means toy sales trends and IP popularity cycles drive overall revenue.

The licensing segment is small but could grow as the IP is used across more categories. The competitive picture depends on business model. The Pinkfong Company (The Pinkfong Company), which also owns kids' IP, is the closest comparison.

Hybe, SM Entertainment, JYP Ent. and YG Entertainment center on artist management, records and concerts, so their revenue structure differs. In broadcast and content production, CJ ENM, Studio Dragon and SBS are the reference names, while Cheil Worldwide and Innocean Worldwide represent advertising agencies. Aurora World stands apart by combining IP ownership with toy distribution.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual revenue rose from KRW 231.7 billion in 2022 to KRW 232.6 billion in 2023 (up 0.4%), KRW 275.7 billion in 2024 (up 18.6%) and KRW 328.1 billion in 2025 (up 19.0%), three consecutive years of growth from 2023 through 2025. 2025 was the largest year in the 2022 to 2025 period.

Operating profit went from KRW 18.3 billion in 2022 to KRW 28.4 billion in 2023 (up 54.9%), KRW 31 billion in 2024 (up 8.9%) and KRW 44.5 billion in 2025 (up 43.6%). The operating margin was 7.9% in 2022, 12.2% in 2023, 11.2% in 2024 and 13.6% in 2025.

In 2024 revenue grew 18.6% but the operating margin fell 1.0 percentage point from the prior year. Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 7.34 billion in 2022, KRW 7.11 billion in 2023 (down 3.0%) and KRW 6.3 billion in 2024 (down 11.4%), before jumping to KRW 21.8 billion in 2025 (up 245.3%).

Net income including non-controlling interests was KRW 21 billion in 2025, below the controlling-shareholder figure, which means the non-controlling share was a loss.

Operating cash flow was -KRW 1.85 billion in 2022, then KRW 16.1 billion in 2023, KRW 20.3 billion in 2024 and KRW 19.7 billion in 2025, positive from 2023 onward. The debt ratio (total liabilities divided by total equity) rose from 245.0% in 2022 to 274.2% in 2023, then eased to 269.3% in 2024 and 227.5% in 2025.

At the end of 2025 total liabilities were KRW 404.7 billion and total equity was KRW 177.8 billion, of which KRW 175.6 billion belonged to controlling shareholders. Quarterly results swung widely.

From the third quarter of 2025, with revenue of KRW 87.9 billion and operating profit of KRW 16.7 billion, the fourth quarter brought revenue of KRW 83.4 billion (down 5.0% from the prior quarter), operating profit of KRW 9.33 billion (down 44.0%) and controlling-shareholder net profit of KRW 1.89 billion (down 79.0%).

The first quarter of 2026 recovered to revenue of KRW 97.5 billion (up 16.8% quarter on quarter) and operating profit of KRW 14.2 billion (up 52.2%). The second quarter fell back to revenue of KRW 91.2 billion (down 6.5%) and operating profit of KRW 12.2 billion (down 14.2%).

The third quarter of 2026 posted revenue of KRW 109.2 billion (up 19.8% quarter on quarter and up 24.3% year on year) and operating profit of KRW 19.4 billion (up 59.6% quarter on quarter and up 16.7% year on year).

Operating profit for the first three quarters of 2026 totals KRW 45.8 billion, above the full-year 2025 figure of KRW 44.5 billion. Controlling-shareholder net profit was KRW 9.03 billion in the third quarter of 2026, down 10.1% from the prior quarter.

Over the last four reported quarters (fourth quarter of 2025 through third quarter of 2026), controlling-shareholder net profit totaled KRW 29.2 billion.

05

Industry analysis

Revenue at character IP companies moves with content broadcasts, toy sales and licensing working together. In this model, the popularity cycle of hit characters drives toy sales, so quarterly swings tend to be larger than annual trends.

The operating profit decline in the fourth quarter of 2025 and the revenue decline in the second quarter of 2026 illustrate this pattern. In kids' content, the closest direct comparison is The Pinkfong Company, which also owns kids' IP.

Both companies commercialize characters and content aimed at children, so their business models are close. Hybe, SM Entertainment, JYP Ent. and YG Entertainment earn mostly from artist activities, so their revenue structure differs from Aurora World's.

Grouping the entire entertainment sector as one benchmark can therefore distort comparisons. CJ ENM, Studio Dragon and SBS are driven by content scheduling and production revenue, while Cheil Worldwide and Innocean Worldwide depend on advertising, so their end demand differs.

In toy distribution, Aurora World owns its IP and designs and sells the products itself. As a result, operating leverage tends to be large while a hit cycle lasts, and revenue falls quickly when the popularity fades.

Toy consumption trends and demand through overseas channels such as the United States are the variables that separate quarterly results. In cycle terms, the reported figures show a phase since 2024 in which revenue and operating profit have risen together. How long that phase lasts depends on whether the IP keeps its popularity.

Bear factors

Quarterly earnings volatility

Revenue fell 6.5% quarter on quarter in the second quarter of 2026, so the swings continued. Quarterly profit remains volatile even when the annual trend is positive.

Large debt load

Total liabilities at the end of 2025 were KRW 404.7 billion, more than twice total equity of KRW 177.8 billion. The debt ratio peaked at 274.2% in 2023 and eased to 227.5% in 2025, but it remains well above 200%. The burden depends on how well cash flow holds up going forward.

Concentration in toy sales

According to Butler's data aggregation, character toys made up 92% of second-quarter 2026 revenue, with trademark royalties and similar income at 6% and rental income at 2%. A decline in toy sales would pull down total revenue quickly. Second-quarter 2026 revenue fell 6.5% quarter on quarter.

Risk factors

IP popularity cycle

Character IP popularity rises and falls over time, so a fading hit can reduce toy sales and royalty income together. If new IP acquisition or expansion of existing IP is delayed, revenue gaps can last longer. The declines in the fourth quarter of 2025 and the second quarter of 2026 show how this risk can materialize.

Borrowing and leverage

Total liabilities of KRW 404.7 billion are more than twice total equity. Rising interest rates or changes in borrowing terms could erode profit through higher interest costs. Because operating cash flow has been positive since 2023, whether that cash generation supports debt repayment is the central question.

Overseas business variables

Rising US revenue makes local distribution structure and exchange rates more relevant to profit. Because the June 2026 report highlighted US growth, the impact of these external variables grows as the overseas share rises. If overseas sales depend on a single product, results could shift with that product's life cycle.
