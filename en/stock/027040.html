Report overview

Narrower Losses, but the Business Pivot Is Still Being Tested

Seoul Electronics & Telecom has cut its operating loss every year since 2022, yet it posted a net loss attributable to controlling shareholders again in the third quarter of 2026, while debt, loan maturities and a change of largest shareholder remain open issues.

02

Business structure

Seoul Electronics & Telecom listed on KOSDAQ in December 1999 and operates in power components and EMS. As of the first half of 2021, power components accounted for 60.9% of segment revenue and EMS for 39.1%.

The power component line includes audio power parts and industrial power parts such as power transformers and SMPS used in ships and power plants. The EMS segment handles production of electronic products such as POS terminals.

The company's profile lists KIOSK, touch pads, POWER TRANS and SMPS as main products, with SONY headquarters, YAMAHA, LG-linked and other customers named as key counterparties in its profile materials.

Its direction has shifted from audio and industrial power parts toward EV power components and next-generation power solutions. Its value-up plan puts ODM and OEM sales share and cost structure improvement at the center and sets a medium-term target of KRW 50 billion in revenue by 2029.

In August 2026, it was reported that the company would enter a pay-after-purchase rewards platform built on POS and payment infrastructure, partnering with Hyzwang and pursuing 50,000 merchants. Revenue from the new business does not appear as a separate segment figure in the disclosures.

Compared with large component makers such as Samsung Electro-Mechanics and LG Innotek, the company's capital base is much smaller, with total equity of KRW 8.75 billion at the end of 2025.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual revenue fell from KRW 51.8 billion in 2022 to KRW 34.6 billion in 2023, a 33.3% decline, rose to KRW 35.8 billion in 2024 (up 3.6%), and dropped to KRW 33.7 billion in 2025 (down 5.8%). 2022 was the largest year and 2025 the smallest in the 2022–2025 window.

The operating loss narrowed each year, from KRW 7.73 billion in 2022 to KRW 3.76 billion, KRW 2.23 billion and KRW 1.61 billion in 2025, and the operating margin improved from -14.9% to -4.8%.

In 2025 the company attributed the revenue decline to lower sales at domestic and overseas subsidiaries, while cost reductions and cost improvement narrowed the operating loss.

The net loss attributable to controlling shareholders widened to KRW 14.5 billion in 2023, narrowed to KRW 2.74 billion in 2024, and widened again to KRW 5.34 billion in 2025, when early redemption of bonds weighed on results.

Operating cash flow moved from KRW 2.82 billion in 2022 and KRW 2.41 billion in 2023 to negative KRW 522 million in 2024 and negative KRW 2.67 billion in 2025. The debt-to-equity ratio rose from 137.9% in 2022 to 362.7% in 2024 and was 351.1% in 2025.

Quarterly, the operating loss has persisted for five consecutive quarters from the third quarter of 2025 through the third quarter of 2026. Revenue bottomed at KRW 5.94 billion in the first quarter of 2026, then rose to KRW 8.8 billion and KRW 11.6 billion in the second and third quarters.

The operating loss in the third quarter of 2026 was KRW 11.26 million, narrower than the prior quarter.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders turned positive at KRW 3.09 billion in the second quarter of 2026, then returned to a net loss of KRW 488 million in the third quarter, and the last four quarters total KRW 1.08 billion.

Whether the second-quarter profit was one-off is a question the detailed profit and loss items in the periodic reports will answer.

05

Industry analysis

Seoul Electronics & Telecom occupies a different position from large listed component makers in the electronics and components sector.

Peers such as Samsung Electro-Mechanics, LG Innotek and Isupetasys have scaled through large customer programs, while the company is a small operator centered on power conversion devices such as transformers and SMPS, plus EMS.

End demand is broadening from consumer and audio power into industrial power, SMPS for AI data centers and EV power components. The value-up plan names EV power components and high value-added power solutions as core tasks.

A report said the company signed a long-term supply contract with a global company for next-generation SMPS for AI data centers, but the contract size and the timing of revenue recognition are not shown in the disclosed figures.

Components sold to large customers often face a lag between design adoption and volume production, so the timing of orders converting into revenue can drive quarterly swings. The operating margin of -4.8% in 2025 places the company in loss territory, and its profitability gap versus the sector is clear.

If the business pivot takes hold, power design capability could support entry into new applications. However, a small capital base means facility investment and working capital rely on external funding, which constrains its position in the industry.

06

Outlook

Because the company has set a medium-term target of KRW 50 billion in revenue by 2029, the growth path from the KRW 11.6 billion third-quarter 2026 base is the key item to track. EV power components and a higher ODM and OEM sales share are listed as core items of the value-up plan.

The pay-after-purchase rewards platform involves building dedicated POS and payment infrastructure and developing iOS and global platforms, and reports said the Android version is complete. The company said it would expand partner stores and franchises nationwide and pursue entry into China and Southeast Asia.

On funding, the company decided in June 2026 on a third-party allotment rights offering of KRW 15 billion, but the payment date moved from July 29 to October 29, 2026, with uses stated as KRW 12.4 billion for operating funds and KRW 2.6 billion for debt repayment.

A KRW 3 billion small public offering had its use changed, with about KRW 900 million for operating funds and KRW 1.5 billion for acquiring securities of another company, and the target company had not been determined as of the report. A KRW 2.4 billion bank loan from Shinhan Bank is due in December 2026.

The results of the payment and the target company for the securities acquisition will be settled through later disclosures. In the 2026 annual report, the full-year operating margin and operating cash flow will show whether the business pivot is delivering results.

Bear factors

High leverage and a thin equity base

At the end of 2025 the debt-to-equity ratio was 351.1%, with total equity of KRW 8.75 billion and total liabilities of KRW 30.7 billion. Total equity was KRW 15.0 billion in 2024. A KRW 2.4 billion Shinhan Bank loan is due in December 2026.

Funding timetable adjustments

The payment date for the KRW 15 billion third-party rights allotment was moved to October 29, 2026. The use of the KRW 3 billion public offering was changed, with KRW 1.5 billion allocated to acquiring securities of another company, and reports said the target company was undetermined.

Cash and cash equivalents were reported at KRW 1.0 billion at the end of June 2026, down 86% from the end of 2025.

Negative operating cash flow and overseas subsidiary burden

Operating cash flow was negative KRW 522 million in 2024 and negative KRW 2.67 billion in 2025. Commentary has pointed to loan losses at an overseas subsidiary and guarantee obligations. The net loss attributable to controlling shareholders returned to KRW 488 million in the third quarter of 2026.

Risk factors

Achieving the medium-term revenue target

The company has set a medium-term target of KRW 50 billion in revenue by 2029, while revenue in 2025 was KRW 33.7 billion. Reaching the target requires a higher ODM and OEM sales share and improved cost structure to proceed together.

Components sold to large customers often face a lag between design adoption and volume production, so if order revenue is recognized later, the target timing could slip. A widening gap between the medium-term plan and actual results could change how the plan's credibility is assessed.

Governance and change of largest shareholder

Daon International became the largest shareholder with a 14.9% stake, financed by KRW 3.87 billion in borrowings. Its 2,073,196 common shares were offered as collateral for the loan. The repayment of that loan and further changes in the ownership structure could affect management decisions.

Execution of the business pivot

EV power components, SMPS for AI servers, the rewards platform and overseas expansion are being pursued at the same time. A small capital base may make it hard to run several businesses in parallel.

A share consolidation approved in March 2026 changed the par value from KRW 100 to KRW 500, leaving 13,917,769 common shares outstanding.
