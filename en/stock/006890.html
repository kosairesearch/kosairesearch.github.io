Report overview

Taekyung Chemical: Four Straight Loss-Making Quarters

Taekyung Chemical, a leading domestic carbon dioxide supplier, has seen operating profit fall sharply since 2025 and has posted operating losses in four consecutive quarters from the fourth quarter of 2025 through the third quarter of 2026, making the timing of any earnings recovery the key point to watch.

  1. 1

    Operating profit for 2025 was KRW 2.06 billion, down 84.9% from the prior year, and the operating margin was 3.4%. That compares with a 25.3% margin in 2023.

  2. 2

    The third quarter of 2026 recorded an operating loss of KRW 852 million and a net loss attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 893 million, both swinging to loss from a year earlier. Revenue was KRW 13.4 billion, down 17.9% year on year.

  3. 3

    Financial capacity remains. Total equity at the end of 2025 was KRW 172.6 billion and total liabilities were KRW 51.9 billion, with the debt ratio rising from 12.5% in 2024 to 30.1% in 2025.

  4. 4

    The business is concentrated in carbon dioxide. By segment the split is 92.2% carbon dioxide and 8% environment, and a large share of liquid CO2 demand comes from shipyard welding.

  5. 5

    Price-to-book sits well below 1x, with market capitalization below total equity. Earnings-based multiples are harder to compare with the past because profit has shrunk.

02

Business structure

Taekyung Chemical makes liquid carbon dioxide and dry ice, and press reports and company materials describe it as the domestic leader in carbon dioxide (Maeil Business Newspaper, November 2022). The business is split into a carbon dioxide segment and an environment segment.

A Saramin company profile citing NICE Credit Rating data lists the carbon dioxide segment at 92.2% and the environment segment at 8%. In the first half of 2024 the product mix was carbon dioxide products 67.9%, general gases and other 19.7%, other products 3.5% and magnesium hydroxide 6% (Daily Invest, 2024).

The core of the carbon dioxide segment is high-purity liquid CO2 and dry ice, supplied to industrial sites and food distribution customers. Production runs through four liquid CO2 plants supported by seven raw material suppliers, according to a K5 company profile.

A 2022 brokerage analysis by the Korea IR Association's research center estimated that about 60% of domestic liquid CO2 demand comes from shipbuilding welding and about 20% from semiconductor processes (March 2022).

The same report put the company's share at about 25% in liquid CO2 and about 31% in dry ice, both first place. Delivery companies handling fresh food were named as dry ice customers (Pinpoint News).

The environment segment sells magnesium hydroxide and liquid lime used in wastewater treatment, and the company said it supplies wastewater neutralizers to Samsung Electronics (Maeil Business Newspaper, November 2022).

Shares held by the largest shareholder side were 58.76% in aggregate as of March 2022, with Taekyung BK and two others as holders (Korea IR Association, March 2022).

04

Earnings analysis

On a reported basis, revenue rose from KRW 59.6 billion in 2022 to KRW 70.7 billion in 2023, up 18.7% year on year, then was nearly flat at KRW 71.1 billion in 2024, up 0.5%. It fell to KRW 61.3 billion in 2025, down 13.7%. Among 2022 to 2025, 2024 was the largest year and 2022 the smallest.

Operating profit went from KRW 10.4 billion in 2022 to a peak of KRW 17.9 billion in 2023, up 71.9%, then fell to KRW 13.7 billion in 2024, down 23.3%, and to KRW 2.06 billion in 2025, down 84.9%. Operating profit has declined for two consecutive years, from 2024 through 2025.

Operating margin went from 17.4% in 2022 to 25.3% in 2023, 19.3% in 2024 and 3.4% in 2025. Press coverage tied the 2023 profit jump to tight liquid CO2 supply and rising dry ice demand (Gas News).

In 2024 revenue stalled while operating profit fell, which suggests the 2023 capacity plans did not translate fully into sales growth. In 2025 the company acquired shares in Lion Chemtech in March and absorbed Taekyung Green Chemical through a merger in April (company history), and the debt ratio rose to 30.1%.

Profit attributable to controlling shareholders peaked at KRW 17.4 billion in 2023 and fell to KRW 8.75 billion in 2025. Quarterly, operating profit of KRW 950 million in the third quarter of 2025 turned into an operating loss of KRW 706 million in the fourth quarter.

Operating losses followed in the first quarter of 2026 (KRW 653 million), the second (KRW 1.81 billion) and the third (KRW 852 million), so four consecutive loss quarters.

Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders moved from KRW 3.15 billion in the fourth quarter of 2025 to a net loss of KRW 893 million in the third quarter of 2026, yet the four-quarter sum is KRW 4.33 billion.

The gap between an operating loss and a positive four-quarter total points to non-operating items and taxes, and the breakdown needs the income statement notes in the annual report.

05

Industry analysis

Demand for liquid CO2 comes from shipyard welding and semiconductor processes. A 2022 brokerage analysis by the Korea IR Association estimated that shipbuilding welding accounts for about 60% of domestic liquid CO2 demand and semiconductor processes for about 20% (March 2022).

The same analysis noted that a ship typically takes about 24 months to build. A 2023 report cited the shipbuilding upturn as a driver of sales and mentioned new supply contracts with large shipbuilders (Asia Time, September 2023).

Dry ice demand links to fresh food delivery, food refrigeration and beer fermentation, and the 2022 analysis cited growth in online fresh food sales as a backdrop. In cycle terms, 2023 was a period when the shipbuilding upturn and capacity additions overlapped, followed by flat sales in 2024 and lower profit in 2025.

Quarterly revenue dropped from the KRW 14 billion range in early 2026 to the KRW 12 billion range in the second quarter before recovering to KRW 13.4 billion in the third quarter. On competition, the company is described as first in both liquid CO2 and dry ice domestically (Korea IR Association, 2022).

About 90% of revenue sits in carbon dioxide, so unlike diversified large chemical groups, a shift in shipbuilding orders or food logistics demand flows straight into results.

A 2022 press report also described a domestic raw material CO2 supply disruption, making raw material supply stability another variable for the sector.

06

Outlook

No official company guidance for 2026 or 2027 appears in the material reviewed for this report. A past report quoted the company's investor relations head saying daily production capacity would rise from about 500 tons to about 1,100 tons once new equipment was running (Asia Time, September 2023).

Another report cited daily capacity of about 820 tons and said a plant built with LG Chem would add 600 tons, for 1,420 tons in total (Gas News). The two reports use different capacity bases, so the scale of the expansion cannot be pinned down.

Revenue growth of just 0.5% in 2024 raises the question of how much of the expansion reached sales. A 2024 brokerage estimate put 2024 operating profit at KRW 14.8 billion (Daily Invest, 2024), while the reported figure was KRW 13.7 billion.

On new business, the company's chief executive said in 2022 that it had formed a hydrogen partnership with LG Chem to use CO2 generated in blue hydrogen production (Maeil Business Newspaper, November 2022). Progress on that partnership in 2026 is not covered by the reports reviewed here.

Quarterly revenue in 2026 was KRW 14.0 billion in the first quarter, KRW 12.2 billion in the second and KRW 13.4 billion in the third, with the third quarter down 17.9% year on year. A return to profit needs both revenue recovery and cost improvement. The next checkpoint is the production capacity and utilization data in the 2026 annual report.

Two years of declining profit and thinner margins

Dependence on shipbuilding and customer concentration

Risk factors

Industry cycle and customer concentration

If shipbuilding orders and food logistics demand weaken together, sales could fall on both fronts. The 17.9% year-on-year drop in third-quarter 2026 revenue shows how demand shifts reach reported results. Customer diversification and the share of shipbuilding welding demand are items to track in the annual report.

Raw material supply and costs

The company is described as having seven raw material suppliers. However, a 2022 report mentioned a domestic raw material CO2 supply disruption, and a supply shock could bring production shortfalls and higher costs at the same time. Changes in raw material sourcing and the cost-of-sales ratio are points to watch.

Investment execution and balance sheet changes

The 2023 expansion plans did not fully translate into sales growth. In 2025 the company bought Lion Chemtech shares and merged Taekyung Green Chemical, and the debt ratio rose to 30.1%. The effects of these transactions on profit and capital structure need to be confirmed in the annual report notes.

Overall view

Taekyung Chemical holds a leading position in carbon dioxide and a low debt load, but reported profit has declined for two consecutive years since the 2023 peak, and the operating margin reached 3.4% in 2025.

Quarterly results in 2026 show operating losses in four straight quarters, and the third quarter turned into a net loss attributable to controlling shareholders. The positive four-quarter net profit total of KRW 4.33 billion appears to reflect non-operating items.

Because about 92.2% of the business sits in carbon dioxide, shifts in shipyard welding and food logistics demand feed directly into results. On the asset side, price-to-book sits well below 1x, and all KRW 172.6 billion of equity belongs to controlling shareholders.

On the other hand, as profit has shrunk, earnings multiples and dividend capacity have become harder to read. The next checkpoints are the 2026 full-year results, the production and profit notes in the 2026 annual report, and first-quarter 2027 operating results.
