After the Return to Profit, Core Recovery Is the Test

Union's return to operating profit in 2025 and its lower debt ratio improved its financial footing, but sales fell for three consecutive years from 2023 to 2025 and the company posted an operating loss again in the third quarter of 2026.

  2. 2

  3. 3

02

Business structure

Union operates across cement, ferrite, ceramics and environmental machinery. Based on second-quarter 2026 revenue, cement manufacturing accounted for 48%, ferrite for 30%, ceramics for 20%, and environmental machinery and other businesses for 2%.

The cement segment produces white cement, alumina cement and specialty cement, while ferrite and ceramics are handled by a subsidiary. Consolidated subsidiaries include a Vietnamese ferrite magnet maker, a quarry and aggregate producer and a ground granulated slag producer.

Cement demand is tied to construction activity, so changes in housing construction volumes feed directly into sales. White cement faces price competition from low-priced imports. Non-cement businesses make up 52% of revenue, so sales do not depend on cement prices alone.

04

Earnings analysis

Revenue on a disclosed basis fell from KRW 235 billion in 2022 to KRW 224.6 billion in 2023, KRW 218.2 billion in 2024 and KRW 202.4 billion in 2025. Sales declined for three consecutive years from 2023 to 2025, with 2022 the largest year and 2025 the smallest in the 2022–2025 period.

Operating profit swung from KRW 2.9 billion in 2022 to an operating loss of KRW 1.99 billion in 2023, widened to an operating loss of KRW 11.5 billion in 2024, and returned to operating profit of KRW 5.5 billion in 2025. Operating margins were 1.2% in 2022, -0.9% in 2023, -5.3% in 2024 and 2.7% in 2025.

The company attributed the 2025 change mainly to lower special personnel costs compared with 2024, lower asset impairment charges in 2023 and 2024 versus the prior period, a smaller bad-debt provision and a fair-value gain on financial assets at fair value through profit or loss.

Net results therefore combined cost reductions with a non-operating gain. Net income was a loss of KRW 9.14 billion in 2022, a loss of KRW 2.56 billion in 2023, a loss of KRW 68.9 billion in 2024 and a profit of KRW 25.6 billion in 2025.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders was a loss of KRW 10.1 billion in 2022, a profit of KRW 7.3 billion in 2023, a loss of KRW 42 billion in 2024 and a profit of KRW 23.4 billion in 2025.

Operating cash flow moved from -KRW 9.62 billion in 2022 to KRW 12.5 billion in 2023, KRW 20.2 billion in 2024 and KRW 18.7 billion in 2025. The debt ratio was 115.5% in 2022, 117.4% in 2023, 200.6% in 2024 and 157.3% in 2025.

In the quarters, second-quarter 2026 sales of KRW 54 billion rose 19.4% from the prior quarter and operating profit of KRW 1.2 billion marked a return to profit, while third-quarter 2026 sales of KRW 49.6 billion fell 8.1% from the prior quarter and the company posted an operating loss of KRW 1.71 billion.

Third-quarter 2026 sales rose 0.8% year on year, but the operating loss was wider than the KRW 709 million loss in the third quarter of 2025. Net income attributable to controlling shareholders for the third quarter of 2026 was KRW 461 million.

05

Industry analysis

The cement segment faces two pressures: falling housing construction volumes and low-priced imported white cement. The company's cumulative commentary for the first nine months of 2025 described these factors as pushing market prices lower.

Over the same period, sales fell 6.1% year on year while operating profit and net income turned positive. Cement demand is linked to construction activity, so the direction of housing starts affects both volumes and prices. Ferrite, held through a subsidiary, represents 30% of revenue and ceramics 20%.

With non-cement businesses at 52% of the mix, the company is not confined to one end market. Market share and cost competitiveness versus listed peers in each segment are not presented here in numerical form.

Outlook

Quarterly results were mixed in 2026, with a return to operating profit in the second quarter after a loss in the first, followed by an operating loss of KRW 1.71 billion in the third quarter.

One analysis site listed a recovery in sales and repeat shipments of specialty cement under binding contracts as conditions supporting the investment case.

The same site named a renewed operating loss, weaker cash conversion and failure to convert trial specialty cement projects into contracts as signals that would weaken it. Neither condition is yet visible in reported results, so the next quarterly sales and operating profit figures will show the direction.

The next periodic report is the 2026 annual report, which will settle fourth-quarter and full-year 2026 results and the gap between net income attributable to controlling shareholders and total net income. Repeat specialty cement shipments are the first item to check in the quarterly sales mix.

08

Bull factors

Return to profit and cash generation

Operating profit of KRW 5.5 billion in 2025 reversed the 2024 operating loss of KRW 11.5 billion. Operating cash flow was positive in each year from 2023 to 2025, at KRW 12.5 billion, KRW 20.2 billion and KRW 18.7 billion. Profit scale is modest, but the continuity of cash generation is the core support for this view.

Improved financial structure

The debt ratio fell from 200.6% in 2024 to 157.3% in 2025. At end-2025, total liabilities were KRW 203.8 billion and total equity was KRW 129.6 billion, of which controlling-shareholder equity was KRW 107.8 billion. The direction of capital recovery provides a buffer against swings in earnings.

Possible conversion of specialty cement trials

Specialty cement, alongside ferrite, forms part of the revenue base. If commercial trials convert into contracts, it could become a revenue line separate from cement price pressure.

09

Bear factors

Continued sales decline

Sales fell in 2023, 2024 and 2025, reaching KRW 202.4 billion in 2025, the smallest year in the 2022–2025 period. Third-quarter 2026 sales of KRW 49.6 billion rose only 0.8% year on year, so the recovery in top-line growth remains limited.

Pressure on cement prices

The company's cumulative commentary for the first nine months of 2025 said housing construction volumes declined and low-priced imported white cement pushed market prices lower. Cement is the largest segment at 48% of revenue. If prices fall alongside lower volumes, operating margins come under direct pressure.

Renewed quarterly losses and earnings quality

Operating losses of KRW 711 million in the first quarter of 2026 and KRW 1.71 billion in the third quarter show that losses have recurred. The 2025 result included a fair-value gain on financial assets at fair value through profit or loss. One analysis site noted that a large part of 2025 net income may come from non-operating factors.

10

Risk factors

Industry conditions

If housing construction volumes continue to decline while imported white cement keeps entering the market, cement selling prices could fall. With cement at 48% of revenue, price declines would quickly weigh on consolidated operating margins. Ferrite and ceramics can offset part of this, but their pricing may not move in line with cement.

Financial leverage

Total liabilities at end-2025 were KRW 203.8 billion, larger than total equity of KRW 129.6 billion. The debt ratio of 157.3% is lower than 200.6% in 2024 but still reflects liabilities exceeding equity. Continued operating losses could constrain the pace of earnings recovery given the debt load.

Earnings quality and volatility

Net income of KRW 25.6 billion in 2025 differs from net income attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 23.4 billion because of non-controlling interests. The company's stated drivers include a fair-value gain, so non-operating items can have a large effect on reported earnings.

Quarterly profit has alternated between profit and loss in 2026, so a trend is hard to read from one or two quarters.

Overall view

Union returned to operating profit in 2025 with KRW 5.5 billion, and operating cash flow stayed positive from 2023 to 2025. The debt ratio fell from 200.6% in 2024 to 157.3% in 2025, indicating improvement in the capital structure.

Sales, however, declined for three consecutive years from 2023 to 2025, and KRW 202.4 billion in 2025 was the smallest year in the 2022–2025 period. Quarterly results were mixed in 2026: operating profit of KRW 1.2 billion in the second quarter contrasted with an operating loss of KRW 1.71 billion in the third quarter.

The cement segment faces falling housing construction volumes and low-priced imported white cement. Binding contracts and repeat shipments of specialty cement are the conditions cited for an earnings recovery.

The next checkpoints are the preliminary full-year 2026 results in mid-February 2027 and the 2026 annual report in late March 2027, which will set out the direction of operating profit and the composition of net income attributable to controlling shareholders.
