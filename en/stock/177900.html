02

Business structure

3ALogics is an NFC fabless chip company founded in 2004 that designs and supplies NFC tag ICs, reader ICs and NFC modules. It localized the first domestic 13.56MHz RFID reader chip in 2006, and it now relies on external foundries to produce its NFC reader and tag ICs.

By segment, NFC chips accounted for 64.1% of 2023 sales and NFC modules for 20.3%. Its main applications are electronic shelf labels (ESL), automotive digital keys, smart logistics and authentication, and digital product passports (DPP), with expansion into smart appliances and industrial IoT.

In ESL, it supplies the TNB132M NFC chip to SOLUM, the No. 2 ESL maker globally, and in April 2026 it signed a KRW 11.4 billion supply contract for NFC dynamic tag ICs for North America and Europe.

In automotive, the company said its TNR200 automotive NFC reader chip, which meets Digital Key 2.0, is installed in dozens of models from global automakers. Of 2025 revenue of KRW 14.5 billion, exports were KRW 8.3 billion and domestic sales KRW 6.2 billion.

Overseas large semiconductor companies hold much of the existing market, so the company is concentrating on about ten applications including automotive digital keys and ESL. For DPP, it highlights dual RFID technology that combines NFC and RFID on one chip.

04

Earnings analysis

On a DART-filed basis, annual revenue rose from KRW 14.2 billion in 2023 to KRW 18.1 billion in 2024, up 27.9% year on year, then fell to KRW 14.5 billion in 2025, down 20.0%. Of 2023 to 2025, 2024 was the largest year and 2023 the smallest.

In 2024, operating profit of KRW 1.75 billion turned the business profitable from a loss, at a 9.7% operating margin. The company cited higher sales of NFC chips for automotive and smart logistics, and ESL revenue rose 48% year on year to KRW 7.9 billion.

For the 2023 operating loss of KRW 7.95 billion, with a -56.2% margin, the company attributed the result to derivative valuation losses on the conversion rights of redeemable convertible preferred shares, recognized before the listing. In 2025, operating results swung to a loss of KRW 2.69 billion, a -18.5% margin.

The company pointed to lower sales of NFC chips for smart logistics and lower sales in its access control division amid a construction slump, and it also cited higher non-recurring selling and administrative expenses such as bad-debt write-offs.

Controlling-shareholder net income moved from KRW 8.56 billion in 2024 to a controlling-shareholder net loss of KRW 3.61 billion in 2025.

On a quarterly basis, revenue peaked at KRW 6.13 billion in Q1 2026, up 123.0% from the prior quarter, then fell to KRW 5.35 billion in Q2 and KRW 5.24 billion in Q3, two straight quarterly declines.

Operating profit of KRW 274 million in Q1 2026 gave way to an operating loss of KRW 70.15 million in Q2 and KRW 114 million in Q3, two straight loss quarters. Q3 revenue rose 62.0% year on year, but the controlling-shareholder net loss widened to KRW 636 million. The last four quarters combined show a controlling-shareholder net loss of KRW 2.89 billion.

05

Industry analysis

NFC is a short-range wireless technology that lets chips and devices exchange data, and its uses are widening into ESL, automotive digital keys, authentication and smart logistics.

In a March 2025 report, Korea Investment & Securities analyzed that the global NFC market grew at an average annual rate of 16% over 2017 to 2024. The same report expected the company's growth to accelerate over the following three years as automotive and ESL markets expand.

In a June 2026 report, Mirae Asset Securities forecast that a recovery in its ESL and NFC tag business would bring a full-scale earnings turnaround. The CEO said the global NFC market would reach USD 50 billion by 2028, a projection made by the company itself.

On the chip cycle, the company acknowledged uncertainty in the semiconductor market. Competitively, it faces large overseas chip companies, and it highlights being the first domestic company to localize automotive NFC chips and obtain international certification.

Automotive coverage is widening as connected cars and EV adoption grow. In DPP, the EU's mandate push is expected to create demand for traceability solutions. In a February 2026 report, Daishin Securities presented 2026 as the year the company enters an earnings step-up phase.

06

Outlook

The company attributed its Q1 2026 turnaround to business restructuring and higher sales of higher-value products. Its stated direction is to upgrade its integrated DPP solutions and accelerate global expansion, aiming to pursue growth and profitability together after the turnaround.

A confirmed order is the April 2026 ESL supply contract with SOLUM, which extends the ESL relationship into North American and European volumes. Mirae Asset Securities said Hyundai Motor Group plans to expand NFC-equipped models through 2026, and KG Mobility is scheduled to add NFC to a new model.

In October 2026 the company's VOLTEND battery-free lock solution won an NFC Forum innovation award, and in November 2026 its TNR200 automotive NFC reader chip received the Jang Yeong-sil Award.

The company says it is preparing to enter the DPP market with an 8-channel dual-band product that integrates NFC and RFID on one chip. Its long-term vision targets becoming a top-three global NFC company by 2034.

The next checkpoints are how much SOLUM contract volume shows up in revenue in the Q1 2027 quarterly report, and whether the quarters with operating losses return to profit.

Bull factors

Q1 2026 turnaround to profit

Q1 2026 revenue of KRW 6.13 billion rose 123.0% from the prior quarter, and operating profit of KRW 274 million marked a turnaround. Mirae Asset Securities noted in a June 2026 report that NFC tag IC sales rose 121% to KRW 4.2 billion and led growth. The company said operating profit rose 164% year on year.

Large ESL supply contract with SOLUM

According to an April 2026 DART-linked disclosure report, the company signed a KRW 11.4 billion supply contract with SOLUM for NFC dynamic tag ICs for North America and Europe. The contract equals 78.5% of 2025 annual revenue and exceeds 2025 export sales of KRW 8.3 billion. It extends an existing ESL supply relationship into North American and European volumes.

Recognition in automotive digital keys and DPP

The TNR200 automotive NFC chip, the first domestic product to meet Digital Key 2.0, received the Jang Yeong-sil Award on November 2, 2026. The company said it is supplied as a core component across dozens of models from global automakers.

In October 2026 its battery-free lock solution VOLTEND won an NFC Forum innovation award, and the company said dual RFID orders for DPP increased.

Bear factors

2025 revenue decline and swing to operating loss

2025 revenue fell 20.0% year on year to KRW 14.5 billion, and the company swung to an operating loss of KRW 2.69 billion. The company cited lower NFC chip sales for smart logistics and lower sales in its access control division.

In March 2025, Korea Investment & Securities had forecast record results for 2025, but full-year results ended with lower sales and an operating loss.

Operating losses in Q2 and Q3

After peaking in Q1 2026, revenue declined in Q2 to KRW 5.35 billion and in Q3 to KRW 5.24 billion, two straight quarterly declines. Operating results were a loss of KRW 70.15 million in Q2 and a loss of KRW 114 million in Q3.

The Q3 controlling-shareholder net loss widened to KRW 636 million from the prior quarter, and the last four quarters total KRW 2.89 billion.

Recurring operating cash outflows

Operating cash flow was negative at KRW -2.3 billion in 2023 and KRW -1.87 billion in 2025, with only 2024 positive at KRW 94.58 million. In 2025 operating cash flow turned negative alongside the operating loss. The debt-to-equity ratio fell to 45.0%, but repeated cash outflows from core operations remain a weakness.

Risk factors

Customer concentration and supply chain

The SOLUM contract equals 78.5% of 2025 annual revenue, so the business depends heavily on one ESL customer. Changes in contract execution or in customer orders could move quarterly revenue sharply. Because NFC chips are produced at external foundries, shifts in their production schedules could also affect shipments.

Financing and governance

At the regular shareholders meeting on March 30, 2026, an amendment to the articles raising the issuance limits for convertible bonds and bonds with warrants was approved. Higher issuance limits could increase the number of shares if these instruments are later converted or exercised.

The company planned to use the KRW 20.4 billion in IPO proceeds for next-generation NFC product development and debt repayment.

Policy, regulation and FX

Export sales of KRW 8.3 billion in 2025 exceeded half of annual revenue, so currency swings could affect won-denominated results. The EU digital product passport mandate is reported to be in the advancement stage, and a change in timing or scope could delay expectations for DPP orders. Expansion in automotive digital keys depends on the model launch schedules of automakers.

Overall view

3ALogics is a fabless company specialized in NFC chip design, with ESL, automotive digital keys and DPP as its main applications. On a DART-filed basis, after turning profitable in 2024, revenue fell to KRW 14.5 billion in 2025 and operating results returned to a loss of KRW 2.69 billion.

Q1 2026 produced revenue of KRW 6.13 billion and operating profit of KRW 274 million, but Q2 and Q3 both recorded operating losses. On the balance sheet, the debt-to-equity ratio fell from 145.4% in 2023 to 45.0% in 2025, yet operating cash flow was still negative in 2025.

Bullish factors include the KRW 11.4 billion ESL supply contract with SOLUM, wider adoption of the TNR200 in automakers' models, and rising dual RFID orders for DPP.

Bearish factors are the decline in quarterly revenue since the Q1 2026 peak and a combined controlling-shareholder net loss of KRW 2.89 billion over the last four quarters. Valuation sits low relative to net assets, but earnings-based multiples cannot be calculated.
