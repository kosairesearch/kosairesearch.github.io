02

Business structure

3ALogics is a near-field communication (NFC) fabless chip designer founded in 2004, supplying NFC tag ICs, reader ICs and NFC modules. As of 2023, NFC chips accounted for 64.1% of main sales and NFC modules for 20.3%, according to a Korea Investment & Securities report from March 2025.

The largest application is electronic shelf labels (ESL) used in supermarkets and other stores; the company said ESL sales reached KRW 7.9 billion in 2024, up 48% year on year, in a February 2025 announcement.

The flagship TNB132M is supplied at roughly 100 million units a year and was reported on November 18, 2026 as holding the No. 2 global share in its segment. In automotive, the digital key IC TNR200 is the main product, and supply is being widened to Hyundai Motor Group and other vehicle makers.

In smart logistics and authentication, the TNP200M tag chip supplied to a domestic cosmetics maker lifted 2024 sales by 680% year on year, according to the company. The access-control business also exists, and the company attributed its 2025 sales decline in that unit to a construction downturn.

In 2026 the company said orders for its dual RFID technology grew alongside the push toward mandatory digital product passports (DPP), centered on Europe. Press reports said 2025 sales of KRW 14.5 billion comprised KRW 8.3 billion in exports and KRW 6.2 billion domestic. A 2025 report noted 106 NFC semiconductor patents, and the company listed on KOSDAQ in December 2024.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual revenue rose from KRW 14.2 billion in 2023 to KRW 18.1 billion in 2024, up 27.9% year on year, and then fell 20.0% to KRW 14.5 billion in 2025. Among 2023 to 2025, 2024 was the largest year and 2023 the smallest.

Operating results swung from an operating loss of KRW 7.95 billion in 2023 to an operating profit of KRW 1.75 billion in 2024, which turned back into an operating loss of KRW 2.69 billion in 2025. The operating margin was -56.2% in 2023, 9.7% in 2024 and -18.5% in 2025.

For the 2025 swing, the company pointed to lower sales of NFC chips for smart logistics and lower sales in the access-control unit amid the construction slowdown, and it also cited higher non-recurring selling and administrative costs, including bad-debt provisions.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders was a net loss of KRW 18.9 billion in 2023, a net profit of KRW 8.56 billion in 2024 and a net loss of KRW 3.61 billion in 2025.

In 2024, controlling-shareholder net income of KRW 8.56 billion was well above operating profit of KRW 1.75 billion, which points to a large contribution from non-operating items.

Net income including non-controlling interests was reported as a net loss of KRW 18.9 billion in 2023, a net profit of KRW 8.56 billion in 2024 and a net loss of KRW 3.61 billion in 2025.

Quarterly revenue was KRW 3.23 billion in Q3 2025 and KRW 2.75 billion in Q4 2025, down 15.0% quarter on quarter, before jumping to KRW 6.13 billion in Q1 2026, up 123.0% quarter on quarter.

Revenue then fell for two straight quarters, to KRW 5.35 billion in Q2 2026 (down 12.7%) and KRW 5.24 billion in Q3 2026 (down 2.2% quarter on quarter but up 62.0% year on year).

Operating results were an operating loss of KRW 436 million in Q3 2025 and KRW 2.15 billion in Q4 2025, before a KRW 274 million operating profit in Q1 2026. Operating losses followed in Q2 2026 (KRW 70.15 million) and Q3 2026 (KRW 114 million), two straight loss quarters, with the Q3 loss smaller than a year earlier.

Controlling-shareholder net income was KRW 449 million in Q1 2026, then net losses of KRW 385 million in Q2 and KRW 635 million in Q3 2026, and net losses over the last four quarters total KRW 2.89 billion.

According to the company, first-quarter 2026 operating profit rose 164% year on year, and a brokerage attributed the improvement mainly to higher NFC tag IC sales.

05

Industry analysis

The global NFC market grew at a compound annual rate of 16% over 2017 to 2024, according to a Korea Investment & Securities report from March 2025. The same report said the company sold a cumulative 168 million units and achieved a 34% annual growth rate.

In February 2025, the company's CEO projected the global NFC market would reach USD 50 billion by 2028. In the ESL segment, the company said its No. 2 global share was formally recognized when the TNB132M was designated a Current World Class Product on November 18, 2026.

In automotive, the company became the first domestic firm to obtain AEX-Q100 approval for automotive semiconductors and was the third globally to mass-produce automotive NFC chips, according to the March 2025 report.

For DPP, Europe-centered mandates are cited as a demand driver, and Mirae Asset Securities said in a June 2026 report that NFC and RFID-based traceability is essential to building digital product passports.

As an NFC-specialized fabless designer, the company emphasizes proprietary technology in ESL and automotive, and its focus on NFC tag chips differs from that of large domestic semiconductor makers.

On the cycle, the company said it responded to uncertainty in the semiconductor market by diversifying customers and improving operating efficiency.

Weakness in non-semiconductor applications such as construction is reflected directly in its results through lower access-control sales, so application-level variation feeds straight into earnings.

Outlook

In its announcement of the first-quarter 2026 turnaround, the company set out goals of advancing an integrated DPP solution and accelerating overseas expansion.

The April 2026 ESL NFC dynamic tag IC supply contract for North America and Europe is worth KRW 11.4 billion, and press reports said it exceeds the KRW 8.3 billion of 2025 export sales.

The company said it achieved a cumulative 300 million units of NFC chip sales in March 2026, and in September 2026 it signaled cumulative sales of 400 million units by year-end.

In automotive, Hyundai Motor plans to expand NFC-equipped models through 2026, and another automaker's new model is scheduled to adopt the technology, according to a June 2026 report.

The company aims to become a global top-three NFC company by 2034 by advancing next-generation in-vehicle NFC and on-device AI convergence technology.

In the second half of 2026 it also gained technology recognition, including an innovation award at the NFC Forum in October 2026 and the Jang Yeong-sil Award in November 2026. At the extraordinary shareholders meeting on May 7, 2026, agenda items on executive and outside director appointments were tabled.

The next checkpoint is whether the net losses in the second and third quarters of 2026 narrow as DPP orders and the large supply contract are recognized in revenue. The 2026 annual report will also show how the full-year operating margin moved from -18.5% in 2025.

Bear factors

Continuing quarterly losses

Operating losses continued in Q2 and Q3 2026, making two straight loss-making quarters. The Q3 operating loss was KRW 114 million, and the controlling-shareholder net loss was KRW 635 million.

With net losses over the last four quarters totaling KRW 2.89 billion, the failure of the first-quarter profit to carry into later quarters is the core bearish point.

Customer concentration and sales volatility

The KRW 11.4 billion contract signed in April 2026 equaled 78.5% of 2025 annual revenue, showing heavy reliance on a single large deal. 2025 revenue fell 20.0% to KRW 14.5 billion from 2024. The result was also far below the KRW 30.4 billion annual revenue forecast Korea Investment & Securities presented in early 2025.

Non-semiconductor exposure and cost pressure

The 2025 swing to an operating loss reflected the construction downturn hitting the access-control business along with higher non-recurring selling and administrative costs, including bad-debt provisions. Revenue fell 12.7% quarter on quarter in Q2 2026 and 2.2% in Q3, two straight quarterly declines. The company itself cited uncertainty in the semiconductor market, so external demand risks remain.

Risk factors

Customer and order concentration

NFC revenue from ESL and automotive is concentrated among a few large customers, so changes in contract size and volume can swing quarterly results. The KRW 11.4 billion contract signed in April 2026 exceeds 2025 export sales of KRW 8.3 billion, so the timing of revenue recognition shapes quarterly flows. How the expanded supply shows up in quarters from 2027 onward needs to be confirmed.

Regulatory and market timing

Because DPP mandates are being pursued mainly in Europe, the timing and scope of implementation will determine when demand appears. The company's DPP-related revenue contribution is not broken out as a separate line in quarterly disclosures, making its scale hard to gauge. Expansion of automotive models also depends on automakers' production schedules.

Dilution and financing

At the regular shareholders meeting on March 30, 2026, an agenda item raising the convertible bond and bond-with-warrant issuance limits passed with 95.4% approval among shares voting. Higher issuance limits leave open the possibility of a larger share count depending on the financing route. Net losses over the last four quarters could be a background for additional funding needs.

Overall view

3ALogics is a fabless chip designer supplying NFC tag ICs for ESL, automotive and DPP applications. It moved from a loss in 2023 to a profit in 2024, returned to an operating loss in 2025, and after a first-quarter 2026 turnaround, posted operating losses in the second and third quarters.
