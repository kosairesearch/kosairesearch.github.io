KAON Group: Profit Turnaround Meets Quarterly Margin Pressure

KAON Group returned to operating profit in 2025 and reported a record first quarter in 2026, but in Q3 2026 revenue rose while operating profit fell sharply, so whether profitability holds is the key question.

02

Business structure

KAON Group is a communications and broadcasting equipment maker that presents itself as an AI total solution provider, listed on KOSDAQ in May 2001. Its core businesses are OTT set-top devices and network equipment, with SW and XR solutions and a robotics integration platform added as new businesses.

In Q1 2026, OTT generated about KRW 72.5 billion and network about KRW 49.3 billion of KRW 121.8 billion in consolidated revenue, roughly a 60:40 split, according to an Asia Economy report from May 2026. OTT's share rose from 54.6% of 2022 annual revenue to roughly 60% in Q1 2026.

OTT devices lean on AI-combined high-end 4K products, while network products include Wi-Fi 7 routers and equipment tied to FTTH optical infrastructure. The company says it serves customers across about 90 countries through a network of 240 companies, and 66.2% of its revenue comes from overseas.

Key customers include domestic telecom operators, Japanese telecom groups and North American internet service providers, and a 2021 supply contract with a major Middle East and Africa carrier also exists.

A robotics subsidiary, renamed in September 2025, handles service robots and a control platform, and has been confirmed to supply three core top-module types for Hyundai Motor's MobED platform.

Set-top box competition is intense because technology changes quickly, and the market is shrinking as pay-TV subscriptions decline and penetration saturates. The company is therefore widening its revenue base through overseas network equipment and robotics.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual revenue fell from KRW 611.2 billion in 2022 to KRW 536.4 billion in 2023, a 12.2% decline, and to KRW 489.3 billion in 2024, an 8.8% decline, before rising 5.8% to KRW 517.5 billion in 2025. 2022 was the largest year in the 2022 to 2025 period, and 2024 was the smallest.

Operating profit of KRW 14.4 billion in 2022 turned into an operating loss of KRW 18.9 billion in 2023, and the loss widened to KRW 41.9 billion in 2024 as the operating deficit continued. Operating profit returned to KRW 12.5 billion in 2025, a turnaround to profit.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders moved from KRW 10.9 billion in 2022 to a loss of KRW 17.4 billion in 2023 and a loss of KRW 54.1 billion in 2024, before turning to a profit of KRW 6.66 billion in 2025. Net income including non-controlling interests is a different figure, at KRW 5.81 billion in 2025.

The operating margin was 2.3% in 2022, -3.5% in 2023, -8.6% in 2024 and 2.4% in 2025. Quarterly, the turn to profit gathered pace in Q4 2025, with operating profit of KRW 8.52 billion on revenue of KRW 141.2 billion, a 307.7% rise from the prior quarter.

In Q1 2026 revenue was KRW 121.8 billion, down 13.8% quarter on quarter, while operating profit reached KRW 11.3 billion, up 32.7%. The company attributed this to new overseas customers and expanded supply of high-value new products, and it called Q1 its record quarter.

Q2 2026 revenue was KRW 127 billion, up 4.3%, but operating profit fell 30.1% to KRW 7.9 billion. Q3 2026 revenue rose 6.5% sequentially to KRW 135.2 billion and was up 16.8% year on year, while operating profit fell 78.0% sequentially to KRW 1.74 billion and was down 16.9% year on year.

The specific causes of the margin squeeze are points to examine in the quarterly report's cost lines.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders rose 13.9% sequentially to KRW 4.4 billion in Q3, moving in a different direction from operating profit, and changes in non-operating items are a matter for the quarterly report.

Operating cash flow moved from negative KRW 23.2 billion in 2022 and negative KRW 47.6 billion in 2023 to KRW 41.5 billion in 2024, then shrank to KRW 2.18 billion in 2025. The debt ratio rose from 129.8% in 2022 to 290.4% in 2024 and eased to 226.7% in 2025.

05

Industry analysis

The downstream market splits into set-top boxes and telecom network equipment. Set-top boxes face a shrinking pay-TV base as online video services take share, penetration is near saturation, and many analyses say the overall market is contracting.

Competition is intense because technology changes quickly, and forecasts expect AI-combined high-end products to lead. Network equipment faces a different cycle. The telecom industry expects Wi-Fi 7 equipment built on optical cable to grow more than 60% a year on average through 2030, according to a May 2026 report.

An Asia Economy report said equipment replacement tied to the shift to Wi-Fi 7 and expanding FTTH optical infrastructure benefited the network segment. In North America, a February 2026 brokerage brief said that after Chinese equipment was phased out, demand came from local operators that received BEAD funding.

Another analysis says equipment margins in North America and Japan are higher than in Korea. On cycle position, the OTT segment grew 22.1% year on year in Q1 2026, which one report read as confirmation of a recovery in the core business.

In terms of position, the company is pushing into Japan and the United States on the back of exclusive Wi-Fi 7 supply references with a domestic telecom operator. Memory prices feed directly into set-top box costs, so downstream demand and component prices together shape profitability across the sector.

06

Outlook

The company said that new overseas customers and expanded supply of high-value new products lifted first-quarter profitability, and that order volumes are rising in both OTT and network businesses into the second quarter, so full-year results should improve meaningfully on the prior year (May 2026 report).

Eugene Investment and Securities, a Korean brokerage, projected 2026 full-year revenue of KRW 552 billion, up 7%, and operating profit of KRW 17.7 billion, up 41% (May 2026). Adding Q1 to Q3 2026 operating profit gives about KRW 20.9 billion, already above that full-year projection.

Another Korean brokerage projected Japan and US sales of about KRW 60 billion and KRW 100 billion, respectively, for 2026 (May 2026). A February 2026 brokerage brief also expected North American sales to reach about KRW 100 billion in 2026.

The same brief said robotics sales were expected to reach about KRW 8 billion in 2026 and that the company targets more than double that in 2027. Confirmed business wins include supply of top modules for the MobED platform and BEAD-related contracts with three to four North American local operators.

These market projections are estimates from brokerages and media, not formal company guidance. The next checkpoints are the 2026 fourth-quarter results, segment revenue in the 2026 annual report, and whether the 2027 robotics sales target is met.

Bull factors

Overseas network equipment wins

In North America, a February 2026 brokerage brief said the company signed contracts with three to four local operators that received BEAD funding after Chinese equipment was phased out. One Korean brokerage projected Japan and US sales of about KRW 60 billion and KRW 100 billion for 2026, as reported in May 2026. The company said new overseas customer wins are growing in both its OTT and network businesses.

Profit base and operating leverage

Operating profit returned to KRW 12.5 billion in 2025, and Q1 2026 reached KRW 11.3 billion, a quarter the company described as a record. Adding operating profit for Q1 to Q3 2026 gives about KRW 20.9 billion, above the KRW 17.7 billion full-year projection one Korean brokerage gave in May 2026.

Analysts note that North American and Japanese equipment margins exceed domestic ones, so operating leverage could grow as revenue rises.

Robotics as a new growth leg

The robotics subsidiary was selected as a partner for Hyundai Motor's autonomous mobile robot platform MobED and confirmed supply of top modules for three core services, delivery, patrol and guidance, according to May 2026 reports.

It carries ROMNI, an in-house integrated operations platform, which supports the view that its capability extends beyond hardware supply. A February 2026 brokerage brief cited robotics sales of about KRW 8 billion in 2026, with the company targeting more than double that in 2027.

09

Bear factors

Quarterly profit reversal

Operating profit peaked at KRW 11.3 billion in Q1 2026 and then declined in two consecutive quarters, to KRW 7.9 billion in Q2 and KRW 1.74 billion in Q3.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders rose to KRW 4.4 billion in Q3, moving against operating profit, so changes in earnings composition are a matter for the quarterly report.

Cash generation and debt load

Operating cash flow fell from KRW 41.5 billion in 2024 to KRW 2.18 billion in 2025. Total liabilities were KRW 277.8 billion at end-2025, with a debt ratio of 226.7% against total equity of KRW 122.6 billion.

As of the late July 2026 disclosure date, total guarantees for subsidiary borrowings stood at about KRW 113.8 billion.

Set-top market and cost structure

Set-top boxes face a shrinking market as pay-TV subscriptions decline and penetration saturates, while fast technology change keeps competition intense. Memory costs feed into device costs, and an analysis of the first half of 2023 noted that DRAM purchase costs raised the cost-of-sales burden.

With 66.2% of revenue overseas, a past analysis also flagged dependence on a few overseas customers and limited ability to pass cost increases through to selling prices.

10

Risk factors

Balance sheet

The debt ratio fell from 290.4% in 2024 to 226.7% in 2025, but total liabilities of KRW 277.8 billion still far exceed total equity of KRW 122.6 billion. Several subsidiary guarantees run into 2027, leaving contingent obligations in place.

Operating cash flow dropped from KRW 41.5 billion in 2024 to KRW 2.18 billion in 2025, so operating cash covers a smaller share of debt repayment.

Policy-driven demand timing

North American growth is tied to government budget timing under programs such as BEAD, so revenue recognition can shift even when contracts are signed. The roughly KRW 100 billion North American sales forecast in a February 2026 brokerage brief depends on that timing. A change in the policy that removed Chinese equipment would also alter the demand picture.

Customer concentration and product mix

A past analysis noted high dependence on major overseas customers, which limits the ability to pass cost increases through to selling prices. With 66.2% of revenue overseas, changes in order timing from a few customers could move quarterly revenue materially.

OTT accounted for about KRW 72.5 billion, roughly 60% of Q1 2026 revenue, which also concentrates the mix in one product line.

Overall view

KAON Group returned to operating profit of KRW 12.5 billion in 2025, leaving behind the 2024 operating loss of KRW 41.9 billion. Q1 2026 operating profit of KRW 11.3 billion was a quarter the company called a record, and adding Q1 to Q3 2026 gives about KRW 20.9 billion.

However, Q3 2026 revenue rose 16.8% year on year to KRW 135.2 billion while operating profit fell 16.9% to KRW 1.74 billion. Growth drivers cited include overseas network equipment and robotics, with North American BEAD demand and confirmed MobED module supply as the main examples.

On the other side, a shrinking set-top market, total liabilities of KRW 277.8 billion at end-2025, and the reversal in Q3 2026 profit are cited as weaknesses. A May 2026 brokerage projected 2026 operating profit of KRW 17.7 billion, and Q1 to Q3 2026 operating profit already exceeds that level.

The valuation reading depends on whether profitability holds and how fast the asset and debt structure improves, and the next checkpoints are segment revenue and operating margin in the 2026 annual report and full-year 2026 results expected around February 2027.
