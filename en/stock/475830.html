02

Business structure

Orum Therapeutics, founded in 2016 and based in Daejeon, builds antibody-degrader conjugates (DAC) that link a targeted protein degrader to an antibody through a linker.

The company develops oncology and immuno-oncology drugs on its proprietary TPD² platform and operates a Boston research unit, so Korean and US research teams collaborate.

Its approach degrades target proteins instead of delivering the cytotoxic payloads used in conventional antibody-drug conjugates (ADC), which is the main point of differentiation.

Revenue depends on B2B licensing deals with global pharmaceutical companies, where upfront payments, milestones and royalties are the key financial items.

The representative deal is the October 2023 agreement with BMS for ORM-6151, with a total value of USD 180 million, of which USD 100 million was received as an upfront payment. BMS ended the program in September 2026 after reviewing clinical data.

The blood cancer candidate ORM-1153 has IND clearance from the US FDA, while the solid tumor candidate ORM-5029 was halted during Phase 1 because of liver toxicity.

Among competitors, DAC is drawing attention as a next-generation antibody technology, and Roche's technology collaboration with DAC developer C4 Therapeutics in April 2026 shows that large pharma interest continues.

Annual revenue, however, fell from KRW 135.4 billion in 2023 to KRW 23.56 million in 2025, so the business model is highly sensitive to the timing of licensing deals.

Earnings analysis

On a regulatory-filing basis, revenue went from KRW 135.4 billion in 2023 to KRW 20.9 billion in 2024, a decrease of 84.6% from the prior year, and then to KRW 23.56 million in 2025, a decrease of 99.9%.

Revenue declined for two consecutive years from 2024 to 2025, with 2023 the largest year and 2025 the smallest of the three years.

The 2023 figure falls in the same year as the October 2023 BMS agreement, which suggests deal income was concentrated in that year, though revenue recognition details need confirmation in the periodic report.

Operating profit was KRW 95.6 billion in 2023 with a 70.6% operating margin, then turned to an operating loss of KRW 8.34 billion in 2024, a -39.9% margin. In 2025 the operating loss widened to KRW 51.8 billion, with an operating margin of -219,651.4%.

Controlling-shareholder net income moved from KRW 68.2 billion in 2023 to a controlling-shareholder net loss of KRW 5.74 billion in 2024 and KRW 42 billion in 2025.

Operating cash flow shifted from KRW 94.2 billion in 2023 to negative KRW 11.3 billion in 2024 and negative KRW 36 billion in 2025, reflecting cash used in R&D and operations.

Quarterly revenue was KRW 15.89 million in the third quarter of 2025 and KRW 0.56 million in the fourth quarter, then KRW 24.11 million in the second quarter of 2026 and KRW 24.85 million in the third quarter, up 3.1% from the prior quarter and up 56.4% from a year earlier.

Operating losses ran at KRW 13.9 billion in the third quarter of 2025, KRW 18.3 billion in the fourth quarter, KRW 18.1 billion in the first quarter of 2026, KRW 15.4 billion in the second quarter and KRW 16.1 billion in the third quarter, five consecutive loss quarters.

The controlling-shareholder net income of KRW 63.4 billion in the second quarter of 2026 came amid continued operating losses, so non-operating items may have played a role and the breakdown should be checked in the periodic report.

As a result, the four-quarter controlling-shareholder net income total of KRW 13.7 billion depends heavily on that single profitable quarter.

05

Industry analysis

Antibody-degrader conjugates (DAC) combine targeted protein degradation (TPD) with antibody delivery in place of the cytotoxic payloads of conventional ADCs, and the field is viewed as a next-generation targeted therapy with potential to overcome resistance.

No commercial product exists yet, and most programs remain in clinical development, so the outcome in this field will be decided by clinical data. Large pharma interest continues, including Roche's April 2026 technology collaboration with DAC developer C4 Therapeutics.

Orum is often described as a leader in TPD-based DAC development, yet the BMS program was expanded twice, in June 2025 and April 2026, before being discontinued in September 2026, which illustrates the uncertainty of clinical validation.

Among listed Korean biotech peers such as Alteogen, Celltrion and Samsung Biologics, larger companies have manufacturing and commercial revenue bases, whereas Orum is a small company centered on research-stage pipelines, so the difference in revenue structure and financial capacity is large.

Press reports in early 2026 described Orum as one of the cash-rich companies among KOSDAQ biotech firms ranked around the top 50 by market capitalization, but that cash must be read alongside the pace of R&D spending.

For the sector, the key variable for Orum is whether large pharma DAC partnerships translate into more signed deals. Discovery-stage assets the company has said it plans to license lack clinical data, so the timing of such deals is hard to gauge.

Outlook

The company has said it will keep developing its own pipeline on the DAC platform after the BMS agreement ended. The lead candidate ORM-1153 targets relapsed or refractory acute myeloid leukemia (AML) and has received IND clearance from the US FDA.

The company has also said it will pursue licensing of additional discovery-stage assets, but the stage of those assets and the timing of any deal should be confirmed through disclosures.

With BMS's decision to end development, the obligation to pay up to USD 80 million in further milestones fell away, so near-term deal income will depend on new licensing agreements.

At the end of 2025, total equity was KRW 130.9 billion and total liabilities were KRW 38.4 billion, which leaves financial room, but operating losses have continued through the third quarter of 2026, making the sustainability of R&D spending the main variable.

Press reports in early 2026 said a convertible preferred share issuance added about KRW 288.9 billion in cash, and that the funds were expected to go toward in-licensing and the company's own pipeline.

The conversion period runs from January 14, 2027 to January 13, 2031, so conversions could increase the number of shares outstanding. The next checkpoint is the 2026 annual report, where licensing deal count and R&D spending trends will provide the first formal evidence of any structural change.

The first-quarter 2027 quarterly report will show whether the operating loss narrows and how the revenue mix changes.
