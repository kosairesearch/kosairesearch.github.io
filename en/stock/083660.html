Capital Injections Buy Time as Losses Persist; Return to Profit Is the Test

02

Business structure

CSA Cosmic was founded in 1989, listed on KOSDAQ in 2006, and added its cosmetics business in 2015. It established G&B-linked subsidiary Gentro Group in 2019 and completed a merger with Chocho Factory in 2020.

Company profile data describe seven brands in cosmetics manufacturing and sales, while press coverage cites sixteen brands, so the counting basis differs by source.

The business is organized into three segments: cosmetics manufacturing and sales, construction materials manufacturing and installation, and gaming facility management.

According to press coverage, cosmetics accounted for KRW 5.5 billion, or 77.5%, of the KRW 7.1 billion consolidated revenue in Q1 2026, with construction materials at KRW 1.2 billion and gaming facility management at KRW 400 million.

A separate breakdown for Q2 2026 shows cosmetics at 54%, gaming facility management at 29%, and construction materials at 17%, which indicates the segment mix shifted quarter to quarter.

A corporate information site lists a different mix for March 2026, with cosmetics at 78%, construction materials at 17%, and casino, tourism and leisure at 6%, so the weights depend on the classification method. Construction materials products include water tanks, barrier walls, livestock tanks and linings.

The cosmetics unit both runs its own brands and manufactures products, and the domestic market combines large ODM and OEM players with smaller brands. The company reported 49 employees as of June 2026. Because the three segments face different demand, consolidated revenue cannot be explained by any single segment.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual results show a pattern in which both revenue and operating loss fluctuated.

Revenue fell from 612 eok won (1 eok = 100 million won) in 2022 to 446 eok won in 2023 (down 27.1% year on year), 364 eok won in 2024 (down 18.4% year on year), and 279 eok won in 2025 (down 23.2% year on year), a third consecutive annual decline from 2023 through 2025.

Among 2022-2025, the year with the highest revenue was 2022 and the year with the lowest was 2025.

The operating loss shrank from 92.7 eok won in 2022 to 59.1 eok won in 2023 and 37.7 eok won in 2024, then widened again to 56.3 eok won in 2025, and the company posted an operating loss for four consecutive years from 2022 to 2025. The operating margin was -15.1%, -13.3%, -10.4%, and -20.2%, with 2025 the lowest.

The net loss attributable to controlling shareholders was 123 eok won in 2022, 66 eok won in 2023, 60.1 eok won in 2024, and 66.7 eok won in 2025, while the net loss including non-controlling interests was 123 eok won, 66 eok won, 59.7 eok won, and 66.7 eok won in the same years.

The gap between the two figures is within 0.4 eok won, so the effect of non-controlling interests on earnings was small. Operating cash flow was positive at 2.9 eok won in 2024 but turned negative again at -45.1 eok won in 2025.

The debt ratio fell from 1,088.3% in 2022 to 82.3% in 2025, and at the end of 2025 total equity was 166 eok won (166 eok won attributable to controlling shareholders) and total liabilities were 136 eok won.

On a quarterly basis, results worsened from revenue of 80.8 eok won and an operating loss of 7.9 eok won in Q3 2025 to revenue of 57.9 eok won (down 28.3% quarter on quarter) and an operating loss of 21.9 eok won in Q4.

In Q1 2026, revenue recovered to 70.8 eok won (up 22.3% quarter on quarter) and the operating loss narrowed to 15.8 eok won, but in Q2 the loss widened to an operating loss of 56.9 eok won on revenue of 84 eok won.

In Q3, revenue was 78 eok won (down 7.1% quarter on quarter and down 3.4% year on year) and the operating loss was 51.3 eok won (narrower than the previous quarter and wider than a year earlier).

In Q2 2026 the net loss attributable to controlling shareholders was only 4.2 eok won while the operating loss was 56.9 eok won, which shows that non-operating gains and losses largely determined the quarterly result.

The combined net loss attributable to controlling shareholders over the latest four quarters is 98.7 eok won. The key point of current results is that the revenue recovery has continued but has not yet reduced the operating loss.

05

Industry analysis

The cosmetics manufacturing and sales segment belongs to the KOSDAQ chemicals sector, but under the WiseReport classification it is grouped with cosmetics, apparel, and toys.

Large companies in the same chemicals sector, such as LG Chem, Hanwha Solutions, and Lotte Chemical, center on petrochemicals or battery materials, so their business structures differ and their results are hard to compare directly.

Cosmetics demand is sensitive to domestic and overseas consumption trends and to changes in distribution channels, and the company's total revenue has declined for three consecutive years from 2023 through 2025.

The construction materials segment has a different demand cycle from cosmetics, and the gaming facility management segment depends on leisure-related demand, so its trend may differ from that of cosmetics demand.

Because revenue is small, there is a large gap in production and marketing scale compared with large cosmetics manufacturers. As a change of largest shareholder and financial support from affiliates continue, the company's financial capacity depends more on whether funding can be raised than on industry conditions.

To look for signs of an industry recovery, one needs to check how cosmetics segment revenue moves quarter by quarter. Looking at the cosmetics share of 77.5% in Q1 2026 together with the 54% share in Q2 2026, the segment mix is changing within a short period.

Profitability indicators relative to competitors are meaningful only when disclosure data on the same basis are compared side by side.

06

Outlook

The press reported assessments that the company's incorporation into KH Group and financial support from affiliates have laid the groundwork for a return to profit in 2026.

However, because operating losses continued from Q1 through Q3 of 2026, whether it turns profitable will be decided by the full-year earnings in the 2026 annual report.

The 6th convertible bond, worth 180 eok won (1 eok = 100 million won) and issued in September 2026, was disclosed as to be used for 30 eok won in operating funds and 150 eok won in loans to affiliates.

How the loans to affiliates are recovered or translate into earnings is a major variable for future profit and cash flow.

For the acquisition of the 14th convertible bond of Bitgwa Electronics, decided in April 2026 (40 eok won, an in-kind acquisition based on profit distribution from the PLO partnership), the conversion request period is stated as December 29, 2026 to November 29, 2028.

Since the gaming facility management segment accounted for 29% of revenue as of Q2 2026, whether this segment grows becomes one pillar of the overall revenue recovery.

The cosmetics segment had revenue of about 55 eok won as of Q1 2026, and the brand operating direction and whether sales recover are the points to check. The general public offering rights issue in October 2026 recorded a subscription rate of 61.43%, and the new shares are scheduled to be listed on November 6, 2026.

Whether the company has presented a full-year earnings target is an item that needs to be cross-checked further against disclosure materials.

Bull factors

Improvement in balance sheet ratios

The debt-to-equity ratio fell from 1,088.3% in 2022 to 82.3% in 2025, and total liabilities at year-end 2025 were KRW 13.6 billion. A KRW 15 billion third-party rights offering in 2026 brought in funds from an affiliate of the largest shareholder. Press coverage described this funding as easing concerns about working-capital strain.

Revenue recovery from the low point

Revenue rose from KRW 5.79 billion in Q4 2025 to KRW 7.08 billion in Q1 2026 and KRW 8.4 billion in Q2 2026. Q3 2026 revenue of KRW 7.8 billion also stayed above the Q4 2025 level. If the recovery continues, it could spread fixed costs over a larger revenue base.

Shift in business mix

In Q1 2026, construction materials generated KRW 1.2 billion and gaming facility management KRW 400 million in revenue. A Q2 2026 breakdown put gaming facility management at 29% of revenue, indicating a larger share of the mix. Broader revenue sources outside cosmetics could reduce reliance on a single business.

Bear factors

Five consecutive quarters of operating losses

The company has recorded operating losses for five consecutive quarters from Q3 2025 through Q3 2026. In Q3 2026 the operating loss was KRW 5.13 billion and the net loss attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 4.77 billion, the largest net loss of the last five quarters. The fact that losses did not narrow even as revenue recovered is a weakness.

Share dilution pressure

Press coverage reported that the KRW 15 billion third-party rights offering would issue new shares equal to 70.33% of the previously issued shares.

The KRW 18 billion convertible bond would convert into shares equal to about 60% of total shares, and including existing outstanding bonds the convertible shares total 77.62% of previously issued shares. The dilution burden on existing shareholders is significant.

Negative operating cash flow

Press coverage reported operating cash flow of negative KRW 700 million in Q1 2026, so the negative trend continued. Because the core business is not generating cash, the company continues to rely on equity raises and convertible bond issuance.

Risk factors

Funding and related-party lending

Of the KRW 18 billion convertible bond issued in September 2026, KRW 15 billion is slated for related-party lending. If the timing and terms of repayment of these loans remain unclear, cash outflows could persist. Conversion rights begin in September 2027, so the use of proceeds should be tracked alongside that date.

Revenue decline and business concentration

Total revenue fell from KRW 61.2 billion in 2022 to KRW 27.9 billion in 2025, declining for three straight years from 2023. Cosmetics has been reported as the largest revenue segment, so weak sales there would weigh on overall results.

Because segments face different demand, recovery in one segment alone may not be enough to improve total profit.

Ownership change and affiliate transactions

The largest shareholder changed to an investment partnership affiliated with KH Group, which has led the rights offerings and funding. The KRW 4 billion Vigilant CB acquisition was completed through an in-kind distribution from a fund's profit allocation.

Reverse stock split and share-split decisions have changed the share structure several times, so related-party transactions should be reviewed together.

Overall view

CSA Cosmic recorded operating losses in each year from 2022 through 2025, and the losses have continued for five consecutive quarters through Q3 2026. Revenue fell from KRW 61.2 billion in 2022 to KRW 27.9 billion in 2025, and quarterly revenue recovered to between the mid-KRW 7 billion and KRW 8 billion range in 2026.

However, the operating loss widened to KRW 5.69 billion in Q2 2026 and KRW 5.13 billion in Q3 2026, and operating cash flow was negative in 2025. The debt-to-equity ratio improved from 1,088.3% in 2022 to 82.3% in 2025, but funding has leaned heavily on equity raises and convertible bonds.

Proceeds from 2026 financings are slated for working capital and related-party loans, and conversion could increase the share count by about 60% of total shares. Strengths include affiliate funding and revenue recovery, while weaknesses include persistent operating losses and dilution. The next checkpoints are operating results in the 2026 annual report and the operating loss trend in Q1 2027.
