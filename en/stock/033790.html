Report overview

Sales scale up, but the earnings base is still thin

Fino is shifting from a telecom equipment maker to a secondary battery precursor processor. Sales rose sharply in 2025 and early 2026, but the company posted an operating loss in the third quarter of 2026, and whether its own production capacity starts up is the next key checkpoint.

02

Business structure

Fino is a KOSDAQ-listed company founded in 1990. It was previously known for telecom equipment such as repeaters for mobile networks.

A rights issue allocated to a third party in 2024 made a CNGR affiliate the largest shareholder, and in September of that year the company renamed itself Fino to formalize its shift toward secondary battery materials.

The core business now centers on consignment processing of nickel-cobalt-manganese (NCM) precursors and supply contracts for raw materials such as copper.

Under the model described by the independent research firm Aris in a December 2025 report, when a customer orders precursors, Fino procures nickel feedstock from overseas mines, entrusts it to CNGR, and delivers the precursors CNGR produces to the end customer.

In this structure Fino combines raw material sourcing with sales. Aris said supply contracts signed from January to November 2025 totaled 33 deals with cumulative orders of about KRW 230.2 billion.

In the first quarter of 2026, NCM precursor orders from L&F, a key customer, rose about 150% year on year, according to the company. Management points to CNGR's raw material sourcing capability and cooperation with global battery makers as drivers of sales growth.

CNGR is described as holding a share of roughly 25% of the global precursor market. Telecom equipment (repeaters, Wi-Fi access points) and mobile games remain part of the business, but growth in 2025 was led by precursor sales. The company says it has a dual production setup for NCM precursors and LFP cathode materials in Korea.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual results show the transition under way. Sales fell from KRW 11.4 billion in 2022 to KRW 6.97 billion in 2023, a 38.9% decline, then rose to KRW 30.7 billion in 2024 (up 340.3%) and KRW 264.8 billion in 2025 (up 763.1%). 2025 was the largest sales year among 2022 to 2025.

Operating profit went from KRW 1.46 billion in 2022 to an operating loss of KRW 2.12 billion in 2023, turned to a profit of KRW 7.61 million in 2024, and reached KRW 8.85 billion in 2025.

The operating margin was 12.8% in 2022, -30.5% in 2023, 0.0% in 2024 and 3.3% in 2025, so the profit cushion relative to sales remains thin.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders moved from KRW 1.73 billion in 2022 to a net loss of KRW 1.94 billion in 2023, then widened to a net loss of KRW 2.47 billion in 2024 and KRW 5.85 billion in 2025. The company has posted losses for three consecutive years from 2023 to 2025.

Net income including non-controlling interests shows the same losses and should be read separately from the figure attributable to controlling shareholders. Operating cash flow deteriorated from KRW 1.36 billion in 2022 to -KRW 11.1 billion in 2025.

The debt-to-equity ratio fell to 66.5% at the end of 2025 from 107.4% in 2024. Total equity at the end of 2025 was KRW 98.3 billion and total liabilities were KRW 65.4 billion.

On a quarterly basis, first-quarter 2026 sales were KRW 109.1 billion, up 25.4% from the prior quarter, with operating profit of KRW 9.47 billion and net income attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 8.87 billion, a swing to profit.

The company said the improvement came as selling and administrative expenses stayed near the prior-year level and one-off restructuring costs booked in 2025 disappeared. Second-quarter 2026 sales were KRW 104.4 billion, down 4.3% from the prior quarter, and operating profit was KRW 2.32 billion, down 75.5%.

Third-quarter 2026 sales were KRW 55.2 billion, down 47.2% from the prior quarter and 27.0% from a year earlier, and the company recorded an operating loss of KRW 927 million, a swing to loss.

In August the company announced that first-half 2026 sales of KRW 213.5 billion and operating profit of KRW 11.8 billion were record highs, and it attributed the third-quarter decline to a smaller trading business.

Losses have now persisted for two consecutive quarters from the second quarter to the third quarter of 2026. Among the last four quarters from the fourth quarter of 2025 to the third quarter of 2026, only the first quarter of 2026 was profitable at the net income attributable to controlling shareholders level.

05

Industry analysis

Fino's end market is demand for secondary battery materials. In its first-half 2026 results briefing, the company said the battery market is gradually recovering. Its CEO said AI data center expansion is driving energy storage system (ESS) demand quickly, and that ESS growth is now faster than electric vehicle growth.

The CEO added that battery demand is also expanding in areas such as urban air mobility, electric ships and robots. This is management's own outlook, arguing that demand is broadening beyond electric vehicles. On raw materials, price swings in nickel, cobalt and manganese are cited as a business risk.

The company said it scaled back precursor and copper trading in the third quarter to account for metal price volatility. Views on the cycle position differ.

The company says cumulative third-quarter results grew sharply and that the single-quarter decline is temporary, but single-quarter sales and operating profit did fall. On competition, the company says it has a dual setup producing NCM precursors and LFP cathode materials in Korea.

This is management's claim and no independently verified market share data is cited here. CNGR's share of roughly 25% in the global precursor market is the reference point. The company also cited changes in international tax policy as an external factor reshaping its supply chain.

06

Outlook

The company said in August 2026 that it is building a dedicated LFP cathode plant in Pohang, North Gyeongsang Province, through its joint venture C&P New Materials Technology, with capacity to be expanded in stages to a maximum of 50,000 tons a year.

In a December 2025 report, the independent research firm Aris said Fino plans to start mass production in 2027, with NCM precursor capacity of 110,000 tons and LFP cathode capacity of 50,000 tons a year.

The same report said the precursor business has entered a strong growth phase and that Fino is aiming for KRW 1 trillion in sales in 2027. This is Aris's assessment, not company guidance.

On its third-quarter 2026 results, the company said it is scaling back its NCM precursor consignment trading business and building a supply chain based on overseas capacity it controls. It said the related business is going through sample testing.

On financing, a media report citing an electronic disclosure filed with the Financial Supervisory Service said the company decided to issue 14,294,467 new shares, with Samsung SDI expected to be allotted 6,126,200 shares, for an investment of about KRW 30 billion at KRW 4,897 per share.

If the allotment is completed, Samsung SDI would become a major shareholder, but completion of payment and the final stake must be confirmed in later filings. A 2025 report also said the company plans to enter the recycling business from 2026 in two stages, through acquisition and construction of a plant.

Progress on this plan will need to be confirmed in future disclosures. Fourth-quarter and full-year 2026 results have not yet been disclosed, so the timing of in-house capacity start-up and the operating profit trend after the trading reduction are the next checkpoints.

08

Bull factors

Sales growth and order flow

2025 sales reached KRW 264.8 billion, up 763.1% year on year. The company announced first-half 2026 sales of KRW 213.5 billion as a record. According to Aris, cumulative orders from January to November 2025 were about KRW 230.2 billion, and first-quarter 2026 NCM precursor orders from L&F rose about 150% year on year. Bulls point to the link between sales growth and order flow.

Planned in-house production capacity

The Pohang joint venture plant for LFP cathode materials is planned to expand in stages to a maximum of 50,000 tons a year. Aris reported the company's plan to start mass production from 2027 with NCM precursor capacity of 110,000 tons and LFP cathode capacity of 50,000 tons.

Bulls argue that moving away from consignment processing toward owned production and raw material supply could open room for margin improvement.

Raw material tie-up with the controlling shareholder

A CNGR affiliate became the largest shareholder through a third-party rights issue in 2024, and the company cites CNGR's raw material sourcing and sales network as a foundation of its business.

According to a media report citing an electronic disclosure filed with the Financial Supervisory Service, Samsung SDI is expected to receive an allotment of 6,126,200 shares. If completed, a major battery maker would become a significant shareholder, which bulls cite as support for a broader customer base.

09

Bear factors

Third-quarter sales and profit both fell

Third-quarter 2026 sales were KRW 55.2 billion, down 47.2% from the prior quarter, with an operating loss of KRW 927 million and a net loss attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 4.77 billion. Losses have continued for two consecutive quarters.

The company cited a smaller trading business, but how far the sales base shrinks will need to be confirmed in upcoming results. Bears question whether the first-quarter swing to profit was temporary.

Thin profitability and weak cash generation

The operating margin was 3.3% in 2025, and the net loss attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 5.85 billion, with losses in each year from 2023 to 2025. Operating cash flow was -KRW 11.1 billion in 2025, a sharp deterioration from -KRW 677 million in 2024. Bears argue that profit and cash generation did not keep pace with the 763.1% sales increase.

Dependence on the controlling shareholder and limits of the consignment model

Fino's business relies on consigning raw material processing to CNGR. Bears argue that margins are tied to the consignment and trading structure and are hard to lift. In-house capacity is planned to start mass production in 2027, and the overseas-capacity supply chain is still in sample testing. If this transition is delayed, dependence on the controlling shareholder will continue.

10

Risk factors

Raw material prices and trading volatility

Price swings in metals such as nickel, cobalt and manganese directly affect precursor selling prices and trading profit and loss. The company scaled back trading in the third quarter to account for this risk, and single-quarter sales and profit declined as a result. If raw material prices move unfavorably, quarterly earnings volatility could rise again.

Share dilution, controlling shareholder and policy risk

The decision to issue 14,294,467 new shares leaves room for dilution of shareholder value until the allotment is completed. Heavy reliance on the CNGR affiliate means changes in the controlling shareholder's strategy feed directly into company results.

The company cited changes in international tax policy as an external factor, and an overseas-capacity supply chain could be affected by policy in those countries.

Capital investment and plant start-up timing

The Pohang LFP plant and the planned 2027 mass production assume large capital outlays. The debt-to-equity ratio was 107.4% in 2024 and fell to 66.5% at the end of 2025, but financial strain could rise again as investment spending increases.

If construction or start-up is delayed, the consignment and trading model that currently supports sales could weaken.

Overall view

Fino is shifting from a telecom equipment maker to a company centered on consignment processing and trading of secondary battery precursors. 2025 sales of KRW 264.8 billion rose 763.1% year on year, and operating profit of KRW 8.85 billion stayed positive, but the net loss attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 5.85 billion extended a third straight year of losses, and operating cash flow was -KRW 11.1 billion.

First-quarter 2026 sales of KRW 109.1 billion and operating profit of KRW 9.47 billion marked the peak, and sales declined after the second quarter, turning to an operating loss of KRW 927 million in the third quarter.

The company attributes the third-quarter decline to a smaller trading business and changes in international tax policy. Bullish factors include the raw material tie-up with CNGR, the Pohang LFP plant with its planned 2027 mass production, and the planned allotment to Samsung SDI.

Bearish factors include an operating margin of only 3.3%, metal price and trading volatility, dependence on the controlling shareholder, and possible dilution from new shares. The next checkpoints are the fourth-quarter 2026 results, the 2026 annual business report, and the timing of in-house capacity start-up.
