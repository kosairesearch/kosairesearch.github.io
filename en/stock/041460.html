02

Business structure

Korea Electronic Certification Authority was founded in March 1999 as a certification specialist and runs several certification businesses, with public certificate issuance at its core.

According to its company profile, its business spans public certification, global certification, simple certification, IoT certification, and AI-based security and authentication services, and it is widening its scope around next-generation authentication technology.

The service most often cited as the central pillar of earnings is public certification, which the company describes as digital infrastructure with steady demand.

An earnings commentary on a corporate information site says the company holds a high share of the general corporate certificate market and that stronger customer management supported earnings.

Its milestones include being selected in July 2022 as a government web security certificate issuer and launching an ultra-simple, non-face-to-face public certificate service in February 2023.

Corporate certificates have validity periods, so issuance and renewal recur, and retaining existing customers supports the floor of revenue. Several private certification providers compete in the market, which makes pricing and customer retention key competitive variables.

As of December 2024 the company had six affiliates, including several German entities and an AI-related company, which leaves room for new businesses beyond core certification.

However, the revenue contribution of global certification, IoT certification and AI-based security is not separated in the disclosed earnings table, which makes their scale hard to gauge. With annual revenue of KRW 36.7 billion in 2025, the core public certification business serves as the baseline for earnings.

04

Earnings analysis

On an annual basis, revenue went from KRW 38.5 billion in 2022 to KRW 38.3 billion in 2023 (down 0.5% year on year), KRW 37.1 billion in 2024 (down 3.3%) and KRW 36.7 billion in 2025 (down 0.9%), a decline for three consecutive years from 2023 through 2025.

Operating profit fell from KRW 4.96 billion in 2022 to KRW 3.14 billion in 2023 (down 36.7%), the low point, and then rose to KRW 4.72 billion in 2024 (up 50.4%) and KRW 6.73 billion in 2025 (up 42.4%), an increase for two consecutive years.

The operating margin was 12.9% in 2022, 8.2% in 2023, 12.7% in 2024 and 18.3% in 2025, so the margin was highest in 2025, the year revenue was lowest.

In its preliminary disclosure for 2025, released on February 23, 2026, the company pointed to lower cost of sales as a main driver of the change and also cited a smaller gain from fair-value changes in financial assets.

Profit attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 3.5 billion in 2022, KRW 2.38 billion in 2023 (down 32.1%), KRW 3.96 billion in 2024 (up 66.8%) and KRW 4.67 billion in 2025 (up 17.8%).

Net profit including non-controlling interests was KRW 3.49 billion, KRW 2.37 billion, KRW 3.95 billion and KRW 4.66 billion over the same years, a gap small enough that non-controlling interests had little effect.

Operating cash flow was KRW 5.37 billion in 2022, KRW 4.49 billion in 2023, KRW 5.81 billion in 2024 and KRW 6.45 billion in 2025, the highest of the four years.

On a quarterly basis, revenue was KRW 8.66 billion in the third quarter of 2025, KRW 9.23 billion in the fourth quarter (up 6.5% from the prior quarter), KRW 11 billion in the first quarter of 2026 (up 19.0%), KRW 10.5 billion in the second quarter (down 4.0%) and KRW 10.9 billion in the third quarter (up 3.1% from the prior quarter and up 25.5% year on year).

Operating profit was KRW 1.82 billion, KRW 1.58 billion (down 13.0%), KRW 2.52 billion (up 59.4%), KRW 1.63 billion (down 35.6%) and KRW 2.56 billion (up 57.3%, or 40.4% year on year), so profit swings were larger than revenue swings.

Profit attributable to controlling shareholders fell to KRW 651 million in the fourth quarter of 2025 (down 55.8%) and then recorded KRW 2.34 billion, KRW 1.24 billion and KRW 2.08 billion over the next three quarters, for a four-quarter total of KRW 6.31 billion from the fourth quarter of 2025 to the third quarter of 2026.

At the end of 2025, total equity was KRW 64.7 billion, of which KRW 64.6 billion was attributable to controlling shareholders, and total liabilities were KRW 21.7 billion.

05

Industry analysis

The domestic certification market changed in late 2020, when the mandatory use of public certificates was abolished and private certificates came to operate alongside them.

Since then, certification demand has moved with the digitization of financial, public and corporate transactions, and it differs in character from manufacturing, which swings sharply with the business cycle.

The company's earnings commentary also stresses that its public certification service maintains steady demand as digital infrastructure. A reported high share of the general corporate certificate market is read as the company's biggest competitive asset.

However, the private certification market has many providers, so keeping that share requires continued alignment on pricing and service.

Compared with large platform and system integration companies in the same IT and software sector, its revenue is small, and its business is concentrated in a narrow certification niche. That concentration means earnings can react sharply to regulatory changes or to entry by larger players.

Global certification, IoT certification and AI-based security are presented as growth areas, but their revenue contribution is not separated in the earnings table. In short, the company rests on a stable core demand base and a limited set of growth areas.

Outlook

The business direction shown in the company profile is to widen its scope from public certification, which has steady demand, into global certification, simple certification, IoT certification and AI-based security.

The KRW 11 billion in revenue for the first quarter of 2026 was the peak among the quarters in the earnings table, and the KRW 10.9 billion for the third quarter kept revenue above KRW 10 billion, so core demand appears to be holding.

On profit, operating profit of KRW 2.56 billion in the third quarter of 2026 rose 40.4% year on year, offsetting the decline in the second quarter.

The KRW 3.5 billion treasury share trust contract was terminated early on November 12, 2026 after the acquisition was completed, and treasury shares held as of November 11, 2026 were reported at 1,109,829 shares. A further 569,891 shares were cancelled over the same period.

Cancelling treasury shares reduces the number of issued shares, which affects per-share interpretation. On finances, the debt ratio of 33.6% at the end of 2025 allows room for further investment, but the scale of new-business spending has not yet appeared in disclosures.

Whether the AI expansion through overseas affiliates turns into revenue will be visible in the affiliate disclosures of the next periodic report. The next checkpoint is the 2026 preliminary annual results, where the question is how fourth-quarter profit swings feed into annual operating profit.

The 2026 annual business report will contain segment revenue and affiliate earnings, which will show the weight of the core public certification business against new businesses.

08

Bull factors

Recovery in operating margin

The operating margin fell from 12.9% in 2022 to 8.2% in 2023 and then climbed to 18.3% in 2025. Margin improvement while revenue fell for three years points to a better cost structure. In the preliminary 2025 disclosure, the company cited lower cost of sales as a main factor.

Operating profit of KRW 2.56 billion in the third quarter of 2026 returned to the KRW 2 billion range, and whether the margin trend continues is the next point to watch.

Financial capacity and cash generation

The debt ratio at the end of 2025 was 33.6%, down from 46.7% in 2022, with total liabilities of KRW 21.7 billion against total equity of KRW 64.7 billion. Operating cash flow rose from KRW 5.37 billion in 2022 to KRW 6.45 billion in 2025, so earnings and cash flow moved together.

The large financial capacity is cited as a strength because it provides funds for new-business investment or shareholder returns. Whether that capacity has translated into actual investment will appear in the cash flow items of the next periodic report.

Treasury share cancellation and shareholder returns

With the acquisition complete, the trust contract ends earlier than scheduled, so whether the remaining treasury shares are further cancelled or disposed of is the next point to watch. Treasury shares held as of November 11, 2026 were reported at 1,109,829 shares, and 569,891 shares were cancelled over the same period.

Cancelling shares reduces the issued share count, which can be read as a positive factor for shareholders. Shareholder returns are also linked to the profit increases recorded in 2024 and 2025.

Bear factors

Revenue down three years in a row

Revenue fell from KRW 38.5 billion in 2022 to KRW 38.3 billion in 2023, KRW 37.1 billion in 2024 and KRW 36.7 billion in 2025, a third consecutive annual decline. The year with the smallest revenue in the 2022 to 2025 range is 2025. The declines are small, but the direction is consistent.

Quarterly revenue above KRW 10 billion in 2026 is not yet enough evidence that the annual downtrend has reversed, and slowing growth in the core public certification service remains a long-term issue.

Quarterly earnings volatility

Profit attributable to controlling shareholders fell 55.8% from the prior quarter to KRW 651 million in the fourth quarter of 2025. Operating profit fell 35.6% to KRW 1.63 billion in the second quarter of 2026, a much larger drop than the 4.0% decline in revenue.

Profit attributable to controlling shareholders also fell 47.1% to KRW 1.24 billion that quarter. The four-quarter total of KRW 6.31 billion sits on an earnings base that swings sharply from quarter to quarter.

Concentration in core business and unproven new ventures

Because public certification is the central pillar of earnings, regulatory changes or pricing pressure feed directly into results. Global certification, IoT and AI-based security are presented as growth areas, but their revenue contribution is not separated in the earnings table.

The company had six affiliates as of December 2024, yet how much those affiliates contribute to core operating profit has not yet become visible.

10

Risk factors

Regulation and competition

If certification rules or policy change, demand for the public certification service could shift. Many private providers operate in the market, so price competition and customer defection remain ongoing risks.

Because the share of the general corporate certificate market is the core of earnings, a decline in that share could translate quickly into lower revenue. The direction and timing of any regulatory change must be confirmed through official policy announcements.

Fair-value swings in financial assets

In its preliminary 2025 disclosure, the company cited a smaller gain from fair-value changes in financial assets as a factor in profit changes. Fair-value gains and losses move apart from operating performance and widen the volatility of net profit.

In the fourth quarter of 2025, operating profit fell 13.0% from the prior quarter while profit attributable to controlling shareholders fell 55.8%, a wide gap between the two measures. This is why operating and net profit trends should be read separately.

New ventures and overseas affiliates

As of December 2024, the company had six affiliates, including several German entities and an AI-related company. If spending on new ventures rises, cash use and earnings burden could both increase.

Affiliate revenue and profit are not separated in the earnings table, which makes them hard to distinguish from the core business. Until investment scale and recovery timing appear in disclosures, the size of this risk is difficult to gauge.

12

Overall view

The disclosed results of Korea Electronic Certification Authority show revenue declining while profit recovers at the same time.

Revenue fell for three consecutive years, from KRW 38.5 billion in 2022 to KRW 36.7 billion in 2025, while operating profit rose from a low of KRW 3.14 billion in 2023 to KRW 6.73 billion in 2025 and the operating margin reached 18.3%.

Profit attributable to controlling shareholders also increased for two consecutive years in 2024 and 2025. Quarterly revenue stayed in the KRW 10 billion range through 2026, at KRW 11 billion, KRW 10.5 billion and KRW 10.9 billion, but operating profit swung between KRW 1.63 billion and KRW 2.56 billion.

On finances, the debt ratio was 33.6% at the end of 2025 and operating cash flow rose to KRW 6.45 billion, so the base looks stable. The KRW 3.5 billion treasury share trust was terminated after the acquisition was completed, and treasury shares have been cancelled.

Strengths include the margin recovery and financial capacity, while weaknesses include the downtrend in revenue, quarterly profit volatility and the effect of financial asset fair-value swings.

The next checkpoints are the 2026 preliminary annual results and the 2026 annual business report due by March 31, 2027, and whether new businesses show a visible revenue contribution is the medium-term issue.
