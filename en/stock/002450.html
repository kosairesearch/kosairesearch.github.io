02

Business structure

Founded in 1958 and listed on the KOSPI in 1988, Samick Musical Instruments is Korea's largest comprehensive musical instrument maker, producing and selling upright, grand, and digital pianos as well as guitars and string instruments.

The company operates 15 overseas production and sales subsidiaries across Indonesia, the United States, China, and Germany, and a large portion of revenue comes from an entrepot trade structure in which the Indonesian subsidiary (PT.

Samick Indonesia) manufactures goods that the parent purchases and re-exports through sales subsidiaries in the US, China, and Europe. The 2008 acquisition of German premium piano brand Seiler serves as a foothold for targeting the high-end market, particularly in China.

While domestic piano demand appears to be declining amid market saturation, China's mid-to-high-end piano demand is reportedly expanding on the back of a growing middle class and rising income levels.

Through subsidiary Suwan Energy, the company also runs a district heating and cooling business supplying roughly 40,000 households in the Gwangju area, which helps offset some of the seasonality and cyclicality of the instrument business.

Competitively, the company holds a near-monopoly position domestically but competes globally with Japanese brands such as Yamaha and Kawai as well as Chinese local brands like Pearl River and Hailun.

The controlling shareholder is founder Chairman Kim Jong-seop, with affiliate Speco, eldest son Vice Chairman Kim Min-su's personal company Nexa Korea, and the Samick Culture Foundation forming a friendly shareholder bloc.

Recently, a succession process leveraging this friendly shareholder structure has advanced, bringing governance-related issues into focus.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual results show consolidated revenue declining for four straight years, from KRW 325.7 billion in 2022 to KRW 247.9 billion in 2023, KRW 230.4 billion in 2024, and KRW 224.4 billion in 2025.

Operating margin also fell, from 3.5% in 2022 to 2.6% in both 2023 and 2024, and to 1.0% in 2025, reflecting continued pressure on core profitability.

In contrast, net profit attributable to owners stayed low at KRW 3.4 billion in 2023 and KRW 3.1 billion in 2024 before jumping to KRW 17.6 billion in 2025, highlighting a notable gap between operating performance and net earnings.

On a quarterly basis, Q2 2025 posted revenue of KRW 48.5 billion and an operating loss of KRW 2.4 billion, yet net profit attributable to owners reached KRW 15.1 billion, suggesting a significant non-operating impact.

Q3 2025 revenue was KRW 45.8 billion with a near break-even operating loss of KRW 0.03 billion, while Q4 2025 was weak, with revenue of KRW 54.8 billion, an operating loss of KRW 1.5 billion, and a net loss of KRW 3.4 billion.

Entering 2026, Q1 showed clear improvement with revenue of KRW 70.4 billion, operating profit of KRW 8.7 billion (roughly 12.4% margin), and net profit of KRW 12.7 billion, while Q2 revenue was KRW 53.9 billion with operating profit of KRW 2.9 billion, but net profit surged to KRW 31.1 billion, the largest in the observed window, again pointing to substantial non-operating factors.

Cumulative net profit attributable to owners over the trailing four quarters (Q3 2025 through Q2 2026) reached KRW 41.5 billion, considerably exceeding the sum of operating profit over the same period.

Such swings in net profit indicate that operating profit trends should be examined alongside net earnings when assessing the durability of the recovery.

05

Industry analysis

The global musical instrument market is expected to grow, supported by subscription-based remote learning, IoT-enabled smart instruments, and government support for music education, though growth varies significantly by region and product category.

China is considered the world's largest instrument consumer market, but its total instrument industry revenue fell 4.5% year-on-year to RMB 26.956 billion in 2025, with traditional piano sales declining sharply while electric guitars and other electronic instruments showed strong growth.

Korea's domestic piano market appears to be on a declining trend due to market saturation, coinciding with the normalization of hobby-instrument demand that had surged during the pandemic.

Against this backdrop, forecasts suggest that China's expanding middle class and rising income levels will boost demand for mid-to-high-end pianos, an area where Samick is positioning premium brands such as Seiler.

Chinese local competitors including Pearl River and Hailun appear to be relatively more exposed to the slowdown in traditional acoustic piano demand, suggesting a growing divide between the branded premium segment and the low-cost mass-production segment.

Structural shifts in the industry toward digital and hybrid instruments present a product transition challenge for traditional acoustic manufacturers.

06

Outlook

No specific quantitative earnings guidance from the company has been confirmed, but consolidated H1 2026 results showed clear year-on-year improvement, with revenue up 0.4%, operating profit up 204.0%, and net profit up 119.7%.

This improvement appears to reflect both the maintenance of instrument-segment sales through the Indonesian production base and US, China, and European sales subsidiaries, and improved results in the district energy segment from stable heat supply in the Gwangju area.

In China, expanding demand for mid-to-high-end pianos amid a growing middle class and rising sales of premium brands such as Seiler have been observed, making it worth watching whether the premium brand strategy continues to contribute to results.

On the governance side, a succession process centered on the founder's eldest son is underway, and whether funding can be secured to repay debt incurred in the process of transferring the Speco stake to Nexa Korea is cited as a variable that could affect shareholder value and capital allocation going forward.

Minority shareholders have raised objections to the governance restructuring process and are preparing legal action, so the progress of these proceedings also warrants attention. Overall, the near-term picture can be seen as one where an earnings recovery trend and governance issues are unfolding in parallel.

08

Bull factors

Return to Operating Profit and Improved Net Income

The company posted operating profit in both Q1 and Q2 2026, marking a departure from the low-margin phase seen in 2025. Q1 operating margin of roughly 12.4% was the highest among the trailing four quarters.

Across H1 overall, operating profit and net profit rose sharply despite only modest revenue growth, confirming an improving earnings trend.

Positioning in China's Premium Piano Market

China's expanding middle class and rising income levels are driving demand for mid-to-high-end pianos, with confirmed sales growth in premium brands such as Seiler.

Even as overall Chinese instrument industry revenue declines, the premium segment appears relatively resilient, potentially favoring companies with strong brand equity.

Cash Flow Stability from the District Energy Business

Subsidiary Suwan Energy provides a stable earnings base by supplying heating and cooling to roughly 40,000 households in the Gwangju area. This business has low correlation with the seasonality and demand fluctuations of the instrument segment, potentially offering some earnings buffer. Recent improvement from stable heat supply has also been confirmed.

09

Bear factors

Structural Revenue Decline Trend

Consolidated revenue declined for four straight years since 2022, falling to KRW 224.4 billion in 2025, down roughly 31% from 2022. Korea's domestic piano market appears saturated, and the normalization of hobby-instrument demand that had risen during the pandemic is also cited as a factor in the revenue slowdown. If this declining trend continues, concerns about a shrinking business scale itself could grow.

Deteriorating Operating Profitability

Operating margin steadily declined from 3.5% in 2022 to 1.0% in 2025. While net profit improved over the same period due to non-operating factors, profit generated from core operations itself remains at a low level. If cost and currency pressures persist, margin recovery could remain limited.

Governance Uncertainty from the Succession Process

The succession process has advanced with the founder's eldest son's personal company, Nexa Korea, acquiring the controlling stake in Speco through bank borrowing, and minority shareholders are preparing legal action over this process.

Concerns have been raised that dividends or assets from the listed affiliates could be used to fund the repayment of this debt. Such governance uncertainty is a variable that could affect capital allocation and shareholder value.

10

Risk factors

Governance and Related-Party Transaction Risk

Nexa Korea funded most of the KRW 8.6 billion needed to acquire the Speco stake through bank borrowing, with a maturity confirmed as July 15, 2027. Concerns have been raised in the market that, absent a clear repayment source, dividends or assets from affiliated companies could be mobilized.

Minority shareholders are preparing legal procedures, including requests to inspect the shareholder registry, raising the possibility of future disputes.

Foreign Exchange and Raw Material Risk

Given a business structure concentrated on Indonesian production with a high share of entrepot trade, the company is exposed to fluctuations in the Korean won, US dollar, and Indonesian rupiah, as well as raw material costs such as timber and plywood. This can contribute to quarterly margin volatility.

China Demand and Trade Environment Risk

China's overall musical instrument industry revenue declined in 2025, with a notable contraction in traditional acoustic piano sales. Changes in China's domestic economic conditions, consumer sentiment, and the Korea-China trade and policy environment could also affect sales of premium brands.

12

Overall view

Despite the structural pressure of declining revenue and falling operating margin through 2025, Samick Musical Instruments showed a substantial improvement in both operating and net profit in H1 2026.

However, since a significant portion of the net profit improvement stemmed from non-operating factors that far exceeded operating profit, whether core business profitability is genuinely recovering warrants further confirmation through upcoming quarterly results.

On the business side, expanding premium piano demand in China and stable cash flow from the district energy segment stand out as positive factors, while structural demand softness in both domestic and traditional global instrument markets remains a burden.

In addition, governance issues stemming from debt incurred during the founding family's succession process and minority shareholder pushback have emerged as a new source of uncertainty.

A balanced approach that considers both the qualitative improvement in operating performance and the evolving governance risk appears warranted for investors.
