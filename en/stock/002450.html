02

Business structure

Samick Musical Instruments is a piano-centered instrument maker founded in 1958, running an instrument business, a district energy business and real estate leasing.

Instrument sales fell 34% to KRW 122.9 billion in 2023, while district energy sales of KRW 100.8 billion made up 40.7% of revenue, the highest share on record. Its main instrument categories are pianos, guitars, electronic instruments and orchestral instruments.

A 2022 report said the piano-to-guitar sales mix shifted from 6:4 in 2019 to 3:7 after the pandemic. The guitar line includes OEM production for Gibson and Fender. The company acquired German handmade piano brand Seiler in 2008, which was described as lifting high-end sales in China.

Separate-basis sales by region in 2021 were reported as 55% Americas, 20% Korea, 20% Asia (China) and 4% Europe. Production is centered on the Indonesian plant, with sales subsidiaries in the United States, China and Europe, according to a 2026 first-half earnings summary.

The district energy business supplies heat in Gwangju through a local subsidiary. In Q1 2026, consolidated instrument sales were KRW 27.3 billion, down 4.8% year on year, while district energy sales were KRW 35.4 billion, down 9.9%.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual revenue went from KRW 325.7 billion in 2022 to KRW 247.9 billion in 2023 (down 23.9% year on year), KRW 230.4 billion in 2024 (down 7.1%) and KRW 224.4 billion in 2025 (down 2.6%), falling for three consecutive years from 2023 through 2025, with 2022 the largest year in the period.

Operating profit went from KRW 11.6 billion in 2022 to KRW 6.47 billion in 2023 (down 44.1%), KRW 5.93 billion in 2024 (down 8.2%) and KRW 2.28 billion in 2025 (down 61.5%), and the operating margin slipped from 3.5% in 2022 to 2.6% in 2023 and 2024 and 1.0% in 2025.

Coverage of the 2023 downturn linked it to weak piano and guitar demand after the pandemic boom and a rise in used units that pulled down prices. The breakdown of cost of sales and selling expenses is a point to verify in the annual report.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders went from KRW 15.1 billion in 2022 to KRW 3.42 billion in 2023 (down 77.3%) and KRW 3.15 billion in 2024 (down 8.0%) before rising to KRW 17.6 billion in 2025 (up 459.7%), while total net income including non-controlling interests was KRW 17.3 billion.

In reporting on 2025, the company attributed the result to weaker operating performance from the industry slump and one-off non-operating gains, according to a February 2026 report.

Operating cash flow was positive each year, at KRW 23.3 billion in 2022, KRW 26.8 billion in 2023, KRW 35.2 billion in 2024 and KRW 28 billion in 2025. On a quarterly basis, Q3 2025 posted an operating loss of KRW 31.92 million and Q4 2025 an operating loss of KRW 1.52 billion.

Q1 2026 revenue was KRW 70.4 billion, up 28.6% from the prior quarter, with operating profit of KRW 8.74 billion and a swing to profit, while net income attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 12.7 billion. A first-quarter earnings report put net gains from financial asset operations at about KRW 4.8 billion.

Q2 2026 revenue was KRW 53.9 billion (down 23.4% quarter on quarter), operating profit KRW 2.91 billion (down 66.7%) and net income attributable to controlling shareholders KRW 31.1 billion (up 144.8%), and a August 14, 2026 disclosure report said disposal and valuation gains of KRW 41.9 billion on financial assets lifted first-half net income.

Q3 2026 revenue of KRW 56 billion rose 22.2% from a year earlier, but an operating loss of KRW 1.55 billion returned and the net loss attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 28.3 billion. Net income attributable to controlling shareholders over the last four quarters totaled KRW 12.1 billion.

05

Industry analysis

The instrument cycle appears to have moved from the pandemic-era home hobby boom into a correction phase. Samick's instrument sales fell 34% in 2023, and industry commentary attributed the drop to unsold pandemic-era inventory flowing into the used market, which pushed down both used and new prices.

Instrument sales in Q1 2026 were KRW 27.3 billion, down 4.8%, which suggests the correction is still under way. A 2022 analyst note from NH Investment & Securities expected lower entry barriers to music learning to support demand, while describing China-bound piano sales as slow to recover.

In the domestic piano market, the company held about 30% share as of 2019, and management has described its position there as dominant. The company holds positions in OEM guitar production and high-end pianos, but the timing of a recovery in instrument sales has not yet appeared in reported results.

On costs, a 11.2% decline in cost of sales in the first half of 2026 was reported as a driver of the operating profit recovery. District energy operates on a different cycle from instruments, and a first-half 2026 summary said stable heat supply in Gwangju has kept it a steady pillar of consolidated results.

Whether instrument sales recover from the KRW 27.3 billion Q1 2026 level is also a point to track. The durability of district energy results and how far they offset weakness in instruments can be checked in the detailed line items of the next periodic reports.

Bull factors

First-half earnings recovery and cost relief

Operating profit turned positive at KRW 8.74 billion in Q1 2026 and reached KRW 2.91 billion in Q2. A 11.2% decline in first-half cost of sales was reported as the main driver. With the operating margin at 1.0% in 2025, whether operating indicators recover from this base is a point to watch.

Balance sheet improvement and cash generation

Debt relative to equity was lighter at the end of 2025 than in 2022. Operating cash flow reached KRW 28 billion in 2025, above the KRW 23.3 billion recorded in 2022. Regular cash dividends continued from 2023 through 2025.

District energy and overseas production base

A first-half 2026 summary noted that district energy results improved on stable heat supply in Gwangju. The Indonesian production base and sales subsidiaries in the United States, China and Europe maintain instrument sales. Still, district energy revenue fell 9.9% year on year to KRW 35.4 billion in Q1 2026.

Bear factors

Operating loss returns in Q3

Q3 2026 operating loss of KRW 1.55 billion marked a swing from profit in Q2. Revenue of KRW 56 billion rose 22.2% from a year earlier, yet did not translate into operating profit. The 2025 operating margin of 1.0% is below the 3.5% recorded in 2022.

Reliance of net income on financial assets

Net gains from financial asset operations were about KRW 4.8 billion in Q1 2026, reported at roughly 37% of that quarter's consolidated net income. In Q3 2026, the net loss attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 28.3 billion was far larger than the KRW 1.55 billion operating loss. Net income has at times moved with valuation and disposal gains rather than core operations.

Continued decline in instrument sales

Instrument sales fell 34% in 2023 to KRW 122.9 billion and declined a further 4.8% to KRW 27.3 billion in Q1 2026. Industry commentary describes piano and guitar demand as in a correction after the pandemic.

Without a visible recovery in instrument sales, consolidated results continue to lean on the district energy business.

Risk factors

Volatility of financial asset gains and losses

Disposal and valuation results on financial assets have changed sign from quarter to quarter. First-quarter 2026 net income was heavily affected by financial asset operations, and first-half results included KRW 41.9 billion of disposal and valuation gains.

Swings in this item can move net income in either direction, which limits how much weight earnings figures can bear.

Overseas revenue mix and currency exposure

Separate-basis sales in the Americas were reported at 55% in 2021. Production in Indonesia and sales through subsidiaries in the United States, China and Europe expose the company to overseas demand and exchange rate movements. A slowdown in any major region would pass through directly to instrument sales.

Dependence on the district energy business

District energy sales made up 40.7% of revenue in 2023, the highest share on record. Q1 2026 district energy sales fell 9.9% year on year to KRW 35.4 billion. Because that business feeds consolidated operating profit directly, weakness in energy results without an instrument recovery would weigh on overall earnings.

Overall view

Samick Musical Instruments combines a piano and guitar instrument business with a Gwangju district energy business. Annual revenue declined from KRW 325.7 billion in 2022 to KRW 224.4 billion in 2025, operating profit fell for three consecutive years, and the operating margin dropped to 1.0%.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders rose to KRW 17.6 billion in 2025, though the company pointed to weak operations and one-off non-operating items.

Operating profit recovered in the first half of 2026, but Q3 returned to an operating loss of KRW 1.55 billion, with a net loss attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 28.3 billion. On the balance sheet, the debt ratio fell to 83.8% in 2025 and regular cash dividends continued.

The price-to-book ratio sits below its year-end levels of the past few years. The next checkpoints are the non-operating income breakdown in the 2026 annual report, the operating profit trend in the Q1 2027 quarterly report, and whether instrument sales recover.
