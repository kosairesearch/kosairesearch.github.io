Report overview

A Holding Company Tied to Its Food Subsidiary

ORION Holdings' earnings depend heavily on the confectionery results of its food subsidiary Orion and on the dividends and royalties that flow from it, and attributable net income has fallen for two consecutive quarters through Q3 2026.

02

Business structure

ORION Holdings became a pure holding company in June 2017, when it spun off its food business into Orion and kept the subsidiary stakes. Its core subsidiary Orion, in which ORION Holdings held a 37.4% stake at end-June 2026, makes Chocopie, Kkobuk chips and Poca Chip.

As of November 2024, the company held 57.47% of the film distributor Showbox, 60.00% of its bio joint venture ORION Bioscience, and 94.56% of a water and beverage subsidiary.

According to a domestic credit rating agency, Orion accounted for 98.2% of consolidated revenue and 96.0% of EBITDA in 2025, so the holding company's business and finances rest largely on confectionery.

Overseas subsidiaries operate mainly in China, Vietnam and Russia, and Orion said overseas sales made up 70.6% of its revenue in Q3 2026. Holding-level income consists mainly of subsidiary dividends and trademark royalties, while film distribution and bottled water sales form the non-confectionery income.

Showbox returned to profit in 2025 on the box-office run of the film 'The King's Warden', which reportedly produced operating profit of KRW 21.7 billion that year. The bio business runs through a 60% stake in ORION Bioscience and a 65% stake in a China joint venture with Shandong Lukang. The company has positioned beverages, convenience meals and bio as its three new growth businesses.

04

Earnings analysis

Consolidated revenue rose from KRW 2.93 trillion in 2022 to KRW 2.95 trillion in 2023 (up 0.7%), KRW 3.2 trillion in 2024 (up 8.2%) and KRW 3.39 trillion in 2025 (up 6.2%), marking three consecutive years of growth from 2023 through 2025.

Operating profit was KRW 399.8 billion in 2022, KRW 405.5 billion in 2023, KRW 506.2 billion in 2024 and KRW 487.7 billion in 2025, with 2024 the largest year in the 2022 to 2025 span. One news outlet reported that 2025 operating profit was the second highest on record.

The operating margin went from 13.6% in 2022 and 13.7% in 2023 to 15.8% in 2024 before easing to 14.4% in 2025. Revenue grew in 2025 but operating profit fell 3.7%, so margin compression translated into lower profit.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders fell from KRW 103 billion in 2022 to KRW 85.6 billion in 2023 (down 16.8%), jumped to KRW 160.6 billion in 2024 (up 87.6%) and dropped to KRW 121 billion in 2025 (down 24.7%).

Total net income of KRW 337.8 billion in 2025 was well above the attributable portion, so the remainder belongs to non-controlling interests.

Operating cash flow was KRW 530.1 billion, KRW 451.8 billion, KRW 676.3 billion and KRW 514.1 billion across 2022 to 2025, and the debt-to-equity ratio improved from 21.6% in 2024 to 19.9% in 2025.

On a quarterly basis, Q4 2025 revenue of KRW 939 billion (up 11.1% from the prior quarter) and operating profit of KRW 149.2 billion (up 21.4%) were followed by Q1 2026 revenue of KRW 1.01 trillion (up 7.4%) and operating profit of KRW 172.9 billion (up 15.9%), the highest quarterly figures in the period.

An analyst at Daol Investment & Securities described that Q1 operating profit as the best quarterly result, citing a 22.1% rise in dividend income and a 21.3% rise in royalty income tied to the subsidiary's higher dividend.

Revenue then slipped to KRW 960.5 billion in Q2 (down 4.8%) and operating profit fell 26.1% to KRW 127.7 billion, before Q3 revenue of KRW 938.5 billion (down 2.3%) and operating profit of KRW 142.8 billion (up 11.8%) showed a recovery.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders, however, was KRW 27 billion in Q3, down 15.0% year on year, and totals KRW 135.6 billion over the last four quarters.

Operating profit can rise while attributable income moves by a different amount, because a large share of earnings belongs to non-controlling interests.

05

Industry analysis

Most of the holding company's earnings come from confectionery, so the industry picture is shaped largely by the overseas operations of its subsidiary Orion. Orion announced on November 16, 2026 consolidated Q3 2026 revenue of KRW 922.7 billion and operating profit of KRW 150.9 billion, both higher year on year.

Its China unit posted revenue of KRW 384.0 billion and operating profit of KRW 73.2 billion, up 13.8% and 9.3% respectively, and the Russia unit also grew in both lines.

The Korean unit's revenue rose 4.9% to KRW 296.6 billion, but its operating profit fell 4.3% to KRW 40.3 billion, and the Vietnam unit's profit also declined on cost pressure.

Overseas sales made up 70.6% of Orion's revenue, so overseas consumption and cost trends weigh more heavily on results than domestic confectionery demand. A domestic credit rating agency said that slowing population growth at home limited growth potential and prompted Orion to expand abroad early.

Lunar New Year and Tet are the peak selling seasons in China and Vietnam, and reports attributed the 24.8% revenue rise of the China unit in Q1 2026 partly to the holiday effect and higher sales of snacks, pies and jelly.

The bio business runs through joint ventures, including a 60% stake in ORION Bioscience and a 65% stake in a Shandong Lukang venture, so its economics differ from confectionery.

06

Outlook

In the first half of 2026, the subsidiary Orion spent KRW 173.7 billion on building its Jincheon integrated center and a new plant in Vietnam, along with production line expansions at home and abroad.

The Jincheon center brings production, packaging and logistics under one roof and carries a total project cost of about KRW 460 billion, while the expansion of the Tver plant in Russia is expected to take about KRW 240 billion.

Over the same period, free cash flow after capital spending and dividends was a deficit of KRW 27.1 billion, based on operating cash flow of KRW 284.9 billion. In July 2026, Orion put an additional KRW 125 billion into convertible preferred shares and convertible bonds of a bio company, Ligachem Biosciences.

The board of the subsidiary and of the holding company approved cash dividends on February 11, 2026. A domestic credit rating agency said consolidated cash and equivalents of KRW 1.51 trillion at end-June 2026 comfortably exceed short-term borrowings of KRW 253.9 billion.

In the same assessment, the holding company's standalone total borrowings rose from KRW 246.5 billion at end-2025 to KRW 310.3 billion at end-June 2026. The agency said it will keep watching whether overseas plants and bio investments translate into results and how a larger dividend affects the capital structure.

Whether the overseas capacity is absorbed and how much financial room remains after higher dividends are therefore the key variables for the outlook.

08

Bull factors

Overseas growth and product mix

Orion's Q3 2026 consolidated revenue rose to KRW 922.7 billion and operating profit to KRW 150.9 billion. The China unit led the growth, with revenue up 13.8% to KRW 384.0 billion and operating profit up 9.3% to KRW 73.2 billion, while the Russia unit also rose.

Because holding-level income flows through dividends and royalties, overseas growth is cited as a supporting factor.

Improved holding-level income

Dividend income rose 22.1% and royalty income 21.3% year on year in Q1 2026. Reports attributed the gains to a higher dividend from the subsidiary and its strong first quarter. The widening base of subsidiary dividends is cited as a basis for the bullish case.

Financial stability and credit rating

The debt-to-equity ratio was 19.9% at end-2025, and consolidated cash and equivalents of KRW 1.51 trillion at end-June 2026 well exceed short-term borrowings of KRW 253.9 billion. A domestic credit rating agency kept the commercial paper rating at A1, citing the subsidiary's cash generation and liquidity. The agency also noted the holding company's low standalone financial burden.

09

Bear factors

Slowing profit momentum in the second half of 2026

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders fell for two consecutive quarters, to KRW 27.9 billion in Q2 2026 (down 46.0%) and KRW 27 billion in Q3 (down 3.4%).

Q3 attributable income was down 15.0% year on year, even though operating profit rose 16.2%, so the gain did not pass through to the holding company's share. Quarterly profit has turned since the Q1 2026 peak, which is cited as a bearish factor.

Cash pressure from subsidiary investment and dividends

According to a domestic credit rating agency, the subsidiary Orion spent KRW 173.7 billion on capital expenditure in the first half of 2026 and paid KRW 138.4 billion in dividends. Subtracting both from net operating cash flow of KRW 284.9 billion left free cash flow in deficit by KRW 27.1 billion. Continued work on the Jincheon and Tver expansions keeps this pressure in place.

Rising holding-company borrowings

Standalone total borrowings of the holding company rose to KRW 310.3 billion at end-June 2026 from KRW 246.5 billion at end-2025. About 74.2% of that debt matures within one year, mostly as KRW 225 billion of commercial paper. The standalone debt ratio also rose from 14.8% to 18.6% over the same period.

10

Risk factors

Concentration in one subsidiary

Orion accounted for 98.2% of consolidated revenue in 2025, so changes in confectionery volumes and costs pass through almost directly to the holding company. In Q3 2026, cost pressure in the Korean and Vietnam units reduced the subsidiary's operating profit. If the subsidiary's results weaken, dividend and royalty income could weaken with them.

Overseas units and holiday demand swings

As the China, Vietnam and Russia units grow in weight, local consumption, holiday demand, distribution rules and currency moves increasingly shape holding-level results. Overseas sales were 70.6% of Orion's Q3 2026 revenue. Q1 2026 results included the Lunar New Year peak, which can make quarterly results swing widely.

Non-confectionery volatility and new-business investment

Showbox's results depend on box-office performance, and reports said one film's run in 2025 produced operating profit of KRW 21.7 billion. Bio investments tie up capital until results become visible.

In July 2026, the subsidiary Orion added KRW 125 billion in convertible preferred shares and convertible bonds of Ligachem Biosciences.

12

Overall view

ORION Holdings is a pure holding company whose earnings are built from dividends and royalties tied to the confectionery business and overseas growth of its subsidiary Orion.

Revenue rose from KRW 2.93 trillion in 2022 to KRW 3.39 trillion in 2025, while operating profit peaked at KRW 506.2 billion in 2024 and eased to KRW 487.7 billion in 2025.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders fell from KRW 160.6 billion in 2024 to KRW 121 billion in 2025, and of total equity of KRW 5.16 trillion at end-2025, controlling shareholders' equity was KRW 2.46 trillion.

Q1 2026 revenue of KRW 1.01 trillion and operating profit of KRW 172.9 billion marked the quarterly peak, and attributable net income then declined for two consecutive quarters, bringing the last four quarters to KRW 135.6 billion.

Supporting factors include growth at the overseas units, higher dividend and royalty income, a debt-to-equity ratio of 19.9% and an A1 commercial paper rating.

Weighing against them are cash demands from subsidiary investment and dividends, higher standalone borrowings and dependence on box-office results in non-confectionery businesses. The balance between these two sets of factors has not yet tilted clearly either way.

On the confirmed facts, the most evident point is that the direction of the subsidiary Orion's results largely sets the direction of attributable net income at the holding company.
