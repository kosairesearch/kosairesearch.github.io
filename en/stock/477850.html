02

Business structure

MakinaRocks is a software company founded in December 2017 whose main product is a platform for building and operating industry-specific AI. It is listed in the KOSDAQ technology growth company segment under ticker 477850.

Its core product, Runway, is an enterprise AI operating system that manages the whole lifecycle from development through training, deployment, operation, and monitoring.

The company positions its ability to run AI in closed networks, on in-factory servers, and at the equipment level, rather than only in the cloud, as a differentiator.

Revenue consists of annual or monthly license sales and project-based solution sales. Runway revenue scales with the number of users and GPU usage, and raising that share is the center of the company's growth strategy.

In 2025, orders were KRW 20.5 billion and revenue was KRW 11.5 billion, with 67% of revenue coming from five large manufacturers and the defense sector. That also points to meaningful customer concentration.

First-half 2026 orders were spread across defense and aerospace (27%), heavy industry (23%), advanced manufacturing (22%), and general manufacturing (21%), with the Agency for Defense Development, Doosan Enerbility, Samsung Electronics, Yokogawa, and Hyundai Motor named as key customers.

Overseas orders were about KRW 3 billion in the first half, centered on Japanese and European manufacturers. The company set up a Japanese subsidiary in April 2025 as a base for global expansion.

The company says Palantir, often cited as a similar business model in defense and manufacturing, focuses on decision-making, while MakinaRocks focuses on execution.

In AI operations platforms, domestic firms such as Wiseitech are classed as similar companies, and overseas vendors such as Dataiku are cited as competitors.

Large IT services groups in the same sector, such as Samsung SDS, LG CNS, and POSCO DX Company, may also overlap with MakinaRocks in manufacturing AI transformation projects.

The company is broadening its references through collaboration with Hanwha Systems, Amazon Web Services (AWS), and the Korea Water Resources Corporation. It has also launched a partner program in which partners build and deliver specialized solutions directly on Runway, naming Dagem as its first partner.

03

Recent trends

Per filed results, cumulative H1 2026 revenue was KRW 3.99 billion, up 168.6% from the same period a year earlier. The operating loss was KRW 1.8 billion and the net loss was KRW 1.55 billion, so losses continued but were smaller than a year earlier.

First-half figures in the company's press release and in news reports use a different scope and differ from the filed numbers, and this report follows the filed figures. For 2025, consolidated revenue was KRW 11.459 billion, the operating loss was KRW 8.041 billion, and the net loss was KRW 13.774 billion.

Orders, a leading indicator of revenue, grew even faster. First-half new orders exceeded KRW 20 billion, more than three times the roughly KRW 6.4 billion of a year earlier.

Trade receivables fell from KRW 4.2 billion at the end of the prior year to KRW 1.3 billion, and the debt ratio dropped from 53% before listing to 12%. According to the company, defense and manufacturing projects are staffed in the first half, with final inspection and billing concentrated in the second half.

The share price has moved sharply since listing. On the May 20, 2026 listing day, the stock opened at KRW 60,000, four times the KRW 15,000 offer price, for a market capitalization of about KRW 1.0525 trillion. The next day it touched KRW 78,000 intraday.

On May 22, the third trading day, it fell as much as 28.97% intraday. Closes on June 23 and 24 were KRW 13,750 and KRW 14,050, both below the offer price.

Share-supply factors piled on. Employees exercised stock options for 213,096 shares, which were listed as new shares equal to 1.2% of total shares outstanding. The tradable float, about 38% at listing, rose to 52.43% of issued shares as lock-ups ended and new option shares were listed.

On October 27, 2026, the stock closed at KRW 14,650, down 5.06% from the previous session, with a market capitalization of KRW 265.6 billion and trading value of KRW 3.5 billion. The close is below the KRW 15,000 offer price, and the market capitalization is close to the figure estimated at the offer price.

04

Outlook

The company expects supply of AI solutions to the defense and public sectors to expand in stages from the second half. However, industrial AI projects must pass through field data collection, model development, system build-out and validation, and final inspection before billing and revenue recognition.

The speed at which secured orders convert into revenue is therefore central to second-half results. Venturesquare noted that the next results should be read for new order size, the conversion rate of existing orders into revenue, and project-level profitability.

The company's 2026 revenue target is KRW 22.5 billion. Reaching it requires both recognition of the existing order backlog and additional orders and new contracts in the second half.

CEO Yoon Seong-ho has set a target of returning to profit in 2027 and a plan for roughly 80% of about KRW 100 billion in 2030 revenue to come from Runway-centered sales. IBK Investment & Securities forecast in a post-listing report that a return to profit is possible from 2027. These are forecasts from the company and a brokerage, not confirmed results.

Some supply-side dates remain. Six months from the May 20, 2026 listing date falls around November 20, 2026, so the release of six-month lock-up shares is approaching. Six-month commitments accounted for 20.5% of the shares applied for in the institutional book-building.

A further 279,148 stock option shares remain after the exercise. Employee stock ownership association shares carry a one-year lock-up. Whether these shares are actually sold is unknown, and how the market absorbs them could affect price volatility.

05

Bull factors

Order surge and diversified customers

First-half new orders exceeded KRW 20 billion, surpassing the full-year 2025 order total of KRW 20.5 billion in six months. Orders are split across defense and aerospace, heavy industry, advanced manufacturing, and general manufacturing, so there is no heavy tilt toward one sector.

The filed results also show cumulative H1 revenue up 168.6% year on year, which suggests orders are translating into reported sales. The company's explanation is that revenue recognition can continue as second-half inspection and billing proceed.

Balance sheet strengthened by the IPO

The IPO raised KRW 38.4 billion, bringing cash and equivalents to KRW 44.1 billion at the end of the first half. The debt ratio fell from 53% to 12%, and trade receivables also declined.

Defense and manufacturing projects require upfront labor and development spending before revenue is recognized, so the cash cushion supports running several large projects at once while waiting for customer inspection and payment.

Platform renewals and ecosystem growth

According to the company, the Runway customer renewal rate was 94% in 2025 and delivery time shortened to one to three months. When use cases built in individual projects spread to other sites, they feed recurring license revenue.

The partner program lets partners build solutions directly on Runway rather than simply resell, which could help the platform spread. If this model takes hold, it could help reduce the share of labor-dependent project revenue.

06

Bear factors

Continuing losses on a small revenue base

Per filed results, the cumulative H1 operating loss was KRW 1.8 billion and the net loss was KRW 1.55 billion, so losses continued. Losses narrowed, but they remain large relative to revenue of KRW 3.99 billion. Venturesquare noted that further scale growth is needed before higher sales turn into operating profit. A return to profit in 2027 is a company target and is not assured.

Lag between orders and revenue

Industrial AI has a long lag between contract signing and revenue. The company did not disclose the exact first-half order amount.

The core of its growth strategy is shifting from project-based solution revenue to Runway-centered license revenue, and a delay in that shift could keep labor-intensive projects as a large share of sales.

In addition, 67% of 2025 revenue came from five large manufacturers and the defense sector, so customer concentration is high.

Offer-price basis and rising float

The offer price was set on estimated 2028 earnings rather than current results, and it was pointed out that delays in recognizing backlog or growing Runway's share could undermine that premise. The tradable float rose from about 38% at listing to 52.43%. The remaining 279,148 stock option shares also stay as an additional supply variable.

07

Risk factors

Macro

During the post-listing decline, adverse macro conditions were cited as having sped up the fall. Weaker overall market sentiment can amplify volatility in growth and newly listed stocks. Slower manufacturing capital spending or defense budget execution could affect order and inspection timelines.

Exchange rates and overseas economic conditions are also variables for the plan to expand orders in Japan and Europe.

Business and competition

Revenue is concentrated in large manufacturers and the defense sector, so changes in key customers' investment schedules could materially affect results. AI operations platform vendors at home and abroad, along with large IT services firms, may compete in the manufacturing AI transformation market.

A longer-term question is whether customers won in individual build projects convert into Runway license and maintenance business. Delays in project inspection could push back the timing of revenue recognition.

Supply and financial

On July 20, 2026, about 1.995 million shares held by financial investors were released, equal to 11.37% of issued shares. Six-month commitment shares are expected to be released around November 20, 2026. With the stock trading near the offer price, remaining stock options may be seen as additional supply pressure. As long as losses continue, the possibility of further capital raising cannot be ruled out.

08

Overall view

MakinaRocks is an early-stage listed company that is growing orders and revenue quickly with an AI operating system built for the closed-network environments of manufacturing and defense sites.

Per filed results, cumulative H1 revenue was KRW 3.99 billion, up 168.6%, while the operating loss of KRW 1.8 billion and net loss of KRW 1.55 billion were smaller than a year earlier. The cash raised in the IPO and the lower debt ratio give it the financial base to carry out large projects.

On the other hand, continuing losses, the lag between orders and revenue, customer concentration, and an offer price that relied on future earnings all call for monitoring. The larger tradable float from lock-up expiries and stock option shares is also part of the backdrop to price volatility.

The October 27, 2026 close was below the offer price, and the conversion of second-half inspection and billing into revenue and the growth of Runway's revenue share are the indicators to watch. This report is for information only and does not recommend trading any security.
