Report overview

Q2 Swung to Profit, Listing Risk Still Unresolved

Hyungji Global swung to both consolidated operating profit and attributable net profit in the second quarter of 2026, but the company remains under a dual administrative-issue designation for falling below both the KRW 1,000 share price floor and the KRW 20 billion market-cap floor, leaving its continued listing status unresolved.

  1. 1

    Q2 2026 revenue reached KRW 23.3 billion (up 130.4% year on year), with operating profit of KRW 264 million (turning positive) and attributable net profit of KRW 781 million (turning positive).

  2. 2

    On an annual basis, revenue fell for three straight years from KRW 61.8 billion in 2022 to KRW 37.7 billion in 2025, while operating and net losses continued for four consecutive years.

  3. 3

    On August 13, 2026, the company was placed on the administrative-issue list for both falling below the KRW 20 billion market-cap floor and the KRW 1,000 share price floor, and the resulting event of default caused it to miss roughly KRW 5.5 billion in convertible bond principal and interest payments.

  4. 4

    The company filed for an injunction on October 14, 2026 seeking to suspend the effect of its administrative-issue designation; follow-up delisting procedures for market-cap deficiency have been suspended under a separate court precedent, but the price-deficiency track was excluded from that suspension.

  5. 5

    The consolidation of a 51.12% stake in Hyungji Esquire, acquired for KRW 6.9 billion in March 2026, was the main driver behind the company's second-quarter revenue growth.

02

Business structure

Hyungji Global changed its corporate name from Castelbajac Co., Ltd. to Hyungji Global in March 2025, and its core business remains the domestic distribution of the French golf-wear brand Castelbajac, a licensed brand the Fashion Group Hyungji network took over in 2016 and sells through department stores, outlets, online channels and home-shopping.

In March 2026 the company acquired a 51.12% stake in Hyungji Esquire, a footwear and accessories affiliate that emerged from court receivership and was folded into the group in 2015, for KRW 6.9 billion, taking over the stake previously held by controlling shareholder Fashion Group Hyungji and consolidating it as a subsidiary.

Hyungji Global sits alongside KOSPI-listed Hyungji Elite and KOSDAQ-listed HYUNGJI Innovation and Creative Company as a listed affiliate of Fashion Group Hyungji, though the three are legally separate entities.

On governance, founder Choi Byung-oh's son, Choi Jun-ho, serves as chief executive of both Hyungji Elite and Hyungji Global, yet holds a direct stake of under 1%, while minority shareholders collectively hold roughly 72%.

Controlling shareholder Fashion Group Hyungji, an unlisted entity, has expanded its holding partly through participation in third-party share allotments.

Unlike large listed department-store and discount-retail operators, Hyungji Global is a small-cap distributor focused on mid-tier fashion brands and is pursuing scale expansion through subsidiary consolidation.

Its sales channels are gradually diversifying from street-level stores and outlets toward online retail, home shopping and overseas buyer exports.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual revenue fell for three straight years, from KRW 61.8 billion in 2022 to KRW 48.4 billion in 2023 (down 21.6% year on year), KRW 39.8 billion in 2024 (down 17.8%), and KRW 37.7 billion in 2025 (down 5.3%), with 2025 the smallest year and 2022 the largest among 2022–2025.

Operating losses persisted for four consecutive years: KRW 9.38 billion in 2022, KRW 1.01 billion in 2023 (loss narrowed), KRW 9.39 billion in 2024 (loss widened), and KRW 8.08 billion in 2025 (loss narrowed).

Attributable net losses also continued for four straight years, from KRW 9.26 billion in 2022 to KRW 4.5 billion in 2023 (narrowed), KRW 16.3 billion in 2024 (widened) and KRW 34.3 billion in 2025 (widened), while total net loss including non-controlling interests widened to KRW 34.5 billion in 2025.

On a quarterly basis, operating losses widened from KRW 963 million in Q2 2025 to KRW 1.93 billion in Q3 2025 and KRW 6.31 billion in Q4 2025, before the company turned to an operating profit of KRW 615 million in Q1 2026 and posted operating profit of KRW 264 million in Q2 2026, down 57.0% from the prior quarter but a turnaround from a year earlier.

Attributable net profit followed a similar path, from a KRW 25.9 billion loss in Q2 2025 to losses of KRW 1.96 billion and KRW 7.18 billion in Q3 and Q4 2025, narrowing to a KRW 55.53 million loss in Q1 2026 before turning to a KRW 781 million profit in Q2 2026, positive versus both the prior quarter and a year earlier.

Even so, the trailing four-quarter sum of attributable net losses from Q3 2025 through Q2 2026 totaled KRW 8.41 billion, meaning the company remains in a cumulative loss position despite the single-quarter turnaround.

Total equity stood at KRW 58.8 billion at the end of 2025 (KRW 58.9 billion attributable to controlling shareholders) against total liabilities of KRW 38.3 billion, pushing the debt-to-equity ratio down to 65.1%, yet operating cash flow deteriorated further to an outflow of KRW 7.87 billion in 2025 following an outflow of KRW 3.92 billion in 2024.

05

Industry analysis

South Korea's mid-tier women's apparel and golf-wear distribution market is going through a structural transition driven by weaker consumer spending and the decline of traditional street-level shopping districts.

Conventional storefront retail networks are under pressure from high rents and shrinking foot traffic, pushing the industry broadly toward online, outlet and home-shopping channels.

The golf-wear segment, after rapid pandemic-era growth, has entered a period of slower expansion, and licensed brands such as Castelbajac appear to be responding through product-quality improvements and channel diversification.

The footwear and accessories segment that Hyungji Esquire operates in faces similar structural pressure given its historical reliance on street-level stores.

Compared with large listed retail operators running department stores, discount marts and convenience-store chains, Hyungji Global is a markedly smaller company in terms of both revenue scale and brand portfolio, making it relatively more sensitive to economic swings and shifts in consumer sentiment.

It is also notable for industry context that sister listed affiliates Hyungji Elite and HYUNGJI Innovation and Creative Company were likewise placed on the administrative-issue list around August 2026, pointing to simultaneous distribution-sector weakness and financial strain across the broader group.

06

Outlook

In its first-half 2026 earnings commentary, management stated that it would continue the recovery momentum into the second half given the simultaneous improvement in sales, profitability and financial structure, attributing the gains to both the Hyungji Esquire consolidation and growth in existing operations.

The key variable for the second half is whether the Hyungji Esquire consolidation effect persists beyond the third quarter and whether channel diversification for the standalone Castelbajac business, across outlets, online, home shopping and overseas buyers, translates into sustained profitability.

At the same time, whether the company can meet listing-maintenance requirements remains the most pressing near-term variable.

Since the company has not met the KRW 1,000 closing-price threshold on any trading day since its administrative-issue designation, whether an additional delisting cause arises around the exchange-flagged deadline near October 22, 2026 is a key point to watch.

For the market-cap deficiency, the exchange has temporarily suspended follow-up delisting procedures following a court injunction ruling, but this could change depending on the outcome of the underlying lawsuit.

Regarding the missed convertible bond principal and interest payments, negotiations with bondholders over a revised repayment schedule are reportedly underway, and the outcome and any related disclosures warrant monitoring.

At the group level, Fashion Group Hyungji is integrating the operating structure for its core brands, Crocodile Ladies, Olivia Hassler and Chatelaine, to take effect from the 2027 spring/summer season; while this involves a separate unlisted legal entity rather than Hyungji Global itself, it offers a reference point for the broader group's distribution strategy direction.

08

Bull factors

Second-Quarter Turnaround Signal

Both consolidated operating profit and attributable net profit turned positive in Q2 2026, with revenue up 130.4% year on year. Standalone revenue and operating profit also rose 31.4% and turned positive, respectively, versus the prior year, indicating growth beyond just the Hyungji Esquire consolidation effect. The company has now posted operating profit for two consecutive quarters, including Q1 2026.

Controlling Shareholder's Capital Support

CEO Choi Jun-ho increased his holdings through an open-market purchase in July 2026, and controlling shareholder Fashion Group Hyungji fully subscribed to a KRW 1 billion third-party share allotment.

These purchases and the capital-increase participation demonstrate that the controlling shareholder side is committing funds toward the company's normalization.

Procedural Suspension for Market-Cap Deficiency

Following a court ruling that granted an injunction suspending the delisting-decision effect for other listed companies, the Korea Exchange temporarily suspended follow-up delisting procedures for companies cited for market-cap deficiency.

While Hyungji Global's market-cap-related administrative-issue designation remains in place, this suspension has halted the immediate progression of delisting procedures, which can be read as buying the company time to pursue remedial measures.

09

Bear factors

Multi-Year Structural Earnings Decline

Revenue fell for three consecutive years from 2022 through 2025, while operating and net losses continued for four straight years. The 2025 attributable net loss widened to KRW 34.3 billion from the prior year, and the accumulated deficit from this pattern has pressured the total equity base.

Despite the quarterly swing to profit, the trailing four-quarter total still shows an attributable net loss of KRW 8.41 billion.

Delisting Risk from Unmet Listing Requirements

On August 13, 2026 the company was placed on the dual administrative-issue list for failing to meet both the market-cap and share-price requirements, and since then its closing price has not met the KRW 1,000 threshold on a single trading day.

While the exchange suspended follow-up procedures for the market-cap deficiency, the price-deficiency track was excluded from that suspension, meaning a delisting cause could arise if the price remains below the exchange-flagged deadline.

The administrative-issue designation also triggered an event of default under convertible bond covenants, adding to the company's financial burden.

Debt Default and Financial Uncertainty

The event of default triggered by the administrative-issue designation left the company unable to pay roughly KRW 5.0 billion in convertible bond principal and KRW 500 million in interest, about KRW 5.5 billion combined.

The company has said it is negotiating a revised repayment schedule with bondholders, but the outcome has not been finalized.

Operating cash flow also worsened to an outflow of KRW 7.87 billion in 2025 from a larger outflow the prior year, making it difficult to cover debt obligations from internally generated cash alone.

10

Risk factors

Listing Maintenance Risk

Since the company has not met the KRW 1,000 closing-price criterion for 45 consecutive trading days following its administrative-issue designation, a delisting cause could arise if it remains below the threshold past the exchange-flagged deadline around October 22, 2026.

The timing of subsequent procedures could shift depending on the outcome of the company's injunction petition, but a possibility remains that delisting procedures could resume if the injunction is not granted.

Financial and Liquidity Risk

The company currently has an outstanding default on roughly KRW 5.5 billion of convertible bond principal and interest, and depending on negotiations with bondholders, this could lead to further events of default or legal disputes.

Operating cash flow was negative in both 2024 and 2025, limiting the company's cash-generating capacity, and the accumulated history of convertible bond and bond-with-warrant issuances raises ongoing dilution concerns for any future fundraising.

Governance and Affiliate Risk

The Hyungji Esquire stake acquisition was a related-party transaction transferred from controlling shareholder Fashion Group Hyungji, and the price was reportedly set without an independent external valuation.

Sister listed affiliates Hyungji Elite and HYUNGJI Innovation and Creative Company were likewise placed on the administrative-issue list around August 2026, raising the possibility that group-wide financial strain and swings in equity-method gains or losses could have a knock-on effect on Hyungji Global's results.

12

Overall view

Hyungji Global posted operating profit for two consecutive quarters in Q1 and Q2 2026, showing turnaround signals driven by both the Hyungji Esquire consolidation and growth in existing operations.

However, the structural weakness reflected in three straight years of revenue decline and four straight years of operating and net losses from 2022 through 2025 continues to weigh on the equity base and cash flow.

The more pressing issue is the unresolved listing-maintenance risk following the dual administrative-issue designation in August 2026: the market-cap deficiency track has had its procedures suspended under a court precedent, but the price-deficiency track faces an exchange-flagged deadline that is fast approaching.

The roughly KRW 5.5 billion in missed convertible bond principal and interest triggered by the designation also remains unresolved.

While share purchases and capital-increase participation by the controlling shareholder side signal intent to normalize the business, both the durability of the earnings improvement and whether listing requirements are ultimately met need to be confirmed.

Investors should watch both the earnings trajectory and the schedule of exchange- and court-related disclosures.
