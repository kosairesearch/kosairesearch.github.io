02

Business structure

Hyundai Motor produces and sells passenger cars, RVs and commercial vehicles, and it runs a financial arm, so consolidated revenue splits into an automotive segment and a financial and other segment.

Third-quarter 2026 revenue of KRW 45.1 trillion comprised KRW 33.1 trillion from automotive and KRW 12.1 trillion from financial and other businesses. In the same quarter, automotive operating profit rose 24.8% to KRW 2.135 trillion, while financial segment operating profit fell 18.2% to KRW 471 billion.

Hybrids are taking a larger role in the product mix. Third-quarter HEV sales reached 167,414 units, up about 6,000 from a year earlier and 18.2% of total sales. Battery EV sales were 69,117 units, down about 9%, and Genesis sales fell 24.5% to about 40,000 units.

Domestic sales dropped 27.2% to 131,468 units because of strike-related production losses, while overseas sales declined 8.0% to 789,242 units. The company points to the all-new Avante and the all-new Tucson as key drivers of domestic volume.

In competition, KIA, a sister company in the same group, competes with Hyundai Motor in the United States, Europe and China against local automakers, and sales weakened in Korea, Europe and China in the third quarter.

The company has presented its China localization strategy, expanded US local production, the shift to software-defined vehicles (SDV) and its robotics business as mid- to long-term growth pillars.

04

Earnings analysis

On a reported basis, revenue rose from KRW 142.5 trillion in 2022 to KRW 162.7 trillion in 2023 (up 14.1%), KRW 175.2 trillion in 2024 (up 7.7%) and KRW 186.3 trillion in 2025 (up 6.3%), a three-year run of increases from 2023 through 2025.

Operating profit peaked in 2023 at KRW 15.1 trillion, up 54.0%, and then fell to KRW 14.2 trillion in 2024 (down 5.9%) and KRW 11.5 trillion in 2025 (down 19.5%), a second straight annual decline. The operating margin moved from 6.9% in 2022 to 9.3% in 2023, 8.1% in 2024 and 6.2% in 2025.

Net income attributable to owners of the parent was highest in 2024 at KRW 12.5 trillion and fell 24.6% to KRW 9.45 trillion in 2025. Net income including non-controlling interests was KRW 10.4 trillion in 2025, a separate figure from owner-attributable net income.

Operating cash flow went from KRW 10.6 trillion in 2022 to negative KRW 2.52 trillion in 2023, and was negative KRW 5.99 trillion in 2025. The debt-to-equity ratio at the end of 2025 was 189.0%, higher than 181.4% in 2022.

Quarterly, second-quarter 2026 revenue of KRW 49.2 trillion and operating profit of KRW 2.85 trillion were the largest of the last five quarters. In the first quarter of 2026, US tariffs weighed KRW 860 billion and rising raw material costs lifted the cost of sales ratio to 82.5%, a sharp profitability setback.

Third-quarter 2026 operating profit of KRW 2.68 trillion, at a 5.9% margin, rose 5.5% year on year but fell 6.1% from the prior quarter, and owner-attributable net income of KRW 1.70 trillion was down 24.6% from a year earlier.

05

Industry analysis

Demand in key end markets has softened in several regions. In its third-quarter results, the company cited geopolitical tension and the Middle East war as drivers of weaker global industry demand.

In Korea, strike-related production losses and delayed new model launches pushed domestic sales down 27.2% in the third quarter. US tariffs remain a shared burden for automakers, with the first-quarter tariff impact put at KRW 860 billion, while reports described a 15% US auto tariff rate as a cost reduction factor.

Reports present the tariff rate differently depending on the date, so the reference point needs to be checked. Higher raw material prices lifted the cost of sales ratio to 83.8%, up 1.5 percentage points.

The currency worked in the other direction, with the third-quarter average won-dollar rate at 1,423, up 2.8% year on year. The company expects continued uncertainty from intensifying competition among automakers, high raw material prices and US tariff effects. The industry picture is one where weaker demand, tariffs, raw materials and FX act at the same time.

06

Outlook

The company cut its 2026 full-year wholesale volume target from 4,158,300 units to 4.0 million and adjusted its operating margin target from 6.3–7.3% to 5.5–6.5%. The revenue growth target of 1.0–2.0% was kept.

The automotive investment plan was reduced from KRW 17.8 trillion to KRW 16 trillion after a review of investment priorities and timing.

The company said the adjustment does not signal weaker competitiveness or earnings power, and that it reflects raw material price increases and production disruptions in the annual outlook.

For the fourth quarter, it plans to raise production and sales, benefit from new models and push company-wide cost reductions to improve profitability. The next checkpoint is how fourth-quarter 2026 results line up with the 5.5–6.5% full-year margin range.

On shareholder returns, the company said it is reviewing the KRW 4 trillion share buyback and cancellation plan announced in 2024, which keeps that direction an open variable. The third-quarter dividend is held at the prior year's level, with a record date of November 30, 2026 and a payment date of December 30, 2026.

Robotics and SDV are presented as mid- to long-term growth pillars, and at its first-quarter results the company said an SDV pace car would be tested on public roads in the second half of 2026.

08

Bull factors

Hybrid mix improvement

Third-quarter HEV sales reached 167,414 units, up about 6,000 from a year earlier, and their share of total sales rose 2.7 percentage points to 18.2%. The company said the HEV-led mix improvement contributed to the operating profit gain. The electrified share of total sales also rose to 26.7%, showing a shift in the sales mix.

Cushion from FX and cost reduction

In the third quarter, the currency effect added KRW 1.28 trillion and cost reductions and other factors added KRW 640 billion. Together they exceeded the KRW 1.11 trillion profit hit from lower volumes. The selling and administrative expense ratio fell 2.0 percentage points to 10.3%.

Tariff relief and local production

First-quarter US tariff costs were put at KRW 860 billion. The company has presented expanded US local production as a way to improve profitability. Reports describe the 15% US auto tariff rate as a cost reduction, and how that shows up in quarterly results is the next thing to check.

09

Bear factors

Volume decline and production disruptions

Third-quarter global wholesale sales fell 11.3% to 920,710 units, and domestic sales dropped 27.2% after strike-related disruptions. The full-year wholesale volume target was lowered from 4,158,300 units to 4.0 million. The company cited higher fixed costs from lower production volumes as a reason for cutting its margin target.

Annual profit decline and lower targets

Operating profit fell 19.5% in 2025 to KRW 11.5 trillion, and the operating margin dropped to 6.2%. Owner-attributable net income also declined 24.6% in 2025 to KRW 9.45 trillion. The 2026 operating margin target was lowered to 5.5–6.5%, which frames the scope of any profit recovery.

Financial segment and cost pressure

Financial segment operating profit fell 18.2% to KRW 471 billion in the third quarter, with higher interest costs, lower EV residual values and higher credit costs cited as causes. The cost of sales ratio rose 1.5 percentage points to 83.8%. Operating cash flow has remained negative since 2023.

10

Risk factors

Trade and tariff policy

Changes in the US tariff rate feed directly into results. First-quarter tariff costs were put at KRW 860 billion. If tariff rates rise again, quarterly profit swings could widen.

Currency movements

The third-quarter currency effect of KRW 1.28 trillion contributed heavily to the operating profit gain. A stronger won would reduce that cushion. The direction of the exchange rate is outside the company's control.

Investment execution and growth pillars

The automotive investment plan was reduced from KRW 17.8 trillion to KRW 16 trillion, and the company said it reviewed investment priorities and timing. The SDV and robotics businesses, presented as mid- to long-term growth pillars, will proceed within this revised investment scope.

If execution slips, the commercialization timing for the SDV shift and robotics could be pushed back. In that case, the benefit from structural improvement in earnings over the medium to long term could also be deferred.

12

Overall view

Recent results for Hyundai Motor show volumes falling while profit has been defended. Third-quarter 2026 revenue fell 3.4% year on year, but operating profit rose 5.5%, helped by hybrid sales growth, currency effects and cost reductions.

On the other side, lower volumes, domestic strikes and higher raw material prices led to cuts in both the full-year wholesale volume target and the operating margin target. On a reported basis, operating profit fell for a second straight year in 2025, and operating cash flow has been negative since 2023.

Strengths include the hybrid mix, currency and cost effects, and a possible easing of tariff costs, while weaknesses include lower volumes, lowered targets, weaker financial segment profitability and weak cash flow.

Fourth-quarter 2026 results and the annual report will show on a periodic-filing basis whether the full-year targets are met and how cash flow develops. The outcome of the review of the buyback and cancellation plan is also a variable for the direction of shareholder returns.

The share price trades at a discount to net asset value, and whether earnings and cash flow recover will shape how that discount is read.
