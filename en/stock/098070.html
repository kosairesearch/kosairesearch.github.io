02

Business structure

Hantech was spun off from the equipment division of Korea Fertilizer in 1998 and makes process equipment at its plant in the Onsan industrial complex in Ulsan. Its manufacturing know-how centers on special materials such as titanium and zirconium, which the company cites as its core edge.

The business runs through two divisions.

The process equipment division designs, fabricates and supplies heat exchangers, pressure vessels, reactors and column-and-tower equipment for refining, petrochemical, LNG and fertilizer plants, while the tank division designs, builds and installs cryogenic storage tanks for industrial gases such as nitrogen, oxygen, hydrogen, ammonia and carbon dioxide.

In line with climate policy, the company set up a green energy division to extend its ammonia-tank design know-how into hydrogen tanks and CCUS pressure vessels.

Overseas customers include Shintech Louisiana and the Wabash low-carbon ammonia project, while domestic partners include Samsung C&T and Korea Southern Power (KOSPO).

A supply contract with Shintech Louisiana disclosed on January 27, 2026 is worth KRW 28.39 billion, equal to 18.21% of 2024 consolidated revenue, and runs through December 31, 2027. The revenue split between the two divisions and their margin gap can be tracked in the segment information of the business report.

Listed peers in the same machinery and equipment sector include Doosan Enerbility, SFA Engineering and BHI, and the company's reliance on a small number of large projects and customers is a structural feature worth watching.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual results show that both sales and profitability grew together between 2024 and 2025. Revenue rose 10.3% year on year, from KRW 155.9 billion to KRW 171.9 billion, and operating profit rose 127.7%, from KRW 16.8 billion to KRW 38.2 billion.

The operating margin climbed from 10.8% to 22.2%, so margin improvement, rather than sales growth alone, drove the profit increase. The company attributed the sales increase to expanded orders from North America and the margin gain to a better product mix and a favorable currency environment.

Profit attributable to controlling shareholders rose 17.0%, from KRW 26.3 billion to KRW 30.8 billion, less than operating profit grew; the non-operating and tax items that bridge the two can be traced in the income statement details of the business report.

Operating cash flow rose from KRW 27 billion to KRW 58.8 billion, the debt ratio fell from 85.8% to 41.4%, and at the end of 2025 total equity of KRW 162.4 billion was entirely attributable to controlling shareholders.

Quarterly revenue peaked at KRW 47.8 billion in Q4 2025 (up 16.3% from the prior quarter) and then fell for three straight quarters, to KRW 47 billion in Q1 2026, KRW 46.3 billion in Q2 2026 and KRW 37.3 billion in Q3 2026.

Operating profit recovered to KRW 10.6 billion in Q2 2026 from KRW 7.63 billion in Q1, then dropped to KRW 3.81 billion in Q3 2026, down 64.0% quarter on quarter and 56.9% year on year.

Profit attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 8.04 billion in Q1 2026 and KRW 8.98 billion in Q2 2026, before turning into a net loss of KRW 3.12 billion in Q3 2026. Cumulative profit attributable to controlling shareholders over the last four quarters totals KRW 18.3 billion.

Operating profit rose in the quarter when revenue slipped, which shows how sharply quarterly margins can move; the cause of the Q3 drop should be checked in the quarterly line items.

05

Industry analysis

Hantech's end demand falls into three groups. The first is oil, gas and chemical plants, where expectations for expanded U.S. LNG facility investment are tied to its order flow. Press reports said the company signed equipment supply contracts worth about USD 80 million at major U.S.

LNG projects, including in Texas, during the first Trump administration, and forecast faster expansion of the U.S. LNG market after his return to office. The second is energy transition equipment, where ammonia co-firing in coal power and hydrogen storage demand are drawing attention to ammonia storage tanks.

A March 2025 report said the liquefied ammonia tank market planned by a state oil company and private firms could reach about KRW 688 billion by 2030, and the company targeted more than half of that market, about KRW 400 billion in orders.

The third is nuclear-related equipment, where a report noted the government reaffirmed plans to build two new large 1.4GW reactors by 2038 and one SMR by 2035. The spent-fuel dry storage (CASK) market has drawn interest on talk of revising the Korea-U.S. nuclear cooperation agreement.

Headwinds also exist: one report said high oil prices, inflation pressure and a delayed start to rate cuts cooled investor sentiment toward nuclear, a sector that needs large capital outlays.

On competition, the company points to its design know-how in difficult special materials such as titanium and zirconium and its capacity for cryogenic and high-pressure tanks as differentiators.

Compared with Doosan Enerbility, SFA and BHI, which fall in the same sector classification, its focus on special-material process equipment and cryogenic tanks is what sets it apart.

06

Outlook

The most concrete target the company has set is to exceed KRW 200 billion in new orders in 2026. As of March 2026, cumulative new orders for the year were estimated at about KRW 60 billion, and the company set its target by adding semiconductor-related tank orders to North American orders.

The heat exchanger order for the Wabash low-carbon ammonia project, announced on March 26, is tied to a project that will produce 500,000 tons of ammonia a year and capture 1.67 million tons of carbon dioxide.

The Shintech Louisiana contract runs through December 31, 2027, so whether follow-on North American volume arrives is the next question. The Samcheok hydrogen compound facility work with Samsung C&T is under way, and the company plans to add a first unit there in 2028.

In 2025 it completed onshore demonstration of a liquefied hydrogen storage tank for ships and plans to upgrade its ammonia cracking and liquefied hydrogen storage technology for power generation.

On nuclear, it is pursuing spent-fuel storage container production and nuclear decommissioning work, and equipment orders for new reactors are expected in the early 2030s after site tenders and basic and detailed design.

The green energy division is at the stage of extending into hydrogen tanks and CCUS pressure vessels. The points to check are whether the 2026 new-order target is met by year-end 2026, whether the sales decline stops in the 2026 results expected in early 2027, and when nuclear and CASK policy turns into actual orders.

08

Bull factors

Annual profitability improvement

The operating margin rose from 10.8% to 22.2% in 2025, and operating profit grew 127.7% to KRW 38.2 billion. The company cited expanded North American orders, a better product mix and a favorable currency environment as the drivers.

The debt ratio fell from 85.8% to 41.4%, which widened financial flexibility, and operating cash flow rose to KRW 58.8 billion. The step-up in annual earnings capacity is the main bullish argument.

Order pipeline spread across regions

In January 2026 the company disclosed a KRW 28.39 billion heat exchanger supply contract with Shintech Louisiana, and in March it announced a heat exchanger order for the Wabash low-carbon ammonia project in the United States. It set a target of exceeding KRW 200 billion in new orders for 2026.

The Samcheok hydrogen compound facility work with Samsung C&T is also under way, so the pipeline is split between North America and Korea.

New-business technology validation under way

Building on its ammonia tank design know-how, the company is widening its scope into hydrogen tanks, CCUS pressure vessels and CASK. If nuclear policy advances, it is mentioned as a supplier of core plant equipment such as heat exchangers and pressure vessels.

Completing onshore demonstration of a liquefied hydrogen storage tank for ships in 2025 is cited as evidence of technical validation.

09

Bear factors

Quarterly earnings slowdown in 2026

Quarterly revenue peaked at KRW 47.8 billion in Q4 2025 and then fell for three consecutive quarters through Q3 2026. Operating profit in Q3 2026 was KRW 3.81 billion, down 56.9% year on year, and profit attributable to controlling shareholders swung to a net loss of KRW 3.12 billion.

Whether the KRW 10.6 billion operating profit in Q2 2026 was a one-off recovery will need to be checked in the quarterly line items of upcoming filings. The combination of falling sales and volatile profit is the center of the bearish case.

New businesses dependent on policy and timing

A large part of the new businesses depends on nuclear policy and the progress of CASK-related rules. Reports noted that IAEA regulatory conditions and certification procedures could take time, so contributions would arrive in stages as policy moves forward.

With nuclear equipment orders expected in the early 2030s, the near-term earnings contribution could be limited. If policy timelines slip, the gap between order expectations and reported earnings lengthens.

Concentration in single contracts and projects

The Shintech Louisiana contract equals 18.21% of 2024 consolidated revenue, so a single contract carries a meaningful weight. Because orders are concentrated in large overseas projects, delays in order timing could push back revenue recognition as well.

With a high stake held by majority shareholder Foosung, the number of shares traded in the market is relatively small, which could act as a supply-demand factor.

10

Risk factors

Order and revenue recognition volatility

Because orders come as project-based contracts, quarterly revenue can swing sharply. Revenue has declined for three consecutive quarters through Q3 2026, and operating profit fell from KRW 10.6 billion in Q2 to KRW 3.81 billion in Q3.

If the timing of revenue recognition on large contracts shifts from quarter to quarter, profit volatility could widen further.

Policy and permitting timelines

New nuclear construction, domestic CASK production and the shift to ammonia co-firing all depend on government policy and permitting schedules. IAEA regulatory conditions and certification procedures may add time.

If the review of the nuclear cooperation agreement does not translate into rules, expectations for related businesses could be pushed back.

Currency, cost and interest-rate exposure

The company said a favorable currency environment contributed to the margin gains in 2025. If exchange rates move the other way, part of the profitability improvement could reverse.

High oil prices driving inflation and a delayed start to rate cuts have already cooled sentiment toward nuclear investment, which could also affect the timing of equipment orders.

Overall view

In its 2025 annual results, Hantech posted revenue of KRW 171.9 billion, operating profit of KRW 38.2 billion and an operating margin of 22.2%, so both scale and profitability grew. The debt ratio fell to 41.4% in the same year, which strengthened the balance sheet.

However, quarterly revenue has declined for three consecutive quarters in 2026, and the company swung to a controlling-shareholder net loss of KRW 3.12 billion in Q3 2026. On orders, U.S.

LNG and ammonia projects and the Samcheok hydrogen compound facility work continued, and the company set a 2026 target of exceeding KRW 200 billion in new orders.

New businesses in hydrogen tanks, CCUS, CASK and nuclear-related equipment depend on policy timelines and permits, so their earnings contribution could come later than expected.

The bullish factors are the improved margin structure and the order pipeline, while the bearish factors are quarterly earnings volatility and concentration in policy and projects.

On valuation, the price-to-book ratio is below 1x, but on a last-four-quarter earnings basis, whether profit recovers will shape how the multiples are read. The next checkpoints are whether revenue and operating profit recover in the 2026 annual business report and the Q1 2027 quarterly report.
