KG Mobility Platform Back in the Black; Sales Recovery Is the Test

KG Mobility Platform returned to operating profit of KRW 7.5 billion in Q3 2026 after an operating loss in Q2, but sales fell 27.8% from a year earlier, leaving sales volume recovery as the main open issue.

02

Business structure

KG Mobility Platform (K Car) runs a directly managed used car platform, with two business lines: a retail business that buys vehicles and sells them to consumers, and an auction business that sells vehicles for export.

Retail segment sales in Q3 2026 were KRW 423.4 billion, up 11.9% from the prior quarter, and accounted for most of the KRW 480.4 billion total. Auction segment sales were KRW 38.4 billion, down 38.3% year on year, so the top line now leans more heavily on retail.

Retail unit sales rose 11.2% quarter on quarter, and total unit sales in Q3 were 33,335. Beyond direct sales, the company is expanding into peer-to-peer brokerage through its Safe Direct Trade service and a vehicle management service called MyCar.

Safe Direct Trade launched in May 2026, and the company aims for full-scale expansion in 2027, a model that reduces the inventory burden because the company does not buy the vehicles itself.

The company joined the KG Group in August 2026 and changed its corporate name from K Car to KG Mobility Platform, while keeping the K Car consumer brand.

Within the group, joint work on certified pre-owned vehicles, overseas expansion using the network of KG Mobility (KGM), and links with group finance and payment affiliates are under review. Concrete collaboration plans have not yet been finalized.

In competition, the company emphasizes inventory soundness and purchasing discipline as its operating priorities.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual results show a recovery from a trough in 2023. Sales fell from KRW 2.18 trillion in 2022 to KRW 2.05 trillion in 2023 (down 6.0% year on year), then rose to KRW 2.3 trillion in 2024 (up 12.4%) and KRW 2.44 trillion in 2025 (up 6.0%), marking two straight years of growth.

Operating profit grew for three consecutive years, from KRW 50 billion in 2022 to KRW 59 billion in 2023, KRW 68.1 billion in 2024 and KRW 76 billion in 2025, and the operating margin rose from 2.3% to 3.1% over the same span.

Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders fell to KRW 28.4 billion in 2023 (down 6.6%) and then grew for two straight years to KRW 44 billion in 2024 and KRW 50.9 billion in 2025. Net profit including non-controlling interests is disclosed at the same amounts for each year.

The balance sheet became more leveraged, with the debt-to-equity ratio rising from 117.4% in 2022 to 138.7% in 2025, when total liabilities were KRW 310.6 billion and total equity was KRW 223.9 billion. Operating cash flow was KRW 77 billion in 2025, up from KRW 43.7 billion in 2022.

On a quarterly basis, profit weakened from Q3 2025, when operating profit was KRW 24 billion on sales of KRW 665.5 billion, to Q4 2025, when operating profit was KRW 12.4 billion on sales of KRW 559.8 billion (down 15.9% quarter on quarter).

Q1 2026 showed a small recovery with sales of KRW 572.1 billion and operating profit of KRW 14.2 billion. Q2 2026 turned to an operating loss of KRW 33.6 billion on sales of KRW 439.2 billion (down 23.2% quarter on quarter), with a controlling-shareholder net loss of KRW 27.1 billion.

The company attributed the Q2 SG&A increase largely to one-off costs tied to the change in the largest shareholder. In Q3 2026, sales rose to KRW 480.4 billion (up 9.4% quarter on quarter), gross profit reached KRW 49.3 billion (up 19.1%), gross margin rose to 10.3%, and operating profit returned to KRW 7.5 billion.

Even so, the Q3 net profit attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 3.73 billion was down 78.3% year on year, and press reports put the Q3 operating margin at about 1.6%, versus about 3.6% a year earlier.

05

Industry analysis

Used car distribution is more sensitive to household purchasing power and the interest rate and price environment than new car demand. In its Q3 explanation, the company cited a shorter number of business days due to the timing of the Chuseok holiday and the burden of vehicle purchases.

It nonetheless pointed to a 11.2% quarter-on-quarter rise in retail sales as a sign of field-level recovery. The export market moved in a different direction. Tighter regulations in major importing countries and logistics uncertainty weakened overseas demand, and auction segment sales fell 38.3%.

Inventory dynamics also moved: the price adjustment burden on inventory bought at high prices was reflected in Q2, and it eased in Q3, lifting the retail gross margin per unit from 8.4% to 9.4%. Press coverage describes K Car as the largest directly managed used car platform in Korea.

Market share and market size data were not sufficiently verifiable to support a peer comparison, so this section relies on the company's own unit sales and revenue trends as industry signals. Unit sales were down 21.5% year on year in Q3 but up 7.1% from Q2, indicating that the direction is turning. At this point in the cycle, retail is recovering while exports are contracting.

06

Outlook

In its Q3 announcement, the company said it will keep prioritizing profitability and inventory soundness while pursuing a recovery in sales volume. Growth in retail sales and a higher share of sales from newly purchased vehicles were cited as the basis for the volume recovery.

Under the KG Group, potential initiatives include collaboration using the certified pre-owned vehicle business, overseas market entry through the KGM overseas network, and customer service improvements linked to group finance and payment affiliates.

The company says it will pursue these in stages, starting with the most feasible areas. Safe Direct Trade targets full-scale expansion in 2027, so any contribution from this brokerage model would likely become visible only after that point, and its profit contribution has not been quantified.

The auction segment depends heavily on export demand and logistics conditions, making regulatory changes in major importing countries and sea freight conditions key variables.

On the retail side, whether unit sales and the retail gross margin per unit hold near Q3 levels in Q4 2026 will indicate whether the return to profit can persist. On inventory, the key question is whether the price adjustment burden on existing stock does not rebuild.

Because a one-off cost item, like the one seen in Q2 2026, can quickly push a quarter back into a loss, the classification of cost items in upcoming disclosures will matter.

The company stresses balance between volume growth and profitability, so restoring earnings strength appears to be the priority ahead of rapid top-line expansion.

08

Bull factors

Signs of earnings recovery

Operating profit grew for three consecutive years from 2023 to 2025, and the operating margin rose from 2.3% in 2022 to 3.1% in 2025. In Q3 2026, operating profit returned to KRW 7.5 billion from a KRW 33.6 billion loss in Q2. Gross margin rose 0.8 percentage points quarter on quarter to 10.3%. With Q2 one-off costs gone, quarterly earnings are moving in a recovering direction.

Retail sales and margin improvement

Retail unit sales rose 11.2% quarter on quarter in Q3, and retail segment sales grew 11.9% to KRW 423.4 billion. The retail gross margin per vehicle improved from 8.4% in Q2 to 9.4% in Q3. Easing price adjustment on existing inventory and a larger share of newly purchased vehicles drove the gain. Retail, the core channel for volume recovery, is improving.

Group ties and expansion paths

Since joining the KG Group in August 2026, the company has been reviewing collaboration in certified pre-owned vehicles, overseas expansion through the KG Mobility overseas network, and links with group finance and payment affiliates. The Safe Direct Trade brokerage service targets full-scale expansion in 2027.

The company says a brokerage model that does not require buying vehicles outright can reduce inventory burden. The collaboration plans are not yet finalized.

09

Bear factors

Continued top-line contraction

Q3 2026 sales of KRW 480.4 billion fell 27.8% year on year, and total unit sales dropped 21.5%. Auction segment sales fell 38.3% to KRW 38.4 billion, so the export channel has not yet recovered. Compared with KRW 665.5 billion in Q3 2025, the scale has not returned. Lower volume weighs on fixed cost absorption and earnings capacity.

Thin margins and recent losses

Press reports put the Q3 2026 operating margin at about 1.6%, compared with about 3.6% a year earlier. Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 3.73 billion in Q3, down 78.3% year on year.

Over the last four quarters, the cumulative net loss attributable to controlling shareholders is KRW 5.88 billion. The return to operating profit is real but the cushion is thin.

One-off costs and balance sheet strain

In Q2 2026, one-off costs tied to the change in the largest shareholder and inventory price adjustments combined to produce a KRW 33.6 billion operating loss. If similar costs recur, a quarterly profit could reverse quickly. The debt-to-equity ratio was 138.7% at the end of 2025, up from 117.4% in 2022. A prolonged sales slump would weigh more heavily given the limited balance sheet cushion.

Risk factors

Inventory and pricing risk

The value of used car inventory is exposed to price adjustments when vehicles were bought at high prices. In Q2 2026, price adjustments on existing inventory combined with large one-off costs to produce a loss. In Q3, a larger share of newly purchased vehicles eased the burden. Shifts in purchasing policy could swing inventory-related profit again.

Export and auction risk

The auction business is exposed to external factors such as tighter import rules in major importing countries, logistics uncertainty, and high oil prices and exchange rates. Q3 2026 auction sales were KRW 38.4 billion, down 38.3% year on year. If overseas demand does not recover, one leg of the business stays weak.

Overseas expansion, which relies on group network use, is still under review and may take time to become a replacement engine.

Uncertainty during group integration

Since the August 2026 KG Group integration, the company's corporate name and management structure have been changing. Collaboration options spanning certified pre-owned vehicles, overseas markets, and finance and payment links remain under review.

The profit contribution and any change in the cost structure have not yet been shown. Until the integration effects appear in results, uncertainty remains.

12

Overall view

KG Mobility Platform's sales, operating profit and net profit attributable to controlling shareholders have recovered since a 2023 trough, with 2025 the largest year in the period for all three.

However, the KRW 33.6 billion operating loss in Q2 2026 and the 27.8% year-on-year sales decline in Q3 show that the business has not yet reached a stable footing. The return to profit in Q3 is attributed to the disappearance of Q2 one-off costs, easing inventory price pressure and better retail margins.

Positive factors include retail recovery and margin improvement, while negative factors include the auction downturn, the shrinking top line and a thin operating margin.

On the balance sheet, the debt-to-equity ratio of 138.7% is above its 2022 level, and the company still shows a net loss attributable to controlling shareholders over the last four quarters.

The next checkpoints are the Q4 2026 preliminary results and the 2026 annual business report, with the key questions being whether retail sales hold, whether auction sales recover and whether one-off costs recur. Any collaboration benefit from the KG Group has not yet appeared in reported results.
