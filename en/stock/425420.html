TFE: Profit Recovery and Capacity Challenges in Test Supply

02

Business structure

TFE was founded in 2003 as a semiconductor test parts company and supplies boards, sockets and COK (Change of Kit) on a turnkey basis for the test stage where chips are checked for performance. By segment, test boards accounted for 49.7%, COK for 25.3% and test sockets for 24.9% of sales in the third quarter of 2025.

The test socket business began in 2019 when the company acquired Japan's JMT from ISC. Samsung Electronics is the largest customer, and a 2024 press report said it accounted for more than half of total sales.

After ISC, the previous main memory socket supplier, was sold to SKC, an SK Group affiliate, analysts said TFE could benefit as the next-largest supplier. Non-memory made up 36% of sales in the first half of 2026 against 64% for memory, short of the 50% non-memory target the company set at its 2022 listing for 2025.

The export share rose from 26.6% in 2023 and 23.9% in 2024 to 29.7% in 2025 and 43.6% in the first half of 2026. A 2023 press report described a production plant in Japan, an office in China and a planned US branch.

ISC is a competitor in test sockets, and TFE's turnkey supply of boards, sockets and COK is cited as a differentiator. Growth in HBM 2.5D and 3D packaging is enlarging the test socket market itself, which is the backdrop for this business structure.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual sales rose from KRW 63.7 billion in 2022 to KRW 80.3 billion in 2023 (up 26.0% year on year), fell to KRW 73.6 billion in 2024 (down 8.3%), and reached KRW 111.7 billion in 2025 (up 51.8%), the largest year in the 2022 to 2025 range.

Operating profit was KRW 6.51 billion in 2022, KRW 9.22 billion in 2023 (up 41.6%), KRW 4.39 billion in 2024 (down 52.4%) and KRW 19.1 billion in 2025 (up 334.0%), with 2024 the smallest year.

Analysts attributed the 2023 gain to better order profitability and cost reduction, and a securities firm estimated that the 2025 gain reflected higher selling prices following raw material cost increases.

The operating margin went from 10.2% in 2022 and 11.5% in 2023 to 6.0% in 2024 and 17.1% in 2025, the highest in the filed period.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 6.72 billion in 2022, KRW 11.1 billion in 2023 (up 65.3%), KRW 1.45 billion in 2024 (down 86.9%) and KRW 18.1 billion in 2025 (up 1,146.7%).

At the end of 2025, total equity was KRW 109.2 billion, all of it attributable to controlling shareholders, and total liabilities were KRW 34.4 billion, with the debt ratio falling from 61.6% in 2024 to 31.5% in 2025. Operating cash flow rose from KRW 4.46 billion in 2024 to KRW 12.8 billion in 2025.

Quarterly sales went from KRW 27.2 billion in the third quarter of 2025 to KRW 37.4 billion in the fourth quarter (up 37.6% from the prior quarter), dipped to KRW 32.3 billion in the first quarter of 2026 (down 13.7%), then rose to KRW 33.6 billion in the second quarter and KRW 54.2 billion in the third quarter (up 61.2%).

Third-quarter 2026 operating profit of KRW 15 billion rose 211.7% year on year, faster than the 99.1% sales gain, and net income attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 10.2 billion rose 119.0%. The company said the first-half 2026 operating margin of 14.3% would be 19.4% excluding one-time costs.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders over the last four quarters, from the fourth quarter of 2025 to the third quarter of 2026, totaled KRW 26.9 billion.

05

Industry analysis

Securities analysts see the semiconductor test industry entering a structural growth phase as AI, HBM and advanced packaging make chip testing more demanding. Data center packaging has grown more complex, lengthening package test steps and test time, and analysts expect test socket demand to rise in earnest.

In an April 2026 report, an NH Investment & Securities analyst cited the expansion of co-packaged optics (CPO) as a positive factor. At its 2022 listing, the company pointed to DDR5 supply expansion in memory and growth in data centers, 5G and autonomous driving in non-memory as drivers of test socket demand.

On the cycle, TFE's sales rose in 2023 even as the semiconductor market was weak, while several peer socket makers saw earnings decline in the same period.

In competition, the supply structure changed after ISC, the former leading memory socket supplier, was sold, and turnkey capability is cited as a factor that separates players.

The pace of non-memory expansion depends on customer diversification, and the 36% non-memory share in the first half of 2026 shows the shift is underway. The rise in the export share to 43.6% in the first half of 2026 also suggests overseas customer development is advancing.

06

Outlook

At its first-half 2026 investor relations briefing, the company set out a '3C' strategy covering technology capability, customer and market diversification, and capital investment. An April 2026 press report said the Hwaseong plant expansion was scheduled for completion in the second quarter of 2026.

In April 2026 the company resold KRW 12 billion of convertible bonds it held for KRW 20.99 billion and said the proceeds would go to capacity expansion and new technology R&D. The first series of convertible bonds was fully converted in April 2026, removing a source of potential dilution.

In an April 2026 report, NH Investment & Securities analyst Jun Ki Baek projected 2026 sales of KRW 149.7 billion (up 34%) and operating profit of KRW 33.8 billion (up 77.6%). The same report cited growth at key customers and CPO expansion as grounds for the possibility of record results since the company's founding.

Because this projection dates from April 2026, it serves as a benchmark to compare against figures in future periodic reports. With Samsung Electronics a major customer and the export share rising in the first half of 2026, how much overseas customer revenue feeds into results is the next point to check.

08

Bull factors

Turnkey supply and rising test demand

Because TFE supplies boards, sockets and COK together, analysts say its position can extend from R&D orders through mass production. Growing AI, HBM and data center packaging is lengthening test steps and test time, which is cited as a demand driver.

The rise in the export share to 43.6% in the first half of 2026 also reads as a sign of customer diversification.

Profit recovery and quarterly rebound

Third-quarter 2026 sales of KRW 54.2 billion rose 99.1% year on year, and operating profit of KRW 15 billion rose 211.7%. Sales and profit have increased for two consecutive quarters from the second quarter of 2026 through the third.

Operating profit for 2025 of KRW 19.1 billion was up 334.0%, and the operating margin recovered from 6.0% in 2024 to 17.1% in 2025.

Financial room and capacity investment

The debt ratio fell to 31.5% at the end of 2025 from 61.6% in 2024, with total liabilities of KRW 34.4 billion and total equity of KRW 109.2 billion. Operating cash flow rose to KRW 12.8 billion in 2025 from KRW 4.46 billion in 2024.

The company announced the Hwaseong expansion for completion in the second quarter of 2026 and said proceeds from the convertible bond resale would go to capacity and R&D.

09

Bear factors

Samsung dependence and memory weight

A 2024 press report said Samsung Electronics, the largest customer, accounts for more than half of sales, so results could hinge heavily on that customer's investment plans.

Memory made up 64% of sales in the first half of 2026 against 36% for non-memory, leaving a channel through which memory market swings reach results. A gap remains between the 50% non-memory target set at the 2022 listing for 2025 and the current mix.

Quarterly earnings volatility

First-quarter 2026 sales of KRW 32.3 billion fell 13.7% from the prior quarter, and operating profit of KRW 3.33 billion fell 54.6%. In 2024 operating profit dropped 52.4% in a single year, showing how quickly earnings can move.

The first-half 2026 operating margin of 14.3% becomes 19.4% excluding one-time costs, so the cost structure changes how results are read.

High price premium and supply-demand swings

The price-to-book multiple sits in a premium range, so earnings expectations are heavily priced in and the shares are sensitive to any change in those expectations. In March 2026 the shares rose 79.73% over 15 trading days and were designated as an investment caution stock. Market commentary also flagged the possibility of profit-taking as the convertible bonds were fully converted.

10

Risk factors

Market cycle and customer concentration

With a memory-heavy mix and high dependence on Samsung Electronics, order volumes could move with the semiconductor cycle. Sales rose in 2023 despite a weak market, but both sales and operating profit declined in 2024. Adjustments to customers' capital spending feed directly into test parts demand.

Capacity expansion and capital deployment

The Hwaseong expansion was announced for completion in the second quarter of 2026, and the bond resale proceeds are going to capacity and R&D. If new capacity does not match customer demand, utilization and margins could weaken. How quickly capital spending shows up in sales is also a point to track.

External factors from wider overseas sales

With the export share rising to 43.6% in the first half of 2026, overseas customer demand and contract terms have a larger effect on results. Overseas bases centered on a Japan plant and a China office, with a US branch reported as under establishment in 2023. Regional regulations and trade conditions are factors the company cannot control directly.

Overall view

TFE restored its earnings base in 2025, with operating profit of KRW 19.1 billion and a 17.1% operating margin, under a turnkey model covering test boards, sockets and COK. Third-quarter 2026 sales of KRW 54.2 billion were the largest of the last five quarters on a filed basis.

Supporting factors are test demand from AI and HBM packaging, the export share rising to 43.6%, and a debt ratio that fell to 31.5%. Offsetting factors are a memory weight of 64%, dependence on Samsung Electronics, large quarter-to-quarter swings, and a share price sitting at a premium to net assets.

The two consecutive quarters of growth from the second to the third quarter of 2026, after the first-quarter low, show the recovery continuing at the quarterly level. The key open questions are how the Hwaseong expansion and bond resale proceeds are deployed and how far the non-memory share can rise.

The next checkpoint is the 2026 annual report, where the question is whether the operating margin holds near 2025's 17.1%. This report offers no investment opinion or target price, and its figures rest on DART periodic report values and the sources cited.
