02

Business structure

Ghost Studio is a subsidiary of ME2ON that operates mobile and PC game services alongside drama production. Its core revenue sources are the social casino title Classic Vegas Casino and casual card games in the Solitaire and TriPeaks lineage, which have built a long-running user base in North America.

Revenue comes from in-app purchases and advertising, and traffic-based ad exposure and user analytics are described as supporting ad revenue growth. Newer releases include the 3D match puzzle game Match Miracle, launched in November 2023, and the restaurant management simulation Cooking Tour.

The company said Match Miracle earned a 4.8 rating on Google Play and 4.9 on the Apple App Store. Subsidiary Zoftsoft developed the third-person shooter Pizza Bandit, which ran a closed alpha test in May 2024.

The drama business links web novel and webtoon IP to game and drama production, and in October 2024 the company said it held more than 10 scenario IP properties. In September 2024 it announced confirmation of production for a Netflix series.

Management has said it aims to become a global entertainment company that plans, produces and distributes games, webtoons, dramas and films. Competitively, the casual and social casino segments compete for player time with other mobile game companies in the sector, including Netmarble, Com2us and Wemade.

Quarterly revenue swings sharply with the success of new releases, which is visible in the quarterly trend of the disclosed results.

Earnings analysis

The company reports in USD, and the amounts below are converted to KRW. Annual revenue was KRW 101.3 billion in 2022 and KRW 101.3 billion in 2023, unchanged year on year, fell 17.2% to KRW 83.9 billion in 2024, and then rose 25.9% to KRW 105.6 billion in 2025.

The largest year of 2022 to 2025 was 2025, and the smallest was 2024. Operating profit was KRW 30.3 billion in 2022, KRW 25.7 billion in 2023 (down 15.3%), KRW 15.1 billion in 2024 (down 41.3%) and KRW 14.4 billion in 2025 (down 4.6%), declining for three consecutive years from 2023 to 2025.

Operating margin moved from 29.9% to 25.3%, 18.0% and 13.6% over the same period. Revenue grew in 2025 while operating profit declined, so top-line growth did not carry through to profitability.

A Korean research house said in 2024 that M&A had weighed on operating margin, and the company attributed the 2025 revenue increase to the recognition of drama production revenue.

Profit attributable to owners of the parent was KRW 26.7 billion in 2022, KRW 22.5 billion in 2023 (down 15.6%), KRW 8.38 billion in 2024 (down 62.8%) and KRW 7.98 billion in 2025 (down 4.7%), declining for three consecutive years from 2023 to 2025.

Net profit including non-controlling interests was KRW 7.38 billion in 2025, a different figure from profit attributable to owners of the parent. Operating cash flow fell from KRW 30 billion in 2022 to KRW 19.3 billion in 2025.

The debt ratio rose to 22.1% in 2024 and fell to 10.0% in 2025, when total equity was KRW 147.5 billion, equity attributable to owners of the parent was KRW 145.2 billion and total liabilities were KRW 14.8 billion.

Quarterly, the third quarter of 2025 showed revenue of KRW 20.1 billion, operating profit of KRW 4.11 billion and profit attributable to owners of the parent of KRW 4.21 billion.

The fourth quarter of 2025 brought revenue of KRW 47 billion, up 133.5% from the prior quarter, but operating profit of KRW 2.87 billion (down 30.0%) and a net loss attributable to owners of the parent of KRW 3.84 billion, a swing into the red.

The first quarter of 2026 recorded revenue of KRW 19.1 billion (down 59.5%), operating profit of KRW 3.96 billion (up 37.7%) and profit attributable to owners of the parent of KRW 3.95 billion, a return to profit.

Operating profit of KRW 4.31 billion in the second quarter of 2026 was the highest among the disclosed quarters. Third-quarter 2026 revenue was KRW 18.1 billion, down 9.9% year on year, and operating profit of KRW 3.03 billion fell 26.3%. Profit attributable to owners of the parent over the latest four quarters totaled KRW 8.59 billion.

Industry analysis

Mobile casual and social casino markets have short release cycles and wide variance in hit rates, so new titles often have to offset declining players on existing lineups.

Ghost Studio's revenue consists of in-app purchases and advertising, and ad revenue is described as depending on traffic-based exposure and user analytics capability.

In 2024, a Korean research house expected earnings growth and a higher corporate value from new casual game launches and expansion into webtoons, web novels and drama.

Yet operating profit declined for three consecutive years from 2023 to 2025, suggesting that the profit contribution from new games and new businesses fell short of those expectations.

This indicates that new-title revenue may not have fully offset cost increases, although the cost breakdown cannot be separated from the disclosed tables alone. Compared with larger game companies in the same sector such as Netmarble, Com2us and Wemade, the company's lineup is smaller in scale.

Its model centers on casual genres and social casino titles with a small set of lineups, which differs from competitors that lead with large IP. Drama production depends on platform commissioning, so quarterly revenue can vary with the number of productions and their release timing.

The sharp fourth-quarter 2025 revenue spike followed by a reversal in the first quarter of 2026 illustrates this structural volatility.

Risk factors

New-title performance risk

Revenue swings sharply with new-title performance, as the fourth-quarter 2025 and first-quarter 2026 movements show. Casual games can lose players quickly after launch, so without updates and new content revenue can fall fast. If new titles fail to offset declines in existing lineups, the trend of falling operating profit could persist.

Drama production risk

Drama production depends on platform commissioning and release schedules, so the timing of revenue recognition can shift with the number of productions and their release dates.

The goal of at least two dramas a year and KRW 300 billion in revenue within five years, stated in 2024, is far above the current annual revenue level. Whether that goal is reached depends on the profitability and durability of drama revenue.

Acquired subsidiary and integration risk

The company has acquired subsidiaries and IP through M&A, and in 2024 a Korean research house said M&A had weighed on operating margin. If acquired businesses contribute to consolidated profit more slowly or more modestly than expected, a recovery in operating margin could be delayed.

The allocation of resources within the group, including its relationship with parent ME2ON, could also affect future results.
