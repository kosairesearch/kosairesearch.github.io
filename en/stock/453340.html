02

Business structure

Hyundai Green Food is a food service company that was spun off and re-listed in 2023, operating four segments: foodservice (institutional catering), food ingredient supply, distribution, and other businesses covering dining and care food.

In 2025, foodservice generated KRW 1,112.8 billion in revenue, about 47.8% of the total, ingredients KRW 589.5 billion (25.3%), distribution KRW 307.7 billion and other businesses KRW 319.6 billion (13.7%).

The ingredients segment earned KRW 50.4 billion of operating profit in 2025, about 47% of the company total, with an 8.5% operating margin, the highest of the four. Foodservice earned a 4.2% margin in the same year, so the largest segment by revenue is not the most profitable one.

Other businesses, which cover dining and retail, returned to operating profit of KRW 0.5 billion in 2025 after a loss in 2024, but its margin was only 0.2%. The care food brand Greating has been reported to grow about 70% a year since its 2020 launch.

In the first half of 2026, the revenue mix was reported as 47.2% foodservice, 26.2% ingredients, 12.7% distribution and 13.9% other. Main customers are corporate and hospital canteens, and rising dining-out prices have lifted in-house catering demand.

In competition, the company overlaps with large food service providers such as CJ Freshway in institutional catering and ingredient distribution. Overseas, it runs subsidiaries in China, the United States and Mexico.

04

Earnings analysis

On a disclosed basis, annual revenue rose from KRW 1.83 trillion in 2023 to KRW 2.27 trillion in 2024 (up 24.3% year on year) and KRW 2.33 trillion in 2025 (up 2.6%), with growth in both 2024 and 2025 and 2025 the largest year of the three.

Operating profit went from KRW 64.8 billion in 2023 to KRW 96.7 billion in 2024 (up 49.1%) and KRW 106.8 billion in 2025 (up 10.5%), also rising in both 2024 and 2025, and the operating margin climbed from 3.5% to 4.3% to 4.6%.

In 2024, profit grew far faster than revenue, so margin improvement drove the earnings gain; in 2025, revenue growth slowed to 2.6% while operating profit still rose 10.5%.

Profit attributable to owners went from KRW 39.3 billion in 2023 to KRW 74 billion in 2024 (up 88.5%) and KRW 87.2 billion in 2025 (up 17.7%), while net profit including non-controlling interests was KRW 87.7 billion in 2025, a different figure.

Operating cash flow, by contrast, fell from KRW 131.9 billion in 2024 to KRW 82.4 billion in 2025, after KRW 101.6 billion in 2023.

The debt ratio was 40.1% in 2023, 42.6% in 2024 and 41.1% in 2025; at the end of 2025, total equity was KRW 729.5 billion, all of it attributable to owners, against total liabilities of KRW 300.1 billion.

By quarter, fourth-quarter 2025 revenue of KRW 589.8 billion fell 4.4% from the prior quarter and the company posted an operating loss of KRW 832 million and a net loss attributable to owners of KRW 180 million.

In the first quarter of 2026, revenue of KRW 621.5 billion and operating profit of KRW 46.4 billion returned to profit, and in the second quarter revenue of KRW 595 billion and operating profit of KRW 38.1 billion fell 17.8% from the prior quarter.

Third-quarter 2026 revenue of KRW 652 billion rose 9.6% from the prior quarter and 5.7% from a year earlier, while operating profit of KRW 44.8 billion grew only 0.7% year on year and profit attributable to owners of KRW 34.5 billion fell 9.4%. Profit attributable to owners totaled KRW 101.1 billion over the last four quarters.

05

Industry analysis

Demand for institutional catering in Korea is being supported by higher dining-out prices, which have increased in-house meal demand at companies and hospitals. In September 2026, Heungkuk Securities said that persistently high oil and consumer prices kept demand for catering and food ingredient services firm.

Reduced military manpower has increased outsourcing of military catering, and an expected military catering market of roughly KRW 2 trillion has been cited as a further opportunity.

In dining, combined revenue from food service and distribution rose 11.2% to KRW 175.5 billion in the third quarter of 2026, according to IBK Investment & Securities, despite weaker restaurant spending amid an economic slowdown.

The ingredient distribution business posted first-quarter 2026 revenue of KRW 161.4 billion, up 13.3% year on year, a quarterly high, according to IBK Investment & Securities. Distribution revenue fell 4.5% in 2025, so whether that growth continues is a useful comparison point.

The ingredients segment had an 8.5% operating margin in 2025, the highest of the four, and it contributes disproportionately to group profit.

Overseas, projects that followed Hyundai group affiliates abroad have expanded, but combined first-half 2026 net profit of overseas subsidiaries fell to KRW 0.9 billion from KRW 1.9 billion a year earlier.

In institutional catering and ingredient distribution, the company competes for contracts with peers such as CJ Freshway.

06

Outlook

The company has set a target of keeping its total shareholder return ratio at around 40%, combining cash dividends with share buybacks. It has also outlined plans to buy back and cancel treasury shares equal to about 10.6% of its holdings over five years and to raise total dividends to at least KRW 20 billion.

Broker estimates differ: Heungkuk Securities expects 2026 revenue of KRW 2,496 billion and operating profit of KRW 133 billion, up 7.1% and 24.5% respectively, while IBK Investment & Securities estimates revenue of KRW 2,512 billion and operating profit of KRW 139.5 billion, according to a November 2026 report.

According to FnGuide, the consensus for fourth-quarter 2026 revenue is KRW 635.2 billion, with operating profit of KRW 4 billion, which would be a return to profit from an operating loss of KRW 800 million a year earlier.

In a September 2026 report, Heungkuk Securities expected catering and ingredients to keep growing, supported by improving conditions at existing clients and new contract wins. The dining business is expected to benefit from more visitors to department stores, and new outlets such as the Cheongju outlet have been cited.

Whether the care food brand Greating can grow higher-value businesses is seen as a factor for medium-term profitability.

The next checkpoints are whether fourth-quarter 2026 preliminary results turn operating profit positive, and whether full-year 2026 operating margin, reported in the business report, exceeds the 4.6% recorded in 2025.

08

Bull factors

Steady catering and ingredient demand

Higher dining-out prices have lifted in-house catering demand at companies and hospitals. In September 2026, Heungkuk Securities said demand for catering and ingredient services stayed firm despite high oil and consumer prices.

First-quarter 2026 ingredient distribution revenue of KRW 161.4 billion rose 13.3% year on year, a quarterly high.

Expanding shareholder return policy

The company has set a total shareholder return target of around 40% and is combining cash dividends with share buybacks and cancellations. It has also outlined plans to cancel treasury shares equal to about 10.6% of its holdings over five years and raise total dividends to at least KRW 20 billion.

Heungkuk Securities assessed that earnings growth and a larger shareholder return policy are progressing together.

Other segment back in profit and dining growth

The other segment returned to operating profit of KRW 0.5 billion in 2025 after a loss in 2024. Combined dining and distribution revenue rose 11.2% to KRW 175.5 billion in the third quarter of 2026, according to IBK Investment & Securities.

09

Bear factors

Thin foodservice margin

Foodservice, the largest segment by revenue, earned a 4.2% operating margin in 2025, below the company-wide 4.6%. The other segment's 0.2% margin means dining contributes little to profit. Ingredients supplied close to half of group operating profit, so earnings lean heavily on one segment.

Slowing profit growth in 2026

Third-quarter 2026 operating profit of KRW 44.8 billion rose only 0.7% year on year, and profit attributable to owners of KRW 34.5 billion fell 9.4%. Second-quarter operating profit of KRW 38.1 billion fell 17.8% from the prior quarter, and Heungkuk Securities said it came in slightly below its earlier forecast.

Slower cash flow and weak overseas and distribution

Operating cash flow fell to KRW 82.4 billion in 2025 from KRW 131.9 billion in 2024. Distribution revenue declined 4.5% in 2025, and first-half 2026 combined net profit of overseas subsidiaries fell to KRW 0.9 billion from KRW 1.9 billion a year earlier. How profit converts into cash and how the segments balance will be checked again in the next regular report.

10

Risk factors

Weaker dining-out spending

A slowdown that weighs on restaurant spending affects traffic and sales in the dining business. The company is responding with new menus and store refurbishments, but results could swing with department store visitor flows. Because the other segment's margin is only 0.2%, a drop in dining sales would quickly affect its profit.

Cost and SG&A pressure

Analysts flagged cost pressure in second-quarter 2026 results. Third-quarter SG&A rose 7.9% to KRW 83.9 billion, and the company said it had controlled costs apart from essential overheads such as labor, rent and logistics. Higher labor and logistics costs could compress margins in catering and ingredients.

Overseas execution and shareholder return delivery

Combined first-half 2026 net profit of overseas subsidiaries fell to KRW 0.9 billion, so profitability in China, the United States and Mexico is volatile. The target of around 40% total shareholder returns depends on profit levels and buyback execution, and a delay could leave a gap versus expectations. Progress on share cancellations can be tracked through regulatory filings.

12

Overall view

Hyundai Green Food's results center on institutional catering and food ingredient supply, and 2025 revenue of KRW 2.33 trillion and operating profit of KRW 106.8 billion were the largest of 2023 to 2025. The operating margin rose from 3.5% in 2023 to 4.6% in 2025, which shows earnings capacity improving.

However, third-quarter 2026 operating profit rose only 0.7% year on year and profit attributable to owners fell 9.4%, so profit growth has slowed. Quarterly results can swing, as shown by the operating loss in the fourth quarter of 2025.

On shareholder returns, a target of around 40% total return is being pursued alongside share buybacks and cancellations. Heungkuk Securities, in a November 2026 report, kept its Buy rating with a target price of KRW 29,000.

Strengths include steady catering and ingredient demand, the shareholder return policy and the other segment's return to profit; weaknesses include a thin foodservice margin, lower third-quarter profit and slower cash flow.

The two sides carry comparable weight, and the next checkpoint is whether the 2026 full-year operating margin in the business report exceeds the 4.6% recorded in 2025.
