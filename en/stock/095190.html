02

Business structure

SHINHWA PRITECH is a Changwon-based precision parts machining company, and defense and aerospace parts sit at the center of the business.

According to the company's own profile, the defense and aerospace business began with T-50 door actuator parts in 2006 and extended to A340 and A380 landing gear parts and aircraft hydraulic reservoirs.

The power generation business makes major assemblies for nuclear and thermal power equipment as well as parts for supercritical power plants, and the company cites domestic and overseas certifications and patents as the basis of its technology.

Press reports say defense and aerospace accounted for 91.2% of sales in the first half of 2026, while the automotive parts segment, newly reflected in the same period, posted sales of KRW 2.3 billion and operating profit of KRW 400 million.

The machine tool segment, which makes CNC lathes among other products, was reported in the audit report as having ceased direct manufacturing at the Haman branch, which closed its registration in September 2025.

Labor union representatives argue that machine tools are still produced under a different name at a leased facility, and this remains their claim. The company was renamed SHINHWA PRITECH in March 2026 after it became an affiliate of the Shinhwa Group.

A May 2026 disclosure showed the largest shareholder group buying 765,624 shares on the market, bringing total holdings including related parties to 30,117,872 shares.

04

Earnings analysis

On a disclosed basis, the company recorded losses for four consecutive years from 2022 to 2025.

Sales rose from KRW 95.5 billion in 2022 to KRW 102.2 billion in 2023 (up 7.0% year on year) and KRW 120.8 billion in 2024 (up 18.1%), then fell to KRW 92.5 billion in 2025 (down 23.4%), making 2024 the largest year in the period.

Operating results moved from an operating loss of KRW 2.29 billion in 2022 to operating profit of KRW 882 million in 2023, a swing to profit, then KRW 2.18 billion in 2024 (up 147.5%), before turning to an operating loss of KRW 4.78 billion in 2025.

The operating margin peaked at 1.8% in 2024 and slipped to -5.2% in 2025.

Net losses attributable to controlling shareholders were KRW 9.46 billion in 2022, KRW 2.25 billion in 2023 (a narrower loss), KRW 4.57 billion in 2024, and KRW 6.94 billion in 2025 (a wider loss), and the total net loss in each year matched these figures.

The audit report states that direct manufacturing at the Haman branch had stopped by year-end 2025 and that production facilities were leased, so the impact of this change on results cannot be determined from the disclosure wording alone.

Operating cash flow was -KRW 5.88 billion in 2022, KRW 1.87 billion in 2023, -KRW 69.19 million in 2024, and KRW 15.1 billion in 2025, improving in 2025. The debt ratio fell from 186.6% in 2023 to 115.6% in 2025, with total liabilities of KRW 125.4 billion and total equity of KRW 108.4 billion at year-end 2025.

Quarterly, an operating loss of KRW 448 million in Q3 2025 and KRW 3.33 billion in Q4 2025 was followed by operating profit of KRW 1.74 billion in Q1 2026, a swing to profit.

Operating profit then fell to KRW 1.53 billion in Q2 2026 (down 12.3% quarter on quarter) and KRW 292 million in Q3 2026 (down 80.9% quarter on quarter, but a swing to profit from a year earlier), a decline over two consecutive quarters.

Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 1.12 billion in Q1 2026 and KRW 213 million in Q2 2026 (down 81.1%), and Q3 2026 returned to a net loss of KRW 2.03 billion. The net loss attributable to controlling shareholders over the last four quarters, from Q4 2025 to Q3 2026, totaled KRW 1.97 billion.

Industry analysis

Demand for defense and aerospace parts moves with order volumes and delivery schedules set by the government and defense contractors. Press coverage noted that this structure can shift the timing of revenue recognition from quarter to quarter.

Because defense and aerospace account for most of the company's sales, order flows in that segment translate directly into earnings swings.

Press reports say defense and aerospace sales rose 5.13% year on year in the first half of 2026, and segment operating profit moved from a loss of KRW 100 million to a profit of KRW 2.7 billion, making that segment the center of the year's recovery.

Defense budgets and aircraft parts demand are the largest external variables for revenue. The machine tool production setup was changed after the Haman branch closed in September 2025, according to the disclosure, and labor representatives claim production continues under another name at the same site.

On competition, the company points to its precision machining technology and its domestic and overseas certifications and patents as strengths. Structurally, the closest comparison group is listed companies in the space and defense sector that handle defense and aerospace parts.

Chemical sector peers differ in raw materials, equipment, and customers, so they are harder to use as direct benchmarks. Labor disputes remain a variable in the operating environment.

Risk factors

Concentration in orders and deliveries

Defense and aerospace made up 91.2% of sales in the first half of 2026 according to press reports, so changes in order volumes or delivery schedules could move quarterly sales and operating profit sharply. Q3 2026 sales were KRW 21.7 billion, down 13.4% quarter on quarter, and operating profit was KRW 292 million. How quickly diversification reduces this volatility remains to be seen.

Labor and legal uncertainty

The end-of-employment date the company notified differs from the actual dismissal date that the labor commission identified, so follow-up procedures after the unfair-dismissal ruling may continue.

The audit report describes production facilities as leased, while press reports say the factory registration remains in the company's name. Such matters can translate into costs and reputational burden depending on how long court or administrative processes run.

Financing and funding

Total liabilities of KRW 125.4 billion at year-end 2025 exceed total equity of KRW 108.4 billion. A reported CB plan could raise the share count if converted and affect the equity structure.

Operating cash flow turned from negative in 2024 to KRW 15.1 billion in 2025, but whether quarterly profit in 2026 can be sustained remains an open question.

Overall view

SHINHWA PRITECH has a business structure centered on defense and aerospace parts, and its return to quarterly operating profit in Q1 2026 signaled a profit recovery. However, operating profit fell over two consecutive quarters in Q2 and Q3 2026, and Q3 returned to a net loss attributable to controlling shareholders.

Four straight years of losses from 2022 to 2025 and the 2025 sales decline raise questions about the size and durability of the recovery. The lower debt ratio and the positive operating cash flow in 2025 show that the financial burden is easing.

Reported CB plans and labor disputes remain issues to examine for funding and governance. Strengths and weaknesses both appear in the disclosed results, so it is hard to say which side dominates.

The price-to-book ratio below 1x means the stock trades at a discount to net asset value on an asset basis, while earnings-based multiples cannot be calculated because of recent net losses. The next checkpoints are Q4 2026 results in the 2026 annual report and Q1 2027 quarterly results.
