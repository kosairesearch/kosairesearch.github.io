02

Business structure

Tapex was founded in 1994 as a maker of adhesive tapes, entered the wrap market in 2003 through the acquisition of Unilab, and entered the electronic materials tape market in 2005.

The company reports two segments: Electronic Materials, which supplies high-value products such as tapes for secondary batteries and semiconductors, and Consumer Goods, which sells food-packaging wrap and OPP tape.

A product-level mix compiled by a financial data service puts electronic materials and secondary-battery tapes, including OCA, at 55.9%, consumer food-packaging wrap at 24.4%, and traded consumer tape products at 19.7%.

That mix follows the data provider's own classification and should be read separately from the segment sales disclosed in filings. In electronic materials, mass production of high-performance UV tape for MLCC began in 2024 and is being used to expand sales and share.

Major customers include global battery and automotive manufacturers, and the company is building a medium- to long-term base by establishing positions in Europe and North America.

In consumer goods, its commercial-use wrap remains number one in Korea, and the company is working to raise the share of higher-function products.

The same data provider reports that new sites and production capacity secured in advance by the electronic materials unit lifted both sales and profitability in the first quarter of 2026.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual sales fell from KRW 182.3 billion in 2022 to KRW 145.8 billion in 2023, a 20.0% decline, and to KRW 136 billion in 2024, a 6.8% decline, before rebounding to KRW 141.1 billion in 2025, a 3.8% increase. Among 2022 to 2025, 2022 was the largest year and 2024 the smallest.

Operating profit dropped from KRW 25.5 billion in 2022 to KRW 6.62 billion in 2023, a 74.0% decline, and to KRW 1.43 billion in 2024, a 78.4% decline, before recovering to KRW 2.51 billion in 2025, a 75.6% increase. The operating margin narrowed from 14.0% in 2022 to 4.5% in 2023, 1.1% in 2024 and 1.8% in 2025.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders declined from KRW 18.9 billion in 2022 to KRW 8.1 billion in 2023, KRW 3.58 billion in 2024 and KRW 2.02 billion in 2025, a decline for three consecutive years from 2023 through 2025.

In 2025 operating profit rose while net income fell, which the company attributed to higher financial costs offsetting growth in electronic materials sales.

Quarterly sales moved from KRW 37.4 billion in the third quarter of 2025 and KRW 36.9 billion in the fourth quarter, a 1.4% quarterly decline, to KRW 38.6 billion, KRW 40.7 billion and KRW 40.8 billion across the first three quarters of 2026.

Operating profit rose from KRW 1.26 billion in the third quarter of 2025 and KRW 1.24 billion in the fourth quarter to KRW 1.84 billion in the first quarter of 2026 and KRW 3.18 billion in the second quarter, then held at KRW 3.11 billion in the third quarter, up 147.4% from a year earlier.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders, however, went from KRW 3.2 billion in the second quarter of 2026 to KRW 291 million in the third quarter, a 90.9% quarterly drop, diverging from the operating profit trend.

The sum of net income attributable to controlling shareholders over the last four quarters, from the fourth quarter of 2025 through the third quarter of 2026, was KRW 7.36 billion.

The gap between flat operating profit and the sharp drop in net income points to non-operating items and taxes, which the next quarterly report should clarify.

Balance-sheet leverage stood at 33.5% in 2022, 22.9% in 2024 and 29.1% in 2025, with total liabilities of KRW 45.3 billion and total equity of KRW 155.7 billion at the end of 2025. Operating cash flow was KRW 41.6 billion in 2022, then KRW 9.35 billion in 2023, KRW 11.5 billion in 2024 and KRW 14.1 billion in 2025.

05

Industry analysis

Tapex's end demand spans secondary batteries and automobiles. The data provider notes that battery demand is expected to expand in Europe and North America, and the company is establishing positions there to build a medium- to long-term base.

Tapes for batteries are materials that enter the battery makers' supply chains, so production capacity and delivery records largely determine volumes. That is why securing new sites and capacity early is cited as a driver of 2026 sales growth.

A sharp swing in end demand, however, could also move electronic materials sales. In consumer goods, food-packaging wrap is a relatively mature market, and the company is keeping its number one position in commercial-use wrap in Korea while shifting its product mix toward higher-function items.

Many listed chemical companies focus on petrochemicals or large-scale materials, so Tapex sits as a comparatively small specialty materials company. Its position within the sector is better judged by which products it supplies to battery supply chains than by scale alone.

08

Bull factors

Expanding electronic materials supply

The electronic materials unit has secured new sites and capacity in advance and is widening secondary-battery tape supply to global battery and automotive makers. A data provider put first-quarter 2026 sales up 24.3% from a year earlier.

Electronic materials and secondary-battery tapes account for 55.9% of the product mix, which shows the weight of this unit.

Operating profit recovery

Operating profit rose 75.6% to KRW 2.51 billion in 2025. It reached KRW 3.18 billion in the second quarter of 2026 and KRW 3.11 billion in the third quarter, the latter up 147.4% from a year earlier. The operating margin rebounded from 1.1% in 2024 to 1.8% in 2025, moving in the same direction.

Financial headroom

The debt ratio was 29.1% at the end of 2025, with total liabilities of KRW 45.3 billion and total equity of KRW 155.7 billion. Operating cash flow of KRW 14.1 billion in 2025 was the highest of the three years since 2023.

With a light debt load, the company has room to continue electronic materials capacity investment, which is the bull case here.

09

Bear factors

Long-term earnings contraction

Operating profit fell from KRW 25.5 billion in 2022 to KRW 1.43 billion in 2024. The operating margin declined over the same span from 14.0% to 1.1%. Net income attributable to controlling shareholders fell for three consecutive years from 2023 through 2025, so earnings power before the recovery shrank considerably.

Net income and financing cost pressure

In 2025 operating profit rose, yet higher financial costs pushed net income down 43.7% from a year earlier. Net income attributable to controlling shareholders fell to KRW 291 million in the third quarter of 2026, down 90.9% from the prior quarter and 85.6% from a year earlier. The divergence from operating profit has to be traced to non-operating items.

Demand concentration and limited consumer growth

Because electronic materials account for a large share, results are sensitive to swings in battery and automotive demand. Expanding battery demand in Europe and North America is presented as an expectation rather than a realized figure. Food-packaging wrap operates in a mature market and may contribute only limited growth.

10

Risk factors

Customer and industry concentration

A large part of sales depends on supply to global battery and automotive makers, so changes in customer production plans or supply-chain restructuring can translate directly into volume swings. Earnings concentrated in a few product lines leave less cushion if demand for one customer or product weakens. Until new capacity is fully running, demand growth cannot be fully converted into sales.

Financing costs and earnings volatility

In 2025, higher financial costs held back the translation of the operating profit recovery into net income. In the third quarter of 2026 operating profit held at KRW 3.11 billion, yet net income attributable to controlling shareholders fell to KRW 291 million. At a company with small absolute earnings, a single non-operating item can swing net income sharply.

Durability of the recovery

Operating profit climbed from KRW 1.26 billion in the third quarter of 2025 to KRW 3.18 billion in the second quarter of 2026 and held at KRW 3.11 billion in the third quarter. Quarterly swings remain large, so two or three quarters are too few to call a trend. Whether this level persists after the third quarter is the benchmark for the durability of the recovery.

Overall view

Tapex began as an adhesive tape maker and has built electronic materials tapes into its core business. Annual operating profit fell from KRW 25.5 billion in 2022 to KRW 1.43 billion in 2024, and net income attributable to controlling shareholders declined for three consecutive years from 2023 through 2025.

Operating profit rose 75.6% in 2025, but higher financial costs pulled net income lower. Sales rose for three straight quarters in 2026 and operating profit reached the KRW 3 billion range in the second and third quarters.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders, however, fell to KRW 291 million in the third quarter, leaving open questions about the quality and durability of earnings. With a debt ratio of 29.1%, the financial burden is light.

The share price trades at a discount to book value, and the dividend yield is below the sector average. The next checkpoints are the operating margin and non-operating items in the 2026 annual report, and whether operating profit holds at the second- and third-quarter 2026 level in the first quarter of 2027.
