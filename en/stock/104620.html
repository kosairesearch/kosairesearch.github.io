Report overview

Lower Sales, Cost Cuts Keep Operating Profit in the Black

Business structure

Yellow Balloon Tour operates mainly as a direct-selling travel agency that sells its own overseas package tours. According to a April 2025 report, about 94% of consolidated revenue came from the traditional package travel business, while platform and overseas subsidiary revenue remained a small share.

By region, short-haul routes to Japan and Southeast Asia carry the largest weight, and long-haul routes such as Europe keep a steady share driven by purpose-based travel demand.

Based on company data, Japan accounted for about 36.5% of Lunar New Year 2026 holiday bookings, Southeast Asia about 30.6%, Europe about 10.3% and Oceania about 6.1%.

The customer base is divided into active seniors, family travelers and the 2030 generation, and the company launched the premium package brand TOP PICK in July 2024.

Cumulative ridership on its Seoul city tour bus, Yellow Balloon City Bus, rose about 30% year on year in 2026, with the share of foreign passengers widening.

In competition, Hanatour, Modetour and Lotte Tour Development have strengthened premium packages, while RedCap Tour has built non-travel income from car rental and event services and Modetour has expanded hotel operations and real estate.

Yellow Balloon pursued a platform-led travel model in 2024, including the acquisition of Wishbin and the setup of a Japan subsidiary, but revenue from those new businesses remains small. The business model therefore depends heavily on package sales volume and securing airline seats.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual revenue rose from KRW 22 billion in 2022 to KRW 98.6 billion in 2023 (up 348.1% year on year) and KRW 131.9 billion in 2024 (up 33.8%), then fell to KRW 119.7 billion in 2025 (down 9.3%). Of the years from 2022 to 2025, 2024 was the largest by revenue.

Operating profit moved from an operating loss of KRW 20.9 billion in 2022 to operating profit of KRW 6.62 billion in 2023, which was a turnaround to profit. It then swung to an operating loss of KRW 6.55 billion in 2024 and back to operating profit of KRW 2.24 billion in 2025.

The operating margin was -95.0% in 2022, 6.7% in 2023, -5.0% in 2024 and 1.9% in 2025, and the slide into loss in 2024 came in a year when revenue grew 33.8%.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 5.92 billion in 2023, then a loss of KRW 4.74 billion in 2024, and a loss of KRW 2.81 billion in 2025, a narrower loss but still a loss.

Operating profit was positive in 2025 while the controlling-shareholder result was still a loss, so the non-operating items behind that gap need to be checked. Operating cash flow went from -KRW 21.55 million in 2022 to KRW 3.02 billion in 2023, KRW 18.5 billion in 2024 and KRW 6.81 billion in 2025, remaining positive.

On a quarterly basis, the company posted an operating loss of KRW 2.18 billion in the third quarter of 2025, then returned to operating profit of KRW 4.1 billion in the fourth quarter as revenue rose 55.5% quarter on quarter to KRW 38.9 billion.

Operating profit then fell 41.9% to KRW 2.38 billion in the first quarter of 2026, 67.5% to KRW 773 million in the second, and 37.1% to KRW 486 million in the third, which marks three consecutive quarterly declines after the fourth-quarter 2025 peak.

According to the company, third-quarter 2026 operating costs were KRW 20.18 billion, down 25.8% year on year, a steeper drop than the 17.4% fall in sales. The third-quarter gain therefore came from cost reduction rather than a sales recovery, since revenue remained below the KRW 25 billion of the third quarter of 2025.

05

Industry analysis

Package travel conditions remain volatile, with exchange rates, fuel surcharges and Middle East tensions all moving demand. Results among four major Korean package travel operators diverged in the third quarter of 2026.

According to a Money Today report dated November 17, 2026, Hanatour saw both sales and operating profit decline, Very Good Tour's operating profit fell sharply, Modetour narrowed its loss, and Yellow Balloon returned to operating profit.

On demand, the company said Japan accounted for 50.5% of its November–December winter package bookings, China bookings rose 28.5% year on year and Eastern Europe bookings rose 47.3%. That points to continued preference for short-haul routes alongside a recovery in long-haul demand.

A April 2025 report said Yellow Balloon was the only major listed travel operator to post an annual operating loss in 2024. In the same year, Hanatour recorded operating profit of KRW 50.9 billion, Modetour KRW 4.9 billion, RedCap Tour KRW 43.6 billion and Very Good Tour KRW 2.1 billion.

Yellow Balloon's return to profit in 2026 has so far depended largely on cost management. Industry participants view premium packages as more profitable for agencies than standard products, and Hanatour, Modetour and Lotte Tour Development have all strengthened premium offerings. Yellow Balloon joined this trend with its TOP PICK launch in 2024.

06

Outlook

In its November 16, 2026 announcement, the company said it will keep adjusting airline seat supply to match travel demand, expand bookings through sales channels and continue improving product operations efficiency.

Demand signals the company highlights are the more than 25% rise in pre-bookings for the 2026 Lunar New Year holiday and the roughly 10% rise in winter bookings for November and December 2026.

Yellow Balloon City Bus has been refining multilingual guidance and themed routes to serve more foreign passengers, and plans to add experience-based routes for domestic travelers and link overseas and domestic tour products.

The premium TOP PICK line is being run to widen the product lineup for seniors, families and the 2030 generation. On the external side, Middle East geopolitical tension and higher fuel surcharges were reported to have raised travelers' cost burden in the third quarter of 2026.

Whether sales recover will be seen in bookings and departure revenue for the fourth quarter of 2026 and the first quarter of 2027. The next checkpoints are whether fourth-quarter 2026 revenue exceeds the KRW 20.7 billion of the third quarter and whether operating profit holds near the KRW 486 million level. Fourth-quarter 2026 figures will be confirmed in the 2026 annual report.

Bull factors

Improving earnings profile

Operating profit was positive in each quarter from the first to the third of 2026, and third-quarter 2026 operating costs fell 25.8% year on year. Cost reduction showing up in results is a supportive factor.

Booking demand recovery signals

According to the company, pre-bookings for overseas packages over the 2026 Lunar New Year holiday rose more than 25% year on year. Winter bookings for November and December 2026 rose about 10%, with China up 28.5% and Eastern Europe up 47.3%. Growth in long-haul bookings suggests the route mix is widening.

Easing financial burden

The debt ratio fell from 269.1% in 2024 to 210.6% in 2025, and total equity was KRW 40.3 billion at end-2025. Operating cash flow stayed positive at KRW 18.5 billion in 2024 and KRW 6.81 billion in 2025. The easing of financial burden offers a buffer against earnings swings.

Bear factors

Continued sales decline

Third-quarter 2026 sales were KRW 20.7 billion, down 17.4% year on year. Second-quarter 2026 sales of KRW 19.3 billion fell 34.8% quarter on quarter. Profitability relies heavily on cost reduction while the revenue base is shrinking, which is the main bearish point.

Thin earnings base and continuity

Controlling-shareholder net losses were posted in both 2024 and 2025, with a KRW 2.81 billion loss in 2025. Operating profit has declined for three consecutive quarters since the fourth-quarter 2025 peak.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 4.82 billion over the last four quarters indicates a still thin earnings base.

External shocks and peer competition

In the third quarter of 2026, a weaker won, higher fuel surcharges and Middle East tensions weighed on the sector, and Hanatour's sales and operating profit both fell in the same quarter. A 2025 report said Yellow Balloon was the only major listed travel operator with an annual operating loss in 2024. Whether it has enough buffer if conditions worsen is an open question.

Risk factors

External demand, FX and fuel

Airfares, fuel surcharges, a weaker won and Middle East tensions all shift both demand and cost for overseas packages. In the third quarter of 2026 the company responded by adjusting airline seat supply, but if seat costs rise faster than selling prices, margins could thin again. A 1.9% operating margin in 2025 leaves little room for cost swings.

Financial leverage

Total liabilities at end-2025 were KRW 84.8 billion, above total equity of KRW 40.3 billion, and the debt ratio was 210.6%. The ratio had climbed to 269.1% in 2024, so a renewed earnings decline could quickly raise financial strain. The controlling-shareholder stake was about KRW 40 billion.

Concentration and fixed costs

According to an April 2025 report, about 94% of consolidated revenue came from the package business, so dependence on one business is high. The same report said salaries of KRW 26.6 billion and advertising costs of KRW 11.2 billion in 2024 added fixed-cost pressure. If the platform business is slow to monetize, that cost base could weigh on results again.

Overall view

Yellow Balloon Tour posted third-quarter 2026 sales of KRW 20.7 billion, down 17.4% year on year, yet cut operating costs 25.8% to produce operating profit of KRW 486 million.

Operating profit was positive in each quarter from the first to the third of 2026, although the amount declined from KRW 2.38 billion to KRW 486 million over that span.

On the annual disclosed record, the company moved from an operating loss in 2022 to a profit in 2023, a loss in 2024 and a profit in 2025, while controlling-shareholder losses ran through 2024 and 2025. The debt ratio improved from 269.1% in 2024 to 210.6% in 2025 but total liabilities still exceed total equity.

Bullish factors are cost improvement, recovery signals in holiday and winter bookings and rising ridership on Yellow Balloon City Bus. Bearish factors are shrinking sales, a thin controlling-shareholder earnings base and external shocks from exchange rates, fuel surcharges and Middle East tensions.

Valuation reads depend on the KRW 4.82 billion of net income attributable to controlling shareholders over the last four quarters, and because market capitalization is above the controlling-shareholder equity stake at end-2025, the read is sensitive to earnings durability.
