GS Global: Core Trading Margins Under Pressure, New Businesses Still in the Red

02

Business structure

GS Global is a general trading company founded in 1954 and brought into the GS Group in 2009, and its trading and distribution segment, centered on steel, petrochemicals and energy distribution, sits at the core of the business.

Trading and distribution accounted for 96.5% of consolidated revenue in the first half of 2026, according to Dealsite. The company says it is focused on strengthening its supply chain and expanding eco-friendly businesses across 25 overseas offices, per its WiseReport company profile.

Its steel trading is built around Chinese sourcing from hubs in Dalian and Shanghai, and in Indonesia it has focused on steel and coal since entering the market in 1968, according to its IR network pages.

Its manufacturing and new businesses include an offshore-wind substructure subsidiary, PDI and logistics for imported cars and special-purpose vehicles, e-mobility, recycling, healthcare, solar and advanced materials.

The e-mobility business began in 2020 with a commercial vehicle distribution agreement with BYD, and after introducing the 1-ton electric truck T4K in 2023 it sold about 200 units in its first year against a 3,000-unit annual target, according to Dealsite and the company history.

In March 2026 the electric truck distribution rights moved to BYD's Korean unit, per Dealsite. New-business revenue was KRW 42.5 billion in the first half of 2026, down 41.4% from a year earlier and equal to 1.9% of total revenue, per Dealsite.

In the first quarter of 2026 the manufacturing segment's revenue fell 87% year on year and it swung to an operating loss of KRW 3.9 billion, which the report linked to price competition from low-cost producers in China and elsewhere, according to 1conomy News. Among listed trading companies, it is typically compared with POSCO International and LX International.

04

Earnings analysis

On a DART-filed basis, annual revenue fell from KRW 5.07 trillion in 2022 to KRW 3.92 trillion in 2023, a 22.8% decline that marked the low point, before rising 3.8% to KRW 4.07 trillion in 2024 and 1.1% to KRW 4.11 trillion in 2025.

Operating profit went from KRW 70.5 billion in 2022 to KRW 76.5 billion in 2023 and KRW 77.9 billion in 2024, the largest year in the period, before dropping 32.8% to KRW 52.3 billion in 2025.

Revenue grew while profit shrank because the margin got thinner, with the operating margin at 1.4%, 2.0%, 1.9% and 1.3% across 2022 to 2025.

According to Dealsite, trading and distribution revenue fell 20.8% from KRW 4.82 trillion in 2022 to KRW 3.82 trillion in 2025, while its operating profit fell 43.7% from KRW 87.3 billion to KRW 49.1 billion, so profit fell more than twice as fast as sales.

New businesses swung from an operating profit of KRW 4.8 billion in 2023 to an operating loss of KRW 16.9 billion in 2024, according to Dealsite.

Profit attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 70.2 billion in 2022, KRW 27.8 billion in 2023, KRW 54.2 billion in 2024 and KRW 19.9 billion in 2025, the smallest year in the period.

In the fourth quarter of 2025 revenue reached KRW 1.07 trillion and operating profit rose to KRW 10.1 billion, but the company posted a controlling-shareholder net loss of KRW 902 million.

In the first quarter of 2026 it returned to a KRW 5.3 billion profit, with revenue of KRW 1.1 trillion and operating profit of KRW 12.9 billion.

Second-quarter revenue was KRW 1.13 trillion with operating profit of KRW 11.7 billion, down 8.8% from the prior quarter, and third-quarter revenue was KRW 1.08 trillion with operating profit of KRW 3.94 billion, down 39.0% year on year.

Cumulative revenue for January to September 2026 was KRW 3.31 trillion, up 8.9%, according to a Digital Today report on the third-quarter filing. The four quarters from Q4 2025 to Q3 2026 together produced KRW 18.6 billion in profit attributable to controlling shareholders.

05

Industry analysis

Steel is the core item in the trading and distribution segment, which accounts for more than half of its sales, and Dealsite reports that the steel cycle is weak because of oversupply coming out of China.

The same report notes that POSCO's steel division operating profit is expected to fall 32.5% to about KRW 1.2 trillion in 2026. Energy and petrochemical markets are also difficult, so steel and petrochemicals are weak at the same time.

Trading businesses carry structural limits, with large earnings swings and thin margins tied to economic cycles and raw-material prices, according to Dealsite.

Macro uncertainty also persists, as US-China tensions, rising protectionism, and instability in the Middle East and Ukraine have sustained uncertainty over global demand, per WiseReport's comment as of late August 2026.

On the other side, DS Investment's late-May 2026 report expected the trading division's operating profit to rise 29% from a year earlier, citing higher selling prices from the Middle East conflict and a focus on higher-margin items. That view reads as the bullish case for trading margins.

Compared with POSCO International and LX International in the same trading sector, GS Global is exposed to losses from both manufacturing and new businesses.

06

Outlook

New businesses are at a turning point. Electric truck distribution rights moved to BYD's Korean unit in March 2026, changing the sales structure of e-mobility, and the company has attributed new-business losses to upfront costs in the early stage of those businesses, according to Dealsite.

The offshore-wind subsidiary is shifting from plant-equipment projects toward monopile substructures, per Dealsite.

Dealsite reports that its order backlog fell from about KRW 300 billion in 2023 to about KRW 1 billion by the end of the first half of 2026, while production capacity was expanded from 40,000 tons to 150,000 tons.

If orders do not keep pace with capacity, fixed costs such as depreciation and labor can weigh on profitability. Korea Ratings expects order uncertainty in manufacturing and continued losses in new businesses to put downward pressure on the group's earning power, according to a Dealsite report from October 2026.

Government tightening of subsidy criteria for Chinese electric buses is another policy variable for the new businesses, per Dealsite. The CEO bought 40,000 treasury shares, a move reported in May 2026 by 1conomy News. Fourth-quarter 2026 results will be fixed in the 2026 annual report.

Overall view

GS Global's revenue rose in 2024 and 2025 after its 2023 low, but its operating margin of 1.3% in 2025 was the lowest in the disclosed period. Operating profit peaked at KRW 12.9 billion in Q1 2026 and then declined in Q2 and Q3.

With trading and distribution at 96.5% of revenue, weak steel and petrochemical markets are pressing on margins. New businesses have accumulated operating losses of KRW 23.8 billion since 2024, and manufacturing carries fixed-cost pressure from weaker orders against expanded capacity.

On the other side, the debt ratio fell from 249.0% in 2022 to 154.6% in 2025, and operating cash flow turned to KRW 64.9 billion in 2025. Profit attributable to controlling shareholders returned to a profit in Q1 2026, then reached KRW 9.66 billion in Q2 and KRW 4.56 billion in Q3.

The next checkpoints are fourth-quarter 2026 results, fixed in the 2026 annual report, and the operating margin in the Q1 2027 quarterly report.
