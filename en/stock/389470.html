02

Business structure

Inventage Lab is a drug delivery system (DDS) platform company founded in 2015, built around the IVL-DrugFluidic long-acting injectable platform, the IVL-BioFluidic subcutaneous formulation platform, and the IVL-GeneFluidic lipid nanoparticle (LNP) platform.

Its revenue relies heavily on technology transfer: according to the Butler company page, tech transfer accounted for 98% of revenue in the second quarter of 2026, with other sales at 1% and service sales at 1%.

In 2025 the company took management control of Curatis to add GMP manufacturing capacity, a transaction that a February 2025 report said involved KRW 25 billion of investment for roughly 40% of Curatis shares. FnGuide's company summary states that this GMP site is being used to expand clinical supply and CDMO work.

In the pipeline, the multiple sclerosis candidate IVL4002 received development-stage orphan drug designation from the Ministry of Food and Drug Safety in 2022 and completed an Australian Phase 1 study, as reported on November 23, 2026.

The addiction candidate IVL3004 completed Phase 1 in Australia, as reported on November 2, 2026, and a U.S. Phase 2 is being discussed with the FDA. A monthly long-acting injectable for Alzheimer's, IVL3003, is also in development.

Long-acting injectables attract continued investment from global pharmaceutical companies, and listed peers such as Alteogen in subcutaneous formulation technology and Samsung Biologics in large-scale CDMO offer reference points.

Because Inventage Lab depends more on formulation licensing and co-development income than on selling its own products, the number of partners and the terms of each deal largely determine revenue swings.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual revenue in the DART periodic reports was KRW 3.72 billion in 2022, KRW 658 million in 2023 (down 82.3% year on year), KRW 1.78 billion in 2024 (up 170.9%), and KRW 2.94 billion in 2025 (up 64.8%).

The largest revenue year in 2022 to 2025 was 2022 and the smallest was 2023, and revenue has risen for two consecutive years since the 2023 low, from 2024 through 2025.

Operating losses were KRW 10.9 billion in 2022, KRW 16 billion in 2023, KRW 19 billion in 2024, and KRW 28.4 billion in 2025, a four-year run of losses with the deficit widening each year.

The operating margin moved from -292.8% in 2022 to -2,425.1% in 2023, then -1,063.6% in 2024 and -965.7% in 2025, and these ratios swing sharply because the revenue base is small.

The company attributed the 2025 revenue increase to the consolidation of a newly included subsidiary, and the larger operating loss to higher R&D and clinical spending together with subsidiary costs, according to its March 2026 disclosure.

The net loss attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 10.6 billion in 2022, KRW 27 billion in 2023, KRW 17 billion in 2024, and KRW 8.46 billion in 2025, a four-year run of losses that narrowed from 2024.

The total net loss including non-controlling interests was KRW 15.9 billion in 2025, and the gap from the controlling-shareholder figure reflects losses assigned to non-controlling interests.

At the end of 2025, total equity was KRW 80.9 billion, of which KRW 64.5 billion belonged to controlling shareholders, and total liabilities were KRW 29.8 billion, with the debt ratio falling from 924.4% in 2023 to 36.8% in 2025.

Operating cash outflow grew each year, from KRW 5.19 billion in 2022 to KRW 20.7 billion in 2025.

Quarterly revenue was KRW 832 million in the third quarter of 2025, KRW 1.07 billion in the fourth quarter (up 28.8% from the prior quarter), KRW 393 million in the first quarter of 2026, KRW 176 million in the second quarter, and KRW 437 million in the third quarter.

Operating losses ran for five consecutive quarters, from KRW 7.43 billion in the third quarter of 2025 to KRW 10.3 billion in the third quarter of 2026.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders turned positive at KRW 1.05 billion in the fourth quarter of 2025 and KRW 2.96 billion in the first quarter of 2026, then returned to losses of KRW 2.35 billion in the second quarter and KRW 5.7 billion in the third quarter of 2026.

The sum of the last four quarters' controlling-shareholder net loss is KRW 4.05 billion, and because profit appeared alongside operating losses, the drivers of those quarters should be checked in the periodic report notes.

05

Industry analysis

The long-acting injectable market is expanding as demand grows for fewer dosing visits, and global pharmaceutical companies participate mainly through licensing and co-development agreements.

Inventage Lab's announced long-acting injectable collaboration with Boehringer Ingelheim in September 2025 came alongside a series of fundraising rounds, which fits this pattern.

In obesity treatment, semaglutide- and tirzepatide-based injectables have expanded the market, which places monthly formulation technology in the spotlight as an alternative.

The company presented its LAI platform results at the American Diabetes Association meeting in May 2026, and commentary suggests this reflects a shift in the industry from single-drug development toward drug delivery platforms.

On competition, Alteogen leads in subcutaneous formulation and Samsung Biologics leads in scale and results for contract manufacturing, so Inventage Lab is positioning itself through its formulation platform and mRNA-LNP manufacturing technology.

At the CRS 2026 meeting in July 2026, the company reported LNP mass-production yields of up to 89%, but these are company-reported figures and have not been independently verified. Domestic mRNA and LNP production has relied heavily on overseas equipment, so localization is cited as an industry-level significance.

Large pharmaceutical companies and established CDMO providers already hold strong positions in LAI and LNP, so a smaller platform company may face disadvantages in competing for licensing agreements.

06

Outlook

The most concrete pipeline milestone the company has disclosed is the start of a Phase 2 study of IVL4002 in 2027.

As reported on November 23, 2026, the company is preparing a Phase 2 study in relapsing multiple sclerosis patients to find the right dose and regimen and assess efficacy and safety, and it said that co-development and licensing talks with U.S. pharmaceutical companies are under way.

IVL3004 moved toward late-stage global clinical development after the company was selected for the Global TIPS program in August 2026, and its U.S. Phase 2 will proceed after consultation with the FDA.

The company stated that of the KRW 98.5 billion raised in March 2026, KRW 25 billion will go to facility funding and KRW 73.5 billion to operating funds.

The facility funding is for building commercial production capacity for long-acting injectables at Curatis's Osong bioplant in Chungbuk, which the company describes as a production base needed to expand cooperation with global pharmaceutical companies.

Results for the fourth quarter of 2026 will be covered in the 2026 annual report, which is due by the end of March 2027. The company also plans to connect its mRNA-LNP mass-production platform to commercial manufacturing, but it has not specified when contracts will be signed or when revenue will follow.

The next checkpoints are therefore whether the IVL4002 Phase 2 starts, the outcome of FDA consultations on IVL3004, and whether a technology transfer contract is signed along with its upfront payment and milestone terms. Only after these are settled can the effect on the revenue structure be confirmed through reported results.

Bull factors

Pipeline programs reaching clinical milestones

IVL4002 received orphan drug designation in 2022, and its Australian Phase 1 measured drug levels in blood up to 28 days after dosing, as reported on November 23, 2026. Preclinical results were accepted by the journal Theranostics, which has an impact factor of 14.9, and the company targets a Phase 2 start in 2027. IVL3004 also completed its Australian Phase 1, according to a November 2, 2026 report.

KRW 98.5 billion raised and production capacity expansion

According to a March 2026 disclosure, the company raised KRW 98.5 billion in total, comprising KRW 37.3 billion in convertible bonds, KRW 8.5 billion in bonds with warrants, and KRW 52.7 billion in third-party convertible preferred shares, above its KRW 80 billion target.

KRW 25 billion of the proceeds is earmarked for facilities to build commercial long-acting injectable production at the Osong plant. Overseas investors also took part, widening the funding base.

Technology validation and foreign investor demand

Company data presented at CRS 2026 in July 2026 showed mRNA-LNP mass-production yields of up to 89% and consistent particle characteristics from research to commercial scale. A November 26, 2026 report by Newspim said foreign investors had net bought shares for eight consecutive sessions, based on provisional data.

Controlling-shareholder profit turned positive in the fourth quarter of 2025 and the first quarter of 2026, showing that quarterly profitability is possible.

Bear factors

Operating losses keep widening

Operating losses widened from KRW 10.9 billion in 2022 to KRW 28.4 billion in 2025, a four-year run of losses. The operating margin stood at -965.7% in 2025, reflecting heavy cost relative to revenue. The operating loss of KRW 10.3 billion in the third quarter of 2026 extended the losing streak to five quarters.

Revenue volatility and concentration in tech transfer

Quarterly revenue fell from KRW 1.07 billion in the fourth quarter of 2025 to KRW 176 million in the second quarter of 2026, then recovered to KRW 437 million in the third quarter, which was still down 47.5% from a year earlier.

Tech transfer accounted for 98% of revenue in the second quarter of 2026 according to Butler, so the timing of contracts drives sharp swings. Annual revenue in 2025 reached only KRW 2.94 billion.

Growing operating cash outflow

Operating cash outflow expanded every year, from KRW 5.19 billion in 2022 to KRW 20.7 billion in 2025. The last four quarters show a combined controlling-shareholder net loss of KRW 4.05 billion, but that figure is cushioned by profitable quarters, while cash outflow ran far larger. If this pattern continues, the company's reliance on external financing will remain high.

Risk factors

Clinical and technology development

The 2027 Phase 2 start for IVL4002 and the design of IVL3004's U.S. Phase 2 are not yet final. If Phase 2 results fall short, licensing negotiations and the valuation of the pipeline could both be affected. Current clinical data are limited to an Australian Phase 1 and animal model results.

Governance and capital raising

If the convertible bonds, bonds with warrants, and convertible preferred shares issued in March 2026 are converted or exercised, the number of outstanding shares will rise. The largest shareholder group held 13.54% as of September 2026. Repeated fundraising could keep questions about dilution and the priority of fund use in focus.

Capacity expansion and GMP approval

The KRW 25 billion facility plan to build commercial long-acting injectable production at the Osong plant could be delayed by GMP approval steps and equipment start-up timing. A slower commercial line could also push back production agreements with global pharmaceutical companies. The company has not publicly specified when the facility will start operating.

Overall view

Inventage Lab has operated at a loss for four consecutive years from 2022 to 2025, and its operating loss widened to KRW 28.4 billion in 2025. Revenue rose in 2024 and 2025 but reached only KRW 2.94 billion, which is not enough to offset the losses.

Quarterly revenue in 2026 ranged between KRW 176 million and KRW 437 million, and technology transfer makes up most of it. The March 2026 fundraising of KRW 98.5 billion has added financial capacity, and the pipeline includes the completed IVL4002 Phase 1 with a 2027 Phase 2 target and an IVL3004 U.S.

Phase 2 under FDA consultation. Operating cash outflow still grew to KRW 20.7 billion in 2025, which keeps reliance on external financing high. Valuation can only be described in relation to net assets, since earnings-based multiples are not meaningful right now.

The next checkpoints are the operating margin in the 2026 annual report and whether the IVL4002 Phase 2 starts, and these two items will show whether the current loss structure is improving.
