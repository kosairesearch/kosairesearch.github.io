Report overview

Microbit growth shapes NEO TECHNICAL SYSTEM CO's earnings mix

NEO TECHNICAL SYSTEM CO's microbit business for PCB drilling is driving growth in nine-month operating profit, while its automotive shaft business is shrinking as auto demand slows, shifting the center of gravity toward microbits.

02

Business structure

NEO TECHNICAL SYSTEM CO was founded in 2000 as a domestic microbit maker that led the localization of ultra-precision drill bits for PCB processing. It listed on KOSDAQ in October 2007 and operates three main lines: microbit tools, automotive shafts and lens grinders.

Based on a domestic brokerage's 2025 results table, segment revenue was about KRW 23 billion for microbits, KRW 25.1 billion for shafts and KRW 19.9 billion for lens grinders, making shafts the largest line in 2025.

Microbit products, mainly Longflute drill bits and the DLC-coated K1 model, serve high-layer-count PCBs, AI accelerator and network boards, and semiconductor packaging such as FC-BGA and FC-CSP, and the company says it keeps a share close to the top of the domestic market.

Overseas rivals named in coverage include UNION Tool of Japan, JINZHOU of China and TOPPOINT of Taiwan. Shafts are gear shafts for automotive power window motors and reach automakers through global parts makers such as Bosch, Mitsuba and Nidec.

Press reports say the company is the sole supplier of shafts for Hyundai Motor's electric vehicles. Its lens grinder business, run through a subsidiary, is linked to the expansion of autonomous driving. Press coverage also reports that microbit products reach SpaceX through domestic customers.

In the first quarter of 2026, microbit revenue was about KRW 8.1 billion, above shaft revenue of about KRW 5.3 billion.

04

Earnings analysis

On the disclosed annual figures, revenue fell from KRW 80.4 billion in 2022 to KRW 58.5 billion in 2023, a 27.2% decline, then to KRW 54.3 billion in 2024, down 7.2%, before rising to KRW 68.7 billion in 2025, up 26.5%. Among 2022 to 2025, 2022 was the largest year and 2024 the smallest.

Operating profit was KRW 11.7 billion in 2022, with a 14.5% margin, turned to an operating loss of KRW 742 million in 2023, and returned to a profit of KRW 3.18 billion in 2024. Operating profit then reached KRW 7.78 billion in 2025, up 144.8%, with an operating margin of 11.3%.

The disclosure cites higher microbit demand, a recovery in lens grinder revenue and gains from a high exchange rate as the main drivers of the 2025 improvement.

Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 5.16 billion in 2022, a net loss of KRW 3.05 billion in 2023, KRW 4.85 billion in 2024 and KRW 4.25 billion in 2025, down 12.5%, so the controlling shareholders' share fell in 2025 even as operating profit rose.

Net profit including non-controlling interests was KRW 6.27 billion in 2025, a different figure from the controlling-shareholder number. The debt-to-equity ratio rose from 66.5% in 2024 to 82.5% in 2025, with total liabilities of KRW 58.1 billion and total equity of KRW 70.4 billion at year-end 2025.

Quarterly operating profit climbed from KRW 2.64 billion in the third quarter of 2025 to KRW 4.49 billion in the second quarter of 2026, up 96.1% from the prior quarter.

Third-quarter 2026 revenue was KRW 21.8 billion, down 3.6% from the prior quarter, and operating profit was KRW 4 billion, down 10.9% from the prior quarter.

Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders in that quarter was KRW 709 million, down 78.0% from the prior quarter, so it moved in the opposite direction to operating profit. Over the last four quarters, net profit attributable to controlling shareholders totaled KRW 8.14 billion.

According to the company's announcement, the cumulative operating margin for the first nine months of 2026 was 17.3%, up 6.3 percentage points from 11.0% a year earlier. The reason for the gap between operating profit and controlling-shareholder profit is a checkpoint for the next regular report's income statement.

05

Industry analysis

Demand from downstream markets runs in two directions. The company says that expansion of AI chips and AI servers is lifting demand for high-layer, high-density PCBs, and with it demand for the precision drill tools that machine them.

In autos, by contrast, the downturn in end markets is clear, with third-quarter 2026 shaft revenue down 22.5% year on year.

A 2025 press report described order gains from a rebound in overseas auto markets, but the company said in its first-quarter 2026 release that partial customer volume adjustments and industry conditions weighed on shafts and the subsidiary segment.

In the company's framing, automotive parts sit in a downturn phase while microbits are growing on AI equipment investment. In competition, microbits hold a share close to the top of the domestic market, but abroad they compete with UNION Tool of Japan, JINZHOU of China and TOPPOINT of Taiwan.

The company cites its proprietary coating technology and ultra-precision machining know-how as the basis for share gains. In a 2025 third-quarter results note, a securities analysis described the company's operating margin as relatively high against peers.

Compared with other listed auto-parts companies, the notable structural difference is that in the first quarter of 2026 microbit revenue exceeded shaft revenue.

06

Outlook

In its first-quarter 2026 release, the company said that existing capacity additions would begin feeding into revenue from the second quarter and that microbit growth would become clearer.

At its second-quarter results on August 13, 2026, the company said it would keep growing microbits in the second half while focusing on recovery in shafts and lens grinders.

Its November 16, 2026 announcement of cumulative third-quarter results set out plans to expand supply of higher-value microbit products and strengthen technological competitiveness.

On capacity, the company is building a new plant aimed at starting operations in the second half of 2027, and it says microbit supply capacity will expand once the plant is running.

The strategic investment disclosed in May 2026 took the form of convertible preferred shares worth about KRW 18 billion, convertible from June 6, 2027 to June 4, 2031. The newly issued shares are locked up for one year after issuance, and the terms include a 10% share sale right.

On the same day the company also disclosed a cancellation of 140,000 common shares, worth about KRW 3.9 billion. On the automotive side, press reports say electric motor controller work has moved into mass production, but the company has not set a timeline for the shaft recovery.

Over the longer term, the company has laid out a roadmap that combines precision shaft machining with motor control for robot actuators and humanoid robots. For now, the second-half 2027 plant start and whether convertible shares are converted from June 2027 are the next turning points for the earnings base.

08

Bull factors

Microbit demand expansion

According to the company's November 2026 announcement, third-quarter 2026 microbit revenue was about KRW 12.5 billion, up 149% year on year and 18.5% from the prior quarter. Growth has continued for three straight quarters in 2026, and January to September revenue reached about KRW 31.2 billion, up 91.5%. The company links demand for high-layer PCBs in AI chips and servers to demand for precision microbit tools.

Improved profitability

The rise in operating margin continued on a cumulative nine-month basis. The 2025 annual operating margin of 11.3% was also above 5.9% in 2024. Second-quarter 2026 operating profit of KRW 4.49 billion was up 96.1% from the prior quarter, lifting quarterly earnings power.

Technology and new business options

The company says it keeps a share close to the top of the domestic microbit market, and press coverage reports that its products reach SpaceX through domestic customers.

On the electric vehicle side, a 2025 press report said development of motor controller hardware and software for PBV modules was completed and mass production started. Reports of sole shaft supply to Hyundai Motor's electric vehicles point to a rising role for shafts as electrification advances.

09

Bear factors

Weak automotive shaft sales

Third-quarter 2026 shaft revenue fell 22.5% year on year, and the company attributed the decline to a slowdown in the automotive end market. Shafts were the largest segment in 2025 at about KRW 25.1 billion, so a fall in this line could limit the recovery in total revenue.

The company says it will focus on a shaft recovery in the second half, but it has not given a numerical timeline for it.

Volatility in controlling-shareholder profit

Third-quarter 2026 operating profit was KRW 4 billion, but net profit attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 709 million, down 56.8% year on year. The 2025 controlling-shareholder profit of KRW 4.25 billion was also down 12.5% from 2024.

Because operating profit gains are not yet flowing through to controlling shareholders, the reason for the gap is a point to check in the next regular report's income statement.

Financial burden and possible dilution

The debt-to-equity ratio rose from 66.5% in 2024 to 82.5% in 2025. Convertible preferred shares worth about KRW 18 billion can be converted from June 6, 2027, and the terms include a 10% share sale right.

If conversion occurs, existing shareholders' stakes could be diluted, while the new plant adds investment burden to the balance sheet.

10

Risk factors

Auto market cycle

The shaft business is tied directly to automaker production and the pace of electrification. The drop in third-quarter 2026 shaft revenue shows this exposure is already reaching the income statement.

The company presents electric motor controller work as a new business, but its revenue scale is not confirmed in disclosed figures. Further weakness in auto demand could delay the recovery in shafts and the subsidiary segment.

Customer concentration and AI cycle

Microbit growth depends on demand for high-layer PCBs used in AI chips and servers. If AI equipment spending slows or key customers adjust volumes, quarterly revenue could swing. The company mentioned partial customer volume adjustments in the first quarter of 2026. Technology and pricing competition with global rivals also remains a source of margin pressure.

Exchange rate and funding

The 2025 operating profit gain included a boost from a high exchange rate, according to the disclosure, so a less favorable currency move could reduce that contribution. The convertible preferred funding goes to capacity expansion, but conversion would bring dilution and shareholder-side burdens.

With the debt-to-equity ratio at 82.5% in 2025, the timing and method of funding new equipment investment matter.

12

Overall view

NEO TECHNICAL SYSTEM CO's disclosed results show a recovery path, from an operating loss and a controlling-shareholder net loss in 2023 to a profit in 2024 and a 144.8% rise in operating profit in 2025.

In 2026, the microbit business has lifted both revenue and profit, and cumulative operating profit for the first nine months rose about 93% year on year according to the company.

On the other side, shaft revenue fell in the third quarter on a weaker auto market, and controlling-shareholder net profit dropped to KRW 709 million even as operating profit rose.

The debt-to-equity ratio rising to 82.5% in 2025, the possible conversion of roughly KRW 18 billion in convertible preferred shares from June 2027, and the targeted second-half 2027 plant start are reasons to read earnings power and funding structure together.

The bullish case rests on demand for PCBs used in AI servers and on improved share and profitability in microbits, while the bearish case rests on weakness in automotive parts and the volatility of controlling-shareholder profit.

The next checkpoints are the confirmed full-year results in the 2026 annual report, first-quarter 2027 results, the start of conversion rights in June 2027, and whether the new plant starts in the second half of 2027.
