02

Business structure

FRTek is a telecom equipment specialist focused on developing and manufacturing mobile repeaters, including RF and optical repeaters, and has repeatedly supplied equipment to Korean carriers including KT.

In March 2026, the company signed a KELIS repeater supply contract with KT worth 3.379 billion won, equal to 11.4% of 2024 revenue, running from March 17 to June 8, 2026.

The company previously supplied 28GHz and 39GHz band 5G repeaters directly to Verizon, the largest US carrier, and operates a US subsidiary, FRTek US LLC, to support this business.

In the domestic repeater market, larger competitors such as SOLiD and private-network/fronthaul specialist HFR overshadow FRTek in scale, positioning it as a smaller mid-tier player.

The company began as a mobile repeater developer and has since expanded into broader wired and wireless communication solutions, and is known to hold technology recognized with Korea's IR52 Jang Young-sil Award.

Revenue is centered on domestic carrier supply contracts, with overseas (North American) sales still largely at a trial-supply stage. Earnings are closely tied to carriers' 5G/6G capital expenditure cycles and the direction of government network-upgrade policy.

04

Earnings analysis

After a loss-making 2022 (revenue of 18.6 billion won, operating loss of 4.49 billion won), the company recovered to 24.8 billion won in revenue in 2023 but still posted an operating loss of 501 million won, while net profit reached 2.29 billion won on the back of other gains.

In 2024, revenue grew to 29.8 billion won with an operating profit of 814 million won (2.7% margin), and in 2025, despite revenue falling 15.1% year over year to 25.3 billion won, operating profit actually rose to 920 million won (3.6% margin).

The company attributed this to cost efficiency measures and increased other income offsetting the revenue decline amid stagnant telecom demand. Owner net profit in 2025 reached 2.08 billion won, up sharply from 1.11 billion won the prior year, marking a clear earnings recovery.

Quarterly trends, however, have been uneven: revenue of 5.6 billion won and an operating loss of 159 million won in Q3 2025 improved to 6.32 billion won in revenue and 454 million won in operating profit in Q4 2025, but Q1 2026 revenue plunged to 4.08 billion won with an operating loss of 514 million won and a net loss of 375 million won, and Q2 2026 revenue fell further to 1.83 billion won with losses widening to an operating loss of 1.18 billion won and a net loss of 1.02 billion won.

As a result, the sum of owner net profit over the most recent four quarters (Q3 2025 through Q2 2026) was a loss of roughly 681 million won, showing that the 2025 profit recovery reversed again in the first half of 2026.

Separately, 2025 operating cash flow was negative 1.94 billion won despite the net profit, a divergence likely tied to working-capital swings in receivables and inventory linked to contract execution timing.

05

Industry analysis

The domestic mobile repeater market hinges on carriers' 5G coverage expansion and in-building solution investment, and recent stagnation in telecom capex was cited as the main reason for the 2025 revenue decline.

In 2026, however, expanding AI data center investment by global big tech firms and announcements around silicon photonics and optical communication technology drew thematic attention to small and mid-cap 6G and optical communication equipment names in Korea.

Reports also linked this attention to the launch of an industry alliance involving Samsung Electronics and SK Telecom following MWC 2026 in Spain.

Still, FRTek's scale is far smaller than SOLiD, which posts roughly 229.3 billion won in annual revenue, while HFR has built a separate position in private-network and fronthaul equipment.

Overseas, the company previously supplied test volumes to Verizon, though whether this progressed into full-volume orders requires further confirmation.

Ultimately, the industry cycle depends on when carriers resume capex, the pace of 6G standardization, and whether AI-data-center-driven demand for optical networks broadens, and as a relatively small player, FRTek's exposure to this cycle may differ from that of larger competitors.

06

Outlook

The company signed a KELIS repeater supply contract with KT in March 2026, running from March 17 to June 8, 2026, and whether follow-on contracts emerge after its expiration is a key variable for future revenue.

Given that both revenue and profit deteriorated again in H1 2026, new order disclosures and Q3 results in the second half will be the next indicators of whether the earlier improvement can resume.

On the industry side, 6G standardization discussions and rising AI-data-center-driven optical communication investment are cited as potentially favorable for equipment makers broadly, though whether this translates into actual orders for FRTek has not yet been confirmed.

The company has said it continues to pursue overseas revenue growth through its US subsidiary, but no specific recent overseas order disclosures have been identified. On shareholder returns, the company raised its 2025 year-end dividend, signaling an intent to strengthen shareholder policy.

Still, given high quarter-to-quarter earnings volatility and dependence on specific contracts, confirming the durability of any revenue recovery over the coming quarters will be important.

Bull factors

Profitability Improvement Through Cost Cuts

Despite a 15.1% revenue decline in 2025, operating profit rose, lifting the operating margin from 2.7% to 3.6%. Cost cuts and increased other income were cited as the main drivers. Maintaining operating profit in both 2024 and 2025 marks a departure from the loss structure of 2022-2023.

Repeat Order Relationship With Major Carriers

The company has repeatedly signed unit-price and supply contracts for repeaters with KT, including a new KELIS contract in March 2026. Such repeat transactional relationships can provide a comparatively stable revenue base relative to acquiring entirely new customers. A past supply relationship with Verizon is also cited as evidence of recognized technical capability.

Low Leverage and Exposure to the 6G/Optical Theme

The debt ratio stood at a low 21.4% at the end of 2025, providing some financial flexibility. The company also holds 5G/6G repeater and RF amplification technology, positioning it as a name associated with the AI-data-center-driven optical communication and 6G investment theme.

Bear factors

Renewed Earnings Deterioration in H1 2026

Both Q1 and Q2 2026 posted operating and net losses, with Q2 revenue falling to 1.83 billion won, well below the 2025 quarterly average. The 2025 profit recovery reversed again in H1 2026, leaving the sum of owner net profit over the trailing four quarters in a loss.

High Dependence on Specific Contracts

The KT KELIS contract signed in March 2026 was a short-term deal spanning roughly 2.7 months, from March 17 to June 8. Because large contracts tend to be one-off and short-term, there is a risk of a revenue gap once a contract ends, which may be linked to the sharp Q2 2026 revenue decline.

Theme-Driven Volatility and Market Alert History

The stock surged sharply on the 6G/AI data center theme in H1 2026 and was designated an investment alert stock. Such thematic swings can occur independently of underlying fundamentals, creating a persistent risk of divergence between share price and fundamentals.

Risk factors

Customer/Contract Concentration Risk

Revenue depends heavily on short-term supply contracts with a small number of carriers such as KT, so delayed renewals or reduced contract sizes can directly hit revenue. The sharp Q2 2026 revenue decline can be read as an illustration of the vulnerability inherent in this contract-dependent structure.

Cash Flow and Working Capital Risk

Despite a net profit in 2025, operating cash flow was negative at 1.94 billion won. This suggests working-capital burdens such as inventory or receivables may be widening the gap between when profit is recognized and when cash is actually received, warranting continued monitoring of cash flow trends.

Market Alert and Liquidity Risk

The stock was designated an investment alert stock in April 2026, and as a small-cap name, its trading volume and price can swing sharply during thematic phases. If unusual price spikes recur, additional market alert measures such as short-term overheating designation or trading halts cannot be ruled out.

Overall view

FRTek succeeded in improving its operating margin in 2025 through cost cuts despite a revenue decline, but the sustainability of that improvement came into question as both revenue and profit weakened again in H1 2026.

The company's business remains heavily dependent on repeat but largely short-term supply contracts with domestic carriers such as KT, leaving the potential revenue gap after contract expirations as a key variable for future earnings.

At the same time, the stock surged sharply in H1 2026 on a 6G/AI-data-center-driven optical communication theme and was designated an investment alert stock, illustrating a case where trading flows moved the price independently of underlying earnings.

Financially, the low debt ratio limits structural risk, but negative operating cash flow despite a net profit warrants attention to working capital management.

Going forward, Q3 earnings, new order disclosures, and the broader spread of 6G and optical communication investment across the industry are likely to be key variables in gauging the durability of any earnings recovery.
