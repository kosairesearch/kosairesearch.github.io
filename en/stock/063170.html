02

Business structure

Seoul Auction, founded in 1998, is a leading domestic art auction house whose business centers on auctions and also includes art sales, brokerage and art-backed lending.

A Hankyung analysis of the third-quarter 2026 quarterly report shows revenue mix shifting toward auction commissions, which rose 11.4% year on year to KRW 1.86 billion. Product sales fell 50.1% to KRW 1.09 billion, and brokerage revenue from private sales dropped 23.6% to KRW 769 million.

As lower-margin product sales shrank, the cost of sales ratio fell from 81% a year earlier to 61.2%, according to the same analysis. Of the KRW 13.1 billion in third-quarter hammer price, online auctions accounted for KRW 2.2 billion and offline auctions for KRW 10.9 billion.

Online hammer price roughly doubled year on year, and online lots offered rose from 969 to 1,559. Clients are domestic and overseas collectors, and in March 2026 the company paired a large offline sale with a Hong Kong preview to attract overseas bidders.

The company is also designated by the National Tax Service as a specialist disposal agency and handles auctions of seized assets. On a research-center tally, its share of domestic hammer price reached more than half among major auction houses.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual revenue went from KRW 55.1 billion in 2022 to KRW 45 billion in 2023 (down 18.4% year on year) and KRW 20.6 billion in 2024 (down 54.2%), before rising to KRW 21.3 billion in 2025 (up 3.6%). 2022 was the largest year in the disclosed period and 2024 the smallest.

Operating profit was KRW 7.71 billion in 2022, with an operating margin of 14.0%, then turned to an operating loss of KRW 3.55 billion in 2023, followed by losses of KRW 3.39 billion in 2024 (operating margin -16.5%) and KRW 3.95 billion in 2025 (operating margin -18.5%).

Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 4.54 billion in 2022 became a net loss of KRW 7.97 billion in 2023, widened to KRW 9.47 billion in 2024, and narrowed to KRW 8.77 billion in 2025. Net profit including non-controlling interests was disclosed at the same amounts in each year.

In 2024, when revenue fell sharply, hammer price rose 30.8% to KRW 71.4 billion, so auction volume and revenue did not move in the same direction.

The debt-to-equity ratio climbed each year from 56.5% in 2022 to 125.4% in 2025, and total liabilities of KRW 123.2 billion at end-2025 exceeded total equity of KRW 98.3 billion.

Operating cash flow moved from negative KRW 27.2 billion in 2022 to KRW 9.6 billion in 2023, then stayed positive at KRW 3.43 billion in 2024 and KRW 3.74 billion in 2025.

Quarterly, the operating loss widened to KRW 1.69 billion in the fourth quarter of 2025 before turning to operating profit of KRW 289 million in the first quarter of 2026, with positive results continuing at KRW 386 million in the second quarter and KRW 240 million in the third.

The company cited its large first-quarter premium auction as the start of the turnaround, with revenue of KRW 15.3 billion, up 173.3% from the prior quarter.

Second-quarter revenue of KRW 4.27 billion (down 72.1% quarter on quarter) and third-quarter revenue of KRW 5.29 billion (up 24.1%) show how widely quarterly sales swing.

Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders turned positive at KRW 305 million in the third quarter of 2026, while the four quarters from the fourth quarter of 2025 through the third quarter of 2026 total a net loss of KRW 3.66 billion.

05

Industry analysis

Domestic art auctions spent nearly two years in a slump before turning to recovery in 2026, a trend that industry reports describe in common. Seoul Auction's January-to-September hammer price reached KRW 75.3 billion on the research-center tally, more than double the same period a year earlier.

Overseas markets are cited as a factor, with a company official saying the combined hammer price of three major New York spring auction houses more than doubled year on year.

The company emphasizes rising demand for proven artists and quality works, and says securing works early determines an auction house's competitiveness. In the first half of 2026, average hammer price per lot roughly doubled, which the company attributes to a focus on high-value works.

High-value sales can swing quarterly results sharply, so market-wide recovery and the auction house's revenue do not always move at the same pace. Non-art luxury goods are included or excluded depending on the tally, so the market-share figure should be read with its definition in mind. Direct comparison with domestic rivals rests mainly on this share tally.

06

Outlook

The company plans to hold large auctions through the fourth quarter of 2026 and expand full-year hammer price to about KRW 100 billion. The nearest event is the National Tax Service special auction on November 14, 2026, with 81 online lots and 18 live-floor lots.

The 2026 AUCTION SHOW at its Gangnam Center runs from November 11 to 24, with 12 auctions, 8 exhibitions and about 1,000 works. Within it, the November 23 evening sale offers 34 works with a combined estimate of about KRW 19 billion, and the November 24 contemporary day sale offers 73 works.

A company official expected hammer price to rise further with the November premium auction and said the company aims to deliver a full-year turnaround. This is the company's own outlook, and fourth-quarter results have not yet been disclosed in a periodic report.

With online lots expanded, whether online hammer price holds up is also part of the picture. Because a hammer price target is not the same metric as revenue, how far auction volume carries into recognized revenue is the next point to confirm.

Bull factors

Sharp Recovery in Hammer Price

Cumulative hammer price for January to September 2026 reached KRW 77.3 billion, which the company said already exceeded its full-year totals for 2024 and 2025. Third-quarter online hammer price of KRW 2.2 billion roughly doubled year on year, and online lots offered rose to 1,559. On the research-center tally, the company's January-to-September share was reported at 54.3%.

Consecutive Operating Profits

Operating profit turned positive at KRW 289 million in the first quarter of 2026, then KRW 386 million in the second and KRW 240 million in the third. The company attributes this to premium auctions and online expansion. A Hankyung analysis also shows the third-quarter cost of sales ratio falling from 81% to 61.2%.

Premium-Lot Strategy

The company says average hammer price per lot roughly doubled in the first half of 2026 as it focused on premium works. A large offline auction in March 2026, paired with a Hong Kong preview, aimed to draw overseas bidders. First-quarter revenue then rose to KRW 15.3 billion, up 173.3% from the prior quarter.

09

Bear factors

Volatile Quarterly Revenue

Revenue in 2026 was KRW 15.3 billion in the first quarter, KRW 4.27 billion in the second and KRW 5.29 billion in the third, swinging sharply each quarter. Third-quarter product sales fell 50.1% year on year and brokerage revenue dropped 23.6%.

Because revenue depends on auction timing and product sales, the direction of quarterly results is hard to read.

Rising Financial Leverage

The debt-to-equity ratio rose from 56.5% in 2022 to 125.4% in 2025. Total liabilities of KRW 123.2 billion at end-2025 exceed total equity of KRW 98.3 billion.

Three consecutive years of losses were disclosed from 2023 to 2025, and the last four quarters total a net loss of KRW 3.66 billion attributable to controlling shareholders.

Gap Between Hammer Price and Revenue

Large individual works can weigh heavily on quarterly results. Hammer price rose 30.8% to KRW 71.4 billion in 2024, yet revenue fell 54.2% to KRW 20.6 billion the same year. The KRW 100 billion full-year hammer price figure is the company's outlook and has not yet been achieved.

10

Risk factors

Market Cycle

The art auction market is sensitive to economic conditions, exchange rates and overseas collector demand. If the market cools again, hammer price and sell-through rates could fall together.

Because the 2026 recovery has been led by high-value works, how far mid-priced demand supports volume is a key factor in gauging where the cycle stands.

Balance Sheet

Operating cash flow moved from negative KRW 27.2 billion in 2022 to KRW 9.6 billion in 2023 and stayed positive afterward. Against total liabilities of KRW 123.2 billion, borrowing costs could become a variable for earnings recovery. With the debt-to-equity ratio elevated, room for additional borrowing may also be limited.

Consignment Volume

The disposal business for seized assets, carried out under a National Tax Service designation, depends on the volume of consigned lots. The March 2026 special auction sold all 76 lots for about KRW 1.2 billion in hammer price. The November 14, 2026 special auction offers 99 lots and is the next data point on this business's volume.

Overall view

Seoul Auction is showing a recovery in which both hammer price and operating profit improve together in 2026. Cumulative hammer price for January to September reached KRW 77.3 billion, exceeding its full-year totals for 2024 and 2025, and operating profit has been positive in each quarter of 2026 so far.

The recovery leans heavily on high-value works and large auction timing, so quarterly revenue remains volatile. On the balance sheet, the debt-to-equity ratio rose from 56.5% in 2022 to 125.4% in 2025, and three consecutive years of losses were disclosed from 2023 to 2025.

With a net loss attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 3.66 billion over the last four quarters, net-profit recovery is still in progress. The share price trades below book value, but a price-to-earnings ratio cannot be calculated, so whether earnings persist is the central variable.

The next points to confirm are the November 2026 special auction and main sale at the 2026 AUCTION SHOW, and the 2026 annual report due by the end of March 2027.
