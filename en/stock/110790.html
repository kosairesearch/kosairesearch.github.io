02

Business structure

Creas F&C is a apparel brand company centered on golf wear. Based on the revenue mix compiled by AlphaSquare, apparel products account for 99.61% of revenue, while golf balls and apparel merchandise make up 0.39%.

As demand for golf weakened, the company began pushing into brands and new businesses beyond golf wear in 2022. The starting point was the 2022 acquisition of a 100% stake in Italian sportswear brand Hydrogen for about KRW 20 billion.

Hydrogen's sales then fell 60.7% from about KRW 5 billion in 2024 to about KRW 1.97 billion in 2025, and its net loss in 2025 was about KRW 3.54 billion.

At the end of June 2026, Hydrogen had assets of about KRW 6.1 billion and liabilities of about KRW 8.5 billion, leaving equity at about minus KRW 2.4 billion, a state of full capital impairment. The golf-resort business is run by a subsidiary, Kris Valley, and the parent lent it about KRW 110 billion in November 2024.

In early 2026 the company decided to sell its entire stake in Ontide, the core of its OEM business, and said it would concentrate on strengthening its global business. Overseas sales remain small: first-half 2026 exports were about KRW 1.36 billion, down 22.4% year on year, and account for 0.91% of revenue.

Analysts say new outdoor brands are struggling to gain a foothold against established outdoor makers. The Korea IR Council reported that the domestic golf-wear market shrank about 12% year on year in 2023.

04

Earnings analysis

The audited annual figures show sales falling from KRW 380.9 billion in 2022 to KRW 367 billion in 2023 (down 3.6% year on year), KRW 331.3 billion in 2024 (down 9.7%), and KRW 311.9 billion in 2025 (down 5.8%).

Sales declined for three consecutive years from 2023 to 2025, with 2022 the largest year and 2025 the smallest in the 2022 to 2025 period.

Operating profit fell from KRW 78.5 billion in 2022 to KRW 46.1 billion in 2023 (down 41.3%) and KRW 12.1 billion in 2024 (down 73.7%), and the company recorded an operating loss of KRW 4.73 billion in 2025, turning from profit to loss.

The operating margin fell from 20.6% in 2022 to 12.6% in 2023, 3.7% in 2024 and -1.5% in 2025. The company attributed the decline in 2025 operating profit mainly to a higher cost ratio and lower operating profit at its subsidiaries.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 26.7 billion in 2022 and KRW 24.2 billion in 2023 (down 9.5%), then turned to a net loss of KRW 25.1 billion in 2024 and back to a profit of KRW 12.8 billion in 2025.

Net income including non-controlling interests was a loss of KRW 4.63 billion in 2025, so the gap between the two measures is wide, reflecting the non-controlling share. Operating cash flow was KRW 18 billion in 2022, KRW -1.05 billion in 2023, KRW 5.73 billion in 2024 and KRW 39.4 billion in 2025.

Quarterly sales were KRW 63.2 billion in the first quarter of 2026, KRW 89.8 billion in the second quarter (up 42.2% from the prior quarter) and KRW 62.3 billion in the third quarter (down 30.6% from the prior quarter and down 46.9% year on year).

Operating profit moved from an operating loss of KRW 6.72 billion in the first quarter of 2026 to operating profit of KRW 10.9 billion in the second quarter, then fell 83.3% to KRW 1.82 billion in the third quarter.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders was a loss of KRW 4.17 billion in the fourth quarter of 2025 and a loss of KRW 8.86 billion in the first quarter of 2026, then a profit of KRW 7.12 billion in the second quarter and a loss of KRW 6.22 billion in the third quarter.

Whether the second-quarter recovery came from operations or from one-off items such as asset disposals will be clearer from the other income and expense breakdown in the next quarterly report. A news report also said investment impairment losses are weighing on the company's finances. The net loss attributable to controlling shareholders over the last four quarters totaled KRW 12.1 billion.

05

Industry analysis

The golf-wear market, the core of Creas F&C's business, continues to face weaker demand. Park Sun-young, a researcher at the Korea IR Council, said the domestic golf-wear market shrank about 12% year on year in 2023.

The same report described the domestic outdoor market as one of the few fashion categories expected to grow, while noting that the rise in new brands has intensified competition. Analysts reported in November 2026 that the company's attempts to expand into new businesses have been difficult while golf demand slows.

According to an industry comparison, Creas F&C's operating margin is below the average of the apparel and fashion accessories sector. The same comparison shows its debt ratio is below the sector average. Because golf wear is the dominant line, the business depends heavily on one category.

Among listed fashion companies, Youngone, F&F and Handsome are generally seen as having broader brand and production bases, so Creas F&C's portfolio is comparatively narrow. Golf wear and outdoor apparel follow different demand cycles, so the company must rebuild profitability in both lines.

06

Outlook

The company is reshaping its group portfolio around core brands such as golf wear and outdoor apparel. The Ontide stake sale went through formal disclosure steps, with the disposal date adjusted to April 3, 2026.

After the sale, the key question is how the global revenue structure changes under the company's stated push to strengthen overseas operations.

Hydrogen's loan balance from the parent rose from about KRW 6.22 billion at the end of 2025 to about KRW 6.5 billion at the end of June 2026, so parent-company funding continues. In 2025, about KRW 2.53 billion of loans to Hydrogen were converted into equity, and about KRW 1.73 billion of new loans were extended.

Part of the receivables became equity, but operating funds continued to be lent. Whether the KRW 110 billion loan to the golf-resort subsidiary is repaid will also shape future financial pressure. Whether the impaired investment stake recovers in value is another point to follow.

The third-quarter 2026 operating profit of KRW 1.82 billion came after a sharp decline from the second quarter, so the next annual report should show whether the profit trend rests on operating strength or on temporary factors.

The next periodic report is the 2026 annual report, and the key checkpoint is how far the operating margin recovers from -1.5% in 2025.

Bull factors

Quarterly operating profit back in the black

Operating profit turned to KRW 10.9 billion in the second quarter of 2026, and the third quarter stayed in the black at KRW 1.82 billion. Compared with the operating loss of KRW 4.73 billion in 2025, quarterly earning power has partly recovered. The third-quarter profit came after a year-earlier operating loss.

Improving operating cash flow

Operating cash flow rose sharply from KRW 5.73 billion in 2024 to KRW 39.4 billion in 2025. Improved cash generation in a year with an operating loss is a positive factor for financial stability. The company's combination of asset sales and business pruning also bears on cash sources.

Low price-to-book versus the sector

The price-to-book ratio is well below the sector average in the AlphaSquare comparison. The dividend yield sits above the sector average. How this gap narrows as profits recover is the next point to watch.

Bear factors

Sustained sales decline

Sales declined for three consecutive years from 2023 to 2025. Third-quarter 2026 sales of KRW 62.3 billion fell 46.9% from a year earlier. Weaker golf demand has pressured the core golf-wear business, and new businesses have not offset the decline.

Subsidiary weakness and loan burden

Hydrogen was in full capital impairment as of the end of June 2026, with equity of about minus KRW 2.4 billion. The parent's loan balance to Hydrogen rose to about KRW 6.5 billion, and the KRW 110 billion loan to the golf-resort subsidiary remains outstanding. Impairment losses on investments have added to financial pressure, according to press reports.

Alternating quarterly losses and higher leverage

The net loss attributable to controlling shareholders in the third quarter of 2026 was KRW 6.22 billion, turning back to a loss. The cumulative net loss attributable to controlling shareholders over the last four quarters was KRW 12.1 billion. The debt-to-equity ratio rose to 99.9% at the end of 2025 from 84.0% in 2024.

Risk factors

Funding and loan risk

The parent continues to lend to Hydrogen and the golf-resort subsidiary. If repayments are delayed or impairments widen, consolidated financial pressure would increase. Additional impairments on investments would directly affect equity and earnings.

Core demand and cost risk

The golf-wear market shrank about 12% in 2023, and sufficient evidence of a demand recovery has not yet appeared. If the higher cost ratio the company cited for 2025 persists, margin recovery would be slower. Third-quarter operating profit fell sharply, so earnings remain sensitive to cost and sales swings.

Overseas and new-business expansion risk

First-half 2026 exports fell 22.4% year on year and account for only 0.91% of revenue. The Japanese sales unit, CREAS JAPAN, recorded 2025 sales of about KRW 1.36 billion and a net loss of about KRW 2.36 billion.

New outdoor brands are struggling to gain a foothold against established competitors, so the overseas push may not translate into earnings.

Overall view

Creas F&C's sales fell from KRW 380.9 billion in 2022 to KRW 311.9 billion in 2025, and its operating margin dropped from 20.6% to -1.5% before the company posted an operating loss in 2025. It returned to operating profit in the second and third quarters of 2026, but it is too early to call quarterly results stable.

Full capital impairment at Hydrogen and rising parent-company loans remain financial burdens. On the other side, the disposal of the Ontide stake and the improvement in operating cash flow can be read as a restructuring of the financial base.

The price-to-book ratio sitting below the sector average and the dividend yield above it are useful as factual comparisons. However, with a net loss attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 12.1 billion over the last four quarters, the durability of the profit recovery is still unconfirmed.

The 2026 annual report, the next periodic filing, will be the key checkpoint for whether the sales decline stops and the operating margin recovers.
