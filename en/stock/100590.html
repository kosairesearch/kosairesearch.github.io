02

Business structure

Mercury is an information and communications equipment maker whose main lines are terminal devices and optical cable.

In a 2019 technical analysis report, the Q3 2019 revenue mix was terminal business (AP, ONT and others) at 69.0%, optical communications (optical cable) at 22.7%, and convergence business (TDXAGW) at 8.3%.

According to Butler, a data aggregator, the Mercury business unit accounted for 80% of Q2 2026 revenue, the optical communications unit for 16%, and other units for 4%.

The optical communications business sits in its wholly owned subsidiary, and the company said wider optical cable supply from that subsidiary drove the Q3 2026 improvement.

An SKS Research report from April 2026 said Mercury owns a drawing tower for optical fiber production and that its capacity is estimated at around second place in Korea. Its customers are mainly telecom operators; a 2019 report said the company supplies all three major Korean carriers.

A 2022 report also described overseas-centred optical cable demand as healthy. On competition, a 2019 report flagged concern that domestic and foreign entrants could push down selling prices. The company is pursuing two growth axes: expanding optical cable production and supplying Wi-Fi 7 equipment.

04

Earnings analysis

On a DART-disclosed basis, revenue went from KRW 163.1 billion in 2022 to KRW 153.6 billion in 2023 (down 5.8% year on year), KRW 134.3 billion in 2024 (down 12.6%) and KRW 123.9 billion in 2025 (down 7.8%).

Revenue has declined for three consecutive years from 2023 to 2025; 2022 was the largest year in the 2022 to 2025 span and 2025 the smallest.

Operating profit was KRW 4.88 billion in 2022 and KRW 4.94 billion in 2023 (up 1.2%), then turned to an operating loss of KRW 887 million in 2024 and remained in loss at KRW 734 million in 2025, with the loss narrowing. The operating margin moved from 3.0% in 2022 and 3.2% in 2023 to -0.7% in 2024 and -0.6% in 2025.

Attributable net income rose to KRW 4 billion in 2023 (up 646.3%), then became an attributable net loss of KRW 1.1 billion in 2024 and widened to KRW 2.47 billion in 2025.

Net income including non-controlling interests differs from the attributable figure: KRW 3.31 billion in 2023, a net loss of KRW 1.51 billion in 2024 and KRW 2.35 billion in 2025.

Operating cash flow was negative KRW 15.7 billion in 2022 and positive in each year from 2023 onward, at KRW 17.2 billion, KRW 5.76 billion and KRW 11 billion. At end-2025 total equity was KRW 142.1 billion, of which KRW 140.6 billion belonged to controlling shareholders, and total liabilities were KRW 34.2 billion.

Quarterly, revenue rose to KRW 40.5 billion in Q4 2025 (up 36.9% from the prior quarter) with operating profit of KRW 2.05 billion, then fell to KRW 26 billion in Q1 2026 (down 35.8%) with an operating loss of KRW 1.17 billion.

Q2 2026 revenue was KRW 30.1 billion with operating profit of KRW 1.33 billion, and Q3 2026 revenue reached KRW 38.1 billion (up 26.5% quarter on quarter) with operating profit of KRW 3.96 billion (up 198.3%).

Attributable net results alternated between a KRW 1.36 billion profit in Q1, a KRW 6.02 billion net loss in Q2 and a KRW 4.59 billion profit in Q3. Because operating profit and attributable net results moved in different directions in some quarters, non-operating items need to be considered alongside operating results.

05

Industry analysis

Optical cable demand moves with telecom infrastructure investment. A 2019 report also described the industry as closely linked to domestic and overseas infrastructure investment.

A newer demand driver is AI data centers, and the company said it will address new demand with optical cable for AI data centers and next-generation communications equipment. Press reports also said a roughly 9,000 pyeong optical cable base in Mungyeong is being pursued.

The capacity ranking of around second place in Korea comes from a securities firm's estimate in April 2026 and does not fix market share. Reports on expanding Wi-Fi 7 supply have also continued.

A 2022 report pointed to chip supply shortages such as Qualcomm parts and rising component and freight costs as reasons for weak profitability, and saw rising home Wi-Fi demand supporting terminal sales. On competition, a 2019 report flagged concern about price declines from new domestic and foreign entrants.

The terminal and optical cable segments face different cycles and are worth viewing separately; the company and media attribute the Q3 2026 revenue increase mainly to wider optical cable supply.

06

Outlook

The company has said it plans to keep growing by expanding Wi-Fi 7 equipment supply and upgrading its optical cable production base. Press reports describe the Mungyeong base as a project of roughly 9,000 pyeong. In optical cable, the key question is how large AI data center products become as a share of sales.

The terminal business is expected to keep supplying existing telecom operators. The 2026 quarterly path shows revenue rising in Q2 and Q3 after a Q1 low, but Q2 also recorded an attributable net loss of KRW 6.02 billion.

Whether higher quarterly revenue converts into steady attributable profit is therefore the next point to verify. The next periodic report is the 2026 annual report, which will confirm full-year operating profit and attributable net results. Q1 2027 results will show whether the seasonal swing seen in Q1 2026 repeats. Whether capacity additions lift operating margin, not only revenue, is also a key point.

08

Bull factors

Q3 turn to profit and two straight quarters of revenue growth

Q3 2026 operating profit was KRW 3.96 billion, turning from an operating loss of KRW 303 million in Q3 2025. Revenue rose in Q2 and Q3, reaching KRW 38.1 billion, up 28.8% year on year. Operating profit has been positive in Q4 2025 and Q2 2026 as well as Q3 2026, which reflects a recovering earnings base.

Optical cable subsidiary and new demand

The optical communications business sits in a wholly owned subsidiary, and the company said wider supply from that subsidiary drove the Q3 improvement. An April 2026 SKS Research report said Mercury owns a drawing tower and that its capacity is estimated at around second place in Korea.

The company has signalled that it will serve AI data center optical cable demand, and press reports have covered plans for a Mungyeong optical cable base.

Improved financial structure

The debt ratio fell from 51.3% in 2022 to 24.1% in 2025, with total liabilities of KRW 34.2 billion and total equity of KRW 142.1 billion at end-2025. Operating cash flow stayed positive from 2023 through 2025, at KRW 11 billion in 2025. The stronger balance sheet provides a base for weathering weaker earnings periods.

09

Bear factors

Three consecutive years of annual revenue decline

Revenue fell to KRW 153.6 billion in 2023, KRW 134.3 billion in 2024 and KRW 123.9 billion in 2025, a third straight annual decline. 2025 was the smallest year of the 2022 to 2025 span. The revenue increase so far in 2026 covers only two quarters, so the full-year recovery will be confirmed in the 2026 annual report.

Volatile quarterly results

An operating loss of KRW 1.17 billion was recorded in Q1 2026, and an attributable net loss of KRW 6.02 billion in Q2 2026. The attributable net result over the last four quarters totals a loss of KRW 390 million.

The Q3 profit is a single-quarter result, so whether profitability persists must be confirmed over further quarters.

Low operating margin structure

The operating margin fell from 3.0% in 2022 and 3.2% in 2023 to -0.7% in 2024 and -0.6% in 2025. On an annual basis, operating losses continued in both 2024 and 2025. Whether wider optical cable supply lifts the annual operating margin has yet to be shown.

10

Risk factors

Customer and demand concentration

With telecom operators as the core customers, changes in network capital spending can affect revenue quickly. A 2022 report viewed overseas-centred optical cable demand positively, but shifts in overseas demand could translate into revenue swings.

The degree to which optical cable sales depend on specific products and customers should be tracked each quarter.

Price competition

A 2019 report flagged concern about price declines as domestic and foreign companies enter the market. The same report noted that revenue through Q3 2019 fell from the prior-year period. Price pressure in the terminal business could weigh on profitability.

Components, materials and currency

A 2022 report cited shortages of chip components such as Qualcomm parts and rising component and freight costs as reasons for weaker profitability. The same report noted a stronger won as a favourable factor for costs. Component supply and currency moves can swing quarterly margins.

12

Overall view

Mercury's DART-disclosed results are summarised by revenue declines since 2023 and consecutive operating losses in 2024 and 2025. In Q3 2026 operating profit of KRW 3.96 billion reversed a loss, with revenue and profit improving together on wider optical cable supply.

However, Q1 2026 brought an operating loss and Q2 an attributable net loss of KRW 6.02 billion, so quarterly swings remain large. The attributable net result over the last four quarters is a loss of KRW 390 million, not yet in the black.

On the balance sheet, the debt ratio fell from 51.3% in 2022 to 24.1% in 2025, with total equity of KRW 142.1 billion. The price-to-book ratio is below 1x, but an earnings-based multiple cannot be calculated.

Bullish factors are the optical cable subsidiary's wider supply and AI data center demand; bearish factors are annual revenue declines and quarterly volatility. The next checkpoints are full-year operating profit in the 2026 annual report and whether quarterly profits persist in the Q1 2027 results.
