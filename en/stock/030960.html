Revenue Down, Losses Persist, Shares Below Book Value

02

Business structure

Yangjisa, founded in September 1976, is a stationery and printing manufacturer whose core lines are notebooks, diaries and notepads, along with stationery and planned publishing. Production runs on a roughly 7:3 split between made-to-order and planned production.

In the fiscal year ended June 2025, exports accounted for 44.75% of made-to-order sales, while planned production was reported as 100% domestic. The sales mix also lists gift-mall, digital device and consignment management items, so the business extends beyond manufacturing.

The central axis is printing and stationery manufacturing, with consignment management and promotional logistics as supporting lines. In the stationery sector, only two listed companies are available for comparison, which limits the weight of rank-based comparisons.

A press report dated June 18, 2026 said the company partnered with a design brand to target consumers in their twenties and thirties.

The largest structural variable is digital transition, since the spread of smartphones and tablets has shifted demand toward digital content and away from paper-based products, according to a financial information site's analysis.

Treasury shares account for about 12.35% as of a change date of June 11, 2025, which leaves a smaller free float than the issued share count suggests.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual revenue moved from KRW 62.1 billion in the fiscal year ended June 2023 to KRW 58.2 billion in June 2024 (down 6.4% year on year), KRW 55.9 billion in June 2025 (down 3.9%), and KRW 49.5 billion in June 2026 (down 11.4%).

Revenue has fallen for three consecutive fiscal years from June 2024 through June 2026, and the fiscal year ended June 2023 was the largest of the four.

Operating results moved from an operating loss of KRW 6.92 billion in the fiscal year ended June 2023 to an operating profit of KRW 156 million in June 2024, a turn to profit, before swinging back to an operating loss of KRW 6.08 billion in June 2025.

The fiscal year ended June 2026 posted an operating loss of KRW 6.41 billion, a continued loss that widened, and its operating margin of -13.0% is the lowest of the four years.

Profit attributable to owners of the parent was KRW 4.45 billion in the fiscal year ended June 2024, up 265.0% year on year, before turning to a net loss of KRW 1.59 billion in June 2025 and a wider net loss of KRW 1.77 billion in June 2026.

Net income including non-controlling interests is shown with the same amounts as profit attributable to owners in all four fiscal years. Operating cash flow went from negative KRW 3.09 billion and negative KRW 1.7 billion in the first two years to positive KRW 1.32 billion and KRW 1.44 billion in the last two.

On a quarterly basis, revenue of KRW 8.89 billion and an operating loss of KRW 4.04 billion in the quarter ended June 2025 gave way to revenue of KRW 10 billion and an operating loss of KRW 737 million in the quarter ended September 2025, and then to revenue of KRW 25.1 billion and an operating profit of KRW 1.32 billion in the quarter ended December 2025, a 150.1% rise in revenue from the prior quarter.

Revenue in the quarter ended March 2026 was KRW 6.88 billion, down 72.6% from the prior quarter, with an operating profit of KRW 701 million.

The quarter ended June 2026 recorded revenue of KRW 7.54 billion, up 9.6% from the prior quarter but down 15.2% from a year earlier, and an operating loss of KRW 7.7 billion, a wider loss than a year before.

Quarterly revenue swings are large, so one quarter is a weak basis for judging the trend, and the sum of profit attributable to owners over the last four quarters is a net loss of KRW 1.77 billion.

The filings do not spell out causes quarter by quarter, but public analysis points to higher raw material prices, export conditions weakened by the prolonged Russia-Ukraine war, and weaker paper-product demand as digital content grows.

Industry analysis

By market classification, Yangjisa belongs to the KOSDAQ publishing and media reproduction sector, and separate sources also classify it under stationery. The largest structural change on the demand side is digitalization.

As smartphones and tablets spread and digital content demand grows, demand for paper-based printed matter and stationery is shrinking, according to the sector commentary on results.

Commentary adds that the prolonged Russia-Ukraine war, global supply chain stress and surging raw material prices have weakened export conditions. On the cycle, the current stage is one where structural demand decline and cost pressure are cited together, rather than clear signs of recovery.

Competitive position can be read from a two-company comparison within the stationery group. In that comparison, revenue and operating margin rank second for the fiscal year ended June 2026, and the debt ratio is below the group average.

The group average itself rests on only two companies, which lowers its representativeness. The made-to-order business, with high export exposure, is sensitive to exchange rate swings and changes in overseas demand. Planned production is mainly domestic, so the business faces both external and domestic variables.

The product line includes diaries and planned publishing alongside digital devices and gift-mall items, but the latest breakdown by segment revenue is not available in a clear form, so the discussion stays qualitative.
