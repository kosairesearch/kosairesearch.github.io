Yangjisa: Sales Decline and Losses Test a Stationery Maker

Yangjisa's sales have fallen for three consecutive fiscal years through June 2026 and it has posted operating losses for two, so whether the narrower third-quarter 2026 loss marks a turn is the next checkpoint.

  1. 1

    Sales were KRW 49.5 billion in the fiscal year ended June 2026, down from KRW 62.1 billion in the fiscal year ended June 2023, and the decline has continued for three years.

  2. 2

    The operating loss widened to KRW 6.41 billion in the fiscal year ended June 2026, and the operating margin was -13.0%.

  3. 3

  4. 4

  5. 5

02

Business structure

Yangjisa is a KOSDAQ-listed manufacturer whose core business is notebooks, diaries, and calendars, along with planning publications. Production is run on an approximately 7:3 split between made-to-order and made-to-plan, and according to WiseReport, exports account for at least about 43.5% of the made-to-order side.

For the fiscal year ended June 2025, made-to-order sales were 55.25% domestic and 44.75% export. Product categories include gift malls, digital devices, consignment management, made-to-plan, and made-to-order, and the gift mall, digital device, and consignment items were shown as 100% domestic over the same period.

This split is a product classification compiled by WiseReport rather than disclosed segment sales, so it is not used as a revenue weight. The company is pursuing new revenue sources in digital devices and product diversification that uses its wholesale and distribution know-how.

Whether this expansion will change the revenue mix is not yet visible in reported results, as digitalization and low birth rates shrink stationery demand. Among the two listed companies in the stationery sector per Alphasquare, Yangjisa ranks first by market capitalization and second by sales.

As of the April 21, 2023 change date, the largest shareholder group held 67.76% and treasury shares stood at 12.35%. Because a large share of its business is made-to-order, customer order flow feeds directly into quarterly sales.

04

Earnings analysis

On a reported basis, sales fell from KRW 62.1 billion in the fiscal year ended June 2023 to KRW 58.2 billion in the fiscal year ended June 2024 (down 6.4% year on year), KRW 55.9 billion in the fiscal year ended June 2025 (down 3.9%), and KRW 49.5 billion in the fiscal year ended June 2026 (down 11.4%).

Sales have declined for three consecutive fiscal years from the year ended June 2024 through the year ended June 2026, with the year ended June 2023 the largest and the year ended June 2026 the smallest in the period.

Operating results moved from an operating loss of KRW 6.92 billion in the fiscal year ended June 2023 to an operating profit of KRW 156 million in the year ended June 2024, which was a swing to profit, before returning to an operating loss of KRW 6.08 billion in the year ended June 2025.

The operating loss widened to KRW 6.41 billion in the year ended June 2026, with the operating margin at -13.0%.

Profit attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 4.45 billion in the year ended June 2024, up 265.0% year on year, and then turned into losses of KRW 1.59 billion and KRW 1.77 billion in the years ended June 2025 and June 2026, two consecutive loss years; net income including non-controlling interests followed the same path.

Quarterly, the fourth quarter of 2025 showed sales of KRW 25.1 billion and operating profit of KRW 1.32 billion, a swing to profit, but first-quarter 2026 sales dropped to KRW 6.88 billion, down 72.6% from the prior quarter.

In the second quarter of 2026, sales recovered to KRW 7.54 billion, yet an operating loss of KRW 7.7 billion appeared and controlling shareholders' net loss reached KRW 5.06 billion.

The third quarter of 2026 brought sales of KRW 9.78 billion, up 29.7% from the prior quarter and down 2.4% from a year earlier, with an operating loss of KRW 901 million that narrowed sequentially but widened versus the prior-year quarter.

The four quarters from the fourth quarter of 2025 through the third quarter of 2026 total a controlling-shareholder net loss of KRW 2.33 billion.

Operating cash flow moved from negative in the years ended June 2023 and June 2024 to positive KRW 1.32 billion and KRW 1.44 billion in the years ended June 2025 and June 2026.

WiseReport cites the prolonged Russia-Ukraine war, supply chain disruption, raw material price increases, and growing digital content demand as drivers of weaker printing-sector demand.

05

Industry analysis

The stationery and printing market in which Yangjisa operates appears to be in a phase of continuing demand decline. WiseReport summarized that the prolonged Russia-Ukraine war, global supply chain disruption, raw material price spikes, and rising digital content demand have reduced demand across the printing sector.

According to the same source, both export and domestic results declined. Digitalization and low birth rates are cited as structural factors that weigh on long-term demand for notebooks and diaries.

The company's sales have trended down since the fiscal year ended June 2024, and third-quarter 2026 sales rose from the prior quarter but have not yet exceeded the year-earlier level.

Among the two listed companies in the stationery sector in Alphasquare's classification, Yangjisa ranks first by market capitalization, while its operating margin is below the sector average.

Because both made-to-order sales and exports are significant, the company is exposed to overseas demand and global supply chain pressure. Until diversification produces visible results, the recovery of core stationery demand remains the main variable for the industry outlook.

Positive cash flow despite net losses since the year ended June 2025 indicates that book losses have not translated one-for-one into cash outflows. Whether this continues after the quarter ending December 2026 is the next thing to confirm.

Whether this pattern repeats in the year-end diary season is the test for this bullish point.

Risk factors

Demand structure risk

Long-term demand for notebooks and diaries could decline with digitalization and low birth rates. The high share of made-to-order production makes quarterly sales sensitive to customer order flow.

First-quarter 2026 sales fell 72.6% from the prior quarter, showing that this volatility has already appeared in reported results.

Earnings and financial resilience risk

The controlling-shareholder net loss over the last four quarters totals KRW 2.33 billion. The audit report carries an unqualified opinion with no going-concern issue disclosed, yet operating losses have continued for two years, making the timing of a return to profit hard to gauge. Even with a large equity base, earnings do not follow unless the income structure changes.

Float structure and share price volatility risk

The company is classified as a low-float stock, so supply and demand shifts can drive large price swings. Recent sharp gains occurred without new disclosures or earnings catalysts, and commentators noted that rallies lacking a clear business driver can reverse. With the largest shareholder group and treasury shares holding a large share, the tradable float is limited.

Overall view

Yangjisa's core business is stationery manufacturing, and sales in the fiscal year ended June 2026 were KRW 49.5 billion, down for three consecutive years, with an operating loss of KRW 6.41 billion marking two consecutive loss years.

The company swung to profit in the fourth quarter of 2025, and its third-quarter 2026 operating loss of KRW 901 million was narrower than the second quarter, but it is wider than a year earlier.

On the financial side, total equity of KRW 186.3 billion at June 2026 and a debt ratio of 13.9% indicate a thick equity base, and operating cash flow has remained positive. On the other side, falling printing demand, digitalization, and raw material and supply chain costs constrain the core business recovery.

The share price can move sharply on supply and demand given its low-float structure, independent of reported results. The next checkpoints are how much the year-end season lifts fourth-quarter 2026 sales and the operating loss trend in the quarterly report for the period ending December 2026.
