02

Business structure

Komelon is a manufacturer of cold-rolled steel and tape measures founded in 1963, with headquarters in Busan and an office in Incheon. The tape measure business is run from Busan and its Qingdao plant in China, while the cold rolling business operates from the Incheon plant.

Its main tape measure products are Steel Pocket Tape, Steel Long Tape and Fiber Glass Tape, and it also produces special steel and stainless steel.

Overseas, it manufactures and sells in China, Vietnam and the United States, and it built a factory in Hanoi, Vietnam in 2021 to expand exports and secure cost competitiveness. The company cites its sales network in 80 countries, more than 50 years of technology and brand recognition as strengths.

Tape measures are consumable products, so demand is sensitive to the economy and tends to weaken in certain months. On export mix, an analyst report several years ago put the United States as the main export region for the tape measure business, at 54% of its sales.

The stake held by the largest shareholder and related parties is 35.42%, so ownership is concentrated.

The cold rolling segment belongs to the steel materials process and responds to different demand drivers than tape measures; whether the two businesses together cushion results across cycles requires segment sales data to confirm.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual revenue fell from KRW 82.7 billion in 2022 to KRW 70.6 billion in 2023, a decline of 14.7%, then recovered to KRW 73.9 billion in 2024, up 4.8%, before slipping 2.3% to KRW 72.3 billion in 2025. Among 2022 to 2025, 2022 was the largest year for revenue and 2023 the smallest.

The 2024 revenue increase was attributed in the disclosures to growth in US export shipments and a weaker won.

Operating profit fell from KRW 15.3 billion in 2022 to KRW 8.97 billion in 2023, down 41.5%, then rose to KRW 19.9 billion in 2024, up 122.1%, and came to KRW 18 billion in 2025, down 9.8%. 2024 was the largest year for operating profit and 2023 the smallest.

The operating margin moved from 12.7% in 2023 to 27.0% in 2024 and 24.9% in 2025, staying within a 24-27% range since 2024. Net profit attributable to owners was KRW 15.9 billion in 2022, KRW 15.3 billion in 2023 (down 3.6%), KRW 27.2 billion in 2024 (up 77.8%) and KRW 22.6 billion in 2025 (down 17.2%).

Net income including non-controlling interests was KRW 15.8 billion, KRW 15.3 billion, KRW 27.2 billion and KRW 22.6 billion over the same years.

The 2025 decline was explained in the preliminary results announcement by base effects from prior-year events such as changes in the consolidation scope, and by weaker profit and loss on investment property.

In 2025, net profit attributable to owners fell more (17.2%) than operating profit (9.8%), suggesting non-operating items moved the bottom line more than operations did.

Quarterly revenue dropped from KRW 20.7 billion in Q3 2025 to KRW 15.5 billion in Q4 2025 (down 25.1% quarter on quarter), then rose to KRW 23.7 billion in Q2 2026 (up 33.5%) and was KRW 23 billion in Q3 2026 (down 3.1% quarter on quarter, up 11.1% year on year).

Quarterly operating profit went from KRW 3.9 billion in Q1 2026 to KRW 9.88 billion in Q2 2026 (up 153.2%) and then KRW 5.62 billion in Q3 2026 (down 43.1%).

Net profit attributable to owners of KRW 14.4 billion in Q2 2026 exceeded the KRW 9.88 billion operating profit, and the reason for this gap should be checked in the periodic report. The sum of net profit attributable to owners over the latest four quarters, from Q4 2025 to Q3 2026, is KRW 25.4 billion.

Operating cash flow was KRW 23.6 billion in 2022, KRW 17.6 billion in 2023, KRW 24.9 billion in 2024 and KRW 16.9 billion in 2025.

05

Industry analysis

Tape measures are consumable products tied to construction, interior and household demand, so they are affected by housing and renovation cycles. An analyst report several years ago suggested that rising US home prices lifted interest in moving and interior work, which was seen as a tailwind.

That was a past estimate, and whether the US housing market still moves in the same direction needs separate confirmation. The company runs plants in Qingdao, China and Vietnam, which gives it production locations split across countries and some room to respond to tariff and exchange-rate changes.

The 2024 revenue rise coinciding with a weaker won shows that currency moves can favor export results, but a reversal in the exchange rate can reduce earnings, so export concentration cuts both ways.

The cold rolling business depends on steel-materials capacity utilization and raw material prices, and its cycle differs from tape measures.

Among listed machinery and equipment peers, companies such as Doosan Bobcat and HD Construction Equipment serve different products and customers, so direct comparison metrics are limited.

Komelon's strength lies in its branded finished goods and overseas distribution, and changes in its market share in that area are hard to track from disclosures alone.

06

Outlook

In March 2026, the shareholder proposal for a KRW 40.5 billion share buyback passed as submitted at the annual general meeting, according to the company. Trust contracts with SK Securities and NH Investment & Securities raised the buyback amount set up through trusts to KRW 30 billion.

The company decided to cancel 1,059,676 treasury shares, or 11.71% of issued shares, with an expected cancellation value of KRW 23.5 billion, and described this as part of executing the KRW 40.5 billion buyback proposal.

On shareholder returns, the company has said it will keep its payout ratio at 30-40% of average net profit over the last five years and acquire and cancel treasury shares each year within 1% of total issued shares.

It also said it would consider additional buybacks if the price-to-book ratio falls to 0.8 times or below. The company said that the amount of additional cancellations could change depending on how the remaining approved amount is executed and when.

The trust contract that ran from March 12 to September 11, 2026 has already expired.

On overseas operations, a capital increase tied to a factory construction and equipment purchase at the Vietnam subsidiary was decided in the past, and expanding export markets and cost competitiveness are presented as the medium-term direction.

Whether this direction changes the run-rate of quarterly revenue, which was KRW 23 billion in Q3 2026, is not yet visible in the disclosures.

The next checkpoints are the execution and cancellation of the remaining treasury shares and whether the operating margin in the 2026 full-year results holds near the 24.9% of 2025.

08

Bull factors

Higher operating margin level

The operating margin rose from 12.7% in 2023 to 27.0% in 2024 and 24.9% in 2025, holding at a higher level since 2024. In 2024, US export growth and a weaker won lifted revenue. Whether this level carries into the 2026 full-year results is the key point for the bullish case.

Shareholder return policy taking shape

At the March 2026 annual meeting, the KRW 40.5 billion buyback proposal passed, and trust contracts totaling KRW 30 billion were set up. The company decided to cancel 1,059,676 treasury shares. It has stated a payout ratio target of 30-40% and annual treasury share cancellation within 1% of issued shares.

Strong balance sheet capacity

The debt-to-equity ratio was 4.1% at the end of 2025, with total liabilities of KRW 10.3 billion. Total equity of KRW 252.2 billion is entirely attributable to owners of the parent. The low debt load gives room to combine shareholder returns with overseas investment.

Bear factors

Wider quarterly earnings swings

Net profit attributable to owners was KRW 14.4 billion in Q2 2026 but fell to KRW 775 million in Q3, down 87.5% year on year. Over the same period, operating profit fell from KRW 9.88 billion to KRW 5.62 billion.

Non-operating items appear to have grown in importance, and the cause of the swing needs confirmation in the periodic report.

Pressure on core tape measure profitability

A summary of results through the first nine months of 2025 showed lower sales of the core tape measure products and a lower gross margin. Tape measures are consumable, so demand can fall quickly in a downturn. Without a recovery in core profitability, results would depend more on one-off factors.

Lower operating cash flow in 2025

Operating cash flow fell from KRW 24.9 billion in 2024 to KRW 16.9 billion in 2025. Over the same period, operating profit declined only from KRW 19.9 billion to KRW 18 billion. Because cash generation fell more than profit, running buybacks and dividends together could become a strain.

Risk factors

FX and export concentration

Revenue growth in 2024 came from both US export shipments and a weaker won. An analysis several years ago put the United States at 54% of tape measure sales. A reversal in the exchange rate or weaker US demand could move revenue and profit together.

Cyclical consumable demand

Tape measures are consumable, so orders move with economic and seasonal conditions. The 25.1% quarter-on-quarter drop in Q4 2025 revenue fits this pattern. When demand weakens, utilization and fixed-cost burden both rise.

Uncertain buyback execution and ownership concentration

Of the KRW 40.5 billion approved, KRW 30 billion has been set up in trust, and the company said the scale of further cancellations could change depending on how and when the remainder is executed. The largest shareholder and related parties hold 35.42%, which leaves a limited free float. A change in the return plan could affect the share price and trading structure.

Overall view

Komelon, founded in 1963, makes tape measures and cold-rolled steel, with a structure built on US-centered exports and consumable products. Across 2022-2025, annual revenue moved between KRW 70.6 billion and KRW 82.7 billion, and the operating margin stayed above the 12.7% of 2023 at 27.0% in 2024 and 24.9% in 2025.

In 2025, net profit attributable to owners fell to KRW 22.6 billion, reflecting weaker investment property results and base effects. Quarterly results in 2026 have been volatile, with Q2 net profit at KRW 14.4 billion and Q3 at KRW 775 million.

On shareholder returns, a KRW 40.5 billion buyback proposal has passed and cancellations are under way, and a payout target of 30-40% has been stated.

Bullish factors are the high operating margin, low debt ratio and expanded returns, while bearish factors are quarterly earnings swings, FX and US export concentration, and cyclical demand.

The next checkpoints are the 2026 full-year operating margin, the execution of remaining treasury shares, and the non-operating items in the 2026 annual report.
