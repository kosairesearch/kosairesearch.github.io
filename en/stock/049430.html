02

Business structure

Komelon was founded in 1963 and listed on KOSDAQ in 2001 as a specialist manufacturer of metal and fiber tape measures, operating its headquarters in Busan alongside an Incheon business site and a factory in Hanoi, Vietnam.

The tape measure segment accounts for more than 90% of total revenue, while the company also runs a cold-rolled steel segment that produces the specialty steel used as raw material, and a hand tools segment covering saws and cutters.

Komelon is regarded as the number one tape measure maker in Korea by market share and ranks among the top three to five globally, expanding overseas sales through large retail channels including Walmart and home improvement centers.

The business is export-heavy, and the share of US-facing sales expanded from 52.8% in 2022 to 62.9% in 2025, becoming a central pillar of earnings. This US concentration means that KRW/USD exchange rate movements and US construction and housing conditions have a direct bearing on results.

The company is described as holding a total of 60 intellectual property rights and operating a vertically integrated structure spanning material production, manufacturing, and marketing. It is also said to hold third and second place recognition in the US and Japanese markets, respectively. That said, recent commentary also points to a softening trend in the core tape measure segment's performance.

04

Earnings analysis

Komelon's 2025 consolidated revenue came to KRW 72.3 billion, a slight decline from KRW 73.9 billion in 2024, while operating profit fell to KRW 18.0 billion from KRW 19.9 billion, pulling the operating margin down from 27.0% to 24.9%.

Net income owner also declined to KRW 22.6 billion in 2025 from KRW 27.2 billion in 2024.

Looking further back, revenue of KRW 82.7 billion and an 18.5% operating margin in 2022 fell to KRW 70.6 billion in revenue and a 12.7% margin in 2023, before the margin rebounded sharply to 27.0% in 2024 and eased slightly to 24.9% in 2025, illustrating a margin that swings year to year.

On a quarterly basis, the solid third quarter of 2025 (revenue of KRW 20.7 billion, operating profit of KRW 5.4 billion) softened somewhat in the fourth quarter of 2025 (revenue KRW 15.5 billion, operating profit KRW 4.4 billion) and the first quarter of 2026 (revenue KRW 17.8 billion, operating profit KRW 3.9 billion).

However, the second quarter of 2026 saw revenue jump to KRW 23.7 billion and operating profit surge to KRW 9.9 billion, the highest of the last five quarters, with net income owner also rising markedly to KRW 14.4 billion.

Because the increase in net income exceeded the improvement in operating profit, a contribution from non-operating items cannot be ruled out, though the specific drivers have not been confirmed through a separate disclosure.

The company had previously attributed the 2025 earnings decline to a base effect from changes in consolidation scope in the prior year and a deterioration in investment property related gains and losses.

Operating cash flow remained substantial at KRW 16.9 billion in 2025 and KRW 24.9 billion in 2024, reflecting consistent large-scale cash generation.

05

Industry analysis

The tape measure and hand tools industry is closely tied to construction, interior renovation, and DIY demand, and Komelon's business is characterized as being linked to construction and infrastructure investment cycles.

Komelon holds the number one position in the domestic tape measure market and ranks among the top three to five globally, while also being credited with third and second place brand recognition in the US and Japanese markets, respectively.

On the cost side, prices for specialty steel raw materials and exchange rate movements directly affect profitability, and a rise in the KRW/USD rate is generally seen as favorable for Komelon given its high export exposure.

On the competitive front, large retailers continue to compete for distribution channel access, and Komelon has responded by expanding sales through major channels such as Walmart.

Over the industry cycle, US interest rates and housing market conditions can influence demand, while a US economic slowdown or trade friction are cited as downside factors.

As a small-cap KOSDAQ stock, trading liquidity tends to be limited, and the recently unfolding shareholder activism issue has added to share price volatility.

06

Outlook

At its March 30, 2026 annual general meeting, Komelon approved shareholder proposals from the Queensguard-HeyHolder alliance for a capital reserve reduction and a KRW 40.5 billion treasury share buyback.

In contrast, company-proposed items on auditor reappointment and director compensation limits, along with the shareholder-side outside director nomination, were all rejected.

Prior to the meeting, the company had already moved to stabilize its share price by signing a KRW 10 billion treasury stock trust contract with SK Securities, running from March 12 to September 11, 2026.

Following the AGM, the Queensguard-HeyHolder alliance stated it would continue monitoring whether the treasury share purchases are executed transparently and would keep pursuing the nomination of an audit committee member and outside director representing minority shareholder interests.

On the dividend front, the company appears intent on maintaining a high-payout stance backed by ample retained earnings and cash generation, having significantly raised its payout ratio year over year.

That said, some media commentary has raised concerns that expanding dividends amid a softer earnings phase could conflict with a long-term growth strategy.

Looking ahead, third-quarter earnings disclosure, the specific execution schedule for the KRW 40.5 billion buyback, and further discussion around board composition are all expected to unfold.

Bull factors

Growing US-Facing Sales and FX Tailwinds

The share of US-facing sales is reported to have risen from 52.8% in 2022 to 62.9% in 2025, driving earnings growth. A rise in the KRW/USD rate tends to work favorably for Komelon's profitability given its high export exposure. If US distribution expansion continues, this structure could keep contributing positively.

Improved Capital Allocation Through Shareholder Activism

The Queensguard-HeyHolder alliance succeeded in passing a capital reserve reduction and a KRW 40.5 billion treasury share buyback proposal at the 2026 annual general meeting. The controlling shareholder also voted in favor, so the items passed without a contentious vote split.

Analysis pointing to ample combined net cash and long-term financial asset resources has also underpinned this capital allocation discussion.

Stable Cash Generation and Low Capital Expenditure

The company is described as generating steady free cash flow given annual capital expenditure of only about KRW 2-3 billion. Operating cash flow reached KRW 16.9 billion in 2025 and KRW 24.9 billion in 2024, showing substantial cash generation each year.

This cash generation capacity has been used to fund shareholder return measures such as dividends and share buybacks.

Bear factors

Concerns Over an Earnings Slowdown Phase

Both revenue and operating profit declined year over year in 2025, which the company attributed to a base effect from changes in consolidation scope and a deterioration in investment property related gains and losses. Diagnoses also point to a softening trend in the core tape measure segment itself.

On a quarterly basis, recovery and slowdown have alternated, making a clear directional trend difficult to discern.

Risk of a Prolonged Governance Dispute

Conflict between activist funds such as Queensguard and Fibonacci and the company has previously escalated into legal procedures including a request for a court-ordered extraordinary general meeting and an injunction lawsuit over agenda items.

Even after the regular AGM, tension over board composition has continued, with related nominations being rejected. A prolonged dispute of this kind could translate into management uncertainty.

Debate Over the Sustainability of the High-Dividend Policy

Concerns have been raised that significantly increasing the payout ratio year over year even as earnings soften could conflict with a long-term growth strategy. There are also worries that continuing a strategy focused on shareholder returns could delay securing resources for business diversification or new investment.

Risk factors

FX and Raw Material Risk

Given the export-heavy business structure, KRW/USD exchange rate movements and changes in raw material prices such as specialty steel directly affect margins. This volatility is reflected in an operating margin that has ranged from 12.7% to 27.0% over the past four years. A slowdown in US construction and housing conditions could also weigh on demand.

Governance and Shareholder Activism Risk

There is precedent for conflict with activist funds escalating into legal disputes, and further shareholder proposals or contested votes could recur. Differing views among stakeholders over board composition and how the treasury share buyback is executed still remain.

Liquidity and Small-Cap Risk

As a small-cap KOSDAQ stock with a modest market capitalization, trading volume can be limited, which may amplify share price volatility. The number of shares outstanding and the supply-demand structure could also change depending on the progress of treasury share purchases and cancellations.

12

Overall view

Komelon has maintained its business foundation on the back of an expanding share of US-facing sales and stable cash generation, but 2025 also showed signs of an earnings slowdown as both revenue and the operating margin declined together.

Net income rose unusually sharply in the second quarter of 2026, though the cause has not been fully confirmed, leaving its durability to be judged through future disclosures.

At the same time, the governance dispute between the company and the Queensguard-HeyHolder alliance was partly resolved through the AGM approval of a capital reserve reduction and a KRW 40.5 billion treasury share buyback, yet tension over board composition remains.

The high-dividend stance and ample cash-type assets stand out as positives for shareholder returns, but there are also concerns that expanding dividends during a softer earnings phase could conflict with a long-term growth strategy.

On balance, Komelon sits at the intersection of three forces at once—export structure and FX sensitivity, governance issues, and capital allocation policy—and how the picture unfolds will depend on how each of these factors develops.
