Quarterly Turnaround Meets a Change of Control at C-Site

02

Business structure

C-Site was founded in 1999 as a knit apparel maker that produces mainly on an OEM basis, with some ODM work. It listed on KOSDAQ in 2023 after a merger with NH SPAC No. 28. Its main customers are overseas fashion brands including Gap, H&M and American Eagle.

The company said higher Old Navy orders and expanded license orders from Gap drove production growth in 2026. Production is based in Indonesia and Guatemala, and exports accounted for 94.39% of 2024 sales. The company said Indonesian plant utilization rose 150% year on year through the second quarter of 2026.

Gap still rates C-Site at its top vendor grade of Gold. In an August 2026 interview, President Seo Bong-su named finding new brands as the top priority and said production sites will focus on efficiency rather than new capacity for now.

Youngone and Hansae are comparison peers in the same textiles, fashion and lifestyle sector. The key swing factors for the business are customer concentration and U.S. apparel demand.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual revenue fell from KRW 185.6 billion in 2022 to KRW 152.3 billion in 2023 (down 17.9% year on year), recovered to KRW 173.9 billion in 2024 (up 14.2%), and was KRW 169.7 billion in 2025 (down 2.4%). Among 2022 to 2025, 2022 was the largest year and 2023 the smallest for revenue.

Operating profit went from KRW 13.2 billion in 2022 to KRW 780 million in 2023 (down 94.1%), KRW 1.25 billion in 2024 (up 60.7%) and KRW 151 million in 2025 (down 88.0%); 2022 was the largest year and 2025 the smallest for operating profit.

Net income attributable to owners moved from KRW 8.89 billion in 2022 to a loss of KRW 4.25 billion in 2023, a profit of KRW 195 million in 2024 and a loss of KRW 1.31 billion in 2025, while net income including non-controlling interests matched it in every year.

Management attributed the 2025 decline to a slowdown in U.S. apparel demand, lower orders from key brands, lower overseas plant utilization and higher raw material and logistics costs. It also said post-tariff costs on DDP-basis trade rose by at least 2.5 times, raising selling and administrative burdens.

In the fourth quarter of 2025, operating loss was KRW 780 million and net loss attributable to owners was KRW 1.19 billion, after operating profit of KRW 661 million in the third quarter.

Operating profit returned to the black in Q2 2026 at KRW 1.61 billion after a KRW 1.48 billion operating loss in Q1 2026, and reached KRW 2.01 billion in Q3 2026, up 203.6% year on year. Net loss attributable to owners in Q3 2026 was KRW 431 million, a swing back to loss from a year earlier.

Quarterly revenue rose for three consecutive quarters from Q1 to Q3 2026 to KRW 49 billion, although Q3 revenue was down 4.8% year on year. First-half 2026 revenue was KRW 79.1 billion, and the operating margin for full-year 2025 was 0.1%.

Equity at the end of 2025 was KRW 53.9 billion and total liabilities KRW 42.1 billion, with the debt ratio easing from 89.8% in 2022 to 78.2% in 2025. Operating cash flow fell from KRW 16.2 billion in 2022 to KRW 6.2 billion in 2025.

05

Industry analysis

C-Site's end market is U.S.-centered apparel demand. The company said higher U.S. tariffs, slower North American consumer spending and inventory adjustments by key customers raised uncertainty. Post-tariff DDP-basis trade costs, including freight, were cited as one reason for the 2025 margin squeeze.

The company described consumer sentiment as slowly recovering and major buyers' inventories as normalizing, a view that reflects management rather than an external industry index.

Apparel OEM makers typically see peak volume in the third-quarter fall and winter season, and the company expected higher production and sales in Q3. Order swings from Gap-family brands feed directly into production volume. Gap's top vendor grade is a point of strength in quality and delivery.

Youngone and Hansae, both overseas-production apparel makers in the same sector, serve as comparison peers. With exports at 94.39% of 2024 sales, the company is directly exposed to changes in U.S. demand.

06

Outlook

In an August 2026 interview, the company said it will focus on restoring profitability and cutting costs in the short term, and expanding brands and higher-value products over the medium to long term. It plans to raise production efficiency and the share of higher-value products as key customers' orders recover.

Finding and securing new brands is the top priority, while strategic ties with existing brands are maintained. Production sites will focus on improving efficiency of existing infrastructure rather than adding capacity for now, so utilization and productivity gains are the points to watch.

Governance change matters too: Gu Mi-hyun's group holds 59.77% as of November 12, 2026, and the company said her specific role has not yet been decided.

The CB and BW issuance limit was raised from KRW 50 billion to KRW 200 billion, but the actual size and timing of issuance must be confirmed through future disclosures. Q3 2026 revenue of KRW 49 billion came in line with the seasonal order growth the company had expected.

The next checkpoints are the Q4 2026 preliminary results and the 2026 annual report, where the operating margin's move from 0.1% is the key test.

Bull factors

Quarterly profit recovery

Operating profit moved back into the black in Q2 2026 at KRW 1.61 billion and rose 24.5% from the prior quarter to KRW 2.01 billion in Q3. Revenue increased for three consecutive quarters from Q1 to Q3 2026, reaching KRW 49 billion in Q3.

The company credited cost efficiency, restructuring and productivity gains for the return to net profit. The full-year 2025 operating margin of 0.1% remains low.

Customer production credibility

The company says Gap keeps C-Site at its top Gold vendor grade. Indonesian plant utilization rising 150% year on year through Q2 2026 is management's evidence that order recovery is reaching production. Making new brand acquisition the top priority also points toward reducing customer concentration.

Funding capacity and a consolidated controlling base

The largest shareholder's group now holds 59.77%, consolidating the controlling base. The CB and BW issuance limit was raised to KRW 200 billion, widening funding capacity. Market observers suggest Gu Mi-hyun could link fashion and beauty operations across the two listed companies she now controls.

Bear factors

Weakened earnings power

Full-year 2025 operating profit fell 88.0% to KRW 151 million, with a 0.1% operating margin. Operating losses appeared in Q4 2025 and Q1 2026, and three of the last four quarters showed net loss attributable to owners. Q3 2026 net loss of KRW 431 million was a swing back to loss from a year earlier. The recovery so far is concentrated in individual quarters, so annual earnings power remains limited.

Tariff and logistics costs, North American demand

Management said post-tariff DDP-basis trade costs rose at least 2.5 times. A slowdown in North American consumption and customer inventory adjustments have overlapped and kept revenue under pressure. First-half 2026 revenue fell 6% year on year.

Control change and trading volatility

C-Site was designated a caution-trading stock on July 1 and October 20, 2026. Under the market warning system, such designations can escalate to warning and risk stages. Disclosures of major shareholder stake changes have accompanied the control transition.

Risk factors

Customer concentration and order swings

Earnings are sensitive to orders from a few key customers, as the Old Navy order increase that lifted production shows. Supplying overseas brands such as Gap, H&M and American Eagle means customer inventory adjustments flow quickly into revenue.

New brands and plant utilization

Winning new brands is management's top priority, but if acquisitions are delayed, production stays tied to orders from existing key customers. The company said it will focus on improving efficiency of existing equipment at its Indonesian and Guatemala plants rather than adding capacity for now.

If new volume is insufficient to fill gaps when orders fall, gains in plant utilization could reverse. This could limit the pace of revenue recovery and operating margin improvement.

Board makeup and audit function

The board's makeup may change at the regular shareholders' meeting around March 2027, and two audit committee appointments were left undecided at the 2026 meeting under the 3% rule.

If audit committee appointments are delayed again, gaps in the board's audit function could weaken the reliability of financial disclosures and internal controls.

If changes to the board overlap with the largest shareholder's effort to set the business direction, the continuity of management decisions could also be affected.

Overall view

C-Site's operating profit turned positive in Q2 2026, and in Q3 both operating profit and sales rose together. Full-year 2025 operating profit, however, fell 88.0% to KRW 151 million with a 0.1% operating margin, so the extent of the earnings recovery is not yet visible in annual results.

Net loss attributable to owners over the last four quarters totals KRW 466 million, and the Q3 2026 result was a swing back to loss from a year earlier. Revenue rose for three consecutive quarters from Q1 to Q3 2026, but Q3 revenue was down 4.8% year on year.

Post-tariff DDP trade costs and softer North American consumption are the main external variables, and management is emphasizing profitability-focused management and customer diversification.
