02

Business structure

Founded in 1999, C-site is an apparel export specialist that combines original equipment manufacturing (OEM) and original design manufacturing (ODM) for knitwear. The company became a KOSDAQ-listed entity on December 21, 2023 through a reverse merger with NH SPAC 28.

Its customer base centers on global fast-fashion and casual brands such as GAP, OLD NAVY, H&M, American Eagle, TARGET and KOHLS, with relationships dating back to the start of business with GAP and OLD NAVY in 2000.

Production is based in Guatemala, chosen for its geographic proximity to the US market and competitive labor costs, and the company also operates a production subsidiary in Indonesia.

The company has said its Indonesian plant utilization rose 150% year over year through the second quarter of 2026, driven by higher Old Navy orders and expanded license-apparel orders.

Revenue is generated from customer orders and shipments, while yarn prices, labor costs, foreign-exchange rates, shipping costs and overseas plant utilization determine the gross margin.

Competitively, the company is often compared with larger apparel vendors such as Hansae and Youngone on order cycles and margin structure, with its knitwear expertise and Guatemala–Indonesia production footprint cited as points of differentiation.

In September 2026, the previous largest shareholder, TS 2018-12 M&A Investment Fund, began selling its stake as lock-up restrictions expired and the fund approached maturity, putting former Ourhome chairwoman Koo Mi-hyun's group on track to become the new largest shareholder.

If the deal is completed, the company will have changed hands in less than three years since listing, making the strategic direction under the new ownership a key point to watch.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual revenue fell from KRW 185.6 billion in 2022 to KRW 152.3 billion in 2023 (down 17.9%), rebounded to KRW 173.9 billion in 2024 (up 14.2%), then slipped again to KRW 169.7 billion in 2025 (down 2.4%). Among 2022–2025, 2022 was the largest revenue year and 2023 the smallest.

Operating income collapsed from KRW 13.2 billion in 2022 to KRW 780 million in 2023 (down 94.1%), rose to KRW 1.25 billion in 2024 (up 60.7%), then fell again to KRW 151 million in 2025 (down 88.0%), reflecting a significant thinning of profitability.

Net income attributable to owners swung from a KRW 8.89 billion profit in 2022 to a KRW 4.25 billion loss in 2023, back to a KRW 195 million profit in 2024, and then to a KRW 1.31 billion loss in 2025, showing considerable volatility.

The company attributed the 2025 swings to order declines tied to US tariff policy, higher export/import costs from a rising share of DDP sales, and higher selling and administrative expenses at overseas subsidiaries due to currency effects.

On a quarterly basis, revenue bottomed at KRW 34.1 billion in the fourth quarter of 2025 (down 33.8% quarter on quarter), then rose for two consecutive quarters to KRW 38.7 billion in the first quarter of 2026 and KRW 40.4 billion in the second quarter of 2026, though the latter was still down 3.3% year on year.

Operating losses widened from KRW 780 million in Q4 2025 to KRW 1.48 billion in Q1 2026 before swinging to an operating profit of KRW 1.61 billion in Q2 2026, up 466.1% year on year.

Net income attributable to owners over the trailing four quarters (Q3 2025 through Q2 2026) totaled KRW 843 million, a modest cumulative profit despite the quarter-to-quarter swings between profit and loss.

05

Industry analysis

The apparel OEM/ODM industry that C-site operates in is directly tied to the order cycles of US and European fast-fashion and casual brands. Inventory levels and consumer sentiment at major customers such as GAP, OLD NAVY, H&M, American Eagle, TARGET and KOHLS drive order volumes.

The sector has been sensitive to US consumption slowdowns and shifting tariff policy, and C-site itself cited North American consumption weakness and customer inventory adjustments as backdrops for weak results in 2025 and the first half of 2026.

Competitors include large apparel export vendors such as Hansae and Youngone, which are compared on scale and production diversification. On the production side, C-site operates subsidiaries in Guatemala and Indonesia, with Guatemala's proximity to the US market cited as an advantage.

The company has said that in 2026 its Indonesian plant's utilization rose 150% year over year, helped by higher Old Navy orders and expanded license-apparel orders.

Still, because the business is fundamentally a low-margin OEM order-taking model, margin pressure from fluctuations in yarn prices, labor costs and foreign-exchange rates remains structurally present.

06

Outlook

In its first-half 2026 earnings release, the company said that despite the revenue decline, it returned net income to a profit through cost efficiency, restructuring and productivity improvements.

A company official said it plans to closely monitor order flows from major customers in the second half, maximize seasonal peak effects, and continue to focus on improving profitability.

The company also said it aims to build a stable growth structure through customer portfolio diversification and production efficiency.

On a standalone basis, first-half 2026 revenue fell 12% year on year to KRW 71.3 billion, yet operating income rose 13% and net income turned profitable, with the parent entity's profitability improvement more pronounced than the consolidated results.

On the change-of-control front, four agenda items—articles of incorporation amendments and the election of in-house directors, independent directors and audit committee members—are slated for the extraordinary shareholders' meeting on November 6, 2026, with details to be finalized through subsequent board resolutions.

Under the deal structure, the KRW 45.36 billion balance payment is due the day before the meeting, and control transfers to former chairwoman Koo Mi-hyun's group upon payment, with the incumbent directors and CEO set to resign when the meeting concludes. Specific business strategy or new investment plans under the incoming ownership have not yet been disclosed.

Bull factors

Quarterly Earnings Swing to Profit

Operating income turned to a profit of KRW 1.61 billion in Q2 2026, up 466.1% year on year, and net income attributable to owners also swung to a profit of KRW 1.75 billion versus both the prior quarter and the year-ago period.

The company attributed the recovery to cost efficiency, restructuring and productivity improvements. On a standalone basis, operating income rose even as revenue declined, suggesting improvement at the parent level.

Financial Capacity of the Incoming Owner

In September 2026, former Ourhome chairwoman Koo Mi-hyun and five others agreed to acquire a 74.94% stake from the previous largest shareholder for KRW 50.4 billion. Koo has been pursuing acquisitions of KOSDAQ-listed companies using proceeds from her prior sale of Ourhome shares to the Hanwha group.

If the board is reshuffled at the November 6 extraordinary shareholders' meeting, the new ownership's management direction could become clearer. Specific business strategy, however, has not yet been disclosed.

Improving Overseas Plant Utilization

The company has said Indonesian plant utilization rose 150% year over year through the second quarter of 2026, driven by higher Old Navy orders and expanded license-apparel orders. The Guatemala production base retains a structural advantage from its proximity to the US market.

If order recovery continues, the resulting fixed-cost absorption could contribute to further margin improvement.

Bear factors

Structural Decline in Annual Profitability

Operating margin has stayed persistently low, at 7.1% in 2022, 0.5% in 2023, 0.7% in 2024 and 0.1% in 2025. Net income attributable to owners also swung to losses twice, in 2023 and 2025, reflecting high volatility. 2025 operating income of KRW 151 million was sharply reduced from KRW 13.2 billion in 2022.

This shows that the quarterly swing back to profit has not yet translated into a recovery in annual profitability.

Wide Swings in Quarterly Results

Operating losses widened to KRW 780 million in Q4 2025 and KRW 1.48 billion in Q1 2026. Revenue also swung sharply, falling 33.8% from KRW 51.5 billion in Q3 2025 to KRW 34.1 billion in Q4 2025. This pattern suggests results can be easily swayed by order timing and seasonality.

Cumulative net income attributable to owners over the trailing four quarters totaled only KRW 843 million, indicating limited underlying earnings power on a cumulative basis.

Short-Term Trading Overheating and Ownership Uncertainty

On October 19, 2026, the Korea Exchange designated C-site as an investment caution stock, citing an 85.04% price gain over the prior 15 trading days and excessive buying concentration among a small number of accounts.

The company had already been designated under the same reason once in the prior five trading days and twice in the prior fifteen. Even before the September disclosure of the ownership change, concentrated buying by a small number of accounts had drawn a regulatory inquiry request.

Because the control transfer is only completed after the balance payment and the extraordinary shareholders' meeting, governance-related uncertainty remains until that process concludes.

Risk factors

Ownership Change Risk

The change of largest shareholder to former Ourhome chairwoman Koo Mi-hyun's group still requires the balance payment and the extraordinary shareholders' meeting to be completed.

If the new director slate is approved, all incumbent directors and the CEO are set to resign, raising the possibility of a management transition gap. The incoming owner's business strategy or investment plans have not yet been specifically disclosed.

End-Demand and Tariff Risk

The company cited order declines linked to US tariff policy as a cause of weak 2025 results. It also said North American consumption weakness and inventory adjustments at major customers affected results in the first half of 2026.

Higher export/import costs from a growing share of DDP sales and increased overseas-subsidiary selling and administrative expenses from currency effects were also cited as margin pressures.

Market Surveillance and Volatility Risk

Since the end of June 2026, C-site has been repeatedly designated an investment caution stock for reasons such as concentrated trading in a small number of accounts and excessive buying involvement.

On October 19, 2026, it was again designated for an 85.04% price gain over 15 trading days and a 49.79% buying-involvement ratio among the top 20 accounts.

The Korea Exchange's market alert system escalates from investment caution to investment warning, investment risk, and finally trading suspension, so further escalation cannot be ruled out.
