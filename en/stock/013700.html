PC Order Growth and Profit Recovery; Customer Concentration Is the Key Issue

02

Business structure

Camus Engineering & Construction is a general construction company founded in 1978 whose core capability is the precast concrete (PC) method, in which structural members are made in a factory and assembled on site.

On the separate basis of the first-quarter 2026 quarterly report, building work accounted for 98.4% of sales, civil engineering for 0.7%, and other income, including office-leasing revenue, for 0.9%.

Building work covers general construction such as apartments, offices and logistics centers, plus the manufacture and installation of PC structural members, and its main customers are public institutions, project developers and general contractors.

Because members are produced in a factory, the method shortens site work periods and keeps quality uniform, and it has become a core construction method for semiconductor fab expansions since the 2020s. By customer, sales to SK ecoplant made up 32% of total sales in 2024 and 24% in 2023.

In July 2026 the company signed a KRW 66.4 billion PC contract for SK hynix's Cheongju P&T7 project through SK ecoplant, and in September 2026 it signed a contract of about KRW 195.7 billion for SK hynix's Yongin Cluster Phase 1 Ph-4 PC work.

The company also acquired 100% of an aluminum-foil and packaging maker, Hankuk Aluminum, at the end of 2023 to diversify its business, and that subsidiary was reported to have generated KRW 18.0 billion of sales in the first quarter of 2026.

Each order is large and the customer base is concentrated among a few major clients, so the business rests on PC technology and a small number of key client relationships.

04

Earnings analysis

On a disclosed annual basis, sales rose from KRW 195.1 billion in 2022 to KRW 278.8 billion in 2023 (up 42.9% year on year), fell to KRW 260.4 billion in 2024 (down 6.6%), and reached KRW 297.5 billion in 2025 (up 14.2%), the largest year in the 2022–2025 span.

Operating results moved from an operating loss of KRW 15.9 billion in 2022 to operating profit of KRW 6.65 billion in 2023, a loss of KRW 18.1 billion in 2024, and operating profit of KRW 23.8 billion in 2025. Operating margins were -8.1%, 2.4%, -7.0% and 8.0%, so 2025 had the highest margin of the four years.

Profit attributable to owners of the parent swung to KRW 20.1 billion in 2025 from a net loss of KRW 21.3 billion in 2024, while net profit including non-controlling interests was KRW 20 billion in 2025, a small difference.

In 2024 the company posted a net loss attributable to owners of the parent of KRW 21.3 billion and a net loss of KRW 21.9 billion, alongside operating cash outflow of KRW 64.9 billion.

Reports indicated that by the third quarter of 2024 receivables and short-term borrowings had risen alongside debt assumptions tied to project completion obligations.

The debt ratio went from 147.0% in 2022 to 192.7% in 2023, 189.6% in 2024 and 165.5% in 2025, when total equity was KRW 112.4 billion and total liabilities were KRW 185.9 billion. Operating cash flow turned positive at KRW 25.8 billion in 2025.

On a quarterly basis, sales were KRW 64.2 billion in the third quarter of 2025 with operating profit of KRW 5.46 billion, rose to KRW 115.6 billion in the fourth quarter (up 80.0% from the prior quarter) with operating profit of KRW 6.27 billion, then came to KRW 104.9 billion in the first quarter of 2026 with operating profit of KRW 11 billion (up 76.1%).

In the second quarter of 2026 sales were KRW 109.3 billion, operating profit KRW 12.2 billion and profit attributable to owners of the parent KRW 8.72 billion.

In the third quarter of 2026 sales fell to KRW 73.8 billion (down 32.4% from the prior quarter, up 15.0% year on year), operating profit to KRW 3.75 billion (down 69.3% from the prior quarter, down 31.4% year on year), and profit attributable to owners of the parent to KRW 1.48 billion (down 83.0% from the prior quarter).

Profit attributable to owners of the parent over the last four quarters totaled KRW 26.8 billion. Third-quarter sales grew from a year earlier while operating profit declined, so higher revenue did not translate into profit in that quarter. The specific causes of the third-quarter drop are to be traced through project progress rates and cost items.

05

Industry analysis

Demand for the PC method is tied to semiconductor capital spending. Blotter reported that expansions of semiconductor fabs in the 2020s increased PC construction volumes, and a 2025 filing-based report described rising memory demand from AI.

That picture assumes the facility investment cycle stays in an upswing, and if the cycle turns, order flow would be directly affected. Housing and property conditions have been less favorable, and a first-half 2025 report described sales declining amid a property slowdown and completions of construction sites.

The 2021–2022 slowdown in construction activity also led to delays and cost burdens that produced operating losses. In the 2026 ranking of construction contractors by capacity, the company was placed 97th, returning to the top 100 according to a report citing the Ministry of Land, Infrastructure and Transport.

Among 56 listed construction companies on an annual 2025 basis, Alphasquare's tabulation places the company 36th in sales and 10th in operating margin. Its sales scale is in the lower-middle range while its profitability ranks higher.

Civil engineering accounts for only 0.7% of sales, so its order structure differs from civil-focused contractors, and its heavy weighting toward semiconductor facility work makes facility investment trends a key sector variable.

06

Outlook

The largest recent contract is the SK hynix Yongin Cluster Phase 1 Ph-4 PC work signed in September 2026, with a contract period from August 27, 2026 to May 31, 2028.

There is no down payment or advance payment, payment is made on a progress-billing basis, and the company has said the contract amount and period may change as work proceeds.

The Cheongju P&T7 PC contract of KRW 66.4 billion, signed in July 2026, runs from July 1, 2026 to June 30, 2028, with payment tied to construction progress.

The backlog of KRW 382.4 billion is as of the end of the first quarter of 2026, so the July and September contracts were added after that figure, and the year-end backlog in the 2026 annual report is the next checkpoint.

Disclosed order tables list completion deadlines of December 31, 2026 for the Pyeongtaek P5 complex building and July 31, 2027 for the Hynix Y1 UT project. Whether those deadlines hold and how progress proceeds will determine the pace of revenue recognition.

At its March 26, 2026 general shareholders meeting, the company approved its 47th financial statements and resolved to pay a cash dividend. Diversification through the aluminum subsidiary Hankuk Aluminum is ongoing.

In October 2024 the company disclosed debt assumptions of KRW 175 billion for a logistics center project in Anseong after missing its completion-guarantee deadline, and said it planned to repay the project-financing loans from sale proceeds after completion. Completion of that site and progress on repaying those loans will shape the next phase of financial burden.

08

Bull factors

Back-to-back large semiconductor PC orders

The company signed a KRW 66.4 billion contract in July 2026 and a contract of about KRW 195.7 billion in September 2026, both for SK hynix-related PC work. The backlog was KRW 382.4 billion at the end of the first quarter of 2026. Semiconductor facility work supports the order flow. Contract values and periods may change as work proceeds.

Improving balance sheet and cash flow

The debt ratio fell from 189.6% in 2024 to 165.5% in 2025. Blotter reported that the ratio declined further to 152.8% in the first-quarter 2026 analysis.

Net construction receivables fell from KRW 56 billion at the end of 2025 to KRW 16.5 billion at the end of the first quarter of 2026, which was reported as easing cash-flow pressure. Operating cash flow turned positive at KRW 25.8 billion in 2025.

Operating margin in positive territory

The operating margin moved from -7.0% in 2024 into positive territory at 8.0% in 2025. Operating profit reached KRW 11 billion in the first quarter of 2026, up 76.1% from the prior quarter, and one report said the margin exceeded 10%. Operating profit rose again to KRW 12.2 billion in the second quarter of 2026. This trend is cited as evidence of margin recovery.

Bear factors

Heavy reliance on key customers

Sales to SK ecoplant equaled 32% of total sales in 2024. The September 2026 contract of about KRW 195.7 billion was reported to equal 65.8% of 2025 sales. Earnings can swing widely depending on the timing of orders and progress from a single major client. The company has also said contract values and periods may change during construction.

Sharp drop in third-quarter 2026 profit

Third-quarter 2026 sales fell 32.4% from the prior quarter to KRW 73.8 billion. Operating profit dropped 69.3% to KRW 3.75 billion, and profit attributable to owners of the parent fell 83.0% to KRW 1.48 billion.

The four-quarter total of KRW 26.8 billion was built on uneven quarters, which reads as a weakness in stability.

Past debt assumptions and cash-flow deterioration

In October 2024 the company disclosed debt assumptions of KRW 175 billion after a completion-guarantee deadline was missed. Cumulative interest expense in the first three quarters of 2024 was KRW 4.2 billion. Operating cash outflow reached KRW 64.9 billion in 2024.

Even with improved financial indicators, completion management and project loan repayment remain items to watch.

10

Risk factors

Subsidiary diversification

Camus acquired 100% of aluminum-foil and packaging maker Hankuk Aluminum at the end of 2023 and is pursuing business diversification.

Hankuk Aluminum was reported to have generated KRW 18.0 billion in sales in the first quarter of 2026, but that is still small compared with the company's overall sales, which are dominated by building work.

If the subsidiary's profitability or its integration into the business does not proceed as planned, the diversification benefit could reach reported earnings later.

Housing market and building orders

Residential building is part of the company's building work, and housing and property conditions were cited as a reason for lower sales in the first half of 2025. If the housing market weakens further and developer orders decline, building order flow could be affected.

Semiconductor facility orders and housing orders follow different cycles, so whether semiconductor orders can fill any gap left by housing orders will determine how well the backlog holds.

Meeting construction deadlines

The completion deadlines are December 31, 2026 for the Pyeongtaek P5 complex building and July 31, 2027 for the Hynix Y1 UT project, as listed in the disclosed order table. If these deadlines are missed, revenue recognition could shift later and the company could again face completion-obligation burdens. Both sites are large orders, so any delay could feed directly into quarterly sales.

12

Overall view

Camus Engineering & Construction returned to annual operating profit of KRW 23.8 billion in 2025, and quarterly operating profit rose through the first quarter of 2026, showing a clear direction of recovery.

Semiconductor-related PC orders and a backlog of KRW 382.4 billion at the end of the first quarter of 2026 support that recovery. The debt ratio fell to 165.5% in 2025 and receivables declined, so the balance sheet improved.

However, third-quarter 2026 sales, operating profit and profit attributable to owners of the parent all fell sharply from the prior quarter, leaving the stability of quarterly earnings an open question. Customer concentration and the 2024 debt-assumption history carry comparable weight.

The next checkpoints are the year-end backlog in the 2026 annual report and the operating margin in the first quarter of 2027, with project completion timing a key variable.
