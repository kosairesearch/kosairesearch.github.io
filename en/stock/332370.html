02

Business structure

IDP, founded in 2005, designs, manufactures and sells desktop card printers and related peripherals and consumables, and listed on KOSDAQ in August 2020.

The company says it developed its printers in-house at a level comparable to competitors in the United States, Japan and Europe, and that it exports to 58 countries according to its company profile.

An Alphasquare company summary instead cites 56 countries and about 87 percent of revenue from exports, so the country count differs between sources.

In the same summary, consumables account for about 47.7 percent of revenue, card printers about 42.1 percent, other products about 7.0 percent, and goods and services together about 3.3 percent; the date of that snapshot is not confirmed. Card printers and their consumables therefore make up most of revenue.

Consumables generate recurring sales from the installed base, which supports earnings stability. The main demand comes from on-site ID card issuance, and a 2021 research report also described desktop card printers for on-site issuance as the core product.

Revenue has been widened through retransfer printers and dedicated consumables, and Alphasquare puts the global share at 6.6 percent in 2020. Competition centers on new product launches and price competitiveness against overseas rivals. The company holds one subsidiary.

Its structure differs from component-focused peers in the electronics sector such as Samsung Electro-Mechanics and LG Innotek, so direct comparison is limited.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual revenue fell from KRW 43.1 billion in 2022 to KRW 36.4 billion in 2023 (down 15.6 percent), then rose to KRW 46.5 billion in 2024 (up 27.9 percent) and KRW 49.1 billion in 2025 (up 5.5 percent), growing for two consecutive years from 2024 to 2025.

Among 2022 to 2025, 2025 was the largest year and 2023 the smallest. Operating profit dropped from KRW 12.4 billion in 2022 to KRW 5.53 billion in 2023 (down 55.3 percent), recovered to KRW 11.1 billion in 2024 (up 100.4 percent), and eased to KRW 10.9 billion in 2025 (down 1.9 percent).

The operating margin went from 28.7 percent in 2022 to 15.2 percent in 2023, then 23.8 percent in 2024 and 22.2 percent in 2025, so margins recovered from the 2023 low and stayed in the low 20s.

Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders was largest in 2024 at KRW 11.5 billion (up 91.6 percent), and in 2025 it was KRW 10.3 billion (down 10.2 percent). Net profit including non-controlling interests was also KRW 10.3 billion in 2025, essentially the same level.

Operating cash flow rose from KRW 2.12 billion in 2022 to KRW 14.5 billion in 2024, then fell to KRW 10.7 billion in 2025. Quarterly revenue rose from KRW 11.1 billion in Q3 2025 to KRW 12.7 billion in Q4 (up 14.3 percent quarter on quarter), while operating profit fell to KRW 1.21 billion (down 46.5 percent).

In Q1 2026 revenue dipped to KRW 11.1 billion (down 12.1 percent), but operating profit rebounded to KRW 2.28 billion (up 89.3 percent). Revenue reached KRW 14.2 billion in Q3 2026 (up 28.3 percent year on year) and operating profit KRW 4.6 billion (up 103.7 percent year on year).

Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders in the same quarter was KRW 2.34 billion, down 5.7 percent year on year and not keeping pace with operating profit. The sum of net profit attributable to controlling shareholders over the last four quarters was KRW 9.54 billion.

The items that separate operating profit from net profit sit in non-operating income and tax lines, which appear in the detailed quarterly report accounts.

05

Industry analysis

The card printer market depends on on-site demand for ID cards, access badges and membership cards. In a summary of first-half 2025 results, rising security demand was cited as a driver of the improvement.

Wider use of digital identity verification could partly substitute physical card demand, which is a variable for the industry. The product model moves equipment and consumables together, and consumable sales tend to follow as the installed base grows.

The company says its printers match competitors in the United States, Japan and Europe, so differentiation rests on price competitiveness and the pace of new launches. A 6.6 percent global share in 2020 places the company in a mid-sized position in a market where leaders hold larger shares.

In Alphasquare's annual sector comparison for computer peripherals, the operating margin sits well above the sector average, while revenue scale ranks lower in the same group, so profitability and scale diverge.

Industry indicators that link demand directly to quarterly revenue were not established, so the industry read stays qualitative. Because overseas sales are a large share, foreign demand and exchange rates form the main axis of the industry read.

Its business structure differs from large electronic component makers such as Samsung Electro-Mechanics and LG Innotek, so they are not easily compared on the same industry indicators.

Quarterly revenue is volatile, however, falling from KRW 12.7 billion in Q4 2025 to KRW 11.1 billion in Q1 2026, so whether the Q3 level holds is the open question.

Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders has declined for two quarters, so whether operating gains carry through to net profit remains to be confirmed.

Q4 2026 results have not yet been released, so whether fourth-quarter revenue holds near the third-quarter level is the next checkpoint. In the 2026 annual report, the full-year operating margin against 22.2 percent in 2025 is the main annual check.

08

Bull factors

Margin recovery and quarterly profit rebound

The operating margin recovered from 15.2 percent in 2023 to 22.2 percent in 2025. Operating profit rose for three consecutive quarters in 2026, to KRW 2.28 billion in Q1, KRW 2.59 billion in Q2 and KRW 4.6 billion in Q3, with Q3 up 103.7 percent year on year. Operating profit rising alongside revenue is cited as the core of the bullish case.

Low debt and steady cash generation

Total liabilities at end-2025 were KRW 5.14 billion and the debt ratio was 7.4 percent, down from 13.6 percent in 2022. Operating cash flow was KRW 14.5 billion in 2024 and KRW 10.7 billion in 2025, above KRW 10 billion in both years. Financial capacity is cited as a buffer in a slowdown.

Low multiple against book value and export base

The price-to-book ratio sits below 1x, so the share price is low relative to equity attributable to controlling shareholders. The company says it exports to 58 countries, and Alphasquare puts overseas sales at about 87 percent of revenue. A wider overseas installed base is cited as a medium-term driver of consumable sales.

Bear factors

Stalled net profit attributable to controlling shareholders

Q3 was down 5.7 percent year on year, in contrast to operating profit up 103.7 percent. Full-year 2025 net profit attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 10.3 billion, down 10.2 percent.

Revenue volatility and memory of low points

Revenue fell 15.6 percent in 2023 and operating profit fell 55.3 percent. Q4 2025 operating profit fell 46.5 percent quarter on quarter to KRW 1.21 billion, and Q1 2026 revenue fell 12.1 percent quarter on quarter. Repeated swings at the quarterly level make it hard to judge whether the Q3 2026 recovery will persist.

Overseas dependence and slower cash flow

Alphasquare puts about 87 percent of revenue from overseas, which leaves the company exposed to foreign demand and exchange rates. Competitors in the United States, Japan and Europe mean price competition could pressure margins. Operating cash flow fell from KRW 14.5 billion in 2024 to KRW 10.7 billion in 2025.

10

Risk factors

Shareholder structure and float

A January 2025 media report said that family-owned and related-party holdings linked to IDIS had lifted their IDP stake from 45.20 percent to 46.18 percent. Concentrated ownership limits the number of shares available for trading, which can affect price volatility and liquidity. Any changes since then should be checked against the latest shareholder disclosures.

Overseas demand and currency

Most revenue comes from overseas, so local demand and exchange rate moves feed directly into profitability. A shift toward digital ID formats could reduce demand for physical cards. With competitors described as comparable in product performance, the operating margin depends on the ability to hold prices.

Quarterly earnings swings and profit conversion

Operating profit was low at KRW 1.21 billion in Q4 2025, and revenue fell to KRW 11.1 billion in Q1 2026. Operating profit rose to KRW 4.6 billion in Q3 2026, yet net profit attributable to controlling shareholders stayed at KRW 2.34 billion, leaving a wide gap between the two. Whether this gap is temporary or structural is not yet clear.

Overall view

IDP is a KOSDAQ company with an export-oriented structure built on card printers and consumables. Annual revenue rose from KRW 43.1 billion in 2022 to KRW 49.1 billion in 2025, and the operating margin recovered from a 15.2 percent low in 2023 to 22.2 percent in 2025.

Operating profit rose for three consecutive quarters in 2026, with Q3 at KRW 4.6 billion the largest among the disclosed quarters. Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders, however, declined for two quarters after KRW 2.88 billion in Q1 2026, diverging from the operating trend.

On the financial side, total liabilities of KRW 5.14 billion and a 7.4 percent debt ratio at end-2025 indicate a small burden.

Valuation reads as a price-to-book ratio below 1x and a dividend yield below the sector average, and the last four quarters of net profit attributable to controlling shareholders total KRW 9.54 billion.

Bullish factors are margin recovery and financial capacity, while bearish factors are stalled net profit and overseas demand and quarterly volatility. The next checkpoints are the provisional Q4 2026 results and the 2026 annual report.
