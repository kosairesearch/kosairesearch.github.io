02

Business structure

NFC was founded in 2012 as a maker of cosmetic materials and contract manufacturer of finished products, and listed on KOSDAQ in 2020. The business spans three segments: finished products, merchandise (resale of cosmetic raw materials), and materials.

By segment, finished products accounted for 67%, merchandise 17%, and materials 16% of revenue in the second quarter of 2026. In the first quarter of 2026, finished products generated KRW 14.9 billion, or 67.3% of revenue, materials KRW 4.3 billion (19.5%), and merchandise KRW 2.7 billion.

A company profile lists a planned 2025 revenue mix of roughly 30% materials and 70% ODM/OEM, so the plan and the actual segment mix are worth reading side by side. In-house development and production of materials is cited as the basis for cost competitiveness and quality differentiation.

Patented solid-balm formats and a patented two-core multi-balm stick format are presented as the core of the finished-product offering. On the technology side, the company holds high-content ceramide stabilization, nanoliposome and fermentation technologies, and 14 domestic patents.

On the customer side, North America is the main target market and customer expansion there is under way. Compared with large domestic ODM players such as Cosmax and Kolmar Korea, NFC is smaller in scale, so its differentiation rests on specific formats and in-house materials.

04

Earnings analysis

On a disclosed annual basis, revenue rose from KRW 31.5 billion in 2022 to KRW 42.9 billion in 2023 (up 36.2% year on year), fell to KRW 40.2 billion in 2024 (down 6.2%), and then jumped to KRW 71.6 billion in 2025 (up 78.1%). Among 2022 to 2025, 2025 was the largest year and 2022 the smallest.

Operating profit was KRW 2.27 billion in 2022 and KRW 4.07 billion in 2023 (up 79.0%), then swung to an operating loss of KRW 7.09 billion in 2024 before returning to an operating profit of KRW 12.5 billion in 2025.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 1.87 billion in 2022 and KRW 3.68 billion in 2023 (up 96.4%), moved to a net loss of KRW 5.24 billion in 2024, and returned to KRW 10.1 billion in 2025.

Net income including non-controlling interests is reported as a separate line and should be read apart from the controlling-shareholder figure. The operating margin went from 7.2% in 2022 and 9.5% in 2023 to -17.6% in 2024, then recovered to 17.5% in 2025.

The debt-to-equity ratio rose from 28.2% in 2022 to 40.8% in 2025. At year-end 2025, total equity was KRW 65.4 billion, all of it attributable to controlling shareholders, and total liabilities were KRW 26.7 billion.

Operating cash flow was KRW 3 billion in 2022, KRW -8.83 billion in 2023, KRW 8.14 billion in 2024, and KRW 1.26 billion in 2025, with wide swings year to year.

Quarterly, revenue was KRW 18.2 billion in the third quarter of 2025 with operating profit of KRW 2.95 billion; in the fourth quarter it rose to KRW 25.3 billion (up 39.2% from the prior quarter) with operating profit of KRW 5.08 billion (up 72.4%).

In the first quarter of 2026, revenue was KRW 22.2 billion (down 12.4% quarter on quarter) and operating profit KRW 4.03 billion (down 20.8%), before the second quarter recovered to KRW 26.9 billion in revenue and KRW 5 billion in operating profit.

The third quarter of 2026 recorded revenue of KRW 23.1 billion (down 14.2% quarter on quarter, up 26.8% year on year), operating profit of KRW 4.48 billion (down 10.4% quarter on quarter, up 51.8% year on year), and controlling-shareholder net income of KRW 3.51 billion (up 40.6% year on year).

Controlling-shareholder net income over the latest four quarters, from the fourth quarter of 2025 to the third quarter of 2026, totaled KRW 14.5 billion.

In September 2026, Hana Securities projected third-quarter revenue of KRW 26.8 billion and operating profit of KRW 4.8 billion, and the disclosed third-quarter figures came in below that projection.

05

Industry analysis

The cosmetics ODM and OEM market has grown as brand companies outsource more development and production. Demand in this market depends heavily on brand companies' channel strategies and overseas sales flows. A past case illustrates this.

According to a December 2022 securities report, NFC's largest customer suffered weak duty-free and China sales that began in the fourth quarter of 2021, and sales to that customer fell sharply. Reliance on one channel therefore passes demand swings straight through to results.

Since then the company has added new customers, including top-tier domestic brands, and widened its focus toward North America.

In competition, large ODM players such as Cosmax and Kolmar Korea anchor the market with scale and R&D capacity, while smaller players typically differentiate through specific formats or materials technology. NFC competes in the middle ground, using solid-balm formats and in-house materials as its differentiators.

R&D spending was KRW 1.43 billion in 2024, or 3.55% of revenue. The industry is best read through customer demand and channel shifts.

Low utilization leaves room for fixed-cost absorption if revenue rises, but the extent depends on the size of customer orders. The company is pursuing customer expansion with North America as its main target market.

The materials share was 16% in the second quarter of 2026, which differs from the roughly 30% materials mix in the 2025 company profile.

Whether that gap reflects lower materials sales or faster finished-product growth should be read from segment disclosures in the annual report. Full-year 2026 results will be confirmed only when the annual report is filed.

08

Bull factors

Earnings base recovered from loss to profit

Operating profit turned to KRW 12.5 billion in 2025, and controlling-shareholder net income returned to KRW 10.1 billion. Quarterly operating profit stayed in the KRW 4–5 billion range from the first to the third quarter of 2026. Controlling-shareholder net income over the latest four quarters totaled KRW 14.5 billion. The recovery from the 2024 loss is cited as a bullish factor.

North America customer expansion and format differentiation

The company has set North America as its main target and is widening its customer base there. One analysis links the growth of North American customers to distinctive formats such as cleansing balms. Second-quarter 2026 revenue rose 21.1% from the prior quarter to KRW 26.9 billion. If this continues, it can be read as an effect of format differentiation.

In-house materials and patent base

In-house development and production of materials is cited as the basis for cost competitiveness and quality differentiation. Patents cover solid-balm and multi-balm stick formats, and a company profile lists 14 domestic patents. R&D spending was 3.55% of revenue in 2024. If these technologies underpin ODM wins, the bullish case becomes more concrete.

Bear factors

Third-quarter results below broker projection

In September 2026 Hana Securities projected third-quarter revenue of KRW 26.8 billion and operating profit of KRW 4.8 billion. Disclosed figures were KRW 23.1 billion in revenue and KRW 4.48 billion in operating profit, both below the projection.

In the same quarter revenue fell 14.2% and operating profit fell 10.4% from the prior quarter. The low predictability of quarterly profit is a bearish point.

Low utilization and fixed-cost burden

Manufacturing utilization was 38.1% and filling and packaging utilization 41.3% at the end of the first quarter of 2026. Spare capacity means room to grow, but without order growth fixed costs weigh on results. Operating cash flow also swung from KRW -8.83 billion in 2023 to KRW 1.26 billion in 2025. These factors are cited as bearish.

Customer concentration and earnings volatility

A December 2022 securities report noted that the largest customer accounted for 50% of sales, and that customer's weak sales led to a revenue decline over four quarters. In 2024 revenue fell 6.2% and the company swung to an operating loss of KRW 7.09 billion.

The 2025 turnaround rested on a 78.1% revenue increase, so a renewed revenue drop could quickly weigh on earnings.

Risk factors

Earnings volatility

Annual results swung from an operating loss of KRW 7.09 billion in 2024 to an operating profit of KRW 12.5 billion in 2025. Quarterly revenue also fell from KRW 25.3 billion in the fourth quarter of 2025 to KRW 22.2 billion in the first quarter of 2026. If quarterly trends weaken, sustaining the annual profit trend becomes harder.

Customer concentration and channel dependence

In the past, weak sales at a single largest customer, which accounted for a large share of revenue, fed directly into weaker results. Expansion among North American customers is now the stated priority. Until new customers add meaningful volume, exposure to demand shifts at specific customers or channels remains.

Rising leverage and major-shareholder stake

The debt-to-equity ratio rose from 28.2% in 2022 to 40.8% in 2025, and total liabilities at year-end 2025 were KRW 26.7 billion. Major shareholders and related parties hold 50.62%, leaving a free-float ratio of 49.38%. A high major-shareholder stake limits the tradable share count, which can amplify price moves.
