02

Business structure

Ajinextek is a precision motion control company that localizes and supplies controllers for automation equipment, including semiconductor, smartphone and secondary battery equipment. Its core product is the motion controller (GMC), which accounts for most of its sales.

Semiconductor applications are growing, and the company is expanding into secondary battery and robot OEM production. The export share of its robot controller segment was 50.7% in 2024, above domestic sales at 49.3%.

The company launched four new products in March 2025 and began full sales in June 2025, and it says a larger share of new products improved the quality of its sales mix. At the end of 2025 it consolidated its Gumi plant to streamline production, and it reduced purchase costs through supply chain management.

Supply of robot components to large customers, including Samsung Electronics, has expanded according to press reports.

Unlike full-equipment makers such as Hanmi Semiconductor and EO Technics, which book revenue on equipment orders, Ajinextek supplies control components built into equipment, so its earnings link indirectly to the timing of capital spending by chipmakers.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual sales fell from KRW 35.7 billion in 2022 to KRW 24.9 billion in 2023 (down 30.3% year on year), rose to KRW 25.2 billion in 2024 (up 1.1%), and then declined to KRW 24.6 billion in 2025 (down 2.2%). Among 2022 to 2025, 2022 was the largest year and 2025 the smallest.

Operating profit of KRW 2.06 billion in 2022 became an operating loss of KRW 913 million in 2023, a turn to deficit. The operating loss widened to KRW 4.19 billion in 2024, a continued deficit with larger losses. In 2025 operating profit was KRW 963 million, a turn back to profit.

Operating margin ran at 5.8% in 2022, -3.7% in 2023, -16.6% in 2024 and 3.9% in 2025. Net income attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 1.63 billion in 2022, KRW 494 million in 2023 (down 69.7%), a net loss of KRW 2.84 billion in 2024, and KRW 837 million in 2025.

Net income including non-controlling interests is reported with the same values in each year. The company attributes the 2025 turnaround to consolidating its Gumi plant, lower purchase costs, and reduced inventory with a more realistic inventory valuation.

Operating cash flow was -KRW 1.9 billion in 2022, -KRW 752 million in 2023, KRW 562 million in 2024 and KRW 2.07 billion in 2025, turning positive from 2024. In Q3 2025 sales were KRW 6.25 billion and operating profit KRW 1.47 billion.

Q4 2025 sales rose 25.6% quarter on quarter to KRW 7.84 billion, and operating profit rose 16.9% to KRW 1.72 billion. Q1 2026 sales fell 5.4% to KRW 7.42 billion and operating profit dropped 88.4% to KRW 199 million. Q2 2026 operating margin was 20.4%, up sharply from 2.7% in Q1.

Q2 2026 sales were KRW 8.87 billion, up 19.6% quarter on quarter, with operating profit of KRW 1.81 billion. Q3 2026 sales were KRW 10.8 billion, up 21.9% quarter on quarter and 73.2% year on year, with operating profit of KRW 2.27 billion and controlling shareholders' net income of KRW 2.14 billion.

About 90% of first-half 2026 operating profit came in Q2. Controlling shareholders' net income over the last four quarters totaled KRW 5.56 billion.

05

Industry analysis

Ajinextek's earnings are sensitive to capital spending cycles at downstream chipmakers and display makers. Capital expenditure cuts by those customers drove lower sales and temporary losses in 2023 and 2024.

Reports also link the 2023 to 2024 operating weakness to delays in smart factory investment in semiconductors and displays. The recovery since then has rested on manufacturing efficiency and expanded supply of robot components.

Press reports describe the company as expanding into the market for manufacturing robot controllers. The company sees the physical AI motion control segment, which executes AI decisions in the physical world, entering a phase of wider adoption.

Technically, the company is described as holding core technology in domestic motion control chips and controllers. Compared with full-equipment makers in the same semiconductor sector, its quarterly results move with the timing of controller orders rather than equipment order cycles.

Whether investment plans from large chip customers such as Samsung Electronics and SK hynix translate into controller orders is the key variable for the industry outlook.

Outlook

The company plans to expand high-value controllers for AI-driven automation in 2026. It intends to extend into the high-end motion control market and industrial robots. It also plans to strengthen its medium- to long-term growth base as physical AI spreads.

A joint development project based on motion control for robotics and logistics automation is reportedly under review. First-half 2026 sales reached KRW 16.3 billion, operating profit KRW 2.01 billion and net income KRW 1.95 billion, showing a turn to profit for the half year.

The new shares from the 1-for-1 bonus issue decided in January 2026 were disclosed for listing on February 13, 2026. The treasury share cancellation at the end of 2025 is described as part of the shareholder return policy. The next checkpoint is the Q4 2026 and full-year preliminary results.

The full-year 2025 results were announced on February 3, 2026, so a similar timing for the 2026 results is the next checkpoint. The full-year 2026 operating margin, confirmed in the 2026 annual report, will show whether it stays above or holds near the 3.9% recorded in 2025.

The company attributes the turnaround to structural changes, including the Gumi plant consolidation and lower purchase costs. Q3 2026 operating profit of KRW 2.27 billion is the largest of the last five quarters on a reported basis.

Expansion into robots and physical AI

The company plans to apply its core technology to physical AI motion control, which executes AI decisions in the physical world. Expanded supply of robot components to large customers including Samsung Electronics has also been reported.

Financial capacity and shareholder return execution

Bear factors

Volatility in annual results

Sales fell from KRW 35.7 billion in 2022 to KRW 24.6 billion in 2025, and operating and net losses continued through 2023 and 2024. The 2025 profit is a single year, so the profitable period is still short.

Quarterly results also swing widely, with operating profit moving from KRW 199 million in Q1 2026 to KRW 1.81 billion in Q2.

Sensitivity of the share price to themes and flows

The stock closed limit-up after the bonus issue decision and growth hopes for robotics, and in May 2026 it was designated an investment warning stock after a sharp rally. One analyst described the price moves as overshooting driven by thematic trading rather than fundamentals.

Q1 trough and reliance on capex cycles

Operating profit in Q1 2026 was KRW 199 million, a 2.7% margin. Losses in 2023 and 2024 followed capex cuts by downstream customers, so a slowdown in their investment cycle could repeat that pattern.

Risk factors

Customer and downstream capex concentration

Delays in smart factory investment by semiconductor and display customers were cited as a cause of losses in 2023 and 2024. A cut in customer investment plans can quickly reduce controller orders and widen sales swings. How far robot component growth offsets this will show up in quarterly sales after 2026.

Regulatory and short-term overheating risk

The exchange announced a short-term overheating designation and a warning designation notice on May 7, 2026, and confirmed the investment warning on May 8. Warning measures such as single-price trading and credit restrictions can limit speculative demand and add to price volatility.

Export exposure

The export share of the robot controller segment was 50.7% in 2024, above domestic sales. Changes in overseas customer investment, exchange rates or trade conditions could affect the sales mix. Given the high export share, these external factors need to be tracked through quarterly results.

Overall view

Ajinextek's sales declined after 2022, it posted losses in 2023 and 2024, and it returned to an annual operating profit in 2025. In 2026, sales and operating profit rose together in Q2 and Q3 after the Q1 low, which points to better quarterly earnings.

However, only 2025 is a full profitable year and 2026 has three quarters so far, so earnings durability has been tested over a short period. Strengths include the turn to profit, expansion into robots and physical AI, and a low debt ratio of 9.6%.
