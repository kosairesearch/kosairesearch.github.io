02

Business structure

YAS is a manufacturer of deposition equipment for OLED display panels, founded in 2002 and listed on KOSDAQ in 2017. The company is reported to be the only one in the world with mass-production experience in both 8th-generation-and-above deposition systems and evaporation sources.

Its main products are deposition systems for large TV panels of 8th generation and above, evaporation sources for small and medium panels of 6th generation and below, and 5th-generation lighting equipment.

Revenue is described as weighted toward equipment refurbishment and maintenance parts rather than new equipment sales.

In June 2026 YAS signed a single-sale supply contract for display evaporation sources worth about KRW 12.4 billion with Guangzhou China Star Optoelectronics Printing Display Technology, equal to roughly 35.74 percent of 2025 revenue.

Media reports say the Paju Fab3 and Fab4 plants are running at full capacity, and the company is preparing Fab4 for next-generation panel production, though reports differ on whether that generation is 8.6 or 10.5.

Customers are centered on Chinese panel makers, while LG Display and BOE are also named as major panel customers. Competition overlaps with domestic OLED deposition equipment maker Sunic System.

04

Earnings analysis

On a reported basis, annual revenue fell from KRW 54.8 billion in 2022 to KRW 42 billion in 2023 (down 23.4 percent year on year) and to KRW 28.6 billion in 2024 (down 31.8 percent), then recovered to KRW 34.8 billion in 2025 (up 21.4 percent). Among 2022 to 2025, 2022 was the largest year and 2024 the smallest.

The operating loss was KRW 4.83 billion in 2022, KRW 5.44 billion in 2023, KRW 9.66 billion in 2024 and KRW 6.46 billion in 2025, a loss in each of the four years, with the loss narrowing in 2025.

The operating margin was -8.8 percent in 2022, -12.9 percent in 2023, -33.7 percent in 2024 and -18.6 percent in 2025, bottoming in 2024 and improving in 2025. The controlling-shareholder net loss widened from KRW 845 million in 2022 to KRW 9.99 billion in 2024 and narrowed to KRW 5.51 billion in 2025.

Net loss including non-controlling interests was largest in 2024 at KRW 10.5 billion. The company cited a change in profit structure from progress-based revenue recognition as the main reason for the 2025 improvement.

Quarterly, the third quarter of 2025 had revenue of KRW 10.6 billion and an operating loss of KRW 24.87 million, and the fourth quarter swung to an operating profit of KRW 2.44 billion on revenue of KRW 13.9 billion.

In the first quarter of 2026 revenue was KRW 32.6 billion with operating profit of KRW 5.18 billion, and in the second quarter revenue jumped to KRW 87.2 billion with operating profit of KRW 28 billion.

The provisional second-quarter release, issued on July 30, 2026, showed controlling-shareholder net income of KRW 27.18 billion, while the regular-report figure is KRW 28.4 billion.

In the third quarter, revenue was KRW 40.3 billion, but operating profit fell to KRW 694 million and the controlling-shareholder result turned to a net loss of KRW 12.4 billion.

The cause of the sharp third-quarter drop cannot be pinned down from reported figures alone and should be checked against the timing of revenue recognition and cost structure in the 2026 annual report. For reference, in 2018 the company posted revenue of KRW 184.5 billion and operating profit of KRW 44.2 billion.

05

Industry analysis

China's resumed OLED investment is the central variable for end demand. Reports say that renewed Chinese OLED investment has lifted equipment orders for YAS. Those revenue projections come from first-half 2026 coverage and are not firm company guidance.

Industry estimates put the TCL CSOT 8.6-generation line order at around KRW 300 billion, but the company has not disclosed the contract amount. Earlier, investment delays by Chinese panel makers BOE and LG Display were cited as a drag on profitability.

Whether panel investment continues through this cycle will largely determine YAS order flow. Against peers, YAS emphasizes its experience in mass producing both 8th-generation-and-above deposition systems and evaporation sources.

It competes with Sunic System, and disclosed data give only a limited view of market share by customer. High customer concentration means investment decisions at a few panel makers flow directly into results.

Because deposition equipment is a large capital expenditure, whether order disclosures continue steadily is the starting point for judging the cycle.

06

Outlook

Near-term results depend on whether order disclosures continue. The June 2026 evaporation source contract reached the end of its contract period on November 6, 2026, so follow-on supply contract disclosures are the indicator to watch for the next revenue stream.

On the capacity side, reports say the Paju Fab5 expansion will run alongside higher production capacity. No confirmed schedule for the start of operations has been disclosed. An early-2026 brokerage report said new orders could rise substantially if customers' equipment orders materialize.

That view depends on orders being realized and is not yet verified. Cumulative revenue for the first three quarters of 2026 is KRW 160.1 billion, which shows that revenue was concentrated in the second quarter.

The 2026 annual report will confirm fourth-quarter results and full-year operating profit and operating cash flow, which will show whether the profit turn held across a full year. After the sharp third-quarter drop, fourth-quarter profit scale and the cost trend are the key items.

Because the company's medium-term plans are only partly visible in disclosures, outlook should be checked against order disclosures and periodic reports.

08

Bull factors

Scale of the quarterly profit swing

Second-quarter 2026 revenue reached KRW 87.2 billion with operating profit of KRW 28 billion, a sharp improvement from the operating loss in the first quarter of 2025. Controlling-shareholder net income over the last four quarters totals KRW 24 billion. Reported results show profits can improve quickly when orders concentrate.

Resumption of Chinese OLED investment

Industry reports say renewed Chinese OLED investment is lifting equipment orders. A deposition equipment order for TCL CSOT's 8.6-generation OLED line has also been reported. If Chinese panel investment continues, demand for evaporation sources and deposition systems could rise together.

Asset base and moderate leverage

Total equity was KRW 133.4 billion at the end of 2025 against total liabilities of KRW 38.3 billion. The debt ratio fell from 35.7 percent in 2022 to 28.7 percent in 2025. The balance sheet gives room to absorb operating strain during periods of order volatility.

Bear factors

Return to a net loss in the third quarter

Third-quarter 2026 operating profit fell to KRW 694 million, down 97.5 percent from the prior quarter, and controlling-shareholder net loss was KRW 12.4 billion. Whether the second-quarter profit was concentrated in one-time order revenue has not been confirmed. Quarterly results swinging sharply with order timing is the main weakness.

Annual losses and negative cash flow

The 2025 operating loss was KRW 6.46 billion, the fourth straight annual loss. Operating cash flow was KRW -12.1 billion in 2025 and negative every year since 2022. Until an annual profit is confirmed, weak cash generation remains a drag.

Customer concentration and panel investment delays

Investment delays by major panel customers BOE and LG Display were cited as a cause of past profitability declines. An order book centered on Chinese customers means results can hinge on a few companies' investment decisions. Whether the customer base is diversifying has not been confirmed, which is a weakness.

10

Risk factors

Customer and investment cycle

If panel makers cut or delay capital spending, equipment orders fall quickly. Past investment delays by BOE and LG Display fed into weaker results. A backlog concentrated in a few customers magnifies this risk.

One-off nature of order disclosures

The June 2026 evaporation source contract reached the end of its period on November 6, 2026. If follow-on contracts do not materialize, a revenue gap could follow. The sharp third-quarter profit decline also shows volatility tied to order timing.

Profitability and funding

The operating margin improved from -33.7 percent in 2024 to -18.6 percent in 2025 but remains negative. Cumulative negative operating cash flow could erode financial flexibility if working capital or capital spending needs grow. The risk persists until annual profitability is confirmed.

Overall view

YAS manufactures OLED deposition equipment and has mass-production experience in both 8th-generation-and-above deposition systems and evaporation sources. On a reported basis, annual losses continued through 2025, but the second quarter of 2026 swung to operating profit of KRW 28 billion.

In the third quarter, revenue of KRW 40.3 billion coincided with operating profit of KRW 694 million and a controlling-shareholder net loss of KRW 12.4 billion. Earnings direction depends heavily on Chinese OLED investment and on individual order disclosures.

Strengths include the size of the quarterly profit swing, reports of resumed Chinese investment, and an equity base of KRW 133.4 billion at the end of 2025. Weaknesses include annual losses, negative operating cash flow, customer concentration and the sharp third-quarter swing.

The valuation sits with the price-to-book ratio below 1x, but without confirmed annual profits, earnings-based multiples are hard to interpret. The next checkpoints are follow-on order disclosures, operating profit and operating cash flow in the 2026 annual report, and the first-quarter 2027 report.
