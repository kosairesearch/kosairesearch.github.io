02

Business structure

Nearthlab is a physical AI drone company that built its autonomous flight and vision AI capabilities on wind turbine blade inspection and has since extended into defense drones.

In its wind inspection business, the company said it has inspected more than 100,000 turbine blades across over 40 countries with a mission success rate above 99%. Its core defense products are the KAiDEN counter-drone hard-kill solution and the XAiDEN swarm strike drone.

KAiDEN is described as an autonomous interceptor drone that flies at more than 250 km/h to neutralize enemy unmanned aircraft within the golden time window.

XAiDEN is a UAE project won in 2025 worth about USD 10 million, shipped to the Middle East at the end of June 2026 and reported as the largest single drone export contract in Korea.

Recent reports say defense made up 86% of first-half 2026 revenue, while existing businesses such as wind turbine inspection grew 38% year on year.

Overseas, the company signed a supply contract for about USD 1.4 million of KAiDEN units with a Singapore defense company in 2024, which it uses as a Southeast Asian reference.

In Korea, it forms a consortium with LIG Defense&Aerospace to carry out a Defense Acquisition Program Administration rapid trial project for counter-drone systems.

Media analysis says large listed defense companies such as Hanwha Aerospace and Korea Aerospace Industries differ markedly from Nearthlab in production track record, order backlog and financial stability.

The company plans to extend its wind inspection technology into counter-drone and swarm strike markets, using Singapore as a Southeast Asia hub and the UAE as a Middle East hub.

03

Recent trends

Based on DART filings, cumulative revenue for the first three quarters of 2026 was KRW 2.22 billion, up 24.3% from the same period a year earlier. Operating loss for the same period was KRW 4.3 billion, and the loss widened versus the prior-year period.

Net loss was KRW 4.68 billion, and the loss narrowed compared with the same period of the prior year. Revenue growth is confirmed, but both operating and net losses remain in the billions of won, so the disclosed figures do not yet show a return to profit.

Media reported that preliminary first-half 2026 revenue was about KRW 17.4 billion and that operating profit turned positive in the second quarter on a company preliminary basis, announced on July 15, 2026. Those preliminary figures use a different basis from the disclosed numbers and should be read separately.

The first-quarter operating loss was KRW 4.2 billion on the securities registration statement basis. For 2025, consolidated revenue was KRW 6.7 billion and the operating loss was KRW 16.6 billion, and operating losses exceeded KRW 10 billion in each year from 2022.

At the end of 2025, capital was negative KRW 87.3 billion, and the company said the capital impairment was resolved after RCPS converted into common shares on January 28, 2026.

As of 07:36 KST on November 14, 2026, the share price was KRW 11,500, down 9.95% from the prior close, with a market capitalization of KRW 66.6 billion. The offering price was fixed at KRW 41,200, the top of the indicative range.

04

Outlook

The company set a 2026 annual revenue target of KRW 27.0 billion, and said first-half preliminary revenue equaled about 65% of that target. Disclosed cumulative revenue of KRW 2.22 billion through Q3 sits far below the target, so the revenue recognized in Q4 is the key variable for whether the target is met.

The company has targeted 2029 revenue of KRW 92.1 billion and operating profit of KRW 15.9 billion, and aims to reach breakeven in 2028.

Of the offering proceeds, KRW 15.8 billion is allocated to facility investment for an in-house factory, KRW 8.9 billion to R&D, and KRW 2.1 billion to operating funds for expanding production staff. Production facility investment is scheduled to proceed in stages from 2027 through 2028.

Media reported an order backlog of KRW 6.2 billion as of first-half 2026 on a preliminary basis. The securities registration statement shows 62.81% of shares under lock-up and 37.19% tradable, so the timing of lock-up expiries remains a supply variable.

Recent reports say the industry views the company's large Middle East export record as raising the likelihood of additional orders. An investor-side representative said inquiries about defense drones rose recently amid the Iran war. However, many defense contracts carry confidentiality clauses, which limits how much order detail is disclosed.

05

Bull factors

Rapid expansion of defense revenue share

Recent reports say defense made up 86% of first-half 2026 revenue. The company said the initial XAiDEN shipment of about KRW 14.9 billion was recognized in first-half results. The company argues that stockpiling, training and operational use can generate repeat purchases. These figures are on a company preliminary basis and should be read alongside the disclosed numbers.

Overseas references and expected follow-on orders

The 2024 KAiDEN supply contract with a Singapore defense company and the XAiDEN shipment to the UAE serve as overseas references. The company plans to use Singapore as a base for Southeast Asia and the UAE for the Middle East, expanding supply to neighboring countries.

It intends to use the follow-on purchasing pattern typical of defense products, arising from training, stockpiling and operational use after initial supply.

Autonomous flight technology proven in wind inspection

The company said it has inspected more than 100,000 wind turbine blades across over 40 countries. Existing businesses grew 38% year on year in the same period, according to media reports.

Vision AI and autonomous flight technology validated in industrial settings are cited as a technical strength carried over into defense drones.

06

Bear factors

Losses continuing through Q3

Cumulative operating loss through Q3 2026 was KRW 4.3 billion and net loss was KRW 4.68 billion, with both measures still in the red. The 2025 operating loss was KRW 16.6 billion, and operating losses have exceeded KRW 10 billion every year since 2022. The company targets breakeven in 2028, which means a sustained move to profit is still some way off.

Gap between revenue targets and cumulative results

Against the 2026 annual revenue target of KRW 27.0 billion, disclosed cumulative revenue through Q3 was KRW 2.22 billion. The company also missed its 2025 target of KRW 13.5 billion, recording KRW 6.7 billion, and attributed the shortfall to differences in revenue recognition timing.

Capital impairment history and near-term debt burden

Capital was negative KRW 87.3 billion at the end of 2025, meaning the company was in capital impairment. Media reports said debt maturing within three months stood at KRW 20.3 billion, above cash and short-term financial assets of KRW 7.6 billion. Dilution of existing shareholders has also been raised in connection with the RCPS conversion.

07

Risk factors

Macro

Demand for defense drones depends on international conflict and Middle East conditions, which are external variables. An investor-side representative said inquiries rose recently following the Iran war.

Because overseas sales weigh heavily, export partner policy and the international environment directly affect results.

Business and regulatory

Bids from the Ministry of National Defense and public institutions, along with Defense Acquisition Program Administration projects, are central, and securing a B2G track record is cited as a key variable. Many defense contracts carry confidentiality clauses, which limits external disclosure of order details. Differences in scale and order backlog versus large defense companies remain a challenge.

Supply and liquidity

Lock-up shares account for 62.81% of total shares, and general subscribers received a put right for three months from the listing date. The share price of KRW 11,500 at the time of writing is far below the KRW 41,200 offering price, showing wide price swings. Market capitalization stands at KRW 66.6 billion.

08

Overall view

Nearthlab moved into defense drones on the back of its wind inspection technology, and defense accounted for 86% of first-half 2026 revenue on a preliminary basis. However, the disclosed cumulative operating loss of KRW 4.3 billion and net loss of KRW 4.68 billion through Q3 2026 remain large.

Revenue rose 24.3% year on year, but cumulative results are far from the KRW 27.0 billion annual target. The 2028 breakeven target and expected repeat purchases overseas are the checkpoints for this business transition.

The company says capital impairment was resolved when RCPS converted into common shares in January 2026, but reported debt of KRW 20.3 billion maturing within three months remains a near-term repayment burden.

The share price at the time of writing, KRW 11,500, is below the KRW 41,200 offering price, and lock-up expiries and the put right period are near-term supply variables. This summary is based on DART filings and media reports and is provided for information only.
