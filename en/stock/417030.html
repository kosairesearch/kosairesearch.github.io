Report overview

First Defense-Driven Profit; Follow-On Orders in Focus

Nearthlab shifted its revenue base from wind-turbine inspection to defense drones and posted a half-year operating profit, but heavy single-customer dependence and weak post-listing share performance coexist.

02

Business structure

Founded in 2015, Nearthlab developed physical-AI-based autonomous flight technology that lets drones perceive their surroundings and fly on their own. Its core technology is Aerial Intelligence, software that lets a drone perceive, decide and carry out missions without a human pilot.

The company says its wind-turbine inspection business has covered more than 100,000 blades in over 40 countries with a mission success rate above 99%.

Building on that operating experience, it entered the defense industry in 2024 and developed KAiDEN, a counter-drone hard-kill solution, and XAiDEN, a swarm attack drone. XAiDEN is a swarm platform in which a single operator controls up to 10 drones at once.

According to NH Investment & Securities, the first-half 2026 revenue mix was 85.6% defense and 14.4% enterprise. In 2025, 88% of revenue came from the enterprise segment, so the revenue structure inverted within a year. Consolidated 2025 revenue was KRW 6.66 billion and the operating loss was KRW 16.61 billion.

The company currently has no production facilities of its own and outsources all manufacturing. The chief executive said overseas sales account for more than 90% of revenue.

In counter-drone systems, Hanwha Systems leads system integration for the critical-area counter-drone program at major military sites, and analysts note that specialist drone makers may play a larger role at the interception stage.

In June 2026, Nearthlab won a rapid pilot project for a counter-drone hard-kill close-in defense system in a consortium with LIG Defense&Aerospace. The structure is one in which Nearthlab competes with, and also partners with, large defense system integrators.

03

Recent trends

Per the DART half-year report, first-half 2026 (cumulative) revenue was KRW 16.9 billion, up 963.0% from the same period a year earlier. Operating profit was KRW 680 million, a swing to profit from the prior-year period. The net loss was KRW 156 million, so losses continued but narrowed. Some press reports cite preliminary figures on a different basis, and this report follows the filed figures.

The revenue surge reflects the XAiDEN shipment to the Middle East at the end of June 2026, and the company said this contract added about KRW 14.9 billion to first-half revenue. The shipment was the portion of a USD 10 million contract won in 2025. Sidae reported that defense revenue came from a single UAE customer.

The same report said the order backlog at end-June was KRW 5.9 billion, and 92% of the defense backlog is a KAiDEN prototype development order from a domestic customer due in November 2028.

Before listing, the institutional book-building was 743.5 times covered and the retail subscription 529.99 times, and the offering price was set at KRW 41,200, the top of the range. On the first trading day, August 24, 2026, the stock closed at KRW 28,650, down 30.46% from the offering price.

Institutional lock-up commitments reached only 39.4%, so lead underwriter Samsung Securities acquired an additional 9,100 shares, equal to 1% of the offering.

The market capitalization implied by the offering price was about KRW 238 billion, whereas at the KRW 16,220 price on October 27, 2026 the market capitalization is KRW 94.5 billion and trading value is KRW 1.2 billion.

04

Outlook

In its registration statement the company set a 2026 revenue target of KRW 27 billion. Sidae reported that the only remaining defense delivery for 2026 is a KRW 400 million XAiDEN training service for the UAE customer. Second-half revenue therefore depends heavily on the timing of revenue recognition from new orders.

NH Investment & Securities put the rapid pilot project at about KRW 17 billion, with KRW 4.5 billion attributable to Nearthlab, and analyzed that development and demonstration in 2027-2028 would lead to mass production from 2029 with a five-year mass-production budget of about KRW 400 billion.

That report did not give an investment rating or target price. The meaningful earnings contribution from this program is therefore a 2029-and-later matter.

The 2027 defense budget proposal is KRW 73.3 trillion, up 8.2%, and the budget for drone and counter-drone capability is KRW 806.1 billion, up 18.8%. By contrast, 2026 spending on drone development and acquisition within the force-improvement budget was KRW 148.4 billion, down 11.5% from the prior year. Headline budget growth and the timing of individual program execution need to be viewed separately.

Press reports say Nearthlab has exported KAiDEN to a Singapore company and is discussing about 22 new contracts.

According to Sidae, the company expects operating losses to continue through 2027, targets full-year profit in 2028, and plans to build a plant with annual capacity of 18,000 units in stages over 2027-2028 using KRW 15.8 billion of offering proceeds.

Whether the half-year operating profit carries into a full-year profit depends on the pace of follow-on orders and in-house production.

05

Bull factors

Civilian-proven technology carried into defense

In wind-turbine inspection the company handled more than 100,000 blades across over 40 countries and built a mission success rate above 99%.

The company describes XAiDEN as a single contract won about a year after entering defense, with the whole cycle from order to production, acceptance testing and delivery completed in one year. One broadcast report also described it as the only domestic drone company that is an NVIDIA partner. Having a delivery record as a newcomer to defense can be read as evidence of technical validation.

Growing drone and counter-drone budgets and domestic programs

In the 2027 defense budget proposal, drone and counter-drone funding is KRW 806.1 billion, up 18.8% from KRW 678.8 billion. The Ministry of National Defense is also pursuing a plan to acquire more than 20,000 low-cost, expendable drones.

In June 2026 Nearthlab won a rapid pilot project for a counter-drone hard-kill close-in defense system, placing it at the start of a domestic path toward mass production. Analysts note that domestic military programs also help secure references for export sales.

Export references and order discussions

KAiDEN has been exported to Southeast Asia, and the company is pursuing further orders based on demonstrations with the Singapore, UAE and domestic militaries. Press reports also say about 22 new contracts are under discussion. The company says the XAiDEN contract is the largest drone export in Korean history. If the discussions convert into contracts, dependence on a single customer could ease.

06

Bear factors

Single-customer dependence and thin backlog

Sidae reported that defense revenue came from a single UAE customer. In the same report, the end-June order backlog was KRW 5.9 billion, about 34% of first-half revenue. Finished-goods inventory fell sharply over the half-year, and raw-material purchases in the first half were only about 4% of full-year 2025 purchases. Until the next large order is confirmed in filings, visibility on second-half revenue is low.

Post-listing supply pressure and share weakness

Press reports said only 1.26% of institutional investors committed to a lock-up of 15 days or more. The first-day close was 30.46% below the offering price. The put-back right that Samsung Securities granted for three months from the listing date has a base repurchase price of KRW 37,080, 90% of the offering price.

The current price of KRW 16,220 is far below that level, which raises concerns about underwriter burden and supply-demand instability.

Continued net loss and an outsourced production model

The first-half 2026 net loss of KRW 156 million shows that the operating profit did not carry through to net income. The 2025 consolidated operating loss was KRW 16.61 billion. Because drones are built by contract manufacturers, one analysis treats merchandise purchase costs as effectively the cost of sales. Until the in-house plant is completed, the scope for cost-structure improvement is limited.

07

Risk factors

Macro

Because the chief executive said overseas sales exceed 90% of revenue, conditions in the Middle East and Southeast Asia, foreign defense budgets and exchange rates bear directly on results. Export contract timing can shift with the counterpart's procurement schedule and political environment.

When risk appetite weakens, trading in recently listed shares can swing more sharply. These variables are largely outside the company's control.

Policy & Regulatory

The domestic counter-drone program moves through development and demonstration in 2027-2028 and into mass production from 2029, so changes to budget execution or program scope would shift the timing of revenue.

Defense drone competitions drew controversy over rule violations and judging fairness, and repeated crashes at public demonstrations were also pointed out. Closer scrutiny of the effectiveness of defense drone programs could alter evaluation criteria and schedules.

Given the nature of defense procurement, delays can arise at the test-evaluation and contractor-selection stages.

Financial & Supply-Demand

According to Korea Financial News, the target shortfall triggered the refixing clause held by financial investors and cut the conversion price to KRW 22,000 per share. If investors do not convert into common shares, the company must repay the investment with 7% annual interest.

In addition, KRW 15.8 billion of offering proceeds is earmarked for building the in-house plant. If the release of converted shares, the repayment burden and capital spending overlap, pressure on the balance sheet and on trading could grow.

08

Overall view

Nearthlab carried autonomous flight technology built in wind-turbine inspection into defense drones, and the first-half 2026 filing shows revenue of KRW 16.9 billion and operating profit of KRW 680 million.

However, a net loss of KRW 156 million continued, and revenue leaned heavily on deliveries to one Middle East customer. The larger drone and counter-drone budget in the 2027 defense proposal and the domestic rapid pilot project are positives for the sector backdrop.

Mass production is scheduled for 2029 or later, so until then follow-on orders and in-house plant investment are the key variables for results.

After listing, the share price has traded well below the offering price, and the put-back period, the lock-up ratio and the conversion and redemption terms of financial investors are cited as supply-demand factors.

Taken together, the filings and press reports show a stage in which the results of the business shift, the durability of orders and supply-demand pressure all coexist. This report is for information only, and investment decisions and their outcomes rest with the reader.
