02

Business structure

JOYWORKS& designs interior furniture, outsources production to domestic and overseas partner manufacturers, and sells mainly online. Its model runs without its own production plant, which is cited as a factor that lowers fixed-cost burdens.

Sales channels include its own mall, Naver Smart Store, Ohouse and about 30 other online shopping malls. A May 2026 report named children's furniture, a heated-furniture brand and a mattress brand among its key and newer product lines. Its furniture brands include Lady, For The Home and Smart Ale.

It consolidated a subsidiary in 2024 and changed its name to JOYWORKS&. In October 2025 it launched eerop, a premium food waste disposer brand, which was reported to have received K-mark certification for the disposal method in 2025, and the company is reported to target a 10% market share.

For HOKA's domestic offline business, a business transfer contract was signed in September 2025, but reports conflict: one says the transfer fell through after HOKA's parent Deckers terminated the distributor contract, while a July 2026 report describes the company as holding the offline business rights.

Segment revenue shares could not be confirmed. The company has a past brand partnership with Hanssem. Competitor market-share data could not be verified.

04

Earnings analysis

On a disclosed basis, annual revenue rose from KRW 32.4 billion in 2022 to KRW 41 billion in 2023 (up 26.6% year on year) and KRW 47.3 billion in 2024 (up 15.2%), then fell to KRW 46 billion in 2025 (down 2.7%). Of the years from 2022 to 2025, 2024 was the largest for revenue.

Operating profit was KRW 1.59 billion in 2022 and KRW 2.06 billion in 2023 (up 29.2%), then turned into an operating loss of KRW 2.39 billion in 2024, and widened to an operating loss of KRW 8.77 billion in 2025. The operating margin fell from 5.0% in 2023 to -5.1% in 2024 and -19.1% in 2025.

Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 2.46 billion in 2023 (up 63.7%), dropped to KRW 1.04 billion in 2024 (down 57.7%), and became a net loss of KRW 18.9 billion in 2025. Net loss including non-controlling interests was KRW 20.7 billion in 2025.

Quarterly, the third quarter of 2025 posted an operating loss of KRW 1.18 billion and the fourth quarter an operating loss of KRW 4.52 billion, with losses widening versus the prior quarter.

The first quarter of 2026 brought revenue of KRW 21.2 billion (up 11.3% quarter on quarter) and operating profit of KRW 3.38 billion. The second quarter returned to an operating loss of KRW 1.3 billion on revenue of KRW 12 billion (down 43.3%).

The third quarter had revenue of KRW 12.1 billion and operating profit of KRW 2.06 billion.

The provisional third-quarter result disclosed on November 2, 2026 was reported on a separate basis with operating profit of KRW 2.24 billion, a different basis from the consolidated figures in the periodic report, so the two are not compared side by side.

At year-end 2025, total equity was KRW 18.7 billion and total liabilities were KRW 36.3 billion. Operating cash flow moved from KRW 151 million in 2024 to negative KRW 3.26 billion in 2025.

The disclosed controlling-shareholder equity of KRW 19.4 billion is larger than total equity of KRW 18.7 billion, so the non-controlling interest line should be checked against the original periodic report.

Industry analysis

The furniture market is described in reports as sluggish, with a prolonged downturn in property markets and weak construction activity holding back demand. Analysts argue that the core furniture business alone makes it hard to secure growth momentum.

Against that backdrop, the company has pursued diversification and has continued expanding into new businesses since its largest shareholder changed in 2025. In an online-centric sales model, revenue swings can depend heavily on platform exposure and promotions, but no figures on this were confirmed.

In terms of cycle position, results since 2025 have moved between losses and profits, with recovery signs and weakness appearing together. Third-quarter 2026 revenue rose year on year, while second-quarter revenue fell quarter on quarter.

Industry-average growth rates and market-share data could not be confirmed through public sources. Hanssem is a large listed furniture company in the same industry and has a past brand partnership with the company, but this report does not present financial comparison figures against it.

On the listing side, a July 2026 report said the expanded business purposes are seen by the industry as a step to address tougher KOSDAQ delisting requirements. In April 2026 the company carried out a five-to-one share consolidation and a bonus share issue as steps to restore corporate value.

Outlook

The company has said that recent share price moves reflect a mix of market conditions and external factors, and it has stated that it is focused on strengthening its core business and improving its earnings structure.

Specific measures it cited include expanding premium product lines, cost efficiency and improving distribution.

According to a July 27, 2026 report, at an extraordinary shareholders meeting held on July 20, the company amended its articles to add 19 business purposes, including sales, transport and disposal of radioisotopes, decontamination and dismantling of radioactive waste, electronics manufacturing and sales, electrical construction and automotive parts manufacturing.

Timelines and revenue scales for these businesses have not been confirmed. A March 2025 record shows a memorandum of understanding with Al-Ojaimi Group of Saudi Arabia on modular housing, but later progress was not confirmed.

Expansion of offline distribution for eerop and development of products for one- and two-person households are reported to be ongoing. Because reports on the status of the HOKA contract conflict, this point needs to be checked against company disclosures.

According to the company, its CEO and senior executives bought 406,315 shares on the open market, and it left open the possibility of further purchases. No revenue or operating profit guidance published by the company was confirmed.

Overall view

JOYWORKS& is a company that designs and sells furniture mainly online. After operating losses began in 2024 and widened in 2025, it recorded operating profit in the first and third quarters of 2026. The 2025 annual operating loss of KRW 8.77 billion and net loss of KRW 20.7 billion show the weight of the recent trend.

On the business side, reports on the HOKA offline rights and the added business purposes conflict, and the CEO change and contract termination reports remain open questions.

On the other hand, the executive share purchases announced by the company and total equity of KRW 18.7 billion are recorded facts of the company's base.

Valuation-wise, a price-to-earnings ratio cannot be calculated and the price-to-book ratio is in a low range against net assets, so whether quarterly profitability continues is central to reading the multiples. The next checkpoints are the 2026 annual report and the 2027 first-quarter results.
