02

Business structure

JOYWORKS& designs furniture and interior products, outsources manufacturing to domestic and overseas partner factories, and sells the finished goods mainly online. Its main sales channels are its own mall, Naver Smart Store, Ohouse and more than 30 other online shopping malls.

The company says that using outsourced production without its own plants lowers its fixed-cost burden. Furniture is the core business, operated under the Lady Furniture brand, with kids furniture, the Ondam warm-furniture brand and the Sonnidoro mattress brand as its main lines.

In its 1Q 2026 results briefing, the company said the core and newly launched lines grew evenly. In 2024 it launched the Irob premium food waste disposer brand and consolidated its subsidiary 3DI.

From October 1, 2025 it took over the domestic offline retail operations of the global running brand HOKA, and the company said first-month sales under the transfer rose about 19% from the prior month.

Customers are mainly online shoppers, and the company is expanding its Suwon Station direct store online-to-offline (O2O) model into premium stores and pop-up stores nationwide.

Competition spans online furniture channels and offline furniture and interior companies, and Hanssem is often cited as a comparison within consumer goods.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual revenue rose from KRW 32.4 billion in 2022 to KRW 41 billion in 2023, up 26.6% year on year, and reached KRW 47.3 billion in 2024, up 15.2%, the largest year in the 2022–2025 span. Revenue then fell 2.7% to KRW 46 billion in 2025. Profit moved in the opposite direction.

Operating profit grew to KRW 2.06 billion in 2023, up 29.2%, but turned into an operating loss of KRW 2.39 billion in 2024, a swing into deficit, and the loss widened to KRW 8.77 billion in 2025 as the deficit continued. The company has posted operating losses in both 2024 and 2025, two consecutive loss years.

The operating margin moved from 5.0% in 2023 to -5.1% in 2024 and -19.1% in 2025. Attributable net income fell from KRW 2.46 billion in 2023, up 63.7%, to KRW 1.04 billion in 2024, down 57.7%, before a swing to an attributable net loss of KRW 18.9 billion in 2025.

Net loss including non-controlling interests was KRW 20.7 billion. The company attributed the 2025 losses partly to impairment of its investment in subsidiary 3DI and to impairment of goodwill tied to the HOKA offline business transfer.

Operating cash flow fell from KRW 151 million in 2024 to -KRW 3.26 billion in 2025, indicating weaker cash generation at the operating level even apart from non-cash impairments.

Quarterly, 3Q 2025 revenue was KRW 7.41 billion with an operating loss of KRW 1.18 billion. 4Q 2025 revenue jumped to KRW 19.1 billion, up 157.1% from the prior quarter, yet the operating loss deepened to KRW 4.52 billion and the attributable net loss widened to KRW 11.6 billion. 1Q 2026 revenue was KRW 21.2 billion, up 11.3% quarter on quarter, with operating profit of KRW 3.38 billion; the company said some one-off factors were included. 2Q 2026 revenue fell 43.3% to KRW 12 billion and the company returned to an operating loss of KRW 1.3 billion. 3Q 2026 revenue was KRW 12.1 billion, flat quarter on quarter and up 63.4% year on year, and operating profit was KRW 2.06 billion, a swing from a year-earlier loss, with attributable net income of KRW 643 million. Over the last four quarters, attributable net losses total KRW 8.15 billion.

05

Industry analysis

Company disclosures point to a weak furniture market.

In a December 2025 company-level earnings comment, the company attributed lower sales at Lady Furniture and a second subsidiary, Podeohom, to a slump in the furniture market, and it cited logistics-center operating costs and new-business investment as pressure on profitability.

Online furniture distribution is competitive across the company's own mall, Naver Smart Store and Ohouse, and JOYWORKS& is responding by pairing outsourced production with online sales to hold down fixed costs.

Its push to add offline stores built on the Suwon Station direct store reflects the same effort to meet demand for in-person experience. On logistics, the company has formed a third-party logistics (3PL) partnership with Cheonil Express to improve delivery accuracy and inventory availability.

Hanssem, a listed furniture and interior company, is cited as a comparison, but JOYWORKS& operates an asset-light model without its own plants, so the structure differs.

Quantitative demand and cycle indicators were not backed by sources beyond company disclosures, so the weak-market reading rests on the company's own description.

With the HOKA retail operations added, the revenue mix is moving away from a furniture-only structure, which is also relevant to where the company sits within its industry.

06

Outlook

In a May 27, 2026 announcement, the company said it would complete its restructuring in the first half and enter a full growth phase centered on the furniture business from the second half.

Its second-half growth levers include expanding the Suwon Station O2O model into premium stores and pop-up stores nationwide, strengthening the Sonnidoro mattress and Ondam sofa lineups, and upgrading storage and kids lines with new product launches.

On logistics, it plans to introduce data-driven inventory management and outbound processes through its 3PL partnership with Cheonil Express.

On capital structure, a 5-to-1 share consolidation raised the par value from KRW 100 to KRW 500 and cut the shares outstanding from about 24.49 million to 4.9 million, and a 300% bonus issue was also decided.

The company also resolved to retire all of its second convertible bond, with a face value of KRW 8.375 billion, removing a source of potential dilution.

At the end of 2025, the CEO and other key executives bought a total of 406,315 shares on the market, which the company described as a sign of management confidence in medium- to long-term growth.

The company has said cumulative revenue from the HOKA retail business rose about 34% year on year over January to October 2025, after the October 2025 transfer.

The return to operating profit in 3Q 2026 is the first numerical check on the second-half plan, but the 2Q 2026 loss and wide quarterly revenue swings mean the sustainability of profit is still unproven.

The next checkpoints are whether full-year 2026 operating profit and attributable net income turn positive and how much the HOKA retail business contributes to annual revenue.

10

Risk factors

Earnings volatility

Quarterly revenue and operating results have swung widely, which makes it hard to judge whether profitable quarters can last. The 1Q 2026 profit included some one-off factors, according to the company.

With 2Q 2026 back in loss, it is necessary to separate whether the profit reflects structural improvement or temporary factors.

Financial and accounting risk

In 2025 the company booked impairments on its investment in subsidiary 3DI and on goodwill tied to the HOKA offline business transfer. With a debt-to-equity ratio of 194.0% and total equity below the parent's equity, further impairments would weigh heavily on capital. The swing to negative operating cash flow is also a reason to monitor financial capacity.

Market and channel concentration

Revenue relies heavily on online shopping malls, and a prolonged slump in the furniture market could weaken demand across those channels. Products depend on outsourced production by partner manufacturers, which exposes the company to supply disruption and cost changes.

If newer businesses such as HOKA retail and the Irob food waste disposer do not take hold, filling gaps in furniture-driven earnings would be difficult.

Overall view

JOYWORKS& kept its online-led furniture distribution model and recorded operating profit in 1Q 2026 and 3Q 2026. Compared with the 2025 period of an operating loss of KRW 8.77 billion and an attributable net loss of KRW 18.9 billion, the direction has changed.

However, the operating loss of KRW 1.3 billion in 2Q 2026 and the large swings in quarterly revenue show that continuity of earnings is still unproven.

Strengths include the larger revenue base, the HOKA retail transfer and capital-structure clean-up through convertible bond retirement and share consolidation. Weaknesses include accumulated annual losses, a weaker balance sheet and a slump in the furniture market.
