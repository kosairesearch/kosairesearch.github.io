02

Business structure

Korea Export Packaging Industrial (002200) was founded in 1957 and listed on the KOSPI market in 1974 as a corrugated paper manufacturer. Its core business is making corrugated sheets and corrugated products, with agricultural and marine produce packaging and e-commerce cited as the main sources of demand.

A subsidiary produces corrugated boxes and accessories, and the two companies are linked through raw material supply and joint production of finished goods. Beyond corrugated manufacturing, the company runs an import-export business and real estate sales and leasing, although no export revenue was booked in 2024.

In early 2023 it added waste treatment to its business purpose, with the stated aim of generating the steam needed for paper production. Production is centered on the Daejeon plant and the Osan plant in Gyeonggi Province.

The Osan plant stopped production after a disaster in February 2026, and damaged equipment is being partly replaced as restoration proceeds. A corrugated box plant is also under construction in Eumseong County, Chungcheongbuk-do.

Demand growth is linked to styrofoam being replaced by corrugated packaging and to the expansion of e-commerce, while small specialized plants are shrinking as large integrated plants expand. Reports said average utilization of production facilities exceeded 95% in 2024.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual results show shrinking sales and deteriorating profitability at the same time.

Sales fell from KRW 341.4 billion in 2022 to KRW 312.3 billion in 2023 (down 8.5% year on year), KRW 301.8 billion in 2024 (down 3.4%), and KRW 289.6 billion in 2025 (down 4.0%), declining for three consecutive years from 2023 to 2025. Within 2022 to 2025, 2022 was the largest year and 2025 the smallest.

Operating profit fell from KRW 25.7 billion in 2022 to KRW 22.9 billion in 2023 (down 11.0%), and then turned into an operating loss of KRW 586 million in 2024. The loss widened to KRW 6.56 billion in 2025, making 2024 and 2025 two consecutive loss years.

Operating margin moved from 7.5% in 2022 and 7.3% in 2023 to -0.2% in 2024 and -2.3% in 2025, and the company cited rising costs and lower selling prices of its products as the main drivers.

Profit attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 20.3 billion in 2022, KRW 19 billion in 2023 (down 6.3%), and KRW 3.4 billion in 2024 (down 82.2%), before turning into a net loss of KRW 3.88 billion in 2025. Net income including non-controlling interests was a net loss of KRW 3.88 billion in 2025.

Operating cash flow fell from KRW 24.3 billion in 2022 and KRW 25.1 billion in 2023 to KRW 8.62 billion in 2024 and KRW 6.31 billion in 2025.

On a quarterly basis, sales bottomed at KRW 67.6 billion in Q4 2025 (down 11.4% from the prior quarter) and then rose for three straight quarters to KRW 78.4 billion in Q3 2026, up 2.7% year on year.

Over the same stretch, operating losses went from KRW 1.05 billion in Q3 2025 to KRW 5.15 billion in Q4 2025, KRW 7.17 billion in Q1 2026 and KRW 9.73 billion in Q2 2026, before narrowing to KRW 8.91 billion in Q3 2026, marking five consecutive loss quarters.

Profit attributable to controlling shareholders turned to a KRW 890 million gain in Q2 2026, then returned to a net loss of KRW 6.87 billion in Q3 2026. The last four quarters together total a KRW 16 billion net loss attributable to controlling shareholders.

Press coverage citing an independent research report put the first-half 2026 gross margin at 1.5%, down from 9.8% a year earlier, and attributed the operating loss mainly to a cost structure in which each additional unit produced adds to losses rather than to higher selling expenses.

05

Industry analysis

Demand for corrugated packaging is described as rising alongside the expansion of the overall economy, the growth of agricultural and marine produce packaging and e-commerce, and the substitution of styrofoam with corrugated materials.

Within this shift, small specialized plants are shrinking while large integrated plants are expanding. Press coverage cited construction activity and weak consumer spending as background to the recent earnings decline.

The key variable behind earnings is the corrugated spread, meaning the gap between the price of corrugated boxes and the price of waste paper, the main raw material. According to an independent research report, the corrugated spread in Q2 2026 reached KRW 399,000 per ton, the second-highest level in the past ten years.

The same report described the second half of 2026 as the period when the spread improvement should show up. A wider spread is read as a sign of industry recovery, but turning it into profit requires normal plant operation and stable selling prices.

In early 2025, coverage warned that weak consumption and falling selling prices could keep profitability under pressure. Within the industry, competitiveness will depend on production scale and cost position as large integrated structures expand.

06

Outlook

Coverage points out that future earnings depend on the pace of the Osan plant restoration and on whether the corrugated spread keeps improving. The Osan plant is being restored through partial replacement and repair of the damaged equipment to speed up operations.

At the same time, construction of a corrugated box plant is proceeding in Eumseong County, Chungcheongbuk-do. Coverage viewed the overlap of these two investments as a possible short-term increase in funding pressure.

Cash and cash equivalents were cited at about KRW 36 billion in first-half 2026 coverage, suggesting no immediate liquidity problem. A low-PBR value-up bill pending in the National Assembly was at the committee review stage as of the time of that coverage, and it has not passed a plenary vote or been promulgated.

The next periodic report is the 2026 annual report, which will settle Q4 and full-year profit and loss. The Q1 2027 quarterly report will show the operating profit and loss trend after the Osan plant returns to normal operation.

Another item to track is whether the three-quarter sales growth that ended in Q3 2026 continues alongside the recovery in plant operations.

Bull factors

Level of the corrugated spread

Press coverage citing an independent research report says the corrugated spread in Q2 2026 was KRW 399,000 per ton, the second-highest level in ten years.

Because the cost structure was named as the direct cause of first-half losses, the speed at which the spread flows into earnings is presented as the main path to profit recovery. How much of the spread improvement shows up in second-half 2026 earnings is the next point to check.

Osan plant restoration and room for margin recovery

The Osan plant is being restored through partial replacement and repair of damaged equipment, with the work aimed at speeding up operations. If operations normalize, output recovery could affect the cost structure, so the room for operating margin improvement depends on the pace of this restoration.

Sales growth for three straight quarters through Q3 2026 may also be a factor that expands production capacity after the restoration.

Low-PBR value-up bill and shareholder returns

Given a price-to-book ratio below 1x for the past three years, one analysis says the company is likely to fall under a pending low-PBR value-up bill. The bill was, however, at the National Assembly committee review stage at the time of that coverage and had not passed a plenary vote or been promulgated. The company signed a treasury share acquisition trust contract in January 2026 and terminated it in April 2026.

Bear factors

Operating losses and cost structure

Operating losses have continued for five consecutive quarters from Q3 2025 to Q3 2026. Press coverage citing an independent research report said the gross margin in the first half of 2026 fell from 9.8% a year earlier to 1.5%.

Because the cost structure was described as one in which each unit produced adds to losses, the fact that higher sales have not translated into profit is a bearish factor.

Shrinking sales and weaker demand

Sales declined from KRW 341.4 billion in 2022 to KRW 289.6 billion in 2025, a three-year run of decreases.

Coverage cited construction activity and weak consumer spending as causes of the earnings deterioration, and in early 2025 concern was raised that falling selling prices and slower consumption could keep profitability under pressure. Because no export revenue was booked in 2024, demand appears concentrated in the domestic market.

Funding pressure and lower cash flow

Running the Osan restoration and the Eumseong box plant construction at the same time has raised the possibility of greater short-term funding pressure. Operating cash flow fell from KRW 24.3 billion in 2022 to KRW 6.31 billion in 2025.

Some analysis points to cash and cash equivalents of about KRW 36 billion and sees no major liquidity issue, but cash capacity could shrink if investment and losses coincide.

Risk factors

Production facility risk

The Osan plant stopped production after a disaster in February 2026 and is being restored. Because the work involves partial replacement and repair, output and costs could vary with the pace of restoration and the timing of full operation.

The disaster and the production halt were both disclosed on February 9, 2026, so any disruption to operations could delay profit recovery.

Industry restructuring and scale competition

Press coverage says small specialized plants are shrinking while large integrated plants are expanding, so the company's position in the industry may depend on production scale and cost competitiveness.

The company produces at the Daejeon plant and the Osan plant, so if Osan operations return slowly, the scale gap with large integrated producers could widen. If this trend continues, price and volume competition for the same demand could intensify.

Ramp-up of new capacity and demand absorption

Once the corrugated box plant in Eumseong County is completed and starts operating, the key variable will be whether new volume can be filled by growing demand from e-commerce and the replacement of styrofoam with corrugated materials.

Coverage cites these as growth drivers, but if they do not turn into actual orders, utilization at the new plant could stay low. Securing output that matches this demand could determine how much the plant contributes to earnings.

Overall view

Korea Export Packaging Industrial has seen sales fall for three consecutive years since 2023 and has posted operating losses since 2024, reflecting structural pressure. Operating losses have run for five consecutive quarters through Q3 2026, while sales have risen for three straight quarters after bottoming in Q4 2025.

Press coverage points to the cost structure as the reason higher sales have not translated into profit. Bullish factors include the possibility of a wider corrugated spread, the Osan plant restoration, and a price-to-book ratio below 1x for the past three years alongside discussion of a low-PBR value-up bill.

Bearish factors include five consecutive quarters of operating losses, falling selling prices alongside rising costs, and funding pressure from restoration and the new plant investment.

These two sides should be weighed equally, and which one prevails will depend on when plant normalization and the spread actually show up in profit and loss.

The next checkpoints are the 2026 annual report, expected around mid-March 2027, and the Q1 2027 quarterly report, due in mid-May 2027, both showing the direction of operating results. Progress of the bill is also a matter to track in National Assembly review after December 2026.
