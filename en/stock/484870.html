02

Business structure

MNC Solution is a defense parts specialist that supplies core ground-weapon components, including drive units and suspension units for K9 self-propelled howitzers and K2 tank turrets, hydraulic systems for Chunmoo and Cheongung missile launchers, and servo valves.

Its main customers are prime defense contractors, and recent counterparties include Hanwha Aerospace, Hyundai Rotem and LIG D&A. In 2026 the product range widened to sound generators and the F100 HSU KIT for Egypt. Exports reached KRW 98.6 billion in 2024, or 52.6% of total sales, exceeding domestic sales.

A credit rating report said export-linked defense sales rose from 5.9% of sales in 2021 to about 40% in 2023. The company became independent through a 2020 spin-off during a Doosan group restructuring, and its hydraulic unit was split off in a December 2023 spin-off, later resold by its owners to Doosan Bobcat in 2024.

The defense parts business remained with MNC Solution. Shares have been listed on KOSPI since December 16, 2024, and the largest shareholder consortium holds about 74% of the stock. Revenue moves with prime contractors' production and order schedules, so quarterly sales can swing sharply. A high share of imported parts also makes costs sensitive to exchange rates.

04

Earnings analysis

Disclosed annual sales were KRW 183.4 billion in 2023, KRW 282.8 billion in 2024 (up 54.2% year on year) and KRW 403.3 billion in 2025 (up 42.6%). Sales rose two years in a row from 2024 through 2025, and 2025 was the largest year of the period.

Operating profit was KRW 20.7 billion, KRW 34.8 billion (up 68.6%) and KRW 56.1 billion (up 61.0%), so the operating margin rose from 11.3% to 12.3% and then 13.9%, with profit growing faster than sales.

Profit attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 14.5 billion, KRW 27 billion (up 85.6%) and KRW 45.6 billion (up 69.1%). Net profit including non-controlling interests was KRW 14.5 billion, KRW 27 billion and KRW 45.6 billion over the same years.

When the 2024 results were announced, the company credited higher K9 and K2 export shipments and government R&D-linked sales, and said sales of jointly developed products had begun to be recognized. The debt ratio fell from 301.9% to 115.9% and then 107.0%.

At the end of 2025, total liabilities were KRW 208.2 billion and total equity was KRW 194.7 billion. Operating cash flow went from KRW 38.9 billion in 2023 to negative KRW 39.9 billion in 2024 and recovered to KRW 25.6 billion in 2025.

Quarterly sales peaked at KRW 143.2 billion in the fourth quarter of 2025, with operating profit of KRW 21.6 billion. First-quarter 2026 sales fell to KRW 66.1 billion (down 53.9% from the prior quarter) and operating profit to KRW 7.97 billion (down 63.2%).

Second-quarter sales recovered to KRW 93.5 billion and operating profit to KRW 12.5 billion. Third-quarter 2026 sales were KRW 71.7 billion, down 18.1% year on year, and operating profit was KRW 9.52 billion, down 17.1%.

Profit attributable to controlling shareholders over the last four quarters (fourth quarter of 2025 to third quarter of 2026) totaled KRW 41.1 billion. A securities firm attributed the first-half 2026 sales decline to delayed orders for K2 tanks and K9 howitzers, which pushed back parts orders.

05

Industry analysis

Defense demand is expanding over the medium term, driven by higher defense budgets in major European countries, the prolonged Russia-Ukraine war and conflict in the Middle East. As exports of Korean ground systems grow, prime contractors' export contracts feed through to parts demand.

However, securities analysts noted that in the first half of 2026, delayed orders for K2 tanks and K9 howitzers pushed back related parts orders. A September 2026 report said the company lagged the defense stock rally, and a June 2026 report said defense shares had mostly fallen that year.

A securities firm said the recent drop in the exchange rate is positive for a company with a high share of imported parts. In early 2025, brokers estimated the order backlog at about KRW 960 billion at the end of 2024 and expected it to pass KRW 1 trillion during 2025.

Brokers also said completing overseas vendor registrations could support further growth. The current phase appears to be one of a growing order backlog with slower release of production orders by prime contractors.

Among competitors, the company is a small to mid-sized defense parts supplier whose customer base is concentrated, so the timing of prime contractors' orders largely determines quarterly results.

Outlook

On March 16 it signed a single-sale supply contract with Hyundai Rotem running until September 30, 2029, with details withheld for business confidentiality. On April 22, 2026, it signed a supply contract for the Egypt F100 HSU KIT, worth about KRW 26.5 billion, running until August 28, 2026.

On November 2, 2026, it voluntarily disclosed a sound generator supply contract with LIG D&A worth about KRW 12.5 billion, running until November 24, 2029, equal to 3.09% of prior-year sales. Because the contract periods are long, revenue recognition is likely to spread across several years.

In late August 2026 the company disclosed a KRW 15 billion trust contract for share purchases, with the plan to cancel all 996,016 shares acquired and purchases running through March 2027. The sale of the largest shareholder's stake ended in talks, but that shareholder has left open the option of a resale.

First-half 2026 sales fell to KRW 159.6 billion, so whether quarterly sales recover in line with the disclosed order volume is the next checkpoint.

Contract disclosures continued in 2026

Disclosures in 2026 covered Chunmoo launcher parts (about KRW 18.3 billion, March), the Egypt F100 HSU KIT (about KRW 26.5 billion, April) and LIG D&A sound generators (about KRW 12.5 billion, November). Some contracts run into 2028 and 2029, which could support a medium-term order base. Analysts said the development reference in sound equipment could open room for additional orders.

Quarterly rebound and treasury share cancellation

Second-quarter 2026 operating profit was KRW 12.5 billion, up 57.0% from the prior quarter. A KRW 15 billion share purchase disclosed in late August 2026 is planned to run through March 2027, with the shares cancelled. A securities firm said a lower exchange rate is positive given the high share of imported parts.

Delayed prime-contract orders

Failed stake sale and share price pressure

Talks with Korea Investment Partners on a stake sale ended on August 11, 2026, and the market viewed share price declines and valuation strain as a barrier to a deal. The largest shareholder has left open a resale, so the stake remains a potential overhang.

Operating cash flow was negative KRW 39.9 billion in 2024, which is also cited as a weakness in cash flow stability.

Risk factors

Customer and order concentration

Recent counterparties are concentrated among a few prime contractors, including Hanwha Aerospace, Hyundai Rotem and LIG D&A. Results depend on those primes' tank and howitzer orders and production schedules.

Quarterly sales can drop sharply, as in the first quarter of 2026, and disclosed contract sizes alone do not make quarterly sales easy to forecast.

Ownership and share overhang

The largest shareholder consortium holds about 74% of the shares, and although the sale talks ended, a resale remains possible. The June 2026 bonus issue and the late-July listing of new shares increased the share count. A large block sale could weigh on the share price.

Financing, cash flow and exchange rates

Total liabilities were KRW 208.2 billion at the end of 2025, and the debt ratio of 107.0% is lower than 301.9% in 2023 but still high relative to equity. Operating cash flow was negative KRW 39.9 billion in 2024.

With a high share of imported parts, exchange rates affect costs and margins, so profit could swing with currency moves.
