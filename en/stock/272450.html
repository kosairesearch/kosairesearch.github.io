Third-Quarter Turnaround on Yen-Driven Demand; LCC Merger Is the Next Test

02

Business structure

JIN AIR is a low-cost carrier within the Hanjin Group, and both its disclosed results and press coverage describe short-haul passenger flights as the core of the business. The company concentrates aircraft on short-haul routes it considers most profitable.

In the third quarter of 2026, added Japan flights and expanded service to smaller regional cities were reported to have driven sales growth. Demand on Japan routes is described as sustained through the seasons by a weak yen, while China route demand has recovered alongside China's visa exemption policy.

Because capacity leans on short-haul routes to Japan and China, results are sensitive to exchange rates and overseas demand. In the LCC market, the company competes with Jejuair. On the full-service side, the merger of Korean AIR Lines and Asiana Airlines is set to launch on December 17, 2026.

JIN AIR received merger approval with Air Seoul and Air Busan on November 12, 2026, and will become the core of a consolidated LCC on March 17, 2027.

Under the merger, Air Busan's Busan infrastructure and employees are to be taken over, and the company has submitted a plan to expand Gimhae international routes from 28 to 33 and weekly flights from 281 to 339.

In the KCGS evaluation announced on November 13, 2026, the social pillar was raised to A+ and the integrated grade of A was kept for the fourth consecutive year.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual sales rose from KRW 593.4 billion in 2022 to KRW 1.28 trillion in 2023, an increase of 115.2%, and reached KRW 1.46 trillion in 2024, up 14.4%, which makes 2024 the largest year in the 2022-2025 period. Sales fell 5.5% to KRW 1.38 trillion in 2025.

Operating results moved from an operating loss of KRW 67.3 billion in 2022 to an operating profit of KRW 182.2 billion in 2023, a swing to profit. Operating profit then declined 10.5% to KRW 163.1 billion in 2024, with operating margins of 14.3% in 2023 and 11.2% in 2024.

In 2025, the company posted an operating loss of KRW 19.2 billion, a return to loss, and an operating margin of -1.4%. Net income attributable to controlling shareholders rose to KRW 133.9 billion in 2023, fell 28.5% to KRW 95.7 billion in 2024, and turned into a net loss of KRW 9.76 billion in 2025.

Net income including non-controlling interests was a net loss of KRW 9.76 billion in 2025 and followed the same direction as the controlling-shareholder figure.

The debt-to-equity ratio fell from 607.9% in 2022 to 423.2% in 2025, with total equity of KRW 224.4 billion and total liabilities of KRW 949.7 billion at the end of 2025.

Operating cash flow went from KRW 175.6 billion in 2022 to KRW 417.4 billion in 2023 and KRW 329 billion in 2024, then fell to KRW 79.8 billion in 2025.

Quarterly results swung from an operating loss of KRW 22.5 billion in the third quarter of 2025 to an operating profit of KRW 57.6 billion in the first quarter of 2026, then an operating loss of KRW 73.1 billion in the second quarter and an operating profit of KRW 7.77 billion in the third quarter.

The sum of controlling-shareholder net losses over the last four quarters, from the fourth quarter of 2025 to the third quarter of 2026, is KRW 26.8 billion.

Cumulative sales for January to September 2026 were KRW 1.1964 trillion, up 16.3%, while the cumulative operating loss of KRW 7.7 billion was wider than the KRW 6.5 billion loss a year earlier (preliminary, disclosed November 6, 2026).

05

Industry analysis

The short-haul LCC market is shaped at the same time by exchange rates, fuel prices and overseas route demand. In describing its third-quarter 2026 results, the company said it concentrated capacity on routes with higher profitability despite a difficult operating environment with high fuel prices.

Press coverage cites high fuel prices and a strong currency as unfavorable external factors. By contrast, a weak yen and China's short-term visa easing are cited as supportive factors that lifted short-haul demand.

On the demand side, passenger traffic on Japan and China routes is reported to have supported third-quarter results. On the full-service side, the merged full-service carrier formed by Korean AIR Lines and Asiana Airlines is set to launch on December 17, 2026.

The Ministry of Land, Infrastructure and Transport said it would also review ways to strengthen the competitiveness of other LCCs as the scale of the Korean AIR Lines group grows. Among LCCs, JIN AIR, Air Seoul and Air Busan are combining into one carrier, which creates a single large low-cost operator.

Jejuair remains a separate company, so competition among LCCs continues after the merger. Against this backdrop, JIN AIR's position rests on short-haul capacity and scale, including its expansion from the Busan base.

06

Outlook

For the fourth quarter of 2026, the company described a mix of supportive factors, including a weak yen and a stabilizing currency, and external risks such as fuel price volatility.

The company said it would diversify route competitiveness and strengthen its preventive maintenance system in the fourth quarter to sustain solid profitability.

For the consolidated LCC, the company submitted a plan to expand Gimhae international routes from 28 to 33 and weekly flights from 281 to 339 to the Ministry of Land, Infrastructure and Transport.

It also plans to develop Gimhae airport as an independent maintenance base, take over Air Busan's infrastructure and employees, rule out forced restructuring, and expand hiring of cabin crew and mechanics.

Conditions attached to the merger approval require completion of a safety operations system change inspection and overseas aviation authority approvals before the combined carrier launches, and progress on consumer protection and merger plans must be reported monthly.

The 2026 wage agreement has been provisionally agreed, with general, technical and aircraft maintenance staff receiving up to 2% increases in total pay and cabin crew receiving 2% increases in base pay and flight allowances.

The provisional deal becomes final after a union membership vote, and any increase would be applied retroactively from April 2026. Post-merger earnings are therefore expected to reflect both the combined route structure and higher labor costs, a point to be confirmed in the next annual report.

Bull factors

Record Quarterly Sales in Q3

Operating profit for the third quarter of 2026 was KRW 7.77 billion, a turnaround from an operating loss of KRW 22.5 billion. The company and the press attribute the growth to strong Japan route demand under a weak yen and a recovery in China routes. Profit improving alongside higher sales reflects the effect of the route strategy.

Scale Expansion Through the Consolidated LCC

A consolidated LCC centered on JIN AIR launches on March 17, 2027. The company submitted a plan to expand Gimhae international routes from 28 to 33 and weekly flights from 281 to 339 as a second hub.

The government approved the merger on November 12, 2026, and the existing Air Busan infrastructure and employees will be taken over. Whether the larger scale translates into profitability is a matter for disclosures after the launch.

Lower Leverage and ESG Recognition

The debt-to-equity ratio fell from 607.9% in 2022 to 423.2% in 2025. Total equity at the end of 2025 was KRW 224.4 billion and total liabilities were KRW 949.7 billion. In the KCGS 2026 evaluation, the company kept an integrated A grade for the fourth consecutive year and the social pillar rose to A+.

The company said proactive investment in aviation safety and consumer-centered management contributed positively.

Bear factors

Cumulative Losses Persist Through Q3

The cumulative net loss of KRW 14.6 billion compares with net income of KRW 2.4 billion a year earlier. Quarterly swings, including the KRW 73.1 billion operating loss in the second quarter of 2026, leave open whether profitable quarters can offset the full-year loss.

External Headwinds from Fuel and Currency

A company official said supportive factors and external risks coexist, so volatility in the operating environment is expected to continue. Press coverage cites high fuel prices and a strong currency as unfavorable external factors.

In 2025, sales fell 5.5%, the company returned to an operating loss of KRW 19.2 billion, and operating cash flow dropped from KRW 417.4 billion in 2023 to KRW 79.8 billion in 2025.

Reliance on Japan and China Route Demand

Third-quarter results are described as relying heavily on Japan route demand under a weak yen and on demand recovery linked to China's visa exemption policy. If conditions on these routes change, the ability to adjust capacity will determine earnings stability.

With a controlling-shareholder net loss of KRW 26.8 billion over the last four quarters, a weaker route demand could halt the earnings recovery again.

10

Risk factors

Merger Execution Risk

The merger approval makes a safety operations system change inspection and overseas aviation authority approvals conditions before the combined carrier launches. Consumer protection and merger progress must be reported to the Ministry every month.

Expanding the Busan base and taking over staff at the same time may add operational strain. Failure to meet these conditions could affect the launch schedule and the route plan overall.

Management Gap and Ownership Change

Press reports say Park Byeong-ryul, the CEO who led the integration work, resigned before the end of his term, leaving a leadership gap ahead of the merger. At the same time, a construction-linked shareholder became the largest shareholder of the group holding company, overlapping with the governance change.

Successor appointments and demands related to moving headquarters are tied to the integration timeline and will be confirmed through future disclosures and reports.

Rising Labor Cost Burden

The 2026 wage agreement is under provisional terms, with general, technical and aircraft maintenance staff receiving up to 2% increases in total pay. Cabin crew base pay and flight allowances rise 2%, and flight crew receive a 2% base pay increase along with a landing fee increase.

The deal becomes final after a union vote, and any increase would apply retroactively from April 2026. How higher labor costs are reflected in earnings after the merger is the next item to confirm.

Overall view

JIN AIR recorded third-quarter 2026 sales of KRW 413.1 billion and an operating profit of KRW 7.77 billion, a quarterly turnaround.

However, the cumulative January-September 2026 results remain in the red, with an operating loss of KRW 7.7 billion and a net loss of KRW 14.6 billion (preliminary, disclosed November 6, 2026), and quarterly swings remain large, including the KRW 73.1 billion operating loss in the second quarter.

Annual disclosed results moved back into a loss in 2025 after returning to profit in 2023, while the debt-to-equity ratio fell from 607.9% in 2022 to 423.2% in 2025.

Supportive factors include Japan route demand under a weak yen, the recovery in China routes, and the scale expansion planned through the consolidated LCC.

Headwinds include external factors such as high fuel prices and a strong currency, the gap in top management, and a controlling-shareholder net loss of KRW 26.8 billion over the last four quarters. On valuation, the net losses of the last four quarters mean an earnings-based multiple cannot be calculated.

The next checkpoints are the December 17, 2026 launch of the merged full-service carrier, preliminary fourth-quarter results in early 2027, the 2026 annual report in March 2027, and the March 17, 2027 launch of the consolidated LCC.
