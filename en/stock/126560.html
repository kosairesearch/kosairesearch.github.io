02

Business structure

Hyundai Futurenet is an ICT company founded in 1992 and part of the Hyundai Department Store Group. Its businesses are cosmetic materials, IT services, digital signage, corporate messaging, and immersive content.

In 2025 the sales mix was cosmetic materials and related items at 49.32%, IT services at 26.83%, digital signage at 10.16%, corporate messaging at 8.79%, and immersive content at 3.26% (fnguide compilation, December 2025). The cosmetic materials share fell from 60.13% in 2023 to 55.25% in 2024 and 49.32% in 2025.

The IT services share rose from 14.59% in 2024 to 26.83% in 2025, following the 2024 acquisition and merger of Hyundai I&E, which broadened system operation, cloud and AI solution work. Digital signage installs large LED displays in public and commercial spaces and distributes content on them.

The immersive content business runs media art venues, including Le Space on Yeongjong Island in Incheon, which opened in 2024. Corporate messaging and IT services mainly serve business customers.

In June 2026 the company said it signed a contract with the operator of Baskin Robbins in Korea to supply an AI chatbot for store use. Growth in cosmetic materials has been attributed to wider demand for natural ingredients and functional raw materials.

Because close to half of sales come from materials, the company is better compared with peers business by business than with telecom service providers. Research and development spending in 2025 was KRW 7.509 billion, equal to 2.84% of sales (fnguide compilation).

04

Earnings analysis

On an annual basis, sales rose from KRW 154.2 billion in 2022 to KRW 168.6 billion in 2023 (up 9.3% year on year), KRW 215.8 billion in 2024 (up 28.0%), and KRW 264.5 billion in 2025 (up 22.6%), growing three years in a row from 2023 through 2025, with 2025 the largest year in the 2022 to 2025 period.

Operating profit fell from KRW 9.52 billion in 2022 to KRW 2.45 billion in 2023 (down 74.2%), recovered to KRW 6.94 billion in 2024 (up 183.1%), and was KRW 6.31 billion in 2025 (down 9.1%). 2022 was the largest operating profit year in the period.

Sales grew while the operating margin fell from 6.2% in 2022 to 1.5% in 2023, 3.2% in 2024 and 2.4% in 2025. The cost of sales ratio also climbed from 69.19% in 2022 to 76.41% in 2025 (fnguide compilation), so top-line expansion has not translated into margin gains.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders moved from a loss of KRW 5.38 billion in 2022 and a loss of KRW 1.88 billion in 2023 to a profit of KRW 6.99 billion in 2024 and KRW 7.53 billion in 2025 (up 7.7%).

Net income including non-controlling interests was a loss of KRW 2.78 billion in 2022, a profit of KRW 2.86 billion in 2023, KRW 11.6 billion in 2024 and KRW 7.16 billion in 2025, a different figure from the controlling-shareholder line.

The 2025 rise in controlling-shareholder profit despite lower operating profit suggests non-operating items and minority interests contributed, and the line-item detail is a task for the 2026 annual report.

Quarterly, third-quarter 2025 sales were KRW 69.3 billion, operating profit KRW 3.73 billion and controlling-shareholder net income KRW 3.62 billion.

Fourth-quarter 2025 sales fell 5.5% to KRW 65.5 billion, the low point, and the quarter swung to an operating loss of KRW 5.56 billion and a controlling-shareholder net loss of KRW 2.54 billion.

Sales then rose to KRW 70.9 billion in the first quarter of 2026, KRW 71.6 billion in the second and KRW 76.7 billion in the third, three straight quarters of growth.

Operating profit was KRW 4.31 billion in the first quarter of 2026 (a return to profit), KRW 6.6 billion in the second (up 53.2% from the prior quarter) and KRW 6.28 billion in the third (down 4.8%).

Controlling-shareholder net income was KRW 5.18 billion in the second quarter and KRW 4.9 billion in the third, and the last four quarters total KRW 10.5 billion. Third-quarter operating profit grew 68.3% year on year against 10.7% sales growth, so the quarterly margin improved from a year earlier.

Because the 2025 annual operating margin was 2.4%, the full-year 2026 margin level still needs confirmation in the annual report.

05

Industry analysis

Hyundai Futurenet is classified in the telecommunications sector, but its sales come from cosmetic materials, IT services, digital signage, corporate messaging and immersive content. Demand differs by business.

Cosmetic materials are supported by the expansion of the natural ingredient market and rising demand for functional raw materials. IT services benefit from growing corporate demand for system operation, cloud and AI solutions. Digital signage depends on advertising and content demand in commercial and public spaces.

The fall in the cosmetic materials share from 60.13% in 2023 to 49.32% in 2025 indicates a more diversified portfolio. Compared with the large telecom operators SK Telecom, KT and LG Uplus, the company differs in both business structure and sales scale.

Its linkage to Hyundai Department Store Group sales channels can be seen as a qualitative strength, but the sales contribution of that linkage is not broken out by segment.

Bull factors

Quarterly Profit Turnaround

Third-quarter operating profit rose 68.3% year on year and controlling-shareholder net income rose 35.4%. Sales growth over three straight quarters alongside profit gains is the core bullish point.

Shareholder Returns Alongside

Between August 4 and October 22, 2026, the company acquired 1,054,861 treasury shares for KRW 3.5 billion. The 2,446,701 treasury shares held are scheduled for cancellation on November 20, 2026, and the company said share capital will not decline because the cancellation is funded from distributable profit. The debt-to-equity ratio was 7.1% at the end of 2025, indicating ample financial capacity.

Rising IT Services Share

The IT services share of sales rose from 14.59% in 2024 to 26.83% in 2025. In June 2026 the company said it signed an AI chatbot supply contract with the operator of Baskin Robbins in Korea. The narrowing reliance on cosmetic materials is a supportive factor.

Bear factors

Shrinking Operating Margin

The operating margin fell from 6.2% in 2022 to 2.4% in 2025. Over the same period the cost of sales ratio rose from 69.19% to 76.41%, adding cost pressure. Margins falling despite three years of sales growth suggests slow conversion of top-line expansion into profit.

Quarterly Profit Volatility

The fourth quarter of 2025 swung to an operating loss of KRW 5.56 billion and a controlling-shareholder net loss of KRW 2.54 billion. Operating profit rose 53.2% in the second quarter of 2026 but fell 4.8% in the third.

Controlling-shareholder net income over the last four quarters totals KRW 10.5 billion, and quarter-to-quarter swings make the trend slower to confirm.

Mix and Liquidity

The cosmetic materials share declined from 60.13% in 2023 to 49.32% in 2025 but still accounts for close to half of sales. Major shareholders hold 79.27%, leaving a free float ratio of 19.28%. A thin tradable float can amplify share price volatility.

Risk factors

IT Services Execution

IT services have broadened into system operation, cloud and AI solutions since the 2024 acquisition and merger of Hyundai I&E. If delivery or operating burden grows while the June 2026 AI chatbot supply contract for Baskin Robbins stores is carried out, profitability may fall short of expectations.

Delays in integrating the people and systems of the merged company could also add cost to IT services. Whether this business sustainably supports overall sales growth needs to be confirmed.

Reliance on Group Channels

As a member of the Hyundai Department Store Group, the company's linkage to group sales channels is seen as a qualitative strength, but its sales contribution by business is not disclosed.

Digital signage and immersive content depend on demand from group stores and commercial spaces, so a change in the operating direction of group channels could directly affect sales. Whether exhibition demand at venues such as Le Space on Yeongjong Island is sustained is another variable. Because the channel contribution is not broken out by segment, the size of this risk is difficult to gauge.

Visibility of New Businesses

The company emphasizes expansion into digital healthcare, AI and big data. However, how quickly these businesses feed into sales is not yet clear in quarterly results. Because segment disclosure is coarse, tracking their contribution separately is difficult.

Overall view

Hyundai Futurenet's sales grew three years in a row through 2025, but the operating margin fell from 6.2% in 2022 to 2.4% in 2025, so the pace at which top-line growth converts into profit has been slow.

After returning to profit in the first quarter of 2026, sales rose in all three quarters, and third-quarter operating profit grew 68.3% year on year, so the quarterly trend has improved.

However, the fourth quarter of 2025 recorded an operating loss of KRW 5.56 billion, and third-quarter 2026 operating profit fell 4.8% from the prior quarter, so the stability of quarterly profit is still being tested.

On the balance sheet, controlling-shareholder equity was KRW 727.9 billion of total equity of KRW 824.3 billion at the end of 2025, and total liabilities were KRW 58.5 billion, a comfortable financial position.

Shareholder returns are proceeding through KRW 3.5 billion of treasury share purchases from August to October 2026 and the planned cancellation of 2,446,701 shares on November 20, 2026. The business mix is shifting toward IT services and away from cosmetic materials.

The 79.27% major shareholder stake and low free float remain features for trading. The next items to confirm are the fourth-quarter 2026 results and whether the operating margin in the 2026 annual report rises above 2.4% in 2025.
