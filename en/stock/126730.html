02

Business structure

Korchip is an electronic components company founded in 1994. In 2002 it took over the DLC business unit from Samsung Electro-Mechanics, which gave it its core technology.

The company runs two businesses side by side: manufacturing small lithium-based and supercapacitor products under its own ChipCell brand, and distributing MLCCs made by Samsung Electro-Mechanics.

The company has set a target of raising its own-product share of revenue to 70%, while a 2026 breakdown shows supercapacitors (ChipCell Carbon) at 59%, MLCCs at 40%, and ChipCell Lithium at 2%, so the sources do not agree on the mix.

The lithium line is moving into lithium titanate (LTO) batteries, which the company packages into large-capacity prismatic modules for energy storage. Its partnership with Samsung Electro-Mechanics on distribution and sales dates to 1992.

Through Delta Electronics of Taiwan, the company's supercapacitors reach NVIDIA's AI server supply chain, a link a company official confirmed in December 2025.

Only four makers, including Samsung Electro-Mechanics, produce high-performance MLCCs for AI servers, and orders reportedly concentrate at Samsung Electro-Mechanics. How the mix shifts between self-made products and distributed parts will shape margins.

04

Earnings analysis

On an annual basis, sales rose from KRW 32.9 billion in 2023 to KRW 33.4 billion in 2024, a 1.4% increase, and then to KRW 38.3 billion in 2025, a 14.7% increase.

Over the same years operating profit fell from KRW 4.19 billion to KRW 2.9 billion (down 30.8%) and then to KRW 1.14 billion (down 60.7%), so it declined in two straight years, with 2023 the largest year of the three. The operating margin was 12.7% in 2023, 8.7% in 2024, and 3.0% in 2025, falling even as sales grew.

Profit attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 4.8 billion in 2023, KRW 3.23 billion in 2024 (down 32.7%), and KRW 2.85 billion in 2025 (down 11.8%). Net profit including non-controlling interests is reported at the same levels.

Equity at the end of 2025 was KRW 55.8 billion, all attributable to controlling shareholders, against total liabilities of KRW 11.4 billion, with the debt ratio at 20.4%. Operating cash flow was KRW 7.49 billion in 2023, KRW 4.4 billion in 2024, and KRW 4.61 billion in 2025.

Quarterly sales rose in each of the four quarters from the fourth quarter of 2025 through the third quarter of 2026, reaching KRW 21.8 billion in the latest quarter.

Operating profit moved from KRW 66.05 million in the third quarter of 2025 to KRW 4.43 billion in the third quarter of 2026, a sharp rise from a very low base. Profit attributable to controlling shareholders peaked at KRW 3.49 billion in the second quarter of 2026 and fell to KRW 1.68 billion in the third quarter.

Its gap with third-quarter operating profit suggests non-operating items or taxes played a role, and the detail should be checked in the periodic report notes.

05

Industry analysis

The MLCC market has tightened as AI chip demand expands. Reports from late 2025 said Samsung Electro-Mechanics' MLCC plants were running at full capacity, and the company's large silicon capacitor orders drew attention to its supply chain.

Korchip is tied to this flow through its long distribution relationship with Samsung Electro-Mechanics, and only four makers produce high-performance MLCCs for AI servers, which is why supply-side benefits are discussed.

In supercapacitors, data center demand is rising, and a company official said Korchip plans to move from single-part supply toward customized integrated solutions. In competition, Korchip is a component maker with its own production but also a large distributor, so its position differs from pure-play MLCC leaders.

Uncertainty remains. Reports tied AI infrastructure credit risk and a Chinese memory maker's server memory plans to concerns about slower AI investment and supply competition. A research analyst noted that actual capex cuts or large-scale Chinese mass production had not yet appeared.

The key variables are how far AI server demand converts into MLCC and supercapacitor orders and whether capex plans hold.

06

Outlook

The most concrete point the company has made concerns supercapacitor demand. In May 2026 a company official said orders surged as new demand from AI data centers grew quickly. The first quarter of 2026 was attributed to larger orders from existing customers and new customers joining at the same time.

Lithium titanate battery expansion was named the central pillar of the new business. A domestic brokerage said in an April 30, 2026 report that the company had finished recognizing costs in 2025 and would enter a normalization phase for earnings this year.

That view gave no target price and reflects the brokerage's own assessment, not KOSAI's. The largest growth path runs through the Delta supply link to NVIDIA AI servers, and the pace at which that supply grows will likely shape quarterly results after 2027.

Whether the revenue mix shifts as the company moves toward integrated AI server solutions is another point to watch. The next checkpoint is whether the operating margin, which was 3.0% in 2025, recovers in the 2026 annual report.

08

Bull factors

Consecutive quarterly growth

Sales rose for four straight quarters from the fourth quarter of 2025 through the third quarter of 2026, and operating profit also grew across the same four quarters. Third-quarter 2026 sales of KRW 21.8 billion were up 124.4% from a year earlier. The operating margin is still low on an annual basis, but the quarterly trend shows a clear recovery.

AI server supply path

A company official said in December 2025 that its supercapacitors were being supplied to NVIDIA through Delta Electronics. A market research firm has also named Korchip among likely beneficiaries of NVIDIA's next-generation chips. Data center demand connects directly to this supply path, which is cited as a growth driver.

Orders and a broader customer base

In May 2026 a company official said supercapacitor orders had grown significantly. In the first quarter of 2026, larger orders from existing customers and new customers arrived at the same time.

The distribution relationship with Samsung Electro-Mechanics, spanning roughly three decades, is cited as a stable trading base.

09

Bear factors

Annual profitability decline

Operating profit fell from KRW 4.19 billion in 2023 to KRW 1.14 billion in 2025, and the operating margin dropped from 12.7% to 3.0%. Sales grew over the same period, but profit did not keep pace. Profit attributable to controlling shareholders also declined in 2024 and 2025.

Volatility in quarterly profit

Profit attributable to controlling shareholders fell 51.9% from KRW 3.49 billion in the second quarter of 2026 to KRW 1.68 billion in the third quarter. Operating profit was only KRW 148 million in the fourth quarter of 2025. Quarterly swings are wide, which makes it hard to judge the trend from one or two quarters.

Cost pressure and rising expenses

In its 2025 disclosure the company cited higher manufacturing cost ratios from rising raw material prices and labor costs. Expenses from R&D and capacity investment also increased. If this cost structure persists, margin improvement may stay limited even as sales grow.

10

Risk factors

Customer and demand concentration

Supercapacitor and MLCC distribution rely heavily on AI server demand and on Samsung Electro-Mechanics' supply structure. A slowdown in AI investment or changes in customer orders could move quarterly sales quickly.

A structure that leans on specific customers and supply routes feeds directly into results when order gaps appear.

Cost and expense burden

Higher raw material prices and labor costs lifted the manufacturing cost ratio, while R&D and capacity spending weigh on operating leverage. With the operating margin down to 3.0% in 2025, results are sensitive to cost pressure. Whether expenses offset sales growth should be checked in the next reports.

Small new-business contribution and conflicting data

ChipCell Lithium accounted for only 2% of the sales mix as of March 2026, so the new business's contribution is still small. Reported figures for the share of self-made products also differ across sources, making one definitive number hard to fix.

Actual new-business revenue should be confirmed from segment data in the periodic reports. A high controlling-shareholder stake and a low free-float ratio also shape trading characteristics.

12

Overall view

Korchip's quarterly sales and operating profit rose sharply in 2026, and profit is in a recovery phase. Third-quarter 2026 sales were KRW 21.8 billion and operating profit KRW 4.43 billion, the largest sales figure among the disclosed quarters.

On an annual basis, however, operating profit and profit attributable to controlling shareholders fell in 2024 and 2025, and the operating margin dropped from 12.7% in 2023 to 3.0% in 2025.

Profit attributable to controlling shareholders totaled KRW 7.78 billion over the last four quarters, so the earnings base is still thin.

Bullish factors include growing supercapacitor orders tied to AI servers, the supply route to NVIDIA through Delta Electronics, and a long distribution relationship with Samsung Electro-Mechanics.

Bearish factors include cost pressure from raw materials and labor, volatile quarterly profit, and the small weight of new businesses. The shares trade at a premium to book value, so changes in quarterly profit feed into the multiple. Differences in reported revenue-mix figures also need to be checked.

The next checkpoints are the operating margin in the 2026 annual report and whether sales growth continues in the first-quarter 2027 report.
