02

Business structure

iFamilySC, founded in 2000, is a content and color cosmetics company that manufactures and distributes globally, anchored by its flagship color brand rom&nd and skincare and color brand nuse. It entered cosmetics in earnest with the launch of rom&nd in 2016 and listed on KOSDAQ in 2021.

As of the end of the first half of 2026, the cosmetics division generated 95% of total revenue, with wedding and living businesses accounting for the rest. Overseas exposure is large: exports reached about KRW 146.2 billion in 2025, or 65.22% of total revenue.

Q3 2026 regional revenue was KRW 18.4 billion in Korea (33% of total, up 5% year on year), KRW 5.1 billion in the United States (up 26%), and KRW 3.5 billion in Europe (up 31%).

Press reports said selling and administrative expenses rose from KRW 39.6 billion in 2024 to KRW 61.1 billion in 2025 and that KRW 42.3 billion was spent in the first half of 2026.

Distribution is also shifting toward direct operation: the Japan e-commerce channel has been converted to direct management, and preparations for direct operation of the Olive Young channel in Korea and a China international mall were reported.

Competitively, the company is compared with Korean listed cosmetics firms that are expanding overseas distribution behind color brands.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual results moved in different directions for sales and profit. Revenue rose from KRW 85.3 billion in 2022 to KRW 148.7 billion in 2023 (up 74.2% year on year), KRW 204.9 billion in 2024 (up 37.8%), and KRW 224.1 billion in 2025 (up 9.4%).

Sales rose for three consecutive years from 2023 through 2025, and 2025 was the largest year and 2022 the smallest among 2022 to 2025. Press reports described 2025 revenue as a record high.

Operating profit rose from KRW 9.51 billion in 2022 to KRW 24 billion in 2023 (up 152.4%) and KRW 33.6 billion in 2024 (up 40.2%), making 2024 the largest year. It then fell 19.3% to KRW 27.1 billion in 2025. Operating margin moved from 11.1% in 2022 and 16.1% in 2023 to 16.4% in 2024 before declining to 12.1% in 2025.

Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders peaked at KRW 28.3 billion in 2024 and fell 22.9% to KRW 21.8 billion in 2025.

Press reports said selling and administrative expenses rose to KRW 61.1 billion in 2025, which the company attributed to offline store launches, new skincare brand launches, and marketing and logistics costs for U.S. expansion. Operating cash flow swung from -KRW 9.31 billion in 2024 to KRW 43 billion in 2025.

On a quarterly basis, operating profit was KRW 6.18 billion in Q3 2025 and KRW 6.62 billion in Q4 2025, fell to KRW 3.69 billion in Q1 2026, rebounded to KRW 6.81 billion in Q2 2026, and dropped to KRW 1.88 billion in Q3 2026.

According to press calculations, the Q3 2026 operating margin was 3.4%, down from 11.6% a year earlier. The company cited front-loaded overseas investment, one-time distribution restructuring costs, and the disposal of excess 2024 second-half stock with inventory provisions as reasons for the Q3 decline.

Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders over the most recent four quarters totaled KRW 14.4 billion.

05

Industry analysis

Cosmetics earnings in this sector tend to move quarter to quarter with the timing of overseas channel listings and major promotional events. For iFamilySC, the U.S. large-format retail rollout, the Amazon channel, and events such as Japan's Mega Warari have been cited as drivers of sales swings.

Growth in Q3 2026 came from Korea, the United States, and Europe, with U.S. and European sales up 26% and 31%, respectively. That points to overseas channel expansion lifting top-line growth.

The Q3 decline in operating profit, by contrast, is explained by the company's own front-loaded investment and inventory clearance costs more than by sector-wide conditions. Competition for overseas distribution is intense, with other Korean color cosmetics and K-beauty listed firms also expanding abroad.

Peers pursuing overseas channel expansion include APR and Silicon 2. The company emphasizes its rom&nd-centered color brand lineup and the direct operation of Olive Young in Korea. Because overseas sales make up a large share, local inventory control and logistics costs are a main source of earnings volatility.

06

Outlook

In its Q1 2026 results release in May 2026, the company said sales growth would accelerate from Q2 and its earnings structure would normalize from the second half. Because operating profit fell in Q3 2026, whether that outlook plays out will become clear from results after Q4.

The channel plans the company has cited include preparations to list in about 600 large U.S. retail stores and the simultaneous rollout of major channels in the United Kingdom, Germany, the Czech Republic, Poland, Russia, and other European markets.

Japan's e-commerce channel has been converted to direct operation, and the company said in March 2026 that it had completed preparations for direct operation of its China international mall.

In its Q3 announcement, the company said it set up the OPS team to manage supply and inventory from demand forecasting through sell-through, with the aim of raising mid- to long-term profitability.

On shareholder returns, the company plans to continue quarterly dividends with a payout ratio around 20% alongside share buybacks and cancellations. About KRW 9 billion of treasury shares were cancelled in June 2026.

The tender offer by Marubeni of Japan has closed, and the company said it is reviewing strategic cooperation and global business alliances based on a business cooperation proposal (BAA) discussed during the offer. It is not yet known whether that review will lead to a concrete agreement or disclosure.

08

Bull factors

Overseas channel expansion and top-line growth

Q3 2026 revenue rose 3.9% year on year to KRW 55.6 billion. U.S. sales in the quarter reached KRW 5.1 billion, up 26%, and European sales reached KRW 3.5 billion, up 31%. With exports at 65.22% of 2025 revenue, growth in overseas sales is the main support for the top line. The company said it is preparing listings in major U.S. retail channels and key European channels.

Evidence of a Q2 profit rebound

Q2 2026 operating profit was KRW 6.81 billion, up 84.6% from KRW 3.69 billion in Q1 2026. The company cited one-time costs such as stock disposals and inventory provisions as the main reason for the Q3 decline.

In March 2026, a company official said logistics cost improvements, a restructured marketing setup, direct operation of Olive Young, and production cost savings would become visible from the second half and lift operating margin.

Expanded shareholder return policy

Press reports from 2025 said the dividend payout ratio was raised from about 7% to about 20%, and quarterly dividends have been paid. The company combines share buybacks with cancellations, and about KRW 9 billion of treasury shares were cancelled in June 2026. The company's plan to continue these returns was also reported in September 2026.

09

Bear factors

Profitability erosion persists

Profitability in Q3 2026 was lower than a year earlier. Selling, general and administrative expenses in the quarter rose 38.8% to KRW 21.5 billion. Cumulative operating profit for the first three quarters of 2026 was KRW 12.4 billion, down 39.6%. Full-year 2025 operating profit also fell 19.3%.

Financial strain at the U.S. subsidiary

The equity of the U.S. subsidiary ROMAND BEAUTY CORP turned negative at KRW -1.7 billion in the year after its founding and widened to KRW -3.6 billion by the end of the first half of 2026. Press reports described this as full capital erosion.

Commentary has said that continued losses at the subsidiary add a burden to consolidated results even as local sales grow.

Risk of repeated inventory charges

To clear excess supply from the surge in global demand in the second half of 2024, the company disposed of products nearing expiry and recorded inventory provisions. Because the inventory issue hit profit in a single period, the company had to rebuild its inventory management system. Whether similar charges recur will show up in later quarterly results and periodic reports.

10

Risk factors

Inventory and supply chain

Excess supply from the second half of 2024 led to the disposal of products nearing expiry. The company created the OPS team to oversee supply chain and inventory, but as overseas distribution widens and operating volumes grow, inventory risk also increases. If inventory provisions hit results again, the swings in operating profit could become larger.

Overseas dependence and cost structure

Exports were 65.22% of 2025 revenue. In the United States and Europe, channel listing and marketing costs arrive ahead of the sales they support. If those costs keep rising faster than sales, the decline in operating margin could repeat. Press reports of selling and administrative expenses rising to KRW 61.1 billion in 2025 illustrate the pattern.

Direction of business alliances

The tender offer by Marubeni of Japan has closed, and the company is reviewing strategic cooperation based on a BAA. The review is described as being at an early stage, so the scope and timing of any cooperation have not been set. Depending on the outcome, the business direction and shareholder base could change.

12

Overall view

Q3 2026 was a quarter in which revenue of KRW 55.6 billion (up 3.9% year on year) diverged from operating profit of KRW 1.88 billion (down 69.5%). Sales grew on U.S. and European channel expansion, while profit fell because of front-loaded overseas investment and the disposal of stock with inventory provisions.

On a full-year basis, 2025 revenue of KRW 224.1 billion was the largest in the 2022 to 2025 period, but operating profit fell to KRW 27.1 billion and the operating margin declined to 12.1%. Strengths include overseas sales growth, signs of a profit rebound in Q2, and an expanded shareholder return policy.

Weaknesses include margin pressure from higher selling and administrative expenses, capital erosion at the U.S. subsidiary, and reduced earnings visibility reflected in a lower broker target price.

The company says the OPS team will strengthen inventory control, and the effect should appear in the 2026 annual report and Q1 2027 results. The outcome of the Marubeni cooperation review and whether U.S. store listings show up in reported sales are the next items to track.
