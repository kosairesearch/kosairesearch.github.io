02

Business structure

Daechang Solution, founded in 1977, is a specialty steel materials company centered on steel castings, which are large cast steel parts made by pouring molten steel into molds, and it also runs cryogenic, living-safety and offshore wind businesses.

Its main products include offshore plant equipment, radioactive waste storage containers, power generation equipment, shipbuilding components and cryogenic valves.

In its first-quarter 2026 disclosure, the shipbuilding components segment accounted for the largest share of revenue, followed by the offshore solutions and radioactive waste segments. The average utilization rate of its casting operations was 75% in the same quarter.

In nuclear power, the core product is the casting-type radioactive waste container (RWC), which the company says was the first of its kind to be commercialized in the world and which, by its account, can increase waste storage capacity more than twofold.

Each RWC is about 2 to 3.2 meters long and weighs about 30 tons, and can be stacked six high. Its nuclear steam turbine components are said to have supplied about 8,000 tons to companies including Doosan Enerbility and GE.

In shipbuilding, the main bearing support (MBS), a core marine engine part, is the flagship product, and in 2025 the company signed a supply contract worth about KRW 20 billion with a marine engine maker. Customers span shipbuilding engine makers, nuclear power operators and offshore plant project owners.

The company has also received quotation requests for large nuclear-grade casting parts from domestic and overseas organizations. Its liquefied hydrogen storage and transport tank subsidiary is planned for a KOSDAQ listing in 2026.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual revenue rose from KRW 52.8 billion in 2022 to KRW 71.7 billion in 2023, an increase of 35.9% that made 2023 the largest year of the 2022 to 2025 period. Revenue then fell 14.4% to KRW 61.4 billion in 2024 and 2.5% to KRW 59.9 billion in 2025, a two-year decline after 2023.

Operating results swung from an operating loss of KRW 4.74 billion in 2022 to operating profit of KRW 5.6 billion in 2023. Operating profit then dropped 92.2% to KRW 437 million in 2024 and turned into an operating loss of KRW 144 million in 2025.

The operating margin fell from 7.8% in 2023 to 0.7% in 2024 and -0.2% in 2025. The company attributed the 2025 operating loss to lower sales at a subsidiary.

Net loss attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 2.71 billion in 2024 and KRW 7.78 billion in 2025, two straight years of losses with the 2025 loss widening.

The total net loss including non-controlling interests was also KRW 7.78 billion in 2025, and the company cited the valuation of derivatives linked to convertible bonds. The debt-to-equity ratio was 329.1% in 2022, 260.2% in 2023, 312.4% in 2024 and 294.0% in 2025.

Operating cash flow was negative in all four years, at -KRW 2.95 billion, -KRW 564 million, -KRW 6.31 billion and -KRW 4.49 billion.

Quarterly revenue was KRW 17.2 billion in the third quarter of 2025 and KRW 17.8 billion in the fourth quarter, a 3.6% increase, while fourth-quarter operating profit fell 85.4% to KRW 171 million and the company recorded a net loss attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 757 million.

In the first quarter of 2026, revenue was KRW 21.2 billion, up 19.5% from the prior quarter, with operating profit of KRW 1.48 billion and a net profit attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 1.04 billion.

In the second quarter, revenue was KRW 18.9 billion, down 11.1%, with an operating loss of KRW 136 million and a net loss attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 1.14 billion.

In the third quarter, revenue was KRW 17.8 billion, down 5.5% sequentially and up 4.0% year on year, while operating profit was KRW 362 million, down 69.2% year on year, and the net loss attributable to controlling shareholders widened to KRW 1.48 billion.

Quarterly revenue has declined for two consecutive quarters since the first quarter of 2026, and the company has recorded two consecutive quarters of losses attributable to controlling shareholders. Over the last four quarters, that net loss totaled KRW 2.34 billion.

05

Industry analysis

Daechang Solution's end markets fall into four groups: shipbuilding, nuclear power, power generation equipment and offshore plants. In shipbuilding, tighter environmental regulation has increased orders for LNG-fueled ships and spread dual-fuel and alternative-fuel engines, which supports demand for core engine parts.

Demand for cryogenic storage tanks is described as stable in the same source. For power generation equipment, supply records with GE Power and Doosan Enerbility are expected to lead to higher volumes. In nuclear power, radioactive waste storage containers and nuclear and SMR turbine parts are presented as growth areas.

The company exhibited RWCs and turbine parts at the Busan International Nuclear Industry Expo held from April 22 to 24, 2026. Shipbuilding and nuclear orders differ in timing and scale from project to project, so the position of the industry cycle is hard to pin to a single point.

Other listed companies in the steel and metals sector include Korea Zinc, Hyundai Steel and Poongsan, but this company's business mix centers on large steel castings and nuclear and offshore projects. Because revenue recognition follows each project's delivery schedule, delays can shift sales across several quarters.

06

Outlook

In a December 2025 report, the company said 2025 orders across shipbuilding, nuclear power and offshore plants would be recognized as revenue in 2026, supporting growth and a recovery in profitability.

The major orders cited were a Canadian radioactive waste storage container project worth about KRW 10.8 billion, offshore plant supply contracts (FPSO, FLNG and FPU) worth about KRW 14 billion, and an MBS supply contract worth about KRW 20 billion.

The same report said a large power turbine supply contract was expected in the first half of 2026.

In an April 2026 report, the company expected RWC sales of more than KRW 30 billion through 2030, including KRW 25 billion already contracted, and sales of more than KRW 10 billion for nuclear turbine parts and more than KRW 5 billion for SMR turbine parts by 2030.

The same article also described a KRW 40 billion RWC supply contract through 2030, so the figures read differently depending on the passage.

However, net losses attributable to controlling shareholders continued in the second and third quarters of 2026, and third-quarter operating profit fell 69.2% year on year to KRW 362 million.

The next checkpoint is whether the pace of revenue recognition from orders, which underpins the 2030 targets, shows up in quarterly results. The planned KOSDAQ listing of the subsidiary was also presented as a 2026 task.

Its 2030 sales expectations for nuclear business were presented as more than KRW 30 billion for RWCs, more than KRW 10 billion for nuclear turbine parts and more than KRW 5 billion for SMR turbine parts. Press coverage also noted that revaluation of the stake could become a factor in the company's valuation if the listing proceeds.

Risk factors

Delays in order revenue recognition

Large projects have long production and delivery cycles, so revenue recognition can slip from one quarter to the next. In its second-quarter 2025 results, the company said sales and profit had temporarily declined because of a product mix shift at a subsidiary and concentrated R&D spending. If such timing shifts recur, the durability of the return to profit could weaken.

Achieving nuclear sales targets

Beyond the KRW 25 billion already contracted, the RWC sales of more than KRW 30 billion expected through 2030, along with nuclear and SMR turbine part sales, depend on new orders.

The company said it received quotation requests for large casting parts from domestic and overseas organizations during the Busan expo, but it is not yet determined whether these requests will become contracts.

If new contracts are not secured within the target period, the sales growth outlook for the nuclear business could be lowered.

Share price volatility and subsidiary listing

The Korea Exchange designated the stock as an investment caution issue for one day on March 26, 2026, after its share price rose 95.78% over the prior 15 days and the top 20 accounts accounted for 34.87% of buying that day.

Whether the planned subsidiary listing moves to an actual schedule will be known from later disclosures. Corporate value assessments could change depending on the listing outcome.
