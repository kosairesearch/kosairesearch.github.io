02

Business structure

Kornic Automation was founded in 1994 as Kornic System and has led the localization of control software for semiconductor and display equipment.

Its flagship product is EasyCluster, the core control software for such equipment; external sources put its domestic market share at roughly 85–90%, and because the figures differ by source, only the range is cited here. The business has three arms.

The platform arm covers EasyCluster control software and IT infrastructure, led by co-CEO Kim Ho-jung. The automation arm covers robotics, logistics, semiconductor and display automation, led by co-CEO Seo Jong-hwi; in July 2026 the company moved from a single-CEO structure under Kim Hyuk to this co-CEO setup.

Automation products include K-LAS, an inspection system for secondary-battery cathode material quality, and K-MAS, a logistics transfer automation system. The company plans to extend K-LAS into laboratory automation for pharmaceuticals and biotech, non-ferrous metals, chemicals and food.

The IT infrastructure arm supplies network security equipment under distribution agreements with global vendors such as Radware and EfficientIP. The company says K-LAS has supply references at several major domestic cathode-material makers.

Against foreign control software, the company emphasizes performance and customer support as its localization advantages.

It was also selected as a participating institution in a national R&D project worth KRW 57.6 billion led by Hanwha Solutions, with about KRW 5.2 billion of project funding allocated to the company to build a research-automation platform.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual revenue went from KRW 30.1 billion in 2022 to KRW 27.3 billion in 2023 (down 9.1% year on year), rose to KRW 55.5 billion in 2024 (up 103.0%), and fell back to KRW 30.1 billion in 2025 (down 45.8%). The largest year within 2022–2025 was 2024, and the smallest was 2023.

Profitability did not move in step with revenue. The operating margin fell from 11.1% in 2022 to 6.7% in 2023 and 0.1% in 2024, and reached -9.1% in 2025. Revenue more than doubled in 2024, yet operating profit was only KRW 45.43 million, so the larger top line did not carry through to operating profit.

Shareholder net profit moved from a net loss of KRW 5.57 billion in 2022 to a profit of KRW 2.34 billion in 2023, then fell 56.2% to KRW 1.02 billion in 2024 and turned into a shareholder net loss of KRW 2.9 billion in 2025.

In 2023 shareholder net profit exceeded operating profit of KRW 1.84 billion, which points to non-operating items adding to earnings. Net profit including non-controlling interests is reported as a separate line and was a net loss of KRW 2.9 billion in 2025.

Operating cash flow went from KRW 2.21 billion in 2024 to -KRW 4.94 billion in 2025. The debt ratio rose from 40.4% in 2022 to 62.9% in 2023, 50.2% in 2024 and 96.4% in 2025; at the end of 2025, total equity was KRW 24.6 billion, all attributable to shareholders, against total liabilities of KRW 23.7 billion.

On a quarterly basis, the third quarter of 2025 showed revenue of KRW 7.41 billion and an operating loss of KRW 300 million, followed by fourth-quarter revenue of KRW 12.9 billion (up 74.5% from the prior quarter) and operating profit of KRW 927 million.

First-quarter 2026 revenue was KRW 8.6 billion (down 33.5% quarter on quarter) with operating profit of KRW 199 million (down 78.5%). In the second quarter of 2026 revenue fell to KRW 4.93 billion (down 42.7%), with an operating loss of KRW 1.56 billion and a shareholder net loss of KRW 5.71 billion.

Third-quarter 2026 revenue recovered to KRW 7.14 billion (up 44.9% quarter on quarter, down 3.5% year on year), and operating profit was KRW 488 million. Over the last four quarters, from the fourth quarter of 2025 to the third quarter of 2026, shareholder net losses totaled KRW 4.57 billion.

Because third-quarter 2026 revenue was below the prior-year level while operating profit turned positive, the drivers of the swing are a point to trace in the 2026 annual report's segment and cost discussion.

05

Industry analysis

Revenue from control software for semiconductor equipment follows the equipment investment cycle. In a November 2025 note, a Korean IR research association identified the upturn in that business as the core investment point for Kornic Automation.

The same note said that reduced capital spending at Samsung Electronics and SK hynix limited equipment development and volume projects for three years, and that control-software revenue fell from KRW 9.0 billion in 2021 to the KRW 6–7 billion range in 2023–2024.

The association judged that the memory market entered a gradual recovery from the second half of 2025 and that memory-focused equipment investment is more likely to resume in 2026.

It also suggested that localization of semiconductor equipment in China could sustain replacement demand for control software over the medium term, and the company plans a Shanghai sales base to address that market.

On the automation side, demand for K-LAS depends on the investment flows of secondary-battery material producers. In competitive terms, the company is described as holding a high share of the domestic control-software market, although the share estimates differ by source and their methodology is not uniform.

The supply contracts disclosed in 2026 each equal roughly 10% of 2025 revenue of KRW 30.1 billion, so single contracts are too small to establish that the cycle has turned.

06

Outlook

The confirmed orders are two IT infrastructure supply contracts. The contract with AJ Networks, an IT distribution company, was signed on September 11, 2026, is worth KRW 3.15 billion, and runs through December 30, 2026.

The network security equipment supply contract disclosed on October 20, 2026 has a confirmed value of about KRW 2.84 billion excluding VAT, runs through December 31, 2026, and pays 70% as an interim payment after equipment arrives and the remaining 30% after inspection.

The national R&D project runs for 36 months from April 1, 2026 to March 31, 2029, with a stated goal of a perovskite/crystalline silicon tandem module of at least 1.7 square meters and 28% efficiency.

Based on the project, the company plans to widen its business to sales of integrated research automation equipment, licenses for integrated control software, and maintenance and equipment upgrade services.

On the product side, at SEMICON Korea 2026 the company unveiled Easy Platform, an integrated control platform for next-generation semiconductor and display manufacturing equipment, and EasyCluster 4.0.

It has also signed an MOU with iGen Science for commercializing an AI-based autonomous laboratory automation platform, combining physical AI with generative AI. On the organizational side, in July 2026 the company said it would run the platform and IT infrastructure arm and the automation arm as separate businesses.

The next periodic report is the 2026 annual report, which will show the quarterly trajectory and how much of the disclosed orders reached revenue.

Bull factors

Memory capex recovery and control-software demand

A Korean IR research association's November 2025 note judged that the memory market entered a recovery from the second half of 2025, with memory-focused equipment investment more likely to resume in 2026.

The note described control software as co-developed with equipment makers from the early design stage, which could link equipment investment to revenue. Third-quarter 2026 revenue was still 3.5% lower year on year, so the recovery has not yet appeared as top-line growth.

2026 orders and a national R&D project

Two supply contracts were disclosed, worth KRW 3.15 billion in September 2026 and about KRW 2.84 billion in October 2026. The national R&D project runs for 36 months from April 1, 2026 to March 31, 2029, with about KRW 5.2 billion of project funding allocated to the company. Third-quarter 2026 operating profit was KRW 488 million, a return to profit.

Expansion into automation and laboratory automation

The company plans to extend K-LAS into pharmaceuticals and biotech, non-ferrous metals, chemicals and food. It unveiled Easy Platform and EasyCluster 4.0 at SEMICON Korea 2026 and signed an MOU with iGen Science on commercializing an AI-based autonomous laboratory platform. In July 2026 it reorganized so that the platform and automation businesses are commercialized separately.

Risk factors

Industry cycle and customer concentration

Control-software revenue is tied to the equipment investment cycle and fell from the KRW 9.0 billion level in 2021 to the KRW 6–7 billion range in 2023–2024. If memory equipment investment resumes later than expected, the revenue recovery could also be delayed. K-LAS demand depends on investment by cathode-material producers.

Financials and cash flow

The company recorded an operating loss of KRW 2.74 billion and a net loss of KRW 2.9 billion in 2025, with operating cash flow of -KRW 4.94 billion. The debt ratio of 96.4% is well above the 40.4% of 2022.

The supply contracts carry interim and final payment conditions tied to delivery and inspection, so cash receipt can lag revenue recognition.

Governance and ownership concentration

APS and its related parties held 67.79% as of a March 2026 disclosure. In July 2026 the company moved to a co-CEO structure. In January 2026 an executive sold 43,000 shares on the open market, and in November 2026 another executive bought 2,560 shares.

Overall view

Kornic Automation holds a localized core franchise in control software for semiconductor and display equipment. In the third quarter of 2026, operating profit and shareholder net profit both turned positive, reversing the direction of quarterly earnings.

However, the second quarter of 2026 recorded an operating loss of KRW 1.56 billion and a shareholder net loss of KRW 5.71 billion, and shareholder net losses over the last four quarters total KRW 4.57 billion.

On an annual basis, 2025 brought an operating loss of KRW 2.74 billion and a shareholder net loss of KRW 2.9 billion, with operating cash flow of -KRW 4.94 billion.

Strengths include the possibility of memory capex resuming, the flow of 2026 supply contracts and a national R&D project, and expansion into automation and laboratory automation.

Weaknesses include revenue volatility, from KRW 55.5 billion in 2024 to KRW 30.1 billion in 2025, a debt ratio that rose to 96.4%, and an ownership structure in which APS and related parties hold 67.79%. Both sets of factors deserve equal weight, and each disclosed supply contract equals roughly 10% of 2025 revenue.

The next checkpoints are the expiry of the two supply contracts at the end of December 2026, fourth-quarter 2026 results, and the operating margin and debt ratio in the 2026 annual report.
