Report overview

After the Brokerage Boom: Capital Build-Up and Payout Under Test

A surge in brokerage and trading income lifted first-half 2026 earnings sharply, but a third-quarter drop in market turnover and lingering property exposure make the durability of those profits the key variable.

  1. 1

    Second-quarter 2026 consolidated operating revenue was KRW 2.5504 trillion, operating profit KRW 359.9 billion and net profit attributable to owners KRW 258.6 billion, a sharp step-up in quarterly earnings scale.

  2. 2

    The company posted a KRW 6.1 billion owners' net loss in the fourth quarter of 2025 before profits expanded again in the first two quarters of 2026, underlining high quarterly volatility.

  3. 3

  4. 4

  5. 5

02

Business structure

Founded in 1962, Daishin Securities operates across four pillars: retail brokerage, wealth management, trading, and investment banking and project finance.

Its consolidated group includes distressed-debt investor Daishin F&I, Daishin Savings Bank and Daishin Asset Management, giving it a wider revenue base than a pure brokerage.

Based on its second-quarter 2026 management results, separate-basis net operating revenue of KRW 452.5 billion included KRW 178.8 billion from trading, KRW 187.8 billion in net brokerage commissions and KRW 80.8 billion from investment banking and project finance (company disclosure and press reports, August 2026).

Within brokerage, domestic equity commissions were KRW 158.4 billion versus KRW 12.2 billion from overseas equities, showing an overwhelming domestic retail tilt (same source). Credit-loan interest income of KRW 18.3 billion is the interest-type revenue that rises alongside bullish market activity.

The company said combined wholesale and retail assets reached KRW 127 trillion as of the second quarter of 2026, up 33% year on year (company second-quarter 2026 materials).

Its December 2024 designation by the Financial Services Commission as a comprehensive financial investment business operator with over KRW 3 trillion in equity enabled corporate credit extension and lifted its credit-provision ceiling from 100% to 200% of equity.

Competitively it sits in the trailing group among the ten such operators by capital, facing top retail players on market share while competing with larger houses in corporate finance.

In October 2026 affiliate Daishin Asset Management launched a public shareholder-return equity fund, broadening the group's wealth management lineup (reported October 6, 2026).

04

Earnings analysis

Annually, consolidated operating revenue fell from KRW 4.2313 trillion in 2022 to KRW 3.8546 trillion in 2023, then recovered to KRW 4.0939 trillion in 2024 and KRW 5.0639 trillion in 2025.

Operating profit slid from KRW 253.5 billion in 2022 to KRW 161.3 billion in 2023 and KRW 83.6 billion in 2024 before rebounding to KRW 301.4 billion in 2025, with the operating margin rising from 2.0% in 2024 to 6.0% in 2025.

Because securities-industry operating revenue is reported gross, including valuation and disposal gains on securities, the direction and mix matter more than the absolute margin level.

Net profit attributable to owners rose steadily: KRW 115.9 billion in 2022, KRW 133.6 billion in 2023, KRW 144.8 billion in 2024 and KRW 186.6 billion in 2025. The quarterly path is far choppier.

From KRW 825.8 billion of revenue and KRW 73.6 billion of operating profit in the third quarter of 2025, revenue rose to KRW 1.7912 trillion in the fourth quarter yet operating profit fell to KRW 53.2 billion and owners' net result swung to a KRW 6.1 billion loss.

First-quarter 2026 then delivered revenue of KRW 2.4014 trillion, operating profit of KRW 102.5 billion and owners' net profit of KRW 149.8 billion, followed by revenue of KRW 2.5504 trillion, operating profit of KRW 359.9 billion and owners' net profit of KRW 258.6 billion in the second quarter.

Summed over the latest four quarters (third quarter 2025 to second quarter 2026), owners' net profit of KRW 443.9 billion far exceeds the full-year 2025 figure.

Management attributed the second-quarter swing to higher brokerage commissions and trading gains amid an active equity market, while also recognizing KRW 42.7 billion of loan-loss provisions on domestic and overseas real estate in the same quarter (company disclosure and press reports, August 2026).

On the balance sheet, end-2025 consolidated equity stood at KRW 4.0572 trillion against liabilities of KRW 35.0716 trillion for a debt-to-equity ratio of 864.4%, while operating cash flow was negative KRW 3.2239 trillion in 2025 and has been negative for several years, a pattern that must be read alongside the industry convention of classifying financial-asset purchases as operating activity.

05

Industry analysis

In the first half of 2026 Korean brokers posted record profits as index gains coincided with a surge in turnover.

Average daily turnover, the primary driver of securities earnings, was estimated at roughly KRW 118 trillion in the second quarter of 2026 and reportedly reached about KRW 137 trillion a day in June (iM Securities analyst, reported July 2026).

Liquidity indicators then reversed quickly: third-quarter average daily turnover was tallied at KRW 77 trillion, down 34% quarter on quarter, while investor deposits fell 23% from end-June to KRW 93.9258 trillion as of September 21 (MTN, September 23, 2026).

One report also noted that the FnGuide consensus for third-quarter 2026 operating profit of the KOSPI securities sector was KRW 2.1895 trillion, implying a 45.6% drop from KRW 4.0275 trillion in the prior quarter (EBN, September 2026).

In cycle terms the sector sits in a mixed phase: past a turnover peak on the volume side, yet still undergoing structural change such as extended trading hours and a policy push toward productive finance.

Because capital size translates directly into leverage capacity and licensing, industry analysis points to a widening gap between brokers that accumulate capital quickly and those that do not.

Within that structure Daishin is a later entrant by equity among the ten comprehensive operators and carries relatively high sensitivity to turnover given its retail brokerage and trading weightings.

By contrast, Daishin F&I's distressed-debt business benefits from the opposite cycle of expanding bank NPL supply, providing a buffer that moves to a different rhythm from the core brokerage.

06

Outlook

The central axis of the company's confirmed plan is capital expansion.

Daishin has said it designated the period through 2028 as a capital expansion phase to pursue mega-IB status, and in November 2025 issued KRW 335 billion of listed convertible preferred shares, pushing equity above KRW 4 trillion (eKoreaNews, October 2026).

Separate-basis equity stood at KRW 4.5937 trillion at the end of June 2026, meeting the financial threshold for a short-term finance (issued-note) license, though tightened review standards requiring two consecutive year-end periods of compliance mean the firm is expected to apply after a preparation period (same source).

The number of issued-note operators rose to nine after Samsung Securities and Meritz Securities were approved in September 2026, with Daishin cited among the next candidates.

On shareholder returns, the February 2026 disclosure calls for cancelling 15.35 million treasury shares in stages at each quarter-end over six quarters, with the remaining 3 million treasury shares earmarked for employee incentives and the employee stock ownership plan.

The tax-exempt dividend program is slated to run for about four years from 2026 within a KRW 400 billion ceiling.

Among subsidiaries, Daishin F&I had its long-term rating lifted from A to A+ by Korea Investors Service and NICE Investors Service in April 2026 in recognition of its shift toward a distressed-debt portfolio, with Korea Investors Service noting that asset-quality risk eased as high-risk exposures were recovered.

That said, with KRW 42.7 billion of real-estate provisions booked in the second quarter of 2026, whether further provisions follow and how far the third-quarter turnover slowdown feeds into brokerage and trading income remain the swing factors for near-term results.

08

Bull factors

High earnings leverage in rising-turnover phases

Second-quarter 2026 consolidated operating profit of KRW 359.9 billion exceeded the full-year 2025 figure of KRW 301.4 billion in a single quarter, illustrating how profits can expand non-linearly when trading conditions are favorable.

Company materials show net brokerage commissions of KRW 187.8 billion and trading income of KRW 178.8 billion in the same quarter, with both pillars contributing simultaneously. Credit-loan interest income also rose, confirming that a buoyant market feeds through to both fees and interest. A retail-heavy business mix works as a strength in this phase.

Capital build-up and a license expansion path

The December 2024 designation as a comprehensive financial investment business operator enabled corporate credit extension and raised the credit-provision ceiling to 200% of equity.

Equity passed KRW 4 trillion after a KRW 335 billion convertible preferred issue in November 2025, and separate-basis equity of KRW 4.5937 trillion at end-June 2026 reportedly meets the financial threshold for an issued-note license (eKoreaNews, October 2026).

The company has stated it set the period through 2028 as a capital expansion phase to pursue mega-IB status. Expanding balance-sheet businesses creates room to diversify a revenue mix that currently leans on commissions.

Concrete payout policy and improving subsidiary credit

The February 2026 plan to cancel 15.35 million treasury shares covers 83.6% of held treasury stock and will be executed in stages at each quarter-end over six quarters.

Alongside it runs a tax-exempt dividend program of up to KRW 400 billion over roughly four years from 2026, and the company has stressed payout predictability, citing 27 consecutive years of cash dividends and dividend guidelines published since 2020.

Subsidiary Daishin F&I saw its long-term rating raised from A to A+ in April 2026, with Korea Investors Service noting that asset-quality risk eased as high-risk exposures were recovered and the portfolio shifted toward distressed debt. Simultaneous progress on payouts and subsidiary quality forms a core part of the bull case.

Bear factors

Direct hit from slowing turnover

Reports tallied third-quarter average daily turnover at KRW 77 trillion, down 34% quarter on quarter, with investor deposits falling 23% from end-June to KRW 93.9258 trillion as of September 21 (MTN, September 23, 2026).

Another report cited the FnGuide consensus for a 45.6% quarter-on-quarter decline in third-quarter operating profit for the KOSPI securities sector (EBN, September 2026). Since a large share of second-quarter 2026 profit came from brokerage commissions and trading gains, that slowdown is a direct headwind. Industry commentary has noted that brokers more dependent on brokerage and trading face the larger impact.

Real-estate exposure and provisioning burden

The company said it recognized KRW 42.7 billion of loan-loss provisions on domestic and overseas real estate in the second quarter of 2026 (company disclosure and press reports, August 2026).

In its December 2024 report on the comprehensive-operator designation, Korea Investors Service assessed that group-wide domestic and overseas property finance exposure was larger than at peers and that financial volatility could rise if business expansion were not accompanied by adequate risk management.

High-risk exposures have since been recovered at the group level, but additional costs could still be recognized depending on property markets and interest rates. Provisions amplify quarter-to-quarter earnings swings.

High leverage and a record of quarterly losses

At end-2025, consolidated liabilities were KRW 35.0716 trillion against equity of KRW 4.0572 trillion, a debt-to-equity ratio of 864.4%, up steadily from 649.4% in 2023. High leverage is normal in financial services, but rising rates simultaneously increase funding costs and bond valuation pressure.

In the fourth quarter of 2025 the company posted a KRW 6.1 billion owners' net loss even as revenue rose to KRW 1.7912 trillion, showing that top-line scale and bottom-line results can diverge. On a quarterly basis the earnings path is anything but smooth.

10

Risk factors

Market and liquidity risk

With turnover, investor deposits and margin-loan balances as the core revenue drivers, shrinking market liquidity translates directly into lower commission and interest income.

Analysts credited valuation gains on held securities for driving second-quarter 2026 results, which means falling asset prices could produce losses through the same channel. Industry commentary has also flagged that rising bond yields can combine higher funding costs with weaker bond valuation results. The magnitude of quarterly swings is already visible in reported figures.

Credit and real-estate risk

Domestic project finance and overseas commercial property exposures translate into provisions and valuation losses when recovery is delayed. The KRW 42.7 billion provision in the second quarter of 2026 shows this risk is still live.

Daishin F&I's distressed-debt investing is an opportunity, but NICE Investors Service noted that a weaker property market could delay asset recovery or dampen near-term performance, making recovery pace and rates key items to monitor. Group-level asset-quality indicators warrant continuous tracking.

Regulatory and capital risk

New licenses such as issued notes require regulatory approval, and reports indicate that tightened review standards require the financial criteria to be met across two consecutive year-end periods (eKoreaNews, October 2026).

Any delay in approval would also push back the point at which added capital converts into earnings.

If convertible preferred or other capital securities continue to be used during the capital build-up, share-count effects arise, and since the company is simultaneously cancelling treasury shares, the net effect on share count should be checked each quarter.

A rating agency has also assessed that expanded risk-taking as a comprehensive operator increases opportunity and risk at the same time.

12

Overall view

Daishin Securities' recent results capture how much earnings can expand at a retail- and trading-centric broker during a market boom.

Consolidated operating profit rose from KRW 83.6 billion in 2024 to KRW 301.4 billion in 2025, and the KRW 359.9 billion recorded in the second quarter of 2026 alone exceeded the full-year 2025 figure.

The same data also show an uneven path, including a KRW 6.1 billion owners' net loss in the fourth quarter of 2025 and KRW 42.7 billion of real-estate provisions in the second quarter of 2026.

With reports of a rapid third-quarter decline in turnover and investor deposits, the first item to be verified is whether first-half earnings power can be sustained.

Structurally, the 2024 comprehensive-operator designation, the capital expansion plan through 2028 and the push for an issued-note license form the path toward revenue diversification, while the staged cancellation of 15.35 million treasury shares and the tax-exempt dividend program of up to KRW 400 billion over four years are confirmed facts on the payout side.

On the other side sit a debt-to-equity ratio of 864.4% at end-2025, property exposure and dependence on market turnover.

What matters for assessment is checking profit composition and provisioning trends in the next quarterly report; this document is for information purposes and contains no buy or sell opinion and no target price.
