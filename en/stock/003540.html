02

Business structure

Daishin Securities runs brokerage, trading (management of held securities), investment banking and project finance (IB and PF), and asset management, with an F&I subsidiary and a savings bank subsidiary.

Separate-basis net operating revenue in the second quarter of 2026 was KRW 452.5 billion, up 39.7% from the prior quarter, made up of brokerage revenue of KRW 187.8 billion, trading income of KRW 176.6 billion and IB income of KRW 80.8 billion.

Brokerage expanded as domestic equity trading volume rose 23.2% from the prior quarter to KRW 187.9 trillion, lifting domestic commission income to KRW 158.4 billion and overseas commission income to KRW 12.2 billion. Trading income improved sharply as valuation gains on held securities grew.

In IB, financial advisory fees rose from KRW 4.3 billion in the first quarter to KRW 29.4 billion in the second quarter on lead roles in high-quality PF deals, and corporate credit balances have kept rising since the company received its comprehensive investment-business designation in 2025.

Consolidated total assets at the end of June 2026 were KRW 42.59 trillion, about KRW 4 trillion above the end of March.

Reported PF exposure was about KRW 2.2 trillion domestically and KRW 0.9 trillion overseas in an August 2026 report, while NH Investment & Securities put overseas exposure at about KRW 1.4 trillion in September 2026.

On the retail side, wholesale and retail total assets were KRW 127 trillion at the end of June 2026, up 33% year on year, and high-net-worth clients with assets above KRW 100 million rose 53.5% to 98,406.

No verified figures for segment revenue shares were found, so the points above describe direction rather than mix: trading and brokerage accounted for most of the second-quarter increase.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual revenue went from KRW 4.23 trillion in 2022 to KRW 3.85 trillion in 2023, down 8.9% year on year, then rose to KRW 4.09 trillion in 2024 and KRW 5.06 trillion in 2025, up 6.2% and 23.7%. Two consecutive years of growth, 2024 and 2025, followed the 2023 low.

Operating profit fell from KRW 253.5 billion in 2022 to KRW 161.3 billion in 2023, down 36.4%, and to KRW 83.6 billion in 2024, down 48.2%, before recovering to KRW 301.4 billion in 2025, up 260.6%. The operating margin was 6.0% in 2022, 4.2% in 2023, 2.0% in 2024 and 6.0% in 2025, so 2025 returned to the 2022 level.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders rose from KRW 115.9 billion in 2022 to KRW 133.6 billion in 2023, KRW 144.8 billion in 2024 and KRW 186.6 billion in 2025, which is three consecutive years of growth starting in 2023.

Net income including non-controlling interests was KRW 186.7 billion in 2025, nearly the same as the controlling-shareholder figure. In 2024 operating profit fell while controlling-shareholder profit rose, so the two measures did not move together, and non-operating items need to be read alongside operating profit.

Quarterly revenue bottomed at KRW 825.8 billion in the third quarter of 2025 and then grew for three consecutive quarters to KRW 2.55 trillion in the second quarter of 2026.

Operating profit bottomed at KRW 53.2 billion in the fourth quarter of 2025 and then grew for two consecutive quarters, to KRW 102.5 billion in the first quarter of 2026 and KRW 359.9 billion in the second.

The fourth quarter of 2025 showed a net loss attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 6.07 billion, a swing into deficit, and the first quarter of 2026 returned to profit at KRW 149.8 billion. The second-quarter 2026 figure of KRW 258.6 billion rose 249.7% year on year.

That quarter's results reflected higher trading volumes together with valuation gains on held securities, and brokers have argued that much of the gain is one-off and that the second half could see a smaller profit base.

Operating cash flow was negative in each year from 2023 through 2025, reaching KRW -3.22 trillion in 2025, a pattern that should be read alongside the size of financial-asset holdings, which move with trading activity.

05

Industry analysis

The brokerage industry is in a phase where earnings are improving alongside capital flowing into the Korean equity market.

Average daily trading value rose from KRW 84 trillion in the first quarter of 2026 to KRW 118 trillion in the second, and early 2026 saw reports that the KOSPI kept setting record highs and lifted expectations for brokerage stocks.

Trading value swings sharply from quarter to quarter, so the position in the cycle depends on whether these volume levels hold. In an August 2026 report, SK Securities said second-half trading value could fall and valuation-loss pressure on securities holdings could rise.

On competitive position, NH Investment & Securities said in a July 2026 report that Daishin's profit scale is not large relative to the big brokers, although it expected growth in retail income to show results.

Compared with large peers such as Mirae Asset Securities, Samsung Securities and KIWOOM Securities, the gap in IB scale and capital base remains the common view among brokers.

Daishin targets a 2028 approval for issuing short-term bills and a super-large IB license, so approval timing is the point where that gap could narrow. Its position is therefore close to large peers in fee-based businesses, while the capital-based IB and bill-issuance business is still a work in progress.

06

Outlook

Instead of a quantitative company target, three directions are visible: shareholder returns, capital-based IB expansion and real-estate exposure management.

On shareholder returns, the February 2026 disclosure calls for cancelling about 9.32 million common treasury shares in six tranches starting when the amended Commercial Act is announced, with about 1.5 million shares set aside for employee performance pay and an employee stock ownership plan.

All 6.03 million preferred shares are to be cancelled. Brokers said the four-year sales ban on these shares limits overhang concerns, and the company also has a minimum per-share dividend policy and a tax-exempt dividend plan.

On capital, the company targets approval to issue short-term bills in 2028, and SK Securities said in a May 2026 report that a super-large IB approval could come in 2028.

On real estate, NH Investment & Securities said in a September 2026 report that a provision of about KRW 80 billion tied to European commercial property was expected in the second half, with timing not yet set.

For the third quarter, NH expected controlling-shareholder net income of about KRW 26.4 billion, which assumes valuation losses and provisions, so the third-quarter report will show how far actual results diverge from that estimate. Fourth-quarter and full-year results will be fixed in the annual report due by the end of March 2027.

08

Bull factors

Trading Recovery and Brokerage Growth

Domestic equity brokerage volume reached KRW 187.9 trillion in the second quarter of 2026, up 23.2% from the prior quarter, and domestic commission income rose 212.7% year on year to KRW 158.4 billion.

Average daily trading value climbed from KRW 84 trillion to KRW 118 trillion over the first half, widening the base for fee income. Credit-related income also grew, which broadens the retail-driven revenue mix.

Concrete Shareholder-Return Plan

Under the February 2026 disclosure, about 9.32 million common treasury shares will be cancelled over six quarters, and all 6.03 million preferred shares will be cancelled. A minimum per-share dividend policy and a tax-exempt dividend plan were also presented.

NH Investment & Securities described the company as an active shareholder-return broker. Brokers also noted a four-year sales ban on the shares, which limits overhang pressure.

Path to Larger IB and Capital Base

Corporate credit balances and related income have grown since the company received its comprehensive investment-business designation in 2025. IB income reached KRW 80.8 billion in the second quarter of 2026, up 96.7% year on year.

The company targets approval to issue short-term bills in 2028, which would widen its capital-based business if granted.

Bear factors

Reliance on Valuation Gains

Much of the second-quarter operating profit came from valuation gains on held securities. SK Securities regards those gains as largely one-off and pointed out that the stock behind them fell 26.5% after the end of June.

NH Investment & Securities expected about KRW 160 billion of valuation losses in the third quarter in its September 2026 report.

Real-Estate Exposure and Provisions

KRW 42.7 billion of domestic and overseas real-estate provisions were booked in the second quarter of 2026. The debt-to-equity ratio at the end of 2025 was 864.4%, the highest of the 2022 to 2025 years.

Total liabilities of KRW 35.1 trillion sit against total equity of KRW 4.06 trillion, so small changes in asset quality weigh heavily on capital. NH Investment & Securities said a provision tied to European commercial property was planned for the second half, with no timing fixed.

Earnings Volatility and Negative Cash Flow

Operating profit fell to KRW 161.3 billion in 2023, down 36.4%, and to KRW 83.6 billion in 2024, down 48.2%, before recovering to KRW 301.4 billion in 2025. Operating cash flow was negative for three consecutive years from 2023, reaching KRW -3.22 trillion in 2025. NH Investment & Securities said the company's profit scale is not large next to bigger brokers.

10

Risk factors

Market Trading Volume

Trading value moves sharply from quarter to quarter. SK Securities said in an August 2026 report that trading value could decline in the second half.

If volumes fall from the KRW 118 trillion daily level, brokerage and credit income could fall together, so how far earnings depend on trading activity is the point to check.

Real-Estate and PF Credit Risk

Domestic PF exposure has been reported at about KRW 2 trillion, while overseas exposure was reported between KRW 0.9 trillion and KRW 1.4 trillion. Brokers gave different overseas figures in the same period, so the exact amount needs to be confirmed from filings. Provisions can swing quarterly profit, making the path of real-estate costs a key risk.

Licensing and Regulatory Timing

Approval for issuing short-term bills and a super-large IB license are both targeted for 2028. If approvals slip, plans to grow the capital base and IB business would be delayed.

Treasury share cancellation also depends on the timing of the amended Commercial Act, so any change in that schedule would shift the return timeline.

12

Overall view

Daishin Securities' second-quarter 2026 results were lifted by rising trading volumes, growth in brokerage and IB income, and valuation gains on held securities, which together pushed operating profit to KRW 359.9 billion.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders reached KRW 186.6 billion in 2025, a third straight year of growth from 2023, and operating profit recovered from its 2024 low to KRW 301.4 billion.

Yet much of the second-quarter profit came from valuation gains, so second-half valuation losses and lower trading volumes could widen earnings swings. Real-estate and PF provisions were still booked in the second quarter of 2026, and the timing of a European commercial property provision remains open.

On shareholder returns, treasury share cancellation, full cancellation of preferred shares and a minimum dividend policy have been set out, while the 2028 target for short-term bill and super-large IB approvals is the main capital milestone.

SK Securities and NH Investment & Securities lowered their target prices to KRW 40,000 and KRW 35,000 in August and September 2026, respectively. The next checkpoint is the third-quarter 2026 report, where valuation gains and provisions will show how much of the profit is recurring.
