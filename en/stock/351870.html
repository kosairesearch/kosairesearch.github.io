Report overview

Operating Profit Recovers, Net Losses Persist

Chai Communication swung back to an operating profit in the second quarter of 2026, but its net loss attributable to controlling shareholders persisted for a second straight quarter, showing that top-line growth and bottom-line recovery have not yet moved in the same direction.

02

Business structure

Chai Communication is an independent digital advertising agency founded in 2004 that listed on KOSDAQ in September 2024 through a SPAC merger.

Its business spans ad production, media agency services, medical institution marketing, and merchandise sales; in the first half of 2026 consolidated revenue of KRW 45.62 billion was split with media agency services at KRW 22.11 billion (48.5%), ad production at KRW 18.02 billion (39.5%), and merchandise sales expanding to KRW 5.01 billion (11.0%).

Gross handling volume in the same period totaled KRW 104.3 billion, comprising KRW 78.8 billion in brand handling and KRW 11.7 billion in performance handling.

In June 2025 the company acquired a 42.55% stake in The SC, a K-beauty and medical-institution-focused marketing firm, for KRW 5.0 billion, bringing it in as a subsidiary; The SC runs K-beauty content advertising and medical marketing across Japan, China, Thailand, and Indonesia.

Key clients cited include Gmarket, KB Insurance, CJ CheilJedang, and the Korea Tourism Organization, and the company applies a proprietary generative AI platform and automated ad-production tools across campaign planning and production.

Competitively, it positions itself as an independent agency distinct from conglomerate-affiliated agencies such as Cheil Worldwide and Innocean Worldwide.

In October 2026 it consolidated previously scattered offices in Cheongdam-dong, Seoul into a single integrated headquarters called 'HAUS701,' which also serves as a new base for space marketing and wellness business.

The largest shareholder is CEO Choi Young-sup and related parties, holding 58.05% as of March 30, 2026, while Soosung Asset Management appears as a notable shareholder with a 12.74% stake as of an April 30, 2026 disclosure.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual revenue rose from KRW 56.4 billion in 2023 to KRW 58.8 billion in 2024 (up 4.2% year over year) and KRW 66.5 billion in 2025 (up 13.1%), marking two consecutive years of growth in 2024 and 2025, with 2025 the largest year and 2023 the smallest in the three-year span.

Operating profit, however, fell from KRW 8.6 billion in 2023 to KRW 3.55 billion in 2024 (down 58.8%) before partially recovering to KRW 4.44 billion in 2025 (up 25.0%), making 2023 the largest year and 2024 the smallest for operating profit over the period.

The operating margin slid from 15.3% in 2023 to 6.0% in 2024 and edged up only slightly to 6.7% in 2025.

Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders swung from KRW 7.29 billion in 2023 to a net loss of KRW 315 million in 2024, then back to a profit of KRW 1.2 billion in 2025, while total net income including non-controlling interests rose more sharply to KRW 2.52 billion in 2025, indicating that the profit-allocation structure involving subsidiaries such as The SC widened the gap between the two metrics.

On a quarterly basis, revenue climbed from KRW 11.5 billion in the second quarter of 2025 to KRW 20.4 billion in the third quarter (up 78.0%) and KRW 23.4 billion in the fourth quarter (up 14.7%), then fell to KRW 19.2 billion in the first quarter of 2026 (down 17.9%) before rebounding to KRW 26.4 billion in the second quarter of 2026 (up 37.6% quarter over quarter and 130.6% year over year).

Operating profit rose from KRW 323 million in the second quarter of 2025 to KRW 1.15 billion in the third quarter and KRW 2.49 billion in the fourth quarter, then swung to an operating loss of KRW 896 million in the first quarter of 2026 before returning to a profit of KRW 2.78 billion in the second quarter, up 759.7% year over year.

Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders, however, posted gains of KRW 454 million in the third quarter of 2025 and KRW 626 million in the fourth quarter before swinging to a net loss of KRW 955 million in the first quarter of 2026 and widening to a net loss of KRW 1.14 billion in the second quarter, a continued and deepening loss both quarter over quarter and year over year, moving in the opposite direction from the operating-profit recovery.

As a result, the cumulative net loss attributable to controlling shareholders over the last four quarters (third quarter 2025 through second quarter 2026) reached KRW 1.01 billion, and operating cash flow dropped sharply from KRW 9.68 billion in 2023 and KRW 12.8 billion in 2024 to KRW 1.18 billion in 2025, revealing a gap between the profit recovery and cash generation.

05

Industry analysis

Korea's digital advertising market has grown on the back of rising internet usage and changing online consumption patterns, but in periods of economic slowdown it also faces pressure from advertisers cutting budgets and falling search-keyword bid prices.

Unlike conglomerate-affiliated agencies such as Cheil Worldwide and Innocean Worldwide, Chai Communication positions itself as an independent agency offering integrated brand-campaign and performance-marketing services.

In a third-quarter 2026 evaluation, the company ranked third by advertising growth rate among ten agencies selected by Naver as top partners, a reference point reflecting actual media-execution performance.

Generative-AI-driven automation of ad production is emerging as a variable reshaping cost structures across the industry, and the company continues to use its own AI tools in an effort to reduce production time and outsourcing costs.

At the same time, expansion into areas adjacent to core advertising, such as K-beauty and medical-institution marketing and wellness and content commerce, is a strategy shared by several small and mid-sized marketing firms in the sector.

However, these new ventures carry inventory, distribution, and regulatory risks distinct from the advertising agency business, so their impact on consolidated results warrants quarter-by-quarter monitoring.

06

Outlook

In July 2026 the company disclosed its 'Corporate Value-Up Plan 2026,' built on three pillars: strengthening its core business, expanding four new businesses (content commerce, global data, inner beauty, and beauty), and improving its financial structure and governance.

On the core business, it stated a goal of raising the retention rate of top-tier advertisers to 90% and commercializing its proprietary integrated AI marketing platform 'CH.AI Agent,' with a SaaS business model targeted from 2027 onward.

On finance and governance, it outlined plans to phase in share buybacks and dividend policy based on a profit turnaround, alongside a goal of raising its core corporate-governance compliance rate from 53.8% in 2025 to 76.9% by 2028.

Korea Investment & Securities assessed in a September 2026 report that improving profitability in the core digital advertising business and the consolidation of subsidiary The SC's results would contribute to medium-term growth, noting that The SC posted first-half 2026 revenue of KRW 14.9 billion and total comprehensive income of KRW 2.0 billion.

The same report cited the advertising peak season effect and continued cost discipline as grounds for expected profitability improvement in the second half.

In October 2026 the company also unveiled plans to consolidate its scattered Cheongdam-dong offices into the integrated 'HAUS701' headquarters, with the 16th and 17th floors dedicated to wellness business space to advance its space-marketing and K-wellness platform initiatives.

Most of these plans, however, are scheduled for the second half of 2026 or later, so the actual timing and scale of their revenue and profit contributions will need to be confirmed through future quarterly disclosures.

08

Bull factors

Second-Quarter Operating Turnaround

Second-quarter 2026 operating profit reached KRW 2.78 billion, swinging back to profit from the prior quarter and rising 759.7% year over year.

Independent research firm CMIR found that while standalone revenue grew 43% in the quarter, salary expense fell 9.8% from KRW 3.96 billion to KRW 3.58 billion, and that keeping headcount flat while growing revenue lifted the standalone operating margin from 2.8% to 8.8%.

The shift from a first-quarter operating loss to profit within a single quarter suggests the cost-discipline stance is translating into actual profitability gains.

Business Diversification and New Ventures

The SC, consolidated in June 2025, posted first-half 2026 revenue of KRW 14.9 billion and total comprehensive income of KRW 2.0 billion, expanding in K-beauty and medical marketing across Japan, China, Thailand, and Indonesia.

In July 2026 the company formalized four new business lines, content commerce, global data, inner beauty, and beauty, and in October it set up dedicated wellness space within its new integrated headquarters, 'HAUS701.' Growing revenue sources outside core advertising could reduce dependence on the volatility of any single business segment.

Rising Industry Recognition

In October 2026, Chai Communication ranked third by advertising growth rate among ten top partners in Naver's third-quarter 2026 Partner Boost Program, and in August of the same year it was named 'Agency of the Year' at the 2026 Effie Awards Korea, winning gold in two categories for its Gmarket campaign.

These awards and selections serve as external reputation indicators that can support client retention and new campaign wins.

09

Bear factors

Persistent Bottom-Line Losses

Both the first and second quarters of 2026 posted net losses attributable to controlling shareholders, a two-quarter losing streak, with the second-quarter loss of KRW 1.14 billion wider than the first quarter's KRW 955 million.

Because this moved in the opposite direction from the operating-profit recovery, below-the-line factors such as non-operating expenses or the allocation of profit to non-controlling interests warrant confirmation in upcoming disclosures.

The cumulative net loss attributable to controlling shareholders over the last four quarters reached KRW 1.01 billion, a non-trivial accumulated burden.

Weaker Cash Generation

Operating cash flow fell sharply from KRW 9.68 billion in 2023 and KRW 12.8 billion in 2024 to KRW 1.18 billion in 2025. Even though operating profit rose 25.0% year over year in the same period, cash flow dropped sharply instead, revealing a gap between the book-level profit recovery and actual cash generation.

The cause of this gap, whether from receivables and inventory build-up or working-capital changes tied to The SC acquisition, needs confirmation through further disclosures.

Elevated Debt-to-Equity Ratio

The debt-to-equity ratio jumped from 132.9% in 2023 to 208.1% in 2024 before easing slightly to 191.3% in 2025, still well above the 2023 level. Year-end 2025 total liabilities of KRW 111.9 billion were nearly double the KRW 58.5 billion in total equity.

With net losses attributable to controlling shareholders continuing, the persistence of this leverage level is a financial-structure factor worth watching alongside earnings.

10

Risk factors

Sensitivity to Advertising Cycle

Advertising budgets are sensitive to economic conditions, and in a downturn advertisers may cut spending while search-keyword bid prices fall at the same time.

With media agency services accounting for nearly half of total revenue, changes in media fee rates or budget adjustments by individual advertisers can feed directly into quarterly results. The 17.9% quarter-over-quarter revenue decline in the first quarter of 2026 illustrates this quarterly volatility.

Execution Risk from Diversification

Following the consolidation of The SC, the company is simultaneously pursuing four new business lines, content commerce, global data, inner beauty, and beauty, along with a wellness-space business.

Running multiple new ventures in parallel can spread resource allocation and monetization timelines, potentially front-loading cost burdens in the near term.

Medical institution marketing and cosmetics wholesale/retail carry different inventory, regulatory, and distribution characteristics than the advertising agency business, adding a distinct source of variability to consolidated results.

Shareholding Structure Volatility

The combined stake of CEO Choi Young-sup and related parties declined from 65.72% in September 2024 to 58.05% in March 2026, a decrease that included a gift of 7.6% of shares to employees in February 2026.

At the same time, institutional investor Soosung Asset Management expanded its stake to 12.74% as of an April 2026 disclosure.

With both a change in the controlling shareholder's stake and an increase in institutional ownership occurring together, continued monitoring of governance and decision-making stability is warranted.

12

Overall view

Chai Communication has shown signs of recovery in revenue scale and operating profitability, with revenue rising for two consecutive years through 2025 and operating profit improving sharply in the second quarter of 2026.

However, in that same quarter the net loss attributable to controlling shareholders widened to KRW 1.14 billion, extending a losing streak to two consecutive quarters following the first quarter, meaning the operating-profit turnaround has not yet flowed through to the bottom line.

The sharp year-over-year decline in 2025 operating cash flow and a debt-to-equity ratio that remains well above its 2023 level are also financial-structure points worth watching.

Diversification efforts, including the consolidation of The SC, four new business lines, and a wellness business built around the new integrated headquarters, broaden the medium-term growth narrative, but running multiple new ventures in parallel also raises execution risk.

External reputation indicators such as the Naver top-partner designation and the Effie Awards Korea win have improved, though it remains unconfirmed whether these translate into reduced quarterly net-income volatility.

Whether the operating-profit trend holds and whether the net loss attributable to controlling shareholders narrows or is resolved in the next quarterly filing will likely be the key indicators for gauging the earnings trajectory ahead.
