02

Business structure

Chai Communication, founded in 2004, is a digital full-service advertising agency whose core areas are digital brand campaigns, performance marketing and digital content production.

The company said in an August 18, 2026 release that its revenue mix is broadening from ad production toward media and marketing agency work and product sales.

On a standalone basis, the agency segment booked cumulative January–September 2026 revenue of KRW 41.9 billion and operating profit of KRW 990 million, up 27.2% and 27.3% year on year, according to the company's November 16, 2026 announcement.

The subsidiary acquired in June 2025 runs medical-institution marketing and sells medical devices and cosmetics, and has been consolidated from the second half of 2025. Because this subsidiary now carries a larger share of consolidated sales, the advertising core and the subsidiary should be read separately.

On technology, the company runs its own AI agent 'CH.AI Agent', the ad performance platform 'AIO for Ads' and the concept agent 'Ai-CEPT', and said proposal drafting time fell 60–80% in internal tests. It was named a 2026 Naver Premier Partner for a second consecutive year in March 2026.

Its Gmarket campaign won two Gold awards in the Retail and Digital Commerce categories at Effie Awards Korea in August 2026. Non-core rental operations are being partly scaled back as it moves into its consolidated headquarters 'HAUS701' in Cheongdam-dong.

Among competitors, large agencies such as Cheil Worldwide and Innocean Worldwide lead on scale, while Chai differentiates on digital performance marketing and AI-driven automation.

04

Earnings analysis

On an annual basis, revenue rose from KRW 56.4 billion in 2023 to KRW 58.8 billion in 2024 (up 4.2% year on year) and KRW 66.5 billion in 2025 (up 13.1%), growing for two consecutive years from 2024 to 2025; 2025 was the largest year of 2023–2025.

Operating profit fell from KRW 8.6 billion in 2023 to KRW 3.55 billion in 2024 (down 58.8%) and recovered to KRW 4.44 billion in 2025 (up 25.0%). 2023 was the largest operating profit year in the period and 2024 the smallest.

The operating margin was 15.3% in 2023, 6.0% in 2024 and 6.7% in 2025, so margins have not returned to the 2023 level even as sales grew. Shareholder net income went from KRW 7.29 billion in 2023 to a net loss of KRW 315 million in 2024, a swing into loss, and then to KRW 1.2 billion in 2025, a return to profit.

Net income including non-controlling interests was KRW 2.52 billion in 2025, above the shareholder figure, and the gap reflects the non-controlling share.

The company attributed the 2025 improvement to consolidating the subsidiary it bought in June and to the removal of one-off costs from the prior year's listing preparation. Cash flow moved differently: operating cash flow fell from KRW 9.68 billion in 2023 and KRW 12.8 billion in 2024 to KRW 1.18 billion in 2025.

The debt-to-equity ratio was 132.9% in 2023, 208.1% in 2024 and 191.3% in 2025, when total liabilities were KRW 111.9 billion against total equity of KRW 58.5 billion, of which KRW 53.7 billion belonged to shareholders of the parent.

Quarterly, Q4 2025 revenue of KRW 23.4 billion (up 14.7% from the prior quarter) and operating profit of KRW 2.49 billion reflected the year's peak advertising season. Q1 2026 revenue was KRW 19.2 billion (down 17.9% from the prior quarter) with an operating loss of KRW 896 million, a swing from profit.

Q2 2026 revenue rose to KRW 26.4 billion with operating profit of KRW 2.78 billion, returning to profit. Q3 2026 revenue was KRW 21.5 billion (up 5.3% year on year), but an operating loss of KRW 276 million turned the year-ago profit into a loss.

Shareholder net losses were KRW 955 million in Q1 2026, KRW 1.14 billion in Q2 and KRW 721 million in Q3, so all three quarters were in the red. Even with operating profit in Q2, the shareholder loss widened to KRW 1.14 billion, so items below operating profit need to be weighed alongside the operating line.

05

Industry analysis

Results at advertising agencies depend heavily on when clients release their budgets. The company says client spending concentrates in the second half, and orders secured in the first half are recognized as second-half revenue.

In a June 2025 report, Korea Investment & Securities described the broader ad industry as weak on macro uncertainty and growth concerns, while crediting Chai with continued revenue growth.

The same report said margins had stayed in the mid-single-digit range and that labor-cost control and AI-driven task automation were supporting profitability. The rising share of digital in total marketing spending favors digital-focused agencies.

Compared with large agencies, Chai is smaller, but it positions itself as a digital specialist built on performance marketing and data-driven operations. Its Naver partner status widens its access to platform-based advertisers.

Content commerce and beauty commerce are product-sales businesses, but their profit contribution is not yet visible in reported results.

Bullish factors are the rising digital share of ad spending and AI-driven cost improvement; bearish factors are the cyclical sensitivity of ad budgets and margin pressure across the sector.

06

Outlook

In its August 18, 2026 release, the company said it expects results to continue on second-half advertising seasonality and orders already secured.

In its November 16, 2026 release, it said Q4, the year's largest advertising season, would concentrate campaign execution and widen the annual profit improvement; this is the company's own outlook. Q4 2026 figures will appear in the 2026 annual report.

For new businesses, the company signed a strategic MOU in July 2026 with two content companies to form a joint-venture for content commerce that combines content production, artist IP and AI-driven marketing. It aims to extend one content IP across broadcast, digital content, brand collaborations and commerce.

In beauty, it expects commerce sales growth starting with a global beauty brand and plans a K-beauty platform to support overseas expansion of domestic brands. It also plans to commercialize its in-house AI agent solutions as external SaaS products, as announced on November 16, 2026.

At the consolidated headquarters 'HAUS701' in Cheongdam-dong it plans a wellness brand launch and pop-up events, and it expects lower rent burden on existing office space.

The company cited lower rental income from vacancies during the remodeling as a factor in the cumulative operating profit decline through Q3 2026, so how quickly the building generates income after consolidation is a key checkpoint.

Revenue scale and profitability of the new businesses have not yet appeared in reported results and will be checked in the next periodic report.

Bull factors

Top-line growth in the advertising core

The standalone agency segment grew revenue and operating profit by 27.2% and 27.3% year on year. The company expects year-end campaign concentration to widen the annual profit improvement.

Platform partner status and award record

It was named a Naver Premier Partner for a second straight year in March 2026 and ranked third by growth among ten Outstanding Premier Partners in October 2026. At Effie Awards Korea in August 2026 its Gmarket campaign won two Gold awards and the agency was named Most Effective Agency. These external recognitions support its advertiser relationships.

AI efficiency and new business moves

The company says its in-house AI agent improved per-person work efficiency by 38%. It plans a content-commerce joint venture and commercial SaaS sales of its AI tools. Whether the cost changes show up in advertising margins is the key test.

Bear factors

Persistent loss quarters

Shareholder net losses have run for three consecutive quarters from Q1 2026, and the four quarters from Q4 2025 through Q3 2026 total KRW 2.19 billion. Even with Q2 operating profit, the shareholder loss widened to KRW 1.14 billion. Q3 2026 swung to an operating loss of KRW 276 million from a year-ago profit.

Balance-sheet burden

The debt-to-equity ratio was 191.3% at end-2025, with total liabilities of KRW 111.9 billion above total equity of KRW 58.5 billion. The company said a convertible bond option-value loss turned cumulative 2026 net income negative. Such valuation swings add volatility to quarterly bottom-line results.

Weaker cash flow and lower rental income

Operating cash flow fell from KRW 12.8 billion in 2024 to KRW 1.18 billion in 2025. Vacancies during the headquarters remodeling reduced rental income, which the company cited for the drop in cumulative 2026 operating profit. The pace of shifting earnings toward the core business remains to be seen.

Risk factors

Uncertain returns from new businesses

New businesses including the content-commerce joint venture and K-beauty commerce have not yet shown up in reported results. New investment and staffing could weigh on the margins of the advertising core. Until the joint venture's setup and capital commitment are clear, its value cannot be gauged from results.

Financing and balance-sheet risk

Press reports say the company raised KRW 10 billion in convertible bonds to fund headquarters construction and reserves while operating cash outflows continued. Operating cash flow fell to KRW 1.18 billion in 2025, and the debt-to-equity ratio stands at 191.3%. Swings in the valuation of the convertible bond options could move quarterly net income.

Ad-market cycle and seasonality

In June 2025 Korea Investment & Securities characterized the ad industry as weak amid domestic and external uncertainty. Because ad budgets concentrate in the second half, first-half losses are structurally possible. Major shareholders and related parties hold 65.72%, a high ownership concentration.

Overall view

Chai Communication grew its top line in 2026, with cumulative January–September revenue passing full-year 2025 sales on the advertising core and its 2025 acquisition. On an annual basis, operating profit and shareholder net income both recovered in 2025 after the 2024 loss.

However, shareholder losses have run for three consecutive quarters from Q1 2026, and the four quarters from Q4 2025 through Q3 2026 total KRW 2.19 billion.

The convertible bond option-value loss, lower rental income, weaker operating cash flow and a 191.3% debt-to-equity ratio are open questions on earnings quality and financial capacity.

Bullish factors include AI-driven efficiency, Naver partner status and award recognition, and expansion into content commerce and K-beauty. Bearish factors include persistent loss quarters, a weak ad-industry backdrop and uncertain returns from new businesses.

On valuation, the price-to-earnings ratio is not calculated while the price-to-book ratio sits below 1x, so the starting point is comparing the asset-based share price with the pace of earnings recovery.

The next checkpoints are the 2026 annual report, which will include Q4 2026 advertising-season results, and the Q1 2027 quarterly report.
