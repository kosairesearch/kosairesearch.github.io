02

Business structure

Anam Electronics, founded in 1973, is an audio and video equipment maker that designs and manufactures products for overseas brand customers under OEM and ODM arrangements rather than selling its own brands.

As of 2022, audio products accounted for 98.5% of sales, audio parts 0.5%, and other items 1.0%, according to a 2023 press report. Audio products are split into the Traditional Audio (TA) and New Category Audio (NCA) segments, and the same report put their sales mix at roughly 6:4.

The NCA segment produces smart speakers, AI speakers, soundbars and Bluetooth speakers. Major customers were reported as Harman, Yamaha and D&M (Denon & Marantz), and Harman was acquired by Samsung Electronics in 2016.

In a recent tally, audio products made up about 95.5% of sales through exports, so dependence on overseas customers is very high. In a 2023 press report, Korea IR Council highlighted the company's audio engineering and manufacturing technology, including sound-field effects.

However, the company's cumulative 2025 third-quarter earnings commentary said audio products have entered a mature stage, intensifying price competition. In March 2024 the company said it was pursuing new businesses, including electronic boards for fitness equipment and sound-control devices for electronic instruments.

04

Earnings analysis

The company reports in USD, so the amounts in this body are converted to KRW.

Annual revenue went from KRW 496.6 billion in 2022 to KRW 341.3 billion in 2023 (down 31.3% year on year), KRW 253 billion in 2024 (down 25.9%) and KRW 202.1 billion in 2025 (down 20.1%), a three-year consecutive decline from 2023 through 2025. The filings do not cover years before 2022.

Operating profit fell from KRW 21.3 billion in 2022 to KRW 9.59 billion in 2023 (down 55.1%), edged up to KRW 9.69 billion in 2024 (up 1.1%), and then dropped to KRW 4.5 billion in 2025 (down 53.6%). The operating margin was 4.3% in 2022, 2.8% in 2023, 3.8% in 2024 and 2.2% in 2025.

The company attributed the main cause of the 2025 decline to lower sales from weaker audio demand.

Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders went from KRW 14.4 billion in 2022 to KRW 7.22 billion in 2023 (down 49.7%), rose to KRW 9.28 billion in 2024 (up 28.4%), and fell to KRW 5.53 billion in 2025 (down 40.4%).

Net profit including non-controlling interests is reported at the same amounts in each of these years. Operating cash flow went from KRW 34.7 billion in 2022 to KRW 61 billion in 2023 and KRW 40.4 billion in 2024, then fell to KRW 15.9 billion in 2025.

On a quarterly basis, revenue was KRW 59.7 billion in Q3 2025, KRW 41.7 billion in Q4 2025 (down 30.2% quarter on quarter), and KRW 26.1 billion in Q1 2026 (down 37.5%), before rising to KRW 60.7 billion in Q2 2026 (up 132.8%) and KRW 73.5 billion in Q3 2026 (up 21.1%).

Operating profit was KRW 832 million in Q3 2025 and KRW 280 million in Q4 2025, turned into an operating loss of KRW 3.97 billion in Q1 2026, returned to a profit of KRW 1.51 billion in Q2 2026, and reached KRW 3.5 billion in Q3 2026.

Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 4.01 billion in Q3 2026, up 387.4% year on year.

The debt ratio fell from 176.4% in 2022 to 54.6% in 2025, and at the end of 2025 total equity and equity attributable to controlling shareholders were each KRW 124.5 billion, with total liabilities of KRW 68 billion.

05

Industry analysis

The company's cumulative third-quarter 2025 earnings commentary characterized audio products as having entered a mature stage, with intensifying price competition. In its own explanation for the 2025 revenue decline, the company cited weaker audio demand.

In a 2023 press report, Korea IR Council described the global high-end home audio (AVR) market as being in a low-growth phase, expecting low growth but not high growth in that segment. The same report pointed to continued growth in smart home markets and new device categories.

The NCA product lineup, including smart speakers, AI speakers, soundbars and Bluetooth speakers, was described in the same year's coverage as an open market. Some observers have viewed the company as a leading ODM player that survived industry restructuring in the high-end audio segment.

In a sector comparison from a recent data provider, the company's 2025 operating margin of 2.23% stood above the sector average of negative 0.60%. That comparison reflects a sector average that is itself loss-making, and the company's own margin has trended down from 4.3% in 2022.

Judging where the company sits in the industry cycle requires reading the 2026 annual report's sales mix and demand commentary alongside these figures.

06

Outlook

In March 2024 the company said it would supply electronic boards and sound-tuning work for treadmills and other fitness equipment to a global fitness equipment maker during the fourth quarter. At the same time it said electronic guitar sound-control devices would enter mass production from the fourth quarter.

The company set a target of more than USD 100 million in new-business sales within three years. A 2026 first-half company analysis listed expanding OEM and ODM supply to global audio brands such as Harman as a key priority.

The same analysis said the company is widening its product portfolio through development and supply of higher-priced audio products. No current company guidance that directly shows progress against the 2024 targets was found in the filings.

Revenue and operating profit both rose in Q3 2026 from a year earlier, but the filings alone do not identify the cause. Whether the quarterly rebound comes from new customers and new businesses or from existing audio volumes is a point the coming disclosures will need to show.

The next periodic report, the 2026 annual report, will show whether annual revenue and the operating margin move away from the 2.2% recorded in 2025. Because the new-business target falls around 2027, the sales mix disclosed around that time will indicate whether the target was met.

Bull factors

Quarterly sales and profit improving together

Revenue rose for two consecutive quarters through Q3 2026, reaching KRW 73.5 billion in Q3, up 23.1% year on year. Operating profit in the same quarter rose to KRW 3.5 billion, up 320.1%. Q1 2026 was the only operating-loss quarter among the last five.

Long-standing supply ties to global brands

OEM and ODM supply to global audio brands such as Harman, Yamaha and D&M forms the core structure. A 2026 first-half company analysis cited expanding supply to brands including Harman as a key priority. The company is also broadening its portfolio through higher-priced audio product development.

Lower debt burden

The debt ratio fell from 176.4% in 2022 to 54.6% in 2025. At the end of 2025, total liabilities were KRW 68 billion and total equity was KRW 124.5 billion. The lower financial burden could widen room for new-business or facility investment.

Bear factors

Three straight years of annual revenue decline

Revenue fell from KRW 496.6 billion in 2022 to KRW 202.1 billion in 2025, converted to KRW. It declined for three consecutive years from 2023 through 2025, and the company cited weaker audio demand for 2025. Whether the quarterly rebound becomes an annual trend change can be judged in the 2026 annual report.

Thin margins and price competition

The operating margin fell from 4.3% in 2022 to 2.2% in 2025. The cumulative Q3 2025 earnings commentary pointed to audio products entering a mature stage and intensifying price competition. Given the export-dependent nature of the industry, margin recovery could be limited if pricing power stays weak.

Large swings in quarterly results

Q1 2026 posted an operating loss of KRW 3.97 billion and a net loss attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 4.21 billion. Q4 2025 operating profit fell to KRW 280 million, down 66.4% quarter on quarter. Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders over the last four quarters totals only KRW 2.9 billion.

10

Risk factors

Customer concentration and demand swings

Major customers are concentrated among a few global brands, including Harman, Yamaha and D&M, according to a 2023 press report. The company directly cited weaker audio demand for the 2025 revenue decline. Because customer order changes flow directly into revenue, demand-swing risk is significant.

Export dependence and currency translation

Audio exports make up about 95.5% of sales, indicating high reliance on overseas markets. Because the company reports in USD, KRW-converted amounts move with the exchange rate. Overseas customers' order terms and settlement currencies can add to earnings volatility.

Delivery on new-business targets

The new-business targets set in March 2024, including fitness equipment parts and electronic instrument parts, and the goal of more than USD 100 million in sales within three years have not yet been shown in annual results.

With the target deadline around 2027, the contribution of these businesses will show in disclosures around that time. If new businesses fail to offset the decline in core audio sales, the revenue base could keep shrinking.

12

Overall view

Anam Electronics has seen annual revenue and operating profit decline since 2022, while revenue rose in Q2 and Q3 2026 and Q3 operating profit grew sharply from a year earlier. The positives are the directional shift in quarterly results and the lower debt ratio, which fell from 176.4% in 2022 to 54.6% in 2025.

On the other side, annual revenue declined for three consecutive years from 2023 through 2025, and the 2025 operating margin was a thin 2.2%.

The Q1 2026 operating loss of KRW 3.97 billion shows how volatile quarterly profit has been, and net profit attributable to controlling shareholders over the last four quarters totals KRW 2.9 billion.

The business relies on global brand customers such as Harman and on exports of about 95.5% of audio sales, so it is directly exposed to customer demand and price competition. The new-business targets are a test that will show up in results around 2027.

In short, the next judgment points are whether the quarterly rebound holds and what annual revenue looks like in the 2026 annual report. This report is provided for information only and contains no buy or sell opinion.
