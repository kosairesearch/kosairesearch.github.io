Report overview

Audio ODM Veteran Navigates Earnings Volatility

Anam Electronics, a KOSPI-listed audio ODM maker with over five decades of history, has seen revenue decline for four straight years since 2022, though it swung back to profit in the second quarter of 2026 after a loss in the prior quarter.

  1. 1

    Consolidated revenue in 2025 came to KRW 151.35 billion, down from KRW 189.47 billion in 2024, with the operating margin falling to 2.2%, the lowest of the four years reviewed.

  2. 2

    After posting an operating loss of KRW 2.97 billion and a net loss of KRW 3.16 billion in Q1 2026, the company rebounded in Q2 2026 with revenue of KRW 45.46 billion, operating profit of KRW 1.13 billion, and net profit attributable to owners of KRW 1.47 billion.

  3. 3

    The preliminary earnings disclosure filed on February 5, 2026 initially reported 2025 revenue of KRW 215.25 billion and operating profit of KRW 4.79 billion, but the subsequently confirmed audited figures were revised down substantially to revenue of KRW 151.35 billion and operating profit of KRW 3.37 billion.

  4. 4

    The company derives most of its revenue from exports and has been broadening its product mix from AVR-centered (TA) business toward newer audio categories (NCA) such as smart speakers and soundbars.

  5. 5

    On a trailing four-quarter basis (Q3 2025 through Q2 2026), net income attributable to owners remained in slightly negative territory.

02

Business structure

Anam Electronics was established in 1973 as Korea Nashonal Electric, began exporting color televisions in 1974 and audio products in 1975, and has a long operating history as a KOSPI-listed company that went public on the exchange in 1984.

Its business is organized into two segments: Traditional Audio (TA), which manufactures and supplies high-end AV receivers (AVR), and New Category Audio (NCA), which covers relatively faster-growing products such as smart speakers, AI speakers, soundbars, and Bluetooth speakers.

As of the 2022 business report, the revenue split between TA and NCA was roughly 6-to-4, with major customers including global audio brands such as Marantz, Denon, Harman Kardon, and Yamaha, plus a major domestic appliance conglomerate, spanning roughly a dozen client accounts.

The company exports the vast majority of its output, and because both raw material procurement and sales are denominated in US dollars, it maintains a natural currency hedge of sorts.

Manufacturing is concentrated at its Vietnam subsidiary, while the headquarters focuses on R&D and planning for new products and businesses.

The AVR market has long been characterized as a low-growth, higher-margin niche sustained by enthusiast demand, and Anam has been regarded as a surviving ODM leader in this space following industry-wide consolidation.

The NCA segment, meanwhile, has been cited as a medium-to-long-term growth driver tied to the expansion of the global smart home market, though recent revenue trends suggest this segment has not yet grown large enough to offset the company's overall sales decline.

Competitively, the firm operates alongside numerous Asia-based audio ODM/OEM manufacturers, and its results remain highly sensitive to order volume swings from its branded customers.

04

Earnings analysis

On an annual basis, consolidated revenue declined for four consecutive years, from KRW 371.83 billion in 2022 to KRW 255.59 billion in 2023, KRW 189.47 billion in 2024, and KRW 151.35 billion in 2025.

Over the same period the operating margin fluctuated at 4.3% in 2022, 2.8% in 2023, 3.8% in 2024, and 2.2% in 2025, while net income attributable to owners broadly tracked the revenue decline at KRW 10.75 billion in 2022, KRW 5.41 billion in 2023, KRW 6.95 billion in 2024, and KRW 4.14 billion in 2025.

The company disclosed that the 2025 result changes were mainly attributable to reduced audio demand impacting sales.

On a quarterly basis, revenue of KRW 39.90 billion and operating profit of KRW 1.18 billion in Q2 2025 gave way to higher revenue of KRW 44.73 billion in Q3 2025 but lower operating profit of KRW 623 million, indicating thinner margins, before revenue fell sharply to KRW 31.22 billion in Q4 2025 with operating profit slipping further to KRW 210 million.

In Q1 2026, revenue contracted further to KRW 19.53 billion, producing an operating loss of KRW 2.97 billion and a net loss attributable to owners of KRW 3.16 billion, before the company swung back to an operating profit of KRW 1.13 billion and net profit of KRW 1.47 billion on revenue of KRW 45.46 billion in Q2 2026.

This wide swing in quarterly results points to a business structure heavily influenced by the timing of specific customer orders and product mix shifts.

Separately, the preliminary earnings disclosure filed on February 5, 2026 had reported 2025 consolidated revenue of KRW 215.25 billion, operating profit of KRW 4.79 billion, and net income of KRW 5.89 billion, but the subsequently confirmed audited figures were revised substantially lower to revenue of KRW 151.35 billion, operating profit of KRW 3.37 billion, and net income of KRW 4.14 billion, underscoring the potential for meaningful revisions between preliminary and final results.

On a trailing four-quarter basis (Q3 2025 through Q2 2026), net income attributable to owners has remained slightly negative, at roughly negative KRW 220 million.

05

Industry analysis

The high-end AVR market has long been characterized as a low-growth segment sustained mainly by enthusiast demand, valued more for its role as a stable cash generator than for large incremental volume growth.

By contrast, the NCA category encompassing smart speakers, soundbars, and Bluetooth speakers is generally viewed as carrying relatively higher growth potential in step with the expanding global smart home market.

Anam Electronics relies on exports for the bulk of its revenue, leaving it exposed to fluctuations in the won-dollar exchange rate.

The won has recently strengthened, with the exchange rate averaging KRW 1,501 in the second quarter before falling to an average of KRW 1,430 in the third quarter, and KB Investment & Securities noted that in past periods of won strength, combined operating profits for export-heavy KOSPI sectors such as energy, materials, industrials, and information technology have tended to fall short of market consensus.

That said, because Anam sources raw materials and generates sales alike in US dollars, the currency impact on its results may be relatively more contained than for some other exporters.

On the competitive front, numerous Asia-based audio ODM/OEM manufacturers operate in the space, and individual companies' market shares can shift depending on branded customers' sourcing strategies and order allocations.

On the demand side, the company itself has attributed its weaker 2025 results to declining audio demand, suggesting the broader industry is not currently in a clear expansion phase.

Outlook

Within the scope of publicly available information, no specific second-half 2026 revenue or profit guidance, nor any major new order disclosure, could be confirmed from the company.

That said, the swing from a sizable loss in Q1 2026 to a rebound in both revenue and profit in Q2 2026 offers a useful reference point for gauging the trajectory of upcoming quarters.

Under the statutory quarterly reporting cycle, the company's Q3 2026 results are due to be disclosed by mid-November 2026, which should help clarify whether the Q2 rebound is continuing.

On the business side, the TA (AVR) segment continues to function as a stable cash generator, while the extent to which the NCA (newer audio product) segment's revenue contribution expands remains the key variable for medium-term structural change.

Additionally, given the substantial gap previously observed between the preliminary 2025 settlement figures and the final audited results, it will be worth checking the consistency between preliminary and confirmed figures at each future quarterly and annual disclosure.

Whether the four-year trend of declining company-wide revenue continues, or whether the Q2 2026 rebound extends into the second half, is a matter to be assessed through upcoming quarterly disclosures.

Bull factors

Stable Cash Generation from the AVR Segment

The high-end AVR market is a low-growth but higher-margin segment sustained by steady enthusiast demand, and Anam has been regarded as maintaining its position as an ODM leader even after industry-wide consolidation.

Long-standing relationships with global brands such as Marantz, Denon, Harman Kardon, and Yamaha contribute to the stability of its revenue base. The rebound from a loss in Q1 2026 to a profit in Q2 2026 can also be seen as evidence of underlying resilience in this segment.

NCA Segment Tied to Smart Home Growth

The NCA segment, spanning smart speakers, soundbars, and Bluetooth speakers, has been cited as a medium-to-long-term growth driver in line with the expanding global smart home market. Given its diverse range of applications, this product category may offer some diversification against slowdowns in any single category.

If this segment's revenue contribution grows, it could allow for portfolio diversification away from an AVR-centered structure.

Dollar-Denominated Revenue and Procurement Structure

Anam Electronics exports the majority of its output, and because both raw material procurement and sales are denominated in US dollars, it maintains a natural hedge structure. This can partially mitigate foreign exchange gain/loss volatility during periods of won strength or weakness. Its manufacturing base at a Vietnam subsidiary is also understood to contribute to cost competitiveness.

Bear factors

Four Consecutive Years of Revenue Decline

Consolidated revenue declined for four consecutive years, from KRW 371.83 billion in 2022 to KRW 151.35 billion in 2025. The company itself attributed the weak 2025 results to declining audio demand, making it difficult to rule out a structural contraction in underlying demand.

The shrinking revenue base can also make fixed costs relatively more burdensome, constraining operating margin improvement.

Wide Swings in Quarterly Results

In Q1 2026, revenue fell to KRW 19.53 billion, producing an operating loss of KRW 2.97 billion and a net loss of KRW 3.16 billion, before rebounding to revenue of KRW 45.46 billion and operating profit of KRW 1.13 billion in Q2, illustrating wide swings in quarterly results.

This volatility is interpreted as reflecting a business structure heavily dependent on the timing of specific customer orders and product mix. It also makes forecasting future quarterly performance more difficult.

Large Gap Between Preliminary and Confirmed Figures

The preliminary 2025 settlement figures disclosed in February 2026 (revenue of KRW 215.25 billion, operating profit of KRW 4.79 billion) differed substantially from the subsequently confirmed audited figures (revenue of KRW 151.35 billion, operating profit of KRW 3.37 billion).

This suggests that initial settlement disclosures should not be relied upon at face value without checking the final figures confirmed through the audit report. This possibility for revision should be kept in mind when interpreting future earnings disclosures as well.

Risk factors

Customer Concentration

The company's client base is limited to roughly a dozen major customers, with a structure understood to carry meaningful concentration among top accounts. Order adjustments or sourcing strategy shifts by any single customer could directly affect results.

The more than twofold difference in revenue scale between Q1 and Q2 2026 also appears related to this concentration.

Slowing End-Market Demand

The company disclosed that reduced audio demand was a major factor behind the 2025 results change. The AVR market has traditionally been classified as low-growth, and the NCA segment does not appear to have expanded fast enough to offset the company-wide revenue decline.

The pace of any recovery in global consumer electronics and audio demand could be a key determinant of future results.

Potential for Financial Statement Revisions

As seen in the 2025 case, a substantial gap can arise between preliminary settlement figures and the final audited results. This potential for revision is something investors should account for when interpreting figures at each disclosure point.

Additionally, given the company's relatively small revenue scale, the impact of quarterly one-off items can appear comparatively larger.

Overall view

Anam Electronics has focused on audio ODM manufacturing for more than half a century, combining stable cash generation from its AVR segment with growth potential in its NCA segment.

However, consolidated revenue has declined for four consecutive years since 2022, and the operating margin has narrowed to 2.2% in 2025, underscoring clear profitability challenges.

The swing to profit in Q2 2026 following a sizable loss in Q1 is a positive signal, but given the wide swings in quarterly results, it remains uncertain whether this trend will persist.

The substantial gap between the preliminary 2025 settlement figures and the final audited results is a factor worth noting when interpreting future disclosures.

On a trailing four-quarter basis, net income attributable to owners has remained slightly negative, leaving the restoration of earnings stability as the key point to watch going forward.

Investors should also monitor subsequent indicators such as the Q3 2026 earnings disclosure and won-dollar exchange rate movements before forming a view.
