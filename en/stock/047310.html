02

Business structure

Power Logics was founded in 1997 and listed on KOSDAQ in 2003. It runs two segments: CM (Camera Module), which makes camera modules for IT mobile and automotive applications, and ES (Energy Solution), which makes secondary battery protection circuits and medium-to-large battery packs.

In Q1 2026 the camera module segment generated KRW 190.2 billion, or 79% of revenue, while the battery protection circuit and battery pack business accounted for about 20%. Exports made up 86% of revenue in the same quarter.

By product, battery packs contributed KRW 31.2 billion (12.9%) and smart modules KRW 13.8 billion (5.7%). The company supplies a global smartphone maker as a primary vendor and is described as one of the core suppliers to Samsung Electronics.

In batteries it is extending into electric vehicle and energy storage battery management systems and packs, and it also runs automotive camera R&D. In smartphone camera modules it competes with large component makers such as LG Innotek and Samsung Electro-Mechanics. Industry analysis cites customer price-cut pressure and weak bargaining power as constraints on profitability.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual results peaked in 2023 and have since moved between losses and profits. Revenue rose from KRW 743.9 billion in 2022 to KRW 773.2 billion in 2023 (up 3.9% year on year), then fell to KRW 733.4 billion in 2024 (down 5.1%) and KRW 686.5 billion in 2025 (down 6.4%), a decline in each of 2024 and 2025.

Among 2022 to 2025, 2023 was the largest year and 2025 the smallest. Operating loss of KRW 39.1 billion in 2022 turned into operating profit of KRW 15.6 billion in 2023, but operating profit fell 84.5% to KRW 2.42 billion in 2024.

The company swung to an operating loss of KRW 6.37 billion in 2025 and attributed the decline to lower revenue from a slump in the secondary battery market and asset impairment recognition.

Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders went from KRW 16.4 billion in 2023 to KRW 10.8 billion in 2024 (down 34.1%), then to a net loss of KRW 12.3 billion in 2025. Operating margin stayed low at 2.0% in 2023, 0.3% in 2024 and -0.9% in 2025.

On a quarterly basis, Q3 2025 revenue was KRW 182.9 billion with operating profit of KRW 6.13 billion, followed by an operating loss of KRW 2.43 billion in Q4 2025. Q1 2026 revenue reached KRW 241.3 billion, up 51.4% from the prior quarter, with operating profit of KRW 7.82 billion, the peak of the recent run.

Q2 2026 revenue was KRW 200.5 billion and operating profit KRW 4.63 billion, and Q3 2026 revenue rose to KRW 214 billion (up 17.0% year on year) while operating profit fell to KRW 3.07 billion (down 49.9% year on year). Net loss attributable to controlling shareholders over the last four quarters totaled KRW 1.34 billion.

05

Industry analysis

Earnings hinge on two industry cycles: the smartphone camera module cycle and secondary battery demand. In camera modules, wider adoption of multi-camera designs has been cited as lifting both volume and product sophistication.

Industry analysis, however, argues that a prolonged slowdown in smartphone growth would likely increase earnings volatility for companies with heavy camera module exposure like this one. On the customer side, price-cut pressure and limited bargaining power are named as factors that weigh on margins.

In batteries, growth in electric vehicles and government support for green car industries have been pointed to as drivers for medium-to-large battery packs. Because the company attributed its 2025 deterioration to a slump in the secondary battery market, the pace of recovery in that market still needs confirmation.

Competitively, camera modules pit the company against large component makers such as LG Innotek and Samsung Electro-Mechanics. With camera modules at 79% of Q1 2026 revenue, its exposure to front-end smartphone demand is relatively large.

Diversification into automotive cameras and medium-to-large batteries is under way, but these businesses remain a limited share of total revenue.

Outlook

According to an aggregator's tally, consolidated revenue rose 28.3% year on year in the first half of 2026 and operating profit turned positive. The same tally describes the camera module segment as growing through wider multi-camera adoption as a primary vendor to a global smartphone maker.

The battery pack business is described as maintaining its revenue base as a partner in medium-to-large battery value chains at home and abroad. On new businesses, company profile materials describe a plan with Hyundai Motor Group to recycle end-of-life EV batteries into energy storage systems.

For automotive cameras, the company is described as expanding high-reliability camera modules aimed at ADAS and autonomous driving. However, when these businesses will become meaningful revenue is not established by the disclosed figures.

To judge the margin trajectory, the Q4 2026 results and the 2026 annual report, including segment revenue mix and operating margin, are the points to check. Revenue rose in Q3 2026 while operating profit fell, so whether the margin structure has shifted is the next checkpoint. Whether customer volumes align with the 2027 first-half smartphone model cycle is also an item to follow.

Bull factors

Camera module volume drives the revenue rebound

Q1 2026 revenue reached KRW 241.3 billion, a sharp rise from KRW 159.4 billion in Q4 2025. In the same quarter the camera module segment generated KRW 190.2 billion, or 79% of revenue. An aggregator reports first-half 2026 consolidated revenue up 28.3% year on year, attributed to wider multi-camera adoption. The core of the rebound sits in camera modules.

Profit recovery and easing balance sheet pressure

Operating results moved from a KRW 6.37 billion operating loss in 2025 to KRW 7.82 billion of operating profit in Q1 2026. Lower debt burden and a larger equity base underlie the improvement in the financial structure. Operating cash flow was KRW 16.6 billion in 2025, after a negative figure in 2024. Improved financial capacity serves as a cushion.

Expansion into medium-to-large batteries and automotive

The ES segment is widening into battery management systems and medium-to-large packs for electric vehicles and energy storage. Company profile materials describe a plan with Hyundai Motor Group to recycle end-of-life EV batteries into ESS. Automotive camera R&D runs alongside, broadening the automotive option. These new businesses could reduce reliance on smartphone camera modules over time.

Bear factors

Two straight quarters of declining profit

Q2 2026 operating profit was KRW 4.63 billion, down 40.8% from the prior quarter, and Q3 fell further to KRW 3.07 billion, down 33.6%. Operating profit has declined for two consecutive quarters since peaking in Q1 2026.

Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 61.71 million in Q3 2026, down 98.3% from the prior quarter. Over the last four quarters the cumulative net loss attributable to controlling shareholders totaled KRW 1.34 billion, showing how quickly quarterly profit has reversed.

Smartphone dependence and price pressure

With camera modules at 79% of revenue, the company depends heavily on smartphone demand and customer volumes. Industry analysis points to customer price-cut pressure and weak bargaining power as limits on margin improvement. Exports at 86% of revenue leave the company exposed to overseas demand shifts. The 2025 operating margin was -0.9%.

Secondary battery slump and asset impairment

For its 2025 deterioration, the company cited lower revenue from a secondary battery market slump and the recognition of asset impairment. 2025 revenue of KRW 686.5 billion sits below the 2023 peak of KRW 773.2 billion.

If medium-to-large battery businesses do not translate into profit as expected, the battery segment remains a burden. The 2025 net loss attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 12.3 billion.

Customer and industry concentration

With camera modules at 79% of revenue, a prolonged slowdown in smartphone growth could increase earnings volatility. Shifts in specific customer volumes and price adjustments flow directly into revenue and margin. Consecutive profit declines in Q2 and Q3 2026 illustrate how this structure transmits changes.

Asset impairment and one-off charges

In 2025, asset impairment recognition weighed on profit. Q4 2025 showed an operating loss of KRW 2.43 billion and a net loss attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 13.9 billion. Future impairments or one-off charges could again disrupt quarterly results, making earnings quality a point to watch.

Overall view

Power Logics grew its top line through a camera module volume recovery, with Q1 2026 revenue of KRW 241.3 billion, and an aggregator reports that first-half 2026 operating profit turned positive.

However, operating profit and net profit attributable to controlling shareholders declined in both Q2 and Q3 2026, and the cumulative net loss attributable to controlling shareholders over the last four quarters totaled KRW 1.34 billion.

On an annual basis, results declined after the 2023 peak in 2024 and 2025, and 2025 swung to an operating loss of KRW 6.37 billion. On the balance sheet, the debt-to-equity ratio fell from 99.4% in 2023 to 84.6% in 2025, with total equity of KRW 197.8 billion.

Strengths include the camera module recovery, battery and automotive diversification, and improved financial capacity. Weaknesses include smartphone dependence, price pressure, quarterly volatility, and concentrated ownership.

The next checkpoints are the preliminary 2026 annual results and the operating margin in the 2026 annual report.
