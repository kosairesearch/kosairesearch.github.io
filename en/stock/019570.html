Business structure

GMI Venture (Plutus Investment) was founded in November 1986 as a Korean corporate venture capital firm, and its main business is forming and managing investment partnerships in finance and corporate investing.

The company has operated under several names, from Daishin Development Finance and Gemini Investment to Plutus Investment, and began trading under its current name on July 31, 2026. As of April 2026 it reported 11 employees.

Earnings generally come from management income on investment partnerships and gains or losses from valuing and disposing of investment assets, so quarterly operating results can swing far more than revenue.

Revenue fell to KRW 1.72 billion in Q3 2026 while the operating loss widened to KRW 5.29 billion, and the breakdown behind that move should be checked in the line-item disclosures of the periodic report.

Segment revenue shares and the composition of major investment assets also need to be confirmed against the segment disclosures in the periodic report.

Peers often compared with it include Shinhan Venture Investment, Smilegate Investment, CJ Investment and Platinum Technology Investment, and as of September 2026 their headcounts were 65, 36 and 14 respectively.

Because the company is small, the results of a few investments weigh heavily on consolidated earnings, which is another source of quarterly volatility.

Earnings analysis

On a reported basis, annual revenue rose from KRW 6.88 billion in 2022 to KRW 6.95 billion in 2023 (up 1.0% year on year), KRW 10.3 billion in 2024 (up 48.7%) and KRW 12.9 billion in 2025 (up 24.7%).

Revenue has grown for three consecutive years from 2023 through 2025, with 2025 the largest year and 2022 the smallest in the 2022 to 2025 period. Profitability has moved from losses to profits.

The operating loss narrowed from KRW 17.3 billion in 2022 to KRW 10.3 billion in 2023, and the company then posted an operating profit of KRW 1.27 billion in 2024, turning positive.

The 2025 operating profit of KRW 3.31 billion rose 160.0% year on year, and the operating margin moved from -251.0% in 2022 and -148.4% in 2023 to 12.3% in 2024 and 25.7% in 2025.

Net losses attributable to controlling shareholders followed the same path, falling from KRW 18.3 billion in 2022 and KRW 11.6 billion in 2023 to KRW 212 million in 2024, before a net profit attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 2.85 billion in 2025.

The reported net loss including non-controlling interests exists only for 2022, at KRW 18.3 billion, so a year-by-year comparison is limited, and that figure differs from the net profit attributable to controlling shareholders.

At the end of 2025, total equity was KRW 40.8 billion, all attributable to controlling shareholders, and total liabilities were KRW 31.6 billion. The debt ratio rose from 27.1% in 2024 to 77.4% in 2025.

Operating cash flow was KRW 8.28 billion in 2022 and KRW 2.7 billion in 2023, but turned to outflows of -KRW 26 billion in 2024 and -KRW 18.7 billion in 2025.

On a quarterly basis, revenue moved sharply: KRW 3.22 billion in Q3 2025, KRW 4.82 billion in Q4 2025 (up 49.5% from the prior quarter), KRW 3.33 billion in Q1 2026 (down 30.9%), KRW 5.66 billion in Q2 2026 (up 70.2%) and KRW 1.72 billion in Q3 2026 (down 69.6%).

Operating profit was KRW 1.17 billion in Q3 2025 and KRW 1.47 billion in Q4 2025, then turned to an operating loss of KRW 3.05 billion in Q1 2026. Q2 2026 returned to operating profit of KRW 1.7 billion before Q3 2026 posted an operating loss of KRW 5.29 billion.

In Q3 2026 operating results deteriorated sharply while revenue fell.

Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 755 million in Q3 2025 and KRW 821 million in Q4 2025, then a net loss of KRW 3.13 billion in Q1 2026, a net profit of KRW 1.26 billion in Q2 2026 and a net loss of KRW 5.76 billion in Q3 2026. Over the last four quarters, net losses attributable to controlling shareholders totaled KRW 6.81 billion.

05

Industry analysis

The profit and loss of a corporate venture capital firm depends on the state of the exit market. When listings and mergers and acquisitions are active, valuation and disposal gains on investment assets tend to increase, and when they weaken, valuation losses tend to grow.

Interest rate levels and funding conditions also affect the value of investment assets and the timing of exits. The large swings in the company's quarterly operating results are linked to these changes in valuation and disposal gains.

In competition, Shinhan Venture Investment, Smilegate Investment, CJ Investment and Platinum Technology Investment are typical comparison points, many of which have financial group or conglomerate backing and larger fund scale.

With 11 employees, GMI Venture is a small corporate venture capital firm, so the results of a few investments weigh heavily on total earnings. Comparing earnings stability with peers requires lining up their results over the same periods.

The price-to-book ratio, which in a September 2026 snapshot was below the average of its peer group, suggests the market is cautious about how much of the asset value can be realized.
