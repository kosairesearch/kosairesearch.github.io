Holding Company Earnings Under Pressure: Cement and Paper Base

Asia Holdings is a holding company whose cement and paper subsidiaries posted sales and operating profit peaks in 2023, followed by declines in both 2024 and 2025, while quarterly shareholder net profit turned positive again in the first quarter of 2026.

02

Business structure

Asia Holdings is a pure holding company that manages subsidiaries and provides management consulting, with its operating subsidiaries grouped into cement, paper and other segments.

According to its 2026 annual report, it directly controls two domestic subsidiaries, the cement subsidiary Asia Cement and Asia Paper Manufacturing, and exercises control over ten sub-subsidiaries and two sub-sub-subsidiaries.

The cement business centers on Asia Cement, which makes cement, ready-mix concrete and mortar and owns its own limestone quarry, covering production from raw material to finished product. A 2022 securities research report said the 2018 acquisition of Halla Cement lifted the group to third place in cement market share.

The paper business is led by Asia Paper Manufacturing, alongside several corrugated-box and recycling affiliates, and the annual report describes a vertical structure that secures sales channels and waste-paper supply.

Other segments include a venture capital unit, an agricultural company, and a property management and amusement park operator. In 2025, the cement subsidiary reported consolidated sales of KRW 1.0228 trillion and consolidated operating profit of KRW 77.1 billion.

The holding company's own results therefore depend heavily on these two operating businesses and on dividends from the subsidiaries.

04

Earnings analysis

On a disclosed annual basis, sales rose from KRW 2.07 trillion in 2022 to a peak of KRW 2.12 trillion in 2023, up 2.1% year on year. They then fell 5.1% to KRW 2.01 trillion in 2024 and 6.1% to KRW 1.88 trillion in 2025, so sales declined in two consecutive years after 2023.

Of the 2022 to 2025 period, 2023 was the largest year and 2025 the smallest. Operating profit peaked at KRW 240.4 billion in 2023 and then fell 28.3% to KRW 172.4 billion in 2024 and 35.6% to KRW 111 billion in 2025, a steeper decline than sales.

The operating margin moved from 11.2% in 2022 and 11.4% in 2023 to 8.6% in 2024 and 5.9% in 2025, showing that margins compressed faster than revenue. Shareholder net profit went from KRW 93.3 billion in 2023 to KRW 62.1 billion in 2024 and KRW 31 billion in 2025.

Net profit including non-controlling interests fell from KRW 175.6 billion to KRW 51.3 billion over the same span, and the gap between the two measures reflects the non-controlling share. Operating cash flow peaked at KRW 333.8 billion in 2023 and was KRW 171.9 billion in 2025, still positive.

Quarterly, third-quarter 2025 sales were KRW 459.9 billion with operating profit of KRW 26.6 billion. Fourth-quarter 2025 sales rose 6.4% quarter on quarter to KRW 489.2 billion, but operating profit fell 71.0% to KRW 7.74 billion and shareholder profit swung to a net loss of KRW 7.25 billion.

In the first quarter of 2026 operating profit recovered to KRW 23.7 billion and shareholder net profit turned positive at KRW 9.33 billion.

Second-quarter 2026 sales were KRW 508.7 billion with operating profit of KRW 36.4 billion, and third-quarter 2026 sales were KRW 465.2 billion with operating profit of KRW 20.5 billion.

The specific drivers of the fourth-quarter 2025 drop and whether one-off items were involved are points to check in the annual report notes by segment.

05

Industry analysis

Cement demand is tied to construction and civil engineering activity, so shipment volumes move with building cycles.

Demand for corrugated base paper, the core product of the paper business, is linked to corrugated box demand, and a 2022 research report said a revival in the parcel delivery market helped lift sales in 2021.

The annual report says the group maintains a leading market position in industrial paper, including corrugated base paper. In cement, owning its own limestone quarry is cited as the basis of its cost position, while in paper, vertical integration among affiliates links raw material supply with sales channels.

Many listed holding companies in the same industry hold portfolios spanning finance, energy, semiconductors and food, whereas Asia Holdings has a concentrated structure centered on cement and paper. As a result, changes in construction activity and corrugated demand feed directly into holding-company results.

On a disclosed basis, sales have declined for two consecutive years since the 2023 peak, and third-quarter 2026 sales rose only 1.2% year on year. Whether the cycle is turning can be gauged by whether quarterly sales and operating margins improve together in the coming quarters.

The group was added to the KOSPI 200 index in November 2025, but index-related flows should be viewed separately from the business trend.

Outlook

The next regular filing is the 2026 annual report, which will fix full-year sales, operating profit, subsidiary-level results and dividend policy. Quarterly operating profit in 2026 ranged from KRW 20.5 billion to KRW 36.4 billion, moving between the low and high tens of billions of won.

Shareholder net profit also varied widely by quarter, from KRW 9.33 billion in the first quarter to KRW 15.9 billion in the second and KRW 7.56 billion in the third. In the third quarter of 2026 sales rose year on year while profit fell, which makes profitability rather than top-line growth the central variable.

The cement subsidiary reported consolidated operating profit of KRW 77.1 billion in 2025, so holding-company earnings depend on that subsidiary's margins and on cost trends in the paper business. Raw material and energy costs and shipment flows at construction sites will set the direction of margins.

Because subsidiary results flow into consolidated results, quarterly reports should be read with the sales and operating profit of both operating subsidiaries. The annual report in late March 2027 will show the drivers of the fourth-quarter 2025 drop and whether the 2026 operating margin improved from 5.9% in 2025.

The first-quarter 2027 report will show whether margins rise above 2026 quarterly levels. Medium-term plans and capital expenditure details require confirmation through future disclosures.

08

Bull factors

Earnings base in the cement subsidiary

In 2025 the cement subsidiary, Asia Cement, reported consolidated sales of KRW 1.0228 trillion and consolidated operating profit of KRW 77.1 billion. Because holding-company results depend heavily on this subsidiary, stable cement margins would support the earnings base. Owning its own limestone quarry is also cited as a cost-side buffer.

Return to quarterly profit

After a shareholder net loss of KRW 7.25 billion in the fourth quarter of 2025, profit returned to KRW 9.33 billion in the first quarter of 2026. The second quarter posted operating profit of KRW 36.4 billion and shareholder net profit of KRW 15.9 billion, which points to recovery in quarterly earnings power.

Easing leverage and asset backing

The debt-to-equity ratio fell from 70.7% in 2022 to 63.0% in 2025, and operating cash flow stayed positive at KRW 171.9 billion in 2025. The share price sits well below book value attributable to shareholders, a point that draws attention from investors focused on asset backing.

09

Bear factors

Two years of declining sales and margins

Sales fell 5.1% in 2024 and 6.1% in 2025, while operating profit dropped 28.3% and 35.6% in those years. The operating margin declined from 11.4% in 2023 to 5.9% in 2025, meaning margin compression outpaced the decline in sales.

Profit fell despite higher third-quarter sales

Third-quarter 2026 sales rose 1.2% year on year to KRW 465.2 billion, yet operating profit fell 22.9% to KRW 20.5 billion. Shareholder net profit also fell 24.0% to KRW 7.56 billion, so higher revenue did not translate into higher profit.

Thin and volatile earnings base

Shareholder net profit totaled KRW 25.5 billion over the last four quarters, and the fourth quarter of 2025 posted a net loss. Full-year 2025 net profit including non-controlling interests was KRW 51.3 billion, so the earnings attributable to the parent is thinner still. Quarterly swings are large, making it hard to judge earnings power from one or two quarters.

10

Risk factors

Construction and corrugated demand risk

Cement shipments depend on construction and civil engineering activity, while paper sales depend on corrugated box demand. A slowdown in construction or weaker parcel and industrial packaging demand could pressure both volumes and utilization.

Sales have declined for two consecutive years since the 2023 peak, so the pace of demand recovery is the key variable.

Holding structure and subsidiary dependence

Holding company profits depend on subsidiary results and dividend flows. Where non-controlling interests take a large share, as the gap between net profit and shareholder net profit shows, less profit reaches the parent's shareholders. With many layers of subsidiaries, changes in intra-group ownership could also become a governance issue.

Cost and borrowing burden

Total liabilities stood at KRW 1.28 trillion at the end of 2025, so changes in interest rates and borrowing terms feed directly into financing costs. Rising energy and raw material prices would first pressure cement and paper margins. With the operating margin down to 5.9% in 2025, the capacity to absorb cost swings is limited.

12

Overall view

Asia Holdings is a holding company whose cement and paper subsidiaries drove sales from a peak of KRW 2.12 trillion in 2023 down to KRW 1.88 trillion in 2025, while operating profit fell from KRW 240.4 billion to KRW 111 billion over the same period.

The operating margin declined from 11.4% to 5.9%, making margin compression the central factor behind the earnings decline. Quarterly operating profit in 2026 moved between KRW 20.5 billion and KRW 36.4 billion, showing both recovery and softness.

The return to a first-quarter 2026 profit after the fourth-quarter 2025 net loss and the improvement in the second quarter are positive points. However, third-quarter 2026 profit fell even though sales rose, so a durable profitability recovery is not yet visible.

On the balance sheet, the debt-to-equity ratio fell from 70.7% in 2022 to 63.0% in 2025 and operating cash flow remained positive, easing financial strain.

Against that, shareholder net profit totaled KRW 25.5 billion over the last four quarters, leaving a thin earnings base exposed to construction and corrugated demand. The next checkpoints are the operating margin in the 2026 annual report and the first-quarter 2027 results.
