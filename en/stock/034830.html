02

Business structure

Korea Real Estate Investment & Trust is a real estate trust company engaged in land trusts, trust-based urban redevelopment projects, REIT operation and asset management (AMC), and investment projects. According to a Shinyoung Securities report, new orders in 2025 reached KRW 324.2 billion, the highest in six years.

Based on IB Tomato's reporting, urban redevelopment accounted for KRW 164.9 billion (50.9%) of those orders and debt-funded land trusts for KRW 115.4 billion (35.6%). These are shares of orders, not of revenue. REIT projects once made up more than 30% of the mix but have fallen to the 5% range.

Debt-funded land trusts require the company to advance project costs from its own funds, and IB Tomato describes a shift from fee-based income toward directly funding projects. REIT assets under management reached KRW 39.8 trillion at the end of 2025, up from KRW 3.04 trillion in 2023.

By asset size, the company ranked first among real estate trust firms as of Q3 2025, at KRW 1.65 trillion, and it ranks at the top of the sector alongside the No. 2 firm.

Its subsidiary, Korate Asset Management, handles asset management, while affiliates in construction and heavy industry are accounted for under the equity method. As of Q2 2026, trust-based redevelopment projects totaled 40 sites and 44,633 households nationwide.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual revenue rose from KRW 212.9 billion in 2022 to KRW 270.1 billion in 2023 (up 26.9% year on year), then fell to KRW 236.3 billion in 2024 (down 12.5%) and KRW 184.4 billion in 2025 (down 22.0%), so revenue declined for two consecutive years from 2024 through 2025.

Among the 2022 to 2025 years, 2023 was the largest and 2025 the smallest. Operating profit fell from KRW 53.3 billion in 2022 to KRW 31.2 billion in 2023 (down 41.4%), recovered to KRW 33.9 billion in 2024 (up 8.7%), and then turned into an operating loss of KRW 20.9 billion in 2025.

The 2025 operating margin was -11.3%. Rising project-cost outlays and borrowing costs are cited as the main drivers. According to IB Tomato, project spending rose from KRW 594.4 billion in 2023 to KRW 922.9 billion in 2025, and borrowing rates ranged from 5.3% to 9.3%.

Korea Investors Service's peer, Nice Investors Service's senior researcher Yoon Jae-sung, said credit losses rose in debt-funded real estate trust projects.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders went from KRW 24.1 billion in 2022 to a net loss of KRW 8.44 billion in 2023 (a swing into loss), a net loss of KRW 16.5 billion in 2024 (losses widened), and KRW 21 billion in 2025 (a return to profit).

Net income including non-controlling interests was KRW 19.4 billion in 2025, a separate figure from net income attributable to controlling shareholders. Operating cash flow moved from KRW 176.2 billion in 2024 to KRW -124.6 billion in 2025.

Quarterly, an operating loss of KRW 15 billion in Q3 2025 narrowed to KRW 5.31 billion in Q4 2025, and Q1 2026 operating profit of KRW 479 million marked a return to profit. Q2 2026 operating profit was KRW 4.4 billion, up 820.3% from the prior quarter, and Q3 2026 operating profit was KRW 1.45 billion, down 67.0%.

Equity-method income in Q1 2026 was KRW 13.2 billion, above that quarter's net income, and this income appears to fill the gap between operating profit and net income attributable to controlling shareholders.

05

Industry analysis

The real estate trust sector has struggled since 2023 as the project financing (PF) market contracted and construction activity slowed. According to IB Tomato, new orders fell to KRW 53.9 billion in 2023, after which the company shifted its strategy toward redevelopment projects.

Hanwha INVESTMENT&SECURITIES said new orders showed a clear recovery from a trough in 2024. Shinyoung Securities analyzed that debt-funded land trusts, which use the company's own capital, absorbed a gap in development financing as real estate-centered credit supply shrank.

Shinhan Investment Corp. said government policy, including PF restructuring and promotion of project REITs, runs in the same direction as the company's business. On the other side, the regional sales market has weakened, reducing sales income from debt-funded projects outside the capital region.

Analysts also note that profitability declines at the two largest real estate trust firms reflect fallout from debt-funded land trust problems. In this competitive landscape, the company ranks first by asset size, yet its core earnings power faces challenges common to the whole sector.

The company says it is strengthening its growth base through urban redevelopment and REIT expansion despite the PF slowdown.

06

Outlook

Three checkpoints appear in brokerage reports and press coverage.

First, the Heukseok 11 redevelopment and Sindeel 10 reconstruction projects began sales in May and August 2026 respectively, and IB Tomato reported that once these sites reach the sales stage, project cost outlays can move into recovery and revenue recognition may accelerate.

Second, Shinhan Investment Corp. said in a September 2026 report that sales take-up could recover from around 40% to around 60% in the second half, and it lowered its full-year operating profit estimate from above KRW 40 billion, citing first-half investment losses.

Third, Hanwha INVESTMENT&SECURITIES projected 2026 operating profit at KRW 15 billion and 2027 operating profit at KRW 34 billion in a September 2026 report. Debt-funded urban redevelopment accounted for 57% of the order backlog at the end of the first half of 2026.

The company booked five new redevelopment projects this year, mostly in the capital region. Analysts note that asset purchase and sale plans in the REIT division could lift fee income. Only four takeover-guarantee (completion-guarantee) sites remain, and the company says large additional exposure is limited.

Bull factors

Recovery in redevelopment orders

Shinyoung Securities said total new orders in 2025 were the highest in six years. Hanwha INVESTMENT&SECURITIES analyzed that redevelopment orders of KRW 164.9 billion were the largest since the company entered the business. Trust-based redevelopment projects totaled 40 sites as of Q2 2026. Debt-funded urban redevelopment reached 57% of the order backlog at the end of the first half of 2026.

Quarterly return to profit

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders turned from a loss to KRW 21 billion in 2025. Quarterly net income attributable to controlling shareholders stayed positive from Q4 2025 through Q3 2026. Operating profit also returned to positive in Q1 2026 after an operating loss of KRW 15 billion in Q3 2025. Q2 2026 operating profit of KRW 4.4 billion rose 820.3% from the prior quarter.

Buy rating maintained by a brokerage

Hanwha INVESTMENT&SECURITIES said in a September 2026 report that it maintained a 'Buy' rating and a KRW 1,800 target price. The same report presented expanding debt-funded urban redevelopment orders as a core medium-term investment point. It projected 2026 operating profit at KRW 15 billion and 2027 at KRW 34 billion. REIT assets under management rose to KRW 39.8 trillion at the end of 2025.

09

Bear factors

Weaker core trust earnings

The 2025 operating loss was attributed to lower fee income combined with higher selling, general and administrative expenses. The 2025 operating margin was -11.3%. A press report citing the Q3 2026 quarterly filing said separate-basis operating profit declined year on year. Lower dividend income and financial product losses were named as causes.

Reliance on equity-method income and cash outflows

A large part of net income attributable to controlling shareholders comes from equity-method gains. Press coverage noted these gains are accounting profits that do not equal cash received. Operating cash flow was KRW -124.6 billion in 2025. If recovery of funds placed in trust-account loans lags, the cash burden could persist.

Target price cut by a brokerage

In Shinhan Investment Corp.'s July 2026 report, the target price was lowered from KRW 2,300 to KRW 1,800. The same report lowered its estimate for this year's earnings. In a September 2026 report it said first-half results fell short of expectations and it lowered its full-year operating profit estimate.

10

Risk factors

Trust-account loans and credit losses

The trust-account loan balance before provisions was KRW 970.9 billion at the end of September 2026, down KRW 71.9 billion from June. The provision ratio nevertheless rose from 15.4% to 17.0%. Loans outstanding for more than three years totaled KRW 362.4 billion, or 37.3% of the total. If recovery is delayed, credit losses could again weigh on earnings.

Contingent liabilities and completion obligations

At the end of Q1 2026 the company bore guarantee and completion obligations totaling KRW 1.6859 trillion across 20 sites, with related loan balances of KRW 1.3513 trillion. The company judged the likelihood of damages from unfulfilled completion obligations to be low. However, more sites with weak sales take-up could increase the burden of managing these contingent liabilities.

Capital adequacy and financial soundness

A December 2025 press report said the net operating capital ratio fell for four consecutive years, from 637% in 2020 to 269% in 2024. The debt-to-equity ratio rose from 67.6% in 2022 to 91.8% in 2025.

Korea Ratings said capital adequacy indicators have trended lower since 2023 as trust-account loans and borrowing expanded. How much capital cushion is preserved is the key question.

12

Overall view

Korea Real Estate Investment & Trust recorded an operating loss in 2025, but net income attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 21 billion, a return to profit, and that profit continued through the quarters of 2026.

Operating profit remains small, at KRW 479 million in Q1 2026 and KRW 1.45 billion in Q3 2026, and the KRW 57.3 billion in net income attributable to controlling shareholders over the last four quarters depends heavily on equity-method gains.

Supporting factors include the recovery in redevelopment orders (KRW 324.2 billion of new orders in 2025, the highest in six years), the expansion of REIT assets under management, and the return to profit at affiliates.

Weighing factors include the rise in the provision ratio on trust-account loans, guarantee and completion obligations totaling KRW 1.6859 trillion, and the negative operating cash flow in 2025.

Brokerage views are mixed: Shinhan Investment Corp. cut its target price to KRW 1,800 in July 2026, while Hanwha INVESTMENT&SECURITIES said in September 2026 that it maintained a 'Buy' rating and the same target.

The next checkpoint is the 2026 annual report, which will show how revenue recognition from sales and operating cash flow developed.
