Report overview

Home-interior pivot tested by five quarters of losses

Enex has recorded operating losses in each of the last five quarters, from 2025 Q3 to 2026 Q3, as the housing slump weighs on its B2B kitchen-furniture business while the company expands into total home interiors.

02

Business structure

Enex is a furniture manufacturer and seller whose core business is kitchen furniture and related furniture products. According to FnGuide, the December 2024 sales mix was furniture goods at 87.34%, furniture products at 7.75%, and service rental at 5.02%. Total employees stood at 188 as of September 2025.

Its sales channels are split between B2B kitchen-furniture supply for new apartment projects and direct sales to consumers. In its 2026 Q1 business commentary, B2B orders for new-apartment kitchen furniture were reported to have fallen amid a housing construction slump and weaker domestic consumption.

In April 2025, the company announced a shift from a general furniture business to a total home-interior company. It widened its lineup to windows, flooring and wallpaper through a ventilation-appliance partner and, according to press reports, through KCC Glass and another partner.

A company official said the expansion is being carried out through collaboration without committing the company's own funds.

Competitively, larger furniture companies lead the sector on market power and brand recognition, while one analysis notes that Enex's small market value makes its shares react sharply to positive news.

At a shareholder meeting on 2 November 2026, 46 business purposes were added, which the company attributed to business diversification.

04

Earnings analysis

Enex's annual results moved from operating losses in 2022 and 2023 to an operating profit in 2024, before profits fell again in 2025. Revenue rose from KRW 206 billion in 2022 to KRW 230.6 billion in 2023, a 12.0% increase, and reached KRW 264.1 billion in 2024, up 14.5%, the largest year of 2022 to 2025.

Revenue in 2025 was KRW 215.2 billion, down 18.5% year on year. The operating loss narrowed from KRW 23.5 billion in 2022 to KRW 6.99 billion in 2023, and the company returned to operating profit of KRW 5.1 billion in 2024.

Operating profit fell to KRW 545 million in 2025, down 89.3% year on year, and the operating margin slipped from 1.9% in 2024 to 0.3% in 2025. Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders swung to KRW 3.87 billion in 2024 from a loss, then turned into a KRW 1.32 billion loss in 2025.

Net income including non-controlling interests was a loss of KRW 1.4 billion in 2025, a different figure from the controlling-shareholder measure. Operating cash flow was KRW 4.59 billion in 2024 but returned to -KRW 1.21 billion in 2025. The debt ratio fell from 269.6% in 2023 to 211.3% in 2025.

Quarterly revenue went from KRW 52 billion in 2025 Q3 to KRW 48.1 billion in Q4, down 7.7% from the prior quarter, then KRW 46.7 billion in 2026 Q1 (down 2.9%) and KRW 42.5 billion in Q2 (down 9.0%). Revenue of KRW 45.6 billion in 2026 Q3 was up 7.3% from the prior quarter but down 12.4% year on year.

Operating losses widened from KRW 454 million in 2025 Q3 to KRW 2.18 billion in Q4, KRW 2.87 billion in 2026 Q1 and KRW 3.11 billion in Q2, then narrowed to KRW 1.9 billion in Q3. Net losses attributable to controlling shareholders totaled KRW 11.9 billion over the last four quarters.

Reported causes include the property slump, weaker consumer sentiment, lower B2B kitchen-furniture orders and higher cost burdens.

05

Industry analysis

FnGuide describes how slower housing construction and lower disposable income have shrunk domestic demand and continued to cut sales in the kitchen-furniture industry. Through the first nine months of 2025, Enex's revenue fell 15.8% year on year, operating profit fell 11.7% and net income fell 47.2%.

The company cited the property slump and weaker consumer sentiment as causes of its 2025 deterioration. In 2026 Q1, B2B orders for new-apartment kitchen furniture fell and cost pressure rose, according to WiseReport's company monitor.

From a cycle perspective, the picture is one of new housing supply and domestic consumption weakening at the same time. A late-2024 press analysis argued that expanding direct sales to consumers was a positive response to weakness in the B2B business.

The same analysis judged Enex weaker than larger players on financial stability. Operating losses narrowed in 2026 Q3 from the prior quarter but remained losses, so signs of a sector recovery are not yet clear in the company's numbers.

06

Outlook

The central question for the outlook is whether the total home-interior shift turns into actual sales. Following the April 2025 announcement, the product range is widening into windows, flooring and wallpaper, with partnerships serving as the main channel.

Press coverage noted the company's explanation that partnership-led expansion can proceed without borrowing or new fundraising. At the 2 November 2026 extraordinary shareholder meeting, 46 business purposes were approved for addition, creating a charter basis for new business areas.

At the same meeting, six of nine proposals passed and three were rejected, and Han Jeong-soo and Kim Ki-deok were appointed as inside directors, Kim Hee-young as an independent director, and Lee Ki-ho as auditor. Whether B2B kitchen-furniture orders recover is the largest next checkpoint.

Revenue in 2026 Q3 rose from the prior quarter but fell year on year, so whether the recovery continues will become visible in later quarters. How cost burdens affect the narrowing of operating losses is another point to watch. The 2026 annual report will be the official checkpoint for 2026 Q4 results and full-year operating margin.

Overall view

Enex has recorded operating losses in each of the five quarters from 2025 Q3 to 2026 Q3, as the housing slump and weaker B2B kitchen-furniture orders weigh on results. The starting point of this report is that the company, which returned to operating profit of KRW 5.1 billion in 2024, slipped back into losses in 2025.

Net losses attributable to controlling shareholders totaled KRW 11.9 billion over the last four quarters, and the 2026 Q3 operating loss of KRW 1.9 billion was smaller than in Q2 but larger year on year.
