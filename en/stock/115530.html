02

Business structure

Cnplus is a connector manufacturer founded in 2003 and listed on KOSDAQ in 2011. Its core connector business entered the PDP, ODD and LCD markets and expanded into precision components for IT equipment, displays and home appliances.

The company added a renewable energy business in 2021 through the merger of wind power equipment firm PK Wind Power, covering offshore wind transport and installation as well as solar power plant construction.

A 2023 research report grouped the company into three segments: electronics (connectors), renewable energy (wind and solar) and ICT (home shopping and future education).

The 2025 revenue increase was supported by higher demand for wind turbine transport and installation and by solar plants entering commercial operation. Because plant construction costs are recognized at once, profitability in the renewable segment varies widely from quarter to quarter.

Drawing on transport and installation work at the Southwest Sea offshore wind demonstration complex and in Jeju, the company plans to expand into direct WTIV (wind turbine installation vessel) operations. This is presented as a way to reduce reliance on external vessels and strengthen control over schedule and cost.

Detailed segment revenue shares are not widely disclosed, so changes in the renewable segment are best read as the main driver of overall results.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual revenue fell from KRW 43 billion in 2022 to KRW 36.3 billion in 2023, down 15.8% year on year, and then rose to KRW 47.5 billion in 2024, up 31.0%, and KRW 53.6 billion in 2025, up 12.9%. Revenue has grown for two straight years since the 2023 low, and 2025 was the largest year of the 2022 to 2025 period.

The operating margin moved from 3.2% in 2022 and 2.7% in 2023 to -0.1% in 2024 and recovered to 0.7% in 2025. Operating profit turned to a loss of KRW 36.85 million in 2024 and then to a profit of KRW 368 million in 2025.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders fell from KRW 1.74 billion in 2022 and KRW 392 million in 2023 (down 77.4% year on year) to a net loss of KRW 2.22 billion in 2024 and KRW 2.46 billion in 2025, the second straight loss year.

Net income including non-controlling interests showed the same direction, with net losses of KRW 2.22 billion in 2024 and KRW 2.46 billion in 2025.

According to FnGuide, consolidated revenue for the first nine months of 2025 rose 82.5% year on year while operating results swung to a loss, with costs of plant construction recognized at once cited as a factor.

Quarterly revenue fell 44.7% quarter on quarter to KRW 8.66 billion in the fourth quarter of 2025, then rose for three straight quarters to KRW 17.5 billion, KRW 21.5 billion and KRW 25.4 billion in the third quarter of 2026.

Over the same period, quarterly operating results alternated, with an operating loss of KRW 800 million in the first quarter of 2026, operating profit of KRW 866 million in the second quarter and an operating loss of KRW 240 million in the third quarter.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders over the last four quarters totaled KRW 718 million, so the quarterly picture differs from the annual trend.

The debt ratio climbed from 200.7% in 2022 to 612.1% in 2025, and operating cash flow was KRW -3.44 billion in 2023, KRW 5.34 billion in 2024 and KRW 245 million in 2025.

05

Industry analysis

Offshore wind is seeing transport and installation volumes grow together as turbines get larger. The transport and installation work Cnplus handles is where this demand feeds directly into results.

The Wise Report company profile notes that the enforcement of the Offshore Wind Promotion Act is speeding up large offshore wind projects. Financial News (report dated September 30, 2026) points out that offshore wind schedules and costs depend heavily on securing installation vessels.

A structure that rents vessels externally keeps schedule and cost as variables. The company's push into direct WTIV operations can be read as an effort to ease this bottleneck.

The position of the offshore wind cycle is tied to policy timelines and to construction and completion dates of large projects, which shift from quarter to quarter.

The connector business serves electronics markets including IT equipment, displays and home appliances, and its scale is small compared with large component makers in the same electronics and components sector.

Cnplus's position can be summarized as a small hybrid structure with a connector core and renewable construction as a second pillar. Competitive dynamics differ between the two, so the renewable segment should be compared with specialized offshore wind transport and installation companies.

06

Outlook

The direction the company has presented with confirmed facts centers on the increase in the Yeonggwang Nakwol offshore wind contract and the push into direct WTIV operations.

The contract was raised from KRW 17.6 billion to KRW 28.4 billion, an increase of about KRW 10.8 billion or 61.4% (Financial News, report dated September 30, 2026). The speed at which this increase is reflected in revenue will vary by quarter depending on project progress.

How much of the KRW 25.4 billion third-quarter 2026 revenue came from the renewable segment should be confirmed in the notes to the periodic report. The same report noted an industry view that Cnplus is targeting follow-on large offshore wind orders.

If direct WTIV operations are realized, the argument is that reliance on externally chartered vessels and the risk of project delays could be reduced. However, the investment size and start date of the vessels are matters to be confirmed through later disclosures.

The outlook for the connector segment depends on demand from IT equipment, displays and home appliances. The next points to check are whether revenue extends its three-quarter growth streak into the fourth quarter of 2026 and whether quarterly operating results settle into profitability.

These can be cross-checked against the quarterly notes in the 2026 annual report and the first-quarter 2027 quarterly report.

08

Bull factors

Upsized offshore wind contract

The Yeonggwang Nakwol offshore wind contract was raised from KRW 17.6 billion to KRW 28.4 billion, an increase of about KRW 10.8 billion or 61.4%. The increase equals 59.75% of 2024 consolidated revenue and is cited as a basis for widening the renewable segment's revenue base. This is according to a Financial News report dated September 30, 2026.

Revenue growth and return to profit

Annual revenue for 2025 was KRW 53.6 billion, up 12.9%, and operating profit turned positive at KRW 368 million. Third-quarter 2026 revenue of KRW 25.4 billion rose 62.3% year on year, extending a three-quarter growth run.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders turned to a profit of KRW 1.97 billion in the fourth quarter of 2025.

Plan for direct vessel operations

The company says it plans direct WTIV operations, building on transport and installation experience at the Southwest Sea demonstration complex and in Jeju. Lower reliance on external vessels is argued to improve control over schedule and cost. Its construction capability is cited as a strength for follow-on offshore wind orders.

09

Bear factors

Large swings in quarterly profit

Operating losses were KRW 800 million in the first quarter of 2026 and KRW 240 million in the third quarter, so quarterly profit alternated between losses and profits. Net loss attributable to controlling shareholders widened to KRW 718 million in the third quarter of 2026 compared with a year earlier. Project costs recognized all at once are cited as the cause of quarterly volatility.

High debt ratio and cash flow

The debt ratio rose from 200.7% in 2022 to 612.1% in 2025, and total liabilities of KRW 41.7 billion at the end of 2025 far exceed total equity of KRW 6.82 billion. Operating cash flow was only KRW 245 million in 2025. It had been KRW -3.44 billion in 2023.

Vessel dependence and schedule risk

Industry commentary notes that offshore wind schedules and costs depend heavily on securing installation vessels. Cnplus's renewable revenue carries schedule and cost variables wherever external vessels are used. The timing and investment size of the direct operation plan remain to be confirmed.

10

Risk factors

Policy and project timing

Renewable segment revenue depends on construction and completion schedules of offshore wind projects. Even if supportive policy remains, delays or contract adjustments on individual projects can change quarterly revenue. When and how much of the Yeonggwang Nakwol increase is recognized remains a matter to monitor.

Balance sheet and liquidity

Total equity at the end of 2025 was KRW 6.82 billion, small relative to the debt load. The debt ratio stood at 612.1% and operating cash flow was KRW 245 million in 2025. Working capital needs could rise as projects expand, so changes in the balance sheet should be tracked alongside results.

Disclosure corrections

A corrected annual report for the year ended December 2025 was disclosed on August 21, 2026. The existence of a correction is a reason to watch how financial figures are verified. The audit opinion and notes in the 2026 annual report will be the next items to check.

12

Overall view

Cnplus combines a connector core with offshore wind transport and installation. Annual revenue of KRW 53.6 billion in 2025 and the turn to operating profit underpin the expansion case. Revenue rising for three straight quarters to KRW 25.4 billion in the third quarter of 2026 reflects growth in the renewable segment.

The increase in the Yeonggwang Nakwol contract and the plan for direct WTIV operations are factors that could improve business metrics.

On the other side, net losses attributable to controlling shareholders in both 2024 and 2025, quarterly operating losses in the first and third quarters of 2026, and a debt ratio that reached 612.1% in 2025 are weaknesses on the financial side.

The practice of recognizing project costs all at once keeps quarterly profit volatile. The 2026 annual report and the first-quarter 2027 report are the next points to check, particularly the annual operating margin trend and the direction of the debt ratio.
