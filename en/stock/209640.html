02

Business structure

Yjlink was founded in 2009 as a maker of automation equipment for the surface mount technology (SMT) process and listed on KOSDAQ in October 2024.

Its main products are PCB transfer equipment, PCB tracing equipment (laser marking), SMT back-end equipment (routers and IFCT inspection machines), and automated loading systems and autonomous mobile robots (AMRs).

By product line, other products accounted for 28% of Q2 2026 revenue, loader/unloader 20%, laser marking 16%, buffer 14% and smart factory solutions 6%, according to Butler's data compilation.

Over 90% of revenue comes from direct exports, with Europe and the Americas each above 30%, per Hana Securities' April 2026 report. The company reports 569 overseas customers and is a registered official supplier to major automotive electronics and EMS companies.

It is shifting from individual equipment sales toward full-line supply of the entire SMT line, and a single contract disclosed in March 2026 was worth KRW 4.14 billion. Broker reports cite direct equipment deliveries to Tesla and SpaceX and a supply relationship with Foxconn.

Production sites are in Korea, Vietnam and Mexico, with a plant under construction in Bengaluru, India, and a Thai subsidiary for PCB assembly was established in 2025. In competition, Hana Securities cites the four-continent production footprint as an advantage over European and Singapore-based rivals.

Customer concentration and the timing gap between orders and inspection-based revenue are cited as sources of earnings volatility.

04

Earnings analysis

On a disclosed annual basis, revenue was KRW 48.6 billion in 2023, KRW 48.5 billion in 2024 (no change year on year) and KRW 46.1 billion in 2025 (down 5.1%). Revenue fell for two consecutive years from 2024 to 2025, with 2023 the largest year and 2025 the smallest in the 2023 to 2025 period.

Operating profit was KRW 9.16 billion in 2023, swung to an operating loss of KRW 1.27 billion in 2024, and the loss widened to KRW 3.47 billion in 2025, which continued the losses.

The operating margin was 18.9% in 2023, -2.6% in 2024 and -7.5% in 2025, so the company posted operating losses in two consecutive years from 2024 to 2025.

Controlling-shareholder net income fell from KRW 7.05 billion in 2023 to KRW 394 million in 2024 (down 94.4%) and turned into a controlling-shareholder net loss of KRW 3.1 billion in 2025. Net income including non-controlling interests was a net loss of KRW 3.1 billion in 2025.

Operating cash flow was KRW 8.46 billion in 2023, -KRW 1.02 billion in 2024 and -KRW 4 billion in 2025. The debt-to-equity ratio was 77.6% in 2023, 48.0% in 2024 and 65.8% in 2025; year-end 2025 total equity of KRW 74.5 billion was entirely controlling-shareholder equity, and total liabilities were KRW 49 billion.

Broker Leading Investment & Securities attributed the 2025 weakness to tariff issues after the listing, which led customers to cut equipment investment. By quarter, Q4 2025 revenue of KRW 17.7 billion rose 69.2% from the prior quarter and operating profit of KRW 554 million turned positive.

Q1 2026 revenue of KRW 14.7 billion fell 17.2% from the prior quarter, but operating profit rose 106.5% to KRW 1.14 billion, followed by Q2 (revenue KRW 18.5 billion, operating profit KRW 2.47 billion) and Q3 (revenue KRW 19.5 billion, operating profit KRW 3.62 billion).

The Q3 2026 controlling-shareholder net loss of KRW 3.28 billion followed controlling-shareholder net income of KRW 3.19 billion in Q2, and the reason operating profit and a net loss appeared together is a matter for the item-level detail in the periodic report.

05

Industry analysis

Demand for SMT equipment is rising as downstream industries such as electric vehicles, semiconductors and smartphones grow and factories shift toward smart manufacturing, according to the company's earnings commentary.

Hana Securities says that geopolitical risk is accelerating the relocation of manufacturing customers to India and Mexico, which is expanding demand for local SMT line construction.

On the data-center side, a press report put the 2026 capital spending of four major US cloud and internet companies at USD 700 billion, and server, power and communications equipment are built on precision circuit boards that link to SMT equipment demand.

In terms of cycle position, 2025 was a trough year as tariff issues curbed customer investment, and Leading Investment & Securities expects customer capital spending to resume from 2026.

On competition, Hana Securities cites the company's local production footprint as an advantage over European and Singapore-based rivals that lack it; this is the broker's view.

Because equipment makers see a gap between orders and inspection-based revenue, a growing order backlog does not translate into quarterly revenue immediately.

Outlook

Leading Investment & Securities estimates 2026 revenue at KRW 80.7 billion and operating profit at KRW 8.2 billion, and expects a return to profit.

The company said its order backlog was about KRW 34 billion as of June 2026 and that the backlog is expected to convert into revenue in sequence. A KRW 100 billion revenue target for 2027 is the company's goal and depends on the Indian and Mexican plants entering mass production.

The Bengaluru plant, with monthly capacity of 500 units, is targeted for completion in December 2026, and the Indian unit is slated to become a production company in 2027. In June 2026, the company said mass production at its Thai subsidiary would add an EMS business to its equipment manufacturing.

Most proceeds from the KRW 25 billion convertible bond approved in March 2026 are planned for overseas production sites in India, Thailand and Mexico, with a dilution rate of about 12% on a disclosed basis.

Leading Investment & Securities sees collaboration with a North American robotics company as a possible basis for a medium-term re-rating, and Hana Securities mentions the possibility of supply to Tesla's humanoid robot production.

Whether guidance is met will show up in the Q4 and full-year 2026 results, and the Indian plant completion will be confirmed in disclosures after December 2026.

Bull factors

Operating profit improving in a row

After turning profitable in Q4 2025, operating profit rose for three consecutive quarters to KRW 1.14 billion in Q1 2026, KRW 2.47 billion in Q2 and KRW 3.62 billion in Q3. Revenue over the same period rose to KRW 14.7 billion, KRW 18.5 billion and KRW 19.5 billion, so sales and profit grew together.

The operating margin has moved out of the losses recorded in 2024 (-2.6%) and 2025 (-7.5%) and is recovering. Whether the 12% operating margin guided for 2026 is reached is the item that confirms this strength.

Order backlog and customer diversification

The company said its order backlog was about KRW 34 billion as of June 2026, which it described as the largest in its history. It reported orders from Google and AMD for data-center equipment worth about USD 4.8 million, and about USD 1 million from a domestic semiconductor customer.

In March 2026 it disclosed an SMT full-line supply contract with an automotive parts customer worth KRW 4.14 billion. Orders from both data-center and semiconductor customers point to a more diversified customer base.

Production footprint and robotics value chain link

Once the Indian plant is completed alongside Korea, Vietnam and Mexico, the company would have production in four continents. Hana Securities regards this setup as an advantage that European and Singapore-based rivals lack.

Broker reports also mention a track record of direct equipment deliveries to Tesla and SpaceX as a basis for possible entry into the North American robotics value chain. These are broker and press views, not contract terms the company has confirmed.

Brokers cite reduced customer investment after tariff issues as the reason for the weak results. Net loss amid operating profit

Operating cash flow was negative at -KRW 1.02 billion in 2024 and -KRW 4 billion in 2025. Whether operating profit converts into cash flow and net income is the core of this bear case.

Dilution and financial burden

The KRW 25 billion convertible bond decided in March 2026 carries a dilution rate of about 12% on a disclosed basis. The debt-to-equity ratio rose to 65.8% at the end of 2025 from 48.0% in 2024, with total liabilities of KRW 49 billion.

Credit rating agency e-Credible was reported to have assigned the company a BB- rating in May 2026. In February 2026, an on-market share sale by a related party of the largest shareholder was disclosed.

Risk factors

Customer concentration and order-to-revenue timing

Equipment makers face a timing gap between orders and inspection-based revenue, and industry analysis holds that customer concentration can amplify earnings swings. The order backlog of about KRW 34 billion as of June 2026 does not translate directly into quarterly revenue.

A reduction in capital spending in specific industries such as automotive electronics or data centers could change the order flow. Tracking this risk means reviewing the size and term of each disclosed single contract.

Exchange rate and inventory burden

Over 90% of revenue comes from direct exports, so won-dollar exchange rate moves affect results. Analysts note that inventory build-ups from delayed deliveries can increase earnings volatility. If inventory and receivables grow faster than sales, cash flow comes under pressure. Operating cash flow was negative through 2025, which makes the working capital trend important.

Share price volatility and theme dependence

The 52-week beta is 1.30 and the free float ratio is 55.24% (Wisereport, as of August 21, 2026). In 2026 the stock posted several double-digit daily gains on SpaceX listing expectations and robotics themes. The largest shareholder group holds 43.10%, which limits the tradable share count. Share price moves can widen when theme expectations diverge from confirmed results.

Overall view

Yjlink recorded operating losses in both 2024 and 2025, but operating profit turned positive from Q4 2025 and rose for three consecutive quarters through Q3 2026. Revenue also grew in Q2 and Q3 2026, reaching KRW 19.5 billion in Q3.

The company said its order backlog of about KRW 34 billion as of June 2026 was its largest ever, with data-center and semiconductor equipment orders continuing.

On the other side, the Q3 2026 controlling-shareholder net loss of KRW 3.28 billion, the debt-to-equity ratio of 65.8% at year-end 2025, and negative operating cash flow are the reasons that profit quality and financial capacity need to be reviewed together.

The KRW 25 billion convertible bond was disclosed with a dilution rate of about 12%, and brokers described the earnings recovery and possible entry into the North American robotics value chain positively. Leading Investment & Securities and Hana Securities did not present an investment opinion or target price.

The operating recovery is visible in the numbers, while the direction of net income and the funding structure are points to confirm in the 2026 annual report.
