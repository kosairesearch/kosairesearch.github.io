Report overview

Synthetic Rubber Margins Are the Key Test

Kumho Petro Chemical's operating profit and net profit have declined for three straight years since 2023, and the company swung to an operating loss in the third quarter of 2026. Reverse-lagging costs, narrower spreads and weak downstream demand combined to cause the loss, and the next checkpoint is whether synthetic rubber and phenol derivatives recover in the fourth quarter of 2026.

02

Business structure

Kumho Petro Chemical is a petrochemical company centered on synthetic rubber, with segments in synthetic resins, phenol derivatives, EPDM and TPV, and energy and fine chemicals.

Based on the preliminary third-quarter 2026 results, segment revenue was KRW 782.4 billion for synthetic rubber, KRW 311.5 billion for synthetic resins, KRW 432.1 billion for phenol derivatives, KRW 210.4 billion for EPDM and TPV, and KRW 185.1 billion for other segments including energy and fine chemicals.

Media reports put the synthetic rubber segment at roughly 40% of total revenue. Its core products are SBR and BR for tires and NB latex, a raw material for medical gloves, and synthetic rubber is mainly used in tires, footwear and medical gloves.

An April 2025 report said Malaysian glove makers accounted for about 80% of the company's NB latex exports. The synthetic resins business, centered on ABS and PS, is sensitive to demand from downstream industries such as automotive and home appliances.

Phenol derivatives earnings depend heavily on how fast customer inventories clear and on the spread between product prices and feedstock costs. EPDM and TPV, a specialty synthetic rubber line, stayed profitable in the third quarter of 2026 with operating profit of KRW 24.3 billion.

The other segment includes the energy business, whose profit moves with the wholesale electricity price. The company is shifting its mix away from commodity products toward specialties such as NB latex and SSBR.

04

Earnings analysis

On a disclosed basis, annual revenue fell from KRW 7.98 trillion in 2022 to KRW 6.32 trillion in 2023 (down 20.7% year on year), rebounded to KRW 7.16 trillion in 2024 (up 13.2%), and then eased to KRW 6.92 trillion in 2025 (down 3.4%). Revenue was largest in 2022 and smallest in 2023.

Operating profit fell from KRW 1.15 trillion in 2022 to KRW 359 billion in 2023 (down 68.7%), KRW 272.8 billion in 2024 (down 24.0%) and KRW 271.8 billion in 2025 (down 0.4%), declining for three consecutive years from 2023 through 2025.

The operating margin was 14.4% in 2022, 5.7% in 2023, 3.8% in 2024 and 3.9% in 2025, so the sharp drop came in 2023 and the margin has moved sideways since.

Profit attributable to owners of the parent fell from KRW 1.03 trillion in 2022 to KRW 446.8 billion in 2023 (down 56.4%), KRW 348.6 billion in 2024 (down 22.0%) and KRW 290.9 billion in 2025 (down 16.5%), also declining three years in a row.

Quarterly results swung widely: operating profit was KRW 84.4 billion in the third quarter of 2025, KRW 1.56 billion in the fourth quarter (down 98.2% from the prior quarter), KRW 59.4 billion in the first quarter of 2026, and KRW 339 billion in the second quarter (up 470.7%).

In the third quarter of 2026, revenue was KRW 1.92 trillion (down 15.3% from the prior quarter and up 16.9% from a year earlier), but the company posted an operating loss of KRW 20.8 billion, a swing into loss, and a loss attributable to owners of KRW 22 billion.

The third-quarter loss reflects reverse-lagging, where expensive inventory bought earlier flows into cost as raw material prices fall, along with narrower spreads, customers holding off on purchases and sharper price competition in NB latex.

The operating result also came in below the market forecast of KRW 64.2 billion compiled by Yonhap Infomax. Profit attributable to owners of the parent over the last four quarters, from the fourth quarter of 2025 through the third quarter of 2026, totaled KRW 376 billion.

05

Industry analysis

Pressure on supply in synthetic rubber and petrochemicals has persisted. Analysis published after the company's results pointed to a prolonged global oversupply driven by capacity additions in China in basic chemicals and synthetic rubber.

A review of full-year 2025 results also described the petrochemical industry as a whole being mired in losses. On the feedstock side, a January 2026 report said butadiene prices rose, lifting the synthetic rubber spread from an average of USD 305 in the fourth quarter of 2025 to around USD 600.

By the third quarter of 2026, however, the spread had narrowed again as expensive inventory fed into production. For NB latex, industry estimates projected global demand rising from about 2.06 million tonnes in 2025 to about 2.37 million tonnes in 2026.

Japan's Zeon plans to close 75,000 tonnes of NB latex capacity, which was cited as a supply-side factor. An April 2025 report expected the company's global share to expand as China's glove oversupply eases.

In the third quarter of 2026, however, price competition in NB latex weighed on results, so rising demand and falling prices are pulling in opposite directions. Customer inventory clearance has slowed, leaving phenol derivatives and synthetic resins waiting for downstream demand to recover.

06

Outlook

In its third-quarter 2026 results briefing, the company gave a fourth-quarter outlook. It expects synthetic rubber profitability to improve on higher glove-maker utilization, greater NB latex sales and easing raw material price volatility.

For phenol derivatives, it expects profitability to improve as the reverse-lagging effect from the third quarter fades, while planned maintenance shutdowns and weak demand were flagged as offsets.

It expects EPDM and TPV to see slightly lower profitability on weak demand, and energy to decline modestly on planned maintenance and lower wholesale electricity prices. Some media reports said the fourth quarter as a whole would remain largely weak.

Over the longer term, the company's 2025 vision targets average annual revenue growth of 6% through 2030, led by specialty products. SSBR is described as a synthetic rubber used in high-performance tires, including for electric vehicles, with benefits in wear resistance and fuel efficiency.

A January 2026 report said the company's MDI joint venture plans to invest KRW 140 billion to raise MDI capacity from 610,000 tonnes to 710,000 tonnes, which analysts estimated could add about KRW 250 billion in revenue. The completion timing was not confirmed in the reporting.

The 2026 annual report will show whether full-year synthetic rubber and phenol derivatives results matched the fourth-quarter outlook. How far the operating margin recovers in the fourth-quarter 2026 preliminary results is the next checkpoint.

08

Bull factors

Possible NB latex supply tightening

Industry estimates project global NB latex demand rising from about 2.06 million tonnes in 2025 to about 2.37 million tonnes in 2026. Japan's Zeon plan to close capacity was cited as a further supply reduction. The company said higher NB latex sales should lift synthetic rubber profitability in the fourth quarter. Whether this eases price pressure is the point to watch.

Management outlook for fourth-quarter recovery

Alongside its third-quarter 2026 results, the company projected better profitability in synthetic rubber and phenol derivatives in the fourth quarter. For phenol derivatives, the basis cited was the fading of reverse-lagging effects seen in the third quarter.

The company attributed much of the third-quarter loss to timing effects during a period of falling raw material prices. Whether the fourth-quarter preliminary results match this outlook is the next checkpoint.

Financial buffer and year-to-date profit

At the end of 2025 the debt-to-equity ratio was 35.7% and total equity stood at KRW 6.25 trillion. Operating cash flow in 2025 was KRW 715.3 billion, up from KRW 322.3 billion in 2024.

Preliminary cumulative operating profit for January through September 2026 was KRW 377.6 billion, announced on November 6, 2026, which already exceeds full-year 2025 operating profit of KRW 271.8 billion. The second-quarter operating profit of KRW 339 billion lifted that cumulative figure substantially.

Bear factors

Reverse-lagging and narrower spreads

Synthetic rubber operating profit in the third quarter of 2026 was KRW 2.3 billion, leaving an operating margin of 0.3%. Expensive raw materials fed into production while product prices did not reflect the higher costs, compressing spreads.

The operating loss came in below the market forecast compiled by Yonhap Infomax. Whether the same squeeze persists into the fourth quarter is the central bear-case question.

Widening losses in phenol derivatives

The phenol derivatives segment posted third-quarter 2026 revenue of KRW 432.1 billion and an operating loss of KRW 62.5 billion. The loss widened from KRW 14.4 billion in the third quarter of 2025. Slower inventory clearance at customer industries combined with reverse-lagging to compress spreads. Most of the third-quarter loss came from this segment, which is a bearish factor.

Prolonged oversupply in commodity capacity

Analysts point to a prolonged global oversupply driven by Chinese capacity additions in basic chemicals and synthetic rubber. Reporting on full-year 2025 results described the broader petrochemical sector as mired in losses.

The company did not name this structure as the cause of the third-quarter loss, but the industry cycle position is unfavorable. Segments with higher commodity exposure feel this pressure most directly.

Risk factors

Feedstock and spread volatility

Spreads in synthetic rubber move sharply with butadiene prices. The spread averaged USD 305 in the fourth quarter of 2025, rose to around USD 600 by a January 2026 report, and narrowed in the third quarter through reverse-lagging. If timing gaps between cost and selling prices recur, quarterly results could swing between profit and loss.

Price competition in NB latex

Price competition in the NB latex market was cited as a drag on synthetic rubber profitability in the third quarter of 2026. Rising Chinese glove supply could intensify competition further. Even if demand grows as forecast, volume gains will not translate into profit gains unless selling prices follow. Customer utilization and pricing negotiations are the variables to monitor.

Policy and demand variables

An April 2025 report expected high U.S. tariffs on Chinese surgical gloves to shift demand toward glove makers in Malaysia and Thailand. A change in the tariff landscape could alter NB latex export flows. The energy business is affected by declines in the wholesale electricity price. Policy and demand variables are largely outside the company's control.

Overall view

Kumho Petro Chemical kept its operating margin at 3.9% in 2025 through a specialty strategy centered on synthetic rubber and NB latex, avoiding losses on a full-year basis.

However, the preliminary third-quarter 2026 results showed an operating loss of KRW 20.8 billion, caused by a combination of reverse-lagging, narrower spreads, larger phenol derivatives losses and price competition in NB latex.

Preliminary cumulative operating profit for January through September 2026 of KRW 377.6 billion already exceeds full-year 2025 operating profit, but quarterly volatility is large.

The company expects profitability in synthetic rubber and phenol derivatives to improve in the fourth quarter, and whether that happens is the next checkpoint. Analysis of prolonged global oversupply in commodity capacity remains an unfavorable structural factor for the company.

Rising NB latex demand and Zeon's capacity closure are supportive factors. The balance sheet, with a debt-to-equity ratio of 35.7% and total equity of KRW 6.25 trillion, appears relatively stable.

The current issue for this stock is how far the operating margin recovers in the fourth-quarter 2026 preliminary results and the 2026 annual report.
