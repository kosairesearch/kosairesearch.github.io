Creverse: Quarterly Profit Recovery and Points to Verify

02

Business structure

Creverse was founded in 1998 and listed on KOSDAQ in 2008. In March 2022, Chungdahm Learning absorbed CMS Edu through a merger, creating a company that covers thinking-skills and gifted education alongside language education.

Its businesses include thinking-skills-based language, math and computing education, academy franchising, overseas education, and sales of textbooks and learning materials.

In 2022, tuition accounted for 62% of revenue, online for 17%, textbooks for 15%, franchising for 3% and overseas for 0.2%, according to a 2023 brokerage report. Because tuition was the largest share, revenue depends heavily on student enrollment.

In 2025, domestic sales made up 99.6% of revenue and exports 0.4%, with service sales such as tuition and online learning materials as the main source. The flagship brands are Chungdahm English Academy and the CMS gifted-education brand.

Chungdahm's 'THE OPEN' course combines native-speaker-based English thinking and expression classes with school exam and grammar preparation and drew new demand, while the CMS brand expanded into a toddler thinking program and a high-school math course.

CMS gifted-education enrollment reached 7,943 at the end of September 2026, up 765 from a year earlier. The company is strengthening its metaverse learning platform Bouncy on the basis of its edtech capabilities.

On the competitive side, Creverse faces the shrinking school-age population that affects the whole education sector, and MegaStudyEdu and Daekyo are among the other listed companies classified under the same sector.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual revenue rose from KRW 227.5 billion in 2022 to KRW 233.5 billion in 2023, a 2.6% increase, then fell to KRW 228.2 billion in 2024, down 2.3%, and to KRW 220.9 billion in 2025, down 3.2%, marking two consecutive years of decline after the 2023 peak.

Operating profit went from KRW 25.7 billion in 2022 to KRW 25.2 billion in 2023, down 1.7%, then to KRW 10.3 billion in 2024, down 59.1%, and to KRW 2.24 billion in 2025, down 78.2%, declining for three consecutive years from 2023.

The operating margin fell from 11.3% in 2022 to 10.8% in 2023, 4.5% in 2024 and 1.0% in 2025. Because operating profit fell much faster than revenue, margin compression was the main driver of the earnings decline.

Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders rose 217.8% to KRW 15.3 billion in 2023, then fell to KRW 7.57 billion in 2024 and KRW 6.61 billion in 2025, down for two consecutive years.

In 2025, net profit attributable to controlling shareholders was well above operating profit, which means non-operating items had a meaningful effect on the bottom line, so the core business is better judged on operating profit.

Total liabilities at the end of 2025 were KRW 134.2 billion against total equity of KRW 45.2 billion, and the debt-to-equity ratio improved to 296.9% from 389.9% in 2024. Operating cash flow fell from KRW 35.5 billion in 2022 and KRW 42.5 billion in 2023 to KRW 25.1 billion in 2024 and KRW 20.9 billion in 2025.

Quarterly results moved from operating profit of KRW 204 million in the third quarter of 2025 to an operating loss of KRW 2.33 billion in the fourth quarter, which turned the business into a loss, and then to operating profit of KRW 2.61 billion in the first quarter of 2026, a return to profit.

Operating profit then edged down to KRW 2.39 billion in the second quarter, down 8.3%, and KRW 2.37 billion in the third quarter, down 1.2%, two consecutive quarterly declines, although third-quarter operating profit was up 1,062.5% from a year earlier.

Third-quarter revenue of KRW 56.9 billion rose 1.7% year on year, so cost structure improvement appears to have been the larger factor behind the profit recovery.

Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders swung from KRW 9.21 billion in the third quarter of 2025 to a net loss of KRW 4.81 billion in the fourth quarter, then to KRW 2.1 billion in the first quarter of 2026, KRW 3.29 billion in the second and KRW 650 million in the third, down 80.2% from the prior quarter.

The company said cumulative operating profit for the first three quarters of 2026 reached KRW 7.4 billion, up KRW 2.8 billion from KRW 4.6 billion a year earlier.

05

Industry analysis

The education sector faces a structurally shrinking school-age population, compounded by a saturated private tutoring market, and in 2025 domestic education companies were visibly competing hard to survive. Creverse is responding with brand-level differentiation.

Chungdahm English Academy combined native-speaker-based thinking classes with school exam preparation to capture new demand, and the CMS gifted-education brand extended its age range from toddlers to high-school students. The company views changes in the curriculum and assessment system as an opportunity.

The revised curriculum emphasizes competencies such as explaining one's reasoning with evidence and solving problems, and the company argues that its accumulated literacy and thinking-skills teaching fits this direction. That is the company's own view rather than an external verification.

In cycle terms, demand for education depends more on the school-age population and parents' spending priorities than on the economic cycle. Among peers in the same sector, MegaStudyEdu and Daekyo as listed education service companies can serve as reference points.

Creverse is also trying to widen its revenue sources through online learning materials and franchising, while overseas education was only 0.2% of revenue in 2022.

06

Outlook

The company views 2026 as a year of strengthening growth momentum and expected the results of brand renewal, curriculum innovation and financial restructuring to become visible from the spring semester of 2026, according to a November 2025 report.

In practice, operating profit turned positive in the first quarter of 2026 and edged down in the second and third quarters. In its third-quarter results, the company attributed growth to new demand for the THE OPEN course at Chungdahm and higher retention among existing students.

On January 28, 2026, the board approved a KRW 4 billion exchangeable bond issue, with operating capital as the stated purpose. In the same filing, the company said it was working to restore its financial soundness indicators, including through disposals of fixed assets.

The Mokdong land and building sale for KRW 25 billion was completed as of October 30, 2026. On shareholder returns, the company paid an interim dividend totaling KRW 4.2 billion in July 2026 and cancelled 900,000 treasury shares in the first half.

The company has said it will continue its tax-free dividend policy in light of earnings and its financial position. Whether this policy can be sustained depends on earnings scale and cash flow, so the operating margin and operating cash flow in the next regular report are key points to verify.

Bull factors

Growing enrollment at core brands

The company says Chungdahm's THE OPEN course drew new demand and raised retention among existing students. If enrollment growth continues, it could support the stability of quarterly revenue in a tuition-based model.

Recovery in operating profit

Operating profit turned positive at KRW 2.61 billion in the first quarter of 2026 and stayed in the black through the third quarter. Third-quarter operating profit was up 1,062.5% from a year earlier, and the company said cumulative operating profit rose KRW 2.8 billion year on year.

If the move out of the fourth-quarter 2025 loss continues, it would provide grounds for a recovery in annual earnings capacity.

Balance sheet repair and shareholder returns

The debt-to-equity ratio improved to 296.9% in 2025 from 389.9% in 2024. The sale of Mokdong land and buildings for KRW 25 billion was completed as of October 30, 2026.

The company paid an interim dividend totaling KRW 4.2 billion in 2026, cancelled 900,000 treasury shares and said it will continue its tax-free dividend policy.

Bear factors

Multi-year decline in revenue and profit

Revenue peaked at KRW 233.5 billion in 2023 and then fell for two consecutive years in 2024 and 2025. Operating profit declined for three consecutive years from 2023 to 2025, and the operating margin was 1.0% in 2025.

Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders has also fallen for two years since reaching KRW 15.3 billion in 2023. Whether the earnings decline stems from the shrinking school-age population or from the cost structure cannot be settled from the filings alone.

Volatility in quarterly earnings

Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders swung to a net loss of KRW 4.81 billion in the fourth quarter of 2025, then fell 80.2% from the prior quarter to KRW 650 million in the third quarter of 2026. Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders over the last four quarters totals only KRW 1.23 billion.

Operating profit also declined for two consecutive quarters in the second and third quarters of 2026, so whether the move back to profit is stable needs to be confirmed over more quarters.

Financial burden and liquidity

Total liabilities at the end of 2025 were KRW 134.2 billion, larger than total equity of KRW 45.2 billion. The current ratio was 53.6% at the end of the third quarter of 2025, and in January 2026 the company decided to issue a KRW 4 billion exchangeable bond.

Although the company says it is working to restore its financial soundness indicators, operating cash flow declined from KRW 35.5 billion in 2022 to KRW 20.9 billion in 2025, which remains a burden.

10

Risk factors

Demographics and regulation

The shrinking school-age population is a structural challenge for the whole education sector and directly affects a revenue base built on tuition. Changes to the curriculum and assessment system could alter the fit of existing course content.

The company sees the revised curriculum as an opportunity, but whether new courses lead to enrollment has to be confirmed through registration trends.

Financial and liquidity

Total liabilities of KRW 134.2 billion exceed total equity of KRW 45.2 billion. Cash and cash equivalents were KRW 18.6 billion and the current ratio was 53.6% at the end of the third quarter of 2025.

The company is managing its financial position through an exchangeable bond issue and asset sales, but financial room could narrow if borrowing and interest costs build faster than earnings recover.

Gap between shareholder returns and earnings

The KRW 4.2 billion interim dividend paid in 2026 exceeds the KRW 1.23 billion of net profit attributable to controlling shareholders over the last four quarters. Dividends rely on both earnings and cash flow.

Even if the company keeps its tax-free dividend policy, a sustained period of small earnings could raise questions about dividend capacity.

Overall view

Creverse's revenue and operating profit have declined since the 2023 peak, and the operating margin fell to 1.0% in 2025.

Profit has recovered in 2026, with a return to operating profit in the first quarter and a sharp rise in third-quarter operating profit, but operating profit declined for two consecutive quarters in the second and third quarters.

Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders over the last four quarters totals only KRW 1.23 billion, so the scale and durability of earnings are still at the verification stage.

On the financial side, the debt-to-equity ratio improved to 296.9% and the Mokdong land and building sale has been completed, but total liabilities still exceed total equity.

Shareholder returns continued with the KRW 4.2 billion interim dividend and treasury share cancellation in 2026, and because the dividend is larger than recent earnings, its balance with cash flow needs to be watched.

Strengths include growing enrollment and the earnings recovery, while weaknesses include multi-year declines and volatile quarterly profit. The next points to verify are the 2026 preliminary annual results, the 2026 annual business report and the enrollment trend in the 2027 spring semester.
