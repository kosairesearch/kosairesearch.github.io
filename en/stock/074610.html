Narrowing losses, but listing and legal risks pile up at Enplus

Enplus narrowed its operating loss and returned to a profit attributable to controlling shareholders in 2025, yet three straight quarterly operating losses in 2026 now sit alongside a listing review and a creditor's bankruptcy petition.

02

Business structure

Enplus has been listed on KOSPI since December 2003 as a general machinery manufacturer, and its business divides into specialty vehicles, mainly fire trucks, and secondary battery materials.

The vehicle unit maintains a long-running partnership with a leading Japanese fire-equipment maker and supplies new vehicles to the Public Procurement Service through a business tie-up with a US partner.

The company says it holds the number one share of the Philippine market and exports specialty vehicles to the Philippines and Vietnam. The battery unit uses its heat-dissipation electrode technology to make customized heat-dissipation materials and lithium-ion battery electrodes.

The company expects supply of heat-dissipation materials to large global battery makers in Korea to expand steadily. In 2024, domestic sales accounted for 69.1% of revenue and exports for 30.9%.

That year, thermal gap fillers and related products were 91.3% domestic, while battery electrodes and related goods split 58.0% domestic and 42.0% export. R&D spending in 2024 was about KRW 2.28 billion, or 3.9% of sales.

In scale, Enplus is small next to cell makers LG Energy Solution and Samsung SDI, and next to cathode makers POSCO Future M and Ecopro BM. Demand in both units is tied to orders from large customers and to public procurement and export contracts.

Earnings analysis

Annual revenue rose from KRW 26.9 billion in 2022 to KRW 44.9 billion in 2023 (up 66.9% year on year) and KRW 58.3 billion in 2024 (up 29.9%), then fell to KRW 29.5 billion in 2025 (down 49.4%). Of the 2022–2025 period, 2024 was the largest year and 2022 the smallest.

Operating losses were KRW 13.2 billion in 2022, KRW 23.9 billion in 2023, KRW 22.4 billion in 2024 and KRW 2.83 billion in 2025, four consecutive years of operating losses. Operating margin was -49.0% in 2022, -53.3% in 2023, -38.4% in 2024 and -9.6% in 2025.

The net loss attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 36.3 billion in 2022, KRW 58.1 billion in 2023 and KRW 77.5 billion in 2024, before a turn to a KRW 811 million profit in 2025.

Net income including non-controlling interests was KRW 391 million in 2025, a different figure from the controlling-shareholder result.

The company said a big bath to cut fixed costs such as personnel expenses drove part of the 2025 improvement, alongside higher sales of specialty vehicles and heat-dissipation materials.

Operating cash flow was KRW 7.26 billion in 2022, then -KRW 6.2 billion in 2023, -KRW 5.74 billion in 2024 and -KRW 2.65 billion in 2025.

The debt ratio was 85.7% in 2022, 117.0% in 2023, 1,243.9% in 2024 and 200.5% in 2025; at the end of 2025 total equity stood at KRW 14.2 billion and total liabilities at KRW 28.4 billion.

Quarterly revenue reached KRW 13.1 billion in the fourth quarter of 2025, up 118.6% from the prior quarter, then came in at KRW 6.3 billion, KRW 2.24 billion and KRW 2.77 billion across the first three quarters of 2026.

Operating losses have run for three straight quarters from the first quarter of 2026 through the third, and the net loss attributable to controlling shareholders has persisted for five straight quarters from the third quarter of 2025 through the third quarter of 2026.

A preliminary release on February 26, 2026 showed net income of KRW 7.7 billion and 46% sales growth against a 2024 comparison base of KRW 20.2 billion. The financial statements approved at the March 31, 2026 shareholders' meeting, however, show net income of KRW 391 million.

Industry analysis

Enplus's battery unit is tied to the production plans and investment schedules of battery cell makers. The company says its heat-dissipation business supplying large global battery makers in Korea has reached a steady footing.

Within the same secondary battery sector, LG Energy Solution and Samsung SDI are cell makers, while POSCO Future M and Ecopro BM are large materials companies centered on cathode materials, and both pairs serve as reference points.

L&F, Daejoo Electronic Materials, Chunbo, Enchem and SungEel HiTech are also listed companies in the sector that handle more specialized materials.

The vehicle unit's volumes move with public procurement and overseas orders, and the company says its secured order backlog and additional orders in the second half of the year should support results.

If the company's claim of the number one position in the Philippine specialty vehicle market holds, its export base supports its competitive position.

Results at secondary battery materials companies tend to swing sharply from quarter to quarter with customer capacity expansion and utilization, and Enplus's revenue rose 118.6% from the prior quarter in the fourth quarter of 2025 before declining through 2026.

Where the company sits in the industry cycle is best judged by reading large customers' capacity announcements together with the company's order disclosures. Financing conditions can also affect how quickly it can expand capacity.

Operating losses, however, have continued through the first three quarters of 2026, and quarterly revenue of KRW 6.3 billion, KRW 2.24 billion and KRW 2.77 billion compares with KRW 13.1 billion in the fourth quarter of 2025. Whether the goal is met will show up in full-year operating results in the 2026 annual report.

Bull factors

Expanding heat-dissipation supply

The company says its heat-dissipation business for large global battery makers in Korea has reached a steady footing and that supply should expand steadily. Higher customer volumes could enlarge the battery unit's share of revenue. Whether those volumes actually grow will shape later revenue trends.

Improved profit structure

Operating losses narrowed from KRW 22.4 billion in 2024 to KRW 2.83 billion in 2025, and operating margin improved from -38.4% to -9.6%. In 2025 the company returned to a KRW 811 million profit attributable to controlling shareholders, after a KRW 77.5 billion loss in 2024. The company cites its big bath, which reduced fixed costs, as a factor behind the improvement.

Specialty vehicle backlog and export base

The company says it holds the number one share of the Philippine specialty vehicle market and expects its secured order backlog and second-half orders to support results. In 2024, the vehicle product group was 79.9% domestic and 20.1% export. Export destinations include the Philippines and Vietnam.

Bear factors

Consecutive quarterly losses

Operating losses turned up in the first quarter of 2026 at KRW 638 million and continued in the second and third quarters at KRW 1.16 billion and KRW 731 million. Second-quarter 2026 revenue of KRW 2.24 billion fell 64.4% from the prior quarter.

All of the last four quarters ended with a net loss attributable to controlling shareholders, totaling KRW 12.2 billion.

Financial burden

Total liabilities at the end of 2025 were KRW 28.4 billion, close to twice total equity of KRW 14.2 billion, and the debt ratio stood at 200.5%. Operating cash flow was negative from 2023 through 2025.

A Bloter report said frequent convertible bond issuance built up interest costs and that a creditor placed a KRW 1.7 billion attachment.

Governance and legal proceedings

A notice of possible designation as an unfaithful disclosure company was issued on February 6, 2026. In June 2026 investors in the largest shareholder brought a governance dispute.

On November 3, 2026 a creditor filed a bankruptcy petition, and the company said it would prepare supporting materials and respond through legal procedures. Uncertainty over business continuity remains until the court decides.

Operating losses have continued through the first three quarters of 2026, and the net loss attributable to controlling shareholders over the last four quarters totals KRW 12.2 billion. The debt ratio is 200.5% and total equity at the end of 2025 was KRW 14.2 billion, so financial strain remains.
