02

Business structure

HDC Hyundai Engineering Plastics is a chemical company whose main business is producing and selling engineering plastic materials. The company was listed on the KOSPI on September 25, 2006, and is classified under other chemical product manufacturing.

Its products centre on compounds based on polypropylene (PP) and polyethylene (PE), and it is expanding sales of eco-friendly polyamide (PA) and engineering plastics for electric vehicles.

A 2022 brokerage report citing the semi-annual filing put PO-segment products at 52.9% of sales as of December 2021, and the latest segment mix will be updated in the 2026 annual report. In automotive, the business overview cites rising hybrid demand and development of composites for lightweight car bodies.

Overseas, the Indian subsidiary is the core base; the company said in a 2024 interview that Chennai Plant 1 (33,800 t), Sriperumbudur Plant 2 (19,600 t) and Pune Plant 3 (14,000 t) would together provide 67,400 t of annual capacity.

The plants are planned to focus respectively on PPS and other EV materials, and on ELV-based materials and recycling products. The current overview lists upgrading the high-value, high-performance materials portfolio and full-scale production at the Mexico subsidiary as key initiatives.

Large petrochemical groups such as LG Chem and Lotte Chemical have far larger commodity resin operations, while HDC Hyundai Engineering Plastics is a mid-sized operator focused on customized compounds.

Its 2025 sales of KRW 992.7 billion are smaller than those of the large petrochemical companies, so its position within niche materials markets is the main basis for comparison.

04

Earnings analysis

On the disclosed annual figures, sales went from KRW 1.05 trillion in 2022 to KRW 1.01 trillion in 2023, down 3.6%, then to KRW 990.6 billion in 2024, down 1.7%, before edging up 0.2% to KRW 992.7 billion in 2025. Of the 2022–2025 years, 2022 was the largest by sales and 2024 the smallest.

Operating profit rose from KRW 20.4 billion in 2022 to KRW 33 billion in 2023, up 62.1%, slipped 1.1% to KRW 32.7 billion in 2024, and then rose 52.1% to KRW 49.7 billion in 2025, the strongest recovery in the period.

The operating margin moved from 1.9% in 2022 to 3.3% in 2023, held at 3.3% in 2024, and reached 5.0% in 2025, so margins improved while sales stayed close to KRW 1 trillion.

Attributable net income was KRW 11.5 billion in 2022, KRW 21.3 billion in 2023 (up 85.2%), KRW 18.8 billion in 2024 (down 11.7%) and KRW 33.1 billion in 2025 (up 75.9%).

Net income including non-controlling interests was KRW 30.2 billion in 2025, lower than the attributable figure, and the gap comes from the non-controlling share.

Operating cash flow was KRW 7.95 billion in 2022, KRW 45 billion in 2023, KRW 17.5 billion in 2024 and KRW 18.5 billion in 2025, so cash generation grew more slowly than profit after 2023.

The debt-to-equity ratio fell from 92.5% in 2022 to 87.1% in 2025, with total equity of KRW 353.4 billion and total liabilities of KRW 307.8 billion at year-end 2025. On a quarterly basis, Q3 2025 sales were KRW 247.4 billion with operating profit of KRW 15.3 billion.

Q4 2025 sales fell 5.9% quarter on quarter to KRW 232.7 billion and operating profit fell 38.8% to KRW 9.33 billion. Q2 2026 sales of KRW 301.4 billion were the highest of the last five quarters, yet operating profit was KRW 12.8 billion.

In Q3 2026, sales rose 18.4% year on year to KRW 292.9 billion, but operating profit fell 51.3% to KRW 7.43 billion and the attributable result was a KRW 639 million net loss. Press reports said first-half 2026 sales rose while the operating margin fell from 4.88% to 3.69%.

In Q4 2026, the Ulsan maintenance shutdown could weigh on output, and the company expects the efficiency gains from maintenance to improve overall profitability.

05

Industry analysis

The company's main demand comes from industrial segments such as automotive and home appliances, and in automotive, rising hybrid demand and lightweight body materials are cited as growth drivers. The 2025 first-half improvement was attributed in a financial data summary to rising demand for eco-friendly vehicles.

A 2024 management interview expected demand for high-performance plastics to grow in electric vehicles, hydrogen vehicles and 6G communications, but this is management's outlook rather than a confirmed market-size figure.

ELV-based materials and recycling products are set as part of the company's response to environmental regulation, linking recycled-material demand to its strategy. Petrochemical conditions are sensitive to feedstock and commodity resin prices, which can make profitability volatile.

Large domestic chemical groups have broader portfolios that include commodity resins, battery and display materials, whereas this company is concentrated in engineering plastic compounds and is therefore more exposed to shifts in automotive component demand.

The concentration also makes the path from qualified material approvals to volume and earnings relatively clear. Expansion of overseas plants in India and Mexico aims to build local supply capacity, and utilisation at those sites will track regional industrial demand.

06

Outlook

The first confirmed event on the company's calendar is the Ulsan plant maintenance shutdown, which started on November 9, 2026, with production scheduled to resume on November 26, 2026. The company said efficiency gains from the maintenance should contribute to overall profit improvement.

Overseas, the completion of India's third plant and full-scale production at the Mexico subsidiary are listed as ongoing projects, alongside diversification of the product portfolio in India, where the company aims to secure 70,000 t of annual capacity.

A 2024 management interview said the company aimed to reach KRW 1 trillion in sales for three consecutive years through capacity expansion and diversification, but sales were KRW 990.6 billion in 2024 and KRW 992.7 billion in 2025, both still below that level.

Cumulative sales for January to September 2026 total KRW 849.2 billion, and cumulative operating profit is about KRW 28 billion. Whether Q4 2026 results recover from the maintenance effect will therefore be a key reference for judging the full-year trend.

Whether a larger share of high-value materials translates into a higher operating margin is another point to check in upcoming regular reports. The 2026 annual report will disclose segment sales and overseas subsidiary results, which will show how much the Indian and Mexican operations contribute to group sales.

Bull factors

Profit recovery and margin gains in 2025

Operating profit rose 52.1% to KRW 49.7 billion in 2025. The operating margin climbed from 1.9% in 2022 to 5.0%, and attributable net income reached KRW 33.1 billion, well above KRW 11.5 billion in 2022. Margins improved even while sales stayed near KRW 1 trillion.

Gradual improvement in the balance sheet

The debt-to-equity ratio fell from 92.5% in 2022 to 87.1% in 2025. Total equity stood at KRW 353.4 billion and total liabilities at KRW 307.8 billion at the end of 2025. Capital has built up as profits grew, supporting financial stability.

Overseas capacity and a shift to high-value materials

The Indian subsidiary aims for 67,400 t of annual capacity across Chennai Plant 1, Sriperumbudur Plant 2 and Pune Plant 3. Chennai focuses on EV materials such as PPS, while Sriperumbudur focuses on ELV-based and recycled materials. Full-scale production at the Mexico subsidiary is also listed as a key initiative.

Bear factors

Q3 2026 loss and margin pressure

Sales grew 18.4%, but profit did not keep pace. Press reports said the first-half 2026 operating margin also fell from 4.88% to 3.69%.

Flat sales and a missed KRW 1 trillion target

Sales moved from KRW 1.05 trillion in 2022 to KRW 992.7 billion in 2025, with little net growth. The KRW 1 trillion sales goal cited in a 2024 interview has not yet been reached, with 2024 sales at KRW 990.6 billion and 2025 sales at KRW 992.7 billion. Profit recovery therefore relies heavily on margin improvement given limited top-line growth.

Volatility in attributable net income

Attributable net income went from KRW 21.3 billion in 2023 to KRW 18.8 billion in 2024 and KRW 33.1 billion in 2025, so the path has not been steady. Attributable profit for the latest four quarters totals KRW 19.9 billion, below the full-year 2025 level. Quarterly results also swung, from KRW 5.97 billion in Q4 2025 to KRW 8.96 billion in Q2 2026.

10

Risk factors

Production disruption and maintenance

The Ulsan plant halts production for maintenance from November 9 to November 26, 2026. The disclosure puts its sales at 31.39% of recent company sales, so the shipment gap during the shutdown could weigh on Q4 results.

The company cites process-safety upgrades and efficiency gains, but normal operation after restart remains to be confirmed.

Automotive demand and raw material swings

The business leans heavily on industrial demand, including automotive components. Hybrid and lightweight materials are cited as drivers, but changes in vehicle production feed directly into order volumes. Feedstock and commodity resin price swings are additional variables that can shift margins.

Overseas plant investment and utilisation

The third plant in India was a project with roughly KRW 20 billion of investment according to a 2024 interview, and full-scale production in Mexico is also underway. New plants need utilisation to rise before they absorb fixed costs. If local demand does not fill planned capacity, the investment payoff may reach earnings later than planned.

Overall view

On the disclosed figures, HDC Hyundai Engineering Plastics has kept sales close to KRW 1 trillion since 2022 while lifting its operating margin from 1.9% to 5.0%. Earnings recovered visibly in 2025, with operating profit of KRW 49.7 billion and attributable net income of KRW 33.1 billion.

In Q3 2026, however, sales rose 18.4% year on year while operating profit fell 51.3% to KRW 7.43 billion, and the attributable result turned into a KRW 639 million loss. Operating profit for January to September 2026 totals roughly KRW 28 billion.

The share price sits well below book value per share, and earnings volatility weighs heavily on how the multiples are read. Positive factors include margin gains, a stronger balance sheet and overseas capacity expansion, while negative factors include the Q3 loss, flat sales and the missed KRW 1 trillion goal.

The next checkpoints are the normalisation of operations after the Ulsan plant restarts on November 26, 2026, and the Q4 2026 results in the 2026 annual report.
