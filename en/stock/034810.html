Report overview

Semiconductor Subsidiary Recovery Lifts Holding-Level Profit

Haesung Industrial's quarterly operating profit has risen for three straight quarters on the recovery at its semiconductor parts subsidiary, but the multi-year decline in annual operating profit and the net losses attributable to controlling shareholders over the last four quarters still weigh on the picture.

  1. 1

    Cumulative revenue through the third quarter of 2026 reached KRW 1,917.3 billion and operating profit reached KRW 77.5 billion, up 13% and 137% year on year according to the November 16, 2026 disclosure coverage, matching the sum of the three reported quarters.

  2. 2

    Subsidiary Haesung DS posted cumulative operating profit of KRW 56.9 billion through the third quarter, up 376% year on year, with leadframes and package substrates cited as the drivers.

  3. 3

    Annual operating profit fell for three consecutive years from KRW 210.4 billion in 2022 to KRW 41.8 billion in 2025, and the operating margin declined from 8.3% to 1.9%.

  4. 4

    Net losses attributable to controlling shareholders over the last four quarters total KRW 52.7 billion, and several quarters with rising operating profit still recorded net losses at the shareholder level.

  5. 5

    The debt-to-equity ratio rose to 101.0% at the end of 2025 from 88.6% in 2022, and the company disclosed a corporate value-up plan on March 31, 2026 that keeps its tax-exempt dividend policy and includes treasury share cancellation.

02

Business structure

Haesung Industrial is a holding company that controls several subsidiaries. Since its 2020 conversion into a holding structure, it has consolidated Gyeyang Electric, Haesung DS, the Hankuk Paper group, and Hankuk Package as subsidiaries, according to press coverage.

By segment, revenue in the second quarter of 2026 was 51% paper, 32% semiconductor parts, 12% electrical and automotive components, 4% industrial goods, and 1% property management, based on figures compiled by Butler.

The paper segment is run by the Korean paper subsidiary and a Chinese unit, which together generated the largest share of profit among non-semiconductor operations through the third quarter of 2026. The semiconductor parts segment is led by Haesung DS, whose main products are leadframes and package substrates.

Leadframes are the components that connect and support semiconductor chips, and the company cites rising demand from automotive, AI, and data center chips as a driver of sales. Package substrates grew on higher DRAM demand and increased mass production of DDR5 products, the company said.

According to WiseReport data, 99.70% of leadframe sales in December 2025 were exports, so the customer base depends heavily on overseas semiconductor demand.

The electrical and automotive segment is run by Gyeyang Electric, which supplies traction motors to global automakers, and the industrial goods segment includes power tools.

Property leasing and holding-company activities were once the core, but today the semiconductor parts subsidiary has the largest influence on consolidated results. Because subsidiary results flow directly into consolidated earnings, differences in each subsidiary's cycle amplify swings in total profit.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual revenue fell from KRW 2.53 trillion in 2022 to KRW 2.31 trillion in 2023 (down 8.8% year on year) and KRW 2.22 trillion in 2024 (down 3.8%), then recovered slightly to KRW 2.26 trillion in 2025 (up 1.8%).

Operating profit declined from KRW 210.4 billion in 2022 to KRW 106.3 billion in 2023 (down 49.5%), KRW 71.4 billion in 2024 (down 32.8%), and KRW 41.8 billion in 2025 (down 41.4%), a decline recorded for three consecutive years from 2023 through 2025. The operating margin moved from 8.3% to 4.6%, 3.2%, and 1.9%.

For 2023, the company attributed the drop to one-time costs from the merger of Hankuk Paper and Saeha.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders swung from KRW 25.6 billion in 2022 to a loss of KRW 29.3 billion in 2023, turned to a profit of KRW 5.84 billion in 2024, and returned to a loss of KRW 24 billion in 2025.

Net income including non-controlling interests fell from KRW 106.4 billion in 2022 to a loss of KRW 44.9 billion in 2025. At the end of 2025, total equity was KRW 1.29 trillion, of which equity attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 715.6 billion, and total liabilities were KRW 1.3 trillion.

The debt-to-equity ratio rose from 88.6% in 2022 to 84.2% in 2023, 92.3% in 2024, and 101.0% in 2025. Operating cash flow was KRW 117.5 billion in 2022, KRW 253.1 billion in 2023, KRW 31.6 billion in 2024, and KRW 135.6 billion in 2025.

Quarterly operating profit bottomed at KRW 9.12 billion in the fourth quarter of 2025 and then rose to KRW 18.2 billion in the first quarter of 2026, KRW 27.3 billion in the second, and KRW 32 billion in the third, three consecutive quarterly increases.

Over the same span, net income attributable to controlling shareholders was a loss of KRW 39.7 billion in the fourth quarter of 2025 and a loss of KRW 32.5 billion in the first quarter of 2026, then turned to a profit of KRW 7.44 billion in the second quarter and KRW 12.1 billion in the third.

Third-quarter 2026 revenue was KRW 658.9 billion, up 14.3% year on year. In the first quarter of 2026, operating profit of KRW 18.2 billion coexisted with a shareholder-level net loss of KRW 32.5 billion, the largest gap between operating profit and controlling-shareholder results in the period.

05

Industry analysis

Downstream demand is most visible in semiconductors. Haesung DS posted first-quarter 2026 revenue of KRW 188.7 billion, up 37% year on year, which the company attributed to higher component demand from a recovering chip market.

Leadframe demand comes from automotive, AI, and data center chips, and new production lines are expanding supply. Package substrates are supported by higher DRAM demand and increased mass production of DDR5 products.

Where this demand sits within the broader semiconductor cycle is not established by outside data reviewed here, so the read relies on company statements. The paper segment has maintained operating profit despite weak industry conditions.

According to WiseReport figures as of March 2026, the company's market share was 19.70% in paperboard, 18.70% in printing paper, and 14.00% in carton packaging.

Because subsidiary results flow into consolidated earnings, the difference in cycles between semiconductor parts and paper is a main source of earnings variability. The Chinese specialty-paper subsidiary posted stable results, according to the company's first-quarter 2026 announcement.

Outlook

In its first-quarter 2026 announcement, the company said operating profit would keep improving from the second quarter on the stable business base of its main subsidiaries.

Operating profit rose to KRW 27.3 billion in the second quarter and KRW 32 billion in the third, which confirms that direction in the reported figures.

In its cumulative third-quarter announcement, the company said it would keep pursuing profitability-focused growth by strengthening its core businesses and relying on steady paper-segment earnings. It also said expanded leadframe supply would follow the full start-up of new production lines.

On shareholder returns, the corporate value-up plan disclosed on March 31, 2026 keeps the tax-exempt dividend policy, pursues treasury share cancellation, and targets a higher effective dividend amount than the prior year.

A capital reduction through treasury share cancellation disclosed in February 2026 set a record date of May 4, 2026 and a listing date for new shares of May 20, 2026. The 2025 payout ratio was 25%, and the company said dividend levels will be set considering shareholder value and the business environment.

Two points remain open: whether fourth-quarter 2026 results change the annual trend, and whether controlling-shareholder net income keeps pace with the operating profit gains. The effect of the Haesung DS production lines on quarterly revenue will also become visible in upcoming quarterly disclosures.

Bull factors

Semiconductor subsidiary expands profit contribution

Subsidiary Haesung DS's cumulative revenue through the third quarter of 2026 reached KRW 645.1 billion, up 36% year on year, and its operating profit reached KRW 56.9 billion, up 376%, according to the November 16, 2026 disclosure coverage.

The company cited this subsidiary as the main driver of the consolidated operating profit increase to KRW 77.5 billion. Leadframe supply expanded as automotive, AI, and data center chip demand met new production lines. Package substrates added support from higher DRAM demand and DDR5 mass production.

Three straight quarters of higher operating profit

Operating profit bottomed at KRW 9.12 billion in the fourth quarter of 2025 and then rose to KRW 18.2 billion in the first quarter of 2026, KRW 27.3 billion in the second, and KRW 32 billion in the third, three consecutive quarterly increases. Third-quarter operating profit was up 52.8% year on year.

Third-quarter revenue of KRW 658.9 billion rose 14.3% year on year, so both sales and profit grew. Controlling-shareholder net income turning positive from the second quarter of 2026 also shows a change in direction.

Shareholder return policy becomes more concrete

The corporate value-up plan disclosed on March 31, 2026 keeps the tax-exempt dividend policy, pursues treasury share cancellation, and targets a higher effective dividend amount than the prior year. The 2025 payout ratio was 25%, and the 2025 dividend amount rose 11.1% from the prior year.

The capital reduction disclosed in February 2026 cancels treasury shares held by the company. The company says dividend levels will reflect shareholder value and the business environment.

Bear factors

Accumulated losses and a gap in net income

The 2025 controlling-shareholder net loss was KRW 24 billion, and the net loss including non-controlling interests was KRW 44.9 billion, a larger figure. In the first quarter of 2026, operating profit of KRW 18.2 billion came with a controlling-shareholder net loss of KRW 32.5 billion, a wide gap.

The composition of non-operating items behind this gap needs to be checked in the detailed sections of the annual report.

Annual operating profit down three years running

Operating profit fell from KRW 210.4 billion in 2022 to KRW 106.3 billion in 2023, KRW 71.4 billion in 2024, and KRW 41.8 billion in 2025, a decline for three consecutive years. The operating margin dropped from 8.3% in 2022 to 1.9% in 2025. Revenue rose 1.8% in 2025, but that did not stop the margin decline. The 2025 operating profit was the smallest of the 2022 to 2025 period.

Financial burden and limits of the paper segment

The debt-to-equity ratio rose from 88.6% in 2022 to 101.0% in 2025, and total liabilities of KRW 1.3 trillion at the end of 2025 exceed total equity of KRW 1.29 trillion. The paper segment has held operating profit in a weak industry, and it accounted for 51% of second-quarter 2026 revenue per Butler's compilation.

Paper subsidiaries posted cumulative third-quarter 2026 operating profit of KRW 11 billion for the Korean paper company and KRW 10.8 billion for the Chinese unit, small relative to the semiconductor subsidiary. With large liabilities, swings in subsidiary results constrain financial flexibility.

Risk factors

Concentration in one subsidiary

A large share of consolidated results depends on semiconductor parts subsidiary Haesung DS. Because leadframe exports are a high share of sales, the company is exposed to overseas chip demand and changes in customer volumes. Whether utilization holds after the new production lines start is the key question. A slowdown in the chip market could reduce consolidated operating profit gains quickly.

Paper segment and overseas unit risk

The paper segment accounted for 51% of revenue in the second quarter of 2026 per Butler's compilation and is exposed to weak industry conditions. Results at the Chinese paper unit, Guori, could also shift with the overseas operating environment.

Paper subsidiaries' cumulative third-quarter 2026 operating profit of KRW 11 billion and KRW 10.8 billion is small relative to the semiconductor subsidiary. If weakness in one segment is not offset by growth elsewhere, consolidated earnings volatility increases.

Balance sheet and funding of shareholder returns

The debt-to-equity ratio rose to 101.0% in 2025 from 88.6% in 2022. With accumulated losses, pursuing tax-exempt dividends, treasury share cancellation, and a higher dividend amount together requires cash flow support. Operating cash flow was KRW 135.6 billion in 2025 but KRW 31.6 billion in 2024, a large annual swing.

Even as operating profit recovers, whether controlling-shareholder net income and cash flow move in the same direction remains to be seen.

12

Overall view

The 2026 earnings recovery at Haesung Industrial has been led by growth at its semiconductor parts subsidiary, Haesung DS.

Of the KRW 77.5 billion in cumulative operating profit through the third quarter of 2026, Haesung DS contributed KRW 56.9 billion, so the recovery at the holding level is concentrated in one subsidiary.

On an annual basis, the three-year decline in operating profit from KRW 210.4 billion in 2022 to KRW 41.8 billion in 2025 has not yet been reversed.

The 2025 controlling-shareholder net loss of KRW 24 billion has been followed by a second-quarter 2026 profit, but the four-quarter cumulative loss of KRW 52.7 billion still stands.

Three straight quarterly increases in operating profit and controlling-shareholder profit from the second quarter of 2026 are positive changes. On the other side, the debt-to-equity ratio rose to 101.0%, adding financial burden, and the paper segment has held profit in a weak industry.

Semiconductor demand and shareholder return policy are the strengths, while accumulated losses and the balance sheet are the weaknesses, and both deserve equal weight.

The items to confirm are fourth-quarter results in the 2026 annual report, whether controlling-shareholder profit stays positive, and the timing of treasury share cancellation. This report is provided for information only and does not include an investment opinion or a target price.
