Three Years of Revenue Growth, Margins Stuck Near 3%

GMB Korea's revenue rose for three straight years from 2023 to 2025, yet operating margin stayed in the low 3% range, and the third quarter of 2026 swung to a net loss attributable to controlling shareholders.

02

Business structure

GMB Korea is an auto parts maker headquartered in Changwon, South Korea, producing water pumps for engine cooling, hydraulic control parts for transmissions, joints, bearings and electric water pumps.

Its product lines are grouped into green energy (electrification) parts, engine parts, transmission parts, chassis parts and bearing parts. On an aggregator basis (consolidated, before intercompany eliminations), the green energy parts share rose from 25.62% in 2022 to 33.03% in 2024.

Over the same period, the engine parts share fell from 29.58% to 27.81%, and the chassis parts share fell from 23.99% to 19.99%. Domestic sales made up 46.3% of revenue in 2022 against 53.7% exports, and by year-end 2025 the split was 32.6% domestic and 67.4% exports.

Hyundai Motor and Kia are the main customers, and aggregator company data also lists GM Korea, Mando and Hyundai WIA among buyers. Because vehicle output and model adoption drive revenue, shifts in customers' model mix flow directly into product mix.

A supply contract for North American plants disclosed on February 5, 2026 is valued at KRW 404 billion, or 55.6% of revenue. Listed peers in the same industry include Hyundai Mobis, Hyundai WIA, HL Mando and SNT Motiv. In a 2024 press report, CEO Jeong Se-young said electric vehicle parts sales were around KRW 200 billion.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual revenue rose from KRW 610.5 billion in 2022 to KRW 690.9 billion in 2023 (up 13.2% year on year), KRW 726.7 billion in 2024 (up 5.2%) and KRW 787.3 billion in 2025 (up 8.3%). Revenue grew three years in a row from 2023 through 2025, with 2025 the largest year and 2022 the smallest within the 2022 to 2025 range.

Operating profit climbed from KRW 11.1 billion in 2022 to KRW 20.6 billion in 2023 (up 84.9%), then KRW 22.7 billion in 2024 (up 10.3%) and KRW 24.2 billion in 2025 (up 6.8%). Operating margin read 1.8%, 3.0%, 3.1% and 3.1% over 2022 to 2025, so after the 2023 step-up, sales growth did not lift the margin much further.

Profit attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 11.8 billion in 2022, KRW 11.5 billion in 2023 (down 2.7%), KRW 15.4 billion in 2024 (up 33.3%) and KRW 14.5 billion in 2025 (down 5.4%).

Net profit including non-controlling interests, which differs from the controlling-shareholder figure, was KRW 12.1 billion, KRW 10.8 billion, KRW 14.8 billion and KRW 13.6 billion for 2022 to 2025.

Operating cash flow was KRW 36.8 billion in 2022, KRW 20.9 billion in 2023, KRW 41.5 billion in 2024 and only KRW 7.1 billion in 2025, when total liabilities were KRW 381.2 billion and total equity was KRW 300.7 billion. The debt ratio rose from 105.7% in 2022 to 126.8% in 2025.

In the third quarter of 2025, revenue was KRW 204.1 billion, operating profit KRW 4.43 billion and profit attributable to controlling shareholders KRW 3.15 billion; in the fourth quarter operating profit rose to KRW 8.29 billion (up 87.2% from the prior quarter) and controlling-shareholder profit to KRW 5.6 billion.

Operating profit fell to KRW 3.58 billion in the first quarter of 2026 (down 56.8%), then recovered to KRW 9.93 billion in the second quarter, when revenue of KRW 215.8 billion was the largest among the disclosed quarters and controlling-shareholder profit reached KRW 8.33 billion.

The third quarter of 2026 recorded revenue of KRW 201.7 billion, operating profit of KRW 2.67 billion (down 39.7% from a year earlier) and a net loss attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 8.18 billion, a swing into loss.

Because operating profit stayed positive while the controlling-shareholder result was a loss, non-operating items and taxes are the variables to read alongside the operating line.

05

Industry analysis

Auto parts revenue moves with automakers' output and with which models adopt a given part. GMB Korea produces both combustion-engine parts and electrification parts, so the pace of the shift to electric vehicles changes the weight of both segments at once.

The green energy parts share rising from 25.62% in 2022 to 33.03% in 2024 shows that electrified parts delivery became one pillar of recent growth. By contrast, the shares of engine parts and chassis parts both declined over the same period.

In an aggregator's sector data, the auto parts industry average operating margin sits in negative territory, so the company's margin in the 3% range is above that average. Listed peers in the same industry include Hyundai Mobis, Hyundai WIA, HL Mando, SNT Motiv and Hankook Tire & Technology.

Hyundai Motor and Kia are the main customers, so their overseas plant operations and new model adoption feed directly into earnings. The North American contract equal to 55.6% of revenue shows that overseas production volumes steer the earnings direction. Overseas orders also carry added variables such as exchange rates, local production conditions and tariffs.

Revenue reached KRW 787.3 billion in 2025, yet the margin did not rise, which leaves profitability improvement slow relative to sales growth. Swing into loss in the third quarter of 2026

Operating profit in the same quarter was KRW 2.67 billion, so non-operating items or taxes are the variables to check. The last four quarters total KRW 10 billion.

Cash flow and debt burden

Operating cash flow dropped to KRW 7.1 billion in 2025 from KRW 41.5 billion in 2024. With thin profits, a heavier debt load could narrow room for investment and shareholder payouts.

Risk factors

Customer concentration

Earnings are tied to production volumes and model adoption at Hyundai Motor and Kia. The North American contract equals 55.6% of revenue, so utilization changes at a single production base could weigh heavily. Model discontinuations or production cuts at automakers could reduce allocated volumes.

Electrification and engine parts

Engine parts' share fell from 29.58% in 2022 to 27.81% in 2024. Faster electrification at automakers would reduce demand for combustion-related parts and could intensify competition for electrified orders. An operating margin staying near 3% shows the profit gap that remains as the product mix shifts.

Major shareholder stake and free float

Its largest shareholder, GMB Corporation, held 54.37% as of March 31, 2026. With a high anchor stake, the float available for trading is relatively limited, which can amplify price swings. Changes in major-holder stakes would be a checkpoint for supply and demand.

Overall view

GMB Korea grew revenue from KRW 610.5 billion in 2022 to KRW 787.3 billion in 2025, rising every year from 2023. Operating margin, however, went from 3.0% in 2023 to 3.1% in 2025, essentially flat, so sales growth has not translated into margin gains.

Profit attributable to controlling shareholders fell from KRW 15.4 billion in 2024 to KRW 14.5 billion in 2025, and the third quarter of 2026 turned into a net loss of KRW 8.18 billion. Operating cash flow dropping to KRW 7.1 billion in 2025 and the debt ratio rising to 126.8% are weak points on the financial side.

Strengths include the growing green energy parts share, the KRW 404 billion North American supply contract and a dividend yield above the sector average. Weaknesses include the margin stuck near 3%, the third-quarter 2026 loss, concentration on Hyundai Motor and Kia, and the declining engine parts share.

The largest shareholder's 54.37% stake as of March 31, 2026 is another background factor in reading the share price. The next checkpoints are whether the operating margin exceeds 3% in the 2026 annual report and whether the North American volumes show up in 2027 revenue.
