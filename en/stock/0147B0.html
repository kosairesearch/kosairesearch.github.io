Recurring revenue grows, profit lags

02

Business structure

Lablup is an AI infrastructure operations software company founded in 2015. Its core product, Backend.AI, manages AI accelerator resources such as GPUs and NPUs and covers model training, inference and serving, and token operations on one platform.

Revenue comes from a subscription model in which customers pay annual license fees tied to the number and type of GPUs they run, so sales rise as clients expand their AI infrastructure.

Recurring revenue accounted for 94.7% of total revenue in 2025, and the net revenue retention rate, which measures how much existing customers' spending grew over a year, was 131.9%. According to press reports, revenue for 2025 was KRW 6.718 billion, up 13.3% year on year.

The platform supports more than 11 types of accelerators, including NVIDIA, AMD and Intel GPUs as well as domestic NPUs, and the company positions this multi-vendor capability as its main differentiator.

In a customer case the company cites, GPU utilization at a single site running more than 2,000 GPUs rose from 30% to 85%.

The platform has been deployed at more than 120 sites in Korea and abroad, and under the government's independent AI foundation model project Lablup operates training infrastructure for the Upstage consortium.

The business is expanding from GPU resource management into AI model operations and token production, with Backend.AI:GO for personal PCs, Backend.AI:DOL for cloud use, and Continuum Router, Continuum Hub and Continuum Enterprise forming the newer lineup.

On competition, CEO Shin Jung-kyu has said that many rivals were acquired by large corporations or chipmakers, whereas Lablup reached monetization as an independent company.

03

Recent trends

Lablup listed on KOSDAQ on November 3, 2026, with the IPO price set at the bottom of the indicative range, KRW 19,000. On the first trading day the stock briefly climbed to KRW 28,350 intraday but closed at KRW 14,220, more than 25% below the IPO price.

As of November 19, 2026, the share price of KRW 12,560 is roughly 34% below the IPO price, and market capitalization stands at KRW 182.9 billion. On the disclosed basis, single-quarter revenue for Q3 2026 was KRW 2.24 billion, up 30.8% year on year.

Operating profit for the same quarter was KRW 341 million, down 59.2%, and net income was KRW 363 million, down 39.2%. Single-quarter operating margin was in the mid-teens percent.

According to press reports, cumulative revenue through Q3 reached KRW 6.99 billion, up 48.2%, and cumulative operating profit was KRW 900 million, up about 36%.

The cumulative net loss of KRW 2.4 billion reflects a derivative valuation loss of about KRW 3.49 billion recognized as a financial cost in the first half, when the convertible preferred shares were converted into common stock in May 2026, according to the company.

The company describes this as a one-off accounting factor unrelated to operating activities. Press coverage also reported a return to quarterly net profit in Q3 on a single-quarter basis, although the disclosed year-on-year figure shows a decline.

The company attributes the revenue growth to continued demand from enterprise customers building out and expanding AI infrastructure.

04

Outlook

The company plans to use the proceeds from its listing for research and development and overseas expansion. Of net proceeds of about KRW 42.4 billion, KRW 21.4 billion is earmarked for facilities such as validation infrastructure for heterogeneous AI accelerators, and KRW 21.0 billion for operating funds.

About half of the offering proceeds are slated for purchasing next-generation AI hardware such as NVIDIA Vera Rubin systems and for technology demonstrations, and the equipment will also be used for AI token production.

According to press reports citing the registration statement, the company's 2026 forecast was revenue of KRW 9.9 billion, operating profit of KRW 900 million and a net loss of KRW 2.2 billion.

The core of the medium-term target is revenue from neocloud and new overseas customers, which the company projected at KRW 27.3 billion combined in 2029, or 45.3% of total target revenue. Global neocloud and overseas reseller channels only began in earnest after 2025, so references are still being accumulated.

Under its token factory strategy, the company is broadening its scope, and CEO Shin Jung-kyu expressed expectations that operating profit will increase further in 2027. The company also plans to expand local partnerships in North America, Asia and Europe.

Separately, the company itself acknowledged that market concerns about excess AI infrastructure investment influenced the demand survey and the decision on the IPO price.

The metrics that bear watching are operating profit in 2027, whether neocloud and overseas customer revenue actually materializes, and whether the recurring revenue share holds.

05

Bull factors

Subscription-based recurring revenue

Recurring revenue accounted for 94.7% of sales in 2025, and the net revenue retention rate of 131.9% points to existing customers expanding their spending sharply within a year. Cumulative revenue through Q3 2026 rose 48.2% year on year, so growth held up in the first disclosure after listing.

Because license fees scale with each customer's GPU holdings, expansion of client AI infrastructure translates directly into higher sales.

Efficiency model without owned hardware

The company says it does not own hardware and instead sells software that raises the utilization of existing AI accelerators. In a customer case it cites, GPU utilization rose from 30% to 85% and the GPU budget fell by 40%. Lablup argues that this structure limits direct exposure to swings in AI infrastructure investment.

Token operations expansion and references

Backend.AI has been deployed at more than 120 sites in Korea and abroad, and Lablup runs training infrastructure for a government-backed AI foundation model project.

Continuum Enterprise secured its first customer after commercial launch, while Continuum Router and Continuum Hub extend the offering into token usage and billing management. The company argues that competitiveness in AI infrastructure is shifting from GPU scale toward token production and operating efficiency.

06

Bear factors

Quarterly operating profit decline

Operating profit for Q3 2026 was KRW 341 million, down 59.2% year on year, so profit fell even though revenue rose 30.8%. The single-quarter operating margin was in the mid-teens percent.

On a cumulative basis operating profit rose to KRW 900 million, but within the quarterly trend the decline in profit was steeper than the revenue gain.

Net loss and lower quarterly net income

The cumulative net loss of KRW 2.4 billion mainly reflects a derivative valuation loss of about KRW 3.49 billion booked during the convertible preferred share conversion. The company calls this one-off, but Q3 2026 net income of KRW 363 million was down 39.2% year on year on the disclosed basis.

Whether underlying earnings power holds steady in later quarters, excluding accounting effects, is the key open question.

Weak share price since listing

The IPO price was fixed at the bottom of the indicative range, KRW 19,000, and the first-day close of KRW 14,220 was more than 25% below it. The share price of KRW 12,560 as of November 19, 2026 is roughly 34% lower than the IPO price.

The company cited market concerns over AI infrastructure investment and domestic market volatility as reasons for pricing at the bottom of the range.

07

Risk factors

Macro

Demand for Lablup is tied to enterprise investment cycles in AI infrastructure. CEO Shin Jung-kyu said the company has received many questions about how recent debt concerns around CoreWeave could affect it.

The company positions itself as a buffer against hardware investment volatility, but a prolonged pullback in corporate spending could slow deployment and expansion demand. Broader equity market volatility could also affect share price performance and future capital raising.

Competition and technology

Competition could intensify if GPU makers and cloud providers strengthen their own operating software. Lablup highlights multi-vendor support for more than 11 accelerator types as a differentiator, but that advantage depends on how the hardware ecosystem evolves.

Its token operations products, including Continuum Router, Continuum Hub and Continuum Enterprise, remain at an early stage of commercial deployment.

Business and financial

Revenue in 2025 was about KRW 6.7 billion, a small base in which changes in one or two large customer contracts can have an outsized effect. A significant share of the 2026 target depends on neocloud and new overseas customers, and the overseas reseller channel only began in earnest after 2025.

Whether the convertible preferred share accounting was truly one-off will become clearer through subsequent quarterly results.

08

Overall view

Lablup's Q3 2026 disclosure shows revenue growth and profit decline occurring at the same time. The subscription structure built on recurring revenue and the expansion of spending by existing customers are presented as strengths on the growth side.

On the other hand, single-quarter operating profit fell 59.2% and net income fell 39.2%, so the underlying earnings capacity is not yet clear.

The company describes the cumulative net loss of KRW 2.4 billion as a one-off accounting factor, and whether that explanation holds will be the central question for future profit figures.

As of November 19, 2026, the share price of KRW 12,560 sits below the KRW 19,000 IPO price, and market capitalization is KRW 182.9 billion.

The medium-term target relies heavily on converting neocloud and new overseas customers into actual revenue, so the pace at which references build in those segments is the key variable. Compared with large listed peers in the same sector such as NAVER and Samsung SDS, revenue scale remains small.

This verdict is a qualitative summary for information purposes and does not include a view on the direction of the share price.
