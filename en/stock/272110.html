02

Business structure

KnJ, founded in 2005, is a semiconductor materials and components maker whose core product is CVD SiC (chemical vapor deposition silicon carbide) focus rings, consumable parts used in wafer etching.

According to a KB Securities report from July 2026, the semiconductor components business accounts for 76.6% of revenue and the electronic components business for 23.4%, and the SiC focus ring alone makes up 75.4% of total revenue.

The company ended its display equipment business in September 2024 and reorganized its portfolio around high-performance semiconductor process parts and automotive parts. The overseas entity it kept to serve existing customers after exiting equipment was transferred on August 1, 2026.

The company and the broker both describe the product as more chemically and abrasion resistant than silicon parts, which extends replacement cycles. KnJ has supplied domestic memory makers since 2016, and KB Securities assessed that it was the first domestic aftermarket supplier to commercialize CVD-SiC focus rings.

Production is based in Asan and Dangjin. Sales to Chinese and Taiwanese IDMs and equipment makers rose through the China joint venture formed in 2023, and the export share exceeded 40% in 2025, up from about 27% in 2023 (The Bell, March 2026).

The electronic components business is run by the FPCB subsidiary consolidated at the end of 2025, which makes automotive, display and touch panel FPCBs. Its first-half 2026 revenue was KRW 18.1 billion, and the company targets KRW 40 billion for the year (The Bell, September 2026).

04

Earnings analysis

Revenue on the disclosed annual basis was KRW 62.3 billion in 2022, KRW 61.9 billion in 2023 (down 0.6%), KRW 62.2 billion in 2024 (up 0.5%) and KRW 84.4 billion in 2025 (up 35.7%). Revenue rose two years in a row from 2024 to 2025, with 2025 the largest year and 2023 the smallest of 2022 to 2025.

Operating profit fell from KRW 13.3 billion in 2022 to KRW 12 billion in 2023 (down 9.5%), then rose to KRW 14.1 billion in 2024 (up 17.7%) and KRW 22.2 billion in 2025 (up 57.2%).

The operating margin recovered from 21.3% in 2022 and 19.4% in 2023 to 22.7% in 2024 and 26.3% in 2025, a recovery attributed to rising SiC focus ring volumes alongside a memory upcycle.

Controlling-shareholder net income was KRW 9.95 billion in 2022, KRW 10.8 billion in 2023 (up 8.1%), KRW 10.8 billion in 2024 (up 0.1%) and KRW 25.6 billion in 2025 (up 137.3%). It rose three years in a row from 2023 to 2025, and the 2025 net income growth rate was well above the 57.2% operating profit growth rate.

Quarterly revenue went from KRW 21.9 billion in the third quarter of 2025 to KRW 23.3 billion in the fourth quarter (up 6.8%) and KRW 34.9 billion in the first quarter of 2026 (up 49.4%), a jump that partly reflects the FPCB subsidiary being consolidated from the first quarter.

Second-quarter 2026 revenue was KRW 34.2 billion (down 1.9%), and third-quarter revenue was KRW 39.1 billion (up 14.3%, or up 78.8% year on year). Second-quarter 2026 operating profit fell 14.0% to KRW 6.46 billion, which the company linked to higher manufacturing costs, R&D spending and depreciation from new capacity.

Controlling-shareholder net income dropped to KRW 360 million in that quarter, and the company said it reflected a non-cash valuation loss on convertible bonds, income tax expense and a loss from discontinued overseas operations.

Third-quarter operating profit recovered to KRW 9.95 billion (up 54.2% from the prior quarter), with controlling-shareholder net income of KRW 5.62 billion. Controlling-shareholder net income for the last four quarters, from the fourth quarter of 2025 through the third quarter of 2026, totaled KRW 22.3 billion.

05

Industry analysis

The focus ring is a consumable part in etch tools that concentrates plasma around the wafer edge and protects the wafer, so it must be replaced regularly.

As chip miniaturization advances and etch steps take a larger share of the process, SiC parts that endure high-temperature, high-plasma conditions are becoming a near requirement (The Bell, March 2026).

The company says that memory makers' shift to leading-edge nodes and capacity expansion are increasing the number of etch tools installed, which raises demand for consumable parts (investor relations event, October 2026).

Application in the aftermarket is starting earlier and is spreading from NAND to DRAM, which the company cites as further demand support. The cycle position of the downstream market is tied to memory capital spending, and The Bell attributed much of the 2025 record result to a memory upcycle.

On competitive position, KB Securities assessed that KnJ was the first domestic aftermarket supplier to commercialize CVD-SiC focus rings, and the company has qualified supply with domestic memory makers since 2016. The company points to repeat orders as the basis for an improving 2026.

However, first-half 2026 manufacturing costs cut the operating margin by about 8 percentage points year on year, which shows margin pressure can appear while supply capacity is being added.

06

Outlook

The company expects its semiconductor components revenue in 2026 to grow more than 30% year on year, citing rising volumes from key customers and overseas sales growth (investor relations event, October 2026).

On capacity, new CVD-SiC chambers at the new Asan plant began operating in August 2026 (The Bell, September 2026). The company said that the expanded capacity was stabilizing in the third quarter and that the effect would become full from the fourth quarter (Newspim, reported November 12, 2026).

In May 2026 KnJ acquired a site of about 42,000 square meters in the Pyeongtaek Brain City industrial complex for KRW 43.6 billion. After permitting, it plans to produce CVD-SiC materials, semiconductor process parts and Si materials and parts there, and construction could start as early as the first half of 2027.

For the large-diameter single-crystal Si parts of 500 mm and above, which the company is developing as a new growth axis, two products have passed overseas customer quality verification. The Bell expected revenue from its own Si products to become meaningful from 2027.

For FPCB, the company targets KRW 40 billion of revenue in 2026. In a July 2026 report, KB Securities projected 2026 revenue of KRW 153.2 billion, up 81.4%, and operating profit of KRW 32.3 billion, up 45.1%.

The same report cited the allocation of shared costs after consolidation, a shift in product mix and higher industrial gas prices as factors that would pull the operating margin down. Both the company's guidance and broker forecasts depend on how much of the capacity expansion shows up in fourth-quarter 2026 results.

08

Bull factors

Expanded supply capacity

Quarterly capacity rose about 40%, from roughly KRW 25 billion to roughly KRW 35 billion (Newspim, reported November 12, 2026). The company expects the expansion effect to be fully visible from the fourth quarter.

Repeat orders and aftermarket growth

At an institutional investor event in October 2026, the company said unit sales of one ring drawing rose about 48 to 49% in the 12 months after repeat sales began. It also said aftermarket adoption is coming earlier and spreading from NAND to DRAM. It expects 2026 semiconductor components revenue to grow more than 30%.

Higher overseas mix and product expansion

The export share exceeded 40% in 2025, up from about 27% in 2023 (The Bell, March 2026). Deliveries to Chinese and Taiwanese IDMs and equipment makers grew through the China joint venture formed in 2023.

For large-diameter Si parts, two products have passed overseas customer quality verification and are being pursued as a new growth axis.

09

Bear factors

Quarterly earnings volatility

Controlling-shareholder net income in the second quarter of 2026 was KRW 360 million, down 94.3% from KRW 6.34 billion in the first quarter. The company cited a non-cash convertible bond valuation loss, income tax expense and a loss from discontinued overseas operations.

Manufacturing and R&D costs rose over the same period, cutting the first-half operating margin by about 8 percentage points year on year (The Bell, September 2026).

Rising financial leverage

At the end of 2025, total liabilities of KRW 133.3 billion exceeded total equity of KRW 97.2 billion. Because convertible bond valuation changes flow through net income, they add to swings in quarterly profit.

Demand concentration and cost pressure

The SiC focus ring makes up 75.4% of total revenue (KB Securities, July 2026 report). Volumes can shift with memory makers' investment cycles.

KB Securities saw higher industrial gas prices raising the cost of the CVD-SiC deposition process, and forecast a 2026 full-year operating margin of 21.1% due partly to shared-cost allocation after consolidation.

10

Risk factors

Customer and cycle concentration

A large share of supply goes to domestic memory makers and to customers in China and Taiwan. If memory investment cycles turn, demand for consumable parts could be affected. The Bell's analysis attributed much of the 2025 record result to a memory upcycle.

Capacity execution and costs

Gas, power, labor and depreciation costs rose while new equipment was being readied. This was a factor behind the 14.0% quarterly decline in second-quarter 2026 operating profit. A broker has also flagged higher industrial gas prices as a cost burden.

Balance sheet and dilution

If convertible bonds are converted, the related debt is removed and equity rises through new share issuance. With the debt-to-equity ratio at 137.2%, changes in convertible bond valuation and in equity, such as treasury share cancellation, can affect per-share metrics and reported net income.

12

Overall view

KnJ's revenue is concentrated in one product, the CVD SiC focus ring, which accounts for 75.4% of total revenue. Even so, 2025 revenue of KRW 84.4 billion and operating profit of KRW 22.2 billion were the largest of 2022 to 2025.

Third-quarter 2026 revenue of KRW 39.1 billion rose 78.8% year on year, and quarterly capacity expanded about 40%. The company has pointed to 30% or more growth in component revenue for 2026 and to the expansion taking full effect from the fourth quarter.

On the other side, controlling-shareholder net income fell to KRW 360 million in the second quarter of 2026, and convertible bond valuation losses and cost pressure drove that volatility. The debt-to-equity ratio rose from 101.0% in 2022 to 137.2% in 2025.

The next checkpoints are whether the capacity expansion converts into fourth-quarter 2026 revenue, how the debt ratio and full-year operating margin move in the 2026 annual report, and when construction starts at the Pyeongtaek site.
