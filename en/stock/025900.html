Report overview

Wood-Board Slump and Electrolyte Price War Keep Losses Going

Dongwha Enterprise has posted losses for five straight quarters as its wood-board sales have declined for three years and its electrolyte subsidiary faces intensifying price competition.

02

Business structure

Dongwha Enterprise was founded in 1948 as a specialist in wood boards and chemical materials, and after its 2013 spin-off it remained the surviving company of the wood business.

It widened its chemical operations by acquiring Taeyang Synthetic and a Finnish business in 2017, and entered battery electrolytes through the 2019 acquisition of Dongwha Electrolite.

The company reports two segments, Materials & Chemicals and Housing, and in the 2025 business report the Materials & Chemicals segment accounted for 82.6% of sales. Within that segment, MDF (medium-density fiberboard) held 13.2% and PB boards held 7.8%.

Overseas sales are large, with the southern Vietnam unit at 23.5% and the northern Vietnam unit at 11.7%. Battery electrolytes and related products made up 8.9% in 2025. Press reports said board-related materials sales fell from KRW 731.1 billion in 2022 to KRW 533 billion in 2025.

The consolidated group includes more than ten subsidiaries, among them units in Vietnam, Finland and Malaysia. Board demand is tied to housing completions and construction activity, so domestic construction weakness feeds directly into volumes.

In 2025 the closure of an MDF plant and the sale of an Australian timber unit reduced volumes, and the divested unit's KRW 18.7 billion in sales was classified as discontinued operations. The electrolyte business runs factories in Hungary and the United States, but price competition in the market has intensified.

04

Earnings analysis

On an annual basis, revenue fell from KRW 1.1 trillion in 2022 to KRW 963.2 billion in 2023 (down 12.5% year on year), KRW 918 billion in 2024 (down 4.7%) and KRW 829.6 billion in 2025 (down 9.6%), a three-year decline running from 2023 through 2025. 2022 was the first year revenue crossed KRW 1 trillion, and 2025 was the smallest year of the 2022–2025 span.

Operating profit was KRW 72.4 billion in 2022, swung to an operating loss of KRW 16.6 billion in 2023, returned to a KRW 16.7 billion profit in 2024, and turned into an operating loss of KRW 13.8 billion in 2025. The operating margin slid from 6.6% in 2022 to -1.7% in 2025.

The company attributed the 2025 deterioration to weak construction demand hurting product profitability, a sharp rise in valuation losses on subsidiaries' foreign-currency borrowings from interest-rate moves and a stronger euro, and discontinued-operations accounting from the sale of a subsidiary stake.

The last two items can be read as partly one-off in nature. Profit attributable to controlling shareholders was a loss of KRW 84.5 billion in 2023, KRW 12.3 billion in 2024 and KRW 45.8 billion in 2025, so losses ran for three consecutive years. Net loss including non-controlling interests was KRW 55.9 billion in 2025.

Operating cash flow fell from KRW 97.2 billion in 2024 to KRW 1.54 billion in 2025. Quarterly, the operating loss was KRW 2.93 billion in 3Q 2025, KRW 4.07 billion in 4Q 2025 and KRW 4.88 billion in 1Q 2026, narrowed to KRW 865 million in 2Q 2026, and widened again to KRW 5.32 billion in 3Q 2026.

Revenue in 3Q 2026 was KRW 199.7 billion, down 0.6% year on year. The last four quarters together show a loss attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 74.7 billion.

Over the same period, the battery electrolyte subsidiary reported 3Q 2026 revenue of KRW 33.4 billion and an operating loss of KRW 7 billion in a quarterly report filed on November 16, 2026, and cited intensifying price competition.

05

Industry analysis

Demand for wood boards is being pulled down by the construction slump and a softer global economy. In 2025 the decline in housing completions widened, which reduced board sales further.

In chemicals, battery electrolytes are linked to rising demand from electric vehicles and energy storage systems, and the company has expanded factories in Hungary and the United States to serve global customers.

Some analysis, however, points to slower EV market growth and higher raw-material costs weighing on chemicals profitability, and the subsidiary says price competition in the market has delayed its earnings recovery.

A 2025 brokerage report noted that slow customer wins delayed the start of the US electrolyte plant, and argued that tariff avoidance and incentives are raising the case for local US production. The segments sit at different points in their cycles.

Boards face a falling housing-completion phase, while electrolytes combine demand growth with falling prices. On competition, the high share of Vietnam sales means Dongwha is exposed to overseas board demand as well as to domestic construction.

06

Outlook

The next confirmation point is the 2026 annual business report, which will cover 4Q 2026 results. With losses in five straight quarters from 3Q 2025 through 3Q 2026, the first item to check is how the size of the 4Q 2026 operating loss fits with the 1Q–3Q 2026 trend.

Revenue reached KRW 233.5 billion in 2Q 2026, the highest quarter of the period, before falling to KRW 199.7 billion in 3Q 2026. For wood boards, the pace of sales recovery depends on whether construction weakness and falling housing completions persist.

For electrolytes, the subsidiary's KRW 7 billion operating loss in 3Q 2026 was attributed to price competition, so price stability is the key variable. Whether customer wins at the Hungary and US electrolyte plants raise utilization is also a point to verify, since higher utilization would spread fixed costs.

With total liabilities of KRW 1.39 trillion at end-2025, the effect of interest-rate and currency moves on interest expense and foreign-currency valuation results also needs tracking. The 2026 annual report and the 1Q 2027 quarterly report will provide the disclosures for these questions.

08

Bull factors

Room to raise utilization at overseas electrolyte plants

The company has built electrolyte plants in Hungary and the United States and expanded them to serve global customers. A 2025 brokerage report argued that local US production is becoming more necessary for tariff avoidance and incentives.

If customer wins lift utilization, fixed costs could be spread over more volume, which is the core of this argument.

Loss narrowing and revenue recovery in 2Q 2026

The operating loss narrowed to KRW 865 million in 2Q 2026 from KRW 4.88 billion in 1Q 2026. Revenue in the same quarter rose 7.1% from the prior quarter to KRW 233.5 billion. The loss widened again in 3Q, but the quarterly trend has not locked into a single direction, which is the basis for the bullish reading.

An overseas sales base centered on Vietnam

In the 2025 business report, the southern Vietnam unit accounted for 23.5% of sales, the largest single share, and the northern unit for 11.7%. Partial exposure to demand outside domestic housing can be seen as a strength. Overseas revenue is still exposed to currency translation and local pricing, however.

09

Bear factors

Five straight loss quarters and a wider 3Q 2026 loss

The company recorded operating losses and losses attributable to controlling shareholders in each of five straight quarters from 3Q 2025 through 3Q 2026. The 3Q 2026 operating loss of KRW 5.32 billion and the KRW 42.2 billion loss attributable to controlling shareholders were both larger than in the prior quarter.

Over the last four quarters the cumulative loss attributable to controlling shareholders reached KRW 74.7 billion, and no earnings recovery point has yet appeared in the reported results.

Three years of falling revenue and a weaker core business

Revenue declined from KRW 1.1 trillion in 2022 to KRW 829.6 billion in 2025. Press coverage named the drop in board-related materials sales as the main cause of the revenue slump.

In 2025 the closure of an MDF plant and the sale of an Australian unit coincided, and revenue rebounded in 2Q 2026 before falling again in 3Q.

Balance-sheet strain and electrolyte price competition

Total liabilities were KRW 1.39 trillion at end-2025, and the debt ratio of 157.9% is higher than in 2022. Operating cash flow fell sharply from KRW 97.2 billion in 2024 to KRW 1.54 billion in 2025.

On top of this, the electrolyte subsidiary posted a KRW 7 billion operating loss in 3Q 2026 while facing price competition.

10

Risk factors

Overseas board demand in Vietnam

The southern and northern Vietnam units account for 23.5% and 11.7% of sales respectively, making them key sources of revenue outside the domestic construction market. If local board demand slows or selling prices fall, overseas unit revenue could decline.

If domestic board demand is also weak and the overseas units cannot offset it, the group-wide revenue decline could persist. Revenue by overseas unit can be checked in the 2026 annual business report.

Exposure to foreign-currency borrowing, rates and FX

In 2025, interest-rate moves and a stronger euro sharply increased valuation losses on subsidiaries' foreign-currency borrowings, widening the net loss. With the debt ratio at 157.9% at end-2025, currency and rate moves could again affect the bottom line. The number of overseas subsidiaries also widens the range of currency exposure.

Electrolyte plant utilization

The company is working to raise utilization at its Hungary and US electrolyte plants, which were expanded to meet demand from global customers. A 2025 brokerage report noted that slow customer wins delayed the start of the US plant.

If customer wins take longer than expected, fixed-cost pressure could persist and delay a narrowing of losses in the electrolyte business. Changes in customer wins and utilization, to be disclosed in the first half of 2027, will show whether this risk is materializing.

12

Overall view

Dongwha Enterprise is a company whose revenue, which exceeded KRW 1 trillion in 2022, has fallen for three straight years to KRW 829.6 billion in 2025. Its earnings lean heavily on wood-board and chemical operations, and the board business is tied to construction activity and housing completions.

Operating profit returned to the black in 2024, but the company slipped back into an operating loss of KRW 13.8 billion in 2025, and losses have continued for five straight quarters through 3Q 2026.

The 2Q 2026 operating loss narrowed to KRW 865 million, which marked a change in the quarterly trend, but the loss widened again in 3Q.

Overseas electrolyte plants and the Vietnam board business are cited as growth drivers, while price competition in electrolytes, the subsidiary's losses and foreign-currency borrowing costs are the main negatives.

A price-to-book ratio well below 1x can be read as a cushion on the asset side, but no earnings-based metric is currently calculable. The next checkpoints are the 2026 annual business report and the 1Q 2027 quarterly report, where the direction of operating losses and electrolyte price competition can be confirmed.
