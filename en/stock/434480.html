Monitorapp: Cloud Shift Tested by Renewed Losses

Monitorapp is moving revenue from web-firewall appliances toward cloud subscriptions. After returning to an annual operating profit in 2025, it posted an operating loss again in 3Q 2026, so margin recovery is the next test.

02

Business structure

Founded in 2005, Monitorapp is a B2B security company that sells web application firewall (WAF)-centred appliances alongside SECaaS (security as a service) delivered by cloud subscription.

The company says it remains the clear leader in the domestic web firewall market, a claim made by management and not independently verified.

Based on 2024 consolidated revenue of KRW 14.9 billion, product sales made up 46.31%, cloud security 30.91%, maintenance 13.42%, managed services 8.64% and other revenue 0.72%; these splits are 2024 figures. In a May 2025 interview, the company said appliance-based solutions generated 80 to 90% of total revenue.

The cloud security share rose from 9.4% in 2020 to 11.2% in 2021 and 15.9% in 2022, and reached 32.6% in the first quarter of 2024 according to Mirae Asset Securities research. Customers cited include Hyundai Motor, Hyundai Card, LG Electronics and KT.

The product line now includes four AIONCLOUD services: WAAP, WSPC, SSE and ZTNA. On competition, several security firms entered the web firewall market and later exited, and management stresses its long fight against foreign vendors.

Exports rose from 4.5% of revenue in 2021 to 7% in the first quarter of 2024, and the company has subsidiaries in the United States and Japan.

04

Earnings analysis

On a disclosed basis, annual revenue went from KRW 14.1 billion in 2022 to KRW 14.2 billion in 2023 (up 0.4%), KRW 14.9 billion in 2024 (up 5.1%) and KRW 19.2 billion in 2025 (up 28.6%). Revenue rose for three consecutive years from 2023 through 2025, and 2025 was the largest year of 2022 to 2025.

Operating profit moved from KRW 1.14 billion in 2022 to KRW 7.2 million in 2023 (down 99.4%), an operating loss of KRW 644 million in 2024 (a swing into loss), and operating profit of KRW 943 million in 2025 (a swing into profit).

Operating margin was 8.0%, 0.1%, -4.3% and 4.9% across the same years, so sales growth and margin improvement did not move together. Management attributed the 2023 shortfall to large projects slipping into the following year or failing to win orders, plus new hires, stock options and listing costs after the IPO.

The 2024 loss was tied by the CEO to heavier investment. Earnings attributable to controlling shareholders were KRW 719 million in 2022, a loss of KRW 1.37 billion in 2023, KRW 2.08 billion in 2024 and KRW 665 million in 2025 (down 68.0%).

Net income including non-controlling interests was KRW 669 million in 2025, close to but not the same as the controlling-shareholder figure. Operating cash flow fell to KRW 861 million in 2025 from KRW 2.75 billion in 2024.

Analysts at Mirae Asset Securities noted that from 2023 to 2024 appliance revenue declined by about the amount that cloud revenue grew.

Quarterly results were uneven in 2026: 1Q revenue of KRW 3.64 billion with an operating loss of KRW 710 million, 2Q revenue of KRW 5.63 billion with operating profit of KRW 427 million, and 3Q revenue of KRW 4.54 billion with an operating loss of KRW 51.79 million.

Controlling-shareholder net income over the latest four quarters totaled KRW 1 billion. In June 2026 Mirae Asset Securities reported that 1Q 2026 employee salary spending rose 14% year on year to KRW 2.2 billion, equal to 61% of revenue, and identified labor cost as the main drag on margins.

In the 2026 half-year report, first-half revenue was about KRW 9.27 billion and the operating loss about KRW 280 million.

05

Industry analysis

The web firewall market has seen repeated entry and exit. The company says only it and one other vendor, referred to as F, have survived in that market. The industry's centre of gravity is shifting toward cloud and zero trust.

Securities research has argued that generative AI adoption is accelerating cloud infrastructure migration among Korean enterprises and expanding the cloud security market.

In zero trust, the company was selected as the lead operator of a Korea Internet and Security Agency (KISA) zero trust adoption pilot, applying an architecture built on its AIONCLOUD SSE platform in a live environment. The design was presented as aligned with the NIST 800-207 zero trust guideline.

Generative AI security is another demand driver: the company's GenAI Security product analyses conversation context in real time against 30 predefined topics, covering personal data leaks, source code leaks and prompt attacks.

On foreign competition, the company's CEO argued that domestic vendors need a coalition model that combines complementary technologies. Analyst views are mixed.

Mirae Asset Securities said in a 2024 report that slowing economic growth and delayed adoption of generative AI solutions were visible, and that the ability of cloud-based security to restore earnings still needed to be watched.

Another analysis holds that cloud revenue carries lower fixed costs than appliance revenue, which could help margins. Within the software sector, operating margin is above the industry average, while market capitalisation and revenue scale are below it.

06

Outlook

The company has extended its business from network security into endpoint security through its acquisition of Somma, an EDR company founded in 2016. The CEO set the top priority as bringing Somma's EDR into the AIONCLOUD platform and supplying it to the market in the first half of 2026.

Figures isolating the effect of that integration have not yet been disclosed. The company filed its merger completion report on July 21, 2026. On the product side, it launched version 6.0 of its AILabs threat intelligence platform on November 4, 2026, adding Pivot Search and an AI Bot for conversational queries.

At FISCON 2026, held on November 12, 2026, the company showed how its WAAP can be combined with CDR file sanitisation to address API attacks and malicious documents in financial institutions.

In the SME cloud programme, four AIONCLOUD services are on the supplier list, and eligible SMEs can receive up to 75% of cloud subscription fees.

For global expansion, the CEO said the company has built physical points of presence in eight core locations, including Korea, Japan and Singapore, and called its fully subscription-based SaaS platform a long-term growth engine.

On governance, the number of unregistered executives fell from five at the end of 2024 to one at the end of September 2025, and as of January 2026 the new appointees had not yet been reflected in quarterly filings.

Whether the Somma integration, the global points of presence and rising subscription share show up in quarterly revenue from 2026 onward is the key thing to watch.

08

Bull factors

Subscription mix rising and revenue rebounding

The cloud security share was 30.91% in 2024, continuing a climb from 9.4% in 2020. Revenue growth of 28.6% in 2025 moved in the same direction as the rising subscription mix, which is the core bullish argument. It suggests the revenue stall of 2023 to 2024 has been left behind.

Government programmes and zero trust demand

Monitorapp was selected as a supplier for the 2026 AI voucher programme and the 2026 data voucher programme. It is also building track record as lead operator of a zero trust adoption pilot on its SSE platform. Its reach into public-sector and SME demand is widening.

Profit reversals and a profitable 2Q 2026

Operating profit of KRW 943 million in 2025 reversed the 2024 operating loss. Operating profit of KRW 427 million in 2Q 2026 came after a 1Q 2026 operating loss of KRW 710 million, showing the business is not locked into losses. The first-half operating loss stands at about KRW 280 million.

09

Bear factors

3Q 2026 loss and falling sales

Revenue for 3Q 2026 was KRW 4.54 billion, down 11.1% year on year, and the quarter produced an operating loss of KRW 51.79 million. A year earlier the same quarter had an operating profit, so the swing into loss is clear. Controlling-shareholder net loss in 3Q 2026 was KRW 334 million.

Labour cost burden and fixed-cost structure

Employee salary spending in 1Q 2026 was KRW 2.2 billion, up 14% year on year, equal to 61% of revenue. Analysts attribute this to investment in security specialists, which is weighing on near-term results. If salary costs stay fixed while revenue grows slowly, margin improvement becomes harder.

Gap to past targets and earnings volatility

The 2024 revenue target of KRW 29.4 billion was set against actual sales of roughly half that level. Commentary also expected 2025 results to fall well short of the KRW 47.4 billion target. Controlling-shareholder net income fell 68.0% in 2025, so earnings volatility remains high.

10

Risk factors

Earnings volatility

Operating profit went from KRW 1.14 billion in 2022 to KRW 7.2 million in 2023 and an operating loss in 2024, returned to profit in 2025, and slipped back into loss in 3Q 2026. Quarterly results alternated, from a 1Q 2026 operating loss of KRW 710 million to a 2Q operating profit of KRW 427 million. A thin earnings base means quarterly swings remain large.

Competition and foreign vendors

As management notes, several domestic security companies entered the web firewall market and later exited, and the company has competed with foreign vendors for a long time. If the shift to cloud subscriptions lags competitors, the benefit may be limited. A stronger push by foreign vendors would weigh on the pace of SSE platform expansion.

Reorganisation and M&A integration

The acquisition of Somma, an EDR company founded in 2016, coincided with the departure of co-founder Ahn Byung-gyu as an executive vice president. The number of unregistered executives also fell sharply. Progress on the integration target is not visible in disclosed figures. The effect of the leadership changes on development pace and sales organisation is uncertain.

12

Overall view

Monitorapp is a domestic web firewall vendor in the middle of shifting from appliances toward cloud subscriptions. Revenue reached KRW 19.2 billion in 2025 and has risen for three consecutive years, and the cloud security share climbed to 30.91% in 2024.

Operating profit recovered from KRW 7.2 million in 2023 and an operating loss of KRW 644 million in 2024 to KRW 943 million in 2025, but the company posted an operating loss of KRW 51.79 million in 3Q 2026.

Bullish factors include the rising subscription mix, government programme supplier status and participation in a zero trust pilot. Bearish factors include the return to quarterly losses, labour cost pressure and the gap to past revenue targets.

The first-half 2026 operating loss was about KRW 280 million, and controlling-shareholder net income over the latest four quarters totalled KRW 1 billion, so the earnings base is thin. The next checkpoints are the effect of the Somma EDR integration and the operating margin in the 2026 annual report. This report is for information only and contains no investment opinion.
