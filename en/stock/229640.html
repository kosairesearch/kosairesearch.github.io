Report overview

Cable-led profit growth; third-quarter margins are the test

LS Eco Energy has grown operating profit for three straight years since 2023, led by its Vietnamese cable subsidiary, and its nine-month 2026 revenue already exceeds full-year 2025 revenue. However, operating profit fell in the third quarter of 2026 even though revenue held roughly flat, so margins are the next item to watch.

02

Business structure

LS Eco Energy is a holding company in the LS group, and most of its earnings come from its Vietnamese production subsidiaries.

Based on an estimate compiled from a February 2026 broker report using 2025 preliminary figures, pre-elimination subsidiary revenue consisted of LS-VINA at KRW 989.0 billion and the Vietnamese subsidiary LSCV at KRW 268.9 billion, with the holding-company segment at about KRW 15.1 billion.

LS-VINA was reported in April 2026 to have reconfirmed its leading position in Vietnam's high-voltage cable market. Its core high-voltage cable line is moving from 220kV to 400kV-class capability, and a 400kV-class product recently completed PQ testing according to an August 2026 report.

Busway revenue tripled on AI data center demand, according to the company's August 2026 announcement. Distribution cable and copper-processed materials are also driving volume growth, according to SK Securities in October 2026.

Key customers include Vingroup's Hai Phong new-city power grid project (awarded April 2026) and a power grid project for Viettel's AI data center (participation announced May 2026). In North America, the company has expanded its UL-certified product range and secured eligibility to supply 230kV high-voltage cable.

Its new-business rare earth metals effort with Australia's Lynas includes a mutual investment structure announced in July 2026, under which Lynas invests directly in the company.

The largest shareholder is LS Cable & System's group partners with a 64.37% stake as of October 19, 2026, and the National Pension Service holds 6.06%.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual revenue fell 10.7% from KRW 818.5 billion in 2022 to KRW 731.1 billion in 2023, then rose to KRW 869 billion in 2024 (up 18.9%) and KRW 960.1 billion in 2025 (up 10.5%), increasing for two consecutive years from 2024 to 2025.

Operating profit rose from KRW 27.5 billion in 2022 to KRW 29.5 billion in 2023 (up 7.4%), KRW 44.8 billion in 2024 (up 51.8%) and KRW 66.8 billion in 2025 (up 49.2%), increasing for three consecutive years from 2023 to 2025.

The operating margin climbed from 3.4% in 2022 to 4.0%, 5.2% and 7.0% in 2025, improving faster than revenue.

Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders moved from a loss of KRW 1.92 billion in 2022 to a profit of KRW 4.13 billion in 2023, a turnaround to profit, then rose to KRW 31.2 billion in 2024 (up 656.4%) and KRW 42.1 billion in 2025 (up 34.7%).

Net income including non-controlling interests was KRW 48.5 billion in 2025, higher than the controlling-shareholder figure.

At the end of 2025, total equity was KRW 225.8 billion, of which KRW 209.2 billion belonged to controlling shareholders, and total liabilities were KRW 265.6 billion, lowering the debt ratio from 220.6% in 2022 to 117.6% in 2025.

Quarterly, revenue of KRW 248.7 billion in the fourth quarter of 2025 rose 6.8% from the prior quarter while operating profit of KRW 13.1 billion fell 11.4%.

Revenue then rose 14.8% to KRW 285.5 billion in the first quarter of 2026 and operating profit rose 53.7% to KRW 20.1 billion, followed by second-quarter revenue of KRW 350.3 billion and operating profit of KRW 25.3 billion, the highest of the disclosed quarters.

Third-quarter 2026 revenue of KRW 351.5 billion rose only 0.4% from the prior quarter, while operating profit of KRW 22.2 billion fell 12.2% and the margin was 6.3%.

Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 5.01 billion in the fourth quarter of 2025, KRW 12.5 billion, KRW 17 billion and KRW 12.4 billion in the first three quarters of 2026, totaling KRW 46.8 billion over the last four quarters. Revenue for the first three quarters of 2026, at KRW 987.3 billion, already exceeds full-year 2025 revenue.

05

Industry analysis

Power infrastructure demand underpins the industry backdrop. The company cited global grid investment, AI data center build-outs and the shift to renewable energy as drivers of its first-half 2026 results.

On raw materials, rising copper prices lifted materials revenue, but profitability gains are limited when the product mix shifts away from high-value items. SK Securities said in October 2026 that it lowered its target price to reflect a decline in valuation multiples across the power equipment sector.

On rare earths, press reports say the United States plans to restrict Chinese-sourced rare earth materials from 2027, which creates demand for non-Chinese supply chains.

Competitively, LS-VINA reconfirmed its leading position in Vietnam's high-voltage cable market and secured 230kV supply eligibility in the United States to widen its entry into North America.

Competition with global cable makers in high-voltage segments may shift with pricing and delivery terms, so share changes are worth tracking quarter by quarter.

The situation reads as overlapping grid replacement and new capacity demand, though public disclosures are still limited for judging whether the cycle has peaked.

06

Outlook

In its first-half 2026 results announcement in August 2026, the company said it would expand its push into high-value markets such as Europe and North America.

Commercialization of 400kV-class high-voltage cable began in May 2026, and IBK Securities expects capacity to expand from the current level of about KRW 30 billion to KRW 80 billion to KRW 100 billion, with mass production of high-value medium-voltage products from the second half of 2027.

As of a September 2026 announcement, the rare earth project invests KRW 11.6 billion in Vietnam to build a samarium production system of 240 tons a year, with trial operation planned for December.

IBK Securities assumes samarium production of about 240 tons starts in 2027 and a system of about 2,500 tons runs in 2028, and expects annual revenue of about KRW 300 billion and an initial operating margin of 7% to 8% at full operation.

A February 2026 broker report said the rare earth production facility would use the LSCV site in Vietnam. Broker estimates differ.

SK Securities estimated 2026 revenue of KRW 1,298.0 billion and operating profit of KRW 85.0 billion, and 2027 revenue of KRW 1,452.0 billion and operating profit of KRW 97.0 billion (October 2026).

IBK Securities projected 2026 revenue of KRW 1,345.4 billion and operating profit of KRW 85.4 billion, and 2027 revenue of KRW 1,744.7 billion and operating profit of KRW 111.5 billion (October 2026). The gap in 2027 revenue reflects different views on when rare earth sales begin to be included.

The next checkpoints are whether trial operation starts in December 2026 and the quality test results for the first shipments in early 2027.

08

Bull factors

Accumulated earnings and rising cumulative revenue

Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders moved from a loss of KRW 1.92 billion in 2022 to a profit in 2023 and reached KRW 42.1 billion in 2025. Cumulative revenue for the first three quarters of 2026 of KRW 987.3 billion exceeded full-year 2025 revenue of KRW 960.1 billion. The operating margin improved from 3.4% in 2022 to 7.0% in 2025.

High-value products and data center demand

The company said in August 2026 that busway revenue tripled on AI data center demand. Its 400kV-class high-voltage cable completed PQ testing, and US UL certification and 230kV supply eligibility widen its North American entry.

External partnership in the rare earth business

The company announced a mutual investment structure with Lynas in July 2026 for rare earth metals. A samarium system of 240 tons a year is planned, with trial operation in December according to a September 2026 announcement. IBK Securities expects about KRW 300 billion in annual revenue at full operation.

09

Bear factors

Revenue growth without matching profit growth

Third-quarter 2026 revenue rose 0.4% from the prior quarter, but operating profit of KRW 22.2 billion fell 12.2%. The margin came in at 6.3%. Operating profit also fell 11.4% in the fourth quarter of 2025, showing quarterly volatility in margins.

Sector multiple decline and target price cut

SK Securities said in October 2026 that it lowered its target price from KRW 90,000 to KRW 69,000 to reflect a decline in multiples across the power equipment sector. The same report said a lower share of high-value products limits room for profitability to improve.

Uncertainty over timing of new-business revenue

As of a February 2026 broker report, the rare earth business had not specified volumes or unit prices, so its revenue scale was not yet defined. Whether to pursue a joint venture with a Vietnamese partner or go alone was also undecided at that time. Revenue was expected to begin between late 2026 and early 2027, subject to quality test results.

10

Risk factors

Raw material prices

Rising copper prices supported growth in materials revenue, but higher input costs could change the margin structure. If the product mix moves away from high-value items, revenue growth may not translate into profit growth again. The pass-through of copper prices into selling prices needs to be tracked each quarter.

New-business execution

The rare earth business must go through pilot facility construction, trial operation in December, first shipments in early 2027 and quality testing. Whether it proceeds through a Vietnamese joint venture was also undecided as of February 2026.

A delay in the timing of rare earth and subsea cable projects could lead to a revision of the medium-term growth outlook.

Ownership structure and financial load

The largest shareholder group holds 64.37% as of October 19, 2026, which limits the free float. Total liabilities of KRW 265.6 billion exceed total equity of KRW 225.8 billion at the end of 2025, and the debt ratio was 117.6%.

Because earnings are concentrated in Vietnamese subsidiaries, local policy and exchange rate changes could affect results.

12

Overall view

LS Eco Energy is a holding company whose revenue and operating profit have grown together since 2023, with operating profit of KRW 66.8 billion in 2025 well above KRW 27.5 billion in 2022.

High-voltage cable and busway products at its Vietnamese subsidiary have driven growth, and cumulative revenue for the first three quarters of 2026 of KRW 987.3 billion exceeded full-year 2025 revenue.

However, third-quarter 2026 revenue rose 0.4% from the prior quarter while operating profit fell 12.2%, bringing the margin down to 6.3%.

Strengths include demand for high-value products, the 400kV cable and data center busway, while weaknesses include a stalled margin, a decline in sector multiples and uncertain timing of rare earth revenue.

Three brokers presented target prices in October 2026 and all maintained buy ratings, but these are each broker's judgment and rest on different assumptions.

The next checkpoints are the December 2026 samarium trial operation, the first shipments in early 2027 and the operating margin in the preliminary fourth-quarter 2026 results.
