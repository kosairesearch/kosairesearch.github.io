02

Business structure

Ecocab manufactures automotive wiring, wiring harnesses, bulb sockets, and LED modules as a vehicle electrical parts maker.

By revenue, the automated electrical parts segment including wiring harnesses accounts for the largest share at 71%, followed by LED modules at 12%, other items at 9%, and automotive wiring at 7% (as of Q1 2026, standalone financials).

Key customers include GM Global and GM Korea, and the company was registered as a tier-one vendor for Stellantis in 2022. In the course of expanding its supply base, the company previously stated it supplied parts to Hyundai Motor, KIA, Ford, Magna, Mazda, and Rivian, though that disclosure dates to around 2020.

Production bases are located in China and Mexico, while its Vietnam subsidiary was liquidated in 2025. In 2024 the company opened an energy storage system (ESS) research center to diversify its business, and in 2025 it established a new subsidiary called Electro Co., Ltd.

Largest shareholder and CEO Kim Chang-gyu and related parties held a 32.71% stake as of April 2026, while former CEO Choi Young-cheon held a 10.05% stake as the second-largest shareholder as of January 2026.

The competitive landscape is shaped by domestic and overseas wiring harness makers and is sensitive to automakers' production volumes and order terms.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual revenue rose from KRW 121.7 billion in 2022 to KRW 135.9 billion in 2023 (up 11.7% year on year), KRW 138.5 billion in 2024 (up 1.9%), and KRW 151.8 billion in 2025 (up 9.6%), marking three consecutive years of growth from 2023 through 2025.

Profitability, however, did not track revenue: operating profit swung from a loss of KRW 1.92 billion in 2022 to a gain of KRW 3.95 billion in 2023, surged 396.0% year on year to KRW 19.6 billion in 2024, then reversed to an operating loss of KRW 3.16 billion in 2025.

Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders followed the same pattern, moving from a net loss of KRW 3.22 billion in 2022 to a net profit of KRW 2.08 billion in 2023, jumping 876.7% to KRW 20.3 billion in 2024, and then reversing to a net loss of KRW 2.46 billion in 2025.

The operating margin climbed from -1.6% in 2022 to 14.1% in 2024 before falling back to -2.1% in 2025.

On a quarterly basis, the company swung from an operating loss of KRW 9.76 billion in Q2 2025 to an operating profit of KRW 8.28 billion in Q3 2025, then back to an operating loss of KRW 4.15 billion in Q4 2025, continued posting an operating loss of KRW 1.56 billion in Q1 2026, before returning to an operating profit of KRW 5.72 billion in Q2 2026.

Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders rose 265.4% quarter on quarter from KRW 1.63 billion in Q1 2026 to KRW 5.96 billion in Q2 2026, a swing back to profit versus the Q2 2025 net loss of KRW 1.38 billion.

The sum of net profit attributable to controlling shareholders over the last four quarters (Q3 2025-Q2 2026) was KRW 9.87 billion, below the full-year 2024 profit of KRW 20.3 billion.

Operating cash flow jumped to KRW 30.6 billion in 2025 from KRW 2.37 billion in 2024, a result that appears linked to working-capital management, including inventory, even as the operating line posted a loss.

05

Industry analysis

Automotive wiring harnesses are core components connecting electronics and power within a vehicle, and the shift to electric vehicles along with growing electrical content are cited as key demand drivers.

A KOTRA report citing overseas research estimated the EV wiring harness market would grow from about USD 1.2776 billion in 2021 to about USD 6.3274 billion in 2026, a compound annual growth rate of 37.7%.

This figure, however, reflects the overall global market outlook and is separate from Ecocab's own order intake or revenue growth.

Domestic wiring harness and electrical parts makers are affected by automakers' production plans, new model launch schedules, and currency movements, and given the labor-intensive nature of the process, many maintain production bases in lower-cost regions such as China and Southeast Asia.

Ecocab likewise operates production bases in China and Mexico, and reorganized its overseas footprint by liquidating its Vietnam subsidiary in 2025. On the industry-cycle side, fluctuations in automakers' production volumes and reliance on specific customers appear to be major sources of earnings volatility.

Rising EV penetration could expand demand for new product lines such as high-voltage cables, but this is a broader industry trend distinct from any individual company's market-share trajectory.

Outlook

Ecocab has been pursuing diversification beyond its core automotive electrical parts business into the energy sector, establishing an ESS research center in 2024 and setting up a new subsidiary, Electro Co., Ltd., in 2025.

At the same time, in 2025 the company liquidated its Vietnam subsidiary and disclosed a series of filings related to capital reduction and equity disposal at subsidiaries, reorganizing its overseas production network and subsidiary structure.

The sharp improvement in 2025 operating cash flow to KRW 30.6 billion appears linked to working-capital management including inventory, but the swing to an operating loss in the same year shows cost-structure pressure persisted.

Entering 2026, quarterly earnings improved, swinging from an operating loss of KRW 1.56 billion in Q1 to an operating profit of KRW 5.72 billion in Q2.

Whether this trend continues into the next quarter has not yet been confirmed through a regular disclosure, and Q3 2026 results will need to be verified once the quarterly report is filed.

Separately, in January 2026 the stock moved sharply amid sector-wide rotation into auto parts names on expectations tied to Hyundai Motor Group's robotics business, a thematic move that should be read as distinct from the company's own earnings trajectory.

The company's medium-term direction appears to hinge on a recovery in core electrical-parts profitability and the revenue contribution of new businesses such as ESS.

Bull factors

Signs of Quarterly Earnings Recovery

In Q2 2026, operating profit was KRW 5.72 billion and net income attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 5.96 billion, turning positive compared to the previous quarter.

The reduction in operating loss from KRW 1.56 billion in Q1 2026 to a turnaround in Q2 suggests the possibility of cost structure improvement. However, given the precedent of alternating profits and losses over five consecutive quarters, it is premature to conclude this as a trend.

Improved Balance Sheet and Cash Flow

The debt ratio at the end of 2025 was 54.0%, down from 75.6% in 2024. Operating cash flow for the same year was KRW 30.6 billion, a significant increase from KRW 2.37 billion in 2024. This can be interpreted as expanded buffer capacity in terms of financial stability.

Business Diversification and Global Customer Base

Following registration as a Tier 1 vendor for Stellantis in 2022, the opening of an ESS research center in 2024, and the establishment of a new subsidiary in 2025, the company has continued efforts to expand beyond its existing core electronic components business. Transaction history with global automakers such as GM has also been maintained over a long period.

Bear factors

Large Year-to-Year Earnings Swings

After posting a large operating profit of KRW 19.6 billion in 2024, the company turned to an operating loss of KRW 3.16 billion in 2025. Net income attributable to controlling shareholders also reversed from KRW 20.3 billion in 2024 to a net loss of KRW 2.46 billion in 2025.

There have been precedents where profit declined or turned into a loss even in years when revenue increased, indicating that revenue growth does not necessarily translate into profit improvement.

Sharp Quarter-to-Quarter Earnings Volatility

Over the five quarters from Q2 2025 to Q2 2026, operating profit alternated in the order of loss-profit-loss-loss-profit. Such volatility makes it difficult to predict the annual trend based on a single quarter's turnaround to profit.

The sum of net income attributable to controlling shareholders over the most recent four quarters was KRW 9.87 billion, less than half of the annual profit in 2024 (KRW 20.3 billion).

Overseas Subsidiary Restructuring Burden

In 2025, the Vietnam subsidiary was liquidated, and disclosures regarding capital reduction of subsidiaries and disposal of shares in other companies followed in succession. During this process of restructuring the overseas production network, the possibility of one-time costs or asset impairments cannot be ruled out. The final financial impact of the restructuring needs to be confirmed through future disclosures.

Risk factors

Customer Concentration Risk

The company is known to have a structure with high revenue dependence on specific automakers such as GM. Changes in the production plans or new model launch schedules of major customers could directly affect Ecocap's sales and operating rate. The pace of customer diversification needs to be continuously monitored through future disclosures.

Foreign Exchange and Raw Material Risk

With production bases in China and Mexico, the company's earnings are exposed to exchange rate fluctuations such as KRW/USD and KRW/MXN. Fluctuations in raw material prices, such as copper, the main raw material for wires, can also affect costs. These external variables can be considered one of the factors that increase quarterly earnings volatility.

Disclosure and Accounting Risk

In August 2026, there was a correction disclosure regarding the semi-annual report (as of June 2026). The past instances of disclosure corrections related to capital reduction of subsidiaries and equity disposals illustrate the potential for changes in financial disclosures. Investors need to distinguish between confirmed figures in regular reports and preliminary figures.

Overall view

Ecocab is an automotive electrical parts maker whose revenue grew steadily from 2022 through 2025, but whose operating profit and net profit attributable to controlling shareholders flipped direction each year—turning profitable in 2023, posting a large gain in 2024, and swinging back to a loss in 2025.

Entering 2026, quarterly results improved from a loss in Q1 to a profit in Q2, but given the alternating pattern of profit and loss over the prior five quarters, whether this improvement persists will need to be confirmed through the next quarterly report.

The decline in the debt ratio and improvement in operating cash flow at the end of 2025 can be read as positive signals for financial stability, while the swing to an operating loss that same year and the liquidation of the Vietnam subsidiary point to the burdens of ongoing business restructuring.

Whether the ESS research center and newly established subsidiary aimed at diversification translate into actual revenue has not yet been confirmed.

Readers should verify confirmed results through regular disclosures from Q3 2026 onward before forming a judgment, and this report does not provide an investment opinion or price target.
