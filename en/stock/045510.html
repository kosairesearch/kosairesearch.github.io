Report overview

AI Mix Rising as Profits Recover

02

Business structure

Zungwon En-Sys has long supplied enterprise IT infrastructure and is now reshaping its business by combining AI hardware, AI solutions and on-site build-out capability with its traditional IT infrastructure supply.

According to the company's announcement, AI-related sales were KRW 7.94 billion in the third quarter of 2026, up 438% year on year, and their share of total sales rose from 2.9% in the third quarter of 2025 to 12.9%.

Of the KRW 10.75 billion increase in total sales in that quarter, about 60%, or KRW 6.47 billion, came from AI, leaving non-AI sales at roughly 87% of the total.

The company said AI hardware sales reached KRW 6.18 billion in the first half of 2026, about 3.4 times the KRW 1.79 billion a year earlier, excluding projects under the National IT Industry Promotion Agency (NIPA).

The largest customer project is a NIPA national AI infrastructure equipment supply contract worth about KRW 23.8 billion, of which KRW 19.3 billion was recognized as first-quarter 2026 sales.

In healthcare, the company supplied on-premise AI inference infrastructure to a Yonsei University Health System pressure-injury record support system, covering server design, installation and maintenance.

Its AI Center, launched in June 2026, is pursuing development projects with a partner company in areas such as manufacturing quality inspection, equipment anomaly detection and predictive maintenance, and medical imaging analysis.

In physical AI, the company signed a hardware supply contract with robot maker Dubot in October 2026 and is pursuing the acquisition of a robot systems integration (SI) specialist.

Management is split, with CEO Han Deok-hee overseeing the core IT infrastructure business and the board chairman leading new AI initiatives.

Analysts note that intensifying competition in hardware distribution could easily compress margins, making the mix between solutions and build-out work a key variable for profitability.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual sales rose from KRW 166.3 billion in 2022 to KRW 179.8 billion in 2023 (up 8.1% year on year), KRW 194.5 billion in 2024 (up 8.2%) and KRW 200.9 billion in 2025 (up 3.3%), marking three consecutive years of growth from 2023 through 2025, with 2025 the largest year in the 2022 to 2025 span.

Operating profit went from KRW 677 million in 2022 to KRW 2.85 billion in 2023 (up 320.5%), fell to KRW 1.56 billion in 2024 (down 45.1%) and recovered to KRW 1.94 billion in 2025 (up 24.3%). The operating margin was 0.4% in 2022, 1.6% in 2023, 0.8% in 2024 and 1.0% in 2025, staying in the one-percent range.

Profit attributable to owners of the parent was KRW 1.2 billion in 2022, KRW 1.7 billion in 2023 (up 41.9%), KRW 946 million in 2024 (down 44.4%) and KRW 1.12 billion in 2025 (up 18.9%), while net income including non-controlling interests was reported as KRW 1.2 billion, KRW 1.7 billion, KRW 946 million and KRW 1.12 billion for the same years.

Operating cash flow was -KRW 2.86 billion in 2022, KRW 7.28 billion in 2023, -KRW 11.6 billion in 2024 and KRW 16.1 billion in 2025, changing sign each year.

The debt-to-equity ratio fell from 131.0% in 2022 to 101.8% in 2025, with year-end 2025 total equity of KRW 45.1 billion (all attributable to owners of the parent) and total liabilities of KRW 45.9 billion.

Quarterly, sales went from KRW 50.8 billion with an operating loss of KRW 6.11 million in the third quarter of 2025 to KRW 65.8 billion and operating profit of KRW 1.59 billion in the fourth quarter of 2025, then KRW 75.3 billion in the first quarter of 2026 with operating profit of KRW 1.28 billion (down 19.3% quarter on quarter), and KRW 58.3 billion in the second quarter with operating profit of KRW 1.87 billion (up 45.5%).

Third-quarter 2026 sales were KRW 61.6 billion (up 5.6% quarter on quarter and 21.2% year on year) and operating profit was KRW 1.37 billion (down 26.4% quarter on quarter).

Profit attributable to owners of the parent rose from KRW 605 million in the fourth quarter of 2025 to KRW 729 million, KRW 1.12 billion and KRW 1.93 billion over the next three quarters, and the last four quarters total KRW 4.39 billion.

Because KRW 19.3 billion of the national AI contract was recognized in the first quarter of 2026, quarterly sales should be read alongside the margin trend from the second quarter onward.

05

Industry analysis

The market Zungwon En-Sys serves is shaped by demand for high-performance computing hardware from expanding AI data centers alongside public-sector AI infrastructure spending. The company says demand for high-performance computing hardware such as GPUs has grown along with AI data center expansion.

Public-sector revenue depends heavily on when large projects are awarded, so an analyst view is that the base effect from the large first-quarter 2026 recognition fades in the second half. Industry observers note that AI has begun to drive actual revenue growth rather than serving only as a new growth engine.

Profitability is widely seen as the key test as the business moves from hardware supply into solutions and build-out work. The CEO has described the company as an AI infrastructure provider helping enterprises with their AI transition now that the shift is under way.

Its base as a long-standing IT infrastructure provider serves as the starting point for linking enterprise customers with AI hardware supply and build-out services. Hardware distribution is, however, a competitive segment where pricing and margin pressure persist.

The company's position in the market is best read through the changing weight of its two core axes, IT infrastructure build-out and AI hardware distribution.

06

Outlook

The company plans to expand its AI business by combining AI hardware supply with its own AI solutions.

Its AI Center is pursuing development projects with a partner company in manufacturing quality inspection, equipment anomaly detection and predictive maintenance, carbon management data platforms, and medical and biological imaging analysis.

In July 2026 the company hired an AI strategy executive with prior experience at a major Korean internet company and at SAP to strengthen its commercialization team.

In physical AI, it has entered a business combining robot hardware, systems integration and AI technology, and is pursuing the acquisition of a robot SI specialist. If completed, the deal could expand on-site build-out capacity and change the SI revenue mix, although the timing and scale have not been disclosed.

Public-sector AI revenue can swing sharply from quarter to quarter depending on award and recognition timing, so the revenue mix and operating margin in the next periodic report are the next checkpoints.

Whether AI hardware keeps its share of sales, which was 4.6% in the first half of 2026 and 7.0% in the second quarter, into the second half is a measure of how fast the shift is proceeding. Whether the company will disclose AI solution sales separately is a point to confirm in future periodic reports and announcements.

Because the company frames its expansion around a broader AI transition, the link between actual contract wins and profitability is the central test for the next stage.

08

Bull factors

Rapid expansion of the AI sales share

According to the company, AI-related sales reached 12.9% of third-quarter 2026 sales, up from 2.9% in the third quarter of 2025, and about 60% of the sales increase came from AI. First-half 2026 AI hardware sales, excluding NIPA projects, rose about 3.4 times year on year.

Commentators say the shift from IT infrastructure supply toward solutions and build-out work is beginning to show up in the numbers.

Return to operating profit and consecutive profit gains

Operating profit turned positive, moving from a KRW 6.11 million operating loss in the third quarter of 2025 to KRW 1.37 billion in the third quarter of 2026.

Profit attributable to owners of the parent rose for three consecutive quarters, from KRW 605 million to KRW 1.93 billion, and the last four quarters total KRW 4.39 billion. Some analysts read the 45.5% rise in second-quarter operating profit, despite a 22.5% sales decline, as a sign of an improving business mix.

Shareholder actions and expansion plans

Financial News reported that the company and its board chairman together bought roughly 430,000 shares in the open market. Other coverage reported that the chairman bought 100,000 shares on the exchange, citing confidence in the AI infrastructure transition. Plans to enter physical AI and pursue a robot SI acquisition also continue to expand the business scope.

09

Bear factors

Thin operating margin structure

The annual operating margin was 0.4% in 2022, 1.6% in 2023, 0.8% in 2024 and 1.0% in 2025, staying in the one-percent range. First-quarter 2026 operating profit fell 19.3% quarter on quarter, and third-quarter 2026 operating profit fell 26.4% quarter on quarter to KRW 1.37 billion. Some analysts warn that margins could fall easily if competition in hardware distribution intensifies.

Base effect from a large project

Of a national AI contract worth about KRW 23.8 billion, KRW 19.3 billion was recognized as first-quarter 2026 sales, lifting that quarter's revenue to KRW 75.3 billion.

Second-quarter sales fell 22.5% quarter on quarter to KRW 58.3 billion, and analysts say the base effect from the large project disappears in the second half of 2026. Public-sector AI revenue is lumpy because it depends on award timing, which limits the predictability of earnings.

Management-control dispute history and balance sheet burden

Reports describe past litigation over shareholdings with a former CEO and suits seeking to void shareholder resolutions. The audit report submitted in March 2026 carried an unqualified opinion, but commentators say legal uncertainty over management control remains.

Total liabilities at the end of 2025 were KRW 45.9 billion, slightly above total equity of KRW 45.1 billion, and the debt-to-equity ratio was 101.8%.

10

Risk factors

Contract concentration and project timing

A large share of the sales growth has relied on the national AI project and large public-sector contracts. After KRW 19.3 billion of the national project was recognized in the first quarter of 2026, second-quarter sales fell to KRW 58.3 billion, and quarterly results can swing sharply with contract timing. Own AI sales are growing, but their absolute scale has not yet replaced large projects.

Profitability and cash flow

The annual operating margin has stayed in the one-percent range, so revenue gains convert into profit only modestly. Operating cash flow swung from -KRW 11.6 billion in 2024 to KRW 16.1 billion in 2025. Analysts note that margins could be pressured by hardware distribution competition and by operating costs.

Governance and legal disputes

Management-control disputes have continued for years, and the governance structure could change depending on litigation outcomes. With the board chairman leading new AI businesses, responsibility between the core business and new ventures may not be clearly separated. Legal risk remains a source of share price volatility.

What to watch next

  1. Around March 2027

    The 2026 annual report and audit report are the next periodic checkpoint. The prior-year audit report was submitted on March 16, 2026, and the key item to confirm is whether the 2026 annual operating margin exceeded the 1.0% recorded in 2025.

  2. After December 2026 (timing not set)

    Completion of the robot SI acquisition and the timing of revenue recognition from the Dubot hardware supply contract are items to watch. If the acquisition closes, on-site build-out capacity and the SI revenue mix could change.

  3. After December 2026 (timing not set)

    Commercialization of AI Center projects such as manufacturing quality inspection, predictive maintenance and medical imaging analysis, and whether the company discloses AI solution sales separately, are items to confirm. How much solutions revenue offsets hardware revenue will be the basis for any shift in profitability.

12

Overall view

Zungwon En-Sys's recent results point to AI-related sales driving top-line growth while operating profit has returned to positive territory.

Third-quarter 2026 sales were KRW 61.6 billion, up 21.2% year on year, and both operating profit of KRW 1.37 billion and profit attributable to owners of the parent of KRW 1.93 billion moved from losses in the third quarter of 2025 to profits.

However, the annual operating margin stayed between 0.4% and 1.6% from 2022 to 2025, so whether revenue growth has translated into margin improvement still needs confirmation. First-quarter 2026 sales included KRW 19.3 billion from the national AI project, so part of the quarterly trend reflects a large project.

On the positive side are the rising AI sales share and shareholder actions; on the negative side are thin margins and the history of management-control disputes. On valuation, the small earnings base makes multiples sensitive to quarterly changes, and the price-to-book ratio sits below 1x.

The next checkpoints are the annual operating margin in the 2026 annual report and the second-half sales mix. This report is for information only and does not include a buy or sell opinion.
