02

Business structure

Q Capital Partners, founded in 1982 and listed on KOSDAQ in 1993, runs specialty finance businesses centered on new technology business financing and private equity funds for institutional investors. As of 2025, it managed KRW 1.41 trillion in committed capital across 8 PEFs and 2 venture investment partnerships.

A July 2026 press report, citing filings with the Financial Supervisory Service, described holdings of about KRW 1.39 trillion across 9 PEFs and about KRW 45.4 billion across 3 new technology investment partnerships, for a total of KRW 1.44 trillion.

A December 2025 company performance comment said the PEF operations accounted for 65% of total revenue. The main revenue sources are investment management fees and performance fees, with equity-method income and interest income also cited.

Management fees rose to about KRW 2.42 billion in Q1 2026 from about KRW 1.84 billion a year earlier as fund sizes grew. Performance fees, which are tied to investment exits, were zero in Q1 2026 after about KRW 1.8 billion was recognized in Q1 2025.

Equity-method losses from fund investments were booked at KRW 800 million-plus in Q1 2026 as operating expenses. The company says it has set up 17 PEFs and 8 partnerships since 2009, with cumulative fund formation of KRW 2.7 trillion, and focuses on mid-sized and small Korean companies in buyout deals.

04

Earnings analysis

Based on DART-filed annual results, revenue was KRW 16.6 billion in 2022, KRW 36 billion in 2023 (up 117.2% year on year), KRW 18.7 billion in 2024 (down 48.1%), and KRW 20.1 billion in 2025 (up 7.7%). Among 2022 to 2025, 2023 was the largest year and 2022 the smallest.

Operating profit moved from a loss of KRW 4.15 billion in 2022 to a profit of KRW 10.5 billion in 2023, a turnaround to profit, then fell 73.0% to KRW 2.82 billion in 2024 before rising 34.1% to KRW 3.79 billion in 2025. The operating margin was -25.1% in 2022, 29.1% in 2023, 15.1% in 2024 and 18.8% in 2025.

In 2024, operating profit fell faster (73.0%) than revenue (48.1%), which compressed the margin. For 2025, the company attributed the profit increase mainly to lower operating expenses.

Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders was a loss of KRW 6.74 billion in 2022, KRW 7.07 billion in 2023, KRW 1.78 billion in 2024 (down 74.8%) and KRW 2.44 billion in 2025 (up 37.3%).

Net profit including non-controlling interests is reported as a loss of KRW 6.74 billion in 2022, KRW 7.07 billion in 2023, KRW 1.78 billion in 2024 and KRW 2.44 billion in 2025.

Quarterly revenue was KRW 4.42 billion in Q3 2025, KRW 5.61 billion in Q4 2025 (up 26.8% quarter on quarter), KRW 3.82 billion in Q1 2026 (down 31.9%), KRW 6.71 billion in Q2 2026 (up 75.6%) and KRW 4.4 billion in Q3 2026 (down 34.4% quarter on quarter and down 0.4% year on year).

Operating profit was KRW 910 million in Q3 2025 and KRW 911 million in Q4 2025, fell to KRW 92.86 million in Q1 2026 (down 89.8% quarter on quarter), rose to KRW 3.71 billion in Q2 2026, and was KRW 725 million in Q3 2026 (down 20.3% year on year).

Press reports attributed the Q1 2026 decline mainly to zero recognized performance fees and an equity-method loss. Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders reached KRW 3.27 billion in Q2 2026 from KRW 165 million in Q1 2026, and the last four quarters totaled KRW 5.17 billion. Quarterly swings are large, so a single quarter does not establish a trend.

05

Industry analysis

Venture capital and PEF businesses tend to recognize performance fees in the quarters when investments are exited, so earnings cluster unevenly.

An October 2026 Alphasquare sector comparison put the average operating margin of 17 venture-capital stocks at 34.42% for the year, and ranked Q Capital Partners around 14th in that group on margin. The same comparison showed its price-to-book ratio below the sector average. Exit conditions are another key variable.

A press report said SK Ecoplant, an investee, did not complete its listing by the January 21, 2026 deadline, which triggered a penalty clause that raised the guaranteed annual return from 5% to 12%. The case illustrates how delayed IPO exits can affect income.

The same report linked the stock's rise to renewed interest in the venture capital theme. On the regulatory side, low-priced stock delisting requirements took effect in July 2026, adding pressure on small-cap share prices.

The company reduced its par value from KRW 500 to KRW 200 and carried out a share consolidation in 2026. Improved exit conditions could lift performance-fee recognition, but timing depends on individual fund exits.

06

Outlook

In an April 13, 2026 voluntary disclosure, the company set a target of KRW 3.3 trillion in cumulative AUM by 2030. It said it would direct at least 40% of net profit to dividends or to share buybacks with cancellation.

An executive pay change requires at least 10% of performance bonuses to go to share purchases, held for at least three years. At the March 26, 2026 shareholder meeting, a capital reduction lowering par value from KRW 500 to KRW 200 and a share consolidation were approved.

Press reports later said most of the consolidation effect faded within about a month. Reports also indicated that whether the guaranteed-return increase tied to SK Ecoplant translates into actual income is a point to watch.

Whether performance fees are recognized again on a recurring basis and whether equity-method losses shrink are the key variables for operating profit. The 2026 fiscal-year results will show whether dividends or cancellation-linked buybacks match the 40% benchmark. Progress toward the AUM target can be tracked through committed capital figures in the periodic reports.

Bull factors

A KRW 1.4 Trillion Fund Base

A July 2026 press report put total holdings at KRW 1.44 trillion across 9 PEFs and 3 new technology investment partnerships. Rising fund sizes lifted management fees to about KRW 2.42 billion in Q1 2026 from about KRW 1.84 billion a year earlier. Because these fees track AUM, they partly offset the absence of performance fees.

Shareholder Return Plan Disclosed

It also set out a plan to buy shares with at least 10% of executive performance pay and hold them for at least three years. Execution will be confirmed through disclosures after the 2026 fiscal year closes.

From Loss to Profit, Earnings Recovered in 2025

Operating profit swung from a KRW 4.15 billion loss in 2022 to KRW 10.5 billion in 2023 and rose 34.1% to KRW 3.79 billion in 2025. Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders reached KRW 2.44 billion in 2025, up 37.3%. The company attributed the 2025 gain mainly to lower operating expenses.

09

Bear factors

Performance-Fee Gap and Quarterly Volatility

According to press reports, performance fees were zero in Q1 2026, compared with about KRW 1.8 billion recognized in Q1 2025. Operating profit fell to KRW 92.86 million in Q1 2026, down 89.8% quarter on quarter.

Q3 2026 operating profit of KRW 725 million was down 20.3% year on year, so the recovery has been interrupted quarter to quarter.

Equity-Method Losses and Delayed Exits

Press reports said an equity-method loss of about KRW 800 million was booked as an operating expense in Q1 2026, reflecting fund losses directly in operating profit. When exits are delayed, performance-fee recognition shifts as well, which widens swings in operating profit.

A report also cited the failure of SK Ecoplant, an investee, to list by January 21, 2026 as an example of delayed exits.

Share Structure and Low-Priced Stock Rules

At the March 2026 shareholder meeting, a par-value cut from KRW 500 to KRW 200 was approved, followed by a share consolidation. Press reports said the share price fell back close to par within about a month after the consolidation.

Concern has been raised that, under low-priced stock rules effective July 2026, regulatory pressure could return if core business competitiveness does not improve.

10

Risk factors

Exit and Equity-Method Risk

Fund losses flow into operating expenses through equity-method accounting, so operating profit can move sharply. When exits slip, performance-fee recognition slips too. Because of this structure, higher AUM does not translate into proportionally higher earnings.

Regulatory and Listing Risk

Low-priced stock delisting requirements effective July 2026 could create listing-maintenance issues depending on share price. The company responded with a par-value reduction and a share consolidation, but press reports said the consolidation effect faded within about a month. If core earnings do not stabilize, the same issue could recur.

Counterparty and Concentration Risk

The SK Ecoplant case shows that whether a counterparty meets its listing commitment can directly affect income. The higher guaranteed return arose from a contractual penalty, so part of the earnings base depends on specific deal terms.

Because results concentrate in a few quarters' performance fees and equity-method items, the exit outcome of a single fund can significantly influence total earnings.

12

Overall view

Q Capital Partners is a PEF manager overseeing roughly KRW 1.4 trillion in funds, and its earnings recovered from 2023's turnaround to profit through 2025.

However, zero recognized performance fees and an equity-method loss pushed Q1 2026 operating profit sharply lower, and Q3 2026 operating profit also fell year on year. Rising management fees, the shareholder return plan and the KRW 3.3 trillion AUM target for 2030 are cited as positives.

Delayed exits, the timing concentration of performance fees, and low-priced stock rules and share structure remain uncertainties. Quarterly volatility is large, so trends are more reliably read from annual figures and the last four quarters combined.

The next checkpoints are the 2026 annual provisional results, the dividend and buyback plans in the 2026 annual report and shareholder meeting, and changes in performance fees and equity-method results. This report is for information only and does not include an investment opinion or target price.
