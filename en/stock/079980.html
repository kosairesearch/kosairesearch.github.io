Huvis: Specialty Mix Rising, Quarterly Profit Still Unstable

02

Business structure

Huvis was established in 2000 as a joint venture between Samyang Corporation and SK Chemicals, and it mainly produces polyester staple fiber (SF), filament yarn (FY) and polyester chips.

Of first-half 2026 consolidated revenue of KRW 464.7 billion, the parent company accounted for KRW 356.4 billion and the Sichuan subsidiary for KRW 108.3 billion, according to the semiannual report released on August 13, 2026.

Combined output for the period was 188,235 tons at an average utilization rate of 78.0%, with filament utilization at the Jeonju plant at 71.4% and staple fiber at 79.4%. The product line rests on three pillars.

The first is low-melt bonding fiber (LMF), used in automotive interiors such as dashboards, headliners and seats as well as building insulation, which a 2021 press report described as a global leader.

The second is the cooling fiber Duraron-Cool, which held a 70% share of the domestic cooling-fiber market according to a 2025 Herald Economy report.

The third is eco-friendly material, including the biodegradable PET fiber ecoen, which passed an ASTM biodegradability test in 2024, and chemically recycled LM products that the company cites as differentiated items.

Customers span bedding and furniture filling in staple fiber, automotive interior suppliers and outdoor apparel brands. Huvis is reported to hold the top domestic share in polyester fiber, but low-priced Chinese supply has surged and competition has intensified.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual revenue went from KRW 1.02 trillion in 2022 to KRW 927.7 billion in 2023 (down 9.2% year on year), KRW 939.4 billion in 2024 (up 1.3%) and KRW 889.5 billion in 2025 (down 5.3%). 2022 was the largest year and 2025 the smallest among 2022 to 2025.

Operating results moved from an operating loss of KRW 83 billion in 2022 and KRW 71.6 billion in 2023 to KRW 38.3 billion in 2024, with the losses narrowing each year, before turning to an operating profit of KRW 19.8 billion in 2025.

The operating margin improved from -8.1% in 2022 and -7.7% in 2023 to -4.1% in 2024 and 2.2% in 2025. Net income attributable to controlling shareholders was a loss of KRW 108 billion in 2023 and KRW 132.6 billion in 2024, with losses widening, before turning to a profit of KRW 2.33 billion in 2025.

The 2025 operating profit had been reported at KRW 21.1 billion in a preliminary disclosure on January 30, 2026, but the regular-report figure is KRW 19.8 billion.

Operating cash flow moved from negative KRW 31.4 billion in 2022, negative KRW 10.5 billion in 2023 and negative KRW 20.1 billion in 2024 to KRW 37.6 billion in 2025. The debt-to-equity ratio rose from 108.2% in 2022 to 188.4% in 2023 and 225.7% in 2024, then eased to 203.0% in 2025.

On a quarterly basis, Q3 2025 revenue was KRW 210.2 billion with operating profit of KRW 1.01 billion and a net loss attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 3.24 billion.

Q4 2025 revenue was KRW 214.7 billion with operating profit of KRW 9.93 billion, a sharp increase from the prior quarter, while the net loss attributable to controlling shareholders widened to KRW 6.73 billion.

Q1 2026 revenue was KRW 226 billion with operating profit of KRW 179 million and a net loss attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 818 million. Q2 2026 revenue was KRW 238.7 billion with operating profit of KRW 3.34 billion and a net loss attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 1.59 billion.

Q3 2026 revenue was KRW 231.9 billion, up 10.3% year on year, but the operating loss reached KRW 8.72 billion and the net loss attributable to controlling shareholders KRW 10.8 billion. Over the last four quarters the net loss attributable to controlling shareholders totaled KRW 20 billion.

In its second-quarter release the company had described the third quarter as seasonally weak because of holiday-season demand and external uncertainty, and the third-quarter result moved in that direction.

The key questions for reading these figures are whether operating profit stays positive quarter by quarter and whether operating earnings can outweigh non-operating burdens.

05

Industry analysis

Profitability in the domestic polyester fiber market has depended heavily on low-priced Chinese supply and raw material price swings.

A 2025 Herald Economy report noted that although Huvis holds the top domestic share in polyester fiber, surging low-priced Chinese products and rising raw material costs kept it in the red. A 2021 report said Chinese producers were chasing the sector with large capacity additions.

By contrast, low-melt bonding fiber serves medical textiles, automotive interiors and building insulation, and was described in that 2021 report as growing 7 to 8 percent a year.

In its second-quarter 2026 release the company said it responded to raw material prices, exchange rates and other external changes by flexibly running its production lines, and it also cited the prolonged Middle East conflict as an external uncertainty.

In its Q3 2025 release, the company had expected European demand, its main market, to recover once the US reciprocal tariff agreement reduced uncertainty.

On the eco-friendly side, one report argued that chemical recycling can overcome the quality limits of mechanical recycling and that its adoption could expand alongside tougher regulation.

The company is reshaping its structure away from commodity products toward higher-value specialty materials, and China's push toward eco-friendly materials has been cited as a supportive backdrop.

06

Outlook

In its second-quarter 2026 release the company said the third quarter would be seasonally weak and that the market environment would stay difficult amid external uncertainty.

In response it pledged to raise the share of differentiated products such as chemically recycled LM, and the Q3 2026 operating loss of KRW 8.72 billion moved in the direction it had flagged.

The company raised new funds in the second quarter to secure liquidity and said it would continue to prioritize profitability on the back of a stable financial structure.

On the product side, a 2025 report said Huvis was negotiating supply volumes with outdoor apparel brands for Duraron-Cool cooling fiber in clothing, with the aim of year-round sales.

The biodegradable fiber ecoen passed an ASTM test in 2024, and a report said Huvis signed a supply agreement with the city of Jeonju to shift public-sector banners to biodegradable products.

Industry commentary, however, notes that biodegradable fiber still lacks an independent environmental certification standard, so the pace of market expansion bears watching. Meta-aramid commercialization is also reported as ongoing, but the timing of any revenue contribution has not been disclosed.

The next confirmed checkpoint is the 2026 annual report, and quarterly earnings before it will also be monitored.

Risk factors

Financial leverage

Total liabilities at year-end 2025 were KRW 503.3 billion, exceeding total equity of KRW 247.9 billion. The company said the new funds raised in Q2 2026 were aimed at securing liquidity against external uncertainty. Changes in financing costs and borrowing terms could weigh heavily on quarterly net results.

Raw materials, FX and external shocks

The company cited raw material prices, exchange rates and the prolonged Middle East conflict as variables for results. The Q2 2025 improvement was partly attributed to export gains from a strong exchange rate. If currency moves turn unfavorable, that benefit could fade.

Chinese competition and eco certification

Large Chinese capacity additions and low-priced supply are a variable that pressures commodity polyester prices. Industry commentary says biodegradable fiber needs an independent environmental certification standard, which could limit the pace of market expansion. Whether broader sales of eco-friendly products translate into better profitability also needs confirmation.

Overall view

Huvis recorded operating and net losses from 2022 through 2024 before turning to an operating profit of KRW 19.8 billion and a profit attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 2.33 billion in 2025.

The annual trend points in the right direction, but net losses attributable to controlling shareholders have now run for five consecutive quarters from Q3 2025, and Q3 2026 brought an operating loss of KRW 8.72 billion, so losses have returned at the operating level as well.

Strengths include a higher share of differentiated materials, a lower debt-to-equity ratio (from 225.7% to 203.0%) and Q2 2026 revenue of KRW 238.7 billion.

Weaknesses include worse non-operating results, financial burden from total liabilities of KRW 503.3 billion, and competition from low-priced Chinese supply alongside raw material and currency swings.

On valuation, a price-to-earnings ratio cannot be calculated given the net losses of the last four quarters, and the price-to-book ratio sits at a low level against net asset value.

The next checkpoints are the 2026 annual report (around March 2027) and Q1 2027 results (around May 2027), and the central questions are whether quarterly operating profit holds and whether net earnings attributable to controlling shareholders turn positive.
