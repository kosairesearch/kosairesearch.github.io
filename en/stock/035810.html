02

Business structure

Easy Holdings was founded in 1988 to develop and manufacture pharmaceuticals, veterinary drugs and functional food ingredients, and it became a holding company through a spin-off in 2020.

It now runs three core businesses, feed, meat processing and biotechnology, and builds its food value chain mainly through subsidiaries such as Easy BIO.

Based on second-quarter 2026 revenue, as compiled by the external data site Butler, feed accounts for 53%, livestock and meat processing for 21%, poultry for 18% and other businesses for 8%. The feed unit produces compound feed and feed additives and has built long-standing technology in the domestic market.

The meat processing unit cites its HACCP-certified hygiene standards and wider branded meat distribution as drivers of improved results. Overseas, the company expanded its footprint by acquiring 100% of a major U.S. feed company in 2021, and after 2024 it acquired several U.S. feed-additive companies in succession.

In April 2025 it acquired another U.S. feed-additive company through its subsidiary Easy BIO, and it raised its stake in the affiliate Pharms World to 90%.

According to press reports, the company is gradually reducing the weight of feed and shifting toward bio and premium food, given raw material price volatility and weaker feed demand. Its biotechnology unit focuses on next-generation bio-materials such as antibiotic substitutes and immunity-boosting products. In this structure, livestock prices and feed raw material costs drive profitability.

04

Earnings analysis

On a disclosed annual basis, revenue rose from KRW 3.01 trillion in 2022 to KRW 3.12 trillion in 2023 (up 3.6%), KRW 3.28 trillion in 2024 (up 5.3%) and KRW 3.39 trillion in 2025 (up 3.2%), growing three years in a row from 2023 through 2025.

Operating profit was KRW 99.9 billion in 2022, KRW 103.8 billion in 2023 (up 3.9%), KRW 123.4 billion in 2024 (up 18.9%) and KRW 151.4 billion in 2025 (up 22.7%). The operating margin was flat at 3.3% in 2022 and 2023, then rose to 3.8% in 2024 and 4.5% in 2025, so margin improvement stood out more than revenue growth.

The company attributed the 2025 operating profit increase to better results at overseas subsidiaries and higher livestock prices for pigs and chickens, and it also cited lower foreign exchange translation losses from a stabler currency.

Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 11.4 billion in 2022, KRW 26.5 billion in 2023 (up 132.9%), KRW 17 billion in 2024 (down 35.8%) and KRW 26.8 billion in 2025 (up 57.3%). Press reports attributed the 2024 decline in total net profit mainly to higher foreign exchange translation losses.

Total net profit including non-controlling interests was KRW 69.3 billion in 2025, a wide gap versus the controlling shareholders' share, which points to a large non-controlling share.

Operating cash flow moved from minus KRW 48.5 billion in 2022 to KRW 61.3 billion in 2023, KRW 164.9 billion in 2024 and KRW 236.4 billion in 2025, while the debt ratio fell from 161.0% in 2022 to 134.5% in 2025.

Quarterly results turned sharply: after operating profit of KRW 64.3 billion and controlling shareholders' net profit of KRW 28.5 billion in the third quarter of 2025, operating profit fell to KRW 15 billion in the fourth quarter (down 76.7% from the prior quarter) and the company posted a net loss of KRW 33.2 billion attributable to controlling shareholders.

In the first quarter of 2026 operating profit was KRW 34.2 billion and controlling shareholders' net profit was KRW 6.65 billion, a return to profit, and in the second quarter operating profit reached KRW 56.7 billion with controlling shareholders' net profit of KRW 20.3 billion (up 204.7% from the prior quarter).

Third-quarter 2026 revenue was KRW 885 billion (up 5.4% year on year), but operating profit was KRW 34.8 billion, down 45.9% year on year, and controlling shareholders' net profit was KRW 20.3 billion, up only 0.1% from the prior quarter.

Over the last four quarters, from the fourth quarter of 2025 to the third quarter of 2026, net profit attributable to controlling shareholders totaled KRW 14 billion.

05

Industry analysis

The feed-additive and compound feed market has grown alongside the domestic meat industry, and its profitability depends heavily on livestock prices and raw material costs.

A securities analysis from February 2026 said domestic pork prices stayed 9.1% higher than a year earlier, while corn and soybean meal prices, key feed inputs, were stabilizing lower.

The Korea Investor Relations Association's research center noted in a report published in April 2026 that about 60% of the company's feed raw material costs depend on imports. The same report estimated that a 10% rise in the won-dollar exchange rate could reduce pre-tax profit by about KRW 26.9 billion on a 2025 basis.

In the domestic compound feed and additive market, raw material price volatility and weaker feed demand are cited as burdens. On competition, company analysis highlights its vertically integrated structure, combining feed technology with in-house meat processing and distribution, as a differentiator.

Overseas, the company is widening its local business base through acquisitions of North American feed-additive companies, and their results are now flowing into consolidated financial statements.

Some industry observers see 2026 as a possible first year of real earnings recovery, citing acquisition synergies and higher domestic pork prices. However, the decline in third-quarter 2026 operating profit year on year runs against that view.

This estimate dates from April 2026 and may change with later quarterly results and shifts in exchange rates and livestock prices. The company disclosed a plan to maintain a 40% payout ratio on a separate-financial-statement basis as part of its corporate value enhancement plan.

Whether overseas acquisitions keep adding to consolidated results, and how fast the shift from feed toward bio and food proceeds, will be key variables for medium-term earnings.

In biotechnology, development of antibiotic substitutes and immunity-boosting products is ongoing, and the company participates in national research and development projects. The April 2026 decision to retire about 1% of treasury shares was also disclosed as part of its shareholder return efforts.

The next checkpoints are the fourth-quarter 2026 preliminary results and the 2026 annual report, which will show how close full-year operating profit comes to that estimate.

Risk factors

Currency and raw materials

A significant share of feed raw materials is imported, so currency moves affect both costs and translation losses. In 2024, higher foreign exchange translation losses weighed on net profit. If the downward trend in corn and soybean meal prices reverses, cost pressure could return.

Integration of overseas acquisitions

Consecutive acquisitions of North American feed-additive companies have expanded consolidated revenue, but integrating acquired assets and funding them remain challenges. Overseas subsidiaries were cited as a major driver of the 2025 operating profit increase. A slowdown at those subsidiaries would directly affect consolidated earnings.

Livestock prices and business shift

Because higher livestock prices drove part of the 2025 operating profit increase, a reversal could weigh on meat processing and poultry earnings. The results of the shift away from feed toward bio and food must be confirmed through disclosures as they show up in consolidated figures. If the strategy stalls, transition costs could pressure profit.

Overall view

Easy Holdings is a holding company whose operating profit rose from KRW 99.9 billion in 2022 to KRW 151.4 billion in 2025, with the operating margin reaching 4.5%.

It built scale around feed, meat processing and biotechnology, and through acquisitions of overseas feed-additive companies, and overseas subsidiaries were cited as a major driver of the 2025 profit increase.

Quarterly profit has been recovering since a net loss attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 33.2 billion in the fourth quarter of 2025, but third-quarter 2026 operating profit fell 45.9% year on year.

Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders over the last four quarters totaled KRW 14 billion, a thin base compared with the improving annual trend. Because earnings move sharply with the exchange rate and livestock prices, currency sensitivity remains a risk.

On shareholder returns, the company has confirmed capital-reduction dividends, the February 2026 year-end dividend, a plan to keep a 40% payout ratio on a separate basis, and the April 2026 treasury share retirement.

Strengths include a price-to-book ratio below 1 times, which has ranged between 0.3 and 0.5 times in past years, while volatility in quarterly earnings is the main weakness. The next checkpoints are the preliminary fourth-quarter 2026 results, the 2026 annual report and the 2026 year-end dividend decision.
