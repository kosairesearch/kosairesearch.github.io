Report overview

Hyosung: Subsidiary Profitability and North American Power Equipment Orders Drive Earnings

Hyosung's operating profit rose for three consecutive years from 2023 to 2025 on the DART filings, but net income attributable to controlling shareholders has swung widely, so subsidiary results and equity-method income need to be read together.

  1. 1

    Operating profit rose from KRW 64.9 billion in 2022 to KRW 393 billion in 2025, and the operating margin climbed from 1.7% to 16.2% over the same period.

  2. 2

    Net income attributable to controlling shareholders fell 27.2% to KRW 329.9 billion in 2025 from KRW 453.2 billion in 2024, moving differently from operating profit.

  3. 3

    Operating profit in Q3 2026 was KRW 175.3 billion, up 43.9% year on year but down 24.3% from the prior quarter.

  4. 4

    Hyosung Heavy Industries booked KRW 3.23 trillion of new orders in Q3 2026, up 122% year on year, and its backlog reached KRW 18.9 trillion, a record. In the same quarter Hyosung Chemical swung to an operating loss, widening the gap among affiliates.

02

Business structure

Hyosung is the holding company of the Hyosung group, and its profit depends on the results of major subsidiaries and equity-method affiliates.

The 3Q 2026 filing showed consolidated revenue of KRW 699.3 billion, and most of the profit gain came from equity-method affiliates such as Hyosung Heavy Industries and Hyosung TNC.

Hyosung Heavy Industries runs high-voltage power equipment, including transformers, gas-insulated switchgear (GIS) and gas circuit breakers (GCB), along with a construction business.

Demand for its power equipment leans heavily on North America, and a brokerage analysis said 77% of its new orders in the first quarter of 2026 were for North American customers.

Hyosung Heavy Industries posted Q3 2026 revenue of KRW 1.74 trillion and operating profit of KRW 243.5 billion, up 7.2% and 10.8% year on year.

Hyosung TNC, a textile and materials business, said it would expand sales of higher value-added products and strengthen cost competitiveness, and its Q3 2026 operating profit was KRW 110.2 billion, up 96.4%.

Hyosung Chemical, a petrochemical business, moved from a quarterly profit to an operating loss in Q3 2026 as logistics costs rose and selling prices fell, compressing its PP and DH spreads.

Because the segments move in different directions, holding-level profit depends on how far power equipment and textiles offset the weakness in chemicals. The company also said improved profitability at consolidated subsidiaries contributed to results.

In power equipment, competition turns on production capacity and delivery capability in North American high-voltage equipment.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual revenue fell from KRW 3.72 trillion in 2022 to KRW 3.44 trillion in 2023 (down 7.6% year on year) and to KRW 2.27 trillion in 2024 (down 33.9%), before rising to KRW 2.43 trillion in 2025 (up 7.0%). 2022 was the largest year for revenue and 2024 the smallest.

Operating profit moved the other way, rising from KRW 64.9 billion in 2022 to KRW 94.4 billion in 2023 (up 45.4%), KRW 221.1 billion in 2024 (up 134.3%) and KRW 393 billion in 2025 (up 77.7%), which marks three consecutive years of increases from 2023 to 2025.

The operating margin reached 9.7% in 2024 even as revenue shrank, which points to a change in the earnings structure rather than a change in scale.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders went from KRW 15.6 billion in 2022 to KRW 68.23 million in 2023 (down 99.6%), then KRW 453.2 billion in 2024 and KRW 329.9 billion in 2025 (down 27.2%).

Net income including non-controlling interests was KRW 422.1 billion in 2025, so the two figures should be read separately to see the share that belongs to the holding company.

At the end of 2025, total equity stood at KRW 2.86 trillion, of which KRW 2.44 trillion belonged to controlling shareholders, and the debt-to-equity ratio rose from 73.3% in 2024 to 88.5% in 2025. Operating cash flow grew from KRW 168.2 billion in 2022 to KRW 421.1 billion in 2025.

On a quarterly basis, revenue was KRW 612.2 billion in Q3 2025 and KRW 663.7 billion in Q4 2025 (up 8.4% from the prior quarter), then KRW 530.2 billion in Q1 2026 (down 20.1%) and KRW 733.6 billion in Q2 2026 (up 38.4%).

Operating profit was KRW 90.2 billion in Q4 2025, KRW 94.6 billion in Q1 2026 and KRW 231.6 billion in Q2 2026, the highest of the last five quarters on the filed figures.

Q3 2026 revenue was KRW 699.3 billion (up 14.2% year on year) and operating profit was KRW 175.3 billion (up 43.9%), with the drop from the prior quarter attributed to delayed revenue recognition in power equipment after Middle East tensions and a US typhoon disrupted logistics. Net income attributable to controlling shareholders for the last four quarters totals KRW 500.9 billion.

05

Industry analysis

The power equipment cycle depends on US grid expansion and power demand from AI data centers. Hyosung Heavy Industries said its Q3 2026 new orders reached KRW 3.23 trillion, up 122% year on year, and its cumulative backlog hit KRW 18.9 trillion, a record.

Chairman Cho Hyun-joon has been pursuing early investment in power equipment on the premise that AI data center expansion will lift demand for power infrastructure.

A brokerage report dated April 27, 2026 said 77% of Hyosung Heavy Industries' first-quarter 2026 new orders, which reached KRW 4.17 trillion, came from North America. An NH Investment & Securities announcement put the division's backlog at KRW 17.5 trillion at the end of the second quarter of 2026.

As orders pile up, the focus is shifting to how quickly that backlog converts into production and revenue. The company has expanded capacity at its Changwon plant in Korea and is pursuing an expansion at its Memphis, Tennessee plant in the United States.

Views on where the power equipment cycle stands differ, depending on how quickly the order surge turns into reported earnings. In textiles, Hyosung TNC is raising profitability through higher value-added products, while in chemicals Hyosung Chemical faces a difficult environment as its PP and DH spreads have narrowed.

Because power equipment and the textile and chemical businesses are moving in different directions, the holding company's consolidated results show an offsetting pattern across affiliates.

06

Outlook

In its Q3 2026 results, the company said part of the power equipment revenue was delayed by logistics disruptions linked to Middle East tensions and a US typhoon, and that it expects this revenue to be recognized in the fourth quarter.

After the second-quarter results, brokerage coverage reported that Hyosung Heavy Industries had raised its 2026 new order target from KRW 8.4 trillion to KRW 12 trillion.

The same coverage said its revenue growth guidance for the division rose from 15% to 25%, and its operating margin outlook was lifted from the mid-teens to the high-teens.

A brokerage preview from April 2026 said the first expansion at the Memphis plant was likely to finish by the end of 2026, with a second expansion extending to the end of 2028. The company has also said it plans to raise high-voltage transformer production capacity by more than 50% by 2028.

Hyosung TNC has stated it will expand sales of higher value-added products and strengthen cost competitiveness to improve profitability. Whether PP and DH spreads at Hyosung Chemical recover has not yet shown a clear direction.

From the holding company's perspective, the next checkpoint is how these three areas feed into fourth-quarter 2026 and 2027 results. The 2026 annual report, the next periodic filing, will set the full-year net income attributable to controlling shareholders as a confirmed figure.

Bull factors

Expanding North American power equipment orders

Hyosung Heavy Industries' new orders in Q3 2026 reached KRW 3.23 trillion, up 122% year on year, and its backlog hit KRW 18.9 trillion, a record. Analysts say the mix shift toward North America and high-voltage products is lifting average selling prices.

If the Memphis expansion is completed around the end of 2026, added capacity could speed up the conversion of backlog into revenue.

Improving operating profit base

Operating profit of KRW 231.6 billion in Q2 2026 was the highest of the last five quarters on the filed figures. Q3 2026 operating profit was up 43.9% year on year, so the improvement has continued.

Cash generation and equity base

Equity attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 2.44 trillion at the end of 2025. Rising equity-method income from affiliates has broadened the holding company's earnings base.

09

Bear factors

Volatility in attributable net income

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders fell 27.2% to KRW 329.9 billion in 2025, and dropped to KRW 68.23 million in 2023. Operating profit rose for three straight years, yet attributable net income did not follow the same direction, which raises questions about the quality and structure of earnings.

The last four quarters total KRW 500.9 billion, but quarterly results vary widely, from KRW 73.3 billion in Q1 2026 to KRW 195.3 billion in Q2 2026.

Swing to losses at the chemical affiliate

Hyosung Chemical swung from a quarterly profit to an operating loss in Q3 2026. Its PP and DH segments saw higher production costs from logistics expenses and lower selling prices, which narrowed spreads. If weakness in chemicals persists, gains in power equipment and textiles could be partly offset.

Higher leverage and delayed revenue conversion

The debt-to-equity ratio rose from 73.3% in 2024 to 88.5% in 2025. In Q3 2026, Hyosung Heavy Industries saw some revenue recognition delayed by logistics disruptions tied to Middle East tensions and a US typhoon. If the pace at which orders turn into revenue slows, earnings visibility declines.

10

Risk factors

Geopolitics and logistics

Logistics disruptions from Middle East tensions and a US typhoon delayed revenue recognition at the power equipment division. The company expects that revenue to be booked in the fourth quarter. If the disruption lasts, quarterly earnings volatility could widen.

Tariffs and policy

A brokerage analysis said partial recovery or reversal of US tariff costs could add upside to earnings. Conversely, if tariff costs keep flowing into cost of sales, profitability on high-margin breaker shipments could decline. How far expanded US production offsets this factor is the point to watch.

Concentration in affiliates and balance sheet

Holding-company earnings are concentrated in a small number of affiliates, including Hyosung Heavy Industries and Hyosung TNC. When affiliate performance diverges, the operating profit trend at the holding company can also become unstable.

The debt-to-equity ratio rose to 88.5% at the end of 2025, so the balance sheet burden also needs to be watched.

12

Overall view

Hyosung's operating profit rose sharply from 2022 through 2025, while net income attributable to controlling shareholders fell 27.2% in 2025 after peaking in 2024, which makes this holding company's earnings structure volatile.

Operating profit in Q3 2026 was KRW 175.3 billion, up 43.9% year on year, and attributable net income for the last four quarters totals KRW 500.9 billion. The strengths are the KRW 18.9 trillion backlog at Hyosung Heavy Industries, North American power equipment demand and improving profitability at Hyosung TNC.

The weaknesses are the swing to an operating loss at Hyosung Chemical, the debt-to-equity ratio that rose to 88.5% at the end of 2025, and delayed revenue recognition caused by logistics disruptions.

Given the segment structure, the key variable for holding-company earnings is how far power equipment and textiles can offset weakness in chemicals.

The next checkpoints are whether delayed power equipment revenue shows up in preliminary Q4 2026 results and whether the first Memphis expansion is completed by the end of 2026. Once full-year attributable net income is confirmed in the 2026 annual report, the earnings trend can be read more precisely.
