02

Business structure

A-Jin Industry develops and produces automotive body parts, including body reinforcement panels, and its main domestic customers are Hyundai Motor and KIA.

The company is headquartered in Gyeongsan, North Gyeongsang Province, and has run overseas production sites, including a US subsidiary with four US plants and a Vietnam subsidiary. Its Chinese production affiliate was reported to have been wound down.

At the completion ceremony for the Hayang plant in Gyeongsan on June 10, 2026, the facility was introduced as the company's 12th domestic production site.

The Hayang plant covers a site of 96,189 square meters with total floor area of 81,955 square meters and is equipped with advanced press equipment and automated lines. The plant is reported to produce parts for the electrified GV70 and hybrid GV80 and G80 models from Genesis.

At CES 2026 the company said integrated body-part design can cut part counts by up to 90% and reduce weight by 30% to 40%, a claim made by the company itself.

Its technology strategy centers on full-carbon doors and metal 3D-printed lightweight body structures, and it plans to pursue new customers among North American automakers such as Tesla, GM and Ford and urban air mobility companies.

At group level, reports cite 15 affiliates, including the KOSDAQ-listed Ajin Electronic Components, with combined group sales of about KRW 1.5 trillion. In body parts, the company is compared with listed peers such as Sungwoo Hitech, Seoyon E-Hwa and SL within the same automaker supply structure.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual revenue rose from KRW 597.8 billion in 2022 to KRW 764.3 billion in 2023 (up 27.9% year on year), KRW 776.8 billion in 2024 (up 1.6%) and KRW 1.01 trillion in 2025 (up 29.9%), with growth in each year from 2023 through 2025. 2025 was the largest revenue year of the 2022 to 2025 period.

Operating profit went from KRW 47.5 billion in 2022 to KRW 83.9 billion in 2023 (up 76.4%), fell to KRW 32.3 billion in 2024 (down 61.5%), and recovered to KRW 64.4 billion in 2025 (up 99.4%). The operating margin over the same years was 8.0%, 11.0%, 4.2% and 6.4%.

In 2024 revenue grew 1.6% while the margin dropped to 4.2%, which drove the sharp profit decline, and in 2025 revenue growth came with the margin recovering to 6.4%.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 29.7 billion in 2022, KRW 51 billion in 2023 (up 71.9%), KRW 18.6 billion in 2024 (down 63.5%) and KRW 23.2 billion in 2025 (up 24.6%).

Net income including non-controlling interests was reported at KRW 29.7 billion, KRW 51 billion, KRW 18.6 billion and KRW 23.2 billion for the same years. Operating cash flow was KRW 89.4 billion in 2022, KRW 151.2 billion in 2023 and KRW 60.6 billion in 2024, then turned to -KRW 8.73 billion in 2025.

At year-end 2025, total equity was KRW 347.4 billion, all of it attributable to controlling shareholders, and total liabilities were KRW 1.19 trillion. The debt ratio rose from 183.6% in 2022 to 342.8% in 2025.

Quarterly, the company posted an operating loss of KRW 6.92 billion in the third quarter of 2025, turned to an operating profit of KRW 16.7 billion in the fourth quarter, then recorded KRW 17.6 billion in the first quarter of 2026 and KRW 5.9 billion in the second quarter, before an operating loss of KRW 8.75 billion in the third quarter of 2026.

The attributable net loss of KRW 40 billion in that third quarter was far larger than the operating loss, so non-operating items are central to interpreting it.

Revenue bottomed at KRW 213.3 billion in the first quarter of 2026 and then rose to KRW 222.1 billion and KRW 240.4 billion in the second and third quarters, two consecutive quarters of growth, with third-quarter revenue up 0.8% from a year earlier.

05

Industry analysis

The auto parts industry is being shaped by the twin pressures of electrification and demand for lighter vehicles. The full-carbon doors and metal 3D-printed structures that A-Jin Industry promotes are described as contributing to longer driving range and better energy efficiency in electrified vehicles.

On the supply side, US-China tensions, supply chain instability and rising overseas production costs have pushed some manufacturers to bring production back home, and national and local governments have been strengthening tax and site support for reshoring companies.

The Hayang plant is presented as a representative case of this trend. On the customer side, the company says it has built lightweighting know-how through its work with Hyundai Motor and KIA and plans to broaden its North American customer base on that basis.

Body parts volumes are set by automakers' new-model development and production schedules, so customer timing largely drives revenue flow.

Among listed peers in the same automaker supply structure, including Sungwoo Hitech, Seoyon E-Hwa and SL, A-Jin Industry is in the position of restructuring its overseas production while starting up a new domestic plant at the same time.

06

Outlook

The nearest business milestone the company has announced is the full operation of the Hayang plant. The plant is expected to produce parts for the electrified GV70 and the hybrid GV80 and G80 models from Genesis, and reports cite 120 new local jobs.

Reported investment sizes differ, with one set of reports giving KRW 133 billion and another giving KRW 163.5 billion. Of a total subsidy of KRW 42.5 billion, the first tranche of KRW 31.9 billion has been paid, and the remaining KRW 10.6 billion is due after a second settlement stage.

On new customers, the company said at CES 2026 that it plans to expand sales to North American automakers such as Tesla, GM and Ford and to urban air mobility companies including Joby Aviation. The company has not announced a timetable for when such plans would turn into orders or revenue.

Since the company has wound down its Chinese production affiliate, the utilization and fixed cost burden of the domestic plants will be a key variable in upcoming results. In coming quarters, the depreciation and early-stage operating costs of the Hayang plant will show how the operating margin is affected.

The operating margin and operating cash flow reported in the 2026 annual report will be the benchmark for judging whether profitability is recovering.

Bull factors

Domestic production base and electrified parts

The Hayang plant was completed on June 10, 2026, broadening the company's domestic production base. After winding down its Chinese affiliate, the company shifted investment to Gyeongsan. The plant is set to produce parts for electrified and hybrid Genesis models, aligning with the shift toward electrification.

Operating profit recovery

Operating profit for 2025 was KRW 64.4 billion, up 99.4% year on year, and the operating margin rose to 6.4%. The fourth quarter of 2025 turned to an operating profit of KRW 16.7 billion.

Operating profit was KRW 17.6 billion in the first quarter of 2026, and revenue rose in each of the second and third quarters of 2026.

Lightweighting technology and customer diversification plans

At CES 2026 the company showed full-carbon doors and lightweight body structures built with metal 3D printing. The company said its design integration can cut part counts by up to 90% and reduce weight by 30% to 40%.

It plans to use this technology to broaden its customer base to North American automakers such as Tesla, GM and Ford and to urban air mobility companies.

Bear factors

Losses widened in the third quarter of 2026

The operating loss in the third quarter of 2026 was KRW 8.75 billion, with losses wider than a year earlier. The attributable net loss in the same quarter was KRW 40 billion, far larger than the operating loss.

The company has posted losses in two consecutive quarters, the second and third of 2026, and the attributable net loss for the last four quarters totaled KRW 14.5 billion.

Financial burden and weaker cash flow

Total liabilities at year-end 2025 were KRW 1.19 trillion, well above total equity of KRW 347.4 billion. Operating cash flow fell from KRW 60.6 billion in 2024 to -KRW 8.73 billion in 2025.

Differing investment reports and customer dependence

Reports give differing investment sizes for the Hayang plant, at KRW 133 billion and KRW 163.5 billion. Of the KRW 42.5 billion subsidy, KRW 10.6 billion remains to be paid after a second settlement stage. Hyundai Motor and KIA are the main customers, so changes in their production plans can quickly affect revenue.

Risk factors

Customer concentration risk

A-Jin Industry's main customers are domestic automakers including Hyundai Motor and KIA. Demand for body parts depends on these customers' new-model development and production plans.

New North American customers have so far been described as plans, so the effect of customer diversification has not yet shown up in results.

Financial leverage and cash generation

The debt ratio rose to 342.8%, and total liabilities stand at KRW 1.19 trillion. Operating cash flow was -KRW 8.73 billion in 2025. If operating and attributable net losses continue into 2026, borrowing costs and room for further investment could be affected.

Overseas production restructuring and related-party dealings

The wind-down of the Chinese production affiliate and the return of investment to Korea are reshaping overseas production. The 2025 annual report notes transactions with related parties, including Woosin Industrial and Ajin Kaintech. Related-party transaction size and terms are relevant when interpreting results.

Overall view

A-Jin Industry's reported results show revenue growing for three consecutive years from 2023 through 2025. Operating profit for 2025 was KRW 64.4 billion, up 99.4%, with the operating margin recovering to 6.4%.

In 2026, however, quarterly operating profit fell from KRW 17.6 billion in the first quarter to KRW 5.9 billion in the second, and the third quarter recorded an operating loss of KRW 8.75 billion and an attributable net loss of KRW 40 billion.

The attributable net loss for the last four quarters totaled KRW 14.5 billion. On the financial side, the debt ratio rose to 342.8%, and operating cash flow turned negative at -KRW 8.73 billion in 2025.

The Hayang plant was completed to produce parts for electrified and hybrid Genesis models, but reports give differing investment sizes and KRW 10.6 billion of subsidy remains to be settled.

Strengths include the domestic production base, the operating profit recovery and lightweighting technology, while weaknesses include loss-making quarters in 2026 and the financial burden. The next checkpoints are the operating margin and operating cash flow in the 2026 annual report.
