02

Business structure

Actoz Soft is a KOSDAQ-listed developer and publisher of online and mobile games, and its earnings center on royalties from licensing intellectual property tied to the Mir franchise.

The company has received royalties under a license agreement with a Chinese partner for Legend of Mir 2, and it attributed the sharp 2023 revenue increase to recognizing previously deferred revenue after signing exclusive Chinese licenses for Legend of Mir 2 and 3.

Some press reports noted that the company relied mainly on IP royalties for a period without developing its own games. On the in-house side, the main project is Seraph: In the Darkness, a blockchain-based action RPG developed by its Swiss subsidiary Actoz Tech AG.

Press reports said development budget of about USD 10 million was set for Seraph, and the company has said it plans a rollout starting with Steam and then moving to online game markets such as Korea and Greater China.

Industry commentary has also said interest in blockchain games is fading quickly, so the revenue contribution from Seraph is uncertain. Royalty income moves with the operating results of partners, which makes partner performance a key variable.

Competition divides into royalty-based and self-operated models; compared with listed companies that hold large in-house IP and operating teams, such as Krafton, Netmarble and PearlAbyss, more of the swing factors for Actoz Soft sit with its partners. Segment revenue shares are most precisely found in the business segment note of the periodic filings.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual revenue rose from KRW 59.1 billion in 2022 to KRW 89.4 billion in 2023, an increase of 51.3% that marked the peak, then fell to KRW 79.6 billion in 2024 (down 10.9%) and KRW 69.9 billion in 2025 (down 12.2%), declining for two consecutive years from 2024 through 2025.

Operating profit was KRW 23.4 billion in 2022, KRW 42.4 billion in 2023 (up 81.0%), KRW 29.5 billion in 2024 (down 30.6%) and KRW 28.6 billion in 2025 (down 3.0%).

The operating margin was 39.7% in 2022, 47.5% in 2023, 37.0% in 2024 and 40.9% in 2025, so it stayed in a 37 to 41 percent band even in the years when sales shrank. In 2025 revenue fell 12.2% while operating profit fell only 3.0%, which shows the cost base absorbed part of the sales decline.

Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 2.54 billion in 2022, KRW 34 billion in 2023 (up 1,241.9%), KRW 7.44 billion in 2024 (down 78.1%) and KRW 31.7 billion in 2025 (up 325.6%), a far wider swing than operating profit.

Net profit including non-controlling interests is reported at KRW 31.7 billion for 2025. The debt ratio was 54.6% in 2022, 44.0% in 2023, 55.4% in 2024 and 47.6% in 2025, and at the end of 2025 total equity was KRW 256.9 billion against total liabilities of KRW 122.4 billion.

Operating cash flow was KRW 19.2 billion in 2022, -KRW 90.4 billion in 2023, KRW 96.4 billion in 2024 and KRW 34.7 billion in 2025, with large year-to-year gaps.

Quarterly revenue fell from KRW 34.8 billion in the third quarter of 2025 to KRW 12.1 billion in the fourth quarter (down 65.1% from the prior quarter), bottomed at KRW 10.1 billion in the first quarter of 2026, and then rose to KRW 17.1 billion in the second quarter and KRW 30.7 billion in the third quarter.

Third-quarter 2026 revenue was down 11.7% from a year earlier, operating profit of KRW 21.8 billion was down 14.7%, and net profit attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 14.1 billion was down 45.9%.

Operating profit of KRW 25.6 billion on revenue of KRW 34.8 billion in the third quarter of 2025 was an unusually high share, so the filing notes should be checked for any one-off revenue recognition. Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders over the latest four quarters totaled KRW 41 billion.

05

Industry analysis

The Korean game industry is a sector where earnings swing widely with the success of new titles. Expectations for new game launches are often priced into shares ahead of time, so share price moves and actual results frequently diverge around launch dates.

Actoz Soft's position in the cycle should be read along two axes: the flow of royalty income and its new-title pipeline. A royalty-based model carries lighter development and marketing costs, but its earnings are tied to the life cycle of partner games and to conditions in the Chinese market.

Reports that interest in blockchain games is fading have left views on the monetization prospects of titles in that genre divided.

Compared with listed companies that have large in-house IP and operating teams, such as Krafton, Netmarble and PearlAbyss, Actoz Soft is smaller, and its earnings capacity leans more heavily on royalty contracts.

Mid-sized peers such as Com2us, Webzen and Neowiz are useful comparisons because their results also move sharply with launch timing and overseas sales. On the disclosed figures, third-quarter 2026 revenue rose from the second quarter but fell from a year earlier, so it is still early to judge an industry recovery.

The key checkpoints for the industry view are whether new IP royalty contracts are signed and when the company's in-house titles launch.

06

Outlook

The company has said the launch schedule for Seraph: In the Darkness is not yet confirmed, so the timing of the launch and its monetization model are the largest variables ahead. Press reports describe Seraph as built around a blockchain-based Play to Own structure centered on NFT item farming.

The Swiss subsidiary is developing the game, and a company representative has said that because the subsidiary is wholly owned, its results are reflected in consolidated earnings.

On the existing IP side, the Chinese licenses for Legend of Mir 2 and 3 underpin royalty income, so contract terms and the operating performance of the partner will shape future results.

Whether the two-quarter run of revenue growth that started in the second quarter of 2026 continues will first show up in the fourth-quarter 2026 results. Fourth-quarter 2026 results will first be confirmed as disclosed figures in the 2026 annual report.

Annual revenue of KRW 69.9 billion in 2025 remains below the KRW 89.4 billion peak in 2023, so whether 2026 annual revenue recovers to the 2025 level is a baseline test for the earnings recovery.

If a launch date is set through a disclosure, the share of revenue beyond royalties could rise, but the scale of that contribution has not been set.

Because the company has stressed that subsidiary performance flows into consolidated results, development progress at the subsidiary should be tracked alongside the disclosed figures.

08

Bull factors

High margins from royalties

The high operating margin sustained through the 2024–2025 revenue decline is the basis for this point. In 2025 revenue dropped 12.2% while operating profit fell only 3.0%. A royalty-based model with lighter production costs is consistent with that margin defense.

Two straight quarters of recovery

Revenue climbed from a first-quarter 2026 low of KRW 10.1 billion to KRW 17.1 billion and KRW 30.7 billion over two quarters. Third-quarter operating profit of KRW 21.8 billion was far above the second quarter. On a quarterly basis the trend has turned up from the low.

Large equity base and manageable debt

Total equity at the end of 2025 was KRW 256.9 billion against total liabilities of KRW 122.4 billion. The debt ratio moved from 54.6% in 2022 to 47.6% in 2025. The equity base is larger than liabilities, which gives a comfortable financial cushion.

09

Bear factors

Sales decline and a year-on-year drop in Q3

Revenue has declined for two consecutive years since the 2023 peak. Third-quarter 2026 revenue of KRW 30.7 billion was down 11.7% from a year earlier.

Operating profit fell 14.7% and net profit attributable to controlling shareholders fell 45.9% in the same quarter, so the year-on-year decline widened down the income statement.

Large swings in quarterly results

Revenue fell from KRW 34.8 billion in the third quarter of 2025 to KRW 12.1 billion in the fourth quarter, down 65.1% from the prior quarter. Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders dropped 77.2% over the same span. Quarter-to-quarter swings are large enough that a single quarter makes trend reading difficult.

Uncertain contribution from in-house titles

Seraph: In the Darkness received a large development budget, but the company has not fixed a launch date. Press coverage has pointed to fading market interest in blockchain games. Because its non-royalty revenue from new titles is not yet visible in disclosed results, the effect of diversifying income remains unproven.

10

Risk factors

Partner dependence

Royalty income depends on the performance of the Chinese partner's game and on license terms. A decline in the partner's game or a change in contract conditions could reduce revenue quickly. The revenue declines in 2024 and 2025 can be read as this sensitivity showing up in results.

Volatility in earnings and cash flow

Operating cash flow swung from -KRW 90.4 billion in 2023 to KRW 96.4 billion in 2024. Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders rose again from KRW 7.44 billion in 2024 to KRW 31.7 billion in 2025. Large year-to-year gaps between earnings and cash make the sustainability of results hard to judge.

New titles and market conditions

Seraph uses a blockchain structure, so it may be sensitive to market interest and to changes in the environment for digital assets. If development and marketing costs are spent before a launch date is set, monetization could be delayed. If new titles fail to attract users as expected, reliance on royalties will remain.

Overall view

Actoz Soft earns mainly from royalties tied to its Mir-franchise IP, and revenue has declined for two consecutive years since the 2023 peak. Even so, the operating margin stayed high at 37.0% in 2024 and 40.9% in 2025.

Quarterly revenue rose for two straight quarters after the first-quarter 2026 low, but on a year-on-year basis revenue, operating profit and net profit attributable to controlling shareholders were all lower in the third quarter.

Financially, total equity of KRW 256.9 billion at the end of 2025 exceeds total liabilities of KRW 122.4 billion.

Strengths include high margins, the quarterly recovery and financial capacity, while weaknesses include the revenue decline, volatile quarterly results and uncertainty about the contribution from in-house titles.

The launch timing of Seraph and the terms of royalty contracts are the main variables that will shape the earnings structure. The next checkpoint is the annual revenue and operating margin in the 2026 annual report.
