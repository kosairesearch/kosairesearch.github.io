Offshore Wind Structures Maker Tests Whether Its Profit Recovery Holds

02

Business structure

Samil C&S was founded in 1965 and produces high-strength concrete piles (PHC) and precast concrete products at its Chungju, Buyeo and Gunsan plants, and also fabricates steel structures. The business is split into three segments: concrete, wind and steel, and aggregates.

Based on a third-party compilation, the 2Q 2026 revenue mix was 50% concrete, 42% steel and 8% other and aggregates. The concrete segment supplies PHC piles and products made with off-site construction methods, while the wind segment supplies offshore wind transition pieces and steel bridge structures.

The aggregates segment produces sand and gravel, and in December 2024 the company merged two aggregate subsidiaries, Geonyang Industry and Samil Industry, into one entity.

In offshore wind, the company's supply of 64 substructures to the Nakwol offshore wind project, the first large-scale offshore wind power project in Korea, is cited as evidence of its competitiveness in bids.

In December 2025 it signed a contract to fabricate and deliver 21 transition pieces for the Katagami offshore wind project in Japan. Jeonbuk authorities said the company was selected in a competitive tender that included global firms, citing precision fabrication, seismic design capability and delivery management.

The Gunsan plant has a dedicated line with automatic welding machines and large painting facilities, built with about KRW 10 billion of investment. Competitively, the concrete segment is tied to the expansion of the PC industry, while the wind and steel segment is tied to offshore wind projects.

04

Earnings analysis

On a disclosed annual basis, revenue fell from KRW 241.2 billion in 2022 to KRW 216.4 billion in 2023, a 10.3% decline, which makes 2022 the largest year and 2023 the smallest year in the 2022 to 2025 range.

Revenue then rose to KRW 221.3 billion in 2024, up 2.2%, and to KRW 223.1 billion in 2025, up 0.8%, marking growth in each of the two years from 2024 to 2025. Profitability swung more sharply.

Operating loss of KRW 6.98 billion in 2022 became operating profit of KRW 5.17 billion in 2023, a turn to profit from a loss, then an operating loss of KRW 1.67 billion in 2024, a turn back to a loss, before operating profit of KRW 5 billion in 2025 returned the company to profit.

Operating margin was -2.9% in 2022, 2.4% in 2023, -0.8% in 2024 and 2.2% in 2025, so margins stayed in a thin band while revenue hovered around KRW 220 billion.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders was a loss of KRW 6.19 billion in 2022, a profit of KRW 2.04 billion in 2023, a loss of KRW 2.01 billion in 2024 and a profit of KRW 1.49 billion in 2025.

Net income including non-controlling interests was KRW 1.49 billion in 2025, the same figure as the controlling-shareholder result. Operating cash flow was KRW 5.69 billion in 2022, negative KRW 7.84 billion in 2023, negative KRW 5.68 billion in 2024 and KRW 14.1 billion in 2025.

The reasons behind the positive 2025 cash flow cannot be separated from the disclosed figures alone and are a matter for the cash flow notes in the annual report.

On a quarterly basis, Q3 2025 revenue was KRW 48.4 billion with operating profit of KRW 539 million, and Q4 2025 revenue rose to KRW 59.3 billion, up 22.7% quarter on quarter, with operating profit of KRW 3.42 billion, up 534.3%.

Q1 2026 revenue fell to KRW 40 billion, down 32.7%, with operating profit of KRW 1.31 billion, down 61.8%. Q2 2026 revenue recovered to KRW 79.6 billion, up 99.2%, with operating profit of KRW 2.47 billion, up 89.2%.

Q3 2026 revenue rose to KRW 86.6 billion, up 8.8% quarter on quarter, but operating profit fell to KRW 1.94 billion, down 21.3% quarter on quarter, and was up 260.5% year on year.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders over the latest four quarters, from Q4 2025 to Q3 2026, totaled KRW 2.22 billion, a small profit base relative to revenue.

Total equity at the end of 2025 was KRW 268.1 billion, all of it attributable to controlling shareholders, and total liabilities were KRW 172.7 billion.

05

Industry analysis

The disclosed figures point to two business pillars, concrete products and offshore wind structures. A July 2024 report said a construction slump had deepened the downturn in the steel structure manufacturing market.

Over the same period, the company said it had invested about KRW 10 billion in dedicated lines, including automatic welders and large painting facilities, to respond to the offshore wind market.

A third-party summary of first-half 2026 results said concrete segment revenue rose on the expansion of the PC industry, and that wind and steel segment results improved on offshore wind projects.

Japan's government has targeted up to 45 GW of offshore wind deployment by 2040, which is cited as an external driver of demand. In Korea, large project orders such as the Nakwol offshore wind project have built the company's volume production experience.

Compared with construction materials peers, SAMPYO Cement and HANIL CEMENTCO are centered on cement production, while Samil C&S is closer to PHC pile and steel structure fabrication, so both its scale and its business structure differ materially.

Offshore wind bids are competitive tenders that include global companies, and the company was selected on the basis of precision fabrication, quality control and delivery capability. On the cycle, a construction slowdown and a rise in offshore wind demand are appearing at the same time.

The concrete segment is exposed to domestic construction activity, while the wind segment depends more on overseas project flows.

06

Outlook

The nearest confirmed milestone is the contract period for the Japanese Katagami project, which runs from December 25, 2025 to February 24, 2027. The contract was disclosed with no advance payment, with billing and payment tied to construction progress.

Revenue recognition is therefore likely to continue from Q4 2026 into early 2027 as the work advances. The disclosure does not give a completion rate, so how much of the remaining contract value is recognized in each quarter has to be checked in the periodic reports.

In September 2026, a series of single sales and supply contract disclosures appeared, including filings on September 15, a corrected filing on September 22 and one on September 23, according to a disclosure list. The amounts and counterparties of those filings need to be confirmed from the original disclosures.

Media reports said the Japanese contract could raise the likelihood of being selected as the preferred negotiating party for follow-on projects in Japan. Reports also say the company plans to use its Gunsan plant, about 57,000 pyeong in size, as a production base for overseas work.

The next points to watch are how the Japanese project progresses and whether follow-on contract disclosures feed into quarterly revenue. For the concrete segment, a third-party summary attributes revenue growth to PC industry expansion. Wind segment revenue depends heavily on the progress of a small number of large projects.

Bull factors

Return to operating profit and quarterly revenue recovery

Operating profit in 2025 was KRW 5 billion, a turn to profit from a loss, and operating cash flow moved to KRW 14.1 billion, positive. Revenue rose for two consecutive quarters, reaching KRW 79.6 billion in Q2 2026 and KRW 86.6 billion in Q3 2026, with Q3 up 79.1% year on year.

Whether the recovery extends beyond a few quarters can be checked against the full-year figures in the 2026 annual report.

Offshore wind order record and entry into Japan

The Katagami supply contract equals 34.8% of 2024 revenue in size. Supplying 64 substructures for the domestic Nakwol offshore wind project is reported as a basis for technical credibility.

One media outlet said the contract could raise the chances of being chosen as preferred negotiator for follow-on Japanese projects. Japan's government has set a goal of deploying up to 45 GW of offshore wind by 2040.

Capital base and signs of improved cash generation

Total equity at the end of 2025 was KRW 268.1 billion, all attributable to controlling shareholders. Total liabilities of KRW 172.7 billion are comparatively modest relative to that equity base.

Operating cash flow turning to KRW 14.1 billion in 2025 can be read as a sign of improved cash generation from core operations.

Bear factors

Earnings base is still small

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 1.49 billion in 2025, and the latest four quarters total KRW 2.22 billion. The 2025 operating margin of 2.2% leaves a thin profit cushion on KRW 223.1 billion of revenue.

In 2024 the company posted an operating loss of KRW 1.67 billion and a controlling-shareholder net loss of KRW 2.01 billion. The small earnings base means quarterly profit can swing with modest cost changes.

Rising debt ratio trend

The debt ratio went from 51.9% in 2022 to 31.9% in 2023, then rose to 47.6% in 2024 and 64.4% in 2025. Total liabilities were KRW 172.7 billion at the end of 2025. If larger orders raise working capital needs, the debt burden could grow further, so the debt trend should be tracked alongside earnings.

Revenue recovery out of step with profit

Q1 2026 revenue fell to KRW 40 billion, down 32.7% quarter on quarter, and operating profit fell to KRW 1.31 billion, down 61.8%. Q3 2026 revenue rose 8.8% quarter on quarter to KRW 86.6 billion, but operating profit fell 21.3% to KRW 1.94 billion. The quarters in which revenue rose without a matching profit increase are the main bearish point.

Risk factors

Liquidity and ownership structure

As of September 30, 2026, the largest shareholder held 59.23% and the free float ratio was 40.72%. Foreign ownership stood at 1.47%. With a limited tradable share base, price volatility can grow when trading volume is low. Changes in the stakes held by the largest shareholder group need to be checked through disclosures.

Share price volatility and investment caution designation

According to a Topstar News report, the company was designated an investment caution stock effective April 1, 2026, after a 77.40% share price rise over the preceding 15 days. The top 20 accounts accounted for 71.66% of buying that day, which raised concerns of short-term overheating. Whether the price action after that spike connects to reported earnings needs to be checked.

Order concentration and currency

The Japanese contract value was disclosed after conversion at a Seoul Money Brokerage fixing rate, and it equals 34.8% of 2024 revenue. Schedule delays or cost changes on a single project could move quarterly results sharply. The won-denominated value changes with exchange rates, and media reports rendered it as roughly KRW 76 billion.

Overall view

Samil C&S returned to operating profit in 2025 with KRW 5 billion, and its operating cash flow turned positive, according to disclosed results. In 2026, Q1 revenue of KRW 40 billion marked the low point, and revenue then rose for two consecutive quarters to KRW 86.6 billion in Q3.

In offshore wind, the Japanese Katagami supply contract, equal to 34.8% of 2024 revenue, has been disclosed and supports the expansion of the business.

On the other hand, the 2025 operating margin was 2.2%, and net income attributable to controlling shareholders over the latest four quarters totaled KRW 2.22 billion, a thin earnings base. The debt ratio rose from 51.9% in 2022 to 64.4% in 2025, so changes in financial capacity need to be followed alongside it.

The largest shareholder's 59.23% stake and the 40.72% free float ratio are structural factors that shape trading and price behavior. Valuation metrics send different signals, with the share price relative to net assets and earnings-based multiples diverging, so one indicator alone is hard to rely on.

The next checkpoints are the end of the Katagami contract period on February 24, 2027, the 2026 annual report and the Q1 2027 quarterly report.
