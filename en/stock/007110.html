02

Business structure

Ilshin Stone combines natural stone quarrying and processing, stone construction works, and architectural stone distribution. The company listed on KOSPI in 1986, and its largest shareholder, a foundation linked to the Unification group, holds 41.32%.

In an October 2026 interview with Edaily, the CEO described the Pocheon quarry as about 40,000 pyeong and the Icheon land and factory as about 60,000 pyeong. The stone construction business installs stone in construction-company sites, and the company's project list includes many apartment and officetel sites.

On September 2, 2026, the company disclosed a supply contract worth KRW 2.841 billion for stone works at the Asan Tangjeong Xi Central City project with GS Engineering & Construction, running through February 29, 2028.

Its product range is built around natural stone and is expanding into Italian porcelain slab brand Infinity, the new brand Gentile, engineered stone, porcelain tiles, and sound-absorbing solutions. The company highlights its supply chain spanning more than 20 countries and its in-house production base in Pocheon.

Sales are shifting from business-to-business toward business-to-consumer through showrooms in Gangnam and Busan and a wider dealer network. A 2023 mid-year research summary put architectural stone sales and stone construction at roughly a 3-to-7 split. The CEO said recent orders have grown mainly from first-tier construction companies.

04

Earnings analysis

Reported sales fell from KRW 137.7 billion in 2022 to KRW 101.2 billion in 2023, KRW 78.7 billion in 2024, and KRW 54.5 billion in 2025. Sales declined 26.5% year on year in 2023, 22.2% in 2024, and 30.8% in 2025.

Sales fell three consecutive years from 2023 to 2025, with 2022 the largest year and 2025 the smallest in the 2022–2025 period.

The CEO said the 2022 top line included a one-off large project that ended afterward, compounded by a construction slowdown, and the company attributes the decline more to weakness in the downstream construction market than to a loss of sales capability.

Operating profit rose from KRW 3.9 billion in 2022 to KRW 4.61 billion in 2023, up 18.1%, then fell 17.5% to KRW 3.8 billion in 2024 and 82.1% to KRW 681 million in 2025, a second consecutive annual decline after the 2023 peak. Operating margin moved from 2.8% in 2022 and 4.6% in 2023 to 4.8% in 2024 and 1.2% in 2025.

In 2025 the company kept an operating profit, but net profit attributable to controlling shareholders turned to a KRW 686 million loss, and net profit including non-controlling interests was also a KRW 686 million loss.

Operating cash flow went from KRW -2.89 billion in 2022 to KRW 8.66 billion in 2023, then KRW 2.02 billion in 2024 and KRW 3.14 billion in 2025.

Quarterly, the company moved from an operating loss of KRW 876 million in the third quarter of 2025 to an operating profit of KRW 605 million in the fourth quarter, then KRW 101 million in the first quarter of 2026, KRW 380 million in the second quarter, and an operating loss of KRW 1.05 billion in the third quarter.

Third-quarter 2026 sales of KRW 12.9 billion rose 21.7% year on year but did not translate into profit, and the operating loss widened versus the prior-year quarter. Net loss attributable to controlling shareholders for that quarter was KRW 1.22 billion, and the third-quarter 2026 report was filed on November 16.

A third-quarter 2025 year-to-date summary cited falling demand for architectural stone, shrinking stone construction projects, and higher raw material and labor costs as factors behind weaker profitability.

05

Industry analysis

The CEO told Edaily in October 2026 that the domestic stone market has shrunk to about 40% of its 2022 size by the company's estimate. He attributed this mainly to weakness in the downstream construction industry rather than to the company's sales capability.

He also observed that orders have grown mainly from first-tier construction companies, and that the order backlog is higher than a year earlier. The company expects stone construction to recover gradually from 2027. To offset weaker demand for natural stone, the company is broadening its ceramic product lineup.

The CEO said premium stone priced at USD 100 or more per square meter accounts for over 20% of the global market but only 2–3% in Korea. He argued the company should shift toward higher-margin products emphasizing design, quality and eco-friendliness rather than price competition.

On relative position, an Alphasquare summary for 2025 full-year data ranked the company 32nd by sales and 16th by operating margin among 35 building materials peers. Its customers include large construction firms such as GS Engineering & Construction and Ipark Hyundai Development Company.

The pace of recovery depends on construction order cycles and cost-settlement terms, so the company's 2027 recovery timeline rests on those two variables.

06

Outlook

The company targets KRW 110 billion in sales and KRW 5.5 billion in operating profit for 2030, according to the CEO's October 2026 interview.

Compared with 2025 reported sales of KRW 54.5 billion, the sales target is more than double that level, and the operating profit target is several times the KRW 681 million reported for 2025.

To support the ceramic business, the company cites contracts and showroom adoption volumes equal to three to four times its sales over the past year.

The CEO said revenue from contracts takes time to be recognized, so ceramic sales should begin to be recognized in earnest from the second half of 2027, and could reach 2.5 to 4 times today's level after 2028. The company expects stone construction to recover gradually from 2027.

On orders, public supply-contract disclosures from January 1 to September 2, 2026 totaled two contracts worth KRW 6.9475 billion, with none disclosed in the same period a year earlier.

The Asan Tangjeong contract runs from September 2, 2026 to February 29, 2028, so its revenue contribution will spread over several quarters. On capital structure, the share consolidation took effect on September 5, 2026, reducing listed shares from 77,456,610 to 15,491,322, and the new shares listed on October 1.

The company presents business growth, improved results, stronger internal management, and investor relations as the means to expand shareholder returns. The next checkpoint for the 2030 targets is whether ceramic revenue is recognized from the second half of 2027 alongside a recovery in stone construction.

Bull factors

Expected Ceramic Revenue Recognition

The company is widening its ceramic lineup with Italian porcelain slab brand Infinity and new brand Gentile, and says it has secured contract and showroom volumes equal to three to four times its past year's sales. The CEO expects ceramic revenue to be recognized in earnest from the second half of 2027. The company argues that existing stone processing and construction relationships reduce the transition risk.

Shift in Order Flow

The CEO said order backlog rose from a year earlier as first-tier construction companies placed more orders. The Asan Tangjeong stone works contract with GS Engineering & Construction is worth KRW 2.841 billion and runs through February 29, 2028. Supply-contract disclosures from January 1 to September 2, 2026 totaled two contracts worth KRW 6.9475 billion.

Balance Sheet and Asset Base

The debt-to-equity ratio fell from 110.3% in 2022 to 56.6% in 2025. Total equity at the end of 2025 was KRW 58.7 billion, all attributable to controlling shareholders, against total liabilities of KRW 33.2 billion. The CEO's own estimate puts the value of owned land, factory and quarry above KRW 100 billion.

Bear factors

Operating Loss Widens Again

The third-quarter 2026 operating loss of KRW 1.05 billion widened from the prior-year quarter, and net loss attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 1.22 billion. The business went from a KRW 380 million operating profit in the second quarter to a loss within one quarter. Sales rose 21.7% year on year but did not lift profit.

Sales Decline and a Shrinking Market

Sales fell for three consecutive years from 2023 to 2025, reaching KRW 54.5 billion. The CEO believes the domestic stone market has shrunk to about 40% of its 2022 size. The 2025 operating margin of 1.2% is the lowest in the 2022–2025 period.

Transition Lag and Administrative Issue History

Ceramic revenue recognition is signaled for the second half of 2027, so new sales that could support earnings before then have not yet been recognized. The company was designated an administrative issue stock in August 2026 and completed a share consolidation to address the price requirement. The 2030 sales target of KRW 110 billion is more than double 2025 reported sales.

Risk factors

Construction Cycle and Cost Pressure

A slowdown in construction activity could reduce stone construction orders and project volumes together. Higher raw material and labor costs would weaken how much of each order turns into profit, depending on cost-settlement terms.

A year-to-date 2025 third-quarter summary also cited higher raw material and labor costs as a drag on profitability.

Execution Risk in the Business Pivot

Ceramic revenue lags contract signings, and the core lineup relies partly on brands introduced from Italy. Expanding B2C channels and showrooms could raise selling and administrative costs. The 2030 targets assume this transition proceeds as planned.

Share Structure and Trading Liquidity

The largest shareholder holds 41.32%, which limits the share of float available for trading. After designation as an administrative issue stock and the KRW 1,000 minimum price requirement, the company carried out a 5-to-1 consolidation that reduced listed shares to 15,491,322. Price volatility and trading liquidity may be affected by this structure.

Overall view

Ilshin Stone's reported sales fell from KRW 137.7 billion in 2022 to KRW 54.5 billion in 2025, yet it kept an operating profit of KRW 681 million in 2025, and its debt-to-equity ratio improved from 110.3% in 2022 to 56.6% in 2025.

However, an operating loss of KRW 1.05 billion in the third quarter of 2026 turned the business loss-making again, and net losses attributable to controlling shareholders total KRW 768 million over the last four quarters.

The company targets KRW 110 billion in sales and KRW 5.5 billion in operating profit for 2030 through a ceramic-led pivot and a higher share of premium products. That target presupposes that ceramic revenue is recognized from the second half of 2027 alongside a recovery in stone construction.

On orders, the September 2026 GS Engineering & Construction contract added to the flow, but cumulative supply-contract disclosures from January 1 to September 2, 2026 total KRW 6.9475 billion.

Supporting factors include the improved balance sheet, the asset base, and growing ceramic contracts, while opposing factors are the renewed operating loss, a shrinking market, and the lag in the pivot.

Because the company is loss-making, earnings-based multiples are hard to compare, and the price-to-book ratio below 1x is the starting point for asset-based comparison.

The next checkpoint is the operating profit for the fourth quarter of 2026 in the annual report due by the end of March 2027, along with whether ceramic revenue appears from the second half of 2027.
