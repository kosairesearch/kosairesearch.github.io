Ilshin Stone Bets on a Ceramic Pivot

Ilshin Stone has kept its operating profit positive even as revenue fell for three straight years and it swung to a controlling-interest net loss in 2025, and after being designated a managed stock and completing a face-value consolidation, it has publicly laid out a shift toward ceramics and premium stone.

  2. 2

  3. 3

02

Business structure

Founded in 1971, Ilshin Stone is the only KOSPI-listed company in Korea's stone industry, with its own quarry in Pocheon providing an annual production capacity of about 2,000,000 jae.

The business is split mainly between stone construction (installation) and stone manufacturing/distribution such as granite and marble, with the construction segment generating KRW 16.4 billion in revenue in 2025 and the manufacturing/distribution segment generating KRW 37.4 billion.

However, construction segment revenue fell from KRW 62.7 billion in 2023 to KRW 36.4 billion in 2024 and KRW 16.4 billion in 2025, shrinking to roughly a quarter within three years, while the manufacturing/distribution segment held up more steadily over the same period at KRW 38.3 billion, KRW 41.9 billion, and KRW 37.4 billion.

Most revenue is domestic, and 2025 export revenue was negligible. Major customers are large construction companies and government projects, combining finishing materials with on-site installation.

To offset declining demand for natural stone, the company has introduced the Italian premium porcelain big-slab brand Infinity and a new brand Gentile to expand its ceramics lineup. It has also added the eco-friendly engineered stone brand Cosmolite and acoustic material brands to broaden its product portfolio.

Sales channels are also expanding from a business-to-business focus toward business-to-consumer sales anchored by showrooms in Gangnam and Busan.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual revenue fell for three straight years, from KRW 137.7 billion in 2022 to KRW 101.2 billion in 2023 (down 26.5%), KRW 78.7 billion in 2024 (down 22.2%), and KRW 54.5 billion in 2025 (down 30.8%).

Operating profit peaked at KRW 4.61 billion in 2023, then declined for two straight years to KRW 3.8 billion in 2024 (down 17.5%) and KRW 681 million in 2025 (down 82.1%), with the operating margin falling sharply from 4.8% in 2024 to 1.2% in 2025.

Controlling-interest net profit slid from KRW 2.35 billion in 2022 to KRW 1.66 billion in 2023 and KRW 1.42 billion in 2024, before swinging to a net loss of KRW 686 million in 2025. Net profit including non-controlling interests moved in the same pattern as controlling-interest net profit.

On a quarterly basis, operating profit of KRW 773 million in the second quarter of 2025 turned into an operating loss of KRW 876 million in the third quarter, swung back to profit of KRW 605 million in the fourth quarter, then fell to KRW 101 million in the first quarter of 2026 before rising 275.3% quarter-on-quarter to KRW 380 million in the second quarter of 2026, though that was still down 50.8% year-on-year.

Controlling-interest net profit followed a similar path, from a net loss of KRW 1.14 billion in the third quarter of 2025 to a profit turnaround in the fourth quarter, then a sharp drop to KRW 32.3 million in the first quarter of 2026 before rising again to KRW 98.62 million in the second quarter, still down 81.8% year-on-year.

Over the last four quarters (third quarter of 2025 through second quarter of 2026), the company posted a cumulative controlling-interest net loss of KRW 683 million, underscoring that both revenue decline and margin pressure are occurring simultaneously.

05

Industry analysis

According to the company, Korea's domestic stone market has shrunk to roughly 40% of its 2022 size, reflecting how the broader construction downturn has weighed on the entire industry.

Ilshin Stone holds a unique position as the only listed company in the domestic stone sector, but it remains exposed to price competition from unlisted small and mid-sized firms as well as competition from imported stone and ceramic materials.

The company has said that orders from top-tier construction companies have increased recently, lifting its order backlog above the prior year, and it expects a gradual recovery in the stone construction segment from 2027.

In the global premium stone market, products priced above $100 per square meter account for more than 20% of the market, while the domestic share is only about 2-3%, suggesting Korea's product mix skews toward lower-priced goods.

The broader building materials sector spans cement, ready-mixed concrete, glass and other sub-industries, making direct competitive comparisons difficult, but in terms of revenue scale Ilshin Stone ranks in the lower-middle tier of the sector.

Given the time lag between construction orders and revenue recognition, any improvement in upstream indicators is likely to take time before showing up in results.

06

Outlook

In an October 2026 interview, CEO Cho Yong-cheol said the ceramics business, including porcelain big slabs, has secured contracts and model-home adoption volume equivalent to three to four times last year's revenue, and set a target of KRW 110 billion in revenue and KRW 5.5 billion in operating profit by 2030.

Because of the lag between contract signing and revenue recognition in construction projects, the company expects revenue to ramp up from the second half of 2027 and ceramics revenue to grow to 2.5 to 4 times its current level from 2028 onward.

Ceramics revenue reportedly grew to about 40% of distribution segment revenue in 2025.

The company plans to diversify its product lineup by targeting premium residential and commercial markets through its Ilsole premium natural stone brand, alongside the introduction of the eco-friendly engineered stone brand Cosmolite and an Italian acoustic material brand.

Its sales strategy is shifting from a business-to-business focus toward business-to-consumer sales through showrooms in Gangnam and Busan and an expanded dealer network.

The CEO also noted that the company's real estate holdings, including roughly 60,000 pyeong of land and factory space in Icheon and a roughly 40,000 pyeong quarry in Pocheon, are estimated to be worth more than KRW 100 billion, an asset-value argument the company is making alongside its business transition.

These targets and outlooks are all plans presented by the company, and actual contract execution and the timing of revenue recognition will need to be confirmed through future disclosures.

08

Bull factors

Operating Profit Held Up Despite Revenue Decline

Even as revenue fell for three straight years from 2022 to 2025, operating profit remained positive through 2025. In media interviews, management emphasized that the core business has consistently generated operating profit despite the construction downturn.

This suggests a distinction between a revenue slowdown and an outright profitability collapse, since the company has not been in a loss-making or discontinued-business situation.

Pivot Toward Ceramics and Premium Materials

The company is shifting its business structure from natural stone toward ceramics such as porcelain big slabs and premium stone. Alongside new brand launches, it has said it secured contracts and model-home adoption volume worth three to four times last year's revenue.

It has also laid out a specific timeline, with revenue recognition expected to accelerate from the second half of 2027 and ceramics revenue projected to grow 2.5 to 4 times by 2028 and beyond.

Real Estate and Quarry Asset Value

Management has said its real estate holdings, including roughly 60,000 pyeong of land and factory space in Icheon and a roughly 40,000 pyeong quarry in Pocheon, are estimated to be worth more than KRW 100 billion.

The Pocheon quarry also serves as a self-owned production base with annual capacity of about 2,000,000 jae, contributing to raw material supply stability. These tangible assets have been cited as a potential financial buffer during the business transition.

Bear factors

Three Straight Years of Revenue Decline and a Swing to Net Loss

Revenue fell for three straight years from KRW 137.7 billion in 2022 to KRW 54.5 billion in 2025, with the stone construction segment in particular plunging from KRW 62.7 billion in 2023 to KRW 16.4 billion in 2025.

In 2025, the operating margin also fell to 1.2% and controlling-interest net profit swung to a net loss of KRW 686 million. A controlling-interest net loss has persisted on a trailing four-quarter basis as well, meaning a profitability recovery has not yet shown up in the numbers.

Managed-Stock Designation and Listing Requirements

Ilshin Stone was designated a managed stock in August 2026 after its share price stayed below KRW 1,000 for 30 consecutive trading days.

Trading resumed after a 5-for-1 face-value consolidation and re-listing, but the designation will only be lifted if the price stays above KRW 1,000 for 45 consecutive trading days within a 90-trading-day window following the designation.

Failure to meet this requirement could lead to formal delisting procedures, meaning regulatory uncertainty could continue to weigh on the stock for some time.

Execution Risk in the Business Transition

The shift toward ceramics and premium materials is a plan premised on revenue recognition from the second half of 2027 onward, and it has not yet been proven in the numbers.

Because of the lag between contract signing and revenue recognition in construction projects, it will take additional time before the results of the transition become visible.

If the broader construction cycle recovers more slowly than expected, there is also a possibility that the targeted timeline could be pushed back.

Risk factors

Governance

The largest shareholder is the World Peace Unification Family Federation Maintenance Foundation, holding a 41.32% stake, and this foundation overseeing Unification Church affiliates has maintained control of the company.

There have been several disclosed changes in the largest shareholder's holdings, including on-market sales, which is worth monitoring from a stake-stability perspective. A shareholder base weighted heavily toward retail investors can also contribute to share price volatility.

Listing Maintenance Requirements

To resolve the managed-stock designation, the share price must stay above KRW 1,000 for 45 consecutive trading days within a 90-trading-day window following re-listing. Failure to meet this standard could trigger formal delisting procedures.

There is also a possibility that broader market volatility or the fading of specific catalysts could push the share price back below the threshold.

Dependence on the Construction Cycle

According to the company, Korea's domestic stone market has shrunk to about 40% of its 2022 size, and revenue is largely dependent on the domestic construction cycle. With an extremely small export share, there is limited room for overseas demand to offset domestic weakness.

Because construction projects involve a lag between order receipt and revenue recognition, a slow recovery in the upstream construction cycle could delay any improvement in results.

Overall view

Ilshin Stone shows clear signs of earnings deterioration, with revenue falling for three straight years and a swing to a net loss in 2025, yet it has kept operating profit in positive territory through 2025.

The managed-stock designation and face-value consolidation have reset its share price structure, and the requirement to stay above KRW 1,000 for 45 consecutive trading days remains a key variable for the time being.

Management has set a mid-to-long-term goal of shifting the business structure from natural stone to ceramics and premium materials, targeting KRW 110 billion in revenue and KRW 5.5 billion in operating profit by 2030, and has reported securing related contracts and orders.

However, these targets are still at the planning stage, premised on revenue recognition from the second half of 2027 onward, and a profitability rebound has not yet been confirmed in disclosed figures given the controlling-interest net loss over the trailing four quarters.

The stable control held by the Unification Church-affiliated foundation as the largest shareholder reduces uncertainty on the governance front, though the frequency of disclosed stake changes is also worth watching.

Before forming any judgment, it appears necessary to sequentially check the upcoming third-quarter results, whether the managed-stock designation is resolved, and the actual timing of ceramics revenue recognition.
