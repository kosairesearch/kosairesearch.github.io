02

Business structure

KCC Glass produces architectural and automotive glass, flooring and interior materials. The company was listed on the KOSPI market on January 21, 2020 and is classified under glass and glass product manufacturing.

It names double-glazed glass, high-insulation windows and high-performance flooring as high-value products and plans to raise their share of sales. Demand for architectural glass is tied directly to housing and construction activity, which makes the business sensitive to prolonged construction slumps.

In its 2025 results explanation, the company cited a prolonged construction and property downturn, intensifying competition from low-priced imported glass, and higher raw material costs from a weaker won. Automotive glass follows vehicle production and moves differently from the construction business.

The company has an Indonesian subsidiary, and the Q3 2026 widening of the net loss was attributed to a foreign currency translation loss on funds lent to that entity.

In its Q2 2026 announcement, the company cited production and organizational efficiency, a higher share of high-value products and market diversification as profitability measures. It also presents glass substrate materials for semiconductor packaging as a new growth driver.

04

Earnings analysis

On a reported basis, annual sales rose from KRW 1.44 trillion in 2022 to KRW 1.68 trillion in 2023 (up 16.4 percent) and KRW 1.9 trillion in 2024 (up 13.3 percent), then edged down 0.1 percent to KRW 1.9 trillion in 2025. Among 2022 to 2025, 2024 was the largest year and 2022 the smallest.

Profit moved in the opposite direction. Operating profit fell from KRW 119.2 billion in 2022 to KRW 95 billion in 2023 (down 20.3 percent) and KRW 57.2 billion in 2024 (down 39.8 percent), and in 2025 the company recorded an operating loss of KRW 75.2 billion, a swing into deficit.

The operating margin declined from 8.3 percent in 2022 and 5.7 percent in 2023 to 3.0 percent in 2024 and -4.0 percent in 2025. Net profit attributable to shareholders fell from KRW 94 billion in 2022 to KRW 37 billion in 2024, and in 2025 it became a net loss of KRW 83.6 billion.

Net income including non-controlling interests was also a net loss of KRW 83.6 billion in 2025. The debt ratio rose from 53.2 percent in 2022 to 67.5 percent in 2024 and 67.8 percent in 2025, with total liabilities of KRW 934.4 billion and total equity of KRW 1.38 trillion at the end of 2025.

Operating cash flow was KRW 173.1 billion in 2023 and KRW 203.6 billion in 2024 before turning to an outflow of KRW 18 billion in 2025.

By quarter, the company posted operating losses of KRW 14.2 billion in Q3 2025, KRW 19.9 billion in Q4 2025 and KRW 10.1 billion in Q1 2026, then an operating profit of KRW 4.81 billion in Q2 2026.

Q3 2026 sales of KRW 553.5 billion rose 15.0 percent year on year, but the company returned to an operating loss of KRW 8.33 billion, and the net loss attributable to shareholders widened to KRW 22.6 billion.

The company attributed the wider Q3 net loss to a foreign currency translation loss on funds lent to its Indonesian subsidiary. Losses attributable to shareholders have run for five straight quarters from Q3 2025 through Q3 2026, and the total for the last four quarters is KRW 62.9 billion.

05

Industry analysis

Demand for architectural glass depends on housing starts and construction activity, and that demand has not clearly recovered since 2025. A 2026 press report said the construction slump persists, and coverage of the 2025 results described a sharp contraction in housing construction.

The company said intensifying domestic competition from low-priced imported glass is pressuring prices and margins. On raw materials, the company cited higher costs from a weaker won in 2025.

Currency effects cut both ways: in Q3 2026 a shift in exchange rates produced a translation loss on funds lent to the Indonesian subsidiary, which widened the net loss.

Sales stayed at KRW 577.1 billion in Q2 2026 and KRW 553.5 billion in Q3 2026, yet operating results have not settled into consistent profitability, which suggests cost and pricing balance is still unsettled. The company is pushing high-value product lines and overseas market diversification as its differentiators.

Glass substrate materials for semiconductor packaging are presented as a new business, but the reported figures do not separate out this business's contribution.

06

Outlook

The company has presented a higher share of high-value products, cost efficiency and new overseas markets as the drivers of earnings recovery. In its Q2 2026 announcement it again cited production and organizational efficiency, a larger high-value mix and market diversification as profitability measures.

Cumulative sales for January to September 2026 reached KRW 1.6 trillion, up 10.7 percent year on year. The cumulative operating loss narrowed to KRW 13.6 billion over the same period, but that loss must be recovered before the full year can show an operating profit.

Whether Q4 2026 can repeat the Q2 2026 operating profit is the first checkpoint. The 2026 annual report will fix full-year operating profit, operating cash flow and the debt ratio. The direction of foreign currency translation gains and losses remains a variable that drives net income.

Glass substrate development for semiconductor packaging is presented as a growth driver, but the timing of any sales contribution has not been disclosed. The pace of construction recovery and the competitive intensity of low-priced imported glass are the external variables that will set the scale of any sales recovery.

Bull factors

Profit turn after three loss quarters

The Q2 2026 operating profit of KRW 4.81 billion followed three straight operating-loss quarters from Q3 2025 to Q1 2026. Sales that quarter rose 23.7 percent from the prior quarter to KRW 577.1 billion.

It is the first quarter in the recent run that broke the loss pattern, which supporters cite as the core bullish point.

High-value mix and cost-structure efforts

The company presents a higher share of double-glazed glass, high-insulation windows and high-performance flooring as a way to improve profitability. Q3 2026 sales rose 15.0 percent year on year to KRW 553.5 billion. Bulls argue that a better product mix could lift the operating margin from its current level.

Share price low relative to book value

The price-to-book ratio sits well below 1x, and total equity was KRW 1.38 trillion at the end of 2025. Some observers argue the stock could be revalued relative to asset value if earnings recover. That argument depends on confirmed profit recovery.

Bear factors

Five straight quarters of losses to shareholders

Losses attributable to shareholders have run for five straight quarters from Q3 2025 through Q3 2026. The last four quarters total KRW 62.9 billion, and the 2025 full-year net loss attributable to shareholders was KRW 83.6 billion. The Q3 2026 loss of KRW 22.6 billion was wider than a year earlier, so the loss has not yet narrowed.

Construction slump and low-priced imports

In its 2025 explanation, the company cited a prolonged construction and property downturn and intensifying competition from low-priced imported glass. In a weak housing market, securing volume is harder and price pressure rises.

Even though sales rose in Q2 2026, the durability of margin gains remains open to question without a demand recovery.

Cash flow reversal and rising debt ratio

Operating cash flow swung from an inflow of KRW 203.6 billion in 2024 to an outflow of KRW 18 billion in 2025. The debt ratio rose from 53.2 percent in 2022 to 67.8 percent in 2025, with total liabilities of KRW 934.4 billion at the end of 2025. Bears argue that financial pressure could build if losses persist.

Risk factors

Currency and foreign-currency gains and losses

The Q3 2026 widening of the net loss was attributed to a foreign currency translation loss on funds lent to the Indonesian subsidiary. The company said this is an accounting valuation loss that does not affect actual cash flow. Still, exchange-rate moves can swing quarterly net results and remain a variable for net income.

Construction cycle and housing demand

Demand for architectural glass depends on housing construction activity. A prolonged construction slump could pressure both volumes and prices. A press report from August 2026 also noted that the construction slump persisted.

Raw material costs and financial structure

In 2025 the company cited higher raw material costs from a weaker won as a factor behind the deterioration. With the debt ratio at 67.8 percent in 2025, continued cost pressure could narrow financial flexibility. Operating cash flow also turned negative in 2025.

Overall view

KCC Glass's reported results show profit declining since 2022 and turning into an operating loss in 2025, and the Q2 2026 operating profit signaled a possible turn.

However, Q3 2026 returned to an operating loss of KRW 8.33 billion and a net loss attributable to shareholders of KRW 22.6 billion, and such losses have run for five straight quarters. The construction slump and competition from low-priced imported glass remain external limits on sales recovery.

Currency-driven translation gains and losses also add volatility to net income. The bull case rests on the shift toward high-value products and a share price low relative to book value, while the bear case rests on continued losses, a negative operating cash flow in 2025 and a higher debt ratio.

The next checkpoints are whether Q4 2026 operating results keep a profit trend and how full-year operating profit and cash flow are confirmed in the 2026 annual report.
