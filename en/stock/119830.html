02

Business structure

Itek was founded in 2005 to run system semiconductor testing and listed on KOSDAQ in 2010. Its core is wafer and package testing for non-memory chips, issuing pass or fail judgments on assembled semiconductors. System semiconductor testing excluding memory is the main business.

According to 2026 press reports, the company counts more than 170 fabless and design-house customers. An analyst at Valuefinder said a high share of automotive semiconductor sales has built advanced 3D data-processing test experience that serves as a competitive edge.

Test references for AI chips and NPUs are being used to extend into next-generation areas such as physical AI and humanoid robots. Overseas, the company obtained bonded-warehouse status, which it described as a base for expanding global fabless customers.

Beyond testing, it runs composite and fusion new-material manufacturing and sales, semiconductor infrastructure operations with cold-chain solutions, and battery safety management systems. In the first half of 2025, initial costs in battery safety management and cold-chain lowered profitability.

Because testing runs on high-mix, low-volume production, swings in customer demand leave fixed costs exposed. It competes with other non-memory test specialists, and a 2025 report said the company planned to add at least five test units by 2026.

04

Earnings analysis

Based on DART-filed annual figures, sales rose from KRW 76.7 billion in 2022 to KRW 83.3 billion in 2023 (up 8.6% year on year), the largest year of the period, and then fell to KRW 62 billion in 2024 (down 25.6%) and KRW 48.5 billion in 2025 (down 21.7%), declining for two consecutive years.

The company attributed the 2025 decline to a reclassification of an affiliate after a change in subsidiary equity and to discontinued-operations accounting. The operating margin moved from 0.9% in 2022 to -8.6% in 2023, -7.9% in 2024 and -0.5% in 2025, so losses persisted but narrowed after 2024.

Operating profit of KRW 718 million in 2022 turned into an operating loss of KRW 7.18 billion in 2023, followed by losses of KRW 4.87 billion in 2024 and KRW 257 million in 2025, which means three consecutive years of losses.

Attributable net income was KRW 21.7 billion in 2022, a net loss of KRW 7.41 billion in 2023, a return to profit of KRW 21 billion in 2024, and a net loss of KRW 4.64 billion in 2025.

Non-operating items shaped the 2022 and 2024 profits: a Hana Securities 2025 field-note financial table shows other non-operating gains of KRW 27.1 billion in 2022, and financial income of KRW 10.9 billion and discontinued-operations income of KRW 14.5 billion in 2024.

Operating cash flow was KRW 11.2 billion in 2022, negative KRW 5.93 billion in 2023, KRW 12.8 billion in 2024 and KRW 18.8 billion in 2025, remaining positive in 2025 despite the operating loss.

On a quarterly basis, sales fell 66.3% to KRW 4.83 billion in the fourth quarter of 2025, when operating profit turned to KRW 3.27 billion while the attributable net loss reached KRW 6.29 billion.

In the first quarter of 2026, sales rebounded 151.0% to KRW 12.1 billion, but an operating loss of KRW 297 million and an attributable net loss of KRW 8.28 billion widened the loss.

The second quarter of 2026 returned to operating profit of KRW 1.08 billion and an attributable net profit of KRW 2.78 billion, while the third quarter posted sales of KRW 15.5 billion, operating profit of KRW 1.08 billion and an attributable net loss of KRW 5.06 billion.

The attributable net loss totaled KRW 16.9 billion over the last four quarters. Operating profit has been positive in recent quarters while net results remained negative, which indicates that items below operating profit, including non-operating items and income tax, have been driving the bottom line.

05

Industry analysis

Demand from the downstream market comes from rising semiconductor content in automotive chips driven by advancing autonomous driving and ADAS, as well as from the growing difficulty of testing high-performance AI chips.

In 2026 press coverage, an analyst at Valuefinder expected external growth from the expansion of global fabless customers. Automotive chips require supply for more than ten years, so customers select test partners based on financial stability and engineering capability, according to a 2025 field note.

Test demand is cyclical and tied to customers' design schedules and order volumes, so quarterly sales can swing sharply. Itek's fourth-quarter 2025 sales drop of 66.3% and first-quarter 2026 rebound of 151.0% illustrate that pattern.

In the first half of 2025, weaker test demand and higher cost burdens worsened profitability, and cost reductions later partly lifted margins according to 2026 earnings summaries. On competition, Itek highlights its advanced 3D data-processing test experience and a customer base of more than 170 companies.

It competes with domestic and overseas test specialists, so the pace of equipment expansion and overseas sales growth will shape its market position.

Bull factors

Sales recovery trend

The shift from a shrinking top line is a factual point that can be read as evidence of a demand recovery in testing.

Entry into operating profit

Whether the profit seen in the second and third quarters continues into the next quarter is the next point to check. The 2025 operating margin of -0.5% compares with -8.6% in 2023, a narrower loss. Earnings summaries cited cost reduction as a factor that improved profitability in the semiconductor test segment.

Financial headroom

Total liabilities were KRW 38.4 billion at the end of 2025, and the debt ratio of 22.4% was the lowest in the 2022 to 2025 period. Operating cash flow of KRW 18.8 billion in 2025 exceeded the operating loss. The balance sheet provides a relatively light financial burden if sales continue to recover.

Overall view

Itek is a KOSDAQ company centered on system semiconductor testing. Its sales fell from KRW 83.3 billion in 2023 to KRW 48.5 billion in 2025, then bottomed in the fourth quarter of 2025 and rose for three consecutive quarters through the third quarter of 2026.

Operating profit was positive in the second and third quarters of 2026, and the 2025 annual operating loss narrowed sharply from 2023.

On the other hand, the attributable net loss reached KRW 8.28 billion in the first quarter of 2026 and KRW 5.06 billion in the third quarter, with a four-quarter total of KRW 16.9 billion.

Because items below operating profit, such as non-operating items and income tax, drive net results, whether operating profit gains carry through to attributable net income is the central issue.

On financial strength, the debt ratio was 22.4% at the end of 2025 and operating cash flow was KRW 18.8 billion, which is positive. Valuation cannot be framed on earnings multiples at present, so net asset levels and the pace of profit recovery must be considered together.

The next checkpoints are the annual operating margin and attributable net income in the 2026 annual report, and net results in the first-quarter 2027 quarterly report.

Bullish factors include the sales recovery and the appearance of profitable quarters, while bearish factors include the shrinking top line and recurring net losses, and both trends coexist.
