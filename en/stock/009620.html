02

Business structure

SAMBO Industrial is a primary non-ferrous metal manufacturer that has been listed on KOSDAQ since December 1993, with aluminum alloy as its core business (per a Digital Today report).

According to Butler, a third-party data aggregator, the Q2 2026 revenue mix was about 60% aluminum alloy ingots, 39% automotive parts and 1% program supply.

FnGuide's business summary for the year ended December 2024 shows die-casting products at 56.29% domestic and 43.71% export, while aluminum alloy ingots were 84.85% domestic and 15.15% export. The company decided to sell its Seosan plant in July 2024 and cites that sale as the main reason for the revenue decline.

In alloys, management is focusing on cost reduction and stronger operating competitiveness after the sale. The automotive parts segment is expanding supply contracts and revenue through cooperation with vehicle makers.

Customers are tied to vehicle production, so with automotive parts at 39% of revenue, model-level production shifts feed directly into results. Sama Aluminium, a listed peer in the same metals sector, overlaps in aluminum processing, so cost ratios and profitability of new orders serve as the comparison points.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual revenue fell from KRW 455 billion in 2022 to KRW 427.1 billion in 2023 (down 6.1% year on year), KRW 341 billion in 2024 (down 20.2%) and KRW 285.7 billion in 2025 (down 16.2%), a three-year decline running from 2023 through 2025.

Operating profit went from KRW 8.38 billion in 2022 to an operating loss of KRW 620 million in 2023, which turned into operating profit of KRW 629 million in 2024 and KRW 7.89 billion in 2025, up 1,155.0% year on year.

The operating margin was 1.8% in 2022, -0.1% in 2023, 0.2% in 2024 and 2.8% in 2025, a low level that improved modestly in 2025. The company attributes the 2025 operating gain to better cost ratios and profitability on new order items.

The net loss attributable to controlling shareholders widened from KRW 12.9 billion in 2022 to KRW 18.4 billion in 2023 and KRW 23.6 billion in 2024, then narrowed to KRW 5.21 billion in 2025, and the company has been loss-making for four consecutive years from 2022 through 2025.

Net loss including non-controlling interests was KRW 11 billion in 2022, KRW 16.7 billion in 2023, KRW 22.6 billion in 2024 and KRW 3.25 billion in 2025, a different measure from controlling-shareholder net income.

Operating cash flow declined every year, from KRW 23.3 billion in 2022 to KRW 14.1 billion, KRW 9.27 billion and KRW 2.69 billion in 2025.

On a quarterly basis, Q3 2025 revenue was KRW 72.9 billion with operating profit of KRW 2.74 billion, Q4 2025 swung to an operating loss of KRW 2.65 billion, and Q1 2026 and Q2 2026 operating profit recovered to KRW 5.39 billion and KRW 7.22 billion.

Q3 2026 revenue was KRW 93.1 billion and operating profit KRW 4.05 billion, down 6.5% and 43.8% from Q2 respectively.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders moved from a KRW 220 million profit in Q1 2026 to KRW 1.82 billion in Q2 and KRW 2.41 billion in Q3, a rise across two consecutive quarters after the Q1 2026 low.

Over the most recent four quarters, the cumulative net loss attributable to controlling shareholders totaled KRW 952 million.

05

Industry analysis

Aluminum is widely used as a core material for vehicle lightweighting. FnGuide's company summary attributes rising aluminum content to lightweighting trends and low-carbon policy, and it expects stable demand from new vehicle development and greater use of aluminum parts. That is the company summary's own outlook.

The aluminum alloy ingot business earns margins depending on how much raw material cost it can pass through to selling prices, so cost-ratio improvement is the key variable for profit. After the Seosan plant sale in 2024, SAMBO's revenue base shrank while it reshapes its portfolio.

Against listed aluminum processors such as Sama Aluminium, its 2.8% operating margin in 2025 is low, which makes cost-structure progress the central yardstick for its competitive position.

Expanding supply contracts in automotive parts broadens the customer base, while dependence on specific customers and vehicle models remains a point to monitor. Reading peer results and sector demand together gives a more precise view of the company's position.

Risk factors

Financial leverage and capital raising

A debt ratio of 1,047.4% and a small equity base for controlling shareholders could constrain financing capacity and raise financial costs. An amended rights-offering report was filed in September 2026, and the scale and structure of any capital raise could affect the value of existing shareholders' stakes.

Listing-maintenance requirements

A September 28, 2026 market notice flagged the stock as a possible administrative-issue candidate because market capitalization fell below KRW 20 billion. If the notice leads to a formal designation, trading conditions and access could change. Market capitalization moves with the share price, so this requirement is directly tied to price action.

Customer concentration and raw material swings

Automotive parts at about 39% of revenue expose the company to volume swings from vehicle production and model-level demand shifts. Aluminum alloy ingots at about 60% of revenue make margins sensitive to how far raw material cost changes can be passed on to customers. If cost gains do not persist, the scope for margin recovery could be limited.

Overall view
