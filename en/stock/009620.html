02

Business structure

SAMBO Industrial was founded in 1974 and listed on KOSDAQ in 1993, with its headquarters in Changwon, South Gyeongsang Province. The company collects used aluminum scrap and re-melts it to produce aluminum alloy ingots, which are supplied to the automotive parts, electronics, and steel industries.

Its business is organized into the Aluminum Alloy Ingot segment, the Automotive Parts segment (through subsidiary Sambo Auto, among others), and a broadcast program supply segment (Cmax Communications). Its core die-casting and foundry aluminum alloy ingots are tied to automakers' demand for lightweight materials.

More recently, the company signed a business cooperation and joint development agreement with Rio Tinto to develop a next-generation scandium-based aluminum alloy. A company executive stated that a test order covering Hyundai/Kia production-vehicle shock tower components was planned for September 2026.

The project is reportedly still at the stage of verifying performance and manufacturability ahead of commercialization. Peers in the aluminum and non-ferrous metals space include Korea Zinc, Sama Aluminium, and Almac, with SAMBO Industrial positioned as a smaller player specializing in recycled aluminum materials.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual revenue fell from KRW 455 billion in 2022 to KRW 427.1 billion in 2023 (down 6.1% year over year), KRW 341 billion in 2024 (down 20.2%), and KRW 285.7 billion in 2025 (down 16.2%), marking three consecutive years of decline from 2023 through 2025.

Over the same period, operating profit swung from KRW 8.38 billion in 2022 to an operating loss of KRW 620 million in 2023, then turned profitable again in 2024 at KRW 629 million before rising 1,155.0% year over year to KRW 7.89 billion in 2025. The operating margin recovered from -0.1% in 2023 to 2.8% in 2025.

By contrast, net profit attributable to controlling shareholders posted losses for four consecutive years from 2022 through 2025, widening from a loss of KRW 12.9 billion in 2022 to KRW 18.4 billion in 2023 and KRW 23.6 billion in 2024, before narrowing to a loss of KRW 5.21 billion in 2025.

On a quarterly basis, revenue bottomed at KRW 69.9 billion in the fourth quarter of 2025 among the last five quarters, then rose for two consecutive quarters to KRW 77.3 billion in the first quarter of 2026 (up 10.5% quarter over quarter) and KRW 99.5 billion in the second quarter of 2026 (up 28.8% quarter over quarter and 31.5% year over year).

Operating profit swung from an operating loss of KRW 2.65 billion in the fourth quarter of 2025 to a profit of KRW 5.39 billion in the first quarter of 2026, then rose further to KRW 7.22 billion in the second quarter, up 34.1% quarter over quarter and 66.4% year over year.

Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders remained volatile quarter to quarter, with losses of KRW 1.4 billion in the third quarter of 2025 and KRW 5.4 billion in the fourth quarter, before turning profitable at KRW 220 million in the first quarter of 2026 and rising to KRW 1.82 billion in the second quarter; however, the sum over the last four quarters (third quarter of 2025 through second quarter of 2026) was still a net loss of KRW 4.76 billion.

The company attributed the 2025 revenue decline mainly to the divestiture of its Seosan plant, while the operating profit improvement was attributed to a better cost ratio and improved profitability on newly won order items.

05

Industry analysis

The aluminum alloy ingot industry is closely tied to automakers' demand for lightweight materials, and the spread of electrification has repeatedly been cited as a driver of demand for lighter materials to offset the added weight of batteries.

The aluminum content per vehicle has reportedly risen steadily over recent years, with adoption of aluminum parts expanding as automakers develop new models. At the same time, the raw material, aluminum scrap, relies heavily on imports, making the business sensitive to global commodity prices and currency movements.

The sector includes listed non-ferrous and aluminum materials companies such as Korea Zinc, Sama Aluminium, and Almac, and compared with these peers SAMBO Industrial ranks among the smaller players by both market capitalization and revenue scale.

Using recycled aluminum as a feedstock aligns with low-carbon policy trends, but it also exposes the company to variability in the supply and pricing of high-purity scrap.

The development of higher value-added materials such as scandium-added alloys has been cited as a factor that could differentiate the company from commodity-grade ingot producers if commercialization succeeds.

Outlook

The company raised funds through several capital increases in 2026, including one disclosure stating that roughly KRW 16.2 billion would be invested in constructing a new plant facility.

According to that filing, the new plant is intended to improve production efficiency through a floor-separated process layout and to expand production capacity. However, the specific completion and start-up timeline requires confirmation through further disclosures.

The scandium alloy project was reported to be at the test-order stage in September 2026 for Hyundai/Kia production parts, and the company has mentioned the potential to expand into directly scandium-added materials and into mobility and industrial applications beyond automotive.

That said, this remains at the research and verification stage, and the timing and scope of any mass-production application have not been confirmed.

On the financial side, repeated third-party and rights-offering capital increases continue, leaving further financing needs and terms as a variable that could affect both share count and the balance sheet going forward.

Whether the operating profit recovery seen in 2025 and the improvement trend through the first half of 2026 continues into the second half will need to be confirmed through upcoming periodic reports.

Bull factors

Operating Profit Recovery

Operating profit turned positive in 2025 at KRW 7.89 billion, up 1,155.0% year over year, and continued rising to KRW 5.39 billion and KRW 7.22 billion in the first and second quarters of 2026, respectively. The operating margin also improved from -0.1% in 2023 to 2.8% in 2025. A better cost ratio and improved profitability on newly won order items were cited as drivers of this trend.

Revenue Rebound Signal

Quarterly revenue, which had fallen to KRW 69.9 billion in the fourth quarter of 2025, rose for two straight quarters to KRW 77.3 billion in the first quarter of 2026 and KRW 99.5 billion in the second quarter. Second-quarter revenue was also up 31.5% year over year. This marks a quarterly pattern that runs counter to the three straight years of annual revenue decline.

New Alloy Development Project

Through cooperation with Rio Tinto, the company is developing a next-generation aluminum alloy using scandium, and a test order for Hyundai/Kia production parts was reported for September 2026.

If commercialized successfully, this has been cited as a potential entry point into a higher value-added product category distinct from commodity-grade ingots. However, this remains at the verification stage and commercialization has not been confirmed.

Bear factors

Shrinking Revenue Base

Annual revenue declined for three straight years, from KRW 455 billion in 2022 to KRW 285.7 billion in 2025. The company cited the divestiture of its Seosan plant as a factor behind the decline, which implies a reduction in the production base.

While quarterly revenue rebounded in the first half of 2026, it has not yet been confirmed whether this restores revenue to prior annual levels.

High Leverage and Repeated Capital Raises

The debt ratio rose for four consecutive years, from 583.2% in 2022 to 1,047.4% in 2025. Over the same period, operating cash flow fell from KRW 23.3 billion in 2022 to KRW 2.69 billion in 2025.

Multiple third-party and rights-offering capital increases continued into 2026, increasing the likelihood of further changes in share count.

Continued Net Losses to Controlling Shareholders

Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders posted losses for four consecutive years from 2022 through 2025.

Although small profits were recorded in the first and second quarters of 2026, the sum over the last four quarters (third quarter of 2025 through second quarter of 2026) was still a net loss of KRW 4.76 billion.

It appears that more time is needed before the operating profit turnaround translates into a stable profit for controlling shareholders.

Risk factors

Balance Sheet Risk

With the debt ratio reaching 1,047.4% at the end of 2025, multiple capital increases continued through 2026. A past audit report had noted uncertainty regarding the company's ability to continue as a going concern, and a filing in March 2026 disclosed a delay in submitting the audit report. If additional financing is required, there is a possibility of further dilution for existing shareholders.

Raw Material Price Volatility Risk

Aluminum scrap relies heavily on imports, exposing the company to global commodity prices and currency fluctuations. Changes in the cost ratio can directly affect the operating margin.

Since an improved cost ratio was cited as a key factor behind the 2025 operating profit improvement, a reversal in cost trends could similarly weigh on profitability.

New Business Uncertainty

The scandium aluminum alloy project was reported to be at the test-order stage in September 2026, but mass-production adoption has not been confirmed. The timeline for completion and start-up of the new plant investment has also not yet been clearly disclosed.

The possibility that these new business investments may not translate into the expected revenue or profit contribution cannot be ruled out.

Overall view

SAMBO Industrial has shown signs of earnings improvement, including a return to operating profit in 2025 and continued revenue and operating profit growth through the first half of 2026.

At the same time, net profit attributable to controlling shareholders posted losses for four consecutive years and remained in loss on a trailing four-quarter basis, while the debt ratio rose for four straight years to reach 1,047.4% at the end of 2025.

The repeated capital raises through 2026 appear aimed at funding working capital and new plant investment, but they also point to financial fragility and the potential for further changes in share count.

New material development such as the scandium aluminum alloy project is cited as a potential medium- to long-term growth driver, but it remains at the verification stage.

Taken together, this is a phase where a profit-recovery direction coexists with balance-sheet strain, and the trend will need to be reconfirmed through upcoming quarterly reports and disclosures related to the new business. Readers should form their own judgment based on these facts.
