Ilshin Spinning: Textile Cost Recovery Alongside Non-Textile Units

02

Business structure

Ilshin Spinning began operations in 1951 and its core business is spun yarn and blended yarn products, including cotton and polyester blends.

In 2023, before intercompany eliminations, its textile manufacturing segment generated KRW 439.8 billion in sales, the cosmetics and related segment KRW 72.4 billion, real estate leasing KRW 35.6 billion, liquor import and sales KRW 35.2 billion, and fund operations KRW 17 billion.

The textile segment accounts for the bulk of revenue, and the 2023 sales decline was attributed to weaker textile sales. In the same year the cosmetics segment rose 2.7%, real estate leasing 9.2%, and fund operations 54%. A 2018 press report put the cosmetics share of 2017 profit at 25.8%.

Beyond cosmetics, the company distributes the Godiva chocolate brand in Korea. A 2018 report ranked it third among domestic cotton spinners by spinning equipment capacity, with a share of 18.69%.

Spun yarn demand comes mainly from weaving and knitting customers, linking the business to apparel demand, while the consumer segment reaches end consumers through retail channels. Total liabilities of KRW 171.1 billion at the end of 2025 are modest relative to equity, leaving room for segment expansion.

Earnings analysis

Annual sales fell from KRW 593.1 billion in 2022 to KRW 539.4 billion in 2023 (down 9.0% year on year), KRW 523.7 billion in 2024 (down 2.9%) and KRW 517.2 billion in 2025 (down 1.2%).

Sales declined for three consecutive years from 2023 through 2025, with 2022 the largest year and 2025 the smallest in the 2022 to 2025 window.

Operating results moved from an operating loss of KRW 16 billion in 2022 to an operating loss of KRW 1.02 billion in 2023, a narrower loss, and then to operating profit of KRW 9.06 billion in 2024, a swing from a loss into profit.

In 2025 operating profit reached KRW 12.4 billion, up 36.9%, and the company attributed the change mainly to a lower textile cost ratio and better consumer goods results. Operating margins improved from -2.7% in 2022 to -0.2% in 2023, 1.7% in 2024 and 2.4% in 2025.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 114.3 billion in 2022, far above the operating loss that year, which suggests non-operating items had a large effect.

It then fell to KRW 7.84 billion in 2023 (down 93.1%), rose to KRW 28.1 billion in 2024 and KRW 33.2 billion in 2025, increasing for two consecutive years.

Quarterly sales rose from KRW 124.3 billion in the third quarter of 2025 to KRW 134.6 billion in the fourth quarter, peaked at KRW 140.5 billion in the first quarter of 2026, and then eased to KRW 138.5 billion and KRW 131 billion in the second and third quarters.

Operating profit was KRW 12.5 billion in the second quarter of 2026 and KRW 11.3 billion in the third, while net income attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 30 billion in the second quarter and KRW 2.55 billion in the third.

Reports said a partial disposal of an investee stake, with total proceeds of about KRW 81 billion, cut the holding to 16.19%, which is a point to examine in the quarterly report notes given the gap between second-quarter operating profit and controlling net income. Net income attributable to controlling shareholders for the last four quarters totals KRW 48.4 billion.

Industry analysis

Cotton spinning economics depend on the spread between raw cotton costs and product pricing, and the company itself credited a lower cost ratio for its 2025 improvement. Because the 2023 sales decline came from weaker textile sales, the pace of demand recovery in the core business is a key variable for earnings.

Listed peers in the same sector include Youngone, Hyosung TNC, Pangrim, Kyungbang, Hansae and F&F, whose business models differ in yarn, finished goods, materials and brands.

Ilshin Spinning combines yarn production with consumer goods, real estate and investment segments, so its results can move differently from pure textile companies.

A 2018 report ranked the company among the top three domestic cotton spinners by equipment capacity, a position that may not match today's share and should be updated with recent data.

Sales have declined for two quarters since the first-quarter 2026 peak, which suggests core demand and pricing pass-through have not yet stabilized. Sales in the third quarter of 2026 still rose 5.4% from a year earlier, a sign of gradual recovery.

Judging the cycle position requires confirming both raw cotton price moves and export demand. Because the sector is exposed to export competition and input costs, how the company's cost-ratio gains compare with industry averages is the next analytical question.

A growing non-textile base

Balance sheet capacity and cash generation

Bear factors

Persistent sales decline

Sales fell from KRW 593.1 billion in 2022 to KRW 517.2 billion in 2025, declining for three consecutive years from 2023. In 2026 sales peaked in the first quarter at KRW 140.5 billion and then fell for two straight quarters. Third-quarter sales rose 5.4% from a year earlier but still declined from the prior quarter.

Quarterly earnings volatility and reliance on one-off items

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 30 billion in the second quarter of 2026, well above operating profit of KRW 12.5 billion, and then fell to KRW 2.55 billion in the third quarter.

Reports of a stake disposal worth about KRW 81 billion and a remaining 16.19% holding point to a non-recurring element. Whether earnings lean on such items needs to be checked.

A history of weak textile sales

Sales fell in 2023 because textile manufacturing sales were weak. The company still posted an operating loss of KRW 1.02 billion that year, and it took until 2024 for the core business to reach profit. If the textile cost-ratio improvement proves temporary, the earnings recovery could weaken.

Risk factors

Cost and demand risk

Spinning profits swing with the spread between raw cotton costs and product prices. Because the 2025 improvement was mainly driven by a lower cost ratio, a rebound in raw material prices could reverse margin gains. If export competition and weaker demand coincide, the sales decline could extend.

Dependence on non-operating income

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders in 2022 was far above that year's operating loss, which suggests large non-operating contributions. The second quarter of 2026 is also linked to an investee stake disposal. Without separating operating earnings from net income, the quality of results can be misread.

Concentration and consumer segment volatility

Textile manufacturing accounted for KRW 439.8 billion of 2023 sales, the bulk of the business. Cosmetics, liquor and real estate complement earnings but can be affected by consumer spending and distribution channels. Because the consumer segment was one driver of the 2025 gains, its durability also needs to be checked.
