02

Business structure

HVM makes Cu-, Fe- and Ni-based specialty alloys using vacuum melting and supplies them as high-cleanliness ingots and billets.

The Seosan No. 2 plant, completed in June 2025, houses what the company describes as a 12-ton vacuum induction melting facility, the largest of its kind in Korea, along with vacuum arc remelting, electroslag remelting (ESR) and reactive vacuum arc remelting (RVAR) equipment.

By end-use industry, space accounted for 56% and aerospace and defense for 14% of sales in the first nine months of 2025, according to a securities research visit report. Semiconductors were about 16% and petrochemicals about 14% over the same period.

Previously the mix centered on materials for semiconductors, displays and petrochemicals, but supply has recently been widening into space, aerospace and defense. On March 24, 2026, HVM announced it had received approval as a Raw Material Tier 1 Flight Supplier after an audit by a major U.S. private space company.

On March 5, 2026, it said a space-related superalloy supply contract was raised from KRW 9.2 billion to KRW 18.5 billion. In aerospace, HVM reportedly obtained NADCAP accreditation and is in contact with aerospace and defense customers over orders.

One corporate analysis site put the share of sales from a single customer at 44.3%, flagging customer concentration as a key risk. Peers such as Poongsan and Korea Zinc focus on defense and non-ferrous materials, so their portfolios differ from HVM's superalloy-centered structure.

04

Earnings analysis

Revenue rose from KRW 41.5 billion in 2023 to KRW 45.1 billion in 2024, up 8.9% year on year, and reached KRW 66.5 billion in 2025, up 47.4%. Revenue has grown two years in a row, from 2024 through 2025, with 2025 the largest year and 2023 the smallest in the 2023–2025 period.

Operating profit went from KRW 3.73 billion in 2023 to an operating loss of KRW 6.79 billion in 2024, a swing into deficit, and then to an operating profit of KRW 5.84 billion in 2025, a turn back to profit. The operating margin was 9.0% in 2023, -15.0% in 2024 and 8.8% in 2025.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders was a loss of KRW 6.06 billion in 2023 and a loss of KRW 8.39 billion in 2024, with the loss widening, before turning to a profit of KRW 12.6 billion in 2025. Net income including non-controlling interests was also KRW 12.6 billion in 2025.

One corporate analysis site calculated that 44% of 2025 net income was non-cash valuation gains, while a securities research report credited a cost of sales ratio improvement from 100.2% to 81.9% for the margin recovery.

Operating cash flow was KRW 897 million in 2023, -KRW 14 billion in 2024 and -KRW 8.83 billion in 2025, so cash outflows continued after the profit turn.

Quarterly operating profit moved from an operating loss of KRW 296 million in 2025 Q3 to an operating profit of KRW 1.24 billion in Q4 and KRW 4.42 billion in 2026 Q1, then KRW 2.01 billion in Q2 and an operating loss of KRW 763 million in Q3.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 9.82 billion in 2025 Q4, KRW 3.41 billion in 2026 Q1, a loss of KRW 9 billion in Q2 and a loss of KRW 3.28 billion in Q3.

Q3 2026 revenue of KRW 26.3 billion rose 96.1% from a year earlier, yet the operating loss widened, so sales growth did not carry through to profit. A securities firm's report from November 2025 cited delayed full operation of the second plant and higher fixed costs as reasons for weak profitability in 2025 Q3.

Industry analysis

Space launch demand is widely cited in securities research as the main growth driver. Securities analysts argue that rising launch frequency by a U.S. private rocket customer is expanding demand for advanced space-grade metals.

In May 2026, media reported that the SpaceX theme boosted expectations for the space and aerospace sector. Aerospace and defense demand is growing alongside it, and NADCAP accreditation is seen as widening the route into aerospace and defense markets.

Space demand, however, swings sharply from quarter to quarter depending on launch schedules and customer orders. Quarterly revenue in 2026 ran KRW 23.3 billion in Q1, KRW 29.2 billion in Q2 and KRW 26.3 billion in Q3, moving up and down.

A securities report from November 2025 projected a decline in the existing semiconductor and electronics business for 2025. Poongsan and Korea Zinc, peers in the same sector, focus on defense and non-ferrous materials, so they are hard to compare directly with HVM's space superalloy business.

In its disclosure, the company noted that shipment schedules, quality inspections, exchange rates and changes in domestic and overseas market conditions can affect deliveries. Where the business sits in the industry cycle depends on how steadily launch volumes and customer orders continue.

06

Outlook

On November 16, 2026, the company disclosed a 2026 fiscal year revenue outlook of KRW 119.1 billion under fair disclosure rules. No forecasts for operating profit, pre-tax income or net income were included.

The outlook is based on an internal business plan that reflects recent operating results, planned shipments from major customers and the order backlog.

The disclosure states that actual results may differ from the outlook depending on shipment schedules, delivery procedures including quality inspections, exchange rates and changes in domestic and overseas market conditions.

Based on disclosed results, the highest quarterly revenue in 2026 so far was KRW 29.2 billion in Q2, so reaching the outlook would require Q4 revenue well above that level.

A superalloy supply contract disclosed in March 2026 was listed with a contract period from January 8 to November 2, 2026, and how much of the increased contract value is recognized as revenue is the next item to track.

A securities research report said that after the second plant reached full operation, the company expects to secure up to KRW 350 billion in additional production capacity. Talks with aerospace and defense customers over orders are reportedly ongoing.

The size of the financing for expansion, including convertible bonds, and its dilution effect should be revisited in the original disclosures. When the 2026 annual report discloses actual 2026 revenue and operating profit, the gap against the KRW 119.1 billion outlook will become visible.

Bull factors

Space demand and supplier qualifications

Space made up 56% of sales in the first nine months of 2025 per a securities research report, and a superalloy supply contract was raised from KRW 9.2 billion to KRW 18.5 billion in March 2026. The March 2026 approval as a Tier 1 raw material flight supplier widened its position in the global space supply chain. The securities view that rising launch frequency feeds into material demand is the basis for this pillar.

2025 turnaround and margin recovery

Operating profit in 2025 was KRW 5.84 billion, a turn from the 2024 operating loss. A securities research report credited a cost of sales ratio improvement from 100.2% to 81.9% for a sharp margin recovery. The operating margin moved from -15.0% in 2024 to 8.8% in 2025.

Capacity expansion and aerospace and defense entry

The Seosan No. 2 plant was completed in June 2025 with a 12-ton vacuum induction melting facility. A securities research report said full operation of the second plant is a step toward securing KRW 350 billion in additional capacity. With NADCAP accreditation, contacts with aerospace and defense customers are reportedly expanding.

Bear factors

Quarterly earnings volatility

Operating profit fell 54.5% from the prior quarter to KRW 2.01 billion in 2026 Q2, and Q3 swung to an operating loss of KRW 763 million. Attributable net income has been negative for two consecutive quarters, Q2 and Q3 of 2026. Over the last four quarters, attributable net income totals only KRW 947 million.

Cash flow and earnings quality

Operating cash flow in 2025 was -KRW 8.83 billion, diverging from the operating profit turn. One corporate analysis site calculated that 44% of 2025 net income was non-cash valuation gains. Some analysis also flags a heavy valuation allowance on inventory, which makes inventory turnover a key issue.

Customer concentration and dilution

An analysis putting single-customer exposure at 44.3% means order changes from one customer could pass straight into results. The company's outlook gives revenue only, leaving no operating profit guidance. Convertible bond issuance has been discussed, so future dilution is also a bearish factor.

10

Risk factors

Customer and demand concentration

Single-customer exposure is high and space accounts for a reported 56% of sales, so changes in launch schedules or orders could swing quarterly revenue sharply. Sales can rise while profit falls, as in 2026 Q2 and Q3, and that pattern could repeat. Even with contract value increases, delivery delays would push back revenue recognition.

Financial burden and dilution

At the end of 2025, total liabilities were KRW 83.2 billion against total equity of KRW 90.1 billion, a debt-to-equity ratio of 92.3%. With operating cash flow negative in 2025, relying on outside funding for expansion could add financial strain. Conversion of convertible bonds into shares would increase the share count and dilute per-share metrics.

Quality, exchange rate and policy variables

Deliveries depend on customer quality inspections and shipment timing, and the company noted that exchange rates and domestic and overseas market conditions could also make actual results differ from its outlook.

Aerospace and space materials typically require long qualification processes, so expanding the customer base may be slow. Progress on the government-backed superalloy localization project is another key variable.

Overall view

HVM swung to annual profit in 2025 on the back of vacuum melting technology and its qualifications as a space and aerospace supplier, growing both sales and operating profit. A high space share and heavy single-customer dependence are both the source of growth and the cause of volatility.

Attributable net losses have run for two quarters since Q2 2026, and Q3 turned to an operating loss, so profit has not stabilized even as sales grew. Operating cash flow was -KRW 8.83 billion in 2025 and much of net income was valuation gains, which makes earnings quality a point to examine.

The valuation sits at a very high multiple of the last four quarters' earnings, so the reading depends on whether future earnings rise toward the outlook.

The next checkpoints are the 2026 annual report's revenue and operating profit, whether the losses end in the Q1 2027 report, and the expansion timeline and funding method. Because bullish and bearish factors coexist, the key thing to watch is how the weight of these two sets of factors shifts in upcoming disclosures.
