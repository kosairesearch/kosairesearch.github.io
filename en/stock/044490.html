Taewoong: Nuclear and Wind Orders Face an Earnings Test

02

Business structure

Taewoong is a free-forging specialist founded in 1981 that produces core forged parts for end markets including wind power, plants, shipbuilding, power generation and industrial machinery.

The company runs two divisions: a forging division serving wind, shipbuilding, power, plant and industrial machinery customers, and a steelmaking division that produces ingots and round blooms and supplies them to the forging division first.

On equipment, it is described as one of the few operators with a 15,000-ton forging press and a 9,500-class ring rolling mill, said to be the world's largest. Offshore wind flanges form the core of the earnings base.

Taewoong focused on nuclear and offshore wind, where Chinese entrants have limited access, and it has held through the forging downturn. IBK Securities said in a September 2026 report that Taewoong is effectively the only company outside China able to produce 11-meter-class flanges.

In nuclear, it supplied auxiliary forged parts for GE Hitachi's Generation 3.5 SMR, and in June 2026 it won a main-equipment forging order for TerraPower's Natrium SMR unit 1 through Marmen of Canada.

In March 2026 it disclosed a full-set supply contract for spent fuel storage casks (CASK) for a Czech project, a product reported to carry a higher unit price than transport casks.

In gas turbines, it is co-developing rotor shafts with Mitsubishi Heavy Industries, having completed stage 2 of eight last year and started stage 3 in 2026.

04

Earnings analysis

On a disclosed basis, sales rose from KRW 393.9 billion in 2022 to KRW 443.8 billion in 2023, a 12.7% increase, before falling 12.9% to KRW 386.3 billion in 2024 and 9.5% to KRW 349.7 billion in 2025, two consecutive annual declines from 2024.

Operating profit rose from KRW 2.93 billion in 2022 to KRW 39.5 billion in 2023, a 1,249.8% increase, then fell 42.3% to KRW 22.8 billion in 2024 and 78.0% to KRW 5.02 billion in 2025, again two consecutive declines.

The operating margin moved from 0.7% in 2022 to 8.9% in 2023, 5.9% in 2024 and 1.4% in 2025, so the recovery seen in 2023 did not carry through.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 547 million in 2022, KRW 34.2 billion in 2023, KRW 24.7 billion in 2024 and KRW 5.41 billion in 2025, and net income including non-controlling interests was reported at the same amounts.

At the end of 2025, total equity stood at KRW 609.4 billion, all attributable to controlling shareholders, and total liabilities at KRW 200.5 billion, with the debt-to-equity ratio down from 57.6% in 2022 to 32.9% in 2025.

Operating cash flow was KRW 9.18 billion in 2022, KRW 19.9 billion in 2023, KRW 66.7 billion in 2024 and KRW 27.8 billion in 2025.

Quarterly, operating profit of KRW 700 million in Q3 2025 turned into an operating loss of KRW 1.37 billion in Q4 2025 on sales of KRW 90.5 billion, then returned to profit with KRW 2.79 billion in Q1 2026 on sales of KRW 88.3 billion.

Q2 2026 sales were KRW 105.5 billion with operating profit of KRW 3.2 billion, up 14.5% from the prior quarter. Q3 2026 sales were KRW 87.5 billion, down 17.1% from the prior quarter and up 1.2% from a year earlier, yet the company posted an operating loss of KRW 8.28 billion, a swing into loss from a year earlier.

Net losses attributable to controlling shareholders were KRW 375 million in Q4 2025 and KRW 15.9 billion in Q3 2026, and the total over the last four quarters from Q4 2025 to Q3 2026 was KRW 7.72 billion.

Shinhan Investment Corp. said in an August 2026 report that the weakness came from product mix rather than weaker demand, as industrial machinery and shipbuilding rose as a share of sales while higher-margin industrial plant work shrank.

The same report said about KRW 20 billion of offshore wind sales slipped from Q2 into Q3, but the disclosed Q3 result was an operating loss.

05

Industry analysis

The forging industry grew during the heavy-industry boom of the 2000s, but later heavy-industry orders shrank, fixed costs rose and Chinese steel growth added pressure, and forging companies that could not secure demand went under.

Taewoong is viewed as having survived by focusing on energy sectors such as nuclear and offshore wind, where Chinese entrants have limited access.

The global offshore wind market saw orders slow temporarily in early 2026 due to higher interest rates and project delays, and a March 2026 securities report saw order recovery possible after the second half.

European restrictions on Chinese components in offshore wind are cited by IBK Securities in September 2026 as a factor supporting Taewoong's flange orders.

In nuclear, the company is extending its large-component experience into the small modular reactor market, having supplied forged parts for a Canadian Generation 3.5 SMR project, which a March 2026 report described as a reference few domestic peers hold.

Nuclear forgings require strict quality standards and certification, so suppliers that enter a supply chain are hard to replace. In competition, its 11-meter-class flange capability is described as effectively unmatched outside China.

Within the company, however, the 2026 results showed that a larger share of low-margin industrial machinery and shipbuilding work pulls down the margin.

Outlook

Securities analyst estimates for 2026 diverge.

IBK Securities projected 2026 sales of KRW 394.6 billion and operating profit of KRW 21.5 billion in a September 2026 report, while Shinhan Investment Corp. projected KRW 380.3 billion in sales and KRW 29.8 billion in operating profit for 2026 in an August 2026 report, and KRW 440.0 billion and KRW 44.1 billion for 2027.

Both sets of estimates came out in August and September 2026, before the Q3 2026 operating loss was disclosed, so the Q3 result is not reflected in them. On orders, the backlog stood at KRW 184.1 billion at the end of March 2026, and Q1 2026 new orders of KRW 96.0 billion exceeded product sales of KRW 84.9 billion.

For the TerraPower follow-on order, the company expects talks could take concrete form within three to four months, but as of a September 2026 report the order was not yet confirmed.

Talks with HD Hyundai Heavy Industries on SMR cooperation are under way, and HD Hyundai Heavy Industries plans to invest KRW 238.6 billion in a dedicated SMR main-equipment plant at its Ulsan shipyard (September 2026 reports).

On the planned 30% capacity expansion, IBK Securities described it as a turning point signaling the end of the forging downcycle and the start of a growth cycle.

In gas turbines, stage 3 of rotor shaft development with Mitsubishi Heavy Industries is under way, and the company has said it expects a role in the forging supply chain if the first US-investment gas combined-cycle project proceeds.

Shinhan Investment Corp. named a better Q3 operating margin and Q4 revenue recognition from CASK and industrial plant projects as the checkpoints for a profitability recovery (August 2026 report).

Bull factors

Nuclear and SMR order pipeline

Taewoong won a main-equipment forging order for TerraPower's Natrium SMR unit 1 in June 2026, and reports say a unit 2 follow-on is under discussion. In March 2026 it secured a full-set CASK supply contract for a Czech project, a product reported to carry a higher unit price than transport casks.

Shinhan Investment Corp. said in an August 2026 report that SMR, Czech CASK and Sizewell C projects would be reflected from 2027 onward, strengthening medium-term growth.

Offshore wind flange capability and expansion

With Europe restricting Chinese components in offshore wind, IBK Securities said in September 2026 that Taewoong is effectively the only company outside China able to produce 11-meter-class flanges. The same report viewed a 30% capacity expansion as a turning point marking the start of a growth cycle. Enlarging equipment to widen order scale is also cited as a strength.

Backlog and order coverage

The backlog stood at KRW 184.1 billion at the end of March 2026, and Q1 2026 new orders of KRW 96.0 billion exceeded product sales of KRW 84.9 billion. Operating profit returned to profit at KRW 2.79 billion in Q1 2026 and rose 14.5% to KRW 3.2 billion in Q2 2026. If this continues, the conversion of backlog into quarterly results could become visible.

Bear factors

Swing to an operating loss in Q3 2026

Q3 2026 sales were KRW 87.5 billion, up 1.2% from a year earlier, yet the company posted an operating loss of KRW 8.28 billion and a controlling-shareholder net loss of KRW 15.9 billion.

Shinhan Investment Corp. had expected deferred wind volume to be recognized in Q3 2026, but the disclosed Q3 result was an operating loss. If margin recovery is delayed, revisions to estimates become the central question.

Declining sales and thin margins

Sales fell 12.9% to KRW 386.3 billion in 2024 and 9.5% to KRW 349.7 billion in 2025, two consecutive declines, while the operating margin was 1.4% in 2025. Operating profit fell 78.0% to KRW 5.02 billion in 2025.

Shinhan Investment Corp. attributed the weakness to product mix rather than weaker demand, but a larger share of low-margin industrial machinery could keep pressure on margins.

Project delays and unconfirmed orders

About KRW 20 billion of offshore wind sales planned for Q2 2026 slipped into Q3, showing how recognition timing can shift. The follow-on TerraPower order is still an expectation the company holds and was not confirmed as of September 2026 reporting. Global offshore wind orders slowed temporarily in early 2026 due to higher interest rates and project delays.

10

Risk factors

Revenue timing risk

As seen when offshore wind volume slipped from Q2 into Q3 2026, recognition timing for large forgings depends on customer schedules. Nuclear and SMR projects can also shift revenue between quarters depending on certification and delivery timing. This adds to quarterly volatility.

Customer and partner concentration risk

The TerraPower order came through Marmen of Canada, and that relationship is serving as the channel into nuclear. Heavy reliance on a single nuclear customer or intermediary could concentrate order flow. Nuclear parts require long certification, and talks for the first TerraPower order took about seven to eight months.

Equipment transition and utilization risk

In 2025, equipment upgrades reduced operating days and temporarily cut sales and profit, according to one analysis. Fixed-cost pressure eases only after utilization rises following the investment.

Whether the Q3 2026 operating loss reflects utilization or product mix will shape the pace of recovery, making utilization the next checkpoint.

12

Overall view

Taewoong's sales and operating profit peaked in 2023 at KRW 443.8 billion and KRW 39.5 billion, and both declined through 2025, when the operating margin was 1.4%.

Operating profit was positive in Q1 and Q2 2026 but turned to an operating loss of KRW 8.28 billion in Q3 2026, with a controlling-shareholder net loss of KRW 15.9 billion.

Its strengths lie in nuclear and offshore wind flanges, where Chinese entrants have limited access, including the TerraPower SMR forging order, the Czech CASK contract and 11-meter-class flange capability.

Against this, the Q3 swing into loss, the quarterly deferral of offshore wind sales and a rising share of low-margin industrial machinery are cited as obstacles to margin recovery.

Analyst estimates predate the Q3 disclosure, so whether they are revised is the open question, and net losses attributable to controlling shareholders over the last four quarters total KRW 7.72 billion.

The next regular report is the 2026 annual report, and before it the items to confirm are the TerraPower follow-on order and Q4 CASK revenue recognition.
