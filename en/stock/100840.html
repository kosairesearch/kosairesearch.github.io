02

Business structure

SNTEnergy was founded in 2008 as a spin-off from S&TC and designs and manufactures heat exchangers for oil, gas and petrochemical plants. Its core product is the air-cooled heat exchanger, or air cooler, and it also supplies heat recovery steam generators (HRSG) for power plants and condensers.

An LS Securities report published in January 2026 showed 2025 air cooler sales of KRW 543 billion and HRSG and other sales of KRW 64 billion, which means air coolers made up roughly 90% of the year's revenue. Customers are mainly global EPC contractors.

Hyundai Motor Securities noted in March 2026 that its products are installed at plants in the UAE and Saudi Arabia, including Hail&Ghasha, Fadhili and Jafurah, and that Bechtel, the EPC contractor for the Woodside Louisiana LNG project, is already building with its units. The overseas production base is also widening.

The Saudi subsidiary SNT Gulf plans to make its supply volumes directly at its own plant in Dammam. In Louisiana, a joint venture with a group affiliate plans to invest about KRW 80 billion in the first local integrated manufacturing facility, according to LS Securities in September 2025.

On competition, analysts use the average multiples of global air cooler makers as the reference. Comparison with the average of Korean machinery and equipment listings remains a rough reference, because the peer group includes companies with different business mixes.

04

Earnings analysis

On an annual basis, revenue rose from KRW 202.9 billion in 2022 to KRW 322 billion in 2023, a 58.7% increase, then fell to KRW 294.3 billion in 2024, down 8.6%, before reaching KRW 606.1 billion in 2025, up 106.0%, making 2025 the largest year in the 2022–2025 period.

Operating profit went from KRW 3.56 billion in 2022 to KRW 20.8 billion in 2023, up 483.4%, then KRW 22.2 billion in 2024, up 7.0%, and KRW 111.3 billion in 2025, up 400.5%, rising for three consecutive years from 2023 through 2025.

Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders grew from KRW 15.1 billion in 2022 to KRW 84.4 billion in 2025 and also increased for three consecutive years, while 2025 net profit including non-controlling interests was the same KRW 84.4 billion.

The operating margin climbed from 1.8% in 2022 and 6.5% in 2023 to 7.6% in 2024 and 18.4% in 2025. LS Securities said in January 2026 that new orders in 2024 were a historic high and that these orders turned into 2025 sales, creating operating leverage.

The balance sheet also improved: the debt ratio was 68.3% in 2022, 53.3% in 2023, 68.7% in 2024 and 61.7% in 2025, with total equity of KRW 364.7 billion and total liabilities of KRW 225.1 billion at the end of 2025.

Operating cash flow went from KRW 28.9 billion in 2022 and KRW 3.28 billion in 2023 to KRW 11.4 billion in 2024 and KRW 103 billion in 2025. The quarterly trend has turned down.

Revenue peaked at KRW 201.9 billion in 4Q25, then fell to KRW 123.6 billion in 1Q26, KRW 108.1 billion in 2Q26 and KRW 100.8 billion in 3Q26, down 32.1% from the prior year. Operating profit dropped from KRW 46.8 billion in 4Q25 to KRW 22.8 billion in 1Q26, down 51.2%, and reached KRW 17.6 billion in 3Q26.

Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders swung to a net loss of KRW 7.44 billion in 3Q26, and the sum over the last four quarters is KRW 77.4 billion.

Some analysts have noted that a gap in new orders in the first half of 2026 reduced the backlog in part, so the sales decline appears to reflect a lag in order flow.

05

Industry analysis

SNTEnergy's end markets are LNG facility investment and oil, gas and petrochemical plant investment in the Middle East. Hyundai Motor Securities said in a March 2026 report that both LNG producers and LNG power generators are its customers.

LS Securities said in a January 2026 report that aggressive US LNG investment and rising global AI power demand support an upward trend in orders. On the cycle, new orders hit a historic high in 2024, those orders moved into 2025 sales, and a gap in new orders in 2026 has pushed the sales line lower.

Air-cooled heat exchangers cool high-temperature process fluids with air, so volumes move with the timing of orders at refining, petrochemical and gas plants. On competition, analysts use global air cooler makers as the benchmark, and few Korean listed companies run the same business, which limits direct comparison.

In the Middle East, SABIC ordered repair work on damaged equipment, and a company official said damage has occurred at several Middle East plants and that replacement and repair orders are expected to continue as reconstruction gets under way.

Meanwhile, the timing of final investment decisions on large US LNG projects varies by project and can be delayed, so the lag before end-market demand arrives can be long.

06

Outlook

The confirmed order schedule is the SABIC supply contract signed in August 2026, which runs from August 20, 2026 to March 31, 2027 and forms part of a repair project to replace damaged air-cooled condensers. Expectations for new orders center on US LNG projects.

LS Securities said in its January 2026 report that final investment decisions on Commonwealth LNG and Delfin FLNG would be the key variables for order momentum, and it noted the company emphasized HRSG orders at the POWERGEN International 2026 exhibition.

A market view carried by a Thinkpool article in February 2026 said additional orders of about KRW 100 billion could arrive in the second half from the Rio Grande and Woodside LNG projects.

Based on LS Securities' estimate, the backlog for 2026 deliveries stood at KRW 282.5 billion with 56.3% progress, which forms the base for 2026 sales.

However, the same report estimated that sales in 2027 would be flat to slightly lower as the backlog shrinks after lower 2025 orders, and that growth would resume in 2028 on the back of 2026 order wins. The Louisiana manufacturing base is viewed as a medium-term global footprint.

Whether the Middle East reconstruction orders the company has described materialize and whether US LNG investment decisions are made will shape order flow after 2027.

08

Bull factors

Structural shift in operating margin

The operating margin climbed from 1.8% in 2022 to 18.4% in 2025, changing the profit structure as revenue scale expanded. Over the same period, operating cash flow rose to KRW 103 billion in 2025, which strengthens the cash backing for earnings.

Operating profit has risen for three consecutive years from 2023 through 2025, which supports the case for a structural change.

Middle East repair and LNG order pipeline

In August 2026 the company signed a supply contract worth about KRW 32 billion with SABIC, and it expects replacement and repair orders to follow as Middle East reconstruction takes hold. LS Securities views final investment decisions on large US LNG projects as a driver of order momentum. Backlog progress of 56.3% for 2026 deliveries also adds visibility to 2026 sales.

Expansion of the controlling shareholder's stake

The de facto controlling shareholder raised its stake to 51.20% as of November 9, 2026, and recorded open-market purchases of 106,000 shares on November 10 and 11. The increase can be read as an insider's view of the company's value.

It does not, however, guarantee the direction of the share price, and further changes in disclosures are the point to check.

Bear factors

Downturn in quarterly earnings

Revenue peaked at KRW 201.9 billion in 4Q25 and has declined for three consecutive quarters from 1Q26 through 3Q26.

Operating profit fell to KRW 17.6 billion in 3Q26, and net profit attributable to controlling shareholders swung to a net loss of KRW 7.44 billion. 3Q26 revenue was down 32.1% from a year earlier, so the slowdown shows first in quarterly figures.

Order gap and an estimated flat 2027 top line

Some analysts said a gap in new orders during the first half of 2026 partly reduced the backlog. LS Securities estimated in its January 2026 report that a shrinking backlog would leave 2027 sales roughly flat to slightly lower.

The bear case rests on the possibility that this estimate becomes reality if large LNG orders do not arrive as expected.

Project concentration and share price volatility

Sales are tied to global EPC projects linked to the Middle East and US LNG, so a single order timing can move earnings. The sharp share price rise on November 10, 2026 came amid concentrated trading, and press coverage noted the rally does not confirm order or margin contributions. The key question is how far earnings disclosures support the price movement.

10

Risk factors

Order timing and project delays

If final investment decisions on US LNG projects or the timing of Middle East repair orders slip, 2027 sales estimates could come down. The SABIC contract period ends on March 31, 2027, so a gap in volumes afterward is possible. Because results depend on a small number of large projects, the impact of any single delay is significant.

Middle East geopolitics and overseas production disruption

Press coverage in 2026 reported that an olefin plant in Jubail, Saudi Arabia, was damaged in the course of a dispute between the United States and Iran. Because production is partly located in overseas plants, operational or logistics disruption could affect both delivery schedules and earnings. Even if Middle East demand turns into repair orders, regional risk remains.

Ownership structure and trading volatility

With the de facto controlling shareholder holding 51.20%, the number of shares freely traded in the market is limited. When trading concentrates, as on November 10, 2026, price swings can widen.

The controlling shareholder's open-market purchases are a fact, but they do not by themselves determine the direction of the share price.

12

Overall view

SNTEnergy's air cooler revenue scaled up in 2025, lifting annual revenue to KRW 606.1 billion and operating profit to KRW 111.3 billion, a sharp rise in both scale and profitability. The operating margin rose from 1.8% in 2022 to 18.4% in 2025, which indicates a change in the profit structure.

However, revenue and operating profit have fallen for three consecutive quarters since the 4Q25 peak, and 3Q26 swung to a net loss of KRW 7.44 billion attributable to controlling shareholders.

Bullish factors include Middle East repair orders, expectations for US LNG projects and 56.3% backlog progress for 2026 deliveries, while bearish factors include the first-half order gap, an estimated flat 2027 top line and dependence on the timing of project orders.

The price-to-book ratio is above 1x, a premium to net asset value compared with the periods when it was below 1x. The share price has seen concentrated trading since the sharp rise on November 10, 2026, and whether that rise is confirmed by earnings is the central question.

The next checkpoints are the 2026 full-year provisional earnings, the 2026 annual report due by March 31, 2027, and the 1Q27 quarterly report.
