02

Business structure

Q AID operates a business that combines home appliance distribution, installation and care services, and logistics.

According to the company profile, it handles products such as kimchi refrigerators, refrigerators, air conditioners, washing machines, dryers, air washers and dehumidifiers across distribution, logistics and installation, and care services in South Korea.

It also runs home appliance rental and franchise businesses as well as global outsourcing. On the logistics side, its scope includes warehouse operation, call center operation, international transportation and export-import declaration services.

Its home service activities cover appliance service and repair agency work, plus product delivery and installation. The company is classified under personal and household product repair services.

Through a shareholders' meeting on March 27, 2026, it changed its name to Q AID Co., Ltd., citing the goal of raising corporate value and corporate image. Its headcount stood at 309 as of June 30, 2026.

Because revenue is tied to appliance sales flows and installation and logistics demand, the businesses may move together, so synergies and simultaneous weakness are both possible. Competition is likely concentrated where appliance service providers and logistics agencies overlap.

04

Earnings analysis

Based on the audited annual figures, sales fell from KRW 439.5 billion in 2022 to KRW 274.3 billion in 2023, KRW 116.3 billion in 2024 and KRW 91.6 billion in 2025. Sales declined for three straight years from 2023 through 2025, with 2022 the largest year and 2025 the smallest in the 2022 to 2025 span.

Operating profit of KRW 22.3 billion in 2022 turned into an operating loss of KRW 106.1 billion in 2023. Financial data from Hankyung indicates that selling, general and administrative expenses rose sharply in 2023, pointing to a combination of falling sales and higher costs.

The 2024 operating loss of KRW 927 million narrowed from the prior year, while the 2025 operating loss of KRW 1.91 billion widened again. The operating margin moved from 5.1% in 2022 to -38.7% in 2023, -0.8% in 2024 and -2.1% in 2025.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders swung from a KRW 123.5 billion loss in 2023 and a KRW 47 billion loss in 2024 to a KRW 7.16 billion profit in 2025.

Including non-controlling interests, the net result was a loss of KRW 123.6 billion in 2023, a loss of KRW 49.4 billion in 2024 and a profit of KRW 7.16 billion in 2025, with the gap between the two measures coming from non-controlling interests.

Operating cash flow was KRW -60.7 billion in 2022, KRW 7.27 billion in 2023, KRW -4.41 billion in 2024 and KRW -43.3 billion in 2025. The debt ratio rose from 88.5% in 2022 and 256.8% in 2023 to 909.2% in 2024 before falling to 174.3% in 2025.

Total equity at year-end 2025 was KRW 56.8 billion, all of it attributable to controlling shareholders.

Quarterly sales went from KRW 26 billion in Q3 2025 with an operating loss of KRW 1.03 billion to KRW 26.6 billion in Q3 2026, up 13.2% from the prior quarter and 2.6% from a year earlier, with an operating loss of KRW 982 million.

The Q3 2026 net loss attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 1.82 billion was wider than the KRW 344 million loss a year earlier, and it exceeded the operating loss, suggesting non-operating items weighed on results.

The net loss attributable to controlling shareholders over the last four quarters totals KRW 1.99 billion.

A correction to the first-half 2026 semi-annual report changed a reported operating profit of KRW 35.6 billion into an operating loss of KRW 2.09 billion, and the company cited only a clerical correction without detailing how the error arose.

Industry analysis

Q AID is classified under personal and household product repair services, with appliance installation and care services at the core of its operations. Demand for installation and care services tends to grow alongside new appliance sales and rental sign-ups, so it follows the appliance sales cycle.

Product categories such as kimchi refrigerators and large appliances have long replacement cycles, so when sales slow, installation demand tends to slow with them. Whether the decline in sales since 2023 stems from this business structure cannot be pinned down from reported results alone.

The logistics segment, which includes warehouse and call center operation and international transportation, is exposed to logistics demand and sea and air freight rate changes. Competition is likely concentrated where appliance service agencies and logistics agencies overlap.

Listed companies in the same miscellaneous sector classification include MegaStudyEdu, Hansol Paper and Daekyo, which span paper, education and musical instrument businesses, so sector averages make a limited comparison for Q AID.

The most direct operating benchmarks are therefore the sales trend and the operating margin. The return of quarterly sales to growth in 2026 can be read as a sign of recovery, but it should be weighed alongside three straight quarters of operating losses.

Narrower quarterly operating losses
