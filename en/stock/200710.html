02

Business structure

ADTechnology is a chip design house founded in 2002. It ended its TSMC relationship in 2020, partnered with Samsung Foundry and shifted to a Samsung Design Solution Partner model.

Its core business is ASIC design and sales, offered through its SoC, platform and infrastructure segments from design through packaging and testing. As of Q3 2025, development services accounted for 82.25% of revenue and product (mass production) sales for 14.49%.

Kyobo Securities noted that mass-production revenue was about 12% of the total in 2025. Shinhan Investment Corp. analyzed that the share of turnkey projects leading to mass production rose from 60% in 2024 to 81% in 2025.

Overseas customers are also a growing share, and overseas revenue was reported to have risen 133% in 2025 from a year earlier. The company said a North American HPC design firm has been named the first customer for its next-generation CPU, and that it is discussing CPU supply with Rebellions, a Korean AI chip company.

Competitively, the company is compared with domestic design houses, and its inclusion in the Samsung Foundry ecosystem is cited as widening its opportunities in custom chips.

On a separate-basis figure the company announced, 2025 revenue was KRW 124.4 billion and operating profit KRW 8.8 billion, which differ from the consolidated disclosure basis.

04

Earnings analysis

On a DART-disclosed basis, annual revenue fell from KRW 164.2 billion in 2022 to KRW 100.2 billion in 2023, a 39.0% decline, and then rose to KRW 106.5 billion in 2024 (up 6.4%) and KRW 164.5 billion in 2025 (up 54.4%), a two-year run of increases. Among 2022 to 2025, 2025 was the largest year and 2023 the smallest.

Operating profit of KRW 4.4 billion in 2022 turned into an operating loss of KRW 17.4 billion in 2023, and the loss continued in 2024 at KRW 17 billion, a narrower figure. In 2025 operating profit was KRW 2.85 billion, a return to profit, with an operating margin of 1.7%.

Attributable net income moved from KRW 5.93 billion in 2022 to an attributable net loss of KRW 16 billion in 2023 and KRW 14.3 billion in 2024, before returning to an attributable net income of KRW 3.61 billion in 2025.

Net income including non-controlling interests, which is a different figure, was KRW 3.58 billion in 2025. Operating cash flow went from KRW 32.1 billion in 2022 and KRW 19 billion in 2023 to -KRW 13.5 billion in 2024 and -KRW 15.9 billion in 2025.

The debt-to-equity ratio at end-2025 was 115.9%, up from 39.6% in 2022, with total equity of KRW 135.9 billion and total liabilities of KRW 157.6 billion.

Quarterly, Q3 2025 revenue of KRW 36.8 billion and operating profit of KRW 2.67 billion were followed by Q4 2025 revenue of KRW 61 billion (up 65.8% quarter on quarter) and operating profit of KRW 2.76 billion. Q1 2026 revenue was KRW 36 billion (down 41.0% quarter on quarter) with an operating loss of KRW 357 million.

Kyobo Securities noted in a May 20, 2026 report that Q1 IP acquisition costs rose from about KRW 6.9 billion a year earlier to about KRW 19 billion. Q2 2026 revenue was KRW 45.9 billion (up 27.5% quarter on quarter), but the operating loss widened to KRW 1.17 billion.

Q3 2026 revenue of KRW 25.2 billion (down 45.1% quarter on quarter and 31.5% year on year) came with an operating loss of KRW 1.43 billion, a larger loss. Attributable net income turned into a loss from Q2 2026, and the attributable net loss for the latest four quarters totals KRW 7.76 billion.

05

Industry analysis

Demand from downstream markets is shifting toward chip design for AI servers and data-center high-performance computing (HPC). The company said it expects the 2030 market for AI infrastructure and sovereign AI to reach about KRW 1,500 trillion, a projection that comes from the company itself.

At an April 7, 2026 press briefing, CEO Park Joon-gyu set a target of KRW 1.5 trillion in annual revenue by 2030. Other coverage at the same time reported a KRW 1 trillion revenue goal for 2029, so the target year differs across reports.

Competitively, Arm is entering the server CPU market aimed at AI and cloud, while the company says it targets sovereign AI and edge markets and designs its core configuration differently from Arm. Samsung Electronics' 2nm process is cited as the manufacturing base for the CPU business.

Industry observers point to the move from design services to turnkey models that include mass production as a point of differentiation. Its position as a Samsung Foundry DSP partner, however, leaves the balance between customer diversification and dependence on the foundry ecosystem as an open task.

In cycle terms, development revenue is rising first and mass-production revenue is expected to follow, so the growing share of mass-production revenue is the indicator to track for its industry position.

06

Outlook

The core of the company's growth path is the next-generation CPU ADP620. As of November 18, 2026, ADP620 is still in development, with tape-out scheduled for Q2 2027. Customer supply is expected between late 2027 and early 2028, and the launch target has been reported as the second half of 2027.

Many observers expect revenue contribution to become meaningful from 2028. The company estimated that its CPU business could generate about USD 1 billion in revenue over time.

Securities analysts estimated that ADP620 could generate about KRW 1.4 trillion in revenue over roughly two and a half years starting in the second half of 2028. In a May 20, 2026 report, Kyobo Securities presented 2026 revenue estimates of about KRW 256 billion, KRW 402 billion for 2027 and KRW 625 billion for 2028.

However, disclosed revenue for Q1 to Q3 2026 was KRW 36 billion, KRW 45.9 billion and KRW 25.2 billion, so whether revenue recovers in Q4 after the Q3 drop is the key item to check.

Shinhan Investment Corp. said in a February 2026 report that the number of mass-production customers would expand from 2 in 2026 to 11 in 2027. Given that full-year 2025 preliminary results were disclosed on February 9, 2026, the Q4 2026 preliminary results are likely to come in early February 2027.

08

Bull factors

AI turnkey wins and mass-production pipeline

According to Shinhan Investment Corp. analysis, the share of high-value AI and HPC projects rose from 26% in 2024 to 73% in 2025, and the turnkey share rose from 60% to 81%. Press coverage reported that the mass-production pipeline would grow from 1 project in 2026 to 7 in 2027 and 12 in 2028. New orders in 2025 were reported at about 2.5 times the prior three-year average.

2025 annual turnaround and overseas sales

Revenue for 2025 was KRW 164.5 billion, up 54.4%, and operating profit of KRW 2.85 billion marked a turnaround. Overseas revenue rose 133% year on year as high-value projects expanded the base. Kyobo Securities described the return to profit on development revenue alone as an encouraging result.

Next-generation CPU platform with a first customer

ADP620 is scheduled for tape-out in Q2 2027, and the company said a North American HPC firm has been named its first CPU customer. The company estimated the CPU business could reach about USD 1 billion in future revenue. Talks with a Korean AI chip company could widen the customer base.

09

Bear factors

Three consecutive quarterly operating losses

Operating losses have run for three straight quarters from Q1 to Q3 2026, and the Q3 operating loss of KRW 1.43 billion turned a year-earlier profit into a loss. Attributable net losses were KRW 6.77 billion in Q2 and KRW 7.12 billion in Q3. The attributable net loss for the latest four quarters totals KRW 7.76 billion.

Cost pressure and volatile quarterly revenue

Kyobo Securities pointed out that Q1 2026 IP acquisition costs rose from about KRW 6.9 billion a year earlier to about KRW 19 billion. Quarterly revenue also swung sharply, at KRW 36 billion in Q1, KRW 45.9 billion in Q2 and KRW 25.2 billion in Q3 2026. Q3 revenue fell 45.1% from the prior quarter.

Development-led revenue and foundry dependence

As of Q3 2025, development services made up 82.25% of revenue and product sales 14.49%, so the revenue base leans on development projects. The business is tied to its Samsung Foundry DSP partnership and is affected by changes in that ecosystem. The core CPU product is still in development.

10

Risk factors

Customer and project concentration, revenue timing

One brokerage's 2026 mass-production estimate reflected only one customer's volume, which it described as conservative. If recognition of large project revenue is delayed, quarterly results could remain in loss. How far revenue deviates from the estimate path after Q2 2026 is the key question.

Funding needs and possible share dilution

Reports said the company's board approved a KRW 130 billion private convertible bond issuance to fund development of its custom server CPU platform. If the bonds convert into shares, existing shareholders could be diluted. Operating cash flow has been negative in 2024 and 2025, so funding needs may persist while R&D continues.

Foundry dependence and CPU competition

The CPU business assumes use of Samsung Electronics' 2nm process, so changes in foundry schedules or yields could affect launch timing. Arm is entering the server CPU market, which could alter the competitive picture. The company says its target markets differ, but validating that through results will take until 2027 or later.

  4. Second half 2027

  5. Late 2027 to early 2028

12

Overall view

ADTechnology returned to annual operating profit in 2025, its first annual profit since 2022, and 2025 revenue rose 54.4% to KRW 164.5 billion. However, operating losses continued through Q1 to Q3 2026, and Q3 revenue of KRW 25.2 billion fell 31.5% year on year, so quarterly results remain volatile.

Bullish factors include a rising share of high-value AI and HPC projects, a higher share of turnkey work, and the mass-production timeline for the next-generation CPU ADP620 after 2027.

Bearish factors include a revenue base dominated by development services, higher IP acquisition costs in 2026, the attributable net loss over the latest four quarters, and a debt-to-equity ratio of 115.9% at end-2025.

The company's target of KRW 1.5 trillion in annual revenue by 2030 depends on mass-production revenue after ADP620, so a long gap before that could persist.

The next checkpoints are the Q4 2026 preliminary results and the 2026 annual report, which will show whether operating losses recur and whether operating cash flow turns positive. This is an informational analysis and does not contain buy or sell judgments.
