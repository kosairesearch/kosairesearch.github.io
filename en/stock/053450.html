02

Business structure

Sekonix, founded in 1988, is an optics component maker that began with smartphone camera lenses and has expanded into automotive camera lenses and modules and automotive lamps.

The company runs a single optics business segment and makes and sells mobile camera lenses, automotive camera lenses and modules, automotive lamps, optical components and optical films. A news report based on the first-half 2026 filing said product optical components accounted for 99.13% of total sales.

A 2021 breakdown showed mobile camera lenses at 30%, automotive camera lenses and modules at 34%, automotive lamps at 26%, optical films at 6% and optical components at 4%; the current mix should be checked in the periodic report's segment section.

Hyundai Mobis was the core automotive customer, receiving more than 90% of automotive camera sales in 2021. The company has reportedly supplied driver-monitoring camera lenses to Continental and works with Gentex.

Production sites are in Dongducheon (mobile lenses), Pyeongtaek (automotive camera modules), Asan (optical films) and SAL (automotive lamps), with overseas plants in China, Vietnam and Poland.

Its lamp business, which began in earnest in 2016, runs an in-house line from glass lens molding through die-casting, deposition and assembly.

A 2022 brokerage analysis noted fewer domestic mobile-lens rivals after industry restructuring, and a 2021 brokerage view placed the company among the global top 2 to 5 suppliers of automotive camera lenses.

Earnings analysis

On an annual basis, 2023 was the largest year in the period.

Sales rose from KRW 479.6 billion in 2022 to KRW 502.4 billion in 2023 (up 4.7% year on year), then declined to KRW 462.7 billion in 2024 (down 7.9%) and KRW 434.9 billion in 2025 (down 6.0%), with sales falling for two consecutive years from 2024 through 2025.

Operating profit rose from KRW 6.16 billion in 2022 to KRW 16.2 billion in 2023 (up 162.6%), then fell to KRW 14.4 billion in 2024 (down 10.8%) and KRW 3.01 billion in 2025 (down 79.2%). The operating margin moved from 1.3% in 2022 to 3.2% in 2023, 3.1% in 2024 and 0.7% in 2025.

Attributable net income swung from a net loss of KRW 2.17 billion in 2022 to net income of KRW 11.4 billion in 2023, then fell to KRW 9.88 billion in 2024 and KRW 4.78 billion in 2025.

Net income including non-controlling interests was KRW 4.98 billion in 2025, a different figure from attributable net income and to be read separately. The debt ratio fell from 173.9% in 2022 to 105.8% in 2024 before rising to 115.1% in 2025.

At the end of 2025, total equity was KRW 141.7 billion, equity attributable to owners was KRW 141.3 billion and total liabilities were KRW 163.1 billion. Operating cash flow went from KRW 45 billion in 2024 to -KRW 11.3 billion in 2025.

Quarterly, operating profit of KRW 412 million in Q3 2025 became an operating loss of KRW 4.06 billion in Q4 2025, then KRW 1.05 billion of operating profit in Q1 2026 was followed by an operating loss of KRW 32.9 billion in Q2 2026.

Q3 2026 returned to operating profit of KRW 2.83 billion, but the attributable net loss was KRW 7.99 billion. Sales peaked at KRW 104 billion in Q1 2026 and then declined over two consecutive quarters to KRW 97.6 billion in Q2 and KRW 94.3 billion in Q3. The size of any one-off items should be checked in the notes to the periodic report.

Industry analysis

The automotive camera market is described as entering a structural growth phase as autonomous driving spreads. An independent research firm's April 2026 report said camera counts per vehicle are expanding from two or three to more than ten.

The same report noted that higher resolution, HDR and low-light requirements are pushing unit prices up. It also cited the expanding NVIDIA-based autonomous driving platform and Hyundai Motor Group's partnership as demand drivers.

On the other side, mobile camera lenses remain exposed to smartphone shipment swings and price competition from Chinese suppliers, a dynamic a 2022 brokerage analysis described as forcing domestic lens makers to shrink.

A 2021 analysis put automotive camera supply prices at more than five times smartphone camera prices. A 2021 brokerage view placed Sekonix among the global top 2 to 5 suppliers of automotive camera lenses. These assessments date from 2021 to 2022, so the current share ranking needs to be checked against newer data.

06

Outlook

Sekonix's medium-term direction is its reshaping into an automotive optics company centered on automotive camera lenses and automotive lamps. Demand support comes from automotive lens supply through Hyundai Mobis and participation in the NVIDIA-based autonomous driving ecosystem.

An April 2026 report argued that medium- to long-term growth and margin improvement are expected as camera counts rise and specifications advance together.

The product range has widened from rear cameras to surround view, front and lane recognition and driver monitoring, and the company plans to extend optical technology into AI robots, HUDs and AR/MR. The return to operating profit in Q3 2026 can be read as a sign of operating recovery.

However, with an attributable net loss of KRW 7.99 billion still recorded, whether operating profit converts into net profit is the next checkpoint. A capital reduction at the Vietnamese subsidiary SEKONIX VINA had its record date revised to June 30, 2026 after a delay in Vietnamese government approval.

Because the lamp business runs an in-house production line, the recovery of lamp sales also warrants monitoring. A news report said automotive lamp sales rose 12.2% in 2024 on higher volumes tied to pre-tariff inventory building. Whether the causes of the Q2 2026 loss are disclosed in the 2026 annual report is a key item to watch.

Bull factors

Rising camera count per vehicle

An April 2026 report from an independent research firm said camera counts per vehicle are rising past ten. Sekonix has widened its lineup from rear cameras to surround view, front and lane recognition and driver monitoring. The same report said higher specifications could also support unit prices.

Vertical integration and in-house lamp production

A brokerage assessment credits the company with in-house capabilities spanning optical design, molds, injection, coating, assembly and inspection. Its lamp business is described as running a line from glass lens molding through die-casting, deposition and assembly. A news report said automotive lamp sales rose 12.2% in 2024.

Return to operating profit

In 2023 the company recorded operating profit of KRW 16.2 billion and an operating margin of 3.2%. Operating profit turned positive at KRW 2.83 billion in Q3 2026. Whether that operating recovery carries through to net income is the next point to watch.

09

Bear factors

Sales declines tied to customer model transitions

Sales in 2025 were KRW 434.9 billion, down 6.0%, and the company cited a model-transition period in its automotive division. A news report said automotive camera and lens sales fell 16.8% in 2024 on lower customer volumes. Changes in customer programs flow directly into reported results.

Accumulating quarterly losses

The company has posted losses for four consecutive quarters from Q4 2025 through Q3 2026, with attributable net losses totaling KRW 44.3 billion. The Q2 2026 operating loss of KRW 32.9 billion was the largest among the recent quarters. Loss sizes shift considerably from quarter to quarter, which limits visibility into future results.

Narrowing financial headroom

The debt ratio fell from 173.9% in 2022 to 105.8% in 2024 before rising to 115.1% in 2025. Operating cash flow moved from KRW 45 billion in 2024 to -KRW 11.3 billion in 2025. If losses persist, financial headroom could narrow further.

Risk factors

Customer concentration

The automotive camera business depends heavily on a small number of customers, led by Hyundai Mobis. A news report said more than 90% of automotive camera sales went to Hyundai Mobis in 2021. Sales can swing during customer model changeovers, so volume trends at key customers need to be tracked.

Financials and profitability

The operating margin was 0.7% in 2025, and net losses have continued for four quarters. Total liabilities at the end of 2025 were KRW 163.1 billion, above total equity of KRW 141.7 billion. If losses last longer, borrowing costs and liquidity management become key issues.

Overseas production and trade

Production subsidiaries in China, Vietnam and Poland expose the company to trade changes and local restructuring. The record date for the capital reduction at the Vietnamese subsidiary was moved to June 30, 2026 after a delay in government approval. The lamp business has seen volumes rise on pre-tariff inventory building, so it is sensitive to trade events.

Overall view

Sekonix is reshaping itself from a mobile camera lens business into an automotive optics company centered on camera and lamp products. Annual sales declined in both 2024 and 2025 after peaking in 2023, and operating profit fell 79.2% to KRW 3.01 billion in 2025.

Operating profit turned positive in Q3 2026, but the attributable net loss of KRW 7.99 billion extended the losing streak to four quarters. Bullish factors include rising camera counts per vehicle, the autonomous driving platform partnership and the in-house lamp production system.

Bearish factors include sales declines tied to customer model transitions and cumulative quarterly losses. These should be weighed equally, and the current disclosures alone offer limited basis for a directional view.

On the financial side, the debt ratio rose to 115.1% in 2025 from 105.8% in 2024, and operating cash flow turned to -KRW 11.3 billion.

The next checkpoints are the segment sales and the causes of the operating loss in the 2026 annual report, and whether operating profit converts into net profit in the Q1 2027 quarterly report.
