02

Business structure

NH Investment & Securities groups its revenue into brokerage, wealth management (WM), financial product sales, investment banking (IB) and trading and investment.

The company reported first-half 2026 brokerage fee income of KRW 795.0 billion, up 211.7% from a year earlier, with financial product sales fee income of KRW 125.9 billion and IB fee income of KRW 205.5 billion.

Trading and investment income reached KRW 831.5 billion in the first half, of which KRW 407.3 billion came in the second quarter. The first-half release put total customer assets at KRW 612 trillion, with 455,000 clients holding at least KRW 100 million and 33,000 holding at least KRW 1 billion.

Brokerage income moves with domestic stock trading value and market share, which was 10.7% in the first quarter of 2026 and rose 0.7 percentage points in the second quarter. WM fees rose 171.8% quarter on quarter in the second quarter as demand for target-return wrap products grew.

In IB, the company held the top spot in equity capital markets (ECM) lead underwriting with a 30.9% share in the first quarter of 2026 and ranked first in IPO underwriting with 37.4%. It also ranked first in financial debenture (FB) lead underwriting with a 32.0% share. Analysts at several brokerages cited the company's IB capabilities and branch network as strengths.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual operating profit rose from KRW 521.4 billion in 2022 to KRW 725.8 billion in 2023 (up 39.2%), KRW 901.1 billion in 2024 (up 24.2%) and KRW 1.42 trillion in 2025 (up 57.7%).

Operating profit increased for three consecutive years from 2023 through 2025, with 2025 the largest year and 2022 the smallest in 2022–2025.

Profit attributable to controlling shareholders went from KRW 303.4 billion in 2022 to KRW 556.4 billion in 2023 (up 83.4%), KRW 686.7 billion in 2024 (up 23.4%) and KRW 1.03 trillion in 2025 (up 50.2%).

Net profit including non-controlling interests was KRW 1.03 trillion in 2025, a separate figure from profit attributable to controlling shareholders. The 2023 growth rate was the largest of the period, and one brokerage analysis credits broad-based gains in 2025 to a strong stock market in the second half.

Quarterly operating profit was KRW 391.3 billion in the third quarter of 2025 and KRW 418.3 billion in the fourth quarter (up 6.9% from the prior quarter). It reached KRW 636.7 billion in the first quarter of 2026 (up 52.2%), which the company described as a record quarter.

Second-quarter operating profit was KRW 681.2 billion (up 7.0%). Third-quarter operating profit was KRW 521.1 billion, down 23.5% from the prior quarter but up 33.2% year on year, and profit attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 383.6 billion, down 21.6% quarter on quarter.

Press coverage attributed the third-quarter decline to lower trading value, and operating profit beat market consensus by 4% to 8%. A brokerage report said the second quarter included a KRW 20 billion valuation loss on unlisted assets and higher selling and administrative costs after an education tax rate increase.

The four quarters from the fourth quarter of 2025 through the third quarter of 2026 totaled KRW 1.63 trillion in profit attributable to controlling shareholders.

05

Industry analysis

Average daily domestic stock trading value rose 80.5% quarter on quarter to KRW 66.8 trillion in the first quarter of 2026, and reached about KRW 90.2 trillion in the second quarter, according to the company.

An April 2026 brokerage report showed credit loans outstanding rising from KRW 17.6 trillion in the first quarter of 2025 to KRW 32.9 trillion in the first quarter of 2026, and customer deposits rising from KRW 58.5 trillion to KRW 110.3 trillion.

This pattern shows capital flowing into the market and lifting both brokerage and interest income. However, lower trading value weighed on third-quarter 2026 profit, a sign that the market moved from expansion toward a slowdown.

Whether trading value has peaked is hard to judge yet, so the trend in trading value will be the key indicator. On policy, authorities announced a plan in November 2026 to shorten the T+2 settlement cycle in place since 1972, which they expect to reduce unsettled exposure and brokers' margin burden.

In competition, the company relies on a 10.7% domestic stock market share in the first quarter of 2026 and its top rankings in ECM and IPO underwriting. Brokerage reports note that rising participation by high-net-worth investors favors large firms with branch networks.

06

Outlook

In its first-half 2026 release, the company said pension assets passed KRW 20 trillion and had more than tripled over the past four years. In its first-quarter release, CEO Yoon Byung-woon said the company would grow IMA (integrated investment account) into a new core growth engine.

Brokerage research says the company was designated the third IMA operator on March 19, 2026. An April 2026 brokerage report expected deal sourcing in IB to expand through closer links with WM.

On July 24, 2026, Daishin Securities reported that second-quarter profit improved across all business lines to a record, set a target price of KRW 45,000, and noted expectations for a favorable capital-policy announcement.

Trading value eased in the third quarter of 2026, so trading patterns remain the main variable for fourth-quarter 2026 results. How far pension and IMA growth can cushion swings in trading value has not yet been shown in company disclosures. The next checkpoint is the level of fourth-quarter 2026 operating profit as trading value slows.

08

Bull factors

Dividend-centred shareholder returns

An April 2026 report stressed dividend appeal, citing an average shareholder-return ratio of about 50% over the past three years. The core bull argument rests on dividend-centred shareholder returns.

Broader fee-based income

First-half 2026 financial product sales fee income was KRW 125.9 billion, up 127.2% from the same period a year earlier. Second-quarter WM fees rose 171.8% quarter on quarter, helped by wider sales of target-return wraps. The company said total customer assets grew to KRW 612 trillion in the first half. The bull case holds that a smaller reliance on brokerage could soften earnings swings when trading value falls.

IB fees as an added revenue source

Second-quarter 2026 IB fee income was KRW 108.3 billion, up 11.3% from the prior quarter, helped by higher guarantee-related fees. The company said it led the first-half IPO underwriting ranking with listings it managed, including PeacePeace Studio and Cosmo Robotics. The bull argument is that IB broadens revenue beyond trading value.

09

Bear factors

Quarterly profit sensitive to trading value

Third-quarter 2026 operating profit fell 23.5% from the prior quarter, and press coverage attributed the decline to lower trading value. Profit this sensitive to trading value means quarterly earnings can drop quickly when activity slows.

Because the record first half leaned heavily on a trading boom, a further drop in volumes raises questions about how durable earnings are.

Slower growth in the second quarter

Second-quarter profit attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 489.6 billion, up only 2.9% from the prior quarter, after a 67.8% rise in the first quarter. A brokerage report carried on July 24, 2026 judged second-quarter results to be below its own estimate. The sharp slowdown in quarterly growth is cited as a weak point in the earnings trend.

Debt relative to equity and operating cash outflows

Total liabilities at the end of 2025 were KRW 73.9 trillion against total equity of KRW 9.44 trillion, a debt ratio of 783.5%. The debt ratio rose from 639.2% in 2022 to 644.7% in 2023 and 668.9% in 2024, then jumped further in 2025. Operating cash flow was -KRW 1.15 trillion in 2023, -KRW 4.2 trillion in 2024 and -KRW 5.42 trillion in 2025.

10

Risk factors

Regulation (settlement cycle)

Authorities are pursuing a proposal to shorten the T+2 settlement cycle in place since 1972, and are collecting market comments through December 18, 2026. A shorter cycle would cut unsettled exposure and margin burden, but brokers may face costs to change settlement and funding systems. The final design could affect the company's cost structure.

Credit and collateral exposure

Credit loans have grown since 2025, so a sharp fall in share prices could erode collateral value and raise losses on credit exposure. That risk could offset the added interest income from larger balances. The largest risk scenario is a volatility spike while balances remain high.

Interest rates and trading income

Trading and investment income is exposed to bond yield moves, and third-quarter 2026 coverage said the company kept bond duration conservative to limit valuation losses. A reversal in rate direction or a rise in volatility could swing trading results sharply from quarter to quarter. Because trading income is a large part of first-half earnings, this exposure could widen earnings swings.

  3. Late March 2027 (typical annual report deadline)

12

Overall view

NH Investment & Securities recorded operating profit of KRW 1.42 trillion in 2025, the largest year in 2022–2025, and kept quarterly operating profit above KRW 600 billion in the first and second quarters of 2026 as trading value recovered.

Third-quarter operating profit fell from the prior quarter as trading value dropped, showing again how closely results track market volumes. The company highlights reduced reliance on brokerage and a larger share from product sales and IB as signs of progress.

Shareholder returns are viewed as high within the sector, and they sit at the center of the dividend-focused equity case. On the other hand, the 2025 debt ratio was 783.5% and operating cash flow stayed negative, so the balance sheet remains a point to monitor.

Among brokerage targets, Daishin Securities' KRW 45,000 target from July 24, 2026 is confirmed. The next checkpoints are the close of the December 18, 2026 settlement-cycle comment period and the financial indicators in the 2026 annual report.
