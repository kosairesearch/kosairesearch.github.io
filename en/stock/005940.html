Report overview

Earnings Strength Grew With Trading Volumes

NH Investment & Securities posted three straight years of growth in operating profit and profit attributable to owners from 2023 to 2025, and quarterly earnings stayed at elevated levels through the third quarter of 2026. Much of this income depends on domestic trading volumes, so whether future quarterly earnings hold up is the key point to watch.

02

Business structure

NH Investment & Securities earns revenue from four business lines: brokerage, financial product sales and wealth management (WM), investment banking (IB), and asset management.

In its first-half 2026 results the company said income is no longer concentrated in brokerage, with financial product sales and IB also contributing. In the first quarter of 2026, domestic equity commission income was KRW 309.7 billion and domestic equity market share stood at 10.7 percent.

IB fee income for the same quarter was KRW 97.2 billion, and the company held first place with a 30.9 percent share of equity capital markets (ECM) underwriting and a 37.4 percent share of IPO underwriting. The company also said it kept first place in senior debt (FB) lead underwriting with a 32.0 percent share.

Sales of target-return wrap accounts lifted asset management fees by 171.8 percent from the prior quarter in the second quarter of 2026.

Total customer assets expanded to KRW 612 trillion at the end of the second quarter, with more than 455,000 customers holding at least KRW 100 million and more than 33,000 holding at least KRW 1 billion. Asset management income in the second quarter of 2026 was KRW 407.3 billion.

DB Securities analysts said a large brokerage with a branch network benefits as high-net-worth clients increase their participation in the stock market. Brokerage and WM income move with trading volumes and investor sentiment, which brokers' analysts also flag as a structural feature.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual results rose clearly from 2022 through 2025. Operating profit went from KRW 521.4 billion in 2022 to KRW 725.8 billion in 2023 (up 39.2% year on year), KRW 901.1 billion in 2024 (up 24.2%) and KRW 1.42 trillion in 2025 (up 57.7%), growing for three consecutive years from 2023 to 2025.

Profit attributable to owners moved in the same direction, from KRW 303.4 billion in 2022 to KRW 556.4 billion in 2023 (up 83.4%), KRW 686.7 billion in 2024 (up 23.4%) and KRW 1.03 trillion in 2025 (up 50.2%).

Net profit including non-controlling interests was KRW 1.03 trillion in 2025, close to the profit attributable to owners. The debt-to-equity ratio rose from 639.2% in 2022 to 644.7% in 2023, 668.9% in 2024 and 783.5% in 2025.

At the end of 2025, total equity was KRW 9.44 trillion, of which equity attributable to owners was KRW 9.43 trillion, and total liabilities were KRW 73.9 trillion.

Operating cash flow was KRW 6.63 trillion in 2022 but turned negative in 2023 at -KRW 1.15 trillion, then -KRW 4.2 trillion in 2024 and -KRW 5.42 trillion in 2025.

Quarterly operating profit rose from KRW 391.3 billion in the third quarter of 2025 to KRW 418.3 billion in the fourth quarter (up 6.9% from the prior quarter), then jumped to KRW 636.7 billion in the first quarter of 2026 (up 52.2%).

The company called that quarter a record and linked it to a 80.5 percent rise in average daily domestic equity trading to KRW 66.8 trillion. Second-quarter 2026 operating profit was KRW 681.2 billion (up 7.0%) and profit attributable to owners was KRW 489.6 billion (up 2.9%).

Third-quarter 2026 operating profit was KRW 521.1 billion, down 23.5% from the prior quarter but up 33.2% from a year earlier. Profit attributable to owners in the third quarter was KRW 383.6 billion, down 21.6% from the prior quarter and up 35.5% year on year.

Over the last four quarters, from the fourth quarter of 2025 through the third quarter of 2026, profit attributable to owners totaled KRW 1.63 trillion.

In the second quarter of 2026, Daol Investment & Securities pointed to a KRW 20 billion valuation loss on unlisted assets and higher selling and administrative costs from a higher education tax rate as factors outside its earlier expectations.

05

Industry analysis

Domestic stock trading volumes rose sharply in 2026. According to company figures, average daily domestic equity trading was KRW 66.8 trillion in the first quarter, up 80.5% from the prior quarter, and reached about KRW 90.2 trillion in the second quarter.

NH's domestic equity market share was 10.7 percent in the first quarter and rose 0.7 percentage points in the second quarter. Equity trading volumes fell in the third quarter, and brokerage income slowed as a result.

Rates were rising, but the company said its asset management division protected profits through strategic asset allocation. An analyst at iM Securities said retail-driven fund flows and a more active stock market were improving earnings, and that trading profits held up in a rising-rate environment.

Some market commentary holds that first-quarter earnings already reached 46% of 2025 full-year net profit, so growth could be passing its peak. Concerns were also raised that a delay in rate cuts or greater market volatility could make it hard to sustain first-quarter levels of growth.

Within the large-broker group, a firm with a branch network stands to gain as high-net-worth clients join the stock market, according to DB Securities.

06

Outlook

The company is strengthening its medium- to long-term growth base as an IMA (comprehensive investment account) operator, and has said it intends to build IMA into a new core growth engine. The company also cited a more than threefold increase in pension assets over the past four years as evidence of its growth.

The Korea Financial News reported that cumulative profit attributable to owners for the first three quarters of 2026 reached KRW 1.35 trillion, up 80.3% year on year. DB Securities projected in its July 2026 report that full-year net profit would rise 58.9% this year and gave an expected dividend payout ratio of 46.1%.

That same report assumed that recent trading volumes remain near first-quarter levels. On the business side, the company's stated strategy is to grow asset management and IB together. The next regular filing is the 2026 annual business report, and fourth-quarter 2026 preliminary results have not yet been published.

The next points to check are how fourth-quarter operating profit compares with the KRW 521.1 billion of the third quarter, the full-year total, and whether trading volumes hold up in the fourth quarter.

Bull factors

Sustained Earnings Growth

Operating profit and profit attributable to owners grew for three consecutive years from 2023 to 2025, and 2025 operating profit of KRW 1.42 trillion was up 57.7% from the prior year. First-quarter 2026 operating profit of KRW 636.7 billion is the level the company called a record quarter.

First-quarter 2026 net profit of KRW 475.7 billion equaled 46% of full-year 2025 net profit, showing how quickly the earnings base expanded.

Broader Revenue Mix

Financial product sales fee income reached KRW 76.8 billion in the second quarter of 2026, up 56.3% from the prior quarter, while sales of target-return wrap accounts lifted asset management fees by 171.8%.

In the first quarter of 2026, the IB division held first place with a 30.9 percent share of ECM underwriting and a 37.4 percent share of IPO underwriting. The company cited the reduced reliance on brokerage as an achievement.

Shareholder Return Policy

Kyobo Securities put the 2025 cash payout ratio at 47.3 percent on a consolidated basis and 53.7 percent on a separate basis in a May 2026 report. DB Securities projected an expected payout ratio of 46.1 percent for this year.

Daishin Securities, in a July 2026 report, pointed to profitability improvements across all divisions alongside the dividend policy.

Bear factors

Dependence on Trading Volumes

Third-quarter 2026 brokerage income slowed as equity trading volumes declined, and operating profit fell 23.5% from the prior quarter. Because average daily domestic trading had jumped 80.5% in the first quarter, earnings swings have been large.

Concern has been raised that a delay in rate cuts or higher market volatility could make it hard to sustain first-quarter levels.

Peak-Growth Concern and Cost Items

Some market commentary notes that first-quarter 2026 earnings reached 46% of full-year 2025 net profit, so growth could be passing its peak. Daol Investment & Securities said second-quarter 2026 profit beat consensus but fell short of its own estimate.

The same report cited a KRW 20 billion valuation loss on unlisted assets and higher administrative costs from a higher education tax rate as factors that differed from earlier expectations.

Balance Sheet Burden

Total liabilities at the end of 2025 were KRW 73.9 trillion, and the debt-to-equity ratio rose to 783.5%, up from 639.2% in 2022. Operating cash flow was negative in each year from 2023 to 2025, reaching -KRW 5.42 trillion in 2025.

Total equity at that date was KRW 9.44 trillion, leaving a relatively thin capital cushion against the liability base.

Risk factors

Trading Volumes and Rates

Brokerage and WM income move with trading volumes and investor sentiment. Brokerage slowed in the third quarter of 2026 as trading volumes declined. In a rising-rate environment the trading and investment book is exposed to bond yield swings, and the company has emphasized conservative risk management.

Cost and Valuation Swings

In the second quarter of 2026, a KRW 20 billion valuation loss on unlisted assets and higher administrative costs from a higher education tax rate were cited as variables. Items like these can differ in size from quarter to quarter, unlike fee income.

Profit attributable to owners fell 21.6% from the prior quarter in the third quarter, so how far costs and valuation gains or losses move quarterly profit is also worth tracking.

Leverage and Cash Flow

The debt-to-equity ratio stood at 783.5% at the end of 2025, with total liabilities of KRW 73.9 trillion. Operating cash flow has been negative since 2023. Whether cash flow and the debt structure improve alongside earnings is a question for upcoming annual filings.

Overall view

NH Investment & Securities' audited results show a clear upward trend in operating profit and profit attributable to owners from 2022 through 2025.

Operating profit for 2025 was KRW 1.42 trillion and profit attributable to owners was KRW 1.03 trillion, and quarterly operating profit ran above KRW 600 billion in the first and second quarters of 2026.

Operating profit fell to KRW 521.1 billion in the third quarter, which the company attributed mainly to lower brokerage income from reduced trading volumes.

Strengths include first-place positions in ECM, IPO and FB underwriting, growth in financial product sales and asset management fees, and a customer asset base of KRW 612 trillion.

Weaknesses include heavy reliance on trading volumes and sentiment, negative operating cash flow since 2023, and a debt-to-equity ratio that climbed to 783.5% in 2025. Brokerage target prices reported in July 2026 ranged from KRW 43,000 to KRW 50,000.

The next checkpoints are the fourth-quarter 2026 preliminary results, the 2026 annual business report, and dividend decisions.
