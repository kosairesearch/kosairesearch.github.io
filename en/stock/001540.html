Ahn-Gook Pharmaceutical: Cardiovascular Franchise Reshapes Earnings Mix

02

Business structure

Ahn-Gook Pharmaceutical is a prescription-drug (ETC) company that is also building its Health & Beauty (H&B) business. Pharmaceutical segment sales were KRW 89.9 billion in Q1 2026, or 91% of total revenue.

By therapeutic class, cardiovascular product sales rose 39% year on year to KRW 45.0 billion, making it the largest category. Respiratory product sales fell 11% to KRW 15.8 billion, reducing its share from 24% to 16%.

Among individual products, the lipid-lowering combination Pevarozet became the largest product at KRW 11.2 billion, or 11.3% of sales, while Cinetura contributed KRW 8.2 billion (8.3%). H&B sales reached KRW 25.2 billion in the first three quarters of 2026, up 77.4% year on year, and accounted for 8.8% of revenue.

On an annual basis, H&B sales rose from KRW 15.6 billion in 2024 to KRW 20.8 billion in 2025. Tablets generated KRW 200 billion in 2025 sales, equal to 68.2% of standalone sales of KRW 293.3 billion.

Self-manufactured products make up more than 90% of revenue, so production efficiency and cost feed directly into profitability. On R&D, the company added CRO and drug-development advisory services to its business purpose in 2022 and launched an ophthalmology unit in 2023.

Overseas sales were KRW 0.6 billion in Q1 2026, or 0.6% of revenue, which confirms a domestically prescription-driven structure.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual revenue rose from KRW 205.4 billion in 2022 to KRW 233.7 billion in 2023 (up 13.8%), KRW 271.1 billion in 2024 (up 16.0%) and KRW 306.9 billion in 2025 (up 13.2%), and it has grown for three consecutive years from 2023 to 2025.

Operating profit fell from KRW 9.68 billion in 2022 to KRW 5.06 billion in 2023 (down 47.7%), then rose to KRW 6.7 billion in 2024 (up 32.4%) and KRW 9.82 billion in 2025 (up 46.6%), with two consecutive years of growth from 2024 to 2025.

Operating margin moved from 4.7% in 2022 to 2.2% in 2023, 2.5% in 2024 and 3.2% in 2025, a gradual recovery from the 2023 low.

Profit attributable to owners of the parent was KRW 6.88 billion in 2022, KRW 1.29 billion in 2023, KRW 16.8 billion in 2024 and KRW 7.48 billion in 2025, the largest in 2024 and down 55.6% in 2025.

Net income including non-controlling interests was KRW 16.3 billion in 2024 and KRW 7.32 billion in 2025, a different measure that should be read separately. The debt-to-equity ratio rose from 44.8% in 2022 to 87.0% in 2025, when total liabilities were KRW 144.7 billion and total equity was KRW 166.3 billion.

Operating cash flow went from KRW -1.33 billion in 2023 to KRW 4.91 billion in 2024 and KRW 45.7 billion in 2025. Quarterly, Q3 2025 revenue was KRW 77.7 billion with operating profit of KRW 4.88 billion, then Q4 2025 turned to an operating loss of KRW 5.88 billion.

Q1 2026 operating profit of KRW 16.0 billion rose 173.5% year on year, and operating margin improved from 7.7% to 16.2%. The improvement came from a gross margin of 66.8%, up 3.1 percentage points from a year earlier, while selling, general and administrative expenses rose about 21.5% to KRW 46.2 billion.

Q2 2026 operating profit was KRW 8.64 billion (down 46.1% quarter on quarter), and Q3 2026 was KRW 9.97 billion (up 15.3%), with a Q3 operating margin of 10.3%. Profit attributable to owners declined for two consecutive quarters after peaking in Q1 2026.

05

Industry analysis

The domestic pharmaceutical sector faces a difficult operating environment in 2026. The company's CEO said in the New Year address that the pharmaceutical industry is expected to face many difficulties in 2026.

On pricing, analysts note that as drug price cuts continue, lowering production costs and raising product-level cost competitiveness matters more.

In cardiovascular therapy, the company says it aims to keep expanding the share of pitavastatin-based combination drugs in a market led by rosuvastatin and atorvastatin combinations.

Analysts cite clinical results showing LDL-C improvement, with the sugar-metabolism profile of pitavastatin driving prescription growth in clinics.

In hypertension, the company received approval for Revosartan Plus tablets on April 28, 2026 and has licensed the three-drug combination Widaflic, which it expects to strengthen its position in hypertension.

Its heavy reliance on the domestic prescription market is reflected in overseas sales of just 0.6% of revenue in Q1 2026. Direct comparisons with large peers such as Yuhan and Hanmi Pharm require caution because scale and product mix differ.

In 2025 reporting, the company described its overseas expansion starting in Central America and moving into Southeast Asia and the Middle East. Product differentiation and cost control have become central variables for competitiveness within a cardiovascular-focused structure.

06

Outlook

In its 2026 corporate value-up plan, the company said it will secure differentiated products through new product development and in-licensing of overseas drugs, while strengthening cost competitiveness through capacity expansion and process optimization.

On shareholder returns, it introduced a system to set the dividend amount first and confirm the record date afterward, to improve predictability. The payout ratio for the prior fiscal year was 96.17%.

On products, the company obtained approval from the Ministry of Food and Drug Safety for a low-strength Pevarozet 1/10mg tablet, securing the lowest dose line-up.

Industry observers say that with a broader low-dose line-up and more CMO volume, annual Pevarozet sales could rise to around KRW 38.0 billion, above the internal target of KRW 35.0 billion. This is an industry estimate, and the company has not confirmed a formal guidance figure.

In its pipeline, the improved cardiovascular drug AG-1705 is in the approval process and, co-developed with Daewha Pharmaceutical, has reached the approval stage. AG-2304 is being co-developed with Boryung. Production at the Hwaseong plant resumed tablet manufacturing from June 6.

The company plans to keep R&D spending at around 5 to 6% of revenue. In a 2025 report, the company stated a medium-term goal of KRW 500 billion in annual revenue and entry into the top ten of the industry.

08

Bull factors

Cardiovascular franchise driving growth

Cardiovascular sales reached KRW 45.0 billion in Q1 2026, up 39%, and Pevarozet became the largest product. A low-strength approval widens treatment options, and industry observers see annual Pevarozet sales possibly reaching around KRW 38.0 billion. This is an industry estimate, not company guidance.

Profitability recovery

Operating margin recovered from 2.2% in 2023 to 3.2% in 2025 and reached 16.2% in Q1 2026. Cumulative operating profit for the first three quarters of 2026 exceeded the full-year 2025 total. A higher gross margin drove most of the improvement.

Fast-growing H&B business

H&B sales reached KRW 25.2 billion in the first three quarters of 2026, up 77.4%, and made up 8.8% of revenue. That already exceeded the full-year 2025 H&B figure of KRW 20.8 billion within nine months. A second growth engine outside prescription drugs adds diversification.

09

Bear factors

Quarterly earnings volatility

Q4 2025 swung to an operating loss of KRW 5.88 billion, and Q2 2026 operating profit fell 46.1% to KRW 8.64 billion. Profit attributable to owners has declined for two quarters since the Q1 2026 peak. Given this quarterly volatility, the durability of the annual recovery has yet to be tested.

Production halt and tablet dependence

Tablet production at the Hwaseong plant was halted for 15 days under an administrative order. Tablets are central to sales, so production gaps can affect revenue directly. The company said inventory on hand allowed normal sales during the halt.

Rising costs and higher leverage

Q1 2026 SG&A rose 21.5% to KRW 46.2 billion, and advertising costs jumped 146%. The debt-to-equity ratio climbed from 44.8% in 2022 to 87.0% in 2025. Higher selling costs and rising leverage at the same time add pressure on earnings capacity.

10

Risk factors

Production and regulatory risk

Tablet production was halted for 15 days, and tablets accounted for 68.2% of 2025 sales. Whether similar administrative actions recur and how quality controls are strengthened are key points to monitor. Vulnerability to production gaps remains until the capacity expansion and process optimization are completed.

Product concentration and competition

Pevarozet alone accounts for 11.3% of Q1 2026 sales, while Cinetura's share fell from 14.5% to 8.3%. Share competition with existing combination drugs in the lipid-lowering market continues. High dependence on a few products means earnings may respond sharply to prescription shifts.

Cost and investment burden

As drug price cuts continue, product-level cost competitiveness will weigh heavily on profit. The company is carrying out large production investment, so payback timing and financial burden are variables.

Operating cash flow was KRW 45.7 billion in 2025, but investment spending and dividend policy must be managed together amid quarterly earnings swings.

Overall view

Ahn-Gook Pharmaceutical's operating profit rose sharply in 2026, driven by Pevarozet and the expansion of its H&B business. Q1 2026 operating profit of KRW 16.0 billion marked a swing to profit, and cumulative operating profit for the first three quarters reached KRW 34.6 billion.
