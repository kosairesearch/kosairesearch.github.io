02

Business structure

Sang-A Frontec is a parts and materials company founded in 1974 that builds on super engineering plastics and has localized products previously imported. The business runs in three divisions: parts, materials and equipment.

Segment sales for 2025 in a Eugene Investment & Securities report were KRW 99.1 billion for parts, KRW 71.2 billion for materials, KRW 11.1 billion for equipment and KRW 14.5 billion for other. Parts account for roughly 51% of sales and materials for about 36%.

AlphaSquare's company profile says main revenue comes from secondary battery parts, with semiconductors next. Materials include OA belts, release films and membranes, while wafer carriers and FPD cassettes sit in equipment.

Equipment sales fell from KRW 22.0 billion in 2022 to KRW 11.1 billion in 2025, shrinking its share. Parts sales rose to KRW 37.2 billion in the fourth quarter of 2025 from KRW 24.3 billion in the third quarter, and the company's own commentary links this to higher secondary battery sales as electric vehicles expand.

The customer base has spread from home appliances and OA into semiconductors, displays, automotive, secondary batteries and medical devices. A 2019 trade-media profile put export share at 50% and said the company entered markets long dominated by Japanese and US firms.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual sales rose from KRW 181.2 billion in 2022 to KRW 183.6 billion in 2023, a 1.3% increase, fell to KRW 171.8 billion in 2024, a 6.5% decline, and recovered to KRW 196 billion in 2025, a 14.1% increase. The largest year in 2022–2025 was 2025 and the smallest was 2024.

Operating profit went from KRW 12.9 billion in 2022 to KRW 8.96 billion in 2023, a 30.6% decrease, and to KRW 5.85 billion in 2024, a 34.7% decrease, before rising to KRW 6.71 billion in 2025, a 14.7% increase.

Sales grew 14.1% in 2025, yet the operating margin stayed at 3.4%, the same level as 2024, so top-line growth did not lift margins. Net income attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 7.43 billion in 2022, KRW 12 billion in 2023, KRW 6.16 billion in 2024 and KRW 6.76 billion in 2025, with 2023 the largest year.

Net income including non-controlling interests was reported as the same figures in each year. Operating cash flow fell to KRW 8.04 billion in 2025 from KRW 21.6 billion in 2024, and the debt ratio rose to 86.9%.

In the fourth quarter of 2025, sales reached KRW 60.6 billion, up 21.4% from the prior quarter, while operating profit fell 9.2% to KRW 1.93 billion. First-quarter 2026 sales were KRW 48.5 billion, down 20.0%, and operating profit was KRW 1.17 billion, down 39.7%, marking the low point.

In the second quarter of 2026, operating profit jumped 207.2% to KRW 3.58 billion and net income attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 4.97 billion.

Third-quarter 2026 sales were KRW 61.6 billion, up 10.3% from the prior quarter and 23.3% from a year earlier, but operating profit fell 15.8% to KRW 3.02 billion and the company posted a net loss attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 2.04 billion, a swing into deficit.

Operating profit and net income moved in opposite directions that quarter. A company official said the US unit began earning profits in the third quarter of 2026, while a press report said the Hungarian unit still posted losses, with a net loss of KRW 34 million in 2025 and KRW 13 million in the first half of 2026.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders over the last four quarters, from the fourth quarter of 2025 to the third quarter of 2026, totals KRW 6.71 billion.

05

Industry analysis

Sang-A Frontec's main end markets are secondary batteries, semiconductors, displays, automotive and printers, many tied to electric vehicles and IT equipment. In parts, the company's commentary in September 2026 linked higher secondary battery sales to electric vehicle expansion.

For materials, it described continued semiconductor demand from digital transformation. It also said it is mass-producing polymer electrolyte membranes for fuel cells and expects growth as eco-friendly vehicles expand.

Eugene Investment & Securities cited a rise in materials sales for satellite operators as a driver of profit growth in a March 2026 report. Demand is concentrated in electric vehicles, secondary batteries and hydrogen vehicles, so investment pace in those industries feeds quickly into sales swings.

On competition, the company said in a 2019 trade-media profile that it has few domestic rivals, and it described entering a market long held by Japanese and US firms.

Its sales scale is far smaller than chemical majors such as LG Chem and Hanwha Solutions, so it competes mainly on technology in specific product lines as a small and mid-sized materials and parts maker.

Bull factors

A sales base anchored in secondary battery parts

Parts sales reached KRW 99.1 billion in 2025, close to half of the total, and rose to KRW 37.2 billion in the fourth quarter from KRW 24.3 billion in the third. The company's commentary points to higher secondary battery sales as electric vehicles expand. If parts growth keeps lifting materials and equipment, it supports a case for top-line recovery.

A quarterly recovery trend

Sales bottomed at KRW 48.5 billion in the first quarter of 2026 and then rose to KRW 55.8 billion in the second and KRW 61.6 billion in the third. The second quarter saw both sales and operating profit rise together, with operating profit up 207.2% from the prior quarter to KRW 3.58 billion. Whether third-quarter sales growth carried through to profit is the gauge for this trend.

Management outlook and the US unit

A company official said annual sales for 2026 would exceed KRW 200 billion and that the margin would be around 5–6%. The official also said the US unit began earning profits in the third quarter of 2026. These are management statements rather than disclosed figures, so the annual report is the place to test them.

Bear factors

A third-quarter swing to net loss

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders swung to a loss of KRW 2.04 billion in the third quarter of 2026, from a profit in the prior quarter and also against a profit a year earlier. Operating profit fell 15.8% from the second quarter to KRW 3.02 billion.

Because operating profit and net income moved in opposite directions, the gap between the two needs close checking.

Stalled margins and a long decline

The operating margin slid from 7.1% in 2022 to 4.9% in 2023 and 3.4% in 2024, and it stayed at 3.4% in 2025. Sales grew 14.1% that year without a margin gain. Whether the trend turns will be settled by the full-year 2026 margin in the annual report.

Balance sheet and cash flow pressure

Total liabilities at the end of 2025 were KRW 176.7 billion against total equity of KRW 203.3 billion, and the debt ratio rose from 80.2% in 2024 to 86.9%. Operating cash flow fell to KRW 8.04 billion in 2025, down from KRW 15.6 billion in 2022.

A press report says about KRW 60 billion of convertible bonds is being repaid from cash on hand, which could limit room for spending.

Risk factors

Demand concentration and quarterly volatility

Parts revenue leans heavily on secondary battery and electric vehicle demand, so slower investment in those industries could cut quarterly sales quickly. Parts sales jumped in the fourth quarter of 2025, and total sales then fell 20.0% in the first quarter of 2026, showing wide quarterly swings. Some quarters also saw sales growth fail to carry through to operating profit.

Financing and cash flow constraints

The debt ratio rose to 86.9% and operating cash flow fell to KRW 8.04 billion. Because cash on hand is being used for convertible bond repayment and purchases, room for working capital and facility investment could narrow.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders has turned negative in a quarter that still showed operating profit, so earnings quality needs close review.

Overseas units and raw material exposure

A press report says the Hungarian unit still posts losses, with a net loss of KRW 34 million in 2025 and KRW 13 million in the first half of 2026. A 2019 trade-media profile put export share at 50%, which leaves the company exposed to exchange rates and overseas demand. The same profile says key raw materials such as PI, PPS, PTFE and PEEK are imported and processed in-house.

Overall view

Sang-A Frontec is a parts and materials company that supplies secondary battery parts, semiconductor and display materials, and equipment, built on super engineering plastics. Sales of KRW 196 billion in 2025 were the largest in 2022–2025, yet the operating margin fell from 7.1% in 2022 to 3.4% in 2025.

In 2026, sales rose for two straight quarters after the first-quarter low, and second-quarter operating profit recovered sharply.

The bull side rests on the secondary battery parts base and management's annual sales outlook, while the bear side rests on stalled margins, the third-quarter loss and financing pressure. Valuation is framed by book value, dividend yield and a broker target price.
