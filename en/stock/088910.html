02

Business structure

Dongwoo Farm TO Table is a vertically integrated chicken company that covers the whole process from breeding and hatching through raising, slaughtering, processing and distribution.

In first-half 2026 consolidated sales, slaughtered chicken was KRW 140.0 billion, or 84%, followed by feed products at KRW 3.9 billion (2%), trading products at KRW 3.1 billion (2%), and live birds and other items at KRW 20.2 billion (12%).

Slaughtered chicken is sold under the 'Bareun Dakgogi' brand, and among sales by product type, partial cuts were the largest at KRW 76.3 billion, or 45.7%. Analysts attribute the partial-cut growth to expanding large supermarkets and institutional catering.

Customers include poultry raw-material distributors, large supermarkets, franchise chains and catering operators. Including affiliate Chamfre, the group held a 15.0% share of domestic slaughter volume in the third quarter of 2026, while Dongwoo alone held 7.3% (per the Q3 2026 disclosure summary).

In July 2026 the company sold 45% of subsidiary U Nong and removed it from consolidation.

In competition, a 2020 equity research report described Harim and three other integrators as leaders as the market consolidated around a few large players, and the same report said about 91% of domestic chicken is supplied through integrators.

Because feed, breeding, raising and processing are managed in-house, changes in chicken prices and raising costs pass directly into earnings.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual revenue peaked at KRW 349.2 billion in 2023 (up 3.7% year on year) from KRW 336.8 billion in 2022, and was lowest in 2024 at KRW 303.1 billion (down 13.2% year on year). Revenue recovered in 2025 to KRW 325.5 billion (up 7.4% year on year).

Operating profit rose from KRW 11.9 billion in 2022 to KRW 21.1 billion in 2023 (up 77.1% year on year), but swung to an operating loss of KRW 4.31 billion in 2024. In 2025, operating profit returned to the black at KRW 13.2 billion, for an operating margin of 4.0%. The operating margin in 2024 was -1.4%.

Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 26.4 billion in 2023 (up 63.7% year on year), swung to a net loss of KRW 7.76 billion in 2024, and then returned to profit at KRW 28.3 billion in 2025.

Net income including non-controlling interests was KRW 28.2 billion in 2025, a different figure from the profit attributable to controlling shareholders.

On the balance sheet, the debt-to-equity ratio fell from 48.2% in 2022 to 17.8% in 2025; at the end of 2025, total equity was KRW 239.6 billion (KRW 239.5 billion attributable to controlling shareholders) and total liabilities were KRW 42.6 billion.

Cash flow from operating activities turned around from -KRW 6.19 billion in 2024 to KRW 25.5 billion in 2025. The quarterly trend is even clearer.

Operating profit peaked for the quarter in Q4 2025, with revenue of KRW 80.6 billion and operating profit of KRW 4.65 billion (up 52.7% quarter on quarter), compared with revenue of KRW 88.3 billion and operating profit of KRW 3.05 billion in Q3 2025.

Operating profit in Q1 2026 was KRW 4.2 billion (down 9.8% quarter on quarter), in Q2 it was KRW 0.96 billion (down 77.2% quarter on quarter), and in Q3 it was KRW 13.73 million (down 98.6% quarter on quarter). Revenue stayed at a high level, at KRW 91.2 billion in Q2 2026 and KRW 90.9 billion in Q3 2026.

Over the same period, net profit attributable to controlling shareholders fell from KRW 10.8 billion in Q2 2026 to KRW 1.05 billion in Q3 (down 85.0% year on year).

The key feature of the recent trend is that profit margins have dropped sharply while revenue has held steady, and the cause of the steep fall in Q3 operating profit is something to check in the profit and loss discussion of the business report.

05

Industry analysis

Domestic chicken supply is dominated by integrated operators that run breeding, hatching, raising and slaughtering in-house. A 2020 equity research report said about 91% of domestic chicken is supplied through integrators, and named Harim and Dongwoo Farm TO Table among four large players that drove consolidation.

In the current cycle, the expansion of the restaurant industry and franchise chains has been cited as lifting demand for partial cuts and salted meat (per a June 2026 corporate monitor comment). The company's first-half 2026 partial-cut sales were KRW 76.3 billion, or 45.7% of its sales mix.

On the other hand, changes across the industry in chicken consumption, prices and raising costs flow directly into earnings, so exposure to sector swings is high.

On share, the group including affiliate Chamfre held 15.0% of domestic slaughter volume, versus 7.3% for Dongwoo alone, so comparisons need to specify which basis is used. Slaughter volume trends and raising-cost changes are the common indicators for judging where the cycle stands.

Compared with food processors in the wider food and beverage sector, integrated poultry companies have different asset bases and cost structures, which limits direct comparisons.

Outlook

The outlook is framed around verifiable facts. First, the deconsolidation of subsidiary U Nong after the July 2026 sale of a 45% stake is a point to track in how it is reflected in sales and operating profit from Q3 2026 onward.

Second, partial-cut demand rose to KRW 76.3 billion in first-half 2026 on the expansion of large supermarkets and institutional catering, and whether that continues into the second half is central to the sales mix.

Third, the drop in Q3 2026 operating profit to KRW 13.73 million cannot be explained by quarterly figures alone, so the 2026 annual report, with its cost and selling expense breakdown by business, is the most direct source.

Fourth, the group's share of slaughter volume was 15.0% including Chamfre and 7.3% for Dongwoo alone, and holding that share will shape subsequent sales trends.

Fifth, chicken prices and raising costs are external variables the company cannot fully control, so the extent to which cost changes were passed through to selling prices should be checked in the annual report's margin analysis.

Sixth, quarterly sales of KRW 90.9 billion in Q3 2026 have held at a high level, which makes margin recovery the key turning point for earnings. The 2026 annual report is due by the end of March 2027 under the statutory deadline.

Risk factors

Raw material and raising costs

Chicken prices and raising costs are variables the company cannot fully control. When feed, chicks and raising costs rise, slaughtered-chicken margins are affected first. Given the sharp fall in Q3 2026 operating margin, the ability to pass costs through to prices is central to any earnings recovery.

Concentration and perimeter changes

With slaughtered chicken at 84% of sales, results are sensitive to supply shifts in one product. The July 2026 sale of 45% of U Nong changed the consolidation perimeter and therefore the basis of year-on-year comparisons.

The effect of the deconsolidation must be read through the annual report's explanations to understand the underlying change.

Earnings volatility

Annual operating profit moved between increases and a loss between 2022 and 2025. Quarterly operating profit has fallen for three straight quarters in 2026, and profit attributable to controlling shareholders dropped to KRW 1.05 billion in Q3 2026. The KRW 26.7 billion four-quarter total shows a recovery, but the quarterly trend remains weak.

Overall view

Dongwoo Farm TO Table is an integrated chicken company that moved out of its 2024 loss, with 2025 operating profit of KRW 13.2 billion and profit attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 28.3 billion. The debt ratio was 17.8% at end-2025, with total equity of KRW 239.6 billion.

However, quarterly operating profit peaked at KRW 4.65 billion in Q4 2025 and then fell to KRW 4.2 billion in Q1 2026, KRW 957 million in Q2 2026 and KRW 13.73 million in Q3 2026, declining for three straight quarters.

Sales held at KRW 91.2 billion in Q2 2026 and KRW 90.9 billion in Q3 2026, so the margin decline is the central issue. Strengths include financial capacity after the return to profit, growing partial-cut demand, and multiples below the industry average.

Weaknesses include the sharp fall in quarterly profit, dependence on slaughtered chicken at 84% of sales, and the change in sales scale after deconsolidating U Nong. Because the low multiples coincide with the quarterly profit drop, how they are read depends on whether earnings recovery continues.

The next checkpoints are the cause of the Q3 2026 operating profit drop and Q4 2026 results in the 2026 annual report, followed by Q1 2027 results. This report does not present an investment opinion, rating or target price and is provided for information only.
