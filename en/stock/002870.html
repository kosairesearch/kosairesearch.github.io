02

Business structure

Shinpoong is a KOSPI-listed company whose main business is paper distribution. It filed a material matters report in September 2020 on the halt of its white board production division, and since then has restructured around distribution rather than manufacturing. Its main products are manila board and white board.

According to the company, it has shifted to distribution since 2020, selling paper products through its customer network, and it has said that introducing converting equipment would strengthen competitiveness, revenue and operating profit. Segment weights differ by source.

According to FnGuide, paper distribution accounted for 92.16 percent of revenue in December 2024, with imported cars at 4.51 percent and other operations at 3.33 percent.

A company information page, however, lists paper distribution at 66.9 percent and food service at 29.2 percent, so the reference date and basis should be checked before comparing.

In 2025 revenue rose as new equipment increased paper distribution volumes, while the imported electric vehicle business ended and its revenue declined. Subsidiary investments also weighed on results.

According to Asia Economy, KRW 15 billion was invested in subsidiaries by the end of 2023, and KRW 13.4 billion of that was recognized as impairment losses. The company has said that paper distribution is small in scale, making it hard to absorb fixed costs and generate profit.

04

Earnings analysis

On a reported basis, annual revenue rose from KRW 18.8 billion in 2022 to KRW 26.9 billion in 2023, a 43.2% increase, making 2023 the largest year of 2022 to 2025. Revenue fell 15.3% to KRW 22.8 billion in 2024 and recovered 6.4% to KRW 24.2 billion in 2025.

The operating loss was KRW 4.2 billion in 2022, KRW 4 billion in 2023, KRW 4.37 billion in 2024 and KRW 2.29 billion in 2025, so the company has posted operating losses for four consecutive years from 2022 through 2025.

The operating margin was -22.4 percent, -14.9 percent, -19.2 percent and -9.5 percent over the same years, with 2025 showing the smallest loss margin. In 2024 revenue declined while the operating loss widened, and in 2025 revenue grew while the operating loss narrowed.

In a June 2025 article, Asia Economy reported that Shinpoong had recorded five consecutive years of losses.

Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders moved from a loss of KRW 7.54 billion in 2022 and KRW 6.5 billion in 2023 to a profit of KRW 309 million in 2024, then reached KRW 2 billion in 2025, a 547.7% increase.

Net loss including non-controlling interests was KRW 9.21 billion in 2023, larger than the KRW 6.5 billion loss attributable to controlling shareholders.

On a quarterly basis, operating losses were KRW 366 million in the third quarter of 2025, KRW 579 million in the fourth quarter, KRW 564 million in the first quarter of 2026, KRW 613 million in the second quarter and KRW 791 million in the third quarter of 2026, a broadly widening trend.

The third-quarter 2026 operating loss was larger than a year earlier. Revenue peaked at KRW 6.81 billion in the second quarter of 2026 before falling to KRW 6.1 billion in the third quarter, down 3.5 percent from a year earlier.

Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders jumped to KRW 6.89 billion in the second quarter of 2026, up 714.8 percent from the prior quarter, then swung to a net loss of KRW 5.61 billion in the third quarter.

Persistent operating losses alongside sharp swings in attributable net income suggest that non-operating or one-off items had a large effect. Operating cash flow went from negative KRW 3.92 billion in 2022 and negative KRW 17.5 billion in 2023 to positive KRW 2.97 billion in 2024 and KRW 2.66 billion in 2025.

05

Industry analysis

Paper distribution buys and resells paper products such as manila board and white board, so sales volume and equipment utilization drive earnings. Shinpoong's 2025 revenue increased after it introduced converting equipment that raised sales volumes.

The company has said, however, that paper distribution is small in scale, making it difficult to cover fixed costs and generate profit. The scale gap with the sector is wide.

According to FnGuide's aggregate for the KOSPI distribution sector, total sector revenue was KRW 2,470,290 billion, so Shinpoong's 2025 revenue of KRW 24.2 billion equals about 0.01 percent of the sector. Its business model and scale differ from large distributors, so profitability metrics are hard to compare directly.

Because raw paper costs and logistics costs generally determine margins in this industry, the room for improvement in the operating margin depends on selling prices and how well fixed costs are absorbed.

Revenue fell in the third quarter of 2026 after peaking in the second quarter, so the direction of volumes and unit prices should be tracked quarter by quarter. Equipment investment has lifted revenue but has not yet translated into profit improvement, which is the central challenge of its position within the sector.

Outlook

At the regular shareholders' meeting in March 2026, resolutions on articles of incorporation changes, director and auditor pay limits, and a plan for holding and disposing of treasury shares all passed, according to a Digital Today report published on March 30 of that year.

The treasury share plan received 98 percent approval of voting shares. In a June 2025 article, Asia Economy reported that the largest shareholder was exploring a sale of control together with roughly 30 percent of its stake, and that negotiations had stalled over differing demands.

In the core business, the 2025 converting equipment investment showed up in higher revenue, but third-quarter 2026 revenue fell from a year earlier.

The company has said that new businesses and investments are not easy to pursue, and because KRW 13.4 billion of subsidiary investment was previously written down, the results of new investments need verification.

Progress toward core profitability can be tracked through the quarterly operating loss trend and the pace of revenue recovery. If a sale of control is completed, the business direction could change, so changes in ownership structure should be watched as well.

Bull factors

Narrowing operating loss

The operating loss narrowed from KRW 4.2 billion in 2022 to KRW 2.29 billion in 2025, and the operating margin improved from -22.4 percent to -9.5 percent. Revenue rose to KRW 24.2 billion in 2025 as the loss narrowed.

Higher paper distribution volumes after the converting equipment introduction are cited as the background. The loss structure has not disappeared, but the direction of narrowing is clear.

Low debt and cash flow turnaround

Total liabilities were KRW 2.84 billion at the end of 2025, a debt ratio of 3.9 percent, indicating low financial burden. Operating cash flow was positive in both 2024 and 2025, moving away from the cash outflows of the prior two years. Total equity of KRW 73.7 billion is large relative to the operating scale. This points to a limited need for external financing.

Turn to attributable profit

Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders swung from losses of KRW 7.54 billion in 2022 and KRW 6.5 billion in 2023 to KRW 309 million in 2024, then reached KRW 2 billion in 2025. The four-quarter total was KRW 2.11 billion. It jumped to KRW 6.89 billion in the second quarter of 2026. Non-operating factors may have contributed, so the cause should be checked in the annual report.

The improvement seen annually has not carried through at the quarterly level, which is a bearish factor.

Stalled revenue and quarterly decline

Annual revenue rose from KRW 18.8 billion in 2022 to KRW 24.2 billion in 2025, but it has not established a clear growth path since 2023. Without top-line growth, absorbing fixed costs remains difficult, which is a burden.

Earnings quality and subsidiary impairment history

Net loss attributable to controlling shareholders reached KRW 5.61 billion in the third quarter of 2026, diverging from the operating loss. The four-quarter total of KRW 2.11 billion comes with large quarter-to-quarter swings, which makes it hard to treat as stable earnings.

The company invested KRW 15 billion in subsidiaries by the end of 2023 and recognized KRW 13.4 billion as impairment. Costs from new-business investments have been reflected in results repeatedly, which is a bearish factor.

Negotiations were reportedly delayed by differences over the control premium. A change of control could alter business direction and investment policy. Business concentration and fixed cost structure

The business is concentrated in paper distribution, and the company has said the scale is small, making it hard to absorb fixed costs. Segment weights also differ across sources, so the business mix cannot be read from one data point.

Without a meaningful increase in revenue, the operating loss structure is unlikely to change easily. Selling prices and equipment utilization are the key variables.

Earnings volatility and non-operating items

Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders jumped to KRW 6.89 billion in the second quarter of 2026 before turning into a net loss of KRW 5.61 billion in the third quarter. Large swings in net income while operating losses continue point to possible effects from non-operating or one-off items.

Such volatility makes it hard to judge core business trends from quarterly results alone. The breakdown of profit and loss items in the annual report needs to be checked.

Overall view

Shinpoong is a small listed company centered on paper distribution, and its annual operating loss narrowed from KRW 4.2 billion in 2022 to KRW 2.29 billion in 2025.

However, operating losses have persisted for five straight quarters from the third quarter of 2025 through the third quarter of 2026, and the third-quarter 2026 operating loss of KRW 791 million widened from a year earlier.

Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders has shown profit periods since 2024 but swings sharply by quarter, so the four-quarter total of KRW 2.11 billion should be read alongside the operating loss trend.

On the financial side, total equity was KRW 73.7 billion and total liabilities KRW 2.84 billion at the end of 2025, a debt ratio of 3.9 percent, and operating cash flow was positive in 2024 and 2025.

Bullish factors include financial capacity and the narrowing annual loss trend, while bearish factors include stalled revenue, consecutive quarterly operating losses, the history of subsidiary impairments and uncertainty over a possible sale of control.

The next checkpoints are the operating margin and non-operating income breakdown in the 2026 annual report, due by March 31, 2027, and whether the operating loss trend changes in the first-quarter 2027 report due by May 15, 2027.
