Quarterly profit returns; core recovery still to prove

02

Business structure

Omnisystem's core business is MMV (measurement, monitoring and verification), covering digital electricity meters and remote meter reading systems, and it also runs credit card manufacturing and lighting businesses. The MMV unit includes smart metering, energy storage systems (ESS) and energy management systems (EMS).

Based on the product revenue mix as of December 2025, meters accounted for 55.01%, cards 35.94% and lighting 9.06%. At the same date, product revenue split 68.81% domestic and 31.19% export.

Digital electricity meters are described as products that prevent tampering with meter data and electricity theft, and they are exported to developing countries such as Vietnam, Thailand and Myanmar.

In Q1 2026, metering and remote reading revenue fell from KRW 16 billion a year earlier to KRW 7.8 billion, more than halving, according to press reports.

In the same quarter the card unit recorded revenue of KRW 8.5 billion and an operating profit of KRW 300 million, partly cushioning the decline, while the lighting unit stayed loss-making.

The company has set up a dedicated organization for its EV charger business and is pursuing slow and fast charger certification as well as charging platform R&D. R&D spending as a share of revenue was 4.78% in 2022, 3.91% in 2024 and 4.29% in 2025.

Among sector peers, LS ELECTRIC, Hyosung Heavy Industries and HD Hyundai Electric focus on large-scale power equipment, so their product scope differs from Omnisystem's meter, card and lighting mix. The company has said its sales are heavily affected by construction activity in its downstream market.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual revenue fell from KRW 98 billion in 2022 to KRW 91.4 billion in 2023, down 6.8% year on year, then rose to KRW 109.1 billion in 2024, up 19.3%, making 2024 the largest year in the 2022 to 2025 span. Revenue in 2025 was KRW 99.1 billion, down 9.2% year on year.

Operating profit was smallest in 2023 at KRW 305 million, down 83.6% year on year, and largest in 2024 at KRW 5.95 billion, up 1,850.5% year on year. Operating profit in 2025 was KRW 1.02 billion, down 82.9% year on year.

The operating margin was 1.9% in 2022, 0.3% in 2023, 5.5% in 2024 and 1.0% in 2025, so outside 2024 it stayed in a range of 0.3% to 1.9%.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders rose from KRW 570 million in 2022 to KRW 710 million in 2023 and KRW 6.71 billion in 2024, then swung to a loss of KRW 201 million in 2025. Net income including non-controlling interests was a loss of KRW 154 million in 2025.

The company cited lower sales from weak construction demand and the recognition of equity-method losses among the factors behind the 2025 decline. Operating cash flow was KRW 8.49 billion in 2025, up from KRW 1.58 billion in 2024.

On a quarterly basis, Q3 2025 posted an operating loss of KRW 75.53 million, and Q4 2025 turned to an operating profit of KRW 1.42 billion. Q1 2026 revenue was KRW 18.4 billion, down 24.6% from the prior quarter, with an operating loss of KRW 289 million.

Q2 2026 revenue recovered to KRW 24.2 billion, up 31.9% from the prior quarter, with operating profit of KRW 76.51 million. Q3 2026 revenue reached KRW 28.6 billion, up 17.9% from the prior quarter and 47.6% year on year, with operating profit of KRW 2.05 billion.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders over the latest four quarters totaled KRW 2.88 billion. The gap between an operating loss and a net profit in Q1 2026 remains a point of divergence between core earnings and bottom-line results.

05

Industry analysis

Omnisystem's revenue is heavily affected by downstream demand linked to construction activity, according to the company. The company has said a short-term recovery in construction is unlikely, so the pace of construction demand remains a key variable for future results.

The remote meter reading market is described as seeing wider adoption by construction companies as the company develops digital electricity meters and facility meters. At the same time, an investment information site summary notes that profitability remains limited by cost burdens.

Smart metering, ESS and EMS are described as businesses expanding within the MMV unit. The card unit supported results in Q1 2026 with revenue of KRW 8.5 billion and an operating profit of KRW 300 million. A 2024 press report expected solid demand in the card business. The lighting unit remained loss-making in Q1 2026.

On competition, large power equipment makers in the same electrical equipment sector focus on high-voltage and large facilities, which makes direct comparison difficult. Omnisystem's narrower focus on metering and remote reading exposes it to facility demand and policy changes in that niche.

06

Outlook

The company has said a short-term recovery in construction is unlikely. In response, it plans to expand exports, including to Vietnam, and sales activities to shore up its revenue base.

The EV charger business is at a stage where a dedicated organization has been formed and certification for slow and fast chargers and charging platform R&D are underway.

In July 2026 the board approved a share consolidation converting five common shares into one and a buyback of KRW 2 billion worth of shares, with the buyback period running from July 22, 2026 to October 21, 2026.

The stated purpose of the consolidation was stabilizing the share price by maintaining an appropriate number of tradable shares and enhancing corporate value. Following the par value consolidation listing, trading resumed on October 20, 2026.

A company official said continuing to inject funds to support the share price has its limits. Press coverage has said that whether the share price can hold depends on a recovery in core earnings. Because revenue rose in Q2 and Q3 2026, the key checkpoint is how that trend meets a recovery in construction-linked demand.

The next periodic report is the 2026 annual report, which will show full-year revenue and operating margin against the 2025 level.

08

Bull factors

Revenue up two straight quarters

Q2 2026 revenue of KRW 24.2 billion rose 31.9% from the prior quarter, and Q3 revenue of KRW 28.6 billion was up 47.6% year on year. Q3 operating profit of KRW 2.05 billion represents a swing to profit from a year earlier. The recovery in revenue since the Q1 2026 low is cited as the main bullish argument.

Expansion in remote metering and energy management

An assessment holds that adoption of remote meter reading is widening among construction companies as the company develops digital electricity meters and facility meters. The company includes smart metering, ESS and EMS in its MMV unit.

The formation of an EV charger organization and the pursuit of certification also broaden its business scope. The revenue contribution of these new businesses is not broken out separately in the disclosed results.

Larger shareholder stake and buyback

According to a July 2026 press report, the largest shareholder, Biosmart, acquired an additional 1.75 million shares, bringing its holding to about 15.67 million shares and its stake from 23.41% to 26.39%. In July 2026 the company decided on a buyback of KRW 2 billion worth of shares. The stated purpose was share price stability and enhancing shareholder value.

Bear factors

Prolonged weakness in construction demand

The company's sales are heavily affected by construction activity in its downstream market, and a company official said a short-term recovery in construction is unlikely. Metering and remote reading revenue in Q1 2026 fell from KRW 16 billion to KRW 7.8 billion year on year, more than halving. The bearish argument is that without a recovery in this unit, a rebound in annual results will be limited.

Earnings quality and non-operating swings

The company cited lower sales and equity-method losses among the drivers of the 2025 earnings decline. Q1 2026 posted an operating loss of KRW 289 million yet a net profit, which press reports attributed to other and financial income. With the 2025 operating margin at 1.0%, the cushion in core profitability is thin.

Penny-stock rules and share price volatility

On June 15, 2026 the stock was designated for one day as an investment caution stock due to concentrated buying by a small number of accounts.

Stricter KOSDAQ penny-stock rules took effect from July 1, 2026, and companies with a consolidation or reduction in the past year cannot make further consolidations or reductions for 90 trading days after a management-issue designation. The company's own remark that share price support has limits is read as a bearish factor.

Risk factors

Export market risk

Exports account for 31.19% of product revenue, and digital electricity meters are shipped to developing countries such as Vietnam, Thailand and Myanmar. The company plans to expand exports, including to Vietnam, and sales activities to shore up its revenue base.

Export results in developing markets can move differently from expectations due to local policy changes, currency swings or shifts in order timing. Whether this plan grows large enough to offset the gap in domestic construction demand has not yet been determined.

EV charger certification risk

The EV charger business has set up a dedicated organization and is pursuing slow and fast charger certification as well as charging infrastructure platform R&D. R&D spending was 4.29% of revenue in 2025, and if certification is delayed, these costs may remain a drag on profitability.

The timing of certification and commercialization of the charging platform is not yet set, so when the business will contribute to revenue is also uncertain.

Card and lighting profit risk

The card unit posted an operating profit in Q1 2026 that partly cushioned the revenue decline, and cards account for 35.94% of product revenue. If card demand weakens, the unit's role in supporting overall profit could shrink.

The lighting unit remained loss-making in Q1 2026 and continues to weigh on overall profitability. The profit direction of these two units, alongside a recovery in metering, will shape the earnings trend.

Overall view

Omnisystem's revenue turned up from its Q1 2026 low, rising for two consecutive quarters through Q3, and Q3 2026 showed a swing to operating profit. The quarterly trend does show a recovery direction, but the gap between Q1 2026's operating loss and its net profit remains a question about earnings quality.

Weak construction demand is a bearish factor that the company itself says will be hard to reverse in the short term. The price-to-book ratio sits well below 1x, so the share price is low relative to asset value, but earnings volatility means how the multiple is read depends on the pace of earnings recovery.

On the shareholder side, the buyback, share consolidation and the largest shareholder's stake increase have continued, while the 2026 caution designation and penny-stock rules remain sources of price volatility.

Bullish factors are the quarterly revenue recovery and expansion in remote metering and energy management, while bearish factors are weak construction demand and non-operating swings.
