02

Business structure

Hanil Feed is a compound feed maker that also imports and distributes meat, and since the 1990s it has pursued a 'FROM FEED TO FOOD' strategy to expand from feed into livestock distribution.

In the first-quarter 2026 periodic report, meat distribution accounted for 69.16% of segment sales, cattle feed for 22.95%, and poultry feed for 7.29%. Press coverage of full-year 2025 results put meat distribution at 66.3% of sales and feed at 33.5%.

The meat business sells mainly imported beef, so import costs and market prices drive its revenue and profit. Within feed, cattle feed is the core and poultry feed is a small share.

Dairy feed supply anchors the feed business, and Seoul Milk Cooperative is its main feed customer, accounting for more than half of feed segment sales. The company re-signed its supply contract with Seoul Milk in August 2025, covering supply through August 2029 worth KRW 473.4 billion.

Its main meat distribution customers are other large domestic buyers. The group has eight consolidated subsidiaries, including a meat-trading subsidiary with seven group companies and the Vietnamese unit Hanil F&F Vina. Steady dairy feed volume and the more volatile imported meat business together shape the swings in earnings.

04

Earnings analysis

Operating income moved from an operating loss of KRW 8.24 billion in 2022 to operating income of KRW 1.77 billion in 2023, a turn to profit, then rose to KRW 3.7 billion in 2024 (up 109.1% year on year) and KRW 5.86 billion in 2025 (up 58.6%), growing for two consecutive years from the 2023 low.

Sales went from KRW 408.3 billion in 2022 to KRW 396.9 billion in 2023 (down 2.8%), KRW 431 billion in 2024 (up 8.6%) and KRW 454.9 billion in 2025 (up 5.5%), making 2025 the largest year in the 2022–2025 period.

Operating margin rose from -2.0% in 2022 to 0.4% in 2023, 0.9% in 2024 and 1.3% in 2025, still in the low single digits. The company attributed the 2025 operating profit gain mainly to lower raw material costs in the feed segment, and equity-method gains also lifted pre-tax profit.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders swung from a loss of KRW 14.2 billion in 2022 to KRW 115.1 billion in 2023, fell to KRW 3.69 billion in 2024 (down 96.8%) and rose to KRW 6.38 billion in 2025 (up 73.0%).

The 2023 figure sat far above that year's operating income, so the weight of non-operating items should be checked in the notes to the business report. Net income including non-controlling interests was KRW 6.25 billion in 2025, a different figure from the controlling-shareholder number.

Quarterly, operating income peaked at KRW 2.55 billion in the third quarter of 2025, eased to KRW 1.85 billion in the fourth quarter and KRW 1.48 billion in the first quarter of 2026, then turned to an operating loss of KRW 377 million in the second quarter.

Third-quarter 2026 sales reached KRW 133.1 billion, up 6.9% year on year, yet the operating loss widened to KRW 1.95 billion. Net income attributable to controlling shareholders over the last four quarters totaled KRW 3.49 billion.

The debt ratio fell from 244.3% in 2022 to 27.2% in 2025, and operating cash flow improved from -KRW 19.8 billion to KRW 15.7 billion.

05

Industry analysis

Compound feed demand moves with domestic livestock inventories and dairy output, and the company holds steady volume through its dairy feed supply to Seoul Milk Cooperative. Meat distribution depends on beef prices.

The first-quarter 2026 commentary described US beef supply shortages and Australian beef substitution demand pushing import costs higher, keeping market prices firm.

The third-quarter 2025 commentary described a shift to a firmer beef market on holiday demand, limited import volumes and substitution demand for pork, which lifted meat distribution sales. That commentary expected the beef market to stay firm in the second half on high costs and tight supply.

In feed, the company attributed the 2025 operating profit gain to lower raw material costs, so grain and ingredient prices drive feed margins.

The position in the industry cycle is hard to pin down, but beef prices move with import costs, substitute demand and exchange rates, which widens swings in meat distribution margins. The company's competitive edge lies in combining a long-term dairy feed customer with an imported meat distribution channel.

Unlike diversified large food companies, its two businesses make up most of its revenue, so changes in the sector tend to show up in earnings relatively quickly.

Outlook

The most clearly established long-term fact is that the supply contract with Seoul Milk Cooperative runs through August 2029. The contract signed in August 2025 covers four years and 864,000 tons, and the re-signed agreement is worth KRW 473.4 billion.

The second-half beef strength outlook in the third-quarter 2025 commentary pointed in the same direction as the firm market prices seen in the first quarter of 2026.

Meat distribution sales can grow with beef prices and import costs, but operating losses in the second and third quarters of 2026 mean a margin recovery has not yet been demonstrated.

Third-quarter 2026 sales rose to KRW 133.1 billion, yet the operating loss widened to KRW 1.95 billion, which shows that higher sales did not translate into margins. The cause of this gap should be checked in the profit explanation and notes of the 2026 annual business report.

In feed, raw material costs are the key variable, and since lower raw material costs were the main driver of 2025 profit gains, a rebound in ingredient prices could pressure margins. The company's own mid-term sales or profit targets are not included in the disclosures reviewed for this report.

The next checkpoints are therefore the quarterly operating margin trend in the 2026 annual business report and the provisional full-year 2026 results in early 2027.

Bull factors

Growth in meat distribution sales

Third-quarter 2026 sales reached KRW 133.1 billion, up 6.9% year on year. The first-quarter 2026 commentary described firm market prices in meat distribution. Whether the higher sales base translates into better operating margin is the item to watch.

Long-term supply contract with Seoul Milk

The re-signed contract with Seoul Milk covers supply through August 2029, worth KRW 473.4 billion. Because this main customer accounts for more than half of feed segment sales, the volume base is clearly defined. The long contract period gives feed volume relatively high visibility.

Stronger financial structure

The debt ratio fell from 244.3% in 2022 to 27.2% in 2025. Operating cash flow moved from -KRW 19.8 billion in 2022 to KRW 15.7 billion in 2025. Controlling-shareholder equity of KRW 163.9 billion makes up most of total equity of KRW 164.7 billion at the end of 2025.

Bear factors

Quarterly losses in 2026

After an operating loss of KRW 377 million in the second quarter, the third quarter also posted an operating loss of KRW 1.95 billion, two consecutive loss quarters. Net income attributable to controlling shareholders fell 90.7% year on year to KRW 204 million in the third quarter. Weaker quarterly earnings power is a confirmed negative.

Imported beef cost pressure

Meat distribution is directly exposed to imported beef costs. The first-quarter 2026 commentary attributed higher import costs to US beef supply shortages and Australian substitution demand. If price increases cannot fully pass through to selling prices, margins come under pressure.

Volatility from non-operating items

Controlling-shareholder net income of KRW 115.1 billion in 2023 far exceeded operating income of KRW 1.77 billion. In 2025, equity-method gains also helped lift pre-tax profit. Because operating income is small, net income swings widely, which counts as a negative.

Risk factors

Raw material and imported meat prices

Meat distribution is exposed to imported beef prices, exchange rates and substitute demand. The first-quarter 2026 commentary attributed higher import costs to US beef supply shortages and Australian substitution demand. If cost increases cannot be reflected in selling prices, the company-wide operating margin could fall.

Customer concentration

Seoul Milk Cooperative, the main feed customer, accounts for more than half of feed segment sales. The contract runs through August 2029, but high dependence on one customer means changes in terms or volume feed directly into feed results. Any price or volume adjustments during the contract period should be checked in disclosures.

Swings in non-operating items

In 2023 controlling-shareholder net income far exceeded operating income, and in 2025 equity-method gains lifted pre-tax profit. With operating income in the range of a few hundred million to several billion won, non-operating items can change the direction of net income. Separating core operating profit from net income quarter by quarter is necessary.

Overall view

Hanil Feed, which combines meat distribution with dairy and cattle feed, moved from an operating loss in 2022 to profit in 2025 and rebuilt its annual earnings base. Sales of KRW 454.9 billion in 2025 were the largest in the 2022–2025 period, and operating income of KRW 5.86 billion rose for a second consecutive year.

However, operating losses in the second and third quarters of 2026 mean the durability of quarterly profit has not yet been demonstrated.

Positives include the long-term supply contract with Seoul Milk Cooperative through August 2029, a debt ratio that fell to 27.2%, and third-quarter 2026 sales growth to KRW 133.1 billion.

Negatives include two consecutive loss quarters, higher imported beef costs, and net income that swings sharply with non-operating items. On valuation, price-to-book sits below 1 time and the last four quarters' profit total is small, so how the valuation is read depends on whether earnings recover.

The next checkpoints are the early-2027 provisional annual results and the quarterly operating margin trend in the 2026 annual business report.
