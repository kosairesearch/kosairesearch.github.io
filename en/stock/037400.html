02

Business structure

Wooree Enterprise is a light-source specialist founded in 1966. In March 2022 it changed its name from Wooree Lighting to its current name, signaling a push beyond lighting.

The group runs six business areas: LED lighting, health functional foods and bio, FPCB manufacturing, LED PKG modules and SMT, LED BLU and TV assembly, and real estate leasing. Fourteen subsidiaries produce components such as FPCBs, LED packages, SMT LEDs and LED BLUs.

Among these, LED BLU, light bars and PKG components are described as the core business. Larger display sizes and rising demand in China were cited as drivers of first-half 2026 sales. A nanofiber functional fabric business operates through the subsidiary Woori Nanopil.

According to the 2022 annual report, exports accounted for 91.6 percent of consolidated sales, so results are highly sensitive to overseas demand. A company performance comment dated September 3, 2026 said intensifying price competition worsened profitability. The co-CEOs are Yoon Ji-yong and Eom Tae-wook, and the headquarters is in Ansan.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual revenue declined from KRW 1.38 trillion in 2022 to KRW 1.29 trillion in 2023 (down 6.6%), KRW 1.26 trillion in 2024 (down 2.2%) and KRW 1.07 trillion in 2025 (down 15.4%). Revenue fell for three consecutive years from 2023 through 2025, and 2022 was the largest year in the 2022 to 2025 window.

Operating profit dropped from KRW 34.8 billion in 2022 to KRW 26.5 billion in 2023 (down 23.8%), KRW 16.2 billion in 2024 (down 38.7%) and KRW 1.14 billion in 2025 (down 93.0%). The operating margin narrowed from 2.5 percent in 2022 to 0.1 percent in 2025.

For 2025, the company attributed the decline to lower sales across business units amid a global economic slowdown and higher cost of sales, including raw materials (per a February 2026 disclosure report).

Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders fell from KRW 7.1 billion in 2022 to KRW 4.98 billion in 2023 and KRW 2.05 billion in 2024, then swung to a net loss of KRW 3.01 billion in 2025.

Total net income including non-controlling interests was a loss of KRW 10.2 billion in 2025, a larger deficit than the controlling-shareholder figure. The debt-to-equity ratio fell from 119.3 percent in 2022 to 107.6 percent in 2024, then rose to 159.3 percent in 2025.

On a quarterly basis, operating profit of KRW 2.68 billion in the third quarter of 2025 dropped to KRW 7.04 million in the fourth quarter, followed by operating losses of KRW 4.51 billion, KRW 2.71 billion and KRW 5.22 billion in the first through third quarters of 2026.

The third-quarter 2026 operating loss marked a swing to deficit from a year earlier. Operating cash flow declined from KRW 41.4 billion in 2022 to KRW 29.6 billion in 2025.

05

Industry analysis

The domestic LED lighting finished-goods market has been projected to shrink. A October 2023 report by NICE D&B estimated that domestic LED lamp shipment value would decline at an average annual rate of 6.0 percent after 2017, reaching a market of KRW 461.6 billion in 2026.

The same report expected new markets such as automotive, medical, bio and smart lighting to develop LED products that combine with IoT and artificial intelligence. In displays, the report expected continued growth driven by next-generation technologies such as Mini LED and Micro LED.

Sales momentum in the first half of 2026 suggests that demand for BLUs in large panels supports volumes. However, price competition overlaps with that volume growth, so higher volume has not translated into better profitability.

A 2019 quality comparison test by the Korea Consumer Agency found that the company's LED luminaires showed relatively superior luminous efficacy compared with rival products. The business mix is a hybrid, with lighting finished goods and display components moving in different directions.

The third-quarter 2026 operating loss widening suggests that price competition and cost pressure are acting at the same time.

Bull factors

Revenue up for three straight quarters

Revenue rose for three consecutive quarters from the first quarter through the third quarter of 2026. Third-quarter 2026 sales reached KRW 355.2 billion, up 10.4 percent from the prior quarter and 31.5 percent from a year earlier. If the sales trend holds, it provides a base for room to improve operating results. The next item to confirm is whether the sales recovery outpaces cost pressure.

Loss narrowed temporarily

The operating loss in the second quarter of 2026 was KRW 2.71 billion, narrower than KRW 4.51 billion in the first quarter. After operating profit fell to KRW 7.04 million in the fourth quarter of 2025, the loss narrowed for one quarter.

Because the operating margin was 0.1 percent in 2025, room for improvement in operating metrics remains wide. However, the third-quarter loss widened again to KRW 5.22 billion, so a turnaround in trend is not yet visible.

Display-component demand and diversification

Through 14 subsidiaries, the company has widened its scope to display components such as LED BLUs and PKGs, along with FPCBs, health functional foods and nanofiber fabrics. A company performance comment dated September 3, 2026 cited larger display sizes and rising demand from China as drivers of sales growth.

A broad portfolio can act as a buffer against weaker demand for any single product. However, segment-level profit contribution is visible only within consolidated operating results.

Cumulative net loss attributable to controlling shareholders over the last four quarters totals KRW 5.62 billion. Sales growth has not yet been enough to offset cost and selling expense pressure.

Higher debt ratio and net losses

With the debt burden larger relative to total equity, continued losses could narrow financial flexibility.

Price competition and export dependence

According to the 2022 annual report, exports accounted for 91.6 percent of consolidated sales, a high share that makes results sensitive to overseas demand, exchange rates and raw-material prices. A company performance comment dated September 3, 2026 said intensifying price competition worsened profitability. If unit price declines keep eroding profit even as sales grow, the room for losses to narrow may stay limited.

Risk factors

Export and raw-material exposure

The company attributed the 2025 decline to lower sales across business units amid a global economic slowdown and higher raw-material and other cost of sales (per a February 2026 disclosure report).

With a high export share, changes in overseas customers' volumes and pricing can affect both sales and margins at the same time. If raw-material prices rise again, cost pressure similar to 2025 could recur.

Balance-sheet burden

Total liabilities of KRW 527.3 billion at the end of 2025 are large relative to controlling-shareholder equity of KRW 135.9 billion. Operating cash flow was KRW 29.6 billion in 2025, down from KRW 41.4 billion in 2022. If losses persist, borrowing costs and dividend capacity will need closer monitoring.

Share-price volatility

Shares rallied sharply from their 2026 lows, and trading value surged during that stretch. The removal from the administrative issue list on November 13, 2026 eliminated the low-price issue, but the removal does not by itself guarantee an earnings recovery. Sharp swings in trading participation could amplify share-price moves.

Overall view

Since 2022, Wooree Enterprise's sales and profits have declined together, with operating profit falling to KRW 1.14 billion in 2025, and operating losses have continued into 2026.

Third-quarter 2026 revenue reached KRW 355.2 billion, up 31.5 percent from a year earlier, yet the operating loss widened to KRW 5.22 billion. The most important checkpoint now is whether sales growth can carry through price competition and cost pressure into profit.

On the financial side, the debt-to-equity ratio rose to 159.3 percent at the end of 2025, a heavier burden than in 2024. The operating loss narrowed in the second quarter and widened again in the third, so the direction remains mixed.

The removal from the administrative issue list effective November 13, 2026 resolved the low-price issue but does not signal an earnings recovery. Display-component demand and subsidiary diversification stand on the bullish side, while price competition and continued losses weigh on the bearish side.

The next checkpoint is fourth-quarter and full-year 2026 operating results, to be reported in the 2026 annual report.
