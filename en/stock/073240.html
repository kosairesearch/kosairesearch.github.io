Kumho Tire: Defending Profitability Amid Tariffs and Input Costs

Kumho Tire moved from an attributable net loss of KRW 79 billion in 2022 to attributable net profit of KRW 347.4 billion in 2025, but attributable net profit fell to KRW 6.1 billion in the third quarter of 2026 as EU tariffs and raw material costs weighed on earnings.

02

Business structure

Kumho Tire makes tires for automakers, known as original equipment (OE) tires, and sells replacement (RE) tires to consumers. The company attributed third-quarter 2026 sales growth to expanded OE supply in North America and Europe and steady sales of higher-margin RE tires.

Year-on-year growth in the same quarter was 1.7% in North America, 17.1% in Europe and 12.6% in China, according to Business Post.

High-value products include 18-inch and larger tires and electric vehicle (EV) tires, with the cumulative 2026 share of 18-inch and larger tires at 47.0% and EV tires at 24.6% of global OE sales, according to company announcements. For the third quarter alone, the reported shares were 48.8% and 27.7%.

In North America the company runs a second brand, Marshal, to meet competition, according to Yuanta Securities.

Production rests on the Gwangju No.1 plant with capacity of 3 million tires, offtake volumes from a Chinese outsourcing partner of about 2 million tires a year, and partial expansions at seven other global plants.

The company plans to complete the Hampyeong and European plants to build a production network linking Korea, Europe and North America. Among listed peers, Hankook Tire & Technology competes for the same OE contracts, and price, product mix and local production share drive profitability.

04

Earnings analysis

Based on DART annual reports, sales were KRW 3.56 trillion in 2022, KRW 4.04 trillion in 2023 (up 13.5%), KRW 4.53 trillion in 2024 (up 12.1%) and KRW 4.7 trillion in 2025 (up 3.7%). Sales rose for three consecutive years from 2023 to 2025, and 2025 was the largest year of 2022 to 2025.

Growth slowed each year after 2023. Operating profit rose from KRW 23.1 billion in 2022 to KRW 411 billion in 2023 (up 1,676.5%) and KRW 588.6 billion in 2024 (up 43.2%), before easing 2.2% to KRW 575.9 billion in 2025. The operating margin moved from 0.7% in 2022 to 10.2% in 2023, 13.0% in 2024 and 12.2% in 2025.

Attributable net profit turned from a loss of KRW 79 billion in 2022 to profit of KRW 157.8 billion in 2023, then rose 105.9% to KRW 324.9 billion in 2024 and 6.9% to KRW 347.4 billion in 2025. Net profit including non-controlling interests was a loss of KRW 77.5 billion in 2022 and KRW 376.8 billion in 2025.

Operating cash flow improved from negative KRW 180 billion in 2022 to KRW 915.3 billion in 2025.

By quarter, operating profit was KRW 108.5 billion in the third quarter of 2025, KRW 145.6 billion in the fourth quarter (up 34.2% from the prior quarter), KRW 147 billion in the first quarter of 2026, KRW 182.2 billion in the second quarter (up 23.9%) and KRW 130.5 billion in the third quarter (down 28.4%).

Yuanta Securities put the third-quarter operating margin at 10.5%. Attributable net profit in the third quarter was KRW 6.1 billion, down 95.3% from the prior quarter. Yuanta Securities linked this decline to tariff costs, while Hana Securities expected raw material pressure to ease after peaking in the third quarter.

Attributable net profit over the last four quarters, from the fourth quarter of 2025 to the third quarter of 2026, totaled KRW 399.8 billion.

05

Industry analysis

Tire makers in 2026 face two cost pressures at once: higher prices for some raw materials and tariffs. Industry assessments say EV demand has returned to growth, and tariffs on Chinese tires in Europe and other major markets have lifted sales for producers with local manufacturing, according to CNB News.

About 50% of Kumho Tire's European sales volume comes from China, so anti-dumping duties weigh on it directly, according to Yuanta Securities. The company is responding by reshuffling its supply chain and expanding local production in Europe.

In its July 2026 report, Hana Securities expected raw material pressure to ease after peaking in the third quarter and price increases to take effect from the fourth quarter.

In its June 2026 report, BNK Investment Securities listed continued raw material price increases and slower new-car sales in the United States and China as risks. Kumho Tire competes with Hankook Tire & Technology and other domestic and overseas makers for demand in high-inch and EV tires.

Yuanta Securities said defending North American share depends on the launch of the Marshal second brand. Where the industry sits in its cycle is hard to pin down, so raw material price trends and the ability to pass costs on to customers are the next points to watch.

06

Outlook

The company set a 2026 sales target of KRW 5.1 trillion, along with targets of 47% for high-inch tire sales and 30% for EV tire supply. The high-inch share reached its target early within three quarters, and the EV share of 24.6% is close to its 30% goal.

Relocating the Hampyeong plant involves KRW 660 billion of investment for a capacity of 5.3 million tires a year from early 2028. In Europe, a KRW 860 billion investment is planned for a 6-million-tire plant, with construction starting in the second half of 2027 and production in the second half of 2028.

Volume from the Gwangju No.1 plant and the Chinese partner was reduced somewhat by delays, but the plan is to expand it to 3 million tires a year. Yuanta Securities said the company is pursuing partial expansions and larger offtake volumes to lift 2027 sales volume growth to the high single digits.

Hana Securities newly set a target price of KRW 8,300 in a report dated July 31, 2026, and Daishin Securities raised its target to KRW 8,300 in a July 2026 report.

Yuanta Securities set a target of KRW 7,500 in a report dated October 30, 2026, and said the cut reflected lower earnings estimates after tariff costs were added. The next checkpoints are whether construction of the European plant starts on schedule and when the Hampyeong plant begins operations.

08

Bull factors

High-inch and EV mix supports margins

Cumulative high-inch sales reached 47.0% through the third quarter of 2026, meeting the full-year target early, and EV tires made up 24.6% of global OE sales, according to company announcements. The operating margin was 10.2% in 2023, 13.0% in 2024 and 12.2% in 2025.

Hana Securities and BNK Investment Securities cited the high-inch and EV mix shift as grounds for defending earnings.

Expanding OE supply in North America and Europe

Third-quarter 2026 sales rose 17.1% in Europe, 1.7% in North America and 12.6% in China versus a year earlier, according to Business Post. Hana Securities said expanded OE supply to global automakers in North America and Europe, along with new customer wins, drove results. Room for further market share gains is the core of the bullish case.

Balance sheet repair and possible asset sale

The debt-to-equity ratio fell from 277.2% in 2022 to 147.4% in 2025, and operating cash flow was KRW 915.3 billion in 2025. Daishin Securities said a sale of the Gwangju plant site would free cash for new plant investment and reduce interest costs through debt repayment. The possibility of resuming dividends after the deficit is cleared is also being discussed.

09

Bear factors

Tariff costs pulled down third-quarter earnings

Yuanta Securities said EU anti-dumping duties on Chinese tires became final and added about KRW 10 billion a month in costs from July. Around 50% of Kumho Tire's European sales volume is Chinese-made. Third-quarter 2026 attributable net profit was KRW 6.1 billion, down 92.6% year on year. Operating profit was close to consensus but 9.7% below Yuanta's own estimate.

Raw materials and Middle East tensions weigh

Yuanta Securities said raw material pressure remains as prolonged instability in the Middle East persists. Daishin Securities expected the impact of higher rubber and oil prices to be partly reflected after June 2026.

Price increases and seasonal tire demand may offset part of it, but the pace of cost pass-through still needs confirmation.

Production disruption and slower growth

Hana Securities said in its July 2026 report that fire damage continues to constrain production capacity. Sales growth slowed to 3.7% in 2025 from 12.1% in 2024, and operating profit fell 2.2%. Third-quarter 2026 operating profit dropped 28.4% from the prior quarter.

10

Risk factors

Trade and tariff policy

EU anti-dumping duties on Chinese tires directly affect Kumho Tire's European sales structure. Yuanta Securities lowered its earnings estimates to reflect tariff costs. The company also cited U.S. tariff policy as a source of higher costs. Whether tariff costs can be passed on through prices is the key variable.

Raw material and geopolitical risk

BNK Investment Securities cited the possibility of continued raw material price increases due to geopolitical uncertainty as a risk. The effects of Middle East conflict on rubber and oil prices also need to be tracked.

Whether raw material pressure eases after peaking in the third quarter, as Hana Securities expects, is the point to confirm.

Execution risk in production and investment

Fire-related capacity constraints continue, and volume from the Chinese partner has been reduced by delays, according to Yuanta Securities. The Hampyeong and European plants involve investments of KRW 660 billion and KRW 860 billion, with operations planned for 2028.

A Gwangju plant sale could change timing with government projects, so volatility is unavoidable, according to Daishin Securities.

12

Overall view

Kumho Tire's DART-reported results show a recovery from an attributable net loss of KRW 79 billion in 2022 to attributable net profit of KRW 347.4 billion in 2025. The operating margin has stayed in the 10% range since 2023, and the debt-to-equity ratio fell from 277.2% in 2022 to 147.4% in 2025.

Third-quarter 2026 sales rose to KRW 1.24 trillion, but operating profit fell to KRW 130.5 billion and attributable net profit to KRW 6.1 billion. Brokerages pointed to EU tariff costs and raw material pressure as reasons for the drop.

Bullish factors include the high-inch and EV mix, expanding OE supply in North America and Europe, and planned plant investments in Hampyeong and Europe. Bearish factors include anti-dumping duty costs, raw material and Middle East risks, and production constraints after the fire.

The weight of these factors will be judged by how quickly the operating margin and attributable net profit recover in coming quarters. The next checkpoints are preliminary fourth-quarter 2026 results and the 2026 annual report due by late March 2027.
