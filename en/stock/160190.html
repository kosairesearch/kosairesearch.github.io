Motor Maker Shifting Toward Robot Actuators Amid Losses

General-purpose motor sales have fallen for two straight years and operating losses have run for five consecutive quarters, so whether robot actuator investment pays off is the key open question for Higen RNM.

02

Business structure

Higen RNM makes general-purpose electric motors, servo motors and drives, and robot actuators that transmit power and control motion. Based on Q2 2026 revenue, the segment mix was general-purpose motors 76%, servo motors and drives 12%, other 8%, EV motors and inverters 3%, and merchandise 1%.

General-purpose motors are mainly used in industrial equipment such as pumps, fans and compressors, and the core end market is construction, where they are applied in crane motors, according to Korea Investment & Securities.

An SK Securities report published in 2025 assessed the company's share of the domestic general-purpose motor market at about 15%. Production sites are in Changwon, Korea and Qingdao, China, where the subsidiary Zhongsheng Electric supports raw material supply.

Raw material costs account for 54.7% of total costs, so the gap between product prices and raw material prices drives profit. Exports were about 10% of revenue in 2024, mostly general-purpose motors shipped to China.

In robotics, the company is reported to be running several projects with large robot makers, and it is developing next-generation QDD-based actuators for AMRs and commercial drones.

04

Earnings analysis

On a DART-filed basis, 2023 revenue was KRW 77.2 billion, operating profit was KRW 4.34 billion, and net income attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 2.63 billion. In 2024 revenue fell to KRW 75.9 billion, down 1.8% year on year, and the company swung to an operating loss of KRW 369 million.

Revenue fell further to KRW 73.5 billion in 2025, down 3.1%, and the operating loss widened to KRW 8.91 billion. The 2025 net loss attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 7.06 billion, and the net loss including non-controlling interests was KRW 7.1 billion.

The operating margin moved from 5.6% in 2023 to -0.5% in 2024 and -12.1% in 2025. In its results disclosure dated February 12, 2026, the company cited a domestic economic slowdown and weaker demand from downstream industries as the main cause of lower sales.

It added higher cost of sales from rising copper and other raw material prices, and higher personnel and R&D costs tied to robotics and mobility development. By quarter, Q4 2025 revenue rose 43.4% from the prior quarter to KRW 22.8 billion, yet the operating loss was KRW 3.05 billion.

Q1 2026 revenue was KRW 18.6 billion with an operating loss of KRW 3.39 billion, while Q2 2026 revenue was KRW 20.8 billion with a narrower operating loss of KRW 1.46 billion. Q3 2026 revenue was KRW 16.9 billion with an operating loss of KRW 3.06 billion, and the loss widened again. Lower sales combined with cost and R&D burdens have pushed the operating margin down.

05

Industry analysis

The end markets for general-purpose motors are industrial equipment using pumps, fans and compressors, and construction. Korea Investment & Securities judged that a difficult domestic construction market kept general-purpose motor sales weak.

On policy, the expected phased tightening of Korea's minimum motor efficiency standard from IE3 to IE4 was cited in a 2025 brokerage report as a driver of replacement demand. Rising copper and other raw material prices push up cost of sales, and if product prices do not keep pace, the spread narrows.

The company's share of the domestic general-purpose motor market is put at about 15%, and an SK Securities report argued that possible Chinese rare earth export controls could partly benefit domestic motor makers.

On competition, one brokerage noted the company's brand recognition trails global motor makers such as Japanese component majors, but described its cost competitiveness and customer responsiveness as strengths.

In robot actuators, the market is at a stage of supply discussions with large robot makers, alongside expectations for humanoid robot adoption.

06

Outlook

The outlook below draws on brokerage estimates and company explanations. In July 2025, Daishin Securities projected 2026 revenue of KRW 81.8 billion and an operating profit of KRW 600 million, a return to profit.

That projection assumed replacement demand from the IE4 standard and price increases would lift both volume and prices in general-purpose motors. Reported operating results, however, show an operating loss in every quarter from Q1 to Q3 2026, so the actual trend has diverged from that estimate.

In a 2025 report, Korea Investment & Securities expected the Changwon plant expansion to slip to the first half of 2026 to match the timing of robot demand. The same report said recent funding from a block sale of treasury shares means development schedules should stay on track.

In robotics, the company is developing next-generation QDD-based actuators, and a plan for a dedicated actuator plant worth KRW 30 billion this year was reported. Once completed, the plant is expected to be used to offer supply to major domestic robot makers, but this remains at the plan and proposal stage.

Korea Investment & Securities had projected that export sales would rise meaningfully from Q4 2025, and Q4 2025 revenue did rise from the prior quarter. Q1 2026 revenue, however, fell to KRW 18.6 billion.

The next checkpoint is whether the 2026 annual report shows a recovery in general-purpose motor sales and the share of actuator-related revenue.

Bull factors

Possible replacement demand for general-purpose motors

A 2025 Daishin Securities report argued that phased tightening of the minimum motor efficiency standard from IE3 to IE4 could increase replacement demand for general-purpose motors. General-purpose motors made up 76% of Q2 2026 revenue, so changes in demand feed directly into results. The same report also treated price increases as a factor in the expected earnings improvement.

Expansion of the robot actuator business

A 2025 Daishin Securities report described plans to invest about KRW 12 billion in a smart actuator automation line on the second floor of the Changwon headquarters plant. Reports also point to a dedicated actuator plant and supply proposals to domestic robot makers.

These remain at the plan and proposal stage, and their revenue contribution is not yet separable in the reported results.

Easing financial burden

The debt ratio fell from 133.6% in 2023 to 55.7% in 2025. Total equity at the end of 2025 was KRW 106.9 billion, of which KRW 106.2 billion belonged to controlling shareholders. Total liabilities of KRW 59.6 billion give the company more room to keep funding R&D and capital expenditure.

Bear factors

Weak construction and domestic demand

Korea Investment & Securities explained that construction is the core end market for general-purpose motors and that weak construction activity led to lower sales. Revenue was KRW 73.5 billion in 2025, below KRW 77.2 billion in 2023. Q3 2026 revenue of KRW 16.9 billion was also down 18.9% from the prior quarter.

Cost pressure and narrowing spreads

Raw materials account for 54.7% of total costs, so a rise in raw material prices feeds directly into cost of sales. The company itself cited higher copper and other raw material prices as a driver of higher cost of sales in 2025. If product prices lag raw material prices, profit shrinks further.

Persistent losses and cash outflow

Operating losses have run for five consecutive quarters from Q3 2025 through Q3 2026, and the Q3 2026 operating loss of KRW 3.06 billion was larger than in the prior quarter. Operating cash flow was -KRW 2.63 billion in 2024 and -KRW 9.69 billion in 2025. While robotics R&D spending continues, the timing of an earnings recovery could be pushed back.

Risk factors

Expansion and commercialization timing

A 2025 Korea Investment & Securities report expected the Changwon expansion to slip to the first half of 2026. When and how much actuator revenue will materialize is not yet separable in reported results. If development and mass production are delayed, investment costs could rise ahead of sales.

Shareholding structure and trading volatility

Daishin Securities data from July 2025 put major shareholders, led by Danocorp and eight others, at 74.53%. A high controlling stake can limit tradable shares and amplify price volatility. Reports also mention a block sale of treasury shares to raise funds, so changes in share supply are worth tracking.

Valuation and earnings uncertainty

Because the last four quarters show net losses, the price-to-earnings ratio cannot be calculated. One brokerage noted that the expected 2024 price-to-sales ratio was well above the KOSDAQ average. Until earnings recover, interpretation of the share price relies mainly on asset and sales measures.

12

Overall view

Higen RNM started in general-purpose motor manufacturing and is widening its business toward robot actuators, but reported results have not yet shown the payoff from that shift.

Revenue fell from KRW 77.2 billion in 2023 to KRW 73.5 billion in 2025, and the operating loss widened to KRW 8.91 billion in 2025 after turning to a loss in 2024.

Operating losses have continued for five consecutive quarters from Q3 2025 through Q3 2026, and the controlling-shareholder net loss over the last four quarters totals KRW 8.19 billion.

Bullish factors include possible replacement demand from the IE4 standard, the Changwon expansion and robot actuator development, and more financial room as the debt ratio fell from 133.6% to 55.7%.

Bearish factors include lower sales from a weak construction market and a slowing domestic economy, raw material cost pressure from copper, and wider operating losses from higher R&D spending. Operating cash flow was negative in both 2024 and 2025.

With the price-to-earnings ratio not calculable, whether robotics revenue materializes will be the main reference for interpreting the shares. The next checkpoint is the operating margin and revenue mix in the 2026 annual report.
