02

Business structure

Lotte Energy Materials is a battery materials affiliate of the Lotte Group whose core products are battery copper foil and circuit copper foil. Battery foil, used in EV and ESS cells, is produced mainly at the company's Malaysian plants, where the company says Plants 1 to 6 are being brought into full production.

The company is also expanding capacity for 6-micron high-end foil aimed at ESS batteries. Circuit foil, used in AI data centers and networking equipment, is made at the Iksan plant, which the company describes as the only circuit foil production base in Korea.

HVLP (ultra-low profile) products account for about 20% of circuit foil sales, and the company expects that share to exceed 50% after the second expansion stage is completed in 2027.

Iksan capacity rose to 6,700 tons a year after the first stage was completed in May 2026 and will reach 16,000 tons a year when the second stage is done. The company has also entered the 1 to 3 micrometer ultra-thin foil segment for AI optical transceivers and semiconductor packaging.

Based on company remarks and media reports, named customer groups include North American ESS operators, North American automakers, and users of 4680 cylindrical cells. Media reports describe the ultra-thin segment as previously dominated by a Japanese incumbent, which the company has now entered. The company says it will pursue ultra-thin expansion only after binding contracts with customers are signed.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual results moved in divergent directions for sales and profitability. Revenue rose from KRW 729.4 billion in 2022 to KRW 902.3 billion in 2024, making 2024 the largest year of 2022 to 2025, before falling 24.9% to KRW 677.5 billion in 2025, the smallest year in that span.

Operating profit fell 86.1% from KRW 84.8 billion in 2022 to KRW 11.8 billion in 2023, and the company swung to an operating loss of KRW 64.4 billion in 2024. The 2025 operating loss widened to KRW 145.2 billion, the second straight year of losses.

Operating margin moved from 11.6% in 2022 to 1.5% in 2023, -7.1% in 2024 and -21.4% in 2025. Net income attributable to controlling shareholders turned positive at KRW 6.3 billion in 2024 but swung to a loss of KRW 157.5 billion in 2025.

Net income including non-controlling interests, a different measure, was a loss of KRW 167.2 billion in 2025. At the end of 2025, total equity was KRW 1.79 trillion and total liabilities were KRW 415.2 billion, with the debt ratio rising from 20.0% in 2024 to 23.2%.

Operating cash flow went from KRW 87 billion in 2024 to -KRW 11.1 billion in 2025. Quarterly, revenue rose from KRW 143.7 billion in the third quarter of 2025 to a low of KRW 159.8 billion in the first quarter of 2026, then to KRW 194.2 billion and KRW 289.7 billion, two consecutive quarters of growth.

Operating losses narrowed from KRW 33.8 billion in the fourth quarter of 2025 to KRW 4.98 billion in the first quarter of 2026, then widened to KRW 16.9 billion and KRW 21.3 billion.

The third-quarter swing to net profit was attributed by the company to a gain from a subsidiary sale, while media cited higher fixed costs from the early operation of Malaysia's fifth plant.

The last four quarters of net income attributable to controlling shareholders total KRW 13.6 billion, a sign of large swings from quarter to quarter.

05

Industry analysis

On the battery foil side, the company's main case rests on growth in North American ESS demand and the expansion of U.S. local production by global automakers. The company says North American customers are accelerating non-Chinese supply chains under policies such as the U.S.

Inflation Reduction Act, which has lifted its North American volumes. On supply, management judges that major copper foil producers have kept their investment conservative, and CEO Kim Yeon-seop has said both battery and circuit foil are entering a phase of structural shortage.

Circuit foil sits at the intersection of expanding AI data-center investment and rising demand for HVLP foil used in high-speed signal transmission, and the company forecasts high-end circuit foil demand growing more than 30% a year on average.

A 2024 media report, however, described a prolonged EV slowdown and an oversupply of foil from Chinese producers that weighed on industry earnings. In 2026 the company said some customers have already accepted processing-fee increases, making price negotiations a key test of the industry turn.

Media reports describe the ultra-thin foil segment as previously monopolized by a Japanese company, which the company has now entered. The company's supercycle view and the 2024 oversupply reports reflect different points in time, so interpretations of the same industry remain divided.

06

Outlook

The most specific target the company has set is a return to profitability in the second quarter of 2027. The company has designated 2027 as the first year of a shift to a profit structure centered on high-end products, and it expects circuit foil sales in 2027 to more than double the 2026 level.

Circuit foil capacity at Iksan will reach 16,000 tons a year once the second expansion stage is finished in 2027, and the company says it is also considering further expansion. Any additional expansion will proceed only once binding customer contracts are signed and the economics are secured.

On battery foil, the company says the fifth Malaysian plant has been running since July 2026, and the sixth plant is scheduled to start in the fourth quarter of 2026.

Management has flagged temporary cost increases during the ramp-up period, which will weigh on second-half profitability, and expects foil shortages to deepen after 2027, supporting price increases.

Processing-fee increases have already taken effect for some customers, and the company expects talks with the remaining customers to conclude within the fourth quarter of 2026.

In new businesses, the company shared development progress on lithium iron phosphate cathode material and solid electrolyte, with LFP cathode material already approved by some customers and further approvals expected in the second half.

The next checkpoints are the sixth Malaysian plant's start-up and the outcome of fee talks in the fourth quarter of 2026, followed by whether the company reaches profitability in the second quarter of 2027.

08

Bull factors

Sales growth accelerates

Third-quarter 2026 revenue reached KRW 289.7 billion, up 101.6% year on year and 49.2% from the prior quarter. Sales have risen for two straight quarters since the first-quarter 2026 low. The company credits expanding North American ESS supply and the start of shipments to North American automakers.

Utilization recovery and inventory drawdown

The company said plant utilization rose to 77% in the second quarter of 2026, compared with the high-40% range for all of 2025. Accumulated inventory has been drawn down, with output moving straight to customer plants. Management expects utilization to keep rising in the second half.

High-end circuit foil and price talks

The company says sales of HVLP circuit foil for AI data centers are growing and expects 2027 circuit foil sales to more than double the 2026 level. Processing-fee increases have already taken effect for some customers, with talks continuing with the rest.

If price normalization translates into better margins, the operating loss could narrow further, which is the main bullish factor.

Bear factors

Losses persist and widen quarter on quarter

Operating losses have run for five straight quarters from the third quarter of 2025 through the third quarter of 2026, and the third-quarter loss of KRW 21.3 billion widened from KRW 16.9 billion in the second quarter. The 2025 operating loss was KRW 145.2 billion, with an operating margin of -21.4%. Media reports have described nine consecutive quarters of operating losses since the third quarter of 2024.

Ramp-up costs and second-half burden

The company said ramp-up costs for stabilizing production at the sixth Malaysian plant will temporarily rise in the second half. It also said the raw-material lag effect and inventory drawdown that helped the first half will fade. Media cited higher fixed costs from the start of the fifth Malaysian plant as a drag on profitability.

Demand swings and policy dependence

North American sales depend on U.S. restrictions on China and demand for non-Chinese supply chains, so a shift in policy could affect volumes. A 2024 report described a prolonged EV slowdown and oversupply from Chinese producers weighing on industry earnings. Revenue in 2025 fell 24.9% to KRW 677.5 billion.

Risk factors

Price-increase realization

Processing-fee increases have taken effect for some customers, but outcomes with the remaining customers are not yet settled. If talks are delayed or fall short of expectations, the pace of margin improvement after the second half could slow.

The company has said stable supply to customers is its first priority, which leaves room to adjust the pace of increases.

Policy and trade

U.S. policy under the Inflation Reduction Act and restrictions on China support demand for non-Chinese supply, but changes in policy conditions could alter customer ordering patterns. A 2024 report also noted regulatory moves against Chinese products in Europe.

With a large share of production in Malaysia, the company is exposed to regional policy and local operating conditions.

Cash flow and expansion spending

Operating cash flow was -KRW 89.8 billion in 2023 and -KRW 11.1 billion in 2025, both loss-making years. The company says Iksan expansion depends on binding customer contracts, but further expansion is under review, which could enlarge investment needs.

The debt ratio was 23.2% at the end of 2025, and until earnings turn positive, how expansion is funded will be the key issue.

Overall view

In the third quarter of 2026, Lotte Energy Materials' sales reached a level the company calls a quarterly record and net income attributable to controlling shareholders turned positive, yet the operating loss of KRW 21.3 billion persisted.

On an annual basis, the company posted losses in both 2024 and 2025, with a 2025 operating margin of -21.4%. The sales recovery is supported by demand for battery foil from North American ESS and automakers and for circuit foil for AI, and utilization reached 77% in the second quarter of 2026.

Against that, processing-fee increases and ramp-up costs at the sixth Malaysian plant, both key to the turn to profit, have not yet shown results. The third-quarter profit was attributed by the company to a subsidiary sale gain, which is distinct from operating profitability.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders over the last four quarters totals KRW 13.6 billion, showing large quarterly swings. The next checkpoints are whether the targeted return to profitability in the second quarter of 2027 and the doubling of circuit foil sales in 2027 are confirmed in reported numbers.
