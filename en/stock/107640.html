02

Business structure

HanJungNCS grew as an auto parts maker after its founding in 1995, then changed its industry classification to energy storage system manufacturing in 2021 and moved toward ESS cooling systems.

The shift is clear in the revenue mix: auto parts sales fell from KRW 49.3 billion in 2024 to KRW 29.3 billion in 2025 and to KRW 2.1 billion in the first half of 2026. ESS made up KRW 94.3 billion, or 96.9%, of KRW 97.3 billion in first-half 2026 consolidated revenue.

Core products are water-cooled modules, chillers and HVAC (heating, ventilation and air conditioning) systems. Samsung SDI is the main customer, to which the company supplies water-cooling systems for its ESS products.

Samsung Securities views the company as holding a technology moat after being the first to commercialize water-cooled modules. Production sites include domestic plants, Chinese lines converted to ESS, and Hanjung America Co. (Indiana), established in April 2025.

The largest shareholder group holds 35.47% as of late April 2026. In competition, the company is cited as a rare auto parts maker that has pivoted decisively to ESS cooling, and the industry-wide move toward water cooling is seen as a differentiator.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual revenue rose from KRW 88.5 billion in 2022 to KRW 121.6 billion in 2023 (up 37.3% YoY) and KRW 177.3 billion in 2024 (up 45.8% YoY), then eased to KRW 175.3 billion in 2025 (down 1.1% YoY). Among 2022 to 2025, 2024 was the largest year and 2022 the smallest.

Operating losses of KRW 13.9 billion in 2022 and KRW 12.7 billion in 2023 gave way to operating profit of KRW 9.57 billion in 2024, a turn to profit. Operating profit fell to KRW 4.03 billion in 2025, down 57.9% YoY.

The company's filing attributed the 2025 revenue change to roughly KRW 35.5 billion of added ESS sales offset by about KRW 37.4 billion lost from winding down the combustion-engine business.

The same filing cited higher development costs for new models and added staff from functional reorganization as drivers of the lower operating profit.

Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders fell from KRW 17.3 billion in 2024 to KRW 4.08 billion in 2025 (down 76.4% YoY), while net profit including non-controlling interests was KRW 4.89 billion in 2025.

The company said 2025 also reflected a convertible bond valuation loss from the share price rise, which it described as an accounting loss without cash outflow. The operating margin moved from -15.7% in 2022 to 5.4% in 2024 and 2.3% in 2025.

Quarterly, operating profit of KRW 1.53 billion in 3Q 2025 and KRW 2.73 billion in 4Q 2025 (up 78.3% QoQ) was followed by KRW 1.44 billion in 1Q 2026 (down 47.1% QoQ) and an operating loss of KRW 6.95 billion in 2Q 2026.

Revenue in 3Q 2026 was KRW 47.1 billion, up 2.8% YoY, but the operating loss of KRW 6.08 billion marked a swing to loss from a year earlier. Samsung Securities noted that in 1Q 2026 margins were soft due to development costs and higher selling and administrative expenses despite strong revenue growth.

The debt ratio went from 808.5% in 2023 to 107.4% in 2024 and rose to 152.9% in 2025, when total liabilities were KRW 153.1 billion and total equity KRW 100.2 billion, of which KRW 98.1 billion belonged to controlling shareholders. Operating cash flow fell from KRW 8.94 billion in 2024 to KRW 528 million in 2025.

05

Industry analysis

Demand for ESS cooling is being shaped by the expansion of the US market. Growing US demand for Korean-made ESS has led to expectations that the company will benefit. AI data center growth is also raising demand for cooling in uninterruptible power supply and backup power systems.

The CEO said demand for data center UPS and BPS systems is rising quickly. Samsung Securities sees cooling shifting from air-cooled to more efficient water-cooled designs. Samsung SDI's US ESS capacity expansion, due for completion in 2H 2026, was flagged as a near-term driver.

Industry commentary notes that ESS volumes tend to concentrate in the second half of the year. The company is viewed as having built a technology barrier through its commercial record in water-cooled modules.

On the other side, heavy reliance on Samsung SDI means that customer's production plans and yields largely set the company's volumes. Securities analysts expect a 2026 operating loss tied to the restructuring and US plant costs, with a rebound in later earnings anticipated.

06

Outlook

The nearest growth milestone is the Indiana plant. In a September 2026 interview, the CEO said the plant was about 30% complete and targeted for completion in early April 2027. After two to three months of equipment setup, full-scale production is planned from the second half of 2027.

The CEO said customer utilization is rising and production plans run from late 2026 into early 2027, with revenue expected to expand meaningfully from 2027.

As of late June 2026, Hanjung America had about KRW 88.0 billion in assets and KRW 36.4 billion in equity, with no revenue yet and a half-year net loss of about KRW 2.5 billion.

The company decided to issue KRW 80.0 billion in hybrid bonds, with KRW 60.0 billion going to the Indiana unit and KRW 20.0 billion to working capital.

Samsung Securities' June 2026 report tabulated a contract of about KRW 1.5 trillion with a US customer signed in March 2026, covering 2026 to 2029 with production at the US plant, and a domestic ESS central contract bid of about KRW 360 billion in February 2026.

The same table lists the SBB 2.0 product launch for December 2026. Estimates compiled by Epic AI and reported in October 2026 put 2026 operating loss at KRW 2.5 billion and 2027 operating profit at KRW 18.0 billion. Those estimates are third-party figures and have not been confirmed by the company.

Because operating losses continued through 3Q 2026, whether the forecast materializes will need to be checked against results from 4Q 2026 onward.

08

Bull factors

Business pivot largely complete

ESS rose to 96.9% of first-half 2026 revenue, and auto parts sales shrank to about KRW 2.1 billion. With the portfolio narrowed to one core business, the drivers of earnings are simpler. The ESS share rose from 60.5% in 2024 to 81.6% in 2025, which supports this direction.

US production base and large contracts

Samsung Securities tabulated a contract of about KRW 1.5 trillion with a US customer signed in March 2026, with details kept confidential. Once the Indiana plant starts operating in 2027, local production should expand the company's capacity to serve North American customers. Samsung Securities also noted a plan to start a dedicated North American plant for a customer in mid-2027.

Cost structure reset and expected margin mix gains

Samsung Securities expected that with the combustion-engine parts business halted in the first half of 2026, focusing on ESS in the second half could restore profitability. The Invest estimated adjusted operating profit excluding one-off and investment-related costs at about KRW 7.8 billion for 4Q 2025. That is a press estimate rather than a company figure and should be read as a reference only.

09

Bear factors

Losses continuing through 2026

After an operating loss of KRW 6.95 billion in 2Q 2026, 3Q 2026 still posted an operating loss of KRW 6.08 billion. The net loss attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 5.89 billion in 3Q 2026, a swing to loss from a year earlier. Revenue growth has not yet translated into profit, as the last four quarters' net loss totals KRW 3.07 billion.

Financial burden and funding costs

Total liabilities were KRW 153.1 billion at the end of 2025, with a debt ratio of 152.9%. The company provided a guarantee on about USD 60 million in facility borrowing to build the Indiana plant. A KRW 80.0 billion hybrid bond issuance adds to the funding burden. The US unit recorded a half-year net loss of about KRW 2.5 billion with no revenue yet.

Customer concentration and the gap before plant start-up

Samsung SDI is the main customer for the ESS business. Customer production plans, yield in water-cooled units and unit costs determine profitability.

Until the US plant starts operating in 2027, the company relies on domestic production and existing customer volumes for a long stretch, which can produce revenue gaps and earnings swings.

10

Risk factors

Customer concentration risk

ESS sales depend heavily on volume growth at Samsung SDI. Analysts attributed the rise in 4Q 2025 ESS revenue to higher volumes for Samsung SDI. Changes in the customer's production plans could move the company's revenue and utilization together.

With volumes concentrated in one customer, the pace of that customer's capital spending directly drives results.

Plant construction and ramp-up schedule risk

As of the September 2026 interview, the Indiana plant was about 30% complete. The completion target is early April 2027, followed by equipment setup and production ramp-up in the second half. Any delay in completion or equipment setup would push back the start of US revenue and could lengthen the period of losses.

Convertible bond valuation and dilution risk

In 2025, a convertible bond valuation loss linked to the share price rise was reflected in results. Press reports also noted that short-term borrowings rose from KRW 20.1 billion to KRW 41.5 billion within two months during 2025. Share price moves can swing valuation gains and losses, and conversion could increase the share count.

Overall view

HanJungNCS has moved from auto parts to ESS cooling systems, with ESS reaching 96.9% of first-half 2026 revenue. Operating profit turned positive in 2024, but it fell to KRW 4.03 billion in 2025, and operating losses have now run for two consecutive quarters from 2Q through 3Q 2026.

The bull side highlights the near-complete business pivot, a customer base centered on Samsung SDI, and a North American production base through the Indiana plant.

The bear side highlights the KRW 3.07 billion net loss attributable to controlling shareholders over the last four quarters, a debt ratio of 152.9%, a US unit with no revenue, and customer concentration.

Securities and estimate providers' 2027 earnings recovery forecasts are far from current results, so whether they materialize needs to be confirmed. The next checkpoints are the 4Q 2026 preliminary results, the 2026 annual business report, and the early April 2027 target completion of the Indiana plant.
