Business structure

J.estina is a jewelry, handbag and watch company founded in 1988 and listed on KOSDAQ. The company runs the J.ESTINA brand alongside sub-brands COCO for casual wear and STINA for simple designs, splitting its target customers accordingly.

Based on Q2 2026 revenue, jewelry accounted for 83%, handbags for 13% and watches for 4%. In jewelry, gold prices pass directly into product costs, and one press report put the jewelry share of product purchases at 79.2% in Q1 2026.

The handbag line was launched in 2011 and is aimed at women in their twenties and thirties, with most products priced at KRW 200,000 or below. Distribution combines department stores, duty-free shops and the company's own online mall, and the company is expanding its online-led sales.

Vietnam, entered in 2024, saw several new stores open in succession during 2026. The company is also reportedly considering a lab-grown diamond product line as the jewelry market polarizes between high-end and low-end.

Listed peers in the same sector include F&F, Handsome, Shinsegae International and LF, but their segment mixes differ, which limits direct comparison. Headcount fell from 249 in 2024 to 184 in 2025, and the company had 194 employees as of June 30, 2026.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual revenue fell from KRW 77.9 billion in 2022 to KRW 75.4 billion in 2023 (down 3.2% year on year), KRW 74.4 billion in 2024 (down 1.2%) and KRW 72.2 billion in 2025 (down 2.9%), a three-year decline running from 2023 through 2025. Among 2022 to 2025, 2022 was the largest year for revenue and 2025 the smallest.

Operating profit of KRW 2.58 billion in 2022 turned into an operating loss of KRW 583 million in 2023, and the loss widened to KRW 2.6 billion in 2024. The company returned to operating profit of KRW 706 million in 2025, with operating margin at 3.3% in 2022, -0.8% in 2023, -3.5% in 2024 and 1.0% in 2025.

Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 1.31 billion in 2022, KRW 792 million in 2023 (down 39.4%), a net loss of KRW 1.5 billion in 2024, and KRW 1.83 billion in 2025.

Press coverage attributed the 2025 swing to the return to operating profit to a headcount reduction from 249 to 184 and salary costs falling from KRW 13.5 billion to KRW 9.8 billion, noting that the profit came even though gold prices rose 65% that year.

The 2025 recovery therefore rested more on cost cuts than on revenue growth. By quarter, Q4 2025 revenue was KRW 19.1 billion with operating profit of KRW 192 million, but Q1 2026 revenue of KRW 18.7 billion produced an operating loss of KRW 556 million.

The operating loss narrowed to KRW 325 million in Q2 2026, and Q3 2026 revenue of KRW 17.9 billion came with an operating loss of KRW 642 million, a swing into loss from a year earlier.

Press reports tied the Q1 2026 pressure to a gold purchase price rise that lifted the cost ratio from 34.28% to 36.58% and the selling expense ratio from 62.02% to 66.29%.

Equity stood at KRW 45.4 billion and total liabilities at KRW 9.9 billion at the end of 2025, with the debt ratio falling from 27.2% in 2022 to 21.8% in 2025. Operating cash flow, however, moved from KRW 3.56 billion in 2022 to -KRW 1.48 billion in 2025.

Industry analysis

Demand in the apparel, fashion and lifestyle sector moves with consumer sentiment and with the flow of traffic through department stores and duty-free channels. Because J.estina's jewelry share is high, gold prices feed directly into its product costs, making raw-material prices a central driver of results.

Press coverage says gold rose 65% in 2025, a year in which the company still recorded operating profit. A July 2026 report said international gold prices were correcting from their early-2026 peak, which could ease input costs somewhat.

Analysts are split on whether that relief will offset selling-expense pressure, which remains elevated. Macro conditions cited include high interest rates and geopolitical unrest linked to the Middle East.

The handbag market is led by foreign premium luxury brands, and the company is targeting it with more accessible price points.

Compared with sector peers such as F&F, Handsome, Shinsegae International, LF and Youngone, J.estina differentiates itself through a jewelry-centered portfolio and a mix of online and offline distribution.

Comparisons of growth and profitability with peers should be checked against each company's periodic filings as they are updated.

Outlook

The 2026 corporate value-up plan lays out three aims: stable profit generation, stronger cost competitiveness, and securing and expanding dividend funding. The plan includes strengthening cost competitiveness to widen operating margin.

On distribution, the company opened several new stores in Vietnam during 2026 and is widening its product range from jewelry into handbags. In global commerce, it plans to gradually expand sales through Japanese and Chinese platforms.

On products, a lab-grown diamond line is under review, and handbags are being pushed through online-led sales and celebrity marketing. The company has not published quantitative annual earnings guidance in its public materials.

With operating losses continuing through Q3 2026, the key question is whether new stores and channel expansion translate into a better cost structure. A July 2026 report named rising selling expenses as a barrier to a profit recovery.

Separately, the Seoul Eastern District Court scheduled a first-instance sentencing hearing for the CEO and three other executives on October 15, 2026. An industry official said that if the CEO position were left vacant, new business initiatives and major management decisions could be disrupted. The pace of strategy execution is therefore tied to how stable the management structure proves to be.

Bull factors

Room to improve cost structure

A July 2026 report said international gold prices were correcting from their early-2026 peak, which could ease cost pressure if it persists. The 2026 value-up plan puts cost competitiveness and a wider operating margin at the center.

If the cost reduction behind the 2025 return to profit holds, selling expenses could also ease over time. The improvement, however, depends on both gold prices and expense control.

Quarterly revenue recovery

Q2 2026 revenue reached KRW 19.7 billion, up 5.5% from the prior quarter. The quarter sat between KRW 18.7 billion in Q1 2026 and KRW 17.9 billion in Q3 2026, showing a rebound in Q2.

Annual revenue has slipped from KRW 77.9 billion in 2022 to KRW 72.2 billion in 2025, so a sustained quarterly recovery would help absorb fixed costs. Revenue fell 9.1% from the prior quarter in Q3, however, so the recovery has not been continuous.

Overseas channels and product diversification

Vietnam, entered in 2024, saw several new stores open in succession during 2026, and the product range is expanding from jewelry into handbags. Gradual expansion of sales through Japanese and Chinese global commerce platforms is planned, and a lab-grown diamond line is under review.

Moving beyond a jewelry-heavy mix could spread exposure to gold price swings. The revenue contribution from the new channels is not yet separately visible in the filings.

Bear factors

Three consecutive quarters of operating losses

Operating losses of KRW 556 million in Q1 2026, KRW 325 million in Q2 and KRW 642 million in Q3 have been reported for three consecutive quarters. Q3 swung into an operating loss from a year earlier, and the operating margin has not held the 1.0% positive level of 2025.

Because the 2025 return to profit leaned heavily on headcount and salary reductions, it remains unclear whether the cost structure has improved at its root. The selling expense ratio reached 66.29% in Q1 2026, up from 62.02% a year earlier.

Gold input costs and selling expense burden

Press reports say the gold raw-material purchase price rose 41.31% year on year in Q1 2026, lifting the cost ratio from 34.28% to 36.58%. Jewelry accounted for 79.2% of product purchases in the same period, so gold price moves pass straight into costs.

Reports also said selling expenses rose on higher commissions and advertising in the first half of 2026, while outsourced processing costs grew sharply.

Legal risk facing the CEO

Prosecutors sought a three-year prison term in late August 2026 for CEO Kim Yu-mi, who was indicted over allegedly selling Chinese-made watches as domestic products. The Seoul Eastern District Court scheduled a first-instance sentencing hearing for the CEO and three other executives on October 15, 2026.

The largest shareholder and related parties hold 33.34%, and an industry observer described decision-making as centered on the owner family. A vacancy in the CEO post could affect management continuity.

Risk factors

Listing maintenance

The KOSDAQ delisting market-capitalization threshold was below KRW 20 billion from July 1, 2026, and rises to below KRW 30 billion from July 1, 2027. Market value swung widely between late June and late September 2026, from a low of KRW 25.4 billion to a high of KRW 43.4 billion.

If profit recovery stalls and the share price stays weak, listing-maintenance pressure could grow near the threshold. The threshold risk is unlikely to ease unless earnings and the share price improve together.

Management legal risk

The first-instance outcome for CEO Kim Yu-mi and other executives could directly affect the management structure and external credibility. Industry observers say weak results combined with legal risk weigh on investor sentiment.

If the CEO post were left vacant, new business initiatives and external credibility management could be disrupted. Any subsequent appeal procedures are also a variable to track.

Gold inputs and cost structure

With jewelry making up most of revenue, gold prices move the cost ratio directly. The company still posted a profit in 2025 despite a 65% gold rally, but in 2026 both the cost ratio and selling expense ratio rose and the company slipped into operating losses.

Even with gold prices correcting, profit recovery may be limited if selling expenses do not ease. Expanding new stores and online sales may also add to selling expenses, which is another burden.
