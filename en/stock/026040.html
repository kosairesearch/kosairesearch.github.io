02

Business structure

J.estina began in 1988 as a watch brand and later expanded into jewelry, handbags, cosmetics, and perfume, operating jewelry and handbags under the 'J.ESTINA' brand and watches under 'ROMANSON'.

In the most recent product mix, jewelry items such as necklaces, earrings, and rings account for roughly 81% of sales, handbags about 13%, watches around 5%, and paid after-sales service about 1%.

The jewelry business built its brand identity around 'Tiara' princess-themed marketing, and the 2011 launch of a handbag line expanded the business into what is now a core revenue driver.

Products are sold mainly through premium channels such as department stores, duty-free outlets, and online malls, targeting younger female consumers with trend-driven design and celebrity marketing.

On production, the company outsources 100% of jewelry, handbag, and watch manufacturing, focusing its own resources on planning, design, and branding rather than owning manufacturing facilities.

Marketing has consistently featured singer IU as brand muse, with collaborations such as the 'UNIS' collection pop-up continuing into 2026.

Overseas, the company entered Vietnam for the first time in 2024 and has opened new stores there continuously in 2026 while widening its product range from jewelry into handbags as a new growth avenue.

The largest shareholder is KBIZ chairman Kim Ki-mun, who founded the company in 1988 and holds a 21.69% stake, while his daughter Kim Yu-mi serves as CEO under a family-centered governance structure.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual revenue fell for three straight years, from KRW 77.9 billion in 2022 to KRW 75.4 billion in 2023 (down 3.2%), KRW 74.4 billion in 2024 (down 1.2%), and KRW 72.2 billion in 2025 (down 2.9%), with 2022 the largest year and 2025 the smallest in this span.

Profitability swung widely: operating profit of KRW 2.58 billion in 2022 turned into an operating loss of KRW 583 million in 2023, widened to a loss of KRW 2.6 billion in 2024, then swung back to a profit of KRW 706 million in 2025.

The operating margin moved from 3.3% in 2022 to -0.8% in 2023 and -3.5% in 2024 before recovering to 1.0% in 2025.

Controlling net income went from KRW 1.31 billion in 2022 to KRW 792 million in 2023 (down 39.4%), a loss of KRW 1.5 billion in 2024 (turning negative), and back to a profit of KRW 1.83 billion in 2025, with net income including non-controlling interests matching the controlling figure in every year, indicating minimal minority-interest impact.

On a quarterly basis, revenue rose from KRW 17.2 billion in Q2 2025 to KRW 18.5 billion in Q3 (up 7.6%) and KRW 19.1 billion in Q4 (up 3.2%), dipped to KRW 18.7 billion in Q1 2026 (down 2.0%), then rose again to KRW 19.7 billion in Q2 2026 (up 5.5% quarter-on-quarter, up 14.8% year-on-year).

Operating results, however, turned profitable in Q3 2025 (KRW 12.45 million) and rose to KRW 192 million in Q4, before swinging back to an operating loss of KRW 556 million in Q1 2026 and remaining in loss at KRW 325 million in Q2, marking two consecutive loss-making quarters.

Despite this, controlling net income in Q2 2026 jumped to KRW 6.91 billion, turning positive quarter-on-quarter and surging 36,851.7% year-on-year, a divergence from the operating loss that suggests non-operating items played a role and warrants confirmation once the next periodic filing details the composition.

Operating cash flow turned negative at -KRW 1.48 billion in 2025, reversing the positive trend of 2022-2024 (KRW 3.56 billion, KRW 1.55 billion, KRW 1.02 billion), showing that cash generation weakened even as the income statement returned to profit.

05

Industry analysis

Korea's fashion jewelry market grew about 21% from KRW 615.0 billion in 2022 to KRW 745.1 billion in 2023, according to the Wolgok Jewelry Industry Research Institute, indicating the broader sector is expanding.

Within that growth, however, J.estina has been characterized as occupying an ambiguous brand position squeezed between imported luxury brands and lower-priced emerging designer labels, limiting its ability to capture consumer preference.

Given its fully outsourced production model, rising gold prices feed directly into cost pressure, and higher gold prices in the first half of 2026 were cited as one factor behind weaker results.

At the same time, selling and administrative costs such as commissions and advertising, as well as outsourcing fees, have been rising, with outsourcing costs up 36.6% year-on-year to KRW 9.7 billion in the first half of 2026 from KRW 7.1 billion a year earlier.

On distribution, the company continues to rely on traditional department store and duty-free channels alongside online malls, supplemented by pop-up stores and collaborations featuring IU to expand brand visibility.

There are few directly comparable pure-play jewelry issuers on the local market, so comparisons with larger diversified apparel and lifestyle brands involve significant differences in scale and channel mix.

Overseas, entry into emerging markets such as Vietnam represents an attempt to reduce domestic dependence, though the contribution to overall revenue still appears to be at an early stage.

Outlook

In its value-up plan disclosed on April 20, 2026, the company set goals of stable profit generation and maintaining financial soundness, and said it would pursue a stable dividend policy within the range of distributable profit.

Concrete measures cited included strengthening cost competitiveness to expand the operating margin and securing and increasing dividend resources through improved profitability.

The 2025 dividend payout ratio was disclosed at 82.91%, qualifying the company as a high-dividend firm under the relevant tax incentive law, underscoring a policy of allocating a meaningful share of earnings to shareholder returns.

Operationally, the company has continued expanding its Vietnam presence entered in 2024, opening new stores in 2026 and broadening its product range into handbags. Brand marketing featuring IU as muse has continued into 2026 through various collections and campaigns, sustaining brand visibility.

Given the two consecutive quarters of operating losses in 2026, it will be important to monitor when and to what extent the cost-competitiveness initiatives feed through to quarterly results.

At the same time, while the CEO's legal case concluded at the first-instance level with a suspended sentence, the possibility of an appeal leaves management continuity as a variable worth watching for strategic execution.

Bull factors

Annual Swing to Profit

2025 revenue fell 2.9% to KRW 72.2 billion, but operating profit of KRW 706 million marked a swing from the KRW 2.6 billion operating loss in 2024.

Controlling net income also reversed to a profit of KRW 1.83 billion from a loss of KRW 1.5 billion, and the sum of controlling net income over the last four quarters (Q3 2025-Q2 2026) reached KRW 7.61 billion. Compared with two consecutive loss-making years in 2023-2024, this marks a clear reversal in profitability.

Revenue Recovery and Overseas Expansion

Quarterly revenue rose from KRW 17.2 billion in Q2 2025 to KRW 19.7 billion in Q2 2026, up 14.8% year-on-year. In the Vietnam market entered in 2024, the company has opened new stores continuously in 2026 and widened its product lineup into handbags. This can be read as an attempt to move away from a historically domestic-heavy business structure.

Low Leverage and Shareholder-Return Commitment

The debt ratio has steadily declined from 27.2% in 2022 to 21.8% in 2025, indicating limited financial leverage burden. The 2025 dividend payout ratio of 82.91% reflects the company's stated goal in its voluntary value-up plan to maintain a stable dividend policy.

Disclosures confirm a policy stance of returning part of profit to shareholders while preserving financial soundness.

Bear factors

Return to Losses in H1 2026

An operating loss of KRW 556 million in Q1 2026 was followed by another loss of KRW 325 million in Q2, marking two consecutive loss-making quarters. Even as revenue grew, rising commissions and advertising costs pushed up SG&A, and outsourcing costs rose 36.6% year-on-year, offsetting the revenue gain. This can be seen as the 2025 profit turnaround losing momentum again in 2026.

CEO Legal Risk

CEO Kim Yu-mi was sentenced on October 15, 2026 to one year in prison, suspended for two years, over charges including selling Chinese-made watches mislabeled as Korean-made between 2017 and 2023 and falsifying production requirements in a public procurement bid.

The court found that the CEO was aware of the mislabeling but tolerated it for business reasons. Industry observers have raised concerns that any disruption to the CEO's duties under the family-centered governance structure could affect major management decisions.

Multi-Year Revenue Decline and Listing-Requirement Pressure

Annual revenue declined for three straight years from KRW 77.9 billion in 2022 to KRW 72.2 billion in 2025, and the company's competitiveness has been questioned even as the overall fashion jewelry market grows.

At the same time, KOSDAQ's market-cap listing-maintenance threshold is being raised in stages toward KRW 15 billion, KRW 20 billion, and eventually KRW 30 billion by 2028, meaning a prolonged share price slump could heighten listing-related pressure. This suggests earnings recovery and share price performance need to move in tandem.

Risk factors

Governance and Legal Risk

Under the owner-centered governance structure in which chairman Kim Ki-mun holds a 21.69% stake, his daughter CEO Kim Yu-mi faces legal risk. Whether the prosecution or the defendant appeals the first-instance suspended sentence could prolong management uncertainty.

Appeal deadlines for criminal cases are typically set within a short period after sentencing, so the appeal decision is likely to become clear in the near term.

Listing-Maintenance Requirement Risk

KOSDAQ's delisting-related market-cap threshold, previously KRW 4 billion through 2025, is being raised in stages to KRW 15 billion, then KRW 20 billion, and KRW 30 billion by 2028.

While the current market capitalization of around KRW 35.6 billion remains above the near-term threshold, continued share price weakness amid rising thresholds could bring management-issue designation into focus.

This is an institutional factor where share price trends, independent of earnings, can affect continued listing.

Input Cost and Currency Risk

Rising gold prices, a key raw material for jewelry, were cited as one factor behind weak performance in the first half of 2026. Given its fully outsourced production structure, the company is directly exposed to subcontractors' pricing policies and currency-driven changes in raw material procurement costs.

The 36.6% year-on-year increase in outsourcing costs in the first half of 2026 illustrates this cost sensitivity.

Overall view

J.estina posted 2025 revenue of KRW 72.2 billion, operating profit of KRW 706 million, and controlling net income of KRW 1.83 billion, breaking a two-year loss streak, but it recorded operating losses again in both Q1 and Q2 2026, leaving the durability of the profit recovery unconfirmed.

The surge in Q2 2026 controlling net income to KRW 6.91 billion stands out, but its divergence from the operating loss warrants further confirmation of its composition.

At the same time, while the CEO's legal risk concluded at the first-instance level with a suspended sentence, an appeal could prolong management uncertainty, and the phased tightening of KOSDAQ's market-cap listing-maintenance requirement is another variable to monitor.

On the financial side, a steadily declining debt ratio and a high dividend payout ratio present more stable elements. Expansion into Vietnam and IU-led brand marketing are cited as medium-term growth drivers, though their revenue contribution still appears limited at this early stage.

Overall, earnings, governance, and regulatory changes are intertwined, making it important to track both the next periodic filing and the progress of the legal process.
