02

Business structure

Ascendio is a diversified entertainment company engaged in film investment, production and distribution, drama planning and production, and live performance and exhibitions. The company was founded in 1977, listed on KOSPI in 1989, and renamed itself Ascendio in 2021.

After a downturn in the film industry, it concentrated on drama production and delivered productions for MBC and KBS2, but total revenue fell to KRW 5.92 billion in 2025. Drama production is delivered against broadcasters' scheduling and production timelines, so annual revenue tends to be volatile.

The company profile says it is pursuing a fabric business tied to abaya textiles in the Middle East to build a more stable revenue base. In 2026, supply contracts with a global beauty distributor followed one another.

The contract disclosed on September 3, 2026 equals 27.2% of latest revenue, the one disclosed on October 29 equals 9.38%, and the one disclosed on November 17 equals 15.70%.

According to a Bloter report, the company dropped its previously planned environment-related businesses and selected virtual idol and AI character planning and bio R&D as new businesses.

On October 27, 2026, the board appointed Jung Hoe-jung as CEO, succeeding Kim Sung-kyu, and two inside director nominees were approved at an extraordinary shareholders' meeting the same day. In drama production it competes with Studio Dragon, CJ ENM and SBS, and it is smaller than these peers.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual revenue fell from KRW 18.6 billion in 2022 to KRW 6.88 billion in 2023, a 63.0% decline, recovered to KRW 13.5 billion in 2024 (up 95.6%), and dropped again to KRW 5.92 billion in 2025 (down 56.0%). 2022 was the largest year in the period and 2025 the smallest.

Operating losses were KRW 8.97 billion in 2022, KRW 10.4 billion in 2023, KRW 4.12 billion in 2024 and KRW 4.32 billion in 2025, which is four consecutive years of losses from 2022 to 2025.

The company attributed the 2025 results to lower revenue from reduced content production and a larger impairment loss on assets held, according to press reports. Operating margins were -48.3% in 2022, -150.4% in 2023, -30.6% in 2024 and -73.0% in 2025, so the loss ratio rises as revenue shrinks.

The 2025 net loss attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 1.37 billion, while the total net loss including non-controlling interests was KRW 1.38 billion, and the gap between the two is the non-controlling share.

The debt ratio fell from 126.7% in 2022 to 20.9% in 2024 before rising to 50.6% in 2025, when total equity was KRW 7.24 billion and total liabilities were KRW 3.66 billion.

Quarterly revenue went from KRW 2.3 billion in the third quarter of 2025 to KRW 1.34 billion in the fourth quarter (down 41.5% from the prior quarter) and KRW 655 million in the first quarter of 2026 (down 51.3%), which was the low point.

It then rose to KRW 1.65 billion in the second quarter of 2026 (up 152.2%) and KRW 3.9 billion in the third quarter (up 136.5%), and the third-quarter figure was 70.0% higher than a year earlier.

Operating losses, however, went from KRW 444 million in the third quarter of 2025 to KRW 1.08 billion in the fourth quarter, KRW 209 million in the first quarter of 2026, KRW 692 million in the second quarter and KRW 1.19 billion in the third quarter, so the loss grew even as revenue recovered. The net loss attributable to controlling shareholders over the latest four quarters totals KRW 6.71 billion.

05

Industry analysis

Domestic drama production is structured around orders from broadcasters and OTT platforms, so a producer's revenue depends heavily on scheduling.

According to the company profile, the business expanded into film and drama production to meet rising content demand after COVID-19, but a prolonged downturn in the film industry has since shifted its weight toward drama.

Given the company's explanation that the 2025 revenue decline stemmed from lower content production, the steady securing of production volume is the key variable for results.

Within the sector, drama-production peers include Studio Dragon, CJ ENM and SBS (Seoul Broadcasting System), and Ascendio is smaller than these companies by revenue.

Hybe, SM Entertainment and JYP Ent., which earn mainly from music and fan-based businesses, and Cheil Worldwide, an advertising agency, have different revenue models, so direct comparison is limited. The 2026 cosmetics supply contracts can be seen as an attempt to broaden revenue beyond content into product supply.

Individual contract values range from 9.38% to 27.2% of latest revenue, and how far they change the revenue base will show in the composition of revenue in the next annual report.

Bear factors

Four straight years of operating losses and shrinking sales

Operating losses have continued from 2022 through 2025, with the 2025 operating loss at KRW 4.32 billion. Revenue fell 56.0% to KRW 5.92 billion in 2025, and the operating margin was -73.0%.

Because the company attributed the revenue decline to lower content production, the bear case persists until production volume is shown to recover.

Wider quarterly losses despite higher revenue

Third-quarter 2026 revenue reached KRW 3.9 billion, but the operating loss was KRW 1.19 billion, larger than KRW 444 million in the third quarter of 2025. The net loss attributable to controlling shareholders in the third quarter of 2026 was KRW 1.27 billion.

The four-quarter total of such losses is KRW 6.71 billion, so revenue growth has not yet translated into earnings improvement.

Reliance on convertible bonds and governance uncertainty

Five convertible bond issues totaling KRW 32 billion were made over the last five years, and the 45th to 47th series were for working capital. Corrections to disclosures after issuance have been frequent, and a management-control lawsuit has been reported. A press analysis estimates year-end 2025 cash and cash equivalents at about KRW 170 million.

Risk factors

Liquidity and funding

Operating cash flow was KRW -4.71 billion in 2025, and the company has relied heavily on convertible bonds over the last five years. The October 2026 rights issue added some capital, but continued losses could require further funding. Dilution from convertible bond terms should be checked alongside share count changes in periodic reports.

Governance and management turnover

In October 2026 the CEO changed from Kim Sung-kyu to Jung Hoe-jung, and new inside directors were appointed. Disclosures of changes in the CEO and the largest shareholder have followed one another, and reports describe a management-control lawsuit. Frequent turnover at the top adds uncertainty to the consistency of medium-term business plans.

Revenue volatility and limited contract disclosure

Drama production revenue varies widely from quarter to quarter depending on scheduling and delivery. Some supply contract details are kept confidential at the counterparty's request and may change as work progresses.

A press analysis reports that receivables rose about 49.5% to KRW 1.16 billion at year-end 2025, so the pace of collections also needs monitoring. The stock was also designated as a short-term investment warning stock in July 2026 after a sharp rally.
