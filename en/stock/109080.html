02

Business structure

Opticis is a company that makes optical link solutions, which transmit video signals over optical fiber. The company says it developed the world's first optical transmission product for DVI signals in 2000.

It says it holds a vertically integrated structure that lets it develop core technologies such as optical devices, optical systems, transmission circuits and module production in-house. Video signal optical links are the largest product line.

On a standalone basis at the end of 2025, the product mix was 77.07% video signal optical links, 10.01% cable, 6.00% IP solutions, 1.48% PC interface optical links and 5.44% other products, according to one data provider.

Another data provider shows the video signal optical link share at 79.40%, so the figure depends on the compiler. Video signal optical links accounted for 92.40% of that product's sales through exports. The company has said more than 80% of its sales come from exports to North America and the EU.

The company is extending applications into medical, defense, education, broadcasting and metaverse video solutions. In April 2026 it began developing a dedicated optical link solution for ophthalmic medical devices with a global partner.

Press reports say its detachable one-fiber product competes with a U.S. company in global markets. R&D spending was about KRW 2.69 billion in 2025, or 10.46% of revenue, and the company had 99 employees at year-end 2025.

04

Earnings analysis

The annual figures show a sharp gap between the 2022 peak and the 2023 trough. Revenue went from KRW 28.7 billion in 2022 to KRW 22 billion in 2023, a 23.4% decline year on year, then rose to KRW 23.8 billion in 2024, up 8.0%, and KRW 25.7 billion in 2025, up 8.3%.

Revenue has grown for two consecutive years from 2024 through 2025, following 2023. Operating profit fell from KRW 7.74 billion in 2022 to KRW 831 million in 2023, a decline of 89.3%, then rose to KRW 2.71 billion in 2024, up 225.5%, and KRW 4.51 billion in 2025, up 66.9%.

The operating margin moved from 26.9% in 2022 to 3.8% in 2023, 11.4% in 2024 and 17.5% in 2025. According to a securities research report, the cost ratio fell from 59.8% to 52.5% in 2022, which lifted operating profit, while sales to the U.S. and Europe eased somewhat in the first half of 2023.

Profit attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 4.42 billion in 2025, down 6.7% year on year, so the operating recovery did not carry through to that line.

By quarter, revenue peaked at KRW 6.62 billion in Q1 2026, up 4.8% from the prior quarter, then fell 0.6% to KRW 6.58 billion in Q2 and 15.5% to KRW 5.56 billion in Q3, which is two consecutive quarterly declines.

Operating profit rose from KRW 543 million in Q4 2025 to KRW 1.25 billion in Q1 2026, up 130.7%, then reached KRW 1.26 billion in Q2 and KRW 1.19 billion in Q3. Profit attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 1.59 billion in Q1 2026 and KRW 1.88 billion in Q2, but turned to a net loss of KRW 1.3 billion in Q3.

Over the last four quarters, that profit line totals KRW 2.55 billion. A provisional earnings disclosure filed with DART on November 13, 2026 also showed the pre-tax result turning to a loss.

The reason for the divergence between operating profit and net profit lies below the operating line, and the income statement in the next annual report is the point to check.

05

Industry analysis

Opticis is classified as a telecommunications and broadcasting equipment maker. On the demand side, reports point to rising optical link demand as the display transition moves from 4K to 8K. The company says it is expanding through 4K HDMI IP video walls and KVM solutions.

A securities information site notes that medical systems and other core applications are expected to grow over the long term.

In April 2026 the company was newly designated under the Public Procurement Service's G-PASS program for overseas procurement, and in September 2026 it was selected for a Ministry of SMEs and Startups program for global small and mid-sized champions.

The company intends to spread its optical link cable and display transmission products across global public procurement markets. On competition, press reports mention technology rivalry with a U.S. company. Because most sales go to North America and Europe, overseas demand and trade conditions carry significant weight.

The company describes its position as a niche in high-speed video transmission within the broader electronics and components sector.

06

Outlook

The value-enhancement plan the company disclosed on March 20, 2026 has three elements. It aims to launch a high-resolution optical link that also carries high-speed USB signals, to release user-focused new products through technology partnerships with other companies, and to continue R&D investment and dividends.

The plan does not specify a launch date for the new product. On dividends, the company said it qualifies as a high-dividend company under the Restriction of Special Taxation Act. The payout ratio for 2025 was presented as 26.7%, and total dividends for 2025 were KRW 1.18 billion, up from KRW 472 million in 2024.

At the shareholder meeting on March 26, 2026, the year-end and interim dividends were confirmed. In April 2026 the company began a development partnership for an optical link solution dedicated to ophthalmic medical devices.

The company plans to use its G-PASS designation to expand its main optical link cable and display transmission products in public procurement markets. On results, a securities information site reported that the video signal optical link segment declined in Q1 2026 after a high-value project's investment was postponed.

Revenue turned lower in Q2 and Q3 2026, so the next points to check are the launch timing of new products and whether the high-value project resumes.

Shareholder returns and policy support
