Business structure

EB Tech is an electron accelerator specialist that was spun off from Samsung Heavy Industries in 2000. The company's own profile describes it as the only domestic maker of electron accelerators.

Its business splits into manufacturing and selling accelerators, and providing electron-beam irradiation services in which products are processed with the beam. Accelerators are used in wire and cable, heat-shrink tubing, tires, foamed resin, curing, semiconductors, food sterilization and medical product sterilization.

The company listed on the KONEX market on June 22, 2016, and is classified as an other electrical equipment manufacturer. No verified disclosure gives the revenue split between equipment sales and irradiation services, so this report treats that split qualitatively only.

Overseas accelerator makers include IBA Industrial, Mevex and ScandiNova, and a market research report says these firms have introduced next-generation equipment with better energy efficiency and throughput. One research firm lists EB Tech among the main operators in the electron-beam irradiation service market.

The company had 44 employees as of December 31, 2025. Equipment sales tend to be order-by-order, while irradiation services can generate repeat revenue. A shift in the mix between the two could not be confirmed from disclosed figures.

Recent trends

Outlook

One research firm projects the e-beam sterilization equipment market growing from USD 1.8 billion in 2025 to USD 3.6 billion by 2034, a 7.9% compound annual rate. Another estimates growth from USD 970 million in 2025 to USD 1,926 million in 2032, a 10.2% annual rate.

The two reports define the market differently, so absolute figures diverge widely and only the direction of growth is a useful reference. The first report names Asia Pacific as the fastest-growing region.

The Korea Atomic Energy Research Institute's advanced radiation research facility operates a demonstration building with three accelerators of 200 keV, 2.5 MeV and 10 MeV, widening uses such as medical product sterilization.

This hints at a growing domestic base of electron-beam applications, but no evidence links it directly to orders at EB Tech. Future results will depend on the timing of equipment orders and the utilization of the irradiation service.

With the operating margin at about 21% in 2025, further margin improvement hinges on revenue growth and cost control. The order backlog and 2026 quarterly results could not be confirmed, so the direction of this year's earnings is hard to judge.

Bull factors

Two Straight Years of Profit Growth

Operating profit rose from KRW 755 million in 2023 to KRW 1,513 million in 2024 and KRW 2,714 million in 2025. Revenue over the same period grew from KRW 8,062 million to KRW 12,916 million. Profit grew faster than revenue, which points to better profitability. Further room for margin improvement depends on whether revenue growth is sustained.

Low Debt and a Thick Equity Base

At the end of 2025, total liabilities were KRW 2,953 million and total equity KRW 15,123 million. The debt ratio was 19.53% and the equity ratio 83.66%. The small debt burden gives the company a financial cushion if conditions weaken. The market capitalization of KRW 13.0 billion is below year-end 2025 total equity of KRW 15,123 million.

Structural Backdrop for Alternative Sterilization

Analysts note that growth in single-use medical devices and tighter ethylene oxide rules are helping electron beam penetrate as an alternative. Electron beam is cited as using no radioactive source and processing products in seconds.

As the only domestic electron accelerator maker, EB Tech has a business structure able to address such demand. Whether this has actually widened its order flow has not been confirmed.

The reason cash generation fell despite higher profit cannot be determined from disclosures alone. If the pattern persists, it raises questions about earnings quality.

Small Scale and Limited Information

At the end of 2025 the company had 44 employees and revenue of about KRW 12.9 billion. Results can swing widely on a small number of large orders. Segment revenue, order backlog and 2026 quarterly results could not be confirmed at the time of writing. No brokerage research was found either, so external verification is limited.

Risk factors

Macro

Equipment orders depend on capital spending plans at medical device, pharmaceutical and materials makers. If clients delay investment because of high interest rates or a slowdown, equipment revenue timing may slip.

A market research report also flags the economic sensitivity of the pharmaceutical and medical device markets as a threat. Exchange-rate moves could affect earnings if there are overseas transactions, but EB Tech's export share could not be confirmed.

Industry & Regulatory

A market research report cites competition from alternative sterilization technologies and regulatory uncertainty across regions as threats, and notes that gamma irradiation retains significant share. Looser rules or progress in competing technologies could change the demand outlook for electron beam.

Large overseas manufacturers and service firms have an advantage in scale and capital. Radiation equipment is subject to safety and licensing rules, so regulatory changes could add costs.

Company & Market Structure

In the report dated March 27, 2026, voting shares outstanding were 2,255,250, and the employee stock ownership association held 157,500 shares (6.98%). With a small free float, trades by a few holders can move the price easily.

The KONEX market has limited disclosure and analyst coverage, so information asymmetry is large. The all-time high was KRW 14,800 on March 29, 2017, and the all-time low KRW 1,285 on June 16, 2020. That range shows how volatile the long-run price has been.

Overall view
