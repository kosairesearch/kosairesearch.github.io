Sales Growth, Losses Widen Again

02

Business structure

Bigwave Robotics, founded in September 2020, is a robot automation platform company headquartered in Gangnam-gu, Seoul. Its business rests on two platforms.

Marosol, an online robot automation matching platform, connects buyers that want robots with suppliers, drawing on a supplier network of about 400 companies and a customer database of more than 30,000 cases to support robot selection and deployment.

SOLlink is integrated control software designed to manage roughly 30 robot models from different manufacturers on one screen. The company does not build robot hardware itself; it earns revenue through a RaaS (Robot-as-a-Service) model covering adoption, installation, operation, maintenance and asset disposal.

Press reports said the share of revenue linked to SOLlink rose from 10.2% in 2024 to 37.1% in 2025. The company cited 640 cumulative customers and a repurchase rate of 55%.

In manufacturing and logistics, its focus is process automation using collaborative robots and autonomous mobile robots, while in services it targets delivery robots for hospitals and hotels.

The company said brownfield projects, meaning those at operating factories, account for 86% of the manufacturing projects it has identified. It is now extending its platform into industrial humanoids and physical AI.

In terms of competition, the company says it differentiates itself from rivals focused on a single solution or a narrow market.

03

Recent trends

Based on DART-disclosed figures, cumulative sales through the third quarter of 2026 reached KRW 3.63 billion, up 69.3% from a year earlier. Over the same period the company recorded an operating loss of KRW 1.04 billion and a net loss of KRW 971 million, with losses widening from the prior year.

For full-year 2025, reported sales were KRW 20.74 billion, operating profit was KRW 673 million and net profit was KRW 805 million, which marked the turnaround to profit. Sales rose from KRW 6.85 billion in 2023 to KRW 13.85 billion in 2024 and KRW 20.74 billion in 2025.

Press coverage attributed part of the 2025 improvement to a decline in financial costs from about KRW 5.4 billion in 2024 to roughly KRW 60 million in 2025.

According to first-half 2026 reports, selling and administrative expenses reached KRW 3.34 billion, up 41% from the first half of 2025, which the company linked to advance hiring and prepaid spending on humanoid demonstration robots, engineers and marketing.

Company-prepared interim figures reported by the press, including cumulative sales of KRW 10.27 billion through July 2026 and first-half sales of KRW 9.98 billion, use a different basis from the disclosed figures and are not directly comparable.

The IPO was scheduled for listing on the KOSDAQ market on September 29, 2026, and the market capitalization stood at KRW 149.6 billion at the latest close.

04

Outlook

The company projected 2026 sales of KRW 35.86 billion, with a plan to expand them to KRW 49.3 billion in 2027 and KRW 65.5 billion in 2028. Operating profit was presented as KRW 1.8 billion for 2026 and KRW 4.3 billion for 2027, and the company targets operating profit of KRW 7.7 billion in 2028.

Because cumulative operating loss through the third quarter of 2026 was KRW 1.04 billion, reaching the full-year 2026 operating profit target would require fourth-quarter profit large enough to offset the nine-month loss and still leave a surplus.

Indicators the company has cited for near-term visibility include an export order backlog of KRW 670 million and a North American pipeline of about KRW 7.3 billion. Of the IPO proceeds, KRW 16.5 billion is allocated to global expansion and marketing, and KRW 4.2 billion to humanoid development and new businesses.

The company expects humanoid solution revenue to exceed KRW 5 billion in 2026. The IPO price was set using a price-to-sales approach, which the company argued suits a growth-stage robotics industry.

Items to track next are the fourth-quarter revenue recognition scale, the conversion of North American orders into revenue, and whether the share of SOLlink-linked revenue holds.

05

Bull factors

Steep Sales Growth

Sales rose from KRW 6.85 billion in 2023 to KRW 20.74 billion in 2025, and the company cites a five-year compound annual growth rate of 147.8%. The company also contrasted this with a 17% average growth rate for peer robotics listings, a comparison it presented itself.

Cumulative sales through the third quarter of 2026 rose 69.3% year on year, indicating that top-line expansion has continued.

Platform Model With Repeat Demand

The company reports 640 cumulative customers and a repurchase rate of 55%, with the repurchase rate reported to have risen from 35.1% in 2023 and 49.7% in 2024. The rising share of SOLlink-linked revenue, at 37.1% in 2025, suggests software-based income is growing relative to one-off installation work.

The company argues that a platform connecting robots from multiple manufacturers becomes more valuable as the number of supported models increases.

Overseas and New-Business Expansion

The company set up a Delaware subsidiary in November 2025 and entered the North American robot market from a base in Detroit.

Its North American pipeline was reported at about KRW 7.3 billion, and two projects, including a research robot system and dashboard assembly automation, have been secured, according to the company.

Press reports said humanoid solutions accounted for 21.2% of revenue in the company's July 2026 preliminary cumulative results. A portion of the IPO proceeds has been earmarked for global expansion and marketing.

06

Bear factors

Return to Losses After One Year of Profit

Cumulative operating loss through the third quarter of 2026 was KRW 1.04 billion and net loss was KRW 971 million, both larger than a year earlier. Because the 2025 operating profit was reversed within a year, the durability of that profit has not been proven, according to a view that has been reported.

First-half 2026 reports cited an operating loss of KRW 859 million, which the company linked to prepaid spending. Lower financial costs also contributed to the 2025 result, which is relevant when judging how much operating improvement was achieved.

Rising SG&A and Earnings Volatility

Selling and administrative expenses reached KRW 3.34 billion in the first half of 2026, up 41% from the first half of 2025. The business depends on project awards and acceptance timing, which is cited as a factor that amplifies quarterly volatility.

The gap between the company's 2026 sales estimate of KRW 35.86 billion and cumulative disclosed sales of KRW 3.63 billion implies that revenue recognition would need to be heavily concentrated in later quarters. Project delivery delays and customer-specific customization costs are also named as profitability variables.

Lower IPO Band and Valuation Gap

The indicative IPO band was lowered twice, from KRW 22,000–27,000 to KRW 20,000–24,000 and finally to KRW 15,000–18,000. Price-to-sales-based value was calculated at KRW 30,428 per share, while the earnings-based value was KRW 10,880, a gap of 64% that shows how sensitive the valuation is to method.

Press analysis noted that the IPO price reflects much of the expected growth given that 2025 operating profit was about KRW 700 million against forecasts of KRW 1.8 billion for 2026 and KRW 4.3 billion for 2027. The current price of KRW 14,550 is below the bottom of the final band of KRW 15,000.

07

Risk factors

Macro

Robot adoption is sensitive to manufacturing capital spending cycles, interest rates and exchange rates. If customers delay automation investment during an economic slowdown, both installation revenue and SOLlink-linked revenue could be affected.

U.S. expansion depends on the trade environment, local labor costs and the operating expense of the new subsidiary, all of which will shape its profitability.

Business

The company's medium-term targets lean heavily on new businesses such as industrial humanoid RaaS. The timing of commercialization for humanoid technology and the pace of customer validation are difficult to gauge from publicly available information.

If robot manufacturers strengthen their own direct sales channels, the brokerage fee structure could come under pressure.

Listing and Supply

In the IPO, lock-up commitments covered 18.14% of subscribed shares on a requested-quantity basis. Lock-up expirations and rising free float after listing could amplify share price volatility. In the early period after listing, the record of reported results is short, which can make valuation benchmarks less stable.

08

Overall view

Based on DART-disclosed figures, Bigwave Robotics' cumulative sales through the third quarter of 2026 rose 69.3% year on year, but its operating loss of KRW 1.04 billion and net loss of KRW 971 million both widened.

Press reports attributed the 2025 operating turnaround to both sales growth and lower financial costs, and it remains too early to judge whether the 2026 deterioration stems from the same drivers.

The platform structure, 640 cumulative customers and a 55% repurchase rate are strengths that the company itself highlights.

On the other side, higher selling and administrative expenses, earnings swings tied to project acceptance timing, and differences in reporting basis between disclosures and press figures add uncertainty to interpretation.

The company's 2026 operating profit estimate of KRW 1.8 billion and its 2028 target of KRW 7.7 billion sit far from the current loss trend, so how closely quarterly results track those goals is the key item to monitor.

On valuation, the gap between the price-to-sales-based IPO value and the earnings-based value is wide, so perspectives differ depending on the method.

Overall, the company is in a phase where growth is being demonstrated while profitability is still being re-established, and this is a qualitative summary for informational purposes only.
