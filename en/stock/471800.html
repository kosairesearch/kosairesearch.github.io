02

Business structure

AL Robot is a robot core component company founded in 2017 that develops and supplies force-torque sensors, which measure the force and torque applied to robots, along with fusion control devices that regulate robot motion.

Its product lineup includes joint-type torque sensors, 6-axis force-torque sensors, rotary torque sensors and fusion control devices.

Revenue mix still leans toward control devices: of roughly KRW 15 billion in 2025 revenue, sensor revenue was only about KRW 900 million, with most revenue coming from motor control units for ventilation appliances.

The company plans to lift sensor revenue from KRW 2.9 billion in 2026 to KRW 35.8 billion in 2028, a figure that reflects NH Investment & Securities research estimates and would lift sensors to about 65% of total revenue.

Its customer base is centered on domestic collaborative robot makers, to which it has supplied joint-type torque sensors exclusively since 2019, with continued adoption in follow-on models. Overseas, it began volume supply of joint-type torque sensors to a Japanese robot maker in March 2026.

On competition, the strain gauge method common in the industry requires manual calibration by skilled workers and is considered less suited to mass production, and AL Robot says it automated calibration with a reinforcement-learning-based algorithm.

According to the company, this raised productivity per worker 14 times and cut total production cost by about 66%. Robotis, which makes joint actuators, is cited as a listed comparison point.

03

Recent trends

According to the 2026 third-quarter DART filing, cumulative revenue reached KRW 4.18 billion, up 10.1% year on year. Over the same period, the operating loss was KRW 580 million and the net loss was KRW 503 million, both wider than a year earlier.

Revenue growth did not narrow the operating loss, which is the central feature of these results. For comparison, the prospectus shows 2025 full-year revenue of about KRW 14.985 billion and an operating loss of KRW 1.343 billion.

The shares listed on November 18, 2026 at an offer price of KRW 17,600, and on the first morning they briefly fell to KRW 13,800, below the offer price. On November 20, 2026, the stock dropped 21.40% in a session with no notable disclosure or major news, closing at KRW 10,690.

On November 26, 2026 the share price was KRW 10,030, down 14.86% on the day and roughly 43% below the offer price. During bookbuilding, 2,227 institutions took part at a competition rate of 871.74 to 1, and the offer price was set at the top of the indicative range. The total offering amounted to KRW 42.2 billion.

The expected market capitalization at the offer price was about KRW 212.4 billion, against the current market capitalization of KRW 121.0 billion.

04

Outlook

The potential for operating improvement depends on the sensor revenue mix and capacity expansion. The company has said it plans to raise sensor production capacity from about KRW 11.8 billion a year to roughly KRW 50 billion in 2027 and KRW 72.9 billion in 2028.

About KRW 11.5 billion of the offering proceeds will go to buildings, facilities and equipment, with the remainder directed to working capital and R&D. The company expects volume supply to its Japanese customers to expand in stages across 2027 and 2028.

The prospectus revenue estimates are KRW 18.08 billion for 2026, KRW 35.43 billion for 2027 and KRW 54.81 billion for 2028. Set against cumulative reported revenue of KRW 4.18 billion through the third quarter of 2026, the 2026 estimate would require a very large fourth-quarter contribution to be achieved.

The company has said these estimates do not include humanoid-related revenue. In humanoids, it is co-developing actuators with a global auto parts company and targets volume production in 2027, while as of a November 25, 2026 report its tactile sensor had reached the stage where samples can be provided to customers. Full-year 2026 results will be confirmed in the annual report to be published in 2027.

05

Bull factors

Sensor-led mix and cost improvement potential

NH Investment & Securities researcher Kang Kyung-keun projects that a higher sensor revenue share will cut the cost ratio from 46.7% to 34.8%, bringing operating leverage into play. The same report credits automated calibration with delivering performance, manufacturability and price competitiveness together. These are brokerage projections, and the actual cost reduction will be visible only after production scales up.

Customer stickiness from certification and track record

An NH Investment & Securities researcher notes that switching a safety-certified component to another supplier forces a robot maker to re-certify the robot, making replacement practically difficult. The company says it has supplied volume production for over seven years without field quality complaints.

It also says Japanese robot makers compare several sensors before choosing a supplier and, once selected, are bound by non-disclosure agreements that make substitution difficult.

Japan volume ramp and humanoid optionality

The company began volume supply of joint-type torque sensors to a Japanese robot maker in March 2026, and another Japanese industrial and collaborative robot maker has a volume program scheduled for the third quarter of 2027.

Humanoid actuators are being co-developed with a global auto parts company, and tactile sensors have reached the sample-supply stage. The company says the timing of revenue from these programs depends on how quickly the humanoid market scales.

06

Bear factors

Persistent losses and wider net loss

Cumulative operating loss through the third quarter of 2026 was KRW 580 million and the net loss was KRW 503 million, both wider than a year earlier. Revenue of KRW 4.18 billion rose 10.1%, yet it did not reduce the operating loss. The 2025 operating loss of KRW 1.343 billion also shows that losses have persisted.

The timing of a return to profitability depends on whether cost ratio improvement and revenue growth arrive together.

Single-customer exposure and long adoption cycles

According to the prospectus, CA accounted for 72.34% of revenue in the first half of 2026. The company presents overseas customer expansion as a way to reduce this dependence. However, the CFO has said proof-of-concept adoption with new customers can take two to four years, so diversification will take time.

Post-listing share price below the offer

The shares traded below the KRW 17,600 offer price from the first day of listing on November 18, 2026, and on November 20 dropped 21.40% without a notable disclosure. The November 26 price of KRW 10,030 is about 43% below the offer price.

Market capitalization of KRW 121.0 billion compares with the roughly KRW 212.4 billion expected at the offer price. Some market observers attribute the volatility to short-term supply and demand imbalances.

07

Risk factors

Macro

The pace at which the humanoid and collaborative robot markets develop will drive commercialization timing. The company says the mass production and commercialization schedule for its actuators is tied to how the humanoid market opens up. If capital spending by robot makers slows, the payoff from the sensor capacity expansion could be delayed.

Capacity expansion execution

Sensor production capacity is planned to rise from about KRW 11.8 billion a year at present to roughly KRW 50 billion in 2027 and KRW 72.9 billion in 2028. About KRW 11.5 billion of the offering proceeds will go to buildings, facilities and equipment.

If equipment installation or the shift to volume production slips past the 2027 target, the timing of the KRW 35.43 billion 2027 revenue estimate could also be delayed. Low utilization of the expanded capacity could further enlarge losses through fixed-cost burden.

R&D spending burden

The humanoid actuator is being co-developed with a global auto parts company, and the tactile sensor has reached the sample-supply stage for customers. Both programs require continued R&D spending through development and validation.

The portion of offering proceeds allocated to working capital and R&D could be consumed faster the longer development takes. If revenue from these programs arrives late, R&D costs will remain a factor widening the operating loss.

08

Overall view

AL Robot is shifting its revenue mix toward force-torque sensors, and the cumulative 2026 third-quarter filing shows revenue growth alongside wider operating and net losses. Its strengths are more than seven years of volume supply, an automated calibration process and expanding supply to a Japanese robot maker.

Its weaknesses are continued losses, high single-customer exposure and the post-listing share price decline.

The prospectus estimates, including KRW 54.81 billion in 2028 revenue, differ widely from the results through the third quarter of 2026, so how future quarterly filings reconcile with those estimates remains a key indicator.

Sensor capacity expansion and humanoid commercialization are medium-term options whose evaluation will depend on when they reach revenue. Some observers view the share price trend after the offering as driven by supply-demand factors separate from business results.

Improvement in operating metrics and the pace of customer diversification remain the main variables for future assessment.
