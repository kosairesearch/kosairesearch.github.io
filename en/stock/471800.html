Report overview

Tech-listed AL Robot falls below offer on debut

AL Robot listed on KOSDAQ through a technology-based special listing while still loss-making, and its first-day close finished below the 17,600-won offer price.

  1. 1

    Listed on a technology-based track, AL Robot still posted an operating loss of KRW 1.343 billion in 2025 and KRW 0.6 billion in the first half of 2026.

  2. 2

    Institutional demand of 871.74 to 1 and retail demand of 834.73 to 1 did not carry into the first-day close, which was 24.32% below the offer price.

  3. 3

  4. 4

  5. 5

02

Business structure

AL Robot, founded in 2017, is a core component supplier for robots that develops and produces force-torque sensors, which measure the force and torque generated when a robot contacts an external object, along with motor control units.

The sensors are mounted at robot joints or end-effectors and are used for precision control and collision detection in collaborative and industrial robots, with medical and surgical robots, smart factories and humanoids also cited as application areas.

The company says it raised productivity and price competitiveness by automating sensor calibration, which had been manual, with its own algorithms, though this is the company's own explanation.

Its in-house control of force-torque sensing and control technology was cited as a reason it received an A grade in a technology evaluation. The company supplies core parts to makers of collaborative and industrial robots and has shipped mass-production joint torque sensors to a Japanese robot maker since March 2026.

It is also co-developing products with a Chinese collaborative robot company, which is seen as widening its overseas supply experience. Overseas sales remain a small share, at 3.51% in 2026, and the company's registration statement plans to lift that to 31.33% in 2027 and 45.58% in 2028.

Because it supplies components rather than complete robots, its revenue appears tied to the volume of its customers' robot production. Public figures comparing its cost or performance with rivals are limited, so its cost-competitiveness claims currently rest mainly on company explanations.

The company plans to use KRW 23.9 billion of the KRW 42.24 billion offering proceeds for research and development, with the rest going to production capacity and overseas expansion.

03

Recent trends

Sales rose to KRW 15.1 billion in 2024 from KRW 8 billion in 2023, nearly doubling, and the company recorded an operating profit of KRW 1.9 billion that year. Net profit for 2024 was reported at about KRW 1.2 billion.

Sales in 2025 were KRW 14.985 billion, roughly flat on the prior year, and the company swung to an operating loss of KRW 1.343 billion. Based on separate-basis figures reported for 2025, the net loss was KRW 21.891 billion, a far larger figure than the operating loss.

First-half 2026 sales were KRW 8.4 billion with an operating loss of KRW 0.6 billion.

The projections in the registration statement call for sales of KRW 18.1 billion and operating profit of KRW 0.2 billion in 2026, followed by sales of KRW 35.4 billion and operating profit of KRW 3.7 billion in 2027, and sales of KRW 54.8 billion and operating profit of KRW 14.2 billion in 2028.

On the first trading day, November 18, 2026, the stock opened at 15,100 won against the 17,600-won offer price. It fell to an intraday low of 13,800 won, while the intraday high was reported as 18,400 won by one outlet and 18,690 won by another.

The stock closed at 13,320 won, down 24.32% from the prior close, and reports said no negative disclosure surfaced that day. The institutional lock-up commitment ratio was 1.13%, and about 35.01% of issued shares were reported as tradable right after listing.

On November 17, 2026, shares of robot bellwether Robotis fell more than 8% after it reported a third-quarter operating loss, which weighed on sentiment across robot stocks.

04

Outlook

The company's projections show sales and operating profit rising sharply from 2026, but the gap from the starting point of flat 2025 sales and a first-half 2026 operating loss is wide.

Reaching the 2026 operating profit estimate of KRW 0.2 billion would require the second half to swing to a profit large enough to offset the first-half loss. Because the profit forecasts are linked to the pace of sales growth, whether sales targets are met becomes the leading variable for the profit outlook.

The export plan rests on the assumption of supplying mass-production products to one overseas complete-robot maker in 2027 and four in 2028. Volumes from the Japanese customer and results from the co-development with the Chinese collaborative robot maker are seen as the first checkpoints for this plan.

The offering proceeds are earmarked for KRW 23.9 billion of R&D and for production capacity and overseas expansion, so utilization after capacity investment becomes the next item to track.

On valuation, Daishin Securities calculated a per-share appraisal value of 23,108 won and set the offer price after applying discounts of 23.84% to 32.06%, according to reports.

A financial industry official said future sales growth and improvement in profitability, rather than current earnings, are what underpin the company's valuation. The next checkpoints are the full-year 2026 results and the actual 2027 export share.

05

Bull factors

In-house technology and an A-grade evaluation

In-house control of force-torque sensing and control technology was cited as a reason for the A-grade technology evaluation. The sensors are used for precision control and collision detection in collaborative, industrial and medical robots, which gives them a broad application base.

The company says automated calibration with its own algorithms raised productivity and price competitiveness. Investment bankers see this capability as consistent with the policy push to localize core robot components.

Overseas mass-production supply begins

Since March 2026 the company has supplied mass-production joint torque sensors to a Japanese robot maker, and it is co-developing with a Chinese collaborative robot company. This is seen as extending domestic supply experience overseas. The company's plan lifts the export share to 31.33% in 2027 and 45.58% in 2028. Growth in overseas customers could change a sales mix that has been domestic-centered.

Strong subscription demand and a 2024 profit

Institutional demand in the offering reached 871.74 to 1, and retail subscriptions reached 834.73 to 1, showing strong interest from both groups. Subscription deposits totaled about KRW 44.074 trillion.

The KRW 1.9 billion operating profit recorded in 2024 is cited as evidence that the business model can generate profit. The offer price was set at 17,600 won, the top of the indicative range.

06

Bear factors

Swing to an operating loss in 2025

Sales in 2025 were KRW 14.985 billion, roughly flat on the prior year, and the company posted an operating loss of KRW 1.343 billion. The losses continued into the first half of 2026, with sales of KRW 8.4 billion and an operating loss of KRW 0.6 billion.

A separate-basis net loss of KRW 21.891 billion was reported, which is far larger than the operating loss and could raise concern about financial burden.

A gap between valuation and earnings

Pre-IPO valuation of about KRW 81 billion rose to about KRW 160 billion on a pre-money basis in May 2026. Daishin Securities calculated a per-share appraisal value of 23,108 won and set the offer range after applying discounts.

Whether earnings can support this valuation is cited as a key variable, given the stall in sales growth. Media coverage also raised the point that valuation rose despite stagnant results.

Post-listing supply and a weak debut

With institutional lock-up commitments at only 1.13%, about 35.01% of shares became tradable right after listing. The first-day close of 13,320 won was 24.32% below the offer price. The debut shows that high subscription ratios do not determine post-listing prices, which are set by actual buy and sell orders. The intraday low of 13,800 won was 21.59% below the offer price.

07

Risk factors

Macro

On November 17, 2026, the Dow fell 1.64% in the US, and the KOSPI opened lower that day after a selloff in US AI stocks. Because the strategy depends on expanding overseas demand, shifts in global growth and robot capital spending cycles could affect the timing of sales. Sentiment in robot stocks has reacted sharply to earnings news from sector leaders.

Execution

Projected sales must rise from KRW 18.1 billion in 2026 to KRW 54.8 billion in 2028, and the export share must reach 31.33% in 2027. If the assumption of supplying mass-production products to overseas complete-robot makers does not hold, the entire forecast could be revised.

Because offering proceeds are concentrated in R&D and production capacity, the pace of investment execution and capacity utilization are also key variables.

Supply and demand

With lock-up commitments at only 1.13%, a large volume of shares can trade soon after listing, and trading has continued below the offer price. Whether a technical support level forms is cited as the near-term focal point. Newly listed stocks tend to be volatile, so price swings can widen as supply and demand shift.

08

Overall view

AL Robot is a technology company that holds in-house force-torque sensing and control technology, supplies core parts to collaborative and industrial robot makers, and has begun mass-production supply to a Japanese customer.

Subscription deposits exceeded KRW 44 trillion, yet the first-day close finished below the offer price. On earnings, the 2025 operating loss of KRW 1.343 billion and the first-half 2026 operating loss of KRW 0.6 billion mean that a swing to profit is the central question.

The company's projections assume operating profit rises from KRW 0.2 billion in 2026 to KRW 14.2 billion in 2028, and how far sales growth and a higher export share close that gap is the item to watch.

After listing, the low lock-up ratio creates supply pressure, and shifts in sentiment across robot stocks also affect the share price. Media coverage has presented two views, one that valuation rose despite stagnant results and another that it reflects future earnings estimates.

Summarized for information purposes, the picture is strengths in technology and early overseas supply set against continued losses and the conditions needed to meet the projections.
