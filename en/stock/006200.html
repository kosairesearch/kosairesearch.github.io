Back to Operating Profit in Q3, but Results Still Hinge on Subsidiary KEC

KEC Holdings is a holding company whose results track its semiconductor subsidiary KEC; it returned to operating profit of KRW 1.08 billion in Q3 2026, while net loss attributable to controlling shareholders over the last four quarters totals KRW 1.24 billion.

02

Business structure

KEC Holdings is a holding company founded in 1969 that owns stakes in its subsidiaries and handles group strategy, new investment and synergies among affiliates. Its main income sources are dividends and brand fees received from subsidiaries.

In the 2026 first-quarter report, the semiconductor segment accounts for most consolidated revenue, with the service segment making up the rest.

The semiconductor core is subsidiary KEC Corporation, which specializes in non-memory power semiconductors, from small-signal transistors to power MOSFETs, IGBTs, diodes and ICs.

Its main products are transistors, ICs, diodes and automotive semiconductors, with Samsung Electronics, LG Electronics, Mitsubishi and Midea listed among key customers. The holding company owns 29.59% of KEC, and KEC operates overseas units in Thailand, Wuxi in China, Singapore, Hong Kong, Taiwan, Shanghai and Japan.

The service segment, run through subsidiaries such as KEC Device, covers transport and warehousing, real estate leasing, plant work and wholesale and retail trade, with Coupang named among its customers. KEC's core strategy is focusing on automotive electronics and industrial semiconductor markets.

Holding-level revenue consists of IT services and dividend income, so subsidiary results flow directly into the holding company's profit and loss.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual revenue fell from KRW 324 billion in 2022 to KRW 266.8 billion in 2023, a 17.6% decline, then recovered to KRW 294.3 billion in 2024, up 10.3%, before dropping to KRW 275.9 billion in 2025, down 6.3%.

Operating profit of KRW 30 billion in 2022 turned into an operating loss of KRW 24.7 billion in 2023, narrowed to an operating loss of KRW 4.13 billion in 2024, and widened again to an operating loss of KRW 14.1 billion in 2025.

Operating margin moved from 9.3% in 2022 to -9.2% in 2023, -1.4% in 2024 and -5.1% in 2025, and the company recorded operating losses for three consecutive years from 2023 to 2025.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders turned positive at KRW 4.91 billion in 2024 after a loss in 2023, then swung to a net loss of KRW 1.31 billion in 2025.

Net income including non-controlling interests was a net loss of KRW 3.37 billion in 2024 and KRW 19.8 billion in 2025, with a wider gap than the controlling-shareholder figures, which appears to reflect results attributable to non-controlling interests.

At year-end 2025, total equity was KRW 377.5 billion, of which equity attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 145.5 billion, a little over one-third, and total liabilities were KRW 139.7 billion. The debt ratio rose from 35.8% in 2022 to 37.0% in 2025.

Operating cash flow was KRW 4.01 billion in 2022, KRW 38 billion in 2024 and KRW 16 billion in 2025, lower in 2025 than in 2024.

Quarterly revenue grew from KRW 63.3 billion in Q3 2025 to KRW 80.6 billion in Q2 2026, then edged down to KRW 77.4 billion in Q3 2026, a 4.0% decline from the prior quarter but up 22.3% year on year.

Operating losses of KRW 5.39 billion in Q3 2025, KRW 4.86 billion in Q4 2025, KRW 2.86 billion in Q1 2026 and KRW 2.37 billion in Q2 2026 narrowed before Q3 2026 posted operating profit of KRW 1.08 billion, a turn to profit both quarter on quarter and year on year.

Because revenue fell from the prior quarter even as the company turned profitable, the specific drivers of the margin improvement can be traced through the operating discussion in the Q3 2026 quarterly report.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders alternated between losses and profits, with a net loss of KRW 3.33 billion in Q4 2025, a profit of KRW 497 million in Q1 2026, a loss of KRW 72.91 million in Q2 2026 and a profit of KRW 1.67 billion in Q3 2026, for a four-quarter net loss total of KRW 1.24 billion.

05

Industry analysis

KEC Holdings' results depend heavily on the power semiconductor market in which its subsidiary KEC Corporation operates. KEC specializes in non-memory power semiconductors and is described as an integrated device manufacturer that handles design, production and sales in-house.

Its core end markets are automotive electronics and industrial semiconductors, both of which are sensitive to vehicle production trends and industrial capital spending cycles. Unlike memory chips, power semiconductors tend to compete more on quality reliability and customer qualification history than on price.

According to company information materials, KEC serves domestic and overseas appliance and automotive manufacturers and is also developing SiC power semiconductors.

In a February 2026 disclosure, KEC said it plans to spend about KRW 64.8 billion on expanding next-generation power semiconductor production lines and upgrading processes through the end of December 2026. Whether this investment aligns with medium-term demand is a point to monitor in upcoming results.

Serving several customer groups across appliances and automotive reduces exposure to swings in any single end market. In the same February 2026 disclosure, the holding company said KEC's price-to-book ratio fell from 0.93x at end-2022 to 0.42x at end-2025.

Compared with other listed holding companies, KEC Holdings' income structure differs because its holding-level earnings rely on subsidiary dividends and brand fees.

Outlook

The nearest checkpoint is the end of December 2026, when subsidiary KEC is scheduled to finish its capital expenditure program. The KEC investment was disclosed at about KRW 64.8 billion, and the capacity effect is likely to appear in revenue and depreciation from 2027 onward.

In February 2026, the holding company said it would use the KRW 2.2 billion exchangeable bond proceeds together with existing cash to buy additional KEC shares. The company expected that, if the purchase is carried out, its and its related parties' KEC stake would rise from 31.29% to the mid-34% range.

However, a report dated 30 April 2026 showed the holding company's own stake unchanged at 29.59%.

In August 2026, the company decided to cancel 770,960 common treasury shares with a scheduled cancellation date of 25 August 2026, and the same month it agreed to repurchase KRW 600 million of unredeemed fifth-series exchangeable bonds before maturity.

The exchangeable bonds can be exchanged from 19 February 2026 through 13 January 2031. The 2026 annual business report, the next periodic filing, will be the most direct check on whether full-year operating margin turned positive.

Because Q4 2026 results will first be confirmed through that annual report, whether the Q3 operating profit held through year-end is the next point to verify.

Bull factors

Quarterly operating profit turns positive

Q3 2026 operating profit of KRW 1.08 billion turned positive from an operating loss of KRW 2.37 billion in Q2 2026, and it also swung to profit year on year. Quarterly revenue rose from KRW 63.3 billion in Q3 2025 to KRW 80.6 billion in Q2 2026 before edging down to KRW 77.4 billion in Q3 2026. The shift to quarterly profit after a long run of losses is cited as a bullish factor.

Next-generation power semiconductor capex

KEC disclosed in February 2026 a plan to spend about KRW 64.8 billion on next-generation power semiconductor line expansion and process upgrades through end-December 2026. With automotive electronics and industrial semiconductors as core markets, completed expansion could widen production capacity for that demand. The timing and scale of profit contribution from the expansion still need to be confirmed in future results.

Shareholder returns and capital structure moves

The March 2026 annual general meeting approved a year-end cash dividend with a total payout of KRW 877.1 million. In August 2026, the company decided to cancel 770,960 common shares and agreed to repurchase unredeemed fifth-series exchangeable bonds before maturity. The company describes these steps as aimed at enhancing shareholder value.

Bear factors

Three consecutive years of operating losses

Operating losses began with KRW 24.7 billion in 2023, then continued at KRW 4.13 billion in 2024 and KRW 14.1 billion in 2025. The 2025 operating loss widened from 2024 while revenue fell 6.3%. The losses also carried through to a 2025 net loss attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 1.31 billion.

Quarterly profits not yet sustained

Net loss attributable to controlling shareholders over the last four quarters totals KRW 1.24 billion, and Q2 2026 returned to a loss of KRW 72.91 million. Profits in Q1 and Q3 2026 did not form a continuous run. Whether operating profit consistently carries through to controlling-shareholder profit remains unsettled.

Rising leverage and weaker operating cash flow

The debt ratio rose from 31.3% in 2024 to 37.0% in 2025, and operating cash flow fell from KRW 38 billion to KRW 16 billion over the same period. In February 2026 the company issued exchangeable bonds worth KRW 2.2 billion to fund a subsidiary stake purchase. Accumulated funding costs could limit the holding company's capacity to support subsidiaries.

Risk factors

Customer and end-market concentration

KEC's key customers include Samsung Electronics and LG Electronics, as well as overseas manufacturers, so a change in orders from one client can flow quickly into subsidiary revenue. Automotive and industrial demand is sensitive to economic cycles and could widen revenue swings.

Exchangeable bond supply and treasury share release

The February 2026 exchangeable bonds can be exchanged from 19 February 2026 through 13 January 2031. The company expects exchange shares to be released over a long period according to investors' cash recovery strategies. The pace and size of that supply could affect float and trading supply-demand.

Holding structure and subsidiary dependence

Because the holding company's income relies on subsidiary dividends and brand fees, weaker KEC results pass directly into holding-level profit and loss. The 1-for-5 share consolidation effective 30 April 2026 reduced total common shares outstanding to 9,360,627, and its effect on liquidity also warrants attention.

Overall view

Annual revenue fell from KRW 324 billion in 2022 to KRW 275.9 billion in 2025, with operating losses in each year from 2023 to 2025. Quarterly revenue eased from KRW 80.6 billion in Q2 2026 to KRW 77.4 billion in Q3 2026 but was up 22.3% year on year.
