Report overview

Indian Procurement Drives a Profit Surge

Remedi, a portable X-ray equipment maker, is posting rapid profit growth on Indian public procurement deliveries, but a share price below the IPO price, supply overhang and country concentration are part of the debate.

02

Business structure

Remedi was founded in July 2012, and its headquarters are in Chuncheon, Gangwon Special Self-Governing Province. Built on low-dose, compact, high-resolution X-ray core technology, it has developed and commercialized portable medical X-ray systems, industrial non-destructive testing equipment and key X-ray components.

The company has a full-stack capability that spans core components through finished products. Its flagship product, the Remex-KA6, is a compact unit of about 2.4 kg used in emergency rooms and operating rooms as well as in ambulances and disaster sites.

Medical X-ray dominates the revenue mix. The share of medical X-ray products and goods rose from 67.2% in 2023 to 87.3% in 2025 and 92.7% in the first quarter of 2026. Overseas sales exceed 90% of revenue, with India the largest market.

The company has outlined a strategy of selling small volumes in 45 countries to build references, then moving through emerging markets toward advanced markets. In India, it plans to build on the model of its local subsidiary, Remescan Healthcare, and to make Indonesia, the Philippines and Bangladesh its core hubs.

Weight and imaging performance are the main claimed advantages. The KA6 is lighter than competing products, which weigh 3.5 to 7.4 kg. Its focal spot is 0.4 mm, half of the 0.8 mm that global competitors commonly adopt. These are company claims and should be read alongside independent verification.

Industrial non-destructive testing is a mid- to long-term growth axis, with plans to expand into secondary batteries, food and semiconductor sites. Its revenue share is still small, so dependence on portable medical X-ray remains high.

03

Recent trends

Per reported filings, first-half 2026 revenue was KRW 6.18 billion, up 61.2% year on year. Operating profit was KRW 2.83 billion, up 423.0%, and net income was KRW 1.86 billion, up 453.0%.

Press reports, however, put cumulative first-half consolidated results at revenue of KRW 15.57 billion, operating profit of KRW 6.17 billion and an operating margin of 39.6%.

The two sets of figures differ in scale, so differences in the reporting basis, such as the period covered, may exist and should be checked against the original filing.

The annual trend shows fast profit improvement. Operating profit swung from a KRW 2.3 billion loss in 2023 to a KRW 0.9 billion profit in 2024, then expanded to KRW 2.8 billion in 2025 and KRW 3.3 billion in the first quarter of 2026.

For 2025, revenue was KRW 14.628 billion, operating profit was KRW 2.75 billion and net income was KRW 5.087 billion. During the offering, the company presented 2026 targets of KRW 24.2 billion in revenue, KRW 6.3 billion in operating profit and KRW 5.0 billion in net income. The reported cumulative first-half operating profit is already close to that full-year target.

The share price has swung widely since listing. On the first trading day, July 13, 2026, the stock rose early in the session to KRW 28,700, 38.65% above the IPO price. It later fell to a 52-week low of KRW 6,980 on July 29, and the 52-week high is KRW 34,650.

After the first-half results became known, it closed up 22.76% at KRW 13,590 in one session. On September 29 it closed down 14.83% at KRW 19,760, and volatility has continued.

At the time of writing, the price is KRW 13,040 (up 2.60% from the previous day), with a market capitalization of KRW 101.3 billion and trading value of KRW 4.1 billion.

04

Outlook

The central variable for growth is whether Indian public procurement continues.

Remedi supplied 1,534 units through the CMSS tuberculosis elimination program tender in June 2025 and delivered a further 767 units, about KRW 4.0 billion, in the first half of 2026, bringing installations to 2,301 Indian public health facilities.

In its October 21, 2026 report, NH Investment & Securities said that the Indian government plans tenders of about 2,000 units a year over the next several years. The brokerage forecast full-year revenue of KRW 24.0 billion and operating profit of KRW 6.8 billion. Eugene Investment & Securities projected revenue of KRW 24.6 billion for 2026 and KRW 40.6 billion for 2027.

There are also new business axes. In August 2026, Remedi began a primary-care business with Daewoong Pharmaceutical and is running a proof of concept for a model that combines AI reading with compact X-ray and ultrasound devices.

In Southeast Asia, it plans to make Indonesia, the Philippines and Bangladesh its core hubs and to join more public tenders. Revenue contributions from industrial non-destructive testing and aerospace remain unclear.

The medium-term targets presented during the offering were revenue of KRW 40.6 billion and operating profit of KRW 14.2 billion for 2027, and revenue of KRW 63.2 billion and operating profit of KRW 24.9 billion for 2028. The IPO pricing also rested on estimated 2028 net income.

Whether those targets are met, and whether expanded capacity converts into actual orders, remains to be verified. The company plans to raise capacity from about 3,500–4,000 units a year to roughly 7,000 by year-end.

The operating margin has improved as fixed-cost pressure eased, and the same mechanism could weigh on it if orders slow.

05

Bull factors

Public Procurement Track Record

Remedi won 8 of 9 government procurement tenders from 2024 through 2026. Its equipment is installed at 2,301 public health facilities in India. NH Investment & Securities estimated penetration at only 1.1% of local clinic-level facilities. If repeat orders continue, revenue visibility could improve.

Product Specifications and Vertical Integration

NH Investment & Securities said the flagship KA6 weighs about one-third as much as a U.S. competitor's product. The company says it can shoot 270 to 290 images per charge, versus 100 to 150 for competing products. It also claims cost and technology advantages from in-housing tubes, emitters and high-voltage generators.

Operating Leverage and Balance Sheet Support

Lower fixed-cost burden as revenue scaled is cited as a driver of better profitability. Per reported filings, first-half 2026 operating profit rose 423.0% year on year. The listing raised KRW 24.84 billion, which can fund capacity expansion and debt repayment. Being profitable before listing also distinguishes it from loss-making technology-track companies.

06

Bear factors

Country and Tender Dependence

Overseas sales exceed 90% of revenue, with India the largest share. Revenue depends heavily on government tender timing and volume, so quarterly volatility could be high. Delayed tender plans or wins by competitors could change the earnings path. Whether the surge in the first half of 2026 repeats will be seen in follow-up tender results.

Demanding Pricing Basis

The IPO price was set on estimated 2028 net income rather than current earnings. Based on 2025 net income, the price-to-earnings ratio was about 33, and some saw the pricing as aggressive. Because the estimates depend on follow-on products and overseas expansion, a shortfall could add pressure. The current price below the IPO price is read by some as the market assessing these assumptions differently.

Receivables and Cash Collection

With a high share of sales through overseas distributors and fast-growing receivables, collection speed was flagged as a variable for financial stability. Public procurement payments can be delayed, so operating cash flow may lag profit growth.

A large part of the offering proceeds was also allocated to working capital and debt repayment. Cash collection should be checked in subsequent periodic reports.

07

Risk factors

Supply Overhang

Shares available for trading right after listing were 41.03% of the post-offering total. Including lock-up expirations and exercisable stock options, the supply burden in the early listing period was assessed as significant. Concerns were raised that financial investors could realize gains. With trading value at about KRW 4.1 billion, large trades could have an outsized price impact.

Business

The company must sustain its current growth after the technology-track grace period to meet tightened listing maintenance requirements comfortably. Medical device certification and tender requirements differ by country, so entering new markets may take time.

The high medical share means heavy dependence on a single product family. The industrial and aerospace businesses have not yet been confirmed in results.

Macro

With a high overseas revenue share, exchange rate moves and the fiscal conditions of importing countries can affect results. If emerging-market governments change health budget priorities, procurement schedules could shift. Newly listed KOSDAQ stocks tend to react sharply to shifts in market risk appetite. Changes in sector-wide sentiment can also affect the share price.

08

Overall view

Remedi entered the Indian public procurement market with portable X-ray equipment and scaled its profits within a short period. Per reported filings, first-half 2026 operating profit was KRW 2.83 billion, up 423.0% year on year.

Because the scale differs from cumulative first-half figures in press reports, the reporting basis should be confirmed. Bulls point to its tender track record, product specifications and low market penetration.

Bears point to dependence on India, an IPO price set on estimated profit, rising receivables and supply overhang. Indicators to watch are repeat Indian tender wins, whether quarterly results fit the full-year outlook, receivable collection, and whether industrial and aerospace revenue materializes. The judgment is left to the reader, and this report is provided for information only.
