Non-China Polysilicon Hopes Meet a Still-Thin Profit Recovery

OCI Holdings has posted operating profit for four straight quarters since the fourth quarter of 2025, but it still recorded a net loss attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 19 billion in the third quarter of 2026, so the durability and breadth of the recovery remain the central question.

02

Business structure

OCI Holdings is a holding company whose core subsidiaries include OCI TerraSus, a polysilicon producer, OCI Energy, which develops and sells US solar projects, a chemicals subsidiary handling semiconductor materials and carbon black, and a cogeneration subsidiary.

Polysilicon, used as the raw material for solar wafers and cells, is the business that most strongly drives swings in consolidated profit.

The company said OCI TerraSus posted third-quarter 2026 revenue of KRW 1,622 hundred million and operating profit of KRW 208 hundred million, turning profitable on higher sales volumes.

The company's strategy is vertical integration from polysilicon production in Malaysia to wafers in Vietnam, aimed at securing a non-China (Non-PFE) supply chain that the US market requires. OCI Energy develops US solar projects and sells them, so revenue and profit are recognized in lumps when projects are sold.

The chemicals subsidiary lifted third-quarter 2026 revenue on higher sales of semiconductor materials and carbon black, according to the company. The cogeneration subsidiary improved on higher system marginal prices during summer power demand and higher REC and steam sales.

Growth in the urban development business was also reported as a contributor to third-quarter results. Under the holding structure, consolidated results move together with the cycle of core subsidiaries such as polysilicon and with changes in the value of subsidiary stakes.

Competition sits between Chinese polysilicon suppliers and domestic and foreign producers expanding non-China capacity.

04

Earnings analysis

On the disclosed annual figures, revenue was KRW 4.67 trillion in 2022, the largest year in the period, and KRW 2.65 trillion in 2023, the smallest, a 43.3% decline. Revenue rebounded to KRW 3.58 trillion in 2024, up 35.0%, and was KRW 3.38 trillion in 2025, down 5.5%.

Operating profit fell from KRW 976.7 billion in 2022 to KRW 531.2 billion in 2023, down 45.6%, and to KRW 101.5 billion in 2024, down 80.9%, before turning into an operating loss of KRW 57.6 billion in 2025. The operating margin slid from 20.9% in 2022 and 20.0% in 2023 to 2.8% in 2024 and -1.7% in 2025.

Profit attributable to controlling shareholders fell from KRW 880.3 billion in 2022 and KRW 713.5 billion in 2023 to KRW 97.7 billion in 2024, and then became a net loss of KRW 89.9 billion in 2025.

Net income including non-controlling interests was a loss of KRW 146.2 billion in 2025, a larger loss than the one attributable to controlling shareholders. The debt-to-equity ratio eased to 66.3% in 2025 from 67.4% in 2024, and operating cash flow rose to KRW 553.9 billion in 2025 from KRW 116.4 billion in 2024.

Quarterly, an operating loss of KRW 53.3 billion in the third quarter of 2025 gave way to operating profit of KRW 27.3 billion in the fourth quarter of 2025, which turned profitable.

First-quarter 2026 operating profit of KRW 10.9 billion, down 60.3% from the prior quarter, was reported to reflect scheduled maintenance and inventory valuation losses, which the company described as one-off factors.

Second-quarter 2026 operating profit rose to KRW 108.1 billion, with the recovery at OCI TerraSus and a project sale gain reflected in the same period. Third-quarter 2026 operating profit of KRW 68 billion fell 37.1% from the prior quarter but turned positive from a year earlier.

The third-quarter 2026 net loss attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 19 billion shows that a loss remained at that level even as operating profit was positive. Profit attributable to controlling shareholders over the last four quarters totaled KRW 54.8 billion.

05

Industry analysis

Polysilicon demand is being reshaped around non-China volumes under US policies that exclude Chinese supply and tighten supply-chain rules. Reporting in April 2026 said the company is accelerating a solar supply chain built around non-China production in response to policy uncertainty.

In the same period, capacity expansion talks were reported to have grown from 20,000 tons to more than 30,000 tons, which was read as non-China polysilicon being redefined from a commodity to a strategic material.

Possible restructuring among Chinese polysilicon producers has also been cited as a factor supporting non-China supply growth.

An analyst report from May 2026 said non-China polysilicon prices had fallen below their level in early April 2025, and the pace of any price recovery could depend on the outcome of US trade investigations. Because prices swing widely, profitability in this business is sensitive to the industry cycle.

Against peers, the vertically integrated structure running from polysilicon in Malaysia to wafers in Vietnam is presented as a point of differentiation. Pairing US solar project development with polysilicon is an uncommon combination in the listed solar value chain. Where the chain sits in its cycle depends on how prices recover and whether capacity expansion is confirmed.

Outlook

Securities analysts had treated the second quarter of 2026 as an earnings inflection point, and second-quarter operating profit was reported at KRW 108.1 billion.

Outlook commentary expected a sale gain on an OCI Energy power plant to be added in the fourth quarter. In a report in early November 2026, iM Securities estimated fourth-quarter 2026 consolidated operating profit at KRW 88.7 billion.

On capacity, talks were reported to expand non-China polysilicon output from 20,000 tons to more than 30,000 tons, but whether and when that is confirmed will be set by company disclosures.

Over the medium term, expansion of Malaysian production, vertical integration into Vietnamese wafers, and diversification into new demand such as space solar were presented as priorities.

Given that scheduled maintenance in the first quarter affected output and sales volumes, operating schedules are also a variable for quarterly results. Full-year 2026 results will be disclosed in the annual report due by the end of March 2027.

The fourth-quarter 2026 figures will reflect whether sale gains are recognized and how polysilicon sales volumes trend.

Bull factors

Non-China polysilicon redefined as a strategic material

According to reporting in April 2026 that cited a brokerage report, capacity expansion talks for non-China polysilicon grew from 20,000 tons to more than 30,000 tons. That was read as non-China polysilicon being redefined from a commodity to a strategic material.

An iM Securities report also argued that continued US exclusion of Chinese supply could let non-China suppliers capture early demand. The company is pursuing Malaysian capacity growth alongside vertical integration into Vietnamese wafers.

Four straight quarters of operating profit

Operating profit turned positive at KRW 27.3 billion in the fourth quarter of 2025, then stayed positive at KRW 10.9 billion, KRW 108.1 billion and KRW 68 billion in the first through third quarters of 2026.

Revenue rose for three consecutive quarters from the first quarter of 2026 to KRW 1.06 trillion in the third quarter. Third-quarter revenue was up 25.7% from a year earlier. Moving out of the loss period of 2025 is the starting point of the recovery.

Subsidiary turnaround alongside shareholder returns

The company said OCI TerraSus posted third-quarter 2026 revenue of KRW 162.2 billion and operating profit of KRW 20.8 billion. iM Securities cited higher sales volumes and the effect of higher selling prices as drivers of the turn to profit.

The company said it would complete a share cancellation of KRW 15 billion within October 2026. iM Securities presented a target price of KRW 300,000 in early November 2026.

Bear factors

Sharp first-quarter drop and a short profit history

First-quarter 2026 operating profit of KRW 10.9 billion fell 60.3% from the prior quarter. Reporting said it came in far below market consensus, with scheduled maintenance and inventory valuation losses cited as one-off factors.

Quarterly operating profit then moved from KRW 108.1 billion in the second quarter to KRW 68 billion in the third. With only four profitable quarters, there is little data yet on whether profit can persist.

Losses persist at the controlling-shareholder level

Net loss attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 89.9 billion in 2025, and a loss of KRW 19 billion remained in the third quarter of 2026. Operating profit in that quarter was KRW 68 billion.

A gap between operating profit and profit attributable to controlling shareholders means the breadth of the recovery is still limited. Net income including non-controlling interests was a loss of KRW 146.2 billion in 2025, larger than the controlling-shareholder figure.

High multiple against earnings and policy dependence

Profit attributable to controlling shareholders over the last four quarters totaled KRW 54.8 billion, so the market capitalization is high relative to earnings. Results depend heavily on polysilicon prices and the outcome of US policy.

Until capacity plans turn into sales volumes, the basis for an earnings-based valuation is limited. Even with a low multiple on book value, the earnings-based burden should be assessed separately.

Risk factors

Policy and trade risk

Demand and prices for non-China polysilicon could shift with US exclusion of Chinese supply and the outcome of trade investigations related to solar. The company is building a non-China-centered supply chain in response to policy uncertainty, so policy changes affect the pace of returns on investment.

If oversupply from China persists, price recovery could be limited. Policy outcomes affect the business broadly.

Price and utilization volatility

Quarterly profit in polysilicon swings sharply with selling prices and sales volumes. The first quarter of 2026, when scheduled maintenance and inventory valuation losses cut operating profit to KRW 10.9 billion, shows this. The operating loss of KRW 57.6 billion for full-year 2025 came from the same structure.

Industry conditions at other subsidiaries, such as chemicals and cogeneration, also flow into the holding company's profit.

Investment and holding-structure risk

Expanding wafer and project capacity requires large investment and could strain cash flow. In a holding structure, subsidiary results and stake values drive swings in consolidated profit. Gains from selling subsidiaries or projects are largely one-off, so they shape the path of quarterly profit.

Total equity of KRW 4.73 trillion and total liabilities of KRW 3.14 trillion at the end of 2025 are the reference points for investment capacity.

Overall view

OCI Holdings has posted operating profit since the fourth quarter of 2025 on the recovery at its polysilicon subsidiary, and third-quarter 2026 operating profit was KRW 68 billion.

Still, 2025 included an operating loss of KRW 57.6 billion and a net loss attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 89.9 billion, and a net loss of KRW 19 billion attributable to controlling shareholders remained in the third quarter of 2026.

Strengths include the shift of non-China polysilicon into a strategic material, US supply-chain expansion and capacity talks, the subsidiary turnaround, and shareholder returns through share cancellations.

Weaknesses include earnings volatility tied to polysilicon prices and policy, large swings in quarterly operating profit, with KRW 10.9 billion in the first quarter of 2026 and KRW 108.1 billion in the second, and a high market capitalization relative to earnings.

On an asset basis, market capitalization sits below equity attributable to controlling shareholders, while on the last four quarters of profit the multiple is high, so the two measures point in different directions. In early November 2026, iM Securities presented a target price of KRW 300,000.

The next checkpoints are the fourth-quarter 2026 results, whether the OCI Energy sale gain is recognized, and full-year profit attributable to controlling shareholders confirmed in the 2026 annual report.
