02

Business structure

BTB Korea traces its roots to Dongin Biotech, founded in 1999, and reached its current structure after absorbing Dongin Biotech in March 2024. The company operates a Medical Device Business Division and a Bio Business Division, combining aesthetic and lifting devices with distribution of research reagents and kits.

Its device line centers on RF and ultrasound-based solutions sold mainly to clinics and hospitals. The company pairs this B2B model with B2C home-care devices as a two-track strategy. Three new products were first unveiled at KIMES 2026 in March 2026.

Its bio unit distributes research reagents, diagnostic kits and lab equipment and has developed the AI-based research platform BTBMarket.kr. The largest shareholder, Hankook BNC together with one other party, holds 81.02%, leaving a low free-float ratio of 18.95%.

Competition overlaps with domestic and overseas aesthetic device makers and research-reagent distributors. The sector classification is electrical equipment, but its customers and product structure differ sharply from listed peers such as LS Electric.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual sales fell from KRW 5.67 billion in 2022 to KRW 2.7 billion in 2023, a 52.4% decline, then rose to KRW 30.1 billion in 2024 (up 1,016.8%) and KRW 32 billion in 2025 (up 6.3%). Sales increased in 2024 and 2025, and 2025 was the largest year of 2022 to 2025.

The 2024 jump coincides with the March 2024 absorption of Dongin Biotech, so the figure likely includes merger effects.

Operating results moved from an operating loss of KRW 7.61 billion in 2022 and KRW 7.33 billion in 2023 to an operating profit of KRW 988 million in 2024, then back to an operating loss of KRW 2.85 billion in 2025. Operating margin went from -134.2% in 2022 and -271.6% in 2023 to 3.3% in 2024 and -8.9% in 2025.

The company attributed the 2025 operating loss to higher costs from the weaker won, development expenses and higher selling and administrative expenses.

Net loss attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 12.8 billion in 2022, KRW 9.23 billion in 2023, KRW 161 million in 2024 and KRW 3.93 billion in 2025, so the company posted losses in each of these four years.

The 2025 consolidated net loss included a higher income tax expense from reviewing deferred tax assets, which the company described as a non-cash charge. Operating cash flow was KRW 1.37 billion positive in 2022, then negative in each year from 2023 to 2025, reaching minus KRW 5.97 billion in 2025.

Quarterly sales went from KRW 7.7 billion in 2025 Q3 to KRW 7 billion in Q4 (down 9.0% quarter on quarter), bottomed at KRW 5.91 billion in 2026 Q1 (down 15.6%), then rose to KRW 6.31 billion in Q2 and KRW 8.47 billion in Q3.

Operating losses widened from KRW 1.05 billion in 2025 Q3 to KRW 1.73 billion in 2026 Q1, then narrowed to KRW 1.52 billion in Q2 and KRW 781 million in Q3. The controlling-shareholder net loss peaked at KRW 2.86 billion in 2026 Q2 and was KRW 869 million in Q3, with KRW 8 billion of losses over the last four quarters.

Whether the Q3 margin improvement came from gross margin or from cost control will become clearer in the 2026 annual report's cost structure. The debt ratio fell from 9,076.0% in 2023 to 51.0% in 2024 and 82.1% in 2025, with equity of KRW 24.3 billion and total liabilities of KRW 19.9 billion at year-end 2025.

05

Industry analysis

BTB Korea is classified under electrical equipment, but its actual business is aesthetic and medical devices plus research-reagent distribution, so its end demand and regulatory framework differ from listed electrical equipment companies such as LS Electric.

On demand, a company-related analysis noted that a surge in skin-care treatments at dermatology and plastic surgery clinics has increased demand for skin-care devices. The same commentary cited government bio-industry policy and expanded R&D as growth drivers for the bio business.

On regulation, new medical devices need approval before domestic sales, and revenue follows only after production and a clinic sales network are in place.

Competition in domestic aesthetic devices involves many domestic and overseas players, and the company is pursuing domestic and overseas approvals in parallel to enter overseas markets. The bio distribution unit depends on reagent demand from research institutions.

Media coverage expected the AI-based research platform to drive digital transformation of research markets at home and abroad. Strategically, the company combines hospital-focused B2B sales with home-use B2C devices to widen its customer base.

A direct comparison with electrical equipment peers is limited by the business gap, so the company's own earnings trend matters more than sector averages.

Outlook

At KIMES 2026 in March 2026, the company unveiled three new products and is pursuing domestic and overseas approvals in parallel, targeting staggered launches from late Q2 through Q3 2026.

An October 2026 report said the company is pursuing approval for 1726nm and 2910nm fractional lasers and the HIFU platform HEXA, with the two laser devices targeted for domestic approval in the second half of 2026.

The Q3 2026 launch window has already closed, so actual launches and their revenue contribution must be verified in the 2026 annual report. Medical device revenue requires production and a sales network after approval, so there is a lag between approval and sales.

The company decided on a share consolidation in April 2026 that changed the par value from KRW 100 to KRW 500, citing share price stability and corporate value. Trading resumed on June 25, 2026 after the share consolidation.

The company profile also cites a contract with a Thai partner to accelerate Southeast Asian expansion. Whether the AI research platform gains traction with researchers will show up in the revenue mix of the 2026 annual report.

With sales up in Q2 and Q3 2026, cost of sales and selling expenses will be the key variables for changing the loss structure.

New product pipeline

The company unveiled three new products at KIMES 2026 and is pursuing domestic approval for two laser devices with a target in the second half of 2026. Its lineup also spans a focused ultrasound platform and a B2C home-care device. Approval would open a path to commercializing the new products.

Bear factors

Operating cash flow turned from KRW 1.37 billion inflow in 2022 to outflows of KRW 2.24 billion in 2023, KRW 522 million in 2024 and KRW 5.97 billion in 2025. The 2025 outflow widened sharply from 2024. Without a recovery in cash generation, financial flexibility could remain limited.

Cost pressure

Continued product development and hiring could keep cost pressure elevated. The KRW 1.73 billion operating loss in 2026 Q1 was the largest of the last five quarters.

Risk factors

Persistent losses and cash burn

Prolonged operating losses and cash outflows could raise the need for external funding. The 2025 operating cash outflow widened to KRW 5.97 billion. If losses persist, financial flexibility on KRW 24.3 billion of equity could narrow.

Approval and launch delays
