02

Business structure

RS Automation is a specialist in robot motion and energy control built on industrial motion-control technology. The business has two segments. The robot-motion segment, which covers PLCs, servo drives and motors, generated about KRW 33.0 billion, or 63.82%, of sales in January to September 2026.

The energy-control segment accounted for about KRW 18.7 billion, or 36.18%, over the same period. Servo-drive sales, the core robot-motion product, rose 124% year on year in Q1 2026.

Robot-motion gross margin reached 27.8% in H1 2026, up 4.4 percentage points from 23.4% a year earlier, and the company says it is shifting its portfolio toward these higher-margin core businesses. Its main export customer is Rockwell Automation, and the company says that customer's share remains solid.

The energy-control segment extends real-time control technology from robot motion platforms into energy storage systems (ESS) and fuel cells.

The company signed a strategic MOU with the global power and energy firm Schneider Electric in 2024 and is also co-developing and supplying with large domestic automotive, heavy-industry and energy groups.

Its product mix differs from heavy-electrical listed peers focused on transformers and distribution equipment, and the company argues that high-speed, precision motion control is its differentiator.

04

Earnings analysis

On the disclosed annual figures, revenue fell from KRW 102.4 billion in 2022 to KRW 81.3 billion in 2023 (down 20.7% year on year), KRW 76.8 billion in 2024 (down 5.5%) and KRW 67 billion in 2025 (down 12.7%), a decline in each of the three years from 2023 to 2025.

Of the four years, 2022 was the largest and 2025 the smallest. Operating profit of KRW 1.92 billion in 2022 turned into an operating loss of KRW 2.98 billion in 2023, the loss widened to KRW 3.71 billion in 2024, and narrowed to KRW 3.56 billion in 2025.

The operating margin moved from 1.9% in 2022 to -3.7%, -4.8% and -5.3% in 2023, 2024 and 2025. Attributable net income of KRW 868 million in 2022 became an attributable net loss of KRW 5.7 billion in 2023, KRW 9.08 billion in 2024 and KRW 3.99 billion in 2025.

Quarterly revenue went from KRW 17.4 billion in Q3 2025 to KRW 19.8 billion in Q4 2025 (up 14.1% quarter on quarter), then KRW 16.6 billion in Q1 2026 (down 16.2%), KRW 18.3 billion in Q2 2026 (up 10.3%) and KRW 16.8 billion in Q3 2026 (down 8.2% quarter on quarter and down 3.2% year on year).

The operating loss widened from KRW 426 million in Q3 2025 to KRW 1.02 billion in Q3 2026, after reaching KRW 1.24 billion in Q4 2025, KRW 1.12 billion in Q1 2026 and KRW 1.45 billion in Q2 2026.

The company attributed the Q1 loss to pre-emptive spending on headcount, IT, security and new equipment after its capital increase, and said about KRW 600 million of one-off consulting costs hit H1.

Cumulative January to September 2026 revenue was about KRW 51.7 billion, up 9.5% year on year, while the cumulative operating loss of about KRW 3.59 billion was wider than the KRW 2.32 billion a year earlier. Operating cash flow swung from an outflow of KRW 1.92 billion in 2024 to an inflow of KRW 1.98 billion in 2025.

05

Industry analysis

The company credits the growth in robot motion to a recovery in the semiconductor cycle, expanding domestic automation investment and rising exports, as stated at its Q1 2026 results announcement on May 15, 2026.

Motion-control parts such as servo drives and PLCs are highly sensitive to factory-automation capital spending cycles. The company says robot-motion and energy-control growth could continue if global supply-chain problems normalize.

On the energy side it is trying to ride demand for distributed energy assets such as ESS and fuel cells, where its control technology can apply. Many listed electrical-equipment peers, including LS ELECTRIC, Hyosung Heavy Industries and HD Hyundai Electric, focus on heavy electrical equipment.

RS Automation is a smaller specialist centered on motion control and servo technology, so its exposure differs from those groups. Its front-end demand therefore tracks semiconductor and automation capex on the robot side and the pace of new customer wins on the energy side.

The company also mentioned expanding into new areas such as defense. On balance, the strengths are an automation capex recovery and energy-asset expansion, while the weaknesses are capex cyclicality and supply-chain uncertainty.

06

Outlook

At its H1 2026 results announcement on August 14, 2026, the CEO said he expects profit improvement to take hold in the second half as robot-motion's high growth and investment effects combine.

The company said about KRW 1.78 billion of foundation investment in H1, covering a manufacturing execution system, an ERP upgrade, ISO 27001 certification and SMD process-equipment expansion, should lower costs from the second half. In May 2026 the company said order growth in robot motion had continued after Q1.

It also said energy-control sales have entered a growth path on the back of new customer wins. In February 2026 it said it would expand its real-time control technology into energy-control products such as ESS and fuel cells.

The company also cited plans to expand into new businesses including defense, accelerate new product launches and widen strategic alliances. However, the Q3 2026 operating loss was larger than a year earlier, so the timing of the second-half profit improvement has not yet appeared in the disclosed results.

The most direct checkpoint is whether the cost-reduction effect shows up in gross margin and operating loss in the next periodic filings.

08

Bull factors

High growth and better margins in robot motion

Robot-motion sales rose 78% year on year in H1 2026 and lifted their share of total sales to 34.3%. Over the same period the segment's gross margin rose to 27.8% from 23.4%. The company attributes this to a portfolio shift toward higher-margin core businesses.

A widening base in energy control

The company is extending its robot-motion platform technology into energy-control products such as ESS and fuel cells, and signed a strategic MOU with Schneider Electric in 2024. The company said H1 2026 energy-control sales rose more than 10% year on year. It also said new customer wins have put the segment on a growth path.

Balance-sheet repair and a cash-flow turn

The debt-to-equity ratio fell from 135.2% in 2024 to 57.2% in 2025. Operating cash flow moved from an outflow of KRW 1.92 billion in 2024 to an inflow of KRW 1.98 billion in 2025. The company said net income improved 32.1% year on year in Q1 2026 on debt repayment and lower financing costs.

Bear factors

Five straight quarters of operating losses, with Q3 wider

Operating losses have run for five consecutive quarters from Q3 2025 through Q3 2026. The Q3 2026 operating loss of KRW 1.02 billion was larger than KRW 426 million a year earlier, and the attributable net loss of KRW 1.3 billion widened from KRW 506 million. The cumulative January to September operating loss also grew from the prior year.

Revenue recovery has yet to show continuity

Annual revenue declined for three straight years from 2023 to 2025. Cumulative January to September 2026 revenue rose 9.5%, but Q3 2026 revenue of KRW 16.8 billion fell 3.2% year on year. Quarterly revenue has moved up and down, at KRW 16.6 billion in Q1, KRW 18.3 billion in Q2 and KRW 16.8 billion in Q3.

Investment costs and an unrealized turnaround

After its capital increase at the end of 2025 the company raised headcount, IT and equipment spending, and H1 2026 absorbed about KRW 600 million of one-off consulting costs. The second-half cost savings are the company's own expectation, and the disclosed Q3 2026 results do not yet show a profit improvement. If investment costs keep rising ahead of revenue recovery, the narrowing of losses could be delayed.

10

Risk factors

Capex and industry-cycle exposure

The company cites the semiconductor recovery and rising domestic automation investment as drivers of robot-motion growth. Demand for servo and motor products can swing sharply with capex cycles. A slowdown in semiconductors or automation investment could slow robot-motion growth.

Customer concentration

The company identifies Rockwell Automation as its main export customer. When sales depend heavily on one overseas customer, changes in that customer's orders feed directly into results.

The company is adding new energy-control customers, but how quickly these translate into meaningful sales has to be confirmed in quarterly results.

Cumulative losses and financial strain

At the end of 2025 total equity was KRW 58.3 billion and total liabilities were KRW 33.3 billion. If losses continue, the capital cushion could shrink and further funding or a slower investment pace may become necessary.

12

Overall view

RS Automation is a specialist built around robot motion and energy control. Its H1 2026 robot-motion sales rose 78% and gross margin improved, which are signs of growth.

On the disclosed figures, however, operating losses have run for three straight years from 2023 to 2025, and the losses have continued for five consecutive quarters through Q3 2026. In Q3 2026 revenue fell year on year while the operating loss widened.

Strengths include robot-motion growth, energy-business expansion, and improvements in leverage and operating cash flow. Weaknesses include persistent operating losses, dependence on the capex cycle and customer concentration.

The company expects second-half cost savings, but these have not yet appeared in disclosed results. The next checkpoints are the operating margin in the 2026 annual report and the gross margin in Q1 2027.
