02

Business structure

Nextchip is a fabless company that designs image-processing semiconductors for automotive cameras. It was spun off in 2019 from the automotive division of NCN and listed on KOSDAQ in July 2022.

Its core product is the ISP chip, which converts signals from image sensors into corrected video and functional outputs, and the company is promoted for relatively short tuning periods for each vehicle model.

The second line is AHD, its proprietary analog technology for transmitting high-resolution video over long distances, with a reported supply case to Japanese commercial vehicles.

The third line is the ADAS SoC series named APACHE, which processes ISP and AHD data for recognition; press reports say supply for Level 3 and higher autonomous driving chips is ramping.

Customers include camera system makers and automakers in Korea and abroad, and a 2023 brokerage report described plans to apply its ISP across 56 Hyundai Motor and KIA models.

The company has also widened cooperation with image-sensor makers, working with onsemi on automotive camera ISPs and developing in-cabin camera solutions with STMicroelectronics.

A KRW 7.1 billion ISP intellectual property development contract, disclosed in December 2025, was signed with a large domestic IT and semiconductor company and runs through the end of 2028. The company is extending ISP applications from in-vehicle cameras to robots and drones.

In competition, overseas player indie Semiconductor is cited, while several domestic developers are reported to be unlisted firms.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual revenue rose from KRW 12.9 billion in 2022 to KRW 16.2 billion in 2023 (up 25.5% year on year), KRW 32.3 billion in 2024 (up 99.7%), and KRW 39.7 billion in 2025 (up 22.9%). Revenue grew for three consecutive years from 2023 through 2025, and 2025 was the largest year of the 2022–2025 period.

Over the same span, operating losses narrowed from KRW 27.4 billion in 2022 to KRW 22.5 billion in 2023, KRW 18.4 billion in 2024, and KRW 12.9 billion in 2025, and the company has recorded operating losses for four consecutive years.

The operating margin improved from -212.9% in 2022 to -139.4% in 2023, -57.2% in 2024, and -32.6% in 2025. The sharpest narrowing came in 2024, when revenue nearly doubled, and losses also shrank in 2025 though the company stayed in the red.

Parent-shareholder net losses were KRW 27.4 billion in 2022, KRW 26.7 billion in 2023, KRW 20.7 billion in 2024, and KRW 16 billion in 2025. In 2025 the net loss exceeded the operating loss of KRW 12.9 billion, which indicates that non-operating items also weighed on earnings.

The debt ratio was 12.3% in 2022, 138.3% in 2023, and 530.3% in 2024 before falling to 221.0% in 2025, when total equity was KRW 18.2 billion and total liabilities were KRW 40.1 billion.

Press reports said the first convertible bond tranche was fully repaid in late 2025 using proceeds of a shareholder-allotted rights offering of KRW 18.1 billion plus company cash.

Operating cash flow was negative in all four years, at KRW -31.6 billion in 2022, KRW -9.39 billion in 2023, KRW -10.7 billion in 2024, and KRW -12.2 billion in 2025.

Quarterly revenue rose from KRW 9.97 billion in the third quarter of 2025 to KRW 11.6 billion, KRW 11.7 billion, KRW 13.2 billion, and KRW 13.3 billion in the third quarter of 2026, marking four consecutive quarters of growth.

Operating loss shrank from KRW 5.44 billion in the third quarter of 2025 to KRW 42.96 million in the fourth quarter, widened to KRW 2.43 billion in the first quarter of 2026, and then narrowed to KRW 590 million in the second quarter and KRW 790 million in the third quarter.

Parent-shareholder net income moved from a KRW 6.71 billion loss in the third quarter of 2025 to KRW 1.25 billion of profit in the fourth quarter, which was a profit turn even though operating loss remained, and then to a KRW 3.68 billion loss in the first quarter of 2026, KRW 711 million in the second quarter and KRW 665 million in the third quarter.

Parent net losses for the last four quarters total KRW 3.81 billion. A WISEreport compilation put first-half 2026 separate-basis revenue up 37.8% year on year and operating loss down 59.5%, which it linked to demand for high-resolution cameras and wider ADAS mandates.

In a September 2024 interview, the CEO targeted a quarterly operating profit turn in the second half of 2025, and the fourth quarter of 2025 still recorded an operating loss.

05

Industry analysis

In a 2024 report, Meritz Securities projected that the number of automotive cameras would grow from 218 million units in 2023 to 568 million units in 2030, a compound annual growth rate of 12.7%, and said the ISP chip market could grow at least as fast on demand alone. The number of cameras per vehicle is also rising.

In a November 2023 report, Samsung Securities projected that cameras per car would increase from 5 in 2017 to 10 in 2023 and up to 24 by 2026. The camera's role is also shifting from viewing for parking to sensing for recognition, which is cited as a driver of ISP demand.

On regulation, a 2024 report said ADAS fitment mandates for new cars in Europe would phase in starting July 2024. A 2024 press report said Nextchip shows the highest share of the ADAS market, though no specific share figure was given.

In competition, overseas player indie Semiconductor is cited, and a 2023 analysis noted that many domestic developers are unlisted. Reported tuning times differ across sources, ranging from a few days to three months, so direct comparisons of competitiveness need caution.

In robotics, the CEO said in September 2024 that autonomous robot technology and automotive ADAS technology are about 80% the same.

Other listed companies in the semiconductor sector have different business structures, such as memory, equipment, or packaging, which makes direct revenue and profitability comparisons difficult.

06

Outlook

In a September 2024 press report, the company said it was pursuing the automotive and robotics markets with APACHE 6, its next-generation ADAS application processor, and that customer acquisition in automotive had progressed. In the same report, the CEO expected ISP and AHD revenue to remain solid in 2024 and 2025.

A December 2024 Samsung Securities report projected that autonomous-driving SoC revenue would begin in 2026 and turn the company profitable.

In an April 2025 report, Hyundai Motor Securities expected operating profit to turn positive from 2026 on robotics revenue growth and gave a 2026 revenue estimate of KRW 76 billion. Both forecasts have not materialized, since operating losses continued through the third quarter of 2026.

The KRW 7.1 billion ISP IP contract disclosed in December 2025 runs through the end of 2028. Whether and when a volume supply contract for APACHE 6 is disclosed will be visible in future filings and quarterly results.

Whether revenue growth and narrower operating losses seen in the first half of 2026 continue after the fourth quarter is the next point to check. The pace at which robotics and ADAS revenue scale will determine how quickly operating losses narrow.

The balance sheet has improved through the rights offering and bond repayment, but development spending remains heavy, so the scale of R&D outlays from 2027 onward should be checked in the annual report notes.

Bull factors

Revenue growth and narrower losses

Revenue rose from KRW 12.9 billion in 2022 to KRW 39.7 billion in 2025, with growth for three consecutive years from 2023. Operating losses shrank from KRW 27.4 billion in 2022 to KRW 12.9 billion in 2025, and the third-quarter 2026 operating loss of KRW 790 million compares with KRW 5.44 billion a year earlier. A WISEreport compilation shows separate-basis revenue in the first half of 2026 up 37.8% year on year.

Profit signal in the fourth quarter of 2025

Parent-shareholder net income turned positive at KRW 1.25 billion in the fourth quarter of 2025. That quarter's operating loss of KRW 42.96 million was far smaller than the KRW 5.44 billion loss in the prior quarter. The third-quarter 2026 parent net loss of KRW 665 million was also smaller than a year earlier.

Technology contracts and wider applications

The KRW 7.1 billion ISP development contract disclosed in December 2025 is a long-term deal running through the end of 2028. Expansion of ISP use into robots and drones is cited as a possible revenue source beyond automotive. The CEO said autonomous robot and automotive ADAS technologies are about 80% the same.

Bear factors

Operating losses persist

Operating losses have been recorded for four straight years from 2022 through 2025, and every quarter from the first quarter of 2026 through the third quarter was also in the red. Parent net income was positive in the fourth quarter of 2025, but the operating line still showed a KRW 42.96 million loss.

The quarterly operating profit target for the second half of 2025, stated in a September 2024 report, has not been met at the operating level.

Capital and debt burden

The debt ratio was 530.3% at the end of 2024 and remained high at 221.0% at the end of 2025. Total liabilities of KRW 40.1 billion at the end of 2025 exceed total equity of KRW 18.2 billion. Operating cash flow was negative in every year from 2022 to 2025, reaching KRW -12.2 billion in 2025.

Revenue target and development costs

In a September 2024 report, the CEO said revenue in 2024 would more than double from the prior year, but actual 2024 revenue grew 99.7%. The same report said developing next-generation chips alone costs more than KRW 100 billion. Revenue growth slowed to 22.9% in 2025 from 99.7% in 2024.

10

Risk factors

Delayed application expansion

The company is extending ISP use from automotive cameras into robots and drones, and the CEO said autonomous robot and automotive ADAS technologies are about 80% the same. However, customer adoption and volume supply contracts in robotics and drones have not yet been confirmed in disclosures.

If these applications are slow to convert into revenue, the company may remain dependent on existing ADAS and ISP sales, and the pace of operating-loss narrowing could be limited.

Long automotive qualification cycles

A December 2024 Samsung Securities report explained that automotive chips take at least three years for supplier selection and qualification, followed by about one more year of tuning and testing.

Revenue builds over five to seven years after mass production begins, so the gap between an award announcement and actual sales is long. If the volume transition for new products such as APACHE 6 slips, the timing of the earnings recovery could also slip.

Integration of ISP into chips

A 2024 Samsung Securities report expected ISP functions to move from standalone camera modules into chips over time. The company was described as developing technology to prepare for that shift. Whether SoC revenue can offset any decline in ISP sales will be the key test.

Overall view

From 2022 through 2025, Nextchip's revenue rose from KRW 12.9 billion to KRW 39.7 billion and its operating loss narrowed from KRW 27.4 billion to KRW 12.9 billion, but the company has not yet exited a four-year run of operating losses.

At the end of 2025, equity recovered to KRW 18.2 billion after a shareholder-allotted rights offering, and the debt ratio fell to 221.0%. In the fourth quarter of 2025, parent-shareholder net income turned positive, yet that quarter's operating loss was KRW 42.96 million.

Parent net losses continued from the first through third quarters of 2026, and the parent net loss over the last four quarters totals KRW 3.81 billion. Bullish factors include rising camera content per vehicle, ADAS mandates, the KRW 7.1 billion ISP development contract, and continued revenue growth.

Bearish factors include persistent operating losses, long automotive qualification cycles, and development and funding burdens. The next checkpoints are the 2026 annual report, due by the end of March 2027, and the first-quarter 2027 quarterly report, due in mid-May 2027.
