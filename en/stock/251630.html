Battery Orders Expand as Losses Narrow, Still Under Test

V-One Tech is widening its business from display inspection equipment into battery, semiconductor and automation work. It posted an operating profit in the third quarter of 2026, but net losses attributable to controlling shareholders over the last four quarters still total KRW 8.57 billion.

02

Business structure

V-One Tech was founded in 2006 and listed on KOSDAQ in 2017. It makes inspection equipment based on machine vision and image processing.

Its roots are indentation inspection equipment that checks whether ICs and FPCs are properly attached to LCD and OLED panels, and this remains a key revenue source in display manufacturing.

Battery inspection systems and autonomous mobile robots (AMR and AGV) that move materials inside factories without staff make up the other two pillars.

The company now pairs its machine vision technology with its in-house industrial AI platform, 'with AI', to extend inspection solutions into semiconductors, automotive and logistics. Its main customers are reported to include major domestic display panel makers, LG Energy Solution and Hyundai Motor Group.

Its company profile claims a 64% global share in display indentation inspection equipment, a figure the company itself asserts. Competition comes from domestic machine vision inspection equipment makers, and its battery leak test competes as a substitute for the conventional helium leak test.

The company is moving away from a structure centered on indentation inspection toward whole display module line orders, industrial AI solutions and semiconductor materials inspection equipment.

In June 2026 it said it was co-developing a SiC ring appearance inspection system with the materials maker TCK, with customer testing planned for the third quarter. SiC rings are consumable parts inside etching equipment, where surface defects directly affect process yield.

In November 2026 its robotics subsidiary announced a semiconductor process automation contract worth about KRW 3.4 billion from Company A, a semiconductor materials and components maker. This order extends the robotics business from material transfer into production equipment automation.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual revenue rose from KRW 59.8 billion in 2022 to KRW 81.8 billion in 2023, up 36.8% year on year, and that was the peak. It then fell 11.1% to KRW 72.7 billion in 2024 and 32.6% to KRW 49 billion in 2025, two consecutive annual declines from 2024 through 2025.

Among 2022 to 2025, 2023 was the largest year and 2025 the smallest. Operating profit went from KRW 38.01 million in 2022 to KRW 1.98 billion in 2023, a 5,100.6% increase, then turned into an operating loss of KRW 4.36 billion in 2024 and widened to an operating loss of KRW 12.8 billion in 2025.

The operating margin moved from 0.1% in 2022 to 2.4% in 2023, then -6.0% in 2024 and -26.2% in 2025.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders swung from a loss of KRW 656 million in 2022 to a profit of KRW 2.96 billion in 2023, then turned to losses of KRW 3.21 billion in 2024 and KRW 9.39 billion in 2025, two consecutive years of losses.

Net loss including non-controlling interests was KRW 14.9 billion in 2025, and the gap from the controlling-shareholder figure reflects profit and loss attributable to non-controlling interests. Operating cash flow fell from KRW 6.12 billion in 2023 to KRW 1.19 billion in 2024 and was negative KRW 11.6 billion in 2025.

The debt-to-equity ratio rose from 80.4% in 2022 and 53.4% in 2023 and 41.8% in 2024 to 62.4% in 2025. At the end of 2025, total equity was KRW 126.5 billion, of which controlling-shareholder equity was KRW 106.8 billion, and total liabilities were KRW 79 billion.

Quarterly revenue went from KRW 10.4 billion in Q3 2025 to KRW 20 billion in Q4 2025, up 92.4% from the prior quarter, then stood at KRW 18.9 billion in Q1 2026, KRW 13 billion in Q2 2026 and KRW 16.8 billion in Q3 2026.

Quarterly operating results were a loss of KRW 1.62 billion in Q3 2025, KRW 3.97 billion in Q4 2025 and KRW 4.81 billion in Q1 2026, narrowed to KRW 2.39 billion in Q2 2026, and turned to an operating profit of KRW 678 million in Q3 2026.

Net losses attributable to controlling shareholders ran for five consecutive quarters from Q3 2025 through Q3 2026, and the total for the last four quarters was KRW 8.57 billion. Press reports attributed the Q1 2026 revenue increase to the reflection of display module line orders.

05

Industry analysis

In battery inspection, the continuing stream of orders for secondary battery equipment makers is read by the industry as a signal of a market recovery.

The conventional helium leak test consumes helium gas continuously during operation, while the company estimates internally that its machine vision and AI approach without helium cuts operating costs by about 50%. That positions it as a substitute competing against helium-based test equipment.

In displays, indentation inspection still generates large sales, and a securities information provider's company commentary describes the company as leading the market in new segments such as flexible OLED.

In semiconductors, the SiC ring inspection system is the first example of extending into semiconductor materials inspection, replacing visual checks with automated machine vision and AI inspection.

SiC rings are core consumables inside etching equipment, so the accuracy of surface defect inspection directly affects yield. In robotics, AMR, AGV and machine tending robots sit in the move toward automation of production equipment, and the semiconductor process automation contract shows this direction.

Competition splits into two groups: domestic small and mid-sized machine vision inspection makers and robotics automation companies. The 64% share cited in the company profile is the company's own claim and should be read that way when judging market size.

06

Outlook

The company aims to complete on-site installation of the two mass-production battery leak inspection units in North America within 2026. Based on verification results, it plans to extend the application to follow-on lines and other production sites of the same customer.

It also said the battery leak test technology could extend to automotive and electrical component parts that need airtightness inspection.

For the SiC ring inspection system, the company plans customer testing after completing equipment production in the third quarter, and an official said additional orders and capacity expansion are under review depending on test results.

The robotics subsidiary's KRW 3.4 billion contract extends the robotics business into production equipment automation, and the company plans to apply machine vision image recognition to improve positioning accuracy.

The company's strategy is to extend its industrial AI platform, 'with AI', across display, secondary battery, semiconductor and logistics applications. No quantitative company guidance was found in the sources, so revenue and profit outlook here is limited to orders and plans that have been confirmed.

The next check point is whether the 2026 annual report shows how much of the order effect reached annual revenue and operating margin. North American installation and SiC ring test results will be confirmed through company disclosures and press reports as they occur.

Whether follow-on battery lines are added depends on the results of North American mass-production verification.

08

Bull factors

Securing a Battery Inspection Reference

On October 7, 2026, the company announced two mass-production leak inspection units won from Company A, a global battery maker, for a North American production site. It plans to use this order as a reference to repeat the same inspection system across multiple production lines.

The company also presented an internal estimate that its helium-free method could cut operating costs by about 50%.

Expanding into Semiconductors and Automation

The SiC ring appearance inspection system automates surface defect inspection of consumable parts used in etching equipment and was co-developed with TCK, which supplies SiC rings to Samsung Electronics and SK hynix.

The robotics subsidiary won a semiconductor process automation system worth about KRW 3.4 billion, extending its robotics business into production equipment automation. The company is also reviewing additional orders and capacity expansion depending on customer test results.

Narrowing Losses and a Q3 Operating Profit Turn

Operating losses narrowed from KRW 4.81 billion in Q1 2026 to KRW 2.39 billion in Q2 2026, and Q3 2026 turned into an operating profit of KRW 678 million. Following KRW 20 billion in revenue in Q4 2025, Q3 2026 revenue held at KRW 16.8 billion. The narrowing of operating losses is the most direct improvement signal in the disclosed quarterly results.

09

Bear factors

A Continuing Streak of Controlling-Shareholder Losses

On disclosed results, the company has reported controlling-shareholder net losses for five consecutive quarters from Q3 2025 through Q3 2026. The four-quarter total was KRW 8.57 billion, and Q3 2026 still recorded a controlling-shareholder net loss of KRW 1.42 billion.

No quarter in the disclosed results yet shows the operating profit turning into a controlling-shareholder net profit.

Revenue Decline and Reliance on Display

Annual revenue fell for two consecutive years after peaking at KRW 81.8 billion in 2023, to KRW 49 billion in 2025. Display equipment remains a major revenue source, so results may swing with capital spending cycles at panel makers.

The battery order disclosed so far covers two units for North America, so its contribution to annual revenue is still limited.

Signs of Weaker Financial Cushion

The debt-to-equity ratio rose from 41.8% in 2024 to 62.4% in 2025, and operating cash flow was a deficit of KRW 11.6 billion in 2025. Total equity at the end of 2025 was KRW 126.5 billion, of which controlling-shareholder equity was KRW 106.8 billion. How accumulated losses affect this equity base is the next point to watch.

10

Risk factors

Customer and Order Concentration

The battery order confirmed so far covers two units at one North American site of Company A. If verification does not lead to follow-on line expansion, the growth assumption for the battery segment weakens.

The cost savings from replacing helium are an internal company estimate and could vary with the pace of customer adoption.

Cash Flow and Financial Burden

Operating cash flow turned to a deficit of KRW 11.6 billion in 2025, and the debt-to-equity ratio rose to 62.4%. Of total equity of KRW 126.5 billion at the end of 2025, KRW 106.8 billion belongs to controlling shareholders. If losses continue, funding working capital could become more burdensome.

Execution Risk in New Businesses

The semiconductor SiC ring inspection system is in customer testing, and the robotics subsidiary's automation contract shifts its business from logistics robots toward production equipment automation. Semiconductor materials and production automation serve different customers than the existing display business. The timing of revenue from these new businesses remains uncertain until confirmed through company disclosures.

Overall view

V-One Tech is extending from indentation inspection equipment for displays into battery leak inspection, semiconductor materials inspection and automation robots.

Annual revenue peaked at KRW 81.8 billion in 2023 and fell for two years to KRW 49 billion in 2025, while the operating loss widened to KRW 12.8 billion in 2025.

In Q3 2026 revenue was KRW 16.8 billion and operating profit was KRW 678 million, a turn to operating profit, but the controlling-shareholder net loss of KRW 1.42 billion extended the five-quarter loss streak.

On the battery side, the company plans to use the October 2026 North American mass-production order as a reference, and on the semiconductor side the SiC ring system and the KRW 3.4 billion automation contract are building up as new pillars.

Strengths include order diversification and a narrower operating loss, while weaknesses include accumulated net losses, a higher debt-to-equity ratio and negative operating cash flow.

On valuation, the share price is below the book value attributable to controlling shareholders, but PER cannot be calculated because of net losses over the last four quarters.

The next checkpoints are the North American installation target at the end of December 2026, the annual profit and loss in the 2026 annual report, and the net loss trend in the Q1 2027 quarterly report.
