02

Business structure

Ajusteel is a color steel developer and manufacturer founded in 1995 and listed on KOSPI in August 2021. According to Butler's aggregation, its revenue mix in the second quarter of 2026 was 50% building materials, 43% home appliances, 5% automotive and 2% other.

The home appliance segment centers on color steel for video and household appliances, and its main customers are domestic appliance makers, European local appliance makers and construction companies.

The building materials business expanded after the change of largest shareholder in January 2025, which the company cites as a driver of its sales mix shift.

Production sites include the Gumi and Gimcheon plants in Korea, a color steel plant in Poland, and an oven plant and trailer container molding and extrusion plant in Mexico.

Overseas subsidiaries carry significant weight: the Polish unit generated KRW 146.4 billion of revenue in the first nine months of 2025, offsetting a KRW 211.5 billion decline at the US unit (down 45.6% year on year), according to a press report.

In a 2024 report by Steel Metal News, Ajusteel was described as the fourth-largest color steel maker in the industry. The cold-rolling unit of the Dongkuk Steel Mill group acquired a majority stake around the end of 2024 and consolidated Ajusteel from January 2025.

Since then the two have cooperated, with Ajusteel's Polish unit exporting the cold-rolling unit's products. The company itself attributed its 2024 profit deterioration to intensifying competition in the domestic appliance market.

04

Earnings analysis

On a reported annual basis, revenue fell from KRW 1.06 trillion in 2022 to KRW 956.5 billion in 2023, a 9.4% decline, and then rose to KRW 999.7 billion in 2024 and KRW 1.09 trillion in 2025, growing for two consecutive years after the 2023 low. The 2025 figure is the largest of the 2022 to 2025 period.

Operating profit of KRW 4.6 billion in 2022 turned into an operating loss of KRW 19.1 billion in 2023, widened to KRW 42.4 billion in 2024, and narrowed to KRW 25.6 billion in 2025, marking three consecutive years of losses from 2023 through 2025.

The operating margin moved from 0.4% in 2022 to -2.0% in 2023, -4.2% in 2024 and -2.4% in 2025. Attributable net income of KRW 1.34 billion in 2022 became an attributable net loss of KRW 31.4 billion in 2023, KRW 108.2 billion in 2024 and KRW 41.2 billion in 2025.

Net loss including non-controlling interests was KRW 41.2 billion in 2025. For 2024, the company cited intensifying competition in the appliance market and weak results at a subsidiary; for the 2025 improvement, it pointed to a changed sales mix, expansion into building materials and quality improvement activities.

Quarterly, a KRW 5.91 billion operating profit in 3Q 2025 turned into a KRW 19.9 billion operating loss in 4Q 2025. The operating loss narrowed to KRW 853 million in 1Q 2026, and 2Q 2026 posted operating profit of KRW 1.06 billion, a turn to profitability that a May 2026 press report had anticipated.

Third-quarter 2026 operating profit of KRW 1.36 billion rose 29.1% from the prior quarter but fell 76.9% from a year earlier. Attributable net loss of KRW 12.9 billion in 3Q 2026 turned from profit a year earlier.

Operating cash flow ran at KRW -22.4 billion in 2022, KRW 26.7 billion in 2023, KRW 37 billion in 2024 and KRW -19.5 billion in 2025, with the direction changing almost every year. The debt ratio climbed from 220.1% in 2022 to 711.2% in 2024 before easing to 596.5% in 2025.

05

Industry analysis

Color steel sits across appliance and building-material demand, so shifts in end-market demand pass through quickly to results. An April 2026 report pointed to a slowdown in profitability across the global color steel market.

In the same report, an industry source said that given weaker demand from downstream industries and the global steel cycle, a quick rebound would be difficult. A May 2026 report also noted that domestic color steel demand has slowed.

According to that report, the parent's cold-rolling unit is gaining cost savings, joint raw-material purchasing and wider export opportunities in Poland and Mexico from owning Ajusteel, then ranked fourth in color steel. On the appliance side, the company itself cites intense competition in the domestic market.

With building materials near half of 2Q 2026 revenue, construction-related demand has also become a factor behind earnings swings.

06

Outlook

The company has set profitability-focused product management and maximum utilization of its facilities as its core goals for 2026.

The CGCL line producing galvalume and galvanized steel has been in full operation since January 1, 2026, and the company expected its output to rise from 89,987 tons in 2025 to 150,000 tons in 2026.

The same filing projected 2026 raw-material purchases of about KRW 121.2 billion, up KRW 38.6 billion from the prior year, so higher output also brings heavier input costs. The company plans to expand higher-value galvalume products and develop differentiated thin and heavy plate products.

The parent's cold-rolling unit set a target at the time of the deal of KRW 3.2 trillion in revenue and an operating margin above 5% within five years; this is the parent's plan, not Ajusteel's standalone guidance.

In January 2026 the company decided to issue exchangeable bonds worth about KRW 2.89 million, exchangeable into 877,820 treasury shares equal to 2.27% of total shares issued, with exchange claims possible from February 13, 2026 through January 6, 2031.

An April 2026 press report noted a credit rating cut from BB+ (negative) to BB (stable). The next checkpoint is whether the CGCL capacity expansion shows up in quarterly revenue and operating margin.

08

Bull factors

Narrowing operating losses and a turn to profit

The 2025 operating loss shrank to KRW 25.6 billion from KRW 42.4 billion in 2024. Operating profit of KRW 1.06 billion in 2Q 2026 marked a turn to profit, and 3Q 2026 added KRW 1.36 billion. The 1Q 2026 operating loss of KRW 853 million was far smaller than the KRW 19.9 billion loss in 4Q 2025.

Top-line growth and planned capacity expansion

Top-line growth is clear, and it is tied to the start-up of the CGCL line. The company expected output to reach about 150,000 tons in 2026. Bulls argue that higher production and sales would spread fixed costs over a larger base.

Parent support and group synergies

A April 2026 press report said the cold-rolling unit of the Dongkuk Steel Mill group injected KRW 57 billion through a third-party rights offering, extended KRW 50.2 billion in loans and provided KRW 415 billion in debt guarantees. Joint raw-material purchasing and export cooperation through the Polish unit continue. Polish unit revenue reached KRW 146.4 billion in the first nine months of 2025.

09

Bear factors

Persistent net losses and a profit reversal

Attributable net losses have continued across the four quarters from 4Q 2025 through 3Q 2026, aggregating KRW 54.2 billion. The 3Q 2026 attributable net loss of KRW 12.9 billion moved from profit a year earlier. Operating profit in the same quarter fell 76.9%, so higher sales did not translate into better earnings.

Balance-sheet strain and reliance on outside funding

Total liabilities at the end of 2025 were KRW 861.5 billion against total equity of KRW 144.4 billion, leaving a debt ratio of 596.5%. Operating cash flow was KRW -19.5 billion in 2025.

An April 2026 press report said the company borrowed KRW 18 billion from its parent at a 4.60% interest rate to repay debt, and its credit rating was lowered from BB+ to BB.

Industry slowdown and cost and dilution pressure

Industry sources point to slowing profitability in the global color steel market and weaker downstream demand, which could slow any short-term rebound. Planned 2026 raw-material purchases of about KRW 121.2 billion are KRW 38.6 billion higher than the prior year.

If the exchangeable bonds are fully exchanged, 877,820 shares would be added to the float and dilute existing holders.

10

Risk factors

Financing and liquidity

Operating cash flow turned negative in 2025, and with a debt ratio of 596.5%, interest costs could slow the pace of profit recovery. The credit rating downgrade has reportedly tightened external funding conditions. Overlapping debt maturities could add liquidity pressure.

Dependence on the parent

Ajusteel relies heavily on parent support for working capital and guarantees. Industry observers note that the parent's own cash generation has weakened since the acquisition. If the parent's capacity to support changes, the cushion for Ajusteel's balance sheet could thin.

Input costs and share dilution

The company's projection of about KRW 121.2 billion in 2026 raw-material purchases raises cost pressure. The 877,820 exchangeable shares equal 2.27% of total shares issued. If the spread between input costs and selling prices narrows, the improvement in operating margin could be limited.

12

Overall view

Ajusteel's profit structure is improving, with operating losses narrowing in 2025 and operating profit recorded in the second and third quarters of 2026.

However, the 3Q 2026 attributable net loss turned from profit a year earlier, and attributable net losses totaled KRW 54.2 billion across the four quarters from 4Q 2025 through 3Q 2026.

A debt ratio of 596.5%, with total liabilities of KRW 861.5 billion against total equity of KRW 144.4 billion, makes whether profit recovery leads to capital strengthening and a better borrowing structure the central question.

Parent-level rights offerings, loans, guarantees and emergency borrowing are propping up short-term liquidity, so the durability of that support is a precondition for financial stability.

On the industry side, slowing profitability in global color steel and weaker downstream demand are cited as limits on a near-term rebound. The CGCL capacity expansion is planned, but higher raw-material purchases could offset margin gains.

Strengths and weaknesses coexist, so the operating margin and operating cash flow in the 2026 annual report and 2027 quarterly results will serve as the key checks.
