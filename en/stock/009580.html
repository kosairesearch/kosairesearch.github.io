02

Business structure

Moorim P&P is a pulp, paper and paperboard manufacturer listed on KOSPI since June 1989. The business is split between a paper segment, centered on printing paper, and a pulp segment that produces the wood pulp used as paper feedstock.

The paper segment depends on printing and publishing demand in Korea as well as export sales. The pulp segment is linked to international pulp prices and relies on imported raw materials, so swings in raw material prices and the exchange rate flow directly into profit.

A corporate information site's commentary said the paper segment expanded market share through cost reduction, while the pulp segment continued to post operating losses and needs to improve profitability.

In its corporate value enhancement plan disclosed in October 2026, the company proposed expanding label sales, growing sales of printing paper in higher-margin export markets, strengthening its pulp business and expanding eco-friendly pulp molded products as profitability measures.

Labels and pulp molded products can be read as attempts to diversify away from a revenue base that leaned heavily on printing paper. The Fair Trade Commission ordered six printing paper manufacturers and sellers to cease the conduct, and Moorim P&P is among them.

Its competitive field in printing paper overlaps with sister companies Moorim Paper and Moorim SP in the Moorim Group, as well as Hansol Paper and Hankuk Paper MFG. The largest shareholder and related parties hold 66.97 percent according to the stake data on record.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual results started at revenue of KRW 774.1 billion and operating profit of KRW 68.3 billion in 2022.

In 2023, revenue slipped to KRW 768.9 billion (down 0.7 percent year on year) and operating profit fell to KRW 11.6 billion (down 83.0 percent), and the net loss attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 22 billion, a swing into loss.

In 2024, revenue recovered to KRW 811.7 billion (up 5.6 percent) and operating profit rose to KRW 36.5 billion (up 214.1 percent), with net profit attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 22.5 billion, a return to profit. 2024 was the largest year of 2022 to 2025 by revenue.

In 2025, revenue fell to KRW 731.6 billion (down 9.9 percent), the smallest year in the period, and the company posted an operating loss of KRW 24.5 billion, turning into a loss.

In its disclosure on February 20, 2026, the company attributed the decline mainly to one-off fixed costs from new boiler facility investment and lower international pulp prices. The operating margin moved from 8.8 percent in 2022 to 1.5 percent in 2023, 4.5 percent in 2024 and negative 3.3 percent in 2025.

Operating cash flow went from KRW 97.8 billion in 2024 to negative KRW 21.6 billion in 2025. At the end of 2025, total liabilities stood at KRW 1.21 trillion, above total equity of KRW 606.8 billion, and the debt ratio rose from 142.1 percent in 2022 to 200.0 percent in 2025.

On a quarterly basis, the operating loss was KRW 8.56 billion in Q3 2025, KRW 5.59 billion in Q4 2025, KRW 5.83 billion in Q1 2026, KRW 5.06 billion in Q2 2026 and KRW 11.9 billion in Q3 2026, which makes five consecutive loss-making quarters from Q3 2025 through Q3 2026.

Q2 2026 revenue of KRW 207.9 billion was the highest of the last five quarters, yet the net loss attributable to controlling shareholders widened to KRW 63 billion in the same quarter.

Press reports said the fine imposed on Moorim P&P in this period was KRW 57.5 billion, and that other expenses rose from KRW 0.5 billion in the first half of 2025 to the KRW 500 million range in the first half of 2026. Reported fine amounts differ across outlets. The net loss attributable to controlling shareholders for the last four quarters totals KRW 98 billion.

05

Industry analysis

The printing paper market is caught between falling selling prices and rising raw material costs. In Q3 2026, three paper companies in the Moorim Group all posted operating losses, and Moorim Paper's operating loss widened from KRW 3.5 billion to KRW 15.5 billion.

Reports attributed this to lower printing paper selling prices alongside higher prices for some raw materials such as pulp and wood chips. Reports differ on the direction of pulp prices.

A News1 report dated September 30, 2026 said that international pulp prices were continuing to fall, while another article headline described pulp prices rising at a high level on high oil prices and a strong dollar.

Because the pulp segment's profit moves with international prices and the exchange rate, this difference bears directly on Moorim P&P's earnings. In the paper segment, the company was credited with expanding market share through cost reduction.

Hansol Paper and Hankuk Paper MFG compete in the same printing paper market, and Moorim P&P, which also runs a pulp business, is more directly exposed to raw material prices. A 2023 securities report also pointed to year-end peak demand for printing paper in the second half.

Based on current reporting, the company appears to be in a period where printing paper price declines and raw material cost increases overlap.

06

Outlook

The verifiable plans the company has put on record are its corporate value enhancement plan and its shareholder return policy.

In its October 2026 disclosure, the company set a minimum dividend floor for fiscal 2026 to 2028 and stated that it will pursue a shareholder return ratio of 30 percent or more, using share buybacks and cancellations.

On profitability, it proposed expanding label sales, growing printing paper sales in higher-margin export markets, strengthening its pulp business and expanding eco-friendly pulp molded products.

Whether the one-off fixed costs from the new boiler facility investment, one of the causes of the 2025 loss, decline after 2026 is the first checkpoint for earnings recovery.

Because other expenses rose in the first half of 2026 with the fine reflected, the outcome of the fine litigation is a key variable for second-half earnings. The direction of international pulp prices remains a variable affecting both pulp segment profitability and paper segment raw material costs.

With the Q3 2026 operating loss widening to KRW 11.9 billion, whether the loss streak ends from Q4 2026 onward is the next checkpoint. The medium-term plan points to a direction for profitability improvement, but no specific quarterly targets appear in the disclosed materials.

Bull factors

Narrowing loss trend

A financial information site's summary said Q1 2026 operating loss narrowed 34.8 percent year on year, with cost competitiveness cited as the basis. On a quarterly basis, the Q2 2026 operating loss of KRW 5.06 billion was smaller than the KRW 5.83 billion in Q1 2026.

Revenue base recovery

Dealsite reported that Q2 2026 revenue rose 23.5 percent year on year to KRW 207.9 billion. The same report said the revenue base grew but profitability recovery was not yet sufficient. Q2 2026 was the highest-revenue quarter of the last five quarters on the disclosed basis.

Shareholder return framework

In its October 2026 disclosure, the company set a minimum dividend floor for fiscal 2026 to 2028 and recorded a plan to pursue a shareholder return ratio of 30 percent or more using buybacks and cancellations. The prior year's dividend was resolved in March 2026, even though fiscal 2025 ended with a net loss. Presenting a dividend framework during weak earnings is cited as a positive factor.

Bear factors

Five consecutive loss quarters

The company has recorded losses in five consecutive quarters from Q3 2025 through Q3 2026. The Q3 2026 operating loss of KRW 11.9 billion widened from KRW 5.06 billion in the prior quarter and also grew from the year-ago quarter. External commentary that the pulp segment continues to post operating losses remains a profitability burden.

Fine and other expense burden

Press reports put the fine imposed on Moorim P&P at KRW 57.5 billion, and other expenses rose from KRW 0.5 billion in the first half of 2025 to the KRW 500 million range in the first half of 2026.

The fine reflection is cited as the main background for the KRW 63 billion Q2 2026 net loss attributable to controlling shareholders. Reported fine amounts differ across outlets, and the financial burden could change depending on the litigation outcome.

Rising financial burden

Total liabilities at the end of 2025 stood at KRW 1.21 trillion, above total equity of KRW 606.8 billion, and the debt ratio was 200.0 percent in 2025. Operating cash flow turned negative at KRW 21.6 billion in 2025. The debt ratio has risen from 142.1 percent in 2022, adding to pressure on financial capacity.

Risk factors

Regulation and litigation

The outcome of the fine litigation tied to the Fair Trade Commission sanctions on printing paper has not been finalized. Reported fine amounts differ by outlet, so the baseline itself is unsettled. The outcome could change other expenses and the net loss attributable to controlling shareholders.

Raw materials and exchange rate

Reports conflict on international pulp prices, with one saying declines continue and another describing high prices amid high oil prices and a strong dollar. The pulp segment depends on imported feedstock, so price and exchange rate swings flow directly into profit.

If raw material costs rise while printing paper selling prices fall, the benefit of cost reduction could be offset.

Financial structure and capex

The debt ratio rose from 142.1 percent in 2022 to 200.0 percent in 2025. One-off fixed costs from new boiler facility investment weighed on 2025 earnings. If facility investment burdens continue, the recovery in cash flow could be slower.

Overall view

Moorim P&P's annual operating profit fell from KRW 68.3 billion in 2022 to KRW 11.6 billion in 2023, recovered to KRW 36.5 billion in 2024, and turned into an operating loss of KRW 24.5 billion in 2025.

The company attributed the 2025 loss to one-off fixed costs from new boiler facility investment and lower international pulp prices. Losses have continued for five consecutive quarters from Q3 2025 through Q3 2026, and the Q3 2026 operating loss widened to KRW 11.9 billion.

Positive factors are cost improvement in the paper segment, the Q2 2026 revenue recovery and the shareholder return framework for fiscal 2026 to 2028.

Negative factors are higher other expenses from the fine, reported fine amounts that differ by outlet and pending litigation, a debt ratio of 200.0 percent and negative operating cash flow in 2025.

With the net loss attributable to controlling shareholders for the last four quarters totaling KRW 98 billion, the price-to-earnings ratio cannot be calculated, so valuation is mainly compared on assets and dividends.

The next checkpoints are the FY2026 preliminary results, the 2026 annual report and the outcome of the fine litigation.
