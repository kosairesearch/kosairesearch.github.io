02

Business structure

UXN is a CGM developer that was founded in May 2012 and listed on the KONEX market in November 2019. The market classifies it under electrical equipment, but its real business is medical devices, so it does not map directly onto the electrical equipment peer group.

Its core technology is a glucose sensor that uses a nanoporous platinum electrode instead of enzymes. According to the company, the platinum sensor is chemically stable, does not degrade at room temperature, and allows long-term use that enzyme-based designs find hard to achieve.

A technical analysis report posted on the KRX KIND system in September 2025 states that the company holds 23 registered and 70 pending patents, including foundational patents. Revenue relies on prototype and other sales rather than product sales.

The same report expected that in the near term, prototype sales such as sensor substrates would drive growth. In the competitive landscape, enzyme-based products lead the market, and the company positions its product as a differentiated alternative.

On the shareholder side, SD Biosensor was reported to have decided in September 2021 to invest about KRW 40 billion for a stake. As of September 2026, the company had 27 employees, so it is a small research and development organization.

Recent trends

On a 2025 standalone basis, revenue was KRW 153 million, operating loss was KRW 4,926 million, and net loss was KRW 3,025 million. On the same basis, total assets were KRW 10,539 million, total liabilities were KRW 11,647 million, and total equity was negative KRW 1,108 million.

For the 2024 comparison, revenue was KRW 513.47 million, which fell to KRW 153.12 million in 2025. Operating loss narrowed from KRW 5,805 million in 2024 to KRW 4,926 million in 2025.

According to FnGuide, total equity at the end of 2024 was negative KRW 19.1 billion, so the negative equity position narrowed sharply by the end of 2025. We could not confirm the cause of this improvement.

On the business side, the confirmatory trial plan was approved in April 2026, and a U.S. patent allowance was received for the implantable CGM technology.

The company's IR page lists a patent acquisition on July 16, an amended convertible bond decision on July 27, a rights offering decision on August 4, and a securities issuance result on August 12, 2026.

The stock traded at KRW 6,250 intraday on October 21, 2026, and closed at KRW 6,140 on October 27, down 0.16% from the prior day. According to an investment data site, the one-year change was -40.6% as of the September 30, 2026 quote, and the three-month average volume was only around one thousand shares.

04

Outlook

The near-term question is the progress and outcome of the A2 confirmatory trial, followed by whether marketing approval is obtained. According to an April 2026 report, the trial enrolls 125 adults aged 19 or older with type 1 or type 2 diabetes, and efficacy is assessed against venous blood glucose.

In the earlier exploratory trial, the mean absolute relative difference (MARD) was 10.2%. Whether marketing approval has been granted could not be confirmed within the scope of our research. The company said it plans to pursue a transfer from KONEX to KOSDAQ once domestic marketing approval is obtained.

Overseas, it signed a memorandum of understanding with King Faisal University in Saudi Arabia for joint research and clinical trials, and it is reviewing an expansion into long-term implantable products.

The KIND technical analysis report projected that operating cash flow and the financial structure would improve once product launch gains momentum. The same report also noted that delays in approval and commercialization would add volatility to the growth path.

Even after approval, sales channel build-out, reimbursement and distribution negotiations, mass-production stability and competition with incumbents remain, so the pace of revenue conversion is uncertain.

05

Bull factors

Differentiated sensor technology and patents

The enzyme-free nanoporous platinum approach differs from conventional enzyme-based CGMs. A cited feature is that it shows less performance loss from temperature changes and can be stored and shipped at room temperature.

The technical analysis report assessed the intellectual property base as relatively solid, with 23 registered and 70 pending patents. In April 2026, the company also received a U.S. patent allowance for an implantable CGM.

Regulatory progress and early clinical data

The ministry's approval of the confirmatory trial plan moved the company into a key stage of the approval process. The review took about 15 months after the application was filed. According to the company, the exploratory trial recorded a MARD of 10.2%, comparable to leading global products.

Having had the validity and safety of a new measurement principle reviewed may be a useful reference in the later approval process.

Overseas trials and potential product expansion

The company runs a two-track strategy that combines domestic and Middle East trials. Its chief executive said data from diverse ethnic groups and environments would serve as a reference for entering the U.S., Europe, India and China.

According to the company, the sensor's lower lifespan constraint could allow expansion into long-term products such as implantables. The relationship with SD Biosensor is often cited as background for possible business cooperation.

06

Bear factors

Minimal revenue and continuing losses

Standalone revenue in 2025 was KRW 153 million against an operating loss of KRW 4,926 million. This is a development-stage cost structure that far exceeds revenue, so losses may persist until a product launches.

Total equity is negative KRW 1,108 million, which means capital impairment, and 2025 revenue was lower than the KRW 513.47 million recorded in 2024.

History of delayed commercialization

The chief executive himself said that commercialization is later than expected because of technical difficulty and other factors. Approval of the confirmatory trial took 15 months, and the shift of the business model from a short-term health-checkup product to a long-term personal product is analyzed as one factor. The timing of trial results and marketing approval may continue to shift.

Financing burden and low liquidity

Between July and August 2026, the company disclosed an amended convertible bond decision, a rights offering decision and a securities issuance result. Repeated external financing can raise the possibility of share dilution.

Trading value on the report date was KRW 1,641,650 and the three-month average volume was only around one thousand shares, so executing trades may be difficult and prices can move sharply on small volumes.

07

Risk factors

Macro

KONEX is a small market, so prices can react sensitively to changes in interest rates, risk appetite and sentiment toward biotech and medical devices. Development-stage companies depend heavily on external financing conditions. If the market environment worsens, financing terms may become less favorable or timelines may slip.

Industry

The CGM market is led by global incumbents using enzyme-based methods. A product built on a new measurement principle must demonstrate accuracy, safety and long-term stability, and regulators may apply strict standards.

Even after approval, sales channels, the reimbursement environment and price competition will determine how fast revenue materializes.

Company

According to a 2024 report, the Suwon District Court ordered the company and its chief executive to pay about KRW 19.6 billion to SD Biosensor. At that time, current liabilities due within one year were KRW 51.4 billion. We could not confirm how the debt has been handled since.

With capital impairment continuing, any amendment or reversal of financing-related disclosures could weigh on market confidence.

08

Overall view

UXN is a development-stage medical device company with almost no revenue and negative total equity. The core of the business is the confirmatory trial and marketing approval of its enzyme-free, nanoporous-platinum CGM.

The bullish case rests on the differentiated sensor technology, the patent base, the approval of the confirmatory trial, and early clinical metrics. The bearish case rests on continuing operating losses, capital impairment, a history of delayed commercialization, repeated financing disclosures and low liquidity.

Even though the market lists the company under electrical equipment, its business is medical devices, so a direct comparison with electrical equipment companies is not appropriate.

Clinical results, approval timing and financing terms should be checked through disclosures, and this report is provided for information purposes only.
