Electroceutical Developer: Losses and Funding

02

Business structure

OceansBio is a medical-device manufacturer founded in 2018. It listed on the KONEX market on January 14, 2025. Its core business is extracorporeal vagus-nerve electrical-stimulation electroceuticals and implantable electroceuticals.

The implantable product (iVNS) is a personalized device that uses an AI model built on biometric data to predict seizure precursors and adjust pulses. The earphone-type external device (TaVNS) has been in exploratory clinical work targeting stress relief, depression, panic disorder and sleep disorders.

According to coverage at the time of listing, neither product generated revenue, and sales came from ISO-certification consulting and an energy-harvester module. A breakdown of revenue by segment could not be confirmed in this research.

The company's explanation is that competing devices have a rated battery life of 10 years but last only about 3 to 5 years in practice, and that a replacement surgery costs about KRW 50 million. It positions its rechargeable battery and wireless charging as a point of differentiation. This is the view of the company and its investors, and no independently verified clinical results were found.

Its industry is disclosed as medical-device manufacturing. Companies in the same exchange category, such as LS Electric, HD Hyundai Electric and Hyosung Heavy Industries, mass-produce power equipment. Their business structures and revenue models differ greatly, so they are hard to treat as direct comparables. SK oceanplant and Ocean In W, whose names are similar, are separate companies.

03

Recent trends

Revenue for 2025 was KRW 102.18 million, down 47% from the prior year, when revenue was KRW 193.93 million. The operating loss for 2025 was KRW 2.98 billion, wider than the roughly KRW 1.93 billion loss in 2024. The net loss for 2025 was about KRW 2.01 billion.

Non-operating income of about KRW 1.03 billion offset part of the operating loss. Total equity fell from about KRW 2.22 billion in 2024 to about KRW 0.2 billion in 2025, and the debt ratio was 667.76%.

These figures come from a corporate-information site's compilation of the 2025 annual results, and 2026 half-year results could not be confirmed through search.

In a disclosure on March 19, 2026, the company decided on a third-party allotment rights offering directed at Capstone Scale-up Investment Partnership.

On March 23, 2026, it also disclosed an issue of 19,048 redeemable convertible preferred shares to Busan Regional Innovation TIPS Investment Fund No. 1, to fund R&D and operations.

In a disclosure on October 14, 2026, it decided on a third-party allotment of 85,714 redeemable convertible preferred shares to raise KRW 900 million for working capital. That issue was priced at KRW 10,500 against a reference price of KRW 2,921, a premium of 259.47%.

The share price hit a low of KRW 870 on March 9, 2026. On May 19, 2026, it was newly designated an investment-warning stock after a continued surge. For measures effective June 1, 2026, a trading halt was flagged while the stock was already under the investment-risk designation.

The October 21, 2026 close was KRW 2,675, the 52-week high is KRW 7,930, and the 12-month change is shown as -46.00%. On October 27, 2026 the price was KRW 2,210, down 2.86% from the previous day, on turnover of only KRW 221,000.

04

Outlook

The near-term watch point is whether funding continues rather than R&D progress. The operating loss for 2025 was dozens of times revenue, so operations depend heavily on outside financing. Redeemable convertible preferred issues have been disclosed three times in 2026 alone, a high frequency.

On the product side, regulatory approval and clinical progress for the implantable device are the key variables. In 2021, an investor's chief executive said OceansBio was the only company in Korea attempting a manufacturing license for an implantable electroceutical.

However, no specific disclosure on approval timing or clinical results was found in this research. The timing and scale of revenue can therefore be judged only qualitatively.

On the trading side, the history of market-warning designations and very thin volume affect price formation. Conversion of the preferred shares would raise the common share count and dilute existing holders.

Room to narrow the operating loss depends on revenue growth and cost control, and neither is spelled out in current disclosures.

05

Bull factors

Claimed differentiation in personalized, rechargeable design

The company says its personalized system extends battery life. It highlights rechargeable-battery wireless charging and precursor-triggered stimulation that reduces power consumption. If confirmed in clinical use, the design could reduce the burden of repeat surgery. For now it remains the company's own description.

Continued participation by venture investors

In an April 1, 2026 filing, major shareholder Capstone Partners reported a 10.07% stake. A Busan-region investment fund took part in the March 23, 2026 issue. A further KRW 900 million working-capital issue was decided on October 14, 2026. As long as institutional money keeps flowing in, near-term operating funds are covered.

Government support and research base

The company said that a joint R&D support program helped it secure KRW 100 million in government grants, KRW 200 million in R&D funds, KRW 200 million in investment and KRW 100 million in loans. In 2019 it opened a corporate research institute and obtained venture-company certification.

For an early-stage company, use of public support and a research base can be read as supporting development continuity.

06

Bear factors

Minimal revenue and a wider loss

Revenue for 2025 was KRW 102.18 million, down 47% from the prior year. The operating loss that year was KRW 2.98 billion. The core products had no revenue according to coverage at the time of listing, and current sales appear to come from ancillary activities. The operating loss continues to far exceed revenue.

Weak balance sheet

Total equity fell to about KRW 0.2 billion in 2025 and the debt ratio was 667.76%. Total assets declined 55.17% from the prior year. With thin equity, there is little financial cushion if losses keep accumulating. This is why capital raises recur.

Gap between issue price and market price

The October 14, 2026 issue was priced at KRW 10,500 against a reference price of KRW 2,921. Against the current price of KRW 2,210, the gap between the price paid by investors and the market price is very large. The 12-month change is -46.00%.

The gap suggests either that thin trading means the market price does not fully reflect value, or that the issue price was above the market's assessment.

07

Risk factors

Macro

Among the 89 comparable KONEX December-year-end companies, the combined operating loss for 2025 was KRW 39.1 billion. Loss-making companies numbered 46, a majority. With profitability low across KONEX, a pullback in new investment could quickly worsen the funding environment for development-stage companies. Changes in interest rates and risk appetite work through the same channel.

Business & Regulatory

TaVNS is a product still at the exploratory clinical stage. For the implantable electroceutical, regulatory approval and clinical trials are prerequisites for commercialization. Approval delays or weak clinical results could push back the start of revenue.

The competitive landscape, in which large overseas device makers hold an early position, must also be considered.

Liquidity & Dilution

Volume over the three trading days used for the offering's reference price totaled only 382 shares. Even small trades can move the price sharply. The market-warning system has three stages (caution, warning and risk), and designation at warning level or above can bring trading halts and limits on margin lending.

The conversion window for the preferred shares runs from March 31, 2026 to March 30, 2036, with a conversion price of KRW 10,500. For the issue of March 23, 2026, the conversion price can be adjusted down to a minimum of KRW 7,350 if the market price falls. Conversion would increase the common share count and dilute holders.

08

Overall view

Although classified under electrical equipment by the exchange, OceansBio is in practice an early-stage medical-device company developing electroceuticals for epilepsy. Revenue for 2025 was KRW 102.18 million and the operating loss was KRW 2.98 billion, a large loss relative to the scale of the business.

Convertible preferred issues were disclosed three times in 2026, indicating heavy reliance on outside funding. The claimed technical differentiation in personalized, rechargeable design and the participation of venture investors can be read as supporting development continuity.

On the other hand, clinical and regulatory results remain unconfirmed, and the thin balance sheet, extremely low trading volume and history of market-warning designations are burdens. This report is for informational purposes only, and any judgment is left to the reader.
