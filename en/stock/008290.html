02

Business structure

Founded in 1972, Wonpoong Corporation is a men's apparel manufacturer and wholesaler/retailer that produces suits, casual wear, shirts, coats, and pants through outsourced manufacturing.

Under a domestic license for the Scottish brand Kinloch Anderson, the company operates a leading formal-wear brand in Korea alongside a casual line called Kinloch by Kinloch Anderson.

To pursue online growth, it launched Connect Kinloch as an exclusive line for the Musinsa platform, and it has also renewed the Kinloch Anderson Edinburgh line targeting department stores and premium retail, expanding across online and offline channels including 29CM and OCO.

Since 2022 the company has added the Italian import brand Gimo's to broaden its portfolio of imported labels. Its business model relies primarily on outsourced production rather than in-house manufacturing, concentrating resources on brand planning and distribution.

In early 2025 the company sold the Gajwa-dong site and headquarters building in Incheon that it had held since its 1972 founding and relocated its headquarters to Bupyeong-gu, monetizing an asset that had accounted for a substantial share of total assets to secure liquidity.

Competitively, prolonged domestic economic weakness combined with rising market share for imported and online brands has continued to erode the offline store share of domestic apparel brands, according to industry commentary.

The company has stated it is pursuing brand repositioning, stronger online distribution, new product lines, expanded online-specialist staffing, and in-house content production to improve its earnings structure and expand sales.

04

Earnings analysis

Wonpoong's revenue declined for four consecutive years, from KRW 33.30 billion in 2022 to KRW 28.94 billion in 2023, KRW 22.72 billion in 2024, and KRW 16.94 billion in 2025.

Operating profit shifted from a slight gain of KRW 0.21 billion in 2022 to losses of KRW 1.92 billion in 2023, KRW 4.70 billion in 2024, and KRW 7.20 billion in 2025, with the operating margin deteriorating from 0.6% to negative 42.5% over the period.

Net loss attributable to owners also widened from KRW 3.51 billion in 2022 to KRW 7.58 billion in 2025.

On a quarterly basis, revenue of KRW 2.38 billion and an operating loss of KRW 1.41 billion in Q3 2025 was followed by Q4 2025 revenue rising to KRW 5.25 billion even as the operating loss expanded to KRW 3.48 billion, likely reflecting one-off factors such as provisioning against related-party loans.

Subsequently, operating losses narrowed noticeably to about KRW 0.77 billion in Q1 2026 and KRW 0.67 billion in Q2 2026 compared with prior quarters. On the balance sheet, equity attributable to owners fell from KRW 20.54 billion in 2024 to KRW 14.29 billion in 2025 as accumulated losses mounted.

Total liabilities, however, dropped sharply from KRW 31.80 billion in 2024 to KRW 5.52 billion in 2025, cutting the debt ratio from 154.8% to 38.6%, a result attributed to using proceeds from the early-2025 sale of the Incheon headquarters site to repay short-term borrowings.

Operating cash flow remained negative every year from 2022 through 2025, ranging roughly between negative KRW 2.7 billion and negative KRW 5.8 billion, indicating persistently weak core cash generation.

05

Industry analysis

The domestic men's formal and casual apparel market faces structural pressure from prolonged economic weakness, reduced demand for suits amid the spread of remote and flexible work, and expanding market share for imported and online brands.

Industry observers point to the continuing decline in offline store share for domestic brands as a key driver of earnings deterioration.

Wonpoong's revenue has generally trended downward with fluctuations since 2019, before the COVID-19 pandemic, a pattern consistent with structurally shrinking domestic demand for formal wear.

Consumption has also been shifting toward online platforms such as Musinsa and 29CM, making channel transition an urgent task for domestic suit brands that have traditionally relied on department stores and franchise distribution.

Wonpoong has attempted channel diversification through online-exclusive lines such as Connect Kinloch, though publicly available information offering a clear comparison of scale economics or brand recognition versus competitors remains limited.

Within the KOSDAQ textile and apparel sector, small-cap names such as this one tend to exhibit high earnings volatility, and whether individual companies can achieve structural improvement has become a key area of investor attention.

Outlook

The company has stated its intention to improve its earnings structure and expand sales through brand repositioning, stronger online distribution, new product lines, expanded online-specialist staffing, and in-house content production.

Proceeds from the early-2025 sale of the Incheon headquarters site were largely used to repay short-term borrowings, contributing to the improvement in the 2025 debt ratio.

However, related-party loans to Sundiode and Sundiode Korea are to be recovered, according to the company, in connection with technology development, commercialization, partnerships, or M&A progress, with no specific recovery timeline or terms disclosed.

The narrowing of operating losses in Q1 and Q2 2026 relative to prior quarters could signal some improvement in cost structure, but whether this trend continues into the third quarter and beyond will require confirmation through upcoming disclosures.

The stock consolidation implemented in April 2026 appears to be an administrative measure related to share management rather than an event with direct impact on business fundamentals.

Key items to monitor going forward include the growing sales contribution of online-exclusive lines, progress on recovering related-party loans, and whether continued operating losses trigger any listing-eligibility disclosures.

Bull factors

Improved Debt Structure

Proceeds from the early-2025 sale of the Incheon headquarters site were used to repay short-term borrowings, sharply cutting the debt ratio from 154.8% in 2024 to 38.6% in 2025. This can be viewed as a move that eased near-term financial pressure by securing liquidity and streamlining the balance sheet. However, it should also be considered a one-time effect stemming from asset disposal.

Narrowing Loss Trend

Operating losses of about KRW 0.77 billion in Q1 2026 and KRW 0.67 billion in Q2 2026 were substantially smaller than the KRW 1.41 billion and KRW 3.48 billion losses recorded in Q3 and Q4 2025, respectively. This can be interpreted as a sign that some cost control is taking effect even amid declining revenue. Whether this trend continues into the second half remains to be confirmed.

Brand and Channel Diversification Efforts

The company has broadened its brand portfolio through the Musinsa-exclusive Connect Kinloch line and the Italian import brand Gimo's. It has also stated plans to strengthen online distribution and produce in-house content, which can be seen as an attempt to reduce reliance on traditional offline channels.

Whether these efforts translate into a revenue recovery will need to be confirmed through future quarterly results.

Bear factors

Prolonged Revenue Decline

Revenue fell for four consecutive years, from KRW 33.30 billion in 2022 to KRW 16.94 billion in 2025. Structural factors cited include shrinking domestic demand for formal wear and a shift in market share toward imported and online brands.

With the revenue base itself shrinking, a recovery in top-line scale is a key precondition for any earnings improvement.

Related-Party Loan and Capital Impairment Issues

According to media reports, a total of about KRW 8.69 billion in loans, accrued interest, and equity acquisition costs related to Sundiode and Sundiode Korea, both related parties, was written off as unrecoverable, contributing to a capital impairment ratio that reportedly reached 29.76% as of fiscal year 2025.

Concerns over potential conflicts of interest have also been raised because the CEO concurrently serves as the head of the entity that received the funds. The absence of specific interest rate, maturity, and collateral terms for these loans in the business report has also been flagged as a transparency concern.

Listing-Eligibility Concerns from Consecutive Operating Losses

Wonpoong previously experienced a temporary trading halt in 2022 when the exchange disclosed concern over management-issue designation due to a risk of four consecutive years of operating losses.

Under KOSDAQ rules, four consecutive years of operating losses can trigger management-issue designation, and five consecutive years can lead to a listing-eligibility review.

Having posted operating losses for three consecutive years from 2023 through 2025, a further loss in fiscal year 2026 could raise similar concerns again.

Risk factors

Listing Eligibility Risk

Having recorded operating losses for three consecutive years (2023-2025), another loss in fiscal year 2026 could again raise concerns about management-issue designation under KOSDAQ rules. A similar issue previously led to a temporary trading halt in 2022.

With the capital impairment ratio reportedly already close to 30%, further losses could bring the company closer to capital-impairment-related thresholds as well.

Related-Party Transaction Risk

Funds have continued to be lent to entities such as Sundiode Korea, where the CEO holds a concurrent executive role, and a substantial portion has been written off as unrecoverable.

Limited disclosure of specific loan terms raises transparency concerns, and the possibility of further lending or losses cannot be ruled out.

Industry Demand Risk

Domestic demand for formal wear continues to shrink structurally, while imported and online brands keep expanding their market share. If the company's brand diversification efforts fail to translate into a revenue recovery, the revenue decline could persist over the longer term.
