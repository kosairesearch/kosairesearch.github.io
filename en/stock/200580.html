Widening the U.S. Spine Implant Line-Up

02

Business structure

Medyssey is a spinal and orthopedic implant maker founded in 2003, and it listed on Konex on October 20, 2015. Its core product is screw-fixation implants for spinal surgery used in orthopedics and neurosurgery, and its range covers the thoracolumbar and cervical spine.

It introduced metal 3D-printing equipment in 2011, a first in Korea, to develop patient-specific implants, and has extended the range to cheekbone, orbital, limb and iliac areas. Reported revenue types are products (orthopedic implants), merchandise and rental income, with products at the center. The exact split by type could not be confirmed in this research.

A high overseas share is a structural feature. At the end of 2025 the company exported to 25 countries, and 2025 exports of KRW 16.4 billion were 57.82% of revenue. Its sales base rests on overseas subsidiaries and distribution networks in the U.S., Chile, Thailand and elsewhere.

U.S. sales were about 24% of the total in 2025, and the local subsidiary was reported to have turned profitable. The largest shareholder is Dongwha Pharm, which became its parent in 2020 and now holds a 62.07% stake.

In the competitive landscape, domestic spinal implant makers pursuing the U.S. are natural comparisons. L&K Biomed is building U.S. surgical track record with an expandable cervical cage, while another domestic maker is pursuing a package combining spinal fixation devices and bone substitutes.

According to Technavio, the global spinal implant market is projected to grow at an average 5.3% a year through 2029, with North America accounting for about 43% of the growth.

03

Recent trends

Consolidated revenue for 2025 was KRW 28,309 million, up 11% from the prior year, and operating income was KRW 3,953 million, up 23.9%. The operating margin was reported at 14%. Net income was KRW 3,328 million.

The consolidated balance sheet showed total assets of KRW 45,102 million, total liabilities of KRW 7,405 million and total equity of KRW 37,696 million.

On a standalone basis, revenue was KRW 27,689 million, operating income KRW 4,053 million and net income KRW 3,795 million, and earnings per share were higher than on a consolidated basis.

The prior year, 2024, followed a different path. Consolidated revenue fell 4% in 2024, and domestic sales declined on the impact of the trainee-doctor strike, while overseas sales increased. In May 2026 the company won the top management award at the Konex awards.

On the product side, after NeckTune 3D SA Cervical Cage (K260036) in June 2026 and NeckTune SA Cervical Cage (K262726) in September 2026, the company disclosed on October 16, 2026 that it had obtained FDA 510(k) clearance for the Odys-SI Screw used in sacroiliac joint fusion.

Quarterly or half-year results after the first half of 2026 could not be confirmed in this research. The share price is KRW 12,000, unchanged from the previous close, and market capitalization is KRW 44.7 billion.

04

Outlook

The near-term focus is how the three 2026 FDA clearances translate into actual U.S. sales. The company expects U.S. sales from the NeckTune 3D product, cleared in June, from the second half of 2026, and from the PEEK-based product from the first half of 2027. The latest clearance extends its U.S. range from a thoracolumbar focus to cervical and sacroiliac joint fusion.

However, FDA 510(k) clearance does not guarantee sales, and a separate sales process of registering with hospitals and distributors and reaching actual surgical use is required.

Industry commentary holds that the speed of hospital entry and repeat use after clearance, rather than the number of clearances, will shape the U.S. competitiveness of Korean makers. On that yardstick, Medyssey's U.S. sales trend from the second half of 2026 is the key indicator to watch.

On profitability, the 2025 operating margin of 14% is a starting point for gauging room for improvement. The company continues to invest to meet demand, including additional equipment and more design and production staff. Higher investment can raise fixed costs, so the balance between sales growth and margin is the key question.

On the listing side, a transfer to KOSDAQ is a long-standing agenda item. The CEO said in an earlier interview that the company intends to pursue a KOSDAQ transfer. No confirmed schedule or filing was found in this research.

Quarterly results from the fourth quarter of 2026 and U.S. sales disclosures will provide the basis for later assessment.

05

Bull factors

Broader U.S. product range

The company obtained three FDA 510(k) clearances in a row, in June, September and October 2026. Its integrated cervical cage can now be supplied in both 3D-printed titanium and PEEK. Adding a sacroiliac joint fusion product broadens a U.S. line-up that had centered on the thoracolumbar spine. The fact that new products are layered on an existing U.S. subsidiary and sales base is cited as a positive.

Profit improvement and balance sheet

Consolidated operating income rose 23.9% in 2025, with an operating margin of about 14%. Consolidated total liabilities of KRW 7,405 million compare with total equity of KRW 37,696 million, so the debt burden is not heavy. Average annual revenue growth of about 9% over the past five years was also reported. Room for improvement in business metrics will be tested through product mix and a larger overseas share.

3D-printing customization capability

The company has operated in patient-specific implants since introducing metal 3D-printing equipment, a first in Korea. The newly cleared products also use a porous structure and multiple sizes to offer options matched to individual patient conditions.

The 62.07% stake held by the parent, Dongwha Pharm, is stable, which can be read as supportive of business continuity. Export experience across 25 countries provides a base for expanding approvals country by country.

06

Bear factors

Lag between clearance and revenue

Obtaining clearance does not by itself mean sales begin. The company expects U.S. sales of its PEEK cervical cage from the first half of 2027. If hospital registration and repeat use are delayed, the revenue contribution relative to investment could be postponed. With U.S. sales at about 24% of the 2025 total, the near-term earnings contribution may also be limited.

Konex liquidity and timing uncertainty

The company has 3,726,079 shares outstanding, and traded value on the day was only KRW 24,359,800. Thinly traded stocks can see prices move on small orders. An intention to transfer to KOSDAQ was voiced previously, but no confirmed schedule was found. If the transfer is delayed or does not occur, hoped-for improvements in market access may not materialize.

Domestic exposure and competition

In 2024, domestic sales fell on the impact of the trainee-doctor strike and consolidated revenue declined 4%. Domestic surgical demand can be sensitive to healthcare policy variables.

In the U.S., domestic peers such as L&K Biomed and another Korean spine implant maker are competing on product approvals and local surgical track records. It is also hard to rule out that the 2025 profit growth rate was influenced by a low base effect.

07

Risk factors

Macro

With exports at 57.82% of revenue, exchange-rate moves can affect earnings. In the 2020 consolidated fiscal year, financial costs of KRW 2.4 billion from foreign exchange losses, translation losses and derivative valuation losses pushed the company to a net loss despite an operating profit.

Debt is small relative to consolidated assets for 2025, but currency-related valuation gains and losses remain a source of volatility. Overseas economic conditions and changes in national insurance and healthcare policies could also affect demand.

Industry and regulatory

In medical devices, country-by-country approvals and quality compliance are prerequisites for the business. Even with FDA 510(k) clearance, sales arise only after registration with U.S. hospitals and distributors and selection by surgeons.

The spinal implant market includes both global majors and domestic makers, so pressure on product differentiation and pricing exists. Regulatory changes or quality issues could delay sales.

Governance and liquidity

Under a structure in which Dongwha Pharm holds 62.07%, the influence of minority shareholders is limited. The tradable share count is small and traded value is low, so execution feasibility and price impact need to be weighed together.

Konex stocks tend to have narrower information access and a smaller investor base than KOSPI or KOSDAQ stocks. Whether and when a listing transfer will be pursued has not been confirmed.

08

Overall view

Medyssey is a Konex-listed medical device company whose scale and profitability both improved in 2025, with consolidated revenue of KRW 28,309 million and operating income of KRW 3,953 million.

Three FDA 510(k) clearances in June, September and October 2026 widened its U.S. spinal implant range into cervical and sacroiliac joint fusion.

The company has indicated that U.S. sales will begin in the second half of 2026 and the first half of 2027, and the speed at which clearances lead to hospital registration and repeat surgical use needs to be confirmed.

A high export share exposes it to exchange-rate moves and overseas approval conditions, and trading liquidity is low as is typical for a Konex stock. Results after the first half of 2026 and any KOSDAQ transfer schedule were not confirmed in this research.

U.S. sales trends, operating margin and currency-related gains and losses in upcoming quarterly filings are therefore items to review in a balanced way. This report is for information only, and investment decisions are left to the reader.
