02

Business structure

Kisan Telecom was founded in 1994 and listed on KOSDAQ in 1999 as a maker of wired and wireless telecom equipment. Its core telecom division makes mobile repeaters, Wi-Fi access points, VoIP equipment and other products that it supplies to telecom operators on a B2B basis.

Its main products, mobile repeaters and Wi-Fi APs, are sold to operators, and press coverage describes the company as keeping its competitiveness in a market where technology changes quickly.

It has two subsidiaries: a defense subsidiary that makes defense industrial materials and telecom equipment, and an air navigation subsidiary that makes safety-of-flight radio equipment for aviation.

The air navigation subsidiary has supplied navigation safety systems to 23 countries, and the company cited its overseas sales growth as a main reason for 2025 revenue and profit gains. The defense subsidiary reportedly grew sales through export work in Poland and delivery of satellite communication terminals.

On the telecom side, the overseas push includes bidding for repeater orders from three Japanese carriers and completing development of a multi-operator, multi-band DAS for Japan. Domestically, supply tied to the expansion of 5G service coverage, particularly in-building repeaters, forms the base of the business.

Defense and aviation revenue can swing sharply from quarter to quarter because it depends on public and export contracts. Listed peers in the same electronics and components sector that handle RF equipment include KMW and Rfhic.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual revenue rose from KRW 67.8 billion in 2022 to KRW 73.9 billion in 2023 (up 9.0% year on year), KRW 93.1 billion in 2024 (up 26.0%) and KRW 99.5 billion in 2025 (up 6.9%), with three consecutive years of growth from 2023 to 2025.

Operating income bottomed at KRW 4.41 billion in 2023 (down 4.5%), then rose to KRW 4.95 billion in 2024 and KRW 8.08 billion in 2025, two consecutive years of growth. Operating income in 2025 grew 63.3%, and the operating margin reached 8.1%, above 6.8% in 2022, 6.0% in 2023 and 5.3% in 2024.

Because operating income grew 63.3% while revenue grew 6.9%, the margin improvement accounts for most of the profit gain. The company cited growth in overseas sales at its air navigation subsidiary as a main driver of 2025 results.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders was highest in 2024 at KRW 7.01 billion (up 115.3%) and fell to KRW 4.23 billion in 2025 (down 39.6%).

Net income including non-controlling interests fell from KRW 8.09 billion in 2024 to KRW 5.8 billion in 2025, and the gap between the two measures reflects the non-controlling share. Operating cash flow swung from KRW 12.4 billion in 2024 to -KRW 3.5 billion in 2025.

By quarter, Q4 2025 revenue of KRW 41.6 billion and operating income of KRW 6.01 billion were the highest of the last five quarters, while Q1 2026 revenue fell to KRW 17.8 billion (down 57.1% from the prior quarter).

Revenue rose for two consecutive quarters from Q2 to Q3 2026, but Q3 2026 recorded an operating loss of KRW 940 million and a net loss attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 2.83 billion, a swing into loss year on year.

The sum of net income attributable to controlling shareholders over the last four quarters (Q4 2025 to Q3 2026) was KRW 5.43 billion.

05

Industry analysis

Kisan Telecom's telecom division depends on repeater demand tied to the expansion of 5G service coverage in Korea. Telecom equipment is sensitive to carriers' capital spending cycles, so domestic investment trends and overseas bid outcomes are the main variables for telecom revenue.

Overseas, the key effort is bidding for repeater orders from three Japanese carriers, and reports describe growing sales of integrated repeaters in the Japanese telecom market. In defense, export work in Poland and delivery of satellite communication terminals were cited as growth drivers at the defense subsidiary.

Defense exports are recognized contract by contract, so quarterly swings can be large. Navigation safety systems at the aviation subsidiary follow airport infrastructure orders, and that subsidiary has supplied systems to 23 countries.

KMW and Rfhic, peers in the same sector, focus on RF equipment and components, so their product mix differs from Kisan Telecom's. Kisan Telecom combines telecom repeaters with defense and aviation subsidiaries, so its results are not tied to a single equipment cycle.

As the telecom share shrinks relative to defense and aviation, results may become more sensitive to the timing of orders.

Bull factors

Operating profit recovery and margin gains

Operating income in 2025 was KRW 8.08 billion, up 63.3% year on year, and the 8.1% margin was the highest of 2022–2025. Profit growth outpaced revenue growth of 6.9%, which points to a better earnings structure. A tally also shows operating income turning positive in the first half of 2026.

Overseas sales and defense and aviation diversification

The air navigation subsidiary has supplied navigation safety systems to 23 countries. The defense subsidiary is growing sales through export work in Poland and satellite communication terminal deliveries.

Improving balance sheet

The debt-to-equity ratio fell to 95.9% in 2025 from 114.7% in 2024. Total equity at the end of 2025 was KRW 66.5 billion, of which KRW 54.6 billion belongs to controlling shareholders. Operating cash flow reached KRW 12.4 billion in 2024, so the company has shown the ability to generate cash in some years.

Bear factors

Swing to an operating loss in Q3

Q3 2026 revenue rose 4.2% year on year to KRW 21.6 billion, yet the quarter posted an operating loss of KRW 940 million. The net loss attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 2.83 billion, a swing into loss year on year. Higher revenue did not translate into profit, which puts the durability of the profit recovery in question.

Earnings volatility and weaker cash flow

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders fell 39.6% from KRW 7.01 billion in 2024 to KRW 4.23 billion in 2025. Because operating cash flow turned negative in 2025, whether cash generation recovers is a point to check.

Controlling-shareholder net income then declined to KRW 2.43 billion in Q1 2026 and KRW 2.03 billion in Q2 2026.

Growth dependent on uncertain overseas orders

Results of the bids for repeater orders from three Japanese carriers have not yet been disclosed. The payoff from the overseas strategy depends on winning bids and on export results. Because overseas sales growth drove 2025 results, a slowdown in that flow could weaken the base for the profit recovery.

Risk factors

Earnings volatility

Quarterly revenue swings are large. Revenue was KRW 41.6 billion in Q4 2025 and fell to KRW 17.8 billion in Q1 2026, down 57.1% from the prior quarter. Defense and aviation sales can bunch or drop out depending on contract timing, which lowers the predictability of quarterly results.

Governance and major shareholder stake

The combined stake held by the largest shareholder and related parties was 34.41% in the 2026 half-year report, down from 35.22%. An executive, Kim Po-hyun, sold 10,000 shares on the open market on May 6, 2026, which reduced his holding to zero. The sale is an individual matter, but it adds to changes in the shareholder structure.

Overseas bidding and business concentration

Overseas business depends on Japanese bids and export contracts. The telecom division is tied to domestic 5G investment and carrier orders. If defense exports in Poland and overseas aviation supply concentrate in specific countries or contracts, changes in those buyers' orders feed directly into results.
