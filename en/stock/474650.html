Lincsolution: Losses Persist Ahead of Foundry Completion

Lincsolution's revenue rose to KRW 13.1 billion in 2025, yet its operating loss widened to KRW 7.38 billion, and the Daejeon foundry scheduled for completion in December 2026 and mass-production orders will determine whether earnings can turn.

02

Business structure

Lincsolution is an industrial 3D printing specialist founded in 2015 that developed and commercialized SLA, FDM and MBJ 3D printers with its own technology.

The company listed on KOSDAQ in June 2025 and in January 2026 acquired a stake in AM Solutions, a robot-based metal additive manufacturing firm, consolidating it as a subsidiary.

As of the first nine months of 2025, equipment sales made up 77% of revenue and 3D printing services, which deliver printed parts to customers, made up 23%. The core strategy is a 3D printing foundry in Daejeon that handles high-volume printing and mass production based on customers' design data.

The Daejeon plant is scheduled for completion around the end of 2026, and the construction investment has been reported at KRW 49 billion.

Large domestic companies such as Hyundai Motor, LG Electronics and Boston Dynamics have been cited as target customers, and the company has also signed an equipment sales contract with Samsung Electronics.

Disclosed major orders include a KRW 4.7 billion metre-class PBF metal additive manufacturing system supply contract with the Daejeon Regional Procurement Office in March 2026, a KRW 900 million equipment sales contract with Samsung Electronics in April 2026, and a KRW 656 million additive manufacturing development project with Hanwha Aerospace disclosed at the end of September 2026.

The company has set a goal of shifting its revenue mix from equipment sales toward services. In competition, the company's SLA and FDM technologies are cited as holding the number one global market share, and a domestic peer offering both equipment and services is described as rare.

04

Earnings analysis

Based on disclosed results, annual revenue rose from KRW 7.36 billion in 2023 to KRW 11.2 billion in 2024 (up 52.0% year on year) and KRW 13.1 billion in 2025 (up 17.3% year on year), growing for two consecutive years from 2024 to 2025. Of the 2023 to 2025 period, 2025 was the largest year for revenue.

Over the same span operating income went from KRW 779 million in 2023 to an operating loss of KRW 4 billion in 2024, which marked a swing into loss, and then to an operating loss of KRW 7.38 billion in 2025, when the loss widened.

The operating margin fell from 10.6% in 2023 to -35.7% in 2024 and -56.2% in 2025, so losses grew relative to sales even as revenue increased.

Research and development spending has been cited as a driver, with reports noting average annual R&D of about KRW 1.3 billion over 2022 to 2025 on the business reports, roughly 15% of revenue over the same period.

In 2025, cost increases tied to preparations for the Daejeon plant and expanded R&D investment were described as enlarging the operating loss.

The net loss attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 12.2 billion in 2023, KRW 2.54 billion in 2024 (loss narrowed) and KRW 6.98 billion in 2025 (loss widened), marking three consecutive years of losses since 2023.

Total equity at the end of 2025 was KRW 38.2 billion and total liabilities were KRW 27.8 billion, with the debt ratio rising from 60.5% in 2024 to 72.9% in 2025. Operating cash flow was -KRW 786 million in 2023, -KRW 5.76 billion in 2024 and -KRW 5.46 billion in 2025.

On a quarterly basis, revenue of KRW 1.93 billion and an operating loss of KRW 1.39 billion in the third quarter of 2025 were followed by revenue of KRW 1.57 billion and an operating loss of KRW 3.69 billion in the first quarter of 2026.

Second-quarter 2026 revenue reached KRW 4.27 billion, up 172.1% from the prior quarter, but the operating loss widened to KRW 4.64 billion, while the controlling-shareholder net income swung to a profit of KRW 20.68 million.

Third-quarter 2026 revenue was KRW 1.37 billion, down 67.9% from the prior quarter and down 29.0% year on year, and the operating loss of KRW 4.3 billion was smaller than in the prior quarter but larger than a year earlier.

As a result the controlling-shareholder net loss returned to KRW 4.19 billion in the third quarter, and the controlling-shareholder net losses over the last four quarters totaled KRW 10.1 billion.

The company has posted operating losses for three consecutive quarters from the first quarter of 2026 through the third quarter.

05

Industry analysis

3D printing is an additive manufacturing method that builds parts without molds, and its demand is spreading from automotive and aerospace toward robotics and data centers.

In a June 2026 report, Shinhan Investment Corp. said Lincsolution is likely to grow into mass-production infrastructure based on 3D printing across advanced industries such as humanoid robots, space, defense and data centers, while noting that it did not present an investment opinion or target price.

The same report said the company is running PoC projects in parallel with global companies in humanoid, space, defense and data center segments.

A February 2026 report noted continued requests for additional PoC work on robot parts and an assessment that data center heat exchangers have reached a technical level suitable for mass production.

In defense, the Hanwha Aerospace development project disclosed at the end of September 2026 was described by the company as an additive manufacturing development project for space launch vehicle parts.

On demand growth, FnGuide company information cites a forecast of 17.94% annual growth for the Asia-Pacific 3D printing market.

In terms of cycle position, the business appears to be before large mass-production orders are confirmed, and Shinhan said the shift from PoC to mass production during the third and fourth quarters of 2026 is important.

In competition, the company points to its SLA and FDM technologies, which hold the number one global share, and to the rarity of domestic players offering both equipment and services, according to Korea IR Council's assessment.

Korea IR Council also noted that equipment sales are sensitive to economic cycles, while service revenue can support earnings stability through recurring demand and long-term customers.

06

Outlook

The nearest checkpoint for the company is completion of the Daejeon plant. In a June 2026 report, Shinhan Investment Corp. described the Daejeon plant, scheduled for completion in December 2026, as the first and largest 3D printing foundry in Korea.

The same report projected that if customer PoC projects move into mass production, order sizes per item could expand from several hundred billion won to more than KRW 100 billion.

Shinhan's estimates call for revenue of KRW 24.5 billion and an operating loss of KRW 2.1 billion in 2026, turning into a profit in 2027 with revenue of KRW 60.9 billion and operating income of KRW 11.9 billion.

These are broker estimates, and the company had reportedly set a 2027 net income target of KRW 7.7 billion during its technology-based listing process. On orders, the contract period for the KRW 4.7 billion Daejeon Regional Procurement Office contract runs to March 6, 2027.

Reports said KRW 17 billion of the KRW 30 billion convertible bond issued in September 2026 would be added to facility funding, and a second plant investment could move forward earlier depending on contracts.

Data center heat exchangers have reached a technical level suitable for mass production, and additional capacity investment has been mentioned if mass-production orders are received. Shinhan emphasized that orders matter more than earnings for 2026.

The next items to confirm are whether mass-production orders are finalized in the fourth quarter of 2026 and the company's preliminary full-year 2026 results.

Bull factors

Technology lead and process integration

The company is cited as holding the number one global share in SLA and FDM technologies. With the January 2026 acquisition of AM Solutions, it says it added robot-based large-scale manufacturing capability to metal 3D printing and completed a portfolio covering the full metal manufacturing process.

Its combination of equipment sales and printing services is also cited as a rare strength among domestic players.

Order flow and national projects

Disclosed orders have included a KRW 4.7 billion contract with the Daejeon Regional Procurement Office in March 2026, a KRW 900 million contract with Samsung Electronics in April 2026, and a KRW 656 million Hanwha Aerospace project at the end of September 2026.

MoneyToday reported that national project orders known since June 2025 exceed KRW 20 billion. Shinhan Investment Corp. analyzed that order sizes per item could grow from several hundred billion won to more than KRW 100 billion if PoC projects move into mass production.

Foundry shift and wider demand

The Daejeon plant is scheduled for completion in December 2026, and NH Investment & Securities analyzed that it would secure annual capacity of about KRW 70 billion. Demand is broadening into space, robotics and data centers, which leaves room for recurring service revenue to rise as a share of sales.

The company has presented a plan to lower the share of equipment sales and shift its profit structure toward services.

Bear factors

Persistent operating losses and wider deficits

The operating margin fell to -35.7% in 2024 and -56.2% in 2025. The company has posted operating losses for three consecutive quarters from the first quarter of 2026 through the third quarter, and the KRW 4.3 billion operating loss in the third quarter was larger than a year earlier. Controlling-shareholder net losses over the last four quarters total KRW 10.1 billion.

Funding needs and shrinking equity

MoneyToday reported that the company issued a KRW 30 billion convertible bond in September 2026, more than the KRW 20.7 billion raised in its IPO. The same report said total equity fell to KRW 35.3 billion at the end of June 2026 and the accumulated deficit stood at KRW 30.9 billion. Operating cash flow has been negative every year from 2023 to 2025.

Verification tasks and governance questions

MoneyToday reported that the specifications of the KRW 4.7 billion equipment contract in March 2026 match those of German EOS products, and that questions have been raised because the bidding partner is a domestic EOS reseller. The auditor has changed twice in the most recent three fiscal years.

Shinhan Investment Corp. explained that its 2027 turnaround forecast depends on the confirmation of orders in 2026.

Risk factors

Plant investment and order conversion

The Daejeon plant's investment is reported at KRW 49 billion. An industry source said general factory construction costs run about KRW 3 million to KRW 5 million per pyeong, while the plant reportedly cost about KRW 6 million per pyeong. If orders do not follow after operations begin, fixed-cost burdens could flow directly into results.

Financing and balance sheet

Total liabilities were KRW 27.8 billion at the end of 2025, and the debt ratio rose from 60.5% in 2024 to 72.9% in 2025. The company raised funds through a KRW 30 billion convertible bond in September 2026, and any dilution from conversion would depend on its terms. Operating cash flow of -KRW 5.46 billion in 2025 has prompted criticism of heavy reliance on outside funding.

Demand concentration and contract verification

Demand is concentrated in specific sectors such as humanoid robots, defense and data centers, so orders could be affected if investment cycles in those industries slow. The KRW 4.7 billion contract runs until March 6, 2027, and revenue depends on completing performance and acceptance.

The questions about equipment specifications and the change of auditors remain factors that call for verification of disclosed information.

Overall view

Lincsolution's disclosed results show revenue rising from KRW 7.36 billion in 2023 to KRW 13.1 billion in 2025, while the operating loss and the controlling-shareholder net loss persisted over the same period.

Quarterly results in 2026 also showed large swings in revenue, with three consecutive quarters of operating losses, and controlling-shareholder net losses over the last four quarters total KRW 10.1 billion.

On orders, disclosed contracts include KRW 4.7 billion with the Daejeon Regional Procurement Office, KRW 900 million with Samsung Electronics and KRW 656 million with Hanwha Aerospace, and reports say national project orders exceed KRW 20 billion.

Completion of the Daejeon plant and confirmation of mass-production orders are preconditions for a turnaround, and Shinhan Investment Corp. forecasts a 2027 profit while stating that 2026 orders are the key.

On the other hand, the KRW 30 billion convertible bond, the decline in equity, questions about equipment specifications and the change of auditors remain items to verify on financing and governance.

The company currently shows both bullish and bearish factors, and the pace of its business transition and its funding conditions are being tested at the same time.

The next checkpoints are the completion of the Daejeon plant in December 2026, whether mass-production orders come in the fourth quarter of 2026, and the operating margin in the 2026 annual business report.
