Sales Scale Up, but the Strong Won Pressures Margins

02

Business structure

Silicon 2 is a global K-beauty distributor that sells cosmetics in overseas markets, buying products outright and then supplying overseas retail and wholesale accounts. Samsung Securities analyzed that, because the company buys products outright and then sells them abroad, exposure to exchange rates is unavoidable.

According to company IR data for the third quarter of 2026, Europe was the largest region at KRW 160.6 billion, or 38% of revenue. North American revenue reached KRW 108.5 billion, up 49.4%, with growth linked to expanded business with large US retailers such as Ulta Beauty and Target.

Latin American revenue reached KRW 34.2 billion, up 123.3%, the fastest growth among the regions. The company began with online and B2B channels, and according to a Seoul Economic Daily report it is opening its MOIDA K-beauty select-shop format across the United States through year-end.

It also operates a global K-beauty e-commerce platform. On infrastructure, a UK logistics warehouse began operating in the third quarter, and Brazil and Japan subsidiaries are building local sales systems.

Competitively, Silicon 2 sits more on the overseas distribution and sales side than on product manufacturing, so its role differs from manufacturing-focused peers in the same industry.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual revenue rose from KRW 165.3 billion in 2022 to KRW 342.9 billion in 2023 (up 107.5%), KRW 691.5 billion in 2024 (up 101.7%) and KRW 1.12 trillion in 2025 (up 61.4%), and it grew three years in a row from 2023 through 2025.

Operating profit went from KRW 14.2 billion in 2022 to KRW 137.6 billion in 2024 (up 187.8%) and then KRW 205.4 billion in 2025 (up 49.3%). The operating margin was 8.6% in 2022, 13.9% in 2023 and 19.9% in 2024, then eased to 18.4% in 2025, a year in which revenue growth outpaced operating profit growth.

Attributable net income rose from KRW 11.2 billion in 2022 to KRW 168.6 billion in 2025 (up 39.6%), and net income including non-controlling interests was disclosed at the same amounts.

The debt ratio fell to 55.1% at end-2025 from 75.0% in 2024, with total liabilities of KRW 252 billion and total equity of KRW 457.4 billion, all of it attributable to owners of the parent.

Operating cash flow swung widely, at KRW 9.85 billion in 2022, -KRW 18.5 billion in 2023, KRW 60.1 billion in 2024 and KRW 1.14 billion in 2025. Quarterly revenue rose four quarters in a row, from KRW 299.4 billion in the third quarter of 2025 to KRW 424.4 billion in the third quarter of 2026.

Operating profit fell to KRW 42.4 billion in the fourth quarter of 2025, recovered to KRW 83 billion in the second quarter of 2026, and then dropped to KRW 69.6 billion in the third quarter, down 16.1% from the prior quarter.

The main cause of the third-quarter margin decline was a stronger won, as the average rate for key currencies fell about 6% from the prior quarter and gross margin slid from 32.0% to 28.7%.

Selling and administrative expenses rose 66.2% year on year to KRW 52.2 billion, and attributable net income fell 84.9% to KRW 8.8 billion.

Non-operating items posted a KRW 47.5 billion loss, and the company said unrealized translation losses can reverse and that one-off gains, including a US tariff refund, partly offset the loss.

05

Industry analysis

Cosmetics exports have kept growing even as cosmetics stocks have weakened. E-Today reported that monthly cosmetics exports first topped USD 1.2 billion in September 2026, while cosmetics shares fell over the same period.

Korea Investment & Securities said Silicon 2's revenue growth has been faster than the growth in cosmetics exports, so its influence in the industry is widening, while also noting greater sensitivity to exchange rates.

The average rate for key trading currencies fell about 6% from the prior quarter in the third quarter, reinforcing the won's strength. Samsung Securities reflected a decline in price-to-earnings multiples across global cosmetics and duty-free stocks in its target price cut.

Cosmax posted record quarterly results in the third quarter, and a Financial News analysis expects its quarterly revenue to reach KRW 900 billion next year. In the United States, securing large retail channels such as Ulta Beauty and Target has become a central growth driver.

Korea Investment & Securities expects European retailers to increase restocking orders in the fourth quarter, while Kiwoom Securities describes Latin America as an early stage, with account expansion centered on large wholesalers that already dominate department stores and duty-free shops.

06

Outlook

The company started operating a UK logistics warehouse in the third quarter, and its Brazil subsidiary will begin full operations once warehouse space and permits are secured. Japan's subsidiary has been established, and deliveries become possible after March 2027 following approval from the Japanese health authority.

In the US, talks with Walmart and Costco are ongoing, and Kiwoom Securities expects related results to be reflected from 2027 onward. Korea Investment & Securities said a price increase could restore gross margin to around 30% in the fourth quarter.

Samsung Securities expects gross margin to recover gradually in the fourth quarter as currency volatility eases and selling prices are adjusted. Kiwoom Securities, by contrast, expects the won's strength to persist in the fourth quarter and keep translation pressure elevated.

The company said it is defending margins through price adjustments and renegotiated trading terms, and will strengthen currency hedging with FX-linked financial products. Korea Investment & Securities projected 2026 consolidated revenue of KRW 1.602 trillion and operating profit of KRW 274 billion, as estimates.

Inventory at the end of September 2026 was KRW 364.9 billion, down 19.1% from June, and Samsung Securities noted inventory turnover shortened from about 150 days to about 110 days.

After issuing KRW 300 billion of redeemable convertible preferred shares, quarter-end cash and cash equivalents rose to KRW 447.5 billion, which the company plans to use for global infrastructure and market share expansion.

08

Bull factors

Breadth of top-line growth

Third-quarter 2026 revenue reached KRW 424.4 billion, up 41.7% year on year, and preliminary cumulative revenue for January to September of KRW 1.17 trillion already exceeded full-year 2025 revenue. Europe rose 57.7%, North America 49.4% and Latin America 123.3%, so every major region grew by double digits. The company said quarterly revenue set a record for the seventh straight quarter.

Inventory and working-capital improvement

Inventory at the end of the third quarter was KRW 364.9 billion, down 19.1% from the end of June. Samsung Securities analyzed that inventory turnover shortened from about 150 days to about 110 days, easing working-capital pressure. The company said its shipping cost ratio fell to 3.1% from 3.4% in the prior quarter.

Financial capacity

Cash and cash equivalents at quarter-end rose to KRW 447.5 billion, reflecting the KRW 300 billion redeemable convertible preferred share issuance in September. The company plans to use the funds for global infrastructure and market share expansion. The debt ratio fell to 55.1% at end-2025 from 75.0% in 2024.

09

Bear factors

Margin pressure from currency

Gross margin fell to 28.7% in the third quarter from 32.0% in the prior quarter, mainly because the average rate for key currencies dropped about 6%. A company official said most revenue is generated in foreign currency, which requires stronger currency management. Kiwoom Securities expects the won's strength to persist into the fourth quarter.

Cost growth and an operating profit miss

Selling and administrative expenses rose 66.2% year on year to KRW 52.2 billion, against KRW 31.4 billion a year earlier. Third-quarter operating profit of KRW 69.6 billion fell about 15% short of market consensus, according to Kiwoom Securities.

Korea Investment & Securities analyzed that payroll-related spending rose 71% from a year earlier due to hiring for new businesses.

Sharp drop in net profit

Attributable net income for the third quarter fell 84.9% year on year to KRW 8.8 billion. The KRW 47.5 billion non-operating loss came mainly from foreign-currency translation losses, which the company said can reverse for the unrealized portion.

Samsung Securities also pointed to KRW 2.3 billion in bad-debt provisions related to overseas accounts as a drag on profitability.

10

Risk factors

Currency movements

Because most revenue is denominated in foreign currency, a sustained stronger won lowers both won-converted revenue and gross margin. The company says it is responding with selling price adjustments and FX-linked financial products.

Translation losses have swung quarterly net profit sharply, so the direction of the currency remains a major variable.

Overseas expansion costs

Overseas subsidiaries and hiring for new businesses have been lifting selling and administrative expenses quickly. Early hiring for the MOIDA stores and the Japan business raised payroll-related spending by 71% from a year earlier. Costs can land on the income statement before revenue from new markets reaches scale.

Permits and customer concentration

Deliveries to Japan are possible only after health authority approval, and the Brazil business depends on securing warehouse space and permits first. North American growth relies on large retail accounts such as Ulta Beauty and Target. Inventory built at peak levels in the second quarter to hedge freight increases also remains a cost burden.

12

Overall view

Silicon 2 expanded its sales base quickly through the third quarter of 2026. Third-quarter revenue reached KRW 424.4 billion, up 41.7% year on year, with North America and Latin America standing out.

However, a stronger won pushed gross margin down to 28.7%, and higher selling expenses combined with translation losses cut attributable net income to KRW 8.8 billion.

On a full-year 2025 basis, revenue was KRW 1.12 trillion, operating profit KRW 205.4 billion and attributable net income KRW 168.6 billion, while the debt ratio fell to 55.1%.

Brokers cut target prices on November 12 and 13, 2026, but Kiwoom Securities, Samsung Securities and Korea Investment & Securities said they maintained Buy ratings. On the strength side, regional diversification, lower inventory and higher cash stand out.

On the weakness side, currency sensitivity and rising costs remain. The next checkpoints are gross margin in the fourth quarter of 2026 and the full-year figures in the 2026 annual report.
