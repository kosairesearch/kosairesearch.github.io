Sales Up, Profit Down at a Welfare-Mall Leader

02

Business structure

Hyundai Ezwel operates a closed online welfare mall for corporate and public-sector employees, making it a B2B welfare platform company.

Its model rests on selective welfare systems in which employees choose products within a budget allocated to them, and its customer base grew from about 2,100 companies in 2021 to 2,700 at the end of 2024.

The mall carries about 2.1 million products, which the company describes as the largest assortment in the industry, and food, furniture and fashion items from group affiliates are a major part of the supply.

Revenue comes mainly from commissions that partners pay when employees buy products, and most sales come from the selective welfare business.

The scope has widened: in November 2022 the company acquired a meal-voucher operator that is among the top sellers in Korea, and it is expanding business process outsourcing for general affairs such as funeral services and flower arrangements, along with public welfare programs and a local currency mall.

Small and mid-sized partners also play a large role; in May 2025 the company said such partners account for 80% of mall transaction value. The competitive structure is an oligopoly of three companies in domestic selective welfare, and group affiliates' product strength and sales channels are cited as differentiators.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual results show steady growth in the top line. Sales rose from KRW 112.5 billion in 2022 to KRW 118 billion in 2023 (up 4.9% year on year), KRW 131.1 billion in 2024 (up 11.1%) and KRW 142.6 billion in 2025 (up 8.8%), and sales have increased for three consecutive years from 2023 through 2025.

Operating profit dipped to KRW 18.3 billion in 2023 (down 0.9%) before rising to KRW 20.2 billion in 2024 (up 10.4%) and KRW 24.2 billion in 2025 (up 19.5%), a two-year run of gains from 2024 to 2025. The operating margin moved from 16.5% in 2022 to 15.5% in 2023 and 15.4% in 2024, then recovered to 17.0% in 2025.

In 2023 profit attributable to owners of the parent swung to a net loss of KRW 532 million, and in 2024 it returned to a profit of KRW 11.9 billion. In 2025 that profit reached KRW 24 billion, up 101.3% year on year, and net profit including non-controlling interests was also KRW 24 billion.

Operating cash flow ranged from KRW 24 billion in 2022 and KRW 31 billion in 2024 to KRW 11.4 billion in 2023, showing wide swings. The debt ratio went from 119.4% in 2022 to 131.6% in 2024 and then 121.2% in 2025. The quarterly direction has reversed.

Sales were KRW 46.3 billion in the first quarter of 2026, with operating profit of KRW 7.35 billion as the peak, and fell 35.1% quarter on quarter to KRW 30.1 billion in the second quarter. Third-quarter sales of KRW 37.8 billion rose 12.4% year on year, but operating profit of KRW 2.33 billion fell 51.4%.

Profit attributable to owners of the parent was KRW 2.74 billion in the third quarter, and the four-quarter total is KRW 15.4 billion. With sales rising while profit falls, cost pressure is the central question, and the cost items behind it can be checked in the 2026 annual report.

05

Industry analysis

The welfare-mall market is shaped by growing employee interest in corporate welfare, which has led more companies to outsource operations to specialist providers. Based on 2024 estimates, the market was about KRW 3.2 trillion and is expected to grow to about KRW 4.8 trillion by 2030.

Selective welfare systems are considered to have low economic sensitivity because they run continuously and employees tend to spend their budgets within the year.

Domestic selective welfare is an oligopoly of three companies including Hyundai Ezwel, and a Kiwoom Securities analysis puts the company's share at about 50%. In that analysis, the market is in a growth phase, expanding as more workplaces adopt the system.

On competition, the company points to its group affiliates' products and its large customer base as strengths. Kiwoom Securities also judged that existing customers are unlikely to cut their welfare policies.

Listed companies in the same IT and software classification have different business models, such as platforms or system integration, so direct comparisons of margins or multiples are limited. Expansion into public welfare and local currency malls is a structural change to monitor.

06

Outlook

The near-term picture shows up first in first-half 2026 results. First-half sales rose 5.3% year on year, but operating profit fell 28.4% and net profit fell 45.8% (corporate monitoring data as of August 31, 2026).

On a nine-month basis through September 2026, the sales growth rate was 7.6% while the operating profit decline widened to 33.9%.

In 2025 the company said it would put KRW 30 billion into developing a next-generation welfare-mall platform, and it has been pursuing AI adoption and next-day delivery for food, home appliances and furniture.

Kiwoom Securities judged that amortization tied to building the next-generation platform could be reflected later. Expansion is proceeding in several directions, including meal-voucher services, business process outsourcing, public welfare and local currency malls.

On May 6, 2026 the company voluntarily disclosed the 2025 implementation status of its corporate value-up plan. The term of an executive director ends in March 2027, which is a schedule item for watching management changes.

08

Bull factors

Leading position in selective welfare malls

A Kiwoom Securities analysis puts Hyundai Ezwel's market share at about 50%, and three companies dominate domestic selective welfare. Most revenue comes from the selective welfare business through commissions on employee purchases. Third-quarter 2026 sales of KRW 37.8 billion rose 12.4% year on year, so the revenue base is intact.

Expanding customer and product base

The customer base grew from about 2,100 companies in 2021 to 2,700 at the end of 2024, and the company says the mall carries about 2.1 million products. Group affiliates' food, furniture and fashion items add assortment depth. The company also said transaction value from small and mid-sized partners rose 98% compared with 2020.

Shareholder return policy and continued sales growth

In its 2025 corporate value-up plan the company set a policy of paying at least 20% of separate operating profit as dividends. On May 6, 2026 it voluntarily disclosed the 2025 implementation status of that plan. Nine-month 2026 sales of KRW 114.2 billion rose 7.6%, so top-line growth continues.

Bear factors

Widening profit decline

Third-quarter 2026 operating profit of KRW 2.33 billion fell 51.4% year on year. Nine-month 2026 operating profit fell 33.9% to KRW 13.17 billion (provisional, disclosed November 3, 2026), and profit attributable to owners of the parent fell 42.9% to KRW 11.48 billion. Because sales grew while profit fell, the change in cost structure needs to be confirmed in the annual report.

Consecutive quarterly declines

Operating profit peaked at KRW 7.35 billion in the first quarter of 2026 and then fell to KRW 3.48 billion in the second quarter and KRW 2.33 billion in the third. Second-quarter sales of KRW 30.1 billion fell 35.1% from the prior quarter. First-half 2026 operating profit fell 28.4% year on year, so the quarterly weakness spans the first half.

Debt burden relative to equity

At the end of 2025, total liabilities were KRW 132.4 billion against total equity of KRW 109.3 billion, a debt ratio of 121.2%. That is slightly above the 119.4% of 2022 and below the 131.6% of 2024.

In 2023 the company recorded a net loss attributable to owners of KRW 532 million and operating cash flow of KRW 11.4 billion, showing large earnings swings.

10

Risk factors

Margin pressure

If the operating margin weakens while sales grow, top-line growth does not reach profit. The margin was 17.0% in 2025, but operating profit fell in both the second and third quarters of 2026. On a nine-month basis the operating profit decline far exceeds the sales growth, so cost items need to be reviewed.

Next-generation platform investment burden

The company has said it plans to invest KRW 30 billion in developing a next-generation welfare-mall platform. Kiwoom Securities judged that amortization related to building that platform could be recognized later.

Once the investment is fully reflected in the accounts, it could add pressure to the operating margin, and the results of AI and other technology investment also need to be confirmed.

Customer policy and competitive landscape

The market grows as more employers adopt selective welfare systems, so reduced welfare budgets at client companies could affect transaction value. Although three companies dominate domestic selective welfare, rivals' technology investment or price competition could intensify. Reliance on group affiliates for supply is both a source of product strength and a structural variable.

12

Overall view

Hyundai Ezwel is a B2B welfare platform company with a market share of about 50% in selective welfare malls, placing it among the leaders. Sales rose from KRW 112.5 billion in 2022 to KRW 142.6 billion in 2025, and operating profit peaked at KRW 24.2 billion in 2025 within that period.

Profit attributable to owners of the parent recovered from a 2023 net loss to KRW 11.9 billion in 2024 and KRW 24 billion in 2025, which is the basis for the improvement in earnings.

In 2026, however, the top line and profit have diverged: nine-month sales rose 7.6% while operating profit fell 33.9%, and quarterly operating profit has fallen for two straight quarters after peaking in the first quarter.

Strengths are the base of 2,700 client companies, sourcing from group affiliates and an oligopolistic market structure, while weaknesses are the risk of worsening cost structure and the burden of platform investment.

The next checkpoints are the fourth-quarter and full-year 2026 provisional results, the cost items in the 2026 annual report, and the first-quarter 2027 provisional results.
