Ground-station growth, profitability only just turning

Contec has lifted 2025 revenue to KRW 86.9 billion by pairing ground-station services with satellite manufacturing, but operating profit only turned positive in the third quarter of 2026.

02

Business structure

Contec spans several stages of the space value chain, from building and operating satellite ground stations to analyzing satellite data.

Its downstream business covers ground-station systems, GSaaS (Ground Station as a Service) network solutions that let customers rent ground-station capacity, and satellite imagery data processing solutions.

According to a Meritz Securities estimate table in its August 2025 report, the largest 2024 revenue item was the ground-station system engineering solution. Its satellite communication terminal business serves THURAYA and Inmarsat as core customers across several regions.

The upstream business, built on satellite manufacturing capability acquired through a stake purchase in July 2024, supplies satellite components and bodies to the UAE and Europe. Its antenna-manufacturing subsidiary TXSpace handles antenna mass production, according to Daishin Securities' June 2025 report.

Hana Securities stated in its January 2026 report that the company operates 16 ground stations across 11 countries. The company says it keeps antenna manufacturing through data reception, processing and analysis within one in-house system.

Its customers include long-term supply contracts with government agencies and local governments as well as multinational turnkey projects. The satellite communication terminal business has been described as limited in profitability because of cost burdens. The business structure still rests on two pillars: ground-station services and satellite manufacturing.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual results show diverging trends in revenue and losses. Revenue rose from KRW 12.8 billion in 2022 to KRW 15.8 billion in 2023 (up 23.9% year on year) and KRW 69 billion in 2024 (up 336.2%), then reached KRW 86.9 billion in 2025 (up 26.0%), the largest year in the 2022 to 2025 period.

Daishin Securities explained that the satellite manufacturing subsidiary acquired through a stake purchase in July 2024 was fully reflected in 2025 results. Operating losses widened, however, from KRW 1.86 billion in 2022 to KRW 9.98 billion in 2023, KRW 12.2 billion in 2024 and KRW 16.2 billion in 2025.

The operating margin was -14.6% in 2022, -63.1% in 2023, -17.7% in 2024 and -18.6% in 2025. Cited drivers of the wider losses include heavy capital spending for building ground stations and in-house technology, cost burdens, and higher R&D expenses.

Net loss attributable to controlling shareholders narrowed from KRW 64.9 billion in 2023 to KRW 22.6 billion in 2024 and KRW 7.35 billion in 2025.

The 2025 net loss including non-controlling interests was KRW 10.3 billion, larger than the loss attributable to controlling shareholders, and the gap reflects losses borne by non-controlling interests.

At the end of 2025, total liabilities were KRW 95.1 billion, total equity KRW 178 billion, and equity attributable to controlling shareholders KRW 83.3 billion, with a debt ratio of 53.4%. Operating cash flow was KRW 3.31 billion in 2022, -KRW 6.07 billion in 2023, KRW 1.85 billion in 2024 and -KRW 778 million in 2025.

On a standalone basis, the company said in a May 14, 2026 announcement that the fourth quarter of 2025 was its first quarterly profit since listing on KOSDAQ.

Consolidated revenue peaked at KRW 26.3 billion in the fourth quarter of 2025, then fell to KRW 18.2 billion in the first quarter of 2026, KRW 18 billion in the second and KRW 16.2 billion in the third, three straight quarterly declines.

Operating results went from a KRW 5.02 billion loss in the third quarter of 2025 and a KRW 665 million loss in the fourth quarter to a KRW 2.44 billion loss in the first quarter of 2026 and a KRW 6.8 billion loss in the second, before the third quarter of 2026 posted an operating profit of KRW 255 million.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders swung between KRW 4.66 billion in the fourth quarter of 2025, KRW 738 million in the first quarter of 2026, KRW 6.61 billion in the second and KRW 735 million in the third. How much of this was one-off is a question for the footnotes of the regular reports.

05

Industry analysis

The satellite ground-station and satellite data services market is described as one where demand for ground stations grows as the number of satellites increases. Daishin Securities introduced Contec in its June 2025 report as Asia's only private ground-station company.

Domestically, the normalization of aerospace licensing procedures and budget execution after the new government took office was cited as an improving bidding environment, according to Daishin Securities' June 2025 report.

KB Securities, in its February 2026 report, pointed to rising ground-station maintenance demand and the expansion of the GSaaS market as downstream growth drivers. On the upstream side, revenue recognition from new orders and the shift to mass production was cited as a growth factor.

In cycle terms, the company appears to have moved past heavy capital spending that began in 2024 into a phase of a broader revenue base, but profit remains near break-even. Overseas turnkey orders and government project timing largely determine when results land.

Few listed domestic IT and software companies deal directly with satellite ground stations, so sector-average comparisons carry limited meaning. Against peers, Contec positions its combination of satellite manufacturing and ground-station operation as its differentiator.

06

Outlook

The company's medium-term direction can be summarized as expanding global orders and broader system development after its own satellite launch. In its May 2026 first-quarter results announcement, the company said it is aggressively expanding global orders.

System development has been advancing since its self-developed satellite launched in 2024, and development of next-generation network solutions is also under way. Broker forecasts diverge.

Hana Securities, in its January 2026 report, projected 2026 revenue of KRW 100.41 billion and an operating profit of KRW 6.27 billion, a swing to profit. KB Securities, in its February 2026 report, projected 2026 revenue of KRW 120 billion and an operating loss of KRW 6 billion.

Disclosed revenue for the first three quarters of 2026 ran at roughly KRW 16.2 billion to KRW 18.2 billion per quarter, so reaching either forecast would require a sharp revenue recognition step-up in the fourth quarter.

On the other hand, the return to operating profit in the third quarter of 2026 can be read as evidence of improving earnings capacity. KB Securities also noted that delays in participation schedules for public satellite development projects could push back revenue recognition.

The next checkpoint is full-year 2026 revenue and operating profit in the annual report, followed by whether profitable quarters continue in the first quarter of 2027.

08

Bull factors

Recurring services and overseas orders

The customer base for recurring-use services such as GSaaS is widening. Daishin Securities said in its June 2025 report that GSaaS customers in more than ten countries were under contract and using the service repeatedly. The company said in May 2026 that it is aggressively expanding global orders.

A shift in quarterly profit direction

On a standalone basis, the fourth quarter of 2025 was the first quarterly profit since listing, and the first quarter of 2026 continued that profit. On a consolidated basis, operating profit of KRW 255 million in the third quarter of 2026 marked a turn from loss to profit. The change in profit direction is evidence of an improving profit structure.

In-house capability from satellite build to data analysis

Contec's differentiator is that it has internalized the value chain from satellite manufacturing to ground-station communication and data analysis. The company describes an in-house system spanning antenna manufacturing through data reception, processing and analysis. System development has picked up since its self-developed satellite, broadening the technology base.

09

Bear factors

Prolonged losses and volatile cash flow

Operating losses have continued for four straight years from 2022 to 2025, with a 2025 operating margin of -18.6%. Operating cash flow was also negative in 2023 and 2025. The 2025 operating loss of KRW 16.2 billion was far larger than the KRW 1.86 billion loss in 2022.

A run of quarterly revenue declines

Revenue peaked at KRW 26.3 billion in the fourth quarter of 2025 and has declined for three consecutive quarters from the first to the third quarter of 2026, reaching KRW 16.2 billion in the third quarter. Meeting the full-year revenue levels in broker forecasts would require a sharp increase in the fourth quarter.

Cost and R&D burden

The satellite communication terminal business has been described as limited in profitability due to cost burdens. Higher R&D spending and large capital investment are cited as factors weighing on earnings.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 735 million in the third quarter of 2026, with large swings between quarters.

10

Risk factors

Project schedule delays

In its February 2026 report, KB Securities noted that delays in participation schedules for public satellite development projects lowered its fourth-quarter 2025 outlook. When project timing slips, revenue recognition slips with it. The structure depends heavily on government projects, so schedule risk remains.

Cost and investment burden

Capital spending continues for building ground stations and internalizing technology. Cost burdens and higher R&D spending could delay the recovery in profitability. For operating profit to be confirmed as positive, both revenue scale and cost ratios need to improve together.

Subsidiary losses and earnings volatility

The 2025 net loss of KRW 10.3 billion was larger than the net loss attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 7.35 billion, meaning part of the loss sat with non-controlling interests. Because the group structure is complex, how much consolidated profit remains for controlling shareholders needs checking.

Quarterly net income attributable to controlling shareholders has ranged from KRW 4.66 billion to KRW 7.3 billion, a wide band for forecasting.

12

Overall view

Contec combines ground-station services with satellite manufacturing, and its 2025 revenue of KRW 86.9 billion was far above 2022. However, operating losses have continued for four straight years from 2022, widening to KRW 16.2 billion in 2025.

The KRW 255 million operating profit in the third quarter of 2026 can be read as the first profitable quarter in the current turnaround.

Revenue, though, has declined for three consecutive quarters since peaking in the fourth quarter of 2025, and quarterly net income attributable to controlling shareholders swings widely. Positive factors include the expansion of recurring services, the shift in profit direction and in-house value chain capability.

Negative factors include long-running losses, cost and R&D burdens and project schedule delays. Broker forecasts for 2026 diverge between a full-year swing to profit and continued losses, making the scale of fourth-quarter revenue recognition the key variable.

The next checkpoints are full-year 2026 operating profit and cash flow in the annual report, followed by first-quarter 2027 results.
