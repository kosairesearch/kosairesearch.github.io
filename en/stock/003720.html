02

Business structure

Samyoung was founded in 1959 and listed on KOSPI in 1976 as a film and heavy-industry maker. Its business spans capacitor film (PPC), BOPP film, wrap and PVC products, and a heavy-industry subsidiary that makes cylinder liners.

Hana Securities' May 2025 report put 2024 segment revenue at KRW 30.6 billion for BOPP, KRW 46.2 billion for capacitor film, KRW 17.4 billion for other film and KRW 31.9 billion for heavy industry, making capacitor film the largest segment.

The same brokerage's May 2026 report estimated 2025 capacitor film revenue at about KRW 52.9 billion. Capacitor film is an ultra-thin film used in power electronics; sales fell during the slowdown in electric vehicles in 2024, while demand tied to ESS and data centers has since filled that gap.

In July 2025 the company bought a stake in a marine engine parts maker, adding an engine-frame casting business for about KRW 35.1 billion. That partner reportedly has price-linkage contracts with major customers, which passes part of raw material cost changes through to prices.

Samyoung is therefore shifting toward a model that combines casting and machining, and one report describes it as forming a near-monopoly position in Korea's marine engine parts market.

Hana Securities also noted that the heavy-industry subsidiary, which processes outsourced liners, has kept its operating margin in the mid-5% range. Its overall size is small next to large petrochemical and materials peers, so line yields and utilization in capacitor film drive most of the revenue trend.

04

Earnings analysis

On the annual filings, revenue fell from KRW 147.7 billion in 2022 to KRW 122.6 billion in 2023 (down 17.0%), then rose to KRW 126 billion in 2024 (up 2.8%) and KRW 153.2 billion in 2025 (up 21.6%). Revenue has grown two years in a row since 2023, and 2025 was the largest year in the 2022–2025 span.

Operating profit went from KRW 1.04 billion in 2022 to KRW 6.03 billion in 2023 (up 480.2%), KRW 9.1 billion in 2024 (up 51.0%) and KRW 14.9 billion in 2025 (up 63.9%), rising three years in a row from 2023 through 2025.

The operating margin moved from 0.7% in 2022 to 4.9%, 7.2% and 9.7%, so margin gains outpaced sales growth.

Profit attributable to controlling shareholders went from KRW 1.09 billion in 2022 to KRW 18.9 billion in 2023, fell to KRW 8.35 billion in 2024 (down 55.9%) and rebounded to KRW 13.2 billion in 2025 (up 57.7%), with 2023 the largest year.

In 2023 that net figure was far above operating profit, which points to a large non-operating contribution. Net income including non-controlling interests reached KRW 15 billion in 2025, up from KRW 8.35 billion in 2024.

At end-2025 total equity was KRW 88.3 billion, of which KRW 85.5 billion belonged to controlling shareholders, and total liabilities were KRW 110.1 billion. The debt-to-equity ratio rose from 83.4% in 2024 to 124.7% in 2025. Operating cash flow was KRW 18.5 billion in 2025, the largest of the 2022–2025 years.

By quarter, fourth-quarter 2025 revenue of KRW 42.6 billion fell 7.0% from the prior quarter, a period that overlapped with a plant overhaul that temporarily lowered utilization, according to Hana Securities' February 2026 report.

Revenue and operating profit both rose in the first quarter of 2026 (KRW 48.4 billion and KRW 6.01 billion) and the second quarter (KRW 54 billion and KRW 7.18 billion).

Third-quarter 2026 revenue fell to KRW 41.2 billion (down 23.8% from the prior quarter and 10.2% year on year), operating profit fell to KRW 2.52 billion (down 64.9% from the prior quarter and 55.1% year on year), and the company posted a net loss attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 3.05 billion, a swing into loss from the prior quarter. Over the most recent four quarters, profit attributable to controlling shareholders totals KRW 11.9 billion.

05

Industry analysis

Samyoung's core end markets are power infrastructure and power electronics. In a May 2026 report, Hana Securities explained that surging data-center power demand and the need to stabilize voltage for renewable generation have created new demand for marine low-speed engines used as power generators.

Reports from the first half of 2026 describe the shipbuilding sector as entering a super-cycle, which is lifting orders for engine-frame castings at the Casco unit, with power-generation demand adding to that.

One aggregator says capacitor film lines are running at full capacity with an export focus after new lines came online. Hana Securities also pointed to growing demand for insulating film used in batteries for prismatic-cell makers.

On the cycle, capacitor film sales fell in 2024 during the slowdown in electric vehicles, but ESS and data-center demand has since become the alternative driver. In competition, Korea's marine engine parts market is described as a near-monopoly after the Casco deal, with casting and machining under one roof.

Compared with larger materials peers, Samyoung is a mid-sized mix of film capacity and a heavy-industry subsidiary, and Hana Securities noted that the subsidiary's margin structure lags rivals that have their own casting plants.

06

Outlook

In a May 2026 report, Hana Securities said it raised its 2026 consolidated estimates from the prior level. The same report noted that the sixth capacitor film line has reached stable operation, which means new-line revenue recognition is now in full swing.

On the heavy-industry side, the company has a planned investment of roughly KRW 30 billion in new capacity, with cylinder casting equipment expansion moving the business from a machining-centered structure toward integrated casting and machining.

In August 2026 a site purchase agreement was signed, with a 10% down payment paid and an acquisition cost of KRW 34.7 billion excluding VAT, recorded as a purpose of capacity expansion and new growth capacity.

The timing of the balance payment settlement has not yet been disclosed and should be checked in future filings. First-half 2026 cumulative results rose sharply, but third-quarter 2026 revenue fell from the prior quarter, so whether fourth-quarter utilization and yields recover is the next checkpoint.

It also remains to be seen whether the shipbuilding super-cycle and power-generation demand sustain order flow for the heavy-industry unit. How far Casco's price-linkage structure absorbs raw material swings can be checked in the segment discussion of the annual report.

Bull factors

Power-infrastructure demand for capacitor film

In a May 2026 report, Hana Securities explained that data-center power demand and the need to stabilize voltage for renewable generation have created new demand for marine engines used as generators.

The same report noted that the sixth capacitor film line reached stable operation, so new-line revenue recognition has begun in earnest. An aggregator notes that capacitor film lines run at full capacity with an export focus. Room for utilization and yield gains is the key operating metric for this business.

Shipbuilding recovery and the Casco contribution

Hana Securities analyzed that the Casco consolidation widened its contribution to first-quarter 2026 operating profit. A shift in the heavy-industry earnings structure is one pillar of the bullish case.

A shift in the earnings profile

Operating profit rose three years in a row from 2023 through 2025, and the operating margin climbed from 0.7% in 2022 to 9.7% in 2025. Operating cash flow was KRW 18.5 billion in 2025, the largest of the filed years.

After the fourth-quarter 2025 overhaul, revenue and operating profit both rose in the first and second quarters of 2026. The improvement in the earnings profile underpins the bullish case.

Bear factors

A sharp reversal in the third quarter

Third-quarter 2026 revenue fell to KRW 41.2 billion, down 23.8% from the prior quarter, and operating profit fell to KRW 2.52 billion, down 64.9%. The company posted a net loss attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 3.05 billion, a swing into loss from the prior quarter.

Over the latest four quarters, that profit line totals KRW 11.9 billion. Whether the third-quarter loss was one-off or structural remains a question to be answered in the filings.

Plant overhauls and utilization swings

Hana Securities explained in a February 2026 report that a fourth-quarter 2025 overhaul replacing more than half of 40 transformers halted production and temporarily lowered utilization. Revenue in that quarter fell 7.0% from the prior quarter to KRW 42.6 billion.

Capacitor film sales previously declined around 2024 when electric vehicle growth slowed, so the business is sensitive to specific end-market demand.

A heavier financial burden

Total liabilities reached KRW 110.1 billion at end-2025, above total equity of KRW 88.3 billion, and the debt-to-equity ratio rose from 83.4% in 2024 to 124.7% in 2025.

The August 2026 site agreement covers KRW 34.7 billion excluding VAT, and Hana Securities cited a planned heavy-industry investment of roughly KRW 30 billion. The effect of these outlays on cash flow and debt levels should be checked in the annual report.

10

Risk factors

Demand concentration

Both capacitor film and marine engine castings depend on specific industry investment cycles. Film sales have fallen before when electric vehicle growth slowed, and shipbuilding orders move with ordering trends. The revenue weight of each business should be confirmed in the annual report.

Plant operation and yields

Hana Securities reported that the new line's yield rose from about 70% in the second quarter of 2025 to roughly 85% by July. Utilization has also dropped during past overhauls and stoppages. Production interruptions can move quarterly results sharply.

Financing and capital structure

The debt-to-equity ratio stood at 124.7% at end-2025. If the site purchase and casting equipment investment overlap, borrowing capacity could narrow. Investment continuing while liabilities are already large relative to equity is the core financing risk.
