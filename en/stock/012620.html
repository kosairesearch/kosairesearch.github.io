Business structure

WonilSpecialSteel, founded on October 14, 1977, is a steel processing and distribution company centered on special steel. According to JobKorea company data as of March 2026, metal mold steel products account for about two-thirds of sales, heavy plate-related products for about one-fifth, and forged products for 7%.

Other items, including leasing and by-products, make up roughly 5% combined. Its forging segment extends into precision forged parts for ships and automobiles through forging subsidiaries.

Automotive and shipbuilding are the main end markets; the company's 2025 year-end explanation cited recovering automotive mold volumes and inflows of domestic marine engine orders as drivers of forging revenue.

Competition includes the SeAH Group, whose anti-dumping petition on special steel bars has led to moves to raise prices on domestic products. Earlier steel trade press reports identified specialty tool steel and mold steel as products facing weaker demand.

The largest shareholder and related parties hold 44.33% as of the May 13, 2026 quote data, so ownership is concentrated. Dongkuk Steel Mill and Hyundai Steel Company are often cited as sector peers, but differences in product mix and scale limit direct comparison.

Earnings analysis

On a reported annual basis, revenue went from KRW 385.1 billion in 2022 to KRW 380 billion in 2023 (down 1.3% year on year), then KRW 379.3 billion in 2024 (down 0.2%), before rising to KRW 398.1 billion in 2025 (up 5.0%). Among 2022 to 2025, 2025 was the largest year for revenue and 2024 the smallest.

Operating profit fell from KRW 17 billion in 2022 to KRW 13.4 billion in 2023 (down 21.0%) and KRW 12 billion in 2024 (down 10.9%), then recovered to KRW 12.4 billion in 2025 (up 3.2%). The operating margin declined each year, from 4.4% in 2022 to 3.5%, 3.2% and 3.1% in 2025.

Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 10.6 billion in 2022, KRW 8 billion in 2023 (down 24.6%), KRW 6.65 billion in 2024 (down 16.8%) and KRW 8.21 billion in 2025 (up 23.5%), and net profit including non-controlling interests was reported at the same amounts.

Operating cash flow moved from -KRW 6.43 billion in 2022 to KRW 13.2 billion in 2023 and KRW 15.4 billion in 2024, then to -KRW 11 billion in 2025. The debt-to-equity ratio rose from 59.8% in 2024 to 69.6% at the end of 2025, with total equity of KRW 166.7 billion and total liabilities of KRW 116.1 billion.

Quarterly results bottomed in the fourth quarter of 2025. Revenue went from KRW 100.7 billion in that quarter through KRW 100.3 billion in the first quarter of 2026 to KRW 114.2 billion in the second quarter (up 13.9% quarter on quarter) and KRW 116.5 billion in the third quarter (up 1.9%).

Operating profit rose from KRW 1.72 billion in the fourth quarter of 2025 (down 45.6% quarter on quarter) to KRW 2.44 billion, KRW 4.02 billion and KRW 4.12 billion across the first three quarters of 2026. Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders over the last four quarters totaled KRW 9.29 billion.

In the third quarter of 2026, that net profit of KRW 4.93 billion exceeded operating profit of KRW 4.12 billion, so items below operating profit had a meaningful effect; details will be confirmed in the next periodic report.

On the supply side, the SeAH Group's anti-dumping petition on special steel bars has led to moves to raise prices on domestic products, which could change domestic bar pricing. WonilSpecialSteel has been described as a large special steel processing and distribution company.

Bull factors

Quarterly profit recovery

Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders reached KRW 4.93 billion in the third quarter of 2026, up 132.3% year on year. The KRW 9.29 billion total over the last four quarters also shows the earnings base being rebuilt.

Automotive and shipbuilding forging demand

Automotive mold volumes that had been postponed continued to recover, and inflows of domestic marine engine orders lifted forging revenue. Eco-friendly engine expansion and rising shipbuilding orders were cited as growth drivers for the forging industry.

With metal mold steel accounting for about two-thirds of sales, the recovery in automotive mold volumes is a major sensitivity for results.

Price increases under the anti-dumping petition

Price increases on domestic products are being pursued following the SeAH Group's anti-dumping petition on special steel bars, which could affect domestic bar pricing. How much of any price increase reaches margins will show up in the operating margin of future periodic reports.

A change in the pricing structure could become a route to margin recovery, which forms one pillar of the bull case.

Risk factors

Trade policy and pricing

Following the SeAH Group's anti-dumping petition, price increases on domestic bar products are being pursued, but the size and timing of the increases have not been set. If selling price increases are delayed or only partly reflected, the margin benefit in the bar business could be limited.

If competition with imported products intensifies, domestic bar sales volumes could also be affected. This will be checked against operating profit trends in the first-quarter 2027 quarterly report.

End-market and product concentration

Metal mold steel accounts for about two-thirds of sales, and demand depends on automotive and shipbuilding. If automotive mold orders are postponed again or shipbuilding orders fall, forging and mold steel sales would both be affected.

Tool steel and mold steel were once identified as products facing weak demand, so changes in product mix feed directly into results.

Governance and share float

The largest shareholder and related parties hold 44.33%, so ownership is concentrated. After the 2026 annual general meeting, the board has four directors, of whom one is an outside director, giving an outside director ratio of 25%.

Concentrated ownership and the outside director share are items to review alongside the decision structure and share float of a small-cap company.
