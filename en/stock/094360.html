Royalty Recovery Is the Key Test for a Video IP Licensor

Chips&Media's licence revenue is growing while royalty income has stalled on memory-driven chip production cuts at customers, and attributable net income swung to a loss in 2026 Q3, making the timing of royalty recovery the key point to monitor.

02

Business structure

Chips&Media licenses semiconductor design IP to chipmakers and collects usage fees. Its revenue has three components: licence, royalty and services. Licence revenue comes from IP supply contracts, while royalty revenue is a fee earned when chips that embed its IP are actually produced.

In 2026 Q2, licence revenue was KRW 3.99 billion, up 52.7% from the prior quarter, royalty revenue was KRW 2.58 billion and services revenue was KRW 520 million. In 2026 Q3, licence revenue reached KRW 4.43 billion, up 33.0% year on year, but royalties fell as customers cut chip output amid memory supply constraints.

By industry in 2025, the industrial segment, which includes a US customer's AI PC licences, accounted for 48% of revenue. The United States and China remain the main markets, and Japan's share expanded to 12.1% in 2025 from 4.8% a year earlier. The product line extends from video IP to the WAVE-N NPU and an AI ISP.

The NPU business is still early: it had one licence customer over two and a half years, with revenue of KRW 500 million, under 2% of 2025 annual revenue of KRW 28.4 billion. R&D spending in 2025 was KRW 11.5 billion, about 40% of annual revenue.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual revenue was KRW 24.1 billion in 2022, KRW 27.6 billion in 2023 (up 14.7% year over year), KRW 27.1 billion in 2024 (down 2.0% year over year), and KRW 28.5 billion in 2025 (up 5.1% year over year), making 2025 the largest year in the period.

Operating profit was KRW 7.3 billion in 2022, KRW 7.77 billion in 2023 (up 6.4% year over year), KRW 5.28 billion in 2024 (down 32.0% year over year), and KRW 6.96 billion in 2025 (up 31.9% year over year); 2023 was the highest and 2024 was the lowest.

The operating margin fell from 30.3% in 2022 to 28.1% in 2023 and 19.5% in 2024, then recovered to 24.5% in 2025. In 2025, operating profit growth (31.9%) exceeded revenue growth (5.1%), so the margin recovery drove the improvement in profit.

Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 9.96 billion in 2022, swung to a net loss of KRW 26.7 billion in 2023, returned to profit at KRW 10.0 billion in 2024, and then fell 40.8% to KRW 5.92 billion in 2025. 2023 was a year in which a net loss occurred even though operating profit was positive, so non-operating items need to be checked separately.

Net profit for the year was disclosed as the same amount as net profit attributable to controlling shareholders in each year.

The debt ratio moved from 50.3% in 2022 to 10.7% in 2023, 10.8% in 2024, and 25.8% in 2025; at the end of 2025, total equity was KRW 84.5 billion (equity attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 84.5 billion) and total liabilities were KRW 21.8 billion.

Cash flow from operating activities declined from KRW 15.2 billion in 2022 to KRW 5.53 billion in 2023, KRW 6.76 billion in 2024, and KRW 3.34 billion in 2025.

On a quarterly basis, the peak was in the fourth quarter of 2025, with revenue of KRW 9.87 billion (up 49.1% quarter over quarter) and operating profit of KRW 3.86 billion (up 153.1%). In the first quarter of 2026, revenue plunged to KRW 5.78 billion and operating profit to KRW 0.18 billion.

Royalty revenue rose 31.6% year over year, but total revenue declined in that quarter. In the second and third quarters of 2026, revenue was KRW 7.09 billion and KRW 7.16 billion and operating profit was KRW 1.13 billion and KRW 1.66 billion, rising for two consecutive quarters.

The net loss attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 0.6 billion in the third quarter of 2026 was explained as reflecting foreign exchange losses and valuation losses on financial assets, among other items.

The sum of net profit attributable to controlling shareholders over the most recent four quarters (Q4 2025 to Q3 2026) is KRW 6.49 billion.

05

Industry analysis

Demand is shifting toward on-device AI and physical AI. The company said it secured new licences for AI SoCs and humanoid robots in China and continued mobile licences with major North American customers. In automotive, it said it holds IP meeting the ASIL-B level of the ISO 26262 functional safety standard.

With Mobilint, a Korean edge-AI chip company, its video processing IP has entered mass production in a chip and a further multimedia IP licence was signed. The key industry variable is memory supply, which reduced customer chip output and royalty revenue in Q3.

Because licences are recognised at signing while royalties build after mass production, there is a time lag in reported results. Chip development typically takes two to three years, so royalty revenue from new NPU customers would come years later.

The company's strategy is to add AI ISP and NPU products on top of its video IP licensing base. Its claimed competitiveness in video-specialised NPUs rests on company statements and will need validation through growth in customer count.

06

Outlook

The company expects royalty growth to resume as domestic AI SoC fabless customers move robot, home appliance and IoT chips into mass production next year.

In 2026 Q2, new projects with major North American customers, new licences with a Chinese robotics platform company and mobility licence and royalty growth were reflected in results.

The company is widening its customer base in autonomous driving, robotics and drones, and plans to extend application into cameras, robots and drones, building on commercialisation of the APV standard and the launch of AV2.

It has also signed next-generation multimedia IP licence agreements with Ambarella, a physical AI vision chip company, and a North American big-tech company. On technology, it plans to commercialise AI ISP and strengthen NPU and compression IP.

On sales, it is strengthening its focus on global big-tech and large customers. The outlook centres on the timing of royalty conversion, so post-2027 results will depend on how quickly licences turn into mass-production volumes and royalties.

08

Bull factors

Room for licences to convert into royalties

The company expects royalties to return to growth as domestic AI SoC fabless customers begin mass-producing robot, appliance and IoT chips in 2027. Because licences have accumulated, mass production would convert them into royalties. Royalties rose year on year in 2026 Q1, so a starting point for conversion is already visible.

Two consecutive quarters of operating profit growth

Operating profit bottomed at KRW 181 million in 2026 Q1, then rose to KRW 1.13 billion in Q2 and KRW 1.66 billion in Q3, two consecutive quarters of growth. Q3 operating profit was up 8.7% year on year. Licence revenue grew 33.0% year on year in Q3, supporting the recovery.

Progress in customer and regional diversification

Japan's revenue share widened from 4.8% in 2024 to 12.1% in 2025. In its Q2 results announced in July 2026, the company said it had signed next-generation multimedia IP licence agreements with Ambarella and a North American big-tech company.

With a Korean edge-AI chip company, its IP is in robot chip mass production, extending reach into robotics and physical AI.

09

Bear factors

Stalled royalties and memory supply

In 2026 Q3, royalty revenue declined as memory supply constraints reduced customers' chip output. Even when licence revenue grows, a royalty decline changes the revenue mix and adds volatility. If royalty recovery slips past 2027, the pace of earnings recovery could be limited.

Limited early results from the NPU business

As of early 2026 reporting, the NPU IP business had one customer over two and a half years, with revenue of KRW 500 million, under 2% of 2025 annual revenue. R&D spending of KRW 11.5 billion in 2025 was about 40% of annual revenue, a heavy cost burden. Chip development cycles of two to three years add further delay before royalty contributions.

Volatility in net income

In 2023 the company posted KRW 7.77 billion of operating profit yet a attributable net loss of KRW 26.7 billion. Attributable net income fell 40.8% to KRW 5.92 billion in 2025, and 2026 Q3 turned to a net loss of KRW 605 million.

Foreign exchange losses and fair-value losses on financial assets were cited as main factors, leaving a wide gap between operating results and net income.

10

Risk factors

Customer and regional concentration

Major revenue sources are concentrated in the United States and China, so changes in chip investment and trade conditions in those regions could directly affect demand. The industrial segment accounted for 48% of 2025 revenue, so dependence on a specific customer group is also high. The timing of a single customer's licence can move quarterly results materially.

Foreign exchange and fair-value swings

The Q3 2026 net loss was attributed to foreign exchange losses and fair-value losses on financial assets. Net income can fall even when operating profit improves, because non-operating items can swing. Checking the gap between operating profit and net income each quarter is therefore necessary.

Standards transition and development cost

The IP demand structure could shift as the industry moves to next-generation standards such as APV and AV2. Ongoing R&D into NPU and AI ISP products adds cost, and if development does not convert into royalties, the burden persists. The longer the lag between development spending and royalty collection, the slower the earnings recovery.

Overall view

Chips&Media runs a business built on video IP licensing, with 2025 annual revenue of KRW 28.5 billion and operating profit of KRW 6.96 billion. Licence revenue has grown through 2026 Q3, but royalties have stalled as customers cut chip output.

Revenue and operating profit rose year on year in 2026 Q3, yet attributable net income turned to a loss of KRW 605 million, so earnings quality needs to be checked alongside growth.

The company expects royalty growth to resume as domestic AI SoC customers move robot, appliance and IoT chips into mass production in 2027. Supporting factors are the accumulation of licences and diversification across customers and regions.

Offsetting factors are stalled royalties, limited early results from the NPU business and volatility in net income. Valuation sits in a structure where a small earnings base makes multiples sensitive to quarterly profit.

The next checkpoints are the 2026 full-year preliminary results, the 2026 annual report and the direction of royalty revenue in the 2027 Q1 results.
