02

Business structure

Daesung Energy is a city gas utility serving all of Daegu, all of Gyeongsan in North Gyeongsang Province, and the Dongmyeong area of Goryeong and Chilgok counties. Roughly 1.2 million gas demand points in this area use its service, covering household, industrial and commercial supply.

City gas businesses are divided by supply territory, so the company's position rests on a regional franchise. In its 2025 annual business report, city gas segment revenue was KRW 1.02 trillion, and natural gas sales made up 97.18% of that amount.

Electricity sales accounted for 1.09% and heat sales for 0.86%, so non-gas activities remain small. Selling prices are set on a cost-plus basis, and purchase costs follow government-notified rates. Within its supply territory the company builds and operates 12 CNG refueling stations directly.

In August 2025 it completed the Hayang complex hydrogen refueling station, widening its hydrogen footprint. By the end of 2025, six city gas management subsidiaries had completed liquidation, simplifying the group's service entities. The largest shareholder is Daesung Holdings, which held 72.07% as of June 30, 2026. Two domestic credit rating agencies both assign the company an A+ rating.

04

Earnings analysis

On the basis of the periodic reports, annual revenue moved from KRW 1.02 trillion in 2022 to KRW 999.5 billion in 2023 (down 2.1% year on year) and KRW 995.1 billion in 2024 (down 0.4%), before recovering to KRW 1.02 trillion in 2025 (up 2.3%).

Across 2022 to 2025, 2022 was the largest year by revenue and 2024 the smallest. Operating profit rose from KRW 939 million in 2022 to KRW 20.4 billion in 2023 (up 2,072.3%), KRW 20.5 billion in 2024 (up 0.4%) and KRW 30.3 billion in 2025 (up 48.2%), increasing for three consecutive years from 2023 through 2025.

Credit rating reports describe 2022 as a year when rising raw material costs reached selling prices with a lag, temporarily cutting operating profit sharply, and when lower LNG purchase costs restored profitability in 2023 and 2024 even as sales volumes fell.

The operating margin climbed from 0.1% in 2022 to 2.0% in 2023, 2.1% in 2024 and 3.0% in 2025.

Profit attributable to owners of the parent swung from a KRW 755 million loss in 2022 to KRW 14.9 billion in 2023, a turnaround to profit, then rose to KRW 17.3 billion in 2024 (up 15.6%) and KRW 24.6 billion in 2025 (up 42.6%), increasing for two consecutive years from 2024.

Net income including non-controlling interests was the same KRW 24.6 billion in 2025. Operating cash flow fell from KRW 53 billion in 2022 to KRW 20.2 billion in 2023 before recovering to KRW 45.4 billion in 2024 and KRW 49.6 billion in 2025.

The debt-to-equity ratio declined from 165.9% in 2022 to 128.9% in 2025, when total liabilities were KRW 445.5 billion and total equity KRW 345.7 billion. Quarterly results peaked in the first quarter of 2026, with revenue of KRW 392.4 billion and operating profit of KRW 25.7 billion.

Second-quarter 2026 revenue fell to KRW 161 billion, down 59.0% from the prior quarter, and operating profit dropped to KRW 2.73 billion, down 89.4%.

Third-quarter 2026 revenue was KRW 139.9 billion, down 13.1% sequentially but up 9.0% year on year, while the company posted an operating loss of KRW 5.57 billion, a swing into loss from the prior quarter, and a loss attributable to owners of the parent of KRW 3.81 billion.

Revenue has declined for two consecutive quarters since the first quarter of 2026. Profit attributable to owners of the parent for the last four quarters, from the first quarter to the fourth quarter of 2025, totaled KRW 24.6 billion.

05

Industry analysis

City gas is a regulated business that operates within government-notified rates and a cost-plus pricing framework. Natural gas is gaining importance as a bridge fuel replacing coal and nuclear-based power generation, a point highlighted in 2025 assessments tied to the annual business report.

Credit rating reports view the regional franchise and cost-plus pricing as the basis of earnings stability. At the same time, supply penetration in the service area is already relatively high, which could limit further growth.

The same assessments cite a firm demand base and gradual growth in customers, and conclude that the company's market position remains solid. According to a credit rating report, the company accounts for 4.4% of city gas supply volume and 8.1% of regional supply volume.

Samchully and Seoul City GAS are listed peers in city gas, while Daesung Energy's operating area is confined to the Daegu and North Gyeongsang region.

Viewed through its disclosures, the industry sequence moved from a 2022 lag in passing through raw material cost spikes, to stabilized LNG prices in 2023 and 2024, to sustained selling prices in 2025, and into a period of quarterly profit swings in 2026.

The lag between LNG cost changes and tariff adjustments has repeatedly shown up as a driver of quarterly profit swings.

Bull factors

Regional monopoly and cost-plus pricing

The company serves about 1.2 million gas demand points across all of Daegu and Gyeongsan. Selling prices follow a cost-plus framework, so raw material cost changes reach tariffs with a set lag. Natural gas made up 97.18% of city gas segment sales in 2025, a high degree of core-business concentration.

Three years of operating profit growth and margin gains

Operating profit rose for three consecutive years from 2023 through 2025, reaching KRW 30.3 billion in 2025, up 48.2%. The operating margin improved from 0.1% in 2022 to 3.0% in 2025. Profit attributable to owners of the parent turned positive in 2023 and then increased in both 2024 and 2025.

Lower leverage and credit ratings

The debt-to-equity ratio fell from 165.9% in 2022 to 128.9% in 2025. Total equity at year-end 2025 was KRW 345.7 billion against total liabilities of KRW 445.5 billion. Two domestic credit rating agencies both assign an A+ rating, with the most recent assessment dated April 24, 2026.

The third quarter brought an operating loss of KRW 5.57 billion and a loss attributable to owners of the parent of KRW 3.81 billion. Revenue has declined for two consecutive quarters from the second quarter through the third quarter of 2026.

Downside from the lag in passing through raw material costs

In 2022, a rise in raw material costs reached selling prices with a delay, and operating profit dropped to KRW 939 million. The 2022 episode shows that changes in raw material costs can move quarterly results. Whether the third-quarter 2026 loss reflects a similar lag cannot be determined from the disclosures alone.

Limited growth room and diversification

Electricity sales were only 1.09% of segment revenue and heat sales 0.86% in 2025, so diversification effects are small. New businesses such as hydrogen refueling are small relative to the core gas business.

Risk factors

Policy and tariff risk

Selling prices and purchase costs are tied to government-notified rates and a cost-plus framework, so policy changes flow directly into earnings. If tariff adjustments fall out of step with raw material costs, profit can fall temporarily. The 2022 episode, when operating profit dropped to KRW 939 million, illustrates this.

Quarterly earnings volatility

Quarterly revenue moved from KRW 392.4 billion in the first quarter of 2026 to KRW 161 billion in the second and KRW 139.9 billion in the third. Profit and loss have alternated from quarter to quarter, making quarterly results wide-ranging. Annual operating profit improved to KRW 30.3 billion in 2025, yet quarterly swings remain large.

Major shareholder concentration and float

Daesung Holdings held 72.07% as of June 30, 2026, which limits the share of freely tradable stock. This structure can affect share price volatility and trading liquidity. Changes in the major shareholder's stake, when disclosed, are the next point to track.
