02

Business structure

GC Cell is a cell and gene therapy developer within the GC Biopharma group. The company says it has experience across the full cycle, from product development through GMP manufacturing to commercialization.

Its core product, Immuncell-LC, isolates immune cells from a patient's blood, cultures them and returns them to the patient, and it is approved in Korea as a liver cancer treatment.

In a retrospective analysis of liver transplant patients presented in February 2026, the two-year recurrence-free survival rate was 87.5% in the treated group versus 62.9% in the control group.

In 2024 the company signed a technology transfer and licensing agreement with Indonesia's Bifarma for local commercialization, and the first Indonesian patient was treated in May 2026.

In October 2026, Türkiye's health ministry requested a visit to the company's headquarters and manufacturing facility, and both sides discussed licensing and commercialization steps; an NK cell therapy is also under review there.

On the technology side, the company registered patents in Korea and Japan in February 2026 for a CIITA control technology aimed at reducing immune rejection in allogeneic cell therapies.

Allogeneic products face a limit because the patient's immune system can attack cells from another person, which is the problem this platform is meant to ease.

Manufacturing capability is a competitive point the company emphasizes, and its claim that Immuncell-LC holds the world's largest manufacturing record among cell therapy products is the company's own statement.

On regulation, the WHO drug classification now places the product in the antineoplastic cell and gene therapy category. The business combines domestic product sales, overseas technology transfer and platform patents.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual revenue fell from KRW 236.1 billion in 2022 to KRW 187.5 billion in 2023 (down 20.6% year on year), KRW 174.5 billion in 2024 (down 7.0%) and KRW 165.5 billion in 2025 (down 5.1%). Revenue declined for three straight years from 2023 through 2025, and 2022 was the largest year of the 2022 to 2025 period.

Operating profit dropped from KRW 44.3 billion in 2022 to KRW 4.08 billion in 2023 (down 90.8%), and the company swung to an operating loss of KRW 20 billion in 2024. The 2025 operating loss of KRW 13.8 billion was smaller than the year before, but the company has now posted operating losses for two straight years.

The operating margin moved from 18.8% in 2022 to 2.2% in 2023, -11.5% in 2024 and -8.3% in 2025. Net income attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 25 billion in 2022, turned to a loss of KRW 174 million in 2023, and widened to a loss of KRW 73.9 billion in 2024 and KRW 272.9 billion in 2025.

Total net loss including non-controlling interests was KRW 273.5 billion in 2025, close to the controlling-shareholder figure.

The fourth-quarter 2025 loss attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 243.2 billion, while that quarter's operating loss was KRW 4.33 billion, so the gap between operating and bottom-line results was large.

Whether non-operating items or one-off charges drove that gap should be checked in the notes to the annual report.

Quarterly revenue in 2026 bottomed at KRW 37.4 billion in the first quarter (down 1.7% from the prior quarter), then rose to KRW 41.6 billion in the second quarter (up 11.3%) and KRW 44.4 billion in the third (up 6.6%), the second straight quarterly increase; third-quarter revenue was still 1.5% below the year-earlier quarter.

Operating losses widened from KRW 397 million in the third quarter of 2025 to KRW 4.33 billion in the fourth quarter and KRW 5.11 billion in the first quarter of 2026, narrowed to KRW 1.01 billion in the second quarter, and widened again to KRW 2.52 billion in the third quarter.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders turned positive at KRW 2.37 billion in the second quarter of 2026 before returning to a loss of KRW 10.8 billion in the third quarter. The combined net loss attributable to controlling shareholders over the most recent four quarters was KRW 254.5 billion.

Operating cash flow fell from KRW 66.4 billion in 2022 to KRW 9.19 billion in 2025, though it stayed positive from 2023 onward.

05

Industry analysis

Cell therapies depend on manufacturing records and quality control, which act as barriers to entry, and the company emphasizes Immuncell-LC's manufacturing capability.

On regulation, the WHO drug classification serves as a reference point in overseas approvals and licensing talks, which the company says can support its explanation of the product to foreign partners.

On demand, Türkiye's health ministry requested the visit, which the company presents as a sign of local interest, though revenue would only follow after approvals. Across allogeneic cell therapies, immune rejection is a recognized technical limit, and GC Cell presents its CIITA control technology as a way to reduce it.

Larger listed companies in the biopharma sector, such as Samsung Biologics and Celltrion, run different business models from a company concentrated in cell therapy.

Regulatory events such as approvals, classifications and overseas entry appear more likely to shape the earnings inflection than the broader industry cycle. Which of domestic expansion or overseas licensing produces financial results first is likely to define the company's position within the sector.

06

Outlook

The company has pointed to three threads it describes as established. First, overseas expansion of Immuncell-LC: the first patient in Indonesia was treated in May 2026, and the company is using that base to continue licensing talks with other countries.

Second, introduction in Türkiye: after the October 2026 visit, the company says it will keep reviewing approval and commercialization cooperation, with local investment and manufacturing or clinical collaboration also on the table.

Third, regulatory classification: the detailed WHO code L01XL17 applies from January 2027, and the company says it will pursue overseas expansion and technology licensing around that final application date.

On the clinical side, a study in liver transplant patients was presented at a conference in February 2026, and the company intends to widen its use in adjuvant liver cancer therapy. That study is retrospective, so follow-up trial design and results will determine how strong the evidence becomes.

No disclosed figures yet show when or how much overseas licensing will contribute to reported results. Revenue rose for two straight quarters in 2026, but there is no confirmed sign yet that the operating loss structure has changed.

The next checkpoints are the operating loss and operating margin in the 2026 annual report, and whether the Türkiye talks move to a contract or approval stage.

Risk factors

Finances and Funding

At the end of 2025, total liabilities were KRW 113.3 billion and total equity was KRW 190.4 billion, with a debt-to-equity ratio of 59.5%. Operating cash flow was positive at KRW 9.19 billion in 2025 but small, so continued operating losses could increase funding pressure. The cash impact of losses and the debt structure should be checked in the next periodic report.

Regulatory and Clinical Evidence

The efficacy data for Immuncell-LC in liver transplant patients come from a retrospective analysis, which limits the clinical strength of the evidence. Overseas approval procedures differ by country and could delay timelines.

The detailed WHO code applies from January 2027, so the actual pace at which it appears in overseas approval documents will become clear only after that.

Business Concentration and Technical Limits

Growth expectations are concentrated in Immuncell-LC and overseas technology licensing, so product or partner developments could weigh heavily on results.

Allogeneic cell therapies face the technical limit that a patient's immune system can attack donor cells, so the commercialization timing of the CIITA platform is uncertain. Patent registration does not translate directly into product revenue.
