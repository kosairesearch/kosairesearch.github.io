SK Securities: Cumulative Profit Recovers Despite Q3 Loss

SK Securities swung to an operating loss of KRW 3.83 billion in the third quarter of 2026, yet its cumulative results for the first three quarters remained in the black, so whether the profit recovery that began with the 2025 return to profitability holds is the next point to check.

02

Business structure

SK Securities is a financial investment company whose main revenue lines are brokerage commissions from securities trading and proprietary trading income from its own capital.

In the first half of 2026, higher domestic stock trading value expanded brokerage commissions, and proprietary trading income also rose, which the company said improved cumulative profit. In the third quarter of 2026, however, stock and bond markets broadly weakened, which hurt trading gains.

The company's earnings are therefore highly sensitive to trading volume, interest rates and market direction. The company also cited cost efficiency and stronger risk management as factors supporting profitability.

Its net capital ratio (NCR) stood at 251.52% in the third quarter of 2026, down 17.84 percentage points from the prior quarter but still well above the 100% regulatory minimum.

On the balance sheet, total liabilities far exceed total equity, with the debt-to-equity ratio at 1,034.5% at the end of 2025, reflecting the large asset and borrowing structure typical of brokerages.

Its competitors include Mirae Asset Securities, NH Investment & Securities, Samsung Securities and KIWOOM Securities, which compete for the same trading commissions. All five of the largest brokerages reported lower third-quarter net income than in the prior quarter, so the slowdown was broad across the sector. The business is a mix of brokerage and trading income.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual operating profit fell from KRW 17.9 billion in 2022 to KRW 13.1 billion in 2023, a 26.8% decline from the prior year, before the company swung to an operating loss of KRW 107.9 billion in 2024. It returned to profit in 2025 with operating profit of KRW 7.79 billion.

Profit attributable to controlling shareholders followed the same path, moving from KRW 9.42 billion in 2022 and KRW 2.55 billion in 2023, a 72.9% decline, to a net loss of KRW 82.5 billion in 2024, before recording KRW 28.8 billion in 2025.

Net income including non-controlling interests, which is a different figure, was KRW 28.7 billion in 2025 and a net loss of KRW 83.3 billion in 2024.

At the end of 2025, controlling shareholders held KRW 602.4 billion of the KRW 604.6 billion total equity, while the debt-to-equity ratio rose from 908.0% in 2022 to 1,034.5% in 2025.

Operating cash flow went from KRW 328.4 billion in 2022 and KRW 215 billion in 2023 to KRW 217.2 billion in 2024, and then to -KRW 374.3 billion in 2025. The quarterly pattern was sharper.

Operating profit of KRW 8.26 billion in the third quarter of 2025 was followed by an operating loss of KRW 5.69 billion in the fourth quarter, a swing from the prior quarter.

The first quarter of 2026 returned to profit with KRW 28.7 billion, and the second quarter came in at KRW 22.5 billion, down 21.4% from the prior quarter. The third quarter of 2026 recorded an operating loss of KRW 3.83 billion, a swing from both the prior quarter and the same quarter of 2025.

Profit attributable to controlling shareholders rose to KRW 33.4 billion in the second quarter of 2026, up 43.3%, before falling to KRW 1.79 billion in the third quarter, down 94.7% from the prior quarter. Over the last four quarters, profit attributable to controlling shareholders totaled KRW 53 billion.

Reported reasons for the third-quarter decline include lower stock trading value and higher market interest rates. Cumulative operating profit of KRW 47.4 billion and net income of KRW 58.6 billion for January to September 2026 are preliminary figures from the November 16, 2026 disclosure report.

05

Industry analysis

The domestic brokerage industry depends heavily on stock trading value and the level of market interest rates. In the first half of 2026, a strong equity market lifted trading value and brokerage commissions, but in the third quarter lower trading value and rising market rates weakened profitability across the sector.

Net income at all five of the largest brokerages fell from the prior quarter in the third quarter of 2026. Smaller and mid-sized brokerages recorded quarterly operating losses, which shows greater sensitivity to changes in market conditions.

SK Securities followed the same pattern, with cumulative profit in the first half giving way to an operating loss in the third quarter. Analysts have noted that higher market rates can lead to valuation losses on bonds and other holdings, as well as higher funding costs.

In cycle terms, the sector appears to have moved from a first-half equity rally into a second-half slowdown. On competitive position, SK Securities kept its NCR at 251.52%, above the regulatory minimum of 100%.

However, the 17.84 percentage point drop in the NCR from the prior quarter is a variable to monitor for capital headroom. Across the industry, the gap between large and smaller brokerages has tended to widen or narrow with trading volume and the economic cycle.

The direction it presented can be summarized as risk management responsive to market volatility and a stable earnings base. Fourth-quarter 2026 results are likely to depend on the path of stock trading value and market interest rates.

Factors weighing on it include the third-quarter operating loss, higher market interest rates, lower trading value and the decline in the NCR. Because both sets of factors are present, which side gains the upper hand should be checked against results in the coming quarters.

Bull factors

Cumulative profit stays in the black

Cumulative operating profit of KRW 47.4 billion and net income of KRW 58.6 billion for January to September 2026 are preliminary figures. They reflect higher brokerage commissions and proprietary trading income in the first half.

The third quarter alone was a loss, but cumulative results rose sharply from the same period of 2025. Supporters point to this cumulative profit as evidence that the recovery that began with the 2025 return to profitability is still holding.

Cost efficiency and risk management

The company said cost efficiency and stronger risk management helped its results. Its disclosure also stated that it will closely monitor market conditions and actively manage risk. If the company can manage its cost base during weaker periods, that could narrow the swings in profit, which is the bullish argument.

First-half 2026 earnings base

Operating profit was KRW 28.7 billion in the first quarter of 2026 after the swing back to profit, and KRW 22.5 billion in the second quarter. Profit attributable to controlling shareholders rose to KRW 33.4 billion in the second quarter, up 43.3% from the prior quarter. Supporters read the first-half strength as evidence that earnings power appears when trading activity is high.

Bear factors

Q3 operating loss and sharp profit decline

The third quarter of 2026 recorded an operating loss of KRW 3.83 billion, a swing from both the prior quarter and the same quarter of 2025. Profit attributable to controlling shareholders fell to KRW 1.79 billion, down 94.7% from the prior quarter.

The fourth quarter of 2025 also posted an operating loss of KRW 5.69 billion, so whether quarterly profit remains stable is the core bearish concern.

High leverage and weaker cash flow

At the end of 2025, the debt-to-equity ratio was 1,034.5%, with total liabilities of KRW 6.25 trillion well above total equity of KRW 604.6 billion. The ratio rose from 908.0% in 2022 to 1,034.5% in 2025. Operating cash flow changed to -KRW 374.3 billion in 2025 from KRW 217.2 billion in 2024. Critics cite these figures as evidence of rising leverage and funding burden.

Market dependence and rate pressure

Lower stock trading value and higher market interest rates were cited as causes of the weaker third quarter. Stock and bond markets both weakened, which reduced trading gains.

Because net income fell at all five of the largest brokerages in the third quarter, critics argue that a sector-wide slowdown is hard for any single company to offset.

Risk factors

Market volatility risk

Brokerage commissions move sharply with stock trading value. Lower trading value was cited as a cause of the third-quarter swing to a loss. If trading value falls again, the commission gains seen in the first half would be hard to repeat, and quarterly profit could swing widely.

Interest rate and trading gain risk

Higher market interest rates can lead to valuation losses on bonds and other holdings and raise funding costs. In the third quarter of 2026, both stock and bond markets weakened and trading gains deteriorated. If rates keep rising, volatility in the trading book could continue.

Balance sheet and capital headroom risk

The debt-to-equity ratio was 1,034.5% at the end of 2025, with total liabilities of KRW 6.25 trillion. The NCR fell 17.84 percentage points from the prior quarter to 251.52% in the third quarter of 2026.

If the decline continues, capital headroom could shrink, so the trend in this regulatory capital measure is a key risk.
