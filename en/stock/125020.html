02

Business structure

TC Materials is a specialist copper-products maker that processes copper into winding materials for electric power equipment.

Its main products are enamelled copper wire, CTC (continuously transposed conductor) and rectangular copper wire, supplied to large Korean groups as core materials for transformers, generators and rotating machines.

According to aggregated data, the sales mix in the second quarter of 2026 was 67% power infrastructure, 21% automotive materials, 6% home appliances and other, and 5% scrap.

The power-infrastructure segment centres on CTC and rectangular wire for transformers and materials for extra-high-voltage cables, and the company says it developed enamelled copper wire technology for submarine cable materials, a first in Korea.

The automotive segment supplies materials for electric vehicles and is widening its customer base. The home-appliance and other segment is exposed to weak global appliance demand. Major customers are large makers of power equipment, and 2025 press reports say products also ship to the United States and Europe.

Production runs from the headquarters plant in the Onsan industrial complex in Ulsan and a plant in Busan, and the company emphasises an integrated process from materials to finished products and repair. It has also expanded into motor stator manufacturing and large-motor repair and maintenance.

Using listing proceeds, the company planned to repurchase plants it had sold to KAMCO to cut rent costs and improve profitability. Competition centres on delivery time, cost and insulation technology in materials supply, a different stage from large power-equipment makers such as Doosan Enerbility.

04

Earnings analysis

The full year was marked by falling sales and falling profit together. Sales declined 1.6% from KRW 303.9 billion in 2024 to KRW 299.1 billion in 2025, while operating profit fell 37.8% from KRW 10.9 billion to KRW 6.8 billion.

The operating margin narrowed from 3.6% to 2.3%, and profit attributable to controlling shareholders fell 73.3% from KRW 6.27 billion to KRW 1.67 billion.

At the 2025 results announcement on February 9, 2026, the company said the temporary factors that weighed on results had mostly been resolved, and the press reported that a slowdown in downstream markets and higher cost burdens combined to temporarily lower profitability.

Over the same period the debt ratio rose from 33.9% to 55.5%, and operating cash flow fell from KRW 13.5 billion to KRW 4.04 billion. Quarterly results bottomed in the fourth quarter of 2025, when sales were KRW 76.2 billion, down 2.4% from the prior quarter, and operating profit was KRW 1.08 billion, down 43.9%.

In the first quarter of 2026, sales recovered to KRW 101.4 billion, up 33.2% quarter on quarter, and operating profit rose to KRW 6.37 billion, up 492.6%. The company attributed that improvement to lower fixed-cost pressure from higher volume and a better cost ratio from productivity gains.

In the second quarter sales rose 22.1% to KRW 123.8 billion, but operating profit fell 40.2% to KRW 3.81 billion, so higher sales did not translate into higher profit that quarter.

Third-quarter sales reached KRW 128.2 billion, operating profit was KRW 5.05 billion, and profit attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 3.68 billion, each up sharply from a year earlier. Profit attributable to controlling shareholders over the last four quarters totals KRW 12.6 billion.

On August 18, 2026 the company said first-half operating profit, up 167.54% year on year, had already exceeded full-year 2025 operating profit.

05

Industry analysis

The core of downstream demand is the expansion of power-grid investment. At its first-quarter 2026 results, the company said demand for transformer and extra-high-voltage cable materials is growing.

CTC and rectangular copper wire for transformers and enamelled wire for high-voltage cables are tied directly to transmission and distribution investment, so results track the order schedules of large power-equipment makers.

In an upcycle this is a volume tailwind, but it also means quarterly results can swing sharply with the investment timing of specific customers or segments. Power infrastructure accounts for 67% of sales, so exposure to the grid-investment cycle is high.

The automotive segment faces short-term volatility in electric-vehicle demand, but the company says diversifying its customer base is widening its supply base. Home-appliance demand remains weak globally, and sales in that segment fell 17.4% year on year in the first quarter of 2026.

Compared with large power-generation equipment makers in the same machinery sector, this company sits at the materials stage rather than building equipment, so downstream investment flows reach it through order volumes.

On competition, delivery time, cost and insulation technology are the main factors, and the company stresses cost-ratio gains from production efficiency.

06

Outlook

The most concrete plan the company has described is the full operation of its new capacity. At its first-half 2026 results on August 18, the company said it would fully run the added facilities in the second half to meet demand in its main growth markets.

At its third-quarter results on November 13, 2026, it again said it would fully run the production lines added for transformer and extra-high-voltage cable materials, expand supply volume and raise production efficiency.

The direction the company has set is to pursue sales growth and profitability improvement at the same time. For the automotive segment, the company says supply of electric-vehicle materials will keep expanding as its customer base diversifies, while home-appliance demand remains under pressure.

The company also plans to continue product-mix improvement and production-efficiency work. The effect of the new capacity should show up in quarterly results once the lines are running.

The next checkpoints are whether higher sales in the fourth quarter of 2026 and in full-year results translate into a better operating margin, and how much of the capacity effect appears in first-quarter 2027 results.

These are the company's stated plans, and the outcome will be verified through later periodic reports and earnings releases.

Bull factors

Demand from grid investment

Sales in power infrastructure rose 80.45% year on year in the first half of 2026, as demand grew for CTC and rectangular wire for transformers and for extra-high-voltage cable materials. With power infrastructure at 67% of sales, the company has direct exposure to grid investment. The company says demand for transformer and high-voltage cable materials is expanding.

Recovery in quarterly results

Sales have risen for three straight quarters from the fourth-quarter 2025 low of KRW 76.2 billion through the third quarter of 2026. Third-quarter 2026 operating profit of KRW 5.05 billion rose 163.6% year on year. The company has said first-half 2026 operating profit already exceeded full-year 2025 operating profit.

Customer diversification in automotive

Electric-vehicle material sales grew as the company diversified its customers, and first-half 2026 automotive sales rose 65.2% year on year according to the company. The automotive segment accounted for 21% of sales in the second quarter of 2026. The company says it will pursue sales growth and profitability gains at the same time.

Bear factors

Sales growth not matched by margins

Second-quarter 2026 sales rose to KRW 123.8 billion, but operating profit fell 40.2% quarter on quarter to KRW 3.81 billion. The annual operating margin narrowed from 3.6% in 2024 to 2.3% in 2025. Whether sales growth will lift margins has not yet been tested over several quarters.

Heavier balance sheet and weaker cash flow

The debt ratio rose from 33.9% in 2024 to 55.5% in 2025, and total liabilities at the end of 2025 were KRW 43.2 billion. Operating cash flow fell from KRW 13.5 billion in 2024 to KRW 4.04 billion in 2025. How capacity spending and plant repurchases affect this trend will show up in later filings.

Weakness in home appliances and other

Home-appliance and other sales fell 16.43% year on year in the first half of 2026 and 17.4% in the first quarter. The segment is small at 6% of second-quarter 2026 sales, but global appliance demand remains weak. The automotive segment is also exposed to short-term swings in electric-vehicle demand.

Risk factors

Concentration in customers and grid investment

With 67% of sales in power infrastructure, quarterly results can swing with the order timing and investment schedules of large customers. In the second quarter of 2026, sales rose while operating profit fell, which points to possible effects from volume shifts and cost structure. High dependence on one segment adds to earnings volatility.

Share-price volatility

Press reports said foreign investors bought 520,000 shares in the five days before the first-quarter 2026 results, while the share price fell 13.1% over the same period. The stock's beta is well above 1, so it tends to react strongly to market moves. Share-price swings can widen when trading flows and earnings expectations diverge.

Balance sheet and cash flow

The debt ratio rose to 55.5% in 2025, and operating cash flow fell to KRW 4.04 billion. How much the capacity investment and repurchased plants weigh on cash flow still needs to be checked. Whether the 2026 profit recovery converts into cash generation will determine how much financial risk remains.

Overall view

TC Materials is a company whose sales and operating profit have recovered in 2026 alongside expanding grid investment.

Full-year 2025 results showed sales of KRW 299.1 billion and operating profit of KRW 6.8 billion, both lower, but sales have risen for three straight quarters from the first quarter through the third quarter of 2026.

The growth is centred on CTC and rectangular wire for transformers and on extra-high-voltage cable materials, and the automotive segment has also grown through customer diversification.

On the other side, home-appliance and other sales are declining, and margins move from quarter to quarter, as the second quarter of 2026 showed when operating profit fell even though sales rose.

The debt ratio rose to 55.5% in 2025, so the financial burden and cash-flow trend after the capacity build-out also need to be watched.

Weighing demand recovery against margin volatility and financial burden equally, the next checkpoint is whether the capacity effect appears in operating margins in full-year 2026 and first-quarter 2027 results. This report is for information only and presents no investment opinion or target price.
