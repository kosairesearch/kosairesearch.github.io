02

Business structure

Wonik Holdings is the holding company of the Wonik group, operating in four segments: semiconductor equipment, gases, new businesses and investment.

Under the 2025 annual report, gases contributed 51.8% of revenue, semiconductor equipment 38.0%, new businesses 6.3% and investment 5.0%, so equipment and gases together account for more than 90% of sales.

The gas business runs through WONIK Materials, which produces and sells specialty gases such as NH3, CO2 and N2O for semiconductor and industrial use. The equipment segment supplies GSS and GPU systems and piping work on a made-to-order basis.

Its main customers are global semiconductor and display manufacturers, and the company is described as a first-tier supplier to Samsung Electronics and SK hynix. The new-business segment centers on D2i, which designs display driver ICs, and on a robotics subsidiary that makes humanoid robot hands.

New-business revenue rose to KRW 39.4 billion in 2025 from a prior-year level of KRW 17.4 billion, yet it remains only 6.3% of the total. Among affiliates, Wonik IPS makes deposition equipment, and Wonik Holdings books its share of that company through equity-method accounting.

Wonik QNC supplies quartz parts and Wonik PNE makes secondary-battery equipment. Ownership is held by Wonik Corp. at 30.0% and by chairman Lee Yong-han at 18.1%, and commentary has pointed to a layered governance structure since the holding-company conversion.

04

Earnings analysis

Based on the disclosed annual figures, 2022 was the largest year of the 2022 to 2025 period, and revenue and operating profit have both declined since.

Revenue went from KRW 881 billion in 2022 to KRW 749.5 billion in 2023 (down 14.9% year on year), KRW 645.5 billion in 2024 (down 13.9%) and KRW 623 billion in 2025 (down 3.5%), a three-year decline from 2023 through 2025.

Operating profit fell from KRW 105 billion in 2022 to KRW 45.8 billion in 2023 (down 56.4%) and KRW 30.6 billion in 2024 (down 33.1%), and the company swung to an operating loss of KRW 418 million in 2025. Operating margin moved from 11.9% in 2022 to 6.1% in 2023, 4.7% in 2024 and -0.1% in 2025.

The company attributed the 2025 decline in sales and operating profit to the timing of new investments by customers. Net income attributable to controlling shareholders, however, moved from a loss of KRW 74.5 billion in 2024 to a profit of KRW 27.4 billion in 2025.

Total net income for 2025 was KRW 50.4 billion, a separate figure that includes non-controlling interests. The company linked the improvement to higher equity-method gains from affiliates and to asset sales.

On a quarterly basis, operating losses of KRW 8.07 billion in Q3 2025 and KRW 723 million in Q4 2025 were followed by a KRW 2.32 billion operating loss in Q1 2026, before a KRW 12.8 billion operating profit in Q2 2026 and KRW 25.3 billion in Q3 2026, which was up 97.7% from the prior quarter.

Controlling-shareholder net income, by contrast, was KRW 7.14 billion in Q3 2026, down 74.7% from the prior quarter. Operating cash flow was positive in each of 2023, 2024 and 2025, at KRW 132.2 billion, KRW 101.6 billion and KRW 118.1 billion.

05

Industry analysis

Wonik Holdings' end demand is tied to the semiconductor investment cycle. Its gas business supplies specialty gases for chip processes, so it is directly affected by the timing of customer investments and process transitions.

Revenue at the Xi'an site fell 19% to KRW 38.7 billion in 2025 from KRW 47.6 billion a year earlier, as a shift to next-generation equipment in China cut plant utilization and reduced gas supply from WONIK Materials.

Some observers point to AI data-center expansion and rising HBM demand as tailwinds for the equipment segment.

A February 2026 news report cited industry expectations that the semiconductor supercycle would continue, with demand recovering into the second half of the year, but this remains an industry view rather than a company figure.

Competitively, the company stresses a group value chain spanning equipment, gases and components, and it showcased Wonik IPS, Wonik QNC and WONIK Materials together at SEMICON Korea 2026.

On the new-business side, physical AI and humanoid robot themes drew investor attention, and robotics-related affiliates were cited as a driver of share-price gains in 2025.

New businesses still account for only 6.3% of revenue, so how much industry growth flows into consolidated results will show up in the quarterly disclosures.

In new businesses, an April 2026 news report cited expectations that OLED DDI revenue at D2i would gain traction in 2026. The robotics subsidiary focuses on humanoid robot hands, and early 2026 reports of robot-arm supply to a global tech company were treated as share-price catalysts.

Many observers still view robotics revenue as not yet at scale, so its size remains uncertain. In gases, the timing of the end of the China process transition is a key variable. The company's latest formal guidance was not found in the disclosures reviewed.

The next checkpoints are therefore the Q4 2026 preliminary results and segment revenue trends in the 2026 annual report.

Bull factors

Recovery in 2026 results

Operating profit turned positive at KRW 12.8 billion in Q2 2026 and rose to KRW 25.3 billion in Q3. Revenue reached KRW 203.3 billion in Q3, up 41.7% from a year earlier and rising for two straight quarters after the Q1 2026 low. If profitability holds, it would separate the recovery from the 2025 losses.

Room to grow in new businesses

New-business revenue rose to KRW 39.4 billion in 2025, more than doubling from the prior year. D2i designs OLED display driver ICs for smartphones, and reports expected its revenue to gain traction in 2026.

The robotics subsidiary focuses on humanoid robot hands, which could diversify the revenue mix if the segment grows.

Large order backlog

An order-tracking service listed, as of April 13, 2026, an EPC turnkey project ordered by Linde Korea worth KRW 296.3 billion. Large items also include semiconductor equipment for Samsung (China) at KRW 116 billion and display equipment for Samsung Display at KRW 67.6 billion. The pace at which these orders convert into revenue will show up in quarterly results.

Bear factors

Weaker core profitability

Operating margin fell from 11.9% in 2022 to 6.1% in 2023, 4.7% in 2024 and -0.1% in 2025, while revenue declined for three straight years.

The 2025 return to positive controlling-shareholder net income owed much to equity-method gains and asset sales, which the company itself cited, so it should be distinguished from a recovery in core operations.

Volatile quarterly earnings

Controlling-shareholder net income fell to KRW 7.14 billion in Q3 2026, down 74.7% from the prior quarter, and the last four quarters total KRW 38 billion with profits and losses alternating.

Operating profit has turned positive, but net income has not moved in step, so the durability of earnings still needs confirming.

China process transition hits gas demand

Revenue at the Xi'an site fell 19% to KRW 38.7 billion in 2025 from KRW 47.6 billion a year earlier. A shift to next-generation equipment in China lowered plant utilization and reduced gas supply from WONIK Materials. Gases account for 51.8% of revenue, so customer process changes flow directly into consolidated sales.

Risk factors

Customer investment timing

The company cited the timing of customer investments as a reason for lower 2025 sales and operating profit. Both the equipment and gas segments depend on customer capacity expansion and process changes, so a delay in investment can sharply increase quarterly volatility.

Governance and largest-shareholder stake changes

In December 2025, a family company linked to the largest shareholder sold its entire 830,000-share stake in Wonik Holdings, and reports said the KRW 28.2 billion proceeds would go to repaying borrowings.

Commentary has also pointed to a multilayer structure, with the group controlled through several holding entities, which has persisted since the holding-company conversion.

Theme-driven trading and market conditions

The share price has moved sharply on physical AI, robotics and semiconductor themes. In February 2026, an alternative trading system also removed the stock from its matching list.

With new businesses at only 6.3% of revenue, theme-driven expectations running ahead of reported results could keep share-price volatility high.

Overall view

Wonik Holdings' revenue and operating profit declined after 2022, the company swung to an operating loss in 2025, and operating profit turned positive from Q2 2026.

The 2025 return to positive controlling-shareholder net income reflected equity-method gains and asset sales, as the company explained, so core-business recovery and net income improvement should be read separately.

Q3 2026 operating profit of KRW 25.3 billion and revenue of KRW 203.3 billion show a recovery trend, yet controlling-shareholder net income fell to KRW 7.14 billion in the same quarter. On orders, large EPC and equipment contracts are listed, so the timing of revenue recognition is the next checkpoint.

New businesses are growing quickly but remain 6.3% of revenue. Governance changes, the largest shareholder's stake sale and theme-driven trading are risk factors to track alongside results. The 2026 annual report will show full-year operating margin and segment revenue trends.
