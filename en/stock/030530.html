Back in the Black in Q2 2026, but Profit Recovery Leans on Affiliates

02

Business structure

Wonik Holdings is a business holding company within the Wonik Group, founded in 1991 and listed on KOSDAQ in 1996.

It split off its semiconductor and display equipment business into Wonik IPS in 2016 to form the holding structure, and was designated a large business group subject to public disclosure by the Fair Trade Commission in May 2024.

Based on the 2025 business report, the gas segment, operated through WONIK Materials, generated KRW 322.5 billion (51.8% of sales), and the semiconductor equipment segment generated KRW 236.6 billion (38.0%).

The new-business segment, which includes a display driver IC design subsidiary and a robotics subsidiary, contributed KRW 39.4 billion (6.3%), while the investment segment, covering real estate, leisure assets and venture funds, contributed KRW 31.1 billion (5.0%).

The gas business supplies specialty gases such as NH3, CO2 and N2O to semiconductor and display customers in Korea and abroad. The equipment business manufactures and sells gas supply systems (GSS and GPU) and provides piping work, which the business report classifies as TGS operations.

Wonik IPS is an equity-method affiliate in which the company holds a stake of about 33%, while WONIK Materials and Wonik QNC are consolidated subsidiaries. Although gases make up more than half of sales, quarterly revenue in the equipment business swings sharply with customers' facility investment schedules.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual sales moved from KRW 881 billion in 2022 to KRW 749.5 billion in 2023 (down 14.9% year on year), KRW 645.5 billion in 2024 (down 13.9%) and KRW 623 billion in 2025 (down 3.5%), a decline for three straight years from 2023 through 2025. Among the 2022 to 2025 years, 2022 was the largest and 2025 the smallest.

Operating profit fell from KRW 105 billion in 2022 to KRW 45.8 billion in 2023 (down 56.4%) and KRW 30.6 billion in 2024 (down 33.1%), before the company recorded an operating loss of KRW 418 million in 2025. The operating margin declined from 11.9% in 2022 to 6.1% in 2023, 4.7% in 2024 and -0.1% in 2025.

The company attributed the drop in sales and operating profit to the timing of new investments by its customers.

Profit attributable to controlling shareholders went from KRW 128.8 billion in 2022 to a net loss of KRW 31.7 billion in 2023, widened to a net loss of KRW 74.5 billion in 2024, and turned to a net profit of KRW 27.4 billion in 2025. Net profit including non-controlling interests was KRW 50.4 billion in 2025.

The company cited higher equity-method gains from improving affiliate conditions and the sale of idle assets as the drivers of the improved bottom line. The 2025 earnings recovery therefore relied more on equity-method gains and an asset sale than on operations.

At the end of 2025, total equity was KRW 1.3 trillion, of which equity attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 999.7 billion, and total liabilities were KRW 668.5 billion, with the debt-to-equity ratio rising from 37.4% in 2022 to 51.4% in 2025.

Operating cash flow moved from KRW -3.67 billion in 2022 to KRW 132.2 billion in 2023, KRW 101.6 billion in 2024 and KRW 118.1 billion in 2025.

On a quarterly basis, Q2 2025 sales were KRW 157.6 billion with an operating loss of KRW 1.86 billion, and Q3 2025 sales were KRW 143.4 billion (down 9.0% from the prior quarter) with an operating loss that widened to KRW 8.07 billion.

Q4 2025 sales were KRW 151 billion with an operating loss narrowed to KRW 723 million. Q1 2026 sales were KRW 117.1 billion (down 22.4% from the prior quarter) with an operating loss of KRW 2.32 billion.

Q2 2026 sales were KRW 176.5 billion (up 50.6% from the prior quarter and 12.0% year on year), and operating profit reached KRW 12.8 billion. Profit attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 24.2 billion in Q3 2025 and KRW 28.2 billion in Q2 2026, while Q1 2026 posted a loss of KRW 2.52 billion.

Over the last four quarters (Q3 2025 to Q2 2026), profit attributable to controlling shareholders totaled KRW 55.1 billion.

05

Industry analysis

The equipment and gas businesses at Wonik Holdings are tied to the semiconductor capital expenditure cycle. Data provider Company Monitor notes that rising demand for AI accelerators and HBM is reshaping the equipment market around high-end products, and that Wonik Holdings is focusing on securing global customers.

The gas segment supplies high-purity specialty gases to semiconductor and display customers worldwide, while equipment revenue is recognized when customers install new facilities.

Its affiliate Wonik IPS supplies CVD, ALD and metal deposition equipment used in core DRAM, NAND and logic processes and is pursuing localization of low-temperature PECVD tools.

Wonik IPS reported provisional consolidated results for Q2 2026 on August 6, 2026, with sales of KRW 216.5 billion (down 10.6% year on year), operating profit of KRW 18.4 billion (down 49.6%) and net profit of KRW 89.9 billion (up 225.2%).

Its operating trend softened while its bottom line rose, a mix that flows into Wonik Holdings through equity-method accounting.

Unlike SK Square or LG, which hold more diversified investment portfolios, Wonik Holdings derives most of its earnings from semiconductor materials and equipment manufacturing, making its results more sensitive to chip industry conditions.

A Money Today report from April 2026 highlighted growth in the new businesses, including fabless design and robotics.

06

Outlook

The disclosed Q2 2026 result of KRW 12.8 billion in operating profit marks a swing back into the black, and the next checkpoint is whether that profit holds through the third quarter and beyond.

In the new-business segment, the display driver IC design subsidiary is focused on OLED driver chips for smartphones, and the robotics subsidiary is continuing with robotic hands for humanoid robots, according to a Money Today report from April 2026.

In December 2025, the subsidiary Wonik PNE merged with PNE Systems, consolidating its secondary battery equipment operations.

Wonik IPS reported lower provisional sales and operating profit for Q2 2026 but a sharp rise in net profit, so how that equity-method income flows into Wonik Holdings will be visible in the third-quarter report.

WONIK Materials' gas business accounted for more than half of 2025 sales and has offset part of the volatility in equipment revenue. Equipment revenue has swung sharply from quarter to quarter in line with customers' facility schedules, so the pace at which equipment sales recover after Q3 2026 is a key variable.

New-business sales were KRW 39.4 billion in 2025 (6.3% of sales), so their scale remains modest relative to the group. With the debt-to-equity ratio at 51.4%, funding needs for new businesses and subsidiaries will also be worth tracking in upcoming reports.

Bull factors

Operating profit back in the black in Q2 2026

High-end reshaping of the chip equipment market

Company Monitor notes that rising demand for AI accelerators and HBM is shifting the equipment market toward high-end products. Affiliate Wonik IPS disclosed provisional Q2 2026 net profit up 225.2% year on year. Equity-method income from affiliates can support the group's overall bottom line.

Diversification through new businesses

New-business sales were KRW 39.4 billion in 2025, or 6.3% of the total, according to the 2025 business report. Money Today reported in April 2026 that the fabless and robotics businesses are growing as the company diversifies beyond semiconductor materials. Whether this widens the earnings base will depend on how these businesses scale.

Bear factors

Sales decline and the 2025 operating loss

Sales declined from KRW 881 billion in 2022 to KRW 623 billion in 2025, with declines in each year from 2023 through 2025. The operating margin fell from 11.9% in 2022 to -0.1% in 2025, when the company swung to an operating loss of KRW 418 million. Recovery in core operating profitability has yet to show up in the reported figures.

Quality of the earnings recovery and quarterly volatility

The company attributed the 2025 return to net profit mainly to higher equity-method gains and the sale of idle assets. Q1 2026 posted an operating loss of KRW 2.32 billion and a net loss attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 2.52 billion, showing renewed quarterly volatility. Earnings driven by equity-method income and one-off asset sales may be less recurring.

Rising financial leverage

The debt-to-equity ratio rose from 37.4% in 2022 to 51.4% in both 2024 and 2025. At the end of 2025, total liabilities were KRW 668.5 billion against total equity of KRW 1.3 trillion. Continued funding needs for new businesses and subsidiaries could increase financial strain.

10

Risk factors

Semiconductor capex cycle

Equipment and gas sales move sharply from quarter to quarter with customers' facility investment schedules. Sales fell 9.0% from the prior quarter in Q3 2025 and 22.4% in Q1 2026. A slowdown in memory investment could drive simultaneous swings in sales and operating profit.

Reliance on equity-method income

The 2025 return to profit attributable to controlling shareholders relied heavily on equity-method income from affiliates such as Wonik IPS. Wonik IPS's provisional operating profit for Q2 2026 fell 49.6% year on year. A weaker operating trend at affiliates could reduce equity-method income and affect the group's net profit.

Governance and succession

Blotter reported that the group's chairman has been transferring control of Wonik Group to his children through the family company Horizon. Wonik Holdings is a holding company that is controlled through Wonik, a structure media have described as layered.

Because the succession process appears to be near completion, the effect of governance changes on minority shareholders is also worth reviewing.

Overall view

Wonik Holdings' sales and operating profit declined from 2022, and the company swung to an operating loss in 2025. Profit attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 27.4 billion in 2025, but the company attributed the improvement mainly to equity-method gains and the sale of idle assets.

Q2 2026 sales of KRW 176.5 billion and operating profit of KRW 12.8 billion marked a return to the black, but Q1 2026 had posted an operating loss again. Profit attributable to controlling shareholders totaled KRW 55.1 billion over the last four quarters.
