Report overview

Core Profit Returns; Robotics, Equity Income in Focus

Wonik Holdings swung to an operating profit of KRW 12.8 billion in Q2 2026. Its recent net profit, however, has leaned heavily on equity-method income from affiliates and asset sales, and market attention is centred on its robotics subsidiary, Wonik Robotics. Whether the core business recovery holds is the key point to verify.

  1. 1

    Revenue fell from KRW 881 billion in 2022 to KRW 623 billion in 2025, three consecutive years of decline from 2023 to 2025. Over the same period the operating margin slid from 11.9% to -0.1%.

  2. 2

    In 2025 the company posted an operating loss of KRW 418 million, yet net profit attributable to controlling shareholders turned positive at KRW 27.4 billion. The company attributed this to higher equity-method income as affiliates' business conditions improved, and the sale of idle assets.

  3. 3

    The gas segment carried the profit base. In 2025 it posted revenue of KRW 323 billion (51.8% of the total) and operating profit of KRW 56.5 billion. Its share of revenue rose to 61.5% in the first half of 2026.

  4. 4

    In December 2025 the company signed a KRW 296.3 billion turnkey EPC contract with Linde Korea for semiconductor specialty gas supply. The contract runs through April 30, 2028.

  5. 5

    Wonik Robotics is an unlisted subsidiary 96.37% owned by Wonik Holdings. It is pursuing a KRW 350 billion capital raise, in which its pre-money valuation was assessed at about KRW 1.35 trillion.

02

Business structure

Wonik Holdings is the surviving entity of the 2016 spin-off of the former Wonik IPS. It kept the investment and Total Gas Solution (TGS) businesses, while equipment manufacturing moved to the newly formed Wonik IPS.

Its consolidated operations now fall into four areas: semiconductor equipment, gas, new businesses and investment. The gas segment, centred on subsidiary WONIK Materials, supplies high-purity specialty gases to chip and display makers, and it generated most of the group's 2025 operating profit.

The semiconductor equipment segment focuses on process gas supply systems, piping and EPC work. The company is a tier-one supplier to Samsung Electronics and SK hynix. In the first half of 2026, gas made up 61.5% of revenue and semiconductor equipment 32.9%.

Its 32.95% stake in deposition-equipment maker Wonik IPS (including related parties) flows into consolidated results as equity-method income. The new-business segment, which includes fabless chip designer TLi and Wonik Robotics, lost KRW 41.5 billion in 2025 and dragged down consolidated operating profit.

Wonik Robotics has developed the Allegro Hand robotic hand since 2012, and since 2024 it has been co-developing a dedicated AI hand with Meta.

04

Earnings analysis

Revenue declined for three consecutive years, from KRW 881 billion in 2022 to KRW 749.5 billion in 2023, KRW 645.5 billion in 2024 and KRW 623 billion in 2025.

Operating profit fell from KRW 105 billion in 2022 to KRW 45.8 billion in 2023 and KRW 30.6 billion in 2024, then turned into an operating loss of KRW 418 million in 2025. The operating margin dropped accordingly: 11.9%, 6.1%, 4.7% and -0.1%.

The company blamed the weak 2025 results on the timing of customers' new investments. Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders went from a net loss of KRW 31.7 billion in 2023 to a wider net loss of KRW 74.5 billion in 2024, before swinging to a profit of KRW 27.4 billion in 2025.

Net profit including non-controlling interests was KRW 50.4 billion in 2025. Quarterly, the company posted operating losses every quarter from Q2 2025 (KRW 1.86 billion) through Q1 2026 (KRW 2.32 billion), then turned to an operating profit of KRW 12.8 billion in Q2 2026.

Q2 2026 revenue was KRW 176.5 billion, up 50.6% from the prior quarter and 12.0% from a year earlier. In Q3 2025 the company recorded an operating loss of KRW 8.07 billion, yet net profit attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 24.2 billion.

The KRW 55.1 billion in attributable net profit over the last four quarters therefore also leans heavily on non-operating items. On the balance sheet, the debt-to-equity ratio rose from 37.4% in 2022 to 51.4% in 2025, while operating cash flow stayed above KRW 100 billion each year from 2023 to 2025.

05

Industry analysis

Semiconductor end-demand is being driven by AI data center investment and HBM. Wonik Holdings' semiconductor equipment segment cites this trend as a tailwind. According to a February 2026 report, the industry expected the semiconductor super-cycle to continue.

However, gas-system and EPC revenue depends directly on when customers spend on new fabs, so quarterly results can swing widely even in a strong cycle. Specialty gas is a consumable whose demand tracks fab utilisation, which makes it relatively stable; the 8.8% rise in gas operating profit in 2025 reflects this.

Affiliate Wonik IPS supplies deposition tools to key customers' mass-production lines as the equipment market shifts toward high-end tools. In robotics, robotic hands, together with actuators, are seen as core parts making up 70% of humanoid cost.

Wonik Robotics is the first robotics company to receive a direct equity investment from the National Growth Fund. Still, robotic hands are an early market in which direct-drive designs (a motor at each joint) compete with tendon-driven designs (joints pulled by wires), and no standard has emerged yet.

06

Outlook

On the order side, the confirmed item is the Linde Korea EPC contract. It will be recognised as revenue through April 2028, and press coverage described it as a factor raising mid-to-long-term earnings visibility.

In February 2026, the company's internal view was reported as expecting recovering demand to improve the cost structure. No quantitative earnings guidance has been disclosed, however.

On September 2, 2026, Wonik Robotics launched the five-finger, 20-degree-of-freedom Allegro Hand V6 F, and it plans to release a V6 V model with vision-based tactile sensors. The Financial Services Commission approved the equity investment in Wonik Robotics on August 27, 2026.

The proceeds will fund a robotic-hand plant and AX center in Wanju, North Jeolla Province. Of the KRW 350 billion, KRW 150 billion comes from the Advanced Strategic Industries Fund via convertible preferred shares, and the rest from private investors including IMM Credit & Solutions.

Bringing in outside capital will lower Wonik Holdings' stake in Wonik Robotics. The post-deal stake has not been confirmed.

08

Bull factors

Gas segment anchors earnings

Although consolidated operating profit was negative in 2025, the gas segment actually grew its operating profit to KRW 56.5 billion. Specialty gas is a consumable tied to fab utilisation and fluctuates less than the equipment investment cycle.

With gas at 61.5% of revenue in the first half of 2026, the consolidated operating margin has room to improve if new-business losses narrow.

Backlog plus equity income

The KRW 296.3 billion Linde Korea EPC contract is close to half of 2025 annual revenue, and it will be recognised through 2028. Affiliate Wonik IPS grew first-half 2026 revenue by 4.1%. If memory investment continues, equity-method income could also support attributable net profit.

Robotics unit priced by outsiders

In a capital raise involving a policy fund and private investors, Wonik Robotics was valued at about KRW 1.35 trillion pre-money. It has secured more than 180 research partners and also works with Meta. Once the Wanju plant is running, its revenue base could shift from mainly research units toward volume production.

09

Bear factors

Recovery rests on one quarter

Each of the four quarters before Q2 2026's KRW 12.8 billion operating profit was an operating loss. Annual revenue declined for three straight years from 2023 to 2025. Because quarterly revenue swings with customers' investment schedules, whether profits persist must be confirmed from Q3 2026 results onward.

Net profit leans on non-operating items

The KRW 27.4 billion attributable net profit in 2025 came from equity-method income and idle-asset sales despite an operating loss. Asset sales are hard to repeat, and equity income depends on the business conditions of affiliates such as Wonik IPS. If non-operating items shrink, earnings-based multiples could change sharply.

New-business losses and dilution

The new-business segment, including fabless chips and robotics, lost KRW 41.5 billion in 2025, offsetting much of the gas segment's profit. The Wonik Robotics capital raise will reduce Wonik Holdings' ownership. Until the large plant investment is complete, the subsidiary's depreciation and operating costs may also rise.

10

Risk factors

Customer concentration and capex timing

Gas-system and EPC revenue depends on the fab investment schedules of a few customers such as Samsung Electronics and SK hynix. The company itself attributed the 2025 revenue decline to the timing of customers' new investments. If customer expansions are delayed, quarterly results can swing sharply even with a backlog in hand.

Governance

According to a February 2025 report, the group's control chain runs from Chairman Lee Yong-han's family to Horizon, then WonIk, then Wonik Holdings and on to affiliates. Media outlets have questioned the propriety of the succession process under this structure. Regulatory changes such as amendments to the Commercial Act could affect intra-group capital transactions.

Balance sheet and theme volatility

The debt-to-equity ratio rose from 37.4% in 2022 to 51.4% in both 2024 and 2025, and the company pays no dividend. The share price has moved sharply with physical-AI and robotics themes. In February 2026, alternative trading venue Nextrade removed the stock from its tradable list. If robotics results fall short of expectations, volatility could rise again.

12

Overall view

Wonik Holdings' consolidated revenue and operating profit have both declined since peaking in 2022. In 2025 it posted an operating loss, but equity-method income and asset sales turned attributable net profit positive.

Stable gas-segment profits and the Linde Korea EPC contract running to 2028 underpin a core-business recovery. On the other hand, new-business losses and quarterly swings tied to customer capex timing remain a burden.

Wonik Robotics has been valued highly by outside investors, but dilution from the raise and its volume-production results are not yet proven. The next points of evidence are whether operating profit continues from Q3 2026 onward and how the robotics subsidiary's capital-raise terms are finalised.
