Report overview

Hanatour Service: Margin Pressure as Package Demand Lags

Hanatour Service's operating profit grew for two straight years through 2025, but both revenue and operating profit declined for three consecutive quarters from the first quarter of 2026 through the third. High oil prices and a weaker won have pushed back the recovery in package demand, which is the central issue now.

  1. 1

    Full-year 2025 results show revenue of KRW 586.9 billion (down 4.8% year on year) and operating profit of KRW 57.6 billion (up 13.2%). Operating profit has risen for two straight years, from 2024 through 2025.

  2. 2

    Operating profit in the third quarter of 2026 was KRW 5.13 billion, down 38.0% from a year earlier, with an operating margin of 4.6%. Revenue and operating profit have declined for three consecutive quarters since the fourth quarter of 2025 peak.

  3. 3

    Package passengers fell while independent travel passengers rose, so total travel demand held up but the mix of earnings is shifting. In the third quarter of 2026, package passengers were about 400,000, down 11%, while independent travel passengers were about 1.26 million, up 15%.

  4. 4

02

Business structure

Hanatour Service plans and sells package tours, and also runs independent travel (FIT), where customers combine flights and hotels on their own. In the third quarter of 2026, package passengers were about 400,000, down 11%, while independent travel passengers were about 1.26 million, up 15%.

Medium- and high-priced packages reached 51% of package gross merchandise value in 2025 and 32% of passengers, a record share. Monthly active users of the mobile app were 790,000 in the fourth quarter of 2025, up 22% from a year earlier.

Japan and China have been the growth engines: first-quarter 2026 package passengers to Japan rose 26% to 201,000 and to China rose 35% to 84,000. Southeast Asia, however, accounted for more than 40% of passengers over the past three years. MODE TOUR NETWORK INC is the closest listed peer in package travel.

04

Earnings analysis

On a reported basis, revenue rose from KRW 115 billion in 2022 to KRW 411.6 billion in 2023 (up 258.0%) and KRW 616.6 billion in 2024 (up 49.8%), making 2024 the largest year in the 2022 to 2025 window. Revenue then fell to KRW 586.9 billion in 2025 (down 4.8%).

Operating profit moved from an operating loss of KRW 101.2 billion in 2022 to a profit of KRW 34 billion in 2023, then rose to KRW 50.9 billion in 2024 (up 49.6%) and KRW 57.6 billion in 2025 (up 13.2%), an increase in each of 2024 and 2025. The operating margin was 8.3% in 2023, 8.3% in 2024 and 9.8% in 2025.

Net income attributable to owners of the parent peaked at KRW 81.5 billion in 2024 (up 73.2%) and fell to KRW 31.9 billion in 2025 (down 60.8%), while total net income for 2025 was KRW 48.1 billion. Fourth-quarter 2025 operating profit rose to KRW 27.4 billion, up 231.1% from the prior quarter.

The 2025 gap between operating profit and attributable profit reflects fourth-quarter non-operating items, including KRW 18.7 billion in provisions at an equity-method affiliate and KRW 3.6 billion in demolition costs at a Japanese hotel subsidiary.

In the first quarter of 2026, revenue was KRW 174.8 billion and operating profit KRW 16.8 billion. In the second quarter, revenue fell 34.0% to KRW 115.4 billion and operating profit fell 67.5% to KRW 5.44 billion.

In the third quarter, revenue was KRW 111.7 billion (down 9.4% year on year) and operating profit was KRW 5.13 billion (down 38.0%). Net income in the third quarter exceeded operating profit because of KRW 2.4 billion in foreign-exchange-related gains. These third-quarter figures are preliminary. The last four quarters together produced KRW 38.5 billion in attributable net income.

05

Industry analysis

Outbound demand depends heavily on oil prices and the exchange rate. High oil prices tied to the Middle East conflict and a weaker won have been cited as reasons travel demand has weakened.

Brokers expect annual package passengers to fall for a second straight year in 2026, the first back-to-back decline outside the COVID period since 2008 to 2009. Hana Securities projects Korean outbound travelers to rise about 7% in 2027, citing the weak yen and expanded short-haul routes to China and Japan.

Changes in consumption are also visible: independent travel is growing while package demand is shrinking. Kiwoom Securities has said Hanatour Service is maintaining a rising market share. Still, the shift of Southeast Asia traffic toward Japan and China has not fully offset the loss.

06

Outlook

Management has said new bookings slowed in the second quarter, so the direction of near-term demand is still being confirmed. The company is pushing promotions built on flights without fuel surcharges and expanding premium and themed products.

The winter travel festival, running from November 2 to November 29, 2026, offers discounts of up to 60% on winter tours. Online channels and AI-based demand forecasting for seat and hotel purchasing are the main cost levers.

Shinhan Investment Corp. projects 2026 consolidated revenue of KRW 612.5 billion and operating profit of KRW 55.1 billion, slightly below the KRW 57.6 billion reported for 2025. Hana Securities expects 2027 revenue of KRW 632 billion and operating profit of KRW 65.9 billion.

Daol Investment & Securities set a target of KRW 37,000 on September 18, 2026, down from KRW 42,000, and cited fuel surcharges and high exchange rates weighing on package sentiment. Shinhan Investment Corp. lowered its target from KRW 61,000 to KRW 46,000 on July 14, 2026.

The next confirmation point is the 2026 annual report, where the full-year operating margin and package passenger trend will be the key items.

08

Bull factors

Recovery in China and Japan outbound

First-quarter 2026 package passengers rose 12.1% to 650,000. Japan and China outbound package passengers grew 26% and 35%, respectively, according to Kiwoom Securities. Visa exemption for China and the spread of small-town travel in Japan were cited as drivers.

Signs of a shift in earnings mix

The fourth-quarter 2025 operating margin of 15.6% was the highest since 2012. Medium- and high-priced packages reached 51% of 2025 GMV, a record share, and held 54% of third-quarter 2026 gross merchandise value.

Independent travel and online base

Independent travel passengers in the third quarter of 2026 rose 15%. The company said first-quarter FIT passengers reached a record. Online membership grew to 9.44 million.

09

Bear factors

Weaker package demand

Third-quarter 2026 package passengers fell about 11% to roughly 400,000. High oil prices and a weaker won raised the cost of overseas travel. Hana Securities expects annual package passengers to fall for a second straight year.

Falling profitability

Third-quarter 2026 operating profit fell 38.0% to KRW 5.13 billion, with a 4.6% margin. The decline in operating profit outpaced the drop in revenue. Shinhan Investment Corp. pointed to a lower revenue conversion rate and higher local costs.

Southeast Asia dependence and limited substitution

Southeast Asia accounted for more than 40% of passengers over the past three years. Airlines are shifting long-haul capacity to short-haul routes, and Japan and China may not fully replace the lost Southeast Asia demand. Shinhan Investment Corp. said the operating environment is unlikely to improve easily.

10

Risk factors

FX, oil prices and geopolitics

High oil prices from the Middle East conflict and a weaker won have been cited as weakening travel demand. In the second quarter, higher fuel surcharges reportedly dampened outbound sentiment. When the exchange rate and oil move together, package prices and demand can shift at the same time.

Shift in travel consumption

Independent travel is growing while package demand declines, putting the competitiveness of the package-centered model to the test. Online channels can reduce commission costs and are viewed as a lever for profitability. Whether independent travel can fully replace package profit remains to be seen.

Non-operating items and preliminary figures

One-off costs such as provisions at an equity-method affiliate and demolition costs at a Japanese hotel subsidiary can swing quarterly net income significantly. Third-quarter 2026 net income exceeded operating profit largely because of KRW 2.4 billion in foreign-exchange gains. These third-quarter figures are preliminary and may change in the external audit.

12

Overall view

Hanatour Service's operating profit grew for two straight years through 2025, following a revenue peak in 2024, and the earnings mix has been shifting. The 15.6% operating margin in the fourth quarter of 2025 reflected higher-priced packages and better charter-capacity management.

However, high oil prices, a weaker won and falling package demand led to three consecutive quarters of declining revenue and operating profit from the first quarter through the third of 2026, with a third-quarter margin of 4.6%.

Strengths include recovering outbound demand to China and Japan, a larger share of higher-priced packages and growth in independent travel and online channels. Weaknesses include fewer package passengers, reliance on Southeast Asia routes and volatility in non-operating items.

Broker targets have moved lower, with Daol Investment & Securities at KRW 37,000 (September 18, 2026) and Shinhan Investment Corp. at KRW 46,000 (July 14, 2026). The next checkpoints are the fourth-quarter 2026 preliminary results and the 2026 annual operating margin, and whether package passenger numbers turn upward.
