Business structure

Dgenx is an automotive new-parts manufacturer listed on the KOSDAQ market on November 22, 2012, and its main product is mufflers for vehicle exhaust systems. The company is described as making and selling automotive parts in South Korea and China, and it belongs to the DH Korea group.

Its main mufflers are developed alongside automakers' new model programs, which is cited as a basis for stable revenue. The overseas subsidiary DH HONG KONG is confirmed as 100% owned, and DH2 Energy was established in Samcheok, Gangwon Province in October 2022.

Revenue centers on parts supplied in line with automobile production volumes, so changes in the domestic revenue mix feed directly into results. For the decline in 2025 operating profit, the company pointed to lower domestic sales.

The company is classified under aerospace and defense, but the business confirmed in public sources is automotive mufflers, and defense or aerospace revenue cannot be separated from the disclosed figures.

Peers in the same classification tend to have results tied to government and export contract schedules, while Dgenx is tied to automaker production schedules and new model development cycles.

Competition is largely decided when automakers select parts suppliers, so winning or losing a single model can affect long-term revenue. Revenue peaked at KRW 89.5 billion in 2024 within the 2022 to 2025 range and fell to KRW 80.4 billion in 2025, which shows the volatility of this business structure.

04

Earnings analysis

On a disclosed basis, annual revenue rose from KRW 65 billion in 2022 to KRW 86 billion in 2023, up 32.5% from the prior year, and to KRW 89.5 billion in 2024, up 4.1%. It then fell to KRW 80.4 billion in 2025, down 10.3%. Within 2022 to 2025, 2024 was the largest year for revenue and 2022 the smallest.

Operating profit moved from KRW 218 million in 2022 to KRW 2.99 billion in 2023, up 1,275.5%, and KRW 5.52 billion in 2024, up 84.4%, before falling to KRW 963 million in 2025, down 82.6%. The operating margin was 0.3% in 2022, 3.5% in 2023 and 6.2% in 2024, then dropped to 1.2% in 2025.

The margin peaked in 2024, and in 2025 it compressed sharply alongside lower revenue. Net income attributable to controlling shareholders turned from a loss of KRW 2.1 billion in 2022 to a profit of KRW 2.96 billion in 2023, rose to KRW 4.49 billion in 2024, up 51.5%, and fell to KRW 745 million in 2025, down 83.4%.

For 2023 to 2025, total net income including non-controlling interests equaled the figure attributable to controlling shareholders. The debt ratio, defined as total liabilities divided by total equity, fell from 240.8% in 2022 and 243.7% in 2023 to 169.1% in 2024 and 142.0% in 2025.

At year-end 2025, total equity was KRW 23.9 billion and total liabilities were KRW 34 billion. Operating cash flow was KRW 1.59 billion in 2022, negative KRW 509 million in 2023, KRW 5.9 billion in 2024 and KRW 3.29 billion in 2025.

Quarterly, Q4 2025 revenue reached KRW 21.6 billion, up 40.2% from Q3, yet the company posted an operating loss of KRW 1.01 billion and a loss attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 1.19 billion.

Q1 2026 revenue was KRW 22.4 billion, up 3.7% quarter on quarter, and operating profit of KRW 1.2 billion turned the quarter profitable. Q2 2026 revenue was KRW 23.5 billion, up 5.0%, with operating profit of KRW 2.22 billion, up 84.9%, and net income attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 2.12 billion.

Q3 2026 revenue was KRW 18.7 billion, down 20.6% from Q2 but up 21.3% from a year earlier, and operating profit of KRW 496 million turned from a loss a year earlier.

The detailed causes of operating profit swings should be read alongside revenue movements, and cost and selling expense breakdowns are items to check in the next periodic report.

Industry analysis

Mufflers are components of exhaust systems in internal combustion vehicles, so demand is linked to automaker production volumes and new model development cycles.

Because the company attributed its 2025 operating profit decline to lower domestic sales, domestic automobile production volumes are a key variable for results. As automakers shift toward electrification, questions arise about the long-term demand for exhaust parts, which structurally affects a muffler-focused company.

The pace of that shift and model-level schedules cannot be settled from the public sources used here, and they are areas to be verified against company disclosures.

Within the aerospace and defense classification, listed companies include Hanwha Aerospace, Korea Aerospace Industries, Hyundai-Rotem and Hanwha Systems, but their results rest on defense orders and government programs, so direct comparison with Dgenx is limited.

Dgenx is a small company with annual revenue in the KRW 80 billion range, and the difference in business structure is wider when measured against large defense firms.

Within automotive parts, the comparison criteria are the relationship with automaker customers, whether new models are adopted, and the production footprint. The company has overseas operations through DH HONG KONG, so links to automobile production abroad, including China, are also items to check.

In its industry position, the company reads as a small parts supplier with high dependence on domestic volumes, which adds to earnings volatility.
