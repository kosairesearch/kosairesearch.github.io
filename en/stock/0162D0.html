Margin Upgrade Meets Customer Concentration

Dongwon Parts' operating margin reached 23% in the first half of 2026 on a richer product mix, yet 79.3% of first-half sales came from two customers, so the durability of earnings is still being tested.

02

Business structure

Dongwon Parts was founded in 1997 as a precision component maker for semiconductor process equipment. It produces showerheads, chamber parts, heaters and spin chucks used in etching, deposition, cleaning and ion implantation.

The company says it runs nine in-house manufacturing steps, from design and precision machining to bonding, surface treatment, assembly and inspection. In the first half of 2026, showerheads accounted for 37.4% of sales and chamber parts for 32.8%.

Sales split into parts for new equipment and consumables used to maintain installed tools, and the consumables share rose from 36.4% in 2022 to 45.1% in 2025.

Key customers include Lam Research, SEMES (a Samsung Electronics subsidiary), and Axcelis, while a showerhead qualification with Applied Materials is still under way.

Sales to global equipment makers rose from 37% of the total in 2023 to 56.4% in the first half of 2026, and the company targets the 70% range over the long term. NH Investment & Securities analyst Kang Kyung-keun described Dongwon Parts as the only domestic supplier among Lam Research's metal showerhead partners.

Overseas sales accounted for about 37% of the total in 2025. New businesses include aviation MRO and metal 3D printing, with the MRO unit planned to start operations in December 2026.

03

Recent trends

Dongwon Parts' full-year 2025 results show revenue of KRW 92.3 billion and operating profit of KRW 7.8 billion. Revenue in 2025 rose 55% from the prior year, and operating profit grew more than sevenfold. Net income for 2025 was KRW 3.4 billion, turning a KRW 5.6 billion net loss in the prior year into a profit.

First-half 2026 revenue was KRW 63.3 billion and operating profit was KRW 14.5 billion, a 23% operating margin. Cumulative preliminary results through the third quarter of 2026 show revenue of KRW 100.2 billion and operating profit of KRW 22.4 billion, a margin of about 22.4% for the period.

Operating margin moved from the 1% range in 2023 and 2024 to 8.4% in 2025 and then to 23% in the first half of 2026. NH Investment & Securities analyst Kang Kyung-keun estimated that the share of legacy products in showerhead sales fell from above 70% to around 10%.

The cost of sales ratio declined from 84.4% in 2025 to 71.5% in the first half of 2026. The debt-to-equity ratio at end-2025 was 296.9%, slightly below 297.16% a year earlier, while the borrowing dependence ratio at end-first-half 2026 was 35.3%.

On November 10, 2026, the first trading day, the stock closed at KRW 29,400, up 10.94% from the offering price, after touching KRW 57,300 intraday and giving back most of that gain. Market capitalization at the close was KRW 272.6 billion.

04

Outlook

The No. 7 plant in Namchon-dong, Incheon, with a total floor area of 13,246 square meters, broke ground in October 2026 and targets completion in July 2027. The company expects mass production to begin only in 2028, after post-completion procedures.

Of the net proceeds of KRW 52.76 billion from the offering, KRW 31.32 billion will fund facilities and KRW 21.41 billion will repay debt. Debt repayment is scheduled at KRW 8.2 billion in 2026 and KRW 13.206 billion in 2027. The company projected an annual operating margin of about 22% for 2026.

The order backlog stood at KRW 49.5 billion as of August 2026. Hana Securities analyst Han Yoo-geon said the aviation MRO business would ramp up at the end of 2026 and that the company obtained AS9100 quality certification in October 2026.

Metal 3D printing is at the stage of discussing production of parts with semiconductor customers and developing precision parts for aerospace and defense.

Points to track next are the start date of the No. 7 plant, the company's stated practice of keeping any single customer below 50% of sales, the pace of debt repayment, and whether the aviation MRO business wins its first order.

05

Bull factors

Margin gains from legacy replacement

According to NH Investment & Securities, the legacy share of showerhead sales fell sharply. The company also says its newer in-house products are replacing legacy items as older tools are discontinued, lifting margins. A lighter cost burden is the backdrop to the margin improvement.

Operating margin rose from 8.4% in 2025 to 23% in the first half of 2026, showing how higher-margin supply is flowing through the profit line.

HBM replacement demand and a wider global customer base

The company says that as HBM stacking increases, etch depth and process difficulty rise, which can accelerate wear on tool parts and shorten replacement cycles. Sales to global equipment makers grew from about KRW 18.7 billion in 2023 to about KRW 47.3 billion in 2025.

Sales to global equipment makers were 56.4% of the total in the first half of 2026, and the company has set a long-term goal in the 70% range. The order backlog was KRW 49.5 billion as of August 2026.

A consumables-driven revenue base

The consumables share of sales rose from 36.4% in 2022 to 45.1% in 2025. Vice President Jo Jun said the company has built a range of parts that wear out steadily even without new equipment investment, and that talks with new customers center on consumables.

Sales are supported by both new-tool demand and maintenance demand for installed tools, which is cited as a relative strength.

06

Bear factors

Two-customer concentration and pricing pressure

Lam Research (45.9%) and SEMES (33.4%) generated 79.3% of first-half 2026 sales. In 2025, two customers that each accounted for more than 10% of sales together contributed KRW 76.8 billion, about 83% of the total.

The company's securities filing lists customer concentration as a risk factor that could increase price-cut pressure from large clients. The company says it manages each customer's share to stay below 50% of sales.

High leverage and short-term borrowings

The debt-to-equity ratio at end-2025 was 296.9%, a high level. Total borrowings at end-first-half 2026 were KRW 45.79 billion, with a borrowing dependence ratio of 35.3%.

Current portion of long-term borrowings and short-term borrowings combined reached KRW 32.5 billion at end-2025, more than double the KRW 15.0 billion at end-2024. The use of offering proceeds for debt repayment is described as a step to ease this burden.

Post-listing supply overhang

Free-float shares immediately after listing were 17.52%, below the 29.47% originally expected. The mandatory holding commitment ratio, based on demand-forecast applications, was 16.6%, and most of it was a 15-day commitment. Shares of 478,593 subject to the mandatory lock-up will be released on November 15, 2026. The gap between the intraday high and the close on the first trading day marks a period of large price swings.

07

Risk factors

Macro

Revenue depends directly on memory makers' HBM investment and the pace of tool replacement. A slowdown in semiconductor equipment spending would affect both new-part and consumables demand. A change in industry conditions could also alter the pace of the shift to higher-margin products.

Customer concentration

Dependence on two customers and pressure on pricing are the main sources of earnings variability. If diversification among global equipment makers falls short of target, concentration will remain high.

Shower head supply to Applied Materials is still in qualification, and the company said it cannot yet specify timing or scale.

Execution and financing

If completion of the No. 7 plant (targeted for July 2027) or the start of mass production (expected in 2028) slips, the supply expansion plan will slip as well. Facility investment and debt repayment are running at the same time, so cash flow management matters.

The new businesses in aviation MRO and metal 3D printing are at an early stage, and their timing for contributing to reported results is still early.

08

Overall view

Dongwon Parts' earnings profile has changed noticeably through the shift away from legacy products and a higher consumables share. Cumulative operating profit through the third quarter of 2026 exceeding full-year 2025 results shows improvement in operating metrics.

However, 79.3% of first-half 2026 sales came from two customers and the borrowing dependence ratio stood at 35.3%, so stability in scale and financing still needs further confirmation.

Bullish factors commonly cited include a higher share of sales to global equipment makers and the No. 7 plant expansion, while bearish factors include customer concentration, borrowings and post-listing supply.

NH Investment & Securities highlighted the margin gains from the legacy transition, and Hana Securities focused on the timing of the aviation MRO business ramp-up.
