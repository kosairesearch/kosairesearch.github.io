Report overview

Absorbed by Woori Financial: Delisting in Motion

Tong Yang Life became a wholly owned subsidiary of Woori Financial Group via a comprehensive share swap on Aug 11, 2026, and with trading suspended since Aug 7 and delisting due at end-August, its life as a listed equity is effectively over.

02

Business structure

Founded in 1989, Tong Yang Life became a Woori Financial Group subsidiary in July 2025 and, following the comprehensive share swap completed on Aug 11, 2026, is now 100% owned by the holding company.

Its core products are protection-type policies such as whole-life and health cover, alongside savings and annuity lines, with contractual service margin (CSM) growth central to profitability under IFRS 17.

Since CEO Sung Dae-kyu took office, the company has cut low-margin short-payment whole-life sales and shifted toward long-payment whole-life products.

Distribution spans general agencies (GA), bancassurance, captive financial consultants and direct marketing; press reports citing company IR materials say the GA share of 2025 new-business annualized premium equivalent fell below half to 48.3% while bancassurance rose to 25.2%.

Tong Yang Life's share of Woori Bank's bancassurance sales also rose from 2.8% at end-June 2025, just before the acquisition, to 8.3% by end-December.

In the first quarter of 2026 the GA share of protection new-business CSM fell 18.3 percentage points to 51.5% while bancassurance expanded 10.8 points to 23.8%, evidencing channel diversification.

The company has also revamped commission and recruiting schemes and opened a training center to rebuild its captive salesforce.

Competitively, the market splits between large players such as Samsung Life, Hanwha Life and Kyobo Life and holding-company affiliates like Shinhan Life and KB Life, with Tong Yang Life now positioned to cross-sell through Woori's banking, card and securities units.

That said, it is also a target of the group's insurance restructuring alongside ABL Life, so its standalone business structure may change.

04

Earnings analysis

On confirmed figures, insurance revenue rose steadily from 1.1072 trillion won in 2023 to 1.1865 trillion in 2024 and 1.2245 trillion in 2025, but profits moved the other way.

Operating profit fell 46.0% to 198.8 billion won in 2025 from 368.1 billion in 2024 and 334.3 billion in 2023, while net profit attributable to owners dropped 60.4% to 124.5 billion won from 314.3 billion.

Press coverage attributes the 2025 weakness to simultaneous declines in insurance and investment results, in contrast with peers that offset weaker underwriting with investment gains.

Quarterly, 2025 owner net profit shrank for four straight quarters—46.2 billion, 36.2 billion, 27.4 billion and 14.6 billion won—mapping the path of the annual decline.

In the first quarter of 2026, insurance revenue hit a quarterly high of 321.8 billion won, up 7.1% year on year, yet operating profit fell 47.0% to 31.1 billion won and net profit 45.8% to 25.0 billion won.

The company and media pinned this on base effects from prior-year bond disposal gains and a rate-driven collapse in investment income to 8.7 billion won (-84.0%), while insurance income improved to 22.4 billion won.

Second-quarter 2026 net profit rebounded to 66.9 billion won, about 2.7 times the prior quarter, lifting first-half profit to 91.9 billion won (+11.5%), as first-half insurance income of 97.0 billion won (+37.8%) offset investment income of 20.9 billion won (-29.4%).

On the balance sheet, equity declined from 2.9040 trillion won in 2023 to 1.9667 trillion in 2024 and 1.5489 trillion in 2025, with the liabilities-to-equity ratio rising from 1,031.9% to 1,656.6% and then 2,182.1%, largely reflecting fair-value measurement of insurance liabilities and the rate environment.

Operating cash flow, meanwhile, swung from minus 388.2 billion won in 2024 to plus 2.6458 trillion won in 2025, underscoring high volatility in cash flows.

05

Industry analysis

Korea's life insurance industry is shifting its competitive axis from top-line growth to profitability and capital quality as IFRS 17 and K-ICS bed in.

The Korea Insurance Research Institute projects 2026 industry-wide premium growth of 2.3% and life insurance premium income growth of just 1.0%, with shrinking savings and variable products capping expansion.

In the first half of 2026, holding-company life insurers saw underwriting profit slow on wider experience variances and higher loss ratios: Shinhan Life (290.6 billion won, -15.6%), KB Life (150.6 billion, -20.4%) and Hana Life (13.4 billion, -21.6%) all declined, leaving Tong Yang Life the sole gainer.

The gap in future earnings capacity is stark, however: Shinhan Life's in-force CSM stood at 7.9147 trillion won at end-June versus roughly 2.51 trillion won for Tong Yang Life as of the first quarter. On capital, KB Life's first-half K-ICS ratio of 244.9% exceeded Tong Yang Life's 205%.

On regulation, the Financial Services Commission will implement a core-capital K-ICS ratio from Jan 1, 2027, imposing prompt corrective action below a 50% threshold but with a nine-year transition.

The Financial Supervisory Service is also phasing in actuarial assumption guidelines on loss ratios and expenses from second-quarter 2026 closings, which could trigger CSM adjustments at insurers that used optimistic assumptions.

Combined with product reforms such as the fifth-generation indemnity health insurance, differentiation in earnings and solvency across life insurers is widening.

06

Outlook

The clearest scheduled events concern ownership. Tong Yang Life completed the comprehensive share swap on Aug 11, 2026, becoming a wholly owned Woori Financial subsidiary; trading is suspended from Aug 7 to 28 with delisting set for Aug 31, the same day Woori's new shares list.

Dissenting shareholders exercised appraisal rights on about 21.86 million shares worth 204.6 billion won, exceeding the 200 billion won contractual termination threshold, but Woori's board opted on Aug 4 not to invoke the termination right and proceeded as planned.

Operationally, Woori cites simpler decision-making, savings from removing a duplicate listing, and expanded cross-selling with its bank, card and securities units, and says it will move quickly on integration with ABL Life.

A combined entity is discussed as entering the industry's top five with roughly 55 trillion won in assets, though whether, how and when the merger happens is not yet fixed.

Product strategy centers on trimming health insurance and expanding long-payment whole-life policies; the company attributes the second-quarter rebound in new business partly to a 20-year-payment step-up whole-life product launched in June 2026.

On its first-half earnings call, Woori said the goal is to lift the insurers' own profitability and their contribution to group earnings, focusing on firming the K-ICS ratio and rebuilding the captive sales channel.

After delisting, Tong Yang Life's results will be visible through Woori Financial's subsidiary disclosures rather than as a standalone listed company.

Price discussion here has already been replaced by transaction terms rather than market supply and demand.

The share exchange value was fixed at 8,720 won per share, while the appraisal price for dissenting holders was raised 10% from the statutory 8,505 won to 9,356 won, and trading has been suspended since Aug 7, halting price formation altogether.

Against book value, the shares had been trading below net assets—broadly in line with the wider life insurance sector, which also trades beneath book.

On the confirmed dataset, earnings per share is 662 won and book value per share 11,821 won (figures differ from KRX-published values depending on calculation basis), and profits turned back toward recovery in the first half of 2026 after the sharp 2025 decline.

There is effectively no dividend yield to discuss, as recent disclosures show no cash dividend per share; Woori argued that after the swap, former holders would participate in the group's quarterly dividend and buyback policies.

Some minority shareholders, however, objected to the gap between the 10,562 won per share Woori paid for the controlling stake in 2024 and this exchange value, and the Financial Supervisory Service required supplementary disclosure of the valuation basis, leaving the fairness debate on the record.

Bull factors

Core underwriting recovery, sole gainer among peers

First-half 2026 net profit rose 11.5% year on year to 91.9 billion won, the only increase among financial-holding life insurers as Shinhan Life, KB Life and Hana Life all declined. Insurance income of 97.0 billion won, up 37.8%, offset a 29.4% drop in investment income, placing the improvement in the core business.

Second-quarter net profit of 66.9 billion won was about 2.7 times the first quarter's 25.0 billion, which the company attributed to stabilizing loss ratios, better claims experience and steady CSM amortization.

Improved solvency and group capital backing

The K-ICS ratio rose 58.6 percentage points year on year to 185.8% in the first quarter of 2026 and to a preliminary 205% in the first half, up 28 points and above 200% for the first time since joining Woori.

That is well clear of the regulator's 130% guideline, with the company citing higher available capital and lower required capital. Reports link the drop in required capital to reduced real estate project financing exposure, tighter risk-asset management and asset-liability management strategy.

Group channel synergy and scale from integration

Tong Yang Life's share of Woori Bank's bancassurance sales rose from 2.8% at end-June 2025 to 8.3% at end-December, and bancassurance accounted for 23.8% of protection new-business CSM in the first quarter of 2026, up 10.8 percentage points.

The company says full subsidiary status simplifies decision-making, removes duplicate listing costs and broadens cross-selling with the group's bank, card and securities arms. Media reports suggest a merger with ABL Life would create a top-five life insurer with roughly 55 trillion won in assets.

Bear factors

Shrinking new-business CSM

First-half 2026 new-business CSM fell 19.9%, or 60.4 billion won, to 242.5 billion won, and in the first quarter total new-business CSM plunged 50.4% to 94.5 billion won. Over the same period Shinhan Life (716.1 billion won) and KB Life (282.7 billion won) both grew their new-business CSM, sharpening the contrast.

The in-force CSM of about 2.51 trillion won in the first quarter of 2026 also trails Shinhan Life's seven-trillion-won level, and 2025 saw CSM erosion from actuarial assumption changes.

Investment income volatility and the 2025 profit slump

On confirmed figures, 2025 operating profit of 198.8 billion won and net profit of 124.5 billion won fell 46.0% and 60.4% respectively, with quarterly net profit shrinking for four straight quarters from 46.2 billion to 14.6 billion won.

In the first quarter of 2026, investment income collapsed 84.0% to 8.7 billion won and net profit fell 45.8%, attributed to a rate spike and base effects from prior-year bond disposal gains. With a large share of earnings tied to market rates and asset-disposal timing, results are inherently hard to forecast.

Loss of liquidity on delisting and valuation dispute

Trading in Tong Yang Life shares is suspended from Aug 7 to 28, 2026, with delisting due Aug 31, ending market tradability altogether.

The 8,720 won exchange value is about 17% below the 10,562 won per share Woori paid for the controlling stake in 2024, prompting minority shareholder objections, and the Financial Supervisory Service demanded a corrected securities registration statement on May 26.

Appraisal rights claims of 204.6 billion won exceeded the 200 billion won contractual termination threshold, indicating meaningful disagreement over the terms.

10

Risk factors

Regulation — core-capital K-ICS

The Financial Services Commission will implement the core-capital K-ICS ratio from Jan 1, 2027, with prompt corrective action below a 50% threshold and a nine-year transition period. Insurers below 50% as of end-March 2027 will be assigned company-specific minimum compliance targets.

Reports put Tong Yang Life's pre-transitional core-capital K-ICS at 60.70% for 2025, with estimates that meaningful capital raising would be needed to reach the roughly 80% level typical of major insurers.

Integration execution risk

Woori has publicly committed to integrating ABL Life, but the structure and timing are undecided.

Media and industry observers flag IT integration costs, rationalizing overlapping products and the risk of agent attrition, and note that while consolidation can create medium-term synergies, it typically brings near-term costs and volatility. Rebuilding the captive channel likewise entails upfront spending on agent commissions and settlement subsidies.

Interest rates and actuarial assumptions

Rate moves affect investment income and the K-ICS ratio simultaneously and can swing quarterly profits sharply, as seen in the first quarter of 2026.

The Financial Supervisory Service is phasing in actuarial assumption guidelines on loss ratios and expenses from second-quarter 2026 closings, leaving room for CSM adjustments.

The Korea Insurance Research Institute expects 2026 K-ICS ratios to be broadly flat to slightly lower and identifies capital quality management as the key task.

12

Overall view

Tong Yang Life is in its final phase as a listed company. It became a wholly owned Woori Financial subsidiary through the Aug 11, 2026 share swap, and with trading halted from Aug 7 to 28 and delisting due Aug 31, market pricing and trading have already stopped.

On earnings, after 2025's slump to operating profit of 198.8 billion won (-46.0%) and net profit of 124.5 billion won (-60.4%) on confirmed figures, first-half 2026 net profit turned back up to 91.9 billion won (+11.5%) and the K-ICS ratio reached a preliminary 205%.

Yet first-half new-business CSM fell 19.9% and the in-force CSM still trails peers such as Shinhan Life, leaving the pace of future profit accumulation unproven.

The issues to watch from here are not the share price but execution of the ABL Life integration, the rebuilding of the captive channel and preparation for the 2027 core-capital K-ICS rules—all of which will now be visible through Woori Financial's subsidiary disclosures.
