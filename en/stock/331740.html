02

Business structure

Autocrypt, founded in 2019, is an automotive cybersecurity company whose business covers the full vehicle production cycle, from design-stage consulting to security solution development, testing and certification support.

Its products are integrated security solutions that include in-vehicle networks and V2X communication, sold mainly to component makers as a package of software integrity protection, connectivity encryption, digital key physical security, and anomaly and intrusion detection.

The company uses a per-vehicle license model; in a 2025 press interview it said its solutions were installed in about 70 vehicle models and 3.2 million vehicles.

Royalty share is rising: according to company figures reported for the first nine months of 2026, royalties rose from 4.8% to 9.5% of total revenue, and combined product revenue of solutions and royalties reached KRW 6.71 billion, up 43.6%.

A securities data aggregator instead reported a second-quarter 2026 mix of 53% services, 26% solutions and 21% royalties, which suggests a different classification basis. The company says it works with more than 200 customers, including 171 domestic and 48 overseas.

Competitively, the company notes that most component makers remain focused on hardware, while the cybersecurity rules are still new to many of them.

The company has also moved into physical AI, converting its research unit into a Physical AI Security Research Center in May 2026 and naming robotics, defense and bio-healthcare as target sectors, with bio-healthcare as the first.

04

Earnings analysis

On the basis of filed results, 2025 revenue was KRW 25.5 billion, up 9.4% from KRW 23.3 billion in 2024. The operating loss narrowed from KRW 18 billion in 2024 to KRW 13.5 billion in 2025, and the operating margin improved from -77.5% to -53.1%.

The company attributed the smaller operating loss to higher revenue from its vehicle cybersecurity solutions and lower operating costs. The net loss attributable to controlling shareholders narrowed from KRW 48.9 billion in 2024 to KRW 12.5 billion in 2025, and the company cited lower financing costs.

Both 2024 and 2025 were loss years at the operating and controlling-shareholder net levels.

The debt-to-equity ratio fell from 453.3% at the end of 2024 to 125.9% at the end of 2025, with total liabilities of KRW 38.5 billion against total equity of KRW 30.6 billion, all of which was attributable to controlling shareholders.

Operating cash flow was negative in both years, at -KRW 12 billion in 2024 and -KRW 11.8 billion in 2025. Quarterly, the operating loss fell from KRW 4.52 billion in the third quarter of 2025 to KRW 553 million in the fourth quarter, when the controlling-shareholder result turned to a KRW 1.49 billion profit.

For the consolidated fourth-quarter loss, the company pointed to timing differences in overseas subsidiaries' closing, while its standalone result showed the first quarterly operating profit in its history.

In the first quarter of 2026 revenue was KRW 5.1 billion, down 46.6% quarter on quarter, and the operating loss widened to KRW 4.65 billion.

The second quarter brought revenue of KRW 7.46 billion and an operating loss of KRW 793 million, and the third quarter brought revenue of KRW 6.13 billion and an operating loss of KRW 3.34 billion.

The four quarters from the fourth quarter of 2025 through the third quarter of 2026 add up to a controlling-shareholder net loss of KRW 5.88 billion. Press reports put the cumulative operating loss for the first nine months of 2026 at KRW 8.79 billion, against KRW 12.97 billion a year earlier. Royalty income tracks vehicle sales volume, so quarterly swings are likely to remain.

05

Industry analysis

Automotive cybersecurity is a market shaped by regulation.

Autocrypt focuses on early rules, UN Regulation 155 and 156, covering cybersecurity and OTA update safety verification, and the company says it holds accreditation from a Dutch transport authority-affiliated certification body for European export vehicles subject to mandatory cybersecurity requirements.

Regulation is broadening. The EU AI Act and the Cyber Resilience Act are pushing security requirements into robotics, medical devices and industrial control systems, and standards such as IEC 62443 are tightening in industrial control.

The market is still early-stage, and the company's research spending has been a large driver of operating losses. Against Hyundai Autoever, which focuses mainly on vehicle software platforms, Autocrypt specializes in security verification and certification, so the two are more complementary than head-to-head.

The company was named Automotive Cybersecurity Company of the Year at the 2026 AutoTech Breakthrough Awards, its third win in that category. An industry observer described vehicle cybersecurity as becoming an essential technology as vehicles evolve into software-defined platforms.

For North America, however, there is no explicit cybersecurity check for vehicles exported to the United States, so the pace of demand there depends on when local rules arrive.

06

Outlook

The company has framed its earnings improvement around a higher royalty share and expansion of its security business beyond vehicles into physical AI.

It is testing and partnering to extend its technology to agricultural and construction machinery, robots and drones, and it expanded its red team and CSTP testing platform to cover AI-based systems beyond cars. The biggest variable is the European partnership.

Leading Investment & Securities said the Electrobit (EB) partnership, under which Autocrypt was delegated full authority over Electrobit's Korean business, pushed the company into a real turnaround phase.

The same note projected 2026 consolidated revenue of KRW 48.8 billion and an operating profit of KRW 300 million, versus a prior market consensus of KRW 35 billion revenue and an operating loss of KRW 4 billion that did not include the partnership. The note gave no rating or target price. The gap is large.

Cumulative revenue for the first nine months of 2026 was KRW 18.69 billion, so reaching the projection would require roughly KRW 30 billion or more in fourth-quarter revenue, while the cumulative operating loss was KRW 8.79 billion.

The next checkpoints are the 2026 annual report and the first-quarter 2027 quarterly report, where royalty share and operating profit trends will be visible.

08

Bull factors

Royalty-based revenue is expanding

Press reports say cumulative third-quarter 2026 royalty revenue rose 131.5% year on year and already exceeded the full-year 2025 royalty total. Royalty share rose from 4.8% to 9.5% of revenue. The company says its per-vehicle license model should grow revenue as more vehicles carry its software.

European partnership and broker outlook

In a June 2026 note, Leading Investment & Securities described the Electrobit deal, which gave Autocrypt authority over Electrobit's Korean business, as the start of a turnaround. The same note projected a 2026 operating profit turn, above a prior consensus that excluded the partnership.

Technology recognition and wider scope

Autocrypt was named Automotive Cybersecurity Company of the Year at the 2026 AutoTech Breakthrough Awards, its third win in that category. In May 2026 the company expanded its research unit into a Physical AI Security Research Center and is pursuing robotics, defense and bio-healthcare.

Bear factors

Five straight quarters of operating losses

The company has posted an operating loss in each of the last five quarters, from the third quarter of 2025 through the third quarter of 2026.

The first-quarter 2026 loss widened to KRW 4.65 billion, and the third-quarter 2026 loss of KRW 3.34 billion was smaller than a year earlier but larger than the prior quarter's KRW 793 million. The last four quarters add up to a controlling-shareholder net loss of KRW 5.88 billion.

Fourth-quarter surge needed to meet the outlook

Cumulative revenue for the first nine months of 2026 was KRW 18.69 billion, up 17.3% year on year. Reaching the KRW 48.8 billion full-year projection would require more than KRW 30 billion in fourth-quarter revenue, and the cumulative operating loss of KRW 8.79 billion leaves a wide gap to the projected profit.

Financial capacity and cash outflows

Total liabilities at the end of 2025 were KRW 38.5 billion, above total equity of KRW 30.6 billion. Cash left the business through operations in both 2024 and 2025, which adds to the financial strain.

The first-quarter 2026 controlling-shareholder net loss of KRW 4.93 billion is another reminder that profitability has not yet been sustained.

Risk factors

Partner dependence

The Electrobit agreement, a core basis for the upgraded outlook, depends on the partner's awards and execution, so the timing of its effect on reported results could shift. The broker's projection includes this partnership, so a delay would widen the gap between forecast and reported results. The prior consensus, which excluded the deal, was KRW 35 billion in revenue and a KRW 4 billion operating loss.

Regulatory timing and regional demand gaps

Demand for automotive cybersecurity depends heavily on when UN and EU rules take effect. There is no explicit cybersecurity check yet for vehicles exported to the United States, which leaves North American demand uncertain.

If compliance timelines under the EU AI Act and the Cyber Resilience Act slip, the expansion into physical AI could also slow.

New business execution

The physical AI security business targets robotics, defense and bio-healthcare, with bio-healthcare as the first application.

Testing and partnerships to extend the technology to agricultural and construction machinery, robots and drones are still under way, so the timing and results of commercialization could differ from plans.

If research spending rises as the red team and CSTP platform widen their scope beyond vehicles, the pace of narrowing operating losses could slow.

Overall view

Autocrypt is an automotive cybersecurity company that reported 2025 revenue of KRW 25.5 billion, up 9.4%, and an operating loss of KRW 13.5 billion, which narrowed. Both 2024 and 2025 were years with losses at the operating and controlling-shareholder net levels.

The controlling-shareholder result turned to a profit in the fourth quarter of 2025 but returned to a loss in the first quarter of 2026, and operating losses have continued for five straight quarters through the third quarter of 2026.

Bullish factors include fast royalty growth, demand from expanding regulation in Europe and elsewhere, and the June 2026 partnership with the broker's projected turn.

Bearish factors include the sharp fourth-quarter revenue increase needed to meet the projection, continuing losses, and a balance sheet in which liabilities exceed equity. Valuation reads differently depending on whether one looks at the premium to net assets or at the timing of a profit turn.

The next checkpoints are the 2026 annual operating results and the operating trend in the first-quarter 2027 report.
