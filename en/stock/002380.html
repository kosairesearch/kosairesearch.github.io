Report overview

KCC: Silicone Recovery Versus Swings in Investment Gains

KCC's silicone and building-materials units are rebuilding operating profit, yet mark-to-market swings in its listed equity holdings move net income far more, so operating and net results need separate reading.

  1. 1

    Q3 2026 operating profit was KRW 85.2 billion, down 27.4% year on year, while the Q3 net loss attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 1.67 trillion, a swing into loss.

  2. 2

    Net income attributable to controlling shareholders rose for three straight years from 2023 to 2025, while operating profit fell 9.2% in 2025.

  3. 3

    Silicone operating profit climbed from KRW 2.6 billion in Q1 2026 to KRW 37.3 billion in Q2, while Paints was the largest unit by first-half operating profit at KRW 94.3 billion.

  4. 4

    Q2 2026 net income of KRW 2.84 trillion reflected large financial-asset valuation gains, and Q3 saw roughly KRW 2 trillion of valuation losses flow into a net loss.

  5. 5

    The company plans to pass at least half of its Samsung C&T special dividend on to shareholders and to retire 1.17 million treasury shares in stages through September 2027.

02

Business structure

KCC runs three core businesses, Silicones, Paints and Building Materials, with a smaller Others segment.

In the first half of 2026, Silicones was the largest unit by external revenue at KRW 1,691.4 billion, or 49.5%, ahead of Paints at KRW 1.017 trillion (29.8%) and Building Materials at KRW 493.1 billion (14.4%), according to the half-year report as reported by the press.

Segment operating profit for the same period was KRW 94.3 billion for Paints, KRW 64.9 billion for Building Materials and KRW 39.8 billion for Silicones, so the largest revenue contributor generated the smallest profit share.

The silicone business expanded after the 2019 acquisition of Momentive Performance Materials (MPM), and demand from electronics, automotive and healthcare customers is cited as its main pillar.

Management attributed the second-quarter silicone recovery to price increases, a richer mix of high-value products and production efficiency gains.

Building Materials has held up through a weak housing market by serving large industrial sites such as data centers and high-tech facilities, with first-half operating profit rising from KRW 55.7 billion to KRW 64.9 billion.

Paints depends on automotive and shipbuilding demand and overseas sales, but raw-material cost increases pressured its margins. A 2025 press analysis noted that KCC's paint demand leans toward automotive and shipbuilding rather than the construction exposure that many domestic rivals carry.

Silicones rose from about 11 percent of KCC's revenue in 2018, before the Momentive deal, to roughly 47 percent in 2025.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual revenue went from KRW 6.77 trillion in 2022 to KRW 6.29 trillion in 2023 (down 7.2% year on year), then KRW 6.66 trillion in 2024 (up 5.9%) and KRW 6.48 trillion in 2025 (down 2.6%). Among 2022 to 2025, 2022 was the largest year and 2023 the smallest.

Operating profit fell from KRW 467.7 billion in 2022 to KRW 312.5 billion in 2023 (down 33.2%), recovered to KRW 471.1 billion in 2024 (up 50.7%) and was KRW 427.6 billion in 2025 (down 9.2%).

Operating margin was 6.9% in 2022, 5.0% in 2023, 7.1% in 2024 and 6.6% in 2025, so 2024 was the highest margin year in the period.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders rose from KRW 33.9 billion in 2022 to KRW 212.7 billion, KRW 310.6 billion and KRW 1.54 trillion in 2025, marking three consecutive annual increases from 2023 to 2025.

In 2025, that profit rose 395.4% even as operating profit declined, so the two measures moved in opposite directions. Total net income including non-controlling interests was the same KRW 1.54 trillion in 2025.

The debt-to-equity ratio fell from 160.1% at end-2024 to 114.8% at end-2025, with total equity of KRW 7.82 trillion and total liabilities of KRW 8.98 trillion. Operating cash flow was KRW 789.5 billion in 2025, up from KRW 596.8 billion in 2024.

On a quarterly basis, operating profit fell from KRW 117.3 billion in Q3 2025 to KRW 66.5 billion in Q4 2025 (down 43.3% quarter on quarter), recovered to KRW 88.1 billion in Q1 2026 and KRW 134.4 billion in Q2 (up 52.5%), then dropped to KRW 85.2 billion in Q3 2026 (down 36.6% quarter on quarter).

Q3 2026 revenue was KRW 1.67 trillion, up 3.1% year on year, yet the company swung to a net loss of KRW 1.67 trillion attributable to controlling shareholders.

Management pointed to the seasonal slowdown, raw materials bought at high prices during the cost spike, and foreign-currency translation losses in silicones from the stronger won. The net loss also reflected roughly KRW 2 trillion of valuation losses on listed shares held, including Samsung C&T.

05

Industry analysis

The silicone outlook is being read as easing pressure from oversupply of organosilicon, as China's production cuts and restructuring among global rivals reduce supply. In August 2026, management said price increases were spreading and the risk of price declines was fading.

Shinhan Investment Corp. said in an August 2026 report that silicones had passed its trough and entered a profitability recovery phase, citing demand from AI server thermal materials, semiconductor packaging and EV adhesives and heat-dissipation materials.

A 2025 comparison, however, showed silicone's third-quarter operating margin at 4.4%, below Building Materials at 12.3% and Paints at 10.7%, which indicates that profitability remains thin relative to revenue scale.

Brokers expect Paints' cost burden to ease from November as raw-material prices stabilize and high-cost inventory runs down, according to IBK Securities.

Building Materials has been supported by demand from data centers and high-tech facilities even as the construction market weakens and broader geopolitical uncertainty persists. Management has highlighted that all three core units were profitable in the first half of 2026, which it presents as a structural strength.

Silicone-based cosmetic ingredients are a newer growth avenue, and the company exhibited at several international trade fairs in 2026. A stronger won helps dollar-denominated raw-material purchasing but adds translation pressure on overseas silicone operations.

06

Outlook

Management expects raw-material costs to ease from November 2026 as prices stabilize and a stronger won improves dollar-based purchasing, with profitability partly recovering by year-end.

Shinhan Investment Corp. said in an October 21, 2026 report that profitability could improve from the fourth quarter as raw-material pressure eases and silicones recover.

In a September 1, 2026 report, the same firm projected full-year 2026 consolidated operating profit of KRW 445.3 billion and silicone operating profit of around KRW 95.0 billion. Set against cumulative operating profit of KRW 307.7 billion for the first three quarters, that outlook implies a sizable fourth quarter.

On shareholder returns, the growth plan announced in August 2026 calls for passing at least half of the Samsung C&T special dividend to shareholders and using the rest to reduce borrowings. The 1.17 million treasury shares are to be retired in stages through September 2027.

Management is also expanding silicone-based cosmetic ingredients and materials for EVs and renewable energy. Building Materials depends on whether demand from data centers and high-tech sites persists.

For Silicones, the key checks are whether oversupply in China keeps easing and whether the high-value product mix keeps widening.

08

Bull factors

Signs of silicone profit recovery

Silicone operating profit rose from KRW 2.6 billion in Q1 2026 to KRW 37.3 billion in Q2, and management attributed the gain to price increases and a richer high-value product mix. The unit had posted an operating loss in Q4 2025 before returning to profit in Q1 2026. Shinhan Investment Corp. has assessed that silicones have passed their trough.

Expected easing of cost pressure

Management said raw-material prices have stabilized since April and that a stronger won has improved dollar-based purchasing conditions. It expects cost pressure to ease from November 2026 and profitability to recover somewhat by year-end. IBK Securities expects cost burdens to decline from November as high-priced inventory is used up.

Shareholder return and asset deployment plans

Under the growth plan announced in August 2026, at least half of the Samsung C&T special dividend will be passed on to KCC shareholders. The remainder is earmarked for debt reduction and balance-sheet improvement. The 1.17 million treasury shares are planned for staged retirement through September 2027.

Bear factors

Q3 profit decline and swing into loss

Q3 2026 revenue rose 3.1% year on year to KRW 1.67 trillion, but operating profit fell 27.4% to KRW 85.2 billion and dropped 36.6% from the previous quarter. Net loss attributable to controlling shareholders reached KRW 1.67 trillion, a swing into loss.

Valuation swings dominate net income

Q2 2026 net income of KRW 2.84 trillion included large gains from higher values of retained stakes. In Q3, falling prices of listed holdings such as Samsung C&T produced roughly KRW 2 trillion of valuation losses. Net results and operating results are moving in opposite directions within the same year.

Paints cost and currency pressure

Paints absorbed higher-cost raw materials bought during the spike, which hit Q3 margins. A stronger won also created foreign-currency translation losses in silicones. First-half silicone operating profit declined from KRW 61.5 billion a year earlier to KRW 39.8 billion.

Risk factors

Construction and housing demand

Housing construction remains weak, so Building Materials leans on industrial sites. A slowdown in data-center and high-tech projects could weaken its profit. A press analysis of half-year filings showed Building Materials operating profit falling from KRW 173.7 billion in 2024 to KRW 115.1 billion in 2025.

Raw materials and currency

Because high-priced raw materials flow through costs with a lag, input-price swings remain a key variable. Management cited higher raw-material prices tied to the Strait of Hormuz disruption as a drag on Paints margins. A stronger won eases purchasing but can enlarge translation losses at overseas silicone units.

Investment valuation and silicone pricing

Because net income swings with the prices of listed holdings such as Samsung C&T, quarterly net results are hard to read as core earnings. If Chinese oversupply intensifies again, the benefit from silicone price increases could fade. The pace of silicone margin recovery will determine how far it can offset these effects.

Overall view

KCC's recent trend shows both signs of core improvement and signs of weakness. Silicone operating profit recovered from KRW 2.6 billion in Q1 2026 to KRW 37.3 billion in Q2, while operating profit in Q3 2026 fell 27.4% year on year to KRW 85.2 billion.

On an annual basis, operating profit moved from KRW 467.7 billion in 2022 to KRW 312.5 billion in 2023, KRW 471.1 billion in 2024 and KRW 427.6 billion in 2025, while net income attributable to controlling shareholders rose three straight years from 2023 to 2025.

Net results depended heavily on valuation swings in listed holdings, which explains why Q2 and Q3 moved in opposite directions within the same year. The debt-to-equity ratio fell to 114.8% at end-2025 from 160.1% at end-2024.

On shareholder returns, the company has announced a pass-through of the Samsung C&T special dividend and a treasury share retirement plan. Brokers differ in their views.

Shinhan Investment Corp. projects silicone profitability to improve, while IBK Securities lowered its target price to KRW 540,000 in a report dated November 6, 2026.

The next checks are whether easing raw-material costs lift Paints margins in the 2026 Q4 preliminary results, and how the 2026 annual report separates valuation items from core operating results.
