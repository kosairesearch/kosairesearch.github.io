02

Business structure

Ildong Holdings is the holding company of the Ildong group, which moved to a holding structure through a spin-off effective August 1, 2016. As of the Q1 2026 quarterly report, the group had 16 affiliates, of which 2 are listed, including Ildong Holdings, and 14 are unlisted.

Parent-only operations consist of one holding business, earning service fees from affiliates, brand royalties, headquarters rent and dividends from affiliates and investees.

In Q1 2026, parent-only revenue of KRW 8.41 billion was made up of service fees at 51.21%, dividends at 27.32%, rent at 11.61% and brand royalties at 9.86%. In the fiscal 2024 business report filed in March 2025, dividends were only 0.93% of parent revenue, so this mix shifts sharply between years.

Fiscal 2025 parent-only revenue was KRW 18.8 billion with operating profit of KRW 1.59 billion, as disclosed in March 2026. The principal consolidated subsidiary is IL Dong Pharmaceutical, while other affiliates include a health-functional food unit and a water-ionizer business.

The holding company has no production facilities, so affiliate operations generate most consolidated results. Parent-only revenue in Q1 2026 was a small fraction of consolidated revenue of KRW 148.9 billion, so the two sets of figures differ sharply.

05

Industry analysis

Ildong Holdings is a holding company with no production facilities, and its income depends on the profits of affiliates in pharmaceuticals, health-functional foods and consumer health, received through equity stakes and fees.

Since the 2016 spin-off, its business has rested on investment income, brand fees, management consulting fees and rent. Demand and cycle conditions in the end markets reach the holding company's results indirectly through affiliates, so reading its earnings depends on affiliate profit trends.

Consolidated R&D expense fell from KRW 108 billion in 2023 to KRW 55.7 billion in 2024 and KRW 35.5 billion in 2025 according to FnGuide, while selling, general and administrative expenses dropped from KRW 291.8 billion to KRW 210.2 billion over the same period.

The cost reduction appears to have helped the operating turnaround, but the filings alone cannot separate how much came from gross profit recovery versus lower costs. Whether lower R&D spending reflects a change in pipeline direction is a question for the pharmaceutical affiliate's business reports.

A simple comparison with other listed holding companies can mislead because affiliate mix and ownership structures differ. The value of a holding company depends on what its affiliates contribute, so the profit recovery of the pharmaceutical affiliate is the central variable.

Because parent-only revenue is a small part of consolidated sales, earnings attribution depends on ownership structure, as the gap between 2025 net income of KRW 21.6 billion and net income attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 10.9 billion shows.
