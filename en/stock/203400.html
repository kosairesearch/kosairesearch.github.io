02

Business structure

Abion is a KOSDAQ-listed company developing companion-diagnostic-based anticancer drugs, listed in September 2021 through the technology special listing route. Its lead pipeline asset is vabametkib (ABN401), an oral, selective c-MET tyrosine kinase inhibitor.

Vabametkib is in a global Phase 2 trial in MET exon 14 skipping mutation non-small cell lung cancer and received orphan drug designation for development from the Ministry of Food and Drug Safety in 2025.

ABN501 is an antibody platform targeting CLDN3 and four other proteins, and an exclusive worldwide license was signed in June 2025. The counterparty also holds priority negotiation rights for three other protein targets.

ABN101, an interferon-beta based candidate, has been presented as a broad-spectrum antiviral therapy. ABN202, an antibody-cytokine fusion protein platform for solid tumors, remains at the preclinical stage.

Revenue has been reported as coming in small amounts from government research service contracts, and it was KRW 880 million in 2025 on a filed basis. Most of the six earlier technology transfers were diagnostic and therapeutic technologies handed to domestic partners.

In August 2026 the company said a US patent covering the core technology of its iRAC platform had been registered.

04

Earnings analysis

On a filed basis, revenue went from KRW 2.51 billion in 2022 to KRW 1.27 billion in 2023 (down 49.2% year on year) and KRW 760 million in 2024 (down 40.4%), before rising to KRW 880 million in 2025 (up 15.9%). Among the 2022 to 2025 years, 2022 was the largest and 2024 the smallest.

The operating loss was KRW 25.2 billion in 2022, widened to KRW 31.3 billion in 2023 and KRW 34.1 billion in 2024, and narrowed to KRW 19.4 billion in 2025. Operating margins were -1,004.1% in 2022, -2,452.5% in 2023, -4,484.2% in 2024 and -2,201.0% in 2025.

With revenue this small, costs push margins to extreme negative levels. A 2025 press report put annual R&D spending above KRW 20 billion, which points to pipeline development as the main driver of losses.

Net loss attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 19.9 billion in 2022, KRW 29.2 billion in 2023, KRW 43.4 billion in 2024 and KRW 29.1 billion in 2025, and the net loss including non-controlling interests was reported at the same amounts.

The debt ratio rose from 59.9% in 2022 to 347.7% in 2023, then fell to 126.7% in 2024 and 17.9% in 2025. At the end of 2025, total equity was KRW 68.4 billion, all attributable to controlling shareholders, and total liabilities were KRW 12.2 billion.

Operating cash flow was negative every year, from -KRW 19.9 billion in 2022 to -KRW 33 billion in 2025.

Quarterly revenue was KRW 162 million in the third quarter of 2025, KRW 66.43 million in the fourth quarter (down 59.0% from the prior quarter), KRW 574 million in the first quarter of 2026, KRW 137 million in the second quarter and KRW 320 million in the third quarter (up 133.8% from the prior quarter and up 97.6% from a year earlier).

Operating losses were KRW 4.39 billion, KRW 5.63 billion, KRW 6.54 billion, KRW 4.32 billion and KRW 5.27 billion across the same five quarters. Net loss attributable to controlling shareholders over the last four quarters totaled KRW 28.7 billion.

05

Industry analysis

A June 2025 press report described the ABN501 deal as the third-largest global licensing transaction by a Korean biotech at that time. The deal drew industry attention as a case of a global pharmaceutical partner valuing an antibody platform.

Potential development routes for the ABN501 antibodies were mentioned as antibody-drug conjugates and T-cell engagers. Vabametkib sits in a c-MET-driven market that is narrowly focused on MET exon 14 skipping non-small cell lung cancer.

The company describes its response rate and duration as differentiated versus approved competing therapies, but that is management's characterization. Earlier licensing deals were mostly with domestic partners and therefore smaller, while this deal involved a global counterparty at a much larger scale.

Compared with listed peers that rely on commercial product sales, Abion's earnings base depends more heavily on licensing income.

Outlook

At ASCO in June 2026 the company presented the independent central imaging review results from the global Phase 2 trial of vabametkib and said it would expand global partnership talks. In August 2026 it said it would attend WCLC 2026 to meet global clinical experts and potential partners for licensing discussions.

In the same period, a US patent covering the core technology of the iRAC platform was registered, and development of ABN202 is being advanced alongside business talks with potential partners.

At a closed expert meeting held during WCLC 2026 in September 2026, Korean approval prospects after completion of the Phase 2 trial were reportedly discussed. A company official said follow-on development would move quickly based on those discussions.

In a September 2026 report, CEO Shin Young-gi said the company would pursue a platform licensing deal within 2026. In 2025 the company had described ABN202 as a strategic asset with high licensing potential.

The timing of the next vabametkib trial design, any domestic approval filing, and the size and counterparty of any new deal have not been disclosed. The next checkpoints are whether the 2026 licensing target is met by year-end and the 2026 annual report due by the end of March 2027.

08

Bull factors

Large licensing deal on record

The ABN501 co-development and license agreement disclosed on June 24, 2025 totals USD 1.315 billion. Upfront payments are USD 5 million per target antibody, or USD 25 million in total, with development and commercial milestones of USD 290 million and USD 1 billion.

The counterparty is undisclosed and holds priority negotiation rights for three additional targets. The industry has treated it as the company's first global licensing result.

Phase 2 response data for vabametkib

At ASCO in June 2026 the company reported a 55% objective response rate for vabametkib in its global Phase 2 trial, based on independent central imaging review. Median duration of response was 14.5 months in the overall population and 19.9 months in treatment-naive patients.

The company reported grade 3 or higher treatment-related adverse events at 11% and no grade 4 events. These are company-presented trial results, not regulatory review outcomes.

Platform patent and pipeline breadth

The company said in August 2026 that a US patent covering the core technology of its iRAC platform had been registered. The platform fuses an in-house interferon-beta mutein with a cancer-targeting antibody. In 2025 the company said ABN202 was being developed against targets including TROP2, EGFR and MET. A company vice president said in 2025 that about 20 preclinical antibodies remained in the pipeline.

09

Bear factors

Operating losses persist, narrower in 2025

The 2025 operating loss was KRW 19.4 billion, narrower than KRW 34.1 billion in 2024, but the company has posted losses for four consecutive years from 2022 to 2025. Net loss attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 29.1 billion in 2025.

Over the last four quarters, that loss totaled KRW 28.7 billion, and losses have continued for five consecutive quarters from the third quarter of 2025 through the third quarter of 2026. The third-quarter 2026 operating loss of KRW 5.27 billion was wider than a year earlier.

Revenue scale far below operating costs

Revenue of KRW 880 million in 2025 covered only a small fraction of the KRW 19.4 billion operating loss. The operating margin was -2,201.0% in 2025. Quarterly revenue in 2026 swung widely, at KRW 574 million in the first quarter, KRW 137 million in the second and KRW 320 million in the third.

Revenue has been reported as centered on government research service contracts, and no commercial product sales appear in the filings.

Accumulated operating cash outflows

Operating cash flow was -KRW 19.9 billion in 2022, -KRW 26.9 billion in 2023, -KRW 28.1 billion in 2024 and -KRW 33 billion in 2025, a larger outflow each year. The 2025 outflow of KRW 33 billion is close to half of total equity of KRW 68.4 billion at year-end 2025.

The debt ratio of 347.7% in 2023 also shows the financial strain of that period. A 2025 press report put annual R&D spending above KRW 20 billion.

10

Risk factors

Clinical development: Phase 3 design and approval timing

Vabametkib's data come from a Phase 2 study, so the design of a Phase 3 trial and the timing of any approval filing will determine what follows. A September 2026 report said Korean approval prospects were discussed at a closed meeting during WCLC, but the company has not announced an approval timeline.

Whether the duration and safety figures hold in follow-on trials against approved therapies for the same indication remains to be seen.

Deal execution and pipeline partnering

In a September 2026 report the company set a goal of a platform licensing deal within 2026, and that outcome is not yet determined. Partner terms and sizes for ABN401 and ABN202 have not been disclosed.

Milestone payments from the ABN501 deal are tied to development and commercial stages, so cash inflows beyond the upfront amount are a long-term matter. A delay in new deals would extend the period of losses.

Listing requirements and impaired-capital ratio

A 2025 press report put the impaired-capital ratio at 59% as of the first quarter of 2025. Under KOSDAQ rules, a company can be designated as a managed issue if the impaired-capital ratio exceeds 50% of capital on two or more occasions within three years.

Analysts said receipt of the ABN501 upfront payment could bring the ratio below 50%. Because the timing and size of that payment determine compliance, the link between licensing cash inflows and listing requirements matters.

12

Overall view

Abion completed the ABN501 licensing deal in June 2025, with a total value of USD 1.315 billion, but its filed results still show operating losses.

The 2025 operating loss narrowed to KRW 19.4 billion from KRW 34.1 billion in 2024, yet losses have continued for five consecutive quarters through the third quarter of 2026. Revenue of KRW 880 million in 2025 leaves operating margins deeply negative.

Vabametkib has drawn attention for a company-reported 55% objective response rate and low severe adverse event rates, but these are Phase 2 data and approval and commercialization timelines remain open. Strengths cited include the large licensing deal, the vabametkib trial data and the iRAC platform patent.

Weaknesses include four consecutive years of losses, operating cash outflows and reliance on licensing income. The next checkpoints are the licensing target for 2026, the 2026 annual report and the Q1 2027 quarterly report.
