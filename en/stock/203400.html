02

Business structure

Abion is a KOSDAQ-listed innovative drug developer that researches precision-medicine biopharmaceuticals, including targeted and immuno-oncology therapies. The center of its pipeline is ABN401 (babamekip), a highly selective tyrosine kinase inhibitor aimed at the c-MET receptor.

ABN401 is in a global Phase 2 trial in patients with MET exon 14 skipping non-small cell lung cancer, and it holds orphan drug designation at the development stage from Korea's Ministry of Food and Drug Safety.

The second pillar is the iRAC platform, an interferon beta antibody conjugate for next-generation immuno-oncology, for which the company reported securing a U.S. patent in August 2026. Candidates built on iRAC and on CLDN3, including ABN202 and ABN501, are being expanded with the aim of global co-development.

According to the company, unlike conventional antibody-drug conjugates that release a cytotoxic payload inside cells, iRAC is designed to act in the tumor microenvironment. Revenue is small for a company at this stage, so the business model leans on development progress and technology transfer talks.

Listed peers in the same biopharma sector include Alteogen, Hanmi Pharm, Yuhan, and LigaChem Biosciences, although differences in pipeline stage and indications limit direct comparison.

The MET-targeted field where ABN401 competes already has approved drugs, and the company emphasizes efficacy and safety comparable to those.

Earnings analysis

The operating loss was KRW 25.2 billion in 2022, KRW 31.3 billion in 2023, and KRW 34.1 billion in 2024, each year recorded as continued losses with widening deficits, before narrowing to KRW 19.4 billion in 2025 as continued losses with a smaller deficit.

Losses were recorded for four consecutive years from 2022 through 2025. The operating margin deteriorated from -1,004.1% in 2022 to -4,484.2% in 2024 before improving to -2,201.0% in 2025.

Net loss attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 19.9 billion in 2022, KRW 29.2 billion in 2023, KRW 43.4 billion in 2024, and KRW 29.1 billion in 2025, while net income including non-controlling interests was reported as the same net losses for each year.

In 2024 and 2025, the loss attributable to controlling shareholders was larger than the operating loss, and the non-operating items behind that gap cannot be identified from the disclosed table alone, so they are a point to check in the annual report notes.

In Korean biopharma, technology transfer deal size and clinical data are often central to how companies are valued. Bull factors

ABN401 Phase 2 data

In the global Phase 2 BICR results presented at ASCO in June 2026, the objective response rate was 55%. Median duration of response was 14.5 months in the overall population and 19.9 months in treatment-naive patients, according to the company.

Grade 3 or higher treatment-related adverse events occurred in 11% of patients, with no Grade 4 events reported. A Korean securities research team in July 2026 assessed the antitumor effect as comparable to approved drugs.

Technology transfer and pipeline expansion

A Korean securities research team reported that the company plans to finish the ABN401 Phase 2 trial in Q4 2026 and pursue further technology transfer. In August 2026, the company said it would meet potential partners during WCLC 2026 to discuss licensing and commercialization.

The iRAC platform has a U.S. patent, and ABN202 is in preclinical work with a target of filing a clinical trial application in the first half of 2027.

Narrower losses and a stronger balance sheet

The operating loss narrowed from KRW 34.1 billion in 2024 to KRW 19.4 billion in 2025. The debt-to-equity ratio fell from 347.7% in 2023 to 126.7% in 2024 and 17.9% in 2025. Q3 2026 sales of KRW 320 million rose 97.6% year on year.
