Daedong's AI Pivot Hinges on Profit Recovery

Daedong is pivoting from a domestic No. 1 farm machinery maker toward agri-physical AI, but a KRW 39.3 billion operating loss in Q3 2026 leaves profit recovery unproven.

  1. 1

    On the filed figures, revenue moved in a narrow band from KRW 1.46 trillion in 2022 to KRW 1.48 trillion in 2025, while operating profit fell to KRW 18.5 billion in 2024 before recovering to KRW 31.2 billion in 2025.

  2. 2

    Q2 2026 revenue of KRW 467.1 billion and operating profit of KRW 41.2 billion marked the highest revenue among the last five quarters, but Q3 2026 revenue fell to KRW 352.4 billion and the company posted an operating loss of KRW 39.3 billion.

  3. 3

02

Business structure

Daedong was founded in 1947 as a domestic farm machinery company supplying complete machines such as tractors, combines and rice transplanters.

Within the group, DaedongGear handles power transmission parts such as transmissions and gears, and DaedongMetal supplies engine castings, forming a vertically linked structure.

The company groups its operations into existing businesses covering tractors, combines, transplanters, compact construction equipment, engines and auto parts, and new businesses including AI farm machinery, agricultural robots, precision farming, smart farming and smart mobility.

The share of new-business revenue was 11.9 percent in 2025, and the company targets 25.9 percent by 2030. A domestic credit rating agency assessed that Daedong maintains the No. 1 share of the domestic farm machinery market, based on a 2025 report.

The sales network rests on about 150 dealers nationwide and the NH agricultural cooperative distribution channel. Overseas sales weigh heavily, and the United States accounts for more than half of revenue as its core market.

Its own brand KIOTI held an 8.7 percent share of the North American compact tractor market in 2024. Revenue at the European subsidiary rose to KRW 1.14 billion in 2025 from KRW 783 million a year earlier.

Competition centers on global farm machinery makers, and the company is broadening its lineup with medium and large tractors and higher-value implements.

04

Earnings analysis

On the filed annual figures, revenue went from KRW 1.46 trillion in 2022 to KRW 1.43 trillion in 2023 (down 2.1 percent year on year) and KRW 1.42 trillion in 2024 (down 1.2 percent). Revenue rose to KRW 1.48 trillion in 2025 (up 4.9 percent), the largest year in the 2022 to 2025 span, while 2024 was the smallest.

Operating profit fell from KRW 88.3 billion in 2022 to KRW 65.4 billion in 2023 (down 25.9 percent) and KRW 18.5 billion in 2024 (down 71.8 percent), then recovered to KRW 31.2 billion in 2025 (up 69.2 percent).

The operating margin was 6.0 percent in 2022, 4.6 percent in 2023, 1.3 percent in 2024 and 2.1 percent in 2025, so 2025 remained below the 2022 level.

Attributable net income fell from KRW 38.6 billion in 2022 to KRW 17.6 billion in 2023 (down 54.5 percent), and the company swung to an attributable net loss of KRW 30.7 billion in 2024. It recorded a further attributable net loss of KRW 16.8 billion in 2025.

Net income including non-controlling interests was a loss of KRW 45.1 billion in 2024 and KRW 29.9 billion in 2025. Operating cash flow was positive at KRW 255.5 billion in 2024 but negative at KRW 56.7 billion in 2025.

On a quarterly basis, the company posted an operating loss of KRW 16 billion in Q4 2025, returned to operating profit of KRW 6.02 billion in Q1 2026, and reported KRW 41.2 billion in Q2 2026, up 584.7 percent from the prior quarter.

News1 reported on August 10, 2026 that Q2 2026 revenue of KRW 467.1 billion was a record for any quarter. Q3 2026 revenue fell 24.6 percent from the prior quarter, and the company posted an operating loss of KRW 39.3 billion. An industry analysis attributes the profit pressure to rising manufacturing costs (IB Tomato, May 11, 2026).

05

Industry analysis

Demand for farm machinery depends on the shrinking farming population and farm household count. A Daedong official said the number of domestic farm households has dropped below 1 million, to about 950,000 (Greened, a trade outlet, September 2026).

Amid weak North American and domestic machinery markets, sales of medium and large tractors and compact construction equipment grew, yet North American revenue in the first half of 2026 fell about 3 percent year on year (News1, August 10, 2026).

The mechanical farm machinery business has entered a mature phase, and some analysts argue it is hard to raise profitability per unit. Government support is expanding: subsidized loans for autonomous farm machinery rose from KRW 1.1 billion in 2023 to KRW 19.5 billion in 2025 (IB Tomato).

In the United States, buyer subsidies for autonomous farm machinery have been paid since the technology first appeared. Rather than importing technology from leaders such as John Deere, the company said it is developing its own technology from scratch at group level.

Autonomous driving without an operator remains restricted by domestic law, which limits the pace of field trials. As a result, the new businesses depend heavily on government fiscal policy in the early stage of market formation.

06

Outlook

The medium-term targets were laid out in the value-up plan disclosed on May 7, 2026. The company targets KRW 3.59 trillion in consolidated revenue by 2030 and a 25.9 percent share from new businesses, with new businesses targeted to grow 39.4 percent a year and existing businesses 15.3 percent a year.

Overseas, it plans to secure more than 1,000 dealers in North America and more than 700 in Europe by 2030. For shareholder returns, the payout ratio is to be raised in stages to around 20 percent by 2030. The 2025 cash dividend totaled about KRW 2.7 billion, up 12.93 percent from about KRW 2.4 billion in 2024.

On products, the vision-AI autonomous tractor launched in April 2026 uses six cameras to recognize field boundaries and obstacles and can work without an operator aboard.

In robotics, the AGROID agricultural robot, co-developed with Rainbow Robotics, is in testing on tasks such as tomato harvesting with a first prototype, and the company plans to deploy it in a fourth-generation AI greenhouse to be built in Jeonnam in 2028 before commercialization.

On November 4, 2026, reports said Hyundai Motor Group and Daedong took part in a briefing on a Saemangeum robot farm factory project. Industry observers, however, expect two to three years before field trials reach the domestic market. The next checkpoint is whether the share of new-business revenue climbs along the 2030 target path.

08

Bull factors

Agri-physical AI transition targets

In its value-up plan of May 7, 2026, the company set out a shift centered on AI-driven autonomous farm machinery, agricultural robots and subscription services. The vision-AI autonomous tractor launched in April 2026 uses six cameras to detect field edges and obstacles.

The company said it aims to link machine data to precision farming services so that revenue continues after equipment is sold.

Overseas network and European growth

News1 reported on August 10, 2026 that first-half revenue at the European subsidiary rose about 31 percent to KRW 74.1 billion, lifting its market share 0.2 percentage points to 2.5 percent. The same report put first-half parts revenue at KRW 56.7 billion, up 20 percent.

Policy support and group collaboration

Subsidized loans for autonomous farm machinery rose from KRW 1.1 billion in 2023 to KRW 19.5 billion in 2025. The company is co-developing the AGROID agricultural robot with Rainbow Robotics.

On November 4, 2026, its participation with Hyundai Motor Group in a Saemangeum robot farm factory briefing was followed by reports of a more than 13 percent intraday gain in the shares, which the market attributed to expectations for agricultural robotics.

09

Bear factors

Weaker earnings base and cumulative losses

The operating margin fell from 6.0 percent in 2022 to 1.3 percent in 2024 and was 2.1 percent in 2025. Attributable net losses occurred in both 2024 and 2025, and the attributable net loss for the last four quarters totals KRW 80.7 billion. In Q3 2026 the company swung to an operating loss of KRW 39.3 billion.

Market slump and falling domestic sales

Amid a slump in North American and domestic machinery markets, North American revenue in the first half of 2026 fell about 3 percent year on year. Domestic farm machinery sales declined from about 15,000 units a year to around 8,000.

The mechanical machinery business has entered a mature phase, and some analysts see limited room to raise profit per unit.

Financial burden and cash flow swings

The debt-to-equity ratio was 251.9 percent at the end of 2025, with total liabilities of KRW 1.63 trillion and total equity of KRW 646.5 billion. Operating cash flow moved from KRW 255.5 billion in 2024 to negative KRW 56.7 billion in 2025.

Net borrowings at the end of 2024 were reported at KRW 785.1 billion, with the increase attributed to affiliate consolidation and North American warehouse leases.

10

Risk factors

Regulation and field-trial timing

Unmanned operation of farm machinery is not yet permitted domestically, delaying field deployment. An industry official expects at least two to three years before domestic field trials are complete. Whether public funding for autonomous machinery keeps expanding is seen as a key variable for early market formation.

Profit conversion and funding

The 2030 targets rest on the premise that mechanical farm machinery sales alone cannot fund the transition, as one analysis argues. The new-business share must rise from 11.9 percent in 2025 to 25.9 percent by 2030, so execution speed is critical. The Q3 2026 operating loss of KRW 39.3 billion could add to financial pressure during the transition period.

Overseas concentration and market conditions

Exports make up about 75 percent of total sales, and the United States accounts for more than half of revenue. Because North American revenue has declined, dependence on one market can increase earnings volatility. Some assessments note that high interest rates and weaker demand have slowed the global farm machinery market.

12

Overall view

Daedong is shifting from a machinery sales model toward an AI and robotics-based agricultural services business. On the filed figures, operating profit recovered to KRW 31.2 billion in 2025 from the 2024 trough, but the operating margin remains below the 6.0 percent recorded in 2022.

Q2 2026 posted the highest quarterly revenue among the last five quarters, yet Q3 2026 swung back to an operating loss of KRW 39.3 billion. Supporting factors include growth at the European subsidiary, expanding government support for autonomous machinery, and co-development of robots with Rainbow Robotics.

Offsetting factors include weak North American and domestic machinery markets, cumulative attributable net losses over the last four quarters, the swing to negative operating cash flow in 2025, and a debt-to-equity ratio of 251.9 percent.

Field deployment and commercialization of the new businesses are expected to become visible after 2028. The next checkpoints are the operating margin and cash flow in the 2026 annual report and changes in the share of new-business revenue.
