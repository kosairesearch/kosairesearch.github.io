A power-supply maker's shift, with quarterly profit still uneven

02

Business structure

Powernet Technologies was founded in 1994 to manufacture and sell electronic components and listed on KOSDAQ in 2018. Its core product is the switch-mode power supply (SMPS), made at production subsidiaries in Shenyang, China and Hanoi, Vietnam, while the Seoul headquarters handles management and R&D.

The company is widening its product lines into B2B and B2C from a structure built around SMPS for home appliances and displays. Its new EMS (electronics contract manufacturing) and battery businesses have taken hold and drove sales growth after 2024, according to the company.

In batteries, it began supplying lithium iron phosphate (LFP) battery packs to a K-beauty device maker at the end of 2024. EMS revenue began in the Americas in 2025, and reports say supply has expanded to seven countries.

The company is also developing an 8kW gallium nitride (GaN) server power unit with the French power-semiconductor firm Wise Integration, aimed at AI data centers. The business mix is therefore shifting from traditional appliance SMPS toward EMS, batteries and server PSUs.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual results show sales growth arriving first and operating profit following. Sales were KRW 211.1 billion in 2022, KRW 186.2 billion in 2023 (down 11.8% year on year), KRW 322.7 billion in 2024 (up 73.3%) and KRW 447 billion in 2025 (up 38.5%).

Sales have risen for two consecutive years from 2024 through 2025, and 2025 was the largest year in the 2022-2025 period.

Operating profit was KRW 5.67 billion in 2022, KRW 7.15 billion in 2023 (up 26.0%), KRW 13.3 billion in 2024 (up 86.2%) and KRW 24.6 billion in 2025 (up 85.0%), rising for three consecutive years from 2023 through 2025. The operating margin rose every year from 2.7% in 2022 to 3.8%, 4.1% and 5.5% in 2025.

The company said 2025 was a record for the second straight year, and brokers attributed the margin gain to wider supply of premium SMPS and a better product mix. On a standalone basis, the company reported 2025 sales of KRW 247.1 billion (up 7.4%) and operating profit of KRW 18.1 billion (up 36.0%).

Profit attributable to controlling shareholders swung to a net loss of KRW 197 million in 2023, returned to a profit of KRW 20.8 billion in 2024, and then fell 26.8% to KRW 15.2 billion in 2025.

Total net income was KRW 21.3 billion in 2025, and the gap from the controlling-shareholder figure reflects non-controlling interests. Operating cash flow went from -KRW 2.05 billion in 2022 to KRW 25.7 billion in 2023, KRW 24.5 billion in 2024 and KRW 21.9 billion in 2025.

Quarterly sales peaked at KRW 123.9 billion in the fourth quarter of 2025, up 23.3% from the prior quarter and the highest of the last five quarters, then fell to KRW 106.6 billion in the first quarter of 2026 and KRW 104.2 billion in the second quarter before recovering to KRW 118.5 billion in the third quarter (up 13.7% quarter on quarter and 17.9% year on year).

Operating profit bottomed at KRW 3.64 billion in the first quarter of 2026, then rose to KRW 6.9 billion in the second quarter (up 89.6%) and KRW 9.17 billion in the third quarter (up 32.9% quarter on quarter and 37.5% year on year), two consecutive quarters of growth.

Profit attributable to controlling shareholders over the same stretch was KRW 772 million in the fourth quarter of 2025, KRW 422 million in the first quarter of 2026, KRW 5.09 billion in the second quarter and KRW 1.11 billion in the third quarter, totaling KRW 7.4 billion over the last four quarters.

In the third quarter of 2026, operating profit rose while controlling-shareholder profit fell sharply, so the items between the two lines, including non-operating items, taxes and non-controlling interests, are the key points to examine.

The debt ratio went from 56.2% in 2022 to 58.3% in 2023 and 81.8% in 2024, then eased to 74.5% in 2025, when total equity was KRW 334.5 billion, of which KRW 152.2 billion belonged to controlling shareholders.

05

Industry analysis

Powernet Technologies' end market is shifting from appliance and display SMPS toward AI data-center power infrastructure. Data-center power supplies must deliver high efficiency and high output, so PSU performance feeds directly into reliability and operating cost.

Reports covering the topic cite forecasts that global AI data-center investment could reach a cumulative KRW 7 trillion-scale figure, expressed in US dollars, by 2030. The company says it is pushing ahead with AI server PSUs built on high-efficiency, high-density technology.

Hanyang Securities, in a May 2026 report, viewed the PowerShelf concept as extending beyond PSU supply into integrated rack-level power solutions. The same report judged that Powernet is likely to be re-rated from a maker of SMPS for appliances and displays into a power-infrastructure company for AI data centers.

In competition, the sector includes large component makers such as Samsung Electro-Mechanics and LG Innotek, and Powernet is far smaller than these peers. Its distinguishing feature is combining an SMPS manufacturing record with EMS and battery capabilities.

On the cycle, the AI server PSU is still before commercialization, and the same report sees 2026 growth driven by larger volumes from major customers.

06

Outlook

The company says it is preparing to enter the AI data-center infrastructure market to secure new growth drivers. An 8kW GaN-based next-generation server power unit is targeted for commercialization in 2027, according to an April 2026 report.

The company says it aims to develop and commercialize high-density products that combine dynamic load prediction with high-efficiency Digital GaN designs.

In a May 2026 report, Hanyang Securities projected 2026 sales of KRW 501.4 billion and operating profit of KRW 31 billion, with operating profit up 26% and a likely new record. The same report sees 2026 growth driven by larger volumes from major customers, with AI server PSUs expected to drive medium-term revaluation.

The same coverage said completion of the Vietnam production line expansion would add room for further EMS growth. EMS revenue is ramping mainly in the Americas, and battery pack supply is expanding.

Sales and operating profit rose year on year through the third quarter of 2026, but profit attributable to controlling shareholders declined, so whether the forecasts are realized depends on profit at the net level.

The next checkpoints are whether annual sales and operating profit in the 2026 annual report reach the forecast levels, and whether the 2027 PSU commercialization schedule holds.

08

Bull factors

Sales and margins grew together over three years

Sales more than doubled from KRW 211.1 billion in 2022 to KRW 447 billion in 2025, and the operating margin rose from 2.7% to 5.5%. Operating profit increased for three consecutive years from 2023 through 2025. The company says its EMS, battery and B2C businesses have entered a structural growth track.

Operating profit recovered in 2026

Operating profit rose to KRW 6.9 billion in the second quarter of 2026, up 89.6% from the prior quarter, and to KRW 9.17 billion in the third quarter, up 32.9%. That is two consecutive quarters of growth, and third-quarter sales of KRW 118.5 billion rose 17.9% year on year. Whether this operating recovery continues is the next reference point.

AI server PSU and brokerage forecasts

In a May 2026 report, Hanyang Securities projected 2026 sales of KRW 501.4 billion and operating profit of KRW 31 billion, and estimated operating profit would set a record. The report points to AI server PSUs as a medium-term revaluation factor. The company is developing an 8kW GaN-based server power unit with 2027 commercialization targeted.

09

Bear factors

Sharp drop in profit attributable to controlling shareholders

Profit attributable to controlling shareholders fell 26.8% to KRW 15.2 billion in 2025, and the KRW 1.11 billion in the third quarter of 2026 was down 77.6% year on year. The last four quarters total KRW 7.4 billion. If operating gains keep failing to reach controlling shareholders, questions about earnings quality remain.

Gap between operating profit and net profit

Third-quarter 2026 operating profit was KRW 9.17 billion, but profit attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 1.11 billion. In 2025, controlling shareholders received KRW 15.2 billion of KRW 21.3 billion total net income.

Until the items between operating profit and net profit are clearer in filings, the earnings trend is hard to read with confidence.

Unproven new businesses and financial burden

The AI server PSU is targeted for commercialization in 2027 and is not yet reflected in results. The debt ratio rose to 81.8% in 2024 before easing to 74.5% in 2025, with total liabilities of KRW 249.3 billion. Operating cash flow declined from KRW 25.7 billion in 2023 to KRW 21.9 billion in 2025.

10

Risk factors

Customer concentration and volume swings

Brokerage forecasts for 2026 lean on larger volumes from major customers. If orders from a key customer fall, sales and operating profit could move together. Sales fell 14.0% quarter on quarter in the first quarter of 2026, which shows how volatile quarterly volumes can be.

Commercialization timing of new businesses

The 8kW GaN server power unit is targeted for 2027 commercialization, and the timing for completing the Vietnam line expansion is not fixed in public reports. Delays would push back the medium-term growth story for AI data-center PSUs. Whether the high-efficiency design keeps its edge amid technology competition is another variable.

Overseas production and trade conditions

Production subsidiaries in Shenyang, China and Hanoi, Vietnam expose the company to local labor costs, trade rules and exchange-rate moves that can affect profitability. As EMS sales in the Americas grow, logistics and trade conditions carry more weight. At an operating margin of 5.5%, cost changes can have a relatively large effect.

Overall view

Powernet Technologies more than doubled sales from 2022 and raised its operating margin from 2.7% to 5.5%, with operating profit rising for three consecutive years from 2023 through 2025.

However, profit attributable to controlling shareholders fell 26.8% to KRW 15.2 billion in 2025, so gains in sales and operating profitability did not fully carry through to shareholders.
