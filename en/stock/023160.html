02

Business structure

TK Corporation manufactures pipe fittings for industrial plants and shipbuilding, supplying butt-weld, forged, and flange fittings made of carbon, stainless, and alloy steel to the oil and gas, petrochemical, power generation, and shipbuilding industries.

By segment, plant-equipment fittings account for 86.49% of revenue, secondary-battery equipment components for 13.02%, and real-estate leasing and exhibition-related business for 0.49%.

Its main subsidiaries are Foundry Seoul Co., Ltd. and HYTC Co., Ltd., through which the company has diversified into secondary-battery equipment components.

Key customers include major domestic shipbuilders such as Samsung Heavy Industries and Hanwha Ocean, as well as global EPC (engineering, procurement, construction) contractors, with end demand coming from LNG terminals and carriers, refining and petrochemical plants, and power generation facilities.

The company has a high export exposure, making its results closely tied to overseas project order cycles in the Middle East, North America, and Asia.

It competes with domestic and global peers in the butt-weld and forged fitting markets, offering an extensive product range spanning tens of thousands of fitting variants. Production is centered at its Busan-based plants, with plant-equipment fitting manufacturing remaining its core business.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual revenue rose from KRW 249.7 billion in 2022 to KRW 312.1 billion in 2023, up 25.0% and the largest figure in the 2022-2025 period, before falling 14.5% to KRW 266.8 billion in 2024 and recovering 11.3% to KRW 297 billion in 2025.

Operating profit rose from KRW 45.4 billion in 2022 to a peak of KRW 57.7 billion in 2023 (up 27.3%), then declined for two consecutive years to KRW 39.9 billion in 2024 (down 30.9%) and KRW 38.7 billion in 2025 (down 2.9%), making 2025 the smallest figure in the period.

Operating margin accordingly slipped from 18.2% in 2022 and 18.5% in 2023 to 14.9% in 2024 and 13.0% in 2025.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders, however, moved differently: from KRW 35.8 billion in 2022 to KRW 55.1 billion in 2023 (up 54.0%), KRW 44.9 billion in 2024 (down 18.5%), and KRW 63.9 billion in 2025 (up 42.2%), the largest figure in the period despite softer operating profit.

Net income including non-controlling interests also rose from KRW 36.8 billion in 2022 to KRW 65.5 billion in 2025.

On a quarterly basis, revenue grew from KRW 72.3 billion in Q2 2025 to KRW 81.4 billion in Q3 2025 (up 12.6%), dipped to KRW 80.6 billion in Q4 2025 (down 1.0%), then rose for two consecutive quarters to KRW 82.3 billion in Q1 2026 (up 2.1%) and KRW 83.7 billion in Q2 2026 (up 1.8% quarter over quarter, up 15.8% year over year).

Operating profit, however, peaked at KRW 12.1 billion in Q4 2025 and then fell for two straight quarters to KRW 9.35 billion in Q1 2026 (down 22.8%) and KRW 7.21 billion in Q2 2026 (down 22.9% quarter over quarter, down 5.8% year over year), moving opposite to revenue.

At the same time, net income attributable to controlling shareholders bottomed at KRW 10 billion in Q4 2025 and then surged for two consecutive quarters to KRW 27 billion in Q1 2026 (up 169.4%) and KRW 58.9 billion in Q2 2026 (up 118.3% quarter over quarter, up 301.3% year over year), bringing the trailing four-quarter total (Q3 2025-Q2 2026) to KRW 124.9 billion.

The divergence between operating profit and net income trends suggests a significant contribution from non-operating items, which, combined with the sharp drop in 2025 operating cash flow to KRW 19.4 billion from KRW 59.2 billion the prior year, warrants closer attention to earnings quality.

05

Industry analysis

The industrial-plant fitting market that TK Corporation operates in is heavily influenced by the order cycles of large capital-expenditure projects in LNG terminals and carriers, petrochemicals, refining, and power generation.

According to recent industry data, a combination of energy supply-chain recovery demand, a surge in new LNG and petrochemical plant construction, and rising LNG carrier orders in the shipbuilding sector has driven improvement in the plant-equipment segment.

In Q1 2026, consolidated revenue rose 31.4% year over year, operating profit rose 26.3%, and net income rose 164.2%, reflecting the recovery in upstream plant orders flowing through to results.

As the global energy transition is delayed, continued demand for natural gas as a bridge fuel is seen as a key growth driver for this industry.

However, plant orders remain volatile given oil prices, interest rates, and geopolitical factors, and the multi-month lag between order intake and revenue recognition structurally widens quarterly earnings swings.

Amid competition with domestic and global fitting makers, order pricing and product mix—particularly the share of non-carbon-steel products—act as key variables for profitability.

The secondary-battery equipment segment still accounts for a relatively small share of revenue, meaning the core plant-equipment cycle is likely to remain the dominant driver of overall results for the time being.

06

Outlook

Under the value-up plan disclosed in March 2026, the company plans to buy back and cancel KRW 10 billion worth of treasury shares within 2026, and in addition to its existing 2024-2026 target of returning 30% of net income to shareholders annually, it has set a goal of raising this ratio to 45% for 2027-2029 while targeting a price-to-book ratio of 1x or higher over the medium term.

According to related reporting, the total 2025 dividend payout disclosed rose 114% from the prior year, and the 2024 shareholder return ratio relative to net income reached 30%, a sharp increase from 7.6% in 2023 within a single year.

On the business side, the company has also indicated plans to expand into new growth areas such as aerospace, including rocket-launch infrastructure, alongside efforts to strengthen profitability in its core plant-equipment business.

According to a 2024 site-visit note from Hana Securities, the company had secured production capacity of roughly KRW 400 billion in revenue terms at the time with no near-term expansion plans, and it was not conducting separate foreign-exchange hedging.

If new LNG terminal investment and shipbuilding orders continue, this could gradually feed through to revenue via a growing order backlog, though the timing of final investment decisions on large overseas projects in North America and the Middle East remains fluid, which could cause quarterly order intake and revenue recognition to diverge.

The utilization trend at the secondary-battery equipment subsidiary is also worth monitoring as a secondary variable for overall results.

08

Bull factors

Expanding LNG and Energy Plant Order Cycle

The overlap of energy supply-chain recovery demand, a surge in new LNG and petrochemical plant construction, and rising LNG carrier orders in shipbuilding has driven improvement in the plant-equipment segment.

In Q1 2026, consolidated revenue rose 31.4% year over year, with operating profit and net income also increasing. As long as natural gas continues to serve as a bridge fuel during the energy transition, related equipment investment demand could persist.

Expanding Shareholder Return Policy

Under its 2026 value-up plan, the company is pursuing a KRW 10 billion treasury share buyback and cancellation, and has set a target to raise its shareholder return ratio to 45% of net income for 2027-2029.

The 2024 shareholder return ratio reached 30%, a sharp improvement from 7.6% in 2023 within a single year, and the total 2025 dividend payout was reported to have risen 114% from the prior year. If the return policy is executed as planned, the predictability of shareholder returns could improve.

Low Leverage and Stable Financial Structure

The debt-to-equity ratio stood at 10.6% at the end of 2025, remaining in the low-single-to-low-double-digit range throughout 2022-2025.

At the same point, total equity was KRW 669.6 billion, of which equity attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 624.6 billion, providing substantial capital buffer against total liabilities of KRW 71.2 billion.

This low reliance on borrowing could serve as a financial cushion against volatility in large plant project orders.

Bear factors

Softening Operating Margin

Operating margin fell from 18.5% in 2023 to 14.9% in 2024 and 13.0% in 2025, and on a quarterly basis, operating profit declined for two consecutive quarters in Q1 and Q2 2026 after peaking in Q4 2025.

The fact that profitability did not improve alongside revenue growth suggests a need to examine changes in cost structure or product mix. If this trend continues, earnings strength may not keep pace with revenue growth.

Sharp Drop in Operating Cash Flow

Operating cash flow fell sharply to KRW 19.4 billion in 2025 from KRW 59.2 billion the prior year. Contrasted with net income attributable to controlling shareholders rising to KRW 63.9 billion in the same year, this reveals a gap between reported earnings and actual cash generation.

The impact of working-capital items such as receivables and inventory on cash flow warrants further examination.

Net Income Volatility Diverging From Operating Results

While operating profit fell 22.8% and 22.9% in Q1 and Q2 2026 respectively, net income attributable to controlling shareholders instead rose 169.4% and 118.3% in the same quarters.

This suggests a significant contribution from non-operating items, and without confirmation of the sustainability of these items, future quarterly earnings volatility could increase. Without a recovery in core operating profitability, it is difficult to judge earnings quality from net income growth alone.

Risk factors

Foreign Exchange and Raw Materials

According to a past company visit note, the company does not conduct separate foreign-exchange hedging, meaning that given its export-heavy business structure, currency fluctuations can directly affect results.

Volatility in raw material prices such as steel can also flow straight through to costs, serving as a source of margin variability. If currency and raw material prices move unfavorably at the same time, defending profitability could become more difficult.

Customer and Project Concentration

A large share of revenue is tied to a small number of major shipbuilders, such as Samsung Heavy Industries and Hanwha Ocean, and to large plant projects concentrated in specific regions such as the Middle East and North America.

Order timing can be delayed or accelerated depending on oil prices, interest rates, and geopolitical factors, which can increase quarterly earnings volatility. A reduction in orders from a specific customer or project could have a significant impact on overall results.

Cash Flow and Working Capital

The sharp decline in 2025 operating cash flow relative to the prior year suggests a possible increase in working-capital burden from items such as receivables and inventory.

If the funding for the shareholder return policy—simultaneously pursuing treasury share buybacks, cancellations, and higher dividends—coincides with slowing operating cash flow, the financing approach may warrant closer scrutiny. Whether cash generation recovers is a variable to confirm in upcoming quarterly results.

12

Overall view

TK Corporation posted a revenue rebound in 2025 and continued quarterly revenue growth through the first half of 2026, but operating profit slowed for two consecutive quarters after peaking in Q4 2025, and the operating margin has shown a multi-year downward trend.

In contrast, net income attributable to controlling shareholders hit its highest level in 2025 and surged further in the first half of 2026, creating a pronounced gap between operating results and net income.

The sharp decline in 2025 operating cash flow relative to the prior year is a basis for examining earnings quality alongside reported profit figures.

The company is pursuing an industry-favorable environment of expanding LNG and energy plant orders alongside an active shareholder return policy, including treasury share buybacks, cancellations, and a planned rise in the shareholder return ratio.

Its financial structure remains relatively stable on a low debt ratio, but order-cycle volatility and the cash flow slowdown are variables that warrant continued monitoring.

Confirming the sustainability of earnings will require tracking upcoming quarterly results, order disclosures, and the execution of the shareholder return policy.
