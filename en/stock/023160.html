T. K: Third-Quarter Loss Puts Plant Order Recovery to the Test

T. K's plant equipment demand lifted its first-quarter 2026 results, but operating profit fell 57.4% in the third quarter of 2026, and net income attributable to controlling shareholders turned into a loss of KRW 20.4 billion.

  1. 1

    Operating profit in 2025 was KRW 38.7 billion, below the KRW 57.7 billion peak in 2023, while net income attributable to controlling shareholders reached KRW 63.9 billion, the largest year of 2022 to 2025.

  2. 2

    Third-quarter 2026 revenue was KRW 75.1 billion, down 7.7% from a year earlier, and operating profit was KRW 4.93 billion, down 57.4%. The provisional results disclosed on November 9, 2026 showed the same direction.

  3. 3

    Plant equipment revenue follows orders with a lag of roughly five months, so order flow shows up in results a few quarters later. A Korea Investment & Securities analyst cited this lag when explaining third-quarter results.

  4. 4

    The company plans to buy back and cancel KRW 10 billion of treasury shares within 2026 and has set a PBR of 1x or higher as its medium-term target for 2027 to 2029.

  5. 5

02

Business structure

T. K makes pipe fittings used in LNG facilities, offshore plants and shipbuilding, and that plant equipment business is its core. As of the first nine months of 2023, plant equipment accounted for 86.9% of sales, secondary battery equipment for 12.6%, and real estate leasing for 0.5%.

Plant equipment customers include LNG export terminals, petrochemical plants and shipbuilders building LNG carriers. The fitting business has a lead time of roughly five months from order to revenue recognition, so order flow feeds into results a few quarters later.

The secondary battery equipment segment has grown with demand for medium- and large-format batteries, supported by carbon neutrality policy and wider electric vehicle adoption. Exports were about 80% of total sales, and the company did not hedge currency exposure, according to an August 2024 broker site visit note.

Raw materials were sourced domestically for plate and from Japan for heavy plate, per the same note. Production capacity was roughly KRW 400 billion on a sales basis, with no near-term expansion plan stated in that note. Pricing power is limited, so the company focuses on production efficiency to widen margins.

On competition, the share of Middle East project orders and access to short-lead-time orders are cited as factors behind quarter-to-quarter differences in results.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual revenue rose from KRW 249.7 billion in 2022 to KRW 312.1 billion in 2023 (up 25.0% year on year), fell to KRW 266.8 billion in 2024 (down 14.5%), and recovered to KRW 297 billion in 2025 (up 11.3%).

Operating profit peaked at KRW 57.7 billion in 2023 (up 27.3%) from KRW 45.4 billion in 2022, then fell to KRW 39.9 billion in 2024 (down 30.9%) and KRW 38.7 billion in 2025 (down 2.9%), with declines in both 2024 and 2025.

The operating margin moved from 18.2% in 2022 and 18.5% in 2023 to 14.9% in 2024 and 13.0% in 2025, so margins stayed thin even as revenue recovered in 2025.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders went from KRW 35.8 billion in 2022 to KRW 55.1 billion in 2023, KRW 44.9 billion in 2024 and KRW 63.9 billion in 2025, the largest year of the four. Net income including non-controlling interests was KRW 65.5 billion in 2025.

That net income ran well ahead of operating profit suggests that items outside operating profit had a large effect. Operating cash flow went from KRW 46.3 billion in 2022 and KRW 59.2 billion in 2024 to KRW 19.4 billion in 2025, a sharp drop.

At the end of 2025, total equity was KRW 669.6 billion, of which KRW 624.6 billion belonged to controlling shareholders, and total liabilities were KRW 71.2 billion. The debt ratio rose from 8.3% in 2024 to 10.6% in 2025 but remains low.

Quarterly revenue was KRW 81.4 billion in the third quarter of 2025, KRW 80.6 billion in the fourth quarter (down 1.0% from the prior quarter), KRW 82.3 billion in the first quarter of 2026 and KRW 83.7 billion in the second quarter, then KRW 75.1 billion in the third quarter (down 10.3% from the prior quarter).

Operating profit peaked at KRW 12.1 billion in the fourth quarter of 2025 and then declined for three consecutive quarters, to KRW 9.35 billion, KRW 7.21 billion and KRW 4.93 billion in the first through third quarters of 2026.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 27 billion in the first quarter of 2026 and KRW 58.9 billion in the second quarter, then turned into a net loss of KRW 20.4 billion in the third quarter.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders over the latest four quarters totaled KRW 75.6 billion. The first-quarter improvement was attributed to plant equipment, driven by post-conflict energy supply chain restoration demand, new LNG and petrochemical plant construction and fitting demand for LNG carriers.

For the third-quarter decline, a Korea Investment & Securities analyst pointed to weak first-half orders starting to hit revenue from the third quarter and a weaker won.

Industry analysis

Demand from downstream markets is being supported by LNG-related facilities and export terminals, according to a FnGuide company summary that describes the downstream industry as recovering.

The first-quarter 2026 company monitor commentary cited post-conflict energy supply chain restoration demand, new LNG and petrochemical plant construction, and fitting demand tied to LNG carrier orders at shipbuilders as drivers.

Korea Investment & Securities said in an early November 2026 report that US orders could fill the gap left by Middle East orders and offset weak first-half orders. The same analyst said the lead time from order to revenue is about five months, so weak first-half orders began to affect revenue from the third quarter.

A view also emerged that short-lead-time orders could concentrate at T. K once Middle East conditions settle. The secondary battery segment is affected by carbon neutrality policy and wider electric vehicle adoption, which lift demand for medium- and large-format batteries.

Plant orders and shipbuilding orders are overlapping, so whether backlog recovers is likely to shape the industry picture. Pricing power was described as limited in the competitive context, so cost and production efficiency drive margins.

Because exports are a large share of sales, the won's movement feeds directly into revenue, and a weaker won was cited as a drag on third-quarter sales.

Outlook

Under its corporate value enhancement plan, the company disclosed that it will pursue buying back and cancelling KRW 10 billion of treasury shares within 2026.

It had set a target of paying out about 30% of net income as shareholder returns for each year from 2024 to 2026, and it decided to add treasury share buybacks and cancellations within 2026. The 2025 dividend payout ratio was presented as 21.12%, and the 2025 dividend amount was reported as up 114% from 2024.

For 2027 to 2029, the medium-term target is a PBR of 1x or higher, with the company saying it aims to secure a market premium through active shareholder returns. On orders, whether Middle East orders recover and whether US orders fill the gap will be the key variables for revenue after the fourth quarter of 2026.

LNG carrier orders and LNG export terminal investment are the foundation of fitting demand, so whether related orders continue is the item to confirm.

Whether the decline in third-quarter 2026 operating profit to KRW 4.93 billion, and the swing to a controlling-shareholder loss, reverses in the fourth quarter is the next checkpoint. The company had no near-term expansion plan as of 2024, so any change in production capacity needs to be confirmed through disclosures.

The 2026 annual report is the next periodic report, and it will show the full-year operating margin alongside the execution of shareholder returns.

Risk factors

Order volatility and Middle East exposure

Plant equipment orders depend on specific projects and regional tenders. Order gaps could last longer depending on how the Middle East conflict develops, and with a lead time of about five months, gaps reach results late. The third-quarter revenue decline has been analyzed as reflecting this lag.

Currency and raw material conditions

A 2024 broker note recorded exports at about 80% of sales with no currency hedging. Plate is sourced domestically and heavy plate from Japan, so currency and raw material prices can affect margins at the same time. Limited pricing power makes it harder to pass cost increases on to customers.

Earnings volatility and provisional figures

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders swung widely between quarters, from KRW 10 billion in the fourth quarter of 2025 to KRW 27 billion in the first quarter of 2026 and KRW 58.9 billion in the second.

In the third quarter, operating profit fell to KRW 4.93 billion and the company posted a net loss attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 20.4 billion. The company stated that the third-quarter provisional figures may change during the external auditor's review.
