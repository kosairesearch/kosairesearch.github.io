02

Business structure

Taekwang (023160), established in 1982, is a KOSDAQ-listed manufacturer of pipe fittings for industrial plants; the domestic welded-fitting market is split between Taekwang and Seokwang Bend, while Taekwang has maintained a leading position in exports.

Based on a January 2026 investor presentation, LNG-related fittings accounted for roughly 50% of sales, followed by oil refining and petrochemicals at about 20% and shipbuilding/offshore at about 10%.

The company serves more than 200 overseas clients across 48 countries, with over 80% of revenue generated abroad, making it a representative export manufacturer.

It is a registered qualified supplier to major global energy firms including Aramco, with a long supply track record at key Middle East energy facilities such as Ras Laffan in Qatar, Ras Tanura in Saudi Arabia, and Ruwais in the UAE.

Its subsidiaries include Foundry Seoul, engaged in real estate leasing and exhibition planning, and KOSDAQ-listed HYTC, which manufactures secondary-battery equipment parts and slitters.

More recently, the company has been expanding beyond its traditional petrochemical and energy-plant focus into new areas such as data center piping for global big-tech firms and rocket launch-pad fittings for US aerospace companies including SpaceX.

The fitting industry has an oligopolistic structure globally because it requires a proven supply track record and manufacturing capability spanning roughly 90,000 product types, limiting the number of credible competitors. The company itself has estimated its Middle East market share at around 50-70%.

04

Earnings analysis

Consolidated revenue in 2025 reached KRW 296.97 billion, up 11.3% from KRW 266.81 billion in 2024, while operating profit of KRW 38.73 billion was slightly below 2024's KRW 39.87 billion, pulling the operating margin down from 14.9% to 13.0%.

By contrast, owner net profit rose 42.3% to KRW 63.87 billion from KRW 44.91 billion in 2024, diverging from the operating-profit trend.

Compared with 2023, when revenue was KRW 312.07 billion, operating profit KRW 57.72 billion, and operating margin 18.5%, the 2024-2025 period shows top-line growth but profitability still below the prior peak.

On a quarterly basis, operating margin improved progressively from 10.6% in Q2 2025 (revenue KRW 72.3 billion, operating profit KRW 7.7 billion) to 14.2% in Q3 (revenue KRW 81.4 billion, operating profit KRW 11.6 billion) and 15.0% in Q4 (revenue KRW 80.6 billion, operating profit KRW 12.1 billion).

However, the operating margin fell back to 11.4% in Q1 2026 and dropped further to 8.6% in Q2 2026, as operating profit declined to KRW 7.2 billion despite revenue of KRW 83.7 billion.

Notably, owner net profit of KRW 27.0 billion in Q1 2026 and KRW 58.9 billion in Q2 2026 far exceeded those quarters' operating profit of KRW 9.3 billion and KRW 7.2 billion, respectively, pointing to a substantial non-operating contribution whose specific source is not clearly detailed in currently available disclosures.

A similar gap appeared in Q3 2025, when owner net profit of KRW 29.0 billion well exceeded operating profit of KRW 11.6 billion, suggesting this divergence between operating and net profit has recurred across the most recent four quarters.

From 2022 to 2025, annual owner net profit moved from KRW 35.8 billion to KRW 55.1 billion, KRW 44.9 billion, and KRW 63.9 billion, showing an overall improving trend despite year-to-year fluctuations.

05

Industry analysis

The global welded-fitting industry is an oligopoly dominated by a handful of players including Taekwang, Seokwang Bend, Tectubi, and Tecnoforge, and is classified as capital- and technology-intensive with high barriers to entry.

Recent demand drivers include pipeline and terminal investment tied to US LNG export expansion policy, Middle East energy-security reinforcement and post-conflict reconstruction demand, and a boom in eco-friendly vessel orders at Korean shipyards.

In 2026, localized conflict in the Middle East delayed some projects and sharply reduced the region's export share for a period, but subsequent US-Iran peace talks reportedly spurred increased replacement-demand inquiries from customers in Qatar, Saudi Arabia, and the UAE.

At the same time, geopolitical tension around the Strait of Hormuz has reportedly prompted some energy companies and importing countries to expand LNG import/export facility investment as part of supply-chain diversification.

Compared with domestic rival Seokwang Bend, Taekwang carries a higher Middle East revenue weighting but has recently been diversifying its geographic footprint by expanding into North America and Canada.

Its expansion into secondary-battery equipment parts, data center piping, and aerospace applications can be read as reducing reliance on the traditional petrochemical and energy cycle.

However, overseas construction order statistics showed Middle East orders falling sharply year on year at points in 2026, underscoring that volatility in the Middle East order cycle remains a sector-wide risk.

Outlook

In a January 2026 investor presentation, the company expressed confidence that North America-driven orders could support a record year, citing that previously tariff-delayed order backlogs were beginning to be recognized as revenue.

The Canadian LNG project supply contract disclosed in August 2026 was signed ahead of final investment decision (FID), with potential follow-on orders discussed for after FID within the year.

While Middle East order flow was disrupted by conflict, the company reported that replacement-demand discussions with customers in Qatar, Saudi Arabia, and the UAE intensified following subsequent peace-talk developments.

On shareholder returns, in addition to its existing 2024-2026 target of returning 30% of net income, the company plans a KRW 10 billion treasury share buyback and retirement within 2026, and has set medium-term targets for 2027-2029 of a combined cash dividend and buyback/retirement payout ratio above 45% and a price-to-book ratio of 1x or higher.

Subsidiary HYTC has been noted as a candidate for a quarterly swing to operating profit on rising utilization, making its secondary-battery segment contribution a point to watch.

The next regular filing, the Q3 2026 report, is due under capital markets law by November 16, 2026, and should reveal whether the Middle East export share shift and the gap between operating and net profit persist.

Since the revenue contribution from newer areas such as aerospace and data centers has not yet been disclosed, the actual scale of their earnings impact will need to be verified through future filings.

08

Bull factors

Simultaneous Presence Across Three Global LNG Supply Hubs

Taekwang has secured a supply contract for a Canadian LNG project in addition to its existing US and Middle East track record, giving it a presence across all three major hubs of the global LNG supply chain.

The Canadian order was signed ahead of a final investment decision, with potential follow-on orders discussed for after FID within the year. Because the project involves multiple global customers, it has been described as a potential springboard for expanding the client base into the Americas, Middle East, and Asia.

Diversification Through Expansion into New Industries

The company is expanding beyond its traditional petrochemical and energy-plant focus into secondary-battery equipment parts, data center piping, and rocket launch-pad fittings for aerospace. This can be interpreted as a direction that eases reliance on the traditional oil and gas cycle.

If utilization at subsidiary HYTC continues to rise, the secondary-battery segment's earnings contribution has room to gradually expand.

Strengthened Shareholder Return Policy

In addition to its existing 2024-2026 target of returning 30% of net income to shareholders, the company has disclosed plans for an additional KRW 10 billion treasury share buyback and retirement within 2026.

For 2027-2029, it has set a medium-term target of a combined cash dividend and buyback/retirement payout ratio above 45%. It has also stated that cash dividends will be maintained or expanded relative to the prior year, giving relatively clear direction to its return policy.

Bear factors

Middle East Revenue Concentration and Volatility

The Middle East export share reached 67.9% in 2025 but fell sharply to 41.5% in Q1 2026, illustrating how revenue concentration in a specific region can directly translate into earnings volatility.

If geopolitical conflict occurs, there is an ongoing risk that project orders could be delayed or revenue recognition timing pushed back. While the company is diversifying into North America and Canada, this diversification will take time to fully materialize.

Widening Quarter-to-Quarter Profitability Swings

Between Q2 2025 and Q2 2026, operating margin swung widely, ranging from 8.6% to 15.0%. This indicates that quarterly results can be heavily influenced by project-specific product mix, including shifts in the share of higher-margin alloy and stainless products.

The low quarter-to-quarter predictability of profitability makes it difficult to judge the earnings trend from any single quarter alone.

Gap Between Operating Profit and Net Profit

In Q3 2025 and both Q1 and Q2 2026, owner net profit substantially exceeded operating profit in the same quarter, and the specific nature of this non-operating profit is not clearly explained by currently available public disclosures.

If non-recurring gains repeatedly drive results, it becomes harder to evaluate the earnings power of the core fitting business alone. It will be worth watching how the gap between operating profit and net profit evolves in coming quarters.

Risk factors

Geopolitical Risk

Localized conflict in the Middle East and tensions around the Strait of Hormuz can directly affect major customers' order schedules and logistics. If the conflict situation becomes prolonged or recurs, revenue recognition timing could be further delayed for Taekwang given its high Middle East revenue exposure.

Conversely, if reconstruction demand materializes in earnest, orders could rise in the near term, meaning risk and opportunity coexist.

Raw Material and Foreign Exchange Fluctuation

Fluctuations in the prices of specialty steel raw materials such as nickel alloys directly affect fitting manufacturing costs, and the company's historical raw-material inventory accumulation approach has previously been cited as a factor behind profitability gaps versus competitors.

Given that over 80% of revenue is generated overseas, foreign exchange movements are also a variable that can affect results. If raw material prices and exchange rates move unfavorably at the same time, margin pressure could intensify.

Volatility of Project-Based Orders

In the fitting industry, order intake and revenue recognition timing are governed by whether individual plant or LNG projects reach final investment decision.

Supply contracts signed ahead of FID, such as the Canadian LNG agreement, carry the risk that if the underlying project is delayed or canceled, expected revenue may not materialize. As dependence on specific large projects increases, volatility in the order backlog can increase correspondingly.

Overall view

Taekwang is broadening its earnings base across the global LNG-plant fitting supply chain spanning the US, the Middle East, and Canada, while simultaneously expanding into new industries such as secondary batteries, data centers, and aerospace. 2025 revenue grew 11.3% year on year, but operating margin fell to 13.0%, and quarterly operating margins in 2026 continued to swing between roughly 8.6% and 15%.

Over the same period, owner net profit repeatedly exceeded operating profit by a wide margin in several quarters, indicating that factors outside the core business have had a notable influence on results.

The Middle East export share's sharp decline from 67.9% in 2025 to 41.5% in Q1 2026 provides a concrete figure for the company's regional concentration risk while also underscoring the rationale for diversifying into North America and Canada.

The company has articulated its own medium-term commitment to shareholder value through a 30% net-income return target for 2024-2026, a 2026 treasury buyback and retirement plan, and 2027-2029 goals of a shareholder return ratio above 45% and a price-to-book ratio of 1x or higher.

The next quarterly report (statutory deadline November 2026), developments in Middle East conditions, and progress on the Canadian project's final investment decision stand out as key variables for gauging the earnings trajectory ahead.
