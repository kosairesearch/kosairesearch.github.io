Report overview

Loan curbs push KakaoBank toward fee income as profits hold up

With household-loan curbs capping loan growth, KakaoBank is widening its fee, corporate-lending and overseas businesses, and net income attributable to controlling shareholders rose for three straight years through 2025.

02

Business structure

KakaoBank is a mobile-based internet-only bank whose business rests on household lending and deposits, extended into platform-style financial services.

Deposit balances reached KRW 71.8 trillion at the end of the third quarter of 2026, up 9.3% year on year, while loan balances were KRW 48.6 trillion, up 7% year on year. The company plans to lift non-interest income to 40% of operating revenue over the medium term, moving away from an interest-driven structure.

In non-interest businesses, advertising platform revenue grew 49% year on year in the third quarter of 2026, and check card and payment services kept growing. Regulatory curbs on household lending, however, have constrained the growth of its loan-comparison service.

On products, the bank is building a balance-payment mortgage launched in September into a new pillar of corporate finance, and it plans to roll out customized check cards and co-branded credit cards in the second half of 2026.

It is also entering retirement pensions and expanding into asset management through an AI-driven investment tab. In corporate lending, it started with joint loans with a regional bank that are linked to guarantee institutions, and it targets launching corporate banking services in the first half of 2028.

Overseas, Indonesia's Superbank became the largest digital bank in the country by market capitalization, the Thai joint venture BankX is preparing to start its virtual bank, and in Mongolia the bank is pursuing a stake in MBank and joint development of an alternative credit scoring model.

The customer base was about 27 million as of April 2026, and the company said it will use this data together with a financial large language model to build its AI services.

04

Earnings analysis

Based on the filed annual figures, revenue rose from KRW 1.61 trillion in 2022 to KRW 3.09 trillion in 2025, and it grew for three straight years from 2023 through 2025. Growth slowed from 55.3% in 2023 to 18.1% in 2024 and 4.8% in 2025.

Operating profit rose from KRW 353.2 billion in 2022 to KRW 478.5 billion in 2023, up 35.5% year on year, to KRW 606.9 billion in 2024, up 26.8%, and to KRW 649.4 billion in 2025, up 7.0%.

Over the same period the operating margin moved from 22.0% to 19.2%, 20.6% and 21.0%, falling in 2023, the year revenue surged, and recovering in 2025.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders rose from KRW 263.1 billion in 2022 to KRW 480.3 billion in 2025, and the 9.1% growth in 2025 exceeded the 7.0% growth in operating profit.

In the filed table, net profit including non-controlling interests shows the same amounts as net income attributable to controlling shareholders for each year.

At the end of 2025, total liabilities were KRW 69.7 trillion and total equity was KRW 6.75 trillion, and the debt ratio climbed from 591.4% in 2022 to 1,032.0% in 2025.

Operating cash flow was KRW 1.88 trillion in 2022, KRW 2.89 trillion in 2023, KRW 841.4 billion in 2024 and KRW 6.01 trillion in 2025, a wide swing across years.

Quarterly revenue bottomed at KRW 759 billion in the fourth quarter of 2025 and then rose to KRW 819.3 billion, KRW 829 billion and KRW 880.9 billion in the first three quarters of 2026, three consecutive quarterly increases.

Operating profit was KRW 151.1 billion in the third quarter of 2025, KRW 145.1 billion in the fourth quarter, KRW 157.6 billion in the first quarter of 2026, KRW 194 billion in the second quarter and KRW 191.8 billion in the third quarter, holding near the same level after the second quarter.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders jumped 78.0% quarter on quarter to KRW 187.3 billion in the first quarter of 2026, and a brokerage report attributed this to steady interest and fee gains plus a one-off valuation gain booked as non-operating income from the Superbank accounting change.

Excluding that effect, quarterly profit returned to KRW 140.8 billion in the second quarter and KRW 141.7 billion in the third quarter. On costs, selling and administrative expenses rose 21% year on year in the third quarter of 2026, driven by data center costs and one-time personnel expenses.

05

Industry analysis

Growth at internet-only banks is constrained by a continued emphasis on managing household lending, and the three internet-only banks are reworking their 2026 growth strategies around common regulation.

KakaoBank has chosen to spread that constraint across overseas and non-interest businesses, with a won stablecoin, a capital-company acquisition and entry into cards and securities all under discussion.

On rates, the company said its net interest margin reached 2.15% in the third quarter of 2026, up 34 basis points year on year, and Kyobo Securities analyzed that higher lending yields lifted the margin despite higher funding costs.

Across the market, management said total deposits at banks were broadly flat over the same period, a contrast with KakaoBank's deposit growth. The margin is expected to pause in the fourth quarter because retirement pension inflows concentrate in that season.

In non-interest competition, platform revenue depends mainly on loan comparison and advertising, and while regulation limits loan comparison, advertising platform revenue grew sharply.

Small business loan balances passed KRW 4 trillion in the third quarter of 2026, up more than 40% year on year, becoming an alternative source of loan growth.

Among the three internet-only banks, strategies diverge: Kbank emphasizes platform and small-business lending, while KakaoBank is pushing harder into overseas expansion.

06

Outlook

The company maintained its view that the full-year 2026 net interest margin will improve by at least 10 basis points year on year. Its fee and platform revenue growth outlook was cut from 20% to 14-15%, and it said it expects further growth in 2027.

Among new products, the balance-payment mortgage launched in September 2026 is being developed into a new loan growth driver. An AI spending analysis service is scheduled for December 2026, and AI Goal Savings is planned for launch by the end of 2026.

On capital, the bank signed a share purchase agreement in June 2026 for 100% of Marston Capital and reaffirmed its plan to pursue additional capital-company acquisitions within the year. The company says an improved credit rating after the acquisition would lower funding costs and support profitability.

Technology spending is planned at about KRW 270 billion through 2028, covering AI and AX, and with data centers coming fully online, computing operating expenses and depreciation were expected to rise more than 40% in 2026.

The company expects its cost-to-income ratio to settle lower from 2028, once investment stabilizes. Its medium-term targets include raising corporate loans to 18% of total loans by 2030 and launching corporate banking in the first half of 2028. On shareholder returns, the company said it plans to raise the payout ratio on 2026 fiscal-year profit to 50%.

08

Bull factors

Deposit base and customer growth

Deposit balances passed KRW 70 trillion for the first time at the end of the third quarter of 2026, and management said this was notable because total bank deposits were broadly flat over the same period.

The customer base is about 27 million, and Kyobo Securities cited new products driving customer growth and rising penetration by age group as positives. A larger deposit and customer base also widens room for cross-selling non-interest products.

Corporate lending and loan diversification

CFO Kwon Tae-hoon described the balance-payment loan market as a stable real-demand market with annual new originations above KRW 18 trillion, and said the mortgage product launched in September is performing better than expected.

The bank plans to expand into interim-payment loans in 2027, and it is entering corporate lending through joint loans with a regional bank. The target of raising corporate loans to 18% of total loans by 2030 aims to change the structure of loan growth.

Overseas footholds showing results

Indonesia's Superbank has become the largest digital bank in the country by market capitalization, and the Thai joint venture BankX is set to begin virtual bank operations in the first half of next year.

In Mongolia, the bank signed a memorandum with the country's largest business group and is pursuing a strategic stake in the digital bank MBank along with joint development of an alternative credit scoring model. The company favors technology exports with local partners over taking a controlling stake directly.

Bear factors

Household-loan curbs cap loan growth

Loan balances at the end of the third quarter of 2026 were KRW 48.6 trillion, up 7% year on year, a pace that remains limited. The company cut its 2026 fee and platform revenue growth outlook from 20% to 14-15%, reflecting regulatory limits on its loan-comparison service. Hanwha Investment & Securities assessed in a May 2026 report that growth momentum was slowing.

Cost burden from heavier investment

Selling and administrative expenses rose 21% year on year in the third quarter of 2026 on data center costs and one-time personnel expenses. The company expects computing operating expenses and depreciation to rise more than 40% in 2026 and plans about KRW 270 billion in technology investment through 2028.

The projection that the cost-to-income ratio will settle lower after 2028 is the company's own expectation, and until then cost burden could hold back profit growth.

Fourth-quarter margin pause and one-off gains

The company said the net interest margin would pause in the fourth quarter because retirement pension inflows concentrate in that season.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 187.3 billion in the first quarter of 2026 included a one-off Superbank valuation gain, so that quarter's jump is hard to read as a recurring trend. Second- and third-quarter figures were KRW 140.8 billion and KRW 141.7 billion.

10

Risk factors

Regulatory risk

If household-lending controls persist, both loan growth and loan-comparison revenue stay constrained. The company has said that because the major-shareholder eligibility issue is not fully resolved, the pace of new M&A and entry into cards or securities depends on the court outcome. Unless the regulatory direction changes, the limits on loan-led growth will remain.

Rate and margin volatility

Kyobo Securities cited external variables such as market interest rates as the reason for its target-price cut. The company still expects the full-year 2026 margin to improve by at least 10 basis points, but it flagged a seasonal pause in the fourth quarter.

A change in rate direction would alter the gap between lending yields and funding costs and directly affect interest income.

Execution risk in new businesses and overseas

The won stablecoin business depends on legislation and on obtaining a license after the law is enacted, so it is tied to the legislative timetable. The company expects an acquired capital company's improved credit rating to lower funding costs, but that benefit will take time to show up in results.

Overseas, the company relies heavily on local partners, so partner execution and local regulation will determine outcomes.

Overall view

KakaoBank's filed results show steady growth in revenue, operating profit and net income attributable to controlling shareholders from 2022 through 2025. Growth rates, however, slowed after 2023, and the first quarter of 2026 was lifted by a one-off valuation gain that should be considered alongside the trend.

With loan growth capped by household-lending curbs, the company is widening its growth pillars across platform fees, corporate lending, a capital-company acquisition and overseas footholds. Bullish factors include deposits passing KRW 70 trillion, growth in non-interest businesses and the value of overseas stakes.

Bearish factors include lending curbs, a cut in the fee and platform outlook, rising costs and a seasonal pause in the fourth-quarter margin. Among brokers, Kyobo Securities maintained a Buy rating in its November 2026 report while lowering its target, and it attributed the change to market-rate and beta assumptions.

The next checkpoints are the AI spending analysis launch in December 2026, progress on the capital-company acquisition within 2026, the fourth-quarter 2026 results in early 2027, and the 2026 annual business report in late March 2027.
