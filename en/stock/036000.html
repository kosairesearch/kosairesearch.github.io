02

Business structure

YeaRimDang Publishing is a children's book publisher whose flagship product is the Why? series of study comics. According to the company's earnings commentary, the Why? series had sold more than 81.87 million copies as of the end of March 2026.

The company is also extending into early-childhood books through its Smart Bear brand.

In a March 2024 press report, the construction-materials PHC pile business accounted for about 18% of consolidated revenue, while the copyright business was reported at 3% and the content business, including e-books and animation, at 1%. Aviation was the dominant external driver in earlier years.

From 2021 the airline was excluded from consolidation, leaving only the results of its holding company in the group accounts. In 2025 the entire stake in that holding company was sold, bringing KRW 212.4 billion into the company.

The business mix shown for 2024 may have changed since then, so it should be checked against the latest annual report's segment information. Competition is shifting from print toward digital content in children's learning material, and a listed children's content peer in the same sector is The Pinkfong Company.

04

Earnings analysis

In the annual results from regular filings, revenue was KRW 26.4 billion in 2022, KRW 20.5 billion in 2023 (down 22.1% year on year), KRW 17 billion in 2024 (down 17.2%) and KRW 10.3 billion in 2025 (down 39.7%). Within 2022 to 2025, 2022 was the largest year and 2025 the smallest.

Operating profit of KRW 291 million in 2022 turned into an operating loss of KRW 2.75 billion in 2023, widened to KRW 4.9 billion in 2024 and narrowed to KRW 1.42 billion in 2025.

The operating margin moved from 1.1% in 2022 to -13.4% in 2023, -28.8% in 2024 and -13.8% in 2025, showing losses alongside a shrinking revenue base.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders swung from a loss of KRW 8.9 billion in 2022 to KRW 9.01 billion in 2023, a loss of KRW 8.72 billion in 2024 and KRW 134.3 billion in 2025.

Press reports attribute the 2025 profit mainly to the one-time gain from the stake sale, which sits far apart from the operating loss of KRW 1.42 billion in the same year.

Net income including non-controlling interests was KRW 126.2 billion in 2025, a different figure, and the gap reflects the share attributable to non-controlling interests.

Equity at the end of 2025 was KRW 255.8 billion, all attributable to controlling shareholders, with total liabilities of KRW 44.9 billion, and the debt ratio rose from 10.6% in 2024 to 17.5% in 2025.

Operating cash flow was negative at KRW 988 million in 2022 and positive in each later year, at KRW 2.27 billion in 2023, KRW 920 million in 2024 and KRW 2.38 billion in 2025.

In the quarterly regular filings, revenue was KRW 2.52 billion in the third quarter of 2025, KRW 2.43 billion in the fourth quarter (down 3.6% quarter on quarter), KRW 3.18 billion in the first quarter of 2026 (up 30.8%) and KRW 2.4 billion in the second quarter (down 24.6%).

Operating results were an operating loss of KRW 640 million in the third quarter of 2025, an operating loss of KRW 372 million in the fourth quarter, an operating profit of KRW 140 million in the first quarter of 2026 and an operating loss of KRW 263 million in the second quarter.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 7.61 billion in the fourth quarter of 2025, up 615.9% on the prior quarter, a net loss of KRW 881 million in the first quarter of 2026 and KRW 9.77 billion in the second quarter.

Within the last four quarters, the cumulative KRW 17.6 billion is concentrated in the fourth quarter of 2025 and the second quarter of 2026.

In the first quarter of 2026 operating profit was positive while the net result attributable to controlling shareholders was a loss, so operating and net results often moved in different directions.

A press report on preliminary third-quarter 2026 figures (Vegan News) cited revenue of KRW 7.3 billion and an operating loss of KRW 3.2 billion on a consolidated basis, and that basis should be matched against the regular filings before the two series are compared.

05

Industry analysis

The children's publishing industry is widely viewed as being in a structural downturn.

The publishing segment faces an unfavorable environment from a shrinking school-age population and a rising share of digital content, and profitability is under pressure from higher royalty payments and rising content production costs.

Falling births, which shrink the child population, and the contraction of the print book market are also cited as constraints on core growth. Clear indicators of a cyclical turn are limited, and whether the 2026 swing to quarterly operating profit is a trend requires more quarters of data.

On competitive position, the company credits sales momentum of the Why? series and rising market share for its Smart Bear early-childhood brand for improvement in its first-quarter 2026 results. The same commentary highlights a high operating margin sustained through direct marketing.

By contrast, much of the 2025 improvement came from the stake sale rather than from the core business. The Pinkfong Company, a listed children's content peer in the same sector, overlaps in early-childhood content.

The central industry question is whether expanding early-childhood series can support core revenue while the print publishing market is under pressure.

06

Outlook

Shareholder-return plans were reported in June 2026 as the resumption of dividends after twelve years. The company declared an interim dividend, set the shareholder register closing date at June 30, 2026, and said the amount and schedule would be disclosed after a board resolution.

The company has said it will set an appropriate payout level while considering investment for sustained growth and the operating environment. Full sale of its holding-company stake in 2025 brought KRW 212.4 billion into the company, and investment assets have since grown.

In September 2025 the company committed KRW 20.3 billion to a fund managed by Purun Investment, and on May 21, 2026 it lent KRW 16 billion to Sejong D&P at an annual interest rate of 7.5%. Press coverage quoted the company saying new businesses are under review with nothing decided.

Some investment banking sources expect investment expansion to take priority over dividends. Because few verified guidance figures are available, no numerical outlook is given.

The next checkpoints are the operating profit trend in the 2026 third-quarter quarterly report, the content of the dividend decision disclosure, and whether the investment assets are recovered.

Bull factors

Scale of net assets and cash

Equity at the end of 2025 was KRW 255.8 billion, against total liabilities of KRW 44.9 billion. Press reports put standalone cash and cash equivalents at KRW 220.6 billion, roughly five times the prior-year level. Cash from the stake sale gives the company a base for dividends and investment.

Quarterly operating profit turned positive

Operating profit of KRW 140 million in the first quarter of 2026 turned positive from the prior quarter. The company's commentary cites sales momentum of the Why? series, rising share for Smart Bear, and a high margin sustained through direct marketing.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders in the fourth quarter of 2025 was KRW 7.61 billion, up 615.9% on the prior quarter.

Dividend resumption after twelve years

A June 2026 report said the company had begun the process to resume dividends for the first time in twelve years. It set an interim dividend with a record date of June 30, 2026, and said the amount would be disclosed after a board resolution.

Standalone retained earnings rose to KRW 149.2 billion at the end of 2025, widening dividend capacity according to the report.

Bear factors

Three straight years of revenue decline and operating losses

Revenue declined from KRW 26.4 billion in 2022 to KRW 10.3 billion in 2025, with three straight annual declines from 2023. Operating results were a loss in each year from 2023 to 2025, and the 2025 operating margin was -13.8%. The combination of shrinking revenue and operating losses is the core business's largest weakness.

One-time nature of the 2025 net profit

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 134.3 billion in 2025 reflects mainly the one-time effect of the stake sale. The operating loss in the same year was KRW 1.42 billion, and excluding the gain, the core business's direction remains loss-making.

The second quarter of 2026 returned to an operating loss of KRW 263 million, so quarterly profitability did not persist.

Publishing downturn and lack of a new growth engine

The publishing segment faces an unfavorable environment from a shrinking school-age population and a growing share of digital content. Press coverage said publishing is becoming structurally hard to make profitable and that the company has not yet secured a growth engine to replace the Why? series. As of the report, new businesses were described as still under review.

Risk factors

Earnings volatility

Quarterly results have swung from an operating profit in the first quarter of 2026 to an operating loss in the second, and net income attributable to controlling shareholders moved from a net loss of KRW 881 million to KRW 9.77 billion.

Most of the four-quarter total of KRW 17.6 billion comes from two quarters, so a few items can dominate results. The reason quarterly net and operating results diverged needs to be checked in the footnotes.

Capital allocation and governance

Press coverage says the use of the company's cash between dividends and new businesses has not been settled. The KRW 20.3 billion fund commitment from September 2025 and the KRW 16 billion loan at 7.5% from May 2026 carry recovery risk.

The 2025 stake sale included holdings of related parties, so the decisions of major shareholders weigh heavily on capital allocation.

Differences in data basis

A preliminary press report on third-quarter 2026 figures cited revenue of KRW 7.3 billion and an operating loss of KRW 3.2 billion on a consolidated basis, which must be matched against the regular filings. These are preliminary figures and may differ from the filed report. A different basis would change the quarterly trend, so the figures should be checked against the DART original.

Overall view

The 2025 net profit attributable to controlling shareholders at YeaRimDang Publishing reflects mainly a one-time gain from the stake sale, so it should be read apart from core operating results.

On a regular-filing basis, revenue fell from KRW 26.4 billion in 2022 to KRW 10.3 billion in 2025, a three-year decline, and operating results were a loss in each year from 2023 to 2025.

Quarterly operating results in 2026 have alternated between a first-quarter profit and a second-quarter loss, so no direction is yet established. On the balance sheet, equity of KRW 255.8 billion and total liabilities of KRW 44.9 billion at the end of 2025 show a broad asset base.

Meanwhile, the core business has not yet converted the growth of the Why? series and Smart Bear into operating profit amid structural pressure on publishing and a shrinking school-age population.

Resuming dividends and deploying investment capital represent two directions of capital allocation, and which one gets priority is the point to watch.

The next checkpoints are the DART figures in the 2026 third-quarter report, annual operating results in the 2026 annual report, and whether first-quarter 2027 operating profit repeats.
