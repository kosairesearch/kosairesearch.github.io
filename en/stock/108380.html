02

Business structure

Daeyang Electric was founded in 1988 as a maker of components for ships and produces marine lighting and electronic systems, among other products. Its 2025 revenue mix was lighting 41.5%, electronic systems 23%, electrical systems 17.2% and others 18.3%.

The lighting business centers on marine lighting, so its results track shipbuilders' orders and construction schedules. According to LS Securities, new marine lighting orders rose from KRW 63 billion in the first nine months of 2024 to KRW 67.6 billion in the first nine months of 2025.

Switchboards and defense-related work continue to show steady order flow, supporting the revenue base. In pressure sensors, vehicle ESC sensors used in braking and stability control are the growth engine. LS Securities noted that this segment's revenue grew from KRW 9.1 billion in 2021 to KRW 23.6 billion in 2024.

Its wholly owned battery subsidiary supplies lead-acid batteries for submarines to the Korean Navy, with annual revenue of roughly KRW 16 billion to KRW 21 billion. A related company that distributes marine lighting is held 95.8% by the CEO.

Compared with large electrical equipment makers such as LS ELECTRIC and HD Hyundai Electric, the company is small, and it is widening its niche in marine lighting and sensors.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual revenue rose from KRW 130.4 billion in 2022 to KRW 154.3 billion in 2023 (up 18.4% year on year), KRW 196.3 billion in 2024 (up 27.2%) and KRW 230.1 billion in 2025 (up 17.2%). Revenue has increased for three consecutive years from 2023 through 2025, and 2025 was the largest year in the 2022 to 2025 period.

Operating profit moved from an operating loss of KRW 9.89 billion in 2022 to operating profit of KRW 3.73 billion in 2023, then rose to KRW 15.7 billion in 2024 (up 320.0%) and KRW 27.1 billion in 2025 (up 72.5%), increasing for two consecutive years from 2024 to 2025.

The operating margin went from -7.6% in 2022 to 2.4% in 2023, 8.0% in 2024 and 11.8% in 2025. LS Securities cited a rising share of vehicle sensor sales, which carry higher margins, and a lower burden of fixed-type costs as revenue grew as the drivers of the margin gain.

Profit attributable to controlling shareholders moved from a net loss of KRW 7.5 billion in 2022 to KRW 8.38 billion in 2023, then KRW 19.6 billion in 2024 (up 133.9%) and KRW 24.8 billion in 2025 (up 26.5%), increasing for two consecutive years from 2024 to 2025.

Operating cash flow was KRW 1.56 billion in 2022, KRW 24 billion in 2023, a negative KRW 4.26 billion in 2024 and KRW 15.6 billion in 2025, a wide swing that makes working capital movements worth checking in the next annual report.

The debt ratio went from 19.5% in 2022 to 32.0% in 2023, 25.8% in 2024 and 19.6% in 2025, and at the end of 2025 total equity was KRW 269.2 billion, all attributable to controlling shareholders, against total liabilities of KRW 52.8 billion.

Quarterly revenue went from KRW 61.5 billion in the third quarter of 2025 to KRW 56.6 billion in the fourth quarter (down 8.0% quarter on quarter), KRW 50.9 billion in the first quarter of 2026 (down 10.1%), KRW 70.8 billion in the second quarter (up 39.2%) and KRW 58 billion in the third quarter (down 18.1% quarter on quarter and down 5.8% year on year).

The first-quarter decline was reported as a temporary contraction caused by adjusted project delivery schedules in marine lighting and electronic systems.

Selling and administrative expenses in the same quarter were KRW 3.4 billion, down 38.2% year on year, and no bad-debt provision was booked, against KRW 1.3 billion a year earlier.

Operating profit was KRW 7.74 billion, KRW 7.82 billion, KRW 6.27 billion, KRW 7.56 billion and KRW 6.47 billion across the five quarters from the third quarter of 2025 to the third quarter of 2026, a narrow range compared with the revenue swings.

Profit attributable to controlling shareholders fell to KRW 2.04 billion in the third quarter of 2026, down 73.4% quarter on quarter and 74.7% year on year, a much steeper drop than the 16.4% decline in operating profit, and it totals KRW 23.5 billion over the last four quarters.

05

Industry analysis

The lighting business takes orders in line with shipbuilders' ordering decisions, so the shipbuilding cycle is the largest external variable for Daeyang Electric's results. LS Securities views the upturn in shipbuilding as lifting marine lighting orders and cites it as the basis for external growth.

However, if shipyard delivery schedules shift, quarterly revenue can slip even with the same order backlog, as the first-quarter decline showed. Defense demand arises independently of the shipbuilding cycle, which helps reduce cyclical sensitivity.

The pressure sensor business is tied to automotive parts demand and is affected by vehicle production volumes and the shift toward more electronics in cars.

Large power equipment makers such as LS ELECTRIC, Hyosung Heavy Industries and HD Hyundai Electric are exposed to power infrastructure investment and have different business structures.

Daeyang Electric's switchboard business overlaps with some of their product areas, but the revenue scale gap is large, so a like-for-like comparison is difficult. The key question for the industry is whether defense and switchboard orders move together with automotive parts demand.

By business, shipbuilding orders follow the shipbuilding cycle while automotive sensors follow the electrification trend, so they run on different cycles. Governance and shareholder return, which sit outside the industry picture, need to be considered separately.

Outlook

Among broker forecasts, LS Securities in a January 2026 report projected 2026 consolidated revenue of KRW 244 billion and operating profit of KRW 27.5 billion, expecting record-level results to continue for a second straight year after 2025.

IBK Investment & Securities was reported in the first half of 2026 to have put its 2026 full-year revenue estimate at KRW 251.8 billion, up 9.4% year on year. LS Securities expects vehicle ESC sensor revenue to expand to KRW 47.3 billion in 2026.

In an April 2026 report, LS Securities analyzed that consolidating the related company would add revenue of about KRW 16.7 billion and operating profit of about KRW 3.9 billion. The same report judged that a 100% subsidiary structure was the most likely route.

IBK Investment & Securities analyzed that the company is reportedly considering treasury share cancellation and a larger dividend. The new sensor business is moving from development into mass production, so whether volume revenue materializes is the next thing to confirm.

Since second-quarter revenue recovered to KRW 70.8 billion after the first-quarter schedule adjustment, whether the third-quarter decline is temporary will be judged by fourth-quarter results.

Broker forecasts were made under different timing and assumptions from the reported figures, so they are better read alongside the disclosed results. Whether the related-company consolidation is completed by the end of fiscal 2026, and how intercompany dealings change afterward, are the key variables for the outlook.

Bull factors

Broadening profit recovery

The operating margin moved from -7.6% in 2022 to 11.8% in 2025, reversing direction. Operating profit rose to KRW 15.7 billion in 2024 and KRW 27.1 billion in 2025, increasing for two consecutive years, and operating cash flow was KRW 15.6 billion in 2025. Profit growth after the swing from operating loss to profit is cited as evidence of structural improvement.

Financial capacity and net cash

The debt ratio fell from 32.0% in 2023 to 19.6% in 2025, and total liabilities at the end of 2025 were KRW 52.8 billion. With a light debt load, the company could in principle fund business investment and shareholder returns at the same time.

Restructuring and shareholder return pressure

The company announced in January 2026 a plan to bring a related company into the group as a subsidiary, with implementation targeted by the end of fiscal 2026. Minority shareholder Quad Asset Management has asked for treasury share cancellation and a higher payout ratio.

If consolidation and shareholder returns move forward together, expectations for governance improvement could grow.

09

Bear factors

Quarterly earnings volatility

Third-quarter 2026 revenue was KRW 58 billion, down 18.1% quarter on quarter, and profit attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 2.04 billion, down 74.7% year on year. The last four quarters total KRW 23.5 billion.

Because the decline in profit attributable to controlling shareholders far exceeded the decline in operating profit, items below operating profit need checking in the next annual report.

Dependence on shipbuilding project timing

Marine lighting is the largest unit, at 41.5% of 2025 revenue. In the first quarter of 2026, revenue fell after project delivery schedules were adjusted in marine lighting and electronic systems. A slowdown in shipbuilding or delays in delivery could again cause quarterly revenue to swing.

Related-party dealings and governance

Quad Asset Management pointed to the risk of profit leakage from intercompany dealings with related companies and to doubts over audit effectiveness. The CEO holds 95.8% of the related company. Until consolidation is complete, the intercompany structure remains, and it is cited as a weakness.

Risk factors

Project delivery timing

Lighting and switchboard revenue is recognized at delivery, so a delay can reduce quarterly revenue even with the same order backlog, as the first quarter of 2026 showed. Larger swings in quarterly revenue can also move the operating margin from quarter to quarter. Annual trends have improved, but delivery management at the quarterly level is the next thing to watch.

Sensor growth assumptions

LS Securities noted that vehicle ESC sensor revenue grew from KRW 9.1 billion in 2021 to KRW 23.6 billion in 2024. The 2026 revenue outlook is a broker estimate and could shift with automotive parts demand and customer order changes. As the sensor business grows in weight, margin improvement becomes more dependent on it.

Governance and intercompany dealings

The deadline for consolidating the related company is the end of fiscal 2026. If the consolidation is delayed or its structure changes, the cleanup of intercompany dealings and the shareholder return plan could slip together.

Quad Asset Management has asked for treasury share cancellation and a higher payout ratio, and whether the company acts on this must be checked in its periodic reports and at the shareholder meeting.

Overall view

Daeyang Electric moved from an operating loss in 2022 to operating profit in 2025, and revenue, operating profit and profit attributable to controlling shareholders all rose in both 2024 and 2025.

However, quarterly revenue in 2026 has been uneven, with a first-quarter decline, a second-quarter rebound and a third-quarter decline, and the drop in profit attributable to controlling shareholders in the third quarter was far steeper than the drop in operating profit.

The bullish factors are the improved margin, a low debt ratio with net cash, and continuing pressure for shareholder returns. The bearish factors are quarterly earnings volatility, dependence on shipbuilding delivery schedules, and concerns over related-party dealings and governance.

Weighing both sides equally, the issues reflected in the share price can be read as earnings sustainability and governance.

The next points to confirm are the preliminary fourth-quarter 2026 results, the operating non-operating items and dividends in the 2026 annual report, and whether the related-company consolidation is completed.
