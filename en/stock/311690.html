02

Business structure

CJ Bioscience has been a subsidiary of CJ CheilJedang since its acquisition in 2021, and it is building two businesses around microbiome technology: drug development and wellness.

Its drug pipeline comprises CJRB-101, a candidate for lung and head-and-neck cancer in combination with Keytruda, CJRB-201, a candidate for inflammatory bowel disease (IBD), and CJRB-302, a candidate for Parkinson's disease.

CJRB-201 is built on beneficial bacteria found naturally in the gut of healthy people, and anti-inflammatory data in a chronic colitis mouse model were presented at a conference in June 2026.

The core of the wellness side is MACPLAN, a personalized nutrition brand based on gut-microbiota-accessible carbohydrates, designed with an in-house gut simulation system and AI.

A human study of four MAC formulations in 210 adults and an AI model predicting product responders from gut microbial patterns were presented as the evidence base.

The gut microbiome testing service Gut Inside is expanding through agreements with medical institutions including Korean medicine hospitals and nursing hospitals. Customers are pharmaceutical companies that may license drug candidates in the drug segment, and consumers and medical institutions in the wellness segment.

In June 2024 the company announced a vision to achieve three technology export deals by 2026. No segment revenue breakdown is available from the public data reviewed, and with revenue this small the weight of the business still sits in research and development.

04

Earnings analysis

On a reported basis, revenue rose from KRW 4.07 billion in 2022 to KRW 5.58 billion in 2023, up 36.8% year on year, which was the largest year of 2022 to 2025. It fell to KRW 3.47 billion in 2024, down 37.8% year on year and the smallest year, and recovered slightly to KRW 3.69 billion in 2025, up 6.5% year on year.

Operating losses were KRW 33.2 billion in 2022, KRW 32.1 billion in 2023, KRW 34.2 billion in 2024 and KRW 24.8 billion in 2025, which is four consecutive years of losses. The loss narrowed in 2023, widened in 2024 when revenue fell, and narrowed again in 2025.

Operating margins were -815.6% in 2022, -575.0% in 2023, -987.8% in 2024 and -671.2% in 2025, so the ratio was most negative in the year revenue was smallest.

Net loss attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 34.9 billion in 2022, KRW 22.9 billion in 2023, KRW 32.8 billion in 2024 and KRW 24 billion in 2025, and net loss including non-controlling interests was reported at the same yearly amounts.

Operating cash flow was negative in every year, at -KRW 21 billion in 2022, -KRW 24.9 billion in 2023, -KRW 27.8 billion in 2024 and -KRW 23.4 billion in 2025. The debt-to-equity ratio moved from 84.0% in 2022 to 27.5% in 2023, 28.1% in 2024 and 34.9% in 2025.

Total equity at the end of 2025 was KRW 53.6 billion, all attributable to controlling shareholders, against total liabilities of KRW 18.7 billion.

By quarter, revenue jumped to KRW 1.26 billion in the fourth quarter of 2025, up 70.5% from the prior quarter, then fell to KRW 783 million in the first quarter of 2026, rose to KRW 898 million in the second quarter and was KRW 863 million in the third quarter.

The operating loss widened from KRW 6.08 billion in the third quarter of 2025 to KRW 6.4 billion in the second quarter of 2026 before narrowing to KRW 4.93 billion in the third quarter.

Net loss attributable to controlling shareholders over the last four quarters, from the fourth quarter of 2025 to the third quarter of 2026, totaled KRW 21.3 billion.

A 2024 press report put ordinary R&D spending at KRW 18.9 billion in 2022 and KRW 22.5 billion in 2023, with KRW 15.6 billion spent through the third quarter of 2024, so a large share of the losses is tied to pipeline research.

05

Industry analysis

The microbiome field is one where drug developers and food and health-supplement companies are moving in parallel, as efforts grow to use gut microbes as drug targets or nutrition solutions.

On the drug side, inflammatory bowel disease is widely linked to pathological changes in gut microbes, which is why CJRB-201 draws attention. The data currently public are at the mouse-model and preclinical stage.

On the wellness side, personalized nutrition services that combine diet and gut microbiome data are being discussed as a new market, and the company is building evidence for MACPLAN through academic channels such as the Korean Society of Food Science and Nutrition.

At a meeting in late October 2026, the company reported that a Korea-specific diet index showed clinical-marker associations exceeding existing diet indices.

Unlike large Korean biotech companies that earn revenue from manufacturing or commercial products, CJ Bioscience is an early-stage company with a thin revenue base. Among listed companies in the same sector, its profile is more concentrated in the pipeline-validation stage.

Gut microbiome testing depends on distribution through hospitals and health checkup channels, so expansion of such agreements can shape the pace of the business. Competitors include microbiome drug developers in Korea and abroad as well as food companies that have entered the gut-health market.

Revenue reached KRW 1.26 billion in the fourth quarter of 2025 and KRW 863 million in the third quarter of 2026, which is higher than a year earlier. Whether the narrowing came from revenue growth or cost control needs to be settled from line-item breakdowns.

Lower leverage on the balance sheet

The debt-to-equity ratio fell from 84.0% in 2022 to 34.9% in 2025. Total liabilities were KRW 18.7 billion and total equity was KRW 53.6 billion at the end of 2025, all of it attributable to controlling shareholders. Operating cash outflows continued every year, however, so equity is being consumed as losses accumulate.

Clearer timing for wellness commercialization

In June 2026 the company said it planned to commercialize and launch a customized MAC solution in the second half of 2026. Supporting data include a human study in 210 adults and an AI model predicting product responders with an ROC-AUC of 0.85.

At a late-October 2026 academic meeting, it added evidence on diet and the microbiome through poster and oral presentations. Whether wellness revenue appears in quarterly results will become clear after the launch.

Quarterly losses have run for five consecutive quarters since the third quarter of 2025, so the losses have not stopped. The timing of a return to profit is hard to gauge from the current data.

Thin revenue base

Revenue of KRW 3.69 billion in 2025 is very small compared with the KRW 24.8 billion operating loss in the same year. Third-quarter 2026 revenue of KRW 863 million fell 4.0% from the prior quarter. In the second quarter of 2026, the operating loss widened to KRW 6.4 billion even with revenue of KRW 898 million. How much technology fee or wellness revenue accumulates will determine whether the revenue base strengthens.

Clinical and technology

Validation data for the pipeline are currently centered on mouse models and preclinical studies.
