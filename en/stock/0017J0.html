02

Business structure

SEMI Total Solution is an automated material handling system (AMHS) specialist founded in 2014. Its core products are a front-end clean conveyor system and a nitrogen purge system. At an April 9, 2026 press briefing, the CEO said most of current revenue comes from the clean conveyor business.

The company claims it is the only Korean firm to have commercialized a clean conveyor usable in front-end fab environments. Its S-Plate purge product is an add-on that does not require modifying existing tools, and the company says it cuts installation time by about 75% versus competitors.

As of a March 2026 interview, overseas sales were about 80% of revenue and domestic about 20%, and the company has worked with memory customers including SK hynix and Chinese NAND fabs.

Overseas, it operates bases in Wuxi and Wuhan in China and Tokyo in Japan, and its two subsidiaries are the Chinese and Japanese local entities. Its next-generation product is a ceiling-mounted autonomous mobile robot (AMR), which the company positions to replace the existing OHT market using swarm-control software. The company characterizes the front-end logistics segment as a market led by foreign equipment.

03

Recent trends

According to the DART filing, cumulative first-half 2026 revenue was KRW 2.49 billion, down 79.6% from a year earlier. Over the same period the company swung to an operating loss of KRW 414 million and to a net loss of KRW 17.5 billion.

Press reports broke out first-quarter revenue of KRW 12.7 billion and operating profit of KRW 4.5 billion, then second-quarter revenue of KRW 2.5 billion and an operating loss of about KRW 0.4 billion. That coverage presented first-half consolidated revenue of KRW 15.2 billion and operating profit of KRW 4.0 billion.

Those figures differ from the filing's presentation, so readers should check the original report. The company explained that major orders cluster at year-end and in early-year, and the second quarter is the design and build stage with lower revenue recognition.

Net results include a one-off listing expense of KRW 16.9 billion arising from the SPAC merger, which the company describes as an expense with no cash outflow. In first-half 2026, one domestic customer accounted for KRW 12.7 billion, or 83.5% of sales.

On the June 10, 2026 listing day the shares fell 29.90% from the KRW 7,760 reference price to KRW 5,440, and on October 23 they traded 10.97% below the prior close of KRW 3,055. The October 27 close was KRW 2,580, down 7.03% on the day, with a market cap of KRW 78.5 billion and turnover of KRW 1.6 billion.

04

Outlook

The company's stated 2026 revenue target is KRW 30.0 billion. Based on the press-reported first-half revenue of KRW 15.2 billion, analysis noted that hitting the target requires an additional KRW 14.8 billion of revenue in the second half.

On August 12, 2026 it disclosed a clean conveyor supply contract of about KRW 7.28 billion. That contract is an intra-group transaction under which the total contract value won by the Wuxi subsidiary is recognized as consolidated revenue, and its term runs through March 31, 2027.

On February 11, 2026 the company announced a KRW 11.5 billion contract with a domestic chipmaker. The nitrogen purge system is undergoing qualification tests at domestic and overseas fabs, and the company says that in Japan approval procedures must be completed before volume adoption can follow.

At the time of the merger an accounting firm estimated purge-system revenue of about KRW 6.4 billion, KRW 13.7 billion and KRW 24.8 billion for 2026 through 2028, but these were merger-valuation estimates and actual recognition depends on qualification outcomes.

05

Bull factors

Differentiated technology and order base

The company claims to be the only Korean firm to have commercialized a front-end clean conveyor. At the end of 2025, its order backlog of KRW 27.3 billion exceeded 2025 revenue. It also signed an additional KRW 11.5 billion contract in February 2026. Its 2025 operating margin was about 28%.

Debt-free balance sheet

The company says it has stayed debt-free since its founding. As of 2025, its debt ratio was 38.4% and its current ratio 314.8%. It reported about KRW 58.0 billion of cash-equivalent financial assets at the end of June 2026. That cash base funds its R&D spending.

Broader product line and markets

The company says its nitrogen purge system is in technical validation with Japanese semiconductor customers. It is developing stocker purge and a ceiling-mounted AMR to build a lineup spanning transport and storage. It plans to extend the technology to adjacent industries such as secondary batteries and biotech. If realized, that could reduce dependence on semiconductor capital spending.

06

Bear factors

Quarterly earnings volatility

In the filing, cumulative first-half revenue fell 79.6% year on year and the company moved to an operating loss. Because equipment is supplied project by project, revenue-recognition timing can swing quarterly sales sharply. The company says orders did not decline, but outsiders can hardly verify recognition timing. Any slippage in second-half recognition would weigh on the annual target.

Customer and intra-group concentration

One domestic customer accounted for 83.5% of first-half 2026 sales. In 2025 an overseas customer made up 55.9% of sales, so the lead customer has shifted. The August supply contract is with the Wuxi subsidiary and is outsourced for production. End-customer order timing and acceptance inspections directly affect results.

Commercialization uncertainty in new products

The nitrogen purge system is still in qualification testing, and the company itself describes Japan's approval process as demanding. The ceiling-mounted AMR is in development, so its revenue contribution timing is not fixed.

The merger-time purge revenue estimate also relied on counting only 35% of order value for some contracts. Disappointing qualification results would change the growth scenario.

07

Risk factors

Macro

Demand for semiconductor equipment depends on chipmakers' capital spending plans. The company recorded 2023 consolidated revenue of KRW 9.3 billion and an operating loss of KRW 6.5 billion, showing exposure to industry downturns.

With meaningful supply to Chinese fabs, regional investment conditions and trade restrictions are variables. Exchange rates also affect the won value of yuan-denominated contracts.

Shares listed through SPAC mergers saw large supply-demand swings right after listing. The stock hit the daily limit-down on its June 10, 2026 first day and fell sharply again the next day. With a market cap of KRW 78.5 billion and turnover of KRW 1.6 billion on October 27, liquidity is thin. The largest shareholder, CEO Min Nam-hong (52.94%), has pledged a 30-month lock-up from the listing date.

Project schedules depend on customers' fab construction and equipment move-in timing. The August contract is outsourced for production, with payments split across move-in and inspection stages. The company itself disclosed that payment dates may shift if inspection dates change.

Reported figures differ across consolidated, separate and quarterly bases, so numbers can diverge between media reports.

08

Overall view

SEMI Total Solution is a small equipment maker with clean conveyor and nitrogen purge technology in the narrow field of front-end logistics automation. Its filed cumulative first-half 2026 results show lower revenue and a swing to losses.

The company attributes this to differences in project revenue-recognition timing and says the net loss includes a non-cash, one-off listing expense. Bulls point to the debt-free structure, about KRW 58.0 billion of cash-equivalent financial assets, and the KRW 27.3 billion backlog at the end of 2025.

Bears point to customer concentration, the share of intra-group transactions, uncertainty over commercializing new products, and thin liquidity. Reported first-half figures differ across media, so the original filings should be checked. Second-half revenue recognition and nitrogen purge qualification results are the key items to monitor.
