02

Business structure

POSCO Steeleon is a steel manufacturer in the POSCO group, listed on the KOSPI market and classified under primary steel manufacturing. The company listed on the KOSPI on August 16, 2002.

Its consolidated total assets stood at KRW 507.3 billion at the end of 2025, and shareholders approved the 38th-term financial statements and a cash dividend at the March 26, 2026 general meeting.

The business centers on manufacturing and processing steel products such as coated steel, and raw steel material prices and energy costs feed directly into unit costs. In a 2023 credit rating report, higher electricity tariffs were cited as a burden on fuel costs during the earnings slowdown.

In 2022, a sharp drop in shipment volume raised the fixed-cost burden and operating profit fell 73.3%, which shows how strongly volume changes flow through to profitability. On the competitive side, the same credit report noted that capacity additions by rival coated steel producers added supply pressure.

Listed peers in the steel and metals sector include Hyundai Steel Company, Dongkuk Steel Mill and SeAH Steel, but the degree of product overlap cannot be separated from the company's disclosures alone.

Segment revenue shares are not presented as figures in the disclosures, so the structure is described by product and cost drivers.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual revenue moved from KRW 1.2 trillion in 2022 to KRW 1.16 trillion in 2023, a 3.6% decline, rebounded to KRW 1.21 trillion in 2024, up 4.4%, and then fell to KRW 1.13 trillion in 2025, down 7.0%. Among 2022 to 2025, 2024 was the largest year and 2025 the smallest.

Operating profit was KRW 38.2 billion in 2022, KRW 30.6 billion in 2023 (down 19.9%), KRW 51.9 billion in 2024 (up 69.5%) and KRW 24.5 billion in 2025 (down 52.9%). The operating margin was 3.2% in 2022, 2.6% in 2023, 4.3% in 2024 and 2.2% in 2025, with 2025 the lowest of the four years.

The 2024 recovery came from a 4.3% margin on KRW 1.21 trillion in revenue, while in 2025 lower revenue and a 2.2% margin deepened the decline in operating profit.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 22.7 billion in 2022, KRW 24.8 billion in 2023 (up 9.0%), KRW 34.3 billion in 2024 (up 38.3%) and KRW 17 billion in 2025 (down 50.3%).

Net income including non-controlling interests was KRW 18.2 billion in 2025, a different figure that should not be read as the same measure. Operating cash flow was KRW 23.3 billion in 2022, KRW 65 billion in 2023, KRW 37.2 billion in 2024 and KRW 41.5 billion in 2025, so 2025 was higher than 2024.

On a quarterly basis, operating profit fell from KRW 4.96 billion in the third quarter of 2025 to KRW 2.12 billion in the fourth quarter, down 57.3% from the prior quarter, then recovered to KRW 6.29 billion in the first quarter of 2026, up 197.3%, and KRW 9.59 billion in the second quarter, up 52.3%.

The third quarter of 2026 then dropped to KRW 161 million, down 98.3% from the prior quarter, and controlling-shareholder net income turned to a loss of KRW 264 million from a profit of KRW 3.91 billion a year earlier.

Revenue of KRW 285.2 billion was 0.9% higher year on year, so volume held while margins nearly disappeared. The quarterly disclosures do not break out one-off items, so it is hard to split the margin change between cost and price factors using the figures alone.

05

Industry analysis

The company's earnings have tracked steel demand cycles and supply conditions. In 2022, a sharp drop in shipments raised fixed-cost pressure and operating profit fell 73.3%.

In 2023, falling construction activity, rising inflows of imported steel and capacity additions by a rival in coated steel compressed roll margins, and higher electricity tariffs added fuel-cost pressure. The pattern suggests margins tend to fall first when demand softens and supply rises at the same time.

Parent-level trends look different. POSCO Holdings reported that its operating profit rose for a third consecutive quarter in the third quarter of 2026, and its steel subsidiary POSCO posted standalone operating profit of KRW 455 billion, up 66.1% from the prior quarter.

The same company, however, expected fourth-quarter 2026 product output to fall by 920,000 tons from the third quarter because of blast furnace repairs at Gwangyang. Whether the group steel recovery reaches affiliated sales will become clearer after that production adjustment.

On the demand side, construction and appliance-related steel demand is the axis that has shaped this company's shipments, consistent with earlier credit rating commentary.

Among listed steel peers such as Hyundai Steel Company, Dongkuk Steel Mill and SeAH Steel, POSCO Steeleon is smaller with a narrower product mix, which points to greater exposure to pricing power and capacity utilization swings.

Bull factors

Quarterly profit recovery

Controlling-shareholder net income in those two quarters was KRW 6.76 billion and KRW 6.93 billion, well above the KRW 62.28 million of the fourth quarter of 2025. Two consecutive quarters of recovery form the main basis for the bullish reading.

Stronger balance sheet

Operating cash flow rose to KRW 41.5 billion in 2025 from KRW 37.2 billion in 2024. Financial headroom that can cushion weaker earnings is cited on the bullish side.

Recovery in parent steel business

POSCO Holdings said its operating profit rose for a third consecutive quarter in the third quarter of 2026, and steel subsidiary POSCO posted standalone operating profit of KRW 455 billion, up 66.1% from the prior quarter.

A margin recovery across the group steel business could be a positive signal for affiliated sales conditions. These figures belong to the parent group and are not the company's own results.

Bear factors

Loss in the third quarter

Third-quarter 2026 operating profit fell 96.8% year on year to KRW 161 million, and controlling-shareholder results turned to a net loss of KRW 264 million. Revenue rose 0.9% to KRW 285.2 billion over the same period, but the increase did not carry through to profit. The near-disappearance of margins despite steady revenue is the core of the bearish case.

Double pressure in 2025

In 2025, revenue fell 7.0%, operating profit fell 52.9% and controlling-shareholder net income fell 50.3%. The 2.2% operating margin was the lowest of the 2022 to 2025 period. Lower revenue and a thinner margin arrived together, which points to a year of weaker earnings capacity.

Shrinking cumulative profit

Operating profit for the first three quarters of 2026 adds up to roughly KRW 16 billion. Controlling-shareholder net income over the last four quarters totals KRW 13.5 billion, less than half of the KRW 34.3 billion recorded for 2024.

Even with quarterly recovery, the cumulative scale remains below the earlier annual level, which is cited on the bearish side.

Risk factors

Raw material and energy costs

Steel raw material prices and energy costs feed directly into unit costs. In 2023, higher electricity tariffs added fuel-cost pressure and deepened the earnings slowdown. If costs rise faster than selling prices, operating margins can slip to low levels such as the 2.2% recorded in 2025.

Demand cycle and oversupply

When falling construction activity coincides with rising inflows, supply pressure increases. A credit rating report cited capacity additions by a rival in coated steel as a factor behind the roll-margin compression in 2023. Lower shipments raise the fixed-cost burden, so a repeat of the sharp profit decline seen in 2022 is possible.

Parent output adjustments and earnings volatility

Parent POSCO Holdings expects fourth-quarter 2026 product output to fall by 920,000 tons from the third quarter because of blast furnace repairs. Changes in group steel volumes and prices could affect affiliated sales conditions.

Quarterly profit swings of a few hundred million won mean that even small changes can produce large earnings volatility.

Overall view

POSCO Steeleon's operating profit and controlling-shareholder net income declined in 2025, and the earnings recovered in the first two quarters of 2026 turned to a loss in the third quarter.

The balance sheet improved, with a debt-to-equity ratio of 31.0% and total liabilities of KRW 120 billion at the end of 2025, and operating cash flow rose to KRW 41.5 billion in 2025.

On the other hand, the third-quarter operating margin stayed close to zero, and controlling-shareholder net income over the last four quarters totals only KRW 13.5 billion.

The parent's steel business showed recovery in the third quarter of 2026, but blast furnace repairs are expected to cut output in the fourth quarter. The bullish case rests on quarterly recovery and financial headroom, while the bearish case rests on the collapse in margins and the shrinking cumulative profit.

When the 2026 annual report fixes full-year figures, the fourth-quarter operating margin will be the key indicator of direction. The valuation sits in a range where the reading depends heavily on how quickly profits recover.
