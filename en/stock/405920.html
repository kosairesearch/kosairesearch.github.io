Report overview

Back in the Black, but Wine Imports Still Set the Pace

Nara Cellar, a wine importer and distributor, returned to a full-year profit in 2025 and grew operating profit in the third quarter of 2026, yet its last four quarters still add up to a net loss attributable to controlling shareholders.

  1. 1

    On a disclosed basis, 2025 operating profit was KRW 984 million and net profit attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 85 million, a swing back to profit from 2024. Revenue, however, has fallen three years in a row to KRW 82.1 billion in 2025.

  2. 2

    Third-quarter 2026 revenue reached KRW 22.2 billion, up 7.3% year on year, operating profit rose 15.3% to KRW 251 million, and net profit attributable to controlling shareholders swung to a KRW 107 million profit.

  3. 3

    Over the most recent four quarters, from the fourth quarter of 2025 to the third quarter of 2026, the net loss attributable to controlling shareholders totals KRW 549 million.   4. 4

02

Business structure

Founded in 1990, Nara Cellar imports and distributes wine and listed on KOSDAQ in June 2023. It is widely known as the exclusive importer of Montes Alpha in Korea and also carries brands such as Caymus, Duckhorn, Montes, a range of French negociant wines, and Donnafugata from Italy.

As of a 2026 first-quarter disclosure, it distributed 252 brands and about 1,613 SKUs. Sales run through on-premise channels such as hotels and restaurants and off-premise channels such as department stores, hypermarkets and convenience stores, plus its own Road Wine Fix outlets.

US wine is the largest country mix, and US sales were KRW 28.6 billion, or 42% of the total, in the third quarter of 2025 before liquor tax. Beyond wine, the company runs separate categories for whisky, sake and other spirits.

A September 2026 report said subsidiary Sojustory had set up its own distillery in Andong and started producing distilled soju, extending the company's brand planning and distribution know-how into manufacturing.

Peers cited alongside it in the same period include Shinsegae L&B, whose results also weakened, while Hite Jinro and Lotte Chilsung Beverage are larger liquor-focused listed companies.

04

Earnings analysis

On a disclosed basis, revenue fell from KRW 107.2 billion in 2022 to KRW 85.3 billion in 2023 (down 20.4% year on year), KRW 82.7 billion in 2024 (down 3.1%) and KRW 82.1 billion in 2025 (down 0.7%), declining three years in a row from 2023 through 2025. 2022 was the largest year and 2025 the smallest among 2022 to 2025.

Profit moved far more sharply than sales. Operating profit fell from KRW 12 billion in 2022 to KRW 196 million in 2023 (down 98.4%), turned into an operating loss of KRW 3.48 billion in 2024, and returned to a KRW 984 million profit in 2025.

Operating margin was 11.2% in 2022, 0.2% in 2023, -4.2% in 2024 and 1.2% in 2025. Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders went from KRW 8.94 billion in 2022 to a KRW 1.1 billion loss in 2023, widened to a KRW 5.71 billion loss in 2024, and swung to a KRW 85 million profit in 2025.

Net profit including non-controlling interests, at KRW 55.01 million in 2025, is a separate figure. The 2023 downturn tracked a shrinking domestic wine market, where import volume peaked in 2021 and fell 20.4% in 2023, with home-drinking demand moving toward whisky and highballs.

Quarterly operating profit jumped to KRW 1.22 billion in the fourth quarter of 2025, a 459.8% rise from the prior quarter, then fell to KRW 525 million in the first quarter of 2026 and KRW 205 million in the second quarter. Third-quarter 2026 operating profit was KRW 251 million.

Equity stood at KRW 6.52 billion at the end of 2025, and the debt ratio fell from 146.8% in 2022 to 83.7% in 2025. Operating cash flow was -KRW 12.7 billion in 2023 and KRW 6.87 billion in 2025.

05

Industry analysis

Domestic wine imports have been shrinking since their 2021 peak. Import volume reached 76,575 tonnes in 2021 and fell to 56,542 tonnes in 2023, down 20.4% year on year.

The company had projected that a market of about KRW 1.3 trillion in 2021 would grow into the KRW 3 trillion range within two to three years, a path that did not materialize. The slowdown is linked to the fading of home drinking after the pandemic and weaker demand for premium spirits at home.

Younger consumers in their 20s and 30s shifting toward whisky and highballs is another factor in demand. A report on France also described a drop of more than 30% in red wine consumption among 18 to 35 year olds over ten years, so the youth shift is not unique to Korea.

A high-exchange-rate environment adds pressure on import costs. Competitively, wine importers are restructuring and widening into whisky and distilled spirits. The company has described itself as the largest listed wine import and distribution company in Korea.

Hite Jinro and Lotte Chilsung Beverage, which both carry liquor businesses within the food and beverage sector, serve as broader comparison points.

06

Outlook

Three business plans can be confirmed. First, subsidiary Sojustory is building a modern distilled soju plant in Andong, and a September 2026 report said production had begun, after a June 25 completion date had been reported.

Second, the soju brand Nara Soju plans a first 25-proof release in November 2026, followed by a premium 43-proof line in February 2027, with a September 2026 report putting the first public launch around year-end. Third, the wine business is widening its range from everyday labels to premium Burgundy and Champagne.

At its third-quarter 2025 results, the company said it would keep expanding consumer touchpoints, relocating stores, and introducing a B2B online ordering system. Once the distillery runs at scale, manufacturing cost and production efficiency could begin to show in results.

Key checkpoints are how much early sales Nara Soju secures after launch and whether the 43-proof line arrives on the February 2027 schedule. It will also matter how the annual report separates new spirits revenue and profit from the wine business.

In wine, quarterly revenue has stayed between KRW 21.5 billion and KRW 22.3 billion through the third quarter of 2026, so the trend in quarterly sales will show whether top-line growth is returning.

08

Bull factors

Profitability is recovering

Operating profit turned to a KRW 984 million profit in 2025 from a 2024 loss, and third-quarter 2026 operating profit of KRW 251 million rose 15.3% year on year. The company has attributed the improvement to cost-of-sales management and lower selling and administrative expenses.

The KRW 1.22 billion operating profit in the fourth quarter of 2025 was the highest of the last five quarters, which suggests cost efficiency is showing up in results.

A broader spirits portfolio

Beyond wine, the company runs separate categories for whisky, sake and other spirits, and through a subsidiary it has begun producing distilled soju at its own Andong distillery. Brand planning and distribution built in wine are now linked to manufacturing. If the 25-proof and 43-proof launches proceed on schedule, the revenue mix could broaden beyond wine.

Brand assets and distribution reach

The company is widely known as the exclusive Korean importer of Montes Alpha and distributes 252 brands according to a 2026 first-quarter disclosure. It sells through both on-premise accounts such as hotels and restaurants and off-premise retail such as department stores and convenience stores.

In the third quarter of 2025, the company said its major wine brands, including Montes, Caymus and Duckhorn, averaged double-digit or higher sales growth.

09

Bear factors

Three straight years of revenue decline

Revenue fell from KRW 107.2 billion in 2022 to KRW 82.1 billion in 2025, declining three years in a row from 2023. The 2024 operating loss of KRW 3.48 billion and net loss of KRW 5.71 billion attributable to controlling shareholders widened the deficit.

If profit is being protected mainly through cost cuts rather than sales recovery, the top-line base could be seen as weak.

Volatile quarterly earnings

The second quarter of 2026 saw a KRW 883 million net loss attributable to controlling shareholders against a KRW 205 million operating profit, a wide gap. Over the last four quarters that net loss totals KRW 549 million.

The third-quarter 2026 return to profit was only KRW 107 million, so one quarter of improvement is too thin to establish a trend.

Wine demand and new-business costs

Domestic wine import volume fell 20.4% year on year in 2023, and a shift by consumers in their 20s and 30s toward whisky and highballs is cited as a demand headwind. Building a distillery and launching products require investment and selling costs.

The profit contribution from the new spirits business has not yet shown up as a separate segment in periodic reports.

10

Risk factors

Industry and demand

Domestic wine import volume has continued to decline since its 2021 peak. Slower home drinking and weaker demand for premium spirits feed directly into revenue. Whether the shift away from wine among younger consumers is structural or temporary remains hard to tell.

Exchange rates and costs

Imported wine makes up more than 80% of revenue, so exchange-rate moves pass quickly into costs. The company uses derivatives such as currency futures to hedge, and it recorded a KRW 76.4 million loss on currency futures in the third quarter of 2025. Because US wine is a large share of sales, dollar-denominated cost exposure is also significant.

New-business execution and balance sheet

Whether the distillery and Nara Soju launches proceed on schedule, and whether early sales cover their costs, will be key. Total liabilities were KRW 54.6 billion at the end of 2025, with a debt ratio of 83.7%. Whether the new-business investment adds to financial strain is a point to monitor.

12

Overall view

Nara Cellar's revenue has declined for three straight years since 2022 and its profit fell sharply before the 2025 return to a full-year profit and the rise in third-quarter 2026 operating profit.

Still, the last four quarters add up to a KRW 549 million net loss attributable to controlling shareholders, and quarterly swings remain large, so it is too early to call the recovery a trend.

Strengths include margin gains from cost control, established wine brands and distribution channels, and the move into spirits manufacturing. Weaknesses include three years of falling revenue, a contracting domestic wine import market, currency exposure and the upfront cost of the new business.

Entering production is a structural change, but whether the distillery and product launches translate into revenue and profit will become clearer only as periodic reports come out.

On valuation, a price-to-earnings figure cannot be calculated and the price-to-book ratio is below 1x, so readings diverge depending on whether one weights asset value or the earnings recovery.

The next checkpoints are the Nara Soju 25-proof launch in November 2026, the 2026 annual report by the end of March 2027, and the first-quarter 2027 report in mid-May 2027.
