Wavice: Defense GaN RF Maker With Volatile Quarterly Results

Wavice is a GaN RF chip specialist tied to defense demand, and its quarterly results have swung sharply through the third quarter of 2026 even after the 2025 annual swing to operating profit.

02

Business structure

Wavice is a GaN RF semiconductor specialist founded in 2017 that listed on KOSDAQ as a technology-growth company in 2024. The company became the first in Korea to localize GaN RF chip mass-production technology.

It describes itself as the only domestic firm that has vertically integrated the full RF power amplifier value chain from chip to packaged transistor to module. A brokerage report notes that the mass-production process after the chip stage is fully in-house at 100 percent.

Its applications span advanced weapons-system radars, anti-drone systems, mobile telecom infrastructure, and satellite and aerospace uses.

Its marquee contract is a KRW 26.5 billion supply deal signed in April 2025 with a Korean defense systems prime contractor for high-power amplifier boards in the L-SAM multifunction radar.

It has also completed localization of a core component for the Army's Cheonma (K-31) short-range air defense system, and it holds a supply contract with an Indian defense company for high-power amplifiers. Most sales come from domestic defense customers, according to the same brokerage.

In competition, a small number of IDMs in Japan are described as dominating the market. A brokerage estimates lead times at about seven weeks, roughly half those of global competitors. A brokerage in 2024 cited a backlog of KRW 38.3 billion.

04

Earnings analysis

On a DART-filed annual basis, revenue rose from KRW 16.9 billion in 2023 to KRW 29.4 billion in 2024 (up 74.0% year on year) and KRW 40.7 billion in 2025 (up 38.5%). Revenue has grown two years in a row, from 2024 through 2025, and 2025 was the largest year of 2023 to 2025.

Operating losses were KRW 9.52 billion in 2023 and KRW 4.86 billion in 2024, a continued deficit with a narrower loss, before a KRW 627 million operating profit in 2025 marked a swing to profit. The operating margin moved from -56.4 percent in 2023 to -16.5 percent in 2024 and 1.5 percent in 2025.

Controlling-shareholder results went from a net loss of KRW 15 billion in 2023 and KRW 5.53 billion in 2024 to net income of KRW 1.8 billion in 2025, also a swing to profit. Net income including non-controlling interests shows the same values in each year.

At the end of 2025, total equity was KRW 51.2 billion, all attributable to controlling shareholders, against total liabilities of KRW 59.1 billion. The debt-to-equity ratio went from 220.9 percent in 2023 to 99.3 percent in 2024 and 115.5 percent in 2025.

Operating cash flow was negative at KRW -8.43 billion in 2023 and KRW -4.58 billion in 2024, then turned positive at KRW 794 million in 2025.

On a quarterly basis, the third quarter of 2025 posted revenue of KRW 8.28 billion, an operating loss of KRW 1.18 billion, and a controlling-shareholder net loss of KRW 773 million.

The fourth quarter then jumped to KRW 17.9 billion in revenue (up 116.2 percent quarter on quarter), an operating profit of KRW 6.69 billion, and controlling-shareholder net income of KRW 7.75 billion. That spike coincides with the concentrated second-half delivery schedule that the company described in 2025.

The first quarter of 2026 recorded revenue of KRW 5.19 billion (down 71.0 percent quarter on quarter), an operating loss of KRW 3.36 billion, and controlling-shareholder net income of KRW 1.76 billion (down 77.3 percent).

The second quarter had revenue of KRW 7.75 billion (up 49.3 percent), an operating loss narrowed to KRW 707 million, and controlling-shareholder net income of KRW 43.3 billion.

The third quarter had revenue of KRW 6.63 billion (down 14.4 percent quarter on quarter and down 19.9 percent year on year), an operating loss of KRW 2.24 billion that widened both sequentially and from a year earlier, and a controlling-shareholder net loss of KRW 36.3 billion.

Controlling-shareholder net income over the last four quarters totals KRW 16.5 billion, a result of the second-quarter gain offsetting the third-quarter loss. Operating results have been in deficit for three quarters, but at the net income level, non-operating items have been driving quarterly outcomes. The breakdown of those non-operating items is a point to verify in the quarterly report footnotes.

05

Industry analysis

Demand from the downstream market spans the shift of domestic weapons systems into mass production and the expansion of the defense maintenance, repair and overhaul (MRO) market.

The company says it takes part in L-SAM, Cheonma (K-31), and naval, aviation and air-defense weapons systems, several of them in development or early production.

According to an industry source reported in December 2024, the air-defense weapons system the company participated in finished development and is preparing for production.

Defense Acquisition Program Administration policy on integrated MRO based on life-cycle cost is widening, and the company views this as favorable for firms that hold localized technology. Policy emphasis on localizing weapons systems and expanding defense exports also supports demand.

In competition, a small number of IDMs in Japan dominate the market, and the company argues that few domestic firms in Korea internalize the process from chip to module. The seven-week lead time is a brokerage estimate that puts the company at about half the level of global competitors.

In terms of cycle position, programs are moving from development toward mass production, but quarterly revenue swings with the timing of large-program deliveries. A 2025 brokerage forecast expected the overseas revenue share to rise in 2026.

Because demand leans on defense budgets, budget execution timing and program pace are the variables that shape the outlook.

06

Outlook

The one concrete plan the company has described is completion of its next-generation GaN RF plant in Cheonan. A brokerage report says the company made a pre-emptive capacity investment to prepare for possible global market entry, with completion planned by 2027.

Europe is planned as a next-generation strategic market, and expansion to India and to broader domestic and overseas customers is under way. The X-band process was in preparation for commercialization as of a 2025 announcement, linking to next-generation radar and satellite applications.

The MRO business is described as expanding from the Cheonma localization into maintenance and performance upgrades. In May 2025 the company said follow-on mass-production contracts for weapons systems such as L-SAM were scheduled in sequence.

A brokerage estimate made before the 2024 listing put 2026 revenue at KRW 54.9 billion, though that was a pre-listing projection. Filed quarterly revenue for 2026 was KRW 5.19 billion, KRW 7.75 billion, and KRW 6.63 billion across the first three quarters.

No formal 2026 target or guidance appears in the material reviewed, so the gap between estimate and filing should be checked against the annual report.

The next checkpoint is the full-year 2026 revenue and operating margin in the 2026 annual report, followed by Cheonan plant progress and L-SAM-related mass-production delivery disclosures.

Bull factors

Participation in Domestic Weapons Mass Production

The company signed a KRW 26.5 billion mass-production contract for amplifier boards in the L-SAM radar and completed localization of a core part for Cheonma (K-31). Industry sources say an air-defense system it worked on has finished development and is preparing for production. If development programs move into mass production, the revenue base could broaden.

Vertical Integration and Lead-Time Edge

Full in-house mass production from chip to module is cited as a basis for cost competitiveness. A brokerage estimate of about seven weeks for lead times puts the company at roughly half the level of global competitors. In a market where a few Japanese IDMs dominate, a localized supplier has room to widen its customer base.

Annual Swing to Profit and Cash Flow Improvement

On a filed basis, 2025 operating profit was KRW 627 million, a swing to profit, and the operating margin improved from -56.4 percent in 2023 to 1.5 percent in 2025. Operating cash flow moved from KRW -8.43 billion in 2023 and KRW -4.58 billion in 2024 to KRW 794 million in 2025.

The fourth quarter of 2025 recorded an operating profit of KRW 6.69 billion, showing that profit has also appeared at the quarterly level.

Bear factors

High Quarterly Volatility in Revenue and Operating Results

First-quarter 2026 revenue fell 71.0 percent quarter on quarter to KRW 5.19 billion, and the third-quarter 2026 operating loss widened to KRW 2.24 billion. Operating losses have persisted for three straight quarters.

Because quarters with spikes such as the KRW 17.9 billion fourth quarter of 2025 depend on delivery timing, quarterly predictability is low.

Gap Between Net Results and Operating Results

The third-quarter 2026 controlling-shareholder net loss of KRW 36.3 billion far exceeded the KRW 2.24 billion operating loss. In the second quarter, controlling-shareholder net income of KRW 43.3 billion came despite a KRW 707 million operating loss.

Non-operating items are swinging quarterly results by large amounts, which makes core business profitability hard to read from net income alone.

Defense Dependence and Financial Burden

Most sales come from domestic defense customers, which makes results sensitive to budget execution and policy variables. At the end of 2025, total liabilities of KRW 59.1 billion exceeded total equity of KRW 51.2 billion.

The debt-to-equity ratio rose from 99.3 percent in 2024 to 115.5 percent in 2025, a financial burden that weighs on the bear case.

Risk factors

Order and Delivery Timing

Defense programs run long from development through mass-production conversion to delivery, and deliveries cluster in specific quarters. If delivery slips into the following year, quarterly revenue can drop sharply. The first-quarter 2026 revenue decline of KRW 5.19 billion can be read as an example of this timing gap. Follow-on order and mass-production contract disclosures should be tracked together.

Equity Erosion and Balance Sheet

The third-quarter 2026 controlling-shareholder net loss of KRW 36.3 billion is large enough to move total equity meaningfully. At the end of 2025, total equity was KRW 51.2 billion against total liabilities of KRW 59.1 billion.

The pace of equity change and borrowing capacity should be checked in the balance sheet of the 2026 annual report.

Ownership Structure and Executive Selling

A 2026 ownership record shows that a founder group led by Kim Jung-gon, with 11 other holders, held 20.39 percent as of April 1, 2026. A director sold shares on the open market between February 27 and March 19, 2026, then acquired shares through an appraisal right exercise on April 23, 2026.

These executive moves are disclosed facts, and whether they add to float should be tracked through future filings.

Overall view

Wavice was the first in Korea to localize GaN RF chips and now runs in-house mass production from chip to module, supplying components for defense weapons systems. On a filed basis, annual results improved from an operating loss in 2023 to an operating profit swing in 2025, and revenue grew in both 2024 and 2025.

Against that, operating losses have persisted for three straight quarters from the first quarter of 2026, and quarterly net results diverged sharply between a KRW 43.3 billion gain in the second quarter and a KRW 36.3 billion loss in the third.

Quarterly revenue moves with the timing of defense deliveries, and the debt-to-equity ratio rose to 115.5 percent in 2025. Strengths include participation in mass-production programs and in-house technology, while weaknesses are quarterly volatility and the influence of non-operating items.

The next checkpoints are the operating margin and non-operating breakdown in the 2026 annual report and progress on the Cheonan plant.
