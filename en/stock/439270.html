02

Business structure

Creatz is a golf technology company founded in 2009 that builds its business around launch monitors that measure the movement of balls and clubs, complemented by simulation software and AI swing analysis services.

The company handles product planning, R&D, manufacturing, software and AI development, sales, and after-sales service in-house. Hardware accounts for the overwhelming share of revenue, and software sales were KRW 2.5 billion in 2025, a single-digit share of total sales.

By region, overseas sales were KRW 85.4 billion in 2025, or 82% of the total, and 94% of overseas revenue came from the United States.

Domestic sales fell to KRW 18.8 billion in 2025 from KRW 38.7 billion in 2022, and the company operates the domestic business under the QED brand and the overseas business under the UNEEKOR brand.

Its core Dimple Optix technology recognizes the dimples on the ball surface without a marked ball, which the company presents as a point of difference from marked-ball measurement systems. The company has registered related patents in ten countries, including the United States.

In the US, about 70% of sales run through local resellers, supported by large retail platforms such as Amazon and Best Buy. Competitively, Creatz first secured the home simulator market and is now widening its customer base to commercial venues such as golf academies and indoor golf facilities.

03

Recent trends

According to the DART filing for cumulative 3Q 2026, revenue was KRW 18.6 billion, down 33.4% year on year. Operating profit over the same period was KRW 2.55 billion, down 57.3%, and the company posted a net loss of KRW 2.38 billion after a profit in the prior-year period.

Because operating profit fell faster than sales, profitability narrowed. Consolidated 2025 revenue was KRW 104.2 billion and operating profit was KRW 25.1 billion, an operating margin of about 24%.

In 2024, operating profit had plunged to the mid-KRW 1 billion range on KRW 82.6 billion of revenue before recovering in 2025. The IPO was priced at KRW 15,000 in October 2026, and according to recent press reports the shares opened at KRW 9,410 on the first trading day.

Press reports also described a single-day drop of more than 16% to KRW 6,470 without any new company disclosure. The current price is KRW 4,245, down 8.71% on the day, with market capitalization of KRW 55.7 billion and trading value of KRW 1.1 billion.

Before listing, the expected market capitalization at the offering price was about KRW 197 billion. Software revenue reached a provisional KRW 2.5 billion in the first half of 2026, matching the full-year 2025 level.

04

Outlook

In the near term, cumulative 3Q 2026 sales and profit both declined and the company slipped into a net loss, so a recovery in profitability is the main variable. After listing, the company plans to shift from hardware-led sales toward recurring revenue from AI and software subscriptions.

Specifically, it aims to expand service use among hardware buyers through the GameDay simulation game and the AIMY AI coaching service. It also plans to pursue commercial venues in North America and expand into Europe and Asia-Pacific, using its Japan and UK subsidiaries as bases.

Of the roughly KRW 20 billion in net offering proceeds, a large portion is earmarked for expanding R&D and logistics centers in the United States, while KRW 7.7 billion is planned for acquisitions in software and commercial solutions.

Press reports indicate a premium hardware launch, the EYE EXO PRO, is approaching, and whether new device sales broaden the subscriber base will be a key factor. Acquisitions and overseas expansion may bring integration costs and execution burdens, so the pace of margin improvement remains uncertain.

The next checkpoints are whether software's share of revenue moves beyond the single digits in the 4Q 2026 results and how large commercial-market sales become.

05

Bull factors

North American distribution base

Overseas sales were KRW 85.4 billion in 2025, or 82% of the total, with 94% of that generated in the United States. The company says roughly 70% of US sales come through local resellers. The sales network also spans large retail platforms and online channels, which the company cites as a base for its distribution.

Fast growth in software revenue

Software revenue was KRW 2.5 billion in 2025 and reached a provisional KRW 2.5 billion in the first half of 2026, matching the full-year figure within six months. The company is linking hardware buyers to services built around GameDay and AIMY. Whether this growth converts into subscription and data revenue is the main open question.

Camera-based measurement IP

Dimple Optix recognizes the dimples on a ball's surface to measure spin without a marked ball. The company says it has registered related patents in ten countries, including the United States.

As of June 2026, 77 of its 168 employees worked in R&D, and its equipment and software are reportedly used at more than 200 commercial venues.

06

Bear factors

Sharp 3Q profit drop and swing to loss

Cumulative 3Q 2026 revenue fell 33.4% to KRW 18.6 billion, and operating profit dropped 57.3% to KRW 2.55 billion. The KRW 2.38 billion net loss reversed a prior-year profit. Operating profit also fell to the mid-KRW 1 billion range in 2024, which points to a business with sizable earnings volatility.

Domestic sales shrink and hardware dependence

Domestic sales declined from KRW 38.7 billion in 2022 to KRW 18.8 billion in 2025. The CEO has described the domestic market as shrinking, placing emphasis on the United States.

Most revenue still comes from hardware such as launch monitors, and software's single-digit share leaves the company directly exposed to any slowdown in device sales.

Post-listing slide and valuation gap

The share price trades at KRW 4,245, far below the KRW 15,000 offering price, and market capitalization stands at KRW 55.7 billion. That compares with an expected market capitalization of about KRW 197 billion before listing.

The company had cut its target valuation during its re-listing attempt to address overvaluation concerns, yet the market value still sits well below the offering-based figure.

07

Risk factors

Macro

With 82% of revenue overseas and most of that in the United States, the company is sensitive to the won-dollar exchange rate and US consumer demand. Home golf equipment is a discretionary purchase, so device sales could weaken first in an economic slowdown. Tariff and trade changes could also pressure North American pricing and channels.

Business

If the shift to subscription software does not progress as planned, the company will struggle to offset lower hardware sales. Expansion into commercial venues and Japan and Europe requires adapting to new customers, regulations, and distribution structures. Delays in selecting and integrating acquisitions could slow the return on offering proceeds.

Market and liquidity

Daily trading value is around KRW 1.1 billion against a market capitalization of KRW 55.7 billion, so trading volume is modest. Recent press reports describe a single-day drop of more than 16% without any new company disclosure. Heightened volatility could widen the gap between the share price and reported financial results.

08

Overall view

Creatz has built a North American distribution network in the home golf simulator market and posted an operating margin of about 24% in 2025. However, cumulative 3Q 2026 results show lower sales and a swing to net loss, which raises questions about whether 2025 profitability can be sustained.

Software revenue is growing quickly, with a provisional KRW 2.5 billion in the first half of 2026, but its share of total sales remains in the single digits.

After listing, the share price has fallen well below the KRW 15,000 offering price to a market capitalization of KRW 55.7 billion, in a period of elevated volatility given modest trading volume.

The shrinking domestic market and US concentration of overseas sales increase sensitivity to external factors such as exchange rates and consumer demand.

Overall, the gap between the growth strategy and reported financial results is the central issue, and the software share of revenue in the 4Q 2026 results will be a useful gauge of that gap.
