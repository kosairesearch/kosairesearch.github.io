Shares slump after listing as profit turnaround is tested

02

Business structure

Creatz, founded in 2009, is a golf technology company that develops and sells high-speed camera-based launch monitors and golf simulation software. It operates mainly under the UNEEKOR brand, and merged its subsidiary QED into the parent before consolidating the brand.

Its core Dimple Optix technology recognizes the dimples on a golf ball's surface to measure spin, and the company says it holds registered patents in 10 countries including the United States.

After hardware sales, it offers GameDay, a 4K Unreal Engine-based simulation game, and AI Trainer, an AI swing analysis service, under annual subscriptions. Revenue remains hardware-centric, and the 2.5 billion won in 2025 software sales is a single-digit share of total revenue.

By region, overseas sales in 2025 were 85.4 billion won, or 82% of the total, and 94% of overseas sales came from the United States. Domestic sales were 18.8 billion won in 2025, down from 38.7 billion won in 2022.

On competition, the company points to its North American sales network, including local resellers and large distribution platforms, as its differentiator.

It is widening its customer base in commercial markets such as golf academies, golf bars and hotels, and says its equipment and software are used at more than 200 commercial sites.

03

Recent trends

Creatz's operating profit fell from 12.9 billion won in 2023 to 1.47 billion won in 2024 before recovering to 25.05 billion won in 2025, according to the company's disclosed figures. Consolidated revenue for 2025 was 104,182 million won, up from 82,634 million won in 2024, and the operating margin was 24.0%.

A April 2025 media report had cited a 2024 operating loss of about 84 million won, while later figures show a 2024 operating profit of 1.47 billion won, so the reporting basis differs. Revenue for the first half of 2026 was 39,189 million won and operating profit was 2,147 million won, a 5.5% margin.

Software revenue in the first half of 2026 was 2.5 billion won, matching full-year 2025. Shares began trading on October 30, 2026 at 9,410 won, well below the 15,000 won IPO price.

On one later trading day the stock fell 16.52% to close at 6,470 won, and media reports said no clear company disclosure or news accounted for the drop. The company's valuation was cut from 329.5 billion won to 283.3 billion won during the 2023 SPAC merger attempt before that deal was withdrawn.

Market talk of a valuation around 400 billion won is far above the current market capitalization of 69.7 billion won.

04

Outlook

The company said it plans to put most of the IPO proceeds into the US market.

About 20 billion won, after issuance costs, will go to expanding its US R&D and logistics centers and acquiring technology companies, and 7.7 billion won of that is earmarked for acquisitions in golf simulation software, commercial solutions and data analytics.

Its medium-term target is to raise software's share of total revenue to 50% by 2030. The company is also expanding into Japan, Europe and Asia-Pacific, and already has local subsidiaries in Japan and the United Kingdom.

Management argues that commercial markets are larger and carry higher unit prices than residential customers, and that they link to software subscription revenue. Key indicators going forward include whether software revenue turns into recurring subscription and data sales rather than one-off income.

In the second half of 2026 results, the commercial revenue share and operating margin relative to the 5.5% first-half margin will show the pace of the shift.

Whether the concentration of overseas sales in the United States persists, and whether acquisition spending feeds into software revenue growth, are also important to track.

05

Bull factors

Overseas growth built on North American channels

Revenue rose from 19.2 billion won in 2020 to 104,182 million won in 2025, and overseas sales grew about 7.6 times over the same period, from 11.2 billion to 85.4 billion won. US sales accounted for 94% of overseas revenue in 2025, making the North American network the practical base for growth.

The company is extending its installed base from residential customers into golf academies and commercial facilities. Its patents on imaging technologies such as Dimple Optix are cited as a source of competitive strength.

Early shift toward software and subscriptions

Software revenue was 2.5 billion won in 2025 and a preliminary 2.5 billion won in the first half of 2026, matching the full-year 2025 total in six months. The company has set a target of raising software to 50% of total revenue by 2030.

It offers AI Trainer and GameDay to hardware buyers on subscription terms to build recurring revenue. Equipment and software are reportedly in use at more than 200 commercial sites, which the company frames as a starting point for the shift.

Evidence of profit recovery in 2025

Operating profit in 2025 was 25,053 million won, up sharply from 1,471 million won in 2024, with an operating margin of 24.0%. That exceeded the 12.9 billion won operating profit recorded in 2023, showing a recovery after the SPAC merger was called off.

The company plans to direct most of the post-IPO funds to US R&D, logistics expansion and acquisitions to speed up its business restructuring.

06

Bear factors

Profitability slowed in the first half of 2026

Revenue in the first half of 2026 was 39,189 million won and operating profit was 2,147 million won, a 5.5% margin. That compares with a 24.0% margin for 2025, indicating large swings in profitability. Operating profit also fell to 1.47 billion won in 2024, so earnings continuity has yet to be proven. Whether 2025 represents a one-time peak will depend on the figures reported for the second half of 2026.

Shares below the IPO price and a valuation gap

Shares closed their first trading day at 9,410 won, below the 15,000 won IPO price, and now trade at 5,310 won. During the 2023 SPAC merger attempt, the valuation was cut from 329.5 billion won to 283.3 billion won before the deal was withdrawn.

The gap between the roughly 400 billion won valuation mentioned in the market and the current market capitalization of 69.7 billion won reflects differing views on fair value.

Concentration in overseas sales and hardware

Overseas sales made up 82% of 2025 revenue, and 94% of that came from the United States, which implies high dependence on one market. Domestic sales fell from 38.7 billion won in 2022 to 18.8 billion won in 2025.

Most revenue still comes from hardware sales, and software is a single-digit share, so slower subscription growth would leave earnings directly exposed to hardware demand.

07

Risk factors

Macro

US consumer conditions and exchange-rate moves are variables that could directly affect earnings, given that overseas sales exceed 80% of revenue. Golf equipment is largely discretionary household spending, so it may be sensitive to interest rates and the business cycle. Won-denominated revenue and profit also move with exchange rates.

Concentration

Because 94% of overseas sales come from the United States, any disruption to US sales channels or the local subsidiary would weigh heavily on results. Commercial markets and new regions such as Japan and Europe could diversify the base, but that effect will appear only in future reported figures. A delay in the software shift would keep the business reliant on hardware sales.

Market flows

Shares have moved sharply since listing, including a 16.52% one-day drop, and media reports said that decline came without a clear company-specific negative. In the early period after a listing, changes in tradable supply and institutional and retail flows can amplify price volatility. The IPO was made entirely of new shares, totaling 1.4 million shares.

08

Overall view

Creatz restored profitability in 2025, with operating profit of about 25 billion won, which supports the recovery in its earnings. Its growth case rests on a North American-centered overseas sales structure and a shift toward software.

However, the first-half 2026 operating margin of 5.5% fell well below the 2025 level, so earnings durability is still being tested. Software revenue rose to 2.5 billion won but remains a single-digit share of total revenue, so dependence on hardware persists.

An overseas share of 82% and concentration in the United States represent both a growth engine and exposure to a single market.

On valuation, the roughly 400 billion won level mentioned in the market is far above the current market capitalization of 69.7 billion won, and the performance basis for bridging that gap is the central question.

Since listing, the shares have traded below the 15,000 won IPO price and have also seen a sharp one-day decline. Overall, the direction of the business shift and the 2025 earnings improvement are clear, but views on earnings strength and the share price level remain divided.
