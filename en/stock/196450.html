Report overview

Profit turn holds as sales slip; robot ToF is the next test

CoAsia CM has kept quarterly operating profit positive since its 2025 annual turnaround, but sales have fallen for two straight quarters since the second quarter of 2026, and its robot ToF module business is aiming for mass production in 2027.

02

Business structure

CoAsia CM is a company whose core products are smartphone camera modules (CCM) and optical lenses. CCM and lenses made up almost all of 2025 consolidated sales of KRW 258.2 billion, and the same two businesses accounted for more than 99% of sales in the first quarter of 2026.

Its main customer is Samsung Electronics, and supply to Samsung's full smartphone lineup continued in the first quarter of 2026, with Galaxy S and A series models selling steadily.

In lenses, the company began supplying 50-megapixel products in 2024, and higher-pixel products spreading across the A series helped it return to profit from 2025.

Press coverage has pointed to the company as the only domestic supplier of both handset camera lenses and camera modules, a combination seen as its main competitive edge. Module production sits with a subsidiary, which has started mass production of 3D ToF modules for robots.

The company is extending its optical design capability from smartphones into physical AI fields such as robotics, automotive electronics and 3D cameras. Among camera-module peers in the same sector are LG Innotek and Samsung Electro-Mechanics, but most of CoAsia CM's revenue still comes from smartphone cameras.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual sales fell from KRW 383.2 billion in 2022 to KRW 294 billion in 2023 (down 23.3% year on year), KRW 267.3 billion in 2024 (down 9.1%) and KRW 258.2 billion in 2025 (down 3.4%).

Sales have declined for three straight years from 2023 through 2025, with 2022 the largest year and 2025 the smallest in the 2022–2025 range.

Operating results went from an operating loss of KRW 4.42 billion in 2022 to an operating loss of KRW 13 billion in 2023 (losses widened), an operating loss of KRW 9.54 billion in 2024 (losses narrowed) and an operating profit of KRW 5.27 billion in 2025, a turn to profit.

The operating margin moved from -1.2% in 2022 and -4.4% in 2023 to -3.6% in 2024 and 2.0% in 2025. Net income attributable to controlling shareholders was a loss of KRW 8.76 billion in 2022, a loss of KRW 22.1 billion in 2023 and a loss of KRW 18.7 billion in 2024, before a profit of KRW 2.71 billion in 2025.

Net income including non-controlling interests is a separate figure, and it was a loss of KRW 5.11 billion in 2022. Operating cash flow was KRW 28.1 billion in 2022, KRW 7.77 billion in 2023, minus KRW 767 million in 2024 and KRW 23.2 billion in 2025. The debt-to-equity ratio fell from 121.5% in 2024 to 98.3% in 2025.

Year-end 2025 total equity was KRW 64.8 billion, of which KRW 58.9 billion belonged to controlling shareholders, and total liabilities were KRW 63.7 billion.

Quarterly sales went from KRW 61.4 billion in the third quarter of 2025 to KRW 48.3 billion in the fourth quarter (down 21.4% from the prior quarter), then peaked at KRW 70.5 billion in the first quarter of 2026 (up 46.0%).

They declined to KRW 63.4 billion in the second quarter (down 10.1%) and KRW 55.4 billion in the third (down 12.7% quarter on quarter and down 9.9% year on year), which makes two straight quarters of decline.

Quarterly operating profit rose from KRW 1.1 billion in the third quarter of 2025 to KRW 1.41 billion and KRW 1.78 billion in the following two quarters, fell to KRW 98.68 million in the second quarter of 2026 (down 94.5%), and recovered to KRW 2.68 billion in the third quarter (up 143.3% year on year).

The company said profitability slowed temporarily because of a slowdown in downstream demand and customer inventory adjustments.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders moved from a KRW 152 million loss in the fourth quarter of 2025 to KRW 1.37 billion of profit in the first quarter of 2026, a KRW 588 million loss in the second quarter and KRW 1.06 billion of profit in the third. The four-quarter total from the fourth quarter of 2025 through the third quarter of 2026 is KRW 1.69 billion.

05

Industry analysis

The smartphone market is being reshaped around AI features and premium models, and the company credited a higher share of high-spec products for improving first-quarter profitability.

The domestic lens industry went through a long restructuring, and press coverage describes the company as one that survived by investing in technology. Demand from downstream customers is passing through an inventory adjustment phase, according to the company's own explanation.

Physical AI markets such as humanoid robots, service robots and autonomous mobile robots are expanding, which raises the importance of vision sensors that recognize surrounding space. In automotive electronics, the company plans to widen its applications to in-cabin and exterior vehicle cameras.

Automotive cameras can lead to long-term supply once a customer adopts them. On competition, the company differentiates by supplying everything from lens design to module manufacturing, while press coverage notes that domestic rivals tend to focus on either lenses or modules. Which new-market customers it wins will therefore shape its position in the sector.

06

Outlook

The company said in its half-year results announced on August 13, 2026 that profitability in its mobile optics business should keep improving from the second half of the year.

A Shinhan Investment Corp. report carried in late June 2026 projected 2026 sales of KRW 309.8 billion, up 20.0% from the prior year, and operating profit of KRW 71 million... more precisely KRW 7.1 billion, up 34.7%.

The same report saw non-handset new products contributing meaningfully to results from 2027 to 2028, with high-pixel lenses of 50 megapixels or more acting as a steady cash generator until then.

The ToF 3D sensing camera module business is planned to expand into non-handset areas such as automakers and robots through 2027. The report said a ToF module sample has been supplied to one of the largest EV and humanoid robot makers globally, with mass production targeted for 2027.

A ToF camera module co-developed with a 3D sensing semiconductor partner has been sampled to prospective customers, and customer qualification for volume supply is under way. An executive has also said robot programs may take two to three years to reach mass production, which could differ from the 2027 target.

A ToF optical system patent registered and announced on November 10, 2026 covers recognition in low-light conditions, and the company plans to extend it to in-cabin and exterior vehicle cameras. On the smartphone side, the company is pursuing entry into the Galaxy S series, beyond its current A series-centered supply.

Bull factors

From losses to profit

Operating profit of KRW 5.27 billion in 2025 turned the company from the 2022–2024 operating losses to profit, and the operating margin moved from -3.6% in 2024 to 2.0% in 2025.

Quarterly operating profit stayed positive in the first and third quarters of 2026, and the third-quarter figure of KRW 2.68 billion was up 143.3% year on year. Net income attributable to controlling shareholders also turned to a KRW 2.71 billion profit in 2025. Whether this shift in earnings holds can be checked against the next annual results.

Robot ToF modules enter production

The subsidiary has signed a 3D ToF camera module supply contract and started production of modules for robots. A ToF optical system patent covering recognition in low light was registered on November 10, 2026, giving the company a base for linking lens design with module capability.

A Shinhan Investment Corp. analyst said samples had been supplied to a global EV and humanoid robot maker. If the new business converts into sales, it could widen a revenue base now concentrated in smartphones.

Lens and module capability under one roof

Press coverage describes the company as the only domestic supplier of both handset camera lenses and camera modules. Its 50-megapixel lens supply began in 2024, and the spread of higher-pixel products is cited as a factor behind the 2025 return to profit.

Being able to design lenses and manufacture modules in one flow is seen as an advantage when entering new markets. That positioning sets it apart from other listed names in the same sector.

Bear factors

Sales keep declining

Sales fell for three straight years from KRW 294 billion in 2023 to KRW 258.2 billion in 2025. They declined again in the second and third quarters of 2026, to KRW 63.4 billion and KRW 55.4 billion, and the third quarter was down 9.9% year on year. Sales were smallest in 2025 across the 2022–2025 range. If the earnings turnaround does not come with a sales recovery, the profit base could stay thin.

Dependence on one smartphone supply chain

Most of 2025 consolidated sales came from CCM and optical lenses, and the two businesses still accounted for more than 99% of sales in the first quarter of 2026.

The main customer is Samsung Electronics, and second-quarter operating profit fell to KRW 98.68 million as downstream demand slowed and customers adjusted inventories. Prolonged inventory adjustments could shake quarterly profit again. This concentration will remain until new businesses contribute meaningfully.

Balance sheet burden and deficit clean-up

The debt-to-equity ratio was 98.3% at the end of 2025 but was reported at 121.8% at the end of the first quarter of 2026. Short-term borrowings rose from KRW 23.1 billion at the end of 2025 to KRW 39.8 billion at the end of the first quarter of 2026.

An extraordinary shareholder meeting in late June 2026 had on its agenda a KRW 90.2 billion reduction in capital reserves to offset accumulated deficits. Operating cash flow was negative KRW 767 million in 2024, a point that also needs weighing.

Risk factors

Customer and cycle concentration

The main customer is Samsung Electronics, and the core products are Galaxy S and A series models. A drop in smartphone demand or a longer customer inventory adjustment would hit sales and quarterly profit directly.

The sharp fall in second-quarter 2026 operating profit to KRW 98.68 million is an example of this risk showing up. Until new businesses carry more weight in sales, this structure is unlikely to change.

Balance sheet and cash generation

Short-term borrowings have been rising, and the debt-to-equity ratio reported for the end of the first quarter of 2026 was 121.8%, above 98.3% at the end of 2025. Operating cash flow was negative in 2024. Whether cash generation stays steady after the return to profit is the point to check. The effect of the deficit clean-up on the capital structure also needs watching.

Timing and execution of new businesses

Mass production of robot ToF modules has been targeted for 2027, but a company official has said robot programs could take two to three years to reach volume production. Customer qualification is still under way, so the timing could slip.

Because new businesses are expected to contribute from 2027 to 2028, whether the core business keeps its profitability in the meantime is a key variable. Whether the patent and subsidiary production news converts into sales also needs checking.

12

Overall view

CoAsia CM turned to a KRW 5.27 billion operating profit in 2025, and quarterly operating profit stayed positive in the first and third quarters of 2026. Sales, however, declined for three straight years from 2023 through 2025 and fell for two straight quarters in the second and third quarters of 2026.

The drop in second-quarter 2026 operating profit to KRW 98.68 million reads as the effect of a slowdown in downstream demand and customer inventory adjustments, as the company explained. Positives include the shift to profit, preparation for robot ToF mass production and the combined lens and module supply structure.

Negatives include concentration on smartphone sales, the declining sales trend, and debt and short-term borrowing pressure. Shinhan's projection of KRW 309.8 billion in 2026 sales and KRW 7.1 billion in operating profit can be checked against the 2026 annual business report.

Because the robot ToF timeline has been given both as a 2027 volume target and as a two-to-three-year process, the commercial timing is the key item to track. The next scheduled checkpoints are the 2026 annual business report and the first-quarter 2027 results.
