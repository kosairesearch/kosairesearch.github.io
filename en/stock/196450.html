02

Business structure

CoAsia CM is an optics specialist that designs and manufactures both smartphone camera modules and optical lenses. The company describes itself as a domestically unique firm that manufactures and sells both camera modules and optical lenses.

Its core products are camera lenses and modules for Samsung Electronics' Galaxy S and A series smartphones, and it began supplying 50-megapixel-class lens modules for the Galaxy A5 series from late 2023.

Past media coverage noted that the domestic lens module supply base has consolidated to mainly Sekonix and CoAsia CM following a prolonged industry restructuring.

CoAsia CM is a member of the CoAsia group, and it should be noted as a separate listed entity from CoAsia (KOSDAQ: 045970) or the group's semiconductor design-house affiliate.

More recently, the company has been expanding beyond mobile into automotive and robotics applications, jointly developing 3D sensing camera modules with a time-of-flight semiconductor specialist. It is also co-developing a robotics stereo depth-camera solution with affiliate CoAsia CM Korea.

Roughly three decades of accumulated lens development experience combined with semiconductor design capability is cited as a differentiating factor versus competitors.

04

Earnings analysis

CoAsia CM's consolidated revenue fell for three straight years, from KRW 383.2 billion in 2022 to KRW 294 billion in 2023 (down 23.3% year-on-year), KRW 267.3 billion in 2024 (down 9.1%), and KRW 258.2 billion in 2025 (down 3.4%), with 2022 the largest year and 2025 the smallest in this span.

Operating profit swung from a loss of KRW 4.42 billion in 2022 to a wider loss of KRW 13 billion in 2023, then a narrower loss of KRW 9.54 billion in 2024, before turning to a profit of KRW 5.27 billion in 2025. The operating margin improved from -1.2% in 2022 and -4.4% in 2023 to -3.6% in 2024 and 2.0% in 2025.

Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders followed the same pattern, moving from losses of KRW 8.76 billion in 2022, KRW 22.1 billion in 2023, and KRW 18.7 billion in 2024 to a profit of KRW 2.71 billion in 2025.

On a quarterly basis, revenue fell from KRW 70.7 billion in Q2 2025 to KRW 61.4 billion in Q3 2025 (down 13.2% q/q) and KRW 48.3 billion in Q4 2025 (down 21.4% q/q), rebounded to KRW 70.5 billion in Q1 2026 (up 46.0% q/q), then declined again to KRW 63.4 billion in Q2 2026 (down 10.1% q/q and 10.4% y/y).

Operating profit rose to KRW 1.78 billion in Q1 2026 before plunging to KRW 98.68 million in Q2 2026, down 94.5% q/q and 94.7% y/y; the company attributed the weaker second-quarter profitability to downstream demand softness and customer inventory adjustments.

Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders also swung from a profit of KRW 1.37 billion in Q1 2026 to a loss of KRW 588 million in Q2 2026, and the sum of the last four quarters (Q3 2025 through Q2 2026) came to just KRW 1.56 billion.

The debt-to-equity ratio declined from 122.5% in 2022 to 98.3% in 2025, and operating cash flow improved from -KRW 767 million in 2024 to KRW 23.2 billion in 2025, pointing to a broadly improving financial health profile.

05

Industry analysis

The domestic camera module and lens industry has undergone years of restructuring amid low-cost competition from Chinese makers and shifting customer sourcing strategies.

Asia Economy reported that CoAsia CM survived a prolonged restructuring period in the domestic lens industry on the back of its technology competitiveness. Past media coverage indicated that effectively only Sekonix and CoAsia CM remained as domestic suppliers of lens modules to Samsung Electronics' smartphones.

However, Sekonix's mobile exposure has fallen below 20% of its total revenue, while CoAsia CM remains centered on mobile lenses and modules, so the two companies' business structures are increasingly diverging.

The downstream smartphone market is clearly shifting toward higher-spec, premium models, which could favor suppliers with high-megapixel lens capabilities.

At the same time, camera applications are expanding into non-mobile areas such as robotics, electric vehicles, and humanoids, prompting existing mobile optics makers to explore new markets.

CoAsia CM is responding to this trend by co-developing 3D sensing camera modules with a time-of-flight semiconductor specialist, though this new business remains at an early stage with no meaningful revenue contribution yet reflected in disclosed financial statements.

06

Outlook

Shinhan Investment Corp. forecast in June 2026 that CoAsia CM's 2026 revenue would reach KRW 309.8 billion, up 20.0% year-on-year, with operating profit of KRW 7.1 billion, up 34.7% year-on-year.

However, this forecast predates the Q2 2026 results, and actual first-half operating profit totaled only about KRW 1.88 billion combining Q1 and Q2, meaning a much larger profit improvement would be needed in the second half to meet the annual target.

The company said it expects mobile optics profitability to improve meaningfully from the second half as it reduces low-margin models and upgrades its portfolio toward premium products.

A new growth driver is an integrated ToF module co-developed with a time-of-flight semiconductor specialist; a Shinhan Investment Corp. analyst stated that "integrated ToF module samples have been supplied to a leading global electric-vehicle and humanoid robotics company, with mass production targeted for next year." That comment was made as of June 2026, meaning the mass-production target points to 2027.

Mass production of a robotics 3D sensing camera module through affiliate CoAsia CM Korea is also being pursued, making visibility into mass production tied to global robotics supply chains a medium-term point to watch.

In August 2026, the company's extraordinary shareholders' meeting approved transferring about KRW 90.2 billion of capital reserves into retained earnings, aiming to improve its financial structure and strengthen the basis for longer-term shareholder returns.

In addition, the CEO and six executives each purchased 10,000 shares of treasury stock on the open market, as disclosed, signaling management's commitment to accountable governance.

08

Bull factors

Profit Turnaround and Structural Improvement

Full-year 2025 operating profit turned positive at KRW 5.27 billion, and Q1 2026 operating profit reached KRW 1.78 billion. The company continues to pursue profitability-focused management by cutting low-margin models and shifting its portfolio toward premium products.

The debt-to-equity ratio also fell from 122.5% in 2022 to 98.3% in 2025, indicating an improving financial structure.

Expansion into Non-Mobile Businesses

The company co-developed 3D sensing camera modules with a time-of-flight semiconductor specialist and supplied samples to a global EV and humanoid robotics company. It is also co-developing a robotics stereo depth-camera solution with affiliate CoAsia CM Korea.

Being one of few domestic firms producing both camera modules and lenses could be an advantage when securing new application customers.

Shareholder Value Initiatives

The company completed a 5-for-1 share consolidation in August 2026 to address its low-share-price image, and around the same time transferred about KRW 90.2 billion of capital reserves into retained earnings to improve its financial structure and strengthen the basis for shareholder returns.

The CEO and six executives purchasing treasury shares on the open market is also seen as a sign of accountable management.

09

Bear factors

Concentration on Samsung Electronics

Revenue is heavily concentrated on Samsung Electronics' smartphone lineup, making earnings sensitive to changes in that single customer's volume and inventory policies. Indeed, Q2 2026 operating profit fell 94.5% quarter-on-quarter due to downstream softness and customer inventory adjustments. There is not yet clear evidence of meaningful customer diversification.

Multi-Year Revenue Decline

Consolidated revenue declined for three consecutive years, from KRW 383.2 billion in 2022 to KRW 258.2 billion in 2025. Revenue rose 46.0% quarter-on-quarter in Q1 2026 but fell again by 10.1% q/q and 10.4% y/y in Q2 2026, showing substantial quarter-to-quarter volatility. Whether revenue scale has recovered to prior levels remains unconfirmed.

Unproven Revenue Contribution from New Businesses

The ToF module and robotics camera solutions remain at the sample-supply and development stage, with their mass-production timing and volumes not yet reflected in disclosed financial statements.

Ongoing restructuring in the domestic lens and module industry amid low-cost competition from Chinese makers also leaves open the possibility of intensified competition in these new applications.

10

Risk factors

Customer Concentration Risk

Since most revenue derives from Samsung Electronics smartphone supply, weak sales of specific models or vendor changes could sharply increase earnings volatility.

Past media estimates suggested that Samsung Electronics' smartphone camera modules accounted for roughly the mid-50% range of combined revenue at CoAsia CM and Sekonix.

Foreign Exchange and Overseas Production Risk

The company relies on overseas production subsidiaries to serve the Samsung Electronics supply chain, so exchange-rate movements and changes in local labor and logistics costs can affect production costs. Supply-chain risks in raw material and component procurement also persist.

New-Business Execution and Competitive Risk

New growth areas such as ToF and robotics modules remain pre-mass-production, with risk that technology validation and customer qualification schedules could be delayed relative to competitors.

Numerous large players compete in the global camera module market, so there is a risk that new-market entry results may fall short of expectations.

12

Overall view

CoAsia CM emerged from a 2023–2025 period of revenue decline and losses to post operating profit of KRW 5.27 billion in 2025, with the debt-to-equity ratio falling and operating cash flow improving, pointing to a broadly healthier financial profile.

However, with Q2 2026 operating profit plunging 94.5% quarter-on-quarter, the durability of the profit turnaround needs to be reconfirmed in coming quarterly results.

The customer-concentration structure, in which most revenue stems from supplying Samsung Electronics smartphones, remains a key source of earnings volatility, while new non-mobile businesses such as ToF and robotics modules remain at the sample-supply and development stage with uncertain timing for revenue contribution.

The company has continued efforts to enhance shareholder value, including a 5-for-1 share consolidation, a transfer of capital reserves to retained earnings, and management share purchases.

Shinhan Investment Corp. offered a positive assessment in June 2026 based on expected annual earnings improvement and a low estimated price-to-earnings ratio, but that view predates the Q2 results and the gap with actual performance warrants monitoring.

Investors should track upcoming quarterly results and the progress of mass production in new businesses before forming a judgment.
