After Returning to Profit, CoAsia CM Bets on Robotics Optics

CoAsia CM returned to operating profit in 2025, but its operating profit collapsed in the second quarter of 2026, re-exposing earnings volatility even as the company tests robotics and automotive optics as its next growth pillar.

02

Business structure

CoAsia CM is an optical solutions company spun off from Chaum&Diostech in 2014, and the company is described as the only domestic supplier that manufactures and sells both camera modules and optical lenses for smartphones under one roof.

Its primary customer is Samsung Electronics, with supply concentrated in the Galaxy A series while the company also pursues entry into the Galaxy S series.

Production runs through its Vietnamese units, COASIAOPTICS VINA Co., Ltd. for optical lenses and CoAsia CM VINA JSC for camera modules, while its wholly owned subsidiary CoAsia CM Korea handles IT component distribution along with new robotics and automotive optics businesses.

The parent group is CoAsia, centered on semiconductor design-house operations, within which CoAsia CM operates as the optics and camera-module affiliate.

In July 2026 the company brought in Son Ho-sung, a former Samsung Electronics executive who led camera-module development, as the new CEO of subsidiary CoAsia CM Korea to strengthen its mobile business.

At an extraordinary general meeting in June 2026, the company added camera modules and optical systems for robots and intelligent automation to its business purposes and is also developing a stereo-depth camera solution for robotics.

In September 2026 the company began mass production and initial shipment of a ToF-based 3D sensing camera module co-developed with ToF semiconductor specialist Hana Optronics, Inc., marking its entry into the robotics supply chain.

Amid a prolonged restructuring phase in the domestic lens industry, the company is viewed as a survivor built on high-resolution lens technology, competing alongside large module makers such as Samsung Electro-Mechanics and LG Innotek as well as smaller peers such as Powerlogics and Camsys.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual revenue fell for three straight years, from KRW 383.2 billion in 2022 to KRW 294.0 billion in 2023 (down 23.3% year on year), KRW 267.3 billion in 2024 (down 9.1%), and KRW 258.2 billion in 2025 (down 3.4%), with 2025 marking the smallest year in the 2022-2025 span.

Operating profit, however, moved from an operating loss of KRW 4.42 billion in 2022 to a wider operating loss of KRW 13.0 billion in 2023, a narrower operating loss of KRW 9.54 billion in 2024, and an operating profit of KRW 5.27 billion in 2025, turning positive from the prior year.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders followed a similar path, widening from a net loss of KRW 8.76 billion in 2022 to a net loss of KRW 22.1 billion in 2023, narrowing to a net loss of KRW 18.7 billion in 2024, and turning to a net profit of KRW 2.71 billion in 2025.

On a quarterly basis, revenue of KRW 70.7 billion and operating profit of KRW 1.85 billion in the second quarter of 2025 gave way to revenue of KRW 61.4 billion (down 13.2% quarter on quarter) and operating profit of KRW 1.1 billion (down 40.5%) in the third quarter, then revenue of KRW 48.3 billion (down 21.4%) and operating profit of KRW 1.41 billion (up 27.7%) in the fourth quarter.

The first quarter of 2026 improved to revenue of KRW 70.5 billion (up 46.0% quarter on quarter) and operating profit of KRW 1.78 billion (up 26.4%), but the second quarter reversed course with revenue of KRW 63.4 billion (down 10.1% quarter on quarter, down 10.4% year on year) and operating profit of just KRW 98.68 million (down 94.5% quarter on quarter, down 94.7% year on year).

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders swung from a net loss of KRW 152 million in the fourth quarter of 2025 (turning negative from the prior quarter) to a net profit of KRW 1.37 billion in the first quarter of 2026 (turning positive) and back to a net loss of KRW 588 million in the second quarter of 2026 (turning negative both quarter on quarter and year on year), leaving the trailing four-quarter sum (third quarter of 2025 through second quarter of 2026) at only KRW 1.56 billion.

Operating cash flow moved from KRW 28.1 billion in 2022 and KRW 7.77 billion in 2023 to negative KRW 767 million in 2024, before rebounding sharply to KRW 23.2 billion in 2025.

The debt-to-equity ratio eased from 122.5% in 2022, 114.3% in 2023, and 121.5% in 2024 to 98.3% in 2025, with total equity of KRW 64.8 billion (of which KRW 58.9 billion attributable to controlling shareholders) against total liabilities of KRW 63.7 billion, indicating a somewhat improved balance sheet.

05

Industry analysis

The domestic lens industry has gone through a prolonged restructuring phase, and CoAsia CM is cited as one of the survivors built on high-resolution lens technology.

The downstream smartphone market is shifting away from pure shipment-volume competition toward premium, high-specification product structures, raising the importance of rapid technical response and close collaboration with key customers.

The average first-half 2026 operating margin across Samsung Electronics' camera-module partner group was reported at 2.4%, while CoAsia CM's own first-half 2026 operating margin, based on revenue of KRW 133.9 billion and operating profit of KRW 1.8 billion, stood at 1.3%, below that peer-group average.

Camera modules and optical lenses are expanding in application beyond smartphones into robotics, autonomous driving, and wearables, with ToF 3D sensing technology emerging as a core component for robots' surrounding-environment recognition and obstacle detection.

In the competitive landscape, large module and component makers such as Samsung Electro-Mechanics and LG Innotek occupy the upper tier of the supply chain, while CoAsia CM sits among smaller peers alongside companies such as Powerlogics and Camsys.

Still, the integrated capability to design and produce both lenses and modules in-house is cited as a differentiating factor when entering new application areas such as robotics and automotive optics.

Outlook

Shinhan Investment Corp. forecast in a June 2026 report that CoAsia CM's 2026 revenue would reach KRW 309.8 billion (up 20.0% year on year) with operating profit of KRW 7.1 billion (up 34.7%), though it should be noted that this forecast predates the second-quarter 2026 results and diverged from the actual first-half cumulative performance.

The company has stated it is pursuing entry into the Galaxy S series for its camera-module business beyond its traditional A-series-centered supply, and has outlined plans to expand its ToF 3D sensing camera module business into non-handset areas such as automobiles and robots by 2027.

In September 2026, subsidiary CoAsia CM Korea began mass production and initial shipment of a ToF module co-developed with Hana Optronics, securing its first reference in the robotics supply chain.

At an extraordinary general meeting in June 2026, the company added camera modules and optical systems for robots and intelligent automation systems to its business purposes while also approving a resolution to transfer roughly KRW 90.2 billion of capital reserves into retained earnings, aimed at improving its balance sheet and strengthening the basis for medium-to-long-term shareholder returns.

In August of the same year, it completed a 5-for-1 reverse stock split (record date August 25, new shares listed September 14), which the company said was intended to improve its low-share-price image and preemptively address tightened listing-maintenance standards.

A series of open-market share purchases by management and subsidiary executives has continued as part of efforts to reinforce accountable management.

That said, with the company attributing the second-quarter 2026 operating profit slowdown to temporary client inventory adjustments amid a downstream industry slowdown, whether this inventory effect persists into the third quarter and beyond is a key variable to confirm in upcoming results.

Bull factors

Foundation for an Earnings Turnaround

After three consecutive years of operating losses from 2022 through 2024, the company turned to an operating profit of KRW 5.27 billion in 2025 and sustained profitability through the first half of 2026.

Operating cash flow also improved sharply from negative territory in 2024 to KRW 23.2 billion in 2025, translating the profit recovery into cash generation. The debt-to-equity ratio eased to 98.3% in 2025, modestly improving financial soundness. Expanded supply of high-resolution lenses has been the main driver of the margin recovery.

Expanding into Robotics and Automotive Optics

Subsidiary CoAsia CM Korea began mass production of a robotics ToF 3D sensing camera module co-developed with Hana Optronics in September 2026, securing its first reference in the robotics supply chain. The company has stated plans to expand this business into non-handset areas such as automobiles and robots by 2027.

Its integrated capability to design and produce both lenses and modules in-house is cited as a strength in entering new markets. The push to enter the Galaxy S series also represents an attempt to expand into higher-value-added mobile product lines.

Continued Shareholder-Value Initiatives

The company completed a 5-for-1 reverse stock split in August 2026 and transferred roughly KRW 90.2 billion of capital reserves into retained earnings, aiming to improve its balance sheet and strengthen the basis for medium-to-long-term shareholder returns.

The CEO and six executives each purchased 10,000 shares on the open market, and executives of subsidiary CoAsia CM Korea also joined in. The company stated its aims were to improve its low-share-price image and respond to tightened listing-maintenance standards.

The series of insider share purchases can be read as a factual signal of management's commitment to accountable governance.

Bear factors

Volatility Exposed by the Second-Quarter Profit Collapse

Second-quarter 2026 operating profit fell to KRW 98.68 million, down 94.5% quarter on quarter and 94.7% year on year, with net income attributable to controlling shareholders also turning negative. The company attributed this to client inventory adjustments amid a downstream industry slowdown.

The trailing four-quarter sum of net income attributable to controlling shareholders totaled only KRW 1.56 billion, underscoring how modest the quarterly profit scale has been. Whether the pattern of a strong first quarter followed by a weak second quarter repeats is something to confirm in upcoming quarterly results.

A Long-Term Contraction in Revenue Scale

Revenue fell for three consecutive years, from KRW 383.2 billion in 2022 to KRW 258.2 billion in 2025. While the company attributes part of this to a deliberate reduction of low-margin volume during its shift toward a profitability-focused product mix, the top line itself has clearly shrunk.

Its customer base is concentrated in Samsung Electronics, particularly the Galaxy A series, meaning results are closely tied to the sales trajectory of specific models.

The gap between Shinhan Investment Corp.'s June 2026 revenue-rebound forecast and the actual first-half results also illustrates the uncertainty around a top-line recovery.

New Business Still at an Early Stage

The robotics ToF 3D sensing module only began mass production and initial shipment in September 2026, and the scale of its contribution to revenue has not yet been confirmed.

The addition of robotics and intelligent-automation optical products to the company's stated business purposes also occurred only recently, in June 2026. Large existing component makers are also pursuing entry into the robotics and automotive optics module market, which could intensify competition. It will likely take time before the new business translates into a meaningful contribution to actual results.

10

Risk factors

Customer Concentration Risk

A substantial portion of revenue depends on supply to Samsung Electronics, particularly the Galaxy A series. Changes in the sales trajectory of specific models or in supplier-diversification policy could directly affect results.

The company is pursuing entry into the Galaxy S series, but the effect of customer diversification has not yet become visible.

Foreign-Exchange Exposure at Overseas Production Bases

Core production runs through Vietnamese units COASIAOPTICS VINA and CoAsia CM VINA JSC. Exchange-rate movements between the Korean won and the Vietnamese dong or U.S. dollar are a structural factor that can affect costs and earnings.

Changes in local labor costs, tariffs, and logistics expenses tied to operating overseas production bases can also affect profitability.

New-Business Execution Risk

New businesses such as robotics ToF modules and automotive lenses are still in the early stages of mass production, and it remains uncertain whether production stabilization and customer expansion will proceed as planned.

Because the addition of new business purposes and related organizational setup occurred only recently, a time lag before any revenue contribution is possible. Whether the company can sustain a differentiated position amid competition from larger existing component makers is also a variable to watch.

Overall view

CoAsia CM turned from three consecutive years of operating losses between 2022 and 2024 to an operating profit in 2025 and sustained that profitability through the first half of 2026, but the sharp decline in second-quarter operating profit again demonstrated considerable quarter-to-quarter volatility.

Revenue declined for three straight years from 2022 before slowing to a modest decline in 2025, with the company citing a deliberate reduction of low-margin volume and a shift toward high-resolution products as the backdrop for its margin recovery.

The start of mass production for its robotics ToF 3D sensing module and its push to enter the Galaxy S series represent efforts to find new growth pillars, but these remain at an early stage with their actual revenue contribution yet to be confirmed.

The reverse stock split completed in August 2026, the transfer of capital reserves into retained earnings, and the series of insider share purchases are factual signals of balance-sheet realignment and an intent to enhance shareholder value.

At the same time, the company's heavy reliance on Samsung Electronics and the Galaxy A series, its foreign-exchange exposure through Vietnamese production bases, and the early stage of its new businesses are factors that warrant balanced consideration.

Upcoming quarterly results should help clarify whether the second-quarter weakness was a temporary inventory adjustment or the start of a more trend-driven change.
