Report overview

Plasticizer Losses Narrow, but Sustained Profit Is Unproven

After an operating loss in 2025, Aekyung Chemical posted operating profit in all three quarters of 2026, while net income attributable to controlling shareholders was positive only in the second quarter.

02

Business structure

Aekyung Chemical runs four business segments: plasticizers, synthetic resins, household and specialty chemicals, and bio & energy. Based on its 2024 annual report, plasticizers accounted for 47.6% of sales, household and specialty chemicals 20.9%, bio & energy 16.1%, synthetic resins 12.8%, and other 2.6%.

The plasticizer business covers PA, plasticizers and lubricant base oils, and the company is described as the largest domestic plasticizer maker. Plasticizer segment sales declined from about KRW 1.01 trillion in 2022 to KRW 865.5 billion in 2023, KRW 781.3 billion in 2024 and KRW 626.2 billion in 2025.

Household chemicals center on surfactants and refined glycerin, while bio & energy centers on biodiesel, bio heavy oil and hard carbon. Synthetic resins cover composite materials, coating resins and PU.

At the end of 2025, exports accounted for 73.33% of PA, plasticizer and lubricant base oil sales, against 0.02% for the biodiesel line, so overseas exposure differs sharply by product.

Key customers are industrial users such as building materials, medical supplies, automotive and electric-wire makers, many of which process PVC. Commodity products such as plasticizers face price pressure from Chinese capacity additions.

The company merged Aekyung Chemical's predecessors in 2021 to build an integrated chemical platform, and it is pursuing efficiency gains alongside new businesses.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual sales fell from KRW 2.18 trillion in 2022 to KRW 1.79 trillion in 2023 (down 17.6% year on year), KRW 1.64 trillion in 2024 (down 8.4%) and KRW 1.45 trillion in 2025 (down 11.6%).

Sales declined for three consecutive years from 2023 through 2025, and 2022 was the largest year and 2025 the smallest within 2022 to 2025.

Operating profit fell from KRW 95.1 billion in 2022 to KRW 45.1 billion in 2023 (down 52.6%) and KRW 15.5 billion in 2024 (down 65.7%), then turned into an operating loss of KRW 10.2 billion in 2025. The operating margin moved from 4.4% in 2022 to 2.5% in 2023, 0.9% in 2024 and -0.7% in 2025.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders went from KRW 60.1 billion in 2022 to KRW 33.1 billion in 2023 (down 45.0%) and KRW 3.98 billion in 2024 (down 88.0%), and became a net loss of KRW 2.95 billion in 2025.

Net income including non-controlling interests, at a net loss of KRW 4.93 billion in 2025, is a separate figure and should not be read as the controlling-shareholder result. Industry analysis points to cost of sales rising from 89.7% of sales in 2022 to above 90% in 2023 and 91.4% in 2024.

Raw material prices fell, but selling prices fell further, which compressed spreads. Quarterly, the third quarter of 2025 showed sales of KRW 357.8 billion and an operating loss of KRW 7.34 billion, and the fourth quarter sales of KRW 348.4 billion with an operating loss of KRW 5.51 billion.

The first quarter of 2026 turned to operating profit of KRW 5.82 billion on sales of KRW 370.6 billion. Second-quarter sales rose to KRW 503.3 billion (up 35.8% quarter on quarter) and operating profit to KRW 42.8 billion (up 636.6%).

Third-quarter sales of KRW 480.6 billion (down 4.5% quarter on quarter, up 34.3% year on year) stayed high, but operating profit fell to KRW 2.85 billion (down 93.3% quarter on quarter).

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders swung from KRW 14.5 billion in the third quarter of 2025 to a net loss of KRW 9.69 billion in the fourth quarter, a net loss of KRW 1.25 billion in the first quarter of 2026, a profit of KRW 16.4 billion in the second quarter and a net loss of KRW 4.84 billion in the third.

Whether second-quarter profit was one-off, and what drove the third-quarter drop, are points to confirm in the quarterly report's segment items and notes.

05

Industry analysis

The petrochemical cycle is under pressure as Chinese capacity additions and weak global demand reinforce each other. Commodity products such as plasticizers and synthetic resins have seen spreads narrow, and industry observers see no quick reversal in the domestic petrochemical cycle.

Between 2022 and 2024, many domestic chemical companies swung to losses while Aekyung Chemical stayed profitable, but it recorded its first annual loss since the merger in 2025. A 2025 report said foreign suppliers, including Chinese producers, were eroding the domestic plasticizer market.

Prosecutors indicted six petrochemical companies, including LG Chem and LOTTE Fine Chemical, along with 37 current and former executives in early October 2026, over alleged price collusion in eight products including PVC and plasticizers from January 2021 to April 2026.

The sales volume prosecutors attributed to the alleged collusion was KRW 16.40 trillion, and four Aekyung Chemical employees were also indicted. A press report noted that official plasticizer selling prices fell even during the period under suspicion.

Aekyung Chemical is a domestic plasticizer leader, but the decline in plasticizer sales has exposed the limits of relying on commodity products. Its new businesses, including TPC and hard carbon, are the company's attempt to reduce that dependence.

06

Outlook

Aekyung Chemical's new businesses fall into four areas: TPC, anode materials, hard carbon and eco-friendly plasticizers. TPC (terephthaloyl chloride) is a core raw material for aramid fiber, and the Ulsan plant has a capacity of 15,000 tons, with full-scale production reported as imminent in 2026.

Reports also suggest the company could replace TPC volumes that had depended on Chinese imports. In December 2025 the company decided to invest in a production line expansion at its Jeonju plant, aiming to build it into the first mass-production facility for sodium-ion battery anode materials.

Hard carbon is at pilot production, and the timing of commercial output will be a key variable. Eco-friendly plasticizers made from recycled PET have moved into mass production, and the company has reported ISCC PLUS and GRS certifications.

On September 30, 2026 the company filed a corrected disclosure on new facility investment, and the subsequent execution will show the scale of capital spending. Borrowings that rose from KRW 250.4 billion in 2022 to KRW 504.9 billion in 2025 may limit room for further investment funding.

The criminal trial over the alleged collusion and any administrative sanctions remain variables for future cash flow and investment capacity. The next checkpoint is segment sales and capex execution for the new businesses in the 2026 annual report.

08

Bull factors

Operating profit back in the black

Operating profit turned positive in the first quarter of 2026 at KRW 5.82 billion and rose to KRW 42.8 billion in the second quarter. Three straight quarters of positive operating profit after the 2025 loss point to partial recovery in operating capacity.

If the operating margin improves from -0.7% in 2025, there is room to widen the earnings recovery. Still, third-quarter operating profit of KRW 2.85 billion fell 93.3% from the second quarter, so quarterly volatility is large.

Expanding new-business portfolio

TPC, used in aramid fiber, is reported to be entering full-scale production in 2026 at a 15,000-ton Ulsan plant. Sodium-ion battery anode material is being built out through a production-line expansion at the Jeonju plant. Eco-friendly plasticizers from recycled PET have secured ISCC PLUS and GRS certifications. If these businesses reduce reliance on commodity products, earnings volatility could narrow over time.

Asset sales and cash build

In 2025 the company sold the Joongbu Country Club golf course for KRW 169 billion to secure cash. Cash and cash equivalents rose to KRW 190.7 billion at the end of the third quarter of 2025, from KRW 66.8 billion at the end of 2024. Over the same period, net borrowings fell from KRW 330.3 billion to KRW 289.2 billion. Asset sales have provided a liquidity buffer that eases the financial burden during the earnings slump.

Bear factors

Cost ratio stuck high amid oversupply

Cost of sales rose above 90% of sales in 2023 and reached 91.4% in 2024, according to industry analysis. Chinese capacity additions are squeezing plasticizer and synthetic resin spreads. Plasticizer segment sales fell from about KRW 1.01 trillion in 2022 to KRW 626.2 billion in 2025. While the cost ratio stays above 90%, the scope for the operating margin to recover may stay limited.

Rising borrowings and weaker cash flow

Borrowings rose from KRW 250.4 billion in 2022 to KRW 504.9 billion in 2025. Operating cash flow fell from KRW 137 billion in 2022 to KRW 27.9 billion in 2025. The liabilities-to-equity ratio climbed from 69.5% to 94.6% over the same span. Borrowings increasing while dividends continued could narrow financial flexibility.

Collusion indictment and unstable earnings

In early October 2026 prosecutors indicted six petrochemical companies and 37 current and former executives for alleged violations of the Fair Trade Act, including four Aekyung Chemical employees. The outcome of the criminal trial will determine the resulting financial burden.

On earnings, net income attributable to controlling shareholders swung back to a net loss of KRW 4.84 billion in the third quarter of 2026, so quarterly profit has not stabilized.

Risk factors

Ulsan TPC ramp-up

The Ulsan TPC plant, with capacity of 15,000 tons, has been reported as moving into full-scale production. If process stabilization is delayed, the replacement of Chinese-imported TPC volumes could be pushed back as well.

TPC is a core raw material for aramid fiber, so new-business revenue depends on the plant reaching stable utilization. If process stabilization or customer qualification takes longer, the new business may do little to reduce reliance on commodity products.

Export dependence

The PA, plasticizer and lubricant base oil line accounts for 73.33% of its sales through exports, a far higher overseas share than in the domestic market. Changes in overseas demand or export prices for this line would affect sales and operating profit directly. If export customers were lost or overseas selling terms worsened, both volumes and margins could move together.

Hard carbon and anode commercialization

Hard carbon remains at pilot production, and the timing of commercial output will decide the outcome of the business. The sodium-ion battery anode material is tied to the production-line expansion at the Jeonju plant, decided in December 2025.

If commercial output comes later than planned, depreciation and fixed costs from the new lines could hit earnings before the business contributes revenue.

Overall view

Aekyung Chemical's sales peaked at KRW 2.18 trillion in 2022 and then declined for three straight years through 2025, while the operating margin fell from 4.4% to -0.7%, turning the year into an operating loss in 2025.

Operating profit was positive in all three quarters of 2026, but net income attributable to controlling shareholders was positive only in the second quarter at KRW 16.4 billion, and the quarter-to-quarter swings mean the durability of the recovery is still unproven.

The main drivers are Chinese capacity additions that created oversupply and a narrowing of spreads in plasticizer-led commodity products, with cost of sales stuck at 91.4% in 2024.

Bullish factors include the recovery in quarterly operating profit, investment in new businesses such as TPC, anode materials and hard carbon, and better liquidity from the Joongbu Country Club sale and higher cash balances.

Bearish factors include a balance sheet in which borrowings rose from KRW 250.4 billion in 2022 to KRW 504.9 billion in 2025 and the liabilities-to-equity ratio reached 94.6%, operating cash flow of KRW 27.9 billion in 2025, and the criminal trial over petrochemical collusion.

Dividends continued after the 2025 loss, but with a thin earnings base their sustainability depends on how fast profit recovers.

The next checkpoints are the outcome of the collusion trial, the full-year operating margin and liabilities-to-equity ratio in the 2026 annual report, and when the new-business plants reach operation.
