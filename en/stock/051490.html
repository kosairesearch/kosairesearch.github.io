02

Business structure

Nara Mold AND DIE was spun off in 1999 from the die-making plant of LG Electronics' production technology center and listed on KOSDAQ in 2001. The core business is precision mold making for automotive, display and home appliance applications.

Building on that technology, the company expanded into mass production of EV battery pack parts and ESS components, including end plates that hold and protect battery cells and gaskets that secure pack sealing. Its overseas footprint includes seven consolidated subsidiaries.

As of December 2025, exports accounted for 61.9% of sales and domestic sales for 38.1%. In molds, management says it is strengthening price competitiveness and shortening delivery times through technology upgrades.

Because both molds and battery parts are tied to automotive and EV demand, a shift in that demand reaches both businesses at once. Detailed product-level sales splits and customer concentration are not presented in numerical form here, so the discussion focuses on the direction of the business structure.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual sales rose 5.9% to KRW 231 billion in 2023, the peak of the period, then fell 11.8% to KRW 203.8 billion in 2024 and 9.4% to KRW 184.6 billion in 2025, a two-year decline.

Operating profit fell from KRW 10.5 billion in 2022 and KRW 10.7 billion in 2023 to KRW 4.98 billion in 2024, and then turned into an operating loss of KRW 79.79 million in 2025. Operating margin moved from 4.8% in 2022 and 4.6% in 2023 to 2.4% in 2024 and -0.0% in 2025.

The company attributed the 2025 deterioration to weaker EV market conditions, which cut sales, and to a higher cost ratio. Net income growth came from financial asset valuation gains and foreign currency translation gains, so the 2025 profit rise did not come from operations.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 5.26 billion in 2025, up 46.3%, after a low of KRW 3.6 billion in 2024. Operating cash flow fell from KRW 16.2 billion in 2024 to KRW 6.87 billion in 2025.

Debt-to-equity rose from 137.0% in 2023 to 148.8% in 2025, with total liabilities of KRW 160.7 billion against total equity of KRW 108 billion at year-end 2025.

Quarterly sales dropped to KRW 38.3 billion in the first quarter of 2026, then rose to KRW 47.7 billion and KRW 58.9 billion in the second and third quarters, two straight quarters of growth. Operating profit moved to KRW 101 million in the second quarter and KRW 5.08 billion in the third.

Yet the controlling-shareholder net loss in the third quarter was KRW 4.55 billion, and the gap between operating profit and net loss depends on non-operating items to be checked in the periodic report.

05

Industry analysis

Demand for Nara Mold AND DIE splits between auto, appliance and display molds and EV battery pack parts. The battery side has been hit by weaker global EV demand and by the effects of environmental regulation and support policies in each country.

The company itself cited a weaker EV market as the cause of its 2025 sales decline. On the mold side, an equity information site, Enterprise Monitor, commented that growth is expected from continued EV market expansion and rising project volume; this is a third-party commentary, not company guidance.

In July 2024, press reports linked the stock's move to LG Energy Solution's large LFP supply contract with Renault, but the reports described this as market interpretation rather than a confirmed supply relationship.

Against peers, the company emphasizes mold technology, price competitiveness and shorter delivery times. Quarterly sales have grown for two consecutive quarters since the first-quarter 2026 low, but they have not yet returned to the level seen in early 2025. Whether the cycle has bottomed depends on EV sales trends and policy changes.

Building on mold technology, it has extended into key battery pack components such as end plates and gaskets. Bear factors

Two years of sales decline and an operating loss

Sales peaked at KRW 231 billion in 2023 and then declined to KRW 203.8 billion in 2024 and KRW 184.6 billion in 2025. Operating profit fell to KRW 4.98 billion in 2024 and turned into a KRW 79.79 million operating loss in 2025. Operating margin dropped from 4.8% in 2022 to -0.0% in 2025. Falling sales and a higher cost ratio arriving together is the main weakness.

Accumulated net losses and financial burden

Net losses attributable to controlling shareholders have run for four consecutive quarters from the fourth quarter of 2025 through the third quarter of 2026, totaling KRW 9.41 billion. The debt-to-equity ratio rose from 137.0% in 2023 to 148.8% in 2025.

Operating cash flow fell from KRW 16.2 billion in 2024 to KRW 6.87 billion in 2025, a sign of weaker cash generation from operations.

EV market conditions and policy variables

The EV battery business has seen profitability weaken under the influence of global EV demand and national environmental regulation and support policies. The company itself cited a weaker EV market as a reason for the 2025 sales decline. Because downstream demand is sensitive to policy, the timing of any demand recovery remains uncertain.

Risk factors

Demand concentration

Both the mold business and the battery pack parts business depend on automotive and EV demand. When both move in the same direction, weakness in one can quickly pass through to earnings in the other. Policy changes or production adjustments by automakers flow directly into results.

Earnings quality and financial items

The 2025 net income increase came from financial asset valuation gains and foreign currency translation gains, while operations posted a loss. Financial items can reverse with exchange rates and valuation changes, so the stability of net income is limited.

Quarters in which operating profit and net losses diverge indicate that the profit structure needs close review.

Balance sheet and cash generation

At the end of 2025, total liabilities were KRW 160.7 billion against total equity of KRW 108 billion, a debt-to-equity ratio of 148.8%. Operating cash flow fell to KRW 6.87 billion in 2025. If net losses continue, equity capacity could erode, making debt management an important task.

Overall view

Nara Mold AND DIE has built on its mold technology to extend into EV battery pack parts, with exports at 61.9% of sales. In 2025, sales fell for a second straight year and operations turned to a loss, while the net income rise came from financial items.

The clearest change in 2026 is that sales rose for two consecutive quarters and third-quarter operating profit recovered to KRW 5.08 billion.
