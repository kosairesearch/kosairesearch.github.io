Lead-acid core meets the battery pivot test

02

Business structure

Sebang Global Battery, founded in 1952, makes automotive and industrial batteries and also runs a lithium-battery parts business. Lead-acid batteries account for about 85% of sales and EV batteries for about 15%, according to a company profile summary.

Within lead-acid, starter batteries for vehicles and industrial batteries are the main lines, and the company is described as the top domestic exporter of starter batteries. Industrial batteries serve as backup power for data-center UPS systems and power-plant emergency supply.

Customers include Hyundai Motor, KIA, BMW and Volkswagen, to which it has supplied starter batteries over the long term. The EV segment sits with subsidiary Sebang Lithium Battery (SLB), which buys cells from outside cell makers and assembles modules and packs for vehicle and ESS customers.

SLB's revenue was KRW 333.4 billion in 2024 and KRW 330.1 billion in 2025, roughly flat, and reporting pointed to the slowdown in EV demand as a cause. The controlling side holds 40.18% through Sebang and four related parties, and GS Yuasa International holds 16.16% (as of August 2026). In batteries, SLB sits between cell suppliers and vehicle and ESS customers.

04

Earnings analysis

On a DART-filed basis, sales rose from KRW 1.47 trillion in 2022 to KRW 2.14 trillion in 2025. Sales reached KRW 1.68 trillion in 2023 (up 14.4% year on year) and KRW 2.06 trillion in 2024 (up 22.2%), then grew only 4.0% in 2025.

Operating profit climbed from KRW 81.1 billion in 2022 to KRW 179.7 billion in 2024, the largest year of 2022 to 2025, before falling 14.4% to KRW 153.8 billion in 2025. The operating margin moved from 5.5% in 2022 to 7.7% in 2023 and 8.7% in 2024, then slipped to 7.2% in 2025.

The 2025 decline was attributed to US tariffs, exchange rates and temporary cost increases. Net income attributable to controlling shareholders rose from KRW 42.8 billion in 2022 to KRW 169.4 billion in 2024 and fell 16.7% to KRW 141.1 billion in 2025.

Net income including non-controlling interests was KRW 141.4 billion in 2025, a different figure from the attributable number.

Quarterly, operating profit was KRW 26.8 billion in the third quarter of 2025, a period when bonus payments, labor costs and freight raised the cost burden, then recovered to KRW 40.2 billion in the fourth quarter.

It was KRW 33.3 billion in the first quarter of 2026 and KRW 35.3 billion in the second, with the first quarter marked by weaker profitability from raw-material price swings.

In the third quarter of 2026 sales were KRW 527.7 billion, flat year on year, but operating profit was KRW 3.29 billion, down 87.7%, and the company posted a net loss attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 24.6 billion, a swing into loss.

At year-end 2025 total liabilities were KRW 580.1 billion against total equity of KRW 1.66 trillion, a debt ratio of 34.9%.

05

Industry analysis

Lead-acid demand rests on vehicle starter batteries and industrial backup power. Coverage in September 2026 noted that EV demand growth has slowed while automakers keep expanding hybrid models, which is lifting demand for related batteries.

NH Investment & Securities noted in a 2025 report that growing AGM battery sales are improving average selling prices and profitability. Raw-material lead prices, exchange rates and US tariffs remain the main swing factors for margins.

In the third quarter of 2025, a gap between cost and selling prices tied to non-ferrous metal costs and a weaker won was cited as a drag on profitability. In batteries, SLB is a system integrator rather than a cell maker: it takes cells from suppliers and turns them into modules and packs for vehicle and ESS customers.

Coverage describes its competitive position as resting on pack design and mass-production capability rather than cell output. In lead-acid, the company's ranking as the top domestic exporter of starter batteries is the basis of its position. The cycle reads as a phase in which the EV slowdown and hybrid expansion are happening together.

Outlook

The mid-term targets the company has published are in its value-up plan posted in March 2026. It sets 7% annual sales growth through 2028 and a dividend payout ratio of 25%, maintained over time.

On orders, a September 2026 report said SLB won long-term supply contracts for battery packs for a new Hyundai hybrid model and for LG Energy Solution's new ESS battery. The combined value is an industry estimate above KRW 3 trillion, which the report said was more than nine times SLB's 2025 revenue.

That figure comes from the report and industry estimates, not from a company disclosure. The Ohio unit in the United States, a North American production base, received funds in a July 2026 share acquisition, and the North American facility investment was reported at KRW 150 billion in total.

The same report said that if these orders are reflected in revenue, the group's core business could shift toward lithium-ion batteries. The November 2026 World Class Product selection is a confirmed fact about export competitiveness.

The next checkpoint is when these orders show up in reported revenue and whether the company discloses their size.

08

Bull factors

Starter battery export strength

The company is described as the top domestic exporter of starter batteries, and its selection as a 2026 World Class Product in November 2026 reconfirmed its quality competitiveness. Industrial batteries serve data-center UPS systems and power-plant emergency supply, giving a broad demand base.

NH Investment & Securities noted in a 2025 report that rising AGM battery sales were lifting average selling prices and profitability.

Subsidiary order visibility

According to a September 2026 report, SLB won multi-year supply contracts for battery packs for a Hyundai hybrid model and for LG Energy Solution's new ESS battery. The industry estimate of the combined value exceeds KRW 3 trillion, more than nine times SLB's 2025 revenue. If these orders are booked as revenue, the group's core could shift toward lithium-ion batteries.

Shareholder returns and value-up plan

In its March 2026 value-up plan the company set 7% sales growth through 2028 and a 25% dividend payout ratio. It retired 140,000 treasury shares in January 2026 and has used treasury stock to pay part of employee bonuses. Progress against these targets will show up in the periodic reports.

Bear factors

Margin decline and a swing into loss

The operating margin fell from 8.7% in 2024 to 7.2% in 2025, and in the third quarter of 2026 operating profit was KRW 3.29 billion with a net loss attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 24.6 billion. A first-quarter 2026 commentary pointed to weaker profitability from raw-material price swings.

Attributable net income for the latest four quarters, KRW 88 billion, is slightly more than half of the KRW 169.4 billion recorded in 2024.

SLB's earnings and funding needs

SLB's revenue was roughly flat, moving from KRW 333.4 billion in 2024 to KRW 330.1 billion in 2025. One financial analysis site described SLB as having turned profitable, while another source said its lithium and ESS business remains in an early loss phase with uncertain payback. Capital injections for the North American ESS push leave the parent as the main source of funding.

Tariff, FX and input-cost pressure

The 2025 operating profit decline was attributed to US tariffs, exchange rates and temporary cost increases. In the third quarter of 2025, bonus payments and rising labor and freight costs added to the burden, and higher non-ferrous metal costs also weighed on profitability. Because exports are a large share of sales, the company is seen as sensitive to exchange-rate and tariff moves.

10

Risk factors

Tariffs and FX

With a large export base, US tariffs and won-dollar moves feed directly into margins. The same factors were cited for the 2025 operating profit decline. How far expanding overseas production offsets this burden will have to be judged from upcoming quarterly results.

Raw-material (lead) prices

Swings in lead prices, the main raw material for lead-acid batteries, are a key variable for profitability. In the first quarter of 2026, raw-material price swings were named as the direct cause of weaker profitability. How much of the cost increase can be passed through to selling prices will determine the operating margin.

Subsidiary investment and reliance on order reports

The size of SLB's orders rests on media reports and industry estimates, not on a company disclosure. North American facility spending and capital raises are linked, so a delay in revenue recognition could lengthen the payback period. Assessments of SLB's earnings conflict, with some describing a turn to profit and others an early loss phase.

12

Overall view

Sebang Global Battery's sales rose through 2025 after 2022, but operating profit peaked in 2024 and fell in 2025, and in the third quarter of 2026 it dropped to KRW 3.29 billion. The attributable net loss of KRW 24.6 billion that quarter shows margin pressure in the core business appearing at the quarterly level.

Strengths include export competitiveness in starter batteries, media reports of large SLB orders, and shareholder-return moves such as the value-up plan with 2028 targets and treasury share retirement.

Weaknesses include cost variables such as tariffs, exchange rates and lead prices, plus uncertainty over SLB's earnings and its funding needs. The order values rest on industry estimates, so the timing of revenue recognition in company disclosures is the most important checkpoint.

On valuation, the current PER is above the top of the year-end range and the PBR below its bottom, and with a thinner earnings base, multiple readings depend on future earnings assumptions. The 2026 annual report, the next periodic filing, will disclose full-year operating margin and SLB's earnings together.

The stock is in a phase where the recovery of the lead-acid core and the commercialization of SLB are proceeding at the same time.
