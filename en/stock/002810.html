Report overview

Chemical profit rebounds as auto parts absorb tariff pressure

02

Business structure

Samyung Trading was founded in 1959 and listed on the KOSPI in 1988. It runs three businesses: the sale of chemical products, export sales of plastic eyeglass lenses for vision correction, and auto parts manufacturing through a subsidiary.

The chemicals business supplies basic petrochemical derivatives to Korea's chemical industry, and revenue is strongly affected by volumes and unit prices. A first-quarter 2026 earnings summary noted that even when volumes rose, average unit prices fell amid a petrochemical downturn, which reduced sales.

The auto parts business is affected by U.S. tariff policy, shifts in mobility technology and the restructuring of global supply chains. The lens business is an export sales model, and the company has said it aims to move lens production to a China plant. Segment sales mix varies by data provider.

On a second-quarter 2026 sales basis, Butler puts chemicals at 50%, auto parts at 44% and eyeglass lenses at 6%. As of year-end 2025, JobKorea shows auto-related parts at 46%, general chemical products at 45%, eyeglass lenses at 6% and electronic parts and leasing at 2%.

The two datasets differ in timing and classification, but both show chemicals and auto parts making up most of revenue. Chemical customers are domestic chemical-industry buyers, and volume secured and unit prices are the core variables. Auto parts revenue depends on changes in the automaker supply chain.

04

Earnings analysis

On a disclosed annual basis, revenue declined from KRW 492.5 billion in 2022 to KRW 446.9 billion in 2025, making 2022 the largest year and 2025 the smallest.

Operating profit fell from KRW 19.4 billion in 2022 to KRW 17.2 billion in 2023 and KRW 18.4 billion in 2024, then to KRW 12.8 billion in 2025, down 30.5% year on year. The operating margin slipped from 3.9% in 2022 to 2.9% in 2025.

Press coverage attributed the 2025 decline to weak petrochemical market conditions and intensifying competition that cut chemicals profit, along with lower auto parts profitability linked to U.S. tariff policy.

Owner-attributable net income bottomed at KRW 50.1 billion in 2023 and then rose to KRW 56.8 billion in 2024 and KRW 58.6 billion in 2025. That 2025 net income rose while operating profit fell fits with a non-operating income structure, based on JobKorea's separate-statement data.

Net income including non-controlling interests was KRW 57.7 billion in 2022 and KRW 61.4 billion in 2025, and should be read separately from owner-attributable figures.

Quarterly, operating income swung from a KRW 1.04 billion loss in Q4 2025 to KRW 4.51 billion in Q1 2026 and KRW 9.03 billion in Q2, up 100.2% quarter on quarter.

Q3 2026 operating income of KRW 3.78 billion fell 58.2% from the prior quarter but rose 14.4% year on year, on revenue of KRW 121.3 billion, up 9.9% year on year. A WiseReport summary as of August 31, 2026 put first-half 2026 revenue down 1.3% year on year while operating income rose 29.0%. Quarterly swings are best explained by chemical unit prices and auto parts sales.

05

Industry analysis

Chemical product distribution depends on the prices and volumes of basic petrochemical products. In 2025, a weak petrochemical market and rising competition reduced profit in the chemicals division.

In the first half of 2026, volumes fell amid the Middle East conflict, but WiseReport summarized that higher product-level prices and early volume purchases lifted profit sharply.

Still, in Q1 2026 chemical volumes rose while average unit prices fell in the petrochemical downturn, so price and volume effects pulled in different directions. The auto parts division saw sales decline as mobility technology shifts, global supply-chain restructuring and U.S. tariff policy overlapped.

Even so, results improved, which WiseReport attributed to cost-structure innovation and cooperation across business units.

In cycle terms, chemical products move between price-recovery and downturn phases, so whether the first-half 2026 profit gain reflects a turn in the cycle or a one-off factor needs to be tracked over several quarters.

Within the consumer and distribution category, Samyung Trading combines chemical product sales with auto parts manufacturing, a mix that differs from integrated trading houses such as POSCO International and LX International. The lens business is the smallest revenue share but is undergoing a production shift to a China plant.

Outlook

WiseReport's earnings commentary attributed the first-half 2026 profit improvement to higher product-level prices in chemicals and early volume purchases.

Q3 2026 operating income of KRW 3.78 billion was below the KRW 9.03 billion in Q2, so whether the price effect carried into periods after the first half is the next point to verify.

The auto parts division is positioning cost-structure innovation and cooperation across business units as a response to external policy changes. The lens division aims to move production to a China plant, so the timing of that move and any change in production costs are items to watch.

At the March 26, 2026 general shareholders' meeting, cash and in-kind dividends for fiscal 2025 were approved. The same meeting also passed an amendment to the articles of incorporation and approved a plan to dispose of treasury shares.

The largest shareholder and related parties hold 46.74%, as of a June 29, 2026 change, and treasury shares account for 2.95%. In the 2026 annual report, the most direct check will be whether the full-year 2026 operating margin compares favorably with 2.9% in 2025.

First-half 2026 operating income stayed positive across both quarters, so the key question is whether full-year profit holds without a return to an operating loss. These points are drawn from reported results and business structure, not from a company forecast.

Shareholder returns and a simple ownership structure

Bear factors

Profitability decline in 2025

Full-year 2025 operating profit was KRW 12.8 billion, down 30.5%, and the 2.9% operating margin is the lowest of the 2022 to 2025 period. Weak petrochemical conditions and competition reduced chemicals profit. Auto parts profitability also declined in 2025 under U.S. tariff policy.

Pressure on auto parts sales

The auto parts division's sales fell in Q1 2026 amid mobility technology shifts, supply-chain restructuring and U.S. tariff policy. Cost innovation is being used as a response, but it has not reversed the sales decline.

Because this division is a large share of revenue, tariff and supply-chain changes feed directly into group results.

Earnings dependent on unit prices

In chemicals, sales can fall even when volumes rise if average unit prices drop in a petrochemical downturn. Q3 2026 operating income of KRW 3.78 billion was down 58.2% from the prior quarter. Because the recovery leaned on prices and early purchases, a reversal in prices could widen quarterly swings.

10

Risk factors

Tariffs and trade policy

U.S. tariff policy was already cited in 2025 coverage as weighing on auto parts profitability and sales. A change in tariff levels or scope could feed into this division's results quickly. The key question is how much cost innovation can absorb the tariff burden.

Petrochemical market and product prices

Chemical-division profit moves widely with the price cycle of basic petrochemical products and the intensity of competition. Weak market conditions and rising competition cut chemicals profit in 2025, and higher prices lifted it in the first half of 2026. External shocks such as the Middle East conflict affect volumes and unit prices together.

Execution of overseas production shift

The lens division aims to move production to a China plant. Production disruptions or higher costs during the move could affect its export sales model. The timing of the move and post-move cost changes should be tracked in company disclosures and quarterly results.

12

Overall view

Samyung Trading combines chemical product sales, eyeglass lens exports and auto parts manufacturing. Full-year 2025 operating profit fell to KRW 12.8 billion and the operating margin to 2.9%, yet owner-attributable net income has risen in each of 2024 and 2025 after the 2023 low.

Operating income stayed positive across the first three quarters of 2026, and owner-attributable net income over the last four quarters totals KRW 67.7 billion. The first-half recovery leans heavily on chemical product prices and early volume purchases, so quarterly swings could widen if prices reverse.

Auto parts sales have declined under U.S. tariff policy and supply-chain restructuring, and the company is responding with cost innovation. The share price sits below book value, and dividend resolutions have continued.
