Capro Shifts From Caprolactam To Hydrogen And Sulfuric Acid

Capro, once Korea's sole caprolactam producer, has halted caprolactam production and is pivoting to hydrogen, sulfuric acid and ammonium carbonate under new owner Taewha Group, with both revenue recovery and profitability still works in progress.

02

Business structure

Founded as a state enterprise in 1969, Capro dominated Korea's caprolactam (nylon raw material) market for over half a century as the country's sole producer, but profitability deteriorated sharply amid expanding low-cost Chinese caprolactam supply and weakening nylon demand.

In October 2022 the No.3 plant that produced hydrogen for caprolactam manufacturing was shut down, and the company subsequently halted caprolactam and ammonium sulfate fertilizer production altogether.

As accumulated losses and capital impairment deepened, full capital impairment triggered delisting grounds in early 2024, leading to a trading halt and administrative-issue designation; that same year a Taewha Group-affiliated TMC consortium took control through a third-party rights issue, debt-to-equity conversion and a 5-for-1 reverse stock split, steering the company toward financial normalization.

After the takeover, Capro fully dismantled its caprolactam facilities and repurposed existing hydrogen infrastructure to restructure around three new businesses: high-purity (99.999%) hydrogen, sulfuric acid produced via direct sulfur combustion (200,000 tons annually), and ammonium carbonate as a urea solution substitute.

In May 2025 the company completed Korea's largest hydrogen shipment center, capable of producing 20,000 Nm3 per hour and simultaneously charging 18 tube trailers, supplying roughly 400 hydrogen stations nationwide, and began piped hydrogen supply to companies including S-OIL, Approtium and SDG.

Sulfuric acid is supplied as a base material to advanced industries such as secondary battery precursors and aramid fiber, and the company has obtained food-additive-grade certification for high-purity products to pursue higher-value markets.

Alongside this, the company has pursued asset efficiency measures such as selling a non-core warehouse site at the Ulsan plant and selling steam generated from waste heat.

Having exited the caprolactam market entirely, Capro is now at an early entry stage in the hydrogen and sulfuric acid markets, competing against industrial gas and chemical conglomerate affiliates as well as regional rivals.

04

Earnings analysis

Consolidated annual revenue fell sharply from KRW 427.19 billion in 2022 to KRW 75.59 billion in 2023 and KRW 7.07 billion in 2024, before rebounding to KRW 24.64 billion in 2025.

Operating losses narrowed each year, from KRW 122.28 billion in 2022 to KRW 39.47 billion in 2023, KRW 32.39 billion in 2024, and KRW 21.12 billion in 2025.

Owner net income, however, swung from losses of KRW 186.21 billion in 2022 and KRW 49.11 billion in 2023 to a profit of KRW 33.19 billion in 2024 — likely reflecting non-operating factors tied to the debt-to-equity conversion and capital restructuring that year — before reverting to a net loss of KRW 32.36 billion in 2025.

By quarter, Q2 2025 revenue was KRW 12.06 billion with an operating profit of KRW 17.20 billion, yet the owner net loss continued at KRW 11.74 billion; Q3 2025 stayed in the black operationally with revenue of KRW 5.68 billion and operating profit of KRW 4.84 billion.

Q4 2025 revenue fell to KRW 4.56 billion while the operating loss widened sharply to KRW 37.07 billion, apparently reflecting a large one-off item.

In 2026, losses narrowed markedly, from a Q1 operating loss of KRW 3.43 billion on revenue of KRW 7.42 billion to a Q2 operating loss of just KRW 0.27 billion on revenue of KRW 12.36 billion.

Over the trailing four quarters (Q3 2025 through Q2 2026), the owner net loss totaled KRW 22.01 billion, indicating that despite revenue recovery, a full turnaround has not yet been completed.

Owner's equity recovered from full capital impairment of negative KRW 23.33 billion in 2023 to KRW 88.18 billion in 2024, then eased to KRW 76.80 billion in 2025, while the debt ratio rose from 171.2% in 2024 to 214.7% in 2025, suggesting the balance-sheet recovery has not yet settled onto a stable trajectory.

05

Industry analysis

The caprolactam and nylon market has suffered years of price declines and stagnant demand amid oversupply from large-scale Chinese capacity expansion and weak global economic conditions.

Amid this structural oversupply, Capro ultimately chose to relinquish its position as Korea's sole caprolactam producer and exit the market.

In contrast, the hydrogen industry's demand base is expanding, supported by government carbon-neutrality and hydrogen-economy policies as well as growth in hydrogen vehicles and refueling stations, while declining petrochemical utilization rates have reduced by-product hydrogen supply, creating room for new dedicated hydrogen production facilities to fill the gap.

Capro managed to repurpose the hydrogen infrastructure previously used for caprolactam production, allowing it to build Korea's largest hydrogen shipment center in a relatively short time without major new investment.

In the sulfuric acid market, demand from advanced industries such as secondary battery precursors and aramid fiber is growing, opening new outlets for Capro's high-purity sulfuric acid production capacity of 200,000 tons per year via direct sulfur combustion.

Still, both new businesses remain at an early stage, facing common industry challenges including competition from established industrial gas and chemical conglomerate affiliates, and profitability exposure to feedstock (LPG, naphtha, propane) price volatility.

06

Outlook

In a March 2025 interview, the company stated a goal of focusing on hydrogen, sulfuric acid and ammonium carbonate producible with existing facilities to reach annual revenue of over KRW 100 billion in 2025, and roughly KRW 500 billion in revenue with over KRW 100 billion in operating profit by 2027.

Actual 2025 consolidated revenue, however, came to just KRW 24.6 billion, a substantial gap from that stated target.

The company said it entered the transport hydrogen market in the second half of 2025 and secured a 3.3% market share, and plans to build on this by expanding into mobility, semiconductor, petrochemical and fuel-cell power generation supply while leveraging its food-additive-grade certification for high-purity sulfuric acid to pursue higher-value markets such as secondary batteries.

On the facilities side, the company has already reserved space and compressor installation slots to prepare for future 450-bar tube trailer charging, and is also considering diversifying hydrogen production methods through direct LNG imports.

It is also pursuing asset efficiency measures, including selling a non-core warehouse site at the Ulsan plant and selling steam from waste heat, to help fund new business investment.

Sale negotiations for the retired caprolactam production facilities with buyers in China, Indonesia and Turkey are reportedly underway, and if completed, could open the way for further investment on the vacated site, such as a hydrogen fuel cell power plant or ammonia cracking facility.

08

Bull factors

Capital Structure Normalization

A 2024 rights issue, debt-to-equity conversion and reverse split led by the Taewha Group-affiliated consortium lifted the company out of full capital impairment, with owner's equity recovering to KRW 88.18 billion in 2024 and KRW 76.80 billion in 2025. This removed the delisting threat and secured funding and time to pursue the new businesses.

Early Positioning in Hydrogen Infrastructure

Capro completed Korea's largest hydrogen shipment center in May 2025, capable of supplying roughly 400 hydrogen stations nationwide, and began piped hydrogen supply to industrial customers such as S-OIL and Approtium.

This coincides with government hydrogen-economy policy and a decline in by-product hydrogen supply, leaving room for market expansion.

Entry Into Higher-Value Sulfuric Acid Markets

The company supplies its 200,000-ton annual high-purity sulfuric acid capacity to advanced industries including secondary battery precursors and aramid fiber, and is diversifying its customer base after obtaining food-additive-grade certification. Repurposing former caprolactam facilities has kept upfront investment costs relatively low.

09

Bear factors

Slow Revenue Recovery, Gap Versus Stated Targets

Actual 2025 consolidated revenue of KRW 24.6 billion fell well short of the company's stated KRW 100 billion target. Despite the pivot to new businesses, the company still posted an annual operating loss of KRW 21.1 billion, suggesting more time is needed to reach breakeven.

Early-Stage Cost Burden From New Businesses

The company's profitability has been assessed as having deteriorated due to the discontinuation of the caprolactam and ammonium sulfate fertilizer businesses along with early-stage cost burdens from the hydrogen and sulfuric acid businesses. Low initial utilization rates and a learning curve on new equipment are weighing on margins.

Rising Financial Leverage

The debt ratio rose from 171.2% in 2024 to 214.7% in 2025, and the trailing four-quarter owner net loss reached KRW 22.01 billion. The wide year-to-year swings between net losses and profits also add uncertainty to financial forecasting.

10

Risk factors

Risk of Renewed Financial Deterioration

In early 2024, full capital impairment triggered delisting grounds, leading to a trading halt, and the auditor issued a disclaimer of opinion at the time. If the new businesses fail to scale as hoped, a similar financial crisis cannot be ruled out.

Feedstock Price Volatility And Safety Incident History

Fluctuations in LPG, naphtha and propane feedstock prices directly affect the cost structure of hydrogen and sulfuric acid production. Past production halts caused by explosion and gas leak incidents mean safety management remains an ongoing risk factor.

Intensifying Competition

Industrial gas and chemical conglomerate affiliates have already entered the hydrogen and sulfuric acid markets, meaning it may take time for new entrant Capro to expand market share.

There is a possibility that structural competitive pressure similar to the low-cost Chinese competition previously experienced in the caprolactam market could recur in the new businesses.

12

Overall view

Capro is undergoing a rare business transformation, shifting from Korea's sole caprolactam producer to a company centered on hydrogen, sulfuric acid and ammonium carbonate.

The 2024 capital injection by the Taewha Group-affiliated consortium, which resolved full capital impairment and lifted the delisting threat, can be seen as having rebuilt the company's financial foundation.

However, actual 2025 revenue fell well short of the company's stated target, and annual operating and net losses persisted, with the trailing four-quarter figures still showing a loss-making structure.

That said, the marked narrowing of quarterly operating losses through 2026 could be read as a sign of improving utilization in the new businesses.

With the hydrogen shipment center in operation, entry into higher-value sulfuric acid markets, and ongoing asset efficiency efforts all proceeding in parallel, the pace of revenue base expansion and the timing of any breakeven will likely be key points to watch going forward.

Downside factors such as renewed financial deterioration, intensifying competition in the new businesses, and feedstock price volatility should also be weighed.
