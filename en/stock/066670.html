02

Business structure

DTC is a company that began developing and selling LCD module technology in 1998 and listed on the KOSDAQ market in 2002.

Its main product is small and medium-sized LCD modules; it has display solutions for cutting-edge digital products made through overseas contract manufacturing and supplies them to Samsung Electronics. As of 2025, LCD module sales were 98.30% domestic and 1.70% export.

The second pillar is the real estate leasing business, which, according to press reports, generates annual revenue of about KRW 8.7 billion and net leasing margin of KRW 5.8 billion.

In 2025, the acquisition of a stake in Lumens brought the LED, LGP (light guide plate), and automotive electronics business segments into the consolidation scope for the first time, and revenue rose significantly.

The company later sold its entire stake in Lumens and was removed from its consolidated subsidiaries, and together with the disposal of its stake in Hanchungjeon, it secured KRW 42.6 billion in cash. The press reported that, as a result, available net cash has built up to about KRW 121.6 billion.

The shareholding of Oncelltech, which is on the side of the largest shareholder's family, expanded to 16.5%. According to the company, the small and medium-sized mobile display market is shifting toward AMOLED, so the company's position is narrowing.

However, the company stated that demand for low- to mid-priced mobile phones in emerging markets is partly supporting sales. The current business structure comes down to two pillars: LCD module manufacturing and rental income.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual revenue fell from KRW 69.1 billion in 2022 to KRW 34.6 billion in 2023, down 49.8 percent, and then to KRW 11.9 billion in 2024, down 65.7 percent. It then jumped to KRW 188.6 billion in 2025, up 1,487.1 percent, which was the largest year in the 2022 to 2025 range.

The 2025 jump reflects the consolidation of Lumens' LED, LGP and automotive electronics businesses.

Operating profit was KRW 3.08 billion in 2022, KRW 2 billion in 2023 (down 35.2 percent) and KRW 2.44 billion in 2024 (up 22.2 percent), before the company recorded an operating loss of KRW 1.76 billion in 2025 after swinging into the red.

The operating margin moved from 4.5 percent in 2022 to 5.8 percent in 2023 and 20.5 percent in 2024, then to -0.9 percent in 2025. The 2024 margin was the highest of the period even though that year had the smallest revenue base.

Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 6.83 billion in 2022, KRW 3.65 billion in 2023 (down 46.6 percent), KRW 2.17 billion in 2024 (down 40.6 percent) and KRW 3.77 billion in 2025 (up 74.0 percent).

The 2025 net loss of KRW 1.42 billion, which includes non-controlling interests, moved in the opposite direction from the controlling-shareholder figure. The debt ratio rose from 3.7 percent in 2024 to 22.6 percent in 2025.

Total equity at the end of 2025 was KRW 248.8 billion, of which KRW 166.1 billion belonged to controlling shareholders, and total liabilities were KRW 56.2 billion. Operating cash flow was KRW 19.4 billion in 2025, up from KRW 2.52 billion in 2024. The quarterly pattern is sharper.

Q4 2025 revenue of KRW 50.1 billion marked the peak, followed by KRW 41.6 billion in Q1 2026 (down 17.0 percent quarter on quarter), KRW 39.5 billion in Q2 (down 5.0 percent) and KRW 28.6 billion in Q3 (down 27.7 percent quarter on quarter and down 39.8 percent year on year), which makes three consecutive quarterly declines.

Operating profit of KRW 1.85 billion in Q1 2026 turned positive, Q2 returned to an operating loss of KRW 660 million, and Q3 narrowed to an operating loss of KRW 45.43 million.

Controlling-shareholder net profit in Q3 2026 was KRW 2.71 billion (up 45.1 percent year on year), moving opposite to the operating loss, which suggests non-operating items may have weighed heavily. Controlling-shareholder net profit for the latest four quarters totaled KRW 6.28 billion.

On a standalone basis, operating profit for fiscal 2025 was in the KRW 3.6 billion range, which points in a different direction from the consolidated operating loss because standalone figures exclude subsidiaries (per the March 2026 shareholder meeting filing).

05

Industry analysis

DTC's core small and mid-sized LCD module market is contracting as mobile displays shift toward AMOLED, and the company itself says its position is weakening in that shift. It argues that steady demand for low- and mid-priced phones in emerging markets supports part of its sales.

On the LED side, a FnGuide summary projects the Mini LED backlight unit market to grow at an annual rate of 23.9 percent through 2027. The same source notes that expanding AI demand for high-performance memory is widening HBM applications.

These figures describe the overall market, however, and because the Lumens stake has been sold and its LED business has left the consolidated group, whether DTC captures that growth directly is a separate question.

The company relies on outsourced production rather than in-house plants, which makes its margins sensitive to volume and cost swings.

The company is classified under real estate and REITs, but unlike listed REITs such as Lotte REIT or SK REIT, its core business is LCD manufacturing, and that difference is the starting point for any comparison.

A listed REIT is a fund structure built on leased assets, while DTC combines manufacturing profit and rental income in one company.

Outlook

The next regular report is the 2026 annual business report, which will cover Q4 2026 and full-year results. Last year's preliminary 2025 results were disclosed on February 13, 2026, so full-year 2026 figures could follow in mid-February 2027 on a similar timeline. The first thing to watch is the revenue trend.

Revenue has declined for three consecutive quarters from Q1 to Q3 2026, and Q3 revenue of KRW 28.6 billion is just over half of the Q4 2025 peak. Whether Q4 2026 revenue keeps falling will show the direction of LCD module volumes.

Operating losses narrowed in Q3, but it is too early to call that a return to profitability, so the next annual report's operating margin, and whether it turns positive, is the checkpoint.

Rental income, reported at about KRW 8.7 billion in annual revenue from the Pangyo building, is the part of the business that cushions the decline in the core. On the capital side, how the KRW 42.6 billion in sale proceeds will be used has not yet appeared in a filing.

On governance, the stake held by Onsaltech has risen to 16.5 percent, and the company has said it is considering a broader restructuring and a second-generation succession plan. Reports point to early 2027 as the timing for that restructuring direction.

Based on the March 30, 2026 shareholder meeting, the next annual general meeting would likely fall around March 2027.

Bull factors

Rental cash generation and net cash build-up

Rental operations at the Pangyo building are reported to generate about KRW 8.7 billion in annual revenue and a net rental margin of about KRW 5.8 billion, a steadier income stream.

The sale of the Lumens stake and the exit from Hanchungjeon brought in KRW 42.6 billion in cash, and press coverage reported usable net cash of about KRW 121.6 billion. Operating cash flow of KRW 19.4 billion in 2025, up from KRW 2.52 billion in 2024, is cited as evidence that the cash base is not thin.

Share price low relative to book assets

The price-to-book ratio is below 1x, so the stock trades below its book net asset value. Controlling-shareholder equity was KRW 166.1 billion at the end of 2025, and market capitalization sits below that figure, which is cited as an asset-side strength.

Total liabilities of KRW 56.2 billion against total equity of KRW 248.8 billion are also noted as a manageable balance sheet even though the debt ratio rose to 22.6 percent.

Narrowing operating loss and a profitable quarter

Q1 2026 posted an operating profit of KRW 1.85 billion after turning positive, and the Q3 operating loss of KRW 45.43 million narrowed from the KRW 660 million loss in Q2. Controlling-shareholder net profit in Q3 reached KRW 2.71 billion, up 45.1 percent year on year. The narrowing loss even as core revenue declines is cited as a point in favor.

Bear factors

Consecutive declines in core revenue

Revenue declined for three consecutive quarters from Q1 to Q3 2026, and Q3 revenue of KRW 28.6 billion was down 39.8 percent year on year. The shift of small and mid-sized mobile displays toward AMOLED is the largest weakness, as LCD module volumes contract.

Demand for low- and mid-priced phones in emerging markets offers partial support but does not clearly offset the overall decline.

Annual operating loss and margin reversal

The company swung to an operating loss of KRW 1.76 billion in 2025, and the operating margin fell from 20.5 percent in 2024 to -0.9 percent in 2025. Operating losses also appeared in Q4 2025 and Q2 2026, which makes it hard to say a stable profit pattern has taken hold.

The highest margin of the period came in 2024, a year with the smallest revenue base, which complicates any reading of underlying business strength.

Divergence between operating results and net profit

Controlling-shareholder net profit reached KRW 2.71 billion in Q3 2026 even though the company posted an operating loss, which suggests non-operating items may have been significant.

In 2025, a net loss of KRW 1.42 billion and controlling-shareholder net profit of KRW 3.77 billion appeared in the same year, with non-controlling interests driving the gap. Distinguishing profit from core operations from one-off items is therefore necessary in reading the earnings.

10

Risk factors

Business structure risk

The LCD module business is 98.30 percent domestic and depends on supply to Samsung Electronics. Dependence on a single major customer and on outsourced production exposes the company directly to volume and cost swings.

If the shift to AMOLED continues, the core business could keep shrinking, leaving the transition of the business structure as an open task.

Governance and capital allocation

The stake held by Onsaltech has risen to 16.5 percent, and the company has said it is in the stage of considering restructuring and second-generation succession. The use of the KRW 42.6 billion secured from the Lumens and Hanchungjeon exits has not yet appeared in a filing.

As governance changes and capital allocation directions take shape, the variables facing minority shareholders increase.

Earnings volatility and changes in consolidation scope

Controlling-shareholder net profit swung from KRW 6.83 billion in 2022 to KRW 2.17 billion in 2024 and back to KRW 3.77 billion in 2025, a wide range. Consolidation scope changed with the Lumens acquisition and sale, which makes year-on-year revenue comparisons difficult.

The 1,487.1 percent revenue jump in 2025 relies heavily on that scope change, limiting how far it can be read as an improvement in the underlying business.

Overall view

DTC is seeing its core LCD module business shrink as the shift to AMOLED and changes in consolidation scope weigh on revenue, while rental income from the Pangyo building and KRW 42.6 billion in proceeds from the Lumens and Hanchungjeon exits have emerged as new pillars of the business and balance sheet.

Revenue jumped to KRW 188.6 billion in 2025, but the company swung to an operating loss of KRW 1.76 billion that year, and revenue declined for three straight quarters from Q1 to Q3 2026.

Operating results moved from a Q1 profit to a Q2 loss and a narrower Q3 loss, and controlling-shareholder net profit of KRW 2.71 billion in Q3 despite an operating loss draws attention to the weight of non-operating items.

On valuation, the price-to-book ratio sits below 1x, but controlling-shareholder net profit for the latest four quarters totaled KRW 6.28 billion, a thin earnings base.

Strengths include rental cash generation, a share price low relative to book assets and a narrowing operating loss, while weaknesses include declining core revenue, the annual operating loss and uncertainty over the governance restructuring.

The next checkpoints are the 2026 annual business report, expected around February 2027 with Q4 2026 results, and the direction of the restructuring in early 2027.
