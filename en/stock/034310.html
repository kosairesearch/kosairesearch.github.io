02

Business structure

NICE (034310) is a holding company whose subsidiaries run payment settlement, credit information, manufacturing and asset management businesses.

In 2024, payment settlement accounted for 43.59% of sales, followed by manufacturing at 24.54%, corporate and individual credit information at 14.96%, unmanned operations at 12.19%, asset management at 2.47% and credit rating at 1.65%.

These are 2024 figures, so the latest-year mix should be checked against the business segment table in the annual report. Within payments, the core is card transaction authorization (VAN) and CD/ATM management; in the 2020 DART segment table, credit card VAN made up 37.35% of sales and CD/ATM 12.03%.

The credit information business is run by NICE Information Service and an affiliate, centered on individual credit inquiry and credit bureau services.

The credit rating unit handles corporate bond, business feasibility and commercial paper ratings, while asset management covers debt collection agency work and bad-debt purchases. Manufacturing consists of subsidiaries producing electronic components and aluminum rolled and extruded products.

NAVER and Kakao are platform-centered, while Samsung SDS and LG CNS are centered on systems integration, so direct comparison with NICE's structure, which combines payment infrastructure and financial credit information, is limited.

Revenue drivers differ by segment: card authorization and ATM management follow transaction volumes, while credit information follows financial-sector demand. Because payment settlement is the largest block, changes in payment conditions affect group sales most directly.

04

Earnings analysis

On a DART-filed basis, revenue rose from KRW 2.75 trillion in 2022 to KRW 3.24 trillion in 2025, growing for three consecutive years from 2023 to 2025, with 2025 the largest year of 2022–2025.

Operating profit was smallest in 2023 at KRW 97 billion, down 33.2% year on year, then rose to KRW 174.3 billion in 2024 and KRW 198.1 billion in 2025, increasing for two consecutive years. The operating margin moved from 5.3% in 2022 to 3.4% in 2023, 5.7% in 2024 and 6.1% in 2025, the highest of the four years.

Net income attributable to shareholders peaked at KRW 47.5 billion in 2024, up 1,123.7%, then fell 20.8% to KRW 37.6 billion in 2025, moving opposite to operating profit.

Net income including non-controlling interests also fell, from KRW 100.9 billion in 2024 to KRW 76.6 billion in 2025, and the reasons for the gap should be checked in the annual report notes.

On a quarterly basis, operating income went from KRW 61.4 billion in Q3 2025 to KRW 37.6 billion in Q4 2025, KRW 42.4 billion in Q1 2026 and KRW 80.8 billion in Q2 2026, before KRW 58.2 billion in Q3 2026, down 5.2% year on year.

The shareholder result turned from a profit in Q1 2026 to net losses of KRW 5.71 billion in Q2 and KRW 13.2 billion in Q3 2026, and the last four quarters total a net loss of KRW 7 billion. Operating cash flow reached KRW 406 billion in 2025, up from KRW 219.7 billion in 2024 and the largest of 2022–2025. The debt ratio rose to 162.0% in 2025 from 152.9% in 2024.

05

Industry analysis

The payment and credit information industry is driven by transaction counts and by financial institutions' demand for credit data. Card authorization and ATM management track payment volumes, while credit inquiry and credit bureau services are affected by lending and financial transaction flows.

The indicators to gauge the sector are card authorization value, credit inquiry counts and lending demand across financial institutions. The cycle position is hard to pin down, and the quarterly direction of these indicators is the starting point for reading the results.

On competition, NICE's distinguishing feature is that it combines payment infrastructure with credit information, which sets its economics apart from platform-based peers such as NAVER and Kakao.

Systems integrators such as Samsung SDS, LG CNS and Hyundai Autoever depend mainly on enterprise IT orders, so their revenue drivers differ. Companies with different revenue structures are more accurately compared using scale-adjusted metrics such as operating margin.

The 1.4% year-on-year decline in Q3 2026 revenue may reflect stalled transaction volumes, but the detailed cause should be confirmed in the quarterly segment commentary. Interest rate and financial regulation changes are external variables that affect lending and credit information demand.

Bull factors

Operating profit up two years running

Operating cash flow expands

Operating cash flow in 2025 was KRW 406 billion, up from KRW 219.7 billion in 2024 and the largest year of 2022–2025. Cash generation improving apart from earnings swings can be read as a positive factor. The capital base of KRW 1.4 trillion in total equity is also being maintained.

Treasury share actions and shareholder returns

A KRW 10 billion buyback plan was announced in September 2025, followed by a KRW 5.4 billion cancellation in November. A KRW 4 billion disposal decision was disclosed in January 2026.

Continued treasury share actions in a period when the share price is below book value are a positive factor from the shareholder-return side.

Bear factors

Shareholder net income turns to losses

Net income attributable to shareholders fell 20.8% to KRW 37.6 billion in 2025 and posted net losses in Q2 and Q3 2026. The KRW 13.2 billion Q3 2026 shareholder loss reflects a swing from a profit a year earlier. The last four quarters total a net loss of KRW 7 billion.

Revenue and operating profit down year on year

Q3 2026 revenue of KRW 832.8 billion rose 13.3% from Q2 but fell 1.4% year on year. Operating income of KRW 58.2 billion was down 5.2% year on year and below the KRW 61.4 billion of Q3 2025. Quarterly rebounds have not yet exceeded year-earlier levels, which is a negative factor.

High debt ratio

The debt ratio rose to 162.0% at end-2025 from 152.9% in 2024, with total liabilities of KRW 2.28 trillion. The ratio stayed above 150% in each of 2022–2025. Borrowing at this level is a negative factor for interest burden and financial flexibility.

Risk factors

Earnings volatility

Quarterly operating income moved sharply, from KRW 61.4 billion in Q3 2025 to KRW 37.6 billion in Q4 and KRW 80.8 billion in Q2 2026. In 2025, operating profit rose while shareholder net income fell, which illustrates the gap. The drivers of quarterly swings should be checked in the notes and quarterly filings.

Financial leverage

Total liabilities of KRW 2.28 trillion and a 162.0% debt ratio mean that higher rates or tougher borrowing terms could weigh on results through interest costs. Debt is large relative to total equity of KRW 1.4 trillion, so the direction of the financial structure needs quarterly monitoring.

Concentration in payments

Payment settlement made up 43.59% of 2024 sales, so changes in card authorization volumes or fee structures pass directly into group revenue. Manufacturing follows downstream industrial demand, so volatility differs across businesses. Because one segment carries so much weight, changes there move total profit substantially.

Overall view

NICE's revenue rose from KRW 2.75 trillion in 2022 to KRW 3.24 trillion in 2025, and operating profit recovered from a KRW 97 billion low in 2023 to KRW 198.1 billion in 2025. The 6.1% operating margin in 2025 was the highest of the period, which points to improving operating strength.

Shareholder net income, however, fell to KRW 37.6 billion in 2025 and posted losses in Q2 and Q3 2026, with the last four quarters totaling a net loss of KRW 7 billion.

Q3 2026 operating income of KRW 58.2 billion and revenue both declined year on year, so whether quarterly recovery continues needs further confirmation. A debt ratio of 162.0% at end-2025 and total liabilities of KRW 2.28 trillion also need to be considered.

On valuation, price-to-book sits below 1x while the PER cannot be calculated, so asset-based comparison fits the picture better than earnings multiples.

Positive factors are the operating recovery, larger operating cash flow and treasury share actions, while negative factors are continued shareholder net losses and financial burden.

The next checkpoints are the 2026 annual report, due by end-March 2027, and the Q1 2027 quarterly report due by mid-May 2027, and the key question is whether shareholder earnings move in step with operating profit in those filings.
