02

Business structure

Innogene was founded in 2005 as a cosmeceutical company focused on hair loss, problem skin and skin regeneration, and listed on KOSDAQ in February 2023.

It supplies home-care products, scalp care devices, health functional foods and pharmaceutical solutions to clinics in Korea, and according to its company profile it exports to about 20 countries, including the United States and China.

Its core line is the hair-loss symptom relief products centered on the BALLVIC brand, offered as a system combined with scalp care devices. Skin-beauty products and hair-loss products form the two main pillars of revenue.

In 2026 a raw farm-produce supply business for care food was added; the company said one month of supply in March 2026 generated KRW 699 million in new revenue, as announced on June 11, 2026.

In the same month it signed an MOU with a partner specializing in functional ingredient infusion from natural extracts and agreed to jointly develop premium care-food products using natural raw materials such as tomato and black soybean.

The company has presented a strategy of widening its focus from cosmetics and medical devices toward ingestible anti-aging products. For China, it runs marketing linked with ISCE, a brand selected among China's top 100.

The company is classified under chemicals, but its operations are closer to consumer cosmeceuticals than to the petrochemical business of large peers such as LG Chem. Whether revenue mix shifts from hair and skin products toward raw materials and care food is a point to track in the annual report.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual revenue rose from KRW 9.14 billion in 2022 to KRW 10.1 billion in 2023, up 10.5% year on year, then declined to KRW 8.94 billion in 2024, down 11.4%, and to KRW 8.21 billion in 2025, down 8.2%. Among 2022 to 2025, 2023 was the largest year and 2025 the smallest.

Operating profit went from KRW 1.49 billion in 2022 to KRW 1.23 billion in 2023, down 17.6%, then to KRW 630 million in 2024, down 48.6%, and to KRW 4.49 million in 2025, down 99.3%, and it declined for three consecutive years from 2023 through 2025.

The operating margin fell from 16.3% in 2022 to 12.1% in 2023, 7.0% in 2024 and 0.1% in 2025. For 2025 the company attributed the decline to a smaller revenue base across domestic and export sales, which left fixed costs relatively heavier.

Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 1.34 billion in 2022, KRW 1.4 billion in 2023 (up 4.2%), KRW 945 million in 2024 (down 32.3%) and KRW 288 million in 2025 (down 69.5%), with two consecutive annual declines from 2024.

Net income including non-controlling interests was reported at the same levels, following the same path. Operating cash flow was KRW 585 million in 2022, KRW 2.24 billion in 2023, KRW 809 million in 2024 and KRW 704 million in 2025.

Total equity at the end of 2025 was KRW 14.1 billion, total liabilities were KRW 939 million, and the debt-to-equity ratio was 6.7%.

On a quarterly basis, the third quarter of 2025 showed revenue of KRW 2.14 billion and an operating loss of KRW 29.83 million, while the fourth quarter brought revenue of KRW 2.41 billion, up 13.0% from the prior quarter, and an operating profit of KRW 109 million, a swing back into profit.

First-quarter 2026 revenue was KRW 2.59 billion, up 7.3% from the prior quarter, but the operating loss of KRW 176 million marked a return to loss. Second-quarter 2026 revenue jumped to KRW 4.8 billion, yet the operating loss widened to KRW 530 million.

Third-quarter revenue was KRW 3.55 billion, down 25.9% from the prior quarter and up 66.2% year on year, and the operating loss narrowed to KRW 123 million.

Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 211 million in the fourth quarter of 2025, then a net loss of KRW 96.11 million in the first quarter of 2026, KRW 472 million in the second quarter and KRW 19.87 million in the third quarter, three straight loss-making quarters.

The net loss attributable to controlling shareholders over the last four quarters totaled KRW 377 million. The company described the first-quarter loss as the cost of strategic investment in digital marketing, its own distribution mall infrastructure and overseas marketing.

05

Industry analysis

Innogene operates in cosmeceuticals centered on hair-loss and scalp care and skin regeneration. Although it is classified under chemicals, its demand drivers differ from the petrochemical cycle and are closer to consumer spending and clinic-channel demand.

Industry coverage repeatedly points to rising spending on appearance and anti-aging concerns as living standards improve.

The anti-aging market is widening from cosmetics and medical devices toward ingestible products such as care food and inner-beauty items, and Innogene added a raw-material care-food business in line with this shift.

Overseas, its export structure spans about 20 countries including the United States and China, so marketing linked to China and expansion of overseas channels are key variables for results.

In hair and scalp care, several companies sell through clinics, and Innogene runs both clinic supply and its own distribution mall, MP Mall.

Large chemical peers such as LG Chem and Hanwha Solutions focus on large-scale petrochemicals and battery materials, so their sales scale and profit structure are hard to compare directly. The company presents its differentiation as a broader product lineup linking hair-care devices and care food. Because its revenue base is small, adding lines can also amplify quarter-to-quarter revenue swings.

Consecutive quarterly losses

The company described the first-quarter loss as investment in marketing and distribution infrastructure, but the operating loss widened to KRW 530 million in the second quarter.

Large swings in quarterly revenue

Risk factors

Product and channel concentration

Revenue is concentrated in hair-loss symptom relief products and the BALLVIC brand. Because the structure relies on clinic channels and exports, changes in channel policy or overseas demand could have an impact. Care-food launch timing may shift depending on joint development schedules with the partner.

Cost burden and cash flow

Marketing and personnel costs tied to new businesses and brand expansion are flowing through 2026 quarterly results. Operating cash flow fell from KRW 2.24 billion in 2023 to KRW 704 million in 2025. If losses persist, the capacity for shareholder returns such as cash dividends and share buybacks could narrow.

Small-cap profile and overseas regulation

With a small market capitalization, the share price can be volatile and trading may be thin. Because marketing linked to China and overseas channels matter, local regulation and currency movements are also variables. Changes in rules for cosmetics and health functional foods could affect the pace of the care-food business.
