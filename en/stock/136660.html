Report overview

Narrowing Losses, Thin Liquidity

QMC is a KONEX-listed maker of laser and transfer equipment for semiconductors and displays; its 2025 operating loss narrowed, but revenue stayed at roughly KRW 7.0 billion and balance-sheet fragility remains.

02

Business structure

QMC was founded in July 2003 and listed on KONEX in July 2014. Its headquarters and plant are in Anyang, Gyeonggi Province.

Built on four core technologies (laser/optics, high-speed precision transport, measurement and inspection, and automation control), it developed equipment such as mini LED chip transfer tools and camera-module 3D-sensing inspection systems.

The company website groups its products into micro/mini LED display, semiconductor, probe card, and conventional LED equipment. The semiconductor line includes wafer back-side laser marking, die inspection and sorting, CIS spin coaters and removers, and die transfer systems.

FnGuide's summary says the LED and display equipment segment is the largest, at 42% of revenue. That share is as of the 2024 fiscal year.

In fiscal 2022, semiconductor equipment sales were KRW 9.11 billion, or 62% of the total. Segment mix therefore shifts sharply from year to year. Customers include Samsung Electronics, Seoul Semiconductor and LG Innotek, and its move into automotive electronics added Hyundai Mobis as a new customer. Overseas, Sony of Japan and Sanan Optoelectronics of China are named as customers.

Because the business is build-to-order, quarterly and annual results vary far more than in typical manufacturing. In 2022 the company developed the MDT-400P mini LED transfer system to compete with low-priced Chinese equipment.

Its competitive set is mainly small display and LED process-equipment makers and Chinese tool suppliers, not large equipment names such as HANMI Semiconductor or Wonik IPS.

03

Recent trends

QMC's revenue moved from KRW 15.0 billion in 2020 to KRW 10.5 billion in 2021, KRW 14.8 billion in 2022, KRW 10.2 billion in 2023, and KRW 7.2 billion in 2024. It posted an operating loss in each of those years.

The operating loss was KRW 2.8 billion in 2020, KRW 4.7 billion in 2021, KRW 2.5 billion in 2022, KRW 4.6 billion in 2023, and KRW 2.0 billion in 2024.

Fiscal 2024 results were revenue of KRW 7.20833 billion, an operating loss of KRW 2.03992 billion, and a net loss of KRW 3.41138 billion. Revenue fell 29.13% from the prior year. FnGuide attributes the decline to lower new investment by customers and a weaker real economy, which hurt sales and profitability.

For fiscal 2025, a corporate-information aggregator reports revenue of KRW 7.00702 billion (down 2.8% year on year) and an operating loss of KRW 423.45 million. A summary of NICE D&B audit data on Incruit shows a 2025 net loss of KRW 1.67 billion and total equity of KRW 1.25 billion.

The net loss is roughly half the KRW 3.41138 billion recorded in 2024. These figures should be reconciled with the original annual report.

The capital structure also changed. A capital reduction was completed on November 28, 2024, and trading resumed with the changed listing on December 10, 2024. FnGuide data show capital stock fell from KRW 4.7 billion to KRW 1.2 billion, and the debt ratio at end-2024 was 1,404.59%.

At end-2024, total assets were KRW 41.8 billion, total liabilities KRW 39.0 billion, and total equity KRW 2.8 billion.

The share price has been volatile. On February 2, 2026, large equipment names fell sharply, with HANMI Semiconductor down 11.52% and Wonik IPS down 11.89%. On the same day, QMC rose to its 15% daily limit of KRW 2,185 on news of expanded micro LED and next-generation inspection solution supply.

Later, the June 15, 2026 close was KRW 1,600. The October 27, 2026 close was KRW 2,400, unchanged from the prior day, with no traded value.

04

Outlook

Future results depend on order volumes for mini/micro LED equipment and back-end semiconductor equipment. No order backlog or quarterly 2026 earnings figures were found as of October 27, 2026, so any improvement is discussed only qualitatively.

The company's main asset is its existing product range. It completed development of micro LED laser lift-off, laser transfer, and laser repair systems, and delivered to a customer in June 2020. However, the timing of full-scale micro LED production investment depends on its customers' decisions.

The company holds investor briefings regularly. It disclosed investor-relations meetings on December 15, 2025 and June 11, 2026. Since the 2025 operating loss narrowed, room for margin improvement depends on revenue recovery and fixed-cost control. Headcount has trended lower, so expanding volume could raise hiring costs.

On the financial side, capital strengthening and debt management are the variables. The debt ratio was 1,404.59% at end-2024, and total equity at end-2025 was reported at KRW 1.25 billion. Any additional financing could bring shareholder dilution. No disclosed plan was found.

The sector backdrop cuts both ways. AI-driven investment has made large semiconductor equipment stocks more volatile, but QMC's LED and display equipment segment follows a separate investment cycle. No evidence was found that a semiconductor upturn translates directly into more orders for this company.

05

Bull factors

Next-generation display equipment know-how

QMC developed the MDT-400P mini LED transfer system and has a lineup for micro LED. The MDT-400P was reported to achieve 10-micrometer transfer accuracy and a tact time of 0.12 seconds per chip. If micro LED investment expands, completed products would be the starting point for orders. Actual order size still depends on customers' investment decisions.

Narrowing operating loss

The 2025 operating loss was KRW 423.45 million, much smaller than KRW 2.03992 billion in 2024. The 2025 net loss was reported at KRW 1.67 billion, down from KRW 3.41138 billion in 2024. Losses narrowed while revenue held in the KRW 7 billion range, which suggests cost restructuring. Whether the company can return to profit has not been established.

Diversified customer base

Customers include Samsung Electronics, Seoul Semiconductor and LG Innotek, and the company has also won automotive-electronics customers such as Hyundai Mobis. It has a record of supplying overseas customers including Sony. This allows demand in one industry to partly offset an investment slowdown in another. Customer-level revenue shares on a current basis could not be confirmed.

06

Bear factors

Shrinking revenue base

Revenue declined from KRW 37.57 billion in 2018 and about KRW 15 billion in 2020 to KRW 7.20833 billion in 2024 and KRW 7.00702 billion in 2025. The 2025 decline slowed to 2.8%, but the company has not returned to growth. Without enough scale to cover fixed costs, profit improvement is hard to sustain. Because it is an order-driven equipment business, a delay in one or two orders can heavily affect results.

Fragile balance sheet

The debt ratio was 1,404.59% at end-2024, with total equity of KRW 2.8 billion. Total equity at end-2025 was reported at KRW 1.25 billion, so further losses reduced equity again.

The company disclosed in 2022 that it would transfer its headquarters building to the Korea Asset Management Corporation for KRW 30.4 billion, showing past reliance on asset monetization. The KRW 5.7 billion market capitalization is more than four times total equity at end-2025.

Thin liquidity and large price swings

Trading on KONEX is thin, and no value was traded on October 27, 2026. The stock hit its upper limit on February 2, 2026, yet closed at KRW 1,600 on June 15, 2026. Small volumes can move the price sharply, which raises execution costs for investors. Market access is also more limited than for KOSDAQ or KOSPI stocks.

07

Risk factors

Macro

LED, display, and semiconductor equipment demand depends heavily on customers' capital-spending cycles. FnGuide attributes the weak 2024 results to lower new investment by customers and a weaker real economy. The US-China trade dispute and slower global consumption have previously curbed exports and orders. Competition from low-priced Chinese equipment adds pricing pressure.

Financial and capital

With total equity of only about KRW 1.25 billion, further losses could raise concerns about capital impairment. If funds run short, the company might need a rights issue or borrowing, and an equity issue would dilute existing shareholders.

The November 2024 capital reduction may also keep market scrutiny on how capital is rebuilt. No confirmed financing plan was found.

Business and operational

In an order-driven business, a few large customers and a handful of orders can determine results. Headcount was 47 in December 2024 and around 40 at end-2025, a downward trend. Staff attrition could strain R&D and delivery schedules. If micro LED commercialization is delayed, the payback on new-product development is also pushed back.

08

Overall view

QMC is a small KONEX equipment maker producing LED, display, and semiconductor tools built on laser and transfer technology. Revenue fell from about KRW 15 billion in 2020 to about KRW 7 billion in 2025, while the operating loss narrowed to KRW 423.45 million in 2025.

Its mini and micro LED transfer equipment and diversified customer base are business strengths. On the other side, the debt ratio of 1,404.59% at end-2024 and total equity of KRW 1.25 billion at end-2025 weigh on the financial profile. Trading is also thin, with no traded value on October 27, 2026.
