02

Business structure

QMC was founded in 2003 and listed on KONEX in July 2014 as an equipment maker. Its headquarters and plant are in Anyang, Gyeonggi Province, and it builds equipment to customer specifications on an order-based production model.

Its segments are LED and display equipment, semiconductor equipment, camera module equipment and automotive electronics module equipment. In 2024 the LED and display equipment segment was the largest, at 42% of revenue.

In 2020, LED and semiconductor equipment together accounted for 70.3% of sales, while camera module equipment made up 13.6%. Its semiconductor equipment centers on chip transfer tools for CMOS image sensing and coater and remover systems.

A 2021 research report said domestic sales averaged 79.3% over the prior three years, with Samsung Electro-Mechanics, LG Electronics and LG Innotek cited as major customers. Its technology base covers laser and optical applications, high-speed precision transfer and vision inspection.

The same report, drawing on NICE data, ranked the company top in growth and scale but bottom in activity and profitability within its peer group. Within the semiconductor equipment sector, EO Technics, TSE and Jusung Engineering are frequently mentioned alongside it.

03

Recent trends

Consolidated revenue fell from KRW 14.8 billion in 2022 to KRW 10.2 billion in 2023 and KRW 7.2 billion in 2024, and reached about KRW 7.01 billion for the year ended December 2025, down 2.8%. The 2024 consolidated operating loss was KRW 2.04 billion and the net loss was KRW 3.41 billion.

The 2023 net loss of KRW 6.2 billion was the largest of the three years shown. For the year ended December 2025, the operating loss narrowed to KRW 423 million.

Consolidated equity declined from KRW 11.9 billion in 2022 to KRW 5.9 billion in 2023 and KRW 2.8 billion in 2024, against liabilities of KRW 39 billion at end-2024. The standalone debt ratio for 2024 was reported at 1,404.59%.

At end-2022, with accumulated deficits of KRW 17.9 billion and liabilities of KRW 31.7 billion, the company agreed to sell its headquarters building for KRW 30.4 billion to secure liquidity.

The share price closed at KRW 1,600 on June 15, 2026, down 8.52% on the day, and the current price is KRW 2,195, up 4.52%, with a market capitalization of KRW 5.2 billion. One media report said QMC hit its daily upper limit on a day when the semiconductor sector fell 6.94%.

04

Outlook

QMC's trajectory depends on whether revenue stabilizes and whether the narrowing of losses continues. The operating loss shrank in 2025, but revenue stayed in the KRW 7 billion range, and indicators of a sales recovery are not yet clear.

The company is focusing on new products, including mini and micro LED display equipment and the OIS Cover Attach Machine. Demand for semiconductor and LED equipment depends heavily on customers' capital spending cycles, and a 2021 research report noted that quarterly and annual results vary widely with order flow.

With equity down to KRW 2.8 billion at end-2024, debt repayment capacity and working capital funding remain key variables. Given the accumulated deficit of KRW 17.9 billion at end-2022, cash flow after the building sale is a point to check in subsequent disclosures.

Whether the revenue decline stopped in the 2026 third-quarter results, and how the operating loss compares with 2025, will also be closely watched.

At sector level, volatility across semiconductor stocks and warnings of overheating in HBM-related shares have been weighing on short-term sentiment, according to media reports.

05

Bull factors

Operating loss narrowed

For the year ended December 2025, the operating loss was KRW 423 million, down from KRW 2.04 billion in 2024. The smaller loss can be read as an improvement in operating metrics. Because revenue also fell, the change has to be read alongside cost and SG&A structure.

Expansion into semiconductor and automotive electronics

In 2022, semiconductor equipment revenue rose to KRW 9.11 billion from KRW 3.95 billion the prior year, lifting its share of sales to 62%. The same year, automotive electronics module equipment added KRW 850 million in new revenue. The figures show a shift away from a structure centered on LED and camera module equipment.

New growth products in development

The company has focused on developing mini and micro LED display products. Camera-related equipment such as the OIS Cover Attach Machine is produced in the same plant, sharing materials and machinery. When these products start contributing to revenue will become visible in later results.

06

Bear factors

Sustained revenue decline

Consolidated revenue fell from KRW 14.8 billion in 2022 to the KRW 7 billion range in 2025. In 2024, lower new investment by customers and a weaker real economy reduced LED and display equipment sales and worsened profitability. A smaller top line can weaken fixed-cost absorption, so the loss structure may not change easily.

Thin equity and high leverage

At end-2024, consolidated equity was KRW 2.8 billion against liabilities of KRW 39 billion. The standalone debt ratio for 2024 was reported at 1,404.59%. At end-2022, with an accumulated deficit of KRW 17.9 billion, the company chose to sell its headquarters building for KRW 30.4 billion as a self-help measure.

Order-driven earnings volatility and customer concentration

LED and semiconductor equipment is built to order, so quarterly and annual results swing with order intake. Customers are concentrated among domestic electronics and component makers, which exposes the company to their investment cycles. A 2021 research report also pointed to this pattern of earnings volatility.

07

Risk factors

Macro

The semiconductor sector is sensitive to global investment sentiment. Recent media reports said concerns about Nvidia's earnings peaking and a sharp drop in the Philadelphia Semiconductor Index pressured Korean chip stocks. Changes in capital spending by domestic downstream customers feed directly into QMC's orders and revenue.

Financial

With equity at KRW 2.8 billion, further accumulated losses could erode its capital base quickly. Debt repayment and working capital needs could affect the pace of any recovery. Whether cash flow improves after the building sale will be a key item to monitor.

Liquidity

QMC is a KONEX-listed company with a market capitalization of KRW 5.2 billion. Its float is small, which can produce large share price swings. On June 15, 2026, the stock fell 8.52% on the day, with a wide gap between the intraday high and low.

08

Overall view

QMC presents a mix of weak and strong factors. Weak factors include shrinking revenue and a thin capital base, while strong factors include a narrower operating loss and a broadening equipment portfolio.

Revenue for the year ended December 2025 was about KRW 7.01 billion, continuing the decline, while the operating loss narrowed to KRW 423 million. Equity of KRW 2.8 billion and liabilities of KRW 39 billion at end-2024 show limited capacity to absorb earnings swings.

Because the business is order-driven, customers' capital spending will continue to drive results. The revenue contribution from new products such as mini and micro LED equipment will become clear in later results.

The share price moved from a close of KRW 1,600 on June 15, 2026 to KRW 2,195 at the time of writing, consistent with the high volatility typical of small-cap stocks. Overall, some operating indicators have improved while financial strength has weakened.
