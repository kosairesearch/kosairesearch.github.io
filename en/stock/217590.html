02

Business structure

TMC is a specialty industrial cable maker whose main products are marine and offshore cables, nuclear power plant cables and fiber-optic cables, and it listed on the KOSPI market in December 2025.

Based on the first half of 2025, the product mix consisted of marine vessel cable products at 67%, offshore plant cable products at 10% and marine vessel cable merchandise at 7%, according to FnGuide. The company is reported to hold roughly 50% or more of the domestic marine vessel cable market.

In nuclear power it is described as the only domestic maker of fiber-optic cable for nuclear plants, supplying about 90% of Saeul Units 3 and 4, with deliveries for Shin-Hanul Units 3 and 4 scheduled from 2026 through 2031.

The fiber-optic business is expanding around the TMC Texas plant, which was set up in April 2025 and began full production in October of the same year. In May 2026 the company announced a KRW 11.0 billion supply contract for single-mode fiber with a U.S. AI data center infrastructure firm.

In August 2026 it signed a KRW 22.01 billion MV-105 distribution power cable contract with KBI Cosmolink, running through May 5, 2027.

The customer base is broadening from Korean shipbuilding and offshore buyers toward U.S. data center, telecom and power companies, and entry into North America depends on UL certification and local production.

04

Earnings analysis

On a DART-disclosed basis, full-year revenue fell 6.0% to KRW 340.9 billion in 2025 from KRW 362.5 billion in 2024. Operating profit dropped 55.0% to KRW 6.43 billion from KRW 14.3 billion, and the operating margin fell from 3.9% to 1.9%.

Controlling-shareholder net income moved from KRW 9.46 billion in 2024 to a net loss of KRW 445 million in 2025, while net income including non-controlling interests went from KRW 9.22 billion to a net loss of KRW 585 million.

KB Securities attributed the decline to the absence of revenue from high-margin offshore and nuclear cable projects.

Debt-to-equity fell from 206.9% in 2024 to 113.9% in 2025, with total equity at KRW 110.8 billion at year-end 2025, of which KRW 110.7 billion belonged to controlling shareholders, and total liabilities at KRW 126.3 billion. Operating cash flow declined from KRW 17.2 billion in 2024 to KRW 10.2 billion in 2025.

Quarterly revenue bottomed at KRW 75.9 billion in Q4 2025, then rose to KRW 93.9 billion in Q1 2026 (up 23.8% quarter on quarter), KRW 106.4 billion in Q2 (up 13.2%) and KRW 109.2 billion in Q3 (up 2.7%), marking three straight quarters of growth.

Operating profit moved from a KRW 2.1 billion loss in Q4 2025 to a KRW 975 million profit in Q1 2026, rose to KRW 1.34 billion in Q2 (up 37.5%), and then turned into a KRW 1.26 billion operating loss in Q3 2026.

Controlling-shareholder net income was KRW 1.88 billion in Q1 2026 and KRW 1.8 billion in Q2 (down 3.9%), before a KRW 4.02 billion net loss in Q3.

For Q1 2026, Eugene Investment & Securities pointed to U.S. subsidiary start-up costs, raw material burdens, subsidiary losses and offshore project delays occurring at the same time. The specific cause of the Q3 loss should be checked against the periodic report.

05

Industry analysis

Demand from end markets comes from three directions. First, offshore plant orders move with oil prices, and KB Securities said TMC's operating margin tends to follow oil price movements with a one- to two-year lag because offshore cable carries a higher margin than other cable.

Second, general commercial vessel cable earns thin margins, so vessel revenue alone limits profitability gains. Third, U.S. AI data center and infrastructure spending is growing, and new gigawatt-scale data centers in Texas and elsewhere are lifting demand for optical cable linking servers and switches.

Nuclear-grade fiber-optic cable is described as a field where TMC is the only domestic maker, so new reactor construction feeds into medium-term demand. In North American power markets, the company expects medium-voltage power cable demand to rise with AI data centers and grid modernization.

Competitively, TMC holds a high share of the domestic marine vessel cable market, but in North America certifications such as UL 1072 and local production act as entry barriers.

The fiber-optic business has secured a first supply order from a global connector company, so customer diversification in North America is still at an early stage. Where the cycle stands depends on whether the restart of offshore projects and the pace of data center investment coincide.

06

Outlook

The company's stated direction is to shift its center of gravity from offshore and shipbuilding toward power and data infrastructure. CEO Ji Young-wan said in a May 2026 interview that the data center business would reach a meaningful share by 2028.

Reports put production capacity at roughly KRW 20.0 billion in 2026, with a plan to expand to as much as KRW 60.0 billion by 2029 depending on market conditions, and a new North American plant has reportedly been completed. Analyst forecasts differ by date.

KB Securities estimated 2026 consolidated revenue at KRW 396.5 billion and operating profit at KRW 13.7 billion in a March 2026 report. Eugene Investment & Securities, in a May 2026 report, projected optical business revenue rising from KRW 22.1 billion in 2025 to KRW 33.6 billion in 2026 and KRW 46.1 billion in 2027.

Summing the operating profit disclosed for Q1 through Q3 2026 leaves the total well below KB Securities' full-year estimate. Three points remain to be confirmed. First, whether the offshore project restart brings high-margin revenue into the accounts.

Second, whether North American optical and power cable orders continue beyond the two contracts announced in 2026. Third, whether utilization at the U.S. plant rises enough to reduce start-up costs.

08

Bull factors

Expanding North American optical cable orders

In May 2026 the company said it signed a KRW 11.0 billion supply contract with a U.S. AI data center infrastructure firm, covering about 25,000 km of cable. It described the deal as the largest single North American data center cable contract it has won.

Reports also covered an initial 2 million dollar supply to a global connector company. Analysts argue that growth in the higher-margin optical business could improve the company's overall margin mix.

A signal of entry into North American power cable

The company said it obtained UL 1072 certification for its North American medium-voltage power cable products MV-90 and MV-105. A KRW 22.01 billion MV-105 contract signed on August 5, 2026 was disclosed at about 6.46% of revenue, running through May 5, 2027.

Converting certification into a first order is cited as evidence that the North American entry route is working. The company plans to widen customer approvals and vendor registrations from its Texas base.

Possible return of offshore and nuclear earnings

KB Securities said offshore cable carries a higher margin than other cable, so a rise in oil prices that feeds into offshore plant orders could lift the company's operating margin.

Nuclear-grade fiber-optic cable is described as a field where TMC is the only domestic maker, with deliveries for Shin-Hanul Units 3 and 4 scheduled from 2026 through 2031. Since the absence of high-margin revenue drove the 2025 profit decline, resumed projects could restore the earnings base.

09

Bear factors

Unstable quarterly profitability

Q3 2026 saw an operating loss of KRW 1.26 billion and a controlling-shareholder net loss of KRW 4.02 billion. The net loss over the last four quarters totals KRW 3.36 billion, and the 2025 operating margin was 1.9%.

Revenue grew for three straight quarters, yet that growth did not carry through to profit in every quarter. Operating profit went from a Q2 profit to a Q3 loss, so the quarterly earnings trend has not yet settled.

Dependence on project timing

The 2025 profit decline came from the absence of revenue from high-margin offshore and nuclear cable projects. The timing of the offshore project restart depends on external factors, including Middle East conditions, and remains uncertain.

General commercial vessel cable is viewed as thin-margin, so without high-margin projects the operating margin may recover only slowly.

Early-stage cost burden from North American expansion

Eugene Investment & Securities pointed to U.S. start-up costs, raw material burdens, subsidiary losses and offshore project delays as the drivers of weak Q1 2026 results. The Texas unit was set up in April 2025 and began full production that October, so its operating history is still short. Even if North American entry turns into orders, fixed costs and certification spending may hit profit first.

10

Risk factors

Project timing risk

Offshore plant orders tend to slip depending on oil prices and external conditions. Reports said delays in offshore projects weighed on revenue and profitability in 2025 as well. When the timing of high-margin revenue is uncertain, quarterly operating profit becomes more volatile.

North American certification and customer concentration

Following UL 1072 certification, the first contract was secured, but the North American customer base still rests on a small number of orders. Optical cable orders are concentrated among data center companies, so results could depend on the investment pace of a few customers.

Additional customer approvals and vendor registrations are still required, which affects the speed of expansion.

Raw material costs and share supply

Rising copper prices have increased cost pressure, and reports indicate it has been difficult to pass those costs on to customers. Lock-up shares after the listing were released in stages, with the last scheduled tranche on June 15, 2026, and some unlocked shares remain according to a June 2026 broker report. Changes in tradable supply after the lock-up releases should be monitored.

Overall view

TMC's 2026 picture shows expansion in revenue and orders, while profitability has shifted direction from quarter to quarter. On a DART-disclosed basis, revenue rose from KRW 75.9 billion in Q4 2025 to KRW 109.2 billion in Q3 2026, marking three straight quarters of growth.

Over the same period operating profit moved from KRW 1.34 billion in Q2 2026 to an operating loss of KRW 1.26 billion in Q3. North American orders announced in 2026 include a KRW 11.0 billion optical cable contract in May and a KRW 22.01 billion power cable contract in August.

Supporting factors are North American orders, long-term nuclear-grade cable deliveries and the possibility of offshore projects resuming. Negative factors are the quarterly loss, the 1.9% operating margin in 2025 and start-up costs at the U.S. unit.

The PER cannot be calculated and the PBR sits in a premium range to book value, so earnings recovery is the main variable for valuation. The next checkpoints are the preliminary full-year 2026 results, the 2026 annual business report and the Q1 2027 quarterly report.
