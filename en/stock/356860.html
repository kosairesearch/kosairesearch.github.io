SOCAMM2 Mix Reshapes TLB's Earnings Profile

02

Business structure

TLB began as a PCB manufacturer in 2011 and listed on KOSDAQ in December 2020; it is a specialist in printed circuit boards (PCBs). Its main products are PCBs for memory modules and SSDs, and its major customers include Samsung Electronics, SK hynix and Micron.

In 2020 it entered PCBs for semiconductor back-end inspection equipment, and in 2025 it obtained AS9100 and ISO27001 certifications to widen its industrial scope. Within its product mix, high-value DDR5 modules have been the main driver of profit.

According to securities industry reports, the share of high-margin DDR5 products using the BVH process rose from 48% in Q2 2025 to 60% in Q3 2025. The most closely watched product is the substrate for SOCAMM2, a next-generation memory module for AI servers.

According to IBK Securities, SOCAMM-related sales rose from KRW 3 billion in Q1 2026 to KRW 10 billion in Q2 and KRW 20 billion in Q3, lifting their share of total revenue from 4% to 11% and then 21%. Industry estimates place SOCAMM substrates at operating margins in the 30% range.

Production is centered on Vietnam Plant 1, and the company plans to double monthly capacity from about 20,000 square meters to 40,000 square meters once Plant 2 is completed. Sales remain concentrated in two major memory customers, and expanding the customer base is cited as a key task.

04

Earnings analysis

On a disclosed basis, revenue fell from KRW 221.5 billion in 2022 to KRW 171.3 billion in 2023, a 22.7% decline, and then rose to KRW 180 billion in 2024, up 5.1%, and KRW 258.5 billion in 2025, up 43.6%, marking two consecutive years of growth. 2025 was the largest year of 2022 to 2025 by revenue.

Operating profit fell from KRW 38.5 billion in 2022 to KRW 3.04 billion in 2023, a 92.1% decline, before recovering to KRW 3.36 billion in 2024 and KRW 26 billion in 2025, up 673.2%. The operating margin moved from 17.4% in 2022 to 1.8% in 2023 and 1.9% in 2024, then rose to 10.0% in 2025.

The low margins of 2023 and 2024 coincided with a slowdown in the memory market. The company attributed the 2025 recovery to a larger share of high-value products and stronger market demand.

Profit attributable to controlling shareholders rose 426.5% to KRW 19 billion in 2025, and net income including non-controlling interests was also KRW 19 billion.

Operating cash flow was KRW 35.4 billion in 2022, KRW -1.51 billion in 2023, KRW 4.7 billion in 2024 and KRW 8.19 billion in 2025, which remained well below the KRW 26 billion operating profit in 2025.

At end-2025, total liabilities were KRW 120.3 billion and total equity was KRW 126 billion, giving a debt ratio of 95.4%.

On a quarterly basis, operating profit of KRW 8.58 billion in Q4 2025, down 0.8% from the prior quarter, was the low point, after which operating profit rose to KRW 10.7 billion in Q1 2026, KRW 12.8 billion in Q2 and KRW 16.6 billion in Q3, up 91.8% year on year, for three consecutive quarters of growth.

Profit attributable to controlling shareholders in Q3 2026 was KRW 11.8 billion, up 80.4% year on year, and the four quarters from Q4 2025 to Q3 2026 totaled KRW 38.2 billion.

05

Industry analysis

TLB's downstream demand comes from high-performance server memory, driven by investment in AI data centers. SOCAMM2 is a memory module that supports NVIDIA's Vera CPU, and its mass production began in earnest in 2026.

IBK Securities argues that the center of AI investment is widening from GPUs to CPUs, and it highlights that only a limited number of suppliers provide SOCAMM substrates. Securities analysts broadly interpret the memory chip market as entering a rebound phase.

On pricing, increases reflecting higher raw material costs are under way, and IBK Securities estimates that blended average selling prices will rise about 10% quarter on quarter in Q4 2026.

However, in early November 2026, substrate stocks sold off on concerns over slowing AI data center investment after the US midterm elections, and reports later described a calming trend.

Within Korean PCB makers, large substrate companies such as Samsung Electro-Mechanics, LG Innotek and Isupetasys are often mentioned together. TLB specializes in PCBs for memory modules and shares AI server demand trends with these larger players, but its product mix and customer structure differ.

Its position in SOCAMM substrate supply is still at an early stage, and how long it holds that position will shape its standing within the industry.

06

Outlook

Among management's outlook items, the figures that have been confirmed relate to SOCAMM sales. The company's 2026 full-year SOCAMM sales guidance was raised from KRW 20 billion at the start of the year to KRW 50 billion after the Q1 results, and was raised again after the Q2 results.

Securities firm estimates are not confirmed results. IBK Securities projects Q4 2026 revenue of KRW 121 billion, up 67% year on year, with a 20% operating margin, and estimates that SOCAMM substrates will account for about 28% of 2027 sales.

Daishin Securities forecasts 2026 revenue of KRW 373.1 billion and operating profit of KRW 61.6 billion, which would be a record, and expects 2027 revenue of KRW 507.6 billion and operating profit of about KRW 100 billion. Expansion timelines differ across reports.

An April 2026 report said Plant 2 would start construction in Q4 2026, complete equipment installation in Q2 2027, and begin to affect earnings fully from 2028. A November 2026 report said the company is preparing to start operations at Plant 2 by the end of 2027.

The expansion is funded by a KRW 120 billion rights offering decided in April 2026. The company is also pursuing new customers to reduce its reliance on its two main memory customers. On the confirmed facts, 2027 results will depend on how quickly SOCAMM sales grow and when Plant 2 begins operating.

08

Bull factors

Rising share of higher-margin SOCAMM2

SOCAMM2-related sales rose from KRW 3 billion in Q1 2026 to KRW 10 billion in Q2 and KRW 20 billion in Q3, reaching 21% of revenue. IBK Securities estimates the share will reach 31% in Q4 and about 28% in 2027.

Securities analysts describe SOCAMM substrates as a product with operating margins in the 30% range, so a larger share is seen as lifting company-wide profitability.

Profit recovery confirmed in Q3 2026

Q3 2026 operating profit was KRW 16.6 billion, up 91.8% year on year, and profit attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 11.8 billion, up 80.4%. Operating profit has risen for three straight quarters from the Q4 2025 low.

Daishin Securities expects 2026 revenue of KRW 373.1 billion and operating profit of KRW 61.6 billion, which would be a record.

Vietnam Plant 2 expansion plan

Once Vietnam Plant 2 is completed, monthly capacity would double from about 20,000 square meters to 40,000 square meters. The plant targets construction start in Q4 2026 and completion of equipment installation in Q2 2027. Full earnings impact is expected from 2028.

09

Bear factors

Concentration in two main customers

Sales are concentrated in two main memory customers, which is cited as a structural weakness. The company is pursuing new customers to broaden its sales base. Order changes from a single customer could feed into quarterly revenue in a meaningful way.

Higher borrowing and weak cash generation

The debt ratio rose from 46.8% in 2022 to 95.4% in 2025, and borrowings as a share of funding were reported at 35.2% for 2025. Operating cash flow in 2025 was KRW 8.19 billion, well below operating profit of KRW 26 billion. A KRW 120 billion rights offering was decided in April 2026.

Cycle swings and share price volatility

Operating profit fell to the KRW 3 billion range in 2023 and 2024, showing that earnings are volatile during memory down-cycles. In early November 2026, substrate stocks sold off on concerns about slower AI data center investment. The stock hit its daily limit-up on November 11, 2026 without a disclosure, underscoring its price volatility.

10

Risk factors

Customer concentration

Dependence on two main memory customers is high, so design changes or volume adjustments by one customer could move quarterly results. How quickly new customers are secured will determine whether the revenue base becomes more diversified.

Funding and balance sheet pressure

Against a debt ratio of 95.4% and rising borrowings, the company is funding expansion partly through a KRW 120 billion rights offering. Interest costs and cash flow pressure could persist until Plant 2 contributes fully to earnings from 2028.

Demand and technology transition

The pace of AI server investment and the timing of CPU memory adoption will determine SOCAMM2 demand. If sentiment toward AI investment weakens, volatility across substrate stocks could increase. A delay in the next-generation standard or in customer adoption could also push back volume expansion.

12

Overall view

TLB is a specialist in memory module PCBs. It recovered from a low of KRW 3 billion-range operating profit in 2023 and 2024 to KRW 26 billion in 2025, and in 2026 operating profit has risen for three straight quarters as SOCAMM2 sales grew.

Q3 2026 operating profit was KRW 16.6 billion, up 91.8% year on year, and IBK Securities described that quarter as the highest operating margin on record. Bullish factors include a rising share of high-margin SOCAMM sales, the Plant 2 expansion plan and securities firms' forecasts of record earnings for 2026.

Bearish factors include concentration in two main customers, a 95.4% debt ratio with weak operating cash flow, and cycle and share price volatility. Because the 2026 earnings figures are securities firm estimates, the Q4 and full-year 2026 preliminary results are the first checkpoints.

The timing of Plant 2 construction and equipment installation will determine when expansion feeds into earnings. The November 11, 2026 surge was reported to have occurred without a disclosure, so share price moves may respond to factors other than reported results.
