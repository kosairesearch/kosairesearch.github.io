02

Business structure

Kwangmu is an electronics and components company listed on KOSDAQ whose core business has been network infrastructure, according to a Hankyung report dated July 8, 2026. It has also been pursuing secondary-battery materials as a growth driver.

According to FnGuide's company summary, the company absorbed a subsidiary through a merger in 2022 and widened its secondary-battery materials and chemical products business.

Butler's aggregated revenue mix for Q2 2026 is 42% other, 31% network integration and system integration (NI/SI), and 27% equipment leasing (ITO).

This split comes from an outside data aggregator rather than a segment table in company filings, so segment trends should be read from the segment disclosures in periodic reports.

In network equipment, FnGuide attributes weaker sales to longer replacement cycles as products became more durable, and to intensifying competition that deepened price cuts.

In its May 15, 2026 announcement, the company said it had been restructuring inefficient businesses and cutting selling, general and administrative expenses. The biggest swing factor in earnings is now the holdings of financial assets.

The company said KRW 24.7 billion of financial income in Q1 2026 contributed significantly to its turnaround to net profit. The holdings include Samsung Electronics shares and shares of another KOSDAQ company whose trading is currently suspended.

The business therefore has two axes: an operating axis in network equipment and secondary-battery materials, and a profit axis driven by the value of financial assets.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual results began with 2022 revenue of KRW 78.2 billion and an operating profit of KRW 2.38 billion. Revenue fell 69.0% to KRW 24.2 billion in 2023, when the company swung to an operating loss of KRW 2.61 billion. In 2024 revenue dropped 73.1% to KRW 6.53 billion and the operating loss widened to KRW 4.71 billion.

In the same year owner-attributable net income rose 1,330.8% to KRW 104.2 billion, a move that ran against the operating picture and points to non-operating items. In 2025 revenue rose 16.0% to KRW 7.57 billion and the operating loss narrowed to KRW 4.23 billion, leaving an operating margin of -55.8%.

The company then recorded an owner-attributable net loss of KRW 29.1 billion in 2025. Operating cash flow was -KRW 35.2 billion in 2022, KRW 28.6 billion in 2023, KRW 4.99 billion in 2024, and -KRW 15.6 billion in 2025, changing direction almost every year.

Quarterly revenue fell from KRW 2.02 billion in Q3 2025 to KRW 1.65 billion in Q4, down 18.2% quarter on quarter.

It then declined to KRW 1.18 billion in Q1 2026, KRW 952 million in Q2 and KRW 803 million in Q3, which was down 60.2% year on year, and has fallen for four consecutive quarters from Q4 2025 through Q3 2026.

The operating loss has persisted for five consecutive quarters from Q3 2025, narrowing to KRW 780 million in Q3 2026.

Owner-attributable net income went from KRW 2.47 billion in Q3 2025 to an owner-attributable net loss of KRW 4.11 billion in Q4, then KRW 18.8 billion in Q1 2026, KRW 13.4 billion in Q2 (down 28.5% quarter on quarter), and an owner-attributable net loss of KRW 16.1 billion in Q3.

Owner-attributable net income for the last four quarters totals KRW 12 billion. The company credited its first-half profit to KRW 118.2 billion of financial income and cost cuts, so quarterly profit appears to respond more to financial asset valuations than to operating improvement.

05

Industry analysis

In network equipment, Kwangmu's core market, replacement cycles are lengthening. FnGuide explains that products have become more durable and performant, which has delayed replacement timing. The same explanation cites intensifying competition and deeper price cuts as factors weighing on sales.

As a result, equipment leasing and system integration, which make up a large share of revenue, appear sensitive to replacement demand. Secondary-battery materials are cited as an expansion area, but with quarterly revenue around KRW 803 million in Q3 2026, their effect on operating results remains small.

Sector-level sales for KOSDAQ electronics and electronic components are far larger than Kwangmu's revenue. Large component makers such as Samsung Electro-Mechanics and LG Innotek operate in markets whose scale and structure differ from Kwangmu's.

For Kwangmu, peer comparisons therefore carry more meaning in asset structure and the value of financial holdings than in operating metrics. The cycle position is visible in the fact that core revenue has declined for four consecutive quarters.

Whether replacement demand recovers and whether price competition eases are the first points to watch in the industry.

Outlook

In its August 14, 2026 announcement, the company said it would keep improving results in the second half through cost reduction and financial management. The same announcement said it would pursue businesses selectively with a focus on profitability.

However, the Q3 2026 disclosure showed an owner-attributable net loss of KRW 16.1 billion and an operating loss of KRW 780 million, so second-half results depend on swings in financial income.

In its May 2026 announcement, the company had said further valuation gains could arise in Q2 if the value of its financial holdings rose. Q2 owner-attributable net income was KRW 13.4 billion, and Q3 then turned to a net loss.

The company plans to sell its entire Samsung Electronics stake by the end of 2026, with proceeds of about KRW 110 billion earmarked for M&A and share buybacks. Once the sale is complete, valuation swings may shrink, and cash holdings and M&A results could become the new drivers of profit.

The company has continued shareholder returns through treasury share retirements, including a roughly KRW 5 billion retirement reported in March 2026, but its dividend yield remains low.

On the operating side, revenue has declined for four consecutive quarters, so whether the operating loss can narrow without a sales recovery is the next point to watch.

Profit sign reversing with asset valuations

When profit comes from valuations rather than operations, profit and loss can alternate from quarter to quarter.

Persistent operating losses and cash outflow

The operating loss persisted for three consecutive years from 2023 through 2025, and operating cash flow in 2025 was -KRW 15.6 billion. An operating loss of KRW 780 million remained in Q3 2026. The key issue in this structure is how fast the operating loss narrows compared with the size of financial income swings.

Risk factors

Financial asset valuation risk

Price swings in listed shares held by the company drive quarterly profit and loss. The Q3 2026 net loss shows that this structure can slide back into losses. Some holdings include a stock whose trading is suspended, so profit can vary with valuation method and timing.

Core business shrinkage and price competition

Delayed replacement of network equipment and price competition are reducing sales. If revenue keeps falling, cutting selling and administrative expenses alone may have limited power to narrow the operating loss. The revenue contribution from secondary-battery materials remains small as of Q3 2026.

Capital allocation and execution risk

Proceeds from the Samsung Electronics sale are planned for M&A and share buybacks, but specific targets and amounts may change with future disclosures. Whether M&A adds to operating results will become visible in periodic reports. If sale proceeds do not translate into business results, dependence on financial income may persist.

Overall view

Kwangmu operates in network equipment, but in 2026 its profit swings have been shaped by financial income and the valuation of its financial holdings rather than by core operations.

Quarterly revenue fell from KRW 2.02 billion in Q3 2025 to KRW 803 million in Q3 2026, and the operating loss persisted for three consecutive years from 2023 through 2025. The next checkpoints are the completion of the Samsung Electronics stake sale, the use of its proceeds for M&A and buybacks, and the 2026 annual business report. The structure becomes clearer when operating and financial results are read separately.
