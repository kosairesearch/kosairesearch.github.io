02

Business structure

SJM Holdings operates as a holding company, and its business is organized into an automotive division, a plant division, and other operations.

The automotive division makes flexible couplings for automobile exhaust systems, the plant division produces expansion joints for buildings and plants, and other operations cover MMIC telecom semiconductors.

According to a third-party financial data site, revenue in the second quarter of 2026 was split 85% automotive, 13% plant and 1% other. Major subsidiaries include SJM, Akers, TNN and SJM Prewell, with SJM Prewell handling telecom semiconductor parts.

The group has overseas subsidiaries and production sites in China, Malaysia, Mexico and Morocco, among others. Its main customers are automakers, and the 2026 first-half earnings summary credited higher-value commercial vehicle programs and expanded dealings with global automakers for revenue growth.

The company's profile highlights high technical capability, quality manufacturing processes, on-time delivery and after-sales service as competitive strengths. In new businesses, the group holds an investment in SJM ECS, which owns the ZLTube technology, to pursue EV cooling-system parts.

On October 20, 2025, subsidiary SJM signed a development agreement with Korea Aerospace Industries (KAI) to localize aircraft parts, including commercial aircraft engine components.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual revenue rose from KRW 184.2 billion in 2022 to KRW 202.8 billion in 2023 (up 10.1% year on year) and KRW 206.2 billion in 2024 (up 1.7%), then slipped to KRW 205.8 billion in 2025 (down 0.2%). Among 2022 to 2025, 2024 was the largest year for revenue.

Operating profit jumped from KRW 8.23 billion in 2022 to KRW 14.7 billion in 2023 (up 78.1%), and the operating margin rose from 4.5% to 7.2%. It then declined for two consecutive years, to KRW 13.7 billion in 2024 (down 6.4%) and KRW 10.6 billion in 2025 (down 23.1%), with operating margins of 6.7% and 5.1%.

Because revenue was broadly flat over this stretch, the margin decline points to rising cost and selling and administrative burdens. Attributable net income rose from KRW 1.72 billion in 2022 to KRW 9.61 billion in 2023 (up 457.4%), then fell 1.5% to KRW 9.47 billion in 2024 and 11.1% to KRW 8.42 billion in 2025.

Net income including non-controlling interests was KRW 21.3 billion in 2024, well above attributable net income, so non-controlling interests account for a meaningful share.

Quarterly, operating profit was KRW 3.05 billion in the third quarter of 2025 on revenue of KRW 51.1 billion, then the fourth quarter swung to an operating loss of KRW 997 million on revenue of KRW 50.4 billion.

Revenue of KRW 54.3 billion in the first quarter of 2026 was the peak of the reported period, with operating profit of KRW 3.2 billion, a swing back to profit.

In the second quarter, revenue was KRW 53.6 billion, operating profit KRW 4.91 billion (up 53.5% quarter on quarter) and attributable net income KRW 6.98 billion.

In the third quarter of 2026, revenue was KRW 50.8 billion (down 5.2% quarter on quarter and 0.6% year on year) and operating profit KRW 2.27 billion (down 53.7% quarter on quarter and 25.5% year on year), with an attributable net loss of KRW 5.31 billion, a swing into loss.

Attributable net income for the last four quarters, from the fourth quarter of 2025 through the third quarter of 2026, totals KRW 741 million.

Because operating profit stayed positive while the attributable result turned to a loss, non-operating items likely had a large effect, and the detail can be checked in the income statement of the 2026 annual report.

05

Industry analysis

SJM Holdings' core market is automotive exhaust-system parts, where demand for flexible couplings that absorb vibration and thermal expansion in exhaust systems moves with automaker production volumes. The shift to electrification is a structural variable for this market.

The 2026 first-half earnings summary attributed a small decline in operating profit to higher cost burdens from faster EV transition and an economic slowdown. Higher-value commercial vehicle products and expanded dealings with global automakers supported revenue.

In short, revenue is supported by existing product demand, while transition costs and cost pressure show up first in profitability. Demand for the plant division's expansion joints tracks building and plant investment, and the division accounted for 13% of second-quarter 2026 revenue, the second-largest share.

Telecom semiconductors (MMIC) sit in other operations at about 1% of revenue. In a 2025 comparison with holding and conglomerate peers on a financial data site, the operating margin was below the industry average, while the dividend yield was above it.

On competition, the company emphasizes on-time delivery and quality processes as its strengths. Overseas production sites in several countries widen its ability to serve global automakers.

The EV cooling systems and localized aircraft parts are new markets that run on different demand cycles from core auto parts, adding portfolio diversification.
