Report overview

Physical AI revenue grows, but losses and funding remain the test

Maum.ai is raising the share of Physical AI in its revenue mix, but operating losses have continued for five straight quarters through the third quarter of 2026, so profit improvement and debt and convertible bond management are the next points to watch.

02

Business structure

Maum.ai was founded in 2014 with a purpose centered on AI-based database and online information services and listed on KOSDAQ in 2021. The company runs two business lines, Agent AI and Physical AI.

Agent AI covers enterprise consultation and workflow automation platforms, including AI contact centers (AICC) and LLM chatbots, and accounted for 62.1% of first-quarter 2026 revenue of KRW 1.71 billion according to press reports.

Physical AI centers on MAIED, an edge AI device installed in robots and autonomous systems, and generated KRW 622 million (36.4%) of that quarter's revenue.

The technology base includes its in-house models MAAL (a multilingual language model), SUDA (an on-device voice AI engine) and WoRV (a world model for robot and vehicle control).

Customers include Doosan Bobcat, which signed an AI proof-of-concept contract worth KRW 1.1 billion, and Hanwha Ocean, which disclosed a KRW 2.106 billion contract to develop AI for autonomous ship welding.

At a shareholders meeting on September 21, 2026, the company approved adding robot hardware development, manufacturing and sales to its business purposes. In August 2026 it said it had been selected as an operator of a government program for rapid commercialization of AI applied products.

Against larger platform operators such as NAVER and Kakao, its Agent AI business is comparatively small, while its Physical AI business is pursuing differentiation through ties with robot hardware companies such as AGIBOT.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual results have fluctuated widely. Revenue was KRW 8.24 billion in 2022, KRW 10.2 billion in 2023 (up 24.0% year on year), KRW 7.77 billion in 2024 (down 24.0%) and KRW 9.79 billion in 2025 (up 25.9%), making 2023 the largest year and 2024 the smallest in the period.

Operating losses were KRW 5.47 billion, KRW 3.86 billion, KRW 7.12 billion and KRW 6.38 billion from 2022 to 2025, a four-year run of losses. Operating margins were -66.4%, -37.7%, -91.6% and -65.1%, with the steepest loss rate in 2024, the year revenue fell.

Net losses attributable to controlling shareholders were KRW 4.68 billion, KRW 5.53 billion, KRW 8.68 billion and KRW 7.64 billion, while the net loss including non-controlling interests was KRW 4.76 billion, KRW 5.56 billion, KRW 8.68 billion and KRW 7.66 billion.

The debt ratio rose from 47.7% in 2022 to 177.3% in 2023 and 275.0% in 2024, then fell to 108.1% in 2025. At the end of 2025, total liabilities were KRW 28.2 billion and total equity was KRW 26.1 billion, of which the controlling shareholders' stake was KRW 26.2 billion.

Operating cash flow was -KRW 4.07 billion in 2022, KRW 272 million in 2023, -KRW 5.14 billion in 2024 and -KRW 5.85 billion in 2025, so outflows persisted apart from 2023.

Quarterly revenue rose from KRW 2.08 billion in the third quarter of 2025 to KRW 3.35 billion in the fourth quarter (up 61.0% from the prior quarter), then fell to KRW 1.71 billion in the first quarter of 2026 and KRW 962 million in the second, before recovering to KRW 2.23 billion in the third quarter (up 7.0% year on year).

Operating losses were KRW 1.61 billion, KRW 1.26 billion, KRW 2.09 billion, KRW 3.01 billion and KRW 1.03 billion across the five quarters from the third quarter of 2025 to the third quarter of 2026.

Press reports attributed the narrower first-quarter 2026 net loss mainly to lower finance costs, which fell from KRW 2.58 billion to KRW 330 million, rather than to operating improvement. R&D spending in that quarter was KRW 377 million, or 22.1% of revenue. The net loss attributable to controlling shareholders over the last four quarters totals KRW 4.86 billion.

05

Industry analysis

The center of gravity in AI is shifting from generative AI toward agentic AI and Physical AI. Wisereport noted that the project-order environment for Maum.ai's AI system construction business has weakened, pulling down its results.

In Physical AI, behavioral data collected by robots, robot AI models and on-device AI are cited as core competitive factors. Domestically, the government is promoting Physical AI, and the program selection announced in August 2026 is part of that push.

According to a KMJ column, Physical AI is moving past demonstration and pilot stages toward mass production and recurring revenue. In competitive position, the Agent AI business is small relative to large platform operators.

The Physical AI business is expanding its work with AGIBOT, and at CES 2026 a demonstration of MAIED mounted on a Unitree Robotics robot drew purchase interest and collaboration discussions. Those discussions were reported from the event rather than as disclosed supply contracts and should be read separately.

A logistics demonstration project combining domestic AI models, robots and NPUs aims to build a Physical AI ecosystem centered on domestic technology.

06

Outlook

The company's role in the government program selected in August 2026 is commercializing a Physical AI application for sorting and moving irregular cargo. The project aims to deploy and operate the system in real logistics sites using domestic AI models, robots and NPUs.

Press reports say Maum.ai is responsible for developing world action models and on-device inference technology. On products, the company previewed its own-brand robot dog Jindo at CES 2026, and at STK 2026 in June it showed SUDA running on an AGIBOT robot.

The addition of robot hardware to its business purposes at the September 21, 2026 shareholders meeting provides a basis for expanding into robot manufacturing and sales.

On orders, the Doosan Bobcat proof-of-concept is scheduled to end in December 2026, and whether it leads to follow-on deployment or a mass-production contract is the item to watch.

The KRW 2.106 billion Hanwha Ocean contract covers developing AI for autonomous ship welding, and the scale of any later orders will need to be confirmed through disclosures. Press reports put the order backlog at KRW 10.4 million at the end of the first quarter of 2026.

On funding, press reports put the debt ratio at about 117% at the end of the first half of 2026, and the planned increase in short-term borrowings of KRW 16 billion and the conversion price adjustment on convertible bonds are the main variables in its funding plan.

08

Bull factors

Growing share of Physical AI revenue

Press reports say Physical AI revenue grew from KRW 42 million in 2024 to KRW 2.648 billion in 2025 and reached 36.4% of revenue in the first quarter of 2026. It is emerging as a new growth axis while Agent AI revenue has declined.

In the fourth quarter of 2025, profit attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 239 million, a turn to profit for that quarter.

Contracts that move beyond pilots

The company signed an AI proof-of-concept contract worth KRW 1.1 billion with Doosan Bobcat and disclosed a KRW 2.106 billion contract with Hanwha Ocean to develop autonomous ship welding AI.

Applications have extended from construction equipment to shipbuilding, and the number of disclosed customers is rising, although contract sizes remain small.

Government program and wider business scope

In August 2026 the company was selected as an operator of a government program for rapid commercialization of AI applied products. Robot hardware development, manufacturing and sales were added to its business purposes at the September 2026 shareholders meeting, and an own-brand robot named Jindo has been previewed. Its vertically integrated structure spanning models, robots and on-device AI forms the basis of this expansion.

09

Bear factors

Persistent operating losses

Operating losses have continued for five straight quarters through the third quarter of 2026. Press reports say the first-half 2026 operating loss was about KRW 5.1 billion, up 45.7% from KRW 3.5 billion a year earlier.

The second-quarter 2026 operating loss of KRW 3.01 billion was the largest quarterly loss in the period. If costs do not adjust while revenue falls, the timing of a turn to profit could be delayed.

Revenue decline and weaker project orders

First-quarter 2026 revenue of KRW 1.71 billion fell 16.5% from a year earlier. Press reports put first-half 2026 revenue down 38.6%. Wisereport attributed the decline to a weaker project-order environment for the AI system construction business. Physical AI order gains have not yet offset the decline in the existing business.

Funding burden and dilution risk

Press reports put the debt ratio at about 117% at the end of the first half of 2026. Operating cash flow was -KRW 5.85 billion in 2025.

A decision to increase short-term borrowings by KRW 16 billion came alongside a downward adjustment of the conversion price on a KRW 8 billion convertible bond, and conversion into shares would leave potential dilution.

10

Risk factors

Funding and balance sheet

Total liabilities of KRW 28.2 billion and continued operating cash outflows coincide with higher short-term borrowings. Repayment timing and the potential conversion of convertible bonds could shape cash flow.

The conversion price has been adjusted after a share price decline, so the scale of dilution could change with the share price.

Conversion from pilots to mass production

The KRW 1.1 billion Doosan Bobcat contract is disclosed as a proof of concept, and whether the loader project scheduled to end in December 2026 leads to mass production is the key question. The Hanwha Ocean contract is also at the technology development stage. If pilots do not convert into mass production and recurring revenue, profit improvement could be delayed.

Policy dependence and technology competition

Selection for a government program creates an opportunity, but results and follow-on support depend on policy schedules. Physical AI relies heavily on partnerships with robot hardware makers, and the Unitree and AGIBOT collaborations were reported from exhibitions and negotiations rather than as contracts.

As large platform companies and robot makers build their own AI, the company must keep proving technical differentiation.

12

Overall view

Maum.ai recorded operating losses in each year from 2022 to 2025, and 2025 revenue of KRW 9.79 billion rose 25.9% from the prior year. The revenue mix is shifting from Agent AI toward a larger Physical AI share, which press reports put at 36.4% in the first quarter of 2026.

On orders, a KRW 1.1 billion Doosan Bobcat proof of concept and a KRW 2.106 billion Hanwha Ocean contract were disclosed, and the government program selection and the addition of robot hardware to its business purposes widen the business scope.

On the other side, operating losses have continued for five straight quarters through the third quarter of 2026, and the net loss attributable to controlling shareholders over the last four quarters totals KRW 4.86 billion.

On funding, total liabilities of KRW 28.2 billion at the end of 2025, an operating cash outflow of KRW 5.85 billion and a planned KRW 16 billion increase in short-term borrowings remain burdens.

The next checkpoints are whether the Doosan Bobcat loader project leads to mass production in December 2026 and the 2026 annual report due by March 31, 2027. This report summarizes facts from disclosures and press coverage and does not offer an investment rating or target price.
