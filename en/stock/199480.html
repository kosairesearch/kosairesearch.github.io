Report overview

Revenue recovers, losses persist; profit durability still unproven

Bankware Global grew 2025 revenue 26.4% and narrowed its operating loss, but five straight quarters of operating losses from the third quarter of 2025 through the third quarter of 2026 leave its earnings durability unconfirmed.

02

Business structure

Bankware Global is a financial software company founded in 2010, centered on core banking software that handles deposits, loans and foreign exchange at banks.

It listed on KOSDAQ through a technology-special listing in August 2024 and has supplied solutions to K Bank and to MyBank, the internet bank under Alibaba's group.

The business divides into custom system build (SWSI), application management service (AMS) for maintenance after delivery, and SaaS, cloud and license sales. Domestic sales in 2025 on a consolidated basis were KRW 55.2 billion, about 87% of the total, of which SWSI was KRW 31.1 billion and AMS was KRW 16.4 billion.

Build projects form the large share of revenue, while maintenance revenue underpins recurring income. Major projects include the Korea Exchange management information system rebuild and its follow-on smart work system operation and maintenance, and the Woori Card integrated terminal build.

The OK Savings Bank core deposit system build, with a contract value of KRW 16.07 billion running through December 2027, and a KRW 5.8 billion K Bank corporate digital channel build contracted with Samsung SDS are also financial-sector wins.

Overseas, Japan was the core target, but delays in large SI orders led the company to shift to supplying modular software toolkits to local banks. In August 2026 the company said it had won a KRW 28.2 billion digital transformation and automation project for Pakistan Post.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual revenue fell from KRW 72.9 billion in 2023 to KRW 50.2 billion in 2024, a 31.2% decline, then recovered to KRW 63.4 billion in 2025, up 26.4%. Of the three years, 2023 was the largest and 2024 the smallest.

The 2024 decline was reported to stem from delayed revenue recognition on large SI projects in Japan, and 2024 revenue fell well short of the KRW 73.5 billion target presented at listing.

Operating losses were KRW 4.56 billion in 2023, KRW 14.9 billion in 2024 and KRW 4.38 billion in 2025, three straight years of losses, with operating margins of -6.2%, -29.7% and -6.9%; 2024 carried the widest margin loss.

The company attributed the 2025 improvement to a stronger financial market, a recovery in investment sentiment in downstream infrastructure, and cost efficiency with a better cost structure.

Net losses attributable to controlling shareholders were KRW 7.98 billion, KRW 14.2 billion and KRW 2.76 billion for 2023 to 2025. The debt ratio fell from 342.4% to 163.5%, yet total liabilities of KRW 29.3 billion at year-end 2025 remained larger than total equity of KRW 17.9 billion.

Operating cash flow was -KRW 2.21 billion in 2023, KRW 1.41 billion in 2024 and -KRW 5.86 billion in 2025. Quarterly, the operating loss widened from KRW 230 million in the third quarter of 2025 to KRW 1.51 billion in the fourth quarter.

Revenue of KRW 19.3 billion in the first quarter of 2026 was the highest of the five quarters, yet the operating loss was KRW 1.18 billion.

The second quarter narrowed the operating loss to KRW 81.44 million, while the third quarter brought revenue of KRW 18 billion, up 6.1% year on year, but an operating loss of KRW 729 million, a wider loss than a year earlier, and a controlling-shareholder net loss of KRW 1.83 billion.

In an August 18, 2026 announcement, the company cited a second-quarter operating profit on a standalone basis, which differs from the consolidated figures above and should be read separately.

05

Industry analysis

Bankware Global's end demand comes from IT spending by banks, card companies and savings banks, and its revenue depends on when projects are ordered. Financial-sector project orders were broadly weak in 2024, the first year after listing.

Recovery in financial-sector projects is seen as the key variable for profit improvement, and the company expects consolidated results to improve gradually if new financial projects continue.

A company official said the domestic business has shifted to a profit trend, while consolidated results stayed in the red through 2025. On competition, the company positions itself as the only domestic developer of a core banking platform and pitches industry-specific solutions.

In overseas core banking, IBS Intelligence, a UK research firm, ranked it fourth in global retail core banking sales in 2021. In domestic financial IT, large SI firms such as Samsung SDS and LG CNS compete for big financial projects, and the company has also contracted with Samsung SDS.

On technology, the company is developing its AI code assistant (BX-CA) and AI portal (BX-AP) as core services. In 2026 it launched a series of new products, including a financial-sector AI security gateway and a vibe coding solution.

06

Outlook

In its August 18, 2026 announcement, the company said it expects the profitability improvement trend to continue in the second half, centered on major projects and its SaaS and cloud business.

The KRW 28.2 billion Pakistan Post digital transformation and automation project, announced in August 2026, can serve as a base for overseas revenue recognition.

The OK Savings Bank core deposit system build, with a contract value of KRW 16.07 billion, runs through December 2027, so revenue recognition is structured to continue over that period. A June 22, 2026 report said the company's AI-based coding automation solution was applied to the K Bank SME loan system build.

In July 2026 the company announced the launch of a vibe coding solution and a financial-sector AI security gateway. In Japan, the company is shifting from large SI projects to toolkit-style license and maintenance contracts to build recurring revenue.

Over the medium term, at a 2024 pre-listing briefing, it projected 7% to 11% annual growth in its domestic and overseas target markets.

However, against a 2024 target of KRW 73.5 billion and actual revenue of KRW 50.2 billion, whether the medium-term plan materializes will depend on how much contracted revenue shows up in reported results.

Bull factors

Revenue recovery and a recurring revenue base

Revenue in 2025 was KRW 63.4 billion, up 26.4% year on year. Every quarter from the third quarter of 2025 through the third quarter of 2026 was at or above KRW 17 billion, and the first quarter of 2026 peaked at KRW 19.3 billion. AMS maintenance revenue of KRW 16.4 billion in 2025 forms one pillar of recurring income.

Narrower losses and quarterly profit swings

The net loss attributable to controlling shareholders narrowed from KRW 14.2 billion in 2024 to KRW 2.76 billion in 2025. Controlling-shareholder net income turned positive in the fourth quarter of 2025 and again in the second quarter of 2026. The second-quarter 2026 operating loss narrowed to KRW 81.44 million.

Overseas and new contract pipeline

A KRW 28.2 billion Pakistan Post digital transformation project was disclosed in an August 2026 contract press release. The OK Savings Bank core deposit system build has a contract value of KRW 16.07 billion and runs through December 2027.

A KRW 5.8 billion K Bank corporate digital channel build contracted with Samsung SDS was also reported as a new financial-sector win.

Bear factors

Persistent operating losses and a wider loss in Q3 2026

Operating losses ran for three consecutive years from 2023 through 2025. Five straight quarters of operating losses were reported from the third quarter of 2025 through the third quarter of 2026, and the third-quarter 2026 operating loss of KRW 729 million was wider than a year earlier. The four quarters from the fourth quarter of 2025 total KRW 2.08 billion in controlling-shareholder net losses.

Weak Japan business and capital impairment at the subsidiary

2024 revenue of KRW 50.2 billion fell well short of the KRW 73.5 billion target set at listing, with delayed Japanese SI orders cited as a main cause. As of the third quarter of 2025, the Japan subsidiary had negative equity of KRW 900 million and was fully capital-impaired.

The company is shifting its Japan approach to toolkits, but the results have not yet shown up clearly in reported figures.

Cash generation and limited financial headroom

Operating cash flow was -KRW 5.86 billion in 2025, turning negative again after a positive KRW 1.41 billion in 2024. Cash and cash equivalents fell from KRW 33.4 billion in 2024 to KRW 23.2 billion in 2025.

Total liabilities of KRW 29.3 billion exceed total equity of KRW 17.9 billion at year-end 2025, which could raise the need for additional funding.

Risk factors

Timing volatility of project revenue

Build (SWSI) projects make up a large share of sales, so quarterly revenue moves with order and acceptance timing. Second-quarter 2026 revenue fell 11.0% from the prior quarter. Revenue fell well short of the pre-listing target in 2024, so the pace at which orders convert into reported revenue is a key variable.

Financial burden of the overseas subsidiary

The Japan subsidiary was fully capital-impaired with negative equity of KRW 900 million as of the third quarter of 2025. Its cumulative revenue stood at KRW 1.6 billion and its cumulative net loss at KRW 1.0 billion. Concerns were raised that delayed results abroad could increase the parent company's funding burden.

Shareholding concentration and small-cap volatility

As of a March 2025 disclosure, the largest shareholder and related parties held 32.10%, a high concentration. With a small market capitalization, the share price can swing widely, as is typical for small caps.

With total equity of KRW 17.9 billion, the company may face constraints in running several large-funding projects at once.

12

Overall view

Bankware Global has partly moved past its 2024 slump, with 2025 revenue of KRW 63.4 billion, up 26.4%, and a narrower operating loss.

Quarterly revenue stayed at or above KRW 17 billion through 2026, but five straight quarters of operating losses ran from the third quarter of 2025 through the third quarter of 2026, and the loss widened again in the third quarter.

Controlling-shareholder net income turned positive in the second quarter of 2026 but returned to a KRW 1.83 billion net loss in the third quarter. On the contract side, the KRW 28.2 billion Pakistan Post project and the KRW 16.07 billion OK Savings Bank project are widening the revenue base.

On the other side, the capital impairment at the Japan subsidiary, the 2024 target shortfall, and total liabilities exceeding total equity at year-end 2025 raise questions about execution and stability.

Whether earnings durability holds depends on whether quarterly operating profits follow, which is currently the clearest open issue. The next checkpoints are the fiscal 2026 annual results and the 2026 annual report, followed by the first-quarter 2027 report to continue tracking the earnings trend.
