Report overview

Robotics and Defense Push Meets Five Straight Loss Quarters

Samhyun is building robotics and defense businesses on an auto-parts base, but operating losses across five straight quarters from Q3 2025 to Q3 2026 make it a key question whether that expansion will show up in earnings.

02

Business structure

Samhyun is a motion control specialist that builds actuators and smart power units by integrating motors, controllers and reducers into one 3-in-1 platform.

It started as a supplier of motors and reducers for automatic transmissions and has since extended into defense, robotics and urban air mobility, after developing South Korea's first DCT motor in 2014.

Most revenue still comes from mobility, with a Samsung Securities analysis from November 2025 putting the 2025 mobility share at about 83%, while another tally puts mobility at about 98% for Q2 2026, so the figures differ by basis.

Mobility products centre on motors and valve-type parts tied to combustion engines and reducers, delivered to Hyundai Motor and Kia through their tier-one suppliers. Defense rose to 13% of revenue in the first nine months of 2025 from 11% in 2024.

Robotics and other segments are reported at roughly 2 to 3% of sales, so their contribution is still small. High-load autonomous mobile robots and robot-joint actuators are reported to be in order talks with customers.

A KB Securities report from September 2026 noted that auto parts typically take three to four years from order to delivery. Competition in auto parts is framed against established suppliers such as HL Mando and Daewonkangup, while in robotics the company's integrated drive modules are presented as its differentiator.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual revenue rose from KRW 68.6 billion in 2022 to KRW 99.8 billion in 2023, up 45.5% year on year, and to KRW 100.4 billion in 2024, up 0.6%, before falling to KRW 95 billion in 2025, down 5.4%. Of the 2022 to 2025 span, 2024 was the largest year for revenue.

Operating profit peaked in 2023 at KRW 9.8 billion, up 255.8%, then fell 43.7% to KRW 5.52 billion in 2024 and 85.4% to KRW 805 million in 2025, a two-year decline after the peak. The operating margin followed the same path, from 9.8% in 2023 to 5.5% in 2024 and 0.8% in 2025.

The company's disclosure attributed the 2025 change mainly to higher ordinary R&D spending and personnel costs from new hiring.

Attributable net profit was KRW 3.32 billion in 2022, KRW 9.45 billion in 2023, KRW 8.37 billion in 2024, down 11.4%, and KRW 10.4 billion in 2025, up 24.3%, making 2025 the largest year in the period.

Net profit including non-controlling interests, at KRW 8.31 billion in 2024 and KRW 9.69 billion in 2025, is a different figure and should be read separately. The debt ratio went from 102.9% in 2022 and 95.3% in 2023 to 21.7% in 2024 and then 53.4% in 2025, when total liabilities stood at KRW 70.4 billion.

Quarterly, Q3 2025 revenue was KRW 22.5 billion with an operating loss of KRW 1.32 billion. Q4 2025 revenue was KRW 21.4 billion, down 5.0% from the prior quarter, with an operating loss narrowed to KRW 1.29 billion, and attributable net profit turned to KRW 4.79 billion even as operating losses continued.

Q1 2026 revenue fell 12.2% to KRW 18.8 billion, and the operating loss widened to KRW 2.49 billion. Q2 2026 revenue recovered 11.9% to KRW 21 billion, but the operating loss grew to KRW 5.39 billion.

Q3 2026 revenue reached KRW 23.4 billion, up 3.9% year on year, yet the operating loss widened to KRW 6.08 billion and the attributable net loss to KRW 8.33 billion, so the revenue recovery did not carry through to profit.

The Q2 2026 revenue drop was attributed to lower volumes from customers building up inventory, while fixed costs and hiring were cited as drivers of the wider loss.

05

Industry analysis

The auto parts cycle is shaped by the shift to electrified vehicles, which reduces demand for combustion-related components. A Samsung Securities analysis from November 2025 said rising electric vehicle output at Hyundai Motor and Kia lowered combustion vehicle output and slowed the company's sales.

A KB Securities report from September 2026 attributed the Q2 2026 sales decline to lower volumes as customers worked through inventory.

Auto parts carry long lead times from order to mass production, so order gains reach revenue with a delay, and that report expected a rebound from 2027 as order increases are recognized.

On the robotics side, rising demand for humanoid robots and logistics automation has drawn attention to actuators as a new growth pillar. Samsung Securities also reported that defense sales rose 15.9% to KRW 9.5 billion in the first nine months of 2025, showing expansion beyond automotive in the numbers.

Robot actuators, however, require long validation and customer qualification, so the timing of their revenue contribution is uncertain. Other listed auto parts makers are investing in electrification, and HL Mando and Daewonkangup are frequently cited as sector comparisons.

The industry is in a transition period in which combustion volume declines and new-business investment proceed at the same time.

06

Outlook

The company has said its order backlog exceeds KRW 1.5 trillion and has pointed to when those orders would begin to show up in revenue. With Q3 2026 revenue at about KRW 23.4 billion, how quickly the backlog converts to sales is the next checkpoint.

Reports describe plans for Changwon Plant 2 and Plant 3, with Plant 3 slated as a production base for drive modules for humanoid robots for big tech customers, though start-up timing differs across reports and should be checked against the annual report's capex disclosure.

Reports also say the company secured a Changwon site from Hite Jinro for KRW 22.3 billion to expand new-business lines. On humanoids, the CEO has described large-scale project talks and said a contract announcement could come as early as year-end, so whether such a deal is disclosed is a key marker of order conversion.

The company plans to raise robotics to about 20% of production over the medium term and has said each customer could support a logistics automation project worth about KRW 100 billion. A local subsidiary in India was established in 2024 as part of overseas expansion. The 2026 annual report will be the next filing to check the revenue mix, order backlog and capex execution.

08

Bull factors

Humanoid and robot actuator expansion

A KB Securities report describes order talks with many humanoid customers, built on the company's integrated 3-in-1 capability. The company has cited an order backlog above KRW 1.5 trillion and a plan to lift robotics to about 20% of production over the medium term. If realized, the revenue mix would diversify away from mobility.

Defense growth and expected relief in R&D costs

The growth in defense sales underlies the higher defense share of revenue in the first nine months of 2025. Defense sales over the same period reached KRW 9.5 billion, up 15.9%. Samsung Securities expected that mass production of defense and robot products in 2026 could ease the R&D burden.

Financial capacity and earnings recovery

Year-end 2025 total equity was KRW 131.7 billion, with KRW 131.2 billion attributable to controlling shareholders. Attributable net profit rose 24.3% to KRW 10.4 billion in 2025, pointing to a direction of recovery.

A convertible bond issued in October 2025 brought in KRW 48.5 billion, which is cited as funding for capacity and new-business investment.

09

Bear factors

Dependence on mobility volumes and electrification pressure

Most revenue comes from mobility, and the core products are motors and valve-type parts tied to combustion engines. In Q2 2026, lower volumes from customer inventory adjustments reduced revenue to KRW 21 billion. If the shift to electric vehicles accelerates, demand for these parts could shrink structurally.

Persistent operating losses and cost burden

The 2025 operating margin fell to 0.8%, and operating losses widened to KRW 2.49 billion in Q1 2026, KRW 5.39 billion in Q2 and KRW 6.08 billion in Q3. R&D equaled 24.7% of sales in Q3 2025 according to Samsung Securities.

Fixed costs and personnel expenses have risen alongside sales, so higher revenue has not translated into profit.

Timing of new-business monetization and financial strain

Humanoid contracts remain in negotiation while factory and land investments proceed in parallel. The debt ratio rose from 21.7% in 2024 to 53.4% in 2025, with total liabilities of KRW 70.4 billion at year-end. The attributable net loss widened to KRW 8.33 billion in Q3 2026, which may increase funding pressure.

10

Risk factors

Customer concentration and electrification risk

Revenue flows through tier-one suppliers to Hyundai Motor and Kia, so it is sensitive to their production plans. Extended inventory adjustments by customers flow straight into quarterly results. Faster electrification could shorten the life of combustion-related products.

Financial and dilution risk

The convertible bond issued in October 2025 could add shares if converted. The debt ratio rose to 53.4% in 2025, which raises sensitivity to interest rates and borrowing terms. Cash outflows could continue while Changwon plant and land investments proceed.

Execution risk in the business transition

Robot actuators and humanoid components generate revenue only after validation and customer qualification. If the size or timing of negotiated contracts changes, factory utilization could fall. If revenue conversion is delayed while fixed costs from hiring are already higher, losses could widen further.

12

Overall view

Samhyun started as an auto parts maker built on integrated motor, controller and reducer technology and is widening into defense and robotics. On a DART-filed basis, revenue has held near KRW 95 billion after peaking in 2023, while operating profit fell to KRW 805 million in 2025.

Revenue recovered to KRW 23.4 billion in Q3 2026, but the operating loss widened to KRW 6.08 billion, and the attributable net loss over the last four quarters totals KRW 12.7 billion.

Bullish factors include the company's order backlog above KRW 1.5 trillion, talks with humanoid customers, a rising defense share and financial capacity.

Bearish factors include reliance on combustion-related volumes, continued operating losses, a higher debt ratio and uncertainty over when new businesses turn profitable.

The next checkpoints are the 2026 full-year results and the 2026 annual report's backlog and capex disclosures, along with whether a robotics contract is disclosed and how fast revenue converts in 2027.
