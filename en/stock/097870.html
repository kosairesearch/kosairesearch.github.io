Fertilizer Slowdown, Smart Farm and Export Diversification

Hyosung ONB Co. is pursuing diversification into smart farm growing media and Southeast Asian exports as its core organic fertilizer revenue has declined for three straight years.

02

Business structure

Founded in 1984, Hyosung ONB Co. specializes in organic fertilizers, producing mixed oil-cake pellets, mixed organic pellets, organic compound pellets, fermented organic fertilizer, and functional bio-products.

The company operates five domestic plants in Asan, Cheongdo, Uiseong, Hampyeong, and Anseong, and has held the top position in organic fertilizer supply through the NH Nonghyup system every year since 1994.

Most revenue flows through annually negotiated contract prices with the National Agricultural Cooperative Federation, which provides a stable sales channel but limits pricing power.

Its subsidiaries include a Sri Lanka production entity and Hwangto Agricultural Cooperative Corporation; the Sri Lanka unit was established to source raw materials and manufacture organic fertilizer, and its sales mostly consist of raw-material and OEM fertilizer sales to the parent company.

Hwangto Agricultural Cooperative, in which Hyosung ONB holds a 73% stake, manufactures byproduct fertilizers in the same industry.

More recently, separate from its traditional fertilizer business, the company has been cultivating smart farm grow bags and liquid nutrient solutions as new growth drivers, and while four to five firms compete in the domestic grow bag market, Hyosung ONB is said to be the only one handling raw material sourcing, local production, and domestic sales in-house.

Overseas, organic fertilizer exports are centered on Vietnam, with supply to Thailand and ongoing efforts to enter the Philippine and Malaysian markets. Competitors cited in the industry include Pungnong, KG Chemical, and Hyupwha, and the company is a small-cap name within the chemicals sector.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual revenue fell from KRW 34 billion in 2022 to KRW 29.7 billion in 2023 (down 12.8% year-on-year), KRW 28.1 billion in 2024 (down 5.4%), and KRW 24.4 billion in 2025 (down 13.1%), marking three consecutive years of decline from 2023 through 2025, with 2025 the smallest figure in the disclosed period.

Operating profit plunged 83.1% from KRW 4.25 billion in 2022 to KRW 720 million in 2023, then surged 491.7% to KRW 4.26 billion in 2024, the largest figure in the disclosed period, before falling 18.5% to KRW 3.47 billion in 2025.

Controlling-interest net income followed a similar pattern, moving from KRW 3.86 billion in 2022 to KRW 1.27 billion in 2023 (down 67.2%), KRW 4.49 billion in 2024 (up 254.9%, the largest in the disclosed period), and KRW 2.81 billion in 2025 (down 37.4%).

The operating margin dropped from 12.5% in 2022 to 2.4% in 2023 before recovering to 15.2% in 2024 and 14.2% in 2025, indicating cost management and sales mix changes materially affected profitability.

On a quarterly basis, revenue of KRW 4.86 billion and operating profit of KRW 56.5 million in the fourth quarter of 2024 turned into an operating loss of KRW 277 million on revenue of KRW 3.05 billion in the first quarter of 2025, before swinging back to an operating profit of KRW 197 million on revenue of KRW 4.22 billion in the second quarter of 2025.

In the third quarter of 2025, revenue jumped 235.1% quarter-on-quarter to KRW 14.1 billion and operating profit rose 1,575.1% to KRW 3.3 billion, highlighting pronounced seasonal concentration in a single quarter.

In the fourth quarter of 2025, revenue fell 78.9% quarter-on-quarter and 38.5% year-on-year to KRW 2.99 billion, operating profit dropped 92.5% quarter-on-quarter to KRW 249 million, and the controlling-interest result swung to a net loss of KRW 548 million, a quarter-on-quarter reversal and a continuation of losses (wider than the year-earlier quarter).

On the balance sheet, the debt-to-equity ratio rose from 16.1% in 2022 to 19.2% in 2023 before easing to 17.3% in 2024 and 14.5% in 2025, with total equity of KRW 79.3 billion (controlling interest of KRW 78.3 billion) and total liabilities of KRW 11.5 billion at the end of 2025.

Operating cash flow slowed from KRW 4.56 billion in 2022 to KRW 4.67 billion in 2023 and KRW 1.19 billion in 2024, before rising sharply to KRW 9.31 billion in 2025, diverging from the profit trend.

05

Industry analysis

The organic fertilizer market faces structural contraction as the rural population shrinks and ages, a factor cited as one background to Hyosung ONB's multi-year revenue decline in its core business.

Because a large share of sales runs through annually negotiated prices with the National Agricultural Cooperative Federation, price volatility is limited, but profitability still shifts with the outcome of each negotiation.

Competition takes place against established fertilizer makers such as Pungnong, KG Chemical, and Hyupwha, with Hyosung ONB having maintained a long-held leading position in NH Nonghyup system supply.

At the same time, the spread of smart farms is creating new demand for grow bags and liquid nutrient solutions, with four to five firms competing in the domestic grow bag market. Among them, the company states that it alone has vertically integrated raw material sourcing, local production, and domestic sales.

On the export side, Vietnam is the largest market, supply to Thailand continues, and new market development in the Philippines and Malaysia is underway, reflecting an attempt to offset domestic market contraction through overseas and new businesses.

These new businesses, however, remain limited in scale and therefore account for only a modest share of overall results.

06

Outlook

On September 29, 2026, the date of its annual general shareholders' meeting, Hyosung ONB disclosed its '2026 Hyosung ONB Corporate Value-Up Plan,' setting goals of improving profitability through revenue expansion and cost-based operating margin enhancement, securing sufficient distributable profit to enhance shareholder value, and improving capital efficiency and financial statements.

In the same disclosure, the company stated it qualifies as a high-dividend company under the Restriction of Special Taxation Act, with a dividend payout ratio of 186.61% and a dividend amount of KRW 1.578 billion for the fiscal year ended June 2025, though the filing did not include a price-to-book improvement plan or a technology-growth company value-up plan.

On the diversification front, reports indicate smart farm grow bag sales rose about 32% from KRW 741 million to KRW 978 million, while liquid nutrient solution sales over the same period grew from KRW 147 million to KRW 207 million (as reported by eToday in October 2026).

These new businesses remain small in absolute terms and will likely need more time to offset the decline in the core fertilizer business.

On the export side, sales continue in Vietnam and Thailand, while discussions on Philippine exports and entry into the Malaysian market are underway, making further geographic expansion a point to watch going forward.

In the traditional fertilizer business, the outcome of annual price negotiations with NH Nonghyup and raw material price trends are likely to continue affecting margins.

The company's plan also specifies maintaining its dividend policy through stable cash flow generation, signaling an intent to continue shareholder returns despite earnings volatility.

08

Bull factors

Smart Farm and Export Diversification

Smart farm-related sales such as grow bags and liquid nutrient solutions are growing quickly, and the vertically integrated structure from raw material sourcing to production and sales is cited as a differentiator versus competitors.

The company is also working to expand exports beyond Vietnam and Thailand into the Philippines and Malaysia. Although still small in scale, these are cited as growth avenues that could offset core business softness.

Stable Distribution Network and Leading Position

The company has held the top position in organic fertilizer supply through the NH Nonghyup system every year since 1994, giving it a stable customer base. The annual contract-based supply structure limits price volatility and improves revenue predictability. This can help preserve relative market standing even as the overall market contracts.

Improving Balance Sheet and Shareholder Return Intent

The debt-to-equity ratio fell from 19.2% in 2023 to 14.5% in 2025, and operating cash flow rose sharply to KRW 9.31 billion in 2025 from the prior year. The 2026 corporate value-up plan specifies maintaining a dividend policy underpinned by stable cash flow.

A payout ratio exceeding 100% of net income in at least one fiscal year confirms a relatively active return posture relative to earnings.

Bear factors

Structural Decline in Core Revenue

Revenue declined for three consecutive years from 2023 to 2025, reaching KRW 24.4 billion in 2025, the smallest figure in the disclosed period. Structural factors such as a shrinking and aging rural population are cited as the background, making a near-term reversal difficult. New businesses have not yet reached a scale that can replace the core business.

High Quarterly Earnings Volatility

The first quarter of 2025 swung to an operating loss of KRW 277 million, operating profit then surged 1,575.1% quarter-on-quarter in the third quarter before falling 92.5% in the fourth quarter, when the controlling-interest net loss reached KRW 548 million.

The seasonal concentration of results in a single quarter makes it difficult to judge business direction from quarterly figures alone. The recent swing to a quarterly loss and continued year-on-year losses warrant attention.

Small Market Capitalization and Limited Liquidity

With a market capitalization of around KRW 37.3 billion, the company is a small-cap name that may face limited trading liquidity. Because new business sales remain a small share of the total, detailed performance is hard to assess from disclosed materials alone. The periodic reporting cycle also creates a time lag before the most recent quarterly results can be confirmed.

10

Risk factors

Raw Material and Cost Volatility

Organic fertilizer costs are sensitive to raw material procurement prices, and as shown when the operating margin fell to 2.4% in 2023, rising cost burdens can quickly erode profitability. The raw material sourcing structure through the Sri Lanka production subsidiary can also be affected by local conditions. Cost management capability is a key variable driving the swings in annual operating margin.

Dependence on NH Nonghyup Price Negotiations

Because most revenue is tied to annually contracted prices with the National Agricultural Cooperative Federation, profitability depends heavily on negotiation outcomes. If supply prices do not sufficiently reflect rising costs, margins could narrow. High dependence on a single sales channel is cited as a risk in terms of bargaining power.

Uncertainty in New Business and Export Expansion

Smart farm and export expansion remain at an early stage, and discussions on Philippine exports and entry into the Malaysian market are still in progress rather than confirmed outcomes.

New overseas markets are exposed to variables such as regulation, customs clearance, and currency movements, so plans may not proceed as expected. It could take time for new business revenue to grow large enough to replace the core business.

Overall view

Hyosung ONB is at a stage of seeking a breakthrough through smart farm and export expansion while its core organic fertilizer business, built on the NH Nonghyup distribution network, undergoes structural contraction.

Annual revenue declined for three consecutive years from 2023 to 2025, while operating profit and controlling-interest net income rebounded in 2024 before slowing again in 2025, showing a direction that has shifted frequently.

Quarterly results display pronounced seasonality concentrated in a single quarter, and the fourth quarter of 2025 swung to a controlling-interest net loss. The balance sheet shows signs of improvement, with the debt-to-equity ratio easing gradually and operating cash flow rising sharply in 2025.

The 2026 corporate value-up plan presented a dividend-centered shareholder return policy but did not include a price-to-book improvement plan. The smart farm and export businesses remain small in scale and will likely need more time to offset the slowdown in the core business.

The pace of new business growth and the margin trajectory of the traditional fertilizer business both warrant continued attention through upcoming quarterly and half-year reports.
