02

Business structure

WOOJIN Ntec was founded in 2013 when the nuclear business unit of Sejong Enterprise was acquired, and it is a core subsidiary of its parent Woojin (105840).

Its main work covers routine maintenance of instrumentation and control systems while plants run, planned preventive maintenance during outages, and commissioning work. The company operates at four nuclear units and five thermal units.

In 2024 nuclear accounted for 64.6% of revenue, thermal for 34.8%, and product sales for only 0.6%. Only four domestic firms, including WOOJIN Ntec, can bid alone for nuclear I&C maintenance. In 2022 it ranked second in nuclear routine maintenance market share and third in thermal.

The sector has high entry barriers, with at least six years of maintenance experience required for sole bidding. Its KEPIC certification also opens core component manufacturing and supply. The parent held 41.29% as of August 28, 2026.

04

Earnings analysis

On a reported basis, revenue rose from KRW 37.4 billion in 2022 to KRW 40.7 billion in 2023 (up 8.9%), KRW 44 billion in 2024 (up 8.1%), and KRW 44.8 billion in 2025 (up 1.9%), with 2025 the largest year of the 2022 to 2025 span.

Operating profit was KRW 5.67 billion in 2022 and KRW 5.89 billion in 2023 (up 3.9%), then KRW 5.32 billion in 2024 (down 9.7%) and KRW 5.06 billion in 2025 (down 4.9%). Revenue growth slowed to 1.9% in 2025 while operating profit fell 4.9%, so cost pressure weighed on margins.

The operating margin moved from 15.2% in 2022 to 14.5%, 12.1% and 11.3% in 2025. Net income attributable to controlling shareholders rose to KRW 5.41 billion in 2024 before dipping to KRW 5.31 billion in 2025.

Because operating profit and this earnings line moved in different directions, non-operating items may have played a role. Operating cash flow fell from KRW 7.98 billion in 2024 to KRW 3.95 billion in 2025.

Quarterly operating profit went from KRW 1.94 billion in the third quarter of 2025 to KRW 1.39 billion in the fourth quarter, peaked at KRW 1.87 billion in the first quarter of 2026, then fell to KRW 1.04 billion in the second quarter and KRW 323 million in the third.

The company credited the first-quarter gain to a larger volume of planned preventive maintenance orders. Third-quarter revenue of KRW 8.95 billion was down 22.0% year on year, while operating profit fell 83.4%, indicating heavier cost pressure.

Controlling-shareholder net income for the third quarter was KRW 427 million, and the last four quarters total KRW 4.6 billion. Any one-off items behind the third-quarter result should be checked in the annual report notes.

05

Industry analysis

The end market for WOOJIN Ntec is planned preventive and routine maintenance ordered by domestic nuclear and thermal operators, and timing depends on each plant's operating schedule. The company itself notes that order volume depends on operating schedules, maintenance tender sizes and competition.

Policy support includes restarting construction of Shin-Hanul Units 3 and 4 and adding two new nuclear units around 2037 to 2038, as cited by a securities analyst in 2025. Decommissioning is a separate growth leg, and a KB Securities analyst had expected the market to start in the second half of 2025 or during 2026.

Whether that has translated into firm orders is not yet clear. Localization of nuclear components is also advancing as supply-chain concerns grow.

The routine maintenance segment has limited sole-bidding entrants, which supports incumbents, while the decommissioning and component localization segments are still at an early stage. Views differ on whether this is a growth transition or a period of volatile maintenance volumes.

06

Outlook

The company's stated direction is rising maintenance demand from expanded domestic nuclear construction and operation. Its backlog of KRW 26.3 billion, reported in late October 2026, is about 60% of 2025 revenue.

Backlog does not translate into revenue on a fixed schedule, so it does not by itself signal a quarterly recovery. The new Shin-Hanul Units 1 and 2 contract for out-of-core neutron flux monitoring is one example, and the company plans further nuclear maintenance orders.

In decommissioning, the company plans to enter the market in earnest from 2026 with targets including Kori Unit 1 and Wolsong Unit 1. A state-funded project to strengthen its G-CAM technology runs through the end of 2026.

In July 2026 its turbine speed control panel for auxiliary feedwater pumps was designated an industrial ministry innovation product. The key variable is whether the three-month public bidding restriction is lifted, and management says sales and existing maintenance work continue normally.

Whether third-quarter 2026 weakness reflects timing or a lasting margin decline should be checked in later quarterly results and the annual report.

Bull factors

Wider nuclear maintenance volume

The company attributed the first-quarter 2026 gain to more planned preventive maintenance orders. It signed a new Shin-Hanul Units 1 and 2 contract in October 2026, and a Kori contract increase lifted backlog to KRW 26.3 billion, about 60% of 2025 revenue.

New business lines in decommissioning and localization

The company plans to enter nuclear decommissioning in earnest from 2026, starting with Kori Unit 1 and Wolsong Unit 1. In July 2026 its turbine speed control panel was designated an industrial ministry innovation product. Its KEPIC certification could widen product sales as core components localize.

Low leverage and financial room

At year-end 2025 total equity was KRW 51.1 billion and total liabilities were KRW 6.54 billion, a debt ratio of 12.8%. The ratio has fallen steadily from 27.3% in 2022, which leaves room for investment and bidding.

Bear factors

Sharp quarterly profit decline

Third-quarter 2026 operating profit was KRW 323 million, down 83.4% from a year earlier, and controlling-shareholder net income was KRW 427 million. Revenue fell 22.0% over the same period, so profit fell much faster than sales.

Weaker annual margins and cash flow

The operating margin fell from 15.2% in 2022 to 11.3% in 2025, and operating profit declined in 2024 and 2025. Operating cash flow dropped from KRW 7.98 billion in 2024 to KRW 3.95 billion in 2025, even as revenue reached KRW 44.8 billion.

Bidding restriction and order volatility

A three-month public bidding restriction has raised concern about project disruption. Maintenance volumes depend on plant operating schedules and tender sizes, so quarterly revenue swings widely. Analysts expected decommissioning to open during 2026, but concrete references remain limited.

Risk factors

Regulation and bidding restrictions

If the public bidding restriction persists, new order announcements may be delayed. The company says existing maintenance work continues normally, but a longer restriction could slow backlog conversion and new contract wins.

Order timing and customer concentration

Maintenance volumes shift sharply by quarter with the planned outage schedules of power operators. Profit fell in the second and third quarters of 2026 after the first-quarter peak, and revenue tied to specific plant calendars makes quarterly results harder to forecast.

Delay in decommissioning market opening

Expectations were for decommissioning to open during 2026, but whether firm orders have followed is still unclear. Technology development and state-funded projects can weigh on near-term profit. A delay would extend the time before growth expectations show up in results.

Overall view

WOOJIN Ntec is one of a few domestic firms able to bid alone for nuclear I&C maintenance, and its KRW 26.3 billion backlog and new Shin-Hanul contract show the base of its business.

On an annual basis revenue rose from KRW 37.4 billion in 2022 to KRW 44.8 billion in 2025, but operating profit peaked at KRW 5.89 billion in 2023 and fell in 2024 and 2025. The operating margin declined from 15.2% in 2022 to 11.3% in 2025.

Quarterly profit is volatile, with operating profit peaking at KRW 1.87 billion in the first quarter of 2026 and falling to KRW 323 million in the third quarter. Leverage is low, with a debt ratio of 12.8% and total equity of KRW 51.1 billion at year-end 2025.

Decommissioning and component localization are cited as medium-term growth lines, but whether orders have followed is not yet clear. Pro-nuclear policy supports demand.

The next checkpoints are the lifting of the bidding restriction, the operating margin in the 2026 annual report, and whether quarterly profit recovers in the first quarter of 2027.
