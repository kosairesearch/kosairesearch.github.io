02

Business structure

Lotte REIT is a listed REIT whose underlying assets are real estate leased to Lotte Group retail operations, centered on department stores, marts and outlets, with hotels and a logistics center added.

A May 2026 press report said retail-linked properties accounted for about 76% of the portfolio, including six department stores, five marts, two mart-outlet complexes and one premium outlet. Non-retail assets include the L7 Gangnam Tower, the Lotte Hotel L7 Hongdae added in January 2026, and a Gimpo logistics center.

Total acquisition cost of the investment assets was KRW 2,637.7 billion, and the fair value of investment property at end-2025 was KRW 3,204.7 billion, KRW 567 billion above acquisition cost.

Retail and logistics assets are under master leases with Lotte Shopping and Lotte Global Logistics on a triple-net basis, under which tenants bear repairs, insurance and property taxes. The structure is described as reducing the company's management burden and making dividend funding more predictable.

Asset management is handled by an affiliate of Lotte Holdings, with a management fee of 0.2% per year of acquisition cost. Because rental income is concentrated in a few group tenants, the earnings stability depends directly on those tenants' performance and credit.

Listed peers in the same real estate and REIT sector include Macquarie Korea Infrastructure Fund, SK REIT and Hanwha REIT. Adding hotels and a logistics center has broadened the sector mix of the leased portfolio compared with earlier years.

04

Earnings analysis

Revenue rose from KRW 59.4 billion in the fiscal year ended June 2023 to KRW 65.6 billion in the fiscal year ended June 2024 and KRW 70.9 billion in the fiscal year ended June 2025, reaching KRW 82.1 billion in the fiscal year ended June 2026, the largest of the four years.

Revenue has grown for three consecutive years from the fiscal year ended June 2024 through the fiscal year ended June 2026, and the latest year rose 15.8% year on year. Operating profit grew from KRW 36.1 billion to KRW 55.7 billion over the same span, and the 20.3% rise in the latest year outpaced revenue growth.

As a result, operating margin improved from 60.8% to 67.8%.

Profit attributable to controlling shareholders rose from KRW 3.18 billion in the fiscal year ended June 2023 to KRW 10.6 billion in the fiscal year ended June 2024, up 234.0%, and reached KRW 23.8 billion in the fiscal year ended June 2026 after three consecutive annual increases.

Net profit including non-controlling interests was disclosed at the same amount as profit attributable to controlling shareholders in each fiscal year.

Operating cash flow rose from KRW 55.2 billion to KRW 86.3 billion in the fiscal year ended June 2024, fell to KRW 64 billion in the fiscal year ended June 2025, and recovered to KRW 73.3 billion in the fiscal year ended June 2026.

The debt ratio climbed from 119.2% in the fiscal year ended June 2023 to 124.3%, 127.7% and then 154.7% in the fiscal year ended June 2026. Total liabilities at end-June 2026 were KRW 1.74 trillion, above total equity of KRW 1.13 trillion.

Over 2025 the company refinanced KRW 580 billion of borrowings, which lowered its average borrowing cost. Profit attributable to controlling shareholders over the last four quarters, from the third quarter of 2025 to the second quarter of 2026, totaled KRW 23.8 billion, the same as the fiscal year ended June 2026.

05

Industry analysis

REIT earnings rest on rent, so the cycle of the tenant sector directly shapes lease terms and income stability. Lotte REIT's leased assets are concentrated in department stores, marts and outlets, a format that faces pressure from the shift to online shopping, weaker consumption and store consolidation.

At the same time, long-term master leases with Lotte Group affiliates are seen as limiting short-term vacancy risk. On financing, the company said it raised funds through a mix of mortgage loans and corporate bonds as interest rate volatility increased.

At end-2025 its loan-to-value ratio was 41.0% on appraised collateral value and 49.9% on acquisition cost.

A press report noted that another listed REIT became the first in the market to file for rehabilitation after a cash trap was triggered when its LTV exceeded covenant levels following a decline in the collateral valuation.

Peers such as Macquarie Korea Infrastructure Fund, SK REIT and Hanwha REIT trade in the same sector, so comparisons are most meaningful on lease and maturity structure. The sector is therefore sensitive to two variables, interest rates and asset valuations.

06

Outlook

The company disclosed in its annual report that KRW 605 billion of borrowings, about 46.0% of total borrowings, was due for repayment in 2026. It said it would fund repayments through mortgage loans and corporate bonds and manage interest costs in light of debt maturities.

Over the medium to long term it plans to consolidate its dispersed borrowing maturities as it adds assets. The Lotte Hotel L7 Hongdae was added to the portfolio in January 2026.

Because the leases are master leases on a triple-net basis as a rule, the operating performance and credit of tenants can affect rent terms when leases are renewed. At the March 2026 shareholder meeting, a cash dividend agenda was approved, and dividends are paid semi-annually.

The company framed further asset additions as a medium-term goal. How refinancing costs change will shape the next annual earnings path. Tenant sales and the outcome of lease negotiations can be followed indirectly through the disclosures of Lotte Shopping.

Bull factors

Income anchored by master leases

Operating cash flow was KRW 73.3 billion in the fiscal year ended June 2026, up from KRW 64 billion in the fiscal year ended June 2025.

Earnings growth and margin expansion

Revenue rose from KRW 59.4 billion in the fiscal year ended June 2023 to KRW 82.1 billion in the fiscal year ended June 2026, and operating margin of 67.8% was the highest of the four years. Profit attributable to controlling shareholders has increased for three consecutive years from the fiscal year ended June 2024. Acquired assets, including the Lotte Hotel L7 Hongdae added in January 2026, broadened the rental base.

Lower funding costs and a maturity plan

During 2025 the company refinanced KRW 580 billion, or 44.2% of total borrowings of KRW 1,313 billion. As a result, its average borrowing cost fell from 4.14% at end-2024 to 3.52% at end-2025. The company has said it plans to consolidate its dispersed borrowing maturities over the medium to long term.

Bear factors

High exposure to the retail cycle

A May 2026 press report said retail-linked properties account for about 76% of the portfolio. Department stores, marts and outlets face pressure from online shifts, weaker domestic consumption and store consolidation. Long-term leases limit short-term vacancy risk, but renewal terms can still move with the retail cycle.

Rising leverage and maturity pressure

The debt ratio rose from 119.2% in the fiscal year ended June 2023 to 154.7% in the fiscal year ended June 2026. KRW 605 billion of borrowings due in 2026 represents about 46.0% of total borrowings.

Sensitivity of valuations and LTV

At end-2025 the LTV was 41.0% on appraised collateral value and 49.9% on acquisition cost. Another listed REIT saw its LTV exceed covenant levels after a decline in collateral valuation, triggering a cash trap and later becoming the first listed REIT in the market to file for rehabilitation. A fall in collateral value could constrain both borrowing capacity and dividend funding.

Risk factors

Tenant concentration

Retail and logistics leases are concentrated in Lotte Shopping and Lotte Global Logistics. A decline in tenant earnings or a credit rating change would feed directly into renewal terms and rental income. Changes in the group's asset monetization policy could also reshape the portfolio.

Interest rates and refinancing

If refinancing of the KRW 605 billion due in 2026 is priced above the 3.52% average cost at end-2025, interest expense would rise. The company has used both mortgage loans and corporate bonds amid higher rate volatility. Higher interest expense would reduce the cash available for distribution.

Funding of new acquisitions

The company presents further acquisitions as a medium-term goal. Funding new purchases with debt or new equity would affect the debt ratio and per-share metrics. Scale and terms will be visible only through future disclosures.

Overall view

Lotte REIT recorded revenue of KRW 82.1 billion, operating profit of KRW 55.7 billion and profit attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 23.8 billion in the fiscal year ended June 2026, and all three have risen for three consecutive years.

Operating margin improved from 60.8% in the fiscal year ended June 2023 to 67.8%, with master leases on a triple-net basis and lower average borrowing costs cited as contributing factors.

On the other side, the debt ratio rose to 154.7%, and total liabilities at end-June 2026 of KRW 1.74 trillion exceeded total equity of KRW 1.13 trillion. Because retail-linked assets make up about 76% of the portfolio, the retail cycle and tenant performance remain relevant to long-term rent terms.

The next checkpoints are how the KRW 605 billion of borrowings due in 2026 was refinanced and the operating margin trend in the quarterly report for October to December 2026.

On valuation, the price-to-book ratio sits below 1x, and because earnings have grown substantially, price-earnings comparisons with earlier years are not straightforward.

Rental stability and earnings growth are the strengths, while debt burden and retail sector exposure are the weaknesses, and both deserve equal weight in reviewing the disclosures.
