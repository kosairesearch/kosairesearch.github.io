Ad Business Turns Profitable, Expands Into Content and IP

Mobidays is diversifying beyond its core digital marketing business into games, content, IP, and commerce through subsidiaries while profitability in its advertising unit recovers.

02

Business structure

Founded in 2014, Mobidays grew into an ad-tech company centered on mobile-focused performance marketing and media rep services.

Through its proprietary mobile marketing platform, it connects advertisers, agencies and media companies in Korea and abroad, holding partnerships with major platforms such as Google, Kakao, Naver and Meta.

Beyond its core digital marketing business, the company has expanded through four subsidiaries: Mobi Intouch (marketing recruitment), MobiGames (game publishing and IP), Mobi Contents Tech (which operates COKODIVE, a K-content commerce platform), and Mobi Career Edu (business education).

MobiGames officially launched the mobile dungeon-farming RPG ELTRIX in Korea in March 2026 and plans to release multiple new games in the second half of 2026.

Mobi Contents Tech is expanding its global K-content commerce business while also entering a new video IP business in 2026 that includes film investment, import and distribution, preparing six to seven titles including Sentimental Value, De Gaulle and The Invite.

The company has said it plans to expand AEO (answer engine optimization) and GEO (generative engine optimization) consulting and solutions to address the generative AI search environment.

Headcount stood at about 230 as of 2026, and CEO Beomryeong Yoo, the largest shareholder, holds close to half of the shares including those of related parties.

The competitive landscape features large platforms expanding automated bidding systems alongside competition among small and mid-sized ad-tech firms and agencies, making core profitability dependent on campaign operating efficiency and the contribution of the subsidiary portfolio.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual revenue rose from KRW 16.1 billion in 2022 to KRW 23.9 billion in 2023 (up 48.8% year over year), KRW 37.2 billion in 2024 (up 55.8%), and KRW 44.4 billion in 2025 (up 19.1%), marking three consecutive years of growth from 2023 through 2025, with 2025 the largest and 2022 the smallest year in this span.

Operating profit swung from KRW 4.59 billion in 2022 to an operating loss of KRW 1.9 billion in 2023, turning negative year over year, then back to an operating profit of KRW 315 million in 2024, turning positive year over year, before rising 1,330.6% year over year to KRW 4.51 billion in 2025.

Net profit attributable to controlling interests posted a net loss of KRW 6.67 billion in 2022, followed by a net loss of KRW 915 million in 2023 (losses continued but narrowed year over year) and a net loss of KRW 1.71 billion in 2024 (losses continued and widened year over year), before turning to a net profit of KRW 3.1 billion in 2025, a year-over-year swing to profit.

The operating margin fell from 28.6% in 2022 to -8.0% in 2023, then gradually recovered to 0.8% in 2024 and 10.2% in 2025.

On a quarterly basis, revenue bottomed at KRW 8.08 billion in Q4 2025 before rising for two straight quarters to KRW 9.56 billion in Q1 2026 (up 18.4% quarter over quarter) and KRW 13 billion in Q2 2026 (up 36.1% quarter over quarter and 6.8% year over year).

Operating profit recorded a loss of KRW 222 million in Q4 2025 (turning negative quarter over quarter) and a loss of KRW 167 million in Q1 2026 (losses continued but narrowed quarter over quarter), before swinging to an operating profit of KRW 2.89 billion in Q2 2026 (turning positive quarter over quarter, up 16.9% year over year).

Net profit attributable to controlling interests was KRW 1.69 billion in Q2 2025, KRW 1.49 billion in Q3 2025 (down 11.9% quarter over quarter), a net loss of KRW 715 million in Q4 2025 (turning negative quarter over quarter), and a net loss of KRW 46.97 million in Q1 2026 (losses continued but narrowed quarter over quarter), before a net profit of KRW 985 million in Q2 2026 (turning positive quarter over quarter but down 41.6% year over year); the sum over the last four quarters (Q3 2025 through Q2 2026) was just KRW 1.71 billion.

Operating cash flow was an outflow of KRW 17.7 billion in 2023 and KRW 2.35 billion in 2024 before turning into an inflow of KRW 22.7 billion in 2025, a marked improvement in cash generation.

05

Industry analysis

According to an analysis by the Korea Broadcast Advertising Corporation (KOBACO), total domestic ad spending in 2025 reached roughly KRW 17.27 trillion, up slightly from the prior year, with most of the growth coming from online and digital advertising.

Online advertising accounted for about 60% of total ad spending, while broadcast advertising declined roughly 13% year over year. Korea's advertising market is forecast to reach about KRW 17.94 trillion in 2026.

KOBACO's 2026 outlook clearly points to polarization among media, with online, digital and retail media continuing to grow while broadcast and print advertising remain in an adjustment phase.

This digital tilt is a favorable backdrop for performance marketing and ad-tech firms like Mobidays, but at the same time large platforms are advancing their own automated bidding tools, putting pressure on agencies' traditional intermediary margins.

Industry reports have identified AI marketing, discovery-based commerce, OTT, and DOOH as key themes for the 2026 advertising and marketing market, noting that AI is expanding across ad operations, content production, and media execution.

In response, Mobidays is broadening its service lineup with generative AI search solutions while diversifying revenue beyond its core advertising business into games, content, IP, and commerce.

Several digital advertising and ad-tech companies operate in similar segments on KOSDAQ, but Mobidays stands out for the relative speed at which it has expanded into non-advertising businesses through its subsidiaries.

06

Outlook

The company has said it intends to strengthen core competitiveness based on AI/AX systems over the medium to long term and expand its new content and IP businesses, aiming for consolidated revenue to more than double within three years.

In the same disclosure, it stated it would overhaul related policies and procedures to improve the continuity and predictability of dividends, pursue a high-dividend stance for at least the next two to three years, and operate dividend and treasury-share policies sustainably and predictably by comprehensively considering financial soundness, investment plans, and cash flow.

In line with this, the company disclosed a share buyback decision in July 2026, putting shareholder-return plans into action.

In the content and IP segment, Mobi Contents Tech is preparing roughly six to seven titles, including the film Sentimental Value followed by De Gaulle and The Invite, which are scheduled for release and distribution in the second half of 2026. The subsidiary is reportedly pursuing a KONEX listing within the year.

In the games segment, MobiGames plans to launch multiple new titles in the second half of 2026.

In the core advertising and marketing business, the company plans to expand AEO and GEO consulting and solution services to address the generative AI search environment as part of a shift toward a higher value-added structure.

However, many of these new businesses remain in an early monetization stage, so whether the three-year revenue-doubling target is achieved will need to be confirmed progressively through future quarterly results and disclosures.

08

Bull factors

Core Business Profitability Recovering

Operating profit turned positive at KRW 4.51 billion in 2025, followed by another swing to profit of KRW 2.89 billion in Q2 2026. Operating cash flow also turned into an inflow of KRW 22.7 billion in 2025, improving cash generation.

Efficiency gains in advertising and marketing operations and expanded campaign execution are cited as the main drivers of the earnings recovery.

Revenue Diversification Into Content, IP and Games

MobiGames' game publishing and Mobi Contents Tech's video IP and global commerce businesses are expanding as new revenue sources beyond the core business. With six to seven films and multiple new games in preparation, portfolio diversification is becoming more visible.

If Mobi Contents Tech's planned KONEX listing proceeds, it could open an opportunity for the subsidiary's value to be separately assessed.

Stated Commitment to Stronger Shareholder Returns

In a March 2026 disclosure, the company said it would pursue a high-dividend stance for the next two to three years, and in July it decided on a share buyback. It also indicated plans to operate dividend and treasury-share policies predictably, considering financial soundness and cash flow. This can be read as a signal of management's commitment to enhancing shareholder value.

09

Bear factors

Quarterly Earnings Volatility

After posting operating losses in both Q4 2025 and Q1 2026, the company swung back to profit in Q2 2026, reflecting large swings in quarterly results.

Net profit attributable to controlling interests fell 41.6% year over year even in the profitable Q2 2026, showing that a return to profit does not necessarily mean a larger profit.

The sum of net profit over the last four quarters was only KRW 1.71 billion, indicating the quarterly scale of earnings generation remains modest.

Rising Financial Leverage

The debt-to-equity ratio has risen every year, from 113.0% in 2022 to 157.3% in 2025. Total liabilities of KRW 77 billion at the end of 2025 significantly exceeded total equity of KRW 49 billion at the same point.

As investment in new content and game businesses continues, greater reliance on external financing could increase strain on the balance sheet.

Uncertainty Over New Business Monetization

Many of the new subsidiary businesses in video IP, games, and commerce remain at an early stage, with uncertain prospects for commercial success or revenue conversion. The three-year revenue-doubling target announced in March 2026 has not yet been accompanied by a disclosed, detailed roadmap. Expanded investment in new businesses could also weigh on costs in the near term.

10

Risk factors

Earnings Volatility Risk

Given the pattern of alternating between operating profit and loss on a quarterly basis, a single quarter's return to profit should not be assumed to reflect a sustained trend. Future quarterly reports will need to be checked to confirm whether profitability persists.

Balance Sheet Risk

The debt-to-equity ratio has risen for four consecutive years, reaching 157.3% at the end of 2025. If new business investment and working-capital needs overlap, it could affect funding costs or financial stability.

Diversification Execution Risk

New businesses such as games, video content, and commerce depend heavily on hit potential and market acceptance, so returns on investment could be delayed or fall short of expectations. The timing of structural events such as Mobi Contents Tech's planned KONEX listing has also not yet been finalized.

12

Overall view

Building on a revenue growth trend that has continued since 2022, Mobidays turned operating profit positive in 2025 and again in Q2 2026, gradually recovering profitability in its core advertising business.

At the same time, through subsidiaries such as MobiGames and Mobi Contents Tech, it has broadened into games, video IP, and global commerce, laying out a medium-term goal of more than doubling revenue within three years.

The company signaled its commitment to shareholder value by disclosing a high-dividend-oriented shareholder return policy in March 2026 and a share buyback decision in July 2026.

However, the large swings in quarterly results and the four-year rise in the debt-to-equity ratio are points that warrant careful financial monitoring.

With most of the new businesses still at an early stage, the pace at which content and game operations monetize, along with whether core-business profitability holds up in regulatory filings from Q3 2026 onward, are likely to be the key variables shaping the company's future earnings path.
