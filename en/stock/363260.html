Business structure

Mobidays, founded in 2014, is a mobile-focused ad-tech and performance marketing company whose core business is intermediating between domestic and overseas advertisers, agencies, comprehensive media reps, and mobile media.

The company operates proprietary ad-tech solutions including MobiOne, a media monetization platform, MobiConnect, a global ad traffic network, MobiAI, an automated campaign solution, and MobiWords, a reward-based offerwall platform.

It has held official partner status with Google, Meta, NAVER, Kakao, and TikTok, and maintains partnerships with roughly 100 advertising agencies and ad networks across about 20 countries.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual revenue rose for three straight years, from KRW 16.1 billion in 2022 to KRW 23.9 billion in 2023 (up 48.8%), KRW 37.2 billion in 2024 (up 55.8%), and KRW 44.4 billion in 2025 (up 19.1%), with 2025 the largest and 2022 the smallest year in this span.

Operating profit swung from KRW 4.59 billion in 2022 to an operating loss of KRW 1.9 billion in 2023, turned positive again at KRW 315 million in 2024, and reached KRW 4.51 billion in 2025, up 1,330.6% year over year.

Controlling-interest net profit posted losses for three consecutive years -- a net loss of KRW 6.67 billion in 2022, KRW 915 million in 2023 (narrower), and KRW 1.71 billion in 2024 (wider) -- before turning to a net profit of KRW 3.1 billion in 2025.

Net income including non-controlling interests followed the same pattern, also reaching KRW 3.1 billion in 2025. The operating margin fell sharply from 28.6% in 2022 to -8.0% in 2023, then gradually recovered to 0.8% in 2024 and 10.2% in 2025.

Operating cash flow swung from an inflow of KRW 5.36 billion in 2022 to outflows of -KRW 17.7 billion in 2023 and -KRW 2.35 billion in 2024, before improving markedly to an inflow of KRW 22.7 billion in 2025.

On a quarterly basis, revenue rose from KRW 12.2 billion in Q2 2025 to KRW 13 billion in Q3 2025 (up 6.6%), then fell 37.8% to KRW 8.08 billion in Q4 2025, before rising for two consecutive quarters to KRW 9.56 billion in Q1 2026 (up 18.4%) and KRW 13 billion in Q2 2026 (up 36.1% quarter over quarter, up 6.8% year over year).

Quarterly operating profit fell from KRW 2.48 billion in Q2 2025 to KRW 1.58 billion in Q3 2025 (down 36.0%), turned into an operating loss of KRW 222 million in Q4 2025, remained a loss of KRW 167 million in Q1 2026 (narrower), and then turned to a profit of KRW 2.89 billion in Q2 2026 (up 16.9% year over year).

Controlling-interest net profit likewise moved from a net loss of KRW 715 million in Q4 2025 and KRW 46.97 million in Q1 2026 to a net profit of KRW 985 million in Q2 2026 (down 41.6% year over year), with the trailing four quarters (Q3 2025 through Q2 2026) summing to KRW 1.71 billion.

05

Industry analysis

The domestic mobile ad-tech and performance marketing market that Mobidays operates in is heavily dependent on the advertising inventory and algorithm policies of large platforms such as Google, Meta, NAVER, Kakao, and TikTok.

Across the industry, the use of generative AI for ad creative production and campaign automation -- often termed AI/AX marketing -- has become a key theme, and Mobidays is restructuring its business in line with this trend.

As advertisers continue to prioritize cost efficiency, competition for media fees among performance marketing agencies persists, with other ad-tech firms offering similar mobile advertising solutions competing for the same pool of advertisers and media partners.

Expansion into adjacent areas such as content and intellectual property and global commerce is widely seen across the industry as an attempt to reduce reliance on pure ad brokerage.

Structural shifts in the data environment, including the deprecation of third-party cookies and tightening privacy regulation, represent variables that can affect targeting precision.

The advertising market itself is sensitive to macroeconomic conditions and consumer sentiment, meaning advertiser budgets can contract during economic slowdowns.

06

Outlook

In its Q2 2026 earnings release in July 2026, Mobidays said it would move forward with an H2 2026 growth strategy centered on AI/AX marketing, content/IP, and commerce.

The company reported H1 2026 results -- revenue of KRW 22.57 billion, operating profit of KRW 2.73 billion, and an operating margin of about 12.1% -- and said it would maintain a profitability-focused management approach in the second half.

In August-September 2026, a 5-for-1 share consolidation that raised the par value per share from KRW 500 to KRW 2,500 was completed, reducing total shares outstanding to roughly 6.43 million, which the company said was intended to normalize the float and improve market credibility.

CEO Yu Beom-ryeong disclosed further open-market purchases in September 2026 that raised his stake to 50.49%. Having swung from a Q1 2026 operating loss to a Q2 2026 operating profit, after consecutive losses in Q4 2025 and Q1 2026, whether this recovery continues through the second half remains to be confirmed.

As of now, disclosed periodic reports cover only through Q2 2026, and results from Q3 2026 onward have not yet been confirmed through a periodic report.

Bull factors

Simultaneous Annual and Quarterly Profit Turnaround

2025 annual operating profit rose 1,330.6% year over year to KRW 4.51 billion, pulling the company out of its 2023 loss. Q2 2026 operating profit of KRW 2.89 billion also marked a turnaround from the prior quarter, showing a quarterly recovery pattern.

The operating margin improved from -8.0% in 2023 to 10.2% in 2025, indicating a structural improvement in profitability.

Diversification into AI/AX, Content, and Commerce

The company has said it is expanding beyond traditional ad brokerage into AI/AX marketing, content and intellectual property, and commerce. Through subsidiaries such as MobiGames (game IP publishing) and MobiContentsTech (K-pop commerce exports), it continues efforts to reduce dependence on ad market cyclicality.

The company itself attributed its Q2 2026 earnings recovery in part to accelerated growth from AI/AX and content/IP businesses, consistent with this strategy.

Continued Stake Increases by the Largest Shareholder

CEO Yu Beom-ryeong disclosed open-market purchases in September 2026 that raised his stake to 50.49%. The fact that the CEO's stake has consistently remained above 50% since listing is a relevant data point for assessing management stability. This is nonetheless a fact about ownership structure rather than a signal about share price direction.

Bear factors

Recurring Quarterly Earnings Volatility

After Q3 2025 operating profit of KRW 1.58 billion, the company swung to an operating loss of KRW 222 million in Q4 2025, and the loss continued into Q1 2026 at KRW 167 million.

Although it turned profitable again at KRW 2.89 billion in Q2 2026, the pattern over the last five quarters has alternated between profit and loss. Controlling-interest net profit likewise alternated between profit and loss over the same period, with the trailing four quarters summing to only KRW 1.71 billion.

Platform Dependence and Industry Sensitivity

A substantial portion of revenue depends on the ad inventory and policies of large media platforms such as Google, Meta, NAVER, and Kakao. Advertiser budget execution is sensitive to the economy and consumer sentiment, so revenue volatility can increase during economic slowdowns.

Ongoing competition for media fees among numerous ad-tech peers with similar solutions can also pressure profitability.

Elevated Debt Ratio and Past Cash Flow Swings

The debt ratio rose for four consecutive years, from 113.0% in 2022 to 157.3% in 2025. Operating cash flow also recorded outflows for two consecutive years, -KRW 17.7 billion in 2023 and -KRW 2.35 billion in 2024, before turning to an inflow of KRW 22.7 billion only in 2025.

While the trend is one of improvement, the prior two years of deteriorating cash flow remain a factor to weigh when assessing financial stability.

Risk factors

Industry and Competitive Risk

Changes in large platforms' ad product and fee policies could directly affect media brokerage revenue. Regulatory shifts in data usage, such as the deprecation of third-party cookies, could negatively affect targeting precision and ad efficiency.

Fee competition with numerous ad-tech and performance marketing peers offering similar solutions is also an ongoing structural variable.

Financial Risk

The debt ratio has risen every year from 113.0% in 2022 to 157.3% in 2025, indicating increasing financial leverage. Operating cash flow recorded outflows for two consecutive years in 2023-2024, reflecting past liquidity management pressure.

While cash flow improved to an inflow of KRW 22.7 billion in 2025, whether this continues needs to be confirmed in upcoming quarterly results.

Governance and Liquidity Risk

With the CEO and related parties holding a stake close to 50%, minority shareholders' voting influence is structurally limited. The 5-for-1 share consolidation in August-September 2026 reduced total shares outstanding to roughly 6.43 million, lowering the float.

As a small-cap stock with a market capitalization around KRW 23.7 billion, trading value and volume are limited, which can amplify short-term volatility.

Overall view

On a full-year 2025 basis, Mobidays posted revenue of KRW 44.4 billion, operating profit of KRW 4.51 billion, and controlling-interest net profit of KRW 3.1 billion, achieving both a third consecutive year of revenue growth and a swing to profitability.

On a quarterly basis, however, the pattern has been uneven, with consecutive operating and net losses in Q4 2025 and Q1 2026 before turning profitable again in Q2 2026.

The debt ratio has risen steadily from 113.0% in 2022 to 157.3% in 2025, pointing to growing financial leverage, while operating cash flow only improved in 2025 after two consecutive years of outflow in 2023-2024.

The company has formalized a strategy of diversifying into AI/AX marketing, content/IP, and commerce to reduce reliance on traditional ad brokerage, and the CEO's stake as the largest shareholder had risen to 50.49% as of September 2026.

A 5-for-1 share consolidation in August-September 2026 reduced the float, and disclosed periodic reports currently extend only through Q2 2026.

Whether the recent profit turnaround proves one-off or structural should become clearer with the upcoming Q3 report and the annual business report, and reviewing these confirmed disclosures before forming any investment judgment is important.
