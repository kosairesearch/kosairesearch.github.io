02

Business structure

Haitai Confectionery and Foods was founded in July 2001 and makes and sells snacks and frozen foods through a nationwide sales network. Its main snack brands are Homerun Ball, Mackdong, Ohyes and Ace, and its frozen food line includes Gohyang Mandu. Honey Butter Chip, Ohyes and Homerun Ball are also exported.

The company is strengthening its mega-brands and expanding high-value products through seasonal limited editions, and it is building brand competitiveness with trend products such as a 'Dubai Style' item derived from Dubai chocolate.

As of June 2017, its production plants were in Cheonan, Gwangju and Cheongju, and in July 2022 it built a new biscuit plant in Asan, South Chungcheong Province.

Overseas sales were 8.1% (KRW 50.5 billion) in 2023, 8.0% (KRW 50.3 billion) in 2024 and 8.4% (KRW 53.6 billion) in 2025, staying in single digits, and the first-half 2026 share was 8.8%.

Among peers in the same snack market, Orion's overseas sales share is reported in the 70% range and Lotte Wellfood's in the 30% range, so Haitai's overseas dependence is low. A sister company in the same group also has a single-digit overseas share. Domestic snack demand is weakening under population decline and a falling birth rate.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual sales went from KRW 590.3 billion in 2022 to KRW 624.9 billion in 2023 (up 5.9% year on year), KRW 629.2 billion in 2024 (up 0.7%) and KRW 641.4 billion in 2025 (up 1.9%), rising for three consecutive years from 2023 through 2025, with 2025 the largest year of 2022 to 2025.

Operating profit rose from KRW 23.2 billion in 2022 to KRW 45.7 billion in 2023 (up 97.3%) and KRW 49.1 billion in 2024 (up 7.3%), making 2024 the largest year of 2022 to 2025.

Operating profit then fell 13.7% to KRW 42.3 billion in 2025, and WiseReport's company monitor summarized the drop as cost pressure from rising international grain prices and a sharp rise in the exchange rate, which cut gross profit and operating profit (as of April 15, 2026).

Operating margin went from 3.9% in 2022 to 7.3% in 2023 and 7.8% in 2024 before easing to 6.6% in 2025.

Profit attributable to controlling shareholders moved from a net loss of KRW 2.25 billion in 2022 to a net profit of KRW 21.6 billion in 2023, a turnaround to profit, then KRW 31.7 billion in 2024 (up 46.8%) and KRW 23.3 billion in 2025 (down 26.6%).

Operating cash flow was KRW 53 billion in 2025, below KRW 80.4 billion in 2024.

By quarter, operating profit was KRW 8.3 billion in the third quarter of 2025, KRW 10.3 billion in the fourth quarter and KRW 10.7 billion in the first quarter of 2026, which was the peak, before falling to KRW 9.29 billion in the second quarter and KRW 5.51 billion in the third quarter, two consecutive quarterly declines.

Third-quarter 2026 sales of KRW 155.9 billion (down 2.8% year on year) came with a cost-of-sales ratio that rose from 66.8% to 68.5%, which Newspim reported as the reason gross profit fell (November 13, 2026).

Cumulative sales for January to September 2026 were KRW 473.7 billion (down 1.9%) and operating profit KRW 25.5 billion (down 20.5%), according to the quarterly report.

Profit attributable to controlling shareholders over the last four quarters totals KRW 20.3 billion, and at the end of 2025 total liabilities were KRW 400.9 billion against total equity of KRW 309.6 billion.

05

Industry analysis

Domestic snack demand is weakening under population decline and a falling birth rate, and industry voices argue that expanding overseas markets is essential. On costs, the company said rising prices of key inputs such as naphtha and imported raw materials built up pressure on third-quarter 2026 results.

An IB Tomato report dated March 24, 2026 said the cost index for imported chocolate items surged, while oil-based inputs also rose.

The government has made food and restaurant price stability a major policy goal and has urged companies to cut prices or refrain from raising them, so the food industry as a whole has kept price increases cautious.

Against this backdrop, Haitai cut prices on some biscuit products by roughly 5% in March 2026 and then raised prices on 25 brands by an average of 7.8% in September 2026.

In competitive terms, Orion's overseas sales share is reported in the 70% range and Lotte Wellfood's in the 30% range, while Haitai's was 8.8% in the first half of 2026. A sister company in the same group said its production-then-export model limited short-term growth in sales.

Exchange-rate spikes added to cost pressure in 2025. Haitai is pushing to open sales channels in the United States, Japan and China for products such as Honey Butter Chip, Ohyes and Homerun Ball.

06

Outlook

The company said it expects the September 2026 price adjustment to show up in results from the fourth quarter, and that stronger cost-reduction efforts should have a positive effect on earnings. How far the increase offsets raw material costs still has to be confirmed in actual quarterly results.

The company plans to strengthen its mega-brands and raise the share of high-value products through seasonal limited editions, and it is building brand competitiveness with trend products such as a Dubai-style item.

Overseas, it is widening sales channels for Honey Butter Chip, Ohyes, Homerun Ball, Ivy and Ace in the United States, Japan and China. A biscuit plant in Asan that was built in July 2022 is being used as an export base, and the group has set a target of raising the overseas sales share to double digits.

The company explained that the March 2026 price cuts were limited to some products with a high flour share as a price stability measure, while acknowledging that current profitability is declining.

It also noted that rising costs are a common problem across the food industry and that uncertainty from geopolitical tensions and higher oil prices continues.

The next checkpoints are the fourth-quarter operating margin and the size of the price-increase effect in the 2026 annual report, and whether that effect persists in the first-quarter 2027 quarterly report.

08

Bull factors

Expected fourth-quarter pass-through of price increases

Reports said the average 7.8% price increase on 25 brands from September 18, 2026 would begin to show up from the fourth quarter. The company said it expects the price adjustment to have a positive effect on future results.

Because first-quarter 2026 operating profit of KRW 10.7 billion marked the recent peak, the bull case rests on whether easing cost pressure could bring quarterly profit back toward that level.

Easing financial burden

Total equity stood at KRW 309.6 billion at the end of 2025, and total liabilities at KRW 400.9 billion. Operating cash flow was KRW 53 billion in 2025, below KRW 80.4 billion in 2024 but above KRW 18.8 billion in 2022. The narrower leverage gap can be read as a buffer against cost pressure.

Room to expand from a low overseas share

Haitai's overseas sales share was 8.4% in 2025 and 8.8% in the first half of 2026, both in single digits. Compared with Orion (in the 70% range) and Lotte Wellfood (in the 30% range), the share is low, which underpins the bull argument that there is room to expand.

The company is widening sales into the United States, Japan, China and Central Asia, and the group uses its Asan plant as an export hub.

09

Bear factors

Profit pressure from a higher cost-of-sales ratio

The cost-of-sales ratio rose from 66.8% to 68.5% in the third quarter of 2026, and rising prices of naphtha and other key and imported raw materials were cited as a cumulative burden. Sales fell while the cost ratio rose, which reduced gross profit.

Because the price increase had not yet been reflected in that quarter, the pressure carried through until the fourth quarter.

Slowing sales growth

Sales growth slowed from 5.9% in 2023 to 0.7% in 2024 and 1.9% in 2025. Cumulative sales for January to September 2026 were KRW 473.7 billion, down 1.9%. Domestic snack demand is weakening, and with an overseas share in the 8% range, the overseas business is still too small to offset domestic stagnation.

Consecutive declines in operating profit

Operating profit fell from KRW 49.1 billion in 2024 to KRW 42.3 billion in 2025, and to KRW 5.51 billion in the third quarter of 2026. Profit attributable to controlling shareholders fell 26.6% to KRW 23.3 billion in 2025.

IB Tomato analysis argues that price cuts can lift volumes yet still reduce operating profit when costs and fixed expenses rise together.

Risk factors

Raw materials and exchange rate

Naphtha and other key inputs, imported raw materials and exchange-rate moves directly push up costs. In 2025, rising international grain prices and a sharp rise in the exchange rate added to the burden.

The company says it is strengthening cost reduction, but uncertainty from geopolitical tensions and higher oil prices persists.

Pricing policy and price-stability stance

The government has made food and restaurant price stability a major goal and has urged companies to avoid raising prices. Haitai cut some product prices by about 5% in March 2026 and raised prices by an average of 7.8% in September. How the size and timing of these adjustments affect volumes and margins remains a variable.

Balance sheet structure

Total liabilities at the end of 2025 were KRW 400.9 billion, above total equity of KRW 309.6 billion. The debt ratio of 129.5% is lower than before but still elevated. WiseReport's company monitor lists NICE's A rating as of May 14, 2026.

12

Overall view

Haitai Confectionery and Foods posted sales growth for three consecutive years from 2023 through 2025, and its operating margin rose from 3.9% in 2022 to 7.8% in 2024, a period in which sales and profitability improved together.

Operating profit fell to KRW 42.3 billion in 2025, and in the third quarter of 2026 it dropped to KRW 5.51 billion, down 33.7% year on year, as raw material costs reached the income statement directly.

Bull-side factors include expected fourth-quarter pass-through of the September price increase, a lower debt ratio and room to grow from a low overseas share.

Bear-side factors include raw material and exchange-rate pressure, weak domestic snack demand and a 1.9% decline in cumulative sales for January to September 2026. Because both sets of factors operate at once, results depend on how pricing strategy interacts with cost trends.

On valuation, the stock shows a low multiple and a dividend yield above the food industry average, and the key question for interpretation is how much of the earnings decline is already reflected in the multiple.

Items to confirm next are the fourth-quarter operating margin in the 2026 annual report, whether the price-increase effect persists in the first-quarter 2027 quarterly report, and the trend in raw material prices.
