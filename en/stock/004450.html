02

Business structure

Samhwa Crown & Closure was founded in 1965 as a maker and seller of bottle caps and was first designated as a tax-stamp bottle cap manufacturer in 1972. It consolidated its scattered factories into a single Ansan plant in 1997, and joined the Geumbi group in 2010, which broadened it into a general packaging company.

The business is split into a CAP segment and a plastic container segment, with bottle caps for liquor and beverage products as the core. In the first half of 2025, the CAP segment accounted for 73.4% of revenue, so bottle caps make up most of sales.

In 2016 it acquired Shinwoo, a cosmetics container maker, extending into plastic containers. Its tax-stamp cap designation is valid through February 29, 2028. Its main customers are concentrated among liquor and beverage makers, so their production and sales flows feed directly into its revenue.

Its largest shareholder holds 53.43%, which gives that holder considerable influence over management and dividend decisions. The government-issued tax-stamp manufacturer status is one of the pillars of its business base.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual revenue rose from KRW 154.4 billion in 2022 to KRW 157.9 billion in 2023, up 2.2%, and to KRW 167.1 billion in 2024, up 5.9%, before falling to KRW 162.9 billion in 2025, down 2.5%. Among 2022 to 2025, 2024 was the largest year for revenue and 2022 the smallest.

Operating profit grew from KRW 3.61 billion in 2022 to KRW 6.95 billion in 2023, up 92.6%, and to KRW 10.6 billion in 2024, up 53.0%, then fell to KRW 6.76 billion in 2025, down 36.4%. The operating margin climbed from 2.3% in 2022 to 6.4% in 2024 and eased to 4.1% in 2025.

Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders was a net loss of KRW 17.2 billion in 2022 and a net loss of KRW 6.65 billion in 2023, with the loss narrowing, before turning to a profit of KRW 8.02 billion in 2024. It was KRW 4.29 billion in 2025, down 46.5%.

Net profit including non-controlling interests matched the controlling-shareholder figure in every year from 2022 to 2025. The company pointed to higher imported raw material prices and a stronger US dollar against the won as the main drivers of its 2025 results.

By quarter, Q2 2026 revenue of KRW 48.4 billion was the highest of the last five reported quarters, yet operating profit fell to KRW 1.08 billion.

Q3 2026 revenue of KRW 45.2 billion rose 10.4% year on year and operating profit of KRW 1.75 billion rose 2.4%, but net profit attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 333 million fell 67.5%.

That net profit peaked at KRW 2.75 billion in Q1 2026 and then declined in Q2 to KRW 778 million and in Q3 to KRW 333 million. Because operating profit and net profit moved in different directions, non-operating items and taxes are points to check in the next annual report.

The debt ratio fell from 86.1% in 2022 to 80.9% in 2025, and operating cash flow peaked at KRW 17.2 billion in 2024 before dropping to KRW 13.4 billion in 2025.

05

Industry analysis

Demand for bottle caps moves with the production and sales of its main customers, which are liquor and beverage makers. Revenue trends therefore track the production volumes of those customers.

Because the company cited imported raw material prices and the won-dollar exchange rate as main drivers of its 2025 results, raw material costs and currency are the key variables for margins. Outside assessments also point to continued vulnerability to a slowing economy and cost swings.

The operating margin rose from 2.3% in 2022 to 6.4% in 2024 and eased to 4.1% in 2025, a pattern that shows profitability swinging sharply with cost conditions.

Its business has widened from bottle caps into plastic containers since it acquired the cosmetics container maker Shinwoo in 2016, yet the CAP segment still made up 73.4% of revenue in the first half of 2025.

Its tax-stamp cap designation, valid through February 29, 2028, reflects its standing as a government-designated manufacturer. Because beverage and liquor consumption shapes the direction of bottle cap demand, the pace of its revenue recovery moves together with the recovery in customer production.

Bull factors

Quarterly sales recovering from the low

Q3 2026 revenue was KRW 45.2 billion, up 10.4% year on year. Q2 2026 revenue of KRW 48.4 billion was the highest of the last five reported quarters. Q4 2025, at KRW 37.2 billion, was the low point of that span, so the sales base has widened since then. Higher sales did not translate into higher profit, but the top line has moved toward recovery.

Traces of operating margin improvement

The operating margin rose from 2.3% in 2022 to 6.4% in 2024 and eased to 4.1% in 2025, still above the 2022 level. Quarterly operating profit reached KRW 1.77 billion in Q1 2026 and KRW 1.75 billion in Q3 2026, both in the KRW 1 billion range. The bull case rests on the room for margin improvement if raw material and currency conditions stabilize.

Asset base and dividend record

The debt ratio fell from 86.1% in 2022 to 80.9% in 2025, and at the end of 2025 total equity and equity attributable to controlling shareholders were both KRW 118.5 billion.

Cash dividends stayed in the range of about KRW 2 billion a year from 2022 to 2024, and a cash dividend was approved at the March 27, 2026 shareholders meeting. The bull side points to this asset base and dividend record.

Bear factors

Shrinking profit base

Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders fell 46.5% to KRW 4.29 billion in 2025. In Q3 2026 it was KRW 333 million, down 67.5% year on year, and it declined in both Q2 and Q3. The four-quarter total from Q4 2025 through Q3 2026 is KRW 4.27 billion.

Exposure to raw materials and currency

Costs rise when imported raw material prices and the won-dollar exchange rate go up. In a period of economic slowdown or cost swings, the operating margin could fall further.

Q3 2026 operating profit rose 2.4% year on year while net profit attributable to controlling shareholders fell sharply, so whether non-operating items and taxes explain that gap is the point to check.

Governance and compensation questions

The largest shareholder holds 53.43% of the shares. Press coverage has raised the point that the dividend policy favors major shareholders over minority holders.

Press coverage also noted that among the companies compared, this company was the only one in which an executive director received compensation of KRW 500 million or more.

Risk factors

Tax-stamp designation renewal

The tax-stamp bottle cap manufacturer designation is valid through February 29, 2028, so whether it is renewed could shape the company's business base. Because the designation is granted by the government, the renewal conditions are outside the company's control.

If the designation is not renewed or its terms change, the premise of the bottle cap business could be affected. The CAP segment, at 73.4% of first-half 2025 revenue, is directly tied to this status, so the scope of impact is wide.

Customer and demand concentration

Its main customers are concentrated among liquor and beverage makers, so changes in their production and sales flow directly into its revenue. The fall from KRW 48.4 billion in Q2 2026 to KRW 45.2 billion in Q3 is one example of that swing.

How much order changes from large customers affect results is a question for the customer disclosures in the annual report.

Weaker operating cash flow

Operating cash flow peaked at KRW 17.2 billion in 2024 before falling to KRW 13.4 billion in 2025. If this decline continues alongside lower profit, the room for internal funding could narrow. Because cash dividends of about KRW 2 billion a year were maintained, weaker cash generation could weigh on dividend capacity. The direction of this trend can be checked in the cash flow statement of the 2026 annual report.
