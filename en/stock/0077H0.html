02

Business structure

Mobicontents Tech was founded on June 1, 2023 and is headquartered in Gangnam-gu, Seoul. It is an affiliate of the Mobidays group. Its main products are K-pop goods and merchandise.

The company runs the global K-content commerce platform COCODIVE and supplies goods to overseas markets through its own online store and other channels.

Its product range has widened from albums and merchandise to K-beauty, fashion and stationery, and it also produces IP goods, runs fan events and offers logistics agency services. Overseas customers account for 99% of total revenue, and North America is the main market.

A wholly owned US subsidiary is part of the structure. A partnership with a Korean design retail brand distributes K-lifestyle goods abroad. The mix is centered on B2C commerce, while B2B and new businesses are in an expansion phase. Among rival K-pop goods platforms, several private startups and SMEs compete in the same niche.

04

Earnings analysis

On a DART-filed basis, revenue in 2025 was KRW 28.6 billion, up 30.5% from KRW 22 billion in 2024. Over the same period operating profit fell from KRW 1.01 billion to KRW 412 million, down 59.3%, and operating margin dropped from 4.6% to 1.4%.

Revenue growth alongside falling profit suggests cost growth outpaced sales growth and compressed margins. Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders fell 73.0% from KRW 1.49 billion to KRW 401 million.

Net profit including non-controlling interests is reported at the same levels, KRW 1.49 billion in 2024 and KRW 401 million in 2025. On the revenue side, the subsidiary running COCODIVE reported cumulative third-quarter 2025 revenue of KRW 23.7 billion, up 58% year on year, alongside the success of KPop Demon Hunters.

That figure is a press-reported nine-month number, not an annual filing. Operating cash flow rose from KRW 274 million in 2024 to KRW 527 million in 2025, moving against the profit decline.

The debt ratio rose from 14.9% to 25.3%; equity of KRW 32.2 billion at year-end 2025 is entirely attributable to controlling shareholders, and total liabilities were KRW 8.13 billion. Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders over the last four quarters of 2025 totaled KRW 401 million.

Whether one-off items contributed to the profit decline should be checked in the detailed income statement lines of the 2025 annual report. Quarterly 2026 results have not yet been filed in a periodic report.

05

Industry analysis

Demand for K-pop goods and merchandise follows the spending patterns of a global fan base, so it is highly sensitive to hit content. The 2025 revenue growth coincided with that rise in K-pop popularity. Across the content industry, IP-based business expansion is accelerating.

The parent Mobidays treats global commercialization of K-pop IP as its core growth strategy. Mobicontents Tech links content IP to commerce and builds overseas fan spending data.

Its 2025 export award counts only direct exports recorded at customs, so transactions routed through domestic freight forwarders are excluded. Rival companies in this space are classed as startups or SMEs, which makes their scale far smaller than that of large entertainment groups.

Unlike large label-and-artist companies such as Hybe or SM Entertainment, this company earns mainly from goods and commerce. Its revenue therefore moves with individual hit titles, which is both a growth driver and a source of volatility.

Outlook

The video IP business has six to seven titles in preparation for 2026, including The Invite and another title slated for release or distribution in the second half of the year. The company aims to keep its B2C growth while expanding B2B and new businesses.

Regionally, the 2026 plan centers on South America with entry into new markets such as the Middle East. At its January 2026 investment round, the company said it would push platform competitiveness and global expansion in earnest. The listing was approved on October 21, 2026 and trading began on October 23.

The parent Mobidays reported a return to operating profit in the second quarter of 2026 on a consolidated basis. The subsidiary's 2026 quarterly results have not yet been filed in a periodic report, so the next checkpoint is the sales growth and operating margin in the third-quarter 2026 report.

The company is strengthening its overseas supply chain on the back of North American performance (19-6). New regional expansion into South America and the Middle East in 2026 adds to the distribution base (19-7).

Bear factors

Thin margins and falling profit

Operating margin fell from 4.6% in 2024 to 1.4% in 2025. Operating profit fell 59.3%, and net profit attributable to controlling shareholders fell 73.0%. Profit shrinking despite 30.5% revenue growth is the core bearish argument.

Share structure and early trading

The top five shareholders hold 95.65% combined, a high concentration (3-12). No shares traded on the regular market on the first listing day (4-8). A thin free float raises the possibility of unstable price formation (3-13).

Dependence on hit titles and export measurement

Third-quarter 2025 cumulative growth coincided with the success of KPop Demon Hunters (15-3). If hit-driven demand fades, revenue growth could slow. Export awards count only direct exports recorded at customs, so reported figures may differ from total overseas sales (19-5).

Risk factors

Profitability risk

With operating margin in the low single digits, rising costs can easily absorb profit. Cost growth appears to have outpaced revenue growth in 2025. If costs are not controlled, profit recovery may lag even as sales grow. The pace of recovery is a point to check in the 2026 quarterly reports.

Ownership and liquidity

Mobidays holds 66.96%, and the top five shareholders combined hold 95.65% (3-11, 3-12). A limited free float after listing could leave trading thin. Strategic changes by the controlling group could also affect share supply and demand.

Reliance on overseas markets and platform

With overseas customers at 99% of revenue, the company is exposed to customs policy, exchange rates and changes in overseas platform rules (19-1). Expanding from North America into South America and the Middle East could raise local logistics and distribution costs. The pace of executing the platform competitiveness plan is a key factor (9-12).
