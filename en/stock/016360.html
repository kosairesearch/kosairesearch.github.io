Samsung Securities: Wealth and IB Offset a Trading Slump

Samsung Securities' operating profit rose 11.7% year on year to KRW 449 billion in the third quarter of 2026, but fell 33.6% from the prior quarter as lower trading volumes weighed on brokerage fees.

02

Business structure

Samsung Securities reports its results across brokerage, wealth management (WM), investment banking (IB) and sales and trading (S&T).

On a standalone basis for the third quarter of 2026, net brokerage fees were KRW 236.5 billion, financial product sales income KRW 75.4 billion, product trading and financial income KRW 299.9 billion, and underwriting and advisory fees KRW 81.8 billion, according to the company's announcement on November 12, 2026.

Of the brokerage fees, domestic equity accounted for KRW 171.8 billion and overseas equity for KRW 64.7 billion, so this line moves closely with trading volumes.

The WM base rests on client assets: retail client assets were KRW 578.7 trillion at the end of the third quarter, up 47.1% year on year but down 11.3% from the prior quarter.

High-net-worth (HNW) clients numbered 524,000, up 52.7% year on year, and retirement pension assets under custody reached KRW 27.7 trillion, up 46.6%. Wrap account net fees rose 75.9% year on year to KRW 20.1 billion but fell 59.5% from the second quarter.

IB income of KRW 81.8 billion comprised structured finance KRW 57.1 billion, ECM KRW 11.4 billion, M&A KRW 9.5 billion and DCM KRW 3.9 billion. The company cited an IPO and an acquisition-financing deal as its major transactions.

In a competitive comparison, NH Investment & Securities offset weak brokerage with asset management and IB income, while Samsung Securities leans more on product sales and fees tied to client assets, according to Smart Biz.

The foreign-investor integrated account, set up with IBKR, has generated about KRW 60 trillion in trade contracts since late April 2026, the company said.

04

Earnings analysis

On a DART annual-report basis, operating profit was KRW 578.1 billion in 2022, KRW 741.1 billion in 2023 (up 28.2% year on year), KRW 1.21 trillion in 2024 (up 62.7%) and KRW 1.38 trillion in 2025 (up 14.1%).

It rose for three consecutive years from 2023 through 2025, with 2025 the largest year and 2022 the smallest in the 2022 to 2025 span.

Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders went from KRW 422.4 billion in 2022 to KRW 547.4 billion in 2023, KRW 899 billion in 2024 and KRW 1.01 trillion in 2025, with 2024 showing the largest growth rate (up 64.2%).

Net profit including non-controlling interests is reported as a separate line and should be read apart from the controlling-shareholder figure.

On a quarterly basis, operating profit was KRW 401.8 billion in the third quarter of 2025, KRW 330.6 billion in the fourth quarter (down 17.7% from the prior quarter), KRW 609.5 billion in the first quarter of 2026 (up 84.4%), KRW 675.8 billion in the second quarter (up 10.9%) and KRW 449 billion in the third quarter (down 33.6% from the prior quarter, up 11.7% year on year).

Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders moved from KRW 309.2 billion to KRW 215 billion, then recovered to KRW 450.9 billion and KRW 488.2 billion, before falling to KRW 352.8 billion in the third quarter. Hankyung described the second quarter of 2026 as a record quarter.

The third-quarter decline traced back to brokerage: standalone net brokerage fees fell 46.2% from the prior quarter, with domestic equity down 46.9% and overseas equity down 44.2%. Over the same quarter, wrap account net fees rose 75.9% year on year and ECM fees rose 147.8%, showing a shift in the fee mix.

On the balance sheet, the debt-to-equity ratio rose from 750.2% in 2024 to 887.6% in 2025, with total equity of KRW 8.07 trillion and total liabilities of KRW 71.6 trillion at year-end 2025.

Operating cash flow ran KRW 7.44 trillion in 2022, -KRW 2.81 trillion in 2023, KRW 1.34 trillion in 2024 and -KRW 5.32 trillion in 2025. Because this line reflects movements in operating assets and liabilities, it does not move in step with operating profit.

05

Industry analysis

The domestic stock market was in a downturn in the third quarter of 2026, and falling average daily trading value made the operating environment for brokers difficult, according to Seoul Times-linked market reporting carried by STRAIGHT NEWS.

While foreign investors net sold KRW 170 trillion on the main board over the same period, the foreign-investor integrated account kept buying, the company said, according to BizWatch.

By contrast, in the second quarter of 2026 a strong stock market lifted domestic equity brokerage fees on a standalone basis 213.8% year on year to KRW 323.7 billion, according to iNews24. This shows how sensitive brokerage income is to the trading-value cycle. Revenue mixes also differ across brokers.

AJU News analyzed that the fee income of five major brokers varied by business unit in the cumulative third quarter of 2026. NH Investment & Securities offset weaker brokerage with asset management and IB income, while Samsung Securities secured earnings on the back of WM client assets, according to Smart Biz.

On the regulatory side, Samsung Securities has moved its short-term bond-issuance business into full operation and is preparing to enter the comprehensive investment account (IMA) business, according to Green Economy News.

Its client-asset scale and IB channel breadth are cited as strengths, while its heavy reliance on brokerage keeps exposure to trading conditions high.

06

Outlook

The medium-term direction the company has outlined combines the full launch of its short-term bond-issuance business, preparation to enter the IMA business, and an expanded medium-to-long-term shareholder return policy, according to Green Economy News. No specific timing or scale for IMA entry has been confirmed.

In investment banking, ECM income rose 147.8% year on year to KRW 11.4 billion, and the company completed an IPO and an acquisition-financing deal that broadened its fee base. IB fees depend on when individual deals close, so the next quarter's trend should be read against the timing of deal recognition.

The company said its net capital ratio (NCR) stays comfortably above the regulatory requirement, according to STRAIGHT NEWS, and that capital headroom underpins business expansion. Analyst views differ.

Hyundai Motor Securities analyst Kim Sung-hoon noted that third-quarter brokerage fee income fell 46.0% from the prior quarter and asset management fee income fell 42.6%, according to Viewers.

In the same report, iM Securities analyst Seol Yong-jin expected IB income to keep a stable earnings flow, led by high-quality real estate project financing deals. Viewers also noted that the dividend appeal remains intact despite the market slowdown.

08

Bull factors

Wealth base expanding

Retail client assets reached KRW 578.7 trillion at the end of the third quarter of 2026, up 47.1% year on year, and HNW clients rose 52.7% to 524,000. Wrap account net fees (KRW 20.1 billion, up 75.9%) and retirement pension assets under custody (KRW 27.7 trillion, up 46.6%) moved in the same direction. Client-based income supported results in a quarter when brokerage fees shrank, which is the main bullish point.

IB income broadening

Of the third quarter's KRW 81.8 billion in IB income, ECM fees reached KRW 11.4 billion, up 147.8% year on year, and M&A fees reached KRW 9.5 billion, up 120.9%. The completion of an IPO and an acquisition-financing deal reduced reliance on structured finance alone. Structured finance fees fell to KRW 57.1 billion from KRW 87.5 billion a year earlier, while ECM and M&A grew.

Shareholder returns and expansion

The company said it is moving its short-term bond-issuance business into full operation while preparing for IMA entry, and is pursuing expanded medium-to-long-term shareholder returns, according to Green Economy News. Viewers reported that the dividend appeal remains intact despite the market slowdown.

The company said its NCR stays comfortably above regulatory requirements, according to STRAIGHT NEWS. Total equity of KRW 8.07 trillion at year-end 2025 forms the base for this expansion.

09

Bear factors

Sensitivity to trading value

Standalone net brokerage fees fell 46.2% from the prior quarter to KRW 236.5 billion in the third quarter of 2026, with domestic equity fees down 46.9% to KRW 171.8 billion. The domestic market downturn and lower trading value were cited as the direct causes.

Overseas equity fees dropped 44.2%, so both domestic and overseas trading were affected. Brokerage income can swing sharply within a single quarter, which is the main bearish point.

Quarterly earnings volatility

Operating profit fell 17.7% from the prior quarter to KRW 330.6 billion in the fourth quarter of 2025 and fell 33.6% to KRW 449 billion in the third quarter of 2026, reversing direction from quarter to quarter.

Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders swung from KRW 215 billion in the fourth quarter of 2025 to KRW 488.2 billion in the second quarter of 2026. Despite a four-quarter total of KRW 1.51 trillion, the quarter-by-quarter path depends heavily on trading conditions.

Balance sheet and cash flow strain

The debt-to-equity ratio rose to 887.6% at year-end 2025 from 750.2% in 2024, with total liabilities of KRW 71.6 trillion. Operating cash flow was negative in both 2023 (-KRW 2.81 trillion) and 2025 (-KRW 5.32 trillion). With leverage rising, the swings in operating cash flow remain a bearish point.

10

Risk factors

Market and trading value

A domestic market slowdown and falling average daily trading value feed quickly into brokerage fees. Heavy foreign net selling in the third quarter of 2026 points to unstable market flows. If trading value shrinks again, product sales income and IB fees may not fully offset the gap. This risk is directly tied to the domestic market cycle.

Regulation and licensing

Full operation of the short-term bond-issuance business and entry into the IMA business depend on regulatory approval. The timing and scale of IMA entry have not been confirmed. A change in the regulatory environment could shift when and how much these new revenue sources contribute. Expansion must also proceed while keeping capital ratios above requirements.

Financial structure and cash flow

The debt-to-equity ratio reached 887.6% in 2025, with total liabilities of KRW 71.6 trillion against total equity of KRW 8.07 trillion. Operating cash flow moved from positive in 2022 to negative in 2023 and 2025, with large year-to-year swings.

Because operating profit and operating cash flow do not move together, the relationship between the two should be checked in the next annual report.

12

Overall view

Samsung Securities' operating profit in the third quarter of 2026 was KRW 449 billion, up 11.7% year on year but down 33.6% from the prior quarter. Brokerage fees fell sharply as trading value shrank, and WM client-asset income and IB's ECM and M&A fees partly filled that gap.

On an annual basis, net profit attributable to controlling shareholders rose from KRW 422.4 billion in 2022 to KRW 1.01 trillion in 2025, rising for three consecutive years from 2023. The last four quarters total KRW 1.51 trillion.

On the other hand, the debt-to-equity ratio rose to 887.6% at year-end 2025, and operating cash flow alternated between negative and positive across years. Bullish factors are the growth of client assets and a broader IB income base.

Bearish factors are sensitivity to trading value, quarterly earnings volatility and the strain on the balance sheet. The next checkpoints are the provisional fourth-quarter 2026 results, the operating profit trend and operating cash flow in the 2026 annual report, and the timing of IMA entry.
