Overseas POS Recovery Meets a Third-Quarter Loss

Overseas POS demand lifted Posbank's second-quarter 2026 results, but the third quarter swung to a net loss attributable to controlling shareholders, and the industrial terminal acquisition and robotics push are the next variables.

02

Business structure

Posbank makes POS (point-of-sale) terminals and kiosks and is listed on KOSDAQ, with overseas ODM and OEM orders for customers as the main growth driver.

According to a Hana Securities analysis reported in December 2025, POS accounted for 74.8% of sales in the first nine months of 2025, kiosks 5.8%, merchandise 5.6%, and other items 13.8%. The same material put overseas sales at about 70% of revenue, with the United States at 40% and Korea at 24%.

Overseas customers named in the press include McDonald's, Dunkin', Popeyes and Zara, and domestic customers include Ediya Coffee, Twosome Place and GS25-group chain E-mart24.

Production runs at the Pyeongtaek plant, and after a 2025 expansion the company planned to raise POS and kiosk capacity from 280,000 to 400,000 units a year. In April 2025 Posbank invested KRW 2 billion in the autonomous robot developer RGT and entered serving robots, which are run mainly through a subsidiary.

In an October 20, 2026 announcement, the subsidiary agreed to take over an industrial mobile terminal business for roughly KRW 20 billion including assets and liabilities, and that business booked about KRW 8.9 billion in sales in the first half of 2026.

The deal carries about KRW 14 billion in assumed liabilities, and segment profitability is not disclosed, so the timing of a profit recovery is hard to pin down from filings alone.

Hana Securities describes Posbank as the largest POS maker in Korea by sales and cites its in-house quality control processes as a strength. At a September 2026 investor briefing, the company presented its POS and kiosk competitiveness overseas and robotics and AI as medium-term growth pillars.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual revenue fell from KRW 91.5 billion in 2022 to KRW 79.3 billion in 2023 (down 13.3% year on year) and KRW 77.1 billion in 2024 (down 2.8%), then recovered to KRW 91.4 billion in 2025 (up 18.6%). Within the disclosed period, 2022 was the largest revenue year and 2024 the smallest.

Operating profit declined from KRW 12 billion in 2022 to KRW 8.43 billion in 2023 (down 29.7%) and KRW 2.98 billion in 2024 (down 64.6%), then edged up to KRW 3.38 billion in 2025 (up 13.4%). The operating margin fell from 13.1% in 2022 to 10.6%, 3.9% and 3.7% in 2025.

Margins fell together with the revenue contraction, and in 2025 revenue recovered without a matching margin recovery. Profit attributable to controlling shareholders peaked at KRW 7.53 billion in 2023 (up 8.1%), then fell to KRW 5.4 billion in 2024 (down 28.2%) and KRW 3.78 billion in 2025 (down 30.1%).

Net income including non-controlling interests was disclosed at the same yearly amounts. Operating cash flow dropped from KRW 21.5 billion in 2022 to KRW 5.65 billion in 2023, turned to KRW -2.13 billion in 2024, and returned to KRW 4.65 billion in 2025.

The debt-to-equity ratio improved from 97.4% in 2022 to 54.0%, 19.6% and 24.6% in 2025, and at year-end 2025 total equity was KRW 78.4 billion with total liabilities of KRW 19.3 billion.

Quarterly results show an operating loss of KRW 392 million in the third quarter of 2025 (revenue KRW 18.3 billion) followed by an operating profit of KRW 1.61 billion in the fourth quarter, a swing to profit. First-quarter 2026 operating profit was KRW 2 billion, up 24.5% from the prior quarter.

Second-quarter 2026 revenue was KRW 36.6 billion (up 38.1% quarter on quarter) and operating profit KRW 6.32 billion (up 215.9%), the largest operating profit of the last five quarters; the company attributed this to overseas sales of its core POS products and expanded ODM supply to B2B customers.

Third-quarter 2026 revenue rose to KRW 28.2 billion (up 54.5% year on year), but operating profit fell to KRW 1.83 billion (down 71.1% from the prior quarter) and the company posted a net loss attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 1.22 billion, turning to a loss.

Pre-tax income also turned negative, and the reasons need to be checked against the detailed items in the quarterly report.

Profit attributable to controlling shareholders over the last four quarters (Q4 2025 to Q3 2026) totaled KRW 10.1 billion, with the second quarter's KRW 6.66 billion making up the largest share, so quarterly swings are large.

05

Industry analysis

Market data cited by the company put the global POS market at USD 33.4 billion in 2024, growing to USD 110.2 billion by 2028 at a 16% compound annual rate. The same material projected the kiosk market growing from USD 32.4 billion to USD 43.6 billion over the same period, at 7.6% a year.

On the demand side, the company says US restaurant franchises are adding kiosks and POS systems to ease labor cost pressure. In the cycle, sales were above KRW 90 billion in 2022, fell into the KRW 70 billion range in 2023 and 2024 amid a global downturn, and recovered to KRW 91.4 billion in 2025.

Hana Securities describes Posbank as the largest domestic POS maker by sales, and the press notes its in-house quality processes spanning production to shipment.

Overseas sales of about 70% spread across the United States, Italy, Malaysia, Japan and the UAE, so exchange rates and overseas economic conditions are relevant variables. Capacity rose from 174,000 to 283,000 units after the IPO, and a plan to reach 400,000 units in 2026 was reported.

Because the robotics and industrial terminal businesses have different demand bases and do not disclose segment profit, their position in the industry is hard to compare with numbers.

Strengths are the domestic leadership and the overseas B2B customer base, while weaknesses are the heavy reliance on overseas markets and exposure to global economic swings, as seen in 2023 and 2024.

06

Outlook

Cumulative revenue for the first half of 2026 reached KRW 63.1 billion, up 34.0% year on year, and operating profit reached KRW 8.3 billion, a sharp increase over the same period.

Revenue for the first three quarters of 2026 totaled roughly KRW 91.3 billion, with operating profit around KRW 10.1 billion, both well above the prior-year cumulative figures. The next confirmation points are the fourth-quarter 2026 results and the 2026 annual report.

The industrial mobile terminal acquisition was announced on October 20, 2026, and once it closes, the business's sales (about KRW 8.9 billion in the first half of 2026) will be consolidated into Posbank's results.

On funding, the subsidiary has received KRW 11 billion in cumulative capital injections, and about KRW 14 billion in liabilities is being assumed, according to press reports.

A 2025 report had planned a formal serving-robot launch in March 2026, but segment profit is not disclosed, so post-launch performance is hard to gauge from filings alone.

At a September 2026 investor briefing, the company said it would widen its robot line from serving robots to commercial robots and link them to existing retail solutions.

In 2025 it said it had invested KRW 2 billion to develop a global PCI wireless payment terminal model, and the commercialization timing needs to be confirmed through future disclosures. If the planned capacity of 400,000 units is operating, the company says its ability to meet overseas ODM volumes would improve.

Bull factors

Recovery in overseas POS demand

Revenue for the first half of 2026 reached KRW 63.1 billion, up 34.0% year on year, and the company attributed the second-quarter gain to higher overseas sales and expanded ODM supply to B2B customers. Revenue in 2025 was KRW 91.4 billion, up 18.6% from 2024. With overseas sales near 70% of the total, overseas POS demand is the main driver of the recovery.

Capacity and balance sheet base

A plan to lift POS and kiosk capacity to 400,000 units has been reported, and the debt-to-equity ratio improved from 97.4% in 2022 to 24.6% in 2025. Total equity of KRW 78.4 billion at year-end 2025 is entirely attributable to controlling shareholders. Operating cash flow returned to KRW 4.65 billion positive in 2025.

Broader product lineup

The industrial mobile terminal business generated about KRW 8.9 billion in sales in the first half of 2026. Once the deal closes, the lineup would extend from POS and kiosks to serving robots and industrial terminals. The company says it aims to create sales synergies between the terminal and serving-robot businesses.

Bear factors

Third-quarter swing to a loss

Third-quarter 2026 revenue rose to KRW 28.2 billion, up 54.5% year on year, but the company posted a net loss attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 1.22 billion. Operating profit fell to KRW 1.83 billion, down 71.1% from the prior quarter, so the second-quarter rebound did not carry into the next quarter. Quarterly earnings volatility remains high.

Long-term margin erosion

Operating profit fell from KRW 12 billion in 2022 to KRW 2.98 billion in 2024 before a modest recovery to KRW 3.38 billion in 2025. The operating margin dropped from 13.1% in 2022 to 3.7% in 2025, so revenue recovery has not translated into margin recovery. Profit attributable to controlling shareholders declined in both 2024 and 2025.

Acquisition and funding burden

Cumulative capital injections into the subsidiary reached KRW 11 billion, and the industrial terminal acquisition assumes about KRW 14 billion in liabilities. Because segment profit is not disclosed, it is hard to tell when the robotics and terminal businesses will begin to earn profits. The investment scale is large relative to current earnings.

Risk factors

Diversification and acquisition risk

The industrial terminal acquisition and serving-robot investments rest on different demand bases from POS. If integration is delayed or the assumed liabilities weigh more heavily, core operating cash flow could be diverted to these investments. Because segment profit is not disclosed, outside observers have limited means to check performance.

Overseas dependence and currency risk

Overseas sales are about 70% of revenue, with the United States at 40%. A slowdown in US restaurant chains' equipment spending or in the global economy could reduce orders. Revenue falling into the KRW 70 billion range in 2023 and 2024 shows this exposure.

Earnings volatility and preliminary-figure risk

The third-quarter 2026 figures are internal, unaudited results and may change in part. Of the KRW 10.1 billion in profit attributable to controlling shareholders over the last four quarters, the second quarter accounts for a large share, so quarterly swings are wide. Dividend and investment capacity could both move with quarterly results.

12

Overall view

As Korea's largest POS maker by sales, Posbank recovered revenue to KRW 91.4 billion in 2025 on overseas B2B demand, and its second-quarter 2026 operating profit of KRW 6.32 billion was the largest of the last five quarters.

However, third-quarter 2026 revenue fell from the prior quarter to KRW 28.2 billion and the company posted a net loss attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 1.22 billion, so quarterly volatility remains high.

Operating profit fell from KRW 12 billion in 2022 to KRW 2.98 billion in 2024, and the 3.7% operating margin in 2025 means whether revenue recovery has become profit recovery needs confirmation over the full-year trend.

The balance sheet is stable with a 24.6% debt-to-equity ratio at year-end 2025, and operating cash flow recovered to KRW 4.65 billion. The industrial terminal acquisition and serving robots widen the sales base but come with about KRW 14 billion in assumed liabilities and initial investment costs.

The next checkpoints are the fourth-quarter 2026 preliminary results, the 2026 annual report, and the consolidated revenue contribution after the terminal deal closes. Both strengths and weaknesses are present, and this report does not offer an investment opinion.
