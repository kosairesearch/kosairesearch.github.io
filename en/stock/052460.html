Report overview

AI Infrastructure Orders Rise, Seasonality Persists

Icraft posted sharply higher revenue and a swing to profit in 2025 on rising AI data center demand, but recorded operating losses again in the first half of 2026 amid pronounced quarter-to-quarter revenue swings.

02

Business structure

Icraft was founded in 2000 and listed on KOSDAQ in December 2004, initially focusing on network integration services building internet core and transmission networks for telecom carriers.

The company later expanded into total network construction services, including BcN and security solutions, for general enterprises and the public sector.

More recently, in response to expanding AI investment, Icraft has been diversifying from a network-equipment-centric business toward the cloud and AI data center market, cultivating GPU-based AI data center design and build-out along with full-stack AI services as new growth pillars.

The company has three affiliates, including one called Sience, whose operations are reflected in consolidated results. Its main customer base consists of cloud service providers (CSPs) such as Naver Cloud, telecom carriers, and government or public-sector agencies procuring AI infrastructure projects.

A distinguishing feature of the business is its capability, as an NVIDIA partner in Korea, to integrate core AI data center components including GPU servers, Infiniband networking, and AI storage.

In terms of competitive positioning, larger system integrators and cloud operators such as Samsung SDS and KT Cloud participate in the market as CSPs, while Icraft positions itself as a vendor or partner that either collaborates with them or participates directly in government-led projects.

Given this order-driven business model, revenue recognition is heavily influenced by the timing and scale of individual project awards and contracts.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual results show Icraft near breakeven in 2022 with revenue of KRW 112.9bn and operating profit of only KRW 0.25bn, before improving in 2023 to revenue of KRW 138.7bn and operating profit of KRW 5.15bn (a 3.7% operating margin).

In 2024, revenue declined to KRW 100.5bn, and while operating profit held at KRW 1.6bn, net income fell to a loss of KRW 0.93bn, suggesting a drag from non-operating items.

In 2025, revenue more than doubled to KRW 206.5bn, operating profit rose to KRW 8.01bn, and net income reached KRW 10.14bn, marking a clear swing to profitability.

This is corroborated by FnGuide data showing consolidated 2025 revenue up 105.5% year-on-year, operating profit up 401.6%, and net income turning to a profit, with the source noting that rising AI infrastructure investment drove data center system demand and accelerated digital transformation, while partnerships with AI hardware and software makers and prior data center build-out experience supported revenue growth.

Quarterly results, however, show extreme volatility.

In the second quarter of 2025, revenue was KRW 29.38bn with an operating loss of KRW 0.72bn, which local media confirmed by reporting a consolidated operating loss of KRW 722 million, down 54.43% year-on-year, and revenue of KRW 29.38bn, up 48.84% over the same period.

By contrast, the fourth quarter of 2025 saw revenue of KRW 109.1bn—more than half of the full-year total in a single quarter—along with operating profit of KRW 11.42bn and net income of KRW 8.19bn, meaning most of the annual result was concentrated in that one quarter.

Entering 2026, however, first-quarter revenue fell to KRW 16.34bn with an operating loss of KRW 2.50bn and a net loss of KRW 1.94bn, and although second-quarter revenue recovered to KRW 40.03bn, the company still posted an operating loss of KRW 0.29bn and a net loss of KRW 1.73bn, extending losses through the first half.

Wise Report attributes this pattern to the fact that consolidated first-quarter 2026 revenue fell 59.4% year-on-year, the operating loss widened 52.7%, and the net loss widened 77.8%, with the deterioration occurring because telecom carrier network investment is concentrated in the second half, leaving first-half results weak.

05

Industry analysis

Korea's cloud and AI infrastructure market is expanding rapidly on the back of large-scale government GPU procurement and build-out policies.

Industry reporting notes that following roughly KRW 1.4 trillion in GPU procurement spending last year, the government is pursuing a GPU build-out project worth about KRW 2.08 trillion this year, and that CSP strategies are being shaped by the government's GPU procurement, build-out, and operations support project, a core pillar of its 'AI highway' initiative.

Participants in this program include Naver Cloud, NHN Cloud, and Kakao selected last year, with Naver Cloud, KT Cloud, Samsung SDS, Coupang, and Elice Group participating this year, with large CSPs leading the market as project sponsors.

Icraft participates as a vendor supplying core infrastructure such as networking and storage as these CSPs expand their GPU clusters and data centers, and its status as an NVIDIA Elite Partner is cited as a differentiator, noting that the company had strategically cultivated NVIDIA-based AI infrastructure business well before the AI market's rapid growth, and has secured a portfolio spanning DGX/HGX, AI networking, AI storage, and AI platforms across the full AI data center stack.

Nonetheless, large system integrators and cloud operators such as Samsung SDS and KT Cloud also operate in this space, creating competition, and rapid GPU architecture generational shifts (such as NVIDIA's Vera Rubin) mean technical responsiveness and execution track record remain continuously important.

Cyclically, the industry is in a growth phase where government/public procurement overlaps with private CSP investment expansion, though the extent of benefit to any single vendor can vary widely depending on budget allocation and individual contract award outcomes.

06

Outlook

Regarding the second half of 2026, the confirmed facts center on three large supply contracts signed since June.

First, Icraft disclosed on June 18 a contract with Naver Cloud for 2026 NIPA NVIDIA Infiniband network switches, running from June 16, 2026 to March 31, 2027, valued at approximately KRW 51.82bn, or 25.09% of recent revenue.

It then signed a '2026 NIPA high-performance AI storage supply contract' with Naver Cloud valued at KRW 104.0bn (excluding VAT), equivalent to 50.36% of consolidated revenue at the end of 2025, running from July 3, 2026 to March 31, 2027, with payment structured as 50% upfront, 31.31% as a progress payment, and 18.69% as final settlement.

Separately, the company signed a KRW 32.28bn 2026 NIPA NVIDIA Vera Rubin network supply contract with BNI Inc., equivalent to 15.63% of recent revenue, running from July 23, 2026 to March 31, 2027.

As all three contracts extend through the end of March 2027, revenue recognition could be spread across the second half of 2026 and into the first quarter of 2027.

In addition, Icraft was selected for the National IT Industry Promotion Agency's (NIPA) '2026 AI Computing Resource Infrastructure Strengthening Project' (GPU procurement, build-out, and operations support), through which it will build key AI network and storage infrastructure through 2027 to support stable operation and efficient resource utilization of public AI data centers.

Based on the disclosed contract values, this order pipeline represents a meaningful share of recent revenue and could support future results, though as prior quarterly patterns show, the actual timing and pace of revenue recognition may vary depending on project execution.

08

Bull factors

Sizable AI Infrastructure Order Pipeline

In June-July 2026 alone, Icraft signed an Infiniband switch contract (KRW 51.8bn) and an AI storage contract (KRW 104.0bn) with Naver Cloud, plus a separate Vera Rubin network contract (KRW 32.3bn) with BNI Inc.

All three are long-duration contracts extending to March 2027, and the disclosed values alone represent a substantial share of recent revenue. This is further supplemented by the NIPA 2026 AI computing resource infrastructure project win, broadening the order base across both public and private sectors.

2025 Earnings Turnaround

Consolidated 2025 revenue rose 105.5% year-on-year and operating profit jumped 401.6%, with net income turning positive. This reflects rising data center system demand tied to expanding AI infrastructure investment and accelerating digital transformation.

Relative to the weaker profitability of 2022-2024, the results suggest that scale effects are beginning to emerge as the business reorients toward AI data centers.

NVIDIA Elite Partner Status

Icraft has cultivated NVIDIA-based AI infrastructure business since before the AI market's rapid growth phase, and has built a portfolio spanning DGX/HGX servers, AI networking, AI storage, and AI platforms.

Its ability to provide integrated GPU, network, and storage offerings enhances its prospects of being selected as a vendor for large CSPs' data center expansion projects.

09

Bear factors

Intensifying Quarter-to-Quarter Earnings Volatility

Fourth-quarter 2025 revenue of KRW 109.1bn accounted for more than half of annual revenue, while first-quarter 2026 revenue was KRW 16.3bn and second-quarter revenue was KRW 40.0bn, showing large swings.

Given that telecom carrier and public-sector orders tend to concentrate in the second half, first-half results may be structurally weak. This volatility makes it difficult to judge the annual trajectory from any single quarter's results.

Order-Based Revenue Concentrated Among Few Clients

The 2026 large contracts are heavily concentrated with a single client, Naver Cloud, supplemented by a small number of other counterparties such as BNI Inc. and NIPA. Any change in budget or schedule at a specific CSP or government project could have an outsized impact on revenue.

Given the order-driven nature of the business, there is an ongoing risk of an earnings gap if new contracts fail to materialize.

Limited Earnings Stability

In 2022, operating profit was only KRW 0.25bn on revenue of KRW 112.9bn, essentially breakeven, and in 2024 the company posted an operating profit yet still recorded a net loss.

The debt ratio has also fluctuated year to year, falling from 168.4% in 2022 to 99.0% in 2023 and 80.4% in 2024 before rising again to 104.8% in 2025, suggesting that investment and working-capital burdens are accompanying the growth phase.

10

Risk factors

Client and Project Sponsor Concentration Risk

Large contracts are concentrated among a small number of counterparties, including Naver Cloud, BNI Inc., and NIPA, so budget adjustments or schedule changes at any one sponsor could directly affect results.

For public-sector projects, the scale and timing of implementation can shift with budget allocation or policy changes.

Seasonality and Revenue Recognition Timing Risk

Structural seasonality, with telecom carrier and public-sector orders concentrated in the second half, raises the possibility of recurring first-half weakness.

If revenue recognition for large contracts is concentrated in particular quarters based on project progress or delivery timing, the predictability of quarterly results is reduced.

Technology Change and Competitive Intensity Risk

As NVIDIA GPU architectures undergo rapid generational shifts, the company must continually keep pace with the latest technology, and failure to do so could weaken its competitiveness.

Large system integrators and cloud operators such as Samsung SDS and KT Cloud also participate in the AI infrastructure market, raising the potential for intensified competition in vendor selection.

12

Overall view

Icraft is in the process of shifting its center of gravity from traditional network integration toward AI data center and cloud infrastructure business, and this transition showed clear results in 2025.

Revenue more than doubled year-on-year, and both operating profit and net income turned positive, though the first half of 2026 saw a return to losses due to seasonal factors, reaffirming the company's characteristic quarter-to-quarter volatility.

The large contracts signed with Naver Cloud and BNI Inc. in June-July 2026, along with the NIPA public-sector project win, are confirmed facts that could underpin future revenue recognition, though the actual timing and pace of that recognition will depend on project execution.

The company's capital structure and cash flow have also shown considerable year-to-year variability, warranting attention to both growth and stability.

For observers, tracking the execution of the secured order pipeline and the emergence of follow-on contracts appears more informative than focusing on any single quarter's swings.
