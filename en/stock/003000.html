02

Business structure

Bukwang Pharmaceutical IND makes and sells prescription drugs, health functional foods and quasi-drugs, with products including Dexid, Feroba, Regalon, Chiotasid, Latuda and Sinzyroid.

Prescription drugs remain the center of the business, and within it the CNS business unit has led growth, rising 36% in the first quarter of 2026. Latuda (lurasidone), launched in Korea in 2024, passed KRW 20 billion in cumulative sales by July 2026.

R&D runs mainly through its Danish subsidiary Contera Pharma, which is advancing CP-012 for morning akinesia in Parkinson's disease into Phase 2. At an R&D event on November 24, 2026, the company disclosed a plan to spin off the RNA unit into a new entity, Three Point Therapeutics (TPT).

Manufacturing capacity is being broadened through a newly acquired affiliate in which the company holds a 75.14% stake, and the company plans to move some over-the-counter production there so its Ansan plant can focus on prescription drugs.

On the sales side, a co-promotion deal with Servier Korea covers five products aimed at chronic-disease prescriptions.

The company competes with other listed Korean drugmakers in domestic prescription markets, and its model pairs cash-generating domestic brands with an RNA and CNS pipeline that could be licensed abroad.

04

Earnings analysis

On an annual basis, revenue fell from KRW 190.9 billion in 2022 to KRW 125.9 billion in 2023, a 34.0% decline, then rose 27.1% to KRW 160.1 billion in 2024 and 25.4% to KRW 200.7 billion in 2025. Revenue grew in both 2024 and 2025, and 2025 was the largest year in the 2022 to 2025 period.

Operating losses widened from KRW 231 million in 2022 to KRW 37.5 billion in 2023. Analysts attributed the weak 2022 and 2023 results to higher R&D investment combined with a management transition.

The company returned to operating profit of KRW 1.62 billion in 2024 and reported KRW 14.2 billion in 2025, up 775.6%, lifting the operating margin to 7.1%.

Net loss attributable to controlling shareholders narrowed from KRW 31.3 billion in 2023 to KRW 2.64 billion in 2024, and turned into a KRW 12.6 billion profit in 2025. Net profit including non-controlling interests was KRW 12.4 billion in 2025, a different figure from the controlling-shareholder number.

Debt-to-equity fell to 39.7% at the end of 2025 from 83.1% in 2023, and operating cash flow was KRW 21.5 billion in 2025. Quarterly results were more volatile.

Operating profit reached KRW 8.16 billion in the fourth quarter of 2025, the largest of the last five quarters, before dropping to KRW 1.1 billion in the first quarter of 2026.

The company attributed the first-quarter decline to higher outsourced production to address shortages and the cost of the Phase 2 trial for CP-012.

Operating profit recovered to KRW 1.41 billion in the second quarter and KRW 1.73 billion in the third quarter of 2026, with the second quarter weighed by currency-driven input costs, outsourced processing tied to an automation line, and early marketing for new launches.

Third-quarter net profit attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 290 million, down 81.1% year on year, and the trailing four-quarter total was KRW 7.54 billion. Cumulative R&D expense through the third quarter of 2026 rose 29.5% to KRW 16.9 billion according to preliminary results released in October 2026.

05

Industry analysis

Korea's listed biopharma sector splits into two parts. One is prescription drugs, where sales drive earnings, and the other is drug development, where value depends on clinical data and licensing outcomes. Bukwang Pharmaceutical IND combines both in a mid-sized structure.

In domestic prescriptions, the company said its total portfolio grew about 8.7% year on year in the first quarter of 2026, with CNS products up 36%. The company cited a CNS market growth rate of 9%, and its strategic CNS products grew faster than that in the first nine months of 2026.

In new drug development, RNA platforms are the competitive focus. Sangsangin Securities highlighted Contera Pharma's platform, which covers target discovery through candidate design.

Large global drugmakers and biotech firms also compete in RNA therapeutics, so a mid-sized Korean company must prove differentiation with clinical data. Supply capacity is another competitive factor, and the company has faced repeated shortages that it is trying to resolve through capacity expansion.

Domestic CNS prescriptions are in a growth phase, while the pipeline sits at the early clinical stage, so valuation of the pipeline may shift considerably around trial readouts.

06

Outlook

For the fourth quarter of 2026, the company plans to expand new prescriptions for cardiovascular and respiratory drugs and push new products into general hospitals. Its manufacturing affiliate is being renamed and will widen its antibiotic and injectable lineup.

Latuda's depression indication entered a Phase 3 trial across 20 Korean sites with 364 patients, with first dosing planned for January 2027, results expected in 2028, and a target of more than KRW 35 billion in single-product sales in 2029.

For CP-012, the company's April 2026 timeline called for first patient dosing after IND approval in the second half, with top-line results expected in early 2028. The company aims to secure results from two key clinical trials in 2028 to pursue global licensing and domestic sales growth.

Its medium-term goal is to rank among the top 20 Korean pharmaceutical companies by sales by 2030. External funding for TPT is planned only after the unit shows stronger results, with the parent continuing financial support until then.

The annual operating margin and controlling-shareholder profit in the 2026 annual report will show how R&D spending and production normalization are balancing.

08

Bull factors

CNS prescriptions and Latuda indication expansion

Latuda passed KRW 20 billion in cumulative sales by July 2026, after its 2024 launch in Korea. The company said CNS strategic products' prescriptions rose 20% in the first nine months of 2026. If the depression indication succeeds, the single-product revenue base could widen. These figures are company-reported, and quarterly prescription growth can shift with wholesaler inventory.

RNA platform and subsidiary technology assets

Contera Pharma has been described as holding an RNA platform that spans target discovery to candidate design. CP-012 is moving toward Phase 2 with top-line results targeted for early 2028. The company has positioned the RNA unit as an independent entity that could open an outside funding route. A successful licensing deal could add income outside the core business.

Capacity expansion and shortage response

The company holds a 75.14% stake in the acquired affiliate and plans to move some over-the-counter production there so that its Ansan plant focuses on prescription drugs. The plan also includes consolidating injectable lines and expanding tablet processing.

The company expects volatility in operating profit to settle once manufacturing capacity is secured. This is the company's view, and execution will show up in the coming periodic reports.

09

Bear factors

Quarterly operating profit remains small

Operating profit fell 86.5% quarter on quarter to KRW 1.1 billion in the first quarter of 2026, which the company linked to higher outsourced production to address shortages.

It recovered to KRW 1.41 billion in the second quarter and KRW 1.73 billion in the third, but quarterly operating profit has stayed in the low single-digit billions of won. Third-quarter revenue of KRW 56.1 billion fell 1.6% from the prior quarter. Outsourcing costs and early launch spending take up a large share of the small operating profit base.

Lower net profit attributable to controlling shareholders

Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 290 million in the third quarter of 2026, down 79.3% from the prior quarter and 81.1% from a year earlier. Operating profit rose while net profit fell, which points to a wide gap between the operating and net lines.

The trailing four-quarter total is KRW 7.54 billion, compared with KRW 12.6 billion for the full year 2025, so the earnings base has thinned.

Rising R&D spending and a short profit track record

Cumulative R&D expense through the third quarter of 2026 rose 29.5% year on year, faster than revenue growth. Operating losses ran through 2022 and 2023, and operating profit has been positive only since 2024.

Because the profit record is short, it is still being tested whether the core business can absorb a larger R&D budget. The CEO said the core business has the capacity to fund the investment.

10

Risk factors

Clinical and technology development

CP-012 is in Phase 2, with top-line results expected in early 2028, and the Latuda depression Phase 3 trial also targets 2028 results. If either trial falls short, the payback on development spending would be delayed and licensing talks could be affected. Until the results are known, the value of the pipeline remains uncertain.

Acquisition and manufacturing integration

The acquisition was done through a court-supervised restructuring M&A process, and the company expected the proceedings to conclude in July 2026. Transferring production, consolidating injectable lines and expanding tablet processing must happen together, so slower integration could delay the end of shortages. Whether the stated normalization timeline turns into real production efficiency is still to be confirmed.

Funding and governance

External funding for the RNA unit TPT has been deferred until it shows stronger results, and the parent has committed to financial support until then. As subsidiary R&D grows, the parent's funding burden grows with it. If outside funding is delayed, the R&D cost stays in consolidated profit.

Dividends were about 98% of 2025 net profit, which leaves limited room to fund R&D and payouts at the same time.

Overall view

Bukwang Pharmaceutical IND returned to profit in 2025, with operating profit of KRW 14.2 billion and net profit attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 12.6 billion. Through the third quarter of 2026, it has pursued prescription growth and manufacturing capacity together.

Latuda's cumulative sales passing KRW 20 billion and rising CNS prescriptions are the main strengths of the core business.

On the other side, net profit attributable to controlling shareholders fell to KRW 290 million in the third quarter of 2026, and the trailing four-quarter total of KRW 7.54 billion leaves a thin earnings base. Cumulative R&D expense rose 29.5% through the third quarter, which adds to quarterly swings.

Clinical and licensing outcomes are expected around 2028, so the value of the pipeline is not yet settled. The next checkpoints are the start of Latuda trial dosing in January 2027, the preliminary fourth-quarter 2026 results, and the operating margin and controlling-shareholder profit in the 2026 annual report.
