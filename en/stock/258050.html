02

Business structure

Tech Trans, founded in 2011, is a specialist in surface treatment of non-ferrous metals. It applies its TAC (Tech Arc Coating) and C-TAC processes to lightweight alloy parts such as aluminum, magnesium and titanium to add corrosion resistance, hardness and color.

The business is split into surface-treated products, electrolyte solutions and equipment, with Hwashin and Ceragem named as key customers. In 2024, revenue was KRW 698.32 million, of which surface-treatment equipment made up 65.9% and dedicated electrolyte solutions 13.2%.

The heavy equipment weighting means a handful of orders can swing annual revenue. A KRW 460 million supply contract with Hwashin in 2024 was equal to 131% of the prior year's revenue.

The company has run R&D projects on electric-vehicle chassis parts, aluminum pedals and magnesium eyewear frames, all tied to lightweight materials.

The exchange classifies it as an other fabricated metal products manufacturer, and its business and scale differ greatly from large machinery makers grouped in the same KONEX sector bucket, such as Doosan Enerbility.

With small revenue and few customers, results are sensitive to order timing and to customers' own equipment-investment plans.

03

Recent trends

For fiscal 2025 (the 15th term), standalone revenue was KRW 299 million, with an operating loss of KRW 817 million and a net loss of KRW 815 million. In 2024, revenue was KRW 698.32 million, with an operating loss of KRW 608.01 million and a net loss of KRW 617.29 million.

That means 2025 revenue fell 57.2% and the operating loss widened 34.4%, which the data provider attributes to fewer surface-treated product orders and weak TAC equipment sales. At the end of 2025, standalone total assets were KRW 157 million, total liabilities KRW 300 million and total equity negative KRW 142 million.

On March 23, 2026 the company disclosed a trading halt tied to the auditor's disclaimer of opinion. A later filing extended the halt until the end of the delisting objection period or the exchange's decision on delisting.

At the March 31, 2026 annual meeting, the financial statements were approved, with 88.5% of the shares voted in favor. The last close before the halt was KRW 449, and with no price movement or turnover since, the market has effectively stopped giving a price signal. One data provider shows a 52-week range of KRW 183 to KRW 1,098.

04

Outlook

The starting point for the outlook is whether the listing is maintained. The exchange's decision after any objection, or whether an improvement period was granted, could not be confirmed in this research.

In cases where the halt continues without the disclaimer being resolved, filing an objection does not itself mean the listing is kept. The rights offering approved on October 14, 2026 has a payment date of October 27, 2026, and the new shares are scheduled to list on November 17, 2026.

The stated purposes are securing working capital and improving the financial position by offsetting borrowings. The preferred-share conversion will create 2,610,922 common shares at a stated conversion price of KRW 766.

However, measures such as capital reductions or rights offerings cannot be assumed to restore the value of existing shareholders' stakes. On the business side, an order recovery is the key issue, but no confirmed data on 2026 orders or new contracts were found.

The next items to check are exchange filings, the new-share listing schedule and the submission of 2026 periodic reports.

05

Bull factors

Funding and debt offset

The company decided on a third-party rights offering to raise KRW 913.84 million. Of that, KRW 313.84 million is classified as other funds, used to offset borrowings against the share subscription.

Against year-end 2025 liabilities of KRW 300 million and equity of negative KRW 142 million, the offering is large enough to change the reported balance sheet. It can be read as a response to the going-concern uncertainty that led to the disclaimer.

Preferred shares converted

A conversion request on 1.79 million convertible redeemable preferred shares leads to the issuance of 2,610,922 common shares. The remaining preferred shares fall to 600,450 after the conversion. A simpler preferred structure makes the capital composition easier to interpret. This does not by itself add capital, and the accounting effect needs to be confirmed in later filings.

Differentiated coating technology

The TAC and C-TAC processes give lightweight alloy parts corrosion resistance, hardness and color. The company has completed R&D projects on lightweight parts such as electric-vehicle chassis components and aluminum pedals. If vehicle lightweighting continues, demand for such processes may persist. How much of that converts into orders is a separate business metric that has to be confirmed.

06

Bear factors

Sharp revenue drop, wider losses

Revenue in 2025 fell 57.2% and the operating loss widened 34.4%. With fewer orders and weak equipment sales, fixed-cost burden and higher costs hurt profitability. Revenue of KRW 299 million against an operating loss of KRW 817 million means operations alone cannot fund the business. Reliance on outside funding may continue.

Capital impairment and disclaimer

Total equity at the end of 2025 was negative KRW 142 million. The auditor issued a disclaimer citing uncertainty over the company's ability to continue as a going concern. This triggered a delisting-cause event. Whether the capital raise is accepted as resolving the cause is up to the exchange.

Dilution from new shares

Common shares before the conversion stood at 11,145,949. Adding 2,610,922 conversion shares and 2,035,279 rights-offering shares expands the common share count by roughly 41.7% (our calculation). The offering price is KRW 449, set by reference to the closing price on the day of the halt. Dilution weighs on per-share value for existing holders.

07

Risk factors

Listing

The halt continues until the objection period expires or the delisting decision is made. If an objection is not accepted, the process can move to final trading and delisting. Expecting a resumption can leave funds tied up if the halt is prolonged. The timing and outcome of the exchange's decision have not been confirmed.

Business

Revenue depends on a few customers and individual equipment orders. A single 2024 Hwashin contract equal to 131% of prior-year revenue illustrates this. Delayed or canceled orders can swing annual revenue sharply. The investment cycle of auto-parts makers and shifts in electric-vehicle demand also matter.

Financial and liquidity

Total assets at the end of 2025 were only KRW 157 million. Completion of the payment and the use of proceeds must be confirmed in follow-up filings. One allottee, Lee Jeong-sun, has a reported money-lending transaction with the company. If operating losses persist after the offering, the need for further funding could return.

08

Overall view

Tech Trans holds non-ferrous surface-treatment technology, but its scale and profitability both weakened in 2025, with standalone revenue of KRW 299 million and an operating loss of KRW 817 million. Equity turned negative, the auditor issued a disclaimer of opinion, and trading has been halted since March 23, 2026.

Filings in October 2026 show a KRW 913.84 million third-party rights offering and a preferred-share conversion, which are visible attempts to repair the balance sheet. Against that, dilution from new shares, uncertainty over an order recovery and a pending exchange decision all remain.

The KRW 449 price is the last close before the halt and turnover is zero, so it is hard to treat it as a market-formed price. This report is for information only, and any improvement should be checked against the exchange's subsequent decisions and periodic reports.
