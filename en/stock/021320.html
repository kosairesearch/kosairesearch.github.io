02

Business structure

KCC Engineering & Construction operates two segments, building construction and infrastructure. Building construction revenue in H1 2026 was KRW 529.1 billion, down 30.1% from the prior-year period, while infrastructure revenue rose 11.8% to KRW 200.4 billion.

Based on H1 2026 revenue of KRW 735.8 billion, building construction accounts for about 72% and infrastructure for about 27%. Within building construction, the company is widening its work beyond housing redevelopment into logistics centers, offices and mixed-use facilities.

Housing redevelopment orders in H1 2026 totaled KRW 1.01 trillion, including the Busan Munhyeon 6 district (KRW 556.6 billion), the Daejeon Sansung-dong 1 district (KRW 317.9 billion) and the Seoul Gwansu 8 district (KRW 139.0 billion).

Clients range from redevelopment associations to private real estate companies and universities. In Seongsu-dong, Seoul, the company has booked about KRW 500 billion across six office projects. Its housing strategy centers on redevelopment and trust-based projects rather than direct sales.

Differentiation comes from specialized facilities and hard-to-build projects. Logistics centers are cited as a segment where construction payments actually flow in. Payments received from major sites in H1 2026 were KRW 98.2 billion, equal to 13.3% of H1 revenue.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual revenue stayed in a narrow band around KRW 1.8 trillion. Revenue is recognized as construction progresses, so when a large project is completed its revenue also drops away.

Revenue was KRW 1.89 trillion in 2022 and KRW 1.91 trillion in 2023, up 0.9%, making 2023 the largest year in the period. 2024 was the smallest year at KRW 1.83 trillion, down 4.3%. Revenue rose 0.3% to KRW 1.83 trillion in 2025. Profitability improved more clearly.

Operating profit moved from an operating loss of KRW 1.09 billion in 2022 to an operating profit of KRW 18.1 billion in 2023, a swing to profit. It then reached KRW 64.6 billion in 2024, up 256.0%, and KRW 88.3 billion in 2025, up 36.8%, marking two consecutive years of increases from 2024 to 2025.

The operating margin rose from -0.1% in 2022 to 4.8% in 2025. Net income attributable to controlling shareholders rose from KRW 4.44 billion in 2022 to KRW 45.4 billion in 2025, with increases in each year from 2023 to 2025, and 2025 was up 184.4%.

Operating cash flow moved from an outflow of KRW 207.5 billion in 2024 to an inflow of KRW 112.9 billion in 2025. Quarterly revenue bottomed at KRW 355.9 billion in Q1 2026 and then rose for two consecutive quarters to KRW 379.9 billion and KRW 407.2 billion.

Operating profit was KRW 20.4 billion in Q1, KRW 16.3 billion in Q2 and KRW 53.3 billion in Q3 2026. Q3 net income attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 11.9 billion, down 18.7% year on year, so the operating profit gain did not carry through to bottom-line earnings.

The composition of that gap is a point to check in the quarterly report notes. The company attributed the H1 2026 building construction decline to the completion of a large office project, calling it temporary.

05

Industry analysis

The housing market downturn has lasted, and housing starts are expected to remain weak. Nationwide apartment permits fell 16.5% from 415,263 units in 2022 to 346,773 units in 2025. Permits feed into construction starts with a lag of up to three years, so further declines in housing starts are expected.

Building materials industry officials also say a construction slowdown makes a near-term market recovery hard to expect. Raw material prices and freight costs have added to the pressure.

In this environment, KCC Engineering & Construction is shifting orders toward redevelopment and non-residential segments such as offices and logistics. Of the 2026 order target of about KRW 2.76 trillion, building construction accounts for about KRW 2.11 trillion.

In office demand, the Ttukseom area near Seongsu recorded a vacancy rate of 1.3%, below the Seoul average of 9.7%. Against larger builders such as Hyundai Engineering & Construction and DL E&C, the company has built a redevelopment order base of over KRW 1 trillion in H1 2026.

Redevelopment projects take time to reach construction, so order growth and revenue recognition are separated by a lag.

06

Outlook

As of an October 2026 report, the backlog was about KRW 5.5 trillion, up more than 25% from year-end 2025, and the building construction backlog rose 44.3%. The company said all 2026 orders except redevelopment projects have started construction and are being recognized as revenue as progress advances.

The Hongik University campus project, KRW 448.9 billion, runs from December 15, 2026 to August 14, 2031, for 56 months. The Cordia complex project, KRW 278.1 billion, runs from October 7, 2026 to September 30, 2030. The Yeoju Yanggwiri logistics center, KRW 305.71 billion, runs through November 19, 2028.

About KRW 298.7 billion of the roughly KRW 372.7 billion in work across four Seongsu office sites is expected to be recognized over two to three years. Newly disclosed 2026 orders totaled about KRW 3.02 trillion, above the target.

The company said it will maintain selective order intake focused on projects that offer profitability and stability in Q4. The contract amounts and periods may change under the construction contract terms. The next checkpoint is how quickly the roughly KRW 5.5 trillion backlog converts into quarterly revenue.

08

Bull factors

Order growth builds the revenue pipeline

New contracts disclosed in 2026 totaled about KRW 3.02 trillion, roughly 9% above the company's target. The backlog reportedly reached about KRW 5.5 trillion by October 2026, up more than 25% from year-end 2025. Non-redevelopment sites are under construction and feed revenue as progress advances.

Lower borrowings and a stronger balance sheet

Total borrowings were KRW 366.7 billion at the end of H1 2026, down 25.34% from KRW 491.2 billion at year-end 2025. The debt ratio fell to 153% in H1 2026. Operating cash flow moved from an outflow of KRW 207.5 billion in 2024 to an inflow of KRW 112.9 billion in 2025.

A rebound in Q3 operating profit

Q3 2026 operating profit rose 73.6% year on year to KRW 53.3 billion. Compared with KRW 20.4 billion in Q1 and KRW 16.3 billion in Q2 2026, the rebound is clear. Revenue also rose for two consecutive quarters after bottoming in Q1 2026.

09

Bear factors

H1 revenue decline and slower building construction

H1 2026 revenue fell 21.4% to about KRW 735.8 billion and operating profit fell 20.2% to KRW 36.7 billion. Building construction revenue fell 30.1%. Annual revenue growth in 2025 was only 0.3%.

Lag in converting orders and lower net income

Q3 2026 revenue fell 9.5% year on year and attributable net income of KRW 11.9 billion fell 18.7%. Revenue follows construction progress, and new sites take time to fill the gap left by completed projects. Revenue recovery may be limited until that gap closes.

Declining housing starts

Nationwide apartment permits feed into starts with a lag of up to three years, so declining starts may continue. The company has shifted toward redevelopment and trust projects, but exposure to the housing market remains.

10

Risk factors

Contract changes and construction delays

Contract amounts and construction periods may change under the contract terms. Several large projects, including the Hongik University campus and the Yeoju logistics center, run beyond 2028, so changes or delays could widen the lag before results are recognized.

Durability of cash flow

Operating cash flow was an outflow in 2022, 2023 and 2024, then turned to an inflow of KRW 112.9 billion in 2025. Whether this turn holds must be confirmed in cash flows from 2026 onward. Construction payments received in H1 2026 were KRW 98.2 billion, or 13.3% of revenue.

Building materials market and input costs

Weaker housing transactions and higher raw material and freight costs linked to the prolonged Middle East conflict are weighing on the building materials industry. A longer slump could add pressure on cost structure and competition for non-residential orders.

12

Overall view

KCC Engineering & Construction has broadened its growth base through higher order intake, a larger backlog and lower borrowings. Operating profit reached KRW 88.3 billion in 2025, extending a two-year rise, and operating cash flow turned to an inflow.

On the other hand, H1 2026 revenue fell 21.4%, with building construction revenue declining sharply. Q3 2026 operating profit jumped to KRW 53.3 billion, yet attributable net income fell 18.7% year on year in the same quarter.

Industry weakness in housing permits and the lag in converting orders to revenue are intertwined. Valuation is best read against book equity and earnings, where the market capitalization sits well below attributable equity.

The next checkpoints are full-year 2026 results in the annual report and the revenue trend in the Q1 2027 quarterly report.
