Drama Pivot Amid Persistent Losses

The Contents On is shifting its weight from film rights distribution toward drama production, but both its operating loss and net loss widened in 2025, so the pivot has yet to show up clearly in the financial statements.

02

Business structure

The Contents On was founded in December 2012 as a film and drama producer and distributor, and it listed on KONEX in August 2018. IBK Investment & Securities served as the listing underwriter.

The business rests on three pillars: non-theatrical rights distribution (IPTV and OTT), film investment and production, and drama production. A Seoul Credit Rating & Information report says the company distributes about 200 titles a year and has built a cumulative library of more than 2,600 titles.

Segment revenue swings widely from year to year. Of KRW 11.71 billion in 2024 revenue, content income was KRW 5.55 billion, theatrical receipts were KRW 1.63 billion and drama production service income was KRW 4.29 billion.

In the first half of 2025, drama production services accounted for KRW 4.26 billion of KRW 5.87 billion in revenue, with content income at KRW 1.37 billion and theatrical receipts at KRW 0.18 billion. Drama production services have therefore moved to the center of reported sales.

The drama business began with the creation of a drama division in 2020. Its first title, 'Twelve', was released on August 23, 2025 through KBS 2TV and Disney+.

The production pipeline leans on co-production. The company said it chose co-production with experienced producers as a way through a subdued drama market. The company said one co-produced series is targeted for broadcast in 2027.

That target dates from a 2024 announcement, and whether the schedule has since changed could not be confirmed. In competitive terms, the company is far smaller than large producers such as Studio Dragon and CJ ENM. The national pension data show 11 employees. A corporate data service lists its market share at 3.30% in TV VOD and 0.30% in theatrical distribution.

03

Recent trends

On a reported basis, the last four fiscal years combine volatile revenue with accumulating losses. Revenue moved from KRW 21.47 billion in 2022 to KRW 8.62 billion in 2023, KRW 11.71 billion in 2024 and KRW 8.90 billion in 2025.

Operating profit was KRW 0.83 billion in 2022, followed by operating losses of KRW 0.62 billion in 2023, KRW 0.38 billion in 2024 and KRW 1.06 billion in 2025. Net losses were KRW 3.22 billion in 2022, KRW 1.81 billion in 2023, KRW 3.43 billion in 2024 and KRW 6.58 billion in 2025.

In several years the net loss was far larger than the operating loss. In 2022 a film investment loss of KRW 4.22 billion was booked, and it fell to KRW 0.13 billion in 2023. The non-operating breakdown behind the 2025 net loss could not be confirmed through search. First-half 2026 figures were also not confirmed, so they are discussed only qualitatively.

The share price has been in a prolonged weak phase. As of December 16, 2025 the price was KRW 3,200, with a 52-week range of KRW 2,910 to KRW 7,000. The one-year change was shown as -50% at that time.

On October 27, 2026 the price stood at KRW 1,280 (-1.54% day on day), the market cap was KRW 5.8 billion, and turnover for the day was recorded at KRW 12,380.

A market-warning episode was added in September 2026. On September 10, 2026 the exchange gave advance notice of a trading halt for the company, which was already an investment-risk issue. The stock was then halted after a further price surge, and it remains managed as an investment-risk issue after trading resumes.

The company held an investor relations session on September 4, 2026. A data-service summary shows book value per share at -251, so the capital position should be checked separately in the periodic reports.

04

Outlook

Future results will depend heavily on when drama production service revenue is recognized. Such revenue tends to be booked in a lump when a title is delivered, so year-to-year swings in sales are almost unavoidable. The company has stated a goal of producing two dramas every year.

Whether that goal has translated into actual broadcast schedules needs to be checked in filings after the first half of 2026.

On the industry side, changes in viewing habits are reshaping the rights distribution business. The Seoul Credit Rating & Information report notes that the market baseline has shifted from box-office results to platform viewing and subscription metrics.

This trend widens distribution channels for a company with an OTT and IPTV rights library, but it also increases the bargaining power of the platforms.

Financial items to watch are whether the operating loss narrows, whether non-operating losses recur, and how total equity changes. Room for improvement in business metrics is limited to production service margins and the stability of library distribution income.

Whether trading normalizes after the market warnings, and any changes in major shareholder or institutional holdings, are also worth tracking. The next periodic report will be the first document to clarify these variables.

05

Bull factors

Large rights library

A Seoul Credit Rating & Information report says the company distributes about 200 titles a year and holds a cumulative library of more than 2,600 titles. The library underpins ancillary revenue that flows through IPTV and OTT after theatrical release.

It can act as a partial cushion in years when the production business is weak, which is the main point supporters raise.

Expansion into drama production

Drama production service revenue is shown at KRW 4.29 billion in 2024 and KRW 4.26 billion in the first half of 2025. Unlike the earlier structure that relied on film production services, repeatable production revenue has begun to appear on the drama side. As its vision, the company has set out a content studio spanning film and drama production and distribution.

Co-production spreads the funding load

The company explained that it chose co-production with producers that hold quality content. Co-production shares funding and box-office exposure compared with solo production. It can be read as a practical route for a small firm that cannot compete with large producers on scale.

06

Bear factors

Accumulating and widening losses

Operating losses persisted for three straight years from 2023 to 2025, and the 2025 loss of KRW 1.06 billion was the largest. Net losses ran for four straight years from 2022 to 2025 and grew to KRW 6.58 billion in 2025. The reported results do not yet show that the drama shift has translated into profit.

Project-dependent revenue swings

Revenue plunged from KRW 21.47 billion in 2022 to KRW 8.62 billion in 2023. It fell again in 2025, by 24.0% from the prior year. Because the timing of a handful of titles drives annual results, predictability is low.

Micro-cap size and thin liquidity

The market cap is currently KRW 5.8 billion and turnover for the day was KRW 12,380. The one-year change as of December 2025 was -50%. With trading this sparse, small orders can move the price sharply, and executing at a chosen time may be difficult.

07

Risk factors

Macro and industry

The drama market is sensitive to production-cost pressure and changes in platform scheduling. The company itself described the drama market as somewhat subdued. As viewing shifts from theaters to subscription platforms, rising platform bargaining power could worsen revenue terms for small producers.

Business

Co-produced titles depend on the schedules of partner producers and platforms. Some titles are targeted for 2027 broadcast, so the lag before revenue recognition is long. A scheduling delay or cancelled production could shake revenue and cash flow in the affected year.

Financial and market

In 2022 a film investment loss of KRW 4.22 billion was booked. If investment losses recur, the net loss can widen regardless of operating performance.

On the market side, there is a history of investment-risk designation and a trading halt, and book value per share is shown as negative in a data summary, so the capital position needs to be verified.

08

Overall view

The Contents On is a small KONEX-listed company that began in film distribution on the strength of a library of about 2,600 titles and is expanding into drama production. In 2024 and the first half of 2025, drama production services made up a large share of revenue, which confirms the change in business structure.

Revenue for 2025, however, fell 24.0% from the prior year, and the losses widened to an operating loss of KRW 1.06 billion and a net loss of KRW 6.58 billion. Sales depend on when a few titles are recognized, and non-operating items such as film investment losses have moved net results before.

In September 2026 an investment-risk designation and a trading halt were announced, and with a market cap of KRW 5.8 billion and very low turnover, liquidity constraints are significant.

The bullish side points to the library and the co-production model, while the bearish side points to accumulated losses and financial and liquidity burdens. The 2026 periodic reports and broadcast-schedule disclosures are the key materials for weighing the two views.
