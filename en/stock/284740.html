02

Business structure

Cuckoo Homesys is a KOSPI-listed rental company whose largest shareholder is Cuckoo Holdings, holding 40.55% as of March 31, 2026. Chief Executive Guk Bon-hak holds 20.53% of the company.

Rental of home appliances such as water purifiers, air purifiers, rice cookers and food waste disposers, billed per account, forms one pillar of the business, and appliance distribution forms the other.

A 2023 media report said that about 43% of 2022 consolidated revenue came from rental and about 57% from appliance distribution. The product range is broad, covering electric rice cookers, induction cooktops, food waste disposers, ice water purifiers and vacuum cleaners.

In the first quarter of 2026, sales growth of food waste disposers, ultra-compact ice water purifiers, AI induction cooktops and vacuum cleaners lifted domestic results. Domestic revenue in that quarter was KRW 179.2 billion, up 9.1% year on year, and cumulative domestic rental accounts rose 8.3% to 3.28 million.

Overseas revenue was KRW 93.7 billion, down 12.3%. Overseas, the Malaysian subsidiary CUCKOO International (Malaysia) carries the largest weight, followed by the U.S. rental unit and the Indian unit. Malaysian subsidiary revenue in the first half of 2026 was KRW 175.3 billion, down 10.3%.

Manufacturing is handled by CUCKOO Electronics, a subsidiary of Cuckoo Holdings, so Cuckoo Homesys weighs more heavily on rental operations and service organization. Coway is the main comparison company in the rental market.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual results grew in both sales and profit from 2022 to 2024, and the pace of growth narrowed in 2025.

Revenue rose from KRW 938.1 billion in 2022 to KRW 954.6 billion in 2023 (up 1.8% year on year), KRW 1.06 trillion in 2024 (up 10.8%) and KRW 1.12 trillion in 2025 (up 6.1%), growing for three consecutive years from 2023 to 2025.

Operating profit rose from KRW 119.9 billion in 2022 to KRW 144.9 billion in 2023 (up 20.8%), KRW 164.8 billion in 2024 (up 13.8%) and KRW 169.4 billion in 2025 (up 2.8%), also rising for three consecutive years.

The operating margin climbed from 12.8% in 2022 to 15.2% in 2023 and 15.6% in 2024, then eased to 15.1% in 2025.

Owner net profit was KRW 114.8 billion in 2022, KRW 112 billion in 2023 (down 2.4%), KRW 122.4 billion in 2024 (up 9.3%) and KRW 119.5 billion in 2025 (down 2.4%), so it did not move in the same direction as operating profit. Net profit including non-controlling interests was KRW 134.3 billion in 2025.

Operating cash flow fell from KRW 27.6 billion in 2022 and KRW 64.1 billion in 2023 to KRW 37.6 billion in 2024 and KRW 8.71 billion in 2025. Measured against operating profit, the 2025 cash flow ratio was the lowest of the four years. The debt ratio rose to 32.0% in 2024 and returned to 24.8% in 2025.

On a quarterly basis, second-quarter 2026 revenue of KRW 318.3 billion and operating profit of KRW 54 billion, up 31.0% from the prior quarter, were the highest of the last five quarters. Third-quarter 2026 revenue was KRW 289.8 billion, down 0.6% year on year.

Owner net profit over the last four quarters totaled KRW 126.9 billion. A media analysis of the half-year report showed first-half 2026 consolidated revenue of KRW 591.3 billion, up 4.6%, and operating profit of KRW 95.3 billion, up 13.1%.

The reason operating profit and owner net profit diverged can be traced through the detailed income statement items in the periodic reports.

05

Industry analysis

The rental industry has been discussing the growth limits of a consumer-centered market. In response, rental companies are widening their reach into business customers (B2B) and service-based businesses, building on their installation and management capabilities.

Media reports say the share of B2B in Cuckoo Homesys' revenue has been steadily rising. The company first topped KRW 1 trillion in annual revenue in 2024. In competition, Coway's U.S. unit posted first-quarter 2026 revenue of KRW 57.5 billion, down 4.1%, while its net profit rose 185.7% to KRW 2 billion.

Over the same period, Cuckoo Homesys' U.S. unit saw revenue fall 13.2% to KRW 8.2 billion and swung to a net loss of KRW 0.9 billion. In food waste disposers, a media report says the sixth-generation Ecowhale model launched in early 2026 lifted market share.

In smart home platforms, Cuckoo Homesys is entering a market led by Samsung Electronics. Across the appliance industry, service-based models that bundle installation and after-sales care are becoming a benchmark for competitiveness.

06

Outlook

The next periodic report is the 2026 annual report, and at that point the full-year 2026 operating margin and overseas subsidiary profits become the main checkpoints. In Malaysia, whether recovering net account additions leads to a sales rebound is the central question.

In an announcement on November 16, 2026, the Malaysian subsidiary CUCKOO International said its third-quarter net profit rose and that cumulative January-to-September net profit increased 16.1% to MYR 79.78 million.

The same release showed cumulative revenue down 17.5% to MYR 715.89 million and a gross margin of 37.1%, up 5 percentage points from a year earlier. The company said it plans to add external partnerships to widen its distribution network and to drive upgrades and add-on sales to existing rental customers.

In the United States, new net account additions in rental-financing products rose 36.3% from the prior quarter, and new net additions of air purifiers rose 32.3%, according to the company. Among new products, the Ecowhale Cube food waste disposer launched in March 2026.

The company's own smart home platform targets completion of technology development in 2027, with the commercial launch date to be set according to market conditions.

The robot business was added to the corporate purpose at the March 2026 shareholder meeting, but the company said no concrete business plan has been finalized.

08

Bull factors

Expanding domestic rental base

Domestic revenue in the first quarter of 2026 rose 9.1% to KRW 179.2 billion, and cumulative domestic rental accounts grew 8.3% to 3.28 million. First-half 2026 operating profit rose 13.1% to KRW 95.3 billion, with the domestic base supporting earnings. A wider account base strengthens the foundation of recurring revenue, which is the core of the bullish case.

Profitability and Malaysian earnings resilience

The operating margin rose from 12.8% in 2022 to 15.6% in 2024 and held at 15.1% in 2025. The Malaysian subsidiary reported cumulative January-to-September 2026 net profit up 16.1%, with gross margin up 5 percentage points to 37.1%. Protecting earnings through margin and cost control despite falling sales supports the bullish view.

Product and new business pipeline

Media reports say the sixth-generation Ecowhale food waste disposer lifted market share after its early 2026 launch. The Ecowhale Cube followed in March 2026, and the company's own smart home platform targets completion of technology development in 2027. Robot solutions and paid repair services were added to the corporate purpose, creating room for B2B expansion.

09

Bear factors

Weak overseas sales and subsidiary earnings

Overseas sales in the first half of 2026 fell 9.2% to KRW 196.9 billion, and Malaysian subsidiary revenue fell 10.3%. The U.S. unit's first-half net loss widened from KRW 0.3 billion to KRW 2.0 billion, and losses also grew at the Indian unit. If overseas sales keep declining, they could weigh on consolidated growth.

Gap between third-quarter operating profit and net profit

Third-quarter 2026 operating profit rose 10.4% to KRW 45.1 billion, but owner net profit fell 46.0% to KRW 17.2 billion. Operating cash flow in 2025 was KRW 8.71 billion, far below operating profit of KRW 169.4 billion. Earnings confidence depends on whether earnings quality and cash generation are confirmed together.

Slowing growth and limits of the rental market

Revenue growth slowed from 10.8% in 2024 to 6.1% in 2025, and the first quarter of 2026 managed only 0.7%. Third-quarter 2026 revenue of KRW 289.8 billion fell 0.6% year on year. With growth limits being discussed in consumer-centered rental markets, the slowdown could be linked to structural factors.

10

Risk factors

Overseas subsidiary capital structure

A May 2026 media report said rental export revenue fell 72.2% from KRW 85.8 billion in 2023 to KRW 23.9 billion in 2025. The same report said some overseas subsidiaries had entered a state of full capital impairment.

Media also reported that the Indonesian and Australian units were sold to the parent Cuckoo Holdings, which may point to further portfolio restructuring.

Legal and labor issues

On June 25, 2026, the Patent Court ruled for competitor @Home in a design dispute over food waste disposers, finding that Ecowhale shares core features with the rival product.

In February 2026, the Ministry of Employment and Labor began a labor inspection after a union raised allegations of disguised employment and union interference. These are union claims, and no legal conclusion has been reached.

Earnings quality and cash flow

Operating profit in 2025 was KRW 169.4 billion, but operating cash flow was only KRW 8.71 billion. Owner net profit in 2025 was KRW 119.5 billion, down 2.4% from 2024. Accounting for rental contracts and receivables may play a role, but the periodic disclosures alone do not allow a firm conclusion on the cause. If the gap between earnings and cash persists, dividend and reinvestment capacity could both be constrained.

12

Overall view

Cuckoo Homesys' annual record shows revenue rising from KRW 938.1 billion in 2022 to KRW 1.12 trillion in 2025, with the operating margin staying in the 15% range since 2023. On the other side, owner net profit fell to KRW 119.5 billion in 2025 and operating cash flow was only KRW 8.71 billion.

In 2026, domestic rental accounts and operating profit grew, but overseas and Malaysian sales declined, and owner net profit fell 46.0% in the third quarter. The bullish side rests on the domestic rental base, margin resilience, and the product and new business pipeline.

The bearish side rests on overseas earnings, the gap between earnings and cash, and legal and labor issues. The next checkpoints are overseas earnings and operating cash flow in the 2026 annual report, and the trend in Malaysian account additions in the first quarter of 2027.
