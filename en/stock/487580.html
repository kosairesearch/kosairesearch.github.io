Sales up, profit down at Poled

02

Business structure

Poled began as an in-house venture at Hyundai Motor in 2016 and was spun off in 2019 as a baby products manufacturer and seller. Its early business centered on car seats, but the product range has since expanded into baby appliances, Airlove-branded products, hygiene products and safety products.

By product, 2025 revenue mix was baby appliances 53.3%, Airlove 25.6%, hygiene products 10.0%, safety products such as car seats 5.1%, and other 6.0%. The largest category, baby appliances, includes the Yupang bottle sterilizer brand, whose operator Blue Care was acquired in 2024 to scale this segment.

The same year, the company acquired IVZ, the Korean distributor of the Babybrezza brand, further broadening its portfolio. IPO materials from a securities firm named Yupang, Airlove and Babybrezza as top brands on Naver's Smart Store channel, and the company's distribution is described as centered on online channels.

The company's consolidated subsidiaries are disclosed as three entities, including IVZ and Yupang. Because its business is centered on appliances and infant goods, its profile differs considerably from the electronic components sector classification.

The baby products market faces a structural headwind from declining birth numbers, but premium demand, in which spending per child rises, is cited as a growth driver. Competition comes from a mix of overseas infant product brands and small domestic manufacturers.

03

Recent trends

Cumulative consolidated revenue for the first nine months of 2026 was KRW 23.2 billion, up 24.9% year on year. Over the same period, operating profit was KRW 588 million, down 67.3%, and net profit was KRW 379 million, down 73.1%.

Sales grew while profit declined, and the gap between the revenue growth rate and the profit decline is the central point of this disclosure. The cumulative operating margin works out to about 2.5%, which is low relative to the sales base.

Whether cost of sales or selling and administrative expenses rose more will determine the path to margin recovery. The company listed on KOSDAQ on May 14, 2026. The IPO price was set at KRW 5,000, the top of the indicative range, and the institutional demand forecast drew a competition ratio of 1,486.66 to 1.

The offering raised about KRW 13 billion through 2.6 million newly issued shares. On the first trading day, the stock rose to the daily price limit of about four times the IPO price, but it later fell below the IPO price, according to press reports. As of 10:54 a.m.

KST on November 16, 2026, the share price was KRW 2,285, up 2.01% from the previous close, with a market capitalization of KRW 57.5 billion, about 54% below the IPO price.

04

Outlook

The near-term path depends on whether sales growth converts into profit improvement. Full-year 2026 results will be confirmed when the annual report is filed in early 2027.

Pre-IPO estimates from securities firms assumed operating profit would rise over the following years, but the third-quarter cumulative disclosure showed profit moving in the opposite direction. Whether fourth-quarter results reverse the decline in profit is the next checkpoint.

On supply, the IPO schedule was structured so that tradable shares would rise to about 71% of total shares three months after listing. Press reports said the stake held by the largest shareholder and related parties fell below 30% after the listing.

The lock-up period for the largest shareholder, 6 to 30 months, is set to release in stages from mid-November 2026 through mid-November 2028 based on the May 14, 2026 listing date. On the share price, one view attributes the decline mainly to supply factors, while another views it as reflecting the earnings trend.

Baby product demand faces pressure from falling birth numbers, while premium products and overseas expansion are cited as offsetting growth factors.

05

Bull factors

Top-line growth and a core brand base

Cumulative revenue for the first nine months of 2026 rose 24.9% year on year, extending the top-line growth trend. Baby appliances and Airlove together accounted for about 79% of 2025 revenue, a high concentration in core product lines.

Yupang, Airlove and Babybrezza are cited as leading brands on Naver's Smart Store, which supports the sales base.

Strong IPO demand and new capital

The institutional demand forecast for the IPO drew a competition ratio of 1,486.66 to 1, indicating strong institutional interest. The retail subscription recorded 410,098 applications, reflecting high early-stage market attention.

The roughly KRW 13 billion raised through new shares provides a base for financial flexibility and facility and operating investment.

Improved leverage and diversification

According to Daum Finance data, the debt-to-equity ratio fell to 45.0% in 2025 from 104.0% in 2024. The shift from car seats to baby appliances and hygiene products broadens the product mix, which is a diversification strength.

The acquisition of the Babybrezza distributorship in Korea also expanded the brand lineup as one driver of growth.

06

Bear factors

Sales and profit moving apart

Revenue rose 24.9%, while operating profit fell 67.3% and net profit fell 73.1%, so sales and profit moved in opposite directions. The cumulative operating margin of about 2.5% is low relative to sales.

Whether cost growth outpaced revenue growth depends on the breakdown of cost of sales and selling and administrative expenses.

Shares trading below the IPO price

The stock hit the upper daily price limit on its first trading day, but it later fell and remained below the KRW 5,000 IPO price. The KRW 2,285 share price on November 16, 2026 is about 54% below the IPO price.

Press reports said early-stage supply pressure was reflected in the share price, which has put supply factors in focus.

Lock-up releases and control changes

The largest shareholder's lock-up is structured to release in stages over 6 to 30 months. Concern has been raised that exits by financial investors could shift control of the largest shareholder.

The decline of the largest shareholder and related parties' stake below 30% is also drawing attention to the governance structure.

07

Risk factors

Macro

Falling birth numbers are a structural constraint on the baby products market. If household spending capacity slows, demand for higher-priced baby appliances could be affected first. Currency and overseas logistics cost swings could add cost pressure as overseas sales expand.

Supply and demand

As lock-up shares are released in stages, share price volatility could increase. Given its small-cap profile, price moves can be large on days when trading value is low. Trading value was about KRW 200 million as of November 16, 2026.

Business

If integration of acquired brands and overseas expansion proceed at the same time, management resources could be spread thin. Rising raw material and logistics costs could push up cost of sales and delay margin recovery. In baby products, product safety issues bear directly on brand trust.

08

Overall view

Taken together, the first nine months of 2026 show a business that has grown in scale while its earnings generation has weakened. Revenue anchored in baby appliances and core brands, brand recognition in online channels, and portfolio expansion through acquisitions are cited as strengths.

On the other side, the cumulative operating margin of about 2.5%, the share price below the IPO price, and supply pressure from lock-up releases are cited as weaknesses. The decline in the largest shareholder's stake and the governance questions are variables the market is watching separately from short-term results.

The gap between the profit growth path in pre-IPO estimates and the actual disclosed results is the central point of disagreement in valuation debates. Changes in the cost structure shown in fourth-quarter results and the 2026 annual report will indicate whether the nine-month trend is temporary or structural.

This is a qualitative summary for information purposes and does not contain an investment opinion or a view on the direction of the share price.
