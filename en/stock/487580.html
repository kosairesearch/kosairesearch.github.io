Below the IPO Price After a Quadruple Debut

Poled grew first-half 2026 revenue on a reported basis but saw operating profit decline, and its share price now sits far below its listing-day peak.

02

Business structure

Poled began as an in-house venture at Hyundai Motor in 2016 and was spun off as a corporation in 2019, making and selling infant products.

It broadened its business from car seats into a wider range of products, and in 2024 it acquired Blue Care, owner of the UPANG bottle sterilizer brand, and IVG, the Korean distributor of Baby Brezza.

By product, 2025 sales were split 53.3% baby appliances, 25.6% airluv, 10.0% hygiene products, 5.1% safety products and 6.0% other. By entity, first-half 2026 sales were 51.7% Poled, 27.9% IVG and 20.1% UPANG.

The independent research house Valuefinder estimated that airluv ventilated seats hold a market share above 70%, with related sales rising from KRW 7.7 billion in 2022 to KRW 20.5 billion in 2025. Exports were 19.5% of 2025 sales, of which Japan was 16.4%, and online was the largest domestic channel at 61.0%.

Three domestic infant and household goods companies were named as peers at the time of the IPO. Valuefinder described Poled as the only listed Korean infant-products company posting meaningful exports from in-house production.

The exchange classifies the company under electronics and components, but its actual business is infant products and baby appliances.

03

Recent trends

On a reported basis, first-half 2026 cumulative revenue was KRW 25.3 billion, up 15.1% from a year earlier. Operating profit over the same period was KRW 2.06 billion, down 25.2%. Net income was KRW 1.67 billion, up 26.3%. With revenue rising and operating profit falling, the operating margin narrowed.

The reason for the net income increase cannot be determined from the reported summary alone.

Separately, Bloter, citing the Financial Supervisory Service's electronic disclosure system, reported consolidated first-half revenue of KRW 50.6 billion, operating profit of KRW 6.6 billion and net income of KRW 6.9 billion, with the operating margin slipping from 17.2% to 13%.

Those figures may use a different scope from the reported numbers above, so the DART filing should be checked directly. Valuefinder attributed the operating profit decline to one-off costs tied to a new brand launch.

Poled's standalone operating profit was KRW 3.6 billion, down 26.9%, and lower overseas sales weighed on the parent's results.

On the stock, it reached KRW 20,000 on its May 14, 2026 debut, four times the KRW 5,000 offer price, but as of October 27, 2026 it trades at KRW 2,390 with a market capitalization of KRW 60.2 billion and daily turnover of about KRW 0.2 billion.

04

Outlook

A company official said full-year 2026 overseas sales would dip slightly from the prior year, but expected growth of more than 50% in 2027 once the B2C transition is complete.

The company plans to widen its export lineup beyond the highly seasonal airluv to items such as the UPANG sterilizer, and it is working to establish new brands such as Baby Brezza in Japan.

It exhibited at Kind+Jugend 2026 in Germany with airluv5 as the lead product and said it plans to expand distribution in Europe and elsewhere. Valuefinder described a push into Indonesia following the U.S., Taiwan and Hong Kong, diversifying export regions.

The same report also cited expanded government childcare support and rising infant-product consumption as growth drivers. Because the company set a target of keeping the operating margin at or above 15% at the time of listing, whether profitability recovers in the second half is a point to watch.

In an April 2026 pre-IPO report, Eugene Investment & Securities forecast 2026 revenue of KRW 118.6 billion and operating profit of KRW 18.0 billion. That forecast predates the half-year results, so the gap to the actual earnings path needs to be checked.

The payoff from the overseas B2C shift and the pace of margin recovery are open questions rather than established facts.

05

Bull factors

Category-leading brand portfolio

One assessment holds that Poled has built a portfolio of category share leaders by adding the acquired UPANG and IVG to its own airluv and safety brands.

Valuefinder pointed to a collision test passed at Hyundai Motor's Namyang research center and an integrated operating system running from design to customer service as competitive strengths. The in-house structure spanning planning, R&D, production, logistics and customer service is also cited as a strength. If brand awareness holds, it can support new launches and cross-selling.

Room for overseas expansion

Exports were only 19.5% of 2025 sales, so there is room to raise overseas revenue. Export value outside Japan rose from about KRW 0.4 billion in 2023 to KRW 2.5 billion in 2025. The company expects overseas sales to grow by more than 50% in 2027, when the B2C transition is complete. That outlook is management's expectation, not a confirmed figure.

Margin headroom if one-off costs fade

Valuefinder analyzed the first-half operating profit decline as the effect of one-off costs tied to a new brand launch. If those costs do not recur, the operating margin has room to recover. The same report added that as brand equity builds overseas, cost efficiencies could lift operating profit.

The 2025 operating margin was 13.0%, and the gap to the 15% target given at listing frames the scope for improvement.

06

Bear factors

Slowing profitability

On a reported basis, first-half 2026 operating profit was KRW 2.06 billion, down 25.2%, failing to keep pace with revenue growth of 15.1%. The reported consolidated operating margin also fell from 17.2% to 13%. Net income rose, but core operating profit declined, so the quality of earnings deserves scrutiny.

Although the decline is explained as one-off costs, uncertainty remains until a second-half margin recovery is confirmed.

Overseas decline and seasonality

Even allowing for airluv being a seasonal product concentrated in spring and summer, total overseas sales fell sharply from KRW 14.7 billion a year earlier. Of KRW 8.0 billion in consolidated first-half overseas sales, Poled itself accounted for KRW 7.2 billion, so much of the overseas business sits in the parent.

Baby appliances including airluv make up roughly 80% of sales, which also means high dependence on the core product lines. The company attributes the decline to the sales-structure shift, but actual demand after the transition is still unproven.

Acquisition effect on growth quality

One report noted that the top-line expansion appears to reflect the effect of bringing UPANG and IVG in as wholly owned subsidiaries in 2024. The two companies accounted for about 47% of 2025 sales. Without separating own-brand growth from acquisition effects, the pace of organic growth could be overstated. Poled's standalone first-half revenue of KRW 26.4 billion, down 3.8%, shows why that distinction matters.

07

Risk factors

Macro

The infant-products market operates under the structural constraint of a declining number of births. Some argue that demand for premium infant goods persists even in a low-birthrate era, but weaker household spending could affect demand for higher-priced baby appliances.

Overseas sales, including Japan, are also exposed to exchange rates and local consumer conditions. The direction and scale of government childcare support are further external variables.

Business

A company official noted that because the buyer (the parent) and the actual user (the child) differ in infant products, psychological barriers to new brands are high. Childcare culture and purchasing habits vary by country, requiring separate sales strategies for each market.

Rising marketing costs during the overseas B2C shift could weigh on margins. airluv is strongly seasonal, which also adds to quarterly earnings volatility.

Supply and governance

The tradable share ratio was set to rise from 27.94% on the listing day to 71.05% three months later. Around the listing, the stake held by the largest shareholder and related parties fell from 31.50% to 28.15%, and CEO Lee Hyung-moo's stake fell from 14.57% to 13.02%.

Potential dilution from unexercised stock options is another factor. Daily turnover of about KRW 0.2 billion on October 27, 2026 points to thin liquidity, so prices can swing more in low-volume sessions.

08

Overall view

Poled is an infant-products company with well-known brands such as UPANG, airluv and Baby Brezza, and on a reported basis its first-half 2026 revenue rose 15.1%. Operating profit fell 25.2% over the same period, opening a gap between top-line growth and profitability.

The company and Valuefinder attribute the decline to one-off factors such as new-brand launch costs and the overseas B2C shift. A second-half margin recovery and expanded overseas sales in 2027 remain unconfirmed expectations.

On supply, the larger tradable float after lockup releases and thin turnover are cited as background to share-price volatility. The bullish factors are brand strength and room for overseas expansion, while the bearish factors are slowing profitability, lower overseas sales, reliance on acquisitions and supply pressure.

The next quarterly filings on operating margin, overseas sales and the gap between standalone and subsidiary results should help in forming a view.
