Daewoong: A Holding Company Tied to Daewoong Pharma's Results

Daewoong is a holding company that owns 52.29% of Daewoong Pharma, and its operating profit rose for three straight quarters through Q3 2026 while net profit attributable to controlling shareholders fell over the same period, making that divergence the central question.

02

Business structure

Daewoong was founded in 1961 and converted into a holding company in 2002, and it had 35 consolidated subsidiaries as of 2026. Its core subsidiary is Daewoong Pharma, in which Daewoong holds a 52.29% stake according to the business report filed on March 18, 2026.

Daewoong Pharma runs the botulinum toxin Nabota, an acid-reflux treatment, the OTC liver remedy Urso, an OTC business and a digital healthcare business. A separate subsidiary handles raw materials and finished drugs, and another handles synthetic and biologic drug R&D, manufacturing and sales.

In provisional separate-basis results for Q3 2026, disclosed on November 4, 2026, Nabota export sales were KRW 106.8 billion, up 127.9% from a year earlier, and exports made up most of the KRW 116.3 billion combined domestic and overseas Nabota sales.

A U.S. partner reportedly stocked up ahead of tariffs taking effect in September, which is cited as the main driver. OTC sales rose 21% and digital healthcare sales rose 40% from a year earlier.

The Pexuclu co-marketing contract with Chong Kun Dang ended on August 31, and the 20mg product moved to sole sales, while Pexuclu sales in Q3 fell 84.5% year on year. The public filings do not break out revenue by segment, so the subsidiary-level mix must be checked in periodic reports.

The business is best understood through its subsidiary portfolio and the value of its stakes rather than through any single product.

04

Earnings analysis

Based on the filed figures, annual revenue rose from KRW 1.7 trillion in 2022 to KRW 1.81 trillion in 2023 (up 6.9%), KRW 1.94 trillion in 2024 (up 6.8%) and KRW 2.07 trillion in 2025 (up 6.8%), marking three straight years of growth from 2023 through 2025.

Operating profit was KRW 207.3 billion in 2022, KRW 195.6 billion in 2023 (down 5.7%), KRW 281.9 billion in 2024 (up 44.1%) and KRW 261.8 billion in 2025 (down 7.1%). Revenue kept rising while operating profit fell after its 2024 peak, and the operating margin slid from 14.6% in 2024 to 12.7% in 2025.

Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 102.6 billion in 2022, KRW 152.7 billion in 2023, KRW 57.9 billion in 2024 (down 62.1%) and KRW 175.1 billion in 2025 (up 202.4%), so earnings recovered in 2025 from the 2024 low.

Net profit including non-controlling interests was KRW 68.9 billion in 2024 and KRW 227.9 billion in 2025, and the gap between that figure and the controlling-shareholder figure in 2025 reflects non-controlling interests.

Quarterly operating profit bottomed at KRW 34.8 billion in Q4 2025, down 56.8% from the prior quarter, and then rose for three consecutive quarters to KRW 47.8 billion in Q1 2026, KRW 76.6 billion in Q2 and KRW 88.8 billion in Q3.

Revenue fell to KRW 492.7 billion in Q1 2026 and then rose to KRW 570.8 billion in Q2 and KRW 594.1 billion in Q3, two straight quarters of growth. In Q3 2026, operating profit grew 10.3% year on year, faster than the 9.6% revenue growth.

Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders peaked at KRW 69.9 billion in Q4 2025 and then fell for three consecutive quarters to KRW 35.3 billion, KRW 34.1 billion and KRW 32 billion in Q3 2026. The sum of the last four quarters of controlling-shareholder net profit is KRW 171.2 billion.

At the end of 2025, equity totaled KRW 1.77 trillion, of which the controlling-shareholder portion was KRW 1.18 trillion, and total liabilities were KRW 1.46 trillion. The debt ratio rose from 64.2% in 2023 to 81.1% in 2024 and 82.9% in 2025.

Operating cash flow was KRW 257.6 billion in 2025, up from KRW 162.4 billion in 2024. Separately, Daewoong Pharma's provisional separate-basis Q3 2026 operating profit was KRW 76.8 billion, up 43.5% year on year (disclosed November 4, 2026), which is a subsidiary figure and not Daewoong's own result.

05

Industry analysis

Daewoong's end market is effectively the pharmaceutical and biotech cycle its subsidiaries operate in. The holding company's earnings move with Daewoong Pharma's operating performance and the value of its subsidiary stakes.

The strength in Q3 2026 Nabota exports is attributed to a U.S. partner stocking up before tariffs took effect in September, which points to shipment timing rather than necessarily a durable jump in demand. Whether overseas demand is stable on a quarterly average basis therefore needs further checking.

Pexuclu moved to direct sales after its co-marketing period ended, creating a revenue gap, and Q3 prescription drug sales fell 3.1% year on year to KRW 218.4 billion at the separate-basis level, per November 2026 reports.

Daewoong Pharma is expanding OTC and digital healthcare to broaden its revenue base beyond prescription drugs. Holding-company shares often trade at a discount to the market value of their subsidiary stakes, so subsidiary results pass through to the parent's share price only indirectly.

Through Daewoong Pharma, Daewoong also holds a stake in the U.S.-listed biotech AEON Biopharma, which extends its portfolio beyond pharmaceutical sales. Comparisons with other listed holding companies are limited because portfolio and ownership structures differ from company to company.

Outlook

The nearest checkpoint is Q4, when a gap in Nabota shipments to the U.S. could emerge after the Q3 shipments were concentrated, according to securities analysts.

Pexuclu 10mg and 40mg will be sold solely by Daewoong Pharma from December 1, 2026, and how quickly revenue recovers after the switch to direct sales is the next item to track. Daewoong Pharma attributed its Q3 gains to export growth as well as OTC and digital healthcare expansion.

Separately, reports in November 2026 said some securities firms lowered their target prices for Daewoong Pharma after results and cut their 2026 and 2027 earnings estimates.

On the asset side, Daewoong Pharma held 30.5% of AEON Biopharma as of November 9, 2026, and full exercise of its warrants could lift that stake to about 37.1%. Daewoong's combined stake, including its direct holding, was 30.6%.

Reports in November 2026 also said Daewoong Pharma is exploring a sale of control of a new-drug development subsidiary. The 2026 business report is due by March 31, 2027, and it will make full-year 2026 results official in the filed figures.

If the pace of revenue recovery under direct sales is slow, the gap in subsidiary earnings could last longer.

Diverging profit trends and higher debt
