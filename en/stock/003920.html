Namyang Dairy: Testing the Quality of Its Quarterly Profit Recovery

After turning operating profit positive in 2025, Namyang Dairy Products' operating profit rose in the second and third quarters of 2026, while controlling-shareholder net income fell over the same two quarters, making the quality of the recovery the key item to track.

    Revenue peaked at KRW 996.8 billion in 2023 and declined in 2024 and 2025, while operating profit turned to KRW 5.17 billion in 2025.

  2. 2

  3. 3

  4. 4

  5. 5

02

Business structure

Namyang Dairy Products is a dairy processor founded in 1964 that listed in 1978 and operates five manufacturing sites. Milk is its core category, followed by milk powder and an 'other' segment that includes protein drinks, soy milk and coffee.

In the third quarter of 2026, reported segment revenue was KRW 129.1 billion for milk (down 0.8% year on year, a 50.1% share), KRW 48.0 billion for milk powder (up 2.7%), and KRW 80.6 billion for the other segment (up 32.8%, a 31.3% share).

The flagship product in the other segment is the Take-Fit protein brand, and sales of soy milk, cheese and coffee mixes also rose. Imperial XO and I'm Mother are the main milk powder brands, and the StarGrow local brand supplies Cambodia.

The subsidiary Baekmidang reported third-quarter revenue of about KRW 8.7 billion and operating profit of about KRW 560 million. Cumulative exports for the first three quarters of 2026 reached about KRW 69.7 billion, or 9.4% of total revenue, with shipments extending to China, the Philippines and Singapore.

In the first half of 2026 the company signed export agreements with distributors in Vietnam and Mongolia. In dairy and beverages it overlaps with peers such as Maeil Dairies and Binggrae, and it is widening its lineup in protein and coffee to differentiate. The company had 1,618 employees as of June 30, 2026.

04

Earnings analysis

In the annual figures, revenue rose from KRW 964.7 billion in 2022 to KRW 996.8 billion in 2023, up 3.3%, making 2023 the largest year in the 2022–2025 span. It then fell to KRW 952.8 billion in 2024 (down 4.4%) and KRW 914.1 billion in 2025 (down 4.1%), a two-year decline, with 2025 the smallest year.

Operating losses narrowed from KRW 86.8 billion in 2022 to KRW 72.4 billion in 2023 and KRW 9.81 billion in 2024, and 2025 turned to operating profit of KRW 5.17 billion. Operating margin moved from -9.0% in 2022 and -7.3% in 2023 to -1.0% in 2024 and 0.6% in 2025.

Controlling-shareholder net losses of KRW 78.4 billion in 2022 and KRW 67.1 billion in 2023 gave way to a KRW 250 million profit in 2024 and KRW 7.11 billion in 2025, up 2,743.4%. Net income including non-controlling interests is reported separately and should not be read as the same figure.

Operating cash flow went from negative in 2022 (-KRW 57.6 billion) and 2023 (-KRW 38.3 billion) to KRW 34.8 billion in 2024 and KRW 26.9 billion in 2025.

Quarterly operating profit was KRW 1.7 billion in the third quarter of 2025 and KRW 2.47 billion in the fourth quarter (up 44.7% from the prior quarter), then fell to KRW 522 million in the first quarter of 2026 (down 78.8%).

It then rose to KRW 1.28 billion in the second quarter and KRW 3.47 billion in the third quarter, up 103.7% year on year. Third-quarter 2026 revenue of KRW 257.6 billion was up 8.5% year on year.

Controlling-shareholder net income peaked at KRW 6.31 billion in the first quarter of 2026, then fell to KRW 1.52 billion in the second quarter and KRW 1.29 billion in the third, a two-quarter decline. The last four quarters together total KRW 12.1 billion. In the third quarter operating profit rose while net income fell, and the company cited exchange-rate effects.

05

Industry analysis

In domestic dairy, milk still accounts for close to half of sales, yet Namyang Dairy Products' milk revenue fell 0.8% year on year in the third quarter of 2026, indicating a stalled core.

The company said rising domestic births supported demand for milk powder, and it cited stronger premium formulas aimed at buyers who prioritize ingredients and quality. First-half 2026 milk powder revenue grew 11% year on year.

In protein drinks, citing aT Market Link POS data the company provided, Take-Fit was the number-one single brand in the domestic offline protein drink market for four straight quarters from the second quarter of 2025 through the first quarter of 2026.

In coffee, coffee mix sales, including French Cafe and Lucas Nine, rose 11.5% in the first half of 2026. Dairy peers such as Maeil Dairies and Binggrae are built largely around milk, so Namyang's differentiation lies in lowering its milk dependence through protein, coffee and overseas milk powder.

Milk's share of first-half 2026 revenue fell to 48.9% from 54.4% a year earlier. Overseas, the company exports milk powder to Asian markets including China, Vietnam, Cambodia, Taiwan and Mongolia.

The long-run trajectory of domestic milk consumption depends on demographics and consumption patterns, so how fast the company's sales recover relative to that trend is the next point to watch.

Outlook

The company said it will keep milk competitive while growing milk powder, processed milk and protein drinks together. Management attributed the improved results to a stronger product portfolio and wider sales of growth lines, and it outlined plans to strengthen key product lines and widen overseas channels.

In protein drinks and coffee, it plans to widen choice with lower-sugar, added-protein and decaffeinated products. On overseas channels, in the first half of 2026 it signed agreements with local distributors in Vietnam and Mongolia to expand milk powder and dairy exports.

Cumulative exports for the first three quarters of 2026 reached about KRW 69.7 billion, above the full-year 2025 export figure of about KRW 39.6 billion, and the export share of revenue rose from 4.1% to 9.4% over the same periods.

How quickly and how much these agreements translate into sales has not yet been disclosed in detail. No numerical full-year target from the company appears in the sources.

The next checkpoints are the fourth-quarter 2026 results and the full-year 2026 provisional figures, followed by the 2026 annual report with segment and export breakdowns. Reported R&D spending declined from the prior year, so how fast the new product pipeline converts into sales is also worth tracking.

08

Bull factors

Quarterly operating profit recovery

Operating profit has continued to recover since turning positive in 2025, with the first quarter of 2026 at KRW 522 million as the low point, followed by KRW 1.28 billion in the second quarter and KRW 3.47 billion in the third, up 103.7% year on year.

Press coverage reported that cumulative operating profit for the first three quarters of 2026 surpassed the full-year 2025 figure.

Export and non-milk diversification

Cumulative exports for the first three quarters of 2026 reached about KRW 69.7 billion, above the full-year 2025 export figure of about KRW 39.6 billion. The export share of revenue rose from 4.1% to 9.4% year on year.

The other segment's revenue grew 32.8% to KRW 80.6 billion, and its exports rose about 9.2 times to KRW 14.1 billion, showing non-milk lines widening the revenue base.

Protein drink brand and governance improvement

Sales of the Take-Fit protein product line rose 76.1% year on year in the first half of 2026.

Citing aT Market Link POS data provided by the company, Take-Fit was the top single brand in the domestic offline protein drink market for four consecutive quarters from the second quarter of 2025 through the first quarter of 2026.

In the 2026 ESG evaluation, the integrated rating moved from B to B+, and governance improvement under the Hahn & Company ownership was cited.

09

Bear factors

Net income decline and exchange-rate effects

Controlling-shareholder net income fell 76.0% from the prior quarter to KRW 1.52 billion in the second quarter of 2026, and the third quarter's KRW 1.29 billion was down 37.8% year on year. This runs against the rise in operating profit over the same stretch.

The company cited exchange-rate movements among the factors behind the third-quarter net income decline. Operating gains have not yet carried through to net income, so the quality of profit needs scrutiny.

Stalled core milk business and two years of revenue decline

Milk revenue in the third quarter of 2026 was KRW 129.1 billion, down 0.8% year on year, and its share of total sales slipped from 54.8% to 50.1%. Annual revenue has also declined two years running since peaking at KRW 996.8 billion in 2023, falling to KRW 952.8 billion in 2024 and KRW 914.1 billion in 2025. A recovery in the core milk business has not yet shown up as sales growth.

Thin operating margin and lower R&D spending

The operating margin only turned positive in 2025, reaching 0.6%, so profit retained per won of sales is still thin. According to a report by Newsway, R&D spending declined from the prior year, which weighs on securing future growth engines. With margins this thin and R&D lower, the pace at which new products expand the base could be constrained.

10

Risk factors

Export dependence and local partners

Exports are still only 9.4% of revenue, but their share is rising quickly. Milk powder depends on supply centred on Cambodia, and the company also ships sterilized milk and fermented milk to China.

Changes in local distributor terms or import rules in those markets would feed directly into other-segment and milk powder revenue. The timing of sales from the Vietnam and Mongolia agreements has not been set.

Birth numbers and milk powder demand

The company said rising domestic births helped demand for milk powder. If births fall again or rules for infant formula change, milk powder sales growth, which was 2.7% in the third quarter of 2026, could slow. Domestic formula demand follows the birth trend, a variable the company cannot control.

Governance follow-up and capital allocation

Governance has improved under Hahn & Company's ownership, but Newsway noted that managing and deploying cash remains an open issue. Operating cash flow was KRW 26.9 billion in 2025, and that headroom could narrow if funds flow toward shareholder returns, overseas investment or expansion at the Baekmidang subsidiary. Capital allocation is the next item to watch.

Overall view

Namyang Dairy Products has shown operating profit recovery since turning positive in 2025 through the third quarter of 2026. Revenue has declined two years running since peaking in 2023, so whether top-line and profit recovery move together is still an open question.

Strengths include a rising export share, 32.8% growth in the other segment and Take-Fit's standing in protein drinks. Weaknesses include two consecutive quarterly declines in controlling-shareholder net income, a stalled milk business and a thin operating margin.

On the balance sheet, year-end 2025 total equity of KRW 949.1 billion against total liabilities of KRW 146.4 billion points to a modest debt load. Because profit scale is small, multiples carry limited meaning, and the price-to-book ratio is below 1x.

The next checkpoints are the fourth-quarter and full-year 2026 provisional results, followed by the 2026 annual report. This report is for information only and does not include buy or sell opinions.
