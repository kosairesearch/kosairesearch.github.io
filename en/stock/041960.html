02

Business structure

Komipharm International, founded in 1972, is an animal medicine company that produces and sells roughly 140 animal health products, including vaccines, antibiotics and supplements, according to its company profile.

It distributes domestically through about 130 sales agents and exports vaccines and injectables to 39 countries across Asia and Europe.

According to JobKorea company data, the segment mix as of March 2026 was poultry vaccines (biological products) at 29%, antibacterial chemical agents at 27%, other chemical products at 12%, swine vaccines at 11%, and other at 21%, so vaccines and chemical products form the main axes.

Government procurement is the largest single customer channel. The foot-and-mouth disease vaccine PRO-VAC FMD began supply under a two-year KRW 12.1 billion procurement contract with the Public Procurement Service in September 2023, with actual supply totaling KRW 8.8 billion, or about KRW 4.4 billion a year.

In October 2025 the company disclosed a new supply contract of about KRW 6.1 billion with the same agency, and that contract lifted the animal vaccine backlog to more than KRW 8.5 billion as of end-October 2025.

In human drugs, PAX-1 is seeking marketing approval in Australia, Taiwan, Korea and the United States, and its clinical and SG&A costs are cited as limits on profitability. Headcount was 172 at the end of 2025. The structure pairs animal medicine sales with the cost burden of human drug investment.

04

Earnings analysis

On a disclosed annual basis, revenue rose from KRW 39.8 billion in 2022 to KRW 42.6 billion in 2023 (up 7.2%) and then to KRW 58.8 billion in 2024 (up 37.8%), the largest year in the 2022 to 2025 span. Revenue then fell 10.7% to KRW 52.5 billion in 2025.

Operating results moved from an operating loss of KRW 3.8 billion in 2022 and KRW 6.27 billion in 2023 to operating profit of KRW 6.19 billion in 2024, a swing to profit.

Operating profit then dropped 68.3% to KRW 1.96 billion in 2025, and the operating margin went from -9.6% and -14.7% in 2022 and 2023 to 10.5% in 2024 and 3.7% in 2025. Net income attributable to controlling shareholders turned from a KRW 13.1 billion profit in 2024 to a KRW 7.21 billion loss in 2025.

Net income including non-controlling interests moved from KRW 14.7 billion in 2024 to a KRW 8.03 billion net loss in 2025.

Reporting on the 2025 year-end settlement cited a convertible bond derivative valuation loss of about KRW 6.45 billion and human drug development costs of about KRW 6.12 billion, both of which sit outside the sales line. Operating cash flow was KRW 8.85 billion in 2024 and -KRW 622 million in 2025.

Quarterly, Q3 2025 revenue was KRW 12.5 billion with operating profit of KRW 6.572 million, while Q4 2025 revenue of KRW 11.9 billion (down 4.7% from the prior quarter) came with an operating loss of KRW 241 million.

Q1 2026 posted revenue of KRW 11.9 billion, operating profit of KRW 301 million and net income attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 857 million. Q2 2026 revenue rose to KRW 16.7 billion (up 39.9%) with operating profit of KRW 1.64 billion.

Q3 2026 revenue fell 27.4% to KRW 12.1 billion, and the company booked an operating loss of KRW 1.27 billion and a net loss attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 4.31 billion, a wider loss than a year earlier. The last four quarters total KRW 6.75 billion in such losses.

05

Industry analysis

Domestic animal vaccine demand is weighted heavily toward government procurement. Press reports estimate the domestic foot-and-mouth disease vaccine market at about KRW 100 billion, and the company secures steady demand through public procurement.

Foot-and-mouth disease can severely hit livestock producers when it breaks out, which is why the government manages it systematically and vaccine demand moves with disease-control policy.

Procurement volumes vary from contract to contract, but the company has said it has renewed foot-and-mouth vaccine procurement contracts with the government every year. In overseas markets, African swine fever (ASF) vaccines are the key variable.

Press reports estimate the global ASF vaccine market at more than KRW 4 trillion. ASF is described as a disease with a fatality rate near 90% that causes heavy damage to pig farming in Southeast Asia. The company ran ASF live-vaccine trials in the Philippines and widened its trial areas to Vietnam.

Exports of the PED vaccine to China entered the initial stage in the second half of 2025. In animal vaccines, procurement bidding and distributor networks shape results, and the company's roughly 130 domestic sales agents form its distribution base.

The human drug pipeline is still in clinical stages, so near-term revenue contribution remains concentrated in animal medicines.

06

Outlook

Three pipelines are confirmed. First, ASF vaccine dosing and observation have run on three designated farms in the Philippines, and trial areas were expanded to Vietnam.

The company expects to obtain marketing approval once the trials are complete, and plans to pursue overseas market entry through local marketing authorization in the Philippines after clinical work ends. Second, the PED vaccine Pro-vac PED-Fc has entered the early stage of exports to China.

Third, the human drug PAX-1 is pursuing global marketing approval in Australia, Taiwan, Korea and the United States. The domestic foot-and-mouth vaccine contract ran from October 2025 to September 2026, and that period has already ended, so whether and at what size it is renewed will shape the revenue trend.

The animal vaccine backlog stood at more than KRW 8.5 billion at end-October 2025. With an operating loss already recorded in Q3 2026, the next checkpoint is how far human drug costs can be reduced while animal vaccine sales hold up.

Until overseas approvals arrive, human drug development costs are likely to keep flowing through the income statement.

Risk factors

Government procurement and disease-control policy

A significant part of sales depends on government procurement contracts, so revenue moves with contract size and renewal timing. The October 2025 contract was fixed through September 2026. Foot-and-mouth disease control policy and outbreaks of livestock disease can also change vaccine demand. Because procurement volumes differ from contract to contract, they add to quarterly revenue swings.

Clinical and approval timelines

The commercial timing of the ASF vaccine and PAX-1 depends on clinical results and country-by-country approvals. In an earlier interview, the company said further R&D investment would be needed to secure final product approval for its new drug.

If trials slip, development costs could keep flowing through the income statement. Until approval timelines are confirmed by disclosure, the timing is difficult to gauge.

Capital structure and derivative valuation swings

Fair value swings in the convertible bond derivatives move reported profit with external variables. The roughly KRW 6.45 billion valuation loss booked in the 2025 settlement shows the same item can swing results again. The debt ratio of 85.8% in 2025 is lower than 97.6% in 2023 but remains elevated. Funding structure is central to financial stability.

Overall view

Komipharm International's disclosed results peaked in 2024 with revenue of KRW 58.8 billion and operating profit of KRW 6.19 billion.

They then fell in 2025 to revenue of KRW 52.5 billion and operating profit of KRW 1.96 billion, and the company swung to a KRW 7.21 billion net loss attributable to controlling shareholders.

In 2026, Q1 returned to profit and Q2 revenue reached KRW 16.7 billion, but Q3 moved back to an operating loss of KRW 1.27 billion. The animal vaccine business rests on foot-and-mouth vaccine procurement contracts and overseas expansion of ASF and PED vaccines.

On the other side, human drug development costs for PAX-1 and convertible bond derivative valuation swings are adding to earnings volatility.

On the financial side, the debt ratio fell from 97.6% in 2023 to 85.8% in 2025, and of the KRW 72.1 billion total equity at year-end 2025, KRW 69.5 billion belongs to controlling shareholders.

Earnings multiples cannot be used for valuation at present, and the price-to-book ratio at year-end 2025 was higher than at year-end 2024, so the premium over net assets widened.

Strengths and weaknesses coexist, so the next checkpoints are the operating margin and net income attributable to controlling shareholders in the 2026 annual report, along with progress on overseas approvals.
