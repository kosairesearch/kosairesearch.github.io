02

Business structure

CJ Seafood is a food company that mainly makes processed seafood products such as fish cake, seaweed snacks, fried tofu pouches and imitation crab. An equity information site that profiles the company says its main sales come from fish cake, followed by seaweed, fried tofu pouches and imitation crab.

The same source puts the company's fish cake market share at 37% and says it remains first, though this is a site estimate rather than an industry statistic. Products are sold through distribution channels such as large department stores and discount stores.

Seafood segment sales declined from KRW 125.5 billion in 2023 to KRW 122.2 billion in 2024 and KRW 114.2 billion in 2025, according to a press report. The same report says seafood production fell to 21,629 tons in 2025, down nearly 10% from 2023.

A 2025 first-quarter company profile notes that both the seafood and seaweed segments posted operating losses. Higher prices for raw seafood and seaweed have been cited as a cost burden, and a 2025-based report said the overall cost of sales ratio rose to 88.5%.

In competition, the company faces mid-sized domestic seafood processors, and some peers reportedly stayed profitable over the same period. Because the business centers on fish cake and seaweed products, its results are directly exposed to raw material prices and consumer demand.

04

Earnings analysis

On a disclosed basis, annual sales were KRW 152.7 billion in 2022, KRW 148.3 billion in 2023 (down 2.9% year on year), KRW 193.7 billion in 2024 (up 30.6%) and KRW 192.1 billion in 2025 (down 0.8%). Among 2022 to 2025, 2024 was the largest year for sales and 2023 the smallest.

Operating profit was KRW 4.14 billion in 2022, KRW 4.14 billion in 2023 (up 0.1%) and KRW 4.36 billion in 2024 (up 5.3%), before turning to an operating loss of KRW 910 million in 2025, a swing to deficit from the prior year. The operating margin moved from 2.7% in 2022 to 2.8% in 2023, 2.2% in 2024 and -0.5% in 2025.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 7.42 billion in 2022, KRW 4.44 billion in 2023 (down 40.2%), KRW 5.02 billion in 2024 (up 13.2%) and KRW 2.83 billion in 2025 (down 43.7%). Among 2022 to 2025, 2022 was the largest year and 2025 the smallest.

Net income including non-controlling interests was reported as KRW 7.42 billion, KRW 4.44 billion, KRW 5.02 billion and KRW 2.83 billion for the same years.

Operating cash flow went from negative KRW 906 million in 2022 to KRW 5.51 billion in 2023, KRW 4.15 billion in 2024 and KRW 1.96 billion in 2025, remaining positive but trending lower.

Quarterly sales were KRW 49.4 billion in the third quarter of 2025, KRW 47.7 billion in the fourth quarter (down 3.5% from the prior quarter), KRW 55 billion in the first quarter of 2026 (up 15.2%), KRW 48.3 billion in the second quarter (down 12.2%) and KRW 63.8 billion in the third quarter (up 32.1%).

Operating profit turned positive at KRW 2.21 billion in the fourth quarter of 2025, but the company has posted operating losses of KRW 1.47 billion, KRW 578 million and KRW 864 million in the first three quarters of 2026, which is three consecutive quarters of losses.

Sales growth has not translated into better profitability.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders swung to a net loss of KRW 1.29 billion in the first quarter of 2026 before recovering to KRW 742 million in the second quarter and KRW 76.79 million in the third quarter, and the last four quarters total KRW 1.82 billion.

When it reported third-quarter 2026 results on November 10, 2026, the company said the weak food market that began in the first half of the year is continuing.

05

Industry analysis

Processed seafood is exposed to raw material prices for seafood and seaweed, logistics costs and consumer sentiment. CJ Seafood's seafood segment sales have declined since 2023 and production has fallen, which points to a shrinking operating base.

In the same food sector, CJ Cheiljedang reported third-quarter 2026 lifestyle food sales of KRW 2.5744 trillion, up 3.8% year on year, with operating profit of KRW 118.7 billion, flat from a year earlier, announced on November 10, 2026.

CJ Cheiljedang said overseas food sales rose 26% in Europe and 14% in Vietnam on K-food demand. Domestically, its grilled fish product sales rose 69%, which can be read as a demand signal for seafood convenience foods.

CJ Cheiljedang also said raw material and logistics burdens persisted but profitability was supported by overseas flagship products and cost efficiency. CJ Seafood said the food market has been weak since the first half and that this trend is continuing.

Some seafood peers stayed profitable, which press coverage contrasts with CJ Seafood's swing to an operating loss. Among nine companies in the seafood sector, CJ Seafood ranked eighth by annual sales on an equity information site. The fish cake leadership is a strength, but the company is relatively small within its sector by sales.

06

Outlook

Based on the facts confirmed so far, the outlook starts from a mixed picture of sales recovery and weaker profitability. Third-quarter 2026 sales of KRW 63.8 billion were the largest of the last five quarters with disclosed results.

However, the operating loss widened to KRW 864 million in the same quarter, so the sales recovery has not yet reached operating profit. The bull case rests on the possibility that operating results improve if raw material cost pressure eases.

The bear case is that fixed cost pressure could persist if seafood segment sales and production keep declining. The company said the weak food market continues, so consumer demand recovery is a key variable.

CJ Cheiljedang, a fellow CJ group company, emphasizes expanding K-food overseas, and whether that demand extends to seafood processed products remains to be confirmed. The next official checkpoints are the fourth-quarter 2026 results and the 2026 annual report.

The 2026 annual report should show seafood segment sales, the cost of sales ratio and non-operating income trends. The annual report is due by the end of March 2027. The first-quarter 2027 results will show whether the three-quarter losing streak continues.

Bull factors

Sales rebound

Third-quarter 2026 sales of KRW 63.8 billion rose 29.0% year on year and were the largest among the last five quarters with disclosed results. First-quarter 2026 sales of KRW 55 billion were up 15.2% from the prior quarter. Whether this sales flow leads to operating profit gains is the core of the bull case.

Improved balance sheet

The debt-to-equity ratio fell from 115.5% in 2023 to 107.0% in 2024 and 91.5% in 2025. Total liabilities were KRW 80.9 billion and total equity KRW 88.4 billion at the end of 2025. Equity remaining larger than liabilities can serve as financial capacity during periods of earnings volatility.

Second-quarter profit and operating cash flow

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders turned to KRW 742 million in the second quarter of 2026 from a loss in the first quarter. Operating cash flow went from negative KRW 906 million in 2022 to KRW 5.51 billion in 2023, KRW 4.15 billion in 2024 and KRW 1.96 billion in 2025. Continued positive operating cash flow is a basis for the bull case.

Bear factors

Persistent operating losses

The company has posted operating losses in three consecutive quarters from the first quarter to the third quarter of 2026, and the third-quarter operating loss of KRW 864 million widened year on year. The KRW 910 million operating loss in 2025 followed operating profit of KRW 4.36 billion in 2024. Sales growth not carrying through to profit is the core of the bear case.

Shrinking seafood segment and cost pressure

Seafood segment sales fell from KRW 125.5 billion in 2023 to KRW 114.2 billion in 2025, and production dropped nearly 10% over the same period according to the report. A 2025-based report put the overall cost of sales ratio at 88.5%. If raw material costs cannot be passed through to selling prices, margin pressure could persist.

Small earnings base and volatility

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders fell 43.7% to KRW 2.83 billion in 2025, and the first quarter of 2026 recorded a net loss of KRW 1.29 billion. The last four quarters total only KRW 1.82 billion. A small earnings base means the trend can change sharply based on one or two quarters.

Risk factors

Raw materials and costs

Price swings in seafood and seaweed directly drive the cost of sales ratio. A 2025-based report put the ratio at 88.5%, showing the scale of this exposure. How much cost increases can be reflected in selling prices is the key question.

Consumer demand and sector conditions

The company said the weak food market that started in the first half of 2026 is continuing. Declines in seafood segment sales and production may also be linked to slower consumption. If sector weakness lasts, a sales recovery may struggle to become profit improvement.

Result finality and share price volatility

The third-quarter 2026 results were prepared before the external audit was complete and may change depending on audit results. As a small-cap stock, the share price may move sharply. Both factors add uncertainty to interpreting earnings and the share price.

Overall view

CJ Seafood recorded third-quarter 2026 sales of KRW 63.8 billion, up 29.0% year on year, the largest quarterly sales among the last five quarters with disclosed results.

However, the operating loss widened to KRW 864 million in the same quarter, and the company has posted losses in three consecutive quarters since the first quarter of 2026. In 2025 it swung to an operating loss of KRW 910 million from operating profit of KRW 4.36 billion in 2024.

Seafood segment sales and production have declined since 2023, and a 2025-based report put the cost of sales ratio at 88.5%. The balance sheet improved, with the debt-to-equity ratio falling from 115.5% in 2023 to 91.5% in 2025.

The share price trades below book equity, and net income attributable to controlling shareholders totaled KRW 1.82 billion over the last four quarters.

The next checkpoints are the fourth-quarter 2026 results and the 2026 annual report, and the key question is whether the operating margin moves out of negative territory.
