02

Business structure

Fantagio is a KOSDAQ-listed entertainment company founded in 2008, engaged in artist management, record planning and production, and film and drama production.

Its management roster includes the boy group ASTRO and singers such as Ong Seong-wu and Lee Chang-sub, and actors including Kim Seon-ho, Lee Sung-kyung and Lee Se-young.

Since it brought its drama production unit in-house, it has produced titles such as Blind, Today Also Lovely Gaep, Fantasy Romance and Treat Me Badly, building production capability.

A single-sales supply contract for an SBS drama co-production appeared in the disclosure history in March 2026, after a KRW 12 billion contract for the tvN drama Blind in March 2025. Kim Boo-jang season 1 recorded a peak audience rating of 23.1%, and the KBS daily drama Mom Went Crazy peaked at 11%.

Press coverage of the November 17, 2026 results pointed to drama hits as the main driver of third-quarter performance. Because drama revenue depends on scheduling and audience response, quarterly results can swing more widely than in a management-focused business.

On the management side, Fantagio is compared with HYBE, SM Entertainment, JYP Ent. and YG Entertainment, while in drama production it overlaps with Studio Dragon and other producers. Its revenue base is smaller than these large agencies, so a few titles and IP carry a large share of results.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual revenue rose from KRW 38.5 billion in the June 2023 fiscal year to KRW 72.3 billion in the June 2024 fiscal year, an 87.7% increase, then fell 47.2% to KRW 38.2 billion in the June 2025 year and 2.5% to KRW 37.2 billion in the June 2026 year.

The June 2024 year was the largest of the four, and the June 2026 year was the smallest. Operating losses were KRW 4.91 billion, KRW 1.54 billion, KRW 8.03 billion and KRW 7.52 billion across the June 2023 to June 2026 fiscal years, a loss in each of the four years.

The operating margin moved from -2.1% in the June 2024 year to -21.0% in the June 2025 year and -20.2% in the June 2026 year. Operating losses widened in the June 2025 year even as revenue fell, and that remains the core challenge for the revenue base.

The net loss attributable to controlling shareholders grew from KRW 9.19 billion in the June 2025 year to KRW 11.8 billion in the June 2026 year, a wider gap than the operating line, and the net loss including non-controlling interests was also KRW 11.8 billion; the drivers beyond operations should be confirmed in the notes to the periodic report.

Operating cash flow moved from an inflow of KRW 12.7 billion in the June 2025 year to an outflow of -KRW 9.39 billion in the June 2026 year. At June 2026 year-end, total liabilities were KRW 37 billion and total equity KRW 41.5 billion, with the debt ratio at 89.1%.

Quarterly, revenue of KRW 10.4 billion and an operating loss of KRW 915 million in Q3 2025 was followed by revenue of KRW 9.28 billion and an operating loss of KRW 1.58 billion in Q4 2025, and a trough in Q1 2026 with revenue of KRW 5.98 billion and an operating loss of KRW 2.22 billion.

Q2 2026 revenue rebounded to KRW 11.6 billion, but the operating loss widened to KRW 2.81 billion.

Q3 2026 revenue of KRW 25.2 billion, up 118.0% from the prior quarter and 142.8% from a year earlier, produced an operating profit of KRW 319 million and a net profit attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 131 million.

Operating profit remains small relative to revenue, and the company's separate-basis operating profit of KRW 570 million differs from the consolidated figure, so the durability of the swing to profit needs confirmation in later quarters.

Company materials describe a change in fiscal year-end from June to December, so the year-end labeling should be rechecked against the periodic reports.

05

Industry analysis

Drama production depends on broadcaster scheduling and platform orders, which set production volume and rights income, so the performance of a single title carries significant weight in quarterly results.

Fantagio is moving away from a management-centered structure and expanding its drama production unit, so its business mix is in transition.

Among domestic entertainment companies, large agencies such as HYBE, SM Entertainment, JYP Ent. and YG Entertainment rely more heavily on record, concert and fandom businesses, so Fantagio, which centers on actor management and drama production, is hard to compare on the same basis.

On the broadcast content side, drama specialists such as Studio Dragon and broadcasters' in-house production units are competitors. The co-production contract with SBS can be read as an example of widening collaboration with a broadcaster.

Cheil Worldwide and Innocean Worldwide focus on advertising and agency work, so their business models differ and they are not direct comparables. Industry supply moves with platform order volume and production budgets, and these variables link directly to Fantagio's chances of having its titles scheduled.

Whether results improve will depend on both the external ordering environment and the pace of building its own IP.

Outlook

The company has stated it will pursue per-title profitability management and new IP acquisition in parallel to strengthen the earnings base of its drama production business.

Chairman Nam Gung-gyeon said the company aims to raise stable profitability and medium- to long-term corporate value by securing competitive titles and IP.

Press reports point to expectations for a season 2 of Kim Boo-jang, built on season 1's performance, but whether and when production is confirmed will be shown in company disclosures.

The co-production contract with SBS signed in March 2026 is a matter to check once broadcast schedules and revenue recognition timing are disclosed. The disclosure history shows the largest shareholder changed in May 2026 from Mirae I&G and two others to X and one other.

How business direction and funding plans develop after this change in ownership is a point to confirm in later filings. Convertible bond issuance and a maturity extension agreement were recorded in 2025, so the funding structure and repayment schedule are also variables.

Because company materials indicate the fiscal year-end was changed to December, how annual results are labeled in the first periodic report after the change matters. Changes in the revenue mix should become visible as drama broadcast schedules and new IP contracts are disclosed.

Risk factors

Concentration risk

Because drama hits are reported as the core of the rebound, the performance of individual titles could determine quarterly results. If upcoming titles fall short of audience expectations, the revenue increase seen in Q3 2026 may not repeat. Production costs and rights income differ by title, making per-title profitability management a key task.

Artist roster and reputation

Reports say tax allegations involving affiliated actors have continued. Because the management business is directly tied to artists' activities and images, reputation issues can affect contracts and scheduling.

Changes in the artist roster could also unsettle the revenue base and should be watched alongside these reports.

Capital structure and dilution

The company faced a designation trigger for administrative issues in March 2025, citing a capital impairment ratio of 50% or more, and the designation was lifted in September 2025. The same year saw a KRW 13 billion convertible bond decision and a KRW 7 billion third-party rights offering.

A large capital reduction cutting issued shares by 80% was later decided, substantially changing the share structure.

Overall view

Fantagio recorded revenue of KRW 25.2 billion and operating profit of KRW 319 million in Q3 2026, driven by drama production, a quarterly swing to profit.

On an annual basis, operating losses and net losses attributable to controlling shareholders have run for four straight fiscal years from June 2023 through June 2026, and the net loss attributable to controlling shareholders widened to KRW 11.8 billion in the June 2026 year.

Operating cash flow turned to an outflow of -KRW 9.39 billion in the June 2026 year, and the debt ratio rose to 89.1%. Positive factors include drama performance, the plan to acquire new IP, and the third-quarter swing to profit.

Negative factors include the cumulative annual losses, the operating cash outflow, earnings volatility from drama hits, and a capital history that includes a capital reduction and convertible bonds.

Both sets of factors carry similar weight, so whether the swing to profit is one-off or sustainable must be checked in later quarterly results. The next checkpoints are the annual periodic report around late March 2027 and the first-quarter report around May 15, 2027.
