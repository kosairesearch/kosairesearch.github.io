A Hit Drama, A Balance Sheet Still in Flux

Fantagio strengthened its standing as a comprehensive content producer with the 2026 hit SBS drama 'Manager Kim,' but it has posted four straight years of controlling-interest net losses through 2025 amid artist tax controversies and repeated capital reduction and share issuance.

02

Business structure

Fantagio is a comprehensive entertainment company built around actor and musician management, alongside drama production and music and performance businesses.

The artist roster includes actors such as Baek Yoon-sik, Cha Eun-woo, Kim Seon-ho, Ong Seong-wu, and Lee Sung-kyung, together with musicians including ASTRO, LUNATE, and Lee Chang-sub. Since 2022 the company has pushed into content production in earnest, expanding beyond its management-centered structure.

Its drama slate has grown from titles aired on tvN, JTBC, and MBC, to two KBS2 series, and then to a Friday-Saturday drama on SBS in 2026. Production is carried out both through in-house planning and through co-production with broadcaster-affiliated studios or platform subsidiaries.

The SBS drama, for instance, was produced jointly with Studio S, the broadcaster's in-house production arm, while another upcoming title is being co-produced with KT Studio Genie and an independent creator group.

Competitively, companies such as Hybe, SM Entertainment, YG Entertainment, and JYP Entertainment are weighted toward management and recorded music, whereas studios like Studio Dragon or the CJ ENM group specialize in drama production, placing Fantagio in a hybrid position that runs both functions.

Its revenue scale and production output, however, remain small relative to larger listed producers. More recently the company has extended beyond miniseries into daily dramas, diversifying its genre and format mix.

04

Earnings analysis

Fantagio's revenue climbed sharply from KRW 15.3 billion in 2022 to KRW 38.5 billion in 2023 (up 152.5% year over year) and KRW 72.3 billion in 2024 (up 87.7%), before falling 47.2% to KRW 38.2 billion in 2025.

Operating profit was KRW 1.25 billion in 2022 but swung to an operating loss of KRW 4.91 billion in 2023, remained in loss at KRW 1.54 billion in 2024 (a narrower loss), and widened again to an operating loss of KRW 8.03 billion in 2025.

As a result, the company posted operating losses for three consecutive years from 2023 through 2025, and the operating margin deteriorated from 8.2% in 2022 to -12.8% in 2023, -2.1% in 2024, and -21.0% in 2025.

Controlling-interest net losses ran for four straight years: KRW 6.76 billion in 2022, KRW 18.3 billion in 2023 (wider loss), KRW 6.4 billion in 2024 (narrower loss), and KRW 9.19 billion in 2025 (wider loss again).

On a quarterly basis, revenue fell from KRW 24.5 billion in the fourth quarter of 2024 to KRW 10.4 billion in the first quarter of 2025 (down 57.6%), KRW 9.28 billion in the second quarter (down 10.8%), and KRW 5.85 billion in the third quarter (down 37.0%), before rebounding to KRW 12.7 billion in the fourth quarter, up 116.7% quarter over quarter but down 48.3% year over year.

Operating losses ran for five consecutive quarters, from KRW 96.2 million in the fourth quarter of 2024, widening to KRW 915 million and KRW 1.58 billion in the first and second quarters of 2025, narrowing to KRW 351 million in the third quarter, then widening again to KRW 5.18 billion in the fourth quarter.

Controlling-interest net income swung to a profit of KRW 1.71 billion in the third quarter of 2025 before reversing to a loss of KRW 8.11 billion in the fourth quarter, underscoring significant quarter-to-quarter volatility, with the full-year sum of controlling-interest net losses across the four quarters of 2025 at KRW 9.19 billion.

On the balance sheet, the debt-to-equity ratio fell from 144.2% in 2022 to 54.3% in 2024 before rising again to 80.8% in 2025, while operating cash flow, after a negative KRW 3.63 billion in 2023, improved to KRW 2.44 billion in 2024 and KRW 12.7 billion in 2025, suggesting cash generation recovered even as net losses continued.

05

Industry analysis

Korea's drama production market draws simultaneous demand from terrestrial, general cable and cable channels alongside OTT platforms, and an industry-wide strategy of adapting webtoon intellectual property into dramas has become prominent.

Fantagio has followed this pattern, producing a string of webtoon-based titles including SBS's 'Manager Kim,' whose source webtoon had held the number one spot in its Naver weekly ranking for close to four years.

In this market, players such as Studio Dragon, CJ ENM, and KT Studio Genie benefit from stable channel or platform affiliations that secure broadcast slots and production financing, while smaller producers like Fantagio rely more on project-by-project co-production agreements to secure broadcast opportunities.

In the management segment, larger agencies such as Hybe, SM Entertainment, YG Entertainment, and JYP Entertainment have diversified revenue through recorded music, concerts, and global fandom businesses, whereas Fantagio has pursued a strategy of expanding its production business on top of a comparatively smaller artist portfolio.

In this context, social controversies involving contracted artists can directly affect management revenue and brand trust.

Given the nature of content production, the success or failure of a single title heavily drives quarterly earnings volatility, and Fantagio's results show wide swings, from a sharp revenue decline in 2025 to renewed proof of production capability through the 2026 success of 'Manager Kim.' Overall, the industry sits at a point where platform diversification is lifting demand even as production cost pressure persists, and a small-cap producer like Fantagio has a structural tendency for individual project outcomes to flow quickly into its share price and earnings.

Outlook

In September 2026, Fantagio signed a production contract with the Korean Broadcasting System (KBS) for a new KBS 1TV daily drama, 'Mom Has Gone Mad,' valued at KRW 4.7 billion, equivalent to 12.41% of the prior year's revenue, with a contract term running through March 31, 2027.

Production began in July 2026, proceeding through script readings and filming before the drama's first broadcast on September 28, marking an attempt to extend the company's largely miniseries-focused production portfolio into the daily drama format.

Following the conclusion of 'Manager Kim,' the company has said discussions on a second season are underway, with the season-one finale introducing a new character in a way intended to build anticipation, though no formal production contract for a sequel has been confirmed.

Separately, in July 2026 Fantagio signed a co-production agreement with KT Studio Genie and an independent creator group for a new drama set in the 1970s and 1980s, aimed at diversifying genre offerings and strengthening its own intellectual-property competitiveness.

On the financing side, part of the roughly KRW 5 billion raised through the May 2026 third-party share allotment is earmarked for operating funds across 2026 and 2027, and a further share offering of about KRW 16.9 billion was decided in July of the same year to expand production investment capacity.

Whether this funding translates into actual broadcast slots and hit outcomes, and whether repeated capital adjustments ultimately translate into improved profitability, remain items to verify through disclosures in coming quarters.

08

Bull factors

Proven Drama Production Capability

The 2026 SBS drama 'Manager Kim' drew nationwide ratings as high as the 23% range and was cited as the year's top-performing broadcast, a result seen as validating Fantagio's in-house planning and webtoon-sourcing capability.

Building on that success, the company is discussing a second season while also expanding its lineup with a KBS daily drama and another co-produced title. The move toward a more diversified production portfolio, reducing reliance on any single title, is a favorable factor.

Signs of Improving Cash Flow

Operating cash flow in 2025 came to KRW 12.7 billion, a marked improvement from negative KRW 3.63 billion in 2023 and KRW 2.44 billion in 2024. This suggests actual cash inflows are recovering even as net losses continue.

If production cost recovery and working-capital management keep improving, that could also support greater earnings stability going forward.

An Attempt to Reset the Capital Structure

After clearing accumulated deficits through an 80% capital reduction in March and April 2026, the company restructured its capital base via a May third-party share allotment and early redemption of convertible bonds.

A further share offering of roughly KRW 16.9 billion was decided in July to expand funding for production investment. If these steps are carried out as planned, there is room for both financial soundness and capacity to invest in new projects to improve together.

09

Bear factors

Structural Profitability Weakness

Operating losses persisted for three consecutive years from 2023 through 2025, with the operating margin deteriorating to -21.0% in 2025, a sharp contrast to the 8.2% profit margin recorded in 2022.

Controlling-interest net losses have also run for four straight years from 2022 through 2025, widening again to KRW 9.19 billion in 2025 compared with the prior year. The structural tendency for production and other costs to rise alongside revenue has repeated itself.

Exposure to Artist-Related Risk

In February 2026, contracted actor Cha Eun-woo received a tax assessment notice of roughly KRW 20 billion, and shortly afterward actor Kim Seon-ho faced similar tax evasion allegations tied to a one-person corporation he had operated.

Both cases drew criticism for using family-held corporations to route income, prompting the company to issue a formal apology in April acknowledging lapses in oversight.

Controversies involving core artists can weigh not only on management revenue but also on investment attraction and brand trust for the production business.

Repeated Capital Actions and Dilution Concerns

Through an 80% capital reduction in March and April 2026, shares outstanding fell from roughly 45.957 million to about 9.191 million, and the May third-party share allotment then changed the largest shareholder from Mirae I&G to a company named X Co.

A further general public share offering of about KRW 16.9 billion was decided in July, meaning capital reductions and issuances repeated within a short span, with ownership structure shifting frequently.

Such repeated capital actions warrant close attention, as they can dilute the value of existing shareholders' stakes.

Risk factors

Artist and Tax-Related Risk

The tax assessment involving Cha Eun-woo remains subject to an ongoing appeal and review procedure, while the controversy surrounding Kim Seon-ho's one-person corporation continues through closure procedures and additional tax payments, leaving the final outcome uncertain.

Because both cases were criticized for using family-held corporations to split income, further tax assessments or sanctions cannot be ruled out depending on the tax authority's eventual determination.

Damage to the image of core artists could also affect management revenue as well as advertising and performance contracts.

Capital Structure and Dilution Risk

In 2026 alone, a capital reduction was followed in quick succession by a third-party share allotment, early redemption of convertible bonds, and a further general public share offering, rapidly reshaping the capital structure.

This process was also accompanied by a change in governance, with the largest shareholder shifting from Mirae I&G to a company named X Co. If additional funding is required going forward, existing shareholders' stakes could face further dilution.

Content Hit-or-Miss Volatility

Revenue plunged 47.2% in 2025 from 2024 before the company drew renewed attention through the 2026 success of 'Manager Kim,' illustrating how heavily the production business's results depend on the success of individual titles.

Because 'Mom Has Gone Mad' and the newly co-produced drama are still in early broadcast or unconfirmed scheduling stages, it is premature to assume their commercial success. Any change in broadcast scheduling or production delays by channels or platforms could shift both the timing and scale of revenue recognition.

12

Overall view

For Fantagio, 2026 was a year in which the success of SBS's 'Manager Kim' produced visible results in its effort to shift from a management-centered company toward a comprehensive content producer.

Yet the disclosed results through 2025 show revenue down 47.2% year over year with both the operating loss and controlling-interest net loss widening from the prior year, leaving the structural profitability challenge of three consecutive years of operating losses from 2023 through 2025 and four consecutive years of controlling-interest net losses from 2022 through 2025 unresolved.

Adding to the uncertainty are tax evasion allegations against two of the company's core actors and the resulting corporate apology, along with an 80% capital reduction and multiple share issuances compressed into a short period, plus a change in the largest shareholder, all of which add to financial and governance-related uncertainty.

On the other side of the ledger, the improvement in 2025 operating cash flow to KRW 12.7 billion and the expansion of the production lineup into daily dramas and new co-productions are positive signals that deserve equal weight in a balanced assessment.

Ultimately, the company's next phase will hinge on whether its upcoming titles succeed commercially and whether repeated capital raises translate into actual profitability gains, points investors should verify through upcoming quarterly reports and title-by-title broadcast disclosures.
