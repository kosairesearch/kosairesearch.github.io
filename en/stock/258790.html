Softcamp: Profit Turnaround Meets Quarterly Volatility

Softcamp returned to an annual operating profit in 2025, but operating losses in Q1 and Q3 2026 keep its quarterly earnings volatile.

02

Business structure

Softcamp is a security software company that supplies zero trust and cloud security solutions centered on document security (DRM).

An independent research firm describes the company as the number one provider by market share in Korea's document security (DRM) market, offering zero trust and secure cloud services alongside.

A 2026 snapshot on a corporate information site shows a document DRM share of 49% to 52% and maintenance revenue of 31% to 33%, with the site displaying two versions, so the ranges are used here. The high maintenance share suggests a recurring base of existing customers supports results.

On the product side, SHIELD Gate, a remote browser isolation (RBI)-based secure remote access service, is presented as the core product for Japan, and SHIELD Web, another RBI-based access platform, was released in 2026. Demand is linked to accelerating cloud migration and wider system integration among businesses.

Overseas, the company works with ALSI and NEC in Japan and is widening its reach toward the Americas and Europe. In competition, the company is compared with the global RBI specialist Menlo Security in the RBI market.

The research firm's view of the number one position is an independent assessment and is best read alongside the company's own account of its market position.

04

Earnings analysis

On a disclosed basis, annual revenue went from KRW 19 billion in 2022 to KRW 18.5 billion in 2023 (down 2.8% year on year) and KRW 16.9 billion in 2024 (down 8.6%), before rising to KRW 25.7 billion in 2025 (up 52.3%). Across 2022 to 2025, 2025 was the largest year and 2024 the smallest.

Operating profit was KRW 1.34 billion in 2022 (a 7.1% margin), turned into an operating loss of KRW 2.57 billion in 2023, and remained a loss of KRW 1.83 billion in 2024 with a smaller deficit. 2025 returned to operating profit of KRW 2.98 billion, a margin of 11.6%.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 3.78 billion in 2025, up 1,583.1%, while net income including non-controlling interests was KRW 3.69 billion; the two are different measures.

The company attributed the 2025 improvement to higher security investment from cloud migration and wider system integration among businesses, and also cited customer system transitions, stronger security demand and price adjustments on key products.

The jump in total net income was linked to deferred tax recognition and investment-asset valuation gains, which are non-operating and are best read apart from operating profit.

The debt ratio rose from 94.1% in 2022 to 111.2% in 2023 and 237.5% in 2024, then eased to 173.3% in 2025; year-end 2025 total equity was KRW 15.4 billion and total liabilities KRW 26.7 billion.

Operating cash flow was positive in each year, at KRW 664 million, KRW 106 million, KRW 3.59 billion and KRW 2.51 billion for 2022 to 2025. Quarterly, revenue rose from KRW 6.64 billion in Q3 2025 to KRW 9.98 billion in Q4 2025 (up 50.3% quarter on quarter), with operating profit of KRW 2.66 billion.

Q1 2026 revenue fell to KRW 4.58 billion (down 54.1%), with an operating loss of KRW 1.13 billion and a controlling-shareholder net loss of KRW 1.21 billion.

Q2 2026 revenue was KRW 7 billion with operating profit of KRW 502 million, while Q3 2026 revenue was KRW 6.37 billion (down 4.1% year on year) and the company posted an operating loss of KRW 311 million. Net income attributable to controlling shareholders over the latest four quarters totaled KRW 4.95 billion.

Industry commentary notes that information-security revenue skews to the second half and security budgets concentrate in the fourth quarter, which limits first-half results.

05

Industry analysis

The security market is projected to grow. An independent research firm estimates that Korea's security market will surpass KRW 10 trillion in 2026, and expects the global cloud security market to grow from about KRW 52 trillion in 2024 to about KRW 109 trillion by 2030, a compound annual rate of roughly 13%.

On the demand side, the company says the center of security is shifting from blocking network access to protecting data, as data exchange with partners becomes routine and AI use expands. Zero trust is described as a segment where market growth is under way as adoption of the model accelerates.

On the partner side, the company's 2026 partner kick-off covered secure AI use, demand from Microsoft 365 rollouts, alignment with the national network security framework (N2SF) and supply-chain security.

Government AI policy, wider MS365 adoption and standardization of network security were also cited as demand drivers. In competition, RBI is a segment where the company is compared directly with global players. The company's document security leadership is the base for its push into zero trust and AI security.

Market size forecasts come from a specific research firm's estimates, and how quickly they translate into reported revenue should be checked against disclosed results.

06

Outlook

The company says it will develop data-centric security and zero trust into core growth pillars, citing wider partner connections and AI adoption. On products, S-Work V3.1 obtained a security function confirmation and SHIELD Web was released in 2026.

At its July 2026 Solution Day, the company unveiled two new products, Security365 Compliance Studio and SHIELD AI Gateway. In the public and defense sector, it signed a business agreement with Inmirae Strategy Institute on August 19, 2026, to expand into the defense security market.

In Japan, a four-stop partner tour running from Tokyo through Fukuoka and Nagoya and back to Tokyo is under way, aimed at strengthening local partner sales. The company's partner kick-off in 2026 was described as more than twice the prior year's scale.

The next checkpoints are whether Q4 2026 results follow the year-end concentration pattern and how the full-year 2026 operating profit and controlling-shareholder net income are reported in the annual business report.

08

Bull factors

Recovery from losses to profit

Operating profit turned to KRW 2.98 billion in 2025 from the 2024 loss, and net income attributable to controlling shareholders rose 1,583.1% to KRW 3.78 billion. The operating margin moved to 11.6% in 2025 from negative levels in 2023 and 2024.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders over the latest four quarters totaled KRW 4.95 billion. Part of the improvement includes non-operating items.

AI and zero trust demand with product expansion

The company names AI expansion and data-centric security as core growth pillars. It unveiled two new products aimed at the AI agent era in 2026 and released the RBI-based SHIELD Web. The partner kick-off was described as more than twice the prior year's scale, pointing to a broader sales channel.

Expansion into Japan and public-defense

In Japan, the company works with ALSI and NEC and held a partner tour across Tokyo, Fukuoka and Nagoya in 2026. In August 2026 it signed an agreement with a strategy consulting firm to advance into public and defense markets. If overseas and public-sector revenue grows, the business mix could diversify.

09

Bear factors

Large swings in quarterly results

Q1 2026 revenue fell to KRW 4.58 billion (down 54.1% quarter on quarter), with an operating loss of KRW 1.13 billion. Q3 2026 revenue was KRW 6.37 billion, down 4.1% year on year, and the company returned to an operating loss of KRW 311 million.

The gap between Q4 2025 revenue of KRW 9.98 billion and Q1 2026 revenue of KRW 4.58 billion shows how wide quarterly swings have been.

Balance sheet burden and earnings quality

At year-end 2025, total liabilities of KRW 26.7 billion exceeded total equity of KRW 15.4 billion, with a debt ratio of 173.3%. The 2025 net income increase reflected deferred tax recognition and investment valuation gains.

These items differ from operating cash generation, so the persistence of earnings is worth examining. Operating cash flow stayed positive at KRW 2.51 billion in 2025.

Global RBI competition and technology shifts

In the RBI market the company is compared directly with global players such as Menlo Security, so it must keep proving product differentiation. New demand for AI security requires faster adaptation from a revenue base built on document security. If the security investment growth cited for 2025 does not continue, the pace of revenue growth could slow.

10

Risk factors

Seasonality and quarterly volatility

Information-security budgets concentrate in the fourth quarter, which limits first-half results. The operating losses in Q1 and Q3 2026 fit this pattern. When quarterly results move against the annual trend, the range of earnings estimates widens. Quarterly figures are best read as part of the annual trend until full-year results are finalized.

Competition and technology change

Competition with global players continues in RBI and zero trust. As AI changes the shape of security demand, the fit of existing product lines must be kept under review. The domestic document security leadership does not by itself show share gains in new segments, which are not yet verified in reported results.

Provisional figures and accounting uncertainty

The 2025 results were provisional and not yet audited when first announced. They have since been reflected in the regular report figures, but net income includes deferred tax and investment valuation gains. These items can change from year to year, so adjustments should be checked in the next annual report.

12

Overall view

Softcamp is ranked as the leader in Korea's document security (DRM) market, and its return to annual operating profit in 2025 and higher net income attributable to controlling shareholders show earnings recovering. However, operating losses in Q1 and Q3 2026 keep quarterly results volatile.

On the balance sheet, the debt ratio fell from 237.5% in 2024 to 173.3% in 2025, but total liabilities still exceed total equity. On growth, the company points to zero trust, AI security, and expansion into Japan and the public-defense market.

Against that, competition with global RBI players and an earnings structure that includes non-operating gains are issues to monitor. The CEO's open-market share purchases in July 2026 are also a fact on record.

The next checkpoints are Q4 2026 results and the full-year operating profit and net income attributable to controlling shareholders in the 2026 annual business report.
