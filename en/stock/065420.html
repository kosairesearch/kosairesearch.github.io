02

Business structure

Founded in 1987 and listed on KOSDAQ in 2002, SI Resources operates a resource sales segment and a smaller other-business segment.

The resource sales segment covers biofuel manufacturing and sales, peat mining and sales, and bituminous coal mining and sales, with biofuel processed from palm oil and other vegetable/animal fat feedstocks accounting for the bulk of revenue, supplied to domestic power generators.

Its main customers are state-run generators such as Korea Southern Power and Korea Midland Power, with which it has repeatedly signed biofuel supply contracts.

The other-business segment involves purchasing building materials in South Korea and China for export to Sakhalin, Russia, and the company also holds peat mining rights in Sakhalin. Earnings are sensitive to feedstock costs, foreign exchange, and the procurement policies of state-run power generators.

The company recently unveiled a value-up program outlining plans between 2026 and 2030 to add six new business lines — wood pellets, bioethanol, biodiesel, battery recycling, petrochemical facilities, and a digital energy asset platform — transforming it from a single-product biofuel firm into what it calls a seven-pillar integrated energy platform.

It also announced plans to hold regular quarterly CEO/CFO-led investor meetings and dual-language disclosure starting in fiscal year 2027. Execution of this diversification plan, however, remains contingent on the outcome of the company's ongoing governance dispute.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual revenue rose for four consecutive years, from roughly KRW 4.72 billion in 2022 to KRW 7.04 billion in 2023, KRW 10.54 billion in 2024, and KRW 20.85 billion in 2025.

Operating losses, however, continued every year — KRW -2.27 billion in 2022, KRW -8.59 billion in 2023, KRW -3.69 billion in 2024, and KRW -2.90 billion in 2025 — with the operating margin deteriorating to -122.0% in 2023 before narrowing to -35.0% in 2024 and -13.9% in 2025.

Net income attributable to owners was also negative throughout, with the large KRW -9.27 billion loss in 2023 narrowing to KRW -2.10 billion in 2024 and KRW -1.96 billion in 2025. Operating cash flow remained negative across 2022-2025, indicating the core business has yet to generate cash internally.

By quarter, Q2 2025 posted a rare profit (revenue KRW 6.73 billion, operating loss KRW 0.10 billion, net income KRW 0.07 billion), but losses resumed in Q3 2025 (revenue KRW 8.85 billion, operating loss KRW 1.04 billion, net loss KRW 1.01 billion) and Q4 2025 (revenue KRW 4.15 billion, operating loss KRW 0.82 billion, net loss KRW 0.60 billion).

Q1 2026 revenue grew to KRW 9.54 billion with an operating loss of KRW 1.22 billion and a net loss of KRW 1.12 billion, while Q2 2026 revenue fell to KRW 3.76 billion, the operating loss narrowed to KRW 0.36 billion, yet the net loss widened to KRW 1.22 billion — suggesting non-operating items, potentially including litigation-related costs, weighed on the bottom line.

Over the trailing four quarters (Q3 2025-Q2 2026), combined revenue reached about KRW 26.31 billion with an owners' net loss of roughly KRW -3.96 billion, indicating that while the revenue scale has grown, quarterly profitability has not yet stabilized.

05

Industry analysis

South Korea's power-generation biofuel market is driven by state-run generators' demand for renewable blending or substitute fuels, with demand concentrated among a handful of public power companies such as Korea Southern Power and Korea Midland Power.

The company has previously signed a roughly KRW 3.5 billion biofuel supply contract with Korea Midland Power and a roughly KRW 6.4 billion contract with Korea Southern Power.

Feedstock costs for palm oil and other vegetable/animal fats are linked to global commodity prices and foreign exchange rates, creating significant cost volatility, and many supply contracts are short-term or tender-based, contributing to revenue seasonality.

The peat and bituminous coal mining businesses, based on overseas resource rights in areas such as Sakhalin, Russia, are exposed to geopolitical and logistics risks.

Within the KOSDAQ chemicals sector, SI Resources is a micro-cap with revenue far smaller than the sector average, competing less on market share with larger peers and more on securing individual supply contracts with specific power generators.

As a result, the timing of individual contract wins and renewals tends to matter more for its results than broader industry cycle positioning.

06

Outlook

The company has outlined a five-year value-up plan for 2026-2030 to shift from a single-product biofuel business into a seven-pillar integrated energy platform that adds wood pellets, bioethanol, biodiesel, battery recycling, petrochemical facilities, and a digital energy asset platform, while committing to regular quarterly CEO/CFO-led investor meetings and dual-language disclosure from fiscal year 2027.

However, the pace and scope of execution will likely hinge heavily on when the ongoing governance dispute is resolved.

New management took control of the board at an April 2026 extraordinary general meeting, but a court blocked a planned 3.3 million-share third-party share issuance via injunction, and in July the court suspended the entire set of resolutions from that meeting as well as the executive authority of the newly appointed CEO and outside directors pending a final ruling in the underlying lawsuit disputing the meeting's validity.

This triggered a temporary trading halt followed by a limit-down session upon resumption, adding to share price volatility.

At a September 29, 2026 extraordinary general meeting, an article amendment and auditor appointment were approved while a director compensation cap proposal was rejected, showing governance decision-making remains mixed and unsettled.

On the business side, biofuel supply contracts with state-run power generators remain the core revenue base, so whether new or renewed contracts are secured will directly affect near-term results. Meeting the market-capitalization listing requirement also remains an important variable to watch.

Bull factors

Expanding Revenue Base, Narrowing Losses

Revenue grew for four consecutive years, from roughly KRW 4.72 billion in 2022 to about KRW 20.85 billion in 2025, while the operating margin improved from -122.0% in 2023 to -13.9% in 2025. Recurring biofuel supply contracts with state-run power generators have helped expand the revenue base. If this trend continues, there is room for gradual improvement in the profit-and-loss structure.

Stable Public-Sector Demand Base

Key customers are state-run power generators such as Korea Southern Power and Korea Midland Power, which carry relatively higher counterparty reliability than private buyers. A history of recurring biofuel supply contracts suggests similar agreements could continue going forward. While customer concentration is a risk, it can also be read as a relatively dependable transaction base.

Diversification Blueprint Announced

Through its 2026-2030 value-up plan, the company has formally outlined the addition of wood pellets, bioethanol, biodiesel, battery recycling, petrochemical facilities, and a digital energy asset platform. If executed as planned, this could reduce reliance on the single biofuel business line. Execution, however, is contingent on resolving the governance dispute first.

Bear factors

In Q2 2026, revenue declined while the net loss widened, indicating profitability improvement has not yet reached a stable trajectory.

Governance Uncertainty from the Ownership Dispute

Control changed hands at an April 2026 extraordinary general meeting, but a court blocked a planned share issuance and, in July, suspended both the meeting's resolutions and the executive authority of the new CEO and outside directors pending final judgment in the underlying lawsuit.

Trading was halted during this process and the stock hit limit-down upon resumption. A September extraordinary meeting also rejected a director compensation cap proposal, underscoring continued instability in corporate decision-making.

Listing-Maintenance Requirement Risk

KOSDAQ sets KRW 15 billion in market capitalization as the listing-maintenance threshold; falling short for 30 consecutive trading days triggers administrative-issue designation, and failing to meet the threshold within the following 90 trading days can lead to delisting.

The company's market capitalization has a history of trading below this threshold, keeping the risk persistently present. A prior attempt to raise capital via a third-party share issuance was also blocked by a court injunction, suggesting capital-raising options may be constrained.

Risk factors

A lawsuit disputing the validity of the current board's shareholder meeting is pending, and courts have already blocked a share issuance and suspended both the April meeting's resolutions and the new executives' authority.

Depending on the outcome, board composition and strategic direction could change again, creating uncertainty about the continuity of strategy execution. Share price swings have repeatedly accompanied dispute-related disclosures.

Listing Maintenance and Capital-Raising Risk

Market capitalization has a history of trading below the KOSDAQ listing-maintenance threshold, so the possibility of administrative-issue designation or delisting cannot be ruled out.

A prior third-party share issuance attempt was blocked by a court injunction, suggesting future capital raises could face procedural constraints. A 10-for-1 share consolidation has already occurred, so further capital structure adjustments remain a point to watch.

Business and Raw Material Risk

Biofuel revenue is concentrated in a small number of supply contracts with state-run power generators, making results highly sensitive to whether specific contracts are signed or expire.

Feedstocks such as palm oil and other vegetable/animal fats are exposed to global price and currency fluctuations, limiting cost stability. The peat and bituminous coal businesses, based on overseas resource development including Sakhalin, are also exposed to geopolitical and logistics risks.

Overall view

SI Resources saw steady revenue growth and a gradually narrowing operating loss ratio from 2022 through 2025, but net losses persisted throughout the period, and quarterly profitability remains unstable, as seen in Q2 2026 when revenue declined and the net loss widened simultaneously.

At the same time, a legal dispute over control has intensified since April 2026, with courts blocking a share issuance and suspending both the extraordinary general meeting's resolutions and the new management's executive authority pending a final ruling, making governance uncertainty a variable distinct from the earnings trend.

A history of trading below the KOSDAQ market-capitalization listing-maintenance threshold also remains a listing-related risk.

The company has announced a 2026-2030 value-up plan to build a seven-pillar energy platform, but the pace and extent of execution will likely depend heavily on resolution of the governance dispute.
