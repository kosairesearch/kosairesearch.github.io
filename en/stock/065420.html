02

Business structure

SI Resources, founded in 1987 and listed on KOSDAQ in 2002, is a resources development and trading company. Its core business is producing and selling biofuel made from palm oil and animal and vegetable fats, supplied to domestic power generators, alongside coal mining and sales.

A February 2026 report said the company had supply contracts worth KRW 3.5 billion with a Korea Midland Power unit for boiler fuel and KRW 6.4 billion with a Korea South-East Power unit for power generation.

The company attributed 2025 revenue growth to expanded biofuel supply through bids from power generation subsidiaries of Korea Electric Power. In 2024, a KRW 5.3 billion biofuel supply contract with a state-owned power generator was signed under its resource sales segment.

Reports also describe exclusive rights to a peat mining license in Sakhalin, Russia, as part of its renewable energy resource development. Peat is used as livestock bedding, and demand is expected to grow with tighter livestock waste regulations.

Because the feedstock is palm oil and animal and vegetable fats, raw material prices and supply contract pricing shape the cost ratio. With customers concentrated among a few power generators, bid and contract outcomes move quarterly revenue sharply.

04

Earnings analysis

Based on disclosed results, revenue grew from KRW 4.72 billion in 2022 to KRW 7.04 billion in 2023 (up 49.3% year over year), KRW 10.5 billion in 2024 (up 49.6% year over year), and KRW 20.9 billion in 2025 (up 97.9% year over year).

Revenue increased for three consecutive years from 2023 to 2025, and 2025 was the largest year between 2022 and 2025.

Over the same period, the operating loss was KRW 2.27 billion in 2022, KRW 8.59 billion in 2023, KRW 3.69 billion in 2024, and KRW 2.9 billion in 2025, meaning the company posted losses for four consecutive years from 2022 to 2025.

The operating margin was -48.2% in 2022, -122.0% in 2023, -35.0% in 2024, and -13.9% in 2025, so the loss ratio was largest in 2023 and has improved since. The cumulative disclosure summary through September 2024 explained that the operating loss widened because of a worsening cost ratio and higher manufacturing costs.

Net loss attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 0.96 billion in 2022, KRW 9.27 billion in 2023, KRW 2.1 billion in 2024, and KRW 1.96 billion in 2025, and net loss including non-controlling interests was disclosed at the same amounts.

The debt ratio was 13.5% in 2022, 36.9% in 2023, and 95.2% in 2024, then fell to 64.2% in 2025. At the end of 2025, total equity was KRW 10.5 billion and total liabilities were KRW 6.72 billion.

Cash flow from operating activities was an outflow in every year: KRW -4.45 billion in 2022, KRW -1.63 billion in 2023, KRW -0.59 billion in 2024, and KRW -2.92 billion in 2025.

Quarterly revenue was KRW 8.85 billion in Q3 2025, KRW 4.15 billion in Q4 2025 (down 53.0% quarter over quarter), KRW 9.54 billion in Q1 2026 (up 129.7% quarter over quarter), KRW 3.76 billion in Q2 2026 (down 60.6% quarter over quarter), and KRW 3.48 billion in Q3 2026 (down 7.6% quarter over quarter and down 60.7% year over year).

A February 2026 news report projected that Q1 revenue would come close to KRW 10 billion, but the disclosed Q1 revenue is KRW 9.54 billion.

The quarterly operating loss was KRW 1.04 billion in Q3 2025, KRW 0.82 billion in Q4 2025, KRW 1.22 billion in Q1 2026, KRW 0.36 billion in Q2 2026, and KRW 0.84 billion in Q3 2026, marking five consecutive quarters of losses from Q3 2025 to Q3 2026.

05

Industry analysis

Biofuel made from palm oil and animal and vegetable fats is used as power generation fuel, and demand moves with power generator bids, contracts and renewable energy policy.

One analysis site says demand for biofuel is likely to expand under government policies such as a higher Renewable Portfolio Standard, and names this as a key variable for the company's growth.

A 2024 filing summary said geopolitical conflicts such as the Russia-Ukraine war raised energy supply chain risk and drew more interest in the company. In terms of cycle, quarterly revenue moves sharply with contract timing, as seen in the drop from KRW 9.54 billion in Q1 2026 to KRW 3.76 billion in Q2.

For peat, tighter livestock waste regulations are cited as a demand driver. Relative to chemical companies that sell to many customers, SI Resources depends on contracts with a small number of power generators, so bid outcomes add to earnings volatility. Palm oil prices and policy changes could also shift profitability.

At the 39th annual general meeting on March 30, 2026, amendments to the articles and the share consolidation were approved, while part of the director compensation limit was rejected.

A correction filing set the post-consolidation issued share count at 7,748,281 shares and removed the end date of the trading halt and the listing date of the new shares.

On the control dispute, a NBN Media report said a court granted an injunction by the existing management side against new share issuance, which blocked a KRW 1 billion private placement. Per that report, the financing route will shape the capital structure.

The CEO had not responded to the report's inquiries at the time of publication. The next checkpoints are the Q4 revenue and full-year operating margin in the 2026 annual report, supply contract filings, and the outcome of the legal proceedings on control.

Bull factors

Rapid revenue expansion

Revenue grew from KRW 7.04 billion in 2023 to KRW 20.9 billion in 2025, nearly tripling in two years and rising for three consecutive years. The company attributed the increase to expanded biofuel supply through bids from power generation subsidiaries of Korea Electric Power. Biofuel demand is linked to power generation fuel policy, which is cited as the basis for growth.

Improving operating margin

The operating margin improved from -122.0% in 2023 to -35.0% in 2024 and -13.9% in 2025. Losses have narrowed relative to revenue as sales scale has increased. The 2025 operating loss of KRW 2.9 billion was smaller than the KRW 8.59 billion recorded in 2023.

Easing financial burden

The debt ratio fell from 95.2% in 2024 to 64.2% in 2025. With quarterly losses continuing into 2026, how long this cushion holds remains the next thing to watch.

Bear factors

Slowing quarterly revenue

After peaking at KRW 9.54 billion in Q1 2026, revenue fell to KRW 3.76 billion in Q2 and KRW 3.48 billion in Q3, declining for two consecutive quarters. Q3 revenue was down 60.7% from the same quarter a year earlier.

Compared with the near KRW 10 billion Q1 figure forecast in a February 2026 report, quarterly swings have been wide.

Continued losses and cash outflows

The company has posted losses in each of the five quarters from Q3 2025 through Q3 2026. Operating cash flow was negative in every year from 2022 through 2025, and was KRW -2.92 billion in 2025.

Governance and listing requirements

Reports described a control dispute and a court injunction, and a caution-designation notice was issued in July 2026. Market capitalization is below the KRW 15 billion level cited for listing maintenance. The share consolidation reduced the share count, but meeting the market capitalization test is a separate issue.

Risk factors

Governance and control

Legal disputes over control are continuing. According to a NBN Media report, the Seoul Western District Court granted an injunction filed by the existing management side against new share issuance, blocking a KRW 1 billion private placement.

The March 2026 share consolidation drew approval of 38.7% of total issued voting shares, and 99.9% of the votes exercised. Financing methods and management structure remain undetermined, leaving decision-making uncertain.

Listing maintenance requirements

A February 2026 report said KOSDAQ companies are tested against a KRW 15 billion market capitalization threshold, with a shortfall lasting 30 trading days leading to management-issue designation and failure to recover within 90 trading days leading to delisting.

Market capitalization is share count times price, so the consolidation alone does not meet the test. Compliance depends on share price movement and the outcome of any capital raise.

Dependence on demand and policy

Revenue is tied to contracts with a few power generators and to policy-driven demand. After KRW 9.54 billion in Q1 2026, revenue fell to KRW 3.76 billion in Q2 and KRW 3.48 billion in Q3, with Q3 down 60.7% year on year.

Because biofuel demand depends on renewable policy and bid results, any gap in contracts could widen quarterly swings.
