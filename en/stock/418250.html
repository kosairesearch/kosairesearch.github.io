02

Business structure

SecuLetter is a cybersecurity solutions company founded in September 2015 that listed on KOSDAQ in August 2023 through the technology special listing route.

Its core products are the MARS line of malware analysis and reverse engineering systems and the SLE and SLF product families, and since late 2022 it has launched CDR Cloud and Email Security Cloud services on AWS Marketplace.

According to a 2026 second-quarter revenue breakdown compiled by a third-party data service, product sales account for the largest share, with other products at 72%, SLF at 8%, services such as maintenance at 7%, merchandise at 6% and SLE at 5%.

The company says it has supply records with public institutions including the Ministry of Culture, Sports and Tourism and the National Health Insurance Service, and with financial firms including Korea Investment & Securities, KB Securities and BNK Busan Bank.

Sales run through distributors, and the 2023 audit disclaimer stemmed from recognizing revenue when products were delivered to distributors rather than to end users, which the Financial Supervisory Service and the auditor said should be the recognition point.

The company said it hired specialists to tighten revenue recognition afterward. Its technology has been described as among the best in the industry in reverse engineering.

However, revenue was KRW 1.17 billion in 2025, so the business remains small and competes with larger security vendors for public and financial contracts.

04

Earnings analysis

On the disclosed annual figures, revenue fell from KRW 2.54 billion in 2022 to KRW 2.14 billion in 2023 (down 15.7% year on year), KRW 1.41 billion in 2024 (down 34.1%) and KRW 1.17 billion in 2025 (down 17.2%), a decline for three consecutive years from 2023 through 2025.

Of the 2022 to 2025 span, 2022 was the largest year and 2025 the smallest. Operating losses were KRW 5.8 billion in 2022, KRW 5.91 billion in 2023, KRW 7.26 billion in 2024 and KRW 4.29 billion in 2025, a loss in each of the four years from 2022 through 2025.

The operating margin moved from -228.2% in 2022 to -275.9% in 2023 and -514.5% in 2024, then improved to -366.9% in 2025.

Net losses attributable to controlling shareholders followed a similar path, at KRW 5.72 billion, KRW 5.79 billion, KRW 9.44 billion and KRW 4.29 billion, and one press report described the 2024 net loss as the largest on record.

According to a Digital Today report on the 2025 filing, the company attributed the 2025 improvement to a KRW 700 million decline in non-recurring commission fees for legal and accounting work and a KRW 2.2 billion decline in selling, general and administrative expenses, while revenue fell on a changed operating environment.

Operating cash flow was negative in all four years, at minus KRW 4.9 billion in 2022, minus KRW 5.32 billion in 2023, minus KRW 6.68 billion in 2024 and minus KRW 6.42 billion in 2025.

On a quarterly basis, revenue rose from KRW 163 million in the third quarter of 2025 to KRW 680 million in the fourth quarter, while the operating loss widened to KRW 738 million.

Revenue then reached KRW 1.3 billion in the first quarter of 2026 with an operating loss of KRW 242 million, and KRW 1.6 billion in the second quarter with an operating loss of KRW 618 million.

In the third quarter revenue was KRW 833 million, down 48.0% from the prior quarter but up 410.8% from a year earlier, and the operating loss was KRW 105 million. One press report tied the revenue decline to contracts under discussion during the trading suspension that fell through.

At the end of 2025 total equity was KRW 3.65 billion against total liabilities of KRW 1.47 billion, and the debt ratio fell from 58.4% in 2024 to 40.4% in 2025.

Industry analysis

SecuLetter's main customers are public institutions and financial firms, so cybersecurity budgets and procurement timing shape its revenue. A Daily Secu report said malware detection technology is drawing attention as Korea shifts to the National Network Security Framework (N2SF).

Against that, revenue declined for three consecutive years from 2023 through 2025, and one press report attributed the decline to contracts under discussion during the trading suspension that fell through. In early April 2026 the company disclosed a supply contract worth KRW 800 million.

In a FnGuide comparison based on 2025 results, the KOSDAQ IT services sector as a whole posted an operating profit, while SecuLetter recorded an operating loss.

In domestic security software, established vendors such as Ahnlab compete for public and financial projects, so the pace of new customer wins is a key variable. The company has been praised for its reverse engineering capability, but whether that translates into more contracts will show up in full-year 2026 revenue.

Quarterly revenue in 2026 has recovered from the late-2025 level, but the quarter-to-quarter swings are large, so a clearer read requires the full-year figures. Its position in the cycle is best judged against the pace of recovery in public-sector security spending.

Bear factors

Prolonged trading suspension

SecuLetter's trading has been suspended since April 5, 2024, and a report on an October 2026 share issuance still described the suspension. After the 2025 annual report received a clean opinion, a resumption decision had been expected around June 2026, yet the suspension continued beyond that point. Resumption requires proof of financial capacity and improved results.

Thin balance sheet and cash outflows

Total equity was KRW 3.65 billion at the end of 2025, with a debt ratio of 40.4%. Operating cash flow was an outflow of KRW 6.42 billion in 2025, a figure larger than year-end total equity. Year-end cash was reported at KRW 600 million, and the auditor kept a going-concern emphasis.

Accounting sanctions and repeated share issuance

In September 2024 the Securities and Futures Commission found that sales had been overstated through false tax invoices, referred the company and executives to prosecutors and imposed a KRW 2.384 billion fine. The chief executive received a recommendation for dismissal and a six-month suspension of duties.

In 2026 a KRW 3 billion capital increase was postponed three times, and in October a KRW 700 million allotment was decided.

Risk factors

Listing maintenance and trading resumption risk

While trading remains suspended, liquidity in the shares is limited, and the listing review requires proof of financial stability and improved results. The timing of resumption could slip if capital strengthening and evidence of better results are delayed.

Even with a clean opinion, the going-concern emphasis remains, so it is hard to say the review standards have been met.

Funding and share dilution risk

A KRW 3 billion investment was attracted in February 2026 and a KRW 700 million third-party allotment followed in October 2026. Shares issued in the February capital increase are subject to a one-year lock-up.

Operating cash outflows have run since 2022, so further raises are possible, and each issuance dilutes existing holders.

Earnings volatility and channel risk

Revenue fell for three consecutive years from 2023 through 2025, and third-quarter 2026 revenue was down 48.0% from the prior quarter. Because sales run through distributors, the timing of supply and the revenue recognition basis could again become an audit issue. The 2023 disclaimer of opinion had the same revenue recognition timing at its center.

Still, the 2025 operating loss of KRW 4.29 billion was smaller than the KRW 7.26 billion in 2024, and losses narrowed in the first and third quarters of 2026.
