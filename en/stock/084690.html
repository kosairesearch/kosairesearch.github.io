Report overview

A holding company tied to subsidiary earnings, after a return to loss

Daesang Holdings is a holding company whose main income comes from dividends and trademark royalties paid by its subsidiaries. Its 2025 operating profit rose for a second straight year, yet net income attributable to controlling shareholders swung to a loss, and the cumulative loss over the last four quarters reached KRW 75.7 billion.

02

Business structure

Daesang Holdings was founded in 2005 through a spin-off from Daesang and operates as a pure holding company. Its main operating income consists of dividends from subsidiaries and trademark usage fees, and its profit is strongly influenced by the operations and economic cycles of those subsidiaries.

The business report filed in March 2026 shows a current-period revenue of KRW 38,068 million for the holding company's own business segment. The same report lists 10 domestic subsidiaries. The core subsidiary, Daesang, is held 39.28% directly by Daesang Holdings and 43.72% including related parties.

The other subsidiaries operate in IT, communications, construction, wellness, networks, cell technology and pet products, and in most cases the holding company owns 100% or a majority stake.

Daesang's own business is split between food and materials, and its core food lineup includes kimchi, seaweed, fermented sauces and seasonings. In materials, the company has signaled a push into higher-value ingredients and alternative sugars.

Overseas, it has entities in the United States, Japan, Vietnam and Indonesia, and the US holding company DSF DE oversees four food subsidiaries. Because the holding company's income depends on subsidiary results, swings at the subsidiary level flow directly into its own earnings.

04

Earnings analysis

On a disclosed basis, revenue rose from KRW 5.23 trillion in 2022 to KRW 5.26 trillion in 2023 (up 0.6%), KRW 5.36 trillion in 2024 (up 1.9%) and KRW 5.63 trillion in 2025 (up 5.1%), growing for three consecutive years from 2023 through 2025.

Operating profit fell from KRW 143.8 billion in 2022 to KRW 114.6 billion in 2023 (down 20.3%), then rose to KRW 184 billion in 2024 (up 60.6%) and KRW 199.8 billion in 2025 (up 8.6%), a second straight year of growth.

The operating margin was 2.8% in 2022, 2.2% in 2023, 3.4% in 2024 and 3.6% in 2025, the highest of the four years.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders moved from KRW 32.2 billion in 2022 to KRW 21.1 billion in 2023 (down 34.5%) and KRW 20.4 billion in 2024 (down 3.5%), then turned into a net loss of KRW 106.9 billion in 2025.

Net income including non-controlling interests was a loss of KRW 293.2 billion in 2025, and the gap with the controlling-shareholder figure shows that losses at the non-controlling level were large.

Operating profit rose in the same year net income deteriorated, which points to a wide gap between the operating and net profit lines. The debt-to-equity ratio edged up from 158.2% in 2022 to 164.4% in 2024 and then jumped to 236.3% in 2025.

At the end of 2025, total equity stood at KRW 1.31 trillion, equity attributable to controlling shareholders at KRW 618.3 billion, and total liabilities at KRW 3.09 trillion.

Operating cash flow went from a deficit of KRW 132.9 billion in 2022 to positive figures of KRW 322.4 billion in 2023, KRW 178.3 billion in 2024 and KRW 242.9 billion in 2025.

On a quarterly basis, the third quarter of 2025 posted revenue of KRW 1.49 trillion, operating profit of KRW 69.3 billion and controlling-shareholder net income of KRW 20.7 billion.

In the fourth quarter of 2025, revenue fell to KRW 1.35 trillion (down 9.2% from the prior quarter), operating profit to KRW 28 billion (down 59.6%), and the company recorded a controlling-shareholder net loss of KRW 138.7 billion, a swing into loss.

In the first quarter of 2026, revenue was KRW 1.43 trillion, operating profit KRW 78.1 billion (up 179.3% from the prior quarter) and controlling-shareholder net income KRW 24.5 billion, a return to profit.

In the second quarter of 2026, revenue was KRW 1.5 trillion, operating profit KRW 59.4 billion (down 23.9%) and controlling-shareholder net income KRW 14 billion.

In the third quarter of 2026, revenue was KRW 1.56 trillion (up 5.0% year on year), operating profit KRW 70.3 billion (up 1.5% year on year) and controlling-shareholder net income KRW 24.5 billion (up 18.4% year on year). The cumulative controlling-shareholder net loss over the last four quarters totals KRW 75.7 billion.

05

Industry analysis

Because a holding company's earnings track its subsidiaries directly, the industry picture centers on Daesang's food and materials businesses.

In its preliminary third-quarter 2026 consolidated results released on November 16, 2026, Daesang said food sales held steady as domestic consumption remained weak for an extended period. It said overseas food sales grew on entry into local distribution channels and new bases.

Materials, however, saw operating profit decline due to higher raw-material costs and rising export-related expenses. The same release put the third-quarter operating margin at about 3.5%, down 0.6 percentage points from 4.1% a year earlier.

On a cumulative basis for January to September 2026, revenue was KRW 3.45 trillion, up 3.1% year on year, and operating profit was KRW 144.7 billion, up 0.4%. Food revenue grew, but cost pressure in materials weighed on overall profitability.

Several listed companies share the holding-company classification, including SK Square, SK, POSCO Holdings, Meritz Financial Group, Hanjinkal, LG, HD Hyundai, Doosan, GS Holdings, LS, CJ, Lotte, and AMOREPACIFIC Holdings. Direct comparisons are difficult because their subsidiary mix and business weights differ.

For Daesang Holdings, the key point for its place in the sector is that its income relies on dividends and royalties from subsidiaries rather than on its own operations.

Outlook

Daesang plans to raise the quality and technical competitiveness of core food products such as kimchi, seaweed, fermented sauces and seasonings, and to grow business-to-business and online channels.

It also intends to expand a portfolio centered on health and convenience and to widen growth channels including B2B and online. Overseas, the plan is to keep expanding through local distribution channels and new bases. In materials, the company has said it will strengthen higher-value ingredient businesses.

Whether raw-material cost increases and export-related expenses continue in materials has not yet been shown in quarterly results.

At the holding-company level, third-quarter 2026 operating profit of KRW 70.3 billion rose 1.5% year on year, but whether controlling-shareholder net income stays positive varies sharply by quarter: KRW 24.5 billion in the first quarter of 2026, KRW 14 billion in the second and KRW 24.5 billion in the third.

The reason for the KRW 138.7 billion controlling-shareholder net loss in the fourth quarter of 2025 and whether it recurs will need to be checked in the next annual report and its notes. With the debt-to-equity ratio at 236.3% in 2025, the borrowing structure and equity changes also warrant attention.

The next check point for results is the operating margin and the controlling-shareholder net income trend in the next business report.

Overseas food business expanding channels

The group has entities in the United States, Japan, Vietnam and Indonesia, giving it a broad sales base. Overseas growth while domestic demand is weak can diversify subsidiary earnings.

Operating cash flow stays positive

Operating cash flow was positive for three straight years, at KRW 322.4 billion in 2023, KRW 178.3 billion in 2024 and KRW 242.9 billion in 2025. This followed a deficit of KRW 132.9 billion in 2022, so cash generation has recovered. It provides a basis for judging capacity to pay dividends and repay debt.

Cost pressure in the materials business

Because the holding company's income depends on subsidiary profits, cost pressure in materials also weighs on its own results.

Higher financial leverage

The debt-to-equity ratio rose from 158.2% in 2022 to 164.4% in 2024 and jumped to 236.3% in 2025. Total liabilities at the end of 2025 were KRW 3.09 trillion, above total equity of KRW 1.31 trillion. Financial structure

Weak domestic demand and subsidiary dependence

Overseas growth may not fully offset limited domestic food demand, which could cap overall revenue growth.

Overall view

Daesang Holdings is a holding company whose main income consists of dividends and trademark income from subsidiaries. Its disclosed figures show revenue rising for three straight years through 2025 and operating profit growing for two, so the operating-level trend is recovering.

At the same time, the company swung to a controlling-shareholder net loss of KRW 106.9 billion in 2025 and its debt-to-equity ratio rose to 236.3%.

Quarterly results returned to profit in the first quarter of 2026 and stayed positive in the second and third quarters, but the cumulative loss over the last four quarters is KRW 75.7 billion.
