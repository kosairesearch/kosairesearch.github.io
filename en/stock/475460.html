02

Business structure

MeatBox Global operates the B2B livestock direct-trading platform MeatBox, connecting sellers such as importers and primary wholesalers with buyers such as restaurants and butcher shops.

It earns revenue from two businesses: brokerage, where it collects fees on transactions, and product sales, where it sells directly imported meat and private-label (PB) items such as its Dangdanghan pork and beef brands.

In the first half of 2026, the revenue mix was 22.7% brokerage, 25.8% PB products, 48.9% non-PB products and 2.6% other, according to Value Finder's analysis. In 2024, product sales were KRW 76.4 billion and brokerage fee revenue was KRW 31.5 billion, based on a Mirae Asset Securities 3Q25 report table.

The brokerage fee rate rose from 7.1% in 2021 to 9.5% in 2025, and the repurchase rate was reported at 83.3% for 2025.

In April 2025 the company acquired a stake in Meatgram (formerly Stakes) to build meat slicing and packaging capability, and its consolidated subsidiaries grew to three with the establishment of MeatMatch Loan.

It also introduced 'Yukmart 2.0', a service dedicated to restaurant operators, extended order cutoff to 10 p.m., and added in-refrigerator delivery. The company says moving livestock trade online reduces inefficiencies in the existing multi-stage distribution structure. The company is classified under IT and software, but its operations are closer to food distribution.

04

Earnings analysis

On a disclosed basis, revenue was KRW 66.9 billion in 2023, KRW 110.3 billion in 2024 (up 64.8% year on year) and KRW 135.6 billion in 2025 (up 23.0%). Revenue rose for two consecutive years from 2024 through 2025, and the company said 2024 was its highest annual revenue since founding.

Operating profit was KRW 2.6 billion, KRW 3.12 billion (up 19.9%) and KRW 2.91 billion (down 6.8%) over the same period. Revenue grew while operating margin fell from 3.9% in 2023 to 2.8% in 2024 and 2.1% in 2025.

For 2024, the company attributed the margin decline to additional hiring and facility investment tied to preparing a new business model. In 2025, the Meatgram stake acquisition expanded the group and analysts linked the profit pressure to one-time costs and higher headcount and marketing spending.

Profit attributable to owners of the parent was KRW 3.5 billion in 2023, KRW 1.83 billion in 2024 (down 47.8%) and KRW 1.74 billion in 2025 (down 4.9%), declining for two consecutive years from 2024 through 2025.

In its 2024 results announcement, a company official said an accounting impairment on logistics facility development had been recognized first, and that talks with Korea Expressway Corporation on a private-investment project were ongoing.

Net income including non-controlling interests was KRW 3.49 billion, KRW 1.12 billion and KRW 1.74 billion. The debt-to-equity ratio was 176.6%, 192.3% and 161.9%. At end-2025, total equity was KRW 37.7 billion, of which KRW 37.3 billion belonged to owners of the parent, and total liabilities were KRW 61 billion.

Operating cash flow was KRW 2.59 billion, KRW 8.36 billion and KRW 1.53 billion. Quarterly revenue went from KRW 41.5 billion in 3Q25 to KRW 34.2 billion in 4Q25, then rose for three consecutive quarters to KRW 34.3 billion, KRW 45.5 billion and KRW 54.2 billion in 3Q26.

Operating profit peaked at KRW 2.03 billion in 4Q25 and then fell for three consecutive quarters to KRW 1.59 billion, KRW 871 million and KRW 700 million. Profit attributable to owners of the parent over the last four quarters totaled KRW 2.92 billion.

05

Industry analysis

Livestock distribution is the market in which MeatBox operates, and the company's April 2026 investor relations material presented channel sizes.

According to that material, large supermarkets were KRW 7.7 trillion (27.6%), butcher shops KRW 7.2 trillion (26.0%), restaurants KRW 6.1 trillion (22.0%), general supermarkets KRW 2.8 trillion (10.2%) and institutional catering KRW 2.0 trillion (7.2%).

The company identifies restaurants and butcher shops as its main buyer groups. The same material expected livlivestock prices to remain at a high level in 2026. On tariffs, it listed a 40.0% beef tariff and said that if talks on beef imports during the Korea-Brazil summit progress, tariff reductions are expected.

Value Finder noted that the Korea-US FTA preferential rate for US beef fell to 0% in 2026, which it said would improve the price competitiveness of directly imported US products.

In competition, livestock trade is split across several stages, including large supermarkets, general wholesalers and butcher shops, and MeatBox uses an online trading platform to shorten those steps.

The brokerage fee rate rising from 7.1% in 2021 to 9.5% in 2025 shows that the platform's share of transaction value has grown. Whether online direct trade can displace the pricing and logistics advantages of existing channels still needs to be confirmed through reported results.

06

Outlook

In its April 2026 investor relations material, the company projected that cumulative operating revenue would rise 23.0% year on year. Nine-month 2026 revenue, however, grew 32% year on year, which the company reported in its quarterly report and which exceeds that projection.

For the second half, the company plans to expand its cut-meat range centered on Yukmart and widen its product categories to add new customers and trading volume.

Overseas, it signed a memorandum of understanding with Thien A, a Vietnamese distributor, and aims to set up a joint venture (JV) in 2027 after validating the market through exports.

In Taiwan, it is working with Natural Meat Corporation, and in the United States it is strengthening local direct sourcing and pursuing a local beef distribution subsidiary. On shareholder returns, the 2025 year-end dividend was the first cash dividend since listing, with a consolidated payout ratio of 32.3%.

In September 2026 the company carried out a bonus issue of two new shares per existing share. The CEO bought 10,500 shares on the open market in three transactions in April 2026, and on September 3, 2026 announced a further purchase plan worth KRW 1.0 billion.

Because the bonus issue increased the share count, per-share metrics are not easy to compare with earlier disclosures. The next checkpoint for the overseas business is whether the 2027 JV is formed and what export results are reported.

08

Bull factors

Quarterly revenue up three straight quarters

Revenue bottomed at KRW 34.2 billion in 4Q25, then rose to KRW 34.3 billion, KRW 45.5 billion and KRW 54.2 billion across the next three quarters. Third-quarter revenue grew 30.4% year on year. The company attributes the growth to using platform customer data to select products and expanding its PB range.

Alongside larger transaction volumes, the brokerage fee rate rising to 9.5% in 2025 supports the income structure.

Improving product margins

First-half 2026 product gross profit reached KRW 3.8 billion, up 57.1% year on year, and gross margin rose from 5.8% to 6.4%, per Value Finder. Value Finder viewed positively that both sales scale and unit profitability improved together. It also cited operating leverage from rising brokerage fee rates.

Shareholder returns and CEO share purchases

The company paid its first cash dividend since listing for the 2025 fiscal year, with a consolidated payout ratio of 32.3%. CEO Kim Ki-bong bought 10,500 shares on the open market in April 2026 and announced a further KRW 1.0 billion purchase plan on September 3, 2026.

09

Bear factors

Operating profit down three straight quarters

Operating profit peaked at KRW 2.03 billion in 4Q25 and then fell to KRW 1.59 billion, KRW 871 million and KRW 700 million over the next three quarters. Third-quarter 2026 operating profit was down 25.7% year on year. Operating margin declined from 3.9% in 2023 to 2.1% in 2025. Profit has not kept pace with revenue growth, which is the core bear argument.

Thin margins from a product-led mix

Non-PB product sales made up 48.9% of first-half 2026 revenue, and product gross margin was 6.4%. Product sales are exposed to inventory and purchase price swings and carry thinner margins than brokerage fees. Operating cash flow fell from KRW 8.36 billion in 2024 to KRW 1.53 billion in 2025.

Balance sheet burden and investment-stage projects

Total liabilities at end-2025 were KRW 61 billion, above total equity of KRW 37.7 billion. The debt-to-equity ratio of 161.9% is lower than 192.3% in 2024 but remains high. The Vietnam JV targeted for 2027 and the planned US distribution subsidiary are at stages requiring investment and staffing.

10

Risk factors

Policy and tariff risk

The 40.0% beef tariff listed for 2026 and the timing of import talks and tariff cuts are policy variables. The FTA rate cut helps directly imported US products but can affect other origins differently. Changes in tariff policy could move the product sales mix and margins.

Input cost and livestock price risk

The investor relations material expects livestock prices to stay high in 2026. High raw material prices can compress margins between selling prices and buyer demand. As product sales grow as a share of revenue, price swings flow more directly into gross margin.

Earnings volatility and new-business execution risk

In 2024 preparation costs for a new business model weighed on margins, and in 2025 costs tied to consolidating subsidiaries had an effect. An accounting impairment on logistics facilities and ongoing talks on a private-investment project with Korea Expressway Corporation remain open items.

The overseas JV and US distribution subsidiary are still at the planning stage, so timing and results could change.

Overall view

MeatBox Global is a B2B livestock direct-trading platform that grew revenue from KRW 66.9 billion in 2023 to KRW 135.6 billion in 2025. Nine-month 2026 revenue rose 32% year on year, and the company described third-quarter revenue of KRW 54.2 billion as its highest quarterly figure.

However, operating margin fell from 3.9% in 2023 to 2.1% in 2025, and operating profit has declined for three consecutive quarters since 4Q25. Turning revenue growth into profit is the central issue.

A higher product sales share and rising brokerage fee rates are structural positives, but a product-led mix carries thin margins and exposure to inventory and price swings. Shareholder returns have included the first cash dividend since listing, a bonus issue and CEO share purchases.

Total liabilities exceed total equity, so the funding burden of overseas expansion and logistics investment should be considered alongside results. The next checkpoints are preliminary full-year 2026 results, the operating margin in the 2026 annual report, and whether the 2027 joint venture is formed.
