CosNine: Trading Halted at a Delisting Crossroads

CosNine faces delisting proceedings triggered by a former CEO's large-scale embezzlement and an auditor's disclaimer of opinion, with its continued listing now hinging on a court ruling over an injunction the company itself filed.

  1. 1

    In 2024, embezzlement and breach of trust by the former CEO and others totaling roughly KRW 9.08 billion, equal to 26.19% of equity, triggered a listing eligibility review and trading halt.

  2. 2

    The KOSDAQ Market Committee voted to delist the company twice, in December 2024 and November 2025, and each time the company bought time through appeals and improvement periods.

  3. 3

    The trading halt was lifted on November 28, 2025 with liquidation trading set for December 2-10 and a delisting date of December 11, but the company filed an injunction on December 1 that suspended the process.

  4. 4

  5. 5

02

Business structure

CosNine was founded in 2001 and listed on KOSDAQ in 2005 as a cosmetics manufacturer, having changed its corporate name several times, including ChamTech, Samwoo EMS, and Nine Complex, before adopting its current name.

Its core business is producing the full range of cosmetics products for client brands through OEM, ODM, and OCM manufacturing methods, covering basic and color cosmetics as well as hand sanitizers.

The company operates three research centers to build product development, design, and marketing consulting capabilities, and has invested in quality assurance infrastructure and a CEP system that lets clients monitor the manufacturing process in real time.

Most of its revenue comes from cosmetics product sales, with its Shanghai-based subsidiary, COSNINE (SHANGHAI) CO., LTD., handling local distribution and sales in China.

Beyond cosmetics, the company has a subsidiary engaged in waste battery recycling and secondary battery material distribution, reflecting past attempts at business diversification, though how much this contributes to actual revenue could not be specifically confirmed through search.

Historically, CosNine's revenue plunged from about KRW 184.4 billion in 2017 to KRW 7.9 billion in 2018 and has failed to recover meaningfully since, staying in the KRW 9 billion to KRW 21 billion range between 2019 and 2023.

The cosmetics OEM/ODM industry depends on outsourcing demand from small and mid-sized domestic and overseas brands, and unlike larger ODM players with scale economies, CosNine's revenue base is very small.

04

Earnings analysis

Confirmed annual results show revenue rising 6.8% from about KRW 8.998 billion in 2022 to KRW 9.608 billion in 2023, while the operating loss narrowed slightly from KRW 7.694 billion to KRW 6.529 billion, and the net loss attributable to owners shrank from KRW 10.768 billion to KRW 5.878 billion.

The operating margin remained deeply negative at -85.5% in 2022 and -67.9% in 2023, though the direction was toward improvement.

Notably, equity attributable to owners actually increased from KRW 23.392 billion at the end of 2022 to KRW 34.666 billion at the end of 2023, likely reflecting capital raises or convertible-bond-related adjustments, though the specific details could not be confirmed through search.

The debt ratio fell sharply from 105.9% in 2022 to 50.3% in 2023, suggesting an apparent improvement in the balance sheet, but operating cash flow remained negative in both years, at -KRW 5.561 billion in 2022 and -KRW 7.827 billion in 2023, indicating the core business continued to fail to generate cash.

Moving to quarterly data, first-quarter 2025 revenue was KRW 1.675 billion with an operating loss of KRW 2.356 billion, while the net loss attributable to owners widened sharply to KRW 15.762 billion, an unusually large figure compared with net losses of KRW 0.160 billion and KRW 1.489 billion in the second and third quarters of the same year, possibly reflecting one-off items related to the former CEO's embezzlement case or convertible bond fair-value adjustments, though the details were not confirmed through search.

Fourth-quarter 2025 figures have not been disclosed, and first-quarter 2026 revenue, operating profit, and net income were all recorded as zero, suggesting that normal business activity was not reported.

According to FnGuide's company guide data, cumulative revenue for the first three quarters of 2025 fell 31.5% year over year, the operating loss narrowed 12.1%, and the net loss widened 9.4%, with revenue declining amid accelerating trend shifts in the cosmetics market and rising domestic outsourcing by global brands, while cost cuts narrowed the operating loss.

Overall, losses have remained structural on an extremely small revenue base, and the recorded zeros for the first quarter of 2026 are consistent with a business that is not operating normally amid the ongoing trading halt and delisting proceedings.

05

Industry analysis

The cosmetics OEM/ODM industry has been growing, led by large players riding expanding global K-beauty demand, but very small companies with a tiny revenue base like CosNine often lose out when brand owners allocate outsourcing volume.

FnGuide identified accelerating trend shifts in the cosmetics market and rising domestic outsourcing by global brands as the industry backdrop, which suggests that while the overall pie is growing, this can actually squeeze weaker small players.

Meanwhile, CosNine's situation is driven far more by regulatory changes to the delisting system than by industry factors.

In February 2026, the Financial Services Commission and the Korea Exchange announced reform measures for the swift and stringent removal of insolvent companies, proposing to progressively raise the KOSDAQ market-cap delisting threshold from KRW 4 billion in 2025 to KRW 15 billion in 2026, KRW 20 billion in 2027, and KRW 30 billion in 2028 while shortening improvement periods, accelerating the pace of delistings.

Indeed, KOSDAQ delisting decisions totaled 38 in 2025, a sharp increase from 8 in 2023 and 20 in 2024, and this trend continued into 2026.

As more companies like CosNine filed injunctions against delisting decisions, a group of firms with prolonged trading halts emerged; as of March 2026, 11 companies remained halted pending injunction lawsuits, including Hanchang, RF Semi, BF Labs, Sunshine Food, Kainos Med, Noble MNB, DA Technology, Kodaco, CosNine, Olipass, and Terra Science.

However, according to regulatory data, only 2 out of 85 delisting-related injunction lawsuits over the past five years (2021-2025) were granted, indicating that the statistical odds of an injunction ultimately succeeding and preserving a listing are relatively low.

06

Outlook

CosNine's future path is likely to be shaped far more by the outcome of legal and administrative procedures than by business prospects.

The company initially averted a delisting crisis when the Seoul Bankruptcy Court opened rehabilitation proceedings in November 2024, but it voluntarily filed to terminate the rehabilitation process in August 2025, reportedly after judging real rehabilitation prospects to be low.

An improvement period tied to an audit opinion disclaimer was subsequently granted and ended in October 2025, after which the exchange decided on the 10th of last month (October 2025) that CosNine met the delisting criteria, noting that the company had failed to improve enough to maintain its listing despite an eight-month improvement period granted in February of that year.

The trading halt was lifted on November 28, 2025, with liquidation trading set to run for seven trading days from December 2 to December 10, and a delisting date fixed at December 11, but the company then filed an injunction with the Seoul Southern District Court on December 1 seeking to suspend the effect of the delisting decision.

In response, the exchange stated that the scheduled delisting procedures, including liquidation trading, would be suspended until the court's decision was confirmed, and this state has persisted.

In other words, the key thing to watch going forward is not new product launches or order growth but whether the court grants or dismisses the injunction, and whether liquidation trading and delisting resume as a result.

No specific guidance or new contract news regarding a recovery in the core cosmetics business or a meaningful contribution from the secondary battery material distribution diversification could be confirmed through search, suggesting that procedural risk currently overwhelms business factors.

08

Bull factors

Signs of balance-sheet improvement

The debt ratio fell sharply to 50.3% in 2023 from 105.9% in 2022, and equity attributable to owners grew from about KRW 23.3 billion to KRW 34.7 billion, which can be read as an attempt at structural improvement through capital raises. Over the same period, both the operating loss and net loss narrowed in size.

However, it is worth distinguishing whether this improvement stemmed from operations or from the effects of capital raising.

Narrowing quarterly net losses

The fact that the net loss attributable to owners, which reached about KRW 15.76 billion in the first quarter of 2025, shrank sharply to about KRW 0.16 billion in the second quarter and KRW 1.49 billion in the third quarter suggests the first-quarter loss may have been closer to a one-off item.

Revenue itself remains small, but there are signs of reduced earnings volatility. However, whether this trend continued is unclear since fourth-quarter and later data are unavailable.

Business diversification attempt

Beyond its core cosmetics business, CosNine has a subsidiary engaged in waste battery recycling and secondary battery material distribution, giving it a separate business line that could help offset weakness in cosmetics.

The secondary battery material distribution segment is classified as a growing industry, which could represent a potential opportunity. However, how much this business actually contributes to revenue and profit could not be specifically confirmed through search, warranting a cautious approach.

Bear factors

Delisting risk is a present reality, not a tail risk

CosNine has already been voted for delisting twice by the KOSDAQ Market Committee, and trading currently remains halted while the court decides on an injunction the company itself filed.

According to regulatory data, only 2 of 85 delisting-related injunction lawsuits over the past five years were granted, meaning there is a non-trivial statistical chance the injunction will be dismissed and liquidation trading/delisting procedures will resume.

This shows that, independent of business prospects, the company's very survival as a listed entity is uncertain.

Structural operating losses and going-concern uncertainty

Under confirmed financials, operating loss margins reached 60-85% of revenue in both 2022 and 2023, and operating cash flow remained negative in both years, indicating the core business failed to generate cash.

The auditor issued a disclaimer of opinion citing uncertainty about the company's ability to continue as a going concern, leaving questions about the reliability and sustainability of the financial statements themselves.

The fact that first-quarter 2026 revenue and profit figures were all recorded as zero also suggests the business is not operating normally.

Repeated governance failures

The roughly KRW 9.08 billion embezzlement and breach of trust by the former CEO and others amounted to 26.19% of equity, an event that went beyond mere underperformance to undermine trust in the internal control system itself.

This was compounded by disclosure reliability issues, including designation as an unfaithful disclosure company for failing to state material information in response to inquiries.

The fact that the company itself abandoned rehabilitation proceedings and that sale attempts produced no meaningful results points to limited internal capacity for normalization.

10

Risk factors

Delisting and exchange regulatory risk

If the court dismisses the injunction, the suspended liquidation trading and delisting procedures could resume immediately. Regulators are overhauling the system to accelerate the removal of insolvent KOSDAQ companies, reducing the scope for buying time through improvement periods or appeals compared to the past. This is a structural risk that can materialize independent of business performance.

Financial soundness and going-concern risk

The company continues to see cash outflows from operations, and the auditor's disclaimer of opinion related to going-concern issues remains unresolved. If the audit opinion is not converted to unqualified in the future, additional delisting causes could recur. If capital raises are repeated, dilution of existing shareholders' equity value cannot be ruled out.

Governance and internal control risk

The past embezzlement and breach-of-trust case involving former management, along with the unfaithful disclosure designation, illustrate weaknesses in internal controls. Given the history of largest-shareholder changes and related-party transactions, the possibility of governance issues recurring cannot be ruled out. Investors need to remain cautious about the reliability of disclosed information itself.

What to watch next

  1. When the court's ruling on the injunction is disclosed (timing not yet fixed)

    If the injunction is dismissed, the suspended liquidation trading and delisting procedures could resume immediately; if granted, the listing-maintenance review could continue, making this the single most important item to monitor.

  2. Around the statutory filing deadline for the third-quarter 2026 report (typically mid-November)

    It is worth checking whether the obligation to file periodic reports continues even under the trading halt, and whether revenue and profit/loss data are disclosed normally.

  3. At the time of the next regular audit report disclosure

    Whether the going-concern-related disclaimer of opinion is converted to an unqualified opinion is a key indicator for assessing the likelihood of maintaining the listing.

  4. When the KOSDAQ Market Committee's follow-up review schedule is disclosed

    After the court decision, it will be necessary to check how the exchange readjusts the liquidation trading schedule or whether it grants any further improvement period.

12

Overall view

CosNine is a company undergoing delisting proceedings driven by weak performance in its core cosmetics OEM/ODM business, compounded by governance and accounting risks from large-scale embezzlement by former management and an auditor's disclaimer of opinion.

Confirmed financials show revenue and net loss trending toward improvement in 2023 compared with 2022, along with a sharp decline in the debt ratio, but the operating loss and negative operating cash flow persisted in both years.

Quarterly results from 2025 onward continued to show losses, and first-quarter 2026 revenue and profit/loss were all recorded as zero, leaving questions about whether the business is operating normally.

CosNine is currently under a trading halt awaiting a court ruling on an injunction it filed against the delisting decision, and the very low approval rate for similar injunction lawsuits over the past five years points to significant uncertainty in how the process will unfold.

Valuation indicators do not reflect normal market pricing due to the trading halt, warranting caution in interpretation. Investors need to focus more on non-operational variables, such as the court's decision and subsequent exchange procedures, than on business performance itself.
