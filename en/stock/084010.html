Report overview

Rebar Profit Recovery Remains Uneven Quarter to Quarter

Daehan Steel's profit attributable to controlling shareholders fell for three straight years through 2025. Its 2026 operating result turned to profit in the first quarter and widened in the second, but it swung back to an operating loss in the third, so the durability of the recovery is still unproven.

02

Business structure

Daehan Steel was founded in 1954 as a steelmaking and rolling company, and its core business is the manufacture and sale of rebar. A 2023 brokerage report put rebar at 91% of sales. Other products listed in the company's filings include billet and bar-in-coil.

The process runs from steelmaking, where steel scrap is melted into billet, to rolling, where billet is rolled into rebar. Raw materials are steel scrap and externally sourced billet, which accounted for 77.12% of purchases in the third quarter of 2023 according to the filing.

In 2020 the company acquired a 70% stake in YK Steel, expanding its rebar capacity to a top-three position in the segment. A 2023 brokerage report described the company as the third-largest steelmaker in Korea.

Its share of the rolling market was 13% in 2020 and 18% in 2021 and 2022, and the same brokerage projected 15% for 2024. Its customers are construction companies.

In the first half of 2026, its bargaining power over steel scrap purchases and rebar sales to construction firms was reported to have strengthened, and export volumes in the steelmaking and rolling units also rose.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual sales went from KRW 2.14 trillion in 2022 to KRW 1.45 trillion in 2023 (down 32.4%) and KRW 1.22 trillion in 2024 (down 15.4%), then rose 1.8% to KRW 1.25 trillion in 2025.

Operating profit went from KRW 215.4 billion in 2022 to KRW 109.9 billion in 2023 (down 49.0%) and KRW 10.3 billion in 2024 (down 90.6%), and turned into an operating loss of KRW 2.87 billion in 2025, a swing into loss from the prior year.

The operating margin declined each year, from 10.1% in 2022 to 7.6%, 0.8% and -0.2% in 2025. Profit attributable to controlling shareholders fell from KRW 125.6 billion in 2022 to KRW 72 billion, KRW 37.5 billion and KRW 14.7 billion in 2025, a three-year decline.

Net income including non-controlling interests was KRW 137.6 billion, KRW 77 billion, KRW 40 billion and KRW 13.8 billion over the same years. The debt ratio fell from 52.9% in 2022 to 28.3% in 2024 and rose to 34.4% in 2025.

At the end of 2025, total equity was KRW 930.3 billion, of which KRW 800.3 billion belonged to controlling shareholders, and total liabilities were KRW 319.6 billion. Operating cash flow was KRW 73.2 billion in 2022, KRW 62.1 billion in 2023, KRW 17 billion in 2024 and KRW 39.6 billion in 2025.

Quarterly, the third quarter of 2025 showed sales of KRW 320 billion and operating profit of KRW 8 billion, then the fourth quarter showed sales of KRW 288.6 billion (down 9.8% from the prior quarter) and an operating loss of KRW 20.7 billion.

The first quarter of 2026 brought sales of KRW 305.6 billion (up 5.9%) and an operating profit of KRW 64.46 million, a swing to profit, while profit attributable to controlling shareholders was a loss of KRW 5.75 million.

The second quarter of 2026 recorded sales of KRW 386.5 billion (up 26.5% from the prior quarter) and operating profit of KRW 16.7 billion.

The third quarter of 2026 recorded sales of KRW 359.5 billion (down 7.0% from the prior quarter but up 12.3% year on year) and an operating loss of KRW 1.42 billion, a swing to loss from the same quarter a year earlier.

The third-quarter figures reflect the classification of the liquidating overseas subsidiary's results as discontinued operations, and they are provisional figures that may change under the external auditor's review.

05

Industry analysis

Rebar demand depends heavily on construction activity. A 2023 brokerage report said the rebar business earned high profitability during the construction upturn of 2021 and 2022. The same report expected that, given new entrants in the sector and a weak housing market, operating profit would struggle to expand in 2023.

After the first-quarter 2026 results, the market pointed to raw material prices, the steel industry environment and the pace of demand recovery as the factors that would shape the outlook. A 2023 brokerage peer comparison listed Hyundai Steel Company, Kisco and Dongkuk Steel Mill as companies in the same steel sector.

A projected drop in rolling market share from 18% in 2022 to 15% in 2024 is consistent with a more competitive market. Stronger purchasing power for steel scrap was cited as a cost-side support in the first half of 2026.

Higher export volumes could widen the sales channels beyond domestic construction, but the data available on export volumes and prices is limited.

The position of the company in the industry cycle cannot be pinned down from the filed results alone, and construction orders and rebar price trends need to be checked together.

Bull factors

Return to quarterly profit

Operating profit turned positive in the first quarter of 2026 at KRW 64.46 million, and the second quarter recorded KRW 16.7 billion, a substantial increase from the prior quarter.

Following an operating loss of KRW 20.7 billion in the fourth quarter of 2025, two quarters of positive operating profit are cited as evidence of a change in earnings structure. Profit scale remains smaller than before 2023, but the direction is toward recovery.

Export volumes and purchasing power

Higher export volumes in the steelmaking and rolling units in the first half of 2026 were cited as a driver of improvement. Stronger purchasing power for steel scrap and bargaining power with construction firms over rebar sales were also reported. Together, these factors point to a sales channel beyond domestic rebar demand and some cost-side resilience.

Low leverage and equity base

The debt ratio was 28.3% in 2024 and remained at 34.4% at the end of 2025. Total equity at the end of 2025 was KRW 930.3 billion, of which KRW 800.3 billion belonged to controlling shareholders.

Total liabilities were KRW 319.6 billion, and the filed figures do not show a sharp deterioration in the balance sheet despite the operating loss.

Bear factors

Third-quarter operating loss

Sales in the third quarter of 2026 were KRW 359.5 billion, up 12.3% year on year, yet the quarter recorded an operating loss of KRW 1.42 billion, a swing to loss from a year earlier. Whether higher sales without profit was a one-quarter pattern will be clearer from the following quarters. The modest size of the first- and second-quarter profits also deserves attention.

Three years of declining profit

Profit attributable to controlling shareholders fell from KRW 125.6 billion in 2022 to KRW 14.7 billion in 2025. Operating profit moved from KRW 215.4 billion in 2022 to an operating loss of KRW 2.87 billion in 2025, and the operating margin dropped from 10.1% to -0.2%. Whether the quarterly profits of 2026 reverse the three-year decline must be confirmed in full-year results.

Provisional figures and discontinued operations

The third-quarter 2026 results are provisional and may change once the external auditor completes its review. The results of Daehan Steel America, Inc., which is in liquidation, are classified as discontinued operations, so continuing-operations figures and net income are on different bases. This makes quarter-to-quarter comparisons harder and remains a point on the weak side.

Risk factors

Raw material prices and sourcing

Raw materials are steel scrap and externally sourced billet, which accounted for 77.12% of purchases in the third quarter of 2023 according to the filing. Changes in the steel market and raw material prices can shift cost pressure quickly.

The market has named raw material prices as a key factor in the direction of results after the first quarter of 2026.

Construction demand and sector competition

Rebar demand is heavily driven by construction activity and the housing market. A 2023 brokerage report cited new entrants in the sector and a weak housing market as constraints on profit expansion.

The projected drop in rolling market share from 18% in 2022 to 15% in 2024 is consistent with rising competitive pressure.

Provisional results and discontinued operations

Quarterly results are provisional before the external auditor's review is complete, and figures may change in the final version. Because the liquidation of an overseas subsidiary led to its results being classified as discontinued operations, continuing-operations results and net income are now on different bases. Any additional gain or loss from completing the liquidation will need to be checked in the filings.

Overall view

Daehan Steel is a rebar-centered business whose profit declined after 2022, and it moved to an operating loss in 2025, while profit attributable to controlling shareholders fell for three straight years.

In 2026, operating profit turned positive in the first quarter and widened in the second, but the third quarter returned to an operating loss of KRW 1.42 billion.

Factors cited on the positive side include higher export volumes, stronger steel scrap purchasing power and improved bargaining with construction customers, and the balance sheet is supported by a low debt ratio and KRW 800.3 billion of equity attributable to controlling shareholders.

On the negative side are the return to quarterly loss, competitive pressure from construction demand and new sector entrants, and the interpretive burden of provisional figures and discontinued operations. Whether the earnings recovery holds on an annual basis will first be confirmed by the 2026 annual report. This report summarizes facts and the balance of factors and does not include a view on the share price.
