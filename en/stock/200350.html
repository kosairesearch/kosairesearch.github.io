02

Business structure

Artist Studio is a story-based content producer founded in 2007 that plans and produces drama, variety and performance content for broadcasters, platforms and overseas markets. The company listed on KOSDAQ in 2021 and has operated under its present name after a 2024 renaming.

Its core activity is TV drama production, and it extends its own IP into NFTs, publishing, OST and character merchandise. Check-in Hanyang and First Lady are among its completed titles, and Jikpil and Ahopsu Urideul were reported in production as of June 2026.

Its customers are broadcasters and OTT platforms including Netflix, Disney+ and tving, and it is planning new-media content with these partners and pursuing co-productions with global partners. Revenue tracks the volume of titles it wins, and the company attributed the 2025 revenue decline to fewer orders.

Compared with large drama producers and distributors such as Studio Dragon and CJ ENM, its revenue base is small. Production cost recovery and settlement timing, which depend on how titles perform, also drive quarterly swings.

In June 2026 the company said it would acquire management control of Corpus Korea with a KRW 29 billion investment, aiming to connect production with domestic and overseas distribution.

As reported in October 2026, a shareholder meeting approved changing the company name to Studio Ssolssol and adding exhibition and art-related activities to its business purpose.

04

Earnings analysis

On an annual basis, revenue went from KRW 44.4 billion in 2022 to KRW 41.9 billion in 2023 (down 5.8% year on year), KRW 27.3 billion in 2024 (down 34.8%) and KRW 14.6 billion in 2025 (down 46.5%).

Revenue declined for three consecutive years from 2023 through 2025, with 2022 the largest year and 2025 the smallest among 2022 to 2025.

Operating losses were KRW 6.25 billion in 2022, KRW 7.79 billion in 2023 (losses widened), KRW 7.32 billion in 2024 (losses narrowed) and KRW 3.01 billion in 2025 (losses narrowed), and the company has been in the red for four consecutive years from 2022.

The operating margin moved from -14.1% in 2022 to -18.6% in 2023 and -26.8% in 2024, then improved to -20.6% in 2025. The company attributed the 2025 narrowing of the operating loss to lower order volume together with cost cuts that improved the profit structure.

Net loss attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 6.37 billion in 2022, KRW 7.43 billion in 2023, KRW 15 billion in 2024 and KRW 4.7 billion in 2025, and total net loss including non-controlling interests was reported at the same amounts. In 2024 the net loss was about double the operating loss.

On the balance sheet, the debt ratio fell from 70.2% in 2022 to 4.6% in 2025, and at the end of 2025 total equity was KRW 49.5 billion, all attributable to controlling shareholders, against total liabilities of KRW 2.29 billion.

Operating cash flow was minus KRW 5.18 billion in 2022, minus KRW 3.03 billion in 2023, KRW 13.81 million in 2024 and minus KRW 10.6 billion in 2025, so the 2025 cash outflow was larger than the operating loss.

Quarterly revenue moved from KRW 5.76 billion in the third quarter of 2025 to KRW 3.12 billion in the fourth quarter (down 45.8% quarter on quarter) and KRW 1.26 billion in the first quarter of 2026 (down 59.8%).

Revenue rose to KRW 10 billion in the second quarter of 2026, up 699.9% from the prior quarter, while the operating loss narrowed to KRW 376 million from KRW 425 million.

Revenue then fell to KRW 3.94 billion in the third quarter of 2026, down 60.7% quarter on quarter and 31.5% year on year, with an operating loss of KRW 482 million. The company has posted losses in each of the five quarters from the third quarter of 2025 through the third quarter of 2026.

Net losses attributable to controlling shareholders over the last four quarters totaled KRW 4.57 billion, with the fourth quarter of 2025 accounting for the largest share at KRW 3.16 billion.

05

Industry analysis

The domestic drama production market is in a structural transition, with slowing growth in traditional broadcast channels coinciding with the spread of OTT and YouTube.

Advertising budgets on traditional channels have spread across more platforms, slowing the growth of new content commissions from broadcasters, which is a shared challenge for producers.

The company has responded by expanding development of new-media content for Netflix, Disney+ and tving and pursuing co-productions with global partners. OTT commissions are often made title by title, so order flow is uneven, and the 2025 revenue decline attributed to fewer orders fits this pattern.

Among competitors, large producers and distributors such as Studio Dragon and CJ ENM run several titles at once with bigger production budgets and distribution networks, while Artist Studio depends more heavily on winning individual projects.

In June 2026 the company moved to extend its capabilities from production into distribution through the Corpus Korea acquisition, which it described as a way to offset the limits of a production-centered model.

The pace of advertising market recovery and the size of OTT commissions remain variables to confirm in the industry data.

06

Outlook

The company has outlined three directions in its filings and public statements. First, the Corpus Korea acquisition and the KRW 29 billion investment are intended to normalize the financial structure and link content production to distribution.

Second, with MBN as a management partner, the company plans to cover content scheduling and domestic and overseas distribution and to co-host performances and exhibitions. Third, it is pursuing a recovery in results through expansion of new-media channels.

On results, the KRW 10 billion of revenue in the second quarter of 2026 reads as a sign of top-line expansion, but the operating loss of KRW 376 million in the same quarter and revenue of KRW 3.94 billion in the third quarter, down 60.7% from the prior quarter, show that the revenue recovery has not been continuous.

On the production pipeline, Jikpil and Ahopsu Urideul were reported in production, and the timing of their broadcast and delivery determines when revenue is recognized.

On capital, participation in a third-party allotment rights offering and the funding needs of the Corpus Korea investment make new share issuance a possibility. Job-Post noted that new share issuance can strengthen business funding but dilutes existing shareholders, so the use of proceeds should be checked.

The largest shareholder's stake sale has been disclosed at the contract stage, and the completion and timing will be confirmed through later filings.

Bull factors

Second-quarter revenue rebound and narrower loss

Revenue in the second quarter of 2026 reached KRW 10 billion, up 699.9% from the prior quarter, while that quarter's operating loss of KRW 376 million was smaller than the KRW 425 million loss in the first quarter. Press reports indicated second-quarter revenue also rose from a year earlier.

If revenue returns to this level in later quarters, it would support a recovery in business scale. A single quarter is not enough to establish a trend, however, and third-quarter revenue fell back to KRW 3.94 billion.

Low-leverage balance sheet

At the end of 2025 the debt ratio stood at 4.6%, with total liabilities of KRW 2.29 billion against total equity of KRW 49.5 billion. The debt ratio had been 70.2% in 2022. All of total equity is attributable to controlling shareholders, so the filings show no non-controlling interest burden. The low debt load means repayment pressure remains modest even while losses continue.

Expansion into distribution and co-production

In June 2026 the company announced plans to take management control of Corpus Korea with a KRW 29 billion investment. It presented the aim as building a value chain linking production to domestic and overseas distribution.

The company has also said it plans to co-host performances and exhibitions with MBN, which is a management partner. If new-media development with Netflix, Disney+ and tving and global co-productions take hold, the number of revenue sources could widen.

Operating losses have run for four consecutive years from 2022, and the company has posted losses in each of the five quarters from the third quarter of 2025.

Operating cash flow in 2025 was minus KRW 10.6 billion, a larger outflow than the operating loss. The operating margin improved to -20.6% in 2025 from -26.8% in 2024 but remained negative.

Shrinking revenue and quarterly volatility

Revenue has declined for three consecutive years from 2023 through 2025, falling 46.5% to KRW 14.6 billion in 2025. Third-quarter 2026 revenue of KRW 3.94 billion was down 60.7% from the prior quarter and 31.5% from a year earlier.

The KRW 10 billion of second-quarter 2026 revenue differs sharply from neighboring quarters, so whether it persists will show in the next quarterly figures. The company attributed the 2025 decline to fewer orders, which shows that gaps in orders feed directly into results.

Ownership changes and dilution risk

Artist Company, the largest shareholder, and director Lee Jung-jae each agreed to sell 408,345 shares to a drama producer that is party to a voting-rights arrangement with MBN, with each transaction worth about KRW 5.5 billion. The company described the sale as part of a change in its largest shareholder.

The name change and added business purposes are proceeding alongside, reshaping the business direction. Funding needs from the Corpus Korea investment and the earlier third-party allotment rights offering could lead to new share issuance, which would dilute existing shareholders.

Risk factors

Content performance and order risk

Revenue depends on titles won and their broadcast performance, so a gap in orders can reduce revenue quickly. The company attributed the 2025 revenue decline to fewer orders. The timing of production cost recovery and settlement adds uncertainty and increases quarterly swings.

Given the large quarter-to-quarter variation, such as third-quarter 2026 revenue of KRW 3.94 billion, future order disclosures are a key point to watch.

Governance and business reorganization risk

The largest shareholder's stake sale and the name change are proceeding together, so the ownership structure and business direction are changing at the same time. When the MBN collaboration and the Corpus Korea acquisition begin to appear in revenue and profit must be confirmed through later filings. During a period of changing direction, production scheduling and order strategy could also be affected.

Funding and cash flow risk

Operating cash flow in 2025 was minus KRW 10.6 billion, a larger outflow than the KRW 3.01 billion operating loss. The planned KRW 29 billion investment in Corpus Korea could require additional funding depending on how it is executed.

If new shares are issued, existing shareholders would be diluted, so the use of proceeds and the effect on consolidated financials need to be checked.
