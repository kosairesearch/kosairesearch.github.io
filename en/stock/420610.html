Report overview

Profitable Safety AI Widens Its Scope

02

Business structure

IntelliVIX, founded in 2000, is a video-analytics AI company that supplies safety control solutions for public safety, industrial safety, transportation, and disaster management based on deep-learning video analysis.

Its main product is the generative AI-based integrated safety control platform GenAMS, supported by the VIXallcam AI camera, which detects objects in bad weather and at night, and the ARGOS quadruped patrol robot.

Domestic revenue is about 60% public sector (B2G), centered on local governments and public agencies, and about 40% private sector (B2B), spanning manufacturing, construction, and logistics.

The company has said that about 70% of local governments nationwide have adopted its AI technology and that its share of the intelligent control market is 68.5%, but these are company-reported figures rather than independently verified data.

Revenue currently comes mainly from build-type deployments of control systems at customer sites, and the company is pursuing a subscription model for video search and analysis report generation.

Defense surveillance is the main new business, while exports were KRW 280 million in 2025, or 0.6% of revenue, so overseas projects in Japan, Australia, Taiwan, and Southeast Asia contribute little to sales so far.

Competitively, it faces established security firms in video control, and expansion into defense and robotics adds new rivals. Its revenue scale is small compared with large IT services companies listed in the same sector.

03

Recent trends

Revenue for 2025 was KRW 46.6 billion, up 37% from the prior year, and operating profit was KRW 4.9 billion, up 113%. Net income was KRW 5.4 billion, up 93%, with an operating margin of 10.5% and accumulated retained earnings of KRW 16.4 billion.

Revenue grew from KRW 18.3 billion in 2023 and KRW 34.0 billion in 2024, about 2.5 times in two years. A year-end preliminary announcement cited a 116% operating profit increase, while figures from the audited 26th-term business report reported in March 2026 showed 113%, so the reported growth rates differ by source.

The company said it has recorded 13 consecutive years of operating profit. First-half 2026 revenue was KRW 23.4 billion, up 8.2% year on year, and operating profit rose from KRW 0.8 billion to KRW 1.2 billion.

However, provisional results showed operating losses of KRW 580 million in July and KRW 444 million in August, reducing cumulative operating profit through August to about KRW 0.2 billion.

The CEO attributed this to a seasonal pattern in which public budget execution concentrates revenue in the second half, especially the fourth quarter.

In the IPO process, retail subscription competition reached 1,410.9 to 1 and institutional demand competition reached 1,234.3 to 1, and the offer price was fixed at 9,000 won, the top of the indicative range.

As of November 11, 2026 at 02:10 KST, the share price was 10,850 won, down 0.46% from the prior close, with a market capitalization of KRW 123 billion and trading value of KRW 9.5 billion.

04

Outlook

The company has presented a 2026 target of KRW 70 billion in revenue and KRW 9 billion in operating profit, a figure announced in December 2025. First-half 2026 revenue of KRW 23.4 billion is slightly above one-third of the revenue target, so the second-half concentration of earnings is a key variable for reaching it.

The company has said net proceeds at the low end of the offer range, KRW 18.57 billion, will be allocated to R&D at KRW 12 billion, facilities at KRW 5 billion, and overseas bases at KRW 1.57 billion.

Overseas, IntelliVIX showed its integrated control platform, edge video-analytics device, and all-weather AI vision sensor at the Smart City Expo World Congress in Barcelona from November 3 to 5, 2026.

In defense, technology development for the AI sentry is slated for completion in 2028, with field trials in 2029, and the company has also cited applications for safe operation of autonomous vehicles and ships.

On supply and demand, 60.12% of shares held by the largest shareholder and other core holders are subject to a three-year lock-up, so the float is expected to expand meaningfully around 2029 when the lock-up expires.

Indicators to watch in upcoming disclosures are the share of subscription revenue, the share of overseas revenue, and the timing of revenue recognition from defense contracts.

05

Bull factors

13 consecutive years of operating profit

The company says it has posted operating profit for 13 consecutive years since its founding in 2000. Operating profit in 2025 was KRW 4.9 billion, with a 10.5% margin and net income of KRW 5.4 billion, and accumulated retained earnings reached KRW 16.4 billion.

Its profitability is highlighted in a market where many AI companies continue to post losses. The company cited its stable financial structure as the basis for choosing a general listing rather than a technology-based listing.

Public-sector customer base

About 60% of domestic revenue comes from the public sector, and the company says about 70% of local governments nationwide have adopted its AI technology. Revenue in 2025 was about 2.5 times the 2023 level, with the GenAMS integrated safety platform and the VIXallcam AI camera as core products.

VIXallcam received a CES 2026 Innovation Award. The company presents its repeat public-sector adoption as the foundation of earnings stability.

Expansion into defense and physical AI

The company says its selection as a defense innovation company in the 100 list opened a path into AI surveillance for GOP and coastal outposts. The ARGOS quadruped patrol robot is being tested at construction sites and in local public safety applications.

VIXallcam is described as able to identify targets up to 200m away at night or in bad weather. The company is also pursuing a plan to extend its build-type business into subscription services.

06

Bear factors

Earnings concentrated in the second half

The CEO explained that public budget execution concentrates revenue in the second half, especially the fourth quarter. First-half 2026 operating profit was KRW 1.2 billion, but provisional operating losses in July and August reduced cumulative operating profit through August to about KRW 0.2 billion.

First-half revenue of KRW 23.4 billion is a relatively low share of the KRW 70 billion target. Quarterly volatility could increase depending on how heavily annual results are concentrated in the second half.

Overseas revenue share of 0.6%

Exports in 2025 were KRW 280 million, or 0.6% of total revenue. The company says it has projects in Australia, Taiwan, and Southeast Asia following earlier exports to Japan, and is pursuing local bases in the Middle East. Converting its overseas sales network into actual revenue remains a challenge. The contribution of overseas sales to total revenue is still limited.

Timing risk of new business commercialization

The AI sentry is slated for technology completion in 2028 and field trials in 2029, so meaningful revenue contribution is still some years away. Defense and physical AI are major destinations for IPO proceeds, but R&D costs typically rise before commercialization.

Of the net proceeds at the low end of the range, KRW 12 billion is allocated to R&D. The pace at which new businesses convert to revenue is cited as the key to earnings growth.

07

Risk factors

Macro

An economic slowdown and weaker private capital spending could affect B2B orders, which account for about 40% of domestic revenue. Interest rate and exchange rate swings could indirectly affect the profitability and cost structure of overseas projects.

Because about 60% of revenue comes from the public sector, macro effects may be relatively contained, but lower private orders could increase earnings volatility.

Policy and budget

Public-sector revenue depends on the budget execution timing of local governments and public agencies. Defense projects are scheduled around technology completion in 2028 and field trials in 2029, and delays would also postpone monetization.

Given the nature of defense procurement, procedural and certification requirements could affect the timing of revenue recognition.

Supply and liquidity

Only 23.95% of listed shares are immediately tradable after listing. Shares held by the largest shareholder and other core holders, totaling 60.12%, are subject to a three-year lock-up. With a limited float, price volatility could increase depending on trading volume and trading value. The float will expand when the lock-up expires.

08

Overall view

IntelliVIX is a video-analytics AI company that has recorded 13 consecutive years of operating profit since its founding in 2000, and its 2025 disclosures show revenue of KRW 46.6 billion and operating profit of KRW 4.9 billion.

Its strengths are a public-sector customer base, a record of profitability, and expansion into defense and physical AI, while its weaknesses are the concentration of earnings in the second half, an overseas revenue share of 0.6% (2025 basis), and uncertainty over when new businesses will convert to revenue.

First-half 2026 revenue rose 8.2% to KRW 23.4 billion, but operating profit was KRW 1.2 billion, and provisional losses in July and August reduced cumulative profit.

The high subscription competition in the IPO and the current market capitalization reflect market interest, but these alone do not fully explain the company's fundamentals.

The 2026 targets of KRW 70 billion in revenue and KRW 9 billion in operating profit rely on strong second-half results, and their achievement will be confirmed through future quarterly disclosures.

The business is shifting from public-sector build-type deployments toward subscription, defense, and robotics, and the pace of that shift and the overseas revenue share will be central to medium-term assessment.
