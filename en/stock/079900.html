Concrete pump car leader: margin pressure versus overseas demand

02

Business structure

Junjin Construction & Robot manufactures concrete pump cars (CPC) as its core product line. The company traces its roots to Jeonjin Special Precision, founded in 1980, and Jeonjin Heavy Industries, the current corporate entity, was set up in 1999 as the pump car business expanded from the 1990s.

The company listed on the KOSPI market in 2024 and says its Geumwang plant in Eumseong County, North Chungcheong Province, has an annual capacity of 600 units.

Cumulative pump car shipments have passed 10,000 units, and the same manufacturing know-how is being used to develop construction-site automation equipment such as concrete distribution robots. Concrete pump products make up the bulk of sales.

According to a April 2026 report by Eugene Investment & Securities, of the KRW 45.4 billion in Q4 2025 revenue, concrete pump products accounted for KRW 38.0 billion, parts sales and maintenance for KRW 3.9 billion, and mobility (special-purpose vehicles) for KRW 3.3 billion.

By region, North America and the Middle East are large markets. U.S. sales reached KRW 77.8 billion in 2024, up 18% year on year, and Middle East sales rose 110%. In North America the company sells through local dealers and added sales and service network at the end of 2024.

Analysts describe it as the leading domestic CPC maker, and in competition it is cited for price competitiveness against German manufacturers.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual revenue was KRW 158.4 billion in 2023, KRW 169.8 billion in 2024 (up 7.2% year on year) and KRW 190.7 billion in 2025 (up 12.3%), so revenue has grown for two straight years from 2024 through 2025, and 2025 was the largest year of the 2023–2025 period.

Operating profit was KRW 32.9 billion in 2023, KRW 31.3 billion in 2024 (down 4.7%) and KRW 28.7 billion in 2025 (down 8.4%), declining for two straight years from 2024 through 2025, with 2023 the largest year in the period.

Operating margin moved from 20.8% to 18.5% and then 15.1%, so higher sales did not translate into higher profit.

For 2024, the company attributed the profit decline to temporary factors such as higher R&D spending on robots and bad-debt provisions linked to a rise in the exchange rate, and said margins excluding these one-off costs were in the 20% range.

Profit attributable to owners of the parent was KRW 28.9 billion in 2023, KRW 31.3 billion in 2024 (up 8.3%) and KRW 31.5 billion in 2025 (up 0.6%), rising for two straight years from 2024 through 2025.

Profit rising while operating profit fell is consistent with the company's explanation that financial income and exchange-rate effects supported net income in 2024.

Net income including non-controlling interests was KRW 28.9 billion in 2023, KRW 31.4 billion in 2024 and KRW 29.2 billion in 2025, a different figure from profit attributable to owners; in 2025 the lower total reflects a negative contribution from non-controlling interests.

Operating cash flow was KRW 36.7 billion in 2023, KRW 19.5 billion in 2024 and KRW 32.3 billion in 2025, a recovery in 2025.

By quarter, revenue went from KRW 44.9 billion in Q3 2025 to KRW 45.4 billion in Q4 2025, KRW 46 billion in Q1 2026 and KRW 47.3 billion in Q2 2026, then fell to KRW 35.5 billion in Q3 2026, down 24.9% from the prior quarter and 21.0% from a year earlier.

Operating profit peaked at KRW 9.61 billion in Q2 2026 across the disclosed quarters, and Q3 2026 operating profit of KRW 5.6 billion was up 13.1% year on year despite lower sales. Profit attributable to owners fell to KRW 1.18 billion in Q3 2026, down 88.9% year on year.

Over the last four quarters, from Q4 2025 through Q3 2026, profit attributable to owners totaled KRW 25.6 billion.

05

Industry analysis

Demand for concrete pump cars moves with construction activity and large infrastructure projects. A 2024 report said the Infrastructure Investment and Jobs Act was expected to support demand in the United States, alongside investment in data centers and factories.

In the Middle East, construction tied to large national events in Saudi Arabia and housing work in Dubai were cited as demand drivers. In Q1 2026 the company said it would respond to a broader global shift toward investment in data centers and power infrastructure.

In Europe, a recovery in demand linked to earthquake reconstruction in Türkiye contributed to growth, and the company is pursuing a European subsidiary to prepare for reconstruction demand after the war in Ukraine ends.

Tariffs remain a variable: temporary adjustments to sales and operating profit followed U.S. tariff policy changes, and a U.S. Court of International Trade ruling against the tariffs was read as easing future tariff burdens.

On competition, price competitiveness against German manufacturers is cited, and construction equipment makers such as Doosan Bobcat and HD Construction Equipment are natural peer groups.

06

Outlook

In March 2026 the company disclosed a shareholder return plan that keeps the fiscal 2026 payout ratio at 50% or higher and expands investor communication.

The European subsidiary has cleared internal approval and procedures are under way, with the aim of serving reconstruction demand after the war in Ukraine and strengthening supply competitiveness through local inventory.

On the product side, the company is developing D-MCR, a mobile concrete distribution robot with autonomous and remote operation, together with a consortium, and plans to widen its lineup to screed robots that level concrete after pouring and finisher robots for floor finishing.

An April 2026 Eugene Investment & Securities report estimated 2026 revenue of KRW 209.8 billion and operating profit of KRW 35.6 billion, both up from 2025, but that estimate predates the disclosed results for Q2 and Q3 2026.

Q3 2026 operating profit rose year on year while revenue fell sharply, so the estimate path is worth checking against the reported quarters. In Q1 2026 domestic revenue rose 121.9% from the prior quarter and European revenue grew 27.4% year on year.

Whether the tariff burden eases will depend on policy developments, so North American sales trends remain a key point to monitor.

08

Bull factors

Broad overseas demand base

U.S. sales reached KRW 77.8 billion in 2024, up 18%, and Middle East sales rose 110%. European revenue grew 27.4% year on year in Q1 2026, showing demand across several regions. With overseas revenue at 84% as of Q3 2024, regional spread is cited as a basis for earnings stability.

Reconstruction and infrastructure pipeline

The company said demand tied to earthquake reconstruction in Türkiye contributed to European growth. It is pursuing a European subsidiary to prepare for reconstruction demand once the war in Ukraine ends.

Reconstruction demand in Iran and the broader Middle East for energy and infrastructure is also seen as possibly growing gradually.

Shareholder return floor and automation expansion

The disclosed payout ratio floor is set in writing and serves as a baseline for future dividends. It is widening its lineup with the D-MCR concrete distribution robot and planned screed and finisher robots to build automation revenue beyond pump cars.

09

Bear factors

Falling operating margin trend

Operating profit fell from KRW 32.9 billion in 2023 to KRW 28.7 billion in 2025, and operating margin dropped from 20.8% to 15.1%. Operating profit declined for two straight years from 2024 through 2025. Sales growth that did not carry through to profit is the core of the bearish case.

Sharp Q3 sales drop and weaker net profit

Q3 2026 revenue was KRW 35.5 billion, down 24.9% from the prior quarter and 21.0% from a year earlier. Profit attributable to owners fell to KRW 1.18 billion, down 88.9% year on year. Because net profit fell far more than operating profit rose, the quality of earnings needs checking.

Heavy overseas exposure and tariff and FX sensitivity

Overseas revenue was 84% of the total as of Q3 2024, which makes results sensitive to regional policy changes. U.S. tariff policy changes caused temporary sales and operating profit adjustments. A rise in the exchange rate led to bad-debt provisions in 2024.

10

Risk factors

Tariffs and trade policy

Changes in U.S. tariff policy directly affect North American sales and margins. A U.S. Court of International Trade ruling against the tariffs has been reported, but whether the policy stays in place is uncertain. A renewed tariff burden could pressure both pricing and profitability in North America through local dealers.

Exchange rates and bad-debt exposure

Bad-debt provisions linked to a rise in the exchange rate were cited as a factor behind the 2024 operating profit decline. With overseas sales making up most revenue, currency moves affect both receivables collection and reported profit. Larger swings in exchange rates could widen the gap between operating profit and net profit again.

R&D spending and earnings volatility

The company said higher R&D spending on robots weighed on 2024 operating profit. Quarterly operating profit fell from KRW 9.61 billion in Q2 2026 to KRW 5.6 billion in Q3, and profit attributable to owners dropped to KRW 1.18 billion. Automation development costs combined with quarterly swings widen the range of possible earnings outcomes.

12

Overall view

Junjin Construction & Robot expanded overseas sales of concrete pump cars and lifted revenue to KRW 190.7 billion in 2025. Over the same period operating profit fell to KRW 28.7 billion and operating margin dropped to 15.1%, a pattern tied to R&D costs, exchange rates and tariffs.

Revenue and operating profit both rose in Q1 and Q2 2026, but Q3 2026 revenue fell to KRW 35.5 billion and profit attributable to owners dropped to KRW 1.18 billion.

The bullish case rests on overseas demand across North America, the Middle East and Europe, reconstruction-related pipelines, and a payout ratio floor of at least 50%. The bearish case rests on the falling margin trend, the sharp Q3 sales decline, and tariff and FX exposure from a high overseas share.

Valuation sits at a large premium to shareholders' equity attributable to owners, so whether earnings recovery materializes is the central question for interpreting it.
