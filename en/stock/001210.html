02

Business structure

Kumho Electric is a manufacturer whose core business is lighting equipment, listed on the KOSPI since November 15, 1973. It is classified in the electrical equipment sector, but its revenue mix is concentrated in lighting.

Based on 2025 full-year figures compiled by a Korean financial data service, LED lighting accounted for 63.91% of sales, systems and other products for 19.69%, discharge lamps for 16.15%, and other items for 0.25%. Systems and other products make up about one-fifth of sales, so non-lighting business is not negligible.

The LED lineup spans linear and ring-shaped fixtures, bulb-type fluorescent lamps and emergency exit lights. The company is positioning lighting know-how built over nearly 90 years, combined with Internet of Things connectivity, as its next growth driver through a product it calls 'SmartMesh'.

Media reports say its push into public-sector orders rests on high-efficiency LEDs tied to carbon-reduction goals. Reports also describe a market expectation that wider public-sector orders could cushion demand swings tied to private spending.

Larger sector peers such as LS ELECTRIC, Hyosung Heavy Industries and HD Hyundai Electric center on power equipment such as transformers and switchgear, so their product sets and customers differ markedly.

As a result, Kumho Electric's results track lighting demand and its cost structure more than the order cycle of power equipment.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual revenue rose 16.0% to KRW 53.4 billion in 2023 from KRW 46 billion in 2022, then fell 0.4% to KRW 53.2 billion in 2024 and 16.6% to KRW 44.3 billion in 2025, two straight years of decline. The largest year in the 2022–2025 span was 2023 and the smallest was 2025.

The operating loss narrowed every year, from KRW 11.8 billion in 2022 to KRW 8.23 billion, KRW 4.96 billion and KRW 1.18 billion in 2025, lifting the operating margin from -25.6% to -2.7%.

Filed annual figures show four consecutive years of losses from 2022 through 2025, while a Korean financial media report described nine consecutive years of operating losses since 2017.

The company attributed the 2025 improvement to better cost of sales, lower selling and administrative expenses and lower interest costs.

Net losses attributable to controlling shareholders were KRW 16.6 billion in 2022, KRW 16.6 billion in 2023, KRW 12.2 billion in 2024 and KRW 3.4 billion in 2025, while the net loss including minority interests was largest in 2023 at KRW 18.3 billion.

Total equity at the end of 2025 was KRW 10.1 billion, below the KRW 24.2 billion attributable to controlling shareholders, which implies a negative minority-interest balance.

The debt-to-equity ratio fell from 640.1% in 2023 to 204.3% in 2025, and operating cash flow turned positive at KRW 4.08 billion in 2025 after an outflow of KRW 8.78 billion in 2022.

In the fourth quarter of 2025 the company posted an operating profit of KRW 88.6 million but a controlling-shareholder net loss of KRW 2.06 billion, and in the first quarter of 2026 an operating profit of KRW 41.61 million and a controlling net profit of KRW 2.73 billion.

Second-quarter 2026 revenue of KRW 7.88 billion fell 27.1% from the prior quarter, with an operating loss of KRW 1.03 billion. Third-quarter 2026 revenue of KRW 8.17 billion was down 28.1% year on year, with an operating loss of KRW 438 million, making two straight loss quarters.

The third-quarter controlling net profit of KRW 645 million rose 430.0% year on year while operating results moved the other way, so non-operating factors need checking. The four quarters from Q4 2025 to Q3 2026 sum to KRW 476 million of controlling net profit.

05

Industry analysis

Kumho Electric is classified in the electrical equipment sector, but its business is centered on lighting products, so it does not follow the cycle of power equipment makers. Media reports describe market expectations that wider public-sector orders will reduce earnings volatility.

Quarterly revenue in 2026, however, was KRW 10.8 billion in the first quarter, KRW 7.88 billion in the second and KRW 8.17 billion in the third, showing no clear recovery. The lighting market is largely in its LED transition, so price competition and product differentiation are the key issues.

The company is trying to differentiate through IoT-connected products, and a media report cites high-efficiency lighting as contributing to national carbon-reduction targets.

Compared with large power equipment makers such as LS ELECTRIC, Hyosung Heavy Industries and HD Hyundai Electric, its sales scale and business structure differ widely. The company cited cost of sales and SG&A reductions as drivers of the 2025 improvement, which points to cost control rather than revenue recovery. The key sector question is therefore whether cost savings can be converted into revenue growth.

06

Outlook

The company has outlined three directions: differentiating through IoT-connected products such as SmartMesh, expanding public-sector orders with high-efficiency LEDs, and continuing cost reduction to narrow losses. On funding, the KRW 11.0 billion 12th-series convertible bond is the key variable.

A Korean financial media outlet reported that the bond is meant to raise operating funds and that its payment was delayed by more than two years after repeated amended disclosures. Two individuals who are classified as related parties of the largest shareholder are linked to the investing fund.

If converted, the potential stake held by the largest shareholder's side would also increase.

The 2026 annual business report is due within 90 days of fiscal year-end, by the end of March 2027, and the capital impairment ratio and full-year operating result reported then will serve as the basis for management-issue designation.

Operating profit was positive in the first quarter of 2026 but turned to losses in the second and third quarters, leaving the nine-month figure in deficit. Whether the smart-product strategy translates into revenue growth can be checked in the 2026 annual report and the first-quarter 2027 quarterly report.

Bull factors

Narrowing operating losses and better cash flow

Operating losses narrowed from KRW 11.8 billion in 2022 to KRW 1.18 billion in 2025 even as revenue declined. The company cited cost of sales and SG&A reductions as drivers. Operating cash flow turned positive at KRW 4.08 billion in 2025, after negative readings from 2022 through 2024.

Falling debt-to-equity ratio

The debt-to-equity ratio fell from 640.1% in 2023 to 274.0% in 2024 and 204.3% in 2025, two straight years of decline. Total liabilities stood at KRW 20.7 billion at the end of 2025. The balance sheet is moving toward a lighter debt burden, which is a positive factor in the effort to repair capital impairment.

Public-sector orders and smart-product strategy

Media reports say the company is expanding public-sector orders with high-efficiency LEDs. The market expects wider public orders to reduce earnings volatility tied to private-sector demand.

Whether the SmartMesh IoT products can lift revenue and support a return to operating profit is the central test of this strategy.

If the annual business report shows the ratio above the threshold, management-issue designation could follow. Funding and dilution

Uncertain earnings recovery and product concentration

LED lighting accounts for 63.91% of sales, so dependence on one product family is high. A Korean financial media report says operating losses have continued for nine years since 2017, and analysts have argued that narrower losses from cost cuts do not show a recovered core business.

Quarterly revenue moved from KRW 10.8 billion in the first quarter of 2026 to KRW 7.88 billion and KRW 8.17 billion, with little evidence of recovery yet.

Overall view

Kumho Electric's filed results show narrowing operating losses and a falling debt-to-equity ratio. The operating loss shrank from KRW 11.8 billion in 2022 to KRW 1.18 billion in 2025, and operating cash flow turned positive in 2025.

Revenue, however, peaked at KRW 53.4 billion in 2023 and then fell in both 2024 and 2025. In 2026 an operating profit in the first quarter was followed by losses in the second and third quarters, so the direction of quarterly profit is still unsettled.

On the financial side, total equity of KRW 10.1 billion and a calculated capital impairment ratio of 67.2% are the central variables tied to listing status. The KRW 11.0 billion CB and potential dilution of up to 33.7% will shape the capital structure.

Bullish points are cost control, a lower debt ratio and expectations for public-sector orders, while bearish points are capital impairment, renewed quarterly losses and stalled revenue.

The next checkpoints are the operating result and capital impairment ratio in the 2026 annual report and the operating trend in the first-quarter 2027 quarterly report.
