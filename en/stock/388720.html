Yuilrobotics: Profit Turnaround Still Pending as Orders Widen

Yuilrobotics has widened its US equipment and smart-factory order book in 2026, yet on a DART-filed basis operating losses have continued for five straight quarters from Q3 2025 to Q3 2026.

  1. 1

    The 2025 filed annual results show revenue of KRW 36.9 billion (up 5.0% year on year), but an operating loss of KRW 12.6 billion, reversing from an operating profit of KRW 395 million in 2024.

  2. 2

    Attributable net losses over the latest four quarters (Q4 2025 to Q3 2026) total KRW 20 billion, and the filings show a loss in each year from 2022 through 2025.

  3. 3

    Quarterly revenue rose 118.5% to KRW 9.96 billion in Q2 2026 before falling to KRW 6.73 billion in Q3, while the operating loss widened to KRW 4.2 billion.

  4. 4

    In 2026 the company added a contract of about KRW 5.9 billion with HD Hyundai Power Transformers USA, set up a US subsidiary, and began a KRW 5.7 billion logistics automation project.

02

Business structure

Yuilrobotics is a robot automation specialist built on two main businesses, industrial robots and automation systems.

As of Q3 2024, automation systems accounted for 58.76% of sales, industrial robots for 22.79%, and other businesses for 18.44%, and the company was reported to serve more than 1,600 customers at that time.

In the automation systems business, 2026 has brought turnkey orders for vapour phase drying equipment, a core facility for extra-high-voltage transformers, covering the main unit, control systems, and on-site installation and commissioning.

In industrial robots, the company said it completed development of the YURA actuator, a core joint component, in January 2026, reducing reliance on imported parts. Building on its Link Factory smart-factory platform, the company is expanding into dark-factory solutions that combine robots, AI and communications.

A June 2026 press report described a robot painting system contract with a domestic construction equipment maker. Customers span automotive, home appliances, cosmetics and secondary batteries, where the partnership with SK On is the most prominent example. Exports rose from 8.7% of sales in 2020 to 22.4% in Q3 2024.

In collaborative and articulated robots, Doosan Robotics and Rainbow Robotics are the usual reference peers. The March 2026 shareholder meeting added agricultural robots and smart-farm solutions to the business purpose, broadening the scope of new businesses.

04

Earnings analysis

On the filed numbers, attributable net losses were KRW 2.31 billion in 2022, KRW 6.56 billion in 2023, KRW 9.05 billion in 2024 and KRW 24.7 billion in 2025, a loss in each of the four years with the size growing.

Revenue peaked at KRW 38.4 billion in 2022, the largest year of the four, then fell to KRW 29.5 billion in 2023 (down 23.4%), before rising 19.4% to KRW 35.2 billion in 2024 and 5.0% to KRW 36.9 billion in 2025, increasing for two consecutive years from 2024 to 2025.

Operating profit turned positive at KRW 395 million in 2024, but the company returned to an operating loss of KRW 12.6 billion in 2025. The operating margin moved from 1.1% in 2024 to -34.2% in 2025.

The company attributed the 2025 operating loss mainly to one-off costs tied to the move to its new Cheongna plant and a rise in selling and administrative expenses from hiring robot manufacturing and R&D staff, as reported in February 2026.

Operating cash flow was an outflow of KRW 11.3 billion in 2025, after an inflow of KRW 246 million in 2024. On a quarterly basis, revenue rose to KRW 9.3 billion in Q3 2025 and KRW 10.5 billion in Q4 2025, then fell to KRW 4.56 billion in Q1 2026 (down 56.4% quarter on quarter).

It recovered to KRW 9.96 billion in Q2 2026 before dropping to KRW 6.73 billion in Q3 2026, down 32.4% quarter on quarter and 27.6% year on year. The operating loss narrowed to KRW 1.46 billion in Q2 2026 and widened again to KRW 4.2 billion in Q3 2026. The profit turnaround the company outlined in February 2026 has not yet appeared in quarterly results.

05

Industry analysis

Demand for robot automation depends heavily on capital spending in manufacturing sectors such as automotive, home appliances and secondary batteries. In a September 2026 announcement, the company said manufacturing is moving from smart factories toward autonomous, unmanned dark factories.

It set out a plan to evolve into an autonomous manufacturing platform that combines robots, AI and communications. On the end-market side, the company's CEO has said extra-high-voltage grid investment is expanding from North America to Europe and the Middle East, as reported in February 2026.

In secondary batteries, discussions on supplying automation equipment for an SK On battery plant in the United States were reported in 2024. In collaborative and articulated robots, Doosan Robotics and Rainbow Robotics are the main reference peers.

Industry commentary says growth prospects are widening as large companies push further into robotics. The company says its in-house actuator development has improved cost and quality competitiveness. Exports rising from 8.7% of sales in 2020 to 22.4% in Q3 2024 show that overseas demand has started to contribute.

However, how quickly these industry trends translate into sales and profit gains has not yet appeared in quarterly results.

Outlook

Order intake in 2026 has widened across US equipment and domestic factory automation. In February 2026 the company signed a firm contract of about KRW 5.9 billion with HD Hyundai Power Transformers USA to supply vapour phase drying plants No. 4 and No. 5, running through April 30, 2027.

Payments are structured in stages: 20% advance, 30% initial, 40% interim on shipment, and 10% balance. The same month it set up a subsidiary in Atlanta, Georgia, to push into North America.

In June 2026 it signed a contract of about KRW 5.2 billion for automation of a new plant for a domestic maker of small automotive motors, covering logistics automation and MES, with completion targeted for March 2027.

In September 2026 the company disclosed progress on that project along with an MES contract from a domestic company C and a smart-factory contract for the US unit of a domestic company J.

The March 2026 shareholder meeting added agricultural robots, smart-farm solutions and military supply to its business purpose, and the company said it plans to pursue new businesses. In February 2026 it outlined a goal of combining revenue growth with a return to profit.

The company has noted that contract terms may change in part through consultation with the client. The next checkpoints are how much of the confirmed order value converts into quarterly revenue, and whether operating results in the 2026 annual report move out of the loss zone.

Bull factors

Overseas equipment orders and a North American base

In February 2026 the company signed a firm contract of about KRW 5.9 billion for two vapour phase drying plants with HD Hyundai Power Transformers USA. The same month it set up a subsidiary in Atlanta, Georgia. Exports rose from 8.7% of sales in 2020 to 22.4% in Q3 2024.

On the bull side, overseas orders are seen as widening a customer base that was previously concentrated in domestic automotive.

In-house technology and a broader smart-factory offer

The company said it completed development of its YURA actuator in January 2026, reducing reliance on imported parts. Turnkey smart-factory work is also expanding, including a KRW 5.7 billion logistics automation project and an MES contract.

The company has set a goal of an autonomous manufacturing platform that combines robots, AI and communications. Bulls see this as scope for better product competitiveness and margins.

The 2024 profit year and shareholder-backed funding

The company reported an operating profit of KRW 395 million in 2024, its first annual profit in three years, and said it stayed profitable from Q2 through Q4. In May 2024 SK Battery America took part in a third-party allotment capital increase of KRW 36.7 billion and became the second-largest shareholder. Reports also pointed to continuing talks on automation for secondary battery plants.

Operating losses have continued for five consecutive quarters from Q3 2025 to Q3 2026. Attributable net losses over the latest four quarters total KRW 20 billion.

Revenue volatility and reliance on single large contracts

Q1 2026 revenue fell 56.4% quarter on quarter to KRW 4.56 billion, and Q3 2026 revenue of KRW 6.73 billion was down 27.6% year on year. The Q3 2026 operating loss widened to KRW 4.2 billion.

The February 2026 contract was reported as a meaningful share of annual sales, so quarterly revenue can swing with the timing of single large projects.

Dilution and convertible bond overhang

Following a conversion request under its fourth convertible bond, 84,550 common shares were newly listed in February 2026. The short-term share price correction at the time was explained in connection with valuation losses on convertible bonds. The company has said selling and administrative expenses rose with the new plant move and staff additions.

Risk factors

Delayed profit recovery

The operating margin fell from 1.1% in 2024 to -34.2% in 2025, and quarterly operating losses continued through Q3 2026. If the turnaround the company outlined does not materialize, losses could extend further. If selling and administrative costs keep rising faster than revenue, profit improvement will be delayed.

Contract terms and currency swings

The company has said large contracts may change in part through consultation with the client even after the amount is fixed. The US contract is denominated in dollars and converted to won, so won-denominated revenue moves with the exchange rate.

Because payments are staged across advance, initial, interim and final stages, the timing of revenue recognition depends on project progress.

Capital structure and changes in major shareholding

After the 2024 rights issue, SK Battery America became the second-largest shareholder, and reports said SK On holds a call option to acquire additional stake within a set period. Additional listings followed a convertible bond conversion in February 2026. Changes in share count can affect per-share metrics, so shifts in the shareholder base matter.

Overall view

On the filed numbers, Yuilrobotics' revenue rose for two straight years from 2024 to 2025, but the operating result swung back to a loss of KRW 12.6 billion in 2025. Attributable net losses have appeared in every year from 2022 through 2025, reaching KRW 24.7 billion in 2025.

Operating losses have continued for five consecutive quarters through Q3 2026, and attributable net losses over the latest four quarters total KRW 20 billion. On the strength side, the company has won US equipment contracts, set up a US subsidiary, added smart-factory orders, and internalized its actuator technology.

On the weakness side, the timing of a profit recovery has not yet appeared in quarterly results, and revenue swings sharply depending on when single large projects are recognized. On the financial side, the debt-to-equity ratio fell from 146.0% in 2023 to 20.6% in 2025.

On valuation, earnings-based multiples cannot be calculated, and the stock trades at a large premium to net asset value. The next checkpoints are the 2026 annual report and quarterly profit results for Q1 2027.
