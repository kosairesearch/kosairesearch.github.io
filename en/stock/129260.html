Intergis: Fleet Expansion Alongside a Return to Quarterly Profit

Intergis has posted three consecutive quarters of higher sales and operating profit in 2026, but its FY2025 operating margin of 2.8% leaves the question of whether earnings strength is recovering on a sustained basis.

02

Business structure

Intergis is a comprehensive logistics company founded in 1956 and affiliated with the Dongkuk Steel Group, mainly operating port cargo handling, land transport, sea transport and freight forwarding.

Based on 2024 segment sales, transport accounted for KRW 214 billion (30.6%), shipping for KRW 194.4 billion (27.8%), port handling for KRW 139.2 billion (19.8%) and forwarding for KRW 97.8 billion (13.9%), according to a 2024 business review published by Monthly Maritime Korea.

Transport and shipping together account for more than half of sales. The port handling business is based on cargo services at Busan port. Before the DK INOX vessel joined, the shipping arm operated seven vessels carrying about 3.3 million tonnes a year.

Its main cargo is steel products from the Dongkuk Steel Group, alongside grain, iron ore and coal. Because steel is its main cargo, the customer base leans toward group affiliation.

In land transport and forwarding it competes with large integrated logistics operators such as CJ Logistics and HANJIN Logistics, while in bulk shipping it is compared with Pan Ocean, Korea Line and Heung-A Shipping. In Q1 2026 it set up a Polish subsidiary to strengthen its European supply chain.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual sales moved from KRW 720 billion in 2022 to KRW 611.6 billion in 2023 (down 15.0% year on year), then rose to KRW 701.2 billion in 2024 (up 14.6%) and KRW 709 billion in 2025 (up 1.1%), growing for two consecutive years after the 2023 low. 2022 was the largest year of 2022–2025 for sales.

Operating profit was KRW 35.4 billion in 2022, KRW 23 billion in 2023 (down 35.0%), KRW 23.9 billion in 2024 (up 3.6%) and KRW 19.8 billion in 2025 (down 17.2%), with 2025 the smallest year.

Sales grew in 2025 while operating profit fell, which shows margin pressure: the operating margin declined every year, from 4.9% in 2022 to 3.8% in 2023, 3.4% in 2024 and 2.8% in 2025.

Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders went from KRW 30.8 billion in 2022 to KRW 13.4 billion in 2023 (down 56.5%), KRW 14.3 billion in 2024 (up 6.9%) and KRW 9.83 billion in 2025 (down 31.3%).

Net income including non-controlling interests was KRW 10.5 billion in 2025, a different figure from the controlling-shareholder number. At the end of 2025, total equity was KRW 296.6 billion, of which KRW 293 billion belonged to controlling shareholders, and total liabilities were KRW 203.1 billion.

The debt-to-equity ratio rose from 63.0% in 2024 to 68.5% in 2025, while operating cash flow was KRW 34.3 billion in 2025 against KRW 24.6 billion in 2024.

Quarterly, Q3 2025 sales were KRW 177.1 billion with operating profit of KRW 5.24 billion; Q4 2025 sales were KRW 175.2 billion (down 1.1% quarter on quarter) with an operating loss of KRW 1.45 billion and a net loss attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 5.47 billion, a swing into loss.

Q1 2026 sales were KRW 194.5 billion (up 11.0% quarter on quarter) with operating profit of KRW 3.48 billion, a turn back into profit. Q2 2026 sales were KRW 224.4 billion (up 15.3%), with operating profit of KRW 7.94 billion (up 128.4%) and net profit attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 5.47 billion.

Q3 2026 sales were KRW 234.6 billion (up 4.6% quarter on quarter and up 32.5% year on year), with operating profit of KRW 7.75 billion (down 2.4% quarter on quarter and up 47.8% year on year) and net profit attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 5 billion (up 16.5% year on year). The sum of controlling-shareholder net profit over the last four quarters is KRW 7.82 billion.

05

Industry analysis

The domestic logistics market in which Intergis operates is centered on integrated competition that bundles port handling, land and sea transport, and freight forwarding.

A 2024 review of 26 port-handling companies by Monthly Maritime Korea pointed to a split in which larger players held steady while smaller and mid-sized firms weakened further. In the same review, Intergis was cited with 2024 sales of KRW 701.2 billion and operating profit of KRW 23.9 billion.

In integrated logistics it overlaps with large operators such as CJ Logistics and HANJIN Logistics, while in bulk shipping it is compared with Pan Ocean, Korea Line and Heung-A Shipping.

On the shipping supply side, the trend toward larger vessels has left room for small bulk carriers serving demand in Asian coastal routes, which is the basis of the company's strategy. The company emphasizes the maneuverability and operating flexibility of smaller ships as a strength.

Its main cargo, steel products from the Dongkuk Steel Group, moves with group production and shipment flows, so the steel cycle is a major driver of volume. External variables such as freight rates and newbuilding prices widen or narrow profit in the shipping segment.

06

Outlook

The most concrete plan the company has announced is to expand the fleet to eight vessels and annual handling volume to 3.4 million tonnes after the DK INOX is delivered, as announced on October 29, 2026.

The company has also outlined plans to secure three more small bulk carriers, including newbuilds and secondhand ships, by 2030.

Coverage at the same time reported that the group had said the mix of newbuilds and secondhand ships and the detailed timetable were still under discussion, and the timing of execution has not been disclosed.

In Europe, the company set up a Polish subsidiary in Q1 2026 to strengthen its supply chain alongside group affiliates' European expansion. On the land logistics side, a groundbreaking ceremony for the Cheongju center was held on March 3, 2026, and investment is under way.

On the annual report, FY2026 sales against the FY2025 level of KRW 709 billion will be the first full-year figure to be confirmed through the filing. The key variables for the outlook are whether sales growth continues through Q3 2026 and whether the operating margin recovers from the FY2025 level of 2.8%.

Related parties led by the largest shareholder hold 55.30%, and on October 29, 2026, director Park Seong-do bought 5,511 shares on the market, raising his holding to 10,111 shares.

08

Bull factors

Quarterly earnings recovery

Sales rose for three consecutive quarters from Q1 to Q3 2026. Q3 2026 sales were KRW 234.6 billion, up 32.5% year on year, and operating profit was KRW 7.75 billion, up 47.8% year on year. Operating profit stayed in the KRW 7 billion range in both Q2 and Q3 2026 after the Q4 2025 loss.

Fleet expansion and small bulk strategy

With the DK INOX vessel, the fleet is set to reach eight ships and annual handling volume to 3.4 million tonnes, according to the company's October 29, 2026 announcement. The company aims to secure three more small bulk carriers by 2030, including newbuilds and secondhand ships, targeting Asian coastal routes. The target routes link Korea and Japan with China, Taiwan and Southeast Asia.

Cash generation and equity base

Operating cash flow rose to KRW 34.3 billion in 2025 from KRW 24.6 billion in 2024. At the end of 2025, total equity was KRW 296.6 billion, of which KRW 293 billion belonged to controlling shareholders. The debt-to-equity ratio of 68.5% in 2025 is below the 70.2% recorded in 2022.

09

Bear factors

Declining operating margin

The operating margin declined every year, from 4.9% in 2022 to 3.8% in 2023, 3.4% in 2024 and 2.8% in 2025. FY2025 operating profit fell 17.2% to KRW 19.8 billion, and net profit attributable to controlling shareholders fell 31.3% to KRW 9.83 billion.

Sales grew in 2024 and 2025, but that growth did not translate into higher profit, which is the main bearish point.

Quarterly earnings volatility

In Q4 2025 the company posted an operating loss of KRW 1.45 billion and a net loss attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 5.47 billion, a swing into loss. Controlling-shareholder net profit over the last four quarters totals only KRW 7.82 billion, a thin earnings base.

Operating profit has been positive for only three quarters, from Q1 2026 onward, so the profitable run is still short.

Uncertainty in fleet plans and group dependence

On the plan to add three vessels by 2030, a Dongkuk Steel Group spokesperson said the mix of newbuilds and secondhand ships is still to be discussed, according to a October 29, 2026 report by BigData News.

The same report cited industry views that the timetable looks tight once financing and shipyard selection are factored in. Because the main cargo is group steel, earnings are tied to group volume flows.

10

Risk factors

Dependence on group cargo

If volumes of the Dongkuk Steel Group's steel, the main cargo, decline, sales in the shipping and transport segments could both be affected. The steel cycle and group production plans are variables the company cannot control directly. Transport and shipping together made up more than half of 2024 sales, so concentration is high.

Fleet investment burden and freight rate swings

Expanding the fleet requires capital for newbuild or secondhand vessels. If freight rates and vessel prices move, the profitability of new ships can change as well. The debt-to-equity ratio rose to 68.5% in 2025 from 63.0% in 2024.

Leverage and float structure

Total liabilities were KRW 203.1 billion at the end of 2025. Related parties led by the largest shareholder hold 55.30%, which limits the share of stock available for trading. This structure can affect trading liquidity and share price volatility.

12

Overall view

Intergis's disclosed results show sales rising for two consecutive years through 2025, while operating profit and net profit attributable to controlling shareholders both fell in 2025, a clear sign of weaker earnings strength.

The operating margin declined from 4.9% in 2022 to 2.8% in 2025, and the debt-to-equity ratio was 68.5% at the end of 2025. After an operating loss and a net loss attributable to controlling shareholders in Q4 2025, operating profit stayed positive from Q1 through Q3 2026.

Q3 2026 sales of KRW 234.6 billion and operating profit of KRW 7.75 billion, up 32.5% and 47.8% year on year, point to a recovery direction. However, controlling-shareholder net profit over the last four quarters totals only KRW 7.82 billion, a thin base, and the profitable run is still short.

On the fleet, the DK INOX brings the vessel count to eight and annual handling to 3.4 million tonnes, and plans to add three more small bulk carriers by 2030 have been reported without a set method.

The next checkpoints are whether the operating margin exceeds the 2.8% of FY2025 in the FY2026 annual report, due by March 31, 2027, and whether profitability holds in the Q1 2027 quarterly report.
