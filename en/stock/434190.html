02

Business structure

Talos is a small company founded in March 2005 that designs and manufactures military lithium-ion battery packs and chargers, and it listed on KONEX in October 2022. Its offerings are power-supply solutions including battery packs, chargers, battery management systems (BMS) and energy storage systems (ESS).

In 2024, TICN module sales were KRW 2.8 billion and TMMR battery and charger sales were KRW 7.6 billion. Together these two lines make up most of the KRW 12.4 billion in revenue that year.

Its customers are domestic defense companies such as Hanwha Systems and LIG Nex1. A 2022 report also said it supplied ESS units for the fire-control equipment of the Cheongung-II missile. Deliveries come through subcontracts with prime system integrators and competitive bids run by government agencies.

Military batteries must meet demanding stability requirements for vibration, shock and temperature, so a proven delivery record is central to competitiveness.

In 2022, KOSDAQ-listed Naintech bought a 60% stake in Talos for KRW 7.2 billion and took control. Naintech supplies secondary battery and display equipment. We could not confirm the current shareholder composition.

Talos is classified under machinery and equipment on KONEX, but its actual business is making military lithium-ion batteries and battery protection circuit electronics.

Its business model and scale differ greatly from machinery peers such as Doosan Enerbility and Rainbow Robotics, which makes direct comparison difficult.

On new businesses, expansion into drone and urban air mobility battery packs and charge-discharge test equipment has been cited. A press report also said it is preparing new businesses such as solid-state batteries. However, we found no disclosure showing that these businesses contribute to revenue. Headcount based on national pension enrollment is around 30, so the organization is small.

03

Recent trends

The standalone financial statements for fiscal 2025 (the 21st term) show revenue of KRW 14,548 million, operating profit of KRW 419 million and net income of KRW 486 million, with net income exceeding operating profit and per-share earnings remaining positive.

In 2024, revenue was KRW 12.4 billion and operating profit of KRW 0.7 billion marked a return to profit, after an operating loss of KRW 0.4 billion in 2023. In 2025, revenue grew 17.25% but operating profit fell 41.35%, and the operating margin was 2.88%. The same data show gross profit also declined from the prior year.

The cost structure explains part of the weaker profitability. According to a report sponsored by the Korea Exchange, the cost-of-sales ratio rose from 74.5% in 2022 to 81.8% in 2024, and the SG&A ratio rose from 7.4% to 12.3%. We could not confirm a detailed breakdown of 2025 costs.

At the end of 2025, total assets were KRW 22,063 million, total liabilities were KRW 5,516 million and total equity was KRW 16,547 million. The debt-to-equity ratio was 33.34%.

The share price has fallen sharply in 2026. The close on 2026-03-31 was KRW 4,565, unchanged from the previous day. The close on 2026-09-30 was KRW 2,695, and the year-to-date change was reported as -32.62%. The price on 2026-10-23 was KRW 2,400.

The close on 2026-10-27 was KRW 2,395 (-0.21% from the prior day), and the market capitalization of KRW 17.6 billion is similar in size to total equity of KRW 16,547 million at the end of 2025.

Trading is thin, which is typical of KONEX. Daily volume between 2026-09-22 and 2026-09-30 ranged from 12 to 1,057 shares. We could not confirm quarterly or half-year results for 2026 through searches.

Broker consensus estimates were also not available, so the direction of current-year earnings can only be judged from future filings.

04

Outlook

On the demand side, there is supportive evidence. A company overview states that as weapon and combat-support systems become more electronic, more systems need batteries, and military battery demand is expected to grow substantially. Defense demand is also strong abroad.

French defense group Thales said new defense orders rose 14% organically in the first nine months of 2026. That figure comes from a global major and does not directly show Talos's orders.

On supply contracts, related article headlines mention the start of military radio supply worth KRW 15 billion and selection as a 2024 excellent procurement company by the Defense Acquisition Program Administration. We could not confirm contract terms or when revenue will be recognized each year. Medium-term revenue visibility therefore has to be verified through future filings.

The key metric to watch is the operating margin. With the 2025 operating margin down to 2.88%, whether the cost-of-sales and SG&A ratios stabilize determines how much room operating metrics have to improve. Because revenue is small, fixed costs weigh heavily on profit.

A capital-market event is a possible transfer to KOSDAQ. Talos applied for a designated auditor in April 2023 to pursue a KOSDAQ transfer. A later press article carried a headline saying the KOSDAQ transfer was sluggish. As of 2026-10-27 the stock remains on KONEX, and no concrete transfer schedule was confirmed.

The contribution of new businesses such as drone and unmanned aircraft batteries and solid-state batteries to earnings has also not been verified.

05

Bull factors

Delivery record in defense power systems

Talos has developed and mass-produced battery packs and chargers for several military systems, including TICN, TMMR and KCTC. Its company history lists mass production of TMMR batteries and chargers for LIG Nex1 and emergency batteries for Hanwha Systems.

Military batteries require environmental testing and system-level validation, so a supplier with an established delivery record may hold an advantage in bids. This is a qualitative assessment of business stability.

Revenue growth continued in 2025

Revenue grew from KRW 10,125,976 thousand in 2023 to KRW 12,408,370 thousand in 2024 and KRW 14,548,416 thousand in 2025. Revenue growth exceeded 17% in both 2024 and 2025. This is in line with industry views that military battery demand is expanding. Whether that growth translated into higher profit is a separate question.

Low leverage on the balance sheet

The debt-to-equity ratio at the end of 2025 was 33.34%, with total liabilities of KRW 5.5 billion against total equity of KRW 16.5 billion. The modest debt load means the short-term borrowing pressure that small companies often face is relatively limited.

Naintech, a listed company, acquired the stake in Talos in 2022, and the parent's presence can be a reference point for funding and credibility. Cash-flow details differ across sources, so we did not draw a firm conclusion on them.

Bear factors

Falling operating margin

Operating profit fell 41.35% in 2025, and the operating margin narrowed from 5.77% to 2.88%. Between 2022 and 2024, both the cost-of-sales ratio and the SG&A ratio rose. If revenue growth keeps failing to carry through to profit, benefits of scale are hard to confirm. Operating profit is only about KRW 0.4 billion, so even small cost swings can change results significantly.

Customer and product concentration

Revenue in 2024 relied heavily on TICN modules and TMMR batteries and chargers. Its customers are also centered on a few defense system integrators such as Hanwha Systems and LIG Nex1. A change in the delivery schedule or volume of a specific system can swing quarterly results sharply. Whether new businesses can reduce this concentration has not yet been verified.

Low liquidity and limited information

Daily volume between 2026-09-22 and 2026-09-30 ranged from 12 to 1,057 shares. At such volumes, even small orders can move the price. Broker consensus is not available and quarterly information is limited, so there is little data to base judgments on. The year-to-date price change as of 2026-09-30 was -32.62%, and the stock has since traded around KRW 2,400.

07

Risk factors

Policy and Macro

Talos's revenue depends on defense budget execution and weapon-system procurement schedules. Deliveries come through subcontracts with system integrators and competitive government bids, so bid outcomes and order timing create earnings volatility.

Small companies may struggle to pass raw material and exchange-rate changes through to prices. Contract-level pricing terms are not public, so we could not confirm them.

Business and Profitability

The cost-of-sales ratio rose from 74.5% in 2022 to 81.8% in 2024. The 2025 operating margin was a low 2.88%. Military batteries carry high certification and testing costs and are often produced in small volumes across many variants, which makes cost control difficult.

New businesses such as drone batteries and solid-state batteries could add to R&D costs without near-term revenue.

Market and Governance

KONEX trading is thin, and disclosure requirements are narrower than on KOSDAQ. Talos applied for a designated auditor in April 2023 to pursue a KOSDAQ transfer, yet it remains on KONEX as of 2026-10-27. If the transfer is delayed or abandoned, expectations of better market access and liquidity could recede.

In addition, the relationship with Naintech, which acquired its stake in 2022, and the parent's own finances could influence Talos's decisions.

08

Overall view

Talos is a small KONEX-listed company specializing in military lithium-ion battery packs and chargers. In 2025, revenue rose 17.25% but operating profit fell 41.35%.

It is classified under machinery and equipment, but its actual business is battery manufacturing, so direct comparison with peers in that sector has limits.

Expanding military battery demand and a low debt ratio are supportive factors, while thin margins, customer and product concentration, and low trading volume are constraints.

The 2026-10-27 close of KRW 2,395 and market capitalization of KRW 17.6 billion are similar in size to total equity of KRW 16,547 million at the end of 2025.

Quarterly and half-year results for 2026 and broker consensus could not be confirmed, so future filings should be checked for any recovery in operating margin and for the timing of contract revenue recognition. This report is for information only, and investment decisions are left to the reader.
