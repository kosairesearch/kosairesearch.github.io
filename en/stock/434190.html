Defense Battery Niche, Thin Earnings Cushion

Talos, a military lithium-ion battery maker, stayed profitable in 2024 and 2025, but its shrinking margins and higher cost ratios leave a narrow earnings buffer.

02

Business structure

Talos designs and manufactures military lithium-ion battery packs, chargers, battery management systems and energy storage systems, with a focus on defense customers. Its headquarters is in Anyang, Gyeonggi Province, and it operates as a single entity without subsidiaries.

Its main products are TICN secondary battery modules used as backup power for military communication equipment, and batteries and chargers for next-generation military radios (TMMR). Reports put 2024 revenue from TICN modules at KRW 2.8 billion and from TMMR batteries and chargers at KRW 7.6 billion.

Together these two lines account for most of the KRW 12.4 billion in 2024 revenue. Products reach end users through defense system integrators, and subcontracts and government competitive tenders are the main routes to orders.

The company says it responds faster than large firms in a defense market built on small-volume, high-spec products. It holds patents on battery protection circuits, explosion-prevention sensors and modular electronic contactors, and obtained defense quality management certification in November 2023. In the civilian market, the company cites technical potential in drones, urban air mobility and EV batteries.

03

Recent trends

On a standalone basis, 2025 revenue was KRW 14.55 billion, operating profit KRW 419 million and net income KRW 486 million, as reported in the financial statements approved at the annual general meeting.

In 2024 the company swung to profit with revenue of KRW 12.41 billion, operating profit of KRW 720 million and net income of KRW 650 million. In 2023 revenue was KRW 10.13 billion with an operating loss of KRW 430 million. Revenue grew in 2025 from 2024, but operating profit fell to the KRW 400 million range.

The cost of sales ratio rose from 74.5% in 2022 to 81.8% in 2024, and the SG&A ratio increased from 7.4% to 12.3% over the same period. As a result, the operating margin fell from 18.1% in 2022 to 5.8% in 2024 and further to 2.88% in 2025. Earnings per share in 2025 were lower than in 2024.

Operating cash flow was negative KRW 1.51 billion in 2024. Equity stood at KRW 16.5 billion at the end of 2025, with a debt-to-equity ratio of 33.34%. The share price was KRW 4,565 as of December 2, 2025, with a one-year return of -21.97%. The current price of KRW 2,230 and market capitalization of KRW 16.4 billion are below that level.

04

Outlook

The main positive factor is the rise in military battery demand as defense equipment becomes more electrified. The company says demand flows through subcontracts with defense system integrators and government competitive tenders, and this structure is highlighted in its disclosures.

Management says its overseas expansion strategy is beginning to bear fruit and cites export diversification. Pressure remains, however, because the stock is still listed on KONEX. The company applied for an external auditor designation for a KOSDAQ transfer in April 2023, but it remained on KONEX as of March 2026.

If cost ratios keep rising and operating margins keep falling, the pace of earnings recovery could stay limited. Nine Tech holds 46.09%, and the free float ratio is 47.57%.

The next checkpoints are the cost of sales ratio and operating margin in the third-quarter 2026 report, and whether the KOSDAQ transfer review resumes.

05

Bull factors

Demand from military electrification

Disclosures highlight that more military systems need batteries as weapon and combat support systems become electrified. Military lithium-ion cells reach customers through subcontracts with system integrators and government competitive tenders, so demand runs within that procurement structure.

Reports note that military batteries must withstand harsh conditions, which makes vibration, shock and temperature stability a barrier to entry. The company points to its responsiveness in small-volume, high-spec defense markets as a strength.

Consecutive profits and low leverage

The company posted profits in both 2024 and 2025, with 2025 operating profit at KRW 419 million. Its debt-to-equity ratio was 33.34% at the end of 2025 and its reserve ratio was 349.08%, indicating a light financial burden.

Equity grew from KRW 6.35 billion at the end of 2022 to KRW 16.5 billion at the end of 2025, strengthening its equity base. Even as margins narrow, this balance sheet provides a cushion.

Overseas expansion and technology projects

The company says its overseas expansion strategy is starting to show in results and highlights export diversification. Next-generation power technology work continues, including a solid-state battery module project reported in July 2024.

The company also points to technical potential in civilian markets such as drones, urban air mobility and EV batteries. Revenue contribution from these areas is not broken out in disclosed figures.

06

Bear factors

Structural margin compression

The operating margin fell from 18.1% in 2022 to 5.8% in 2024 and then to 2.88% in 2025. Operating profit also dropped from KRW 720 million in 2024 to the KRW 400 million range in 2025. Revenue rose in 2025, yet profit declined, so top-line growth did not translate into earnings. This points to a problem in the cost and overhead structure.

Cost and overhead pressure

The cost of sales ratio rose from 74.5% in 2022 to 81.8% in 2024, while the SG&A ratio increased from 7.4% to 12.3%. A product mix centered on small-volume, high-spec items may limit pricing power and production efficiency.

Because customers are concentrated among defense system integrators, passing cost increases on to buyers may be difficult. If cost gains cannot be reflected in prices, margin recovery could be restricted.

Cash flow gap and thin trading base

Operating cash flow was negative KRW 1.51 billion in 2024, showing a gap between reported profit and cash generation. As a KONEX-listed stock, its trading base is thin, and daily turnover of KRW 168,705 points to liquidity constraints.

At a market capitalization of KRW 16.4 billion, small trades can move the price noticeably. The listing status has not changed since the 2023 transfer application, which leaves uncertainty.

07

Risk factors

Macro

Delivery timing may shift with defense budget allocation and the ordering schedules of system integrators. The overseas expansion strategy is exposed to local regulation, exchange rates and contract terms. Under government competitive tendering, revenue can swing sharply depending on bid outcomes. These external factors are largely outside the company's control.

Customer and product concentration

Revenue is concentrated in TICN modules and TMMR batteries and chargers, and sales depend on defense system integrators. A reduction in orders for a specific weapon program could cause large revenue swings. Nine Tech holds 46.09%, which concentrates ownership. Delays in major programs could ripple through overall results under this structure.

Liquidity and listing

Trading volume is small as a KONEX-listed stock, and market capitalization is about KRW 16.4 billion. The company applied for a KOSDAQ transfer in April 2023 but remained on KONEX as of March 2026. Because no timeline for a status change has been set, the timing of a broader trading base is uncertain. As a small-cap stock, its price can be volatile when markets are under stress.

08

Overall view

Talos is a defense-focused maker of military lithium-ion batteries and chargers that posted profits in both 2024 and 2025. Revenue grew to the KRW 14.5 billion range in 2025, and the debt-to-equity ratio was 33.34%.

On the other side, the operating margin fell from 18.1% in 2022 to 2.88% in 2025, with higher cost of sales and SG&A ratios cited as the main causes. Operating cash flow was negative in 2024, and the company's KONEX listing keeps trading volume small.

On the positive side, demand from military electrification and financial stability stand out, while on the negative side, margin compression, cost pressure and customer concentration are prominent.

The current price of KRW 2,230 and market capitalization of KRW 16.4 billion can be read in many ways when set against these operating indicators. The next checkpoints are the cost ratios and operating margin in the third-quarter 2026 report.
