Report overview

Losses persist as Lumens reshapes business and capital base

Lumens is concentrating on LED and automotive electronics and reorganizing its capital structure through a free capital reduction, an acquisition and convertible bonds, but operating and net losses continue, so whether profitability recovers is the central question.

02

Business structure

Lumens is an LED specialist that was founded in 1996 and listed on KOSDAQ in 2006.

It operates three business units: the LED unit supplies lighting, LCD backlight unit and mobile LEDs, the LGP unit makes light guide plates for TVs, lighting and automobiles, and the automotive electronics unit supplies automotive LED modules.

Based on the first three quarters of 2026, LED accounted for KRW 71.44 billion (64.7%), automotive electronics for KRW 24.47 billion (22.2%) and LGP for KRW 14.43 billion (13.1%). Together, LED and automotive electronics made up 86.9% of revenue, up 4.4 percentage points from 82.5% a year earlier.

The LGP business has concentrated production at its Vietnamese subsidiary to build cost competitiveness, according to the company's commentary. On technology, the company promotes its flip-chip solution and is developing micro LED and stacked RGB chips aimed at VR and AR commercialization.

The company is also moving into materials through the pending acquisition of control of a KOSDAQ-listed target that makes wig fibers and chemical materials and has overseas production and sales networks.

04

Earnings analysis

On a reported annual basis, revenue rose from KRW 166.3 billion in 2022 to KRW 171.2 billion in 2023, KRW 174.2 billion in 2024 and KRW 175.5 billion in 2025. That is three consecutive years of growth from 2023 to 2025, and 2025 was the largest year of 2022 to 2025.

Growth slowed, however, to 3.0% in 2023, 1.7% in 2024 and 0.8% in 2025. Profitability moved in the opposite direction. Operating profit reached KRW 2.74 billion in 2023, up 130.4% year on year, then turned into an operating loss of KRW 1.62 billion in 2024 and widened to an operating loss of KRW 5.03 billion in 2025.

The operating margin fell from 1.6% in 2023 to -0.9% in 2024 and -2.9% in 2025. Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 15.9 billion in 2022, KRW 4.01 billion in 2023 and KRW 8.84 billion in 2024, then became a net loss of KRW 6.65 billion in 2025.

Net profit including non-controlling interests matched these figures in each year. Because operating and net results often moved in different directions, non-operating items and tax effects appear to have had a large influence. The quarterly pattern is similar.

Revenue peaked at KRW 46.5 billion in the fourth quarter of 2025, fell to KRW 38.2 billion in the first quarter of 2026 and KRW 34.7 billion in the second quarter, then recovered to KRW 37.4 billion in the third quarter.

For the first three quarters of 2026, the company reported an operating loss of KRW 2.5 billion and a net loss of KRW 7.8 billion on a consolidated basis, according to the disclosure announced on November 13, 2026. The company attributed the wider net loss in the second half to a sharp fall in the exchange rate.

Third-quarter operating loss narrowed year on year to KRW 1.61 billion, but the net loss attributable to controlling shareholders widened to KRW 9.3 billion from KRW 139 million. The net loss attributable to controlling shareholders over the latest four quarters totaled KRW 9.92 billion.

Operating cash flow stayed positive at KRW 24.7 billion in 2023, KRW 4.33 billion in 2024 and KRW 14.9 billion in 2025. The debt ratio was 47.4% at the end of 2025, down from 51.0% in 2024.

05

Industry analysis

The LED business, which is Lumens' core, is facing oversupply and softer demand, according to the company's first-half 2026 commentary. The same commentary notes that first-half 2026 consolidated revenue fell 13.8% year on year and the operating loss shrank 26.8%.

Cost reduction therefore cushioned the short-term losses. Because LED made up the largest share of revenue in the first three quarters of 2026 at 64.7%, changes in LED pricing and volume have a large effect on the overall results.

The automotive electronics business, which supplies LED modules for vehicles, accounted for 22.2% of the same period's revenue and is tied to vehicle component demand. The LGP business serves TV, lighting and automotive demand, and the company is reshaping its cost structure by concentrating production in Vietnam.

In next-generation displays, the company points to micro LED and micro RGB technologies as growth drivers. However, no schedule showing when or how much revenue these technologies will generate has been disclosed.

Within the electronic components sector, the company is classified as an LED specialist with relatively high exposure to changes in set-product demand. Expansion into materials depends on completing the acquisition, so industry adaptation and diversification are proceeding in parallel.

06

Outlook

The company has said it will improve its financial soundness and profitability through medium-term restructuring and continued debt reduction. It plans to expand higher-value businesses such as next-generation micro LED and advanced display technologies to drive revenue growth and an improvement in profit and loss.

At the extraordinary shareholders' meeting, shareholders approved a free capital reduction to offset accumulated deficits and the appointment of a full-time auditor. The acquisition of control of the KOSDAQ-listed target is aimed at closing on December 7, 2026.

Cash paid to the seller falls from KRW 39.275 billion to KRW 37.275 billion. The KRW 2 billion reduction is offset by raising the seventh convertible bond issuance from KRW 18 billion to KRW 20 billion.

Payment for the seventh private convertible bond is due on December 11, 2026, and the conversion price will be fixed at the weighted average share price on the third trading day before payment if that price is higher.

The largest shareholder plans to buy shares on the open market from December 17, 2026 to January 15, 2027, and currently holds a 27.36% stake. Some reports give the end of that buying period as December 15, 2027, so the original disclosure needs to be checked.

The next periodic report is the 2026 annual report, which will show fourth-quarter results and the full-year operating margin.

08

Bull factors

Greater focus on core businesses

The share of LED and automotive electronics rose to 86.9% in the first three quarters of 2026, up 4.4 percentage points year on year. As revenue concentrates in the two core businesses, the direction of the restructuring is becoming clearer. The company says it is reshaping the LGP cost structure by concentrating production in Vietnam.

Plan to increase the largest shareholder's stake

The largest shareholder plans to buy about KRW 1.5 billion of shares on the open market starting December 17, 2026. Its current stake is 27.36%, which is expected to rise once purchases are completed. The company describes the move as a sign of accountable management.

Capital restructuring and early signs of profit recovery

The free capital reduction clears accumulated deficits, and the debt ratio fell to 47.4% at the end of 2025 from 51.0% in 2024. Operating profit turned positive at KRW 913 million in the first quarter of 2026, and net results were in the black for the first half. Because results are changing quarter to quarter, more quarters are needed before this can be read as a trend.

09

Bear factors

Continued net losses and currency sensitivity

The third-quarter 2026 net loss attributable to controlling shareholders widened to KRW 9.3 billion. The company cites the sharp fall in the exchange rate in the second half as the main cause, and the gap between an operating loss of KRW 1.61 billion and this larger net loss shows the influence of non-operating items.

Revenue decline and LED oversupply

Third-quarter 2026 revenue was KRW 37.4 billion, down 15.7% year on year. First-half 2026 consolidated revenue also fell 13.8% from a year earlier. The LED business, the largest unit, saw sales drop because of oversupply and softer demand, and cost cuts only partly offset the losses.

Funding burden from the acquisition and convertible bonds

The acquisition price is KRW 57.275 billion, of which KRW 37.275 billion is paid in cash. To reduce the cash burden, the seventh convertible bond issuance was increased to KRW 20 billion.

Depending on how the conversion price is fixed, the number of shares could increase and affect the value of existing shareholders' stakes.

10

Risk factors

Dilution and conversion price changes

The seventh private convertible bond's conversion price can be adjusted according to the weighted average share price scheduled for December 8, 2026. The disclosed provisional conversion price is KRW 1,142, but the final price depends on the share price before payment. Conversion would increase the number of shares outstanding and could dilute existing stakes.

Execution of the acquisition and business integration

The acquisition of control of the target is aimed at closing on December 7, 2026. The target makes wig fibers and chemical materials, a business that differs in character from Lumens' LED and display operations.

Results could diverge depending on how production facilities and overseas sales networks are combined after the deal.

Industry conditions and the exchange rate

The LED business faces oversupply and softer demand together, which could pressure both prices and volumes. Exchange-rate moves are the factor the company cites for the wider net loss in the second half of 2026.

Because no disclosed timing for revenue from next-generation businesses such as micro LED has been provided, the basis for near-term profit improvement is limited.

12

Overall view

Lumens is narrowing its focus to LED and automotive electronics and concentrating its revenue mix. Annual revenue has grown since 2023, but operating results turned into losses in both 2024 and 2025.

For the first three quarters of 2026, the cumulative operating loss was KRW 2.5 billion and the net loss was KRW 7.8 billion, and the net loss attributable to controlling shareholders over the latest four quarters totaled KRW 9.92 billion.

The free capital reduction, debt reduction and the largest shareholder's share-buying plan are positive factors for the capital base and confidence. On the other side, LED oversupply, a wider net loss tied to the exchange rate, and possible dilution from the acquisition and convertible bonds are burdens.

Whether the acquisition closes and how the seventh convertible bond's conversion price is fixed are the near-term checkpoints. When the 2026 annual report shows fourth-quarter and full-year operating margins, there will be more evidence on the direction of profit recovery.
