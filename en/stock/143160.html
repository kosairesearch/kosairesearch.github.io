IDIS: Operating Profit Recovers, Quarterly Net Income Volatile

IDIS grew revenue for three consecutive years through 2025 and for two consecutive quarters through Q3 2026, yet its operating margin of 6.5% in 2025 remained below the 10.3% of 2022, and Q3 2026 net income attributable to controlling shareholders fell to KRW 994 million.

02

Business structure

IDIS (Intelligent Digital Integrated Security) develops, manufactures and sells video storage devices such as DVRs and NVRs, cameras and video management systems (VMS).

As of 2022, product mix was recorders (DVR/NVR) at 25%, cameras at 48% and VMS and other products at 27%, while by region Korea accounted for 58%, Japan 16%, the United States 14% and Europe and other regions 12%.

In Korea, the company supplies its products through local security contractors and distributors and is expanding its system integration (SI) business. Its main growth route is integrated parking solutions for new apartment complexes, where it wins and delivers turnkey parking control and guidance systems.

In the first half of 2023 it acquired two companies, including a U.S. SI firm, to broaden its group. On the product side it highlights the React camera, which alerts intruders with light and sound, and SmartConnect, a cloud-based video management service.

Among competitors, Chinese video security vendors are cited as the largest globally, and after the U.S.-China trade dispute, preference for domestic products has grown. Among listed Korean video security companies, Hanwha Vision exceeded KRW 1 trillion in revenue for two consecutive years as of 2023.

IDIS has said it plans to launch AI solutions as the market shifts toward total-solution competitiveness rather than single products.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual revenue rose from KRW 269.9 billion in 2022 to KRW 278.2 billion in 2023 (up 3.1%), KRW 313.6 billion in 2024 (up 12.7%) and KRW 327.6 billion in 2025 (up 4.4%), increasing for three consecutive years from 2023 to 2025. Among the 2022–2025 years, 2025 was the largest and 2022 the smallest.

Operating profit fell from KRW 27.8 billion in 2022 to KRW 22.6 billion in 2023 (down 18.7%) and KRW 16.1 billion in 2024 (down 29.0%), then recovered to KRW 21.2 billion in 2025 (up 32.0%). For 2023, the company was reported to have attributed the operating profit decline to higher marketing-related costs.

The operating margin was 10.3% in 2022, 8.1% in 2023, 5.1% in 2024 and 6.5% in 2025, so in 2025, when revenue was above the 2022 level, the margin still did not return to its 2022 level.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 12.2 billion in 2022, KRW 20.2 billion in 2023 (up 65.7%), KRW 14.1 billion in 2024 (down 30.3%) and KRW 17 billion in 2025 (up 21.3%).

In 2023 operating profit fell while controlling-shareholder net income rose, so the two moved in opposite directions; the non-operating items and tax lines behind this gap are a point to check in the annual report notes.

Net income including non-controlling interests was KRW 14.3 billion in 2022, KRW 25.3 billion in 2023, KRW 12.9 billion in 2024 and KRW 23.3 billion in 2025, and the gap with the KRW 17 billion attributable to controlling shareholders in 2025 reflects non-controlling interests.

The subsidiary IDIS Power Tel had a 44.85% ownership stake held by IDIS in the 2022 quarterly report.

Quarterly revenue was KRW 78 billion in Q3 2025 and KRW 89 billion in Q4 2025 (up 14.1% quarter on quarter), dipped to KRW 78.8 billion in Q1 2026 (down 11.5%), then rose to KRW 83.8 billion in Q2 2026 (up 6.3%) and KRW 88.6 billion in Q3 2026 (up 5.8% quarter on quarter and up 13.7% year on year), increasing for two consecutive quarters.

Operating profit went from KRW 5.45 billion in Q3 2025 and KRW 6.47 billion in Q4 2025 (up 18.6%) to KRW 3.41 billion in Q1 2026 (down 47.4%), the low point, then rebounded to KRW 8.56 billion in Q2 2026 (up 151.3%) and reached KRW 7.97 billion in Q3 2026 (down 6.9% quarter on quarter, up 46.2% year on year).

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders in Q3 2026 was KRW 994 million, down 78.0% quarter on quarter and 80.4% year on year, which widened the gap with operating profit. Over the last four quarters, from Q4 2025 to Q3 2026, net income attributable to controlling shareholders totaled KRW 15 billion.

Revenue growth and the operating profit recovery appeared together, but the volatility at the net income level is large, so how the next periodic report explains that gap matters.

05

Industry analysis

The video security market has grown through the shift from analog equipment to IP-based network devices. In its 2022 quarterly report, the company said NVR demand was growing at an average of 38% per year.

A push to exclude Chinese-made CCTV has been cited as a growth factor in markets including the United States, the United Kingdom and Japan. A U.S. bill banning Chinese-made CCTV directly affects public procurement by government agencies and the military.

According to a 2023 securities report, IDIS could not take part in government bids because it lacked local references and production capacity, so its sales were limited to the private market. The same report said the company aimed to generate at least KRW 150 billion in public-sector sales.

In the first half of 2023, the company reported lower sales in the United States and Japan and weaker demand in the United States, Europe and the Middle East. In Korea, mandatory CCTV installation in operating rooms took full effect from September 2023, creating demand for hospital video solutions.

On competition, preference for domestic products is a supportive backdrop, but compared with Hanwha Vision, which exceeded KRW 1 trillion in revenue for two consecutive years as of 2023, IDIS's revenue scale is smaller.

Replacement demand from regulation is a tailwind, while volatile overseas demand and intensifying competition are headwinds.

Outlook

Through Q3 2026, the quarterly pattern shows two consecutive quarters of revenue growth alongside an operating profit recovery. Operating profit rose from KRW 3.41 billion in Q1 2026 to KRW 8.56 billion in Q2 and then eased slightly to KRW 7.97 billion in Q3.

Because Q3 net income attributable to controlling shareholders was only KRW 994 million, the gap with operating profit widened, so how non-operating items and tax lines are presented in the next periodic report is a point to check.

In Korea, mandatory operating-room CCTV, the expansion of turnkey parking solutions and cloud video management services are cited as growth drivers. Overseas, the key checkpoint is whether U.S. public procurement restrictions translate into actual bid participation.

Whether the planned AI solution launch broadens the product lineup is also worth tracking. The next periodic report is the 2026 annual report, and the checkpoint is whether the full-year operating margin moves above the 6.5% of 2025. The Q1 2027 quarterly report will show changes in overseas sales and product mix.

Based on the periodic-report data, operating profit rose 32.0% in 2025, confirming the direction of recovery, but quarterly results in 2026 remain volatile.

08

Bull factors

Three Years of Revenue Growth and Quarterly Recovery

Revenue rose for three consecutive years from 2023 to 2025, reaching KRW 327.6 billion in 2025. Revenue then increased for two consecutive quarters in Q2 and Q3 2026, with Q3 at KRW 88.6 billion. Operating profit also recovered from KRW 3.41 billion in Q1 2026 to KRW 8.56 billion in Q2. This pattern is the bullish point, since it shows demand continuing on a quarterly basis.

Exclusion of Chinese CCTV and Public Procurement Opportunity

The push to exclude Chinese-made CCTV in the United States, the United Kingdom and Japan has been cited as a growth factor for the video security segment. A U.S. bill banning Chinese-made CCTV directly affects public procurement by government agencies and the military.

The company has said it aims for at least KRW 150 billion in public-sector sales. If it builds local references and production capacity, bid participation becomes possible, which is the bullish argument.

Total Solutions and Mandated Domestic Demand

Turnkey parking control and guidance solutions and the SI business are expanding. Mandatory CCTV installation in operating rooms took full effect from September 2023, creating demand for hospital video solutions.

The cloud video management service and the planned AI solutions aim to strengthen total-solution competitiveness beyond single products. Domestic mandated demand and the expansion of total solutions are cited as bullish factors.

09

Bear factors

Delayed Recovery of the Operating Margin

The operating margin declined from 10.3% in 2022 to 8.1% in 2023, 5.1% in 2024 and 6.5% in 2025. Revenue in 2025 was higher than in 2022, yet the margin did not return to its 2022 level. In 2023, higher marketing-related costs were cited as the reason for the operating profit decline. The failure of the margin structure to recover is the bearish point.

Sharp Swings in Quarterly Net Income

In Q3 2026 operating profit rose 46.2% year on year to KRW 7.97 billion, but net income attributable to controlling shareholders fell 80.4% to KRW 994 million. Over the last four quarters, that net income totaled KRW 15 billion.

If the gap between operating profit and net income is not explained, questions about earnings quality remain. This volatility is a bearish point.

Overseas Demand Swings and Intensifying Competition

In the first half of 2023, the company said sales fell in the United States and Japan and demand weakened in the United States, Europe and the Middle East. With a high overseas sales share, demand swings have a large effect.

The largest global competitors are Chinese video security vendors, and Hanwha Vision exceeded KRW 1 trillion in revenue for two consecutive years. This competitive landscape can act as a bearish factor.

10

Risk factors

Overseas Policy and Procurement Risk

The U.S. push to ban Chinese-made CCTV could translate into public procurement opportunities, but limited local references and production capacity restrict bid participation. As of a 2023 report, the company had not taken part in government bids.

If policy develops differently from expectations, overseas sales plans are affected. This can be checked through the overseas sales share in the next periodic report.

Cost Structure and Margin Risk

The operating margin was 6.5% in 2025, below the 10.3% of 2022. In 2023, higher marketing-related costs were cited as the reason for lower operating profit. If selling and marketing costs rise alongside revenue, the margin can still decline. Cost control is a key variable for margin recovery.

Technology Competition and Product Transition Risk

The video security market is shifting toward total-solution competitiveness rather than single products. The company planned AI solution launches, but a delayed product transition could weaken its competitive position. Price and technology competition with Chinese vendors is also part of this risk. Checking the revenue contribution from new products is necessary.

Overall view

IDIS posted revenue of KRW 327.6 billion in 2025, rising for three consecutive years, and operating profit recovered 32.0% to KRW 21.2 billion. However, the operating margin remained at a low level, falling from 10.3% in 2022 to 6.5% in 2025.

In Q3 2026, revenue of KRW 88.6 billion and operating profit of KRW 7.97 billion both rose year on year, but net income attributable to controlling shareholders fell to KRW 994 million, widening the gap with operating profit.

Tailwinds include the push to exclude Chinese-made CCTV, public procurement opportunities, mandatory operating-room CCTV in Korea and the expansion of total solutions.

Headwinds include the slow recovery of the operating margin, large swings in quarterly net income, and overseas demand volatility alongside competition from Chinese vendors.

The next checkpoints are the operating margin and non-operating items in the 2026 annual report, and the overseas sales share in the Q1 2027 quarterly report. This report provides information only and contains no view on share price direction or any buy or sell opinion.
