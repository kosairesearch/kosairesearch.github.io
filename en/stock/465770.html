02

Business structure

STX Green Logis was spun off in September 2023 from the logistics and shipping division of the trading house STX and has been listed on KOSPI since that month. The company is headquartered in Busan and centers on bulk carriers that move coal, iron ore, fertilizer and grain.

Its revenue comes from two lines: time charter, which means leasing out all or part of a vessel, and voyage charter, which means carrying cargo directly. As of 2024 reporting, the fleet comprised two 32.5K-ton handy bulkers and one 171K-ton cape bulker.

It expanded through newbuilding and secondhand vessel acquisitions linked to ship financing. It also acquired a bulk-shipping affiliate that had roughly KRW 77.6 billion in annual revenue in 2022.

A May 2026 report said a plan to sell about 95% of that affiliate's stake, valued near KRW 50 billion, had been made public and that the company had received a letter of intent from a potential buyer. Customers named in a 2024 report included HD Hyundai Oilbank, Hyundai Glovis and HD Hyundai Cosmo.

The logistics business is still a small contributor to reported sales. The company competes with bulk operators such as Pan Ocean, Korea Line and Heung-A Shipping, and with a smaller fleet its results track freight-market moves closely.

04

Earnings analysis

On a disclosed annual basis, revenue rose from KRW 28.3 billion in 2023 to KRW 70.5 billion in 2024 (up 149.2% year on year) and to KRW 191.4 billion in 2025 (up 171.3%). Revenue has increased for two consecutive years, from 2024 through 2025, and 2025 was the largest year of the 2023 to 2025 period.

Operating profit went from KRW 1.85 billion in 2023 to KRW 7.16 billion in 2024 (up 287.5%), then turned into an operating loss of KRW 12.5 billion in 2025. The operating margin moved from 6.5% in 2023 to 10.1% in 2024 and to -6.5% in 2025.

Profit attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 1.44 billion in 2023 and KRW 25.6 billion in 2024 (up 1,678.3%), then turned into a net loss of KRW 37.3 billion in 2025. Net loss including non-controlling interests was KRW 37.7 billion in 2025, a different figure from the controlling-shareholder loss.

At the end of 2025, total equity was KRW 30.1 billion, of which KRW 23.5 billion belonged to controlling shareholders, and total liabilities were KRW 128.7 billion. The debt ratio rose from 142.9% in 2023 to 216.7% in 2024 and 427.8% in 2025.

Operating cash flow was KRW 548 million in 2023, KRW 24.1 billion in 2024 and KRW 16.5 billion in 2025, staying positive even in the loss year.

Quarterly figures show revenue of KRW 49.8 billion and an operating loss of KRW 7.01 billion in Q3 2025, followed by KRW 50.1 billion of revenue and a small operating profit of KRW 47.9 million in Q4 2025.

Q1 2026 revenue was KRW 15.9 billion with an operating loss of KRW 1.38 billion, and Q2 2026 revenue was KRW 14.8 billion with an operating loss of KRW 104 million.

Q3 2026 revenue was KRW 16.8 billion, up 13.3% from the prior quarter and down 66.3% year on year, with an operating loss of KRW 283 million, wider than in Q2 but narrower than the KRW 7.01 billion loss a year earlier.

The controlling-shareholder net loss in Q3 2026 was KRW 2.01 billion, and the net loss attributable to controlling shareholders over the last four quarters totals KRW 11.9 billion.

Because the revenue decline coincides with the planned affiliate stake sale, it is difficult to separate industry effects from company-specific factors.

05

Industry analysis

Shipping is closely tied to the global economy and carries most of the raw-material and trade volumes that move by sea, which is why it is classed as a core national industry.

Bulk freight rates rise and fall with demand for iron ore, coal and grain and with vessel supply, and earnings volatility depends on the mix between long-term charters and spot exposure. The long-term charter signed in June 2026 fixes part of revenue, while the remainder stays exposed to market freight rates.

One research report estimated that a single vessel run on the spot market could contribute roughly KRW 10 billion to KRW 20 billion of annual revenue.

The same report judged that a recovery to KRW 30 billion to KRW 40 billion a year is plausible, but that a return to the 2025 revenue level is difficult; this is the view of that report, not company guidance.

Among listed peers, Pan Ocean, Korea Line and Heung-A Shipping are the closest comparisons because they focus on bulk shipping. HMM, centered on container lines, differs in business model.

Logistics operators such as CJ Logistics and HANJIN Logistics are not direct comparisons given the company's small logistics contribution.

Because the 2025 swing to losses and the 2026 revenue decline overlap with the bulk-asset disposal, the company's position appears to depend on fleet composition decisions as much as on the freight cycle.

06

Outlook

The company said it expects the long-term charter signed in June 2026 to secure stable vessel operating income while strengthening its relationship with a global customer.

The contract runs from June 2, 2026 to June 2, 2027, and the actual period may be adjusted to between 11 and 13 months depending on whether the counterparty exercises its option.

The stake sale in the bulk-shipping affiliate does not appear to have closed yet; if completed, a large part of consolidated revenue would leave the group. On the fleet side, the company bought one 170,000-ton cape-size bulker, but a long-term charter for it had not been confirmed in the reports reviewed.

On the capital side, convertible bond conversion led to the listing of 47,348 additional common shares on July 28, 2026. The logistics push remains at the roadmap stage with little visible contribution to reported results.

Key checkpoints are whether the charter supports quarterly revenue in the coming reports, the terms and timing of the affiliate stake sale, and whether the cape vessel secures a long-term contract. No formal numerical guidance from the company has been confirmed.

08

Bull factors

A long-term charter sets a revenue floor

The company signed an approximately one-year long-term charter with a global customer in June 2026. The contract is worth about KRW 9.6 billion on a 12-month basis, roughly 5% of 2025 consolidated revenue.

Whether it can act as a floor while quarterly revenue sits between KRW 14.8 billion and KRW 16.8 billion is the point to watch.

Losses narrowed and a profitable quarter has occurred

The company posted a small operating profit of KRW 47.9 million in Q4 2025 after turning positive from a loss. The Q2 2026 operating loss narrowed to KRW 104 million from KRW 1.38 billion in Q1. The Q3 2026 operating loss of KRW 283 million was smaller than the KRW 7.01 billion loss a year earlier. The quarterly narrowing shows losses have come down at points in the period.

Fleet reshaping and cash-flow resilience

Operating cash flow stayed positive at KRW 16.5 billion in 2025, a loss year. The purchase of one cape-size bulker in 2026 widened the company's capacity to run larger vessels. Newbuilding and secondhand acquisitions tied to ship financing are structured to spread fleet expansion costs.

How quickly fleet changes translate into earnings, along with whether the cape vessel secures a long-term charter, is the next point to check.

09

Bear factors

Sharp revenue decline and consecutive operating losses

Q1 2026 revenue of KRW 15.9 billion fell 68.2% from the prior quarter. Q3 2026 revenue of KRW 16.8 billion was down 66.3% year on year. Operating losses have continued for three consecutive quarters from Q1 through Q3 2026. Compared with KRW 191.4 billion of revenue in 2025, the contraction in scale is pronounced.

Shrinking equity and heavy leverage

Total equity at the end of 2025 was KRW 30.1 billion against total liabilities of KRW 128.7 billion. The debt ratio rose from 142.9% in 2023 to 427.8% in 2025. The controlling-shareholder net loss turned from a profit in 2024 to a loss of KRW 37.3 billion in 2025.

With a KRW 11.9 billion controlling-shareholder net loss over the last four quarters, rebuilding equity through earnings is slow.

Revenue exit risk from the planned sale

One research report noted that completion of the sale would remove a large portion of consolidated revenue. The same report judged that a return to the 2025 revenue level would be difficult.

The bear-side argument is that a contract worth about KRW 9.6 billion on a 12-month basis is not enough to fill the revenue gap after a sale.

Risk factors

Disclosure and governance risk

The company made a debt-guarantee decision in November 2023 but disclosed it late in April 2026, which led to a notice of designation as an unfaithful-disclosure company. The final designation outcome was to be re-disclosed once confirmed. The late-disclosure record remains a point to monitor in assessing management transparency.

Share price volatility and trading restriction risk

The stock was designated an investment caution issue for one day on June 25, 2026 after concentrated selling by a small number of accounts. It was designated again on July 14, 2026 amid concentrated limit-up buy orders.

It was then designated an investment warning issue on July 21 and released on August 4, with re-designation possible if the price rises again. Trading can be suspended at the warning and risk stages.

Business reshaping and dilution risk

The affiliate stake sale is reported to be at the letter-of-intent stage, and its completion and terms have not been confirmed. Convertible bond conversion led to the listing of 47,348 additional common shares. The cape-size vessel has no confirmed long-term charter. Because sale terms, share supply and fleet plans could all change, the business direction remains fluid.

Overall view

STX Green Logis's revenue rose for two consecutive years from 2024 through 2025 to KRW 191.4 billion, but the company swung to an operating loss of KRW 12.5 billion and a controlling-shareholder net loss of KRW 37.3 billion that year.

Quarterly revenue in 2026 fell to between KRW 14.8 billion and KRW 16.8 billion, with operating losses in three consecutive quarters from Q1 through Q3 2026. Q4 2025 had turned to a small operating profit, and the Q2 2026 operating loss narrowed to KRW 104 million.

The long-term charter signed in June 2026 is worth about KRW 9.6 billion on a 12-month basis and provides a revenue floor, but completion of the affiliate stake sale could remove a large part of consolidated revenue.

The debt ratio rose to 427.8% against equity of KRW 30.1 billion, making financial capacity the central question. The unfaithful-disclosure designation notice and the investment caution and warning history remain matters to monitor.

The next checkpoints are the full-year results in the 2026 annual report, the effect of the charter in the Q1 2027 quarterly report, and whether the contract is renewed when it ends on June 2, 2027.
