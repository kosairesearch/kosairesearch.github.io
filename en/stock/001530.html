DI Dong IL: Aluminum Expansion Meets a Weaker Textile Core

DI Dong IL is weighing an aluminum foil expansion against a textile core whose sales have fallen for three straight years, and its Q3 2026 sales rose while operations swung back to a loss.

  1. 1

    On a filed basis, revenue fell for three consecutive years from 2023 to 2025, reaching KRW 601.4 billion in 2025.

  2. 2

    In 2025 the company posted an operating loss of KRW 1.54 billion and a net loss attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 9.73 billion.

  3. 3

    Q3 2026 revenue rose 21.4% year on year to KRW 177 billion, but the operating loss widened to KRW 1.23 billion.

  4. 4

    The aluminum business supplies battery foil to major domestic secondary battery makers, and a Cheongju line expansion is targeted for completion by late 2026.

  5. 5

02

Business structure

DI Dong IL was founded in 1955 as Dongil Textile and listed on KOSPI in 1964, and it now runs four business pillars: textile materials, aluminum, environmental plant, and furniture and cosmetics.

In the first half of 2024, segment revenue mix was 51.8% textile materials, 34.7% aluminum, 9.5% plant and environment, 3.2% furniture retail and wholesale, and 1.3% cosmetics, according to Business Post.

For 2023, the mix was 52.0% textile materials, 34.1% aluminum, 10.1% plant and environment, and 3.8% other, according to The Scoop.

The textile unit makes mainly commodity yarns and blended yarns for overseas and domestic apparel brands and industrial buyers; it contributes the most revenue but relatively less profit, according to one analysis.

The core aluminum subsidiary holds about a 90% stake and makes secondary battery cathode foil, supplying major domestic battery makers. A 2023 brokerage analysis said this subsidiary accounts for over 60% of group operating profit and ranks as the largest domestic cathode foil maker.

The environmental plant unit handles emission reduction equipment and plasma treatment systems for semiconductor and display fabs. Furniture, cosmetics, and the apparel venture with LACOSTE, which is jointly controlled 50:50 with LACOSTE, sit in the other category.

Among peers, aluminum is typically compared on capacity, sales scale and profitability, while textiles track global apparel pricing.

04

Earnings analysis

On a filed basis, revenue fell from KRW 913.7 billion in 2022 to KRW 682.9 billion in 2023 (down 25.3% year on year), then to KRW 651.7 billion in 2024 (down 4.6%) and KRW 601.4 billion in 2025 (down 7.7%), a decline for three consecutive years from 2023 to 2025.

Within 2022–2025, 2022 was the largest year and 2025 the smallest. Operating profit dropped from KRW 44.8 billion in 2022 to KRW 6.3 billion in 2023 (down 85.9%), recovered to KRW 7.81 billion in 2024 (up 23.9%), and turned into an operating loss of KRW 1.54 billion in 2025.

The operating margin moved from 4.9% in 2022 to 0.9% in 2023, 1.2% in 2024 and -0.3% in 2025. Net income attributable to controlling shareholders fell from KRW 53.8 billion in 2022 to KRW 3.24 billion in 2023 (down 94.0%) and KRW 1.6 billion in 2024 (down 50.6%), then became a net loss of KRW 9.73 billion in 2025.

Net income including non-controlling interests was a net loss of KRW 9.97 billion in 2025. Press coverage linked the 2023 weakness to falling demand from the global slowdown and rate hikes, which hit textile trading and spinning, while aluminum sales fell 22% year on year.

Cotton price declines helped the textile unit in 2024. The segment-level reasons for the 2025 swing to losses should be checked in the annual report's segment discussion.

Leverage rose: the debt-to-equity ratio went from 61.5% in 2022 to 96.9% in 2025, and at end-2025 total liabilities were KRW 504.3 billion against total equity of KRW 520.5 billion, of which KRW 518.4 billion belonged to controlling shareholders.

Operating cash flow shrank from KRW 39 billion in 2022 to KRW 3.94 billion in 2025. Quarterly, revenue rose from KRW 145.8 billion in Q3 2025 to KRW 169.8 billion in Q1 2026 and KRW 180.2 billion in Q2 2026, then reached KRW 177 billion in Q3 2026 (up 21.4% year on year).

Operating profit turned positive at KRW 672 million in Q1 2026 and reached KRW 3.35 billion in Q2 2026 (up 397.8% quarter on quarter), but Q3 2026 returned to an operating loss of KRW 1.23 billion, with the loss wider than a year earlier.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders went from KRW 6.16 billion in Q2 2026 to a net loss of KRW 5.53 billion in Q3 2026. The four quarters from Q4 2025 to Q3 2026 total KRW 430 million in net income attributable to controlling shareholders.

05

Industry analysis

Aluminum demand is tied to the secondary battery market. In 2023, reports said reduced EV subsidies in several countries, a economic slowdown, lower LME aluminum prices and excess inventory at EV makers together weighed on the aluminum subsidiary's results.

One analysis cited the EV slowdown as a factor behind weaker demand for industrial fabrics, so a slower EV market touches both the textile and aluminum units. The textile unit's weakness is mainly attributed to pricing pressure from U.S. retailers and weak demand in Europe, which reduced volumes.

Cotton prices act as a cost variable that drives textile margins, and lower cotton prices in the first half of 2024 were cited as a driver of improvement.

The environmental plant unit depends on semiconductor and display investment cycles, and one analysis said sales fell in 2025 after delays in Samsung's Pyeongtaek investment. On competition, a 2023 brokerage analysis rated the aluminum subsidiary ahead of major domestic peers on capacity, sales scale and profitability.

The battery foil business supplies a small set of major domestic battery makers, so those customers' production plans feed directly into volumes.

06

Outlook

The confirmed medium-term expansion plan is the Cheongju aluminum foil capacity increase. In 2023 the company signed an investment agreement with Cheongju City worth KRW 220 billion, and it said the new plant, with annual capacity of 30,000 tons, targets an 8% global share once complete.

According to a Yuanta-style brokerage estimate dated February 19, 2026 cited in a third-party summary, about KRW 110 billion will be invested to expand production lines from five to eight, with completion scheduled for late 2026.

The same analysis sees the expansion showing up in sales from 2027 and projects the aluminum segment rising to about 45% of total revenue.

The environmental plant unit saw sales fall in 2025 after delays in Samsung's Pyeongtaek investment, and the same analysis expected a recovery in 2026 tied to resumed Pyeongtaek P5 investment and new display customers.

The textile unit has recorded annual revenue declines in the filed figures, so how quickly its business mix changes remains the key question. Whether the Q3 2026 sales increase came from aluminum or from the environmental plant should be checked in segment disclosures.

The next checkpoints are the Cheongju completion and whether the added capacity shows up in aluminum sales in Q1 2027.

08

Bull factors

Aluminum foil expansion tied to batteries

The aluminum subsidiary supplies cathode foil to major domestic battery makers, and Yuanta's February 2026 estimate expected aluminum sales to expand as the Cheongju line goes from five to eight, targeted for late 2026. A 2023 brokerage analysis rated its capacity and sales scale ahead of major domestic peers.

Q2 2026 operating profit of KRW 3.35 billion improved on Q1 2026's KRW 672 million, which bulls point to as evidence of recovery.

Textile cost swings and a plant rebound

In the first half of 2024, lower cotton prices improved textile results, showing that a friendlier cost backdrop can lift the core business. Yuanta's February 2026 estimate expected environmental plant sales to recover from 2025 as Pyeongtaek P5 investment resumes and new display customers come on board.

Group revenue stayed above KRW 170 billion in each of Q1, Q2 and Q3 2026, which bulls cite as a sign of top-line resilience.

Asset value and buyback history

One brokerage analysis argues that owned land, largely carried at 2013 revaluation values, could be worth more today than its book value. In November 2023 the company retired 3.78 million treasury shares and disclosed a plan to buy back more.

Bulls note that sales or development of idle land could fund balance-sheet repair and investment in the aluminum business.

09

Bear factors

Weak demand in the textile core

Textile materials, which made up more than half of sales in the first half of 2024, have reportedly seen sales decline for three straight years since 2023. Analysts link this to U.S. retailer price cuts and weaker industrial fabric demand.

Whether the Q3 2026 sales gain came from textile recovery or aluminum growth can only be confirmed from segment disclosures.

Renewed losses and thin earnings

Q3 2026 brought an operating loss of KRW 1.23 billion and a net loss attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 5.53 billion, so losses returned. Net income attributable to controlling shareholders for the last four quarters totals only KRW 430 million, leaving little earnings cushion. The 2025 operating margin of -0.3% is the lowest of the four years reported from 2022 to 2025.

Balance-sheet strain and weaker cash generation

The debt-to-equity ratio rose from 61.5% in 2022 to 96.9% in 2025. Operating cash flow fell from KRW 39 billion in 2022 to KRW 3.94 billion in 2025, weakening internal cash generation.

At end-2025, total liabilities of KRW 504.3 billion were close in size to total equity of KRW 520.5 billion, which is cited as a financial risk.

10

Risk factors

Battery demand and aluminum prices

The aluminum business is exposed to secondary battery demand, EV subsidies and swings in LME aluminum prices. In 2023 weak results were tied to excess EV maker inventory and lower aluminum prices. If added capacity comes online during a demand slowdown, utilization could fall and fixed costs would weigh more heavily.

Textile demand and cotton costs

Textile materials are exposed to price pressure from U.S. retailers and weak European demand. Cotton prices drive margins through costs, and a rebound would erode the improvement seen in 2024. The effect of the EV slowdown on industrial fabric demand also remains a variable.

Leverage and investment burden

Rising leverage and shrinking operating cash flow point to heavier reliance on borrowing. The planned KRW 110 billion Cheongju investment could further reduce financial room. If losses persist, the expansion may depend more on external funding.

  2. By late March 2027 (annual report filing deadline)

  3. Mid-May 2027 (Q1 quarterly report deadline)

    Whether aluminum sales rise in Q1 2027 as added capacity comes in, and whether environmental plant sales recover. Also watch whether operating profit, which returned to a loss in Q3 2026, turns positive again.

12

Overall view

DI Dong IL was founded in 1955 as Dongil Textile and started in textiles before widening into aluminum, environmental plants and consumer goods.

On a filed basis, revenue fell from KRW 913.7 billion in 2022 to KRW 601.4 billion in 2025, a decline for three consecutive years, and 2025 turned into an operating loss and a net loss attributable to controlling shareholders.

Quarterly, Q3 2026 revenue rose to KRW 177 billion, but operating profit went from a positive Q2 back to an operating loss of KRW 1.23 billion in Q3. Bullish factors are the battery foil business, the Cheongju expansion, owned assets and the treasury share history.

Bearish factors are weak demand in the textile core, thinner earnings, and a higher debt-to-equity ratio alongside weaker operating cash flow.

On valuation, earnings multiples carry little meaning while the company is loss-making, so asset-based readings take center stage, and the market capitalization sits near the book value of the equity attributable to controlling shareholders. This report offers no investment rating or target price.

The next checkpoints are the Cheongju completion, the operating margin and debt ratio in the 2026 annual report, and aluminum sales in Q1 2027.
