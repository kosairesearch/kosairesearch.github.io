02

Business structure

HNK Machine Tool began in 1960 as Hankuk Geumsok Gongeop Sa and specializes in large machine tools; it listed on KOSDAQ in 2009. By segment, machine tools accounted for 91% of sales and other businesses for 9%, based on second-quarter 2026 revenue.

Its core products are vertical turning lathes (VTL) and CNC lathes for crankthrow casting, equipment for processing large, heavy workpieces.

A VTL machine contract with Powerstatic Solutions India, disclosed on January 14, 2026, is worth about KRW 32.6 billion, roughly 51% of 2024 sales, and the equipment is for nuclear projects involving steam turbines.

A vertical crankthrow CNC lathe contract with China's Hengli Engine (Dalian), disclosed in June 2026, is worth KRW 11.6 billion, and a CNC VTL contract with TEI, disclosed in August 2026, is worth KRW 7.8 billion.

A mid-size vertical lathe contract with TEI worth KRW 2.8 billion was disclosed in September 2026, and a lathe contract worth KRW 3.3 billion with a domestic bearing maker was signed in May 2026. The five contracts signed in 2026 total KRW 58.1 billion, and one Indian contract accounts for more than half of that.

An earlier analysis said the company has offices in the United States and China and a high share of overseas sales. Because it is a small player specialized in large machine tools, one or two large orders can move quarterly results substantially.

04

Earnings analysis

On a reported basis, annual sales rose from KRW 42.3 billion in 2022 to KRW 43.7 billion in 2023 (up 3.4% year on year) and reached KRW 63.7 billion in 2024 (up 45.7%), the largest year of 2022 to 2025.

Sales fell 52.0% to KRW 30.6 billion in 2025, the smallest year in the period, and the 2025 figures are on a standalone basis. The company cited lower orders as the main reason for the decline in sales and operating profit.

Operating profit moved from KRW 1.52 billion in 2022 to an operating loss of KRW 7.02 billion in 2023, returned to an operating profit of KRW 2.07 billion in 2024, and then turned into an operating loss of KRW 7.53 billion in 2025. The operating margin was 3.6% in 2022, -16.0% in 2023, 3.3% in 2024 and -24.6% in 2025.

Even in 2024, the year of largest sales, the margin was only 3.3%, so the sales expansion did not translate into margin gains. Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders turned to KRW 1.5 billion in 2024 but swung back to a loss of KRW 8.09 billion in 2025.

Operating cash flow went from an inflow of KRW 8.66 billion in 2024 to an outflow of KRW -9.14 billion in 2025.

Quarterly sales were KRW 11.4 billion in the third quarter of 2025, then KRW 7.31 billion in the fourth quarter (down 36.1% from the prior quarter) and KRW 4.49 billion in the first quarter of 2026 (down 38.6%), which marked the low point.

Sales recovered to KRW 8.36 billion in the second quarter of 2026 (up 86.2% quarter on quarter) and KRW 9.08 billion in the third quarter (up 8.6%), though the third quarter was 20.6% lower than a year earlier.

The operating loss narrowed to KRW 868 million in the second quarter of 2026 but widened to KRW 5.04 billion in the third quarter, so the sales recovery and the loss trend moved in opposite directions.

Net losses attributable to controlling shareholders over the last four quarters totaled KRW 8.83 billion, a sizable amount against equity attributable to shareholders of KRW 34 billion at the end of 2025.

05

Industry analysis

Orders for machine tools move with capital spending in power generation, construction and plant industries. According to a corporate commentary on the Wisereport company monitor, lower capital spending led to lower sales and higher cost burdens, which hurt profitability.

Because of the nature of large machines, one or two orders can determine annual sales, as the 2024 surge and the 2025 drop show. An earlier analysis also noted that raw material prices rose steadily over recent years and weighed on profitability.

The same commentary said defense is expected to keep sustained demand as a high value-added industry and linked the company to automotive, power generation, shipbuilding, wind power and machinery. If the end-market broadens toward power generation and nuclear, demand for turbine-related equipment could align with it.

A high share of overseas sales means results may be affected by overseas order timing and exchange rates. By market capitalization it is a small company within the machinery and equipment sector, and its customer concentration is relatively high given the limited number of buyers for large equipment.

Bull factors

Large Contract Backlog

Five supply contracts signed in 2026 total KRW 58.1 billion. The Powerstatic VTL contract, about KRW 32.6 billion, equals roughly 51% of 2024 sales and runs through June 10, 2027.

Sales rising in two consecutive quarters after the first-quarter 2026 low supports the view that contract progress is starting to appear in results.

Losses Narrowed Temporarily

The operating loss in the second quarter of 2026 was KRW 868 million, narrower than KRW 1.58 billion in the fourth quarter of 2025 and KRW 1.52 billion in the first quarter of 2026.

Net losses attributable to controlling shareholders also shrank from KRW 1.68 billion in the third quarter of 2025 to KRW 1.38 billion in the second quarter of 2026. A quarter in which losses narrowed alongside a sales recovery is the bull case for sensitivity to sales volume.

Largest Shareholder Adds Stake

The combined stake of the largest shareholder, CEO Ha Jong-sik, and related parties rose 0.93 percentage points to 54.97% through open-market purchases, according to a September 17, 2026 filing. The filers stated the purpose of holding is influence over management. Insiders buying company shares directly can be read as a signal of their view of the business.

Bear factors

Sales Volatility and Limited Recovery

Sales fell 52.0% to KRW 30.6 billion in 2025, and the company cited lower orders as the main cause. Third-quarter 2026 sales of KRW 9.08 billion were still 20.6% below the year-earlier level, so the recovery has not yet reached the 2025 pace.

Sales fell from KRW 63.7 billion in 2024 to less than half that level within a year, showing how directly order swings reach results.

Operating Loss Widens Again

The third-quarter 2026 operating loss of KRW 5.04 billion was the largest among the five straight loss-making quarters since the third quarter of 2025. Sales rose from the prior quarter, yet the loss widened, so the sales recovery did not translate into better profit. Net loss attributable to controlling shareholders also grew to KRW 4.45 billion.

Balance Sheet Strain and Cash Outflow

The debt ratio was 109.1% at the end of 2025, with total liabilities of KRW 37.1 billion close to equity of KRW 34 billion. Operating cash flow swung from an inflow of KRW 8.66 billion in 2024 to an outflow of KRW -9.14 billion in 2025.

If net losses of KRW 8.83 billion over the last four quarters continue, how fast equity declines becomes an important point to track.

Risk factors

Order Concentration and Execution

Of the five contracts signed in 2026, the Powerstatic contract alone accounts for more than half. A delay or cancellation in execution would create a revenue gap that shows up directly in quarterly results. A high share of overseas buyers also exposes the company to local conditions and payment term changes.

Ownership Structure and Float

With the combined stake of the largest shareholder and related parties at 54.97%, the number of shares available to general investors is limited. If open-market purchases by the largest shareholder continue, the free float will shrink further. Changes in stake are disclosed through major shareholding reports, so the trend can be tracked.

Raw Material Costs

An earlier analysis pointed out that raw material prices rose steadily over recent years and weighed on profitability. A corporate commentary also noted that sales declines and higher cost burdens can arrive together during periods of lower capital spending. If these two factors overlap, narrowing the operating loss could be delayed.

Overall view

HNK Machine Tool's sales rose to KRW 63.7 billion in 2024 and then fell to KRW 30.6 billion in 2025, and operating and net losses have continued since. Five supply contracts signed in 2026 total KRW 58.1 billion, with periods running through August 15, 2027.
