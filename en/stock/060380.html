Quarterly Profit Recovers, Annual Earnings Power Still Under Test

02

Business structure

Dongyang S·TEC operates as a processing and distribution center for hot-rolled steel supplied by POSCO, cutting and processing hot-rolled coils, heavy plates and thin plates for sale.

Based on the first-quarter 2026 quarterly report, the parent company's wholesale sales of hot-rolled and other products made up 37.95% of sales, followed by manufacturing of hot-rolled and other products at 30.11%, other coil-related sales at 5.34% and toll processing at 0.89%.

Subsidiaries contributed 13.14% from construction and services, 9.26% from metal material processing and 3.31% from construction and steel structures. The company is shifting its emphasis from general steel distribution toward precision processed materials and high-performance construction materials.

Its flagship POS-H is a seismic-resistant welded H-beam certified under POSCO INNOVILT, which the company says can cut steel usage by roughly 10 to 20% versus conventional H-beams.

BH walers, high-strength members that support temporary earth-retaining structures in underground civil works, are also being developed as a core product, and the company says they can shorten related construction periods by more than 60% versus existing methods.

A non-standard material plant with a capacity of 80 tons per month was built at the Pohang factory in December 2025, and a November 2026 report said it has begun full operation.

Demand mainly comes from construction sites, and the company is working with POSCO and structural design firms to apply POS-H from the design stage. Unlike large steelmakers that run their own rolling mills, Dongyang S·TEC processes POSCO hot-rolled products, which leaves its raw material sourcing dependent on POSCO. Affiliates extend its scope into waste treatment and steel structure construction.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual sales moved from KRW 232.5 billion in 2022 to KRW 210.5 billion in 2023 (down 9.5% year on year) and KRW 191 billion in 2024 (down 9.2%), then rose to KRW 197.8 billion in 2025 (up 3.5%). 2022 was the largest year in the 2022 to 2025 span and 2024 the smallest.

Operating profit fell from KRW 6.76 billion in 2022 to KRW 5.99 billion in 2023, KRW 3.15 billion in 2024 and KRW 1.81 billion in 2025, declining for three consecutive years from 2023 through 2025. The operating margin narrowed from 2.9% in 2022 to 0.9% in 2025.

In 2025 sales grew 3.5% while operating profit dropped 42.5%, so the top line and profit moved in opposite directions. The company attributed the decline to weak steel market conditions that reduced gross profit and operating profit.

Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders fell from KRW 4.97 billion in 2022 to KRW 1.36 billion in 2025, also declining for three consecutive years from 2023. Net profit including non-controlling interests dropped from KRW 5.82 billion in 2022 to KRW 1.96 billion in 2025.

Operating cash flow was KRW 22.1 billion in 2022, KRW 2.53 billion in 2023 and KRW 15.9 billion in 2024, then turned to -KRW 10.3 billion in 2025.

The debt ratio fell from 78.8% in 2022 to 49.8% in 2024 before rising to 65.0% in 2025, when total equity was KRW 120.5 billion (KRW 113.8 billion attributable to controlling shareholders) and total liabilities were KRW 78.4 billion.

Quarterly, the company recorded an operating loss of KRW 79.54 million in the fourth quarter of 2025, then returned to operating profit of KRW 12.92 million in the first quarter of 2026, although the net loss attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 28.16 million kept it in the red.

In the second quarter of 2026 sales reached KRW 60.9 billion, up 18.3% from the prior quarter, with operating profit of KRW 2.54 billion and net profit attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 2.25 billion, returning to profit.

The third quarter of 2026 recorded sales of KRW 61.1 billion (up 11.1% year on year), operating profit of KRW 2.48 billion (up 160.9% year on year) and net profit attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 1.53 billion (up 184.8% year on year).

Sales rose for two consecutive quarters in the second and third quarters of 2026. Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders over the latest four quarters, from the fourth quarter of 2025 to the third quarter of 2026, totals KRW 3.67 billion.

Industry analysis

Dongyang S·TEC's earnings are highly sensitive to steel market conditions, and the company cited weak steel market conditions as the reason operating profit fell in 2025. A company official said that intensifying price competition in the steel market has made simple distribution insufficient for securing added value.

In response, the company is upgrading its business toward processing technology and product differentiation that help customers cut costs and improve process efficiency.

On the demand side, construction sites' need to reduce construction costs and shorten schedules supports the case for products such as POS-H and BH walers. The company also expects order opportunities for temporary structural materials in the multipurpose dam safety reinforcement project it plans to pursue.

Within the same steel and metals sector, large steelmakers such as Hyundai Steel Company and Dongkuk Steel Mill operate alongside processing and materials companies such as POSCO M-Tech and Husteel.

Unlike large steelmakers that run their own rolling mills, Dongyang S·TEC concentrates on processing POSCO hot-rolled products, so changes in raw material prices and selling prices have a relatively large effect on its profit.

Sales and operating profit rose together from the second quarter of 2026 after the 2025 decline, but quarterly data alone cannot separate the contribution of a steel market recovery from that of product mix changes.

06

Outlook

In a November 2026 report, the company said it aims to grow precision processed materials and high-performance construction materials as core growth drivers and to build an integrated supply system covering everything from underground civil works to above-ground structures.

To support this, it plans to expand supply of non-standard materials to grow its processing business and to secure differentiation from general steel distribution.

Through package sales, it performs everything from 3D design to factory fabrication and on-site installation, and plans to widen its order scope to large building projects and social overhead capital (SOC) projects.

It is working with POSCO and structural design firms to apply POS-H from the design stage, emphasizing construction cost savings and shorter schedules.

Production is anchored at Pohang, Jinchon and Daejeon, with Pohang's hot-rolled and heavy plate processing lines linked to the section steel fabrication lines at Jinchon and Daejeon to meet material demand by construction stage.

In temporary structural materials, the company expects order opportunities from the multipurpose dam safety reinforcement project it plans to pursue. The key point to verify is whether the start of full operation at the Pohang non-standard material plant leads to higher sales and operating profit.

Operating profit in the second and third quarters of 2026 each stayed in the mid-KRW 2 billion range, so whether the next quarters hold at a similar level is the key question.

The 2026 annual report, the next regular filing, will confirm fourth-quarter results and show whether the annual operating margin moved away from 0.9% in 2025.

Bull factors

Quarterly earnings recovery

Operating profit was KRW 2.54 billion in the second quarter of 2026 and KRW 2.48 billion in the third quarter, and third-quarter operating profit rose 160.9% year on year.

Sales increased for two consecutive quarters over the same period, and net profit attributable to controlling shareholders turned positive in the second quarter and reached KRW 1.53 billion in the third. The shift after the fourth-quarter 2025 loss can be read as a change in the direction of the earnings profile.

Cost competitiveness of processed products

POS-H carries POSCO INNOVILT certification, and the company says it can reduce steel usage by about 10 to 20% compared with standard H-beams.

The company says BH walers can shorten related construction periods by more than 60% versus existing methods, which positions them to ease schedule pressure at construction sites.

With the Pohang non-standard material plant now in full operation, the production base to raise the share of processed products is also in place.

Improving financial capacity

Borrowings fell from KRW 48.9 billion at the end of 2025 to KRW 40.9 billion at the end of March 2026, and the net debt ratio declined from 36.29% to 30.15%, according to the quarterly report. Total equity of KRW 120.5 billion at the end of 2025 provides an equity base against total liabilities of KRW 78.4 billion.

The company described its treasury share sale as a measure to strengthen financial soundness and secure facility funds.

Bear factors

Long-running erosion of profit

The operating margin narrowed from 2.9% in 2022 to 0.9% in 2025, and operating profit declined for three consecutive years from 2023 through 2025. In 2025 operating profit fell 42.5% even though sales rose 3.5%.

The company attributed the decline to weak steel market conditions, and a structure that depends heavily on the steel cycle has amplified earnings swings.

Cash generation and debt burden

Operating cash flow swung from KRW 15.9 billion in 2024 to -KRW 10.3 billion in 2025. The debt ratio rose from 49.8% in 2024 to 65.0% in 2025, and total liabilities at the end of 2025 stood at KRW 78.4 billion.

The fourth-quarter 2025 operating loss and the first-quarter 2026 net loss attributable to controlling shareholders show that quarterly profit has not yet stabilized.

Wholesale weight and raw material dependence

According to the first-quarter 2026 quarterly report, wholesale sales of hot-rolled and other products make up 37.95% of sales, a larger share than manufacturing. Because the company sources hot-rolled products from POSCO, the gap between raw material costs and selling prices determines its margins.

With price competition intensifying in the steel market, the profit effect of the shift toward processing will take time to show up in earnings.

Risk factors

Steel market and pricing competition

The company itself cited intensifying price competition in the steel market as a core variable for its business. If market conditions weaken, a pattern like 2025, when sales rose but operating profit fell, could recur. Whether the strategy of raising the share of processed products absorbs this risk remains to be confirmed.

Dependence on POSCO supply and new plant ramp-up

Dongyang S·TEC depends on POSCO for raw materials as a hot-rolled steel service center. The Pohang non-standard material plant was built in December 2025 and entered full operation in November 2026, so whether the capital invested in it converts into higher sales is the key question. Any change in sourcing terms or supply structure could affect the business as a whole.

Liquidity and ownership concentration

Market capitalization is small, and the largest shareholder and related parties hold 55.75% (per fnguide, last change dated January 20, 2023), which limits the free float. With a small earnings base, quarterly results can have a large effect on the share price and multiples. The swing to negative operating cash flow in 2025 also adds pressure on liquidity management.

Overall view

Dongyang S·TEC processes and distributes hot-rolled steel supplied by POSCO, and its earnings power has weakened since 2022, with the operating margin falling from 2.9% to 0.9% and net profit attributable to controlling shareholders declining for three consecutive years.

In 2026, quarterly results have shown a recovery, with sales rising for two consecutive quarters in the second and third quarters and operating profit reaching the mid-KRW 2 billion range.

Strengths include the shift toward processed products such as POS-H and BH walers, the start of full operation at the Pohang non-standard material plant, and greater financial room from lower borrowings.

Weaknesses include the 2025 swing to an operating cash outflow of -KRW 10.3 billion, the rise in the debt ratio to 65.0%, and the losses recorded in the fourth quarter of 2025 and the first quarter of 2026.

External variables such as steel market conditions, pricing competition and reliance on POSCO supply remain sources of earnings volatility.

With net profit attributable to controlling shareholders totaling KRW 3.67 billion over the latest four quarters, the earnings base is small, so quarterly swings weigh heavily on both reported results and multiple comparisons.

The next points to confirm are whether operating cash flow recovers in the 2026 preliminary annual results and the 2026 annual report, and whether the operating margin moves away from the 2025 level.
