02

Business structure

Ihsungcni focuses on routine maintenance of instrumentation and control systems at nuclear power plants. The company was created through a spin-off in October 2016 and is headquartered in Gijang-gun, Busan.

According to the business report filed in March 2026, its operations fall into two segments: nuclear and electrical and instrumentation construction.

The nuclear segment covers I&C routine maintenance, design and manufacture of digital distributed control systems and plant control systems, and commissioning and tuning services. The electrical and instrumentation construction segment handles design and construction of electrical and instrumentation works.

In competition, press reports indicate that only eight companies in Korea are qualified to bid for nuclear I&C routine maintenance. The designated sponsor stated that the company held about 22% of nuclear I&C maintenance contract value in 2020.

In a Hanul Unit 2 I&C maintenance consortium contract running from August 2020 to July 2023, the business report lists the company's share at 12.90%. The 2021 listing report also noted that a lost bid in the measurement segment reduced revenue.

Because the nuclear segment is awarded through bidding among a small group of firms, contract outcomes weigh heavily on results.

Recent trends

On a consolidated basis, revenue for 2025 was KRW 19,380,597,732, compared with KRW 13,183,940,195 in 2024. Gross profit rose sharply to KRW 1,755,747,000 from KRW 419,555,192, while selling and administrative expenses edged up to KRW 6,348,823,232.

As a result, the operating loss narrowed to KRW 4,593,076,232 from KRW 5,679,188,977. Financial costs fell to KRW 2,147,369,123 from KRW 4,667,946,169, which helped shrink the net loss to KRW 5,740,186,724 from KRW 9,848,690,100. The net loss attributable to owners of the parent was KRW 5,053,630,227.

Consolidated total equity at year-end 2025 was KRW -10,872,567,101, deeper than KRW -5,486,686,238 a year earlier, and the current ratio slipped to 30.91% from 33.72%. Operating cash outflow widened to KRW 6,056,586,405, while short-term borrowings increased by KRW 7,939,000,000 and KRW 6,176,543,110 was repaid.

On a standalone basis, 2025 revenue was KRW 5,885 million, the operating loss KRW 2,037 million, and the net loss KRW 2,625 million, as disclosed on March 31, 2026. Press reports said the share price closed flat at KRW 5,010 as of 4:10 p.m. on March 31, 2026.

The current price is KRW 5,590, market capitalization is KRW 15.9 billion, and trading value at the time of writing is KRW 0.

Outlook

Nuclear I&C routine maintenance revenue depends on plant operating status and the structure of maintenance contracts. The small number of companies qualified to bid in Korea can help the company sustain existing contracts.

However, the company has previously seen revenue fall after losing a bid, so contract outcomes drive earnings volatility. On profitability, the consolidated operating loss continued in 2025, so whether gross profit gains persist is the key point to track.

On the balance sheet, total equity is negative, so external funding will shape financial stability. In 2025, financing inflows included KRW 2,496,199,260 of redeemable preferred stock liabilities and KRW 500,000,000 of bonds with warrants, which changed the capital mix.

Press reports have mentioned a plan to move the listing to KOSDAQ. The next financial disclosure point is the business report for fiscal 2026, when the direction of consolidated equity and operating results will indicate the trajectory of the financial structure.

On the business side, public maintenance order flow and changes in the company's share of new contracts are the items to monitor.

The business report lists a 12.90% share in a Hanul Unit 2 I&C maintenance consortium contract. Because routine maintenance is tied to plant operations, new entrants face difficulty, which is cited as the core bullish argument.

Revenue growth and gross profit recovery in 2025

Consolidated revenue for 2025 was KRW 19,380,597,732, up from KRW 13,183,940,195 in 2024. Gross profit improved to KRW 1,755,747,000 from KRW 419,555,192. The gross profit increase was far larger than the rise in selling and administrative expenses, which points to a better operating structure. Whether contract volume can sustain this trend is the next point to confirm.

Narrower losses and lower financing costs

The net loss also shrank to KRW 5,740,186,724 from KRW 9,848,690,100. Whether the narrowing continues is the central signal for financial improvement.

Risk factors

Macro

Demand for nuclear maintenance depends on national energy policy and reactor operating plans. A shift in policy direction could affect the scale and duration of routine maintenance orders. Interest rates and funding conditions could also raise borrowing costs.

Industry and bidding

Nuclear I&C maintenance is split among a small group of firms through bidding. A lost bid has previously been linked to lower revenue, as noted in the listing-period report. Changes in client schedules or wider wins by competitors would add to earnings volatility.

Financial and liquidity

Total equity is negative while operating losses and cash outflows continue. Funding relies on external borrowing and redeemable preferred stock issuance. Thin trading liquidity on KONEX may also constrain share trading conditions.
