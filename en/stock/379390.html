02

Business structure

Ihsungcni's core activities are routine maintenance at nuclear power plants and instrumentation and control maintenance at thermal power plants. Under the exchange's classification it belongs to architectural technology, engineering and related technical services, and it listed on Konex on June 4, 2021.

The IT and software tag in market data therefore sits uneasily with the actual nature of the business. The company provides maintenance and commissioning solutions for nuclear plant instrumentation and control equipment, and was described as one of eight domestic firms able to win such routine maintenance work.

Korea Hydro & Nuclear Power, which oversees the nuclear industry, evaluates contractors' execution capability strictly, which makes entry difficult for newcomers.

Its award history includes the Hanul Unit 2 instrumentation and control maintenance contract in July 2020, maintenance of the ex-core neutron flux monitoring system at Shin-Kori Units 1 and 2 in August 2022, and plant control system maintenance at the same units in September 2023, and it also handled routine maintenance at Kori Unit 1, the first domestic reactor to be decommissioned.

Its management includes CEO Lee Myung-chun, a former Korea Hydro & Nuclear Power executive, which is often cited as an advantage in client relationships.

The consolidated subsidiary is a fusion-fuel materials company that holds a tritium handling and sales permit, and it also operates military training systems and tritium-based self-luminous products. That subsidiary was reported to have posted revenue of KRW 3 billion in 2023, more than five times the prior year.

No confirmed figures on segment revenue mix were found, so this is described qualitatively only. The competitive structure is an oligopoly with a limited number of eligible contractors, but heavy dependence on a few clients such as Korea Hydro & Nuclear Power is a structural feature.

Recent trends

The filed standalone financial statements for 2025 show total assets of KRW 24,145 million, total liabilities of KRW 17,171 million, total equity of KRW 6,974 million, revenue of KRW 5,885 million, an operating loss of KRW 2,037 million and a net loss of KRW 2,625 million.

On a consolidated basis for the same year, revenue was KRW 19,381 million, with an operating loss of KRW 4,593 million and a net loss of KRW 5,740 million. Because the consolidated operating loss is larger than the standalone one, losses at the subsidiary appear to be reflected in the group results.

According to NICE D&B audit data, standalone revenue fell from KRW 13.48 billion in 2023 to KRW 6.91 billion in 2024 and KRW 5.89 billion in 2025. In the same data, net income of KRW 0.99 billion in 2023 turned into a net loss of KRW 2.21 billion in 2024 and a net loss of KRW 2.63 billion in 2025.

Compared with revenue of KRW 21.92 billion in 2020, the year before listing, the revenue base has shrunk considerably. The cause of the negative consolidated equity could not be confirmed in this research.

At the annual general meeting on March 31, 2026, shareholders re-elected CEO Lee Myung-chun as an inside director and newly elected Ahn Kwang-hwi as a non-executive auditor. The shares closed flat at KRW 5,010 on March 31, 2026, and the close on October 27, 2026 was KRW 5,610.

In coverage from July 2024, the close was KRW 9,590 and market capitalization KRW 27.2 billion. Results after the first half of 2026 could not be confirmed in this research.

Outlook

In July 2024 the company said that power demand growth driven by artificial intelligence, decarbonization and energy security concerns were pushing nuclear expansion, and that domestic promotion of the nuclear industry, including small modular reactors, was accelerating.

It pointed to the working draft of the 11th Basic Plan for Electricity Supply and Demand, which sets the 2030 national greenhouse gas target and 2050 carbon neutrality as top goals, and expected growth for nuclear-related companies.

This is the company's own view, and whether it translates into maintenance order volume and profitability must be verified in future filings.

The company has been widening its scope to decommissioning of ageing reactors and the tritium market, and coverage noted that plants older than 30 years account for 66% of the global fleet, implying a growing decommissioning market.

The subsidiary said it will use the proceeds for production capacity and working capital and is aiming for an initial public offering at the end of 2027. If realized, this could allow the subsidiary's value to be tested by the market, but the timing and outcome are uncertain.

The effect of any change in ownership on Ihsungcni's consolidated results and capital structure could not be confirmed in this research. In terms of business metrics, the main variables are a recovery in standalone revenue and room for operating margin improvement as fixed cost burdens ease.

Coverage in 2024 mentioned a push to transfer to the KOSDAQ market, but as of October 27, 2026 the shares still trade on Konex. New order disclosures, changes in capital structure in interim reports and trading liquidity are items to monitor.

Bull factors

Entry barriers built on qualification and track record

Only eight domestic firms were described as able to win nuclear instrumentation and control routine maintenance work. Ihsungcni has built a maintenance record across plants such as Hanul, Shin-Kori and Kori Unit 1. Because the client evaluates contractor capability strictly, new competitors face difficult entry. Extending that record into decommissioning is the expansion path the company has set out.

Subsidiary funding momentum

The subsidiary announced a KRW 6 billion funding round in July 2026 at a reported enterprise valuation of KRW 50 billion. It holds a tritium handling and sales permit and was reported to be the world's second commercial tritium supplier.

These figures come from the subsidiary's fundraising and do not map directly onto the value of Ihsungcni shares. Ownership percentage and any dilution need separate verification.

Nuclear expansion and decommissioning market

The company has described a continuing push for nuclear expansion amid rising power demand and decarbonization. Coverage noted that plants older than 30 years make up 66% of the global fleet, so the decommissioning market is expected to grow gradually.

Both maintenance and decommissioning call for instrumentation and control expertise. How fast market growth turns into an individual company's revenue depends on the client's order schedule.

Bear factors

Falling standalone revenue and continued losses

Standalone revenue fell from KRW 13.48 billion in 2023 to KRW 5.89 billion in 2025, and the company recorded net losses in both 2024 and 2025. The 2025 standalone operating loss was KRW 2,037 million.

A smaller revenue base may have raised the fixed cost burden, but the exact cause could not be confirmed in this research. A recovery in orders is a precondition for improved earnings.

Negative consolidated equity

Consolidated equity for 2025 was negative KRW 10,873 million, with total liabilities of KRW 46,331 million larger than total assets of KRW 35,459 million. The consolidated net loss also reached KRW 5,740 million.

Standalone equity, by contrast, was positive at KRW 6,974 million, so the gap between consolidated and standalone figures is large. The reason for the gap and any plan to resolve it need confirmation in further filings.

Risk factors

Policy and client concentration

Maintenance orders depend heavily on a small number of clients such as Korea Hydro & Nuclear Power and on government energy policy. A change in nuclear policy or maintenance plans could alter the size and timing of orders.

Because the client evaluates contractors' execution capability strictly, evaluation outcomes can affect awards. The project-based order structure also contributes to revenue volatility.

Financial and business

On a consolidated basis, liabilities exceed assets and the operating loss reached KRW 4,593 million. The subsidiary's capacity expansion and IPO preparation may require additional funding, and the effect of that burden on the consolidated balance sheet has not been confirmed.

The commercialization timing of tritium and fusion-fuel businesses is uncertain. Whether diversification will improve profitability of the core business remains to be seen.

Market and liquidity

Konex-listed shares trade less frequently than KOSPI or KOSDAQ names, so gaps in quotes arise easily. Trading value on October 27, 2026 was zero. Limited investor access may slow how quickly information is reflected in the price. A KOSDAQ transfer effort was reported in 2024, but later progress could not be confirmed.

Overall view

Ihsungcni is a Konex-listed company with a track record and qualifications in the specialized field of nuclear and thermal plant instrumentation and control maintenance. On filed results, standalone revenue has declined since 2023 and net losses continued in 2024 and 2025.

Negative consolidated equity for 2025 is a key item to examine on the balance sheet side. Meanwhile, the subsidiary disclosed a funding round in July 2026 and an IPO target for the end of 2027, signalling intent to expand.

The company's account of nuclear expansion and a growing decommissioning market is cited as a supportive backdrop, but nothing within the scope of this research confirms it has translated into higher revenue or profit.
