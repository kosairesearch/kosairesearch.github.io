Report overview

Sales Up, Profits Down: Otoki's Earnings Gap

Otoki's sales rose for three straight years through 2025, while operating profit and profit attributable to owners declined from 2023. Quarterly profit recovered in early 2026 but fell in the second and third quarters, so earnings still lag sales growth.

  1. 1

    Sales reached KRW 3.67 trillion in 2025, the largest year in the 2022-2025 period, and have increased for three straight years since 2023.

  2. 2

    The operating margin fell from 7.4% in 2023 to 4.8% in 2025, and 2025 operating profit of KRW 177.3 billion was the smallest in the 2022-2025 period.

  3. 3

    After peaking at KRW 59.4 billion in the first quarter of 2026, operating profit declined in the second and third quarters, and third-quarter operating profit of KRW 36.3 billion was down 34.5% year on year.

  4. 4

  5. 5

02

Business structure

Otoki started with curry and soup and has widened into a diversified food company spanning noodles, sauces, instant rice and frozen foods. By segment in 2025, noodle products made up 28.7% of sales, agro-marine processed foods 19.4%, seasoning sauces 17.0% and oils 12.0%.

Noodle products alone generated KRW 1,056.1 billion, the largest single segment. Ramyeon accounts for only about 30% of total products, and the company's large non-noodle lineup of curry, sauces and convenience foods makes it harder to concentrate resources on instant noodles.

Through 2025, Hypermarkets generated KRW 1,140.9 billion (31.05%), distributor-type partners KRW 1,089.3 billion (29.65%), and convenience stores KRW 220 billion (5.99%), so the three channels together made up 66.7% of sales. In instant noodles, Nongshim remains the leader while Samyang Foods is closing in.

Overseas sales in the first half of 2026 reached KRW 220.5 billion, up 12.3%, lifting the overseas share to 11.6%. The company targets global sales of KRW 1.1 trillion by 2030.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual sales rose from KRW 3.18 trillion in 2022 to KRW 3.45 trillion in 2023 (up 8.5%), KRW 3.54 trillion in 2024 (up 2.4%) and KRW 3.67 trillion in 2025 (up 3.8%), increasing for three consecutive years from 2023, with 2025 the largest year in the 2022-2025 period.

Operating profit peaked at KRW 254.9 billion in 2023 (up 37.3%), then fell to KRW 222 billion in 2024 (down 12.9%) and KRW 177.3 billion in 2025 (down 20.2%), declining for two consecutive years.

The operating margin moved from 5.8% in 2022 to 7.4% in 2023, 6.3% in 2024 and 4.8% in 2025, the lowest of the period even as sales grew. The 2025 decline was attributed to higher raw and subsidiary material prices, currency pressure, and increases in labor and advertising and promotion costs.

Profit attributable to owners fell from KRW 274.5 billion in 2022 to KRW 160.3 billion in 2023 (down 41.6%), KRW 136.7 billion in 2024 (down 14.7%) and KRW 69.2 billion in 2025 (down 49.4%). Net income including non-controlling interests was KRW 72.1 billion in 2025.

The debt ratio (total liabilities divided by total equity) fell from 83.3% in 2022 to 65.3% in 2025. At the end of 2025, total equity was KRW 2.2 trillion, equity attributable to owners KRW 2.07 trillion, and total liabilities KRW 1.44 trillion.

Operating cash flow was KRW 93.3 billion in 2022, KRW 410.8 billion in 2023, KRW 361.1 billion in 2024 and KRW 218.9 billion in 2025.

Quarterly, the third quarter of 2025 posted sales of KRW 955.5 billion and operating profit of KRW 55.3 billion, then the fourth quarter fell to KRW 896.2 billion in sales and KRW 19.3 billion in operating profit, with a net loss attributable to owners of KRW 27 billion.

The first quarter of 2026 returned to profit with sales of KRW 955.2 billion, operating profit of KRW 59.4 billion (up 207.4% quarter on quarter) and KRW 36.1 billion attributable to owners.

The third quarter of 2026 recorded sales of KRW 977.1 billion (up 2.3% year on year) but operating profit of KRW 36.3 billion (down 34.5%), with an operating margin of 3.7%. Over the last four quarters, profit attributable to owners totaled KRW 55 billion. Overseas sales in the third quarter of 2026 reached KRW 117.4 billion, up 17.6%.

05

Industry analysis

In instant noodles, K-ramyeon exports are driving growth, and overseas sales share is becoming a key gap between companies.

As of the third quarter of 2025, Samyang Foods' overseas share was reported at 81% and Nongshim's at about 30%, while Otoki's overseas share stood near 10%, raising questions about its ability to benefit from the global K-food boom.

Domestically, Otoki's ramyeon business is widely viewed as relying largely on Jin Ramen, unlike rivals with two or more mega brands. Raw material costs and currency movements have been the main variables squeezing margins.

On the regulatory side, the Korea Fair Trade Commission's probe covers product categories that account for 60.2% of sales. In a prior flour cartel case, the regulator applied a 15% base rate to major business operators.

Otoki has been viewed as restraining price increases, which makes the balance between cost pressure and pricing a central variable for profitability. With overseas expansion trailing peers, the build-out of overseas production capacity is becoming a key marker of its competitive position.

08

Bull factors

Overseas footprint expanding

Overseas sales in the third quarter of 2026 rose 17.6% to KRW 117.4 billion, lifting the overseas share to 12.0%. Otoki set up a Japan sales unit in May 2026 and plans to start its first North American plant in Ramirez, California, within 2026. The company targets global sales of KRW 1.1 trillion by 2030.

Quarterly profit back in positive territory

First-quarter 2026 operating profit of KRW 59.4 billion rose 207.4% quarter on quarter, and profit attributable to owners of KRW 36.1 billion returned to the black. This followed a fourth-quarter 2025 net loss attributable to owners of KRW 27 billion. The quarter marks a recovery point in quarterly earnings.

Continued top-line growth

Noodle products account for 28.7% of sales, followed by agro-marine processed foods at 19.4%, seasoning sauces at 17.0% and oils at 12.0%. Sales have grown for three straight years since 2023, and third-quarter 2026 sales of KRW 977.1 billion rose 2.3% year on year. Revenue is spread across several segments rather than concentrated in one product.

09

Bear factors

Margin compression

The operating margin was 4.8% in 2025, the lowest of 2022-2025. Third-quarter 2026 operating margin fell 2.1 percentage points year on year to 3.7%. Third-quarter operating profit of KRW 36.3 billion declined 34.5%, the second straight quarterly decline.

Cost and SG&A pressure

In 2025, higher raw and subsidiary material prices, currency pressure and rising labor, advertising and promotion costs together cut operating profit by 20.2%. In its third-quarter 2025 results, the company cited cost burden and higher SG&A as sources of profit pressure. When costs rise alongside sales, margins struggle to recover.

Regulatory exposure

Business segments under the Korea Fair Trade Commission's food collusion probe account for 60.2% of sales. The regulator applied a 15% base rate in a prior flour cartel case, so the scale of any penalty and its impact on earnings remain uncertain.

10

Risk factors

Regulation and penalties

Segments within the probe account for 60.2% of sales. If violations are found, penalties could flow directly into operating profit depending on their severity, making the outcome a near-term variable. Because noodle and oil products are both under review, the potential impact is broad.

Input costs and currency

Higher raw and subsidiary material prices and currency pressure were cited as main causes of the 2025 profit decline. With the operating margin at 3.7% in the third quarter of 2026, input cost trends are central to margin recovery. A restrained pricing stance can make it harder to absorb cost increases.

Overseas investment execution

Overseas bases such as the North American plant and the Japan sales unit require upfront investment in production and logistics. Sales growth from overseas may take time to translate into operating profit. Initial costs could keep earnings under pressure even as the overseas share rises.

Overall view

Otoki's sales rose from KRW 3.18 trillion in 2022 to KRW 3.67 trillion in 2025, but operating profit fell from its KRW 254.9 billion peak in 2023 to KRW 177.3 billion in 2025.

The operating margin of 4.8% in 2025 was the lowest of the period, and profit attributable to owners declined for three consecutive years to KRW 69.2 billion in 2025.

Quarterly profit recovered in the first quarter of 2026, with operating profit of KRW 59.4 billion and KRW 36.1 billion attributable to owners, but operating profit then fell in the second and third quarters to KRW 36.3 billion with a 3.7% margin.

Overseas sales rose 17.6% to KRW 117.4 billion in the third quarter of 2026, and the planned North American plant and the KRW 1.1 trillion 2030 target define the next stage.

Growth in sales and overseas expansion are the strengths, while cost and SG&A pressure and regulatory uncertainty from the collusion probe are the weaknesses, and both carry comparable weight.

The next checkpoints are the preliminary fourth-quarter 2026 results, the 2026 annual business report and the outcome of the Fair Trade Commission probe.
