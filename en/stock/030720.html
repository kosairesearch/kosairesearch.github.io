02

Business structure

Dongwon Fisheries is a distant-water fishing specialist founded in 1970 and listed on the KOSPI in 1996, according to press coverage. Value Finder reported that the company operates three trawlers in New Zealand waters and fourteen tuna longliners in Pacific and Indian Ocean fishing zones.

By segment, the Q1 2026 revenue mix was 56.0% fisheries operations, 35.4% seafood distribution and 8.6% grain manufacturing. The fisheries segment covers ocean catch, while seafood distribution sells frozen seafood and imported processed products.

By product, breadcrumbs were 99.36% domestic in December 2025, while sashimi-grade tuna products were 88.05% exported, per WiseReport company data. Overseas, the company is expanding business-to-business ties with partners such as the Japanese lunch box franchise Hotto Motto, as the company stated.

Its core competitive asset is a fishing quota built through about 40 years of cooperation with Sanford Limited, New Zealand's largest seafood company, which Value Finder views as hard for new entrants to replicate quickly. The new 2,342 GT trawler DW NOVA is reported to use existing quotas rather than new ones. Most revenue is dollar-denominated, a feature of the business model that analysts highlight.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual revenue fell from KRW 177 billion in 2022 to KRW 141.9 billion in 2023, a 19.8% decline, then rose to KRW 183.7 billion in 2024, up 29.4%, which was the largest year in the 2022 to 2025 period. Revenue was KRW 172.6 billion in 2025, down 6.0%.

Operating profit moved from KRW 5.8 billion in 2022 to an operating loss of KRW 19.4 billion in 2023, a swing to loss, then returned to a KRW 5.32 billion profit in 2024, a swing to profit, and reached KRW 8.83 billion in 2025, up 65.8%. Operating margin was 3.3% in 2022, -13.7% in 2023, 2.9% in 2024 and 5.1% in 2025.

Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders swung from a net loss of KRW 17.6 billion in 2023 to KRW 5.11 billion in 2024, then rose to KRW 7.75 billion in 2025, up 51.5%. Net profit including non-controlling interests was KRW 7.75 billion in 2025.

On a quarterly basis, operating profit was KRW 1.52 billion in Q3 2025 and KRW 1.19 billion in Q4 2025, down 21.2% from the prior quarter, then rose to KRW 3.64 billion in Q1 2026, up 204.5% quarter on quarter, and KRW 4.5 billion in Q2 2026, up 23.7%.

Q3 2026 operating profit was KRW 1.62 billion, down 63.9% from the prior quarter but up 7.1% from a year earlier.

Securities analysts attributed the Q1 improvement to better fishing efficiency and a higher share of value-added processed products, with operating margin rising to about 9.1% from about 4.9% a year earlier. Q3 2026 revenue was KRW 46.9 billion, up 29.4% year on year but down 6.3% quarter on quarter.

The debt ratio moved from 94.6% in 2022 to 168.7% in 2023, 123.1% in 2024 and 139.1% in 2025, when total equity was KRW 56.4 billion and total liabilities were KRW 78.5 billion. Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders over the last four quarters totaled KRW 10.1 billion.

05

Industry analysis

Distant-water fishing is exposed to both fuel costs and exchange rates at the same time. In describing its first-half 2026 results, the company said it delivered progress despite high oil prices and a strong dollar.

Value Finder argued that because most revenue is dollar-denominated, a rising won-dollar exchange rate can be favorable for earnings. A WiseReport company comment on the first nine months of 2025 noted that fisheries volumes declined while higher tuna and squid prices improved profitability.

Dongwon Industries, a listed company in the same group, was reported to have struggled in Q1 2026 under cost pressure in its fisheries segment, which suggests seafood profitability is sensitive to input costs across the group.

Value Finder also said that the fixed-cost nature of distant-water fishing could produce operating leverage if catch volumes rise. On competition, Dongwon Fisheries stands out for its 17-vessel fleet, its quota position built with Sanford Limited, its cold-chain logistics infrastructure and its MSC certification.

06

Outlook

The nearest operating milestone the company has described is the deployment of the new trawler DW NOVA in New Zealand waters.

Value Finder noted that the vessel is roughly three times larger than the existing trawlers and uses existing quotas rather than new ones, and argued that higher catch volumes could show up in earnings quickly.

DW NOVA was scheduled to begin fishing in October 2026, and the catch effect will be visible only in subsequent quarterly results.

The company said it is reviewing the acquisition of a cold-chain logistics company, which would internalize the chain from fishing through logistics, processing and distribution if completed.

On shareholder returns, the plan calls for returning half of net profit over three years, with progress reported to the board and disclosed at least once a year. In a shareholder letter, the company said it would prioritize reviewing a dividend restart at the annual general meeting in 2027.

The largest shareholder and the CEO disclosed plans to buy shares on the open market from July 23 to August 21, 2026. Whether third-quarter profit weakness continues and how high oil prices and the strong dollar affect costs remain open questions.

08

Bull factors

Shift in earnings profile

Operating profit rose 65.8% to KRW 8.83 billion in 2025, and first-half 2026 operating profit of KRW 8.1 billion came close to that annual level. Operating margin rose from 2.9% in 2024 to 5.1% in 2025. Securities analysts said Q1 2026 operating margin was about 9.1%, up from about 4.9% a year earlier.

Shareholder return plan disclosed

On August 27, 2026, the company disclosed a value-up plan under which it would return half of net profit to shareholders over three years. Progress will be reported to the board and disclosed at least once a year. The medium-term market capitalization target is KRW 100 billion.

Quota and asset base

The fishing quota built over about 40 years with Sanford Limited is viewed as an asset that new entrants would find hard to match quickly. Breadcrumbs, with a 99.36% domestic share in 2025, are cited as a stable cash-generating business. Analysts also note that dollar-based revenue can be favorable when the won weakens against the dollar.

09

Bear factors

Q3 earnings slowdown

Profit had been concentrated in Q1 and Q2, so whether quarterly momentum holds remains an open question. Q3 2026 revenue of KRW 46.9 billion was also down 6.3% from the prior quarter.

Shrinking revenue and cost pressure

Revenue was KRW 172.6 billion in 2025, down 6.0% from the prior year. The company said first-half 2026 revenue fell 7.7% year on year and attributed this partly to exiting low-margin volumes. High oil prices and a strong dollar were cited by the company as cost pressures.

Dividend gap and balance sheet

The company could not pay a dividend at the last year-end settlement because it lacked distributable reserves under the Commercial Act. The debt ratio was 139.1% in 2025, higher than 94.6% in 2022, with total liabilities of KRW 78.5 billion. Following the operating and net losses in 2023, the stability of earnings has been tested over a short period.

10

Risk factors

Fishing operations and catch risk

Catch volumes depend on vessel operations and the state of fish stocks in the fishing zones. A WiseReport comment for the first nine months of 2025 noted lower fisheries volumes. The expectation that DW NOVA can raise catch using existing quotas still needs confirmation from actual fishing results.

Exchange rate and fuel cost variables

High oil prices and a strong dollar were cited by the company as burdens in describing its 2026 results. A dollar-based revenue structure helps when the exchange rate rises, but margins can narrow if cost pressures rise at the same time. Because quarterly operating margins swing widely, cost variables show up in quarterly results quickly.

Execution risk on shareholder returns

The plan to restart dividends and return 50% of net profit depends on securing distributable reserves and on the trajectory of net profit. Press coverage noted that the size of returns would move with earnings. Because the company posted losses in 2023, earnings volatility remains a condition for executing the plan.

12

Overall view

Dongwon Fisheries has moved from an operating and net loss in 2023 to profit in 2024, higher profit in 2025 and concentrated earnings in the first half of 2026. First-half 2026 operating profit of KRW 8.1 billion is about 92% of full-year 2025 operating profit of KRW 8.83 billion.

Q3 2026 operating profit of KRW 1.62 billion fell from the prior quarter, so the durability of quarterly momentum is the next thing to confirm.

The company disclosed a plan to return half of net profit over three years and a medium-term market capitalization target of KRW 100 billion, and said it will review a dividend restart at the 2027 annual general meeting, conditional on securing distributable reserves.

Bullish factors include the fishing quota, the distant-water fleet, the expanding processing and distribution chain and the return plan. Bearish factors include quarterly earnings volatility, lower revenue, cost pressure from high oil prices and a strong dollar, and a higher debt ratio.

The next checkpoints are the Q4 2026 results and distributable reserves in the 2026 annual report, the dividend decision at the 2027 annual general meeting, and the DW NOVA effect in the Q1 2027 quarterly report.

Until these are confirmed in disclosed results, the earnings recovery and the shareholder return plan are moving in parallel.
