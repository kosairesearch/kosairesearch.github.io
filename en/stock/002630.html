02

Business structure

Orientbio is a bio-infrastructure company whose core businesses are supplying laboratory animals, providing contract non-clinical testing (CRO) and supplying lab equipment.

Its main products are rodents such as mice, rats and guinea pigs, beagles and laboratory rabbits, and it also sells lab equipment such as IVC racks, isolators and autoclaves. Its Gapyeong center produces mice, rats and guinea pigs, while its Chungbuk and Jeonbuk centers produce beagles.

At Jeongeup in Jeonbuk, it began producing SPF laboratory rabbits under a technology partnership with Japan's Kitayama Labes, which the company says reduces import quarantine and transport risk.

Through a technology partnership with Charles River, it also produces IGS-standard germ-free laboratory animals, which the company presents as a differentiator. Customer concentration appears low: the company said its largest customer accounted for 6.0% of sales and its top five customers for about 22.5% in 2026 Q2.

Segment revenue splits were not confirmed in the research scope and are therefore not shown. The primate CRO exclusive rights agreement with affiliate Orient Cam underpins a global non-clinical testing business that uses a production base in Cambodia.

The company describes its domestic animal production center, on a site of roughly 20,000 pyeong, as the largest of its kind in Korea. Its drug pipeline includes OND-1, a hair-loss candidate based on a cyclosporin A derivative, which was reported to have received an IND clearance from the US FDA. The revenue contribution of the pipeline could not be confirmed within the research scope.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual revenue went from KRW 28.8 billion in the March 2023 fiscal year to KRW 28 billion in the March 2024 fiscal year (down 2.8% year on year), then KRW 29.4 billion in the March 2025 fiscal year (up 5.0%) and KRW 29.8 billion in the March 2026 fiscal year (up 1.2%).

Revenue rose for two consecutive years from the March 2025 fiscal year through the March 2026 fiscal year, and the March 2026 fiscal year is the largest year among the March 2023 to March 2026 fiscal years. Operating results moved opposite to revenue.

Operating income of KRW 1.45 billion in the March 2023 fiscal year turned into an operating loss of KRW 646 million in the March 2024 fiscal year, then widened to an operating loss of KRW 3.08 billion in the March 2025 fiscal year and KRW 4.13 billion in the March 2026 fiscal year.

The operating margin fell from 5.0% to -13.9%. A Korean financial information site's corporate monitor, summarizing the first nine months of 2025 on a standalone basis, attributed the wider operating loss to cost pressure that reduced gross profit and higher expenses.

Profit attributable to owners of the parent was a net loss of KRW 12.4 billion in the March 2023 fiscal year, a profit of KRW 12.9 billion in the March 2024 fiscal year (a swing to profit), a net loss of KRW 13.2 billion in the March 2025 fiscal year (a swing to loss), and a net loss of KRW 4.57 billion in the March 2026 fiscal year (losses narrowed).

The March 2024 fiscal year recorded profit attributable to owners despite an operating loss, so non-operating or one-off items should be checked in the detailed disclosures. Net income including non-controlling interests moved in the same direction.

Operating cash flow was KRW 1.16 billion, KRW 3.76 billion, KRW 752 million and KRW 2.6 billion across the four fiscal years.

Quarterly revenue went from KRW 7.68 billion in 2025 Q2 to KRW 6.93 billion in 2025 Q3, KRW 8.6 billion in 2025 Q4, KRW 6.54 billion in 2026 Q1 and KRW 6.59 billion in 2026 Q2, which was down 14.2% year on year.

Operating loss narrowed from KRW 1.62 billion in 2025 Q2 to KRW 680 million in 2025 Q3, widened to KRW 1.57 billion in 2025 Q4, narrowed to KRW 259 million in 2026 Q1, and turned to operating income of KRW 50.06 million in 2026 Q2.

Profit attributable to owners of the parent was KRW 4.85 billion in 2026 Q2, and the sum over the last four quarters from 2025 Q3 to 2026 Q2 was KRW 2.31 billion. The gap between 2026 Q2 operating income and profit attributable to owners is large, so the composition of non-operating items needs to be examined.

05

Industry analysis

Demand for non-clinical CRO services and laboratory animals is tied to the size of drug development pipelines. Reports say that the expansion of global drug development has created a shortage of research primates, which is cited as a tailwind for the non-clinical CRO business.

Demand for primate testing is also rising with the growth of antibody-drug conjugates and antibody therapeutics. A report said the price of Chinese-origin cynomolgus monkeys rose 104% in about a year, suggesting that the supply imbalance is feeding into prices.

The company cites the primate production base of its affiliate Orient Cam as a strength, linking supply, testing, quality assurance and data analysis. In domestic animal supply, domestic production of SPF rabbits at Jeongeup is moving toward lower import dependence.

On competition, the company points to its IGS-standard germ-free animal technology and its large domestic production base, but independent third-party market share data was not confirmed within the research scope.

Profitability comparisons with listed peers were also not confirmed, so the competitive position is described only qualitatively.

On regulation, financial authorities are tightening standards for stocks priced below KRW 1,000, which has made share count adjustment and price management an industry-wide issue for small biotech companies.

The animal business is sensitive to R&D budgets, and demand tends to move with the number of drugs entering clinical trials.

06

Outlook

No annual revenue or profit guidance from the company was confirmed within the research scope. The primate CRO business using the Cambodia base is described as moving into full operation.

Because the business rests on an affiliate agreement, a key question is how much of the contract execution shows up in reported results. The company presents an AI-based non-clinical platform and a digital CRO business as its future growth base.

Reports say the company is focusing on moving the hair-loss candidate OND-1 from a topical form to an injectable form for clinical entry. However, clinical timing and data disclosure dates were not confirmed within the research scope.

Alongside the share count adjustments, a plan was announced for the largest shareholder to increase its stake. The largest shareholder said it would buy 3 million shares on the open market over 30 days starting August 10, and that its stake together with related parties would reach 40.7% upon completion.

This is the plan as announced; whether the purchase was completed was not confirmed within the research scope. The standard for stocks priced below KRW 1,000 is reported to remain in force until July 2027, which keeps price defense on the agenda.

The next confirmable filing is the 2026 Q3 quarterly report, with a statutory filing deadline of November 14, 2026, and the checkpoints there are whether operating income stays positive and how non-operating items are composed.

08

Bull factors

Quarterly turn to profit and cost gains

Operating income for 2026 Q2 was KRW 50.06 million, a swing to profit that also compares favorably with the same quarter last year. The company says consolidating its Gapyeong 1 and 2 centers and cutting selling and administrative costs reduced its operating expenses. The lower cost ratio is cited as the background to the quarterly improvement.

Debt-free balance sheet

The company says it has run without borrowings since 2023 and holds about KRW 38 billion in cash and cash equivalents. At the end of March 2026 the debt ratio was 19.1% and total liabilities were KRW 13.1 billion. This financial room can serve as a buffer for new business investment and share price stabilization.

Primate CRO and drug option

An exclusive primate CRO right with affiliate Orient Cam links research primate supply with testing services. Reports point to rising primate demand as global drug development expands. The hair-loss candidate OND-1 is presented as a compound that received FDA IND clearance.

Bear factors

Three straight years of operating losses

The operating loss for the fiscal year ended March 2026 was KRW 4.13 billion and the operating margin was -13.9%, so the loss widened. Operating losses have now persisted for three consecutive years from the March 2024 fiscal year.

Until an annual turn to profit is confirmed, it is hard to tell whether the quarterly profit is one-off or structural.

Uncertain revenue recovery

Revenue for 2026 Q2 was KRW 6.59 billion, down 14.2% year on year. Revenue for 2026 Q1 fell 24.0% from the prior quarter before a slight recovery. Revenue for the fiscal year ended March 2026 rose, but growth was only 1.2%.

Reliance on share supply and demand

Reports say the share price tried to rise after the consolidation but gave back much of the gain. Commentary has also argued that share count adjustments and largest-shareholder purchases alone have limits in restoring corporate value.

Profit attributable to owners of KRW 4.85 billion in 2026 Q2 is far above operating income of KRW 50.06 million, so reliance on non-operating items needs to be checked.

10

Risk factors

Earnings volatility

Quarterly revenue rose 24.1% from the prior quarter in 2025 Q4 and then fell 24.0% in 2026 Q1, showing large swings. Quarterly results have alternated between losses and profits, which makes it hard to judge whether the 2026 Q2 profit is sustained. Whether revenue stabilizes at a consistent level is the first item to confirm.

Listing standards and share count changes

Reports say the standard for stocks priced below KRW 1,000 remains in force until July 2027. Share consolidation and capital reduction raised the per-share price, but several companies saw their stocks fall again after consolidation. Whether supply-demand improvements lead to a sustained price recovery is uncertain.

Affiliate transactions and drug execution

The primate CRO exclusive rights are an agreement with affiliate Orient Cam, so transaction terms and fairness must be checked through disclosures. Where the largest shareholder keeps expanding its stake, the scope of information disclosed to minority holders is also a point to examine.

OND-1 carries the high-cost structure and long research periods typical of drug development, which are cited as risks.

Overall view

Orientbio is a bio company built on laboratory animal production and non-clinical testing infrastructure, and in 2026 Q2 both operating income and profit attributable to owners of the parent turned positive.

However, the operating loss for the fiscal year ended March 2026 extended to a third consecutive year, and the operating margin was -13.9%. Revenue rose for two consecutive years, but whether the improvement in profit has carried into the annual trend is not yet confirmed.

Financially, a debt-free stance, KRW 68.5 billion in total equity and a 19.1% debt ratio serve as a buffer. The growth pillars, the primate CRO business and the OND-1 hair-loss drug, have set out their direction, but their contribution to reported results has not been confirmed.

On the share price side, the share count reduction, capital reduction and largest-shareholder purchase plan have continued, but reports say much of the gain after consolidation was given back, so whether supply-demand factors are backed by results is the central question.

Bullish and bearish factors coexist, and the next checkpoints are whether operating income stays positive and how non-operating items are composed in the 2026 Q3 quarterly report, and whether the March 2027 fiscal year turns positive on an annual basis.
