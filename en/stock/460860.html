02

Business structure

Dongkuk Steel Mill is a steelmaker whose core products are long steel products such as rebar and H-beams, plus heavy plate. Its shares were listed in June 2023 after the hot-rolling business was spun off from Dongkuk Holdings with the cold-rolling business.

Long products made up 76% of Q3 2026 sales and heavy plate 24%, versus 73% and 27% in Q2. Long-product shipments in Q3 rose to 682,000 tonnes from 583,000 tonnes a year earlier, driven by demand from semiconductor plants and AI data centers, which use H-beams and rebar in building frames.

Heavy-plate shipments fell to 215,000 tonnes in Q3 as scheduled major maintenance cut output. As of December 2025, the company held a 21.00% market share in long products and 14.00% in heavy plate, excluding imports, and domestic sales accounted for 88.82% of long-product and 83.21% of heavy-plate revenue in 2025.

A 2025 report ranked the company second in domestic long-product capacity, while a 2025 analysis said it lacks an upstream process among the three domestic heavy-plate producers, a cost disadvantage.

The Incheon plant obtained Czech CCZ certification in August 2026 and the Pohang plant developed an H-beam export specification. Heavy-plate work centers on titanium-clad and other specialty steel products.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual revenue rose from KRW 2.63 trillion in 2023 to KRW 3.53 trillion in 2024, a 34.0% increase, and then fell 9.2% to KRW 3.2 trillion in 2025. The largest year of 2023 to 2025 was 2024.

Operating profit fell from KRW 235.5 billion in 2023 to KRW 102.5 billion in 2024, down 56.5%, and to KRW 59.4 billion in 2025, down 42.1%. Revenue grew in 2024 while profit declined, so the operating margin dropped from 8.9% in 2023 to 2.9% in 2024 and 1.9% in 2025.

A 2025 report linked the drop in long-product profitability to weaker housing-market indicators and a suspended Incheon plant run from July 22 to August 15, 2025. Profit attributable to owners fell from KRW 142.2 billion in 2023 to KRW 34.8 billion in 2024, down 75.5%, and to KRW 8.23 billion in 2025, down 76.4%.

Quarterly operating profit fell from KRW 24.5 billion in Q3 2025 to KRW 718 million in Q4 2025, a 97.1% drop, and Q4 2025 posted an owner-attributable net loss of KRW 13.5 billion. Hana Securities attributed the Q4 2025 deterioration to weak industry conditions and large one-off costs.

In 2026, operating profit recovered to KRW 21.4 billion in Q1 and KRW 45.6 billion in Q2, then fell 34.4% to KRW 29.9 billion in Q3.

Q3 operating profit rose 22.0% year on year, but other expenses increased to KRW 21.0 billion from KRW 4.0 billion a year earlier, and finance costs reached KRW 21.1 billion, leaving pre-tax profit at KRW 8.9 billion. Owner-attributable net profit over the last four quarters totaled KRW 10.6 billion.

The debt ratio rose from 87.7% in 2024 to 119.4% in 2025, with total liabilities of KRW 2.25 trillion at end-2025. Operating cash flow fell from KRW 348.4 billion in 2023 to KRW 213.6 billion in 2024 and KRW 114.6 billion in 2025.

05

Industry analysis

Rebar demand was extremely weak in the second half of 2025 and showed signs of recovery in 2026. Hana Securities said in an April 2026 report that rebar prices rose nearly 20% from the start of the year, helped by production cuts and expanded exports by domestic steelmakers.

Hyundai Motor Securities said in an October 2026 report that domestic rebar prices had slipped from KRW 870,000 to KRW 850,000 by the end of Q2 and estimated that domestic rebar demand fell 4% year on year in Q3. Long-product prices were weak in Q3 amid seasonal slowdown and a construction slump.

Demand from AI data centers and semiconductor plants, which use H-beams and rebar, has been cited as a new source of demand for the steel industry. Heavy-plate shipments rose 16.7% year on year to 238,000 tonnes in Q1 2026 after import curbs on Chinese plate.

Shipbuilding demand has continued on the back of domestic shipyards' order backlogs. Concern over rising raw material costs for heavy plate grew in Q3. Hana Securities said in an October 2026 preview that a weaker won against the dollar and continued curbs on Chinese H-beam imports would be positive for results.

The company is classed with Hyundai Steel Company as one of the two major steelmakers in Korea. A 2025 analysis viewed the lack of an upstream process among the three domestic heavy-plate producers as a relative cost disadvantage.

06

Outlook

The company said it will strengthen a profitability-focused sales mix and adjust long-product and heavy-plate sales strategies to changes in end-market demand. On exports, it has set up a dedicated export organization to develop overseas-spec products and expand sales.

The Incheon plant's CCZ certification was presented as a base for expanding domestic and overseas sales. Hyundai Motor Securities said in a November 3, 2026 report that completion of Q3 heavy-plate maintenance should lift heavy-plate output and sales, with a seasonal boost expected for long products.

The same report projected Q4 2026 operating profit at KRW 35.5 billion, up 19% from the prior quarter. Recovery in construction remains uncertain, and expanded long-product sales and development of higher-value heavy plate were cited as key variables for profitability.

The next confirmation point is the 2026 annual report, where full-year and Q4 2026 results will be disclosed. Whether Q4 2026 operating profit lands near the broker estimate is the first item to check.

For heavy plate, the pace at which specialty-steel development and raw-material handling automation lower unit costs is the next point to track. For long products, whether demand from semiconductor plants and AI data centers holds up in first-half 2027 volumes is the item to watch.

08

Bull factors

Large-project demand for long products

Demand for H-beams and rebar is rising from large semiconductor and AI data-center projects. Q3 2026 long-product shipments rose 17.0% year on year to 682,000 tonnes, and the segment's share of sales reached 76%. That volume growth underpinned a 22.0% year-on-year rise in Q3 operating profit.

Import curbs and shipbuilding demand for heavy plate

After curbs on Chinese plate imports, Q1 2026 heavy-plate shipments rose 16.7% year on year to 238,000 tonnes. In Q2, shipbuilding demand kept volumes rising, with shipments up 13.2% to 265,000 tonnes. Hana Securities cited continued curbs on Chinese H-beam imports as a positive factor for results.

Export certifications and profit-focused sales

The Czech CCZ certification and the H-beam export specification widen the company's export channels. The company said it will strengthen a profit-focused sales mix. A broader export base could reduce reliance on domestic construction demand for long products.

Bear factors

A shrinking earnings base

Operating profit fell from KRW 235.5 billion in 2023 to KRW 59.4 billion in 2025. Owner-attributable net profit dropped 76.4% to KRW 8.23 billion in 2025. Q4 2025 swung to a KRW 13.5 billion owner-attributable net loss.

Cost and financing burden

Q3 2026 other expenses rose to KRW 21.0 billion from KRW 4.0 billion a year earlier, and finance costs increased to KRW 21.1 billion, leaving pre-tax profit at KRW 8.9 billion. The debt ratio climbed from 87.7% in 2024 to 119.4% in 2025. Operating cash flow fell from KRW 348.4 billion in 2023 to KRW 114.6 billion in 2025.

Construction slump and price weakness

Long-product prices were weak in Q3 amid seasonal slowdown and construction weakness. Concern over rising raw material costs for heavy plate has grown.

10

Risk factors

Raw material and product price spread

A Hana Securities estimate put Q1 2026 long-product raw material costs up 7% quarter on quarter, while selling prices rose only 1%. Margins depend on how far product prices keep pace with raw material costs. Concern about heavy-plate input costs has also grown.

End-market demand from construction and shipbuilding

Long products are sensitive to construction weakness, and their prices were soft in Q3. Hyundai Motor Securities estimated domestic rebar demand fell 4% in Q3. Heavy-plate demand relies on domestic shipyards' order backlogs, so a change in shipbuilding orders would feed into volumes.

Financial burden and finance costs

Total liabilities were KRW 2.25 trillion at end-2025, with the debt ratio at 119.4%. Finance costs rose to KRW 21.1 billion in Q3 2026 and pressured pre-tax profit. Operating cash flow fell to KRW 114.6 billion in 2025, which keeps borrowing dependence in focus alongside earnings volatility.

12

Overall view

Dongkuk Steel Mill's operating profit recovered through 2026, reaching KRW 21.4 billion in Q1, KRW 45.6 billion in Q2 and KRW 29.9 billion in Q3, a clear improvement from KRW 718 million in Q4 2025.

However, annual operating profit and owner-attributable net profit declined after 2023, and the debt ratio rose to 119.4% at end-2025. Long-product demand relies on large semiconductor and AI data-center projects, while heavy-plate volumes are supported by shipbuilding demand and import curbs on Chinese plate.

Weak factors include uncertain construction recovery, an estimated second-half softening in rebar prices, and higher other expenses and finance costs in Q3. Strong and weak factors coexist. The next checkpoints are the 2026 annual report and Q1 2027 provisional results. Hyundai Motor Securities lowered its target price to KRW 12,800 in a November 3, 2026 report.
