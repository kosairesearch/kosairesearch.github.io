02

Business structure

Cosmax NBT was founded in 2002 to research, manufacture and distribute functional ingredients for health functional foods, and listed on KOSDAQ in 2015. It offers functional ingredient R&D, ODM and OEM manufacturing, brand solutions and distribution, and works with more than 300 customers in 32 countries.

Its production base consists of the Icheon plant in Gyeonggi-do and local production subsidiaries in Dallas, USA and Melbourne, Australia. Overseas sales reached KRW 127.7 billion in the first half of 2026, or 65.3% of the total, so overseas customers now anchor the revenue mix.

The company says sales growth has come from wider product lines with major local health functional food brands. On the product side, it added a 12-row line to its stick pack production, lifting monthly capacity from about 15 million packs to about 30 million.

It also installed forming and filling equipment on its Gumi line. The company developed the ultra-small format 'Adam' and holds nine individually recognized ingredients. R&D spending in 2025 was KRW 11.79 billion, or 4.10% of revenue. On June 30, 2026, the company absorbed a domestic subsidiary by merger to strengthen its business.

04

Earnings analysis

On a disclosed basis, annual revenue rose from KRW 328.2 billion in 2022 to KRW 333.6 billion in 2023 (up 1.7% year on year), then fell to KRW 318 billion in 2024 (down 4.7%) and KRW 287.5 billion in 2025 (down 9.6%).

Operating profit climbed from KRW 2.24 billion in 2022 to KRW 12 billion in 2023 (up 434.6%), then declined to KRW 9.9 billion in 2024 (down 17.3%) and KRW 4 billion in 2025 (down 59.6%), and the operating margin slid from 3.6% in 2023 to 1.4% in 2025.

The company attributed weakness to rapid market changes that hit overseas offline channels and sales to some customers. Higher cost burdens from lower revenue, smaller FX gains, and the recognition of disposal costs for fixed assets tied to restructuring the US business added to the pressure.

Net loss attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 13.1 billion in 2022, narrowed to KRW 6.49 billion in 2023 and KRW 5.75 billion in 2024, then widened to KRW 15 billion in 2025, a four-year run of losses. Net loss including non-controlling interests was KRW 15.1 billion in 2025.

Operating cash flow was positive in all four years, at KRW 13.6 billion, KRW 16.8 billion, KRW 9.11 billion and KRW 18.3 billion.

At year-end 2025, total equity was KRW 48.8 billion, of which KRW 44.2 billion belonged to controlling shareholders, against total liabilities of KRW 234.1 billion, and the debt ratio was 479.3%.

The quarterly picture moved from an operating loss of KRW 1.16 billion in the third quarter of 2025 to an operating profit of KRW 1.77 billion in the fourth quarter, then to KRW 10.3 billion in the first quarter of 2026 on revenue of KRW 93.3 billion.

Second-quarter revenue rose to KRW 102.3 billion, but operating profit fell to KRW 8.47 billion, and the third quarter brought revenue of KRW 91.5 billion and operating profit of KRW 4.8 billion.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders swung from a loss to KRW 9.07 billion in the first quarter of 2026, rose to KRW 30.3 billion in the second, and fell to KRW 1.16 billion in the third.

The gap between second-quarter operating profit and controlling net income is wide, so the role of non-operating items and one-off effects is a point to verify in the periodic report notes.

05

Industry analysis

According to the company, demand for health functional food ODM work is moving along two axes: rising global demand for K-wellness products and the reshaping of new distribution channels. In its second-quarter 2026 results, the company described this as a structural growth trend.

In China, rising health functional food demand and growth in cross-border online direct purchase channels are widening collaboration with global brands. Overseas sales at 65.3% of revenue in the first half of 2026 fit this pattern.

In 2025, by contrast, weakness in overseas offline channels and lower sales to some customers weighed on results, showing a business whose earnings swing widely with channel cycles. The company said in its 2026 half-year report that restructuring the US business into a sales entity improved profitability.

In product terms, orders for new formats, including liquid and powder lactic acid bacteria products and gummy and jelly formats, continued from key overseas customers. The company emphasizes its functional ingredient R&D, NSF and GMP certifications, and a KOLAS-accredited analytical lab as competitive factors.

In ODM work, ingredient development capability and production certification both bear on customer selection, and the company stresses both.

Because the stick pack expansion roughly doubled monthly capacity, whether that line's utilization shows up in revenue is the next thing to check. The new forming and filling equipment on the Gumi line sits in the same capacity-expansion logic.

The company said its Australian unit is expanding collaboration with major local customers and pursuing new customer wins on schedule. The ultra-small 'Adam' format is the newest product, and the company presents its nine individually recognized ingredients as evidence of technical differentiation.

Revenue fell from the second to the third quarter of 2026, and operating profit has declined for two straight quarters, so how the second-half strategy shows up in quarterly results needs confirming. Whether the 2025 weakness in overseas offline channels persists into 2026 is another key variable.

Overseas subsidiaries and export share

The Australian unit's second-quarter 2026 sales of KRW 23.2 billion grew 76% year on year to a record for a quarter, and both its operating and net results turned profitable. The company says restructuring the US business into a sales entity improved its profitability.

Capacity expansion and format diversification

Slowing quarterly profit

Balance sheet burden and earnings quality

Second-quarter 2026 net income attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 30.3 billion far exceeded operating profit of KRW 8.47 billion, which points to a large non-operating contribution. The company also posted losses in each year from 2022 through 2025, so the durability of profit remains an open question.

10

Risk factors

Channel concentration

Weakness in overseas offline channels and in sales to some customers was cited as the cause of the 2025 decline. When customer orders concentrate in specific channels, channel shifts affect revenue quickly. With overseas sales at 65.3% of the total, sensitivity to overseas channel changes is high.

FX and cost structure

In 2025, FX gains shrank, cost burdens rose, and disposal costs tied to restructuring the US business were recognized. With a high share of overseas revenue, currency moves can act as a variable in profit and loss. Quarterly results for 2026 should be read together with the scale of FX-related items.

Leverage and capacity utilization

Year-end 2025 total liabilities of KRW 234.1 billion and a debt ratio of 479.3% signal a heavy financial load. If fixed costs rise with the stick pack expansion and the new Gumi line, profit swings could widen depending on utilization. Operating cash flow was nevertheless positive in every year from 2022 through 2025.
