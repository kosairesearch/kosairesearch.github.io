02

Business structure

FutureChem began in 2001 by manufacturing and selling radiopharmaceutical precursors, then expanded into developing and producing radiopharmaceutical drugs, producing precursors and synthesis reagents, and selling automated synthesis devices. It listed on KOSDAQ in 2016.

Its main products are the Alzheimer's diagnostic Alzavue and the Parkinson's diagnostic PDVUE, and Prostavue, the prostate cancer diagnostic that received approval in May 2026, was added as its fourth diagnostic radiopharmaceutical.

Prostavue is a PET diagnostic that binds fluorine-18 to a peptide targeting PSMA, a prostate-specific protein. According to the business report, 2025 standalone revenue was about KRW 8.55 billion, and diagnostic radiopharmaceutical products and merchandise made up more than about 70% of it.

Precursors, reagents and automated synthesis devices account for the rest. The treatment pipeline includes FC705, a prostate cancer-targeted radiotherapy, which is in Phase 3 in Korea and has reported Phase 2a results in the United States. Subsidiaries include FutureChem Healthcare, SI Healthcare and FutureChem U.S.A.

Customers are mainly hospitals and medical institutions. The company highlights Phase 3 results showing that Prostavue's positive predictive value is higher than that of conventional imaging (CT, MRI and bone scan). However, direct comparisons with other PSMA diagnostics have not been fully presented in company materials.

04

Earnings analysis

On a disclosed basis, annual revenue went from KRW 12.6 billion in 2022 to KRW 14 billion in 2023 (up 11.1% year on year), KRW 17.3 billion in 2024 (up 23.8%) and KRW 18.7 billion in 2025 (up 8.0%).

Revenue has risen for three consecutive years from 2023 through 2025, and 2025 was the largest year of the 2022–2025 period. Operating losses were KRW 12.9 billion in 2022, KRW 8.41 billion in 2023, KRW 9.47 billion in 2024 and KRW 7.75 billion in 2025, a loss in each of the four years.

The operating margin improved from -102.9% in 2022 to -60.2% in 2023, -54.7% in 2024 and -41.5% in 2025. The net loss attributable to controlling shareholders narrowed to KRW 6.71 billion in 2023, then widened to KRW 9.42 billion in 2024 and KRW 11.3 billion in 2025.

The net loss including non-controlling interests was KRW 9.43 billion in 2024. At the end of 2025, total equity was KRW 54.8 billion, of which KRW 54.7 billion belonged to controlling shareholders, and total liabilities were KRW 19.7 billion. The debt ratio was 14.0% in 2023, rising to 36.5% in 2024 and 35.9% in 2025.

Operating cash outflow narrowed from KRW 7.61 billion in 2022 to KRW 2.43 billion in 2025.

On a quarterly basis, revenue was KRW 3.79 billion in the third quarter of 2025, KRW 6.16 billion in the fourth quarter (up 62.5% from the prior quarter), KRW 6.36 billion in the first quarter of 2026, KRW 4.27 billion in the second quarter (down 32.9% from the prior quarter) and KRW 4.7 billion in the third quarter of 2026.

The operating loss narrowed from KRW 2.53 billion in the third quarter of 2025 to KRW 1.41 billion in the fourth quarter, widened to KRW 3.22 billion in the second quarter of 2026, and was KRW 1.87 billion in the third quarter of 2026.

The company posted a net profit attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 2.31 billion in the second quarter of 2026, turning to a net loss of KRW 3.21 billion in the third quarter.

In its preliminary first-quarter 2026 announcement on May 15, 2026, the company said gross profit rose 85% year on year to about KRW 1.6 billion.

An August 18, 2026 company announcement put first-half 2026 revenue at KRW 10.63 billion, up 22.0%, and the operating loss at KRW 5.43 billion, matching the sum of the disclosed quarters. The company attributed the wider first-half operating loss to higher R&D spending.

05

Industry analysis

Radiopharmaceuticals bind radioactive isotopes to target molecules for diagnosis or treatment.

Diagnostic agents are used to locate lesions on PET imaging, and Prostavue is fluorine-18 based with a half-life of about 110 minutes, which the company cites as a commercial advantage for mass production and multi-site supply.

Prostate cancer is the most common cancer among men in the United States, with about 330,000 new patients a year reported for 2026.

On the treatment side, PSMA-targeted radioligand therapies draw interest from global pharmaceutical companies, and the company says FC705 is being developed to achieve tumor uptake at a lower dose than existing therapies. The company said its U.S.

Phase 2a was designed in a way similar to commercialized prostate radiotherapies so that tumor uptake and safety data can be compared on the same basis. In competition, FutureChem points to its existing diagnostic lineup for Alzheimer's and Parkinson's and its automated synthesis devices as differentiators.

Verified data on competitors' sales and market share are limited to company materials. On the overseas side, a June 2026 news report said FutureChem recovered European rights to FC303 from a partner early and is in talks with two new partners.

Outlook

In Korea, the first sales of Prostavue were announced on August 20, 2026, and the company plans to expand the number of supplying medical institutions in stages.

Overseas, the company said on August 18, 2026 that the China Phase 3 of FC303 has been completed and that it plans to apply for marketing approval with China's drug regulator; no approval timing has been disclosed. In a shareholder letter, the company said it expects 2026 consolidated revenue to grow by 30% or more.

First-half 2026 revenue grew 22.0%, so meeting that target would require faster growth in the second half than in the first. On the treatment side, topline results from the U.S. Phase 2a trial of FC705 were announced on October 23, 2026.

The CEO said complete responses were seen in 5 of 18 evaluable patients, and the PSA50 response rate was reported at 58.8%. Based on these results, the company is negotiating technology transfer with global pharmaceutical companies and says it aims for FC705 to be best in class.

The proceeds of the roughly KRW 40 billion rights offering are planned for FC705 clinical costs and new pipelines, including pancreatic cancer and triple-negative breast cancer. In June 2026, the company also joined a Korean ARPA-H consortium project with Seoul National University Hospital and others.

Bear factors

Continued losses and wider losses in some periods

The operating loss was recorded in each of the four years from 2022 to 2025. The net loss attributable to controlling shareholders widened to KRW 11.3 billion in 2025 from KRW 9.42 billion in 2024. The four-quarter total of such losses through the third quarter of 2026 is KRW 10.5 billion. The third quarter of 2026 still showed an operating loss of KRW 1.87 billion.

Uneven growth pace

Second-quarter 2026 revenue of KRW 4.27 billion fell 32.9% from the prior quarter. First-half revenue growth of 22.0% is below the 30% or more the company expects for the full year. Quarterly revenue has been uneven, so achieving the annual target depends on the second half.

After the second-quarter profit, a loss reappeared in the third quarter, so whether quarterly profit recurs is the next point to check.

Funding needs and a larger share count

The company decided on a rights offering of about KRW 40 billion, with new shares of 3,389,830 at a planned issue price of KRW 11,800 per share. A bonus issue of 0.2 shares per common share would issue 5,163,450 shares. A larger share count dilutes per-share measures, and continued clinical spending keeps funding needs high.

10

Risk factors

Regulatory and approval

In March 2026, the Ministry of Food and Drug Safety issued a warning over procedural violations found while checking the reliability of data for the FC303 approval application. The company said the data were not compromised and that corrective and preventive actions were completed.

The timing and outcome of the China approval application have not been disclosed. Any delay in regulatory timelines could affect the revenue plan.

Clinical uncertainty

The U.S. Phase 2a trial of FC705 evaluated only 18 patients, a small sample. Complete responses in 5 patients come from a small early-stage study and should be read alongside later data. Results of the Korean Phase 3 study and the final approval timing have not been fixed. Technology transfer terms may change depending on how the clinical data are interpreted.

Funding and dilution

The company announced a roughly KRW 40 billion rights offering and a bonus issue to be carried out in the second half of 2026. The proceeds were disclosed as operating funds, and actual spending should be checked in later reports.

The debt ratio was 35.9% at the end of 2025, but while operating losses continue, further funding needs remain. A larger share count lowers existing shareholders' stake.

Overall view

FutureChem has entered a stage of broadening its product revenue base with the approval and first sales of the diagnostic Prostavue. On a disclosed basis, annual revenue has risen for three consecutive years from 2023 through 2025, and the operating margin improved from -102.9% in 2022 to -41.5% in 2025.

However, operating losses have been recorded for four consecutive years and for five consecutive quarters through the third quarter of 2026. The return to profit in the second quarter of 2026 lasted only one quarter. The U.S.

Phase 2a results for the therapy FC705 and the technology transfer talks are key variables for pipeline value, but the trial sample is small and the data are at an interim stage.

The company's expectation of 30% or more annual revenue growth in 2026 compares with 22.0% growth in the first half, so the second half would need to accelerate. The roughly KRW 40 billion rights offering provides funding but also increases the share count.

The next checkpoints are the annual revenue growth and operating loss trend in the 2026 annual report, and whether a FC705 technology transfer agreement is signed.
