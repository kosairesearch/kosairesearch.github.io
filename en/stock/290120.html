DH Autolead: Revenue Growth Versus Softer Quarterly Profit

DH Autolead grew revenue and operating profit in 2025 from its steering wheel business anchored by Hyundai Motor and KIA, but operating profit fell for two straight quarters in 2026 and the third quarter posted a controlling shareholder net loss.

  2. 2

  3. 3

  4. 4

02

Business structure

DH Autolead develops and manufactures automotive steering wheels and produces the core components in-house, from the frame and leather and interior materials to switches, bezels, heating elements and parts linked to advanced driver assistance systems (ADAS).

Its integrated process covers casting, foaming, leather covering and assembly, and the company presents this setup as the basis of its cost competitiveness.

Its main customers are Hyundai Motor and KIA, and in investor relations materials covering the second quarter of 2026 the company put its steering wheel market share with the two automakers at 53.6%. The domestic steering wheel market is an oligopoly in which one other major player competes alongside the company.

Production is centered on the Mexico plant, which the company says accounts for a large share of parts bound for the United States. Labor-cost savings from overseas subsidiaries are also cited as a feature of its cost structure.

The company was previously part of the Daeyu Winia group and was acquired by a group company in November 2023, after which it pursued balance sheet repair.

In September 2025 it invested about KRW 21.5 billion through a private equity fund in the acquisition of Dhautonex, a company then under court-supervised rehabilitation. It is also pursuing physical AI-based automation alongside this business.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual revenue rose from KRW 226 billion in 2022 to KRW 235.6 billion in 2023 (up 4.3%), KRW 270.4 billion in 2024 (up 14.8%) and KRW 355.6 billion in 2025 (up 31.5%). Revenue grew for three consecutive years from 2023 through 2025, and 2025 was the largest year of the 2022 to 2025 span.

Operating profit fell from KRW 22.3 billion in 2022 to KRW 15.8 billion in 2023 (down 29.3%), then recovered to KRW 16.5 billion in 2024 (up 4.8%) and KRW 21.9 billion in 2025 (up 32.5%), and it rose for two consecutive years from 2024 to 2025 after the 2023 low.

The operating margin was 9.9% in 2022, 6.7% in 2023, 6.1% in 2024 and 6.2% in 2025, so margins had not returned to the 2022 level even as sales expanded. For 2023, a company official said part of the borrowing was used for new overseas plant construction and capacity expansion.

Controlling shareholder net income moved from KRW 23.9 billion in 2022 to a net loss of KRW 7.55 billion in 2023, returned to a KRW 2.3 billion profit in 2024, and reached KRW 13.9 billion in 2025.

Net income including non-controlling interests was KRW 13.6 billion in 2025, a different figure from controlling shareholder net income.

Total equity at the end of 2025 was KRW 110.8 billion, of which KRW 99.3 billion belonged to controlling shareholders, total liabilities were KRW 203.1 billion, and the debt ratio rose from 163.5% in 2022 to 183.3% in 2025 (184.1% in 2024).

Operating cash flow was KRW 19.9 billion in 2022, KRW 12.1 billion in 2023, KRW 6.25 billion in 2024 and KRW 12 billion in 2025.

Quarterly revenue went from KRW 87.4 billion in the third quarter of 2025 to KRW 96 billion in the fourth quarter, KRW 98 billion in the first quarter of 2026 and KRW 101.9 billion in the second quarter, then fell to KRW 95.6 billion in the third quarter (down 6.2% quarter on quarter, up 9.4% year on year).

Quarterly operating profit peaked at KRW 5.2 billion in the first quarter of 2026 and then declined for two consecutive quarters to KRW 4.06 billion and KRW 3.37 billion, with the third quarter down 50.8% year on year.

Controlling shareholder net income was KRW 3.77 billion in the first quarter of 2026 and KRW 6.31 billion in the second quarter, then turned into a KRW 4.37 billion net loss in the third quarter; the four quarters from the fourth quarter of 2025 through the third quarter of 2026 total KRW 7.49 billion.

Operating cash flow in the first quarter of 2026 was negative KRW 9.65 billion, compared with a KRW 9.8 billion inflow a year earlier, and short-term borrowings rose from KRW 32.6 billion at the end of 2025 to KRW 41.7 billion at the end of March 2026.

05

Industry analysis

Domestic auto demand rebounded in 2025, helped by interest rate cuts, a cut in the special consumption tax, government policy and a recovery in the electric vehicle market. Exports fell the same year as high US tariffs coincided with the start-up of new plants.

For 2026, new model launches, replacement demand and rising demand for eco-friendly vehicles are expected to support a domestic recovery. Exports are forecast to grow as tariff uncertainty eases and shipments of eco-friendly vehicles hold up.

Steering wheels are tied to automaker production volumes, so shifts in this cycle pass through to the company's volumes fairly directly. The domestic market is an oligopoly in which a small number of suppliers share business, including one other major player.

The company says its share of steering wheel supply to Hyundai Motor and KIA reached 53.6% in the second quarter of 2026, which means the two automakers' production plans strongly shape its revenue direction.

On cost, the Mexico production base and labor-cost savings at overseas subsidiaries are presented as differentiators against domestic peers. Automation spending is a common challenge across auto parts makers, and the company has set production automation and process unmanning as its main direction. The industry is currently in a phase where hopes for a domestic recovery coexist with export-related headwinds.

06

Outlook

The company said it will take part in a Jeonbuk and Gyeongnam physical AI research and development program running from 2026 to 2030, applying AI-based automation to steering wheel manufacturing step by step and aiming over time at unmanned production.

The program's total size of KRW 1.4131 trillion covers the entire research and development project and is not the company's own investment or order value. The company has unveiled a dark factory roadmap that would invest KRW 30.3 billion through 2028 to make the entire steering wheel production process unmanned.

Overseas, it plans to roll out the model in sequence to subsidiaries in China, Slovakia and Vietnam, starting with the Mexico plant in 2026. The company expects logistics handling speed to be five times faster than manual work and sorting accuracy to reach 99.9%, which are its own projections.

The company has received a request for quotation from a US automaker and is responding to technical and quality evaluations, with the aim of moving on to supply contracts and mass production if those evaluations succeed.

Securing new overseas customers depends on the supplier selection process, so that step is still under way. The company is also pursuing a diversified overseas production base to raise efficiency and ease manufacturing cost pressure.

On the balance sheet, KRW 4.3 billion of the KRW 42.2 billion in rehabilitation repayment funds remained as of the second quarter of 2026, and the company targets full repayment within the year.

Borrowing rose after the September 2025 acquisition of Dhautonex, but most of the rehabilitation debt tied to that deal has already been repaid.

Bull factors

Growth in revenue and a recovery in profit

Revenue rose for three straight years from 2023 through 2025, and operating profit grew 32.5% in 2025. Controlling shareholder net income turned to a profit in 2024 and rose to KRW 13.9 billion in 2025, showing a clear direction of earnings recovery. The 53.6% share with Hyundai Motor and KIA and the domestic oligopoly structure serve as a volume base.

Possible expansion of overseas orders

The company has received a request for quotation from a US automaker and is responding to technical and quality evaluations. The Mexico plant already handles a large share of parts bound for the United States, giving it North American supply experience. Diversifying the overseas production base is a stated plan to reduce manufacturing cost pressure.

Automation spending and a plan for cost structure gains

A dark factory roadmap that would invest KRW 30.3 billion through 2028 has been announced, with overseas rollout starting at the Mexico plant. The company expects logistics handling to become five times faster than manual work.

It is also building automation capability through participation in the Jeonbuk and Gyeongnam physical AI program.

09

Bear factors

Slowing quarterly profit and a return to loss

Operating profit fell for two consecutive quarters in the second and third quarters of 2026, and third-quarter operating profit was down 50.8% year on year. Controlling shareholder net income turned into a KRW 4.37 billion net loss in the third quarter.

The KRW 7.49 billion total for the latest four quarters is a little above half of the KRW 13.9 billion recorded for 2025.

A low operating margin and limited margin recovery

The operating margin declined from 9.9% in 2022 to 6.2% in 2025. Revenue expanded after 2023, but margins did not return to the 2022 level. A company official said borrowing increased during 2023 as the company invested in new overseas operations.

Financial burden and weaker cash flow

The debt ratio stood at 183.3% at the end of 2025, a structure with heavy liabilities relative to equity. Operating cash flow in the first quarter of 2026 turned into a negative KRW 9.65 billion outflow, and short-term borrowings rose to KRW 41.7 billion at the end of March 2026.

In June 2025, conversion rights were exercised and 250,000 new shares were issued, lifting total shares outstanding to 15.52 million.

10

Risk factors

Customer concentration and automaker production swings

The revenue base is concentrated in Hyundai Motor and KIA, so swings in those two automakers' output and sales feed directly into the company's volumes. The domestic steering wheel market is an oligopoly, which may limit how quickly new domestic customers can be added. Whether the expected domestic recovery turns into higher production is the next point to confirm.

Trade policy and overseas production structure

A large share of parts bound for the United States is produced at the Mexico plant. Changes in US tariff policy could affect costs and export volumes. The company explained earlier, when tariffs were first imposed, that the size of the burden had not yet been tallied.

Liquidity and equity dilution

Short-term borrowings increased while the debt ratio is high, and operating cash flow turned negative in the first quarter. Borrowing for the subsidiary acquisition overlapped with rehabilitation debt repayment, making liquidity management a key variable. Share count has also increased after conversion rights were exercised and new shares were issued.

12

Overall view

DH Autolead has grown revenue for three straight years since 2023, and in 2025 both operating profit and controlling shareholder net income rose, showing revenue and earnings recovering together.

However, the operating margin slipped from 9.9% in 2022 to 6.2% in 2025, and operating profit declined for two consecutive quarters in the second and third quarters of 2026.

In the third quarter of 2026 the company posted a controlling shareholder net loss of KRW 4.37 billion, so the annual and quarterly trends point in different directions.

Strengths include the 53.6% share with Hyundai Motor and KIA, the US request for quotation, the Mexico production base and the dark factory automation plan. Weaknesses include a debt ratio of 183.3%, a negative first-quarter 2026 operating cash flow, rising short-term borrowings and customer concentration.

On valuation, the price-to-book and price-to-earnings ratios sit at low levels, but earnings swings are large, so the multiples are best read together with the earnings trend.

The next checkpoint is the 2026 annual business report, and before it, completion of the rehabilitation debt repayment, application of the dark factory model at the Mexico plant and progress on the US supply contract can be confirmed as facts.
