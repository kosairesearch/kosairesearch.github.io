Backlog Grows, Quarterly Profit Stays Mixed

HanmiGlobal's end-September 2026 backlog rose from the end of 2025, yet third-quarter 2026 net income attributable to controlling shareholders fell to KRW 560 million, so the earnings trend remains mixed.

  3. 3

  4. 4

02

Business structure

HanmiGlobal is a construction project management (PM/CM) firm whose revenue comes mainly from service fees. Unlike large builders that carry out construction themselves, it manages design, schedule and cost on behalf of clients.

Its business spans high-tech work (semiconductors and data centers), domestic general building and redevelopment, and overseas PM. Growth drivers cited include semiconductor investment, data-center orders and redevelopment projects.

Domestic backlog has continued to grow on a gradual recovery in property and fresh redevelopment demand, according to Hyundai Motor Securities. Overseas, Saudi Arabia, the United States and the United Kingdom are identified as key growth regions, with Saudi revenue expected to expand on a Riyadh urban development win.

US subsidiary Otak has worked on public infrastructure since its 2011 acquisition and has collaborated with the National Park Service for about 20 years.

Newsis reported that overseas sales were KRW 261.3 billion in 2025 on a consolidated basis, about 58% of the total, with about KRW 127.7 billion from the United States. Customers include domestic conglomerates building factories abroad and public clients.

Hyundai Motor Securities also noted that Samsung Electronics' Pyeongtaek plant construction restart began to generate service revenue. Among domestic PM firms, HanmiGlobal is the only one it sees as capable of running large nuclear-type projects.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual revenue rose from KRW 374.4 billion in 2022 to KRW 412.9 billion in 2023 (up 10.3% year on year), KRW 424.8 billion in 2024 (up 2.9%) and KRW 448.8 billion in 2025 (up 5.7%). Revenue has increased for three consecutive years from 2023 through 2025, and 2025 is the largest year of 2022–2025.

Operating profit was KRW 30.7 billion in 2022, KRW 29.6 billion in 2023 (down 3.3%), KRW 33.9 billion in 2024 (up 14.5%) and KRW 30.6 billion in 2025 (down 9.7%), with 2024 the largest year.

Revenue grew over three years while operating profit fell after its 2024 peak, and operating margin slid from 8.2% in 2022 to 6.8% in 2025.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 23.4 billion in 2022, KRW 14.2 billion in 2023 (down 39.2%), KRW 20 billion in 2024 (up 40.6%) and KRW 18.5 billion in 2025 (down 7.4%).

Net income including non-controlling interests was KRW 26.9 billion, KRW 16.7 billion, KRW 22.9 billion and KRW 19.6 billion over the same years, a different line from the controlling-shareholder figure.

Operating cash flow was KRW 21.2 billion in 2022, KRW 769 million in 2023, KRW 26.4 billion in 2024 and KRW 40.8 billion in 2025. Debt-to-equity was 91.7% in 2022, 96.7% in 2023, 106.9% in 2024 and 81.7% in 2025.

At end-2025, total equity was KRW 245.8 billion, of which KRW 227.7 billion belonged to controlling shareholders, and total liabilities were KRW 200.8 billion.

Quarterly revenue went from KRW 105.3 billion in 3Q 2025 to KRW 112.6 billion in 4Q 2025 (up 7.0% quarter on quarter) and KRW 112 billion in 1Q 2026 (down 0.5%), then rose to KRW 119.7 billion in 2Q 2026 and KRW 121.6 billion in 3Q 2026 (up 15.5% year on year).

Operating profit was KRW 2.97 billion in 1Q 2026, the low point (down 55.1% quarter on quarter), before KRW 6.25 billion in 2Q and KRW 6.53 billion in 3Q (down 4.4% year on year).

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 2.84 billion in 1Q 2026, KRW 5.51 billion in 2Q and KRW 560 million in 3Q (down 88.4% year on year). The last four quarters total KRW 10.1 billion.

IBK Securities attributed the 3Q operating shortfall mainly to a one-time bad-debt charge of about KRW 3.5 billion at Otak, and said the standalone parent's operating margin improved to 12.9%.

Revenue and operating profit recovered, but controlling-shareholder net income stayed weak, so the gap between operating profit and that line needs confirmation in the full filings.

05

Industry analysis

The domestic construction market is described as a gradual property recovery combined with fresh redevelopment demand. Hyundai Motor Securities expected a gradual recovery in domestic general building and a rising contribution from high-tech work.

Its September 2026 commentary described the high-tech division as entering an upcycle, with data-center investment translating into orders. Hanwha Investment & Securities expected Saudi sales, weak in the first quarter, to recover on solid order flow.

The US unit has performed services for most large domestic groups' US investment projects. Public infrastructure is a further avenue, with the Gadeok airport win as a base and Middle East airport opportunities under pursuit. Against large builders, HanmiGlobal emphasizes its PM/CM expertise and US local network.

Nuclear participation remains at the possibility stage and depends on Team Korea. The cycle looks expansionary in high-tech and US investment, while domestic general building and the Middle East depend on the pace of recovery.

06

Outlook

The largest project confirmed is the NPS IDIQ contract that Otak announced in April 2026, running five years from March 2026 to February 2031 with a total value of about USD 400 million. Because the contract is multi-award, revenue is recognized project by project rather than guaranteed by the headline amount.

In November 2026 the company announced a design-stage PM role for the Gadeok airport passenger terminal, with pre-construction work running to January 2030. Hyundai Motor Securities estimated about KRW 5 billion in quarterly revenue from the Pyeongtaek extension.

Forecasts diverge: Hanwha Investment & Securities expected about 10% top-line growth for 2026 in a March report, while IBK Securities estimated 6.8% revenue growth and lower operating profit in November 2026. Whether new orders expand again is the key variable IBK flagged.

Next confirmation points are the 4Q 2026 preliminary results, year-end backlog in the 2026 annual report, and whether nuclear projects are awarded.

08

Bull factors

Backlog expansion

The larger backlog means more contracted work is accumulating to be recognized as revenue. Cumulative new service contracts for January to September totaled KRW 358.4 billion. Hyundai Motor Securities said the domestic division's backlog keeps rising.

High-tech and data-center demand

Rising orders in semiconductors, data centers and redevelopment are the backdrop for PM demand. Hyundai Motor Securities reported that data-center investment is already converting into orders. Whether this reaches revenue and margins will show up in upcoming results.

Improving standalone profitability

Standalone operating profit grew faster than revenue, lifting profit relative to sales. IBK Securities estimated adjusted operating profit excluding the one-time charge at about KRW 10 billion. This is an analyst estimate, separate from the filed results.

Bear factors

Quarterly profit volatility

Controlling-shareholder net income fell 88.4% year on year to KRW 560 million in 3Q 2026. Operating profit hit its low point at KRW 2.97 billion in 1Q 2026. Because revenue growth has not consistently translated into profit, the durability of the recovery is not yet confirmed.

Overseas revenue exposure

Hanwha Investment & Securities said in June 2026 that Saudi sales, weak in the first quarter, should recover on solid orders. With about 58% of 2025 sales from overseas, regional risks flow directly into results. The Saudi unit also had partial revenue adjustments after its site moved from NEOM to Riyadh.

Slower new orders

Backlog fell 6.5% from 779.6 billion won in the prior quarter, which IBK Securities viewed as a temporary effect of revenue recognition. Whether the lower new order level expands again is the next point to check. The key question is whether new contracts refill the backlog.

10

Risk factors

Overseas concentration

About 58% of 2025 consolidated sales came from overseas. Regional project timing and local conditions can move quarterly revenue. Saudi sales weakened in one quarter of 2026.

One-time losses and receivables

A one-time bad-debt charge of about KRW 3.5 billion at Otak weighed on third-quarter operating profit. Service businesses can see earnings swing with client payment delays and receivable collection.

Nuclear and policy-project dependence

Hyundai Motor Securities judged the company's participation likely if KHNP awards proceed. A delay could weaken that expectation. Weak first-quarter results and a slump in nuclear-related stocks previously drove sharp share price moves.

Overall view

HanmiGlobal's revenue rose for three consecutive years from 2023 through 2025 to KRW 448.8 billion, but operating profit fell in 2025 to KRW 30.6 billion from KRW 33.9 billion in 2024, and operating margin was 6.8%. Quarterly revenue grew in 2Q and 3Q 2026, reaching KRW 121.6 billion in 3Q, up 15.5% year on year.

Controlling-shareholder net income fell 88.4% to KRW 560 million in 3Q, which IBK Securities linked partly to a one-time bad-debt charge of about KRW 3.5 billion at its US subsidiary.

The end-September 2026 backlog rose 14.6% from year-end 2025 to KRW 728.8 billion, with public infrastructure wins including the Gadeok airport and the US National Park Service contract.

Hanwha Investment & Securities set a KRW 36,000 target in June 2026 and IBK Securities a KRW 26,000 target in November 2026, and the two views point in different directions.

Order expansion and overseas PM reach are the strengths, while quarterly profit volatility and overseas regional risk are the weaknesses, and both coexist. Next checkpoints are the 4Q 2026 preliminary results, the year-end backlog in the 2026 annual report, and whether Saudi sales recover in 1Q 2027.
