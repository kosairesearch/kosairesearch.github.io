02

Business structure

SFA focuses on AI robotics and intralogistics automation equipment. In Q1 2026, robot logistics accounted for 82% of standalone revenue and the AI solutions business for 18%.

The Robotic Material Handling (RMH) segment includes subsea cable burial automation equipment and logistics systems for large retail and appliance customers, and Samsung Securities said RMH made up 44% of Q1 2026 new orders.

Consolidated subsidiary SFA Semicon (55.2% stake) makes semiconductor equipment, entered the HBM OHT equipment market in 2025 and added new inspection-equipment orders in 2026. SFA Semicon's Q1 2026 revenue reached KRW 107.3 billion, up 42% year on year.

Another consolidated subsidiary, CIS (33.2% stake), makes secondary-battery equipment and has been affected by slower electric-vehicle demand. The company attributed the decline in Q1 2026 consolidated operating profit mainly to weakness in CIS's battery business.

By segment, robot logistics depends heavily on large projects, while the chip business turns on how far SFA extends into HBM processes. The company has set a target of supplying fully autonomous manufacturing solutions by 2030.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual revenue rose from KRW 1.68 trillion in 2022 to KRW 1.88 trillion in 2023, up 11.7%, and to KRW 2.05 trillion in 2024, up 8.7%, making 2024 the largest year in the 2022 to 2025 span. Revenue fell 20.3% to KRW 1.63 trillion in 2025, the smallest year in that span.

The company cited delayed customer schedules caused by the slowdown in electric-vehicle batteries as the reason for the 2025 standalone revenue decline. Operating profit fell from KRW 160.9 billion in 2022 to KRW 85.5 billion in 2023, down 46.9%, and the company posted an operating loss of KRW 48.4 billion in 2024.

According to its corporate value-up plan, the 2024 result included a one-time recognition of losses of KRW 212.2 billion tied to the bankruptcy of a battery customer, and the company said operating margin would have been 10.8% excluding that loss.

Operating profit returned to KRW 85.9 billion in 2025, with an operating margin of 5.3%, still below the 9.6% recorded in 2022.

Profit attributable to controlling shareholders went from KRW 77.2 billion in 2022 to KRW 40 billion in 2023, down 48.2%, then to a net loss of KRW 72.6 billion in 2024 before returning to KRW 55.1 billion in 2025.

Net income including non-controlling interests was KRW 52.1 billion in 2025, a different figure from profit attributable to controlling shareholders. Quarterly operating profit went from KRW 26.7 billion in Q3 2025 to KRW 8.99 billion in Q4 2025, down 66.3% from the prior quarter.

It then reached KRW 15.4 billion in Q1 2026, up 71.9%, KRW 29 billion in Q2, up 87.7%, and KRW 22.1 billion in Q3, down 23.7%. Q3 2026 revenue of KRW 430.2 billion rose 9.2% year on year, while operating profit fell 17.1% year on year.

Profit attributable to controlling shareholders over the last four quarters totaled KRW 55.5 billion. Total equity at the end of 2025 was KRW 1.51 trillion, and the debt-to-equity ratio moved from 36.3% in 2022 to 74.6% in 2023, 62.9% in 2024 and 49.8% in 2025.

Operating cash flow was KRW 175.2 billion in 2022, negative KRW 76.7 billion in 2023, KRW 402.6 billion in 2024 and KRW 151.6 billion in 2025.

05

Industry analysis

End-market demand has two drivers: power infrastructure investment tied to AI data center growth and expanding HBM production.

In its Q1 2026 quarterly filing, the company said orders for subsea cables and fuel-cell equipment were rising sharply as offshore wind and hydrogen fuel-cell capacity expanded to meet AI-driven power demand.

Subsea cable orders included a large overseas project at the end of 2025, and the fuel-cell segment accounted for 18% of Q1 2026 new orders according to Samsung Securities.

On the chip side, BNK Investment & Securities read a supply agreement signed with a major DRAM maker at the end of 2025 as a route to supplying equipment to HBM fabs. In batteries, electric-vehicle demand remains sluggish, and the company said CIS's battery business softened for this reason.

From a cycle perspective, AI-linked power and chip demand is expanding while battery demand is in a slower phase, so the two drivers point in different directions. SFA's competitive position rests on its entry into the HBM OHT process and its subsea cable and RMH order record.

Because robot logistics demand moves in large project units, the timing and size of order announcements act as a leading indicator for quarterly results. The chip subsidiary is widening its product mix as it adds inspection-equipment orders.

06

Outlook

The most specific target the company has set is a first-half 2026 order target of KRW 530 billion, up 63% year on year, presented in its Q1 2026 quarterly filing.

Samsung Securities estimated 2026 full-year new orders at KRW 1.1 trillion, up 55% from 2025, citing possible additional RMH and fuel-cell orders and potential orders from Chinese display makers.

BNK Investment & Securities said that, after its Q1 2026 results, second-half chip and RMH orders could push full-year orders above KRW 1 trillion. The same BNK report estimated 2026 revenue at KRW 1.64 trillion and operating profit at KRW 110.1 billion, and expected SFA Semicon to swing to profit in the second half.

For the longer term, the company has set a goal of supplying fully autonomous manufacturing solutions by 2030, with stated priorities of strengthening the chip and robot logistics businesses and expanding subsea cable projects.

On shareholder returns, the company said in its corporate value-up plan that it raised the 2025 dividend payout ratio to 35.2%. The next checkpoints are fourth-quarter and full-year 2026 results and the full-year order volume to be disclosed in the 2026 annual report.

Bull factors

Wider order pipeline

New orders rose 18.3% year on year to KRW 217.9 billion in Q1 2026, led by the RMH segment. Samsung Securities estimated 2026 full-year orders at KRW 1.1 trillion, up 55% from 2025, citing possible additional subsea cable and fuel-cell orders. BNK Investment & Securities put standalone order backlog at KRW 965.4 billion as of Q1 2026.

Growth at the chip subsidiary

SFA Semicon entered the HBM OHT equipment market in 2025 and added new inspection-equipment orders in 2026. BNK Investment & Securities expected the unit to swing to profit in the second half of 2026.

Improving profit structure

Standalone operating margin rose from 11.5% in Q1 2026 to 13.8% in Q3, and gross margin rose from 18.8% to 21.7% over the same period. The company attributed the gains to a larger share of high-value items and stronger cost competitiveness. The company said in its value-up plan that it raised the 2025 dividend payout ratio to 35.2%.

Bear factors

Battery slowdown and subsidiary weakness

CIS's battery business slowed under the electric-vehicle slowdown and was cited as the reason for the drop in Q1 2026 consolidated operating profit. Revenue fell 20.3% to KRW 1.63 trillion in 2025.

The company's value-up plan said losses of KRW 212.2 billion tied to a battery customer's bankruptcy were recognized in 2024.

Volatile quarterly profit

Q4 2025 operating profit fell 66.3% from the prior quarter to KRW 8.99 billion, and Q3 2026 operating profit fell 23.7% to KRW 22.1 billion. Samsung Securities said Q1 2026 operating profit of KRW 15.4 billion fell short of a market expectation of KRW 25.8 billion.

Margin pressure at the chip subsidiary

Samsung Securities said the margin at consolidated subsidiary SFA Semicon was -5.8% in Q1 2026, a larger loss than expected. The same analysis attributed this to a weak product mix in the quarter. With 82% of standalone revenue coming from robot logistics, the business is also concentrated in one segment.

10

Risk factors

Customer and cycle concentration

CIS's secondary-battery business moves with electric-vehicle demand, which widens swings in consolidated results. The 2025 decline in standalone revenue was also attributed to delayed customer schedules caused by the battery slowdown. The timing of any battery demand recovery will determine the direction of CIS's results.

Order timing and project dependence

Large orders in subsea cables, fuel cells and RMH drive quarterly revenue, so delays in project delivery push back revenue recognition. Quarterly revenue went from KRW 361.1 billion in Q1 2026 to KRW 430.2 billion in Q3, but the quarter-to-quarter swings are large. Gaps between full-year estimates and quarterly results can therefore arise.

Subsidiary results and ownership structure

SFA Semicon (55.2% stake) and CIS (33.2% stake) are consolidated, so subsidiary losses and industry changes flow directly into parent-level operating profit. In 2024, losses tied to a battery customer's bankruptcy were recognized in a lump sum, turning the company to an operating loss of KRW 48.4 billion.

The ownership structure also creates gaps between consolidated profit and profit attributable to controlling shareholders.

Overall view

Orders are growing, with Q1 2026 new orders up 18.3% year on year and robot logistics and chips leading the gain. The price-to-book ratio sits below 1x, and on an earnings basis the last four quarters total KRW 55.5 billion of profit attributable to controlling shareholders.

The next checkpoints are fourth-quarter and full-year 2026 results, the full-year order volume in the 2026 annual report, and first-quarter 2027 results.
