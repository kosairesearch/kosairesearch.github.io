Report overview

After the Restructuring: Revenue Recovery Is the Test

Kodaco has re-cut its capital structure through a court-approved debt-to-equity conversion and an eight-to-one share consolidation, yet in the core business quarterly revenue has turned down again and operating results hover around break-even, leaving balance-sheet repair and operating recovery as two separate tasks.

    A tier-two supplier of aluminium die-cast auto parts, shipping engine, transmission, HVAC and steering components through tier-one customers such as Hanon Systems, Hyundai Transys, Mando and Hyundai Mobis, and overseas via BorgWarner and AAM.

  3. 3

02

Business structure

Kodaco was founded in February 1997 as a maker of aluminium die-cast automotive components and listed on KOSDAQ in December 2000. It runs its headquarters and a plant in Cheonan, Chungcheongnam-do, plus plants in the Gongdo and Miyang districts of Anseong, Gyeonggi-do.

Its main products are internal mechanical components grouped into four areas: engine, transmission, air-conditioning and steering.

On the sales side, domestic shipments go through Hanon Systems, Hyundai Transys, Mando, LG Electronics and Hyundai Mobis to Hyundai, Kia and GM Korea, while exports run through BorgWarner, AAM and NSK to GM, Ford, Chrysler, Fiat and Tesla.

It is therefore not a direct tier-one supplier to carmakers but a tier-two supplier within the vehicle industry, and disclosed segment revenue detail is limited, so the mix is better assessed qualitatively.

The order model is distinctive: the company supplies according to carmakers' rolling production plans rather than standalone order contracts, and reports quarterly deliveries as its order total.

Production capacity has been steady, with an average of 60 machines in 2024, 2025 and the first quarter of 2026, implying operation of existing equipment rather than major expansion.

The overseas footprint has shrunk: the company is reported to have raised 20 billion won by transferring business rights related to its Mexican unit, MKDC, to fund rehabilitation-plan payments.

As competitive assets it has highlighted process and tooling technology, including patents for die-casting gas-removal and cooling-spray devices and what it calls the industry's first casting monitoring system in Korea.

04

Earnings analysis

The annual pattern shows repeated contraction and losses followed by a modest 2025 rebound. Revenue fell three years running, from 309.7 billion won in 2022 to 296.1 billion in 2023 and 251.4 billion in 2024, before rising to 271.0 billion won in 2025.

Operating results went from a 9.4 billion won profit in 2022 (3.0% margin) to losses of 28.2 billion won in 2023 (-9.5%) and 37.1 billion won in 2024 (-14.8%), then back to a 0.4 billion won profit in 2025 (0.1%), essentially break-even.

Bottom-line swings are large: owners' net profit of 52.2 billion won in 2024 came in a year with a 37.1 billion won operating loss, so non-operating factors were decisive, while 2025 showed an owners' net loss of 6.2 billion won again.

On the balance sheet, equity was negative at -20.5 billion won at end-2023, then stayed marginally positive at 13.0 billion in 2024 and 11.4 billion in 2025, and liabilities fell from 453.4 billion won in 2023 to 349.2 billion in 2025, yet the thin equity left the 2025 debt-to-equity ratio at 3,059.4% (consolidated December 2025 figures of 360.6 billion won of assets, 349.2 billion of liabilities and 11.4 billion of equity, with revenue of 271.0 billion won, operating profit of 0.4 billion and a net loss of 6.6 billion).

Operating cash flow turned outward after a 12.0 billion won inflow in 2023, at -9.5 billion in 2024 and -19.2 billion in 2025.

Quarterly, revenue slid from 66.0 billion won with a 0.8 billion operating profit in the second quarter of 2025 to 67.5 billion with a 0.8 billion loss, 70.8 billion with a 0.2 billion loss, 58.6 billion with a 0.6 billion profit, and 45.4 billion with a 0.5 billion loss in the second quarter of 2026.

Owners' net profit of 7.9 billion won in that latest quarter diverged from the operating loss, and because the capital and debt restructuring steps were concentrated in the same quarter, the composition should be verified in the quarterly report notes.

Summing the last four quarters from the third quarter of 2025 through the second quarter of 2026 gives revenue of 242.3 billion won, a small operating loss and owners' net profit of 4.3 billion won, meaning profit came from outside operations.

05

Industry analysis

The end market is tied directly to carmakers' output plans. Because the company states it supplies against customers' rolling production plans rather than separate order contracts, any cut in output or model changeover at domestic or overseas clients flows straight into quarterly revenue.

The step down in first- and second-quarter 2026 revenue versus the second half of 2025 illustrates that volume sensitivity.

On the industry side, the view that vehicle lightweighting lifts demand for aluminium die-cast parts has long been the growth argument, and the company has said it supplies electric compressors and steering components for electric vehicles (as stated in 2023).

During the electrification transition, however, volumes for combustion engine and transmission parts structurally shrink while new electrified items require upfront investment and price negotiation, leaving suppliers with a mix-shift challenge.

Its competitive position is constrained less by technology than by financial capacity: the plan calls for repaying about 152.0 billion won in total, comprising 89.6 billion of secured rehabilitation claims and 62.4 billion of unsecured claims, between 2024 and 2033, with reported current rehabilitation debt of 83.5 billion won due within a year, which makes large capacity-adding investment against rivals difficult.

Market rules have also tightened for financially weak issuers, as seen when the exchange raised the delisting market-capitalisation threshold for KOSDAQ from 15 billion to 20 billion won in July.

06

Outlook

The clearest confirmed change is the capital structure. The company received approval of a modified rehabilitation plan from the Suwon Bankruptcy Court on 19 May 2026 and disclosed the issue of 97,328,655 new common shares at 500 won each to third parties to convert rehabilitation claims into equity.

It then decided on a capital reduction consolidating eight common shares into one, an 87.5% reduction ratio, cutting shares outstanding from 112,814,744 to 14,101,673 and paid-in capital from 56.40737 billion won to 7.050836 billion won.

The reduction took effect on 21 May 2026, the business day after the 20 May 2026 effective date of the share issue, and after those steps a filing on the change of largest shareholder was submitted dated 27 May 2026. Listing procedures remain open.

The KOSDAQ Market Division of Korea Exchange notified a delisting decision and the start of liquidation trading in October 2025 over the rejected audit opinions for fiscal 2023 and 2024, but liquidation trading was reported as suspended after the company sought an injunction.

Market participants have described the fiscal 2025 audit opinion as the watershed for any trading resumption and continued listing, and the switch of external auditor for 2025 to Yeji Accounting Corp was cited as another variable.

No company guidance or brokerage estimates are visible in public sources, so the practical approach is to track disclosed quarterly revenue and utilisation, progress on rehabilitation debt repayment, and listing-related filings in sequence.

08

Bull factors

Capital restructuring has moved liabilities down

Liabilities have been managed below the earlier peak, from 453.4 billion won at end-2023 to 346.8 billion in 2024 and 349.2 billion in 2025, while equity moved out of negative territory from -20.5 billion won in 2023 to 13.0 billion in 2024 and 11.4 billion in 2025.

On top of that, the rehabilitation plan's conversion of claims into 97,328,655 new common shares was executed in May 2026. Because part of the debt becomes equity, the next financial statements will show whether interest burden and outstanding debt change materially.

Customer base and equipment operation have held

Revenue stayed in the 250 to 300 billion won range through the rehabilitation period and rose to 271.0 billion won in 2025.

The continuing supply structure through domestic tier-one customers such as Hanon Systems, Hyundai Transys, Mando and Hyundai Mobis and overseas channels including BorgWarner, AAM and NSK, together with an unchanged average of 60 machines in operation, suggests the production base has not been abruptly impaired. The first-half 2026 revenue decline, however, also shows how exposed that base is to volume swings.

Lightweighting and electrification as demand axes

The view that lightweighting increases demand for aluminium die-cast parts has been the sector's long-run demand logic. The company has said it supplies electric compressors and steering components for electric vehicles (as stated in 2023).

Current product-level mix and new order detail are not visible in public sources, so the actual revenue contribution from electrified items must be checked in future periodic reports.

09

Bear factors

Falling revenue and break-even operating profit

Quarterly revenue fell for two consecutive quarters, from 70.8 billion won in the fourth quarter of 2025 to 58.6 billion in the first quarter of 2026 and 45.4 billion in the second, far below the 66.0 billion won of a year earlier.

Operating results oscillate around break-even: -0.8 billion won, -0.2 billion, +0.6 billion and -0.5 billion across those quarters. For the full year 2025 the operating margin was just 0.1%. In casting, where fixed costs are heavy, lower volume feeds straight into margin.

Thin equity and cash outflow

At end-2025 equity was 11.4 billion won against 349.2 billion of liabilities, a debt-to-equity ratio of 3,059.4%. Operating cash flow was negative for two straight years, at -9.5 billion won in 2024 and -19.2 billion in 2025.

Combined with reported current rehabilitation debt of 83.5 billion won falling due within a year, meeting the repayment schedule from internally generated cash looks demanding.

Listing and accounting uncertainty

Samduk Accounting Corp issued consolidated audit opinion rejections for two consecutive years, for fiscal 2023 and 2024, citing insufficient audit evidence on the Mexican unit's financial statements and impairment review.

At end-2024 the company recognised impairment losses totalling 32.2 billion won, equal to 52% of equity, of which about 25.3 billion won, or 78%, related to MKDC.

With the delisting decision and liquidation-trading notice reported as suspended pending an injunction, continued listing and any trading resumption remain variables separate from operating results.

10

Risk factors

Liquidity and repayment schedule

The plan spreads repayment of about 152.0 billion won of claims over ten years from 2024 to 2033, implying recurring cash outflows. Operating cash flow, by contrast, was negative at -9.5 billion won in 2024 and -19.2 billion in 2025.

The company is reported to have raised 20 billion won by transferring business rights tied to the Mexican unit, but funding through asset sales is not readily repeatable.

Dilution and share-count changes

Before the issue of 97,328,655 shares for the debt-to-equity conversion, total shares outstanding were 15,486,089. Afterwards, an eight-to-one consolidation with an 87.5% reduction ratio reset shares outstanding to 14,101,673.

As conversion and reduction run back to back, existing holders' stakes and per-share metrics are mechanically recalculated, and further fundraising could repeat the effect.

Customer volume and input costs

Because supply volume follows carmakers' production plans, output cuts or model discontinuations at a given customer feed directly into quarterly results. The drop in second-quarter revenue from 66.0 billion won a year earlier to 45.4 billion illustrates that sensitivity.

Aluminium input prices, power and labour costs, and the outcome of unit-price negotiations with customers can also swing an operating margin already close to break-even.

12

Overall view

Kodaco has kept its core aluminium die-cast auto parts business while re-arranging capital and debt through court procedures.

On results, 2025 revenue of 271.0 billion won and operating profit of 0.4 billion marked a directional shift away from the large operating losses of 2023 and 2024, yet the 0.1% margin is essentially break-even and an owners' net loss of 6.2 billion won remained.

Into 2026, revenue stepped down clearly to 58.6 billion won in the first quarter and 45.4 billion in the second, with operating results alternating between profit and loss, so the 7.9 billion won owners' net profit in the second quarter should be read as heavily influenced by non-operating factors.

Financially, the thin structure of 11.4 billion won of equity against 349.2 billion of liabilities at end-2025 and two consecutive years of operating cash outflow persist, while the repayment schedule running to 2033 and current rehabilitation debt due within a year sit at the centre of cash planning.

The May 2026 debt-to-equity conversion and eight-to-one capital reduction mechanically changed share count and paid-in capital, weakening the continuity of per-share metrics and historical multiple comparisons.

The bullish case rests on liability reduction and a retained customer and equipment base, the bearish case on volume decline, thin equity and listing-procedure uncertainty, and neither has yet been validated by results.

The four items to watch, therefore, are quarterly revenue and operating results, repayment execution, the audit opinion, and the listing process. This report is for information purposes and does not contain a buy or sell opinion on any security.
