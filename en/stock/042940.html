Report overview

Backlog Rebuilds, Losses Persist, Funding Is the Key Test

Sangji Construction has posted net losses attributable to controlling shareholders for five straight quarters through the third quarter of 2026, yet a company official said its backlog including conditional contracts reached KRW 143.6 billion as of June 2026. 02

Business structure

Sangji Construction was founded in 1979 and listed on KOSDAQ in 2000, operating in real estate development, general construction, and electrical works.

Its in-house premium residential brands include a high-end villa line, a premium housing line, and an officetel line, and real estate development is regarded as the segment that defines the company.

Its subsidiaries are Orgaedeveloppment, Kyleum Dosan, and Sangji Green Energy, and Kyleum Dosan holds the land for the Nonhyeon-dong development in Gangnam, Seoul.

Public-sector contracts on the order list include a port facility repair project at Gwangyang Port for the Yeosu Gwangyang Port Authority with a contract value of KRW 2.5 billion, a youth center construction project for Jangseong County worth KRW 1.5 billion, and a road facility maintenance project worth KRW 900 million for the Iksan regional land office.

Private-sector work includes the headquarters building construction in Sinchon, Seongnam, where the contract value was raised from KRW 11.0 billion to KRW 14.7 billion for a term running from January 2026 to October 2027.

Revenue growth in the first half of 2026 was attributed to new general-construction contracts, and the company is extending its electrical and information and communication capabilities into smart-home and EV-charging infrastructure.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual revenue rose from KRW 53.8 billion in 2022 to KRW 173.9 billion in 2023, a 223.2% increase, before falling 88.3% to KRW 20.4 billion in 2024 and 8.9% to KRW 18.6 billion in 2025, a two-year decline from 2024 through 2025. Of the years from 2022 to 2025, 2023 was the largest and 2025 the smallest.

Operating profit moved from KRW 5.4 billion in 2022 to KRW 32.5 billion in 2023, a 500.8% increase, then turned into an operating loss of KRW 21.8 billion in 2024 and narrowed to an operating loss of KRW 6.85 billion in 2025.

The operating margin went from 18.7% in 2023 to -106.6% in 2024 and -36.8% in 2025, and the company has recorded losses in both 2024 and 2025.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 36.8 billion in 2023, a return to profit, followed by a net loss of KRW 26.7 billion in 2024 and a narrower net loss of KRW 13.5 billion in 2025.

Net income including non-controlling interests, which is a different figure, was KRW 37.2 billion in 2023 and a net loss of KRW 13.5 billion in 2025. The Bell reported that after the completion of its own development projects, the company could not start new projects amid a downturn in construction and real estate.

Operating cash flow was an outflow of KRW 13.1 billion in 2023 and KRW 24.8 billion in 2025, and the interest coverage ratio was -3.9 times in 2024, -1.4 times in 2025, and -4.3 times in the first quarter of 2026.

The debt-to-equity ratio fell from 245.0% in 2022 to 116.4% in 2025, with total equity of KRW 99.5 billion and total liabilities of KRW 115.8 billion at the end of 2025.

Quarterly revenue fell from KRW 8.31 billion in the third quarter of 2025 to KRW 5.36 billion in the fourth quarter, KRW 3.63 billion in the first quarter of 2026, and KRW 2.12 billion in the second quarter, before rebounding to KRW 3.39 billion in the third quarter of 2026, up 59.9% from the prior quarter but down 59.1% from a year earlier.

The fourth quarter of 2025 delivered an operating profit of KRW 472 million, a swing to profit from the prior quarter, but operating losses have continued for three straight quarters since the first quarter of 2026, narrowing to KRW 1.6 billion in the third quarter. Net losses attributable to controlling shareholders over the last four quarters total KRW 12.7 billion.

05

Industry analysis

Sangji Construction's revenue has relied on a mix of in-house development and contracting work, and The Bell reported in June 2026 that after its own development projects were completed, it could not start new projects amid a downturn in construction and real estate.

As a result, quarterly revenue fell from KRW 8.31 billion in the third quarter of 2025 to KRW 2.12 billion in the second quarter of 2026, the lowest of the five quarters.

A 2025 press report described government housing-supply expansion as an indirect positive for construction contract demand, though the same coverage treated it as an additional factor rather than the core growth driver, which it attributed to in-house development capacity and expanded contract wins.

On costs, rising material prices were reflected in design changes that raised a contract value in one project. Within the construction sector of 56 listed stocks, the company ranked 54th by 2025 annual revenue.

Its peer set spans large general contractors such as Samsung C&T, DL E&C, and Hyundai Engineering & Construction, and mid-sized builders such as Seohee Construction and KUMHO Engineering & Construction.

Because the company is built around mid-sized residential and office projects and public works, its project scale and order structure differ from those of the large contractors' redevelopment and overseas businesses. Even if the sector recovers, revenue depends on continuous project-by-project orders.

06

Outlook

The most direct basis for the outlook is the order flow the company has disclosed and reported. Backlog stood at KRW 80.8 billion at the end of 2023, KRW 35.2 billion at the end of 2024, and KRW 88.8 billion at the end of 2025, and reached KRW 96.2 billion at the end of the first quarter of 2026 (The Bell).

A company official said the backlog including conditional contracts was KRW 143.6 billion as of June 2026 (The Bell). Because this figure includes conditional contracts, it does not carry the same weight as reported results, and the pace at which the backlog converts into revenue is the item to watch.

The Sinchon headquarters project recognizes revenue on a percentage-of-completion basis for a term running from January 2026 to October 2027 (The Bell), the Sinchon public housing contract runs until October 31, 2027 (Dealsite), and the Escala project in Nonhyeon-dong started in late 2025 with completion scheduled for September 2029 (The Bell).

A securities filing also stated that because no new sale projects are underway, revenue is expected to decline and large operating and net losses are likely to recur (EKN), which the company itself identified as a risk to earnings.

The Nonhyeon-dong development rests on a bridge loan balance of KRW 68.2 billion, and the company has targeted completion of the zoning upgrade in February 2027 and execution of the main project financing in June 2027 (EKN).

The estimated appraisal value of KRW 120.6 billion for the land after the zoning upgrade assumes the change is completed and is not a present value.

A rights offering of about KRW 18.7 billion, approved by the board in May 2026, had subscription and listing schedules in July and August 2026 (EKN), and its final proceeds can be confirmed in the capital-change section of the 2026 annual report.

08

Bull factors

Backlog expansion and revenue visibility

Backlog rebounded to KRW 88.8 billion at the end of 2025 from KRW 35.2 billion at the end of 2024, and stood at KRW 96.2 billion at the end of the first quarter of 2026 (The Bell). A company official said the backlog including conditional contracts reached KRW 143.6 billion as of June 2026.

The increase in the headquarters contract from KRW 11.0 billion to KRW 14.7 billion is cited as a positive factor.

Narrowing losses and a recent profitable quarter

Operating losses narrowed from KRW 21.8 billion in 2024 to KRW 6.85 billion in 2025, and the fourth quarter of 2025 posted an operating profit of KRW 472 million, a swing to profit from the prior quarter.

The third-quarter 2026 operating loss of KRW 1.6 billion was smaller than the KRW 1.81 billion loss in the second quarter. A separate-basis report noted a KRW 300 million operating profit for the first quarter of 2026 (Dealsite), but this basis differs from the consolidated reported results.

Development option on the Nonhyeon-dong land

The company estimates that the Nonhyeon-dong land held by Kyleum Dosan could be appraised at about KRW 120.6 billion after a zoning upgrade (EKN). This estimate assumes the land-use change is completed, and the company said a delay in the main project financing would not materially affect its finances. The targeted timing for the zoning upgrade is February 2027.

09

Bear factors

Persistent losses and cash outflows

Operating losses have continued for three straight quarters since the first quarter of 2026, and net losses attributable to controlling shareholders over the last four quarters total KRW 12.7 billion. Operating cash flow moved from an inflow of KRW 3.57 billion in 2024 to an outflow of KRW 24.8 billion in 2025.

The interest coverage ratio was -4.3 times in the first quarter of 2026, meaning operating profit still does not cover interest (EKN).

Reliance on external funding

Since 2021 the company has repeatedly used rights offerings and convertible bonds, and its 2025 rights offering raised KRW 10.2 billion against a KRW 20 billion target (EKN). A securities filing said operating cash flow falls short of required levels, so fundraising may continue.

The company also stated that worsening issuance terms could lead to dilution and losses on invested capital (EKN).

Guarantee burden and reliance on the zoning upgrade

The bridge loan on the Nonhyeon-dong project stands at KRW 68.2 billion, and the company provides a joint guarantee of up to KRW 100.8 billion against it. That guarantee limit exceeds consolidated equity of about KRW 97.9 billion at the end of the first quarter of 2026 (EKN).

Commentary notes that a delay or failure of the zoning upgrade could affect project viability and the timing of the main project financing.

10

Risk factors

Development and project-financing timeline

If the zoning upgrade is not completed by the February 2027 target or the main project financing slips past June 2027, repayment of the KRW 68.2 billion bridge loan and the guarantee burden will extend. The KRW 120.6 billion appraisal assumes completion of the land-use change and could change with market conditions.

The company said it would proceed under the approved existing plan if the project financing does not materialize (EKN).

Funding needs and share dilution

Including rights offerings and convertible bonds, the company raised KRW 65.2 billion over the period since 2021, and would reach KRW 83.9 billion in total over five years if the 2026 rights offering is completed (EKN). The 2025 rights offering fell well short of its target, and public subscription was low. More shares outstanding dilute per-share figures and reduce the share of existing holders.

Construction progress and cost variables

Conversion of backlog into revenue depends on completion progress and cost control.

Rising material prices led to design changes in one project (The Bell, June 2026), and commentary notes that most ongoing sites, including the Sinchon headquarters, the Imsil project, and a Magok fire-works job, are at an early construction stage (EKN). Cost pressure at early-stage sites could slow the pace at which operating losses narrow.

12

Overall view

Sangji Construction's revenue fell from KRW 173.9 billion in 2023 to KRW 18.6 billion in 2025, and operating and net losses have continued.

After an operating profit in the fourth quarter of 2025, operating losses have run for three straight quarters since the first quarter of 2026, and net losses attributable to controlling shareholders have run for five straight quarters since the third quarter of 2025.

On the other side, backlog rose from KRW 88.8 billion at the end of 2025 to KRW 96.2 billion at the end of the first quarter of 2026, and a company official put the total including conditional contracts at KRW 143.6 billion as of June 2026.

The operating loss narrowed from KRW 21.8 billion in 2024 to KRW 6.85 billion in 2025 and to KRW 1.6 billion in the third quarter of 2026.

However, operating cash flow was an outflow of KRW 24.8 billion in 2025, and the interest coverage ratio was -4.3 times in the first quarter of 2026, so the company still relies on outside funding.

The Nonhyeon-dong development, backed by a KRW 68.2 billion bridge loan and a KRW 100.8 billion joint guarantee, targets a zoning upgrade in February 2027 and main project financing in June 2027, and whether those dates hold will largely determine the financial burden.

Expanding backlog and narrower losses are weighed against cash outflows and dilution risk, and the next checkpoints are the operating margin and cash flow in the 2026 annual report and the pace of revenue recovery in the first quarter of 2027.
