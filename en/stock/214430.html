i3system: Order Base Widens as Infrared Sensor Profits Rise

i3system, a domestic producer of military infrared imaging sensors, grew sales and profit together from 2022 to 2025, posted three straight quarters of growth through Q3 2026, and signed a KRW 61.8 billion overseas supply contract at the end of September 2026.

  2. 2

  3. 3

02

Business structure

i3system was founded in July 1998 as a specialist maker of infrared imaging sensors and listed on KOSDAQ in July 2015. Infrared sensors are core components that let weapons systems identify targets at night and in bad weather.

The company supplies sensors for K2 tank gunner and commander sights, sensors for K9 self-propelled howitzer and Chunmoo rocket launcher observation equipment, and infrared detectors for guided weapons such as Hyeongung and surveillance systems.

Media describe the company as the only domestic firm mass-producing military infrared imaging sensors. A 2023 supply contract for infrared detectors on Poland's K2 tank program, worth KRW 9.37 billion, was signed with Hanwha Systems as the counterparty.

Exports rose from KRW 24.3 billion in 2021 (30% of sales) to KRW 35 billion in 2022 (41%) and KRW 48.5 billion in the first nine months of 2023 (46%). Space programs accounted for KRW 7.4 billion, or about 10.2%, of the KRW 72.8 billion backlog at the end of September 2024.

The backlog stood at KRW 118.5 billion at the end of September 2025. A KRW 61.8 billion infrared detector assembly supply contract with an overseas defense company, signed on September 30, 2026, runs through September 1, 2029, with disclosure deferred at the counterparty's request and production handled in-house.

Drones and autonomous vehicles are the main civilian applications cited by brokers. In 2024, an NH Investment & Securities researcher said the company had built global competitiveness by developing 8-micron pitch uncooled sensors, while 12 to 17-micron products dominate that segment.

04

Earnings analysis

On a DART-filed basis, sales rose from KRW 83.8 billion in 2022 to KRW 121.5 billion in 2023, up 45.0% year on year, then eased 0.7% to KRW 120.7 billion in 2024 before rising 3.0% to KRW 124.3 billion in 2025.

Operating profit rose from KRW 5.69 billion in 2022 to KRW 12.2 billion in 2023 (up 114.6%), KRW 14.8 billion in 2024 (up 20.9%) and KRW 16.5 billion in 2025 (up 11.5%), increasing for three consecutive years from 2023 through 2025.

The operating margin moved from 6.8% in 2022 to 10.0% in 2023, 12.2% in 2024 and 13.2% in 2025. In 2024, sales dipped slightly while operating profit still grew, so margin expansion was the main driver of profit growth.

NH Investment & Securities expected the share of higher-margin T2SL sales to lift the company-wide margin.

Profit attributable to controlling shareholders rose from KRW 6.18 billion in 2022 to KRW 12.6 billion in 2023 (up 103.2%), KRW 15 billion in 2024 (up 19.2%) and KRW 18.8 billion in 2025 (up 25.5%), increasing for three consecutive years, and net income including non-controlling interests was filed at the same amounts in the same years.

Operating cash flow went from KRW -727 million in 2022 to KRW 21.8 billion in 2023, KRW 8.15 billion in 2024 and KRW 18.8 billion in 2025, falling in 2024 and recovering in 2025. The debt-to-equity ratio fell from 44.1% in 2022 to 35.7% in 2025.

At the end of 2025, total equity was KRW 139.7 billion, all of it attributable to controlling shareholders, and total liabilities were KRW 49.8 billion.

Quarterly sales bottomed at KRW 28.5 billion in Q4 2025, down 12.1% from the prior quarter, and then rose for three straight quarters to KRW 32.4 billion in Q1 2026, KRW 39.5 billion in Q2 2026 and KRW 42.4 billion in Q3 2026.

Operating profit also bottomed at KRW 3.81 billion in Q4 2025, down 9.7% from the prior quarter, and grew for three consecutive quarters to KRW 5.88 billion in Q3 2026.

Profit attributable to controlling shareholders jumped 54.9% to KRW 5.17 billion in Q4 2025, then fell 5.4% to KRW 4.89 billion in Q1 2026 and 21.7% to KRW 3.83 billion in Q2 2026 before recovering 14.1% to KRW 4.37 billion in Q3 2026.

In Q4 2025 and Q2 2026, operating profit and controlling-shareholder net income moved in opposite directions. Over the last four quarters, from Q4 2025 to Q3 2026, profit attributable to controlling shareholders totaled KRW 18.3 billion.

05

Industry analysis

Defense demand is being lifted by higher defense budgets at home and abroad, which has boosted orders for military equipment. Infrared sensors are used in equipment that finds targets at night and in poor weather, so more tanks, guided weapons and unmanned systems mean more sensor demand.

Hyundai Motor Securities has argued that geopolitical risk keeps supply of infrared imaging sensors tight. Brokers also expect demand to broaden beyond defense into space, drones and autonomous driving.

On the technology side, 12 to 17-micron uncooled products dominate that segment, and the company is responding with 8-micron products.

Because defense component suppliers recognize revenue based on prime contractors' ordering timing, quarterly sales can swing, as seen when Q4 2025 sales fell 12.1% from the prior quarter. The company has said overseas sales are concentrated in Europe, the Middle East and Southeast Asia.

Being one of few domestic producers of military infrared imaging sensors implies high technical entry barriers, but public data on share versus overseas rivals is limited, so orders and export results are the main yardsticks.

Hanwha Systems, a listed peer in the same sector, was the counterparty on the company's K2-related detector contract. In civilian markets, space optical cameras are the area that could raise the share of satellite-related orders.

06

Outlook

The most important near-term item is the KRW 61.8 billion supply contract signed on September 30, 2026. The contract runs from September 30, 2026 to September 1, 2029, and 15% of the advance payment is to be paid in installments over three years.

Disclosure was deferred at the counterparty's request, and the company states that supply will be produced in-house. How the contract is recognized across years will become visible through periodic filings.

On capacity, the company announced in August 2024 an investment of KRW 18 billion to build a new plant, with the aim of expanding infrared imaging sensor production capacity.

A later revised filing set the completion of the new plant for the end of October 2025, and meaningful sales were expected to start in 2026, with production volume expansion and yield improvement at the core. Whether 2026 results match that expectation is the next checkpoint.

On products, the share of T2SL detector sales and the mass production progress of 8-micron pitch uncooled sensors are the key items to watch. Civilian expansion centers on drones, autonomous driving and space optical systems. Beyond this, the report does not extend to specific 2026 sales targets.

From Q4 2026, quarterly revenue may vary with the progress of the KRW 61.8 billion contract, export mix and prime contractors' delivery schedules.

The next filings will show whether the 2026 operating margin holds near the 2025 level of 13.2% and whether controlling-shareholder net income moves in the same direction as operating profit again.

Bull factors

Large overseas supply contract secured

A KRW 61.8 billion supply contract signed on September 30, 2026 runs through September 1, 2029. Fifteen percent of the advance is paid in installments over three years. It was added after a KRW 118.5 billion backlog at the end of September 2025.

Three straight quarters of sales and profit growth

Sales and operating profit have both grown for three straight quarters since the Q4 2025 low. The operating margin rose from 6.8% in 2022 to 13.2% in 2025.

Rising export share and higher-value product mix

Export sales rose from KRW 24.3 billion in 2021 (30% of sales) to KRW 48.5 billion in the first nine months of 2023 (46%). NH Investment & Securities expected a higher share of T2SL sales to lift the company-wide margin. Higher-value products and a larger export share are cited as drivers of margin improvement.

Bear factors

Limited detail on the large contract

The counterparty's identity is undisclosed at its request, with disclosure deferred until September 1, 2029. Year-by-year revenue recognition and margin detail for the contract will only be visible through periodic filings during that period. Rising dependence on a single overseas customer could make order changes weigh heavily on results.

Operating profit and net income moving apart

In Q4 2025, operating profit fell 9.7% from the prior quarter while profit attributable to controlling shareholders rose 54.9%. In Q2 2026, operating profit rose 8.8% while controlling-shareholder net income fell 21.7%. When the two measures move in opposite directions, it is harder to judge the quality of earnings.

Quarterly revenue volatility

Q4 2025 sales fell 12.1% from the prior quarter to KRW 28.5 billion. Separate-basis sales for Q1 2026 were reported down 15.1% from a year earlier. Defense deliveries depend on ordering timing, so the run of quarterly growth may not continue smoothly.

Risk factors

Large contract execution and disclosure deferral

The disclosure deferral for the KRW 61.8 billion contract runs until September 1, 2029. Progress on performance and advance-payment settlement can only be tracked through periodic reports. Because the counterparty is undisclosed, any change to the contract could surface late.

CEO share sales and ownership concentration

CEO Jung Han disclosed on December 26, 2025 that he had sold 10,000 shares on the open market between December 22 and 24. He sold 12,000 shares between February 3 and 6, 2026, and the disclosed stake fell to 33.63%.

As the de facto controlling shareholder, changes in his holding can affect trading flows and need regular monitoring.

Capex and transition to mass production

In August 2024 the company disclosed an investment of KRW 18 billion to build a new factory. The core of the new plant is higher output and better yield, and if the plant's effect on results comes later than planned, the investment burden could weigh on margins. The pace of mass production for new products is the next item to check.
