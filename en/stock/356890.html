02

Business structure

CyberOne was founded in 2005 as an information security services company centered on security monitoring and security consulting. In Q2 2026 revenue, security monitoring, consulting and smart-system services accounted for 85%, security solution products for 11%, and resold security solutions and smart systems for 4%.

It runs an in-house integrated monitoring system, PROM SIEM, and delivers monitoring services to corporate and public clients. Its smart-system business combines physical security with IoT, covering access control, electronic attendance, water-dispensing management and attendance systems.

The Ministry of Science and ICT designated it a specialized information protection service firm, and the Ministry of the Interior and Safety designated it a personal information impact assessment body.

A Digital Daily report said it is one of only three domestic firms holding all three of these government-designated security licenses. Its consolidated subsidiary, added in 2023 and majority-owned by voting rights, develops and sells information security solutions.

Ahnlab and S-1 are listed companies with security businesses, while Samsung SDS and LG CNS compete in large-scale system integration. The company said security events in the first half of 2026 rose about threefold from a year earlier. Because most revenue comes from services, sales move with contract wins and staff utilization.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual revenue rose from KRW 22.8 billion in 2022 to KRW 24.7 billion in 2023, up 8.4% year on year, then jumped to KRW 47.9 billion in 2024, up 93.9%. It fell to KRW 33.7 billion in 2025, down 29.5%. 2024 was the largest year and 2022 the smallest in the 2022 to 2025 period.

Operating profit peaked at KRW 11.3 billion in 2024, up 788.0%, before dropping to KRW 414 million in 2025, down 96.3%. The operating margin fell from 23.7% in 2024 to 1.2% in 2025, and 2025 was the smallest year for that margin in the period.

Profit attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 9.84 billion in 2024, but turned into a net loss of KRW 1.21 billion in 2025. Net loss including non-controlling interests was KRW 1.27 billion in 2025. Operating cash flow moved from KRW 15.1 billion in 2024 to an outflow of KRW 1.68 billion in 2025.

At year-end 2025, total equity was KRW 47.6 billion, of which KRW 39.9 billion belonged to controlling shareholders, and total liabilities were KRW 4.9 billion, for a debt ratio of 10.3%.

Quarterly revenue rose from KRW 8.8 billion in Q3 2025 to KRW 9.39 billion in Q4 2025, up 6.7% from the prior quarter, but Q4 2025 operating profit was KRW 578 million and the company posted a net loss of KRW 2.07 billion attributable to controlling shareholders.

Q1 2026 revenue was KRW 7.32 billion, down 22.0% from the prior quarter, with an operating loss of KRW 201 million, while profit attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 182 million. Q2 2026 revenue rose 32.4% to KRW 9.69 billion, and operating profit of KRW 761 million marked a return to the black.

Q3 2026 revenue was KRW 9.94 billion, up 13.0% year on year, and operating profit was KRW 852 million, up 260.5% year on year. Revenue grew in two consecutive quarters from Q2 to Q3 2026. Profit attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 1.06 billion in Q2 and KRW 815 million in Q3 2026.

The last four quarters total a net loss attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 21.79 million, which still includes the Q4 2025 loss. The margin recovery coincided with quarterly revenue moving above KRW 9 billion, so revenue recovery appears to be the main driver.

Industry analysis

Security monitoring and consulting demand is rising in both the public and private sectors as information protection laws and regulations tighten. The corporate information site WiseReport summarized that enacting information protection laws and tighter regulation increased consulting demand in both sectors.

The company's monitoring center said security events in the first half of 2026 rose about threefold from a year earlier, but this is an internal count and should be viewed apart from external statistics.

A rise in events increases the burden of handling analysis and response with staff, which aligns with growing demand for AI and automated response tools. Listed companies with security businesses in Korea include Ahnlab and S-1, while Samsung SDS and LG CNS compete in public and enterprise system integration.

CyberOne highlights its government-designated security licenses and monitoring operations, but its revenue scale is far smaller than the large players. In May 2026 it announced a partnership for integrated monitoring and disaster recovery aimed at the cyber resilience market.

Strengths include rising demand and security certifications, while weaknesses include dependence on public-sector orders and growth constrained by a services-based model. Disclosures do not provide a quantitative market share figure for the company within the industry.

06

Outlook

The value-up plan disclosed on April 22, 2026 set out a dividend policy, stronger financial soundness, streamlined organization and cost competitiveness as execution tasks. On dividends, the company indicated it would secure dividend capacity by expanding profitability and raise payouts.

The filing listed 2025 dividends at KRW 418,334,700, up 17% from KRW 357,447,600 in 2024. Because 2025 net income was zero or below, the company said the 25% payout ratio was entered under the Restriction of Special Taxation Act.

On the business side, the local government consulting contract signed in February 2026 runs through December 31, 2026, making it a major part of second-half revenue.

On products, PROM SIEM AI received CC certification in September 2026, and the company plans to widen its use in public institutions and financial services. At the same September event, it unveiled PROM SIEM 4.0, which combines AI and SOAR, and said it would expand step by step toward Agentic AI-based monitoring.

On organization, it said it had reorganized its security consulting division and completed a red team setup. The next checkpoint is the 2026 annual report, which will show full-year operating margin, profit attributable to controlling shareholders and the dividend amount paid.
