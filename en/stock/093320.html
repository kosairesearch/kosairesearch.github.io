Profit recovery vs. debt and capex: the key variables for Kinx

02

Business structure

Kinx is an internet infrastructure company founded in 2000 that runs IDC, IX, CDN and cloud services on its data centers and network infrastructure. It operates six centers including the Gwacheon center, and strengthens its cloud and CDN businesses through its IXCLOUD and MIDIBUS platforms.

According to a Mirae Asset Securities research report, the 2025 business mix on a standalone basis was IDC 66.8%, CloudHub 13.5%, IX 11.3%, CDN 3.4% and cloud 1.4%. IDC billing combines a space fee with a network fee linked to rack power use and traffic, and contract terms of one, three and five years are mixed.

CloudHub is a direct connection platform for multiple cloud providers including AWS, Azure, GCP, Oracle and NHN, and its revenue has exceeded IX revenue since 2023. IX is billed on a fixed fee per port plus traffic, and Kinx is described as the only neutral L2 IX in Korea.

Its main IX customers are cable TV operators (MSO), content providers (CP) and cloud service providers (CSP). The main subsidiary, S-Fsoft, is reported to handle about 70% of Microsoft SPLA distribution. The largest shareholder group, led by Gabia (with five related parties), holds 39.87% according to FnGuide.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual revenue rose from KRW 110.9 billion in 2022 to KRW 159.2 billion in 2025. Revenue increased for three consecutive years from 2023 to 2025, and 2025 revenue grew 14.6% year on year.

Operating profit was KRW 25.9 billion in 2022, KRW 26.6 billion in 2023, KRW 23.7 billion in 2024 and KRW 23.9 billion in 2025, so 2023 was the largest year and 2024 the smallest. Operating profit fell 10.7% in 2024 and rose only 0.6% in 2025.

While revenue grew, operating margin declined from 23.3% in 2022 to 21.7% in 2023, 17.1% in 2024 and 15.0% in 2025. Mirae Asset Securities pointed to roughly KRW 8.0 billion of annual depreciation from the Gwacheon center as a factor behind the margin decline.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders fell from KRW 25.5 billion in 2022 to KRW 17.1 billion in 2025, and declined for three consecutive years from 2023 to 2025. The steepest drop came in 2024, at 26.8% year on year.

Net income including non-controlling interests was KRW 19.6 billion in 2025, a different figure from the KRW 17.1 billion attributable to controlling shareholders. Operating cash flow fell to KRW 26.3 billion in 2024 and recovered to KRW 45.5 billion in 2025.

On a quarterly basis, revenue rose from KRW 39.5 billion in the third quarter of 2025 to KRW 44.5 billion in the third quarter of 2026, and operating profit rose from KRW 6.2 billion to KRW 8.87 billion in the first quarter of 2026.

Operating profit was KRW 8.82 billion in the second quarter of 2026, and KRW 9.52 billion in the third quarter, up 53.5% year on year.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders peaked at KRW 7.14 billion in the first quarter of 2026, then fell to KRW 6.37 billion and KRW 5.27 billion in the second and third quarters. Operating profit grew faster than controlling-shareholder net income, which rose 28.4% year on year in the third quarter.

The gap likely reflects items below operating profit such as finance costs, income tax and non-controlling interests, and the quarterly report notes are where the detail can be checked.

The sum of controlling-shareholder net income over the four quarters from the fourth quarter of 2025 to the third quarter of 2026 was KRW 25.3 billion.

05

Industry analysis

Demand for domestic data center capacity is tied to growing high-volume content use such as OTT, real-time streaming and online games, together with the expansion of gigabit internet, as described in the company's earnings commentary.

In the first quarter of 2026 these drivers were cited for IDC revenue growth and margin improvement after the Gwacheon center began operating. IDC is a business in which capacity is built first and revenue follows as customers move in, so the pace of occupancy determines where the business sits in its cycle.

The Gwacheon center began service in December 2024, and Mirae Asset Securities said occupancy has exceeded the 80% target and no further tenants can be added. This suggests the existing center has moved past the ramp-up stage, and the ramp-up of new capacity will be the next determinant of growth.

In competition, Kinx is presented as the only neutral IX operator in Korea, and its CloudHub revenue, which connects directly with overseas cloud providers, has exceeded IX revenue. Its IX customers are MSOs, CPs and CSPs.

Large cloud and telecom operators expanding their own data centers would widen customers' options and could put pressure on pricing and contract terms.

On capital capacity, roughly KRW 180 billion went into the Gwacheon investment and much of the retained cash has been used, which Mirae Asset Securities cited as a reason the company took a DBO operator role rather than direct equity investment in the Ansan CoreHub JV.

06

Outlook

On August 21, 2026, the company disclosed through a fair-disclosure filing that it expects 2026 standalone revenue of KRW 122.7 billion and operating profit of KRW 32.1 billion, based on internal criteria that reflect the business environment, customer demand and cost efficiency.

Because reported results are on a consolidated basis and this outlook is on a standalone basis, the two figures cannot be compared directly. The annual report for 2026 is the point at which the outlook can be checked against standalone full-year results.

The Ansan CoreHub joint venture has announced an investment plan of about KRW 600 billion, and Kinx is participating as the DBO operator of a 40MW data center. Mirae Asset Securities said the possibility of starting construction on additional data centers remains open.

Taking into account administrative procedures and construction periods, Mirae Asset Securities estimates that full operation could begin between 2027 and 2030. In the first half of 2026, operating cash flow was KRW 26.1 billion, up 23.8% year on year, according to an IB Tomato report dated November 2, 2026.

Acquisitions of tangible and intangible assets were about KRW 2.0 billion in the same period, sharply lower than about KRW 17.7 billion in the first half of 2025. Bank borrowings were KRW 60.9 billion, and borrowings due within one year rose to KRW 14.0 billion.

The next points to watch are the refinancing and repayment plans and the pace at which new center investment is executed.

08

Bull factors

Speed of the quarterly profit recovery

Third-quarter 2026 operating profit was KRW 9.52 billion, up 53.5% year on year, while revenue rose 12.8% to KRW 44.5 billion. Mirae Asset Securities said tenant ramp-up began in the second quarter of 2025 and revenue started to come in meaningfully. Operating profit growing faster than revenue is consistent with a phase in which fixed-cost pressure is easing.

Improvement in cash generation

Operating cash flow was KRW 45.5 billion in 2025, up sharply from KRW 26.3 billion in 2024. In the first half of 2026 it reached KRW 26.1 billion, up 23.8% year on year. According to the same IB Tomato report, capex fell sharply, so operating cash exceeded capital spending and dividends.

Internal outlook and occupancy

On August 21, 2026, the company disclosed through a fair-disclosure filing an internal outlook of KRW 122.7 billion in standalone revenue and KRW 32.1 billion in operating profit for 2026.

Mirae Asset Securities said occupancy at the Gwacheon center has exceeded the original 80% target and no further tenants can be added. If new capacity begins operating after 2027, another growth stage would open.

Bear factors

Structural decline in operating margin

Operating margin fell from 23.3% in 2022 to 21.7% in 2023, 17.1% in 2024 and 15.0% in 2025. Over the same period revenue rose from KRW 110.9 billion to KRW 159.2 billion, while operating profit fell from KRW 25.9 billion to KRW 23.9 billion.

Mirae Asset Securities cited the roughly KRW 8.0 billion annual depreciation from the Gwacheon center as a factor.

Consecutive declines in controlling-shareholder net income

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders declined for three consecutive years from 2023 to 2025, falling 26.8% in 2024. It then declined in the second and third quarters of 2026, to KRW 5.27 billion in the third quarter.

An IB Tomato report also noted that operating cash flow as a share of operating profit fell in the first half of 2026 compared with the first half of 2025.

Borrowing and capex burden

Bank borrowings were KRW 60.9 billion, and borrowings due within one year rose to KRW 14.0 billion as of the end of the first half of 2026. The Ansan CoreHub joint venture has announced an investment plan of about KRW 600 billion.

Mirae Asset Securities analyzed that much of the retained cash was used for the Gwacheon investment, which is one reason the company chose the DBO model.

10

Risk factors

Capital spending and borrowing

If new data center investment continues, depreciation and interest costs will rise. Borrowings due within one year have risen to KRW 14.0 billion, so less favorable refinancing terms could pressure cash flow. If utilization rises more slowly than expected, fixed costs could again weigh on operating margin.

Customer concentration and tenant timing

Large customers move in gradually over several quarters, so a delay by one customer could slow the revenue ramp. Some space is allocated to Gabia and other specific customers, so customer concentration is also worth tracking.

With the existing center past its occupancy target, the pace of tenant move-ins at new capacity will be a condition for the next phase of growth.

Shareholder structure and free float

The largest shareholder group, led by Gabia with five related parties, holds 39.87%, which limits the share of freely tradable stock. The free float ratio is reported at 59.81%.

Because there have been shareholder changes involving an investment firm in the past, further changes in the shareholder structure remain a point to track.

12

Overall view

Kinx runs IDC, cloud and IX businesses as an internet infrastructure company, and both its revenue and operating profit rose year on year in the third quarter of 2026. Tenant ramp-up at the Gwacheon center is presented as the background to the profit recovery.

However, operating margin fell from 23.3% in 2022 to 15.0% in 2025, and net income attributable to controlling shareholders declined for three consecutive years from 2023 to 2025. It also fell in the second and third quarters of 2026, which is another point to keep in view.

On the balance sheet, the debt-to-equity ratio was 36.2% at the end of 2025 and operating cash flow rose to KRW 45.5 billion, but borrowings due within one year increased to KRW 14.0 billion as of the end of the first half of 2026.

The funding allocation for the new Ansan data center and the full-year results in the 2026 annual report are the next points to check. The multiple sits near the upper part of its past year-end range, and interpretations of whether the earnings recovery will support it diverge between bullish and bearish readings. The durability of the earnings recovery and the burden of capital spending both remain open.
