Report overview

After Profit Recovery, a Q3 2026 Net Loss

Hwaseung's owner-attributable net income reached KRW 78 billion in 2025 after three straight years of growth, but the third quarter of 2026 swung to a net loss of KRW 12.1 billion attributable to owners.

02

Business structure

Hwaseung Corporation, founded in 1978, centers on automotive parts and also runs materials, holding, and trading businesses. In the 2022 business report, automotive parts accounted for 67.3% of sales, materials for 17.2%, holdings for 3.6%, and trading and other businesses for 11.9%.

An equity research report noted that over the most recent three years in that filing, automotive parts stayed at roughly 67% to 71% of sales and materials at roughly 16% to 18%. Korea Ratings said in June 2024 that automotive parts make up about 70% of revenue.

The main customers are Hyundai Motor and Kia, so their production volumes drive parts sales. Subsidiary Hwaseung R&A supplies core automotive components and has stated a plan to raise its share of parts for electric and hydrogen vehicles.

The materials business produces TPV (thermoplastic vulcanizate), and the company has announced line expansions to meet demand. The trading business is run by affiliates including Hwaseung Networks. Overseas, the group operates automotive parts entities in the United States, Mexico, Brazil, Turkey, and India.

Consolidated R&D spending in 2025 was about KRW 37.7 billion, or 2.30% of sales. Hyundai Mobis, Hanon Systems, and Seoyon E-Hwa share the automotive sector classification, but differences in product mix make direct profitability comparisons limited.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual sales rose from KRW 1.59 trillion in 2022 to KRW 1.63 trillion in 2023 (up 3.1% year on year) and KRW 1.7 trillion in 2024 (up 3.9%), then fell to KRW 1.64 trillion in 2025 (down 3.5%). Among 2022 to 2025, 2024 was the largest year for sales and 2022 the smallest.

Operating profit climbed from KRW 43.2 billion in 2022 to KRW 78.2 billion in 2023 (up 81.2%), reached KRW 88.7 billion in 2024 (up 13.4%), and then eased to KRW 83.1 billion in 2025 (down 6.4%). The operating margin moved from 2.7% in 2022 to 4.8% in 2023, 5.2% in 2024, and 5.1% in 2025, staying near 5% after 2023.

Owner-attributable net income rose from KRW 2.79 billion in 2022 to KRW 3.47 billion in 2023, KRW 26.7 billion in 2024, and KRW 78 billion in 2025, increasing for three consecutive years from 2023 through 2025.

In 2025 operating profit declined while owner-attributable net income rose sharply, so the two moved in opposite directions, and non-operating and tax items need to be checked in the business report.

Net income including non-controlling interests was KRW 103.1 billion in 2025, larger than the owner-attributable figure, with the gap belonging to non-controlling interests.

The debt ratio fell from 369.8% in 2022 to 194.4% in 2025, when total equity was KRW 418.7 billion, of which KRW 283.3 billion belonged to owners of the parent.

In the fourth quarter of 2025, sales fell 32.8% from the prior quarter to KRW 299.6 billion and operating profit dropped to KRW 3.56 billion, yet owner-attributable net income jumped to KRW 41.4 billion, suggesting a large non-operating influence.

Sales then rose for three consecutive quarters in 2026, reaching KRW 421.5 billion, KRW 449.1 billion, and KRW 452.8 billion, while operating profit fell for two consecutive quarters to KRW 25.6 billion, KRW 21.9 billion, and KRW 8.56 billion.

FnGuide reported that first-quarter 2026 operating profit was down 13.9% from a year earlier. The third quarter of 2026 produced an owner-attributable net loss of KRW 12.1 billion, a swing into the red from a year earlier, and the last four quarters total KRW 52.1 billion in owner-attributable net income.

05

Industry analysis

Earnings for automotive parts suppliers depend heavily on vehicle production and the exchange rate. In June 2024, Korea Ratings said that higher production at Hyundai Motor and Kia and larger exports from a weaker won were the main drivers of 2023 sales growth.

At the same time, rating agencies expected that slowing demand in the vehicle market could cause a slight sales decline in 2024, but the company's 2024 sales actually rose 3.9% to KRW 1.7 trillion. The gap between that outlook and the result shows that OEM demand forecasts do not translate directly into supplier sales.

A summary of the first quarter of 2026 results cited expanding demand for electrified parts as an expected driver. The company has stated plans to raise its share of parts for electric and hydrogen vehicles, a shift from a combustion-centered portfolio.

In cycle terms, sales rose for three consecutive quarters in 2026 while operating profit declined, so whether volume recovery converts into margin recovery is the central question. The operating margin has stayed around 5% since 2023, which suggests relatively stable margins through volume swings.

Hyundai Mobis, Hanon Systems, and Seoyon E-Hwa operate in the same automotive sector, but product and customer mixes differ, which limits direct comparison.

06

Outlook

Based on disclosed results, 2026 shows sales rising while profit declines. Operating profit was KRW 25.6 billion in the first quarter of 2026 and KRW 8.56 billion in the third quarter, down 58.8% from a year earlier.

Compared with the KRW 83.1 billion operating profit for full-year 2025, fourth-quarter results will determine whether the annual level holds. In June 2024, Korea Ratings said it could consider a rating upgrade if North American OEM sales ramp up and the borrowing dependence ratio falls below 40%.

The borrowing dependence ratio eased from 48% in 2023 to 47.1% in the first quarter of 2024. The issuer rating was kept at BBB-, and the outlook was raised to stable. The first-quarter 2026 summary cited expanding electrified parts demand as an expected driver, so the mix of electrified parts will shape the next phase.

Production bases spanning the United States, Mexico, Brazil, and India could benefit if North American volumes grow. The next checkpoints are the fourth-quarter results in the 2026 annual business report, the non-operating items, and the trend in the borrowing dependence ratio.

Bull factors

Earnings recovery trend

Owner-attributable net income rose from KRW 3.47 billion in 2023 to KRW 26.7 billion in 2024 and KRW 78 billion in 2025, increasing for three consecutive years. The 2025 operating margin of 5.1% stayed near 5% after 2023, so core profitability did not drop sharply. Operating cash flow in 2025 was KRW 159.8 billion, up from KRW 17.3 billion in 2024.

Balance sheet improvement

The debt ratio fell from 369.8% in 2022 to 194.4% in 2025. In June 2024, Korea Ratings raised the outlook to stable, recognizing the improving financial structure. The borrowing dependence ratio eased from 48% in 2023 to 47.1% in the first quarter of 2024.

Electrification and North American expansion

Whether expanding electrified parts demand translates into actual sales growth is the next point to check. Korea Ratings said that a full ramp in North American OEM sales would strengthen the business. Production growth at Hyundai Motor and Kia and a weaker won were cited as drivers of past sales gains.

Bear factors

Q3 2026 swing into a loss

Operating profit in the third quarter of 2026 was KRW 8.56 billion, down 58.8% from a year earlier. The last four quarters total KRW 52.1 billion in owner-attributable net income, with the fourth quarter of 2025 accounting for most of that.

Sales growth without profit growth

Sales rose from KRW 421.5 billion in the first quarter of 2026 to KRW 452.8 billion in the third quarter, yet operating profit fell from KRW 25.6 billion to KRW 8.56 billion. Higher volume did not carry through to profit, and changes in cost ratios or cost structure need to be traced in the business report. Whether the operating margin recovers is the next open question.

High debt and volatile cash flow

Total liabilities at the end of 2025 were KRW 813.9 billion, close to twice total equity of KRW 418.7 billion. Operating cash flow swung from KRW 121.2 billion in 2023 to KRW 17.3 billion in 2024 and KRW 159.8 billion in 2025. If debt burden coincides with earnings swings, financial flexibility could be constrained.

Risk factors

Customer concentration

Automotive parts sales depend heavily on production volumes at major customers Hyundai Motor and Kia. A production cut or order adjustment could move both sales and utilization. Because automotive parts represent about 70% of revenue, the impact would be large.

Currency movements

Because a weaker won supported export values, a reversal in the exchange rate could work in the opposite direction. Overseas subsidiary results change in won terms when translated. Currency moves can also affect non-operating items, which should be checked in the business report.

Debt burden and ownership structure

Total liabilities were KRW 813.9 billion at the end of 2025, and the borrowing dependence ratio was 47.1% in the first quarter of 2024.

Of total equity of KRW 418.7 billion, KRW 283.3 billion belongs to owners of the parent, with the rest attributable to non-controlling interests, which makes profit allocation more complex.

The gap between owner-attributable and total net income is wide, so the share belonging to shareholders needs to be read separately.

Overall view

Hwaseung's operating profit rose from KRW 43.2 billion in 2022 to KRW 83.1 billion in 2025, and owner-attributable net income has grown for three consecutive years from 2023 to KRW 78 billion in 2025. The debt ratio fell from 369.8% in 2022 to 194.4% in 2025, which points to an improving financial structure.

On the other hand, third-quarter 2026 sales rose to KRW 452.8 billion while operating profit fell to KRW 8.56 billion and the owner-attributable result turned to a net loss of KRW 12.1 billion.

The last four quarters total KRW 52.1 billion in owner-attributable net income, and that total leans heavily on the fourth quarter of 2025, so earnings durability is an open question.

Automotive parts make up about 70% of revenue with Hyundai Motor and Kia as main customers, so vehicle production and the exchange rate shape results. On valuation, year-end P/E and P/B ratios have declined since 2023, and the relationship between earnings recovery and multiples is central to interpretation.

Strengths include the earnings recovery and balance sheet improvement, while weaknesses include the third-quarter 2026 swing into loss and a heavy debt load. The next checkpoints are fourth-quarter results and the borrowing dependence ratio in the 2026 annual business report, and first-quarter 2027 operating profit.
