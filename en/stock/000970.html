02

Business structure

Korea Castiron Pipe IND (000970) was founded in 1953 and listed on the KOSPI in 1969. It is based in Busan. Its core product is ductile cast iron pipe for water supply and sewage transmission lines, made from pig iron and scrap at its Busan and Pohang plants.

The product is widely regarded as the most common choice for water transmission pipe because of its ductility and strength. Demand is closely linked to the government's social infrastructure budget and construction activity.

Because volumes are set by government procurement contracts, sales move considerably from quarter to quarter depending on contract timing and quantity. Customers are centered on water-related public projects that go through the Public Procurement Service.

Competition comes from a number of makers of pipe in other materials, in addition to cast iron pipe. The steel pipe business is run by consolidated subsidiaries, and the cosmetics business by a cosmetics subsidiary.

As of June 2026, the consolidated group comprised three steel pipe manufacturing and sales companies, one steel trading company, and one cosmetics company, with 131 employees.

In April 2025 the company was designated a Ppuri (root industry) technology specialist firm, and in July 2025 it received Busan's regional hometown company certification. The largest shareholder and related parties hold a combined 50.50 percent stake, with the last change recorded on November 24, 2025.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual sales went from KRW 484.3 billion in 2022 to KRW 456.3 billion in 2023 (down 5.8% year on year), KRW 419.4 billion in 2024 (down 8.1%), and KRW 406.6 billion in 2025 (down 3.1%). Sales declined for three consecutive years from 2023 through 2025.

Across 2022 to 2025, 2022 was the largest year and 2025 the smallest. Operating profit rose from KRW 14 billion in 2022 to KRW 20.4 billion in 2023 (up 45.7%), the largest year in the period, and then fell two years in a row to KRW 15.4 billion in 2024 (down 24.2%) and KRW 13.3 billion in 2025 (down 13.9%).

The operating margin was 2.9% in 2022, 4.5% in 2023, 3.7% in 2024 and 3.3% in 2025, so it eased after its 2023 peak while sales were shrinking.

Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 12 billion in 2022, KRW 17.4 billion in 2023 (up 44.9%), KRW 14.3 billion in 2024 (down 17.6%) and KRW 17 billion in 2025 (up 18.2%).

In 2025 operating profit fell while controlling-shareholder profit rose, which suggests items outside operating profit affected the bottom line; the breakdown should be read from the notes to the annual report.

Net profit including non-controlling interests was KRW 17.1 billion in 2025, close to the controlling-shareholder figure. Operating cash flow fell from KRW 33.4 billion in 2022 to KRW 25.4 billion in 2023 and KRW 24.3 billion in 2024, then rose to KRW 27.3 billion in 2025.

At the end of 2025, total equity was KRW 365.3 billion, of which KRW 361.8 billion belonged to controlling shareholders, and total liabilities were KRW 70.3 billion; the debt ratio fell from 21.1% in 2022 to 19.3% in 2025.

Quarterly sales ranged from KRW 101.6 billion in the third quarter of 2025 to KRW 111.8 billion in the second quarter of 2026, and were KRW 98.8 billion in the third quarter of 2026, down 2.8% year on year.

Quarterly operating profit peaked at KRW 7.23 billion in the second quarter of 2026, the highest of the last five quarters, before falling to KRW 3.79 billion in the third quarter.

Third-quarter operating profit rose 19.7% year on year, but controlling-shareholder net profit of KRW 2.78 billion fell 32.7%, so the two measures moved in opposite directions.

Controlling-shareholder net profit for the four quarters from the fourth quarter of 2025 through the third quarter of 2026 totaled KRW 15.7 billion.

05

Industry analysis

Demand for water pipe tracks the government's social infrastructure budget and its pipe replacement policy. A 2020 press report said the government's introduction of smart waterworks was expected to increase demand for replacing aging pipes.

Another 2020 report cited a 77% share of the procurement-contract market at the end of 2019, but that figure dates from a different period than today. Cast iron pipe volumes are allocated through government procurement contracts, and the company competes with a number of makers of pipe in other materials.

Because demand is tied to the pace of budget execution, policy timing matters more than the business cycle. In a stock information service's ranking of 57 companies in the steel and metals sector by annual sales, Korea Castiron Pipe IND ranked 24th, placing it in the lower part of the sector by scale.

The same service showed its operating margin above the sector average, so the margin is relatively high for its size. The ranking depends on the data source and timing and should be read as directional only.

08

Bull factors

Profit rebound in the second quarter of 2026

Second-quarter 2026 operating profit of KRW 7.23 billion was the highest of the last five quarters, and sales of KRW 111.8 billion in the same quarter were also the highest of the five.

With the operating margin at 3.3% in 2025, below 4.5% in 2023, the quarter shows what profit capacity can look like on a quarterly basis. Third-quarter operating profit fell back to KRW 3.79 billion, so one quarter is too short a window to judge the trend.

Comparatively strong balance sheet

The debt ratio moved from 21.1% in 2022 to 20.7% in 2023 and 20.7% in 2024, and then declined further. Operating cash flow rose to KRW 27.3 billion in 2025 from KRW 24.3 billion in 2024, so cash generation held up.

Dividend decision and 2025 earnings recovery

A report dated February 24, 2026 said the company decided on a cash dividend. Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders rose 18.2% to KRW 17 billion in 2025, reversing the 2024 decline. A stock information service's data puts the dividend yield above the steel sector average. Whether the dividend continues depends on earnings, so the next dividend decision is the point to watch.

Bear factors

Sales down for three consecutive years

The annual decline carried into quarterly results. Third-quarter 2026 sales of KRW 98.8 billion were down 2.8% year on year, so the decline continues on a quarterly basis.

Because volumes follow government procurement contracts, the timing of any sales recovery is not in the company's direct control, which is the bearish point.

Thin operating margin

Operating profit peaked at KRW 20.4 billion in 2023 and then fell two years in a row to KRW 15.4 billion in 2024 and KRW 13.3 billion in 2025. The operating margin was 3.3% in 2025, below 4.5% in 2023.

Quarterly operating profit has ranged from KRW 2.13 billion to KRW 7.23 billion, so a stable margin level is hard to confirm.

Volatile net profit and diverging quarters

Third-quarter 2026 net profit attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 2.78 billion, down 32.7% year on year, even as operating profit rose. Whether items outside operating profit explain the gap should be checked in the quarterly income statement detail.

Risk factors

Possible weakening of operating cash flow

Operating cash flow fell from KRW 33.4 billion in 2022 to KRW 24.3 billion in 2024 before recovering to KRW 27.3 billion in 2025. If sales keep declining or operating profit stays at a low level, that recovery may not hold, and the room to meet funding needs from operating cash may narrow. The 2026 annual report will show whether operating cash flow exceeds KRW 27.3 billion.

Competition across pipe materials and cost pressure

The company competes with a number of makers of pipe in other materials, which could weigh on its share of volumes. Price swings in pig iron and scrap, the main raw materials, can have a large effect on profit when the margin sits in the 3% range. The fall in the operating margin to 3.3% in 2025 suggests limited room to absorb cost swings.

Concentrated ownership and trading liquidity

Related parties of the largest shareholder hold a combined 50.50%, which limits the number of freely tradable shares. With fewer shares in circulation, trading volume tends to be small and price moves can be larger on modest trades. Changes in ownership should be tracked through shareholding disclosures.

Overall view

Korea Castiron Pipe IND makes ductile cast iron pipe for water supply and sewage, and its results are tied to government procurement contracts and the social infrastructure budget.

Sales fell from KRW 484.3 billion in 2022 to KRW 406.6 billion in 2025, declining for three consecutive years, and the operating margin eased from 4.5% in 2023 to 3.3% in 2025.

On the other side, controlling-shareholder net profit rose 18.2% to KRW 17 billion in 2025, and the debt ratio of 19.3% at the end of 2025 points to a modest financial burden.

Quarterly operating profit moved from KRW 7.23 billion in the second quarter of 2026 to KRW 3.79 billion in the third quarter, and third-quarter controlling-shareholder net profit of KRW 2.78 billion fell 32.7% year on year.

Strengths include the balance sheet and the 2025 earnings recovery, while weaknesses include the sales decline, the thin margin and quarterly volatility. Multiples sit below the five-year average, but they should be read alongside the earnings trend.

The next confirmation points are the 2026 annual report, due by March 31, 2027, and the first-quarter 2027 quarterly report.
