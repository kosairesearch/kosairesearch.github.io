Report overview

Orders Recover, Sales Still Shrinking

GS Engineering & Construction's operating profit has risen for three straight quarters since the first quarter of 2026, yet revenue has declined since 2024, so how quickly urban redevelopment orders convert into sales is the key variable.

  1. 1

    Operating profit recovered from an operating loss of KRW 387.9 billion in 2023 to KRW 437.8 billion in 2025, while revenue has fallen since its peak of KRW 13.4 trillion in 2023.

  2. 2

    Third-quarter 2026 operating profit of KRW 95.5 billion fell 35.7% year on year but rose 4.6% from the prior quarter.

  3. 3

    Plant segment revenue of KRW 457 billion, up 34.6% year on year, has partly offset the decline in the building and housing segment.

  4. 4

    Urban redevelopment construction rights worth about KRW 5.5 trillion were secured through the third quarter of 2026, with the order backlog expanding centered on Seoul's core districts.

  5. 5

    Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders fell to KRW 55 million in the first quarter of 2026 before recovering to KRW 95.4 billion in the third quarter, and totaled KRW 135.9 billion over the last four quarters.

02

Business structure

GS Engineering & Construction is a general contractor whose core is building and housing projects marketed under the Xi apartment brand.

Building and housing was the largest revenue segment in 2025 at KRW 7.8 trillion on a consolidated basis, while new-business revenue, including the water treatment subsidiary GS Inima and modular construction, reached KRW 1.8 trillion, up 27.8% year on year.

Segment revenue for the third quarter of 2026, as announced by the company on October 30, 2026, was KRW 1.5 trillion for building and housing (down 18.7% year on year), KRW 344 billion for infrastructure, and KRW 457 billion for plants (up 34.6%).

Building and housing remains the largest revenue pillar, but a prolonged property downturn reduced housing supply through last year, which weighed on sales recognition from sales and construction.

The plant business grew as large domestic and overseas projects entered construction, and the Polish modular subsidiary Danwood won work worth KRW 196.2 billion in the third quarter of 2026.

Orders are led by urban redevelopment: the 2025 redevelopment order intake was KRW 6.3 trillion, more than double the KRW 3.1 trillion of the prior year.

Through the third quarter of 2026, the company secured construction rights worth about KRW 5.5 trillion, including the Seongsu 1 redevelopment (KRW 2.2 trillion), Songpa Hanyang 2nd reconstruction (KRW 685.6 billion) and Seocho Jinheung reconstruction (KRW 679.3 billion).

Its clients are redevelopment and reconstruction associations, and reporting describes a strategy of selecting sites that weigh both profitability and stability.

In the competitive field, the company is grouped with Hyundai Engineering & Construction and Samsung C&T as one of the three leaders in urban redevelopment, and Hyundai held the top cumulative redevelopment order position in a June 2026 report.

The subsidiary Xi-related small-scale unit focuses on redevelopment projects under 500 households and office construction.

04

Earnings analysis

On the annual basis disclosed in the regular reports, revenue rose from KRW 12.3 trillion in 2022 to KRW 13.4 trillion in 2023 (up 9.2%), then fell to KRW 12.9 trillion in 2024 (down 4.3%) and KRW 12.5 trillion in 2025 (down 3.2%), declining for two consecutive years from 2024 through 2025.

Operating profit moved from KRW 554.8 billion in 2022 to an operating loss of KRW 387.9 billion in 2023, a swing into loss, then back to profit at KRW 286 billion in 2024, and rose 53.1% to KRW 437.8 billion in 2025. Operating margin moved through 4.5%, -2.9%, 2.2% and 3.5% over 2022 to 2025.

Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders swung from a net loss of KRW 481.9 billion in 2023 to KRW 245.6 billion in 2024, but fell 61.9% to KRW 93.5 billion in 2025.

In 2025 operating profit grew while controlling-shareholder profit fell, which indicates a wider gap between the operating and after-tax levels. Total net profit, including non-controlling interests, was KRW 93.4 billion in 2025, practically the same as controlling-shareholder profit.

A February 2026 report attributed the 2025 operating improvement mainly to higher margins in the Xi housing business, while new-business one-off costs arose from modular work in the second quarter and project delays at GS Inima in the fourth quarter.

Quarterly revenue fell from KRW 3.21 trillion in the third quarter of 2025 to KRW 2.98 trillion in the fourth quarter and KRW 2.4 trillion in the first quarter of 2026, then recovered to KRW 2.78 trillion in the second quarter and KRW 2.74 trillion in the third quarter.

Operating profit bottomed at KRW 56.9 billion in the fourth quarter of 2025 (down 61.7% from the prior quarter) and then rose for three consecutive quarters to KRW 73.5 billion, KRW 91.4 billion and KRW 95.5 billion through the third quarter of 2026.

Over the same period, net profit attributable to controlling shareholders went from KRW 89.9 billion in the third quarter of 2025 to KRW 37.9 billion, KRW 55 million, KRW 2.57 billion and KRW 95.4 billion.

Operating profit rose while first-quarter net profit shrank to almost nothing, showing that items below the operating line weighed heavily on quarterly results.

The debt-to-equity ratio rose to 262.5% in 2023 and eased to 234.2% in 2025, with total equity of KRW 5.52 trillion and total liabilities of KRW 12.9 trillion at the end of 2025.

05

Industry analysis

The housing market is in a downturn in which new supply has shrunk, and the company itself attributed its building and housing revenue decline to that cycle. According to a May 2026 report, the company's housing order backlog stood at KRW 37.3 trillion in the first quarter of 2026, up about 16% from a year earlier.

In the same report, the Seoul and metropolitan area accounted for KRW 20.3 trillion, or 55% of the backlog, while regional areas outside major cities held KRW 6.7 trillion, or 18%. The redevelopment order intake in the same period was KRW 4.7 trillion, described as the largest among major builders.

Competition among large builders for core Seoul redevelopment projects is intensifying, and managing bid prices and profitability is cited as a challenge.

As of a June 2026 report, the company's cumulative urban redevelopment orders were KRW 7.5 trillion including a contested Seongnam project, which was KRW 225.3 billion short of the leader Hyundai Engineering & Construction at KRW 7.7 trillion.

In the plant and data center area, a July 2026 report noted that the GS Group's announced AI data center investment could benefit GS Engineering & Construction as the group's construction arm.

The broader construction sector faces a slowdown in property markets and rising cost pressure, and reporting described the company as favoring selective, profitability-focused orders over expanding volume.

Looking at the cycle, the decline in housing supply is still feeding into revenue, while redevelopment orders are moving toward construction starts and sales.

06

Outlook

A February 2026 report stated that the company set a 2026 revenue target of KRW 11.5 trillion, 7.6% below its 2025 result, and that a smaller revenue base was considered unavoidable as large subsidiaries were divested.

The order target is KRW 17.8 trillion, split into KRW 12 trillion for building and housing, KRW 2.1 trillion for plants, KRW 1.2 trillion for infrastructure, KRW 900 billion for new growth businesses and KRW 1.6 trillion for other areas.

New orders in the third quarter of 2026 totaled KRW 3.96 trillion, including the Yongsan Munbae-dong mixed-use project (KRW 571.7 billion) and a private participation project in Namyangju Wangsuk 2 (KRW 217.3 billion) in the building and housing unit.

An October 2026 report said the company was selected as the preferred bidder for the Mokdong 12th complex reconstruction (KRW 1.79 trillion) and for a reconstruction in Yeouido Eunha and Samick apartments worth over KRW 1 trillion.

The timing of construction contract signings after preferred-bidder selection was not reported, so it remains a checkpoint.

According to a May 2026 report, groundbreakings were under way in January 2026 at the Gwangmyeong 12R Cheolsan Station Xi project and in February at the Geoyeo Saemaeul redevelopment, with Bukosan Xi Forest and the Sewoon 5-1 and 3 office project scheduled to start.

The same report listed the Mokdong officetel development, the Bangbae 13 reconstruction and the Osan Yangsan 4 district as second-half projects.

The company said it supplied about 13,000 households through the third quarter of 2026 and expects housing sales to turn upward once construction at new sale sites gets fully under way.

Plant revenue depends on the progress of large domestic and overseas projects now in construction, so its trend after the fourth quarter of 2026 is a point to watch.

The 2026 annual report, the next regular filing, will show how close full-year orders came to the KRW 17.8 trillion target and whether revenue reached the KRW 11.5 trillion target.

08

Bull factors

Operating profit recovery continues

Operating profit rose 53.1% to KRW 437.8 billion in 2025, and quarterly operating profit increased for three consecutive quarters to KRW 95.5 billion in the third quarter of 2026. Operating margin improved from 2.2% in 2024 to 3.5% in 2025.

Although third-quarter operating profit was down 35.7% year on year, the quarterly trend is cited as evidence that the recovery direction is holding.

Expanding urban redevelopment order base

Redevelopment orders reached KRW 6.3 trillion in 2025, more than double the prior year's KRW 3.1 trillion.

Through the third quarter of 2026, the company secured construction rights worth about KRW 5.5 trillion, and it was also selected as preferred bidder for the Mokdong 12th complex reconstruction and a Yeouido project.

Concentration of 55% of the housing backlog in the Seoul metropolitan area is read as a base for future sales recognition.

Growth from plant and overseas businesses

Plant segment revenue reached KRW 457 billion in the third quarter of 2026, up 34.6% year on year. The Polish modular subsidiary Danwood won work worth KRW 196.2 billion in the same quarter. A July 2026 report linked the GS Group's announced AI data center plan to construction benefits within the group. Revenue from outside building and housing is cited as a supporting element of the top line.

09

Bear factors

Revenue decline persists

Revenue fell for two consecutive years, to KRW 12.9 trillion in 2024 and KRW 12.5 trillion in 2025. Third-quarter 2026 revenue of KRW 2.74 trillion was down 14.5% year on year, and the building and housing segment fell 18.7%. A longer lag between reduced housing supply and sales recognition would delay any recovery in the top line.

Earnings volatility and weak net profit

Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders fell 61.9% to KRW 93.5 billion in 2025 and dropped to KRW 55 million in the first quarter of 2026. Operating profit and net profit did not move in the same direction in that stretch.

One-off costs in new businesses appeared in 2025 as well, which keeps earnings quality a bearish point of debate.

Regional concentration and rising competition

Regional housing backlog stood at KRW 6.7 trillion in the first quarter of 2026, only 18% of the total. Competition among large builders for core Seoul redevelopment is intensifying, and bid pricing and profitability management are flagged as challenges.

As of a June 2026 report, the gap with the leader Hyundai Engineering & Construction was KRW 225.3 billion, which is read as a competitive pressure point.

10

Risk factors

Housing market and sales risk

A prolonged property downturn could delay sales schedules and construction starts. The company supplied about 13,000 households through the third quarter of 2026, but a revenue recovery depends on construction at new sites ramping up. A weaker sales market would slow how quickly the order backlog converts into revenue.

Project cost and one-off charge risk

A February 2026 report noted that one-off costs came from modular work in the second quarter of 2025 and from project delays at GS Inima in the fourth quarter. Cost pressure across the construction sector continues, and schedule delays at large sites can swing quarterly profit sharply. In quarters where the gap between operating and net profit widens, the effect of such items becomes larger.

Order concentration and margin management risk

About 55% of the housing backlog is concentrated in the Seoul metropolitan area, which leaves the company exposed to shifts in that single market. With regional backlog at 18%, a prolonged slowdown in regional projects could weaken the supply base in those areas. As Seoul bidding grows more competitive, managing margins on new orders remains an important test.

12

Overall view

GS Engineering & Construction's disclosed results show revenue declining for two consecutive years while operating profit recovered to KRW 437.8 billion in 2025.

Quarterly operating profit rose for three straight quarters to KRW 95.5 billion in the third quarter of 2026, but third-quarter revenue was down 14.5% year on year.

Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders fell 61.9% to KRW 93.5 billion in 2025 and dropped to KRW 55 million in the first quarter of 2026, so how far the operating recovery carries through to after-tax profit remains the central question.

On the strength side are construction rights worth about KRW 5.5 trillion in urban redevelopment and growth in plant revenue. On the weakness side are the decline in housing revenue, regional concentration and intensifying competition for Seoul redevelopment.

The debt-to-equity ratio easing to 234.2% in 2025 reads as a lighter financial burden. The next checkpoints are the preliminary fourth-quarter 2026 results, whether the 2026 annual report shows the order and revenue targets were met, and the signing of the Mokdong 12th complex construction contract.
