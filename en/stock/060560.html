Report overview

Daegu-Gyeongbuk Slump Drags Five Straight Quarterly Losses

HC HomeCenter, built on ready-mix concrete/asphalt concrete and oil distribution, posted an annual operating loss of KRW 18.5 billion in 2025 amid the Daegu-Gyeongbuk construction slump, and has logged five consecutive quarters of operating losses through the second quarter of 2026.

02

Business structure

HC HomeCenter was founded in 1988 as a fire-fighting facility construction business, listed on KOSDAQ in 2002, converted into an operating holding company in 2017, and changed its name from HomeCenter Holdings to its current name in June 2025.

The holding company directly runs building-materials wholesale/retail and leisure and sports facilities (swimming pools, fitness centers, saunas), while numerous subsidiaries handle ready-mix concrete and asphalt concrete production, aggregate quarrying, oil wholesale/retail, logistics, and construction.

On a cumulative basis through the third quarter of 2024, consolidated revenue mix showed ready-mix concrete and asphalt concrete at 41.65% and oil wholesale/retail at 50.30%, with aggregate, leisure, retail goods, and logistics together under 10%.

In the Daegu ready-mix concrete market, (jusik) HomeCenter held a 9.81% share and subsidiary HC Bogwang Ready-Mix held 7.29% as of 2023, placing the combined group among the leading suppliers.

The oil segment, run through subsidiary HC Energy with one storage depot and eleven gas stations, provides one leg of stable cash flow.

In March 2025 the company acquired a stake in HC Donghwa Housing to enter the construction business, and at its March 2026 annual general meeting it added solar power generation and other renewable energy generation to its business purposes as a potential new growth avenue.

One of its ready-mix/asphalt subsidiaries, HC Bogwang Industries, is separately listed on KOSDAQ, with HC HomeCenter holding a 40.74% stake.

Competitors in the same industry include nationwide ready-mix concrete and building-materials firms, while HC HomeCenter is distinguished by a vertically integrated structure anchored in its own quarries in the Daegu-Gyeongbuk region.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual revenue fell for three straight years, from KRW 432.1 billion in 2022 to KRW 427.5 billion in 2023 (down 1.1%), KRW 369.0 billion in 2024 (down 13.7%), and KRW 298.8 billion in 2025 (down 19.0%).

Operating profit rose 67.9% from KRW 23.7 billion in 2022 to KRW 39.7 billion in 2023, then plunged 69.0% to KRW 12.3 billion in 2024, before swinging to an operating loss of KRW 18.5 billion in 2025.

Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders similarly rose to KRW 22.9 billion in 2023, fell 79.3% to KRW 4.75 billion in 2024, and reversed into a net loss of KRW 20.3 billion in 2025. Net income including non-controlling interests followed the same pattern, posting a loss of KRW 23.8 billion in 2025.

The operating margin deteriorated from 9.3% in 2023 to 3.3% in 2024 and -6.2% in 2025, and the company cited the construction slump in the Daegu-Gyeongbuk housing market as the primary cause in its earnings-variation disclosures.

On a quarterly basis, operating losses persisted for five consecutive quarters from Q2 2025 through Q2 2026, widening from KRW 3.01 billion in Q2 2025 to KRW 5.92 billion in Q1 2026 before narrowing to KRW 1.96 billion in Q2 2026.

Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders was still positive at KRW 4.04 billion in Q2 2025 but turned into losses for four straight quarters starting in Q3 2025, bringing the cumulative net loss for Q3 2025 through Q2 2026 to KRW 33.1 billion.

Operating cash flow shrank to KRW 1.17 billion in 2024 before recovering to KRW 25.0 billion in 2025, showing some improvement in cash generation even as the income statement remained in the red.

05

Industry analysis

HC HomeCenter's revenue and earnings are effectively tied to the construction cycle in the Daegu-Gyeongbuk region.

According to the Ministry of Land, Infrastructure and Transport's housing statistics, unsold housing units in Daegu and Gyeongbuk stood at 7,558 and 5,449 units respectively, among the highest for a metropolitan city and a province, pointing to a slow recovery in private housing starts.

In anticipation of the Daegu-Gyeongbuk Integrated New Airport (TK New Airport) project, the company preemptively expanded annual aggregate capacity from 2.07 million cubic meters to 3.1 million cubic meters and acquired HC Gunwi Industries, HC Uiseong Industries, and HC Uiseong Ready-Mix to build out ready-mix concrete supply capability, but repeated delays to the project meant demand failed to keep pace with the capacity buildout, weighing on profitability.

As a result, the ready-mix/asphalt concrete revenue share, which had stayed in the 40% range over the prior three years (2022-2024), fell to 28.7% as of the third quarter of 2025, shifting the structure to roughly equal halves between oil distribution and construction-related (ready-mix/asphalt) segments.

Brokerage research expects public construction order recovery in 2026 on higher SOC budgets and expanded public housing supply, while private construction investment is seen staying limited due to high interest rates and project-financing uncertainty.

In fact, on October 13, 2026, an asphalt/ready-mix concrete theme rally saw shares of regional supplier subsidiary HC Bogwang Industries jump more than 15% intraday, with HC HomeCenter shares also rising, and the accompanying report stated that regional material suppliers such as HC Bogwang Industries and HC HomeCenter had valid earnings momentum from road maintenance and resumed construction work in the region.

Unlike nationwide ready-mix concrete and building-materials companies, HC HomeCenter's heavy reliance on a single region (Daegu-Gyeongbuk) is a structural feature that makes it relatively more sensitive to local economic swings.

06

Outlook

Q1 2026 revenue fell 16.9% quarter-on-quarter to KRW 68.3 billion, but rebounded 24.6% to KRW 85.1 billion in Q2, with the operating loss narrowing from KRW 5.92 billion to KRW 1.96 billion, a signal that may suggest the trough has passed.

The company stated at an investor relations event that its core ready-mix concrete and asphalt concrete businesses would need time to get back on track, implying a lag before any resumption of public orders feeds through to earnings.

With higher SOC budgets and expanded public housing supply planned for 2026, the pace of volume recovery in the ready-mix/asphalt segment is a key variable, and clarity on the construction timeline for the Daegu-Gyeongbuk Integrated New Airport could also affect medium-term order intake.

It remains to be seen whether the construction business entered via the March 2025 acquisition of HC Donghwa Housing, and the solar and other renewable energy businesses added to its stated purposes at the March 2026 shareholder meeting, will develop into concrete new growth pillars.

In April 2026 the company completed a share consolidation changing the par value from KRW 100 to KRW 500 per share, citing share-price stabilization and enterprise value enhancement, reducing total shares outstanding—a change in share supply-demand dynamics rather than the underlying financial structure.

Despite the 2025 operating loss, the company maintained its dividend policy by approving a common-stock cash dividend at its March 2026 annual general meeting.

The oil segment continues to serve as a relatively stable revenue base, suggesting the pace of construction-segment recovery and cash generation from oil distribution will likely remain the two pillars of earnings for the time being.

08

Bull factors

Q2 Rebound and Narrowing Losses

Q2 2026 revenue rose to KRW 85.1 billion, up 24.6% quarter-on-quarter and 14.2% year-on-year, while the operating loss narrowed from KRW 5.92 billion to KRW 1.96 billion. The net loss attributable to controlling shareholders also shrank from KRW 4.09 billion to KRW 2.27 billion, slowing the pace of losses. The four-quarter widening trend through Q1 reversed direction in Q2, a notable positive signal.

SOC Budget Expansion and Expected Public Order Recovery

Expectations are forming for a recovery in public construction orders amid a projected increase in the 2026 SOC budget and expanded public housing supply.

Ready-mix concrete and asphalt concrete are essential materials for road paving and public construction, placing the company within the scope of potential beneficiaries.

Indeed, on October 13, 2026, shares of subsidiary HC Bogwang Industries and HC HomeCenter rose together amid an asphalt/ready-mix concrete theme rally that drew market attention.

Stable Cash Generation from the Oil Segment

The oil segment, operated through subsidiary HC Energy with one storage depot and eleven gas stations, provides a stable base accounting for roughly half of revenue regardless of the construction cycle.

Operating cash flow of KRW 25.0 billion in 2025, a significant increase from 2024, can partly be attributed to this segment's cash-generating capacity. The business portfolio structure allows the oil segment to partially offset volatility in the construction segment.

Bear factors

2025 Loss Swing and Growing Capital-Erosion Concerns

In 2025, both operating profit and net profit swung into losses, with an operating loss of KRW 18.5 billion and a net loss attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 20.3 billion.

As a result, total equity declined and the debt-to-equity ratio rose from 102.9% in 2024 to 139.6% in 2025, weakening financial soundness. Net losses attributable to controlling shareholders persisted for four straight quarters from Q3 2025 through Q2 2026, bringing cumulative losses to KRW 33.1 billion.

Heavy Reliance on the Single Daegu-Gyeongbuk Region

A substantial portion of revenue is tied to the Daegu-Gyeongbuk construction cycle, meaning a regional real estate downturn feeds directly into results.

Ministry of Land, Infrastructure and Transport statistics show unsold housing in Daegu and Gyeongbuk ranking among the highest nationally, suggesting private housing starts may recover slowly.

Compared with competitors with nationally diversified operations, the company is relatively more exposed to swings in regional conditions.

Mismatch Between Preemptive Capacity Expansion and Demand Recovery Timing

In preparation for the Daegu-Gyeongbuk Integrated New Airport project, the company expanded aggregate capacity from 2.07 million to 3.1 million cubic meters and acquired multiple ready-mix concrete affiliates, but repeated delays to the project schedule have left demand unable to keep pace with the capacity buildout.

As a result, the ready-mix/asphalt concrete revenue share fell from the 40% range in 2022-2024 to 28.7% in the third quarter of 2025. Fixed-cost burdens from the expansion could continue to weigh on a profitability recovery.

10

Risk factors

Construction Cycle and Order Risk

If private construction investment recovers only marginally due to high interest rates and project-financing uncertainty, recovery in ready-mix concrete and asphalt concrete volumes could be delayed. There is also a possible lag before expanded public orders translate into actual revenue.

Further delays to the construction timeline for the Daegu-Gyeongbuk Integrated New Airport could leave the preemptively expanded capacity underutilized.

Financial Structure Risk

The debt-to-equity ratio rose from 102.9% in 2024 to 139.6% in 2025, increasing financial burden. If operating losses continue, total equity could shrink further, additionally weakening financial soundness. Changes in the capital structure could affect future borrowing terms or financing costs.

New-Business Uncertainty

The construction business entered in 2025 and the solar and other renewable energy businesses added to the company's stated purposes in 2026 are both at an early stage with no confirmed concrete revenue contribution yet.

It may take time for these new businesses to grow large enough to offset losses in the existing construction and oil segments. The diversification process could also add further investment burden affecting the financial structure.

Overall view

HC HomeCenter is a Daegu-Gyeongbuk-based holding company built on two pillars—ready-mix concrete/asphalt concrete building materials and oil distribution—that posted a 2025 annual operating loss of KRW 18.5 billion and a net loss attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 20.3 billion, marking a clear deterioration in profitability following three consecutive years of revenue decline.

Revenue rebounded in Q2 2026 and the operating loss narrowed to KRW 1.96 billion, hinting at a possible trough, but with net losses attributable to controlling shareholders persisting for four straight quarters since Q3 2025, it is too early to call a definitive trend reversal.

The Daegu-Gyeongbuk Integrated New Airport project and an expanded 2026 SOC budget are medium-to-long-term variables for ready-mix/asphalt demand recovery, but repeated delays to the project timeline mean the benefits of preemptive capacity expansion have yet to materialize.

Whether the financial structure, with debt-to-equity rising to 139.6% at the end of 2025, and the new construction and 2026 solar businesses become durable growth pillars requires confirmation through further disclosures.

Investors will want to track the quarterly shift in ready-mix/asphalt concrete revenue mix, the pace of public order execution, and financial-soundness indicators going forward.
