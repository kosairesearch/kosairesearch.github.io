02

Business structure

DUK SAN Neolux is a materials company that handles OLED organic materials in-house from design and synthesis through purification and mass production. Its core products are emissive materials such as hole transport layer (HTL) materials, red host, and red and green prime materials.

Black PDL, a non-emissive material the company says it was first to mass-produce, is cited as a core item in its higher-value lineup. Kyobo Securities analyzed Black PDL as an essential material that reduces panel thickness and raises luminous efficiency, and expects it to become a core growth driver.

PSPI material for NAND stacking, which the company has localized, is also mentioned as an additional growth source. Samsung Display is the main customer, so its production line utilization largely determines materials shipments.

The business mix changed significantly after DUK SAN Neolux consolidated its turbomachinery subsidiary, acquired in February 2025. Kyobo Securities estimated that the turbomachinery segment accounts for about 37% of revenue after consolidation.

Hyundai Motor Securities noted rising orders for power infrastructure, including small modular reactors, and marine applications at this subsidiary. Through a wholly owned holding subsidiary, the company also pursued the purchase of 100% of industrial valve maker S&S Valve for KRW 144.5 billion.

As a result, the share of industrial businesses outside chemical materials is growing, and one press report noted that as industrial businesses with lower valuation multiples gain weight, the premium previously applied to the OLED materials business could be diluted.

04

Earnings analysis

On a disclosed basis, annual revenue fell from KRW 176.7 billion in 2022 to KRW 163.7 billion in 2023 (down 7.3% year on year), then rose to KRW 212.3 billion in 2024 (up 29.7%) and to KRW 344.3 billion in 2025 (up 62.2%), growing for two consecutive years.

The sharp 2025 revenue increase is consistent with the consolidation of the turbomachinery subsidiary acquired in February 2025. Operating profit fell from KRW 44.9 billion in 2022 to KRW 32.9 billion in 2023 (down 26.6%), then rose to KRW 52.5 billion in 2024 (up 59.4%) and KRW 60.7 billion in 2025 (up 15.8%).

The operating margin was 25.4% in 2022, 20.1% in 2023, 24.7% in 2024, and then fell to 17.6% in 2025, the lowest of the four years, a decline that coincides with the period when the acquired business entered the consolidated accounts.

Net income attributable to shareholders rose to KRW 53.2 billion in 2025 from KRW 45.8 billion in 2024 (up 16.2%), while net income including non-controlling interests was KRW 54 billion in 2025.

Operating cash flow increased each year, from KRW 48.9 billion in 2022 to KRW 60.6 billion in 2025, moving in the same direction as profit.

Quarterly revenue of KRW 127.1 billion in the fourth quarter of 2025 was described by a securities report as a quarterly record, followed by a trough of KRW 72.9 billion in the first quarter of 2026 (down 42.7% from the prior quarter).

Third-quarter 2026 operating profit of KRW 24 billion rose 36.9% year on year, and THE ELEC reported it as the largest quarterly operating profit.

Net income attributable to shareholders in the same quarter was KRW 12.4 billion, down 7.9% year on year, so operating and bottom-line results moved in different directions; the reason sits in non-operating items and income tax, which the next annual report's notes will show.

The sum of net income attributable to shareholders over the last four quarters, from the fourth quarter of 2025 to the third quarter of 2026, is KRW 70.3 billion.

05

Industry analysis

Demand for OLED materials moves directly with smartphone panel shipments and set production. Kyobo Securities expects material demand to grow visibly as OLED adoption rises in notebooks and tablets and as a global customer expands 8.6-generation OLED capacity.

One research report expects Black PDL supply to expand as Apple is expected to launch its first foldable smartphone. Hyundai Motor Securities explained that the foldable model adopts an M16 material structure, applying one common-layer material and two emissive-layer materials.

It also analyzed that the M14 material structure in standard models adds one emissive-layer material compared with the M13 series. On the other hand, Chinese smartphone makers are reportedly lowering shipment targets in response to higher component prices and weaker demand.

The surge in memory chip prices is also cited as a factor raising uncertainty in set demand. At the same time, Samsung Electronics and Apple are pursuing share-expansion strategies with stable component supply.

The key variables for the industry are customer line utilization and how fast new form factors are adopted, and the cycle position can be gauged from customer production trends.

06

Outlook

The company said it expects growth to continue into 2027 through a higher share of high-value materials. Revenue from new materials rose for three consecutive quarters through the third quarter of 2026.

Cumulative revenue through the third quarter of 2026 was KRW 274.2 billion and cumulative operating profit was KRW 59.6 billion, according to THE ELEC.

Kyobo Securities, in a May 22, 2026 report, estimated 2026 consolidated revenue at KRW 402.8 billion and operating profit at KRW 83.6 billion, and expected earnings to improve in the second half on new form factor revenue and wider adoption of Black PDL. iM Securities, in a June 10, 2026 report, raised its 2026 annual estimates to revenue of KRW 442.7 billion and operating profit of KRW 97.5 billion.

Both estimates were published before the third-quarter 2026 disclosure. Kyobo Securities also said that as new materials such as PSPI begin to contribute, earnings could remain solid in 2027. The subsidiary is also showing steady order flow, so its results form another leg of the outlook.

The next checkpoints are the fourth-quarter 2026 preliminary results and the annual operating margin in the 2026 annual report.

08

Bull factors

Higher-value material mix

Wider shipments of Black PDL and a larger share of high-value materials were reported as drivers of third-quarter 2026 operating profit of KRW 24 billion. Revenue from new materials has grown for three consecutive quarters.

One securities firm expects the product mix gain from Black PDL to help sustain profitability despite pressure on OLED panel prices.

Subsidiary orders and diversification

The turbomachinery subsidiary is seeing rising orders for power infrastructure, including small modular reactors, and for marine applications. Hyundai Motor Securities analyzed that the subsidiary's earnings growth is continuing. Kyobo Securities explained that the turbomachinery segment accounts for about 37% of consolidated revenue.

IT OLED penetration and foldables

Kyobo Securities expects material demand to grow as OLED adoption rises in IT devices and as 8.6-generation capacity expands. Analysts also link the foldable expansion to a process where the company is described as the exclusive supplier of Black PDL. One research report sees Black PDL supply expanding if Apple's foldable launch becomes clear.

Bear factors

Lower margin and quarterly swings

The operating margin fell to 17.6% in 2025 from 24.7% in 2024. Revenue bottomed at KRW 72.9 billion in the first quarter of 2026. Net income attributable to shareholders fell 7.9% year on year to KRW 12.4 billion in the third quarter of 2026, so quarterly profit has not moved in a steady path.

Customer concentration and set demand

Results are closely tied to Samsung Display's line utilization. Lower shipment targets from Chinese smartphone makers and the surge in memory chip prices are cited as sources of uncertainty in set demand.

Rumors of a delayed Apple foldable launch were cited as a factor behind weak share prices in an iM Securities note from February 2026.

Higher debt and re-rating risk

The debt ratio rose to 51.8% at the end of 2025 from 13.4% in 2024. As industrial businesses gain weight, the premium once applied to the OLED materials business could be diluted. The funding structure of the acquisitions and the stability of subsidiary earnings are the next items to track.

Risk factors

Customer concentration

Materials shipments link directly to line utilization at Samsung Display. Line adjustments or delays in new model transitions could move shipments sharply from quarter to quarter. The first-quarter 2026 revenue trough illustrates how sensitive results are to this structure.

Balance sheet and acquisition debt

Total liabilities of KRW 243.7 billion against total equity of KRW 470.5 billion produce the debt ratio. Operating profit has increased in some quarters without a matching rise in net income attributable to shareholders, so the path from operating profit to net income needs to be tracked in regular filings.

Business mix and valuation

As industrial businesses gain weight, a lower valuation multiple could apply to the group. As revenue scale expands, order swings at the subsidiary have a larger effect on consolidated results. Broker target prices vary by date of publication, so the report date should be read alongside any target.

Overall view

DUK SAN Neolux is shifting toward a structure in which its core OLED emissive and non-emissive materials business and the turbomachinery subsidiary acquired in 2025 both contribute to earnings.

On a disclosed basis, 2025 revenue reached KRW 344.3 billion, up 62.2%, and operating profit reached KRW 60.7 billion, up 15.8%. However, the operating margin fell to 17.6% in 2025 from 24.7% in 2024, and the debt ratio rose sharply to 51.8%.

In the third quarter of 2026, operating profit rose 36.9% year on year to KRW 24 billion, but net income attributable to shareholders fell 7.9% to KRW 12.4 billion, so operating and final profit moved in different directions.

On the bullish side, the higher share of high-value materials such as Black PDL and the wider penetration of foldable and IT OLED are cited.

On the bearish side, customer concentration tied to Samsung Display's utilization, uncertainty in set demand, and the risk of a lower multiple as industrial businesses grow are raised.

Among broker targets, iM Securities' KRW 70,000 presented in a June 10, 2026 report is the documented example, and all the estimates cited predate the third-quarter 2026 disclosure.

The next checkpoints are the fourth-quarter 2026 preliminary results, the non-operating items in the 2026 annual report, and the pace of the recovery in first-quarter 2027 revenue.
