02

Business structure

Polaris UNO is a KOSDAQ-listed chemical fiber maker that produces synthetic fibers for wigs. Its core product is braiding yarn, used for braided wigs worn by weaving hair directly into the braid, and the company describes it as its largest-selling item.

The product line also includes flame-retardant high-temperature fiber, braiding yarn with added water-repellent function, and PVC-based wig fiber. In late October 2026 the company announced it had completed development of KTS, a next-generation lightweight wig material built on its hollow-yarn technology.

Domestic production runs through plants in Wanju County and Iksan in North Jeolla, while its 100%-owned South African production subsidiary makes yarn close to key demand centers in Africa. An Indonesian subsidiary, established in 2023, began full production after a new plant was completed in March 2024.

Sales are centered on Africa and North America, where demand for braided wigs is broad. Reports from late July 2026 said the company's braiding yarn met safety criteria under California Proposition 65, giving it a basis to expand with North American distributors.

The competitive landscape is dominated by Japanese companies, and the company says its production technology and quality control have allowed it to gain a foothold.

In March 2026 the company also disclosed the disposal of 3,937,559 shares of its subsidiary Polaris Office for roughly KRW 13 billion, trimming an affiliated stake.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual results began in 2022 with revenue of KRW 97 billion and operating profit of KRW 7.48 billion. In 2023 revenue fell to KRW 78.1 billion (down 19.6% year on year) and operating profit to KRW 5.47 billion (down 26.8%).

In 2024 revenue rose 2.7% to KRW 80.1 billion, yet operating profit fell 57.6% to KRW 2.32 billion, pushing the operating margin down to 2.9%. In 2025 revenue rose 27.1% to KRW 101.8 billion and operating profit rose 235.8% to KRW 7.79 billion, restoring the operating margin to 7.7%, the same level as 2022.

The company attributed the 2025 increases in sales and operating profit to expanded global sales, including in Africa and the United States. Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders fell 60.9% to KRW 2.41 billion in 2025, and total net profit was the same KRW 2.41 billion.

In 2023 operating profit declined while net profit attributable to controlling shareholders rose 106.0% to KRW 7.64 billion, so the two moved in opposite directions.

Quarterly, revenue in the fourth quarter of 2025 was KRW 25.4 billion (down 9.8% from the prior quarter), and a net loss attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 1.17 billion turned the quarter into a loss.

In the first quarter of 2026 revenue dropped to KRW 18.8 billion (down 26.1% from the prior quarter), the low point, and net profit attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 2.42 billion returned to profit.

Revenue rose for two straight quarters in the second and third quarters of 2026, to KRW 22.5 billion and KRW 26 billion, but operating profit moved only from KRW 1.46 billion to KRW 1.78 billion.

Third-quarter operating profit fell 35.7% year on year, and although operating profit was positive, the company posted a net loss attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 1.47 billion, a swing into loss from a year earlier.

Non-operating items or income tax may explain the gap, but the details should be checked in the 2026 annual report.

05

Industry analysis

Demand for synthetic wig fiber rests on braided-wig demand in Africa and North America. In a 2023 announcement the company said easing COVID-19 restrictions had increased outdoor activity and wig-yarn demand. The rise in the won-dollar exchange rate that year was also cited as a favorable factor.

Both demand and exchange rates are external variables, so they drive earnings volatility. The competitive landscape is dominated by Japanese companies, and Polaris UNO has widened its position within that structure with functional products such as hollow yarn and flame-retardant high-temperature fiber.

The company says its South African production sits close to key demand centers, cutting logistics costs and transit times. In North America, safety regulatory compliance has become part of supplier qualification.

Securing results under California Proposition 65 created a basis for selling to large distributors and beauty companies. The company has said it expects sales of its flame-retardant high-temperature fiber in the United States to grow quickly.

Its product mix and customer base differ from those of large petrochemical peers such as LG Chem and Lotte Chemical, so sector index trends are hard to apply directly. Its earnings appear to depend more on demand in specific markets and exchange rates than on the broader chemical cycle.

06

Outlook

The company's growth drivers are the KTS product and expanded overseas production. KTS is in sample testing and finished-product evaluation with major global customers, and some customers have shown purchase interest, the company said.

The company plans to complete quality approvals customer by customer before converting them into actual orders and full-scale supply. In North America it plans to use its safety-test documentation to find new customers and turn one-time supply into long-term supply contracts.

The Indonesian plant is being run to improve cost competitiveness and production efficiency. The company has positioned flame-retardant high-temperature fiber and KTS as two main product lines to raise the share of higher-margin products.

On the ownership side, closing of the transfer and the extraordinary shareholders' meeting are both set for December 7, 2026, and the agenda details will be finalized by the board and re-disclosed later. The next periodic report is the 2026 annual report, which will fix fourth-quarter and full-year results for 2026.

Third-quarter 2026 operating profit of KRW 1.78 billion rose from the prior quarter but fell year on year. Whether the higher-value product mix shows up in quarterly profit is therefore the next point to check.

Bull factors

Profit recovery and margin restored in 2025

Operating profit in 2025 rose 235.8% to KRW 7.79 billion, and the operating margin climbed from 2.9% in 2024 to 7.7%, the same level as 2022. The company credited expanded sales in Africa and the United States. 2025 was the highest operating profit year in 2022–2025, which points to a recovering earnings base.

Overseas production base and North American safety results

A 100%-owned South African subsidiary and an Indonesian plant completed in March 2024 form the overseas production base. Braiding yarn that met California Proposition 65 safety criteria provides a basis for expanding with North American distributors. The company plans to widen testing for phthalates and heavy metals to strengthen its regulatory response.

KTS launch and higher-value product lineup

KTS uses hollow-yarn technology to deliver a lighter wig material with volume, and its development was announced in late October 2026. Sample testing is under way with major global customers, and some have shown purchase interest.

The company argues that pairing it with flame-retardant high-temperature fiber as two main lines can raise the share of higher-margin products.

Highly volatile earnings structure

The operating margin fell to 2.9% in 2024, and net profit attributable to controlling shareholders fell 60.9% to KRW 2.41 billion in 2025. In 2023 operating profit declined while net profit attributable to controlling shareholders rose, showing divergent movements. Recoveries at the operating level have repeatedly failed to carry through to net profit.

Competition dominated by Japanese companies

The wig synthetic-fiber market is described as dominated by Japanese companies. The company has gained entry through production technology and quality control, but technology competition with larger rivals continues. If the technology gap narrows, price pressure could increase.

Overall view

Polaris UNO restored its earnings base in 2025, when revenue reached KRW 101.8 billion and operating profit KRW 7.79 billion, the highest year in 2022–2025. The operating margin also rose from 2.9% in 2024 to 7.7%.

However, net profit attributable to controlling shareholders fell 60.9% to KRW 2.41 billion in 2025, and the company swung to a net loss of KRW 1.47 billion attributable to controlling shareholders in the third quarter of 2026.

Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders totals only KRW 1.17 billion over the last four quarters, so whether operating-level recovery carries through to net profit is the central question.

Overseas production, the California Proposition 65 safety results and KTS sample testing have been presented as growth drivers, but none has yet translated into reported orders.

The control-transfer closing and the extraordinary shareholders' meeting are both scheduled for December 7, 2026, and the stock was designated as an investment-caution issue on November 2, 2026. The 2026 annual report, the next periodic filing, will confirm fourth-quarter and full-year results.
