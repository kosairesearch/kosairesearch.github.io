Jungdawn: Testing Margin Recovery Amid Shrinking Sales

After sales and profit declined for two straight years in 2024 and 2025, Jungdawn's quarterly operating profit has swung between recovery and sharp decline through the third quarter of 2026, so the direction of profitability is still unconfirmed.

02

Business structure

Jungdawn is a meat processing and distribution company whose businesses fall into three lines: down feathers (exports), fresh meat, and processed meat. Its company business summary notes that down feather exports have been strong. The company has said it is pursuing an entry into the single-ingredient feed business.

The fresh meat unit is focused on cutting production costs, while the processed meat unit is expanding its B2C channel and launching new products to lift profitability. For 2025, the company attributed the profit decline to lower sales volume amid a weak economy and higher costs.

Because the company combines raw-material-driven and consumer-facing units, cost trends and the ability to pass on prices are the main swing factors for earnings. In processed meat, it overlaps with listed food peers such as Harim and Dongwon Industries.

Down feather prices generally move with demand for apparel fill and raw material supply. The company's strength lies in down feather production and exports, while its weakness is the direct exposure of processed meat sales to domestic consumer conditions. Separating the cost trends of the two units is a useful starting point for reading its earnings.

04

Earnings analysis

Jungdawn's annual results on record changed direction after 2023.

Consolidated sales rose from KRW 182.6 billion in 2022 to KRW 185.5 billion in 2023, an increase of 1.6% on the year, then fell to KRW 182 billion in 2024 (down 1.9%) and KRW 160.9 billion in 2025 (down 11.6%), declining for two consecutive years from 2024 through 2025.

Operating profit went from KRW 18.4 billion in 2022 to KRW 35.8 billion in 2023 (up 93.9%), KRW 14.7 billion in 2024 (down 58.9%) and KRW 6.75 billion in 2025 (down 54.1%). The operating margin was 10.1% in 2022, 19.3% in 2023, 8.1% in 2024 and 4.2% in 2025, falling quickly after 2023.

Profit attributable to controlling shareholders went from KRW 32.6 billion in 2023 to KRW 11.9 billion in 2024 and KRW 6.07 billion in 2025. For 2025, the company cited lower sales volume amid a weak economy and higher costs.

Operating cash flow fell from KRW 33.5 billion in 2023 to KRW 12.3 billion in 2025, while the debt ratio moved within the 60% range, at 59.7% in 2023, 63.7% in 2024 and 63.4% in 2025. The quarterly pattern is sharper.

Operating profit was KRW 2.3 billion in the third quarter of 2025, then KRW 422 million in the fourth quarter (down 81.7% from the prior quarter), before recovering to KRW 1.63 billion in the first quarter of 2026 (up 286.7%) and KRW 2.88 billion in the second quarter (up 76.7%).

Third-quarter 2026 operating profit was KRW 47.32 million, down 98.4% from the prior quarter and 97.9% from a year earlier, while sales of KRW 37.8 billion fell 11.5% year on year.

Profit attributable to controlling shareholders over the last four quarters (fourth quarter of 2025 to third quarter of 2026) totaled KRW 4.96 billion.

05

Industry analysis

Jungdawn's results depend on two factors: end-market consumer demand and raw material costs. In explaining 2025, the company pointed to lower sales volume from a weak economy, which indicates a soft consumer demand backdrop for its processed products.

In the down feather unit, demand for apparel fill is a key driver of prices. A 2018 research note from one Korean brokerage expected down prices to stay high on rising down apparel demand in China and elsewhere, but that is a view from that time and not a current price.

The same note cited the company's capacity to expand down output as a strength. In processed meat, listed food companies such as Harim and Dongwon Industries compete for similar distribution.

Jungdawn is trying to widen consumer reach through its B2C channel and new products, which is a way to offset scale gaps with larger food makers through differentiation.

Second-quarter 2026 sales rose 29.6% from the prior quarter, but third-quarter sales fell 10.1% from the second quarter, so quarter-to-quarter demand remains volatile. On the supply side, strong down feather exports are a positive, while weaker domestic consumption and higher costs are negatives.

The position of the cycle is difficult to determine with confidence, and reading sales alongside cost trends each quarter is the practical way to follow the environment.

06

Outlook

Three directions are confirmed. The first is whether strong down feather exports continue. The second is the planned entry into single-ingredient feed, an attempt to widen the business scope whose investment size and timing should be confirmed through filings.

The third is that the fresh meat unit is working to improve profitability through lower production costs, while the processed meat unit is expanding B2C reach and launching new products. Operating profit recovered to KRW 1.63 billion in the first quarter of 2026 and rose again to KRW 2.88 billion in the second quarter.

However, third-quarter operating profit fell to KRW 47.32 million, so whether the recovery holds still needs confirmation. Third-quarter sales of KRW 37.8 billion fell 11.5% year on year, so it also needs to be seen whether sales recovery accompanies profit recovery.

Profit attributable to controlling shareholders over the last four quarters totaled KRW 4.96 billion, which keeps earnings well below the 2023 peak. The next checkpoint is the operating margin and fourth-quarter sales in the 2026 annual report.

Once full-year results are confirmed, it becomes possible to judge whether quarterly recovery carried into the full year. Progress on the feed business and the sales contribution of new processed meat products can be tracked through future filings and IR materials.

Bull factors

Down feather exports and raw material position

The company's business summary confirms strong down feather exports. A 2018 brokerage note also cited its capacity to expand down output as a strength. If the export share holds, it could partly cushion volatility in the raw-material-driven business.

Quarterly profitability recovery

Operating profit rose from KRW 422 million in the fourth quarter of 2025 to KRW 1.63 billion in the first quarter of 2026 and KRW 2.88 billion in the second quarter. Second-quarter sales rose 29.6% from the prior quarter, so sales and profit moved together. Whether this continues after the third quarter is the point to check.

Asset-based valuation backdrop

The price-to-book ratio is below 1x, meaning the price sits under book value. Profit attributable to controlling shareholders stood at KRW 130.6 billion at the end of 2025. A solid asset base can serve as a reference point if earnings recover.

09

Bear factors

Consecutive declines in sales and profit

Sales declined in both 2024 and 2025, and 2025's KRW 160.9 billion was the smallest year in the 2022 to 2025 span. Operating profit fell from KRW 35.8 billion in 2023 to KRW 6.75 billion in 2025. The operating margin dropped from 19.3% in 2023 to 4.2% in 2025.

Sharp third-quarter profit drop

Third-quarter 2026 operating profit was KRW 47.32 million, down 98.4% from the prior quarter and 97.9% from a year earlier. Sales of KRW 37.8 billion fell 11.5% year on year in the same quarter. Profit attributable to controlling shareholders over the last four quarters totals only KRW 4.96 billion.

Weak demand and cost pressure

The company cited lower sales volume from a weak economy and higher costs as causes of the 2025 decline. Processed meat is directly exposed to consumer conditions. If higher costs are not fully passed on to selling prices, the operating margin could fall further.

10

Risk factors

Consumer demand and sales volume

If the lower sales volume the company cited as the main cause of the 2025 decline persists, sales recovery could be delayed. Third-quarter 2026 sales fell 11.5% year on year. Whether domestic consumption stays weak is the most direct variable.

Raw material and cost trends

Profitability in the down feather unit shifts with raw material prices and demand. If cost increases continue, both fresh meat and processed meat margins could come under pressure. Whether strong export periods coincide with high cost periods is also worth monitoring.

Execution of new business and channel shift

The entry into single-ingredient feed is at the pursuit stage, and its investment scale and profit structure are not yet clear. The B2C expansion and new product launches in processed meat will depend on execution for their sales contribution.

If new businesses fail to offset weaker profitability in existing ones, the earnings recovery could stay limited.

12

Overall view

Jungdawn's sales, operating profit and profit attributable to controlling shareholders declined for two consecutive years in 2024 and 2025 after peaking in 2023, and the operating margin fell to 4.2% in 2025.

Operating profit recovered in the first and second quarters of 2026, but fell to KRW 47.32 million in the third quarter, so quarterly profitability is highly volatile. Strengths include strong down feather exports, the recent quarterly recovery, and an asset base with the price-to-book ratio below 1x.

Weaknesses include consecutive sales declines, the sharp third-quarter profit drop, and margin pressure from a weak economy and higher costs. Profit attributable to controlling shareholders over the last four quarters totaled KRW 4.96 billion, well below the peak.

The feed business entry and the B2C shift in processed meat are still at the execution stage, so it is too early to judge results. The next checkpoints are the operating margin and fourth-quarter sales in the 2026 annual report and first-quarter 2027 results.
