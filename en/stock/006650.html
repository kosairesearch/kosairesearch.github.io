02

Business structure

Korea Petro Chemical IND is centered on naphtha cracking center (NCC) facilities that produce ethylene and related products from naphtha. Reports in November 2025 put the NCC annual capacity at 900,000 tons, about 7% of South Korea's total capacity.

Around 70% of its naphtha feedstock is supplied by S-Oil, which analysts see as relatively stable compared with peers. Brokerage commentary also highlights a higher NCC utilization rate than peers, supported by its high-value PE products.

Its battery separator PE and PP capacity is reported at 250,000 tons, and a company official said in October 2025 that exports to China would keep rising.

The utility business is the other pillar: the company raised its stake in Hanju, which supplies electricity, steam and industrial water, from 49% to 51% and consolidated it as a subsidiary. In a Nice Business Info comparison, the company ranked high on activity, stability and scale, but low on profitability.

Because the petrochemical core earns thin margins, the utility business acts as the base of earnings. The company's business overview from September 2026 notes that it is pursuing NCC expansion and a new butadiene plant.

04

Earnings analysis

On a reported basis, annual sales and operating profit both improved. Sales rose from KRW 2.22 trillion in 2022 to KRW 2.5 trillion in 2023 (up 12.5% year on year), KRW 2.8 trillion in 2024 (up 12.0%), and KRW 3.35 trillion in 2025 (up 19.6%), and sales increased for three consecutive years from 2023 through 2025.

Operating losses were KRW 214.6 billion in 2022, KRW 62.3 billion in 2023 and KRW 59.9 billion in 2024, with the losses narrowing each year, before the company posted an operating profit of KRW 52.7 billion in 2025. The operating margin moved from -9.7% in 2022 to -2.5% in 2023, -2.1% in 2024 and 1.6% in 2025.

Net loss attributable to controlling shareholders narrowed from KRW 149.1 billion in 2022 to KRW 29 billion in 2023 and KRW 8.53 billion in 2024, and turned into a net profit of KRW 31.7 billion in 2025.

Net income including non-controlling interests was KRW 55.9 billion in 2025, a different figure from net income attributable to controlling shareholders, so the two should be read separately.

Quarterly operating profit fell from KRW 42.8 billion in the third quarter of 2025 to KRW 24.3 billion in the fourth quarter (down 43.3% from the prior quarter), then recovered to KRW 73.6 billion in the first quarter of 2026 (up 202.8%).

The company posted an operating loss of KRW 3.88 billion in the second quarter of 2026 and returned to an operating profit of KRW 44.8 billion in the third quarter.

Third-quarter 2026 sales of KRW 910.7 billion rose only 0.1% year on year, while net income attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 19.8 billion fell 33.5%. Brokerage commentary attributes the quarterly swings to a large scheduled maintenance program and weak petrochemical conditions. Operating cash flow rose from an outflow of KRW 48 billion in 2022 to an inflow of KRW 202.8 billion in 2025.

05

Industry analysis

The petrochemical cycle reflects a mix of supply adjustments, feedstock prices and geopolitical factors. Yuanta Securities expected a slower recovery in the petrochemical market because of the Strait of Hormuz blockade. In the same analysis, NCC utilization was projected to fall from 91% in 2025 to about 67% in 2026.

Global ethylene capacity additions were expected to drop from 5.84 million tons in 2025 to 3.01 million tons in 2026, which would ease supply pressure. Plant closures and consolidation are also under way in the United States, Japan and Europe.

In South Korea, a government plan to reduce NCC capacity led to restructuring proposals, and reports say the company submitted a joint reorganization plan with refining and petrochemical affiliates.

One analysis notes that new capacity is scheduled to start mainly in the second half and fourth quarter, so actual output growth may be only about half of the announced capacity additions.

Among peers, the company is seen as having a relatively favorable utilization rate due to its high-value PE mix, while its profitability ranks low among industry peers. Demand for battery separator materials, linked to growth in the energy storage market, is cited as a positive factor.

The pace of Chinese capacity restructuring and feedstock price swings linked to the Middle East remain open uncertainties.

06

Outlook

The first variable shaping the company's results is the fourth-quarter scheduled maintenance. Yuanta Securities, in a report covered in September 2026, projected a fourth-quarter operating loss of KRW 25 billion and said profits built up through the third quarter could be diluted.

How quickly output returns to normal after the maintenance will show up in fourth-quarter results released in early 2027.

On the utility side, the expansion of LNG combined-cycle power plants is cited as a driver, and whether the power plant segment's projected operating profit of KRW 87.8 billion continues to support annual earnings is a checkpoint.

In chemicals, the company is pursuing NCC expansion and a new butadiene plant, according to its business overview.

Battery separator demand is cited as a positive, with growth in the global energy storage market and large Chinese orders, and a company official said in October 2025 that exports to China would keep rising. How this demand interacts with Chinese capacity restructuring needs further confirmation.

Whether the government-led NCC reorganization plan is finalized is another variable that could change the long-term structure. Annual results and the financial position will be formally confirmed in the 2026 annual report, due by the end of March 2027.

08

Bull factors

Annual turnaround and sales growth

The swing of operating profit to positive alongside sales growth is the starting point of the annual improvement. Net income attributable to controlling shareholders also turned into a profit of KRW 31.7 billion. Sales rose for three consecutive years from 2023 through 2025. On an annual basis, the narrowing of losses in the core business is visible.

Earnings contribution from utilities

The company raised its stake in Hanju to 51% and consolidated it, and the utility business supplying electricity, steam and industrial water has become a base for earnings. Yuanta Securities projected 2026 operating profit of KRW 87.8 billion for the power plant segment.

The power business offsets part of the losses in the petrochemical segment. The expansion of LNG combined-cycle plants could enlarge this contribution.

Stable financial structure

At the end of 2025, total liabilities were KRW 618.9 billion and total equity was KRW 2.08 trillion, and the debt ratio rose from 17.9% in 2022 to 29.7% in 2025 but remains low. Operating cash flow reached KRW 202.8 billion in 2025, a sharp improvement from the outflow in 2022. The financial capacity maintained through loss-making years acts as a buffer against cycle swings.

Bear factors

High quarterly volatility

The company posted an operating loss of KRW 3.88 billion in the second quarter of 2026 and returned to an operating profit of KRW 44.8 billion in the third quarter. Net income attributable to controlling shareholders over the last four quarters totaled KRW 63.7 billion.

In the third quarter of 2026, net income attributable to controlling shareholders fell 33.5% year on year. Given these swings, the annual profit does not fully reflect the quarterly trend.

Fourth-quarter maintenance and delayed recovery

Yuanta Securities, in a report covered in September 2026, projected a fourth-quarter operating loss of KRW 25 billion. The same brokerage said the recovery in the petrochemical market is being delayed by the Strait of Hormuz blockade. It also projected NCC utilization falling to about 67% in 2026.

If the production halt from maintenance overlaps with the slower market recovery, the pace of earnings recovery could slow.

Low profitability and losses in the core business

The operating margin in 2025 was 1.6%, which is low relative to sales. Yuanta Securities projected a 2026 operating loss of KRW 23.7 billion for the headquarters chemical segment. In the Nice Business Info comparison, profitability ranked low among industry peers. If the core business does not regain profitability, dependence on utility earnings rises.

10

Risk factors

Feedstock and spread risk

About 70% of naphtha feedstock is supplied by S-Oil, creating high dependence on a single supplier. A decline in petrochemical product spreads could quickly weaken the operating margin. Middle East developments and crude price swings affect both feedstock costs and product prices. These factors add to quarterly earnings volatility.

Policy and facility restructuring risk

Following a government plan to cut NCC capacity, restructuring proposals were submitted, and a joint reorganization with refining and petrochemical affiliates has been discussed. Until the direction and scale of capacity reduction are settled, the business structure remains uncertain.

Facility closures or consolidation could bring one-time costs. Medium-term capacity and earnings structure could change depending on the outcome.

China demand and restructuring risk

The company's expectation of rising battery separator exports to China depends on Chinese battery demand. Faster Chinese capacity restructuring could tighten supply, but continued expansion in China could intensify price competition. Volatility from concentration in a few customers also needs to be considered. The direction of profitability depends on how the global supply structure settles.

Overall view

Korea Petro Chemical IND returned to an annual operating profit of KRW 52.7 billion in 2025, and sales rose for three consecutive years to KRW 3.35 trillion.

Quarterly results in 2026 have varied widely, with operating profit of KRW 73.6 billion in the first quarter, an operating loss of KRW 3.88 billion in the second, and KRW 44.8 billion in the third.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders over the last four quarters totaled KRW 63.7 billion, so the annual profit does not reflect the quarterly path. Profitability in the core business remains low, with a 2025 operating margin of 1.6%, and the utility business supports the earnings base.

Bullish factors include the annual turnaround, utility earnings and a stable financial structure with a debt ratio of 29.7%. Bearish factors include fourth-quarter maintenance, delayed market recovery, dependence on feedstock supply and uncertainty over facility restructuring.

The stock trades at a discount to net asset value, while earnings-based multiples are higher, so interpretation depends on the durability of earnings.

The next checkpoints are the fourth-quarter 2026 results and the 2026 annual report, and the first-quarter 2027 results will show whether the market recovers after maintenance.
