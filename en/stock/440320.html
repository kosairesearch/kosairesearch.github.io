02

Business structure

Openknowl listed on KOSDAQ in June 2023. Its sector tag is games, but the business structure visible in public sources centers on a career platform and digital signage hardware manufacturing. One securities information site classifies the company under software development and supply.

Its flagship platform, Mini Intern, is an AI-based HR and edtech service that runs job matching and training.

According to company announcements, 2025 consolidated revenue comprised KRW 28.5 billion from the platform, KRW 8.6 billion from application software development and supply, and KRW 51.5 billion from digital signage.

Other press coverage put 2025 platform revenue at KRW 11.9 billion, so the segment split differs across reports.

Digital signage and industrial display production is handled by an acquired hardware subsidiary, which reportedly exports to more than 30 countries, including the United States, France and Japan, on the strength of its in-house board design capability.

At the end of 2025, the company merged two subsidiaries to reduce duplicate personnel and shared administrative costs, according to management. In the public sector, Openknowl runs small business digital transformation programs and was re-selected as operator of Sodam Square Dangsan for 2027 to 2031.

Its competitive set overlaps with domestic HR and recruitment platforms, such as Wanted Lab, Callus Company and Geekplanner. Because its business model and product structure differ from the listed game companies, direct comparison with them is limited.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual revenue rose from KRW 16.3 billion in 2022 to KRW 25.1 billion in 2023 (up 54.5%), KRW 50.8 billion in 2024 (up 102.0%) and KRW 88.3 billion in 2025 (up 73.9%), and has grown for three consecutive years from 2023. 2025 was the largest year of 2022 to 2025.

Press coverage of company announcements put 2025 revenue at KRW 88.6 billion and described it as a record, while the DART filing figure is KRW 88.3 billion.

Operating profit went from KRW 685 million in 2022 to KRW 1.35 billion in 2023 (up 96.6%), then fell to KRW 101 million in 2024 (down 92.5%), and turned into an operating loss of KRW 2.76 billion in 2025.

The operating margin was 4.2% in 2022, 5.4% in 2023, 0.2% in 2024 and -3.1% in 2025, so margins narrowed even as revenue grew.

Controlling-shareholder net income turned positive at KRW 1.41 billion in 2023, eased to KRW 1.35 billion in 2024 (down 4.4%), and became a controlling-shareholder net loss of KRW 6.5 billion in 2025. Total net loss for 2025, including non-controlling interests, was KRW 6.43 billion.

The company attributed the 2025 swing to losses to infrastructure build-out for new businesses and temporary integration costs from merging subsidiaries.

By quarter, the fourth quarter of 2025 posted an operating loss of KRW 3.08 billion and a controlling-shareholder net loss of KRW 5.85 billion, before operating profit returned to KRW 997 million in the first quarter of 2026.

Operating profit then declined for two consecutive quarters, to KRW 738 million in the second quarter (down 25.9% quarter on quarter) and KRW 318 million in the third (down 56.9%).

Revenue moved from KRW 17.7 billion in the second quarter to KRW 25.8 billion in the third, up 45.6% quarter on quarter, but only 0.2% higher year on year.

Management said first-half operating profit of KRW 1.73 billion was the largest for any first half, citing lower selling and administrative expenses and a higher share of platform revenue, which rose to 35.8% from 24.5% a year earlier.

Operating cash flow went from negative in 2022 through 2024 to KRW 1.91 billion in 2025. The debt-to-equity ratio rose to 89.3% at the end of 2025 from 82.5% a year earlier, and total equity at the end of 2025 was KRW 39.1 billion, of which KRW 34.6 billion belonged to controlling shareholders.

05

Industry analysis

The career platform addresses information asymmetry in recruiting, which has traditionally relied on resume credentials, and the company frames its model as verifying job-seekers' practical skills with data.

A technology evaluation reportedly rated the platform's technical originality and business potential highly enough to meet the growth-type listing requirement.

On the demand side, growing hiring for experienced staff who use AI tools has been cited as a driver of the AX (AI transformation) training and talent-matching business. On policy, the Ministry of SMEs and Startups has shifted small business support toward growth-oriented scale-up and is committing larger budgets.

The digital signage business delivers content through devices installed in education sites and large companies, so quarterly revenue can swing with installation pace and order timing. Quarterly revenue in 2026 was KRW 22.1 billion, KRW 17.7 billion and KRW 25.8 billion in the first three quarters, a wide range.

In cycle terms, the career platform is in a phase where demand structure shifts with wider AI adoption, while digital signage depends on how fast installations expand at companies and schools.

Competitively, the platform overlaps with domestic HR and recruiting platforms such as Wanted Lab, Callus Company and Geekplanner. The hardware side points to in-house board design and exports to more than 30 countries as its competitive basis.

Because its business model and product structure differ from the listed game companies, the comparison is limited.

06

Outlook

On 21 April 2026, the company disclosed that it expects consolidated revenue for fiscal 2026 of KRW 100 billion. The same outlook left operating profit and net income blank, and management said it was prepared on an internal basis reflecting business plan targets.

Revenue for the first three quarters of 2026 totals KRW 65.6 billion on a filed basis, so whether the annual outlook is reached depends on fourth-quarter revenue. On orders, cumulative order intake at the end of the third quarter of 2026 was KRW 84.5 billion, up 21% year on year.

The company reported an order backlog of KRW 16.9 billion at the end of the third quarter, up 78%, while a press report put the backlog at KRW 24.7 billion at the end of the first half, so the two figures are not directly comparable because of differing dates and bases.

In the first half, the company won physical AI infrastructure construction and operation projects, and it said it plans to expand B2B and B2G orders and strengthen partnerships with domestic robotics and cloud companies.

A one-day AI content production system, which cuts content production time from 10 to 15 days to one day, is also planned. Its selection as operator of Sodam Square Dangsan for five years from 2027 to 2031 is presented as a stable operating base.

For the fourth quarter, management said it will keep running the business with a focus on profitability. The next checkpoint is the 2026 annual report, where the full-year operating margin will be disclosed.

Bull factors

Order growth and wider business scope

The company said first-half orders were the largest for any first half, including a physical AI infrastructure project. Whether these orders convert into revenue will be visible in the 2026 annual report.

Operating profit run and margin recovery

Operating profit turned positive at KRW 997 million in the first quarter of 2026 and stayed positive through the third quarter. The first-half operating margin improved from -2.5% to 4.4%. The company attributes this to lower selling and administrative expenses and a larger share of higher-value business.

Rising platform share

The platform's share of first-half revenue rose to 35.8% from 24.5% a year earlier, which press coverage describes as reducing reliance on subsidiary sales. The five-year re-selection for the small business digital programme and the expansion of AX training are cited as evidence of a wider platform scope.

Bear factors

Third-quarter profit decline

Operating profit for the third quarter of 2026 was KRW 318 million, down 75.4% year on year. It has declined for two consecutive quarters since the second quarter of 2026, so the surplus is narrowing. Third-quarter revenue rose 45.6% from the prior quarter but was only 0.2% higher than a year earlier.

Cumulative net losses and the swing to deficit

The 2025 swing produced an operating loss of KRW 2.76 billion and a controlling-shareholder net loss of KRW 6.5 billion. The fourth quarter of 2025 alone had a controlling-shareholder net loss of KRW 5.85 billion. The second quarter of 2026 also recorded a controlling-shareholder net loss of KRW 1.39 billion.

Hardware dependence and platform concerns

Press coverage puts digital signage at 58.33% of 2025 revenue. The same coverage reported platform revenue falling from KRW 14.9 billion in 2024 to KRW 11.9 billion in 2025, which differs sharply from the KRW 28.5 billion the company reported, so segment figures require care in interpretation.

Risk factors

Business structure and subsidiary dependence

The hardware subsidiary contributes more than half of consolidated revenue, so group sales swing with its results. Platform revenue figures also differ across reports, which makes core business growth hard to gauge. How much of the merger benefit shows up in profit after the fourth quarter is a key variable.

Financial leverage and cash flow

The debt-to-equity ratio rose to 89.3% at the end of 2025 from 82.5% at the end of 2024. Of total equity of KRW 39.1 billion, KRW 34.6 billion belongs to controlling shareholders. Operating cash flow turned positive at KRW 1.91 billion in 2025 after negative readings from 2022 through 2024.

Share price volatility and shareholder flows

Foreign ownership was 1.98% as of 20 August 2026. Political-theme rallies, including one above 20% in a single day in April 2025, have produced large swings in the share price.
