Report overview

Electronic materials gain weight as quarterly profit rebounds from 2025 low

Miwon Commercial is a fine-chemicals maker in which electronic materials and household chemicals each account for nearly half of sales. Operating profit fell for three straight years through 2025, then reached KRW 20.3 billion in the second quarter of 2026 before moving to KRW 14.5 billion in the third quarter.

02

Business structure

Miwon Commercial was founded in 1959 as a fine-chemicals maker producing higher-value products such as surfactants, photosensitizers, UV stabilizers and antioxidants.

Its business groups are household chemical materials, display materials, semiconductor materials, functional materials, polymer additives and lens monomers.

At the end of 2025, the sales mix was electronic materials at KRW 200.7 billion (45.5%), household chemical products at KRW 198.0 billion (44.9%) and other products at KRW 42.3 billion (9.6%).

A 2024 media report said electronic materials sales of KRW 151.4 billion in 2021, KRW 186.7 billion in 2022 and KRW 187.0 billion in 2023 exceeded household chemical sales in those years. The company says demand for localized materials rose after Japan tightened semiconductor materials export controls in 2019.

It began mass production of monoanionic PHS in 2023, a core raw material for photoresist that had previously been fully imported from Japan, and photoresist raw materials are supplied to Dongjin Semichem.

Household chemicals center on surfactants used in cosmetics and household products, and exports of that segment rose 11.2% to KRW 113.4 billion in 2025. Consolidated export sales were KRW 252.2 billion in 2025, or 57% of sales, against domestic sales of KRW 188.9 billion.

Overseas, the company is widening its supply chain through a Chinese subsidiary and related companies in the United States, Europe, India and Vietnam. A company profile describes it as a domestic surfactant producer with experience in detergent products and cosmetic materials.

04

Earnings analysis

On a disclosed basis, sales fell from KRW 438.2 billion in 2022 to KRW 421.4 billion in 2023, then rose to KRW 435.8 billion in 2024 and KRW 441.1 billion in 2025, two straight years of growth.

Operating profit over the same period went from KRW 77 billion to KRW 67.2 billion, KRW 59.9 billion and KRW 43.6 billion, falling for three consecutive years from 2023 through 2025, with 2025 the smallest year of the four.

The operating margin narrowed from 17.6% in 2022 to 16.0% in 2023, 13.8% in 2024 and 9.9% in 2025, so margins fell even as sales grew. Household chemical sales reached KRW 198.0 billion in 2025 on stronger surfactant exports, but press coverage pointed to cost and expense pressure as the cause of weaker profitability.

R&D spending rose 13.8% to KRW 17.3 billion, which was also cited as a drag on operating profit. Net income attributable to controlling shareholders went from KRW 72.2 billion to KRW 61 billion, KRW 50.5 billion and KRW 43.5 billion, falling for three straight years through 2025, when it was the lowest.

Net income including non-controlling interests was disclosed at the same values in each year. At the end of 2025, total equity was KRW 416.4 billion, all attributable to controlling shareholders, total liabilities were KRW 76.5 billion, and the debt ratio was 18.4%.

Operating cash flow rose from KRW 70.8 billion in 2022 to KRW 97.6 billion in 2023, then fell to KRW 68.6 billion in 2025. On a quarterly basis, sales bottomed at KRW 108.6 billion in the fourth quarter of 2025, then rose for three consecutive quarters through the third quarter of 2026.

Second-quarter 2026 sales were KRW 130.1 billion and operating profit was KRW 20.3 billion, up 106.6% from the prior quarter. Third-quarter sales of KRW 130.2 billion were roughly flat on the quarter, and operating profit of KRW 14.5 billion fell 28.6% from the prior quarter but rose 34.8% from a year earlier.

Over the same third quarter, net income attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 8.98 billion fell 13.3% from a year earlier, so operating profit and attributable net income moved in opposite directions.

Attributable net income for the last four quarters, from the fourth quarter of 2025 through the third quarter of 2026, totaled KRW 51.4 billion.

05

Industry analysis

Miwon Commercial's core demand comes from higher-value electronic materials used in displays, semiconductors and secondary batteries. The company says it is building R&D capacity as localization of materials gains momentum in this market.

A January 2023 local media report said the average selling price of electronic materials items rose 38% year on year in the third quarter of 2022 and tied growth prospects to a turnaround in semiconductors.

A company profile on the company monitor service expects demand for high-performance materials to grow with AI servers, high-speed data communication, electric vehicles and eco-friendly industries.

In household chemicals, demand for cosmetics and household products that skip animal testing is rising in Korea and the United States, and in a July 2024 interview the CEO said the company is actively developing products for that trend.

On Alphasquare's 2025 annual ranking of 121 chemical companies, Miwon Commercial ranked 21st on operating profit and 23rd on operating margin, and the same dataset puts the sector average operating margin at 1.00%.

Listed peers in the same sector include LG Chem, Hanwha Solutions, SKC, Kumho Petro Chemical, ISU Specialty Chemical and Lotte Chemical. Sales rose in 2024 and 2025 while the operating margin narrowed, so whether a sector recovery translates into margin gains is the next point to watch. With exports at 57% of sales, the company is exposed to overseas demand and currency movements.

06

Outlook

Miwon Commercial's 2026 corporate value-up plan was disclosed on March 24, 2026. Its growth targets are 10% sales growth and a facility investment program of KRW 30 billion.

On the business side, the plan calls for strengthening higher-value product lines and securing production capacity in household chemicals, and expanding R&D and research capability in electronic materials. On production, it targets automation and efficiency at its plants and deeper use of data.

For shareholder returns, the target is to keep total returns, through share cancellation and dividends, at about 40% of consolidated net income.

The dividend for 2025 totaled about KRW 10.9 billion, up 17.5% from the prior year, while net income attributable to controlling shareholders fell 13.9%, so dividends and earnings moved in opposite directions.

Quarterly sales moved from KRW 117.3 billion in the first quarter of 2026 to KRW 130.1 billion in the second and KRW 130.2 billion in the third, while operating profit went from KRW 9.83 billion to KRW 20.3 billion and then KRW 14.5 billion.

Whether annual sales approach the 10% growth target will first be visible in the 2026 annual report, which is the next periodic filing. The timing and amount of the KRW 30 billion facility investment should also be confirmed in that filing.

Company commentary points to rising demand for high-performance materials, but order volumes and completion timing for new capacity are not yet presented in public disclosures. The overseas supply chain is being expanded through a Chinese subsidiary and related companies in the United States, Europe, India and Vietnam.

The next checkpoints are the full-year operating margin in the 2026 annual report, compared with 9.9% in 2025, and the amount of facility investment actually executed.

08

Bull factors

Electronic materials share is growing

Electronic materials sales reached KRW 200.7 billion in 2025, or 45.5% of the total, slightly ahead of household chemical products at 44.9%. Electronic materials have outsold household chemicals in multiple years since 2021, so the mix is shifting toward higher-value materials.

Quarterly operating profit has recovered

Operating profit in the second quarter of 2026 was KRW 20.3 billion, up 106.6% from the prior quarter. Third-quarter operating profit of KRW 14.5 billion was 34.8% higher than a year earlier and above the KRW 9.02 billion posted in the fourth quarter of 2025.

Shareholder return plan

In its 2026 corporate value-up plan, the company said it would keep total shareholder returns at about 40% of consolidated net income through share cancellation and dividends. Total dividends for 2025 were about KRW 10.9 billion, up 17.5%, and the company said it qualifies as a high-dividend company.

Bear factors

Three straight years of profit declines

Operating profit fell from KRW 77 billion in 2022 to KRW 43.6 billion in 2025, and net income attributable to controlling shareholders fell from KRW 72.2 billion to KRW 43.5 billion. The operating margin narrowed from 17.6% to 9.9%. Because sales rose over the same period, the margin decline remains the main weakness.

Cost and expense pressure

Press coverage pointed to cost and expense pressure as the reason profitability weakened in 2025. R&D spending rose 13.8% to KRW 17.3 billion, a drag on near-term profit. The timing of when R&D spending converts into profit is not yet visible in the disclosed results.

Third-quarter net income decline

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders in the third quarter of 2026 was KRW 8.98 billion, down 13.3% from a year earlier. Operating profit rose from a year earlier but net income fell, so the operating-level improvement did not carry through to the bottom line.

10

Risk factors

Customer and cycle concentration

Electronic materials are tied to display, semiconductor and battery demand, so they are sensitive to downstream cycles. Photoresist raw materials go to a specific photoresist maker, which could mean high customer concentration.

If downstream investment slows, electronic materials, which make up 45.5% of sales, could feel the impact first.

Overseas sales and currency

Exports were 57% of 2025 sales, or KRW 252.2 billion. Changes in overseas demand and exchange rates can affect results directly. Expanding the supply chain through a Chinese subsidiary and overseas related companies also exposes the company to local regulation and competitive shifts.

Ownership structure and free float

According to the company monitor service, Kim Jeong-don and 46 others held 64.62% as of October 28, 2026, and the employee stock ownership association, a holder of more than 10%, held 13.50% as of November 13, 2026. Because the free float is limited, trading volume and share price volatility can be affected.

12

Overall view

Miwon Commercial's disclosed results show sales expanding while profit contracted. Sales rose in both 2024 and 2025, but operating profit and net income attributable to controlling shareholders fell for three consecutive years from 2023.

The operating margin narrowed from 17.6% to 9.9%, and press coverage cited cost pressure and higher R&D spending. The business is split between electronic materials at KRW 200.7 billion (45.5%) and household chemical products at KRW 198.0 billion (44.9%), with the mix moving toward higher-value electronic materials.

Quarterly operating profit in 2026 ran at KRW 9.83 billion, KRW 20.3 billion and KRW 14.5 billion, above the KRW 9.02 billion of the fourth quarter of 2025, and attributable net income over the last four quarters totaled KRW 51.4 billion.

Strengths include the rising electronic materials share, the 2026 quarterly profit recovery and the 40% total shareholder return target. Weaknesses include three straight years of profit declines, the third-quarter 2026 net income drop, cost pressure and limited free float due to the ownership structure.

On valuation, the price-to-earnings multiple and dividend yield sit below sector averages, while price-to-book is above 1x. The next checkpoints are the full-year operating margin in the 2026 annual report, due by the end of March 2027, and the first-quarter 2027 quarterly report.
