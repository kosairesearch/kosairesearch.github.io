02

Business structure

YES24Co started in 1999 as South Korea's first online bookstore and has expanded from books into e-books, performance tickets, music and subscription services. According to a summary of its business report, online channels account for a high share of sales, and a substantial part of that comes through mobile.

The company says its member base is about 24 million. In e-books, its e-book subscription service and e-reader device contributed to sales, as the company reported for the second quarter of 2022.

Based on a 2019 news report, books accounted for roughly 70% of total sales, with the rest coming from performances, tickets, e-books and fashion. Since the book price regulation, competition has shifted toward differentiated services and customer satisfaction rather than price.

Other platforms, including Kyobo Book Centre and Millie's Library, also offer e-book subscriptions, based on a 2019 report. The largest shareholder is the group holding company, Hanse YES24 Holdings, with a 50.01% stake.

On logistics, the company is building a smart logistics center in Paju, Gyeonggi Province, with a total planned investment of KRW 124.2 billion.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual revenue fell from KRW 663.8 billion in 2022 to KRW 657.8 billion in 2023 (down 0.9% year on year), recovered to KRW 671.1 billion in 2024 (up 2.0%), and dropped to KRW 626.5 billion in 2025 (down 6.7%). Among 2022 to 2025, 2024 was the largest year and 2025 the smallest.

Operating profit rose to KRW 16.3 billion in 2024 (up 178.4%) and then fell to KRW 2.94 billion in 2025 (down 82.0%), with the operating margin easing from 2.4% to 0.5%.

Net income attributable to shareholders swung from a profit of KRW 1.45 billion in 2024 to a loss of KRW 10.3 billion in 2025, while the net loss including non-controlling interests was KRW 10.7 billion.

Reported costs in 2025 were pushed up by compensation tied to the security incident and service disruption, with provisions of about KRW 5 billion.

Total liabilities at the end of 2025 were KRW 325.3 billion against total equity of KRW 171.7 billion, and the debt-to-equity ratio rose from 111.1% in 2022 to 189.5% in 2025. Operating cash flow fell from KRW 35.3 billion in 2024 to KRW 1.12 billion in 2025. Quarterly results in 2026 moved in both directions.

Operating profit was KRW 967 million in the first quarter, turned into an operating loss of KRW 3.47 billion in the second quarter, and returned to a profit of KRW 1.49 billion in the third quarter.

Cost of sales fell 8.1% year on year to KRW 114.5 billion in the third quarter, lifting the gross margin from 19.0% to 22.6%, while selling and administrative expenses rose 11.8% and financing costs jumped 70.4%.

For the first nine months, operating results turned to a loss of KRW 1.02 billion, and net income relied on non-operating financial income.

The net loss attributable to shareholders narrowed to KRW 231 million in the third quarter after KRW 9.61 billion in the fourth quarter of 2025, and the last four quarters total KRW 9.53 billion.

05

Industry analysis

YES24Co's core market is domestic book distribution and the e-book and cultural content businesses linked to it. Since the book price regulation, competition has centered on service differentiation and customer satisfaction rather than on price, which is a structural feature of this market.

With a high share of online sales, platform scale and member base largely determine competitiveness. E-book subscriptions compete with Kyobo Book Centre, Millie's Library and RIDIBOOKS, according to a 2019 report.

In a 2022 second-quarter disclosure, some listed publishing-related companies reported continued losses or declining sales. Performance tickets follow a different demand pattern from books, so changes in that segment's weight can change earnings volatility.

The decline in sales has been attributed to weaker book and music sales. The holding company has set a group target of KRW 6 trillion in sales by 2032, and how that goal connects to YES24Co's earnings recovery is a key variable.

Outlook

The company disclosed on October 28, 2026 a consolidated revenue outlook of KRW 620 billion for fiscal 2026, based on its half-year results, growth trends and macro conditions, and it noted that actual results may differ.

Revenue for the first nine months of 2026 was KRW 451.7 billion, down 4.9% year on year, so reaching the outlook would require fourth-quarter revenue of about KRW 168.3 billion or more, by simple arithmetic.

The company said it would open a highly automated smart logistics center in the second half of 2026 with capacity for more than 30 million shipments a year.

The Paju center has a total planned investment of KRW 124.2 billion, of which KRW 91.0 billion had been spent by the end of September 2026, and its effect on earnings will follow the investment pace and the rise in borrowings.

The company is discussing follow-up measures with authorities over the security incident, and additional costs or sanctions remain possible. Of a KRW 41.3 billion advance on performances, 23.84%, or KRW 9.8 billion, has been set aside as an allowance for doubtful accounts. The 2026 annual report will confirm full-year revenue and fourth-quarter results.

Bull factors

Return to operating profit

Operating profit was KRW 967 million in the first quarter of 2026 and KRW 1.49 billion in the third quarter. Cost of sales fell 8.1% in the third quarter from a year earlier, and the gross margin rose to 22.6%. Cost improvement was reported as the main driver of the rebound in operating profit.

Room for a higher operating margin depends on the cost structure and how selling and administrative expenses are managed.

Revenue outlook and member base

The company disclosed a fiscal 2026 consolidated revenue outlook of KRW 620 billion on October 28, 2026. Management pointed to a member base of about 24 million and accumulated data as the basis for its platform competitiveness. At the group level, a 2032 sales target of KRW 6 trillion has been presented. Whether the outlook is met will be confirmed in the 2026 annual report.

Logistics investment and low price relative to book

Of the Paju smart logistics center's total planned investment of KRW 124.2 billion, KRW 91.0 billion had been spent by the end of September 2026. The company targets handling more than 30 million shipments a year after opening.

The price-to-book ratio is well below 1x, and some view the price as low relative to asset value. Whether this level leads to an earnings recovery remains to be confirmed.

Bear factors

Continued sales decline

Revenue in 2025 was KRW 626.5 billion, down 6.7% year on year. Third-quarter 2026 revenue was KRW 147.9 billion, down 3.9% from a year earlier, and cumulative revenue for the first nine months fell 4.9%. Weaker sales of core products such as books and music have been reported as the cause. If the decline continues, fixed-cost pressure could limit the room for operating profit to improve.

Rising borrowings and short-term liquidity

Consolidated borrowings, including bonds and redeemable preferred shares, reached KRW 190.2 billion at the end of September 2026, up 34.3% from the end of 2025. The current ratio was 44.6%, with cash and equivalents of only KRW 15.4 billion.

Financing costs jumped 70.4% from a year earlier, and in September the company issued KRW 20 billion of private bonds at 6.10%. Without large-scale rollovers, concerns about cash tightness have been raised.

Subsidiary capital impairment and compensation costs

A report said that at the end of September 2026, seven of the eight consolidated subsidiaries were in full capital impairment. Compensation provisions tied to the 2025 service disruption were about KRW 5 billion.

Of a KRW 41.3 billion performance advance, KRW 9.8 billion has been set aside as a doubtful-account allowance. Additional costs could arise depending on follow-up measures with authorities.

Risk factors

Liquidity and refinancing risk

At the end of September 2026, current assets were below half of current liabilities. The company issued private bonds at 6.10% on September 30, 2026 to bolster liquidity. Operating cash flow for the first nine months was a deficit of KRW 8.8 billion. Maturity structure and refinancing conditions are key variables for financial stability.

Security incident and service disruption costs

The company is discussing follow-up measures with authorities over the service disruption caused by a ransomware attack. Additional costs or sanctions remain possible. Higher other expenses in 2025 were reported to reflect compensation for the hacking incident. How much of this cost will be recognized in periods after 2026 will be confirmed in the periodic reports.

Governance and group funding flows

The largest shareholder, the group holding company with a 50.01% stake, received a dividend. Over the same period, YES24Co borrowed KRW 25 billion from the holding company at 4.60%, and other costs including management fees rose 53.6% year on year.

The holding company's standalone short-term borrowings were reported at KRW 188 billion. Intra-group funding flows are linked to YES24Co's financial burden.
