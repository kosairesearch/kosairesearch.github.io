02

Business structure

Ace Technologies operates three business segments: wireless communications, automotive and defense. In wireless communications it makes RF components, Radio System products and base station antennas, while its automotive business supplies integrated vehicle antennas and wire harnesses.

Its defense business covers military communication antennas, satellite antennas and RF modules.

Based on second-quarter 2026 revenue, the mix was automotive antennas 43%, RF components 20%, base station antennas for repeaters and defense 13%, defense antennas 13% and Radio System products 11%, according to a financial data aggregator.

Another aggregator shows RF components at 36% as the largest item, so rankings differ by basis. Export shares vary widely by product. As of December 2025, RF components were about 95% exports, base station antennas about 92% and Radio System products nearly all exports, while automotive antennas were about 71% exports.

By contrast, about 96% of defense antenna sales were domestic. Customer names are undisclosed under confidentiality agreements, but an aggregator lists customer A at 21%, customer B at 10% and customer C at 8%, which points to meaningful concentration.

R&D spending in 2025 was KRW 15.09 billion, or 8.46% of revenue, and the company holds 335 intellectual property rights in total, including 87 registered in Korea and 58 overseas.

The new Vietnam plant has tripled production capacity for 5G base station AFUs and Radio System products, according to the company commentary on its results. A subsidiary also runs RF component and base station antenna businesses.

04

Earnings analysis

On a filed basis, revenue fell from KRW 240.4 billion in 2022 to KRW 140 billion in 2023, a 41.7% decline, and then rose to KRW 145.1 billion in 2024 (up 3.6%) and KRW 178.4 billion in 2025 (up 23.0%), growing for two years in a row.

Still, 2025 revenue remains below the KRW 240.4 billion recorded in 2022, the largest year of the four. Operating losses were KRW 20.1 billion in 2022, KRW 62.3 billion in 2023, KRW 31.1 billion in 2024 and KRW 22.2 billion in 2025, a loss in each of the four years, with 2023 the largest loss.

The operating margin was -8.3% in 2022, -44.5% in 2023, -21.5% in 2024 and -12.4% in 2025, so the loss rate widened sharply in the year revenue dropped most and narrowed after 2024.

The net loss attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 30.1 billion in 2022, KRW 78.9 billion in 2023, KRW 40.7 billion in 2024 and KRW 29.1 billion in 2025. The net loss including non-controlling interests was KRW 29.1 billion in 2025.

Operating cash flow was KRW -5.74 billion in 2022, KRW 29.7 billion in 2023, KRW -3.58 billion in 2024 and KRW -560 million in 2025, so the outflow narrowed after 2024. The debt ratio peaked at 1,102.5% in 2023, fell to 218.9% in 2024 and rose again to 260.3% in 2025.

At the end of 2025, total liabilities were KRW 183.7 billion against total equity of KRW 70.6 billion, of which KRW 70.5 billion was attributable to controlling shareholders.

On a quarterly basis, revenue moved from KRW 41.4 billion in Q4 2025 to KRW 33.2 billion in Q1 2026, a 20.0% decline, and KRW 28.7 billion in Q2 2026, a 13.4% decline, before recovering to KRW 31.1 billion in Q3 2026, up 8.3% from the prior quarter but down 17.9% from a year earlier.

Operating results went from an operating loss of KRW 4.25 billion in Q3 2025, KRW 11 billion in Q4 2025, KRW 4.08 billion in Q1 2026 and KRW 8.13 billion in Q2 2026 to an operating profit of KRW 664 million in Q3 2026.

The net loss attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 3.42 billion, KRW 9.38 billion, KRW 3.66 billion, KRW 9.53 billion and KRW 6.87 billion over the same five quarters. Over the last four quarters, the total was KRW 29.4 billion.

A financial data site attributed the Q1 2026 revenue decline mainly to delayed global 5G infrastructure investment and weaker telecom equipment demand in the RF components and base station antenna businesses.

05

Industry analysis

Ace Technologies' RF components and base station antennas are product lines sensitive to the 5G base station investment cycle. The analysis of Q1 2026 results pointed to delayed global 5G infrastructure investment and weaker telecom equipment demand as the reasons for the revenue decline.

By contrast, rising 5G subscriptions worldwide and expanding infrastructure investment in Europe and North America were presented as medium-term demand drivers.

On the defense side, the spread of drone warfare and the growth of low-earth-orbit satellite communications are cited as factors that could lift demand for military antennas.

Automotive antennas are the largest segment at 43% of second-quarter 2026 revenue, and the company is concentrating R&D on autonomous driving navigation devices and integrated 5G/V2X antennas.

In an annual comparison of 46 telecom equipment companies, Ace Technologies' operating margin of -12.4% was close to the industry average of -12.1%, while its debt ratio of 260.3% was well above the industry average of 111.9%.

The same comparison ranked its revenue 13th and its operating profit 44th, so profitability ranks low relative to scale. Among listed companies in the same electronics and components sector, KMW and Rfhic are comparable RF names.

Whether defense and satellite demand grows enough to change the profit structure will become clearer from segment profitability in the next periodic report.

Outlook

Q1 2026 revenue of KRW 33.2 billion and Q3 2026 revenue of KRW 31.1 billion both sit below the KRW 41.4 billion of Q4 2025, so whether Q4 2026 revenue exceeds that level is the next comparison point.

With production capacity at the Vietnam plant tripled, it matters whether base station antenna and Radio System volumes convert into revenue. Defense antennas were 13% of second-quarter 2026 revenue and are mostly sold domestically, so domestic defense contract disclosures will need to be checked for size.

Full-year 2026 results will be confirmed in the 2026 annual report, which must be filed by the end of March 2027. The most direct checkpoint is whether the operating profit narrows the net loss attributable to controlling shareholders.

With the debt ratio at 260.3%, continued operating losses could amplify financial strain on earnings volatility, so debt trends also warrant tracking. Medium-term demand still hinges on when telecom operators resume 5G infrastructure investment, which remains the key variable.

Bull factors

Operating profit turns positive

Operating profit was KRW 664 million in Q3 2026, after an operating loss of KRW 8.13 billion in the prior quarter, and revenue rose 8.3% from the prior quarter. Among the last five quarters on a filed basis, it is the first with a positive operating result.

Whether the profit is temporary or reflects a structural change will be clearer from segment profitability disclosures.

Defense and satellite antenna demand

The defense unit covers military communication antennas, satellite antennas and RF modules, and defense antennas were 13% of second-quarter 2026 revenue on an aggregator basis. Analysts point to growing drone warfare and low-earth-orbit satellite communications as possible drivers of military antenna demand. Because defense sales are mostly domestic, the unit may be affected by domestic defense procurement flows.

Vietnam capacity expansion

The commentary describes the Vietnam manufacturing unit as growing in importance as the production base broadens overseas. This capacity expansion may affect supply capability for 5G base station products. Annual revenue rose for a second straight year to KRW 178.4 billion in 2025.

The reason the operating profit did not carry through to the net line is a point to check in the periodic report.

Revenue declines and delayed 5G investment

Q1 2026 revenue fell 37.4% year on year, and Q3 2026 revenue fell 17.9%. Delayed global 5G infrastructure investment and weaker telecom equipment demand in the RF components and base station antenna businesses were presented as the background. Annual revenue rose in 2025 but remains below the KRW 240.4 billion of 2022.

Financial strain

Total liabilities at the end of 2025 were KRW 183.7 billion, above total equity of KRW 70.6 billion. The debt ratio rose to 260.3% from 218.9% in 2024, and reached 1,102.5% in 2023. An aggregator's annual comparison shows it well above the telecom equipment industry average of 111.9%.

Risk factors

Customer concentration

Customer names are undisclosed, but an aggregator lists customer A at 21%, customer B at 10% and customer C at 8%. Order adjustments by major customers could move quarterly revenue directly. Which customer's volumes drove each quarter's decline cannot be determined from the filings alone.

Balance sheet

At the end of 2025, total liabilities were KRW 183.7 billion against total equity of KRW 70.6 billion, and the debt ratio was 260.3%. The ratio reached 1,102.5% in 2023, so financial headroom is limited. Continued operating losses could raise borrowing pressure and amplify earnings volatility.

Currency and export exposure

As of December 2025, RF components were about 95% exports, base station antennas about 92% and automotive antennas about 71% (aggregator basis). Currency movements and the timing of overseas customer orders can affect profit and loss.

One analysis raised the possibility that segment mix shifts or exchange rates contributed to the sharp profit drop in Q2 2026.

Overall view

Ace Technologies makes RF components and antennas for wireless communications, automotive and defense uses. Revenue rose for two straight years to KRW 178.4 billion in 2025, but operating losses of KRW 22.2 billion marked a fourth straight year of losses.

The company swung to an operating profit of KRW 664 million in Q3 2026, but the net loss attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 6.87 billion continued, and the last four quarters total KRW 29.4 billion.

Bullish factors are the Q3 operating profit, demand for defense and satellite antennas, and the expanded Vietnam capacity. Bearish factors are five consecutive quarters of net losses, revenue declines in Q1 and Q3 2026, a debt ratio of 260.3% and customer concentration.

With these factors pulling in opposite directions, the direction is hard to call. The valuation sits at a premium to net assets, and no earnings-based multiple can be calculated. The next checkpoints are the 2026 annual report, due by the end of March 2027, and the Q1 2027 quarterly report.
