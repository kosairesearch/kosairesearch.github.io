PS Tec: Metering Core Recovers, Quarterly Earnings Swing Widens

PS Tec moved from an operating loss in 2022 to a larger operating profit in 2025, and sales and operating profit both rose in Q2 and Q3 2026, but a controlling-shareholder net loss in Q3 2026 leaves quarterly net earnings volatility as the key open issue.

  1. 1

    FY2025 revenue was KRW 97.2 billion, the largest year in 2022–2025, and operating profit was KRW 5.28 billion, up 54.0% year on year.

  2. 2

  3. 3

    Q3 2026 brought a controlling-shareholder net loss of KRW 4.81 billion, compared with a KRW 8.77 billion profit in Q2, so operating results and net earnings moved in opposite directions.

  4. 4

02

Business structure

PS Tec is a specialist maker of metering and measuring instruments, founded in 1957 and listed on KOSDAQ in 1990. Its core business is electricity meters, supplied mainly to KEPCO along with remote meter-reading systems.

Based on the separate financial statements for 2025, electric power equipment (EM) products generated KRW 62.3 billion of KRW 96.5 billion in sales, about 64.5%, up 28.6% year on year.

Merchandise sales were KRW 10.9 billion, down 11.3%, and the company said the mix has shifted toward power equipment and renewables as low-margin distribution declined. WiseReport puts the company's market share in its power equipment segment (AMR) at 50%.

KEPCO is the main customer, and the company cited higher KEPCO delivery volumes and stable raw material prices as drivers of 2025 sales growth.

The company is expanding into renewable energy and EV charging infrastructure, with power generation handled by two unlisted subsidiaries, one in solar power generation and one in fuel cell power generation. In 2026 it is pushing smart meter deliveries to KEPCO, eco-friendly switchgear, and overseas markets.

The company also cites expanded public housing supply led by LH as a medium-term opportunity. Heavy dependence on a few public-sector customers means changes in procurement timing feed directly into when revenue is recognized.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual results show a turnaround from an operating loss and net loss to profit, followed by growing earnings.

Revenue fell from KRW 710 hundred million in 2022 to KRW 696 hundred million in 2023 (down 1.9% year on year), then rose to KRW 806 hundred million in 2024 (up 15.7% year on year) and KRW 972 hundred million in 2025 (up 20.6% year on year).

After the 2023 low, revenue rose for two consecutive years from 2024 to 2025, and 2025 was the highest year in 2022-2025.

Operating profit swung from an operating loss of KRW 18.4 hundred million in 2022 to an operating profit of KRW 8.5 hundred million in 2023 (a turnaround to profit year on year), then increased to KRW 34.3 hundred million in 2024 (up 302.8% year on year) and KRW 52.8 hundred million in 2025 (up 54.0% year on year).

The operating margin rose from -2.6% in 2022 to 1.2% in 2023, 4.3% in 2024 and 5.4% in 2025. The company cited higher revenue in the renewable energy and AMI segments, and higher non-operating income from fair value gains on financial investment assets, as the drivers of its 2025 results.

Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders rose from a net loss of KRW 46.3 hundred million in 2022 to KRW 91.3 hundred million in 2025 (up 62.1% year on year), and in 2025 net profit for the period was also the same KRW 91.3 hundred million.

Looking at the quarterly trend, revenue went from KRW 227 hundred million in the third quarter of 2025 to KRW 187 hundred million in the fourth quarter (down 17.5% quarter on quarter) and KRW 176 hundred million in the first quarter of 2026 (down 6.1% quarter on quarter), then reached KRW 233 hundred million in the second quarter (up 32.7% quarter on quarter) and KRW 255 hundred million in the third quarter (up 9.5% quarter on quarter).

After the first-quarter 2026 low, revenue rose for two consecutive quarters from the second quarter of 2026 to the third quarter of 2026. The company explained that the first-quarter revenue decline was due to the simultaneous delay of renewable energy revenue recognition timing and meter delivery schedules.

Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders rose to KRW 69.5 hundred million in the first quarter and KRW 87.7 hundred million in the second quarter, but the company explained that this was affected by valuation gains on financial instruments and dividend and disposal gains.

In the third quarter, however, it recorded a net loss attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 48.1 hundred million despite an operating profit of KRW 12.6 hundred million (up 96.0% from the same period a year earlier), so the sum of net profit attributable to controlling shareholders over the latest four quarters comes to KRW 135 hundred million.

05

Industry analysis

In the electric power equipment market, PS Tec is a public-sector procurement supplier centered on electricity meters. WiseReport puts its market share in the AMR power equipment segment at 50%. KEPCO is the main customer, and the company supplies remote meter-reading systems on the back of KEPCO deliveries.

The company said its smart meter awards from KEPCO and its signed solar project backlog support deferred revenue. It also said a large renewable energy project is nearing completion, yet the timing of that project's revenue recognition was cited as a cause of the first-half 2026 sales decline.

A slowdown in private construction was another factor cited for lower first-half sales. The company identified higher raw material prices and a strong foreign exchange rate as profitability pressures, and it is pursuing cost reduction and margin improvement.

Expanded public housing supply was described as a medium-term opportunity. Its relatively small sales base leaves it directly exposed to changes in policy and procurement.

06

Outlook

In its first-half 2026 release, the company said deferred renewable energy revenue would be recognized in sequence from the second half. The same release said a large renewable energy project is nearing completion and that smart meter and eco-friendly switchgear deliveries would add more revenue from Q3.

Q3 sales rose 9.5% quarter on quarter to KRW 25.5 billion, but a controlling-shareholder net loss in the same quarter leaves open whether the sales gains carried through to net earnings. The company said it is pursuing cost reduction and margin improvement to offset raw material and exchange rate pressures.

Shareholder returns are structured around a minimum per-share dividend standard, including an interim dividend, for 2026–2028. About 75%, or 852,750 shares, of the 1,137,000 treasury shares planned for cancellation had been cancelled as of August 2026, and the remainder is to be cancelled in sequence.

The company said it will pursue overseas expansion and new business development alongside its core operations. Expanded public housing supply was presented as a medium-term opportunity.

The next checkpoints are whether deferred revenue shows up in the 2026 annual results and annual report, and the composition of the Q3 net loss.

08

Bull factors

Core meter business and KEPCO backlog

Electric power equipment (EM) sales were KRW 62.3 billion in 2025 on a separate-financials basis, about 64.5% of the total. KEPCO smart meter awards and a signed solar project backlog are the basis for the company's view that deferred revenue will be recognized in sequence. Sales rose 32.7% and 9.5% quarter on quarter in Q2 and Q3 2026, respectively.

Operating profit recovery

Operating profit rose 54.0% to KRW 5.28 billion in 2025, and the operating margin improved from -2.6% in 2022 to 5.4% in 2025. Q3 2026 operating profit of KRW 1.26 billion was up 96.0% year on year. Q2 2026 operating profit of KRW 1.45 billion was the largest of the five quarters on record in the filings.

Shareholder returns and financial capacity

The company set a minimum dividend standard for 2026–2028, including an interim dividend, and said in August 2026 that about 75% of 1,137,000 treasury shares had been cancelled. Its debt ratio was 28.1% at end-2025, and media reported cash and equivalents of KRW 43.6 billion at year-end 2025. Operating cash flow was KRW 19.1 billion in 2025.

Bear factors

Q3 net loss swing

Controlling-shareholder net income swung to a KRW 4.81 billion loss in Q3 2026 from a KRW 8.77 billion profit in Q2. Operating profit rose 96.0% year on year in the same quarter, yet net earnings moved the other way.

The company cited financial product gains as a driver of Q1 and Q2 net income, and the composition of Q3 earnings is a point to check in the next periodic report.

First-half sales decline and margin pressure

Consolidated first-half 2026 sales were KRW 40.9 billion, down 26.6% year on year, according to a Newsis report dated 14 August 2026. The company pointed to higher raw material prices and a strong exchange rate as profitability pressures. With a 5.4% operating margin in 2025, the thin margin leaves the business sensitive to cost swings.

Procurement dependence and revenue timing

The company cited a slowdown in private construction as a cause of the first-half sales decline. Heavy reliance on KEPCO and public procurement means delayed orders can delay revenue recognition. In Q1 2026, delays in renewable energy revenue timing and meter delivery schedules together reduced sales.

Risk factors

Policy and procurement dependence

Heavy reliance on KEPCO deliveries and public procurement means changes in policy or order timing can move quarterly sales directly. The company attributed the Q1 2026 sales decline to delivery delays. The company also said award volumes and the order backlog should absorb the deferred revenue.

Costs and exchange rates

The company named higher raw material prices and a strong exchange rate as profitability pressures and said it is pursuing cost reduction. The 2025 operating margin of 5.4% is thin, so cost burdens could easily reduce profit. How cost savings show up in quarterly margins is the next point to check.

Financial asset gains and share price volatility

When net income relies on gains and losses from financial products, net results can reverse regardless of operating performance, as the Q3 2026 net loss showed. Media have noted the company's earlier classification as a political theme stock, a history of sharp share price swings.

Overall view

PS Tec moved from an operating loss in 2022 to an operating profit of KRW 5.28 billion in 2025, and its revenue of KRW 97.2 billion that year was the largest in 2022–2025.

After the Q1 2026 low, sales rose in both Q2 and Q3, and operating profit rose 96.0% year on year in Q3, which the filings confirm as a recovery in the core business.

However, the Q3 controlling-shareholder net loss of KRW 4.81 billion moved against operating results, so how far net earnings depend on non-operating items such as financial product gains is the central issue.

The company expects deferred renewable energy revenue and smart meter deliveries to be recognized from the second half, but that is the company's own outlook and will be checked in the next periodic reports.

On shareholder returns, a minimum dividend standard for 2026–2028 and treasury share cancellations are in progress. The price-to-book ratio is below 1x. Bullish factors are the core business recovery and shareholder returns, while bearish factors are earnings volatility and dependence on procurement and costs.
