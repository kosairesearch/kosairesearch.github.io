02

Business structure

Nex-I is an immuno-oncology drug developer founded in 2021. The company focuses on antibody therapeutics for patients who do not respond to existing immuno-oncology drugs or who develop resistance during treatment. It holds an in-house drug discovery platform called NEXORA, which underpins its candidate sourcing.

Its flagship achievement is the 2024 licensing of its immuno-oncology candidate NXI-101 to Ono Pharmaceutical of Japan. The follow-on asset, NXI-201, is slated for domestic and overseas Phase 1 and 2 trials funded by IPO proceeds. Separately, development of ADC and bispecific antibody candidates is under way.

Revenue depends largely on partnership outcomes such as upfront license fees and milestone payments. R&D spending is the main driver of profit and loss, which differs from a product-sales model.

Other companies in the same miscellaneous sector, spanning paper, education and equipment, have business profiles too different to serve as direct comparables.

The antibody field for overcoming immuno-oncology resistance is contested by global pharmaceutical firms and biotech companies, so clinical data and licensing terms will be the differentiators.

03

Recent trends

Nex-I ran its retail subscription on November 3 and 4, 2026, with a competition rate of 13.26 to 1. Institutional demand had reached only 66.58 to 1 in the earlier bookbuilding. As a result, the offer price was set at KRW 8,000, 23.8% below the bottom of the KRW 10,500 to 13,000 indicative range.

The company offered 5 million shares and raised KRW 40 billion, with Korea Investment & Securities as lead underwriter. On the listing day of November 12, 2026, the stock opened at KRW 7,620 and peaked intraday at KRW 7,900, below the offer price.

It then fell to KRW 5,610 during the morning session and closed at KRW 4,810. That close was 39.88% below the KRW 8,000 reference price, and market capitalization stood at KRW 220.5 billion. JKN reported that no specific disclosure or management issue was confirmed to explain the decline.

Medicopharma reported heavy early selling and additional tradable shares as pressure on the stock. On a standalone basis for 2025, reported operating revenue was KRW 5.11 billion and the operating loss was KRW 16.524 billion.

Outlook

Whether the ADC and bispecific antibody candidates advance to partnership stages will also serve as an indicator of pipeline expansion. The pace at which the operating loss narrows depends on the scale of R&D spending and the timing of contract revenue.

Because drug development takes a long time to reach commercialization, the timing of profitability depends on partnership timing. Volatility in supply and demand may remain elevated while early tradable shares are absorbed by the market.

Given the low institutional subscription rate, institutional holding trends after the listing will remain a monitoring point. Room for improvement on operating metrics can be framed around a narrower loss, follow-on licensing deals, and clinical progress.

For an R&D-focused company, securing funding right after listing provides the financial base to sustain clinical programs. Korea Investment & Securities acted as lead underwriter.

Drug development takes a long time to reach commercialization, so the timing of a return to profit is hard to pin down. Securing profitability is cited as a remaining challenge and a bearish factor.

Weak demand and a lowered offer price

Institutional bookbuilding demand reached 66.58 to 1 and retail subscription 13.26 to 1. As a result, the offer price was set at KRW 8,000, 23.8% below the bottom of the indicative range. Weak institutional demand at the offer stage is linked to a view that the post-listing shareholder base is thin. The first-day close far below the offer price also reads in that context.

Early float overhang

A first-day decline of 39.88% from the offer price shows the scale of the supply pressure. Whether a technical support level has formed is still unclear.

Risk factors

Clinical and regulatory

The results and timing of the NXI-201 Phase 1 and 2 trials will shape the value of the pipeline. Candidates can be halted at clinical stages because of efficacy or safety issues. Regulatory approval and procedural timelines are also variables. The signing of definitive licensing agreements may also be delayed alongside clinical progress.

Macro and financing

Biotech companies need large sums at each clinical stage, so they are exposed to financial market conditions. If interest rates and sentiment toward the biotech sector weaken, the terms of additional fundraising could deteriorate.

The KRW 40 billion raised in the IPO supports the clinical schedule, but whether it is enough for long-term development costs depends on clinical results.

Supply and volatility

As the first-day decline showed, the share price is highly sensitive to float and supply changes. With a short trading history since listing, price swings can be large. It is difficult to rule out a period in which short-term supply dynamics keep driving the share price.
