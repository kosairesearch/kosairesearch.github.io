02

Business structure

Nex-I is an immuno-oncology drug developer founded in 2021 that focuses on identifying why cancers fail to respond to existing immunotherapy and developing treatments that target those causes.

Its proprietary NEXORA platform is used to discover new targets and build candidate antibodies, which are then licensed or co-developed at the preclinical or early clinical stage. The pipeline comprises four antibody candidates and three antibody-drug conjugate (ADC) candidates, seven in total.

The lead asset NXI-101 was licensed to Japan's Ono Pharmaceutical in 2024 and targets non-small cell lung cancer. The follow-on NXI-201, aimed at colorectal cancer, has IND approval in both Korea and the United States.

The ADC candidates include NXI-S301, NXI-S160 and NXI-S302, and the bispecific antibody NXI-M501 is also in development. Revenue comes entirely from technology-transfer income, and there is no product sales line, so the business is a single segment.

Its closest comparison set among listed antibody and ADC developers includes LigaChem Biosciences and ABL Bio, which also rely on pipeline-based licensing models. With no commercial product yet, operating income depends on deal events.

Recent trends

According to the DART periodic report, cumulative third-quarter 2026 revenue was KRW 0 million, operating loss was KRW 3.05 billion and net loss was KRW 2.28 billion. Both loss measures remained negative compared with the same period of the prior year, though the losses narrowed.

The pattern, with no revenue and R&D spending driving losses, is typical of a clinical-stage drug developer. On an unconsolidated basis, the company reported operating revenue of KRW 5.11 billion and an operating loss of KRW 16.524 billion in 2025.

The balance sheet improved, with total equity turning positive to KRW 103.456 billion at the end of June 2026 from a deficit at the end of 2025, and borrowings falling to 1.5 percent of assets at mid-2026.

The IPO priced at 8,000 won, below the bottom of the indicative range, raising KRW 40 billion through 5 million new shares, and retail subscriptions drew a 13.26-to-1 competition rate. On the first trading day on November 12, 2026, shares fell 26 percent intraday to 5,920 won.

The current price of 4,050 won is down 2.41 percent on the day and roughly 49 percent below the offering price. According to a filing reported by the media, CEO Yoon Kyung-wan held 5.671 million shares, or 12.37 percent, as of November 19, 2026.

Outlook

The company plans to use KRW 50.98 billion of net IPO proceeds for R&D and working capital and to advance Phase 1/2 trials of NXI-201. Its estimated funding need for 2027–2029 is KRW 127.785 billion, which the IPO proceeds alone will not cover.

According to projections in the securities registration statement, the company expects revenue of KRW 8.6 billion and an operating loss of KRW 36.9 billion in 2027 before turning to operating profit in 2028.

That outlook depends on about KRW 45 billion in upfront payments from a NXI-201 licensing deal, so timing and terms of such a deal would materially change the path. The CEO has set a target of one to two licensing deals per year through 2030.

Maintaining the technology-evaluation grades (A and BBB) that supported the special listing route will depend on accumulating clinical data. The next checkpoints are the timing of NXI-201 entry into Phase 1/2 trials and whether any additional licensing deal is signed.

Progress of the ADC pipeline and the bispecific NXI-M501 should be tracked alongside. The company plans to finish process development and toxicology for NXI-401 by the first half of 2029 and enter Phase 1 in the second half. These timelines are company plans and may differ from actual progress.

Bull factors

Platform validated by a licensing deal

Nex-I has recognized total revenue of KRW 22.2 billion from Ono on NXI-101, showing that the licensing deal produced actual income. The result indicates that the target-discovery platform has been assessed by an external pharmaceutical company. Follow-on candidates come from the same platform and serve as the basis for further licensing discussions.

Diversified pipeline

The company holds seven pipeline candidates, four antibodies and three ADCs. The lead ADC candidate NXI-S301 showed sustained tumor shrinkage from a single low dose in a colorectal cancer animal model.

Because indications and modalities are split, the company does not depend entirely on the outcome of any single candidate.

Stronger capital structure and IPO funding

Total equity turned positive at the end of June 2026, and convertible preferred shares were converted into common stock, which strengthened the balance sheet. The listing brought in KRW 40 billion of IPO proceeds to fund clinical programs. Debt ratios remain low, which limits near-term borrowing pressure.

Bear factors

No revenue and continuing operating losses

Per the DART filing, cumulative third-quarter 2026 revenue was KRW 0 million and operating loss was KRW 3.05 billion. Losses narrowed from the prior year, but with no revenue base, R&D spending drives the bottom line. The company's own projections show operating losses continuing into 2027, so the path to profitability is long.

Trading below the offering price

The offering price was set below the bottom of the indicative range, and the lead underwriter said it reflected recent stock-market volatility and domestic and overseas uncertainty. On the first day, shares traded 26 percent below the offering price intraday.

The current price of 4,050 won sits well below the offering price, which suggests the market is pricing in limited near-term value from clinical and licensing prospects.

Revenue dependent on deal timing

Upfront payments are recognized when a contract is signed, and milestones when development stages are reached, so quarterly results swing widely. The company's 2028 operating-profit target also assumes a NXI-201 licensing deal goes through. A delay or weaker terms would push back the entire earnings plan.

Risk factors

Macro

KOSDAQ biotech names are sensitive to interest rates and shifts in risk appetite. Wider market volatility after a listing directly affects how newly listed biotech shares are valued. Changes in the policy and funding environment could also shift the timing of clinical investment plans.

Clinical and regulatory

NXI-201 is approaching Phase 1/2 trials, so safety and efficacy data must be generated. Disappointing clinical results could lower both licensing terms and the value of the pipeline. Immuno-oncology is crowded, so the company must prove its differentiation in the clinic.

Funding and dilution

The company plans to cover the part of its estimated KRW 127.785 billion 2027–2029 funding need that IPO proceeds do not meet through liquid assets and future licensing income. If licensing is delayed, additional fundraising may be needed, which could dilute existing shareholders.

Overall view

Nex-I is a clinical-stage immuno-oncology developer that is broadening its financial base through licensing after listing. Based on the DART filing, cumulative third-quarter 2026 revenue was KRW 0 million, operating loss was KRW 3.05 billion and net loss was KRW 2.28 billion.
