Funding Costs Weigh on Profit, Dividends Stay in Focus

Samsung Card's transaction volume rose 9.1% in the third quarter of 2026, yet higher financing costs and credit-loss expenses cut net profit attributable to controlling shareholders by 13.5% from a year earlier, keeping dividend-led shareholder returns at the center of the discussion.

02

Business structure

Samsung Card operates mainly through credit sales (lump-sum and installment), a financing segment that includes long- and short-term card loans, and installment leasing.

Total transaction volume in the third quarter of 2026 was KRW 49.79 trillion, up 9.1% from a year earlier, and card business volume was KRW 49.55 trillion, up 8.9%.

By segment, credit sales came to KRW 45.02 trillion, the financing segment to KRW 4.52 trillion and installment leasing to KRW 246.2 billion, according to the October 27, 2026 release.

Credit-sales revenue in the same quarter rose 9.1% to KRW 689.2 billion, and personal credit-sales spending grew 8.5% to KRW 39 trillion, based on a Heungkuk Securities report. Card-loan revenue stayed flat at the prior-year level because of household-lending aggregate caps, while cash-advance revenue rose 23.8%.

The company attributed the volume growth to expanded partnerships with high-quality affiliates and to rising membership and spending per member. A personal business loan launched in June 2026 carries an average rate of around 14%, and analysts expect its profit contribution to grow as balances increase.

In personal credit-sales share, the gap with the leading rival narrowed from 0.8 percentage points at the end of 2025 to 0.16 points over January to August 2026, according to press reports.

Because the asset base is expanding with borrowed funds, funding costs and credit losses will largely determine how much of the volume growth reaches profit.

04

Earnings analysis

On annual DART filings, revenue was KRW 3.79 trillion in 2022, KRW 4 trillion in 2023 (up 5.5%) and KRW 4.38 trillion in 2024 (up 9.5%).

Operating profit fell from KRW 848.9 billion in 2022 to KRW 810 billion in 2023 (down 4.6%), recovered to KRW 885.4 billion in 2024 (up 9.3%) and was KRW 853.7 billion in 2025 (down 3.6%). Within 2022 to 2025, 2024 was the largest year and 2023 the smallest.

Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 622.3 billion in 2022, KRW 609.4 billion in 2023 (down 2.1%), KRW 664.6 billion in 2024 (up 9.1%) and KRW 645.9 billion in 2025 (down 2.8%), and total net profit including non-controlling interests was reported at the same yearly amounts.

The operating margin fell from 22.4% in 2022 to 20.2% in both 2023 and 2024. The debt-to-equity ratio was 275.5% in 2022, 254.1% in 2023, 247.6% in 2024 and 263.0% in 2025. At the end of 2025, total equity was KRW 8.86 trillion, all attributable to controlling shareholders, and total liabilities were KRW 23.3 trillion.

Operating cash flow was -KRW 1.25 trillion in 2022, KRW 1.34 trillion in 2023, -KRW 837.7 billion in 2024 and -KRW 1.58 trillion in 2025, changing direction from year to year.

Quarterly operating profit moved from KRW 213.6 billion in Q3 2025 to KRW 194.6 billion in Q4 2025 (down 8.9% from the prior quarter), then peaked at KRW 210 billion in Q1 2026 (up 7.9%).

It then slipped to KRW 208.6 billion in Q2 2026 (down 0.7%) and KRW 187.1 billion in Q3 2026 (down 10.3% from the prior quarter and 12.4% from a year earlier), a two-quarter decline.

Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders ran KRW 161.7 billion in Q3 2025, KRW 148.6 billion in Q4 2025 (down 8.1%), KRW 156.3 billion in Q1 2026 (up 5.2%), KRW 154.2 billion in Q2 2026 and KRW 139.8 billion in Q3 2026 (down 13.5% from a year earlier); the last four quarters totaled KRW 598.9 billion.

In Q3 2026, financing costs rose 28.8% to KRW 196.8 billion and credit-loss expenses rose 15.4% to KRW 223.0 billion. The company pointed to larger borrowings, higher funding rates and more debt restructuring, and the new-borrowing rate reached 3.90%, up 0.23 percentage points from the prior quarter.

05

Industry analysis

Card companies fund asset growth with borrowed money, so changes in market rates pass into costs quickly. The average funding rate reached 3.34% in the third quarter of 2026, up 0.24 percentage points from the prior quarter, and financing costs rose 28.8% alongside a larger debt balance.

Household-lending aggregate caps held card-loan revenue at the prior-year level, and a 23.8% rise in cash-advance revenue partly filled that gap.

When rates rise, borrowers with weaker repayment capacity increase credit losses, and debt restructuring was cited as the main driver of the 15.4% rise in credit-loss expenses in the third quarter. On demand, transaction volume rose 9.1%, which points to sustained payment activity.

On competition, the gap in personal credit-sales share with the leading rival narrowed from 0.8 percentage points at the end of 2025 to 0.16 points over January to August 2026, so the race for volume has intensified.

Samsung Card presents expanded partnerships with high-quality affiliates as the source of its product competitiveness. High-rate products such as the personal business loan can contribute to profitability, but they also bring added credit-management demands.

06

Outlook

In its October 27, 2026 release, the company said financing-cost pressure would continue into the fourth quarter of 2026, leaving the card operating environment difficult. Heungkuk Securities expected the burden from credit losses to persist for some time.

Analysts expect the personal business loan to add more to profit as its balance grows, but the balance size and credit-loss trend have not yet been confirmed in regular filings. On shareholder returns, the company has indicated a medium-term goal of raising the shareholder return ratio to around 50%.

Press reports from April 2026 noted, however, that concrete plans and timing for buybacks and cancellations beyond dividends had not been presented. The fiscal 2025 dividend, about KRW 298.8 billion, was approved at the shareholders' meeting on March 14, 2026.

Bonds and long-term commercial paper outstanding reached KRW 17.02 trillion, up KRW 1.63 trillion from year-end 2025, so if funding rates keep rising, cost pass-through will continue. The next regular filing is the 2026 annual report, and preliminary fourth-quarter 2026 results are likely to come before it.

The points to watch are whether fourth-quarter financing costs and credit losses stabilize near third-quarter levels and whether the move toward a 50% payout ratio becomes concrete.

08

Bull factors

Dividend-centred shareholder returns

Total dividends for fiscal 2025 were about KRW 298.8 billion, with a 46.3% payout ratio. The company has pointed to a medium-term shareholder return ratio of around 50%. Analysts cite the steady dividend in a year of lower net profit as evidence of fundamental resilience. Concrete timing and methods have not yet been disclosed.

Slight improvement in delinquency

The one-month-plus delinquency ratio at the end of September was 0.87%, down 0.02 percentage points from the prior quarter-end. On the Financial Supervisory Service basis, the ratio was 0.95%, down from 0.97%. An improving asset-quality trend can slow the build-up of credit-loss expenses.

Expected profit contribution from new lending

The personal business loan launched in June 2026 with an average rate of around 14%. Heungkuk Securities expects its profit contribution to grow as balances rise, given that the rate is above the company-wide average asset yield. Cash-advance revenue growth of 23.8% is also cited as a positive for volume.

09

Bear factors

Rising funding costs

Borrowings reached KRW 23.14 trillion at the end of September, up KRW 2.73 trillion from year-end 2025. The new-borrowing rate rose to 3.90% in the third quarter, and the overall funding rate is 3.34%. Higher funding rates are offsetting part of the revenue gained from a larger asset base.

Credit-loss expenses and recovery pressure

Credit-loss expenses were KRW 223.0 billion in the third quarter, up 15.4%. Heungkuk Securities pointed to weaker repayment capacity among vulnerable borrowers and more debt restructuring, with recovery rates continuing to fall. Even with lower delinquency, falling recovery rates keep the credit-loss burden in place.

Declining profit and no direct price support

Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders has declined in each of the second and third quarters of 2026 after KRW 156.3 billion in the first quarter. Analysts flagged the lack of direct share-price support, such as share cancellation, as a limit on valuation expansion.

Some observers note that a shareholder-return direction without an execution plan gives a weaker basis for re-rating.

10

Risk factors

Rate and funding risk

If funding rates rise further, financing costs could again absorb the growth in operating revenue. In the third quarter of 2026, revenue grew, but the 28.8% rise in financing costs exceeded that gain. Because the model relies heavily on borrowing, the direction of rates feeds into earnings quickly.

Credit and loss risk

Debt restructuring pushed up credit-loss expenses, and repayment capacity among vulnerable borrowers weakens when rates rise. The delinquency ratio improved to 0.87%, but analysts point to falling recovery rates, so credit-loss management is the next item to watch. As card volume grows, exposure to credit losses grows with it.

Regulatory and cash-flow risk

Household-lending aggregate caps have held card-loan revenue at the prior-year level. Operating cash flow was -KRW 837.7 billion in 2024 and -KRW 1.58 trillion in 2025, negative in both years. Changes in regulatory intensity or funding flows could affect a profit structure that leans on lending.

12

Overall view

Samsung Card's third quarter of 2026 showed volume and profit moving in opposite directions. Total transaction volume rose 9.1%, but financing costs rose 28.8% and credit-loss expenses rose 15.4%, pushing net profit attributable to controlling shareholders down 13.5% to KRW 139.8 billion.

On the filed figures, operating profit has fallen for two quarters since peaking at KRW 210 billion in the first quarter of 2026, and the last four quarters of net profit attributable to controlling shareholders totaled KRW 598.9 billion.

Annual operating profit ranged between KRW 810 billion and KRW 885.4 billion over 2022 to 2025, and 2025 net profit attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 645.9 billion.

The debt-to-equity ratio rose to 263.0% at the end of 2025 from 247.6% in 2024, and borrowings and funding rates are the main cost variables. On the other side, the delinquency ratio improved to 0.87%, and the KRW 298.8 billion dividend and the medium-term 50% return goal support the dividend-centred case.

Analysts see dividend appeal as the main driver of the share price while noting the lack of direct share-cancellation measures as a constraint. The next checkpoints are the preliminary fourth-quarter 2026 results, the 2026 annual report, and whether the 50% payout direction becomes concrete.
