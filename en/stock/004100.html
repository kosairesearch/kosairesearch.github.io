Operating Profit Recovers, but Q3 2026 Turns to Controlling-Shareholder Loss

02

Business structure

Taeyang Metal is an auto parts manufacturer whose main products are cold-forged items for automobiles. Fasteners such as high-strength bolts, nuts, and washers are its core products, and screws for electrical and electronic components and bicycle parts are also listed as major products in its filings.

In 2024, its sales mix was 97.11% cold-forged products and 2.89% wire rods for cold forging and assembled washers. Because the company's entire revenue is concentrated in cold forging, a change in product mix translates directly into a change in earnings. Exports also account for a large share.

In 2024, 71.60% of cold-forged product sales were exports. Its customers are centered on Hyundai Motor and Kia, and it is said to have held the No. 1 position in the industry for a long time on the strength of its business relationships with the two companies.

Past press reports noted that depending on the two companies for 70-80% of sales amplified earnings swings when vehicle sales slowed. Its market position is a 34.98% share of automotive cold-forged products among automakers (as of March 2026).

One report also said that its share of sales among the four domestic bolt suppliers once exceeded 40% but has fallen to 36.3%. Its raw materials are steel materials such as wire rods, and its cost-of-sales ratio generally ranged in the high 80s percent, from 87% to 89%, between 2021 and 2024.

A feature of the filed figures is that, because the cost share is high, the operating margin stays between the mid-2% and mid-3% range.

04

Earnings analysis

On a DART-filed basis, revenue was KRW 569.5 billion in 2022, KRW 617.2 billion in 2023 (up 8.4% year on year), KRW 620 billion in 2024 (up 0.5%), and KRW 620.2 billion in 2025 (flat year on year). The largest year in the 2022–2025 period was 2025, and the smallest was 2022.

Revenue was nearly identical in 2024 and 2025, yet operating profit moved from KRW 16.1 billion (down 27.6%) to KRW 22.4 billion (up 39.7%). The operating margin was 2.8% in 2022, 3.6% in 2023, 2.6% in 2024 and 3.6% in 2025, so margin differences drove profit swings in years when sales were flat.

Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders moved from a loss of KRW 626 million in 2022 to a profit of KRW 65.43 million in 2023, a turnaround to profit, then rose to KRW 2.45 billion in 2024 (up 3,648.3%) and KRW 3.49 billion in 2025 (up 42.2%), increasing for two consecutive years.

Net income including non-controlling interests was KRW 3.05 billion in 2025, below the controlling-shareholder figure, which suggests non-controlling interests were a drag.

By quarter, Q3 2025 revenue was KRW 153.7 billion with operating profit of KRW 2.5 billion, and Q4 2025 revenue rose to KRW 155 billion with operating profit of KRW 11.1 billion, up 344.6% from the prior quarter.

Operating profit then printed KRW 6.13 billion in Q1 2026, KRW 3.89 billion in Q2 and KRW 6.82 billion in Q3. Q3 2026 revenue of KRW 154.1 billion was up only 0.2% year on year.

In the same quarter, the controlling-shareholder result swung to a net loss of KRW 5.36 billion even though operating profit was positive; the split between non-operating items and tax effects should be checked in the quarterly report.

Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders over the last four quarters (Q4 2025 to Q3 2026) totaled KRW 11.6 billion. For Q1 2026, one analysis reported revenue down 2.2%, operating profit down 19.1% and net income up 11.4% year on year.

The same analysis attributed the sales decline to higher oil prices from the Middle East conflict and lower vehicle output after a domestic parts-maker fire.

Industry analysis

The main end market is vehicle production at Hyundai Motor and KIA. A Q1 2026 analysis attributed lower output to higher oil prices tied to the Middle East conflict and a domestic parts-maker fire. A July 2026 report noted that more than 70% of the 50 domestic automakers saw their market capitalization decline.

An April 2026 report said operating profit at the same group of 50 automakers fell 30% in 2025. Because supplier orders follow vehicle output, the company's order flow tracks production data directly.

Competition is among four domestic bolt suppliers, and the company's position as the sales leader rests on long-standing transactions with Hyundai Motor and KIA. Overseas sales were 71.60% of cold-forged product revenue in 2024, so foreign vehicle demand and exchange rates also matter.

A November 2026 report said US consumer prices rose 0.4% in October and core inflation has risen 0.3% for three straight months. The same report described a firm dollar and rising rate expectations. A weaker won has produced foreign-currency translation gains, as a Q1 2026 analysis noted.

Higher raw material and oil costs would add pressure to cost of sales, so the sector faces demand and cost pressure at the same time. The cycle does not yet show a clear recovery in vehicle output, and FY2024 and FY2025 sales were flat with margin improvement doing most of the work.

Bull factors

Position in domestic cold-forged bolts

The company holds a 34.98% share of cold-forged automotive parts sold to vehicle makers (as of March 2026). One source describes it as the largest domestic cold-forged bolt specialist. If that position holds, a recovery in vehicle output would flow directly into volume.

2025 operating profit recovery and margin gain

Operating profit in 2025 was KRW 22.4 billion, up 39.7%, and the margin rose to 3.6% from 2.6% in 2024. Profit grew even though revenue was flat at KRW 620.2 billion, showing that margin gains did the work. Q4 2025 operating profit of KRW 11.1 billion was well above KRW 2.5 billion in Q3 2025.

Possible foreign-currency translation gains

Overseas sales are significant, so a higher exchange rate can produce foreign-currency translation gains. A Q1 2026 analysis said higher exchange rates lifted net income through such gains. A firm dollar and elevated US rates could keep this item supportive.

Bear factors

Q3 2026 loss attributable to controlling shareholders

The controlling-shareholder result in Q3 2026 was a net loss of KRW 5.36 billion, after a KRW 5.48 billion profit in Q2. Operating profit of KRW 6.82 billion was positive, so the gap points to non-operating burdens.

Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders over the last four quarters totaled KRW 11.6 billion, with wide swings from quarter to quarter.

High debt burden and volatile operating cash flow

Total liabilities at the end of 2025 were KRW 443 billion, more than double the KRW 166.3 billion of total equity. The debt ratio fell to 266.4% in 2025 from 378.8% in 2024 but remains high. Operating cash flow turned from KRW 6.6 billion in 2024 to -KRW 7.47 billion in 2025, diverging from operating profit.

Vehicle output slowdown and customer concentration

Q1 2026 revenue fell 2.2% year on year, and lower vehicle output was cited as a reason. A past report noted that 70–80% of revenue depended on Hyundai Motor and KIA. Lower customer output can weigh on volume and utilization at the same time.

Risk factors

Customer output and raw material prices

Higher oil prices tied to the Middle East conflict pressure raw material and logistics costs, and a domestic parts-maker fire indirectly hit vehicle output. Declining market value and operating profit at automakers could translate into pressure on supplier orders. If both variables worsen together, profit faces pressure from both sales and cost of sales.

Balance sheet and cash flow

With total liabilities of KRW 443 billion against equity of KRW 166.3 billion, leverage is elevated. Operating cash flow was negative in 2023 (-KRW 9.17 billion) and again in 2025 (-KRW 7.47 billion), suggesting profit does not convert to cash steadily. Worsening borrowing conditions would reduce room for capital investment.

FX, rates and plant operations

Exchange rate swings can move net profit sharply through translation gains and losses. If US inflation and rates stay high and the dollar remains firm, the direction of the won could change.

In April 2026, the Industrial Complex Corporation conducted an on-site safety inspection at the company's plant in the Banwol industrial complex. Safety issues at a concentrated production site could translate into downtime risk.

Overall view

Taeyang Metal Industrial holds a 34.98% share of cold-forged automotive parts sold to vehicle makers, and operating profit rose 39.7% to KRW 22.4 billion in 2025 as the margin recovered.

However, Q3 2026 operating profit of KRW 6.82 billion, up 172.8% year on year, coincided with a net loss attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 5.36 billion. Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders over the last four quarters totaled KRW 11.6 billion, reflecting wide quarterly swings.

On the balance sheet, total liabilities of KRW 443 billion at end-2025 far exceed total equity of KRW 166.3 billion, and operating cash flow was -KRW 7.47 billion in 2025.

Supportive factors include market position, margin recovery and possible translation gains from a weaker won, while the negatives are the Q3 2026 loss, high debt, and vehicle output weakness with customer concentration.

The next checkpoints are the Q4 2026 results in the 2026 annual report and the profit structure in the Q1 2027 quarterly report.
