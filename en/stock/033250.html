02

Business structure

Chasys Co., Ltd. was established in 1989 and listed on the KOSPI in 1999 as an automotive chassis parts specialist. Its core products include rear axles, cross members, cradles, arms, and oil pans, which form key components of a vehicle's underbody and suspension structure.

The company states that its production technology and production lines were developed independently through in-house engineering. Its chassis design capability allows collaboration with automakers from the concept car stage, which the company cites as a basis for price competitiveness in supplier bids.

The business model centers on order-based (OEM) manufacturing tied to automakers' new-model launch and sales cycles, and, as illustrated by a past contract to supply parts for a new GM Korea model, large model-specific contracts can materially affect results.

As with other auto parts makers, raw material costs (such as steel) and labor costs directly affect production costs. The company pursues both domestic and export sales to manage exposure to cyclical automotive demand.

Chairman Lee Myung-gon is the controlling shareholder, and the combined stake of the controlling shareholder group stood at 40.51% as of September 2026, indicating a stable ownership structure.

04

Earnings analysis

Chasys's consolidated revenue rose sharply from KRW 32.82 billion in 2022 to KRW 49.00 billion in 2023 and KRW 90.26 billion in 2024, before growing further to KRW 93.84 billion in 2025 (+4.0% YoY).

Operating profit swung from a KRW 7.47 billion loss in 2022 to a KRW 4.57 billion profit in 2023, then KRW 4.68 billion in 2024 and KRW 7.53 billion in 2025 (+61.0% YoY), a notable improvement. The operating margin rebounded from -22.8% in 2022 to 9.3% in 2023, dipped to 5.2% in 2024, and recovered to 8.0% in 2025.

The company attributed the 2025 improvement to increased sales volume from new models.

Net income attributable to owners rose for three straight years, from a loss of KRW 4.69 billion in 2022 to KRW 3.30 billion in 2023, KRW 6.07 billion in 2024, and KRW 6.30 billion in 2025, though the 2025 growth rate (+3.7%) lagged far behind operating profit growth (+61.0%).

This gap reflects a foreign currency translation loss tied to won appreciation that offset part of the operating gains at the net income level.

On a quarterly basis, operating profit fell from KRW 2.43 billion in Q2 2025 to KRW 502 million in Q3 2025, rebounded to KRW 2.15 billion in Q4 2025, and then moderated to KRW 1.44 billion in Q1 2026 and KRW 1.48 billion in Q2 2026.

Over the latest four quarters through Q2 2026, cumulative net income attributable to owners totaled approximately KRW 6.68 billion, tracking a similar trajectory to the annual profit level.

Operating cash flow declined from KRW 10.08 billion in 2024 to KRW 7.34 billion in 2025, suggesting working capital changes affected cash generation despite the profit increase.

05

Industry analysis

The domestic auto parts industry is structurally an order-driven business, with results tied to automakers' production and sales plans.

According to industry data sources such as FnGuide, weakness in the domestic auto market and intensifying competition within the parts supply chain have at times weighed on revenue for suppliers in this space.

At the same time, the ongoing shift toward electrification has created a transitional demand structure in which automakers place orders for both internal combustion and electric vehicle components, and chassis and suspension parts are considered relatively less exposed to structural demand shifts since they are required regardless of powertrain type.

Still, competing suppliers in the same industry are also restructuring their portfolios by winning contracts for electric-vehicle-specific platform components, meaning competition for new model awards continues.

Steel raw material prices and currency fluctuations are common cost variables across the industry, and margin resilience varies by company depending on how well these are managed.

According to data providers such as Valueline, Chasys has at times been positioned in a relatively lower valuation-multiple range within the auto parts industry.

Since sector-wide valuations tend to move with order cycles and automaker sales trends, continued monitoring of individual competitive positioning is warranted.

08

Bull factors

Three Straight Years of Operating Profit

After turning to a loss of KRW 7.4704 billion in operating profit in 2022, the company achieved a turnaround to profit in 2023 and has recorded operating profit for three consecutive years through 2024 and 2025.

Operating profit in 2025 was KRW 7.52825 billion, up 61.0% year-on-year, showing an accelerating pace of improvement. The company attributes this to increased sales volume of new vehicle models.

Improving Financial Structure

The debt ratio, which was high at 165.1% in 2022 and 172.5% in 2023, has steadily declined to 115.1% in 2024 and 89.6% in 2025. Total equity also increased from KRW 28.63602 billion in 2022 to KRW 45.37331 billion in 2025, strengthening the financial base. If this trend continues, it could help ease the burden of interest expenses.

Price Competitiveness via Joint Design Capability

Chesys states that it possesses design capabilities for automotive chassis components, enabling co-development with automakers from the concept car development stage. This is cited as a structural strength that allows the company to secure order opportunities from the early stages of new vehicle projects.

The fact that chassis and suspension systems are required components regardless of drivetrain type is also a favorable factor amid the shift to electrification.

Bear factors

Quarterly Earnings Volatility

There is significant quarter-to-quarter volatility, with operating profit sharply declining from KRW 2.42526 billion in Q2 2025 to KRW 501.93 million in Q3, before rebounding to KRW 2.14946 billion in Q4.

This reflects a business structure in which performance is sensitive to the production and sales schedules of specific models. Additional earnings disclosures are needed to confirm a stable quarterly trend.

Net Income Growth Lagging Operating Profit

While operating profit in 2025 increased 61.0% year-on-year, the growth rate of net income attributable to controlling shareholders was only 3.7%. The company explained that this reflects foreign currency translation losses due to a decline in the exchange rate.

A structure in which operational improvements do not fully translate into net income is a risk that could recur depending on exchange rate fluctuations.

Absence of Dividends

As of the most recent fiscal year, no cash dividend per share has been confirmed, indicating that direct shareholder returns remain limited despite the improvement in earnings. If the structure of retaining earnings continues, this may be viewed as less attractive to dividend-seeking investors.

Risk factors

Customer Concentration and Order Dependency

Chesys's revenue is structured as make-to-order production linked to the sales volume of specific models from automakers, meaning that sales volatility could increase in the event of weak sales or discontinuation of a particular model.

There have been cases in the past, such as the contract to supply new models to GM Korea, where the weight of an individual contract was substantial. Changes in an automaker's production plans directly affect the performance of parts suppliers.

Foreign Exchange Volatility

There was a case in 2025 where foreign currency translation losses due to a decline in the KRW/USD exchange rate limited the increase in net income. In a structure with export exposure, non-operating gains and losses can significantly affect performance depending on the direction of exchange rate movements. Similar fluctuations in gains and losses could recur depending on future exchange rate movements.

Ownership and Share-Related Disclosures

The combined stake of the largest shareholder, Chairman Lee Myung-gon, and related parties stood at 40.51% as of September 2026, with a history of disclosures related to share pledges and their release.

In April 2026, there was a change in capital structure due to a reverse stock split that adjusted the total number of issued shares. These changes in shareholding and capital structure are matters that investors should continue to monitor.
