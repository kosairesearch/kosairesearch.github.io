Chasys: Revenue Up Four Straight Quarters, Profit Turns to Loss in Q3 2026

Business structure

Chasys is an automotive new-parts manufacturer that has been listed on KOSPI since August 1999. Its business consists of a single automotive parts segment, which accounted for 99.5% of the business mix in the company's 2026 profile.

Main products include rear axles, cross members, oil pans, front suspension arms and axle housings, supplied to automakers as axle, chassis and suspension components. Its headquarters is in Gyeongsan, North Gyeongsang Province.

Dependence on automakers is high: a 2020 press report said more than 70% of revenue came from a Korean unit of General Motors, and that revenue tracked that customer's vehicle demand. The latest customer-by-customer revenue mix will be updated in the next annual report.

Revenue reached KRW 93.8 billion in 2025, a sharp expansion from KRW 32.8 billion in 2022. On governance, the largest shareholder held 25.34% according to a filing in July 2026, up from 24.84% in an earlier filing that month.

Demand for these parts moves with automakers' production volumes and with whether new platforms adopt Chasys components, so new model awards are the key variable for the business mix.

Earnings analysis

Annual revenue went from KRW 32.8 billion in 2022 to KRW 49 billion in 2023 (up 49.3% year on year), KRW 90.3 billion in 2024 (up 84.2%) and KRW 93.8 billion in 2025 (up 4.0%). Revenue rose for three straight years from 2023 to 2025, and 2025 was the largest year of 2022 to 2025.

Operating profit swung from an operating loss of KRW 7.47 billion in 2022 to operating profit of KRW 4.57 billion in 2023, then rose to KRW 4.68 billion in 2024 (up 2.4%) and KRW 7.53 billion in 2025 (up 61.0%).

The operating margin went from -22.8% in 2022 to 9.3% in 2023, fell to 5.2% in 2024 and recovered to 8.0% in 2025. In 2024, revenue grew sharply while operating profit rose only 2.4%, so the margin was diluted.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders moved from a net loss of KRW 4.69 billion in 2022 to net income of KRW 3.3 billion in 2023, then KRW 6.07 billion in 2024 and KRW 6.3 billion in 2025, increasing for two straight years from 2024 to 2025.

Operating cash flow was -KRW 556 million in 2022, KRW 5.9 billion in 2023, KRW 10.1 billion in 2024 and KRW 7.34 billion in 2025. The debt-to-equity ratio fell from 165.1% in 2022 and 172.5% in 2023 to 115.1% in 2024 and 89.6% in 2025.

At year-end 2025, total liabilities were KRW 40.7 billion and total equity was KRW 45.4 billion.

Quarterly revenue rose from KRW 18.6 billion in the third quarter of 2025 to KRW 24.4 billion in the fourth quarter (up 31.3% from the prior quarter), KRW 24.6 billion in the first quarter of 2026, KRW 26.7 billion in the second quarter and KRW 29.3 billion in the third quarter, four consecutive quarters of growth.

Operating profit jumped to KRW 2.15 billion in the fourth quarter of 2025, then eased to KRW 1.44 billion, KRW 1.48 billion and KRW 1.09 billion in the first three quarters of 2026, so profit did not keep pace with revenue.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders went from KRW 2.42 billion in the fourth quarter of 2025 to a net loss of KRW 1.64 billion in the third quarter of 2026, and the four-quarter total from the fourth quarter of 2025 through the third quarter of 2026 is KRW 4.86 billion.

Cost ratios or other expense items are the main candidates for the margin squeeze despite higher revenue, and the detail will be visible in the next annual report.

Industry analysis

Chasys' end market is domestic automaker production and model-by-model parts orders. Axle and suspension parts move with the life cycle of vehicle platforms, so when a model is discontinued or replaced, supply volumes change with it.

The auto industry is in the middle of an electrification shift that is reshaping underbody structures and chassis design, which creates both new-part opportunities and the risk of existing parts being replaced. Cycle position is typically judged from automakers' production plans and new model launch schedules.

In competition, Chasys is compared with listed auto parts makers such as Hyundai Mobis, HL Mando, Sungwoo Hitech, SL and Seoyon E-Hwa. Its revenue scale is small relative to large chassis and suspension suppliers, and as a small to mid-sized parts maker it depends heavily on a few automaker customers.

Revenue has grown for four consecutive quarters since the fourth quarter of 2025, which may reflect a recovery in volumes, but customer-level volume changes cannot be separated from the summary figures.

The 57.8% year-on-year revenue increase in the third quarter of 2026 should also be read against a low base, with third-quarter 2025 revenue at KRW 18.6 billion. Raw material prices and exchange rates are common variables that affect component costs.

Balance sheet repair and cash generation
