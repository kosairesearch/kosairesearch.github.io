Chasys: Earnings Recovery Since Turning Profitable

Chasys, a specialist in automotive suspension and chassis components, has shown improving operating profit and controlling-interest net income since turning profitable in 2023.

  2. 2

  3. 3

  4. 4

02

Business structure

Founded in 1989 and listed on the KOSPI in 1999, Chasys is a manufacturer of new automotive parts headquartered and manufacturing in Gyeongsan, Gyeongbuk Province.

Its core products are suspension and chassis components, including rear axles, cross members, cradles, arms, and oil pans, supplied to automakers on an OEM basis. Based on its most recent disclosed product mix, AXLE accounts for an overwhelming 82.37% of sales, followed by ARM and CROSS MBR.

Its main customers are Hyundai Motor and General Motors, and the company is positioned as a specialist in suspension systems and chassis parts, supplying rear axle modules and cradles.

Overseas, it operates manufacturing subsidiaries in China (Yantai) and India, and runs a North America office, pursuing entry into the North American market based on an order win for EV battery-module cross members.

Governance runs through Chairman Lee Myung-gon and his son, Vice Chairman Lee Jun-seong, who hold stakes in Chasys via MSHC and AMS, with Lee Myung-gon reporting a 40.51% stake as of September 2026.

Affiliates include AMS, Nel Biotech (animal medicine, feed additives, and fertilizer), and Seojin Industry, meaning the broader group operates businesses beyond auto parts. In March 2026 the company decided on a 1-for-4 common share consolidation, with new shares listed on May 7, 2026.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual revenue rose from KRW 32.8 billion in 2022 to KRW 49 billion in 2023 (up 49.3%), KRW 90.3 billion in 2024 (up 84.2%), and KRW 93.8 billion in 2025 (up 4.0%), marking three consecutive years of growth since 2022, with 2025 the largest year in the period.

The company attributed the operating profit increase to higher revenue driven by growing sales volumes of new vehicle models.

Operating profit swung from a KRW 7.47 billion operating loss in 2022 to a KRW 4.57 billion profit in 2023, then rose to KRW 4.68 billion in 2024 (up 2.4%) and KRW 7.53 billion in 2025 (up 61.0%), two consecutive years of growth following the 2023 trough.

The operating margin also improved from -22.8% in 2022 to 8.0% in 2025.

Controlling-interest net income swung from a KRW 4.69 billion loss in 2022 to a KRW 3.3 billion profit in 2023, then grew to KRW 6.07 billion in 2024 (up 84.2%) and KRW 6.3 billion in 2025 (up 3.7%), two consecutive years of growth following the 2023 trough, though the year-over-year change in net income was limited because a foreign currency translation loss tied to a weaker exchange rate was reflected in the results.

On a quarterly basis, revenue fell to KRW 18.6 billion in Q3 2025 before rising for three consecutive quarters to KRW 24.4 billion in Q4 2025, KRW 24.6 billion in Q1 2026, and KRW 26.7 billion in Q2 2026.

Quarterly operating profit rebounded sharply to KRW 2.15 billion in Q4 2025 but then declined year over year to KRW 1.44 billion in Q1 2026 and KRW 1.48 billion in Q2 2026, while controlling-interest net income fell for two straight quarters from its Q4 2025 peak of KRW 2.42 billion to KRW 2.19 billion in Q1 2026 and KRW 1.9 billion in Q2 2026.

The sum of controlling-interest net income over the trailing four quarters (Q3 2025 through Q2 2026) was KRW 6.68 billion.

05

Industry analysis

The automotive chassis and suspension parts segment in which Chasys operates is closely tied to automakers' new model launch cycles and production volumes.

Large tier-1 suppliers such as HL Mando, SNT Motiv, and Sungwoo Hitech dominate the domestic chassis parts space, meaning smaller players like Chasys tend to carry relatively higher dependence on specific vehicle models and customers.

Across the broader automaker industry, there is a clear trend toward localizing production and parts sourcing in North America to reduce tariff and currency exposure, a shift relevant to Chasys as it pursues North American market entry.

The shift toward electrification is creating new demand for battery-module-related chassis components, prompting parts suppliers historically focused on internal combustion vehicles to broaden their portfolios toward EV-related parts.

Chasys operates manufacturing subsidiaries in China and India, giving it sensitivity to currency fluctuations and local automotive demand. Steel raw material price swings and pricing pressure from automakers are structural factors affecting profitability across the sector.

Bull factors

Earnings Recovery Paired with Balance Sheet Improvement

Since turning profitable in 2023, operating profit has grown for two straight years to KRW 4.68 billion in 2024 and KRW 7.53 billion in 2025, with the operating margin rising to 8.0%. Over the same period the debt-to-equity ratio fell from 172.5% to 89.6%, showing growth alongside improved financial health. Operating cash flow also remained solid at KRW 5.9 billion in 2023 and KRW 10.1 billion in 2024.

Pursuing North American Entry via EV Parts

Chasys is pursuing North American market entry based on an order win for EV battery-module cross members.

This represents an attempt to broaden its portfolio from internal-combustion-focused chassis parts toward electrification components, aligning with the broader automaker trend of localizing production in North America. Its existing North America office also provides a base for local engagement.

Established Supply Track Record with Major Automakers

Chasys is a specialist supplier of core chassis components such as rear axle modules and cradles to Hyundai Motor and General Motors.

The 2025 earnings improvement, as stated by the company, was driven by higher sales volumes of new vehicle models, indicating its position within existing supply chains has been maintained. Its long-standing transaction history with automakers can also serve as a reference for securing new orders.

Bear factors

Slowing Revenue Growth

Revenue growth slowed sharply to 4.0% in 2025, down from 49.3% in 2023 and 84.2% in 2024. Quarterly volatility has also been pronounced, with Q3 2025 revenue falling 29.7% from the prior quarter. Whether profitability improvement can continue as top-line growth decelerates bears watching.

Quarterly Profit Volatility and Year-over-Year Declines

Operating profit fell 33.0% quarter over quarter in Q1 2026 and rose just 3.0% in Q2 2026, with Q2 operating profit down 38.8% year over year. Controlling-interest net income has also declined for two consecutive quarters since peaking in Q4 2025.

In 2025, a foreign currency translation loss from a weaker exchange rate limited the increase in net income, suggesting such non-recurring factors could recur.

Small-Cap Supply-Demand Volatility

Chasys was designated an investment-caution stock on two occasions in 2026, in June and August, due to trading concentration among a small number of accounts. Trading-value concentration and account clustering, characteristic of small-cap stocks, can amplify price volatility.

As a relatively low-liquidity stock, short-term swings driven by shifting supply and demand can also be sizable.

Risk factors

Customer Concentration Risk

Chasys's revenue is concentrated among a small number of automakers, primarily Hyundai Motor and General Motors. Discontinuation of a specific customer's vehicle model or adjustments to production volumes could directly affect results. If the pace of customer diversification remains slow, this dependence could persist.

Currency and Overseas Subsidiary Risk

Chasys operates manufacturing subsidiaries in China and India, exposing it to foreign currency translation gains and losses. In 2025, a translation loss tied to a weaker exchange rate limited the year-over-year change in net income. Depending on currency movements, similar non-recurring swings in earnings could recur.

Governance and Succession Risk

Chasys has a multi-layered governance structure in which Chairman Lee Myung-gon and his son, Vice Chairman Lee Jun-seong, hold stakes through entities such as MSHC and AMS.

As the founder's son takes on an expanded management role, equity transfers or transactions among affiliates could occur, making governance changes worth monitoring for minority shareholders.

The group's holdings also include bio-sector affiliate Nel Biotech, making capital allocation across business areas another point to watch.

Overall view

Chasys has shown improving financial strength since turning profitable in 2023, with operating profit and controlling-interest net income rising for two consecutive years and the debt-to-equity ratio falling over the same period.

However, 2025 revenue growth slowed sharply to 4.0% from the prior year, and operating profit declined year over year in the first two quarters of 2026, suggesting the pace of earnings recovery has moderated.

The pursuit of North American market entry based on an EV battery-module cross-member order win could become a pillar of medium-to-long-term growth, but the specific timing of mass production and revenue recognition has not yet been confirmed.

Revenue dependence on a small number of automakers such as Hyundai Motor and General Motors, currency exposure from overseas subsidiaries, and governance changes tied to the founder-son succession process are factors that warrant continued attention.

As a small-cap stock with a history of investment-caution designations tied to trading concentration, supply-demand volatility is also relatively elevated. Monitoring the next quarterly report and any North America-related disclosures will be important to assess whether the earnings recovery trend continues.
