02

Business structure

Mirae Semiconductor is a semiconductor distribution specialist founded in 1996 by professionals who previously worked in Samsung Electronics' chip division, and it registered as an authorized Samsung Electronics semiconductor distributor at founding, a relationship that continues today.

Its core products are memory chips produced by Samsung Electronics (DRAM, NAND flash, SSD) and system semiconductors (touch controller ICs, camera image sensors, PMICs), with a disclosed product mix as of 2021 on a consolidated basis showing memory semiconductors at 68.7% of sales and system semiconductors at 30.1%.

Since 1998 the company has also operated an after-sales service center under a contract with Samsung Electronics for memory chip servicing, a business structure that extends beyond pure distribution into post-sale support and is cited as a core competitive edge.

It subsequently expanded its product lineup through a Samsung Display agency contract in 2012, an IXYS Semiconductors agreement in 2013, and a Silicon Mitus agreement in 2014, and entered the automotive semiconductor market in 2018.

Samsung Electronics has long maintained a strategy of managing supply chains and receivables through a stable network of distribution partners rather than direct sales, and this arrangement has been consolidated over time into a small number of domestic partners.

Within this restructured system, the company competes with peers such as SAMT and Sinsung Semiconductor as one of the channels for Samsung Electronics chip distribution. It listed on KOSDAQ in early 2023, and its revenue structure remains centered on Samsung Electronics even after listing.

This concentration on a single major manufacturer explains both the stability and the risk embedded in the business model.

04

Earnings analysis

Full-year 2025 revenue reached KRW 635.6bn, up 30.3% from KRW 487.6bn in 2024, while operating profit rose 86.5% from KRW 10.2bn to KRW 19.1bn, and net income attributable to owners climbed 133.6% from KRW 6.0bn to KRW 14.1bn, lifting the operating margin from 2.1% to 3.0%.

Compared with 2022, however, when revenue was KRW 550.2bn, operating profit KRW 22.1bn, and operating margin 4.0%, 2023 saw revenue fall 30.8% to KRW 380.5bn and operating profit drop 57.4% to KRW 9.4bn, illustrating how directly the company's results tracked the memory downturn of that period.

On a quarterly basis, operating margin briefly narrowed moving from Q2 2025 (revenue KRW 142.8bn, operating profit KRW 3.8bn) to Q3 2025 (revenue KRW 157.8bn, operating profit KRW 2.8bn) before turning higher again in Q4 2025 (revenue KRW 175.8bn, operating profit KRW 7.1bn).

That recovery accelerated dramatically in 2026, with Q1 revenue reaching KRW 340.5bn, operating profit KRW 32.2bn (margin near 9.4%), and owners' net income KRW 23.4bn, representing nearly a doubling of scale and a simultaneous margin expansion versus the prior quarter.

Q2 2026 sustained this elevated level with revenue of KRW 340.1bn, operating profit of KRW 28.2bn, and owners' net income of KRW 19.0bn, showing the Q1 surge was not a one-off event.

Summed across the most recent four quarters (Q3 2025 through Q2 2026), owners' net income totals roughly KRW 49.3bn, already well above the full-year 2025 figure of KRW 14.1bn.

According to financial data compiler figures, cumulative nine-month 2025 revenue, operating profit, and net income on a standalone basis rose 23.0%, 33.3%, and 77.3% year over year respectively, a result attributed to rising demand for high-performance memory chips driven by AI adoption combined with higher electronic component distribution sales.

Separately from the profit growth, however, operating cash flow was negative in both 2024 (KRW -30.6bn) and 2025 (KRW -26.7bn), indicating that the expansion in scale has come with a working-capital burden tied to inventory and receivables.

05

Industry analysis

The global semiconductor industry is undergoing a memory-centered restructuring driven by the spread of AI infrastructure, and the World Semiconductor Trade Statistics (WSTS) organization projects the global semiconductor market will grow more than 25% year over year in 2026 to roughly $975bn, with the memory segment expected to grow in the 30% range, outpacing the overall market.

Market views generally hold that 2026 DRAM demand will grow more than 30%, with server DRAM growing in the 40% range, while supply is expected to expand by only around 20%, raising the possibility of a structural supply shortfall persisting.

In the HBM market, SK Hynix has maintained an overwhelming share while Samsung Electronics has been expanding its presence as a major supplier, and KB Securities stated in an August 2026 report that Samsung Electronics' HBM4 market share is estimated at around 44%, ranking it first.

Mirae Semiconductor does not produce HBM directly, but because its business model channels Samsung Electronics' memory and system semiconductor volumes through a distributor network, its results tend to move in tandem with Samsung's shipment volumes and pricing trends.

Competition among distribution channels has already consolidated into a small number of domestic players, and while entry barriers are high, this also means structural dependence on a single supplier, Samsung Electronics, is correspondingly high.

Some market research firms and foreign media outlets have also raised the possibility that intensifying competition and capacity expansion could push memory prices, including HBM, into an adjustment phase from 2026 onward, meaning views on the duration of the current upcycle are not entirely unified.

06

Outlook

No specific company-issued annual revenue or profit guidance was identified, but the near-doubling of Q1 and Q2 2026 revenue versus Q4 2025 appears linked to rising Samsung Electronics memory shipment volumes and pricing.

Industry forecasts generally lean toward the memory upcycle continuing through 2026, though more cautious views on the trajectory beyond 2027 have also been raised, making it difficult to predict the timing of a mid- to long-term cycle inflection.

In a period of rising demand for high-performance memory serving AI servers and data centers, there is room for the distribution volume of the company's system semiconductor products (touch controller ICs, image sensors, PMICs) to expand as well.

However, this expansion is dependent on Samsung Electronics' own production and supply policies, making it more a case of supply-chain-linked growth than an independent growth story for the company itself.

Amid rising brokerage interest in small- and mid-cap domestic semiconductor equipment, materials, and parts names, the company's position as a distribution- and service-specialized player has also drawn attention.

Going forward, the pace of Samsung Electronics' HBM4 ramp-up and the trend in commodity DRAM and NAND pricing are likely to be the key variables determining the direction of the company's coming quarterly results.

08

Bull factors

Direct Beneficiary of the Memory Supercycle

Surging demand for high-performance memory driven by the spread of AI data centers is pushing up both Samsung Electronics' shipment volumes and pricing simultaneously, and the company's results are directly linked to these volumes as a distribution channel.

The near-doubling of Q1 and Q2 2026 revenue versus Q4 2025 illustrates this linkage in practice. Market views generally see 2026 DRAM and server DRAM demand outpacing supply by a wide margin, leaving room for this trend to continue.

Three Decades of Samsung Partnership and High Entry Barriers

The company's history of maintaining authorized Samsung Electronics semiconductor distributor status since its founding in 1996, together with its operation of what is reported to be the world's only memory after-sales service center, represent assets that new competitors would find difficult to replicate.

The consolidation of Samsung Electronics' domestic distribution partners into a small number of players also raises barriers to new entry. This underpins the company's positioning as a comprehensive service provider covering technical support and post-sale service rather than a simple logistics operator.

Confirmed Earnings Leverage

After the annual operating margin improved from 2.1% in 2024 to 3.0% in 2025, it jumped sharply to roughly 9.4% in Q1 2026. With a structure in which revenue expansion translates directly into margin expansion now confirmed, further earnings leverage is possible should favorable volume and pricing conditions persist.

09

Bear factors

Structurally Thin Distribution Margins

Given the nature of the distribution business, the operating margin has historically hovered in the 2%-4% range over multiple years, and in 2023 revenue fell 30.8% and operating profit dropped 57.4% alongside the memory downturn.

This illustrates a business structure with limited pricing power and suggests results could reverse quickly if the cycle turns.

Single-Supplier Concentration Risk

Because a substantial portion of revenue depends on the volume and pricing of a single supplier, Samsung Electronics, any shift in Samsung's production or supply policy, or adjustment to its distribution channel strategy, could directly affect results.

As seen in past restructurings of distribution partners, policy changes at the major manufacturer are a variable that can affect the very survival of distributors.

Trading Flows Decoupled From Fundamentals

The May 2026 advance notice of investment-alert designation by the Korea Exchange following a sharp short-term price rise shows that the share price can move rapidly on thematic trading flows independent of earnings release timing.

This volatility is a risk that must be managed separately from the fundamental story of improving profitability.

Risk factors

Customer and Supply Concentration

Revenue dependence on Samsung Electronics is structurally high, meaning policy or strategy changes at this single customer can directly affect the company's results. The past restructuring of domestic distribution partners into a small number of firms shows that this concentration can cut both ways.

Memory Cycle Volatility

The precedent of revenue and operating profit falling 30.8% and 57.4%, respectively, from 2022 to 2023 shows how quickly the company's results can deteriorate when the memory cycle turns.

Some market research firms have also raised the possibility of memory price adjustments from 2026 onward as competition intensifies and capacity expands, so the current upcycle should not be assumed to be permanent.

Working Capital and Cash Flow

In both 2024 and 2025, years of strong profit growth, operating cash flow was negative at KRW -30.6bn and KRW -26.7bn respectively.

Given the distribution business characteristic of inventory and receivables growing alongside sales, faster top-line expansion can widen the gap between cash generation and reported earnings, a point worth monitoring.

12

Overall view

Mirae Semiconductor is a stock whose results are now confirming the distribution-channel benefits of the 2026 memory supercycle, built on its long-standing status as an authorized Samsung Electronics semiconductor distributor.

Following a clear improvement in full-year 2025 results versus the prior year, both revenue and operating margin jumped sharply in Q1 and Q2 2026, providing tangible evidence of earnings leverage.

However, this growth stems from a structure heavily dependent on the volume and pricing of a single supplier, Samsung Electronics, and the 2022-2023 precedent shows that results can reverse quickly if the cycle turns.

Separately from the profit growth, operating cash flow has been negative in both of the last two years, and the advance notice of an investment-alert designation following a sharp short-term price rise are factors that warrant attention on both the fundamental and trading-flow sides.

Going forward, the pace of Samsung Electronics' HBM4 ramp-up, trends in commodity memory pricing, and whether the company's working capital and cash flow improve will likely be the key points to watch for the sustainability of coming quarterly results.
