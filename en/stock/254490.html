Mirai Semiconductors: Profit Recovery Meets a Cash Flow Gap

02

Business structure

Mirai Semiconductors was founded in 1996 as a semiconductor distributor and began operations the same year under a distribution partnership with Samsung Electronics. The company added Samsung Display as a customer in 2012 and listed on KOSDAQ in 2023.

It buys memory and system semiconductors from manufacturers and supplies them to customers, supported by field application engineering (FAE) help in selecting products and designing circuits, plus specialized logistics.

Based on consolidated figures for 2021, memory accounted for 68.7% of sales, system semiconductors for 30.1%, and other products for 1.1%. System products include touch controller ICs, camera image sensors and PMICs.

The company also runs a memory after-sales service center under a service agreement with Samsung Electronics. Distributors of this kind consult customers on the technology they need and coordinate production schedules between manufacturers and customers.

A 2023 brokerage report on the listing argued that the scale of working capital shapes how much sales the model can support. Competition comes from other distributors that also serve as Samsung Electronics partners. At the time of listing, the largest shareholder and related parties held 74.3%. The structure leans toward procurement and technical service rather than manufacturing.

04

Earnings analysis

On the disclosed annual figures, sales fell from KRW 550.2 billion in 2022 to KRW 380.5 billion in 2023, a 30.8% decline, before rising to KRW 487.6 billion in 2024 and KRW 635.6 billion in 2025. The 2025 figure is the largest of the 2022–2025 period, and sales have risen for two consecutive years after the 2023 low.

Operating profit fell 57.4% from KRW 22.1 billion in 2022 to KRW 9.41 billion in 2023, then rose to KRW 10.2 billion in 2024 and KRW 19.1 billion in 2025, increasing for two consecutive years after 2023.

The operating margin moved from 4.0% in 2022 to 2.5% in 2023, 2.1% in 2024 and 3.0% in 2025, recovering more slowly than sales. In 2023 the drop in operating profit (57.4%) was far steeper than the drop in sales (30.8%), which points to margin pressure.

Controlling-shareholder net income was KRW 15 billion in 2022, KRW 5.06 billion in 2023, KRW 6.05 billion in 2024 and KRW 14.1 billion in 2025, up 133.6% in the last year. Net income including non-controlling interests carried the same disclosed amounts in each year.

At the end of 2025, total equity was KRW 110.2 billion and total liabilities KRW 77.5 billion, and the debt ratio fell from 110.6% in 2022 to 70.3% in 2025. Operating cash flow was KRW 48.7 billion in 2023 but KRW -30.6 billion in 2024 and KRW -26.7 billion in 2025, diverging from the profit recovery.

Quarterly, operating profit recovered from KRW 2.81 billion in the third quarter of 2025 to KRW 7.1 billion in the fourth quarter, then peaked at KRW 32.2 billion in the first quarter of 2026.

Operating profit was KRW 28.2 billion in the second quarter and KRW 19.2 billion in the third quarter of 2026, a decline for two consecutive quarters, while third-quarter sales were KRW 254.3 billion, up 61.2% from a year earlier. Controlling-shareholder net income over the latest four quarters totals KRW 59.9 billion.

FnGuide attributed the recent improvement to rising demand for high-performance memory tied to expanded AI use and higher sales in electronic parts distribution.

05

Industry analysis

Daishin Securities' January 5, 2026 report projected that the 2026 memory semiconductor market would grow 85% year on year to USD 402.1 billion. The same report expected the HBM market to grow 71% to USD 57.7 billion.

It attributed continuing supply bottlenecks to conservative capacity expansion over the past two years and HBM taking up production capacity. The World Semiconductor Trade Statistics organization (WSTS) forecast that the global semiconductor market would grow more than 25% in 2026 to roughly USD 975 billion.

Samsung Electronics reported preliminary first-quarter 2026 consolidated sales of KRW 133 trillion and operating profit of KRW 57.2 trillion, with operating profit up 755.01% from a year earlier.

Daishin Securities put Samsung's memory operating profit for the fourth quarter of 2025 at KRW 17.8 trillion and pointed to a rebound in memory profitability.

Mirai Semiconductors is a distributor and technical service provider rather than a manufacturer, so its sales track customers' memory purchase volumes and pricing. On the competitive side, other distributors that are also Samsung Electronics partners compete for the same customer base.

Expanding system semiconductor offerings and after-sales and technical support are the points the company uses to differentiate itself.

08

Bull factors

Earnings scale has recovered

Operating profit rose from KRW 9.41 billion in 2023 to KRW 10.2 billion in 2024 and KRW 19.1 billion in 2025, increasing for two consecutive years after 2023. The first quarter of 2026 peaked with operating profit of KRW 32.2 billion and controlling-shareholder net income of KRW 23.4 billion.

Net income over the latest four quarters totals KRW 59.9 billion, well above the full-year 2025 figure of KRW 14.1 billion. The clear expansion of earnings scale from the 2023 low is the main bullish point.

Expanding memory market backdrop

Daishin Securities' January 2026 report projected 85% growth in the 2026 memory semiconductor market. The same report highlighted a rebound in Samsung's memory profitability and raised memory price expectations.

Because the company's sales follow customers' purchase volumes and pricing, a larger memory market widens its revenue opportunity. Sales of KRW 340.5 billion in the first quarter of 2026 reached a quarterly peak in the same direction.

Long-standing Samsung partnership

After signing a distribution partnership with Samsung Electronics in 1996, the company secured Samsung Display as a customer in 2012. Its memory after-sales center and technical support were built on this relationship.

A long-running trading relationship gives the company a channel to convert customer volume growth into sales, which supports the bullish case.

09

Bear factors

Quarterly momentum has slowed

Sales fell 25.2% from KRW 340.1 billion in the second quarter of 2026 to KRW 254.3 billion in the third, and operating profit fell 31.7% from KRW 28.2 billion to KRW 19.2 billion. Both measures have declined for two consecutive quarters and remain below the first-quarter peak. Year-on-year growth is still high, but the sequential decline has weakened the quarterly trend.

Profit and cash flow diverge

Operating cash flow went from KRW 48.7 billion in 2023 to KRW -30.6 billion in 2024 and KRW -26.7 billion in 2025, diverging from the profit recovery. A distributor's inventory and receivables can expand with sales, so cash conversion may stay weak even as profit grows.

This gap remains a bearish point when assessing earnings quality against total equity of KRW 110.2 billion at the end of 2025.

Thin margins and customer concentration

The operating margin was 3.0% in 2025, still below the 4.0% recorded in 2022. A distribution model operates on thin margins, which limits how much profitability can improve as sales grow.

Because key customers are concentrated in Samsung-affiliated companies, room to negotiate margins may be limited, which is another bearish factor.

10

Risk factors

Customer concentration risk

The company's key trading relationships are concentrated in Samsung Electronics and Samsung Display. A change in purchasing plans at either customer could adjust sales and profit quickly. How far the company expands beyond Samsung-affiliated customers is the main indicator of whether this risk eases.

Working capital and cash flow risk

A distribution business ties up funds in inventory and receivables. Negative operating cash flow in 2024 and 2025 suggests this funding need has not kept pace with the profit recovery. Whether operating cash flow and the debt ratio improve in the next annual report is the point to check.

Memory cycle turn risk

Daishin Securities expects memory supply bottlenecks to persist, but that is a forecast. If supply expands or demand slows, customer purchases and the company's sales could adjust together.

Whether the sequential sales decline in the third quarter of 2026 signals an early cycle turn will become clearer from the 2026 annual report and first-quarter 2027 results.

Overall view

Mirai Semiconductors' sales and operating profit recovered from the 2023 low through 2025, and the first quarter of 2026 marked the quarterly peak with sales of KRW 340.5 billion and operating profit of KRW 32.2 billion.

Sales and operating profit then fell for two consecutive quarters, to KRW 254.3 billion and KRW 19.2 billion in the third quarter of 2026. The operating margin of 3.0% in 2025 remains below the 4.0% of 2022.

Controlling-shareholder net income over the latest four quarters totals KRW 59.9 billion, while operating cash flow was negative at KRW -30.6 billion in 2024 and KRW -26.7 billion in 2025.

On the outside environment, Daishin Securities' forecast of strong 2026 memory market growth and the rebound in Samsung's memory profitability are bullish factors, while customer concentration and working capital needs are bearish factors. This report does not present an investment opinion or target price.

The next checkpoints are full-year cash flow in the 2026 annual report and quarterly results for the first quarter of 2027.
