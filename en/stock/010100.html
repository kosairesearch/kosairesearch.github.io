02

Business structure

Korea Movenex is an automotive new-parts manufacturer listed on KOSPI in October 1987, and it changed its name to Korea Movenex in 2023. Based on the 2025 breakdown, automotive parts account for 99.2% of revenue, flanges for 0.1%, and industrial machinery for 0.7%, so the business depends heavily on automotive parts.

Its automotive products include H/Shaft for passenger cars and Axle and Brake assembly for commercial vehicles, and the company's own materials present the axle as its core item. Its main customers are Hyundai Motor and KIA, supplying parts for vehicles of both companies operated in Korea and abroad.

Its production base consists of domestic plants in Ulsan and Gyeongju and overseas production subsidiaries in the United States, China and Mexico. At the end of 2025, domestic and export shares of sales were 55.05% and 44.95%, so the overseas production base has grown in importance.

The company has seven consolidated subsidiaries, and its stake in the Mexican unit Seohan Auto Mexico is 68.24%. On technology, it focuses on lightweight product development through Seohan Technical Research Institute.

In competition, it is expanding sales networks and strengthening quality control to reach emerging markets amid intensifying global competition. Flanges and industrial machinery could cushion cycles through power plant and shipbuilding components, but their small shares limit that effect. Group ties are also large, with affiliate loan guarantees of KRW 208.2 billion at the end of Q3 2024.

04

Earnings analysis

Revenue rose from KRW 1.37 trillion in 2022 to KRW 1.51 trillion in 2023, KRW 1.57 trillion in 2024 and KRW 1.7 trillion in 2025, three consecutive years of growth from 2023 to 2025, with 2025 the largest year and 2022 the smallest in the period.

Operating profit went from KRW 37 billion in 2022 to a peak of KRW 59.3 billion in 2023, up 60.4%, and then fell two consecutive years to KRW 43.7 billion in 2024 and KRW 25.3 billion in 2025. In 2025 operating profit fell 42.0% even though revenue rose 8.4%, so growth in sales and profitability diverged.

The main causes of the profit change that the company gave in its DART periodic report were higher sales from increased orders and lower profit from US tariffs on auto parts. The operating margin was 2.7% in 2022, 3.9% in 2023, 2.8% in 2024 and 1.5% in 2025, the lowest of the four reported years.

Net profit attributable to owners peaked at KRW 47.2 billion in 2023, then fell to KRW 44.7 billion in 2024 and KRW 20.9 billion in 2025, a 53.4% decline in 2025. Net income including non-controlling interests was KRW 20.3 billion in 2025, reported separately from net profit attributable to owners.

Quarterly, operating profit of KRW 5.28 billion in Q3 2025 turned into an operating loss of KRW 5.15 billion in Q4 2025, then returned to the black at KRW 9.85 billion in Q1 2026.

Operating profit was KRW 8.7 billion in Q2 2026 and rose to KRW 14.9 billion in Q3 2026, up 70.7% from the prior quarter and up 181.2% year on year.

In the same Q3, net profit attributable to owners was KRW 1.31 billion, down 61.2% year on year, so operating profit and owner-attributable profit moved in opposite directions, and the sum of owner-attributable profit over the latest four quarters was KRW 19.8 billion.

Operating cash flow was KRW 43.6 billion in 2022, KRW 98 billion in 2023, KRW 51 billion in 2024 and KRW 43.2 billion in 2025.

05

Industry analysis

Because auto parts sales follow vehicle production, the industry picture for Korea Movenex depends heavily on production and sales at Hyundai Motor and KIA.

US tariffs were cited as a main cause of lower profit in 2025, and a 2026 news report said the US government had confirmed a retroactive cut in tariffs on Korean auto parts from 25% to 15%. The same report pointed to a high-exchange-rate environment as a factor that could enlarge operating leverage.

On competition, the global auto parts market is becoming more intense, and the company is pursuing an aggressive sales push in emerging markets.

Compared with large parts makers in the same Hyundai Motor Group supply chain such as Hyundai Mobis, Hyundai WIA, Hanon Systems and HL Mando, the company is smaller and more concentrated on one customer group.

Coverage has also pointed to a difference in share price sensitivity versus Hyundai Mobis, the sector's bellwether. Whether the industry is in an upswing or downswing is hard to determine, and vehicle production volumes and tariff policy remain the variables that drive the earnings direction.

Its overseas production bases in the United States and Mexico mean tariffs and local demand affect results directly.

Outlook

The points below are based on confirmed facts. Operating profit in the first three quarters of 2026 was KRW 9.85 billion in Q1, KRW 8.7 billion in Q2 and KRW 14.9 billion in Q3, a sequence that has held up across the quarters.

How much of the tariff cut reaches actual profit can only be assessed from news reports for now, and whether it shows up as a change in the cost structure in periodic reports is the key point.

On overseas production, SEOHAN AUTO GEORGIA received a capital contribution in March 2026 to fund facility investment and operations, and in March 2024 the company took part in a rights offering at affiliate Seohan NTN Bearing to fund an aluminum casting business, raising its stake from 28.6% to 33.4%.

The KRW 38 billion guarantee for Seohan Auto Mexico disclosed on April 3, 2026 is large relative to equity, so the trend in outstanding guarantees is a gauge of financial burden. On robotics, the company said in February 2025 that it has no link to robot business, and attention rose again with 2026 news coverage.

Whether any new business is confirmed through disclosure is the next checkpoint. At the March 2026 shareholder meeting, amendments including the introduction of an independent director system were approved, so the change in governance practice is also worth tracking.

08

Bull factors

Quarterly profit recovery

Operating profit returned to the black at KRW 9.85 billion in Q1 2026, and in Q3 reached KRW 14.9 billion, up 181.2% year on year. Given the operating margin fell to 1.5% in 2025, there is room for improvement in operating indicators.

However, net profit attributable to owners declined for two consecutive quarters over the same period, so whether the recovery stays at the operating level needs further confirmation.

Reported tariff reduction

According to news reports, the US government confirmed a retroactive cut in tariffs on Korean auto parts from 25% to 15%. In its explanation of 2025 results, the company cited lower profit from US auto parts tariffs as a main cause, so a lighter tariff burden could ease cost pressure. How much of the cut is reflected in the company's profit needs to be confirmed in periodic reports.

Overseas production base

The company has overseas production subsidiaries in the United States, China and Mexico, and the export share was 44.95% at the end of 2025. In March 2026, a capital contribution was made to SEOHAN AUTO GEORGIA to fund facility investment and operations.

Combining Hyundai Motor Group volumes with local production widens room to respond to changes in the tariff environment.

09

Bear factors

Weaker earnings base

The operating margin fell from 2.7% in 2022 and 3.9% in 2023 to 1.5% in 2025. Operating profit and net profit attributable to owners both declined for two consecutive years in 2024 and 2025. Revenue grew while profit did not follow, which is the bearish case that sales growth has not translated into profitability.

Dependence on Hyundai Motor Group and competition

Automotive parts make up 99.2% of revenue, and customers are concentrated in Hyundai Motor and KIA. Amid intensifying global competition, the company is responding with sales network expansion and emerging-market push, but its profitability is described as limited. Changes in vehicle volumes pass through directly to results.

Uncertainty around the robot theme

In a February 2025 exchange inquiry response, the company said that its stock, which had been mentioned as a robot theme stock, has no link to its current business. In 2026, the share price surged on news related to Hyundai Motor Group's humanoid robot.

Because the company's explanation and the theme-driven price move diverge, whether any new business is disclosed is worth tracking.

10

Risk factors

Tariffs and exchange rates

US tariff levels have directly driven the company's profit. The 2025 explanation of results cited lower profit from US auto parts tariffs, and the timing and extent of the reported cut remain variables.

A high exchange rate can act as an operating leverage for a company with a 44.95% export share, but its direction can also cut the other way.

Financial structure and guarantees

Total liabilities at the end of 2025 were KRW 576.1 billion and total equity was KRW 433.4 billion, with a debt-to-equity ratio of 132.9%.

A disclosure dated April 3, 2026 set a KRW 38 billion guarantee related to affiliate Seohan Auto Mexico, and according to a disclosure summary the total outstanding guarantee balance was KRW 278.6 billion. Increased support for group affiliates could add financial burden regardless of core business profit.

Governance and share price volatility

At the March 2026 shareholder meeting, amendments including the introduction of an independent director system were approved, and the board has four directors with one outside director. A labor union has challenged the sale of business units as a method tied to governance restructuring.

Because of its small-cap profile, share price swings have been large around theme and tariff reports, which also needs to be considered.

Overall view

Korea Movenex is an automotive parts maker that depends on supply to Hyundai Motor Group, and although revenue rose in 2025, operating profit and net profit attributable to owners fell, weakening its earnings base.

In 2026, quarterly operating profit returned to the black in Q1 and rose 181.2% year on year in Q3, showing a recovery trend, while net profit attributable to owners declined for two consecutive quarters over the same period. The sum of owner-attributable net profit over the latest four quarters was KRW 19.8 billion.

Reported US tariff cuts and the overseas production base are variables on the cost side, while outstanding guarantees and a 132.9% debt-to-equity ratio are burdens on the financial side. The robot theme adds share price volatility while the company has denied any link to its business.

Because bullish and bearish factors coexist, the next checkpoint is whether the operating recovery carries through to owner-attributable net profit and cash flow. The key items to check in the next annual report are whether the 2026 operating margin rises above the 1.5% of 2025 and whether tariff effects are visible.
