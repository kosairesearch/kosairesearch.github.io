Report overview

A weather-data firm tests whether profits can hold

Kweather narrowed its operating loss from KRW 2.47 billion in 2023 to KRW 1.73 billion in 2025 and posted quarterly operating profits in Q1 and Q2 2026, but returned to an operating loss in Q3 2026. Whether quarterly profits can be sustained is the next point to watch.

02

Business structure

Kweather, founded in 1997, is a private weather service company in Korea that offers weather forecasting, meteorological consulting, weather information services and air quality management. Its business splits into a weather and climate data arm and an air measurement and improvement product arm.

Products accounted for about 70% of sales in Q1 2026, and the air measurement segment (KRW 1.4 billion) and the air improvement segment (KRW 1.3 billion) together exceeded KRW 2.7 billion, according to a press report from May 2026.

The shift toward products shows that a company that began in data and services is growing its top line through hardware.

The climate risk management solution and climate adaptation platform are expanding the data arm, and the company said in a September 2025 report that it works with large groups including Doosan, GS and POSCO.

On the public side, the government plans to spend about KRW 1 trillion through 2027 on air quality devices for school cooking rooms, and the company said it signed supply contracts with more than 20 schools for its integrated kitchen air management system.

Key customers are construction companies installing ventilation purifiers in new buildings, public institutions and corporations. Reports from late 2024 and early 2026 repeatedly cited slow construction activity as delaying completions and product installations.

Competition has also intensified: the weather forecasting service market, where Kweather was once the only player, has grown to about 30 companies, according to a February 2024 report.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual revenue fell to KRW 14.7 billion in 2023 (down 15.6% year on year), then rose to KRW 15.6 billion in 2024 (up 6.1%) and KRW 17.7 billion in 2025 (up 13.5%). Revenue has grown in each of 2024 and 2025, and 2025 was the largest year in the 2022 to 2025 range, while 2023 was the smallest.

Profitability has not yet recovered. Operating income of KRW 769 million in 2022 turned into an operating loss of KRW 2.47 billion in 2023, followed by losses of KRW 2.06 billion in 2024 and KRW 1.73 billion in 2025, so the loss has narrowed.

The operating margin moved from -16.8% in 2023 to -13.2% in 2024 and -9.8% in 2025. Net income attributable to controlling shareholders went from KRW 709 million in 2022 to a net loss of KRW 2.44 billion in 2023, then KRW 1.89 billion in 2024 and KRW 1.74 billion in 2025.

Net income including non-controlling interests was a net loss of KRW 1.88 billion in 2024, slightly different from the controlling-shareholder figure. Operating cash flow went from KRW 1.7 billion in 2022 to negative KRW 2.78 billion in 2023, negative KRW 2.29 billion in 2024 and negative KRW 971 million in 2025.

The outflow narrowed, but cash continued to leave the business. The debt ratio fell from 75.4% in 2023 to 42.3% in 2024 and 42.0% in 2025. At year-end 2025, total equity was KRW 11.5 billion, of which KRW 11.3 billion belonged to controlling shareholders, and total liabilities were KRW 4.84 billion.

Quarterly, Q3 2025 revenue was KRW 4.41 billion with an operating loss of KRW 243 million, and Q4 2025 revenue of KRW 4.61 billion came with a narrower operating loss of KRW 102 million. Q1 2026 revenue was KRW 3.94 billion, down 14.5% from the prior quarter, but operating income turned positive at KRW 59.42 million.

Q2 2026 revenue rose 37.6% to KRW 5.42 billion with operating income of KRW 154 million. Q3 2026 revenue of KRW 4.45 billion fell 17.8% from the prior quarter and rose only 0.9% year on year, and an operating loss of KRW 22.53 million returned.

Controlling-shareholder net income was KRW 115 million in Q1 2026 and KRW 211 million in Q2 2026, then a net loss of KRW 175 million in Q3 2026. The sum of controlling-shareholder net losses over the four quarters from Q4 2025 to Q3 2026 is KRW 25.37 million.

A press report from May 2026 attributed the Q1 2026 swing mainly to a gross margin rise from 18.1% to 40.7% year on year, while the company cited certification costs and listing-related expenses as factors behind earlier losses in a February 2026 report.

The gap between the 2025 revenue target of KRW 34.9 billion given before the IPO and the KRW 17.7 billion reported in the regular filing is also part of how past results should be read.

05

Industry analysis

The weather data industry is structured around public meteorological agencies and private weather companies operating side by side.

As of year-end 2023, Korea had about 1,000 registered weather business operators, of which 954 were equipment businesses, 35 were forecasting businesses and 62 were consulting businesses, according to a February 2024 report.

Kweather is registered in most weather business categories except appraisal services, which the report presented as a point of differentiation. Analysts said in early 2024 that growing competition in private forecasting has made revenue growth harder to sustain.

The construction cycle is the largest swing factor: an early 2026 report cited a construction downturn as the reason for delayed orders and deliveries behind the losses. Government budgets for air quality improvement in school cooking rooms support demand for air management products.

Globally, a report citing the market research firm Stratistics MRC projected the ultra-precise weather forecasting market to reach USD 8.52 billion by 2034. On technology, the shift toward AI forecast models built on NVIDIA's FourCastNet and CorrDiff is clear.

CEO Dong-sik Kim said in September 2026 that competitiveness in AI forecasting depends on how high-quality data is used to train models on GPU infrastructure.

Compared with larger listed companies in the same IT and software sector, Kweather's revenue base is small, so industry swings have a relatively larger effect on its results.

06

Outlook

On August 14, 2026, when it announced first-half 2026 results, the company said it would keep improving profitability in its existing businesses in the second half and push new businesses such as its weather AI factory to improve annual results.

CEO Dong-sik Kim described the goal as a full-year return to profitability as a profitable company this year, and no numerical guidance was disclosed.

The first product from the new businesses, ten AI weather forecast products, launched in September 2026, covering global forecasts up to 60 days ahead and very short-range precipitation.

The 60-day forecast is being developed on a Korean AI forecasting model built with NVIDIA's FourCastNet and CorrDiff, with a final target of 1 km grid data updated every hour.

The company plans a trial service in the first half of 2027 and said it is testing models from Google and Microsoft in parallel (reports from May and September 2026). In October 2026, the company posted news on expanding supply of IoT smart ventilation purifiers on its news page.

At the regular shareholder meeting in March 2026, blockchain and digital asset businesses were added to the articles of incorporation. The company is signaling an expansion into climate data assets and weather finance, but how much these new businesses will contribute to results has not yet shown up in the numbers.

There is also a gap against past projections: the KRW 34.9 billion 2025 revenue target presented during the IPO preparation compares with the KRW 17.7 billion reported in the 2025 regular filing. The next regular report's operating margin is therefore the checkpoint for judging the full-year outlook.

08

Bull factors

Quarterly profit recurrence

Operating income turned positive at KRW 59.42 million in Q1 2026, and Q2 2026 operating income rose 158.6% from the prior quarter to KRW 154 million. Q2 revenue increased 37.6% to KRW 5.42 billion.

A May 2026 report said the gross margin rose from 18.1% to 40.7% year on year in Q1, pointing to lower cost ratios as the main driver of the improvement.

Narrowing losses and a firmer balance sheet

The operating loss narrowed from KRW 2.47 billion in 2023 to KRW 2.06 billion in 2024 and KRW 1.73 billion in 2025, and the operating margin improved from -16.8% to -9.8%. The debt ratio declined from 75.4% in 2023 to 42.0% in 2025. A May 2026 report put the Q1 2026 debt ratio at 26.6%, reflecting the effect of the capital raise.

Expansion in AI and weather data

Ten AI weather forecast products launched in September 2026, and a trial of 60-day forecasts is planned for the first half of 2027. In March 2026, a Nasdaq-listed UK-headquartered energy company invested KRW 6.3 billion in a rights placement and became the second-largest shareholder with a 16.67% stake. The company said the proceeds will fund climate risk forecasting solutions.

09

Bear factors

Continued operating losses and cash outflows

Operating losses ran from 2023 through 2025, and operating cash flow stayed negative over the same period, at negative KRW 971 million in 2025. A May 2026 report said Q1 2026 operating cash outflow widened to KRW 1.5 billion from KRW 600 million a year earlier. Even with profitable quarters, cash generation has not clearly improved.

Return to a Q3 loss and flat sales

Q3 2026 returned to an operating loss of KRW 22.53 million. Q3 revenue of KRW 4.45 billion rose only 0.9% year on year. Controlling-shareholder net losses over the four quarters from Q4 2025 to Q3 2026 total KRW 25.37 million.

The 2025 revenue of KRW 17.7 billion fell well short of the KRW 34.9 billion target presented during the IPO preparation.

Construction cycle and rising competition

Reports from late 2024 and early 2026 repeatedly cite a construction downturn as delaying completions and product installations in new buildings, which pushed back orders and deliveries.

The company has said that because its results weigh heavily on Q3 and Q4, construction delays can shift when revenue is recognized within the year. Weather forecasting service providers have grown from one, Kweather, to about 30 companies, according to a February 2024 report.

10

Risk factors

Funding and dilution

The March 2026 KRW 6.3 billion third-party rights placement issued new shares equal to 20% of existing shares. The new shares carry a one-year lock-up that is expected to expire around April 2027.

Rising AI infrastructure spending could require additional funding, and changes in the share count could affect the shareholder mix.

Execution and timing of new businesses

The blockchain and digital asset business purpose, the weather AI factory and the 60-day AI forecast are still in trial or planning stages. The company says testing models from Google and Microsoft alongside NVIDIA will improve accuracy, but the timing of commercialization and revenue contribution are uncertain. If development costs rise before revenue arrives, the narrowing of losses could be delayed.

Dependence on construction and public orders

Revenue from ventilation purifiers in new buildings and air management in school cooking rooms depends on construction activity and the pace of government budget execution. The government has reportedly budgeted about KRW 1 trillion through 2027, but a change in the execution schedule could shift order timing. With products making up roughly 70% of sales, delays in orders translate directly into revenue swings.

12

Overall view

Kweather's regular filings show a narrowing operating loss and a stronger balance sheet. The operating loss shrank from KRW 2.47 billion in 2023 to KRW 1.73 billion in 2025, and the debt ratio was 42.0% at year-end 2025. Q1 and Q2 2026 produced quarterly operating profits.

On the other side, Q3 2026 returned to an operating loss of KRW 22.53 million, and operating cash flow stayed negative through 2025. Revenue has grown in 2024 and 2025, but the KRW 17.7 billion reached in 2025 fell well short of the target set during IPO preparation.

Supporting factors on the positive side include the improved gross margin, the capital raise and the launch of AI forecast products. On the negative side, dependence on construction, continued cash outflows and uncertainty over when new businesses will generate revenue remain.

On valuation, the PER cannot be calculated and the PBR is slightly above 1x. The next checkpoints are the operating margin in the 2026 annual report, whether quarterly profits hold in Q1 2027, and the first-half 2027 trial of AI forecasts.
