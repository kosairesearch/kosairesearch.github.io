02

Business structure

e-Credible is a credit certification specialist whose core business is B2B e-credit certification, which digitizes how large corporations register and manage their suppliers.

Suppliers register on a platform that links credit evaluation results with certificates in real time, and the company earns fees from suppliers while providing supply-chain risk management tools to parent companies.

According to a sector breakdown compiled by AlphaSquare (compilation date not stated in the source), e-credit certificate and credit inquiry services account for 69.70% of the mix, technology evaluation services for 19.80%, corporate information services such as Withspool for 6.80%, and payment-type B2B and other businesses such as TAMZ and EEPSS for 3.80%.

Technology credit evaluation (TCB) is being expanded in line with the government's push to widen technology financing, and the company is also extending into credit evaluation for public tenders.

Customers fall into two groups, large parent companies and their suppliers, so demand from parents to manage suppliers is the base of revenue. The repeated cycle of document submission and grade assignment is generally read as a high barrier to new entrants.

NICE Information Service is among listed companies in the same industry, but e-Credible's model is focused on the narrow niche of supplier certification for large companies. It is not a capital-intensive business and does not require large facility investment.

Revenue concentration by customer and segment-level sales are items to check in the periodic reports. Annual revenue was about KRW 50 billion in 2025, and because credit inquiry services make up a large share, changes in parent-company demand feed directly into results.

04

Earnings analysis

On a disclosed annual basis, revenue fell from KRW 47.1 billion in 2022 to KRW 41.1 billion in 2023, a 12.8% decline, and then rose to KRW 45.1 billion in 2024 (up 9.7%) and KRW 50 billion in 2025 (up 10.9%), so it grew in both 2024 and 2025.

Operating profit went from KRW 19.3 billion in 2022 to KRW 12.6 billion in 2023, a 34.6% decrease and the smallest year in the period, before recovering to KRW 15 billion in 2024 (up 18.7%) and KRW 15.6 billion in 2025 (up 4.1%).

Operating margin dropped from 40.9% in 2022 to 30.7% in 2023, then reached 33.2% in 2024 and 31.1% in 2025, staying in the 30% range. In 2025 revenue grew 10.9% while operating profit grew only 4.1%, which lowered the margin from 33.2% to 31.1%.

Owner-attributable net profit was KRW 15.7 billion in 2022, KRW 11.7 billion in 2023 (down 25.0%), KRW 12.9 billion in 2024 (up 9.9%) and KRW 12.9 billion in 2025 (down 0.3%), and net profit was disclosed at the same amounts.

Operating cash flow was KRW 17.9 billion in 2022, KRW 12 billion in 2023, KRW 14 billion in 2024 and KRW 13.2 billion in 2025, staying above KRW 10 billion each year. Quarterly results were concentrated in Q2 2026.

Revenue of KRW 87.9 million... more precisely, KRW 8.79 billion in Q1 2026 and operating profit of KRW 693 million rose to KRW 23 billion in revenue (up 162.0% from the prior quarter) and KRW 12.3 billion in operating profit in Q2, then fell to KRW 10.2 billion in revenue (down 55.5% from the prior quarter) and KRW 1.35 billion in operating profit (down 89.0%) in Q3.

Q3 operating profit declined 10.7% year on year and revenue rose only 5.4%. Combined operating profit for January to September 2026 is about KRW 14.3 billion, and whether the Q2 concentration reflects one-off contracts or recurring demand is a point to check in the next periodic report's revenue discussion.

05

Industry analysis

The credit certification market moves with large corporations' demand to manage their supply chains. A parent-company structure in which supplier registration and credit checks repeat forms the base of demand, which sets it apart from manufacturing businesses tied directly to capital spending or the business cycle.

An industry official cited in a December 2023 Economist report pointed to rising demand for corporate credit evaluation services and the company's growing market influence as drivers of earnings growth.

Government policy to expand technology financing supports demand for technology credit evaluation (TCB), a tailwind for the business. The company's business summary also cites demand from downstream industries and ESG evaluation as drivers of revenue growth in the first nine months of 2025.

Against NICE Information Service, e-Credible is specialized in supplier certification and the scope of its business differs. On the cycle, the recovery in annual revenue during 2024 and 2025 after the 2023 low is the reference point. On the weak side, revenue growth in Q3 2026 was only 5.4% year on year.

In scale, the company's roughly KRW 50 billion in annual revenue is far smaller than NAVER or Samsung SDS in the same industry.

Outlook

The company disclosed a 2026 corporate value-up plan that names new market entry and stable revenue creation through high-quality services as goals. Disclosed revenue for January to September 2026 totals about KRW 42 billion, and operating profit about KRW 14.3 billion.

The FnGuide consensus projects 2026 revenue of KRW 51.5 billion and operating profit of KRW 16.8 billion, a figure compiled before the 2025 results release. Against that consensus, the nine-month operating profit of about KRW 14.3 billion leaves the fourth quarter as the swing factor for the full-year figure.

Medium-term growth drivers cited include expansion of TCB under government technology financing policy and wider credit evaluation for public tenders. Technology credit evaluation supports the expansion into corporate information services across private and public sectors.

On the other hand, because the certification fee model depends on parent companies' supplier management policies, a change in those policies could alter revenue paths, making customer structure the most direct variable. Whether the Q2-heavy pattern repeats determines how predictable full-year earnings are. The 2026 fourth-quarter preliminary results and the 2026 annual report are the next checkpoints.

08

Bull factors

Operating margin held in the 30% range

Operating profit rose 4.1% to KRW 15.6 billion in 2025. The generally cited reason for the high margin is a business model that is not capital-intensive. Keeping margins high even as revenue grows modestly is the bullish point.

Two straight years of revenue growth and low debt

Revenue recovered after the 2023 low, rising 9.7% in 2024 and 10.9% in 2025 to KRW 50 billion. The debt-to-equity ratio was 23.4% in 2025, up slightly from 22.3% in 2022 but still low. Total liabilities were KRW 9.99 billion at year-end 2025, against KRW 42.7 billion of equity, all owner-attributable. The balance sheet is a bullish factor.

Expansion into policy and public-sector areas

Technology credit evaluation is tied to the government's push to expand technology financing. The company is widening into credit evaluation for public tenders, adding demand beyond the private large-company supplier market. Its 2026 value-up plan also names new market entry as a goal. Broadening the business base is cited on the bullish side.

Bear factors

Quarterly concentration and Q3 slowdown

Revenue was KRW 23 billion in Q2 2026 against KRW 10.2 billion in Q3, and operating profit fell from KRW 12.3 billion to KRW 1.35 billion. Q3 operating profit declined 10.7% year on year, and owner-attributable net profit of KRW 1.3 billion fell 4.9%.

When profit clusters in one quarter, full-year earnings become harder to predict. This is cited as a bearish point.

Flat earnings and margin decline

Owner-attributable net profit was flat at KRW 12.9 billion in 2025, down 0.3% from 2024. Operating margin fell from 33.2% in 2024 to 31.1% in 2025. Revenue rose 10.9% in 2025 but operating profit only 4.1%, so sales growth did not fully carry through to profit. Whether this earnings stall continues is the central bearish question.

Dependence on parent-company demand and limited growth

The revenue model depends on large parent companies' demand to manage suppliers. A policy change at a parent company could alter fee-based revenue paths. Q3 2026 revenue rose only 5.4% year on year. Limited pace of top-line growth is the bearish case.

Risk factors

Earnings volatility

Revenue jumped to KRW 23 billion in Q2 2026 and fell to KRW 10.2 billion in Q3. If quarterly results depend on specific contracts or timing, full-year trends are hard to forecast. Whether the Q2 concentration was one-off or recurring needs confirmation.

Parent-company policy and regulatory changes

Demand to register suppliers depends on large parent companies' supply-chain management policies. If rules on technology financing or public-tender credit evaluation change, the direction of credit evaluation demand could shift. Regulatory change can challenge the assumptions behind the business model. Policy direction should be tracked through periodic reports and company disclosures.

Small-cap liquidity and shareholder base

As a small-cap KOSDAQ stock, trading liquidity may be limited. Foreign ownership was 9.77% as of September 7, 2026. With a small foreign share, supply and demand shifts can amplify price moves. Share prices may be sensitive to trading flows apart from earnings.

Overall view

e-Credible operates a model centered on e-credit certification and has kept operating margin in the 30% range since 2023. Revenue grew in both 2024 and 2025, year-end 2025 equity of KRW 42.7 billion is entirely owner-attributable, and the debt-to-equity ratio was 23.4%.

The next checkpoints are the 2026 fourth-quarter preliminary results and the 2026 annual report, and the nature of the Q2 concentration and customer structure will shape how predictable full-year earnings are.
