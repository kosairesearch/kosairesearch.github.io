02

Business structure

Orient Precision Industries was founded in 1987 to manufacture and sell automotive parts and listed on KOSDAQ in 2002. As a supplier to Hyundai Motor and KIA, it has produced aluminum-machined components such as transmission control housings and engine parts.

The company is now broadening its lineup toward electrified parts, supplying Shift-by-Wire (SBW) gear sets and differential assemblies for dedicated eco-friendly vehicle platforms to major customers.

Management attributes these products to precision machining, die-casting and quality control capabilities built over years of combustion-engine parts production.

EV-related parts account for about 10% of sales, and in August 2026 the company set a goal of raising that share to around 50% within two to three years through new production lines, added capacity and overseas customer growth.

The expansion extends into thermal management materials: in October 2026 the company said it had completed in-house development of a gap pad for batteries, while urethane adhesives and magnesium oxide gap fillers are in customer validation.

On November 12, 2026 it announced a memorandum of understanding with a drive-component developer to extend its drive parts into defense, robotics and future-mobility applications. Industry structure is a constraint, as larger and more modular suppliers and expanding competitive bidding intensify price competition.

Revenue remains tied to carmaker production volumes, so output and labor strikes at Hyundai Motor and KIA pass directly into results.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual results show diverging trends in sales and profitability. Revenue rose from KRW 134.3 billion in 2022 to KRW 162.8 billion in 2023 (up 21.2% year on year) and reached KRW 173.4 billion in 2024 (up 6.5%), the largest year in the 2022 to 2025 period.

Revenue fell 9.4% to KRW 157.1 billion in 2025, which the company attributed to lower sales volumes and revenue after strikes at Hyundai Motor and other carmakers in the second half of 2025.

Operating profit swung from an operating loss of KRW 1.54 billion in 2022 to operating income of KRW 1.98 billion in 2023, then declined 38.2% to KRW 1.22 billion in 2024 and 25.3% to KRW 912 million in 2025, two consecutive years of decline after the 2023 peak.

The operating margin stayed low, at -1.1% in 2022, 1.2% in 2023, 0.7% in 2024 and 0.6% in 2025. Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders turned positive at KRW 1.8 billion in 2025, and the company cited partial improvement at a subsidiary and lower tax expense from recognizing deferred tax assets.

That means tax items and subsidiary effects, rather than operating improvement, weighed heavily on the swing to profit. Losses attributable to controlling shareholders narrowed from KRW 6.68 billion in 2022 and KRW 5.68 billion in 2023 to KRW 10.54 million in 2024.

At the end of 2025, total equity was KRW 39.6 billion, all attributable to controlling shareholders, total liabilities were KRW 58.7 billion, and the debt ratio fell from 161.4% in 2024 to 148.0%.

Quarterly, third-quarter 2025 revenue of KRW 39 billion and operating profit of KRW 393 million gave way to fourth-quarter 2025 revenue of KRW 37 billion and an operating loss of KRW 965 million.

First-quarter 2026 revenue was KRW 37.5 billion with an operating loss of KRW 60.33 million, and second-quarter revenue rose 16.9% quarter on quarter to KRW 43.8 billion while the operating loss widened to KRW 273 million.

Third-quarter 2026 revenue was KRW 41.6 billion, up 6.6% year on year, yet the operating loss of KRW 1.15 billion was a swing from profit a year earlier.

Revenue growth without profit gains in several quarters is the core issue in this period, and net losses attributable to controlling shareholders over the latest four quarters total KRW 2.69 billion.

05

Industry analysis

Auto parts demand depends on carmaker production and sales volumes and is sensitive to economic cycles. Observers point to larger and more modular suppliers and expanding competitive bidding as intensifying price competition.

Lightweighting is raising demand for plastic parts, and the company says it is focusing on new materials and metal-replacement components.

On the demand side, strikes at Hyundai Motor and other carmakers reduced sales volumes in the second half of 2025, and a reported industrial activity release showed auto production falling more than 20% from the prior month.

The shift to electrification is a structural trend, and the company says its eco-friendly vehicle parts sales have grown about 20% a year, although this is the company's own figure and no direct comparison with overall market growth was found.

In thermal materials, the company argues that higher-performance EVs and larger battery packs are increasing demand for heat-dissipation materials.

On competitive position, the company points to its machining and mass-production capabilities inside carmaker supply chains, but compared with peers such as Hyundai Mobis, Hyundai WIA and Hanon Systems, its scale and EV parts share are much smaller.

New businesses in thermal interface materials and robot or defense components remain at an early stage with no clearly confirmed revenue contribution. The company therefore sits at a point where softer demand for legacy parts overlaps with the electrification transition.

06

Outlook

The largest stated goal is raising EV-related parts to around 50% of sales within two to three years. To get there, the company said in August 2026 that it is pursuing new parts production and added capacity, with new production expected to start by early 2027.

The company says proceeds from a third-party share issuance of KRW 2.8975 billion, subscribed by affiliate Orientbio, will go entirely to new equipment for eco-friendly vehicle parts. It expects the new equipment to substantially expand production capacity for its differential assemblies.

In September 2026 the company said it is building in-house design and analysis capabilities for differentials to expand overseas customers.

In new businesses, the gap pad completed in October 2026 is in customer performance validation and supply talks, and urethane adhesive is being prepared for mass production after customer testing.

In November 2026 the company said it was selected as a demonstration company for the Gyeonggi Physical AI Diffusion Center and plans to apply AMR-based logistics automation in its electronics division before considering expansion to EV parts lines.

The cooperation with the drive-component firm is at the stage of reviewing specific products and commercial feasibility, with individual business terms to be negotiated later.

The 2-for-1 par value consolidation announced in September 2026 was approved at the extraordinary shareholders meeting on November 10, 2026, with a record date of November 25, 2026. Whether these milestones translate into higher sales and operating profit is the key question going forward.

Bull factors

Stated target for a larger EV parts share

The company says it aims to raise its EV-related parts share from about 10% to around 50% within two to three years. It also reports that eco-friendly vehicle parts sales have grown about 20% a year. New production and added capacity are scheduled by early 2027, giving a clear checkpoint for execution.

Swing to profit in 2025

Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders reached KRW 1.8 billion in 2025, reversing a net loss in 2024. Operating cash flow rose to KRW 2.73 billion in 2025 from KRW 2.1 billion in 2024. The debt ratio also fell from 161.4% in 2024 to 148.0% in 2025, easing some financial pressure.

Expanding new-business pipeline

The company has completed a gap pad for batteries, is preparing urethane adhesive for mass production and is pursuing drive-component cooperation. It is also building in-house differential design capability to win overseas customers. If these businesses are confirmed in sales, they could reduce dependence on carmaker output.

Bear factors

Four consecutive quarters of operating losses

The company recorded operating losses in each quarter from the fourth quarter of 2025 through the third quarter of 2026, with the third-quarter 2026 loss of KRW 1.15 billion a swing from profit a year earlier. Second-quarter 2026 revenue rose to KRW 43.8 billion, yet an operating loss of KRW 273 million continued. Revenue growth has not yet translated into operating profit.

Thin operating margin and carmaker dependence

The operating margin has stayed around or below 1%, moving from -1.1% in 2022 to 0.6% in 2025. Strikes at Hyundai Motor and other carmakers cut sales volumes and revenue in the second half of 2025. In a market with intensifying price competition, concentration on carmaker customers weighs on profitability.

Concerns over balance sheet changes

Pro forma summary financials in the business acquisition disclosed in 2026 show total liabilities rising from KRW 58.6 billion to about KRW 81.5 billion and total equity falling from KRW 39.6 billion to about KRW 32.8 billion.

The largest shareholder's stake was stated to have moved from 25.3% to 24.15% after the transaction. These pro forma figures are separate from the reported 2025 results.

Risk factors

Execution of the EV transition

The 50% EV parts target depends on new production and capacity additions staying on schedule. The gap pad and urethane adhesive are still in customer testing and validation, and uncertainty remains until they show up as production sales. With a two-to-three-year horizon, any delay could push the target back.

Financial and capital structure

Total liabilities at the end of 2025 were KRW 58.7 billion, larger than total equity of KRW 39.6 billion. The pro forma summary in the business acquisition shows higher liabilities and lower equity, so the balance sheet trend needs close tracking.

Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders alternated between profit and loss from the first quarter through the third quarter of 2026.

Trading and share structure

On November 5, 2026, the Korea Exchange designated the stock as a short-term investment caution issue for concentrated trading in a small number of accounts. Over the three-day period the shares rose 21.48%, and the top 10 accounts accounted for 42.07% of buying.

Trading suspension tied to the 2-for-1 consolidation could change the float and trading pattern in a short period.

Overall view

Orient Precision Industries is an auto parts maker whose revenue depends on Hyundai Motor and KIA production, and it is now widening into EV parts, thermal management materials and robot or defense components.

Its disclosed results show revenue peaking at KRW 173.4 billion in 2024 before falling in 2025, with the operating margin at only 0.6%. Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders turned positive at KRW 1.8 billion in 2025, but the company cited tax items and subsidiary effects as major factors.

Operating losses have continued for four consecutive quarters from the fourth quarter of 2025 through the third quarter of 2026, with net losses attributable to controlling shareholders totaling KRW 2.69 billion over the latest four quarters.

The company has set a target of about 50% EV-related parts within two to three years and a production schedule through early 2027, and whether these show up in sales and profit is the central question.

The trading suspension and 2-for-1 consolidation scheduled from November 23, 2026 will also change how per-share figures compare going forward. The next checkpoints are the operating margin in the 2026 annual business report and whether the new production lines start operating in early 2027.
