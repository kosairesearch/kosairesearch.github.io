02

Business structure

TOP Material was founded in 2012 as a secondary-battery equipment and materials company and listed on KOSDAQ in 2022. Its businesses are system engineering, electrode materials and LFP cathode materials.

System engineering means turnkey supply of complete battery production lines, including installation, commissioning and training. Press reports put the system engineering share of 2023 revenue at about 87%.

In May 2026 the company disclosed a single supply contract with ASPiLSAN Enerji for an LFP battery manufacturing line.

A U.S. subsidiary, TOP MATERIAL Corp., was set up in January 2026, and shortly after it won a USD 10 million project from ECF (Electrochemistry Foundry) in California, under which it will operate the facility for two years. The electrode plant moved from Asan to Pyeongtaek in July 2026, and the Asan branch was closed.

The Pyeongtaek LFP plant has annual capacity of up to 3,000 tons. LFP cathode supply has been dominated by Chinese producers, while domestic peers such as Ecopro BM are also pursuing LFP development. A press report said the dry precursor-free process could reduce reliance on Chinese precursors.

04

Earnings analysis

Revenue rose from KRW 63 billion in 2022 to KRW 122.2 billion in 2023 (up 93.9% year on year), the largest year in the 2022–2025 period. It then fell 13.2% to KRW 106.1 billion in 2024 and 78.2% to KRW 23.1 billion in 2025, a two-year decline from 2024 through 2025.

Operating profit was KRW 11.8 billion in 2022 and KRW 13.8 billion in 2023 (up 17.1%), then turned into an operating loss of KRW 7.21 billion in 2024 that widened to KRW 35.2 billion in 2025. Operating margin moved from 18.7% in 2022 and 11.3% in 2023 to -6.8% in 2024 and -152.2% in 2025.

A third-party summary attributes the operating loss to heavy facility investment and higher R&D spending. Controlling shareholder net income was KRW 10.6 billion in 2022 and KRW 17.6 billion in 2023 (up 66.5%), then a net loss of KRW 8.16 billion in 2024 and KRW 30.7 billion in 2025.

Net income including non-controlling interests matches these figures in each year. Operating cash flow fell from KRW 13.7 billion in 2022 to negative KRW 18 billion in 2023 and negative KRW 29.7 billion in 2024, then turned positive at KRW 782 million in 2025.

Press reports said capital expenditure in the first half of 2025 was KRW 23.6 billion, more than triple the prior-year period, and that the gap was funded by bank borrowing. Total equity at end-2025 was KRW 109.3 billion and total liabilities KRW 78.2 billion.

Quarterly, Q4 2025 revenue was KRW 4.37 billion, down 56.9% from the prior quarter, with an operating loss of KRW 22.5 billion, the largest of the last five quarters.

Q1 2026 revenue was KRW 8.07 billion with an operating loss of KRW 2.74 billion, Q2 revenue KRW 5.08 billion with an operating loss of KRW 9.65 billion, and Q3 revenue KRW 8.91 billion with an operating loss of KRW 2.2 billion.

In Q3 2026 the operating loss narrowed, but the controlling shareholder net loss widened to KRW 12.2 billion from a year earlier, so the operating and net-income lines moved in different directions.

A third-party tracker reported first-half 2026 revenue up 52.6% year on year, with the operating loss and net loss up 46.3% and 84.6%.

05

Industry analysis

LFP cathode demand is described as rising with the growth of the ESS market, and a press report noted that the safety, low-cost and long-life traits of LFP cathodes are driving demand. The company said it aims to address rapidly growing LFP demand from global EV and ESS markets.

On the supply side, Chinese producers hold a large share, which raises barriers for later entrants. Domestic peers including Ecopro BM are also developing and mass-producing LFP cathodes, making competition more visible. In a January 2026 report the company said the 2025 secondary-battery downturn had hit it.

System engineering depends on battery capex cycles, and the company linked its growth to rising global battery equipment investment. Its technology position rests on applying a precursor-free process to high-voltage mid-nickel cathodes.

In the U.S., the company set up a local subsidiary to respond to tariff-related hurdles in winning orders. Whether this technology edge converts into customer adoption and large orders should be checked against future disclosed contract sizes.

Outlook

The LFP mass-production timeline has shifted once. In a December 2025 report the company planned pilot production in the first half of 2026 and full mass production from the second half.

As of September 2026, the company said its roughly 3,000-ton mother line was in pilot production and that it was pursuing mass production and ESS market entry within the year.

So the plan moved from second-half 2026 mass production to a within-2026 target, and whether production actually starts should be checked through disclosures and reports through late December 2026. On orders, the U.S. unit has a two-year operating structure starting from the ECF project.

In Korea the company disclosed a May 2026 supply contract for a lithium iron phosphate battery line, and the end date of a battery assembly line remodeling contract was corrected to December 31, 2026. The electrode business is consolidating into Pyeongtaek.

In a January 2026 report the company said it aims to post its best results since founding through large project wins and full-scale materials sales; this is a company target. Whether Q3 2026 revenue of KRW 8.91 billion and an operating loss of KRW 2.2 billion support that target is the next check.

Because several contract end dates have been extended, the timing of revenue recognition on these orders also needs monitoring. The 2026 annual report, the next regular filing, is where Q4 2026 results and LFP plant utilization are first confirmed.

08

Bull factors

Possible LFP production ramp-up

The company completed a Pyeongtaek LFP cathode plant with annual capacity of up to 3,000 tons. As of September 2026 it was running pilot production of its mother line, targeting mass production and ESS market entry within the year.

The company says adding cathode production broadens revenue beyond system engineering and electrodes. It also cites orders already secured from some customers as support for early utilization.

U.S. project base expanding

Right after its January 2026 founding, the U.S. unit won a USD 10 million system engineering project from ECF. The company says a local subsidiary is meant to overcome order difficulties linked to U.S. tariff policy.

Because the unit will operate the facility for two years after turnkey delivery, the company sees a longer revenue runway from each order.

Quarterly losses narrowing

The operating loss narrowed from KRW 22.5 billion in Q4 2025 to KRW 2.74 billion in Q1 2026 and KRW 2.2 billion in Q3 2026. Q3 2026 revenue of KRW 8.91 billion rose 75.4% from the prior quarter. Operating cash flow turned positive at KRW 782 million in 2025, compared with negative KRW 29.7 billion in 2024.

Bear factors

Continuous losses accumulating

Losses have run for five consecutive quarters from Q3 2025 through Q3 2026. The controlling shareholder net loss over the last four quarters totals KRW 45.1 billion. In Q3 2026 the operating loss narrowed, but the controlling shareholder net loss rose to KRW 12.2 billion from a year earlier. The 2025 operating loss of KRW 35.2 billion was larger than the 2024 loss.

Balance sheet and borrowing reliance

The debt-to-equity ratio rose from 26.0% in 2022 to 71.6% in 2025. Capex expanded in the first half of 2025 and was reportedly funded by bank borrowing. An August 2025 report said that without an improvement in cash generation, financial soundness could weaken further.

Shifting contract and ramp-up timelines

Between May and October 2026, the company filed at least three corrections extending contract end dates on single supply contracts. One June 2026 correction moved an end date to December 31, 2026. The LFP ramp-up has also shifted from a second-half 2026 plan to a within-year target. Slower order timing would delay the return on invested capital.

Risk factors

Financing and balance sheet

Capex in the first half of 2025 was not covered by operating cash and relied on borrowing. Total liabilities were KRW 78.2 billion against KRW 109.3 billion of equity at end-2025. If losses continue and debt keeps rising, the cost of additional financing could increase. Whether the positive operating cash flow of 2025 holds is the item to watch.

Ramp-up execution and utilization

The Pyeongtaek LFP plant was in pilot production as of September 2026, and the timing of mass production has already been adjusted once. Whether the 3,000-ton annual capacity turns into actual utilization remains to be shown.

As a later entrant in a market led by Chinese producers, the company must pass customer qualification while competing on price.

Shareholder structure and liquidity

One tracker puts the major shareholder group's stake at 58.21% with a free float of 39.72%. Another source reports the largest shareholder at 36.32%, so the figures differ by source. A low free-float share can make the trading price more sensitive to limited volume.

Overall view

TOP Material peaked at KRW 122.2 billion in revenue in 2023, then saw revenue decline for two consecutive years from 2024 to 2025 while losses continued.

It is a secondary-battery materials and system engineering company whose equity stood at KRW 109.3 billion and total liabilities at KRW 78.2 billion at end-2025, with the debt ratio rising.

Since the KRW 22.5 billion operating loss in Q4 2025, operating losses narrowed in Q1 and Q3 2026, and operating cash flow turned positive in 2025. The business is broadening from turnkey system engineering orders into LFP cathode production, but the Pyeongtaek plant was still in pilot production as of September 2026.

Bullish factors are the possible LFP ramp-up, U.S. orders and narrowing quarterly losses, while bearish factors are five consecutive quarters of losses, a net loss of KRW 45.1 billion over the last four quarters, reliance on borrowing and extended contract timelines.

Valuation is described by a PER that cannot be calculated and a PBR below its historical annual lows, which should be read alongside whether earnings recover.

The next checkpoints are whether LFP mass production starts by late December 2026, the December 31, 2026 contract end date, and the 2026 annual report due by March 31, 2027.
