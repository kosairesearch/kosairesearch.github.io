02

Business structure

Inzisoft is an AI-based image and document recognition solutions company whose main customers are financial institutions.

Its core products are non-face-to-face identity verification and image systems built on automatic ID-card recognition (OCR) technology, and press reports describe much of the automatic ID capture in bank apps as running on its technology.

A 2024 press report said the company holds a high market share across first-tier and second-tier financial institutions in non-face-to-face identity verification and image systems.

Its cloud-based software-as-a-service product Q-Service is used for electronic documents, outdoor sales (ODS) solutions and digital counter systems, with the aim of shortening customer build periods and cutting costs.

Revenue is split between one-off build projects and maintenance and subscription services that continue after a build.

A 2024 report by a research institute said maintenance is free for the first year after a build and becomes paid thereafter, and estimated annual maintenance revenue at roughly 10% of the initial build revenue.

Robotic process automation (RPA) solutions were growing as of a 2024 press report but contributed a small share of revenue. A company official said the company's after-sales support and training are strengths as a domestic vendor.

In early 2024 the company won a KRW 6.4 billion document management system rebuild project for a large financial institution, which was described at the time as its largest single contract.

04

Earnings analysis

On a filed annual basis, sales rose from KRW 23.6 billion in 2022 to KRW 24.2 billion in 2023 (up 2.7% year on year), then fell to KRW 18.9 billion in 2024 (down 22.0%), and were flat at KRW 18.9 billion in 2025 (up 0.2%). Among 2022 to 2025, 2023 was the largest year and 2024 the smallest.

Profit declines were steeper than sales declines. Operating profit fell from KRW 4.86 billion in 2022 to KRW 3.81 billion in 2023 (down 21.6%) and KRW 1.06 billion in 2024 (down 72.1%), and the operating margin moved from 20.6% to 15.7% and then 5.6%.

Operating profit recovered to KRW 2.34 billion in 2025 (up 120.4%) and the operating margin rose to 12.4%, still below the 2022 level. Net income attributable to controlling shareholders bottomed at KRW 2.62 billion in 2024 (down 51.0%) and jumped to KRW 21.4 billion in 2025 (up 715.8%).

The gap from the KRW 2.34 billion operating profit is large, and the company attributed the improvement to higher non-operating income, including gains on financial asset valuation and disposal. The 2025 jump therefore reflects non-operating gains more than a recovery in core operations.

Quarterly, sales went from KRW 3.66 billion in the third quarter of 2025 with operating profit of KRW 188 million, to a peak of KRW 5.78 billion in the fourth quarter with operating profit of KRW 1.15 billion.

Sales then declined to KRW 4.61 billion in the first quarter of 2026, KRW 4.52 billion in the second and KRW 4.46 billion in the third, three consecutive quarterly declines. Operating profit was KRW 858 million, KRW 1.22 billion and KRW 813 million over the same three quarters, moving up and down.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 8.58 billion in the fourth quarter of 2025, KRW 10.2 billion in the first quarter of 2026 and KRW 47.5 billion in the second quarter, far above operating profit in each period, and the four-quarter total from the fourth quarter of 2025 to the third quarter of 2026 is KRW 45.1 billion.

Operating cash flow was KRW 10.3 billion in 2022, KRW 4.34 billion in 2023, KRW 4.45 billion in 2024 and KRW 6.12 billion in 2025, below 2022 but above 2024.

05

Industry analysis

The company's market depends heavily on demand for digital transformation in financial institutions.

Growth continued after COVID-19 as banks accelerated digital transformation, but a 2024 research report from a Korean investor relations council expected order intake to slow as customers, after several years of heavy spending, turned to more conservative solution investment.

That view lines up with the sales decline in 2024. Maintenance and subscription revenue, by contrast, recurs after a build, so it is less exposed to the swings that affect one-off project revenue, according to the company and industry commentary.

The company said it is reducing the weight of project revenue and expanding maintenance and subscription services. On competition, its technology lead in ID-card recognition OCR is cited as a strength, while expanding beyond financial customers is noted as a challenge.

Listed peers in the same IT and software sector include Ahnlab and Daou Technology, but Inzisoft concentrates on a narrow niche of ID recognition for financial customers, which limits direct comparison.

06

Outlook

On November 11, 2026, the board decided to buy back 102,933 common shares for KRW 2.0 billion on the open market to stabilize the share price and enhance shareholder value. The expected purchase period runs from November 12, 2026 to February 11, 2027, and treasury shares held before the purchase amounted to 27.90%.

The company has said it has maintained a high-dividend policy every year since 2022 as part of shareholder returns. Its executives said in 2024 that the business could extend beyond financial customers over the medium to long term.

Some brokerage notes from 2024 also raised the possibility of M&A using the company's cash, and expected subscription Q-Service revenue to raise the share of recurring billing. Those views date from 2024 and are not confirmed as current business plans. The next points to confirm are as follows.

During the buyback period ending February 11, 2027, the actual volume bought and any cancellation will appear in disclosures. The 2026 annual preliminary results are expected around mid-February 2027, following the 2025 preliminary results released on February 13, 2026.

The 2026 annual business report is due by March 31, 2027. Whether the sales decline that began in the first quarter of 2026 carries into the full year, and whether the 12.4% operating margin of 2025 holds, are the key items to watch.

08

Bull factors

Operating profit recovery

Operating profit rose 120.4% from KRW 1.06 billion in 2024 to KRW 2.34 billion in 2025, and the operating margin climbed from 5.6% to 12.4%. Operating profit in the second quarter of 2026 was KRW 1.22 billion, up 41.7% from the prior quarter. Whether the margin returns toward the 20.6% of 2022 is the point to confirm.

Balance sheet capacity and shareholder returns

Total equity at the end of 2025 was KRW 69.5 billion, total liabilities were KRW 13.5 billion, and the debt-to-equity ratio was 19.4%. The company decided on November 11, 2026 to buy back treasury shares, and has said it has kept a high-dividend policy every year since 2022. The balance sheet gives it room for shareholder returns.

Maintenance and subscription revenue base

Analysts describe the model in which maintenance is free for the first year after a build and paid thereafter as less volatile than project revenue. Growth in subscription Q-Service revenue was expected to raise the share of recurring billing. The continued use of paid maintenance by financial customers is cited as the basis for this structure.

Bear factors

Sales decline followed by stagnation

Sales fell 22.0% from KRW 24.2 billion in 2023 to KRW 18.9 billion in 2024, and rose only 0.2% in 2025. After a peak of KRW 5.78 billion in the fourth quarter of 2025, sales declined for three consecutive quarters through the third quarter of 2026, which came in at KRW 4.46 billion.

Quality of earnings and reliance on non-operating income

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 21.4 billion in 2025 was far above operating profit of KRW 2.34 billion, and the company cited higher non-operating income such as gains on financial asset valuation and disposal.

The KRW 47.5 billion posted in the second quarter of 2026 also far exceeds operating profit of KRW 1.22 billion for that quarter, suggesting a large non-operating component. Sustainability of earnings should be judged on operating profit.

Customer concentration and project volatility

The business is concentrated in financial institutions, and a 2024 research report expected weaker order intake as customers turned to conservative solution investment. Project revenue is strongly affected by seasonality and economic cycles. Operating profit in the third quarter of 2026 was KRW 813 million, down 33.1% from the prior quarter.

Risk factors

Earnings volatility

Project revenue carries large individual contracts but is heavily affected by seasonality and economic cycles. After KRW 5.78 billion in the fourth quarter of 2025, sales declined for three straight quarters, so whether the direction of quarterly results changes matters. Operating profit moved between roughly KRW 800 million and KRW 1.2 billion per quarter in 2026.

Dependence on non-operating gains

The 2025 jump in net income relied substantially on non-operating income such as gains on financial asset valuation and disposal. Such gains can reverse with market conditions, so they should be separated from operating earning power.

The KRW 45.1 billion four-quarter total of controlling-shareholder net income may also depend on non-operating items.

Liquidity and trading structure

With treasury shares at 27.90% and a largest shareholder stake in the mid-30s percent, the number of shares in circulation is limited. Under this structure, low trading volume can make price swings larger. Whether the buyback further reduces the float is also a point to confirm.

Overall view

Inzisoft is a small software company built around ID recognition for financial institutions and maintenance and subscription services. Sales fell sharply in 2024 after 2022, were flat in 2025, and declined for three consecutive quarters in 2026.

Operating profit recovered from the 2024 low to KRW 2.34 billion in 2025, but the 12.4% operating margin remains below the 20.6% of 2022.

Controlling-shareholder net income of KRW 21.4 billion in 2025 is heavily influenced by non-operating income, which the company itself cited, so it should be read apart from core operating profit.

The balance sheet looks stable with a 19.4% debt-to-equity ratio at the end of 2025, and the November 2026 treasury share purchase and high-dividend policy form the shareholder-return axis.

Price-to-book and dividend yield comparisons are cited as strengths, but high treasury share and largest-shareholder stakes leave a limited float.

The next checkpoints are the 2026 preliminary annual results expected in mid-February 2027, the 2026 annual business report due March 31, 2027, and the first-quarter 2027 report due May 15, 2027.
