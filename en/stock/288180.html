Sales Up, Profits Negative at KP Aero Industries

02

Business structure

KP Aero Industries, founded in 1990 and headquartered in Gimhae, Gyeongsangnam-do, makes aerospace and defense parts. Its core activities are machining and assembly of aircraft parts, design and manufacture of jigs and fixtures, and production of defense and space components.

In the aviation segment, the company said at a March 2026 briefing that it has produced structures for global aircraft programs including the A350 cargo door, A320 Sharklet, B737 winglet and B787 APU door. It cites 5-axis high-speed machining and automated equipment that meet the quality standards of Airbus and Boeing.

In defense and space, it mass-produces core components for domestic guided weapons such as Cheongung-II and Hyunmoo, using proprietary technology including split-type mandrel dies for solid-fuel motor casings. It also took part in the Naro launch vehicle fairing dies and Nuri rocket fuselage structures.

The company describes defense and space as a stable earnings base and aviation as a business entering strong global growth. Per a press report on first-half 2026 sales, Korean AIR Lines accounted for KRW 8.7 billion, Space Pro KRW 8.4 billion and Korea Aerospace Industries KRW 4.9 billion, together 78% of sales.

Space Pro is also a shareholder holding 9.02%. The company is expanding production through KP AEROSPACE VIETNAM, a wholly owned subsidiary whose Da Nang plant was completed in October 2024.

It listed on KOSDAQ on May 19, 2026 through a merger with NH SPAC 30, and its largest shareholder holds 16.26% with related parties holding 51.49%. Headcount is 454.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual sales rose from KRW 26.8 billion in 2022 to KRW 34.6 billion in 2023 (up 28.8% year on year), KRW 49.7 billion in 2024 (up 43.6%) and KRW 53.9 billion in 2025 (up 8.6%). Sales have grown for three consecutive years from 2023 through 2025, and 2025 was the largest year in the 2022 to 2025 span.

Operating profit went from KRW 499 million in 2022 to KRW 861 million in 2023 (up 72.5%) and KRW 3.49 billion in 2024 (up 305.1%), then fell to KRW 2.64 billion in 2025 (down 24.4%), with 2024 the largest year in the span. Operating margin rose from 1.9% in 2022 to 7.0% in 2024 before easing to 4.9% in 2025.

Attributable net income peaked at KRW 3.02 billion in 2024 (up 2,055.0%) and dropped to KRW 1.56 billion in 2025 (down 48.5%). Over the same period the debt ratio climbed from 290.7% to 378.9%, with year-end 2025 total equity at KRW 17.1 billion and total liabilities at KRW 64.8 billion.

Quarterly results turned from an operating loss of KRW 129 million in the third quarter of 2025 to operating profit of KRW 384 million in the fourth quarter, and KRW 591 million in the first quarter of 2026 (up 54.0% quarter on quarter).

The second quarter of 2026 swung to an operating loss of KRW 584 million, and the third quarter widened it to an operating loss of KRW 812 million, a deeper loss than a year earlier despite sales of KRW 15.2 billion (up 22.7% year on year).

Attributable net losses were KRW 13.8 billion in the second quarter and KRW 2.28 billion in the third, totaling KRW 15.5 billion over the last four quarters. Press reports say the gross margin fell from 18.8% to 13.4% in the first half of 2026 and the Vietnam unit posted an operating loss. The company called the non-cash merger costs booked in the second quarter one-off.

05

Industry analysis

Demand on the aviation side is reflected in efforts to upgrade manufacturing processes for mainstream programs such as the Airbus A320 and A330 and the Boeing 737 and 787.

The Aerospace Agency is running a KRW 18.5 billion aerospace parts process upgrade program from 2024 to 2028, in which KP Aero Industries participates as one of the lead organizations. A press report puts the company's aircraft wing structure revenue from that program at about KRW 4.0 billion.

On cost, the company said rising domestic labor costs have weakened its price competitiveness in aviation parts, so it plans to expand aviation production in Southeast Asia. Defense production is harder to move offshore, so the company plans additional domestic factories for that segment.

On the defense side, it has agreed an eight-year supply contract with LIG Defense&Aerospace for metal assembly parts tied to an electronic warfare development program. Large defense contracts run for long periods, so revenue is spread over several years.

Among larger players, Hanwha Aerospace and Korea Aerospace Industries act as system integrators, while KP Aero Industries supplies jigs, dies and structural parts, so comparisons of scale and margins should account for that difference. The Vietnam expansion aligns with the goal of winning direct business as a Tier-1 supplier.

06

Outlook

The nearest milestone is the November 13, 2026 disclosed jig contract with Space Pro, worth about KRW 2.9 billion, or 5.37% of 2025 sales, running from November 13 to December 31, 2026. It carries no advance payment and pays in 60-day promissory notes after delivery and inspection.

Management said more than half of the eight-year LIG Defense&Aerospace contract's value falls in the later part of the program, with early revenue mostly design fees.

In Vietnam, the company agreed in June 2026 to build a second plant of 6,702 square meters near its first Da Nang factory, with an investment of KRW 18.0 billion. The second plant adds raw material processing and surface treatment for Boeing wing and engine parts, increasing localization.

In July 2026 it extended a further USD 3 million loan to the Vietnam subsidiary. Management said the Vietnam unit has just started mass production, so costs and losses are being incurred, and it expects the unit to reach breakeven in the second half of 2027. The two priorities it cites are converting new orders into revenue and stabilizing the Vietnam operations.

08

Bull factors

Expanding long-term defense contracts

An eight-year, KRW 96.5 billion supply contract with LIG Defense&Aerospace was signed in August 2026. The company has a record of mass-producing core parts for domestic guided weapons such as Cheongung-II and Hyunmoo, and a roughly KRW 2.9 billion UAV jig contract was disclosed in November 2026. Long contract periods spread revenue recognition across several years.

Stronger balance sheet after the merger

Per press reports, total equity at end-June 2026 reached KRW 35.9 billion, more than double the end-2025 level after the merger. The debt ratio fell from 379% to 190%, and liquid funds rose from KRW 17.7 billion to KRW 31.7 billion. These figures indicate a larger financial cushion than before the listing.

Consecutive sales growth and Vietnam production base

Quarterly sales went from KRW 13.7 billion in the first quarter of 2026 to KRW 14.3 billion in the second and KRW 15.2 billion in the third, rising for two consecutive quarters. Third-quarter sales were up 22.7% year on year.

The second Vietnam plant is designed to bring processing and surface treatment in-house, and the company targets breakeven in the second half of 2027.

09

Bear factors

Two consecutive loss quarters and a wider deficit

After an operating loss of KRW 584 million in the second quarter of 2026, the third-quarter operating loss widened to KRW 812 million. Attributable net loss was KRW 2.28 billion in the third quarter, and attributable net losses over the last four quarters total KRW 15.5 billion. Profit has not followed sales growth.

Lower margins and early Vietnam costs

Press reports say the gross margin fell from 18.8% to 13.4% in the first half of 2026, while selling and administrative costs grew. The same report says the domestic parent earned operating profit on a standalone basis, while the Vietnam subsidiary posted an operating loss of about KRW 1.2 billion. Breakeven in the second half of 2027 remains a management target.

Leverage and cash flow pressure

The debt ratio stood at 378.9% at end-2025, with total liabilities of KRW 64.8 billion. Press reports say operating cash flow turned to net outflow in the first half of 2026 as inventory and receivables increased. Payment in 60-day promissory notes also adds to working capital needs.

10

Risk factors

Customer and contract concentration

Press reports say three customers, Korean AIR Lines, Space Pro and Korea Aerospace Industries, accounted for 78% of first-half 2026 sales. Most revenue from the LIG Defense&Aerospace contract is expected in the later part of the program. Shifts in order timing from any one customer can move quarterly results directly.

Overseas production and the Vietnam subsidiary

The Da Nang plant was completed in October 2024, and the June 2026 contract for a second plant increased investment. The company says the Vietnam unit has only just begun mass production and is incurring costs and losses. If breakeven in the second half of 2027 slips, the payback period on the investment would lengthen.

Ownership concentration and post-listing volatility

After the merger, the largest shareholder holds 16.26% and 14 related parties hold 51.49% in total. Press reports describe large price swings after the May 2026 listing, including a sharp decline the day after the first limit-up session. Changes in major shareholder stakes or in share count could affect supply and demand.

Overall view

KP Aero Industries grew sales for three consecutive years from 2023 through 2025, reaching KRW 53.9 billion in 2025. Profit, however, peaked with KRW 3.49 billion of operating profit in 2024 and fell in 2025, and losses have continued for two consecutive quarters since the second quarter of 2026.

Attributable net losses over the last four quarters total KRW 15.5 billion. Press reports say the merger lifted total equity to KRW 35.9 billion at end-June 2026 and reduced the debt ratio to 190%, a change in financial capacity.

Long-term defense contracts and the Vietnam production base are operational improvement opportunities, but the early Vietnam losses and lower gross margin still have to flow through results.
