02

Business structure

KP Aero Industries is an aircraft and defense parts manufacturer founded in 1990 and headquartered in Gimhae, South Gyeongsang Province. Its business is organized around three pillars: defense and aircraft tooling, aircraft parts, and aircraft assembly.

Its major customers include Korean Air Lines and Korea Aerospace Industries (KAI), to which it supplies aerospace and defense tooling, and it has also produced tooling related to the KF-21 fighter jet program. As of 2024, defense and aircraft tooling accounted for 45.4% of total revenue.

At its Gimhae headquarters, the company mass-produces key components for domestic guided-weapon systems such as Cheongung-II and Hyunmoo, based on proprietary technology including split mandrel molds used to manufacture solid-fuel guided-missile propulsion units.

In 2019, it signed a direct export contract with Boeing, moving beyond a subcontractor role to become part of the global aerospace supply chain.

For overseas production, the company completed its KPC VINA plant at Danang Hi-Tech Park in Vietnam, handling aircraft parts assembly, with total planned investment of KRW 26 billion, of which the first-phase facility is already operating.

The company describes its combined aviation, defense and space portfolio as designed so that defense cushions results during aviation downturns while aviation adds growth momentum during recoveries.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual revenue rose from KRW 26.8 billion in 2022 to KRW 34.6 billion in 2023 (up 28.8% year on year), KRW 49.7 billion in 2024 (up 43.6%), and KRW 53.9 billion in 2025 (up 8.6%), marking three consecutive years of growth from 2023 to 2025.

Operating profit climbed quickly from KRW 499 million in 2022 to KRW 861 million in 2023 (up 72.5%) and KRW 3.49 billion in 2024 (up 305.1%), before falling 24.4% year on year to KRW 2.64 billion in 2025, with the operating margin slipping from 7.0% to 4.9%.

Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders was largest in 2024 at KRW 3.02 billion among 2022-2025, then fell 48.5% year on year to KRW 1.56 billion in 2025.

By quarter, after posting revenue of KRW 15 billion, operating profit of KRW 2.09 billion and controlling-shareholder net profit of KRW 1.41 billion in Q2 2025, the company swung to an operating loss of KRW 129 million in Q3 2025 as revenue fell 17.7% to KRW 12.4 billion, while controlling-shareholder net profit plunged 96.0% to KRW 55.92 million.

In Q4 2025, revenue reached KRW 13.7 billion and operating profit turned positive at KRW 384 million, yet controlling-shareholder net profit fell a further 83.2% quarter on quarter to just KRW 9.39 million.

In Q1 2026, operating profit rose 54.0% quarter on quarter to KRW 591 million and controlling-shareholder net profit jumped to KRW 555 million, but in Q2 2026 the company swung back to an operating loss of KRW 584 million despite revenue of KRW 14.3 billion (up 4.7% quarter on quarter, down 4.7% year on year).

Notably, the Q2 2026 net loss attributable to controlling shareholders reached KRW 13.8 billion, which the company attributed to roughly KRW 13.9 billion in one-time, non-cash merger costs tied to its merger with NH Special Purpose Acquisition Company No. 30.

Under current accounting rules, this cost arose because the gap between the fair value of merger-related shares and the identifiable net asset value of the SPAC is recognized on the income statement as a listing-related merger expense.

As a result, the trailing four-quarter sum of net losses attributable to controlling shareholders (Q3 2025-Q2 2026) reached KRW 13.2 billion, while annual operating cash flow swung from an outflow of KRW 3.92 billion in 2022 to an inflow of KRW 7.22 billion in 2023 before moderating to KRW 4.38 billion in 2025.

05

Industry analysis

The global aviation industry has seen passenger demand recover quickly since the COVID-19 pandemic, increasing pressure on aircraft makers to ramp up production.

The company notes that monthly production of the Boeing 787 and 737 still remains below pre-pandemic levels, suggesting customers still have room to restore output.

At the same time, ongoing geopolitical tensions have supported strong domestic defense exports, creating a structure in which aviation and defense alternately underpin performance.

In the domestic aviation-defense supply chain, large finished-aircraft and engine makers such as Korea Aerospace Industries, Hanwha Aerospace and Hyundai-Rotem sit at the top tier, while KP Aero Industries operates as a specialized supplier of tooling and structures to these large firms and to Korean Air Lines.

Signing a direct export contract with Boeing in 2019, moving beyond a domestic subcontractor role into the global supply chain, is cited as a differentiating factor versus peers.

However, as rising domestic labor costs are seen eroding the cost competitiveness of aircraft parts production in Korea, the company is shifting aviation assembly and processing work to Southeast Asia while keeping defense production at home because overseas production of defense items is difficult.

Since initial losses at the Vietnam production subsidiary were cited as a factor dragging down consolidated profitability in the first half of 2026, there is likely to be a time lag before the overseas production base translates into stable cost competitiveness.

06

Outlook

At the time of listing, the company projected 2025 revenue of about KRW 54 billion and said revenue could approach KRW 100 billion in 2027-2029. It stated that funds raised through the listing would be used for the second Vietnam plant and for expanding defense production facilities.

In Danang, Vietnam, the company signed a design-and-construction contract in June 2026 for a second plant covering 6,702 square meters near the first plant, with planned investment of KRW 18 billion.

While the first plant handles aircraft parts assembly, the second plant is designed to add raw-material processing and surface-treatment steps for Boeing wing and engine components, further internalizing the local production process.

The company expects Vietnam subsidiary revenue to be meaningfully reflected starting in 2027, after accounting for roughly a two-year period to secure new staff and stabilize production.

Because overseas relocation is difficult for defense items, the company plans to expand that business by securing additional domestic production facilities.

A company official said that converting new orders into actual revenue and profit, while stabilizing the Vietnam operation where upfront investment has been concentrated, will be key to future results.

08

Bull factors

Diversified Aviation-Defense-Space Portfolio

The company holds three business legs—aircraft parts and assembly, defense tooling, and space launch vehicle structures—so results are not entirely dependent on a single end market.

Defense revenue supported performance when aviation demand collapsed during COVID-19, and more recently aviation demand recovery and defense export growth have appeared together.

Having signed a direct export contract with Boeing in 2019, moving beyond a domestic subcontractor role into the global supply chain, also sets it apart competitively.

Four Years of Topline Growth

Revenue grew from KRW 26.8 billion in 2022 to KRW 53.9 billion in 2025, including three consecutive years of growth from 2023 to 2025. Operating profit also climbed from KRW 499 million in 2022 to KRW 3.49 billion in 2024, making 2024 the strongest year in 2022-2025.

The revenue growth is attributed to a combination of recovering aircraft replacement demand and the expansion of K-defense exports.

Low-Cost Vietnam Production Expansion

The company has established its KPC VINA overseas production subsidiary in Danang, Vietnam, to conduct low-cost aircraft parts assembly, and in June 2026 it signed a construction contract worth KRW 18 billion for a second plant adding raw-material processing and surface-treatment steps.

The strategy is to offset rising domestic labor costs eroding aircraft-parts cost competitiveness by shifting production to Southeast Asia. The company has stated it expects Vietnam subsidiary revenue to begin scaling up meaningfully from 2027.

09

Bear factors

Rising Quarterly Earnings Volatility

The company posted operating losses in both Q3 2025 and Q2 2026, with quarterly results swinging between profit and loss. In Q2 2026, revenue fell 4.7% year on year and the company booked an operating loss of KRW 584 million, turning negative both quarter on quarter and year on year.

The trailing four-quarter sum of net losses attributable to controlling shareholders reached KRW 13.2 billion, leaving the question of annual earnings recovery to be confirmed in upcoming quarterly results.

Initial Losses at Vietnam Subsidiary

Consolidated gross margin in the first half of 2026 fell from 18.8% to 13.4%, with initial losses at the Vietnam production subsidiary cited as a key factor. Rising selling, general and administrative expense burden also meant the existing revenue base could not absorb the cost increase.

Operating cash flow reportedly also turned to a net outflow during the first half, suggesting a time lag before the overseas production base contributes to profit.

Elevated Debt Leverage

The debt-to-equity ratio rose every year, from 290.7% in 2022 to 378.9% in 2025. Over the same period, operating cash flow moderated from KRW 7.22 billion in 2023 to KRW 4.38 billion in 2025.

With additional investment such as the second Vietnam plant ongoing, a rising reliance on external borrowing or further capital raising cannot be ruled out.

10

Risk factors

Customer Concentration Risk

The company's revenue structure relies heavily on a small number of large customers, including Korean Air Lines, Korea Aerospace Industries and Boeing.

Tooling and structure supply contracts tend to be stable once established because they last for extended periods, but a change in a major customer's order schedule or production plan can directly translate into earnings swings.

Korean Air Lines has deepened the relationship by providing advance payment support to help fund the Vietnam plant's operations, which also means dependence on a specific customer is deepening.

Overseas Production Ramp-Up Risk

The Vietnam KPC VINA subsidiary is still at an early loss-making stage, and with second-plant investment overlapping, cost burdens are appearing before profit contribution.

The company expects revenue to ramp up meaningfully from 2027 after factoring in roughly a two-year period to secure new staff and stabilize production, but overseas plant ramp-up can slip depending on variables such as currency, local labor availability and quality certification.

The second plant is set to handle raw-material processing and surface treatment for Boeing wing and engine components, raising process complexity above that of the first plant.

Financial Leverage and Merger-Related Accounting Risk

With the debt-to-equity ratio at 378.9% at the end of 2025, continued investment such as the second Vietnam plant could increase the need for further borrowing or capital raising.

The possibility that non-cash accounting costs arising from capital transactions such as a SPAC merger, as occurred in Q2 2026, could again be booked on the income statement cannot be ruled out.

Because such one-off costs diverge from actual cash flow, they add to the volatility of quarterly net profit attributable to controlling shareholders.

12

Overall view

KP Aero Industries has shown a growth trajectory with revenue rising from KRW 26.8 billion in 2022 to KRW 53.9 billion in 2025, and operating profit peaking at KRW 3.49 billion in 2024.

However, operating profit fell 24.4% year on year in 2025, and in Q2 2026 the company booked a net loss attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 13.8 billion, reflecting roughly KRW 13.9 billion in one-time, non-cash costs tied to the SPAC merger.

On a quarterly basis, earnings volatility has increased, with operating losses recorded in both Q3 2025 and Q2 2026. The debt-to-equity ratio rose for four straight years to reach 378.9% at the end of 2025, and operating cash flow has moderated since 2023.

The company has set a target of KRW 100 billion in revenue for 2027-2029 through investment in a second Vietnam plant and expanded defense production facilities, but the pace at which Vietnam subsidiary losses narrow and new orders convert into results remains a key variable.

The next quarterly report and the timing of the Vietnam subsidiary's revenue contribution are likely to be the key indicators for gauging an actual recovery in profitability.
