Report overview

Narrower Losses Under Court Receivership: Bumyang Construction's Test

Bumyang Construction narrowed its operating loss to KRW 313 million in the third quarter of 2026, but with court receivership and capital impairment still in place, normalization depends on the rehabilitation plan and the sale process.

  1. 1

    Operating losses ran for four consecutive years from 2022 through 2025, with the 2025 operating loss at KRW 51.3 billion and an operating margin of -71.4%, the lowest of the four years.

  2. 2

    The quarterly pattern shifted in 2026. Operating profit of KRW 522 million in the first quarter turned into an operating loss of KRW 461 million in the second quarter, and the third-quarter loss narrowed to KRW 313 million.

  3. 3

    Third-quarter 2026 revenue of KRW 1.12 billion fell 91.3% from the year-earlier quarter, so the narrower loss came on a much smaller revenue base.

  4. 4

    The company applied for court receivership in early 2026, and in early March 2026 the Korea Exchange disclosed a risk of delisting due to full capital impairment.

  5. 5

    Media reports in April 2026 said the company had moved into a public sale process, while its shares were under trading suspension at the time.

02

Business structure

Bumyang Construction is a Busan-based general contractor founded in 1958 that built its business around public-sector civil works.

As of media reports in April 2026, its main projects were the Gijang Reus City development in Gijang-gun, Busan, with a project value of about KRW 1.44 trillion, and an urban development project in the Yangbok district.

It holds a 22.83% stake in the management company of a development project in Dujeong-dong, Cheonan, and media reports expected a dividend of roughly KRW 54.8 billion when the units convert to sale in August 2030.

By segment, building works generated the largest revenue in the first nine months of 2025 at KRW 53.36 billion, but the same segment also posted the largest operating loss at KRW 18.583 billion, while civil works recorded a loss of KRW 2.46 billion.

Over the same nine months, group revenue was KRW 65.6 billion and cost of sales was KRW 83.2 billion, above revenue, which analysts say shows site-level cost pressure spreading into company-wide results.

Customers are mainly public-sector clients and private developers, and the company has said that unsold units at developer clients delayed the recovery of construction receivables and bridge financing.

Nine construction contracts were terminated between March 2025 and early 2026, with cancelled amounts totaling about KRW 241.7 billion, more than three times 2025 revenue of KRW 71.8 billion. Its wholly owned subsidiary was included in the sale perimeter alongside the parent.

04

Earnings analysis

The annual figures show a structure of continuing losses. Revenue rose from KRW 119.8 billion in 2022 to KRW 120.8 billion in 2023 (up 0.8% year on year), then declined for two consecutive years to KRW 101.4 billion in 2024 (down 16.1%) and KRW 71.8 billion in 2025 (down 29.1%).

Operating losses were KRW 12.3 billion in 2022, KRW 10.4 billion in 2023, KRW 38.2 billion in 2024 and KRW 51.3 billion in 2025, a loss in each of the four years from 2022 through 2025, with operating margins of -10.3%, -8.6%, -37.7% and -71.4%.

Reports attribute the widening losses while revenue was shrinking to sites where cost increases were not passed through to contract prices, which then locked in losses.

Net loss attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 8.05 billion in 2022, KRW 9.11 billion in 2023, KRW 46.3 billion in 2024 and KRW 87.5 billion in 2025, a loss in each of the four years.

Total net loss including non-controlling interests was KRW 8.41 billion in 2022, differing from the attributable figure, and matched the attributable figure in the years from 2023 on.

Operating cash flow moved from negative KRW 58.4 billion in 2022, negative KRW 6.09 billion in 2023 and negative KRW 16.6 billion in 2024 to positive KRW 15.9 billion in 2025.

Media reports put the nine-month operating cash outflow for 2025 at KRW 13.4 billion, so the drivers of the full-year swing need to be traced through the annual cash flow statement.

Quarterly revenue fell from KRW 12.9 billion in the third quarter of 2025 to KRW 6.18 billion in the fourth quarter, a 52.1% quarter-on-quarter decline, when the operating loss reached KRW 24.5 billion and the attributable net loss reached KRW 52.6 billion.

Revenue then rose to KRW 7.65 billion in the first quarter of 2026, with operating profit of KRW 522 million, before falling to KRW 1 billion in the second quarter.

The second quarter showed an operating loss of KRW 461 million while attributable net income was KRW 858 million, so the two profit levels moved in opposite directions. The third quarter of 2026 recorded an operating loss of KRW 313 million and an attributable net loss of KRW 1.57 billion.

The company has booked two consecutive quarterly losses since the second quarter of 2026, and the attributable net loss across the last four quarters totals KRW 54.1 billion.

05

Industry analysis

Media analysis describes the construction cycle as one in which prolonged high interest rates and a stressed project-financing market have pushed mid-sized contractors that failed to manage cost ratios to a tipping point.

Coverage of Bumyang Construction cites the slump in regional sales of housing units and the rise in global construction costs as shared background to rising receivables and payables.

The company is described as having a low-margin structure built on public-sector civil works, and its operating losses are seen as entrenched after new contract wins slowed from 2022 while costs rose.

In fnguide's industry comparison, the KOSPI construction sector posted an operating profit, while Bumyang Construction recorded an operating loss, which points to project-level cost control as a company-specific factor rather than a sector-wide one.

Coverage suggests that survival for mid-sized contractors depends on how fast interest rates and the project-financing market recover, and for Bumyang Construction this is compounded by the court receivership process.

The key indicators for the cycle are how quickly the sales slump eases and how far cost increases are passed through to contract prices. On both measures, coverage places the company in a weak position.

06

Outlook

The core of the outlook is the outcome of the court receivership. Media reports in April 2026 said the sale advisor was preparing a sale notice covering Bumyang Construction and its wholly owned subsidiary, which could be taken on together or separately.

The seller reportedly moved to a public sale after a stalking-horse approach with a pre-selected buyer did not work. fnguide summarized that the company's path to normalization depends on court approval of the rehabilitation plan and whether the M&A process proceeds.

On the project side, the Gijang Reus City development, with a project value of about KRW 1.44 trillion, is under way and was cited by investment bankers as an attraction in media reports.

Media reports also said the Cheonan Dujeong-dong development could yield a dividend of about KRW 54.8 billion when the units convert to sale in August 2030. On the other hand, contract cancellations since 2025 limit the revenue base going forward.

With third-quarter 2026 revenue at KRW 1.12 billion, the timing at which development returns reach construction revenue matters. The next checkpoints are therefore the court decision on the rehabilitation plan, any buyer decision, and the operating result in the 2026 annual report.

08

Bull factors

Narrowing operating losses

After an operating loss of KRW 24.5 billion in the fourth quarter of 2025, the company posted operating profit of KRW 522 million in the first quarter of 2026. The third-quarter 2026 operating loss narrowed to KRW 313 million.

Annual operating cash flow also turned positive at KRW 15.9 billion in 2025, against negative KRW 16.6 billion in 2024.

Scale of development projects held

Media reports in April 2026 said the Gijang Reus City development is under way with a project value of about KRW 1.44 trillion.

The company holds a 22.83% stake in the management company of the Dujeong-dong project in Cheonan, with a dividend of about KRW 54.8 billion expected when the units convert to sale in August 2030. Investment bankers were reported to have cited these two projects as attractive.

A rehabilitation-based path to normalization

fnguide summarized that Bumyang Construction is seeking normalization through an M&A process during court receivership. The same source reported that consolidated revenue for the first quarter of 2026 fell 79.0% year on year, while operating profit turned positive. Bulls argue that as loss-making sites are wound down or resolved, the cost structure could improve.

09

Bear factors

Receivership and capital impairment

Total equity was a negative KRW 69.5 billion at the end of 2025, a state of full capital impairment. The Korea Exchange disclosed a delisting risk tied to full impairment of paid-in capital, and a disclaimer of opinion on the 2024 financial statements had earlier put the company under delisting review. Media reports in April 2026 said the shares were under trading suspension.

Accumulated contract terminations

Nine construction contracts were terminated from March 2025 into early 2026, with cancelled amounts of about KRW 241.7 billion. That is more than three times 2025 revenue of KRW 71.8 billion. In the first nine months of 2025, cost of sales of KRW 83.2 billion exceeded revenue of KRW 65.6 billion.

Sharp contraction of the revenue base

Second-quarter 2026 revenue of KRW 1 billion fell 86.9% from the prior quarter. The third-quarter decline from the year-earlier quarter exceeded 90%, shrinking the revenue base further.

With revenue this small, quarterly figures alone make it hard to tell whether the narrower loss reflects a recovery in the core business.

10

Risk factors

Rehabilitation and listing risk

Whether the rehabilitation plan is approved and how the sale process ends both remain uncertain. The Korea Exchange flagged a delisting risk tied to capital impairment. Depending on whether listing requirements are met, the trading framework itself could change.

Liquidity and interest burden

Media reports put current liabilities at KRW 160.2 billion at the end of September 2025, close to twice current assets of KRW 85.3 billion. Coverage also pointed to interest expense of more than KRW 30 billion. If receipts from site contracts are delayed, liquidity pressure could build again.

Cost and project-level risk

Coverage pointed to a structure in which sites that could not pass cost increases into contract prices locked in losses. The company's own explanation is that unsold units at developer clients slow the recovery of receivables and loans. Coverage argues the same exposure remains while the regional housing sales slump continues.

Overall view

Bumyang Construction posted operating and net losses in every year from 2022 through 2025, and total equity turned negative at the end of 2025.

In 2026 the operating result moved from a first-quarter profit to a second-quarter loss and a narrower third-quarter loss, but third-quarter revenue of KRW 1.12 billion remained low. On the bull side, the scale of development projects such as Gijang Reus City and buyer interest in the sale process are cited.

On the bear side, capital impairment, the delisting risk, accumulated contract terminations and the current-liability burden all remain.

The swing to positive operating cash flow in 2025 can be read as a positive sign, but the nine-month 2025 figure was an outflow, so its durability can only be judged after the 2026 annual report.

The current phase is defined more by the court decision on the rehabilitation plan and the sale outcome than by a change in reported earnings.
