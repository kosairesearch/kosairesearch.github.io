Report overview

Bumyang Construction at a Rehabilitation-Delisting Crossroads

Bumyang Construction entered court-led rehabilitation proceedings in January 2026 and was then handed a delisting decision by the Korea Exchange in September, while the company is fighting the ruling with an injunction and simultaneously pursuing a pre-approval M&A to secure outside capital.

  1. 1

    2025 revenue fell 29.1% year on year to KRW 71.8 billion, marking a fourth consecutive year of operating losses at KRW 51.3 billion.

  2. 2

    Controlling-interest net profit swung to KRW 858 million in Q2 2026, yet revenue shrank to KRW 1 billion, down 93.9% year on year.

  3. 3

    The Korea Exchange decided to delist the company on September 21, 2026, and the company filed for an injunction to stay that decision at the Seoul Southern District Court just a day later, on September 22.

  4. 4

    The Suwon Rehabilitation Court's deadline for submitting a rehabilitation plan, repeatedly postponed, was reset to November 20, 2026 per an October 19, 2026 disclosure.

  5. 5

02

Business structure

Founded in 1958 and built on public-works contracts, Bumyang Construction is a general civil engineering and building contractor that listed on the KOSPI in 1988.

Its business lines span civil works, housing construction, building construction, and heating, electrical, and paving works, with operations centered on Busan. The business is reported to split with building construction accounting for more than half of revenue and the management segment making up the rest.

The company owns a wholly owned subsidiary, Bumyang D&C, and the 2026 sale process underway bundles both Bumyang Construction and Bumyang D&C together.

The company had climbed to the 93rd spot in the construction capability ranking in 2020, but a prolonged construction downturn and mounting cost burdens pushed it down to 182nd as of early 2026.

In the current rehabilitation case, the court did not appoint a separate outside administrator and instead designated CEO Kang Byung-joo as the court-appointed manager.

A structural problem repeatedly flagged is that the company failed to pass rising construction costs through to its contract pricing, so losses widened the more work it completed. Since the rehabilitation filing, a string of contract terminations on existing job sites has sharply eroded its underlying business base.

Given the nature of the construction industry, restoring trust with public and private clients and securing new orders are seen as the key variables for the company's continued operation.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual revenue edged up from KRW 119.8 billion in 2022 to KRW 120.8 billion in 2023, but after peaking in 2023 it fell for two straight years, to KRW 101.4 billion in 2024 (down 16.1% year on year) and KRW 71.8 billion in 2025 (down 29.1%).

Operating losses narrowed from KRW 12.3 billion in 2022 to KRW 10.4 billion in 2023, then widened again to KRW 38.2 billion in 2024 and KRW 51.3 billion in 2025, extending a four-year streak of operating losses from 2022 through 2025.

Controlling-interest net losses also deepened every year across the same four years, from KRW 8.05 billion in 2022 to KRW 9.11 billion in 2023, KRW 46.3 billion in 2024, and KRW 87.5 billion in 2025.

The 2025 operating margin fell to -71.4%, indicating that cost pressure grew faster than the pace at which revenue was shrinking.

On a quarterly basis, the operating loss narrowed from KRW 23.1 billion in Q2 2025 to KRW 3.32 billion in Q3, widened again to KRW 24.5 billion in Q4, swung to an operating profit of KRW 522 million in Q1 2026, then reverted to an operating loss of KRW 461 million in Q2 2026.

Controlling-interest net income likewise swung from a loss of KRW 760 million in Q1 2026 to a profit of KRW 858 million in Q2 2026, but revenue in that same quarter was only KRW 1 billion, down 86.9% from the prior quarter and 93.9% from a year earlier, leaving open the possibility that the profitability swing reflects a cost reversal or a base effect from collapsing revenue rather than an operational turnaround.

The sum of controlling-interest net losses over the last four quarters (Q3 2025 through Q2 2026) reached KRW 55.9 billion, showing that a single quarter's swing to profit has not changed the annual loss trajectory.

Operating cash flow stayed negative from 2022 (-KRW 58.4 billion) through 2024 (-KRW 16.6 billion) before turning positive at KRW 15.9 billion in 2025, a shift that may reflect receivables collection or working-capital changes tied to halted construction activity rather than a sustained operational improvement.

05

Industry analysis

The KOSPI construction sector overall has remained profitable, which stands in sharp contrast to Bumyang Construction's persistent losses.

Numerous mid-sized builders faced liquidity crises in 2024-2025 amid surging construction costs and a sluggish pre-sale market, with Sambu Construction's parallel pursuit of rehabilitation and M&A serving as a comparable case.

Industry sources pointed to the same structural issue at Bumyang Construction, noting that cost increases were "not reflected in contract pricing, so expenses arose before revenue could catch up" as work progressed.

Annual interest expense swelling past KRW 30 billion is also cited as a factor that undercut any recovery momentum.

Many small and mid-sized builders carry contingent liabilities tied to project financing and unmet completion-guarantee obligations, making Bumyang Construction's situation a case where a sector-wide structural risk has materialized.

The company's construction capability ranking slid sharply from 93rd in 2020 to 182nd as of early 2026, underscoring how quickly its standing within the industry eroded.

Unlike large contractors that diversify across overseas projects, plants, and housing, smaller players like Bumyang Construction carry heavier concentration in specific regions and work types, leaving them more exposed to external shocks.

06

Outlook

The company's future path hinges on two parallel processes. One is the injunction against the Korea Exchange seeking to stay the delisting decision; if the court grants it, delisting and liquidation-trading procedures would be suspended until a final ruling, but if denied, the exchange's procedures could proceed.

The other is the rehabilitation process at the Suwon Rehabilitation Court, where the deadline for submitting a rehabilitation plan has been repeatedly pushed back from an original May 8 to September 23, then October 23, and now November 20, 2026.

After its first sale attempt collapsed in April 2026, the company engaged Samil PwC as advisor for a second public sale process (pre-approval M&A) bundling Bumyang Construction with its wholly owned subsidiary Bumyang D&C, and whether it can attract outside capital is likely to be directly tied to whether the rehabilitation plan can win court approval.

Even if the company is normalized, it still faces the task of rebuilding a revenue base after nine existing construction contracts worth roughly KRW 241.7 billion were terminated following the rehabilitation filing.

Given consecutive disclaimer audit opinions for fiscal years 2024 and 2025, how the auditor opines in the next regular filing is also a key variable. Disclosed quarterly results run through Q2 2026, and confirmed results for subsequent quarters have not yet been released in a regular report.

Bull factors

Signs of Temporary Quarterly Improvement

Operating profit swung positive at KRW 522 million in Q1 2026, and controlling-interest net profit turned positive at KRW 858 million in Q2 2026. Both figures are small in absolute terms, but posting profit after consecutive large losses in 2024-2025 suggests some effect from cost restructuring or site wind-downs.

However, revenue fell sharply over the same period, so whether this profitability is backed by a genuine revenue recovery still needs confirmation.

Potential Balance-Sheet Repair via Outside Capital

The company is conducting a second public sale process (pre-approval M&A), advised by Samil PwC, bundling Bumyang Construction with its subsidiary Bumyang D&C. If a buyer is secured and capital flows in through measures such as a third-party allotted rights issue, it could provide a path out of full capital impairment.

In its prior 2011-2014 rehabilitation, the company previously retained its listing through M&A and a capital raise.

Reversal in Cash Flow Metrics

Operating cash flow, negative throughout 2022-2024, turned positive at KRW 15.9 billion in 2025. This improvement, occurring alongside continued large income-statement losses, may reflect receivables collection or working-capital shifts. The shift to positive cash flow is nonetheless a notable change from a liquidity standpoint.

Bear factors

Sharp Collapse in the Revenue Base

Since the rehabilitation filing, nine construction contracts worth roughly KRW 241.7 billion were terminated, exceeding three times the full-year 2025 revenue of KRW 71.8 billion. Q2 2026 revenue fell to just KRW 1 billion, down 86.9% from the prior quarter and 93.9% year on year.

With the existing order book severely damaged, rebuilding revenue could take considerable time even if normalization is achieved.

Full Capital Impairment and Distorted Accounting Ratios

Total equity turned negative at the end of 2025, pushing the company into full capital impairment, and the debt ratio of -248.2% is at a level that defies conventional interpretation.

The company received consecutive disclaimer audit opinions for fiscal years 2024 and 2025, raising questions about the reliability of its financial information itself. Under this balance-sheet structure, normalizing finances without an injection of outside capital is difficult.

Uncertainty Around the Delisting Process

The Korea Exchange decided to delist the company on September 21, 2026, and the company has legally contested this by filing an injunction the following day.

If the injunction is denied, the delisting and liquidation-trading procedures could proceed as planned, leaving shareholders facing genuine uncertainty over whether trading will resume at all.

Because the rehabilitation and delisting processes proceed separately, even court approval of a rehabilitation plan would not guarantee the company's standing on the exchange.

10

Risk factors

Delisting and Litigation Risk

If the Seoul Southern District Court denies the injunction, liquidation-trading procedures could resume immediately. Even if the injunction is granted, an eventual loss in the main lawsuit would still result in delisting. The prolonged nature of the legal process itself is a source of uncertainty for investors.

Risk of Rehabilitation Plan Rejection

The repeated postponement of the rehabilitation plan submission deadline suggests difficulty in reaching creditor consensus or securing an investor. If the plan fails to pass a creditors' meeting, the court could terminate the rehabilitation proceedings, which could lead to liquidation.

The company previously went through rehabilitation once before, in 2011-2014, so the recurrence of financial crises can itself be flagged as a structural risk.

Risk to Rebuilding the Operating Base

The termination of nine contracts worth roughly KRW 241.7 billion has sharply reduced the existing order backlog. If trust with public and private clients is not restored, securing new orders could remain difficult even after normalization.

If the structural problem of failing to pass construction cost increases through to contracts is not resolved, profitability improvement could also remain limited.

Overall view

Bumyang Construction has posted four consecutive years of operating and controlling-interest net losses from 2022 through 2025, with its finances deteriorating to the point of full capital impairment at the end of 2025 as total equity turned negative.

In 2026, two parallel processes are unfolding simultaneously — court-led rehabilitation and a Korea Exchange delisting decision — against which the company is fighting back with an injunction filing and a pre-approval M&A effort.

Operating profit and controlling-interest net profit each turned positive temporarily in Q1 and Q2 2026 respectively, but given the sharp revenue decline over the same period, whether this profitability signals a sustainable operational recovery requires further confirmation.

The termination of nine contracts worth roughly KRW 241.7 billion has severely damaged the existing order base, suggesting that revenue recovery could take time even if a rehabilitation plan is approved.

The rehabilitation plan submission deadline (November 20, 2026), the outcome of the injunction hearing, and whether the second public sale succeeds are seen as the key variables that will determine the company's survival over the coming months.

It is also worth bearing in mind that the current share price reflects a level fixed under a trading halt, placing it in territory that is difficult to interpret through conventional valuation metrics.
