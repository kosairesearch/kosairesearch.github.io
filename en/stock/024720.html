02

Business structure

Kolmar Holdings is a pure holding company that owns stakes in its subsidiaries rather than making and selling products itself. Its main income comes from dividends and trademark royalties paid by subsidiaries, supplemented by management service fees and rent.

Its principal subsidiaries are Kolmar Korea, a cosmetics ODM manufacturer, KolmarBNH, a health functional food ODM company, and a pharmaceutical subsidiary.

Trademark income comes from KolmarBNH, Kolmar Korea and the pharmaceutical subsidiary, so growth at those companies flows through to the holding company's earnings. The company has operated as a holding business since its 2012 spin-off of cosmetics and pharmaceutical operations.

In November 2022 the board approved the transfer of its Kolmar Mask stake to improve finances and reshape the group's business structure. The third-quarter 2026 sales decline is attributed to restructuring, including the intra-group transfer of a cosmetics business unit and Kolmar Mask.

Competition differs by business: Kolmar Korea competes with other cosmetics ODM makers, while KolmarBNH and the pharmaceutical subsidiary face rivals in their own markets. As a holding company, Kolmar Holdings is valued on the combined worth of its subsidiaries and their capacity to pay dividends.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual revenue fell 1.9% from KRW 649.9 billion in 2022 to KRW 637.3 billion in 2023, then rose 6.2% to KRW 676.7 billion in 2024, the largest year of 2022 to 2025. The KRW 630.1 billion recorded in 2025 was down 6.9% year on year and was the smallest year of the period.

Operating profit fell 48.9% from KRW 33 billion in 2022 to KRW 16.9 billion in 2023, the low point, then rose 125.9% to KRW 38.1 billion in 2024 and 25.2% to KRW 47.7 billion in 2025, which was the second consecutive year of growth after 2023. The 2025 operating profit was the largest of the 2022 to 2025 period.

The operating margin improved from 2.6% in 2023 to 7.6% in 2025, so margins widened even in the year sales fell.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders was a loss of KRW 6.09 billion in 2022 and a loss of KRW 3.72 billion in 2023, then turned to a profit of KRW 24.6 billion in 2024 and fell 4.6% to KRW 23.4 billion in 2025.

Net income including non-controlling interests was KRW 11.1 billion in 2025, below the controlling-shareholder figure, which suggests non-controlling interests reduced the total.

At the end of 2025, total equity stood at KRW 806.9 billion, of which KRW 614.8 billion was attributable to controlling shareholders, and total liabilities were KRW 487.5 billion.

Quarterly, Q4 2025 revenue of KRW 136.5 billion fell 17.5% from the prior quarter, operating profit of KRW 11.9 billion fell 27.2%, and net loss attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 14.7 billion marked a swing to loss.

Q1 2026 revenue of KRW 151.9 billion, operating profit of KRW 20.4 billion and net income attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 27.8 billion marked a recovery. Q2 2026 revenue was KRW 142.3 billion, operating profit KRW 25.3 billion and controlling-shareholder net income KRW 31.6 billion.

Q3 2026 revenue was KRW 134.5 billion, operating profit KRW 19.5 billion and controlling-shareholder net income KRW 5.55 billion. The company attributed the third-quarter operating profit gain to subsidiaries' improved profitability and higher equity-method income.

05

Industry analysis

Kolmar Holdings' income reflects demand in cosmetics ODM and health functional food ODM markets, which reaches the holding company through subsidiary earnings and dividends. As a result, changes in K-beauty exports or health functional food demand show up in the holding company's results indirectly.

Press coverage says KolmarBNH is launching products with a range of formats and functions in line with growing demand for customized health functional foods. Kolmar Korea is described as offering a portfolio that spans basic, color and functional cosmetics.

Its position in the market cycle can only be read indirectly through the subsidiaries' earnings trends. Both the 2025 annual sales decline and the third-quarter 2026 decline include restructuring effects, so they are hard to read as a signal of industry conditions alone.

In cosmetics ODM, Kolmar Korea is compared with other ODM makers. As a holding company, the relevant comparison group is holding companies with similar portfolios rather than a single competitor.

The group's distinctive feature is that its subsidiaries hold positions in cosmetics, health functional food and pharmaceutical markets.

Bull factors

Operating profit recovery

Operating profit rose from a KRW 16.9 billion low in 2023 to KRW 38.1 billion in 2024 and KRW 47.7 billion in 2025, a second consecutive year of growth from 2024 to 2025. Q1 2026 operating profit of KRW 20.4 billion was up 71.5% from the prior quarter, and Q2 of KRW 25.3 billion was up 24.2%.

The operating margin also improved from 2.6% in 2023 to 7.6% in 2025, so profitability rose even as sales declined.

Subsidiary improvement and equity-method income

The company attributed the third-quarter 2026 operating profit gain to improved profitability at consolidated subsidiaries and higher equity-method income. Third-quarter operating profit of KRW 19.5 billion rose 19.5% year on year.

For a holding company, subsidiary improvement feeds directly into results through dividends and equity-method gains. Whether equity-method income continues in later quarters, however, can only be confirmed in the periodic reports.

Operating cash flow stays positive

Operating cash flow moved from -KRW 11.3 billion in 2022 to KRW 43.9 billion in 2023, KRW 39.5 billion in 2024 and KRW 31 billion in 2025, and was positive from 2023 through 2025.

As a pure holding company without raw materials or production facilities, its cash inflows rely on subsidiary dividends and trademark royalties. The steady flow supports the holding company's financial cushion. At the end of 2025, total liabilities were KRW 487.5 billion and total equity KRW 806.9 billion.

Bear factors

Continued shrinkage in sales

Annual revenue fell 6.9% to KRW 630.1 billion in 2025. Third-quarter 2026 revenue of KRW 134.5 billion was down 18.7% year on year and 5.5% from the prior quarter. The company attributed the decline to business restructuring, but how far sales recover after the restructuring can only be confirmed in periodic reports.

Quarterly swings in net income

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders swung to a KRW 14.7 billion loss in Q4 2025, then recovered to KRW 27.8 billion in Q1 2026 and KRW 31.6 billion in Q2. In Q3 it fell to KRW 5.55 billion, down 82.4% from the prior quarter.

The four-quarter total is KRW 50.3 billion, but quarterly variation is wide, so a single quarter does not show the earnings trend.

Rising leverage and non-controlling interests

The debt-to-equity ratio rose from 49.2% in 2022 to 58.0% in 2023, 58.4% in 2024 and 60.4% in 2025. Net income of KRW 11.1 billion in 2025 was well below the KRW 23.4 billion attributable to controlling shareholders, so non-controlling interests drove a wide gap between the two measures. Total liabilities at the end of 2025 were KRW 487.5 billion.

Risk factors

Business restructuring

The third-quarter 2026 sales decline is attributed to intra-group business transfers. If restructuring continues, the baseline for consolidated revenue shifts and year-on-year comparisons can be distorted.

Transfers between subsidiaries may also affect the holding company's stake structure and value allocation, so related disclosures need tracking.

Dependence on subsidiary earnings and dividends

Kolmar Holdings earns income from dividends and trademark royalties without production facilities of its own. A downturn at any one of its cosmetics, health functional food or pharmaceutical subsidiaries feeds straight into the holding company's income. Changes in subsidiary dividend decisions or royalty terms would also alter its cash inflows.

Balance-sheet strain and earnings volatility

The debt-to-equity ratio reached 60.4% in 2025, with total liabilities of KRW 487.5 billion. Net income attributable to controlling shareholders moves sharply from quarter to quarter, which makes dividend capacity and financial cushion harder to gauge.

Earnings volatility could widen depending on how repeatable the equity-method income cited as a profit driver proves to be.

Overall view

Kolmar Holdings is a holding company whose income runs on dividends and trademark royalties from its cosmetics, health functional food and pharmaceutical subsidiaries, not on product sales.

On the disclosed figures, revenue peaked in 2024 and fell in 2025, while operating profit rose two consecutive years from 2024 to 2025 after the 2023 low, and the operating margin reached 7.6% in 2025.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders turned to profit in 2024, eased in 2025, swung to a loss in Q4 2025 and recovered in Q1 and Q2 2026. In Q3 2026, revenue of KRW 134.5 billion fell 18.7% year on year, while operating profit of KRW 19.5 billion rose 19.5%.

The company attributed the sales decline to business restructuring and the profit gain to subsidiaries' improvement and higher equity-method income.

The bull case rests on the operating profit recovery and positive operating cash flow, while the bear case rests on shrinking sales, large quarterly earnings swings and a higher debt-to-equity ratio.

The share price trades below the book value attributable to controlling shareholders, and valuation multiples depend heavily on quarterly earnings. The next checkpoints are the preliminary annual 2026 results in early 2027 and the fourth-quarter results and equity-method income in the 2026 annual report.
