02

Business structure

Exicure Hitron traces its roots to Hitron Systems, a video security equipment maker founded in 1986, and listed on the KOSPI in 1998. After a court-supervised rehabilitation in 2023, the company acquired the Nasdaq-listed biotech Exicure, Inc. in December 2024 and adopted its current name.

Its core business is supplying video security equipment, and company materials describe a focus on AI video analytics, cloud data management and preventive security systems.

Seoul Metro Line 7 is identified as a customer, with a modernization contract for an aging monitoring system whose end date was corrected to December 11, 2026. Two single-sale supply contracts signed in 2024 were terminated, which raised questions about the stability of its core order base.

The biotech push centered on equity stakes in Exicure, and reports say those holdings did not deliver the expected results.

On December 24, 2025, the company resolved to sell 2,222,222 Exicure shares to nine buyers, including individuals, at USD 4.50 per share, and reports said its last high-quality asset left the company just after trading was suspended.

Its core supply business competes in a market where large electronics and components companies and smaller security equipment makers coexist.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual revenue moved from KRW 7.9 billion in 2022 to KRW 5.5 billion in 2023 (down 30.4% year on year), KRW 6.58 billion in 2024 (up 19.5%), and KRW 5.3 billion in 2025 (down 19.5%). 2022 was the largest year in the period and 2025 the smallest.

Operating losses were KRW 9.75 billion in 2022, KRW 6.55 billion in 2023, KRW 6.33 billion in 2024 and KRW 5.97 billion in 2025, a four-year run of losses in which the loss narrowed each year. The operating margin improved from -123.4% in 2022 to -96.2% in 2024 before worsening to -112.7% in 2025.

The preliminary 2025 release on February 27, 2026 showed an operating loss of KRW 5.65 billion and a net loss of KRW 32.5 billion, while the business report figures are KRW 5.97 billion and KRW 35.5 billion, so the regular report values should be used.

The net loss attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 9.47 billion in 2022, KRW 7.04 billion in 2023, KRW 59.3 billion in 2024 and KRW 28.1 billion in 2025. The gap between the 2025 total net loss and the controlling-shareholder loss reflects non-controlling interests.

Operating cash outflow widened from KRW 4.43 billion in 2022 to KRW 15.5 billion in 2025. The preliminary 2025 release attributed part of the improvement to lower cost of sales and better financial income from derivative valuation.

By quarter, the fourth quarter of 2025 showed an operating profit of KRW 8.65 billion on revenue of KRW 411 million, a swing from a KRW 4.55 billion loss in the third quarter, but that profit came in a quarter when revenue collapsed, so it should not be read as core business improvement.

Losses then ran KRW 885 million in the first quarter of 2026 and KRW 4.85 billion in the second, before narrowing to KRW 1.23 billion in the third quarter. The four most recent quarters combined show a controlling-shareholder net loss of KRW 12.9 billion.

05

Industry analysis

Demand for video security is expected to grow as smart city and smart factory deployments expand, and the company says it is focusing on AI video analytics and cloud-based data management. Its results have moved against that demand outlook, however.

Annual revenue fell in 2025 after the 2024 increase, and third-quarter 2026 revenue of KRW 1.67 billion was down 18.2% from the same quarter a year earlier, with the gains coming only from the prior quarter. The evidence for a cyclical turn in the company's own revenue is limited.

Relative to large electronics and components listed companies, its financial capacity is much weaker. The capital erosion ratio on a consolidated basis was 79.1% in the first half of 2026, and auditors flagged material uncertainty about the going-concern assumption.

Its ability to fund equipment investment or new orders from internal cash is therefore constrained. External financing has relied on convertible bonds and rights offerings, which also dilute existing shareholders.

On the order side, the Seoul Metro Line 7 project is the one identified contract, and its end date was revised to December 11, 2026. Whether the security market's growth reaches the company's revenue will need to be checked against the next regular report's revenue mix.

Outlook

What is confirmed is that revenue rose for three straight quarters through the third quarter of 2026 and the operating loss narrowed.

Both consolidated and separate first-half 2026 financial statements received disclaimer opinions, with reports attributing this to financial statements and review materials not being provided.

The delisting question depends on the outcome of the provisional injunction at the Seoul Southern District Court, and cleanup trading has reportedly been on hold pending that ruling.

A small shareholder group said it would seek a change of management through an extraordinary shareholders' meeting, and its proposal was not put to the regular general meeting held in March 2026. The shareholder structure is also shifting.

As of October 2, 2026, a savings bank shareholder listed as the largest shareholder held 8.08%, and a 5% shareholder held 7.08%.

A March 2026 report described a plan to sell a control stake together with a third-party rights offering priced at KRW 656 for about 12.19 million new shares, with payment timing delayed by the trading suspension.

The next regular report, the 2026 business report, will update annual revenue, operating margin and the level of capital erosion.

08

Bull factors

Revenue recovery

Revenue rose from a low of KRW 411 million in the fourth quarter of 2025 to KRW 696 million in the first quarter of 2026, KRW 1.34 billion in the second and KRW 1.67 billion in the third, marking three straight quarterly increases.

Third-quarter revenue was up 24.9% from the prior quarter but down 18.2% from the same quarter of 2025. The recovery has not yet reached early 2025 levels, but the quarterly trend has changed direction.

Narrowing losses and cost structure

The third-quarter 2026 operating loss of KRW 1.23 billion narrowed sharply from the KRW 4.55 billion loss in the third quarter of 2025. The preliminary 2025 release cited lower cost of sales from cost reduction as a main driver. Losses narrowing in operating-margin terms is cited as a positive factor.

Legal route and AI video demand

The company filed a provisional injunction with the Seoul Southern District Court against the delisting decision, and the follow-up procedures depend on the ruling. Demand for AI-based security is expected to grow as smart city and smart factory deployments expand. Converting that demand into orders is the next thing to watch.

Bear factors

Delisting decision and trading suspension

The Korea Exchange made its final delisting decision on March 18, 2026, and the listing committee upheld the decision after the company's objection. Trading was reported as suspended after a KRW 15 billion-plus breach-of-trust case involving former management was disclosed on November 21, 2025.

Whether the company stays listed depends on a court ruling, so the timing of any resumption of normal trading is uncertain.

Capital erosion and going-concern risk

The consolidated capital erosion ratio rose to 79.1% in the first half of 2026 from 45.7% a year earlier. Both consolidated and separate first-half 2026 statements received disclaimer opinions, and the 2025 audit report emphasized material uncertainty over the going-concern assumption.

Cash and cash equivalents were reported at KRW 2.25 billion at the end of 2025, a sharp drop from the prior year.

Governance churn and reliance on external funding

The largest shareholder changed seven times after the 2023 rehabilitation, and breach-of-trust conduct by former management became the direct cause of the delisting decision.

Continued operating losses have pushed the company toward convertible bonds and rights offerings, and a March 2026 report described a third-party rights offering priced at KRW 656. Such financing raises concerns about dilution for existing shareholders.

Risk factors

Listing and legal process

The outcome of the provisional injunction determines whether cleanup trading and the follow-on delisting procedures proceed. If the injunction is rejected, the delisting process will continue, and even a favorable ruling would run alongside criminal complaints and shareholder litigation involving former management. Trading conditions remain constrained until the court timing is clear.

Financial health and going concern

The capital erosion ratio was 79.1% in the first half of 2026, and both consolidated and separate statements received disclaimer opinions. The 2025 audit report also highlighted material uncertainty about going-concern status. If operating losses persist while cash shrinks, the burden of additional funding rises.

Governance and dilution

Largest-shareholder changes have recurred, and plans for a control stake sale and a rights offering were reported without confirmed progress in the latest sources. Continued convertible bond and rights issuance would dilute existing shareholders. Criminal complaints and allegations about fund flows and asset disposals add further uncertainty.

Overall view

Exicure Hitron posted operating losses every year from 2022 to 2025, but the loss narrowed each year, and in 2026 revenue rose for three straight quarters while the third-quarter operating loss narrowed to KRW 1.23 billion.

Against that, the final delisting decision and trading suspension in 2026 and the disclaimer opinions on the first-half 2026 statements with a 79.1% capital erosion ratio point to substantial structural strain in both finances and listing status.

Total equity at the end of 2025 was KRW 15.7 billion against liabilities of KRW 26.6 billion, and the controlling-shareholder net loss over the last four quarters totaled KRW 12.9 billion.

On valuation, no earnings-based multiple can be calculated, and the asset-based multiple sits at a high premium, so any book-value assessment will depend on how recapitalization is carried out.

Bull factors are the revenue recovery, the narrower losses and the possibility that the legal challenge keeps the listing, while bear factors are capital erosion, going-concern uncertainty, reliance on external funding and dilution.

The next checkpoints are the injunction ruling, the December 11, 2026 contract end date, and the operating margin and capital erosion level in the 2026 business report.
