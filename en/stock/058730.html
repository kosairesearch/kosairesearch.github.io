02

Business structure

Development Advance Solution is a construction and building-materials company that began with road safety facilities and now runs energy, heat-exchanger and building-materials businesses in parallel.

Its core line is road noise barriers, noise-barrier tunnels and noise-reduction facilities, and construction order disclosures continued through 2026.

Disclosed orders included a KRW 41.6 billion national highway bypass noise-reduction project in February 2026, a KRW 19.1 billion Incheon noise-barrier project in May 2026, and a KRW 18.2 billion Daegu Grand Park noise-barrier tunnel project in August 2026.

Energy is centered on solar and fuel cells, and FnGuide described a record of 1.1 GW of onshore structures and 0.2 GW of floating solar, with a push toward a solar total-solution business.

In July 2026 the company disclosed a KRW 53.2 billion EPC contract for a 72.98 MW solar project, and in September 2026 a KRW 13.2 billion EPC contract for a fuel-cell power project in Busan.

An independent research house said the company had 120 renewable construction and 140 operation-management projects as of September 2026, with more than 1.4 GW of cumulative experience.

The heat-exchanger business, built on fabrication for shipbuilding and offshore plants, is widening into ultra-low-temperature, high-pressure exchangers for liquefied hydrogen production, according to the Busan Chamber of Commerce and Industry, which announced this in December 2025.

FnGuide said the building-materials segment is shrinking as housing sales and new construction starts decline. As of June 2025 the company had 229 employees and 12 consolidated subsidiaries, a specialist profile far smaller than the large general contractors listed in Korea. The largest shareholder group, including related parties, holds 33.39%, and treasury shares account for 9.55%.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual revenue went from KRW 378.5 billion in 2022 to KRW 378.1 billion in 2023 (down 0.1% year on year), KRW 312 billion in 2024 (down 17.5%), and KRW 273.2 billion in 2025 (down 12.4%), declining for three consecutive years from 2023 to 2025.

Among 2022 through 2025, 2022 was the largest year by revenue and 2025 the smallest. Operating profit rose from KRW 11.5 billion in 2022 to KRW 22.5 billion in 2023 (up 94.6%), then swung to an operating loss of KRW 1.75 billion in 2024 and back to an operating profit of KRW 2.07 billion in 2025.

The operating margin was 3.1% in 2022, 5.9% in 2023, -0.6% in 2024 and 0.8% in 2025, so even after the 2025 return to profit it stayed below 1%.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 1.52 billion in 2022 and KRW 14.2 billion in 2023 (up 832.0%), then a net loss of KRW 3.26 billion in 2024, which turned from profit to loss, and a net loss of KRW 4.82 billion in 2025, a continued loss with a wider deficit.

The 2025 net loss including non-controlling interests was KRW 6.96 billion, larger than the controlling-shareholder loss, with the difference reflecting non-controlling interests.

Operating cash flow was KRW 16.2 billion in 2022, KRW 17.9 billion in 2023, KRW 15.9 billion in 2024 and KRW 38.1 billion in 2025, rising sharply in 2025 even as net losses continued.

On a quarterly basis, the company moved from an operating loss of KRW 2.81 billion in Q3 2025 to an operating profit of KRW 6.78 billion in Q4 2025, when revenue reached KRW 95.7 billion, up 66.5% from the prior quarter.

In Q1 2026 revenue fell 37.8% from the prior quarter to KRW 59.5 billion and the operating loss widened to KRW 5.84 billion, and FnGuide cited the delay at the Saemangeum solar phase 1 project, a shrinking housing market and rising costs as drags.

Q2 2026 revenue rose 35.0% quarter on quarter to KRW 80.4 billion with a narrower operating loss of KRW 3.92 billion. Q3 2026 revenue reached KRW 99.4 billion, up 73.0% year on year, and operating profit was KRW 466 million, a swing to profit.

The controlling-shareholder net loss in Q3 2026 was KRW 2.86 billion, narrower than the prior quarter but wider than the KRW 2.23 billion loss a year earlier, and the four-quarter total from Q4 2025 through Q3 2026 is KRW 11.1 billion.

Industry analysis

Demand from the building-materials segment is weakening as housing sales and new construction starts decline. By contrast, energy demand is moving in a different direction, with solar and fuel-cell EPC orders continuing through 2026.

On the policy side of renewable construction, an independent research house singled out policy changes after the US midterm elections of November 3, 2026 as a variable to watch, in a report published before that vote.

Public road noise-reduction work is also converting into orders, as shown by the national highway 3 noise-barrier project in the disclosures. The heat-exchanger business targets liquefied hydrogen production, a new demand source that connects with energy-transition equipment.

In cycle terms, building materials are in a phase of shrinking market size while renewable construction is in a phase of growing orders, so the two pillars are moving in opposite directions.

Among competitors, the company is a specialist of much smaller scale than the large general contractors listed in Korea, building track record in specific trades such as noise barriers and energy construction.

With 229 employees as of June 2025, it is better read as competing in trade-by-trade bidding than in head-to-head scale competition. Taken together, the shrinking building-materials market and expanding energy and noise-barrier work make 2026 results depend on how quickly orders convert into revenue.

Overall view

Development Advance Solution showed improvement in both revenue and profit in Q3 2026, with revenue of KRW 99.4 billion and operating profit of KRW 466 million.

However, controlling-shareholder net losses have continued for three consecutive quarters from Q1 2026 through Q3 2026, and the four-quarter total is KRW 11.1 billion.

On an annual basis, revenue declined for three consecutive years from 2023 through 2025, and net losses attributable to controlling shareholders in 2024 and 2025 pushed the debt ratio to 136.1%.

Bullish factors include the 2026 order disclosures across solar, fuel-cell and noise-barrier projects, and a market capitalization that sits below book value.

Bearish factors include thin operating margins below 1%, rising costs, a shrinking building-materials market and the delay at the Saemangeum solar phase 1 project.

The two sides carry comparable weight, and the central question is whether orders convert into revenue and whether that revenue sustains profit on a controlling-shareholder basis.

The next checkpoints are the 2026 full-year preliminary results around late January 2027 and the 2026 annual business report due by March 31, 2027.
