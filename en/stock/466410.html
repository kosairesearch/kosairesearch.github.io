Report overview

Profitable Document AI Firm, Monetization Still Unfolding

A digital document solutions company with two decades of profitable operations is shifting its focus toward AI, while its most recent quarter showed both revenue and profit decelerating from a year earlier.

02

Business structure

Founded in 2000 as a document-sharing service, SynapSoft has since built proprietary technology for document filters, viewers, web editors, cloud office, AI OCR, and document analysis, positioning itself as an AI digital document SaaS solution provider.

Its flagship product lineup includes Synap Document Viewer, Synap Document Filter, Synap Editor, Synap OCR Pro, and Synap Office, built on in-house technology that directly processes formats such as Hangul (HWP), Microsoft Office, and PDF.

The company supplies both public-sector agencies and private enterprises, with more than 7,000 private companies reported to be using its solutions as of 2024. Synap Document Viewer for Google Workspace had surpassed 700,000 users as of April 2023, reflecting a track record of integration with global platforms.

The leading domestic competitors in electronic document solutions are FORCS and Unidocs, with FORCS reportedly holding roughly a 70% share in the form-based solutions segment, meaning competitive positioning varies by product category.

In recent years the company has layered generative AI features onto its stable core document business, launching three specialized AI services in 2025: Kynaps, Inex, and Voice Analyzer.

A Shinhan Investment analyst noted in a January 2025 report that the company listed on KOSDAQ in November 2024 and had achieved a seven-year revenue compound annual growth rate of 17.3%, with operating margins above 50% since 2023.

The company continues public-sector sales activity, including participation in a 2026 local government digitalization IT exhibition where it demonstrated its document AI lineup spanning Synap Assistant, Synap DocuAnalyzer, Synap OCR IX, Inex, and an AI data foundry offering.

04

Earnings analysis

Consolidated revenue rose for three consecutive years, from KRW 12.72 billion in 2023 to KRW 13.25 billion in 2024 and KRW 13.47 billion in 2025.

Operating profit, however, declined over the same period from KRW 6.43 billion to KRW 5.07 billion to KRW 4.67 billion, with the operating margin falling for three straight years from 50.6% to 38.3% to 34.7%.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders also edged down from KRW 6.21 billion (2023) to KRW 5.99 billion (2024) to KRW 5.70 billion (2025), suggesting revenue growth has not translated directly into profit growth.

By quarter, the fourth quarter of 2025 delivered the year's strongest results, with revenue of KRW 4.49 billion and operating profit of KRW 2.02 billion, a pattern that appears linked to year-end seasonality in public-sector budget execution.

The first quarter of 2026 continued this solid trend with revenue of KRW 3.44 billion and operating profit of KRW 1.09 billion, but the second quarter of 2026 showed a clear slowdown, with revenue of KRW 2.86 billion, operating profit of KRW 0.49 billion, and controlling-shareholder net income of KRW 0.89 billion — down 5.2% and 41.1% respectively from the same quarter a year earlier (2025 second-quarter revenue of KRW 3.02 billion, operating profit of KRW 0.83 billion).

Operating cash flow remained stable at roughly KRW 6.2–6.4 billion annually from 2023 through 2025, indicating limited divergence between accounting profit and actual cash generation.

Hana Securities noted in a December 2024 report that upfront investment in generative AI and solution upgrades had modestly reduced the operating margin, suggesting the multi-year margin decline is likely more structural, tied to AI-transition investment, than a one-off event.

On the balance sheet, the debt ratio fell rapidly from 23.0% in 2023 to 8.8% in 2024 and 3.4% in 2025, a shift largely attributable to the capital raised through the 2024 KOSDAQ listing.

05

Industry analysis

Korea's electronic and digital document-processing software market has expanded along three axes: upgrades to e-government infrastructure in the public sector, the shift toward remote and cloud-based enterprise work, and, more recently, the integration of generative AI.

The market includes established domestic document-solution vendors such as FORCS and Unidocs, alongside global cloud office providers like Google and Microsoft that act as substitutes or competitors, so it cannot be viewed purely as a contest among domestic players.

Across the broader AI software sector, many listed Korean AI-related companies reportedly remain unprofitable, and a Shinhan Investment analyst wrote in a January 2025 report that since 2024 the scarcity of profitable companies in this sector has driven a growing valuation decoupling based on profitability.

SynapSoft is cited as an example of sustained profitability within this landscape, but its recent decline in operating margin shows the company is not immune to the profitability pressure that AI investment is placing across the sector.

Given its heavy reliance on the public sector, the business repeatedly shows seasonality concentrated around year-end budget execution, which amplifies quarter-to-quarter earnings volatility.

Competitor FORCS reportedly holds a high share in form-based electronic document solutions, creating partial product overlap with SynapSoft's core strengths in viewers, filters, and editors while still occupying a somewhat differentiated niche.

06

Outlook

In its 2026 New Year address, the company outlined four core management priorities: sustaining growth in its document solutions business, converting its AI business into tangible earnings, making its robotics AI business visible, and expanding into global and mobile markets.

Having launched all three previously announced specialized AI services — Kynaps, Inex, and Voice Analyzer — in 2025 and claiming a "triple crown" at AI industry awards for its technology, the company has framed 2026 as the year to translate this completed AI lineup into actual revenue.

Media reports indicate the digital document AI segment accounted for 9.05% of revenue in the first quarter of 2026, making it a key point to watch in coming quarters to see whether the AI business is moving beyond an early stage into meaningful scale.

Public-sector sales activity continues as well; in September 2026 the company took part in a local-government digitalization IT exhibition in Jeju, showcasing its Synap Assistant, Synap DocuAnalyzer, Synap OCR IX, Inex, and AI data foundry solutions.

The company's next regular disclosure, its third-quarter 2026 report, is due under capital markets law by November 16, 2026, which should provide clarity on whether the slowdown seen in the second quarter of 2026 was a temporary adjustment or a more sustained trend.

The robotics AI business and global/mobile expansion mentioned in the New Year address have not yet come with specific disclosed timelines or revenue contributions, making them items to confirm through future IR materials or regular filings.

Bull factors

A Rare Multi-Year Profitable AI-Adjacent Name

While many listed Korean AI-related companies remain unprofitable, SynapSoft has recorded operating profit in every reported period from 2023 through 2025, demonstrating a stable earnings base.

Operating cash flow has also exceeded KRW 6.2 billion annually, showing limited gap between accounting profit and actual cash generation. A Shinhan Investment analyst noted in a January 2025 report that this consistent profitability is scarce within the sector.

AI Product Lineup Completed, 2026 Framed as the Monetization Year

The company stated it launched all three of its specialized AI services — Kynaps, Inex, and Voice Analyzer — in 2025, and its 2026 New Year address framed converting this lineup into real revenue growth as a top priority.

Media reports put the digital document AI segment at 9.05% of revenue in the first quarter of 2026, an early but established starting point for growth. Sales activity, including participation in public-sector IT exhibitions, has continued.

Stable Balance Sheet with Low Leverage

The debt ratio fell quickly from 23.0% in 2023 to 3.4% in 2025, a shift likely reflecting the capital raised through the 2024 KOSDAQ listing. This low leverage provides financial flexibility to continue investing in AI and robotics initiatives without pressure to seek external financing.

More than two decades of accumulated document-format processing technology and a broad customer base spanning both public and private sectors further support this stability.

09

Bear factors

Three Straight Years of Margin Decline Plus a Recent Quarterly Slowdown

The operating margin fell for three straight years, from 50.6% in 2023 to 38.3% in 2024 and 34.7% in 2025. In the second quarter of 2026, revenue and operating profit declined 5.2% and 41.1% year-on-year respectively, widening the extent of the slowdown.

Hana Securities noted in a December 2024 report that upfront investment for AI technology adoption was behind this margin pressure, suggesting the investment burden could persist for a time.

AI Business Still Contributes a Modest Share of Revenue

The digital document AI segment was reported at 9.05% of revenue in the first quarter of 2026, indicating the AI transition the company emphasizes still represents a modest share of overall results.

While several new products launched in 2025, the specific scale and timing of their revenue contribution have not yet been formally detailed. The company appears to be in a transitional phase, growing new businesses while relying on the stable cash flow of its established document solutions.

Public-Sector Reliance and Potential Share Overhang

The seasonality of revenue being concentrated at year-end reflects reliance on the public-sector budget execution cycle, a factor that amplifies quarter-to-quarter earnings volatility.

A securities-firm analysis from January 2025 noted that stakes held by early-stage investor Naver (3.96%) and an associated financial investor (10.15%) had been cited as potential future overhang.

Given the limited float typical of small-cap KOSDAQ stocks, changes in the ownership structure are considered a factor that could affect share price volatility.

10

Risk factors

Earnings Volatility

Seasonality tied to the public-sector budget execution cycle produces significant quarter-to-quarter earnings variation, and periods like the second quarter of 2026, when both revenue and profit fell year-on-year simultaneously, could recur. Whether the operating margin continues to decline while AI investment continues is also worth monitoring.

Competitive and Substitution Risk

The domestic electronic document market includes competitors such as FORCS and Unidocs, and global cloud office providers like Google and Microsoft can act as potential substitutes.

If AI functionality becomes standardized across the industry, the differentiation of the company's core viewer and editor business could be diluted.

Ownership Structure and Float Risk

As a small-cap KOSDAQ stock with a limited float, stakes held by early-stage investors and associated financial investors have been cited as potential sources of future selling pressure. Given the characteristics of small-cap stocks, price volatility related to supply-demand shifts can be relatively pronounced.

12

Overall view

SynapSoft is a KOSDAQ-listed software company that has maintained multi-year profitability on the strength of document-processing technology built up over more than two decades; revenue grew steadily from 2023 through 2025, but the operating margin declined for three straight years, and the second quarter of 2026 showed a clear slowdown with both revenue and profit falling year-on-year.

The company states it completed its AI product lineup in 2025 and has framed 2026 as the year to convert that lineup into real revenue, but media reports put the AI revenue share at just 9.05% in the first quarter of 2026, suggesting the business remains at an early stage.

On the balance sheet, fundamentals appear solid, with the debt ratio falling to a single-digit level and operating cash flow remaining stable.

Still, factors that warrant balanced consideration include seasonality tied to public-sector reliance, the presence of competitors and global substitutes, and the potential overhang from stakes held by early-stage investors.

The company's next regular filing, the third-quarter 2026 report due by the November 16 legal deadline, along with the trajectory of the AI revenue share in subsequent quarters, should offer important clues for assessing the nature of the recent earnings slowdown.
