Report overview

Flat Sales and Thinner Margins Put the AI Shift in Focus

Synapsoft's revenue rose from KRW 12.7 billion in 2023 to KRW 13.5 billion in 2025, but operating profit fell from KRW 6.43 billion to KRW 4.67 billion, and third-quarter 2026 revenue was down year on year as the company works to show its AI products in reported results.

02

Business structure

Synapsoft is a Document AI company that develops and supplies document processing software, including document viewers, document filters and optical character recognition (OCR), to companies and public institutions.

According to 2024 press coverage ahead of its listing, the company holds a share above 95% in the document filter segment, and 80% of government agencies along with major private companies such as Samsung Electronics, SK, Hyundai and LG use its solutions.

The company does not run system integration (SI) projects and earns revenue only from licenses and solutions, with renewal and maintenance contracts accounting for more than 40% of sales as stated in 2024.

A 2025 report put the public-to-private sales mix at roughly 50:50, and the private share declined after NAVER, its largest private client, closed its office business. The same report said the top five customers account for 15.1% of total sales.

Revenue from the digital document AI business was KRW 900 million in the first half of 2025, or 14.8% of total sales.

Core products include Synap OCR IX, an AI-based OCR, Synap DocAnalyzer for document structure analysis, Synap Assistant for on-premise public deployments, Ainex as a cloud RAG platform, and Kynapse, an internal knowledge management platform.

Distribution combines direct sales with supply to platform companies, including a document viewer for Samsung SDS's Brity Works and a web editor for Samsung SDS's ESG disclosure platform. According to the company's February 2026 newsletter, 85% of Korea's top 20 financial companies have adopted Synap Document Viewer.

04

Earnings analysis

On a DART-filed basis, annual revenue went from KRW 12.7 billion in 2023 to KRW 13.3 billion in 2024 (up 4.2% year on year) and KRW 13.5 billion in 2025 (up 1.7%), which marks two consecutive years of growth in 2024 and 2025.

Operating profit moved the other way, from KRW 6.43 billion in 2023 to KRW 5.07 billion in 2024 (down 21.1%) and KRW 4.67 billion in 2025 (down 7.9%), which marks two consecutive years of decline. Because sales rose while profit fell, operating margin dropped from 50.6% in 2023 to 38.3% in 2024 and 34.7% in 2025.

At its 2024 listing, the company said it aimed to keep an operating margin around 50% while growing, and the filed figures remain well below that level.

When second- and third-quarter 2024 sales fell year on year, the company attributed part of the decline to the end of NAVER's office service, which it said had cut about KRW 200 million of revenue per quarter.

The IPO plan allocated KRW 5 billion to AI development and KRW 4.4 billion to SaaS and existing products, so part of the cost increase may reflect investment, although the filings alone do not separate the causes line by line.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders fell from KRW 6.21 billion in 2023 to KRW 5.99 billion in 2024 (down 3.6%) and KRW 5.7 billion in 2025 (down 4.8%), and net income including non-controlling interests followed the same path.

Operating cash flow was KRW 6.4 billion, KRW 6.25 billion and KRW 6.23 billion over 2023 to 2025, exceeding controlling-shareholder net income in each year.

Across the five filed quarters, revenue peaked at KRW 4.49 billion in the fourth quarter of 2025 and then declined to KRW 3.44 billion, KRW 2.86 billion and KRW 2.93 billion in the first three quarters of 2026. Third-quarter 2026 revenue was down 6.5% year on year, while operating profit rose 8.6% to KRW 1.01 billion.

Controlling-shareholder net income for the third quarter of 2026 was KRW 1.34 billion, and the last four quarters together totaled KRW 5.69 billion.

05

Industry analysis

Demand for document processing software is shifting as generative AI raises the need to turn documents into data that AI systems can read. In line with this shift, the company said it launched Synap OCR IX, a VLM-based agentic OCR, in February 2026.

The government is pushing an AI transformation policy, and in September 2026 the company demonstrated five document AI products for local government administration at a trade show in Jeju.

In April 2026 the company said it had been listed as a supplier candidate for the NIPA AX One-Stop Voucher program, which supports AI transformation for companies of all sizes through vouchers. In July 2026 it was selected for NIPA's AI-native SaaS development and commercialization program.

On competition, the company points to its document filter share and public and financial references, but large IT and platform companies are also building document AI features into their own services, which raises competitive intensity.

Among listed peers, NAVER and Samsung SDS are both supply partners and companies expanding their own AI services, so the relationship combines cooperation and competition. Because there is limited numerical evidence on market size, policy demand and platform competition are the key variables.

06

Outlook

In its 2026 New Year message, the company set four priorities: sustained growth in document solutions, results from AI business, making its robot AI business visible, and expanding globally and into mobile.

At its 2024 listing it had targeted raising SaaS to 40% of total service revenue by 2026, but the filed tables do not break out SaaS sales, so progress against that goal cannot be checked numerically.

In July 2026 it was selected for NIPA's AI-native SaaS program to build a platform that converts unstructured documents into structured data for AI, and it targets cutting document preprocessing and structuring time by up to 80% compared with manual work.

It plans to pursue global SaaS sales through an AWS Marketplace listing within 2026 and to expand OEM supply in Japan through a local partner. In April 2026 it was listed as a supplier candidate for the AX One-Stop Voucher program, which requires a consortium with demand companies.

In October 2026 it moved its Kynapse knowledge platform to paid service, with a 50% discount for subscriptions of five or more users through December 2026. The January 2026 message also said cumulative references for Synap OCR had passed 200. Numerical confirmation of the new business is most likely to come with the 2026 annual report filing.

Valuation can be read through two lenses, earnings and book value. On earnings, controlling-shareholder net income totaled KRW 5.69 billion over the last four quarters, and the two-year decline from KRW 6.21 billion in 2023 to KRW 5.7 billion in 2025 is the starting point for interpreting any multiple.

On book value, equity of KRW 6.97 billion at the end of 2025 was entirely attributable to controlling shareholders, with total liabilities of only KRW 2.36 billion, and the shares trade at a price-to-book ratio below 1x.

One reading treats that low multiple as a discount to asset value, while another treats it as the market giving limited credit to the company's ability to turn equity into earnings. Cash returns to shareholders through dividends are small.

A June 2026 filing showed the CEO bought 10,000 shares on June 4 and 5 in the open market, raising his stake from 39.36% to 39.56%. Comparisons with historical multiple ranges would need separate data, so earnings trends and the multiple on book value are the main reference points.

08

Bull factors

High profitability from a license-based model

The company earns revenue only from licenses and solutions without SI work, and its operating margin was 50.6% in 2023. It said in 2024 that renewal and maintenance contracts account for more than 40% of sales, and operating cash flow exceeded controlling-shareholder net income in every year from 2023 to 2025.

Even after the decline, the 2025 operating margin of 34.7% remains in the mid-30s, which supports the bull case.

Public and financial references as a cross-selling base

Press coverage in 2024 cited 80% of government agencies as users, and the company's February 2026 newsletter said 85% of Korea's top 20 financial companies have adopted its document viewer. Its January 2026 message said Synap OCR had passed 200 cumulative references. An existing customer base gives the AI products an entry point, which is the core of the bull case.

Financial room and a CEO share purchase

Total liabilities were KRW 2.36 billion at the end of 2025, and the debt ratio fell to 3.4% from 23.0% in 2023. A June 2026 filing showed the CEO bought 10,000 shares in the open market on June 4 and 5, raising his stake to 39.56%.

The company's financial room suggests it can fund AI investment and overseas expansion internally, which supports the bull reading.

09

Bear factors

Slowing sales growth and falling profit

Revenue growth slowed from 4.2% in 2024 to 1.7% in 2025, and third-quarter 2026 revenue was down 6.5% year on year. Operating profit declined in both 2024 and 2025, and operating margin fell to 34.7%.

Quarterly revenue has stayed at a lower level since the KRW 4.49 billion peak in the fourth quarter of 2025, at KRW 3.44 billion, KRW 2.86 billion and KRW 2.93 billion in the three quarters of 2026.

The AI business is still a small share

Revenue from the digital document AI business was KRW 900 million in the first half of 2025, or 14.8% of sales. The 40% SaaS target for 2026 set in 2024 does not appear in the filed tables, so progress cannot be checked numerically.

Whether the new business is large enough to offset slowing sales in the existing products has not yet shown up in the figures.

Customer mix and the change in the NAVER relationship

Public and private customers were roughly split 50:50 in a 2025 report, so the company is sensitive to public budgets and policy timing. NAVER, its largest private customer, ended its office service and steadily sold down its stake. The company said in 2024 that the office service end had cut about KRW 200 million of revenue per quarter.

10

Risk factors

Overseas expansion execution

The company plans to expand global SaaS sales through an AWS Marketplace listing within 2026 and to grow OEM supply in Japan through a local partner. Overseas sales could slip depending on local certification and payment requirements and on the partners' sales capability.

In that case, the revenue contribution of AI products would be pushed back and the offset to slowing domestic sales would be limited. Execution speed could determine when the new business first shows results.

Competition and technology transition

As generative AI and large platform companies widen their document features, standalone document software vendors could lose pricing power. The company is responding with VLM-based OCR and agent features, but there is no numerical evidence that its transition is faster than rivals'. Platform companies that are both partners and competitors could affect negotiating terms.

Earnings seasonality and share float

According to the company's 2024 statements, the fourth quarter has historically carried about 35% of sales, which makes quarterly results uneven. Operating profit was KRW 489 million in the second quarter of 2026, showing how wide the swings can be. With the CEO holding 39.56%, the free float may be limited, which could add to share price volatility.

Overall view

Synapsoft's revenue rose from KRW 12.7 billion in 2023 to KRW 13.5 billion in 2025, but operating profit fell from KRW 6.43 billion to KRW 4.67 billion and operating margin dropped from 50.6% to 34.7%.

Quarterly revenue in 2026 was KRW 3.44 billion, KRW 2.86 billion and KRW 2.93 billion after the fourth-quarter 2025 peak, while third-quarter 2026 operating profit rose year on year to KRW 1.01 billion. Strengths include a license-based earnings model, public and financial references, and a 3.4% debt ratio.

Weaknesses include slowing sales growth, two straight years of lower profit, a still small AI business and a customer base reliant on public budgets. On valuation, a price below book value sits alongside a shrinking earnings trend, so the reading depends on which factor is weighted more.

Whether policy demand or platform competition proves stronger will show up in figures in the 2026 annual report and the first-quarter 2027 results. The key checkpoints are the SaaS revenue share, the scale of overseas OEM supply and the direction of the operating margin in 2027.
