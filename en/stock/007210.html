Byuksan: Inorganic insulation demand amid a construction slowdown

02

Business structure

Byuksan was founded in 1971 and its core business is building materials. Its building materials segment makes and sells insulation, interior and exterior panels, and its main product group is inorganic insulation such as glass wool and mineral wool.

The largest end market for inorganic insulation is reported to be factories and warehouses. In sandwich panel core materials, inorganic materials have been replacing organic ones since the building code amendment took effect in December 2021.

Its kitchen appliance and ventilation business, operated through a subsidiary, has improved as demand for higher-value products grew. In 2025 the company acquired a fastener maker, adding construction and interior fasteners to its lineup.

In January 2026 it set up a wholly owned real estate subsidiary, Byuksan Property, to handle development and project management. Brand-ranking data group Byuksan with KCC, Dongwha Enterprise and SAMPYO Cement among listed building material companies.

The structure is a building materials core, with kitchen and ventilation, fasteners and real estate project management added around it.

04

Earnings analysis

On a reported basis, annual sales rose from KRW 519.9 billion in 2022 to KRW 620.7 billion in 2023, up 19.4%, and reached their largest level in the 2022 to 2025 range at KRW 643.1 billion in 2024, up 3.6%.

Sales in 2025 were KRW 610.7 billion, down 5.0%, and the company said the construction slowdown reduced both sales and operating profit.

Operating profit went from KRW 18.8 billion in 2022 to a peak of KRW 44.7 billion in 2023, up 137.7%, then fell 42.5% to KRW 25.7 billion in 2024 and 9.5% to KRW 23.3 billion in 2025, two consecutive years of decline.

The operating margin rose from 3.6% in 2022 to 7.2% in 2023 and then slipped to 4.0% in 2024 and 3.8% in 2025. Net income attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 9.94 billion in 2022, KRW 33.3 billion in 2023 (up 235.1%), KRW 16.2 billion in 2024 (down 51.2%) and KRW 22.8 billion in 2025 (up 40.5%).

Total net income in 2025 was KRW 27.4 billion, which includes non-controlling interests and differs from the controlling-shareholder figure; the company cited a bargain purchase gain from consolidating a subsidiary.

Quarterly sales went from KRW 158.9 billion in the third quarter of 2025 to KRW 161.8 billion in the fourth quarter, KRW 139.2 billion in the first quarter of 2026, KRW 162.6 billion in the second quarter and KRW 139.2 billion in the third quarter.

Operating profit over the same stretch was KRW 7.06 billion, KRW 6.23 billion, KRW 4.67 billion, KRW 9.83 billion and KRW 594 million. Net income attributable to controlling shareholders over the latest four quarters totaled KRW 9.66 billion.

Operating cash flow was KRW 33.7 billion in 2022, KRW 27.9 billion in 2023, KRW 54.6 billion in 2024 and KRW 36.4 billion in 2025, and the debt ratio fell from 99.1% in 2022 to 76.3% in 2025.

05

Industry analysis

Demand for inorganic insulation is driven by fire-safety regulation.

Since the building code amendment took effect in December 2021, insulation for sandwich panel cores has been shifting from organic to inorganic materials, and NH Investment & Securities said in an April 2025 report that glass wool demand surged afterward.

The same report noted that Byuksan set a record annual profit in 2023. The construction side is mixed. The weak first half of 2025 was attributed to a construction slowdown, higher construction costs and a slump in new-build presales.

In a late 2024 report, NH Investment & Securities said that easing rates and stabilizing construction costs could revive corporate investment and project financing, supporting demand from factories and warehouses.

In April 2025 the same firm said construction orders, building permits and construction start areas were gradually rebounding. Among listed building material companies, Byuksan is grouped with KCC, Dongwha Enterprise and SAMPYO Cement. By market capitalization, Byuksan is smaller than the large materials companies in the same category.

06

Outlook

Company-side developments fall into two groups, products and business structure. In February 2026 the company launched a cryogenic glass wool blanket and announced it had completed development of a high-temperature microporous insulation material.

In April 2026 it launched a 50K mineral wool for lightweight sandwich panels.

Three mineral wool products from its Yeongdong plant received Environmental Product Declaration certification in 2025, valid from February 26, 2025 to February 25, 2028, which the company expects to help with preferential procurement by government and public agencies.

On pricing, The Bell reported in August 2026 that price increases on key products such as organic insulation roughly doubled first-half standalone operating profit.

On structure, Byuksan Property, established in January 2026, is set to consolidate real estate functions scattered across the group and focus on development project management, feasibility analysis and integrated group property management.

Over the medium term the company is considering extending into project management and advisory work for external clients. The next checkpoints are how far the price increases carry into operating profit after the fourth quarter of 2026, and whether the real estate subsidiary wins outside mandates.

08

Bull factors

Fire rules and inorganic insulation demand

Since the building code amendment in December 2021, sandwich panel cores have been shifting toward inorganic materials, and Byuksan has a production base centered on glass wool and mineral wool.

It announced a high-temperature microporous insulation material in February 2026 and a 50K mineral wool for lightweight sandwich panels in April 2026. Bulls argue that because regulation sets the direction of demand, the base for this product group survives even when construction weakens.

Price increases and a quarterly rebound

In August 2026 The Bell reported that price increases on key products such as organic insulation more than doubled first-half standalone operating profit. On a reported basis, second-quarter 2026 operating profit rose 110.2% from the prior quarter to KRW 9.83 billion.

Bulls argue that if cost pressures can be passed on through prices, the operating margin, now near 4%, has room to improve.

Diversification and new subsidiaries

With the 2025 consolidation of a fastener maker, the company added construction and interior fasteners, and in January 2026 it set up Byuksan Property for development and project management. Its kitchen appliance and ventilation business is said to have improved on demand for higher-value products. Bulls argue that businesses outside building materials could cushion swings in earnings.

09

Bear factors

Construction cycle and falling sales

Sales in 2025 were KRW 610.7 billion, down 5.0%, and the company cited the construction slowdown as the cause. Third-quarter 2026 sales were also down 12.4% year on year at KRW 139.2 billion.

Bears argue that if a slowdown in new-build presales combines with higher construction costs, shipments of building materials could fall again.

Earnings volatility and thin margins

The operating margin peaked at 7.2% in 2023, then fell to 4.0% in 2024 and 3.8% in 2025. Third-quarter 2026 operating profit was KRW 594 million, down 91.6% year on year. Quarterly operating profit has swung between KRW 594 million and KRW 9.83 billion, and bears argue that this makes earnings hard to predict.

One-off support to 2025 net income

The company said 2025 net income of KRW 27.4 billion was helped by a bargain purchase gain. The rise in controlling-shareholder net income to KRW 22.8 billion cannot be read purely as an operating recovery. Because operating profit fell 9.5% in the same year, bears argue the core business has weakened.

10

Risk factors

Construction and presale cycle

Byuksan's sales and profit are sensitive to the construction and new-build presale cycle. In the first half of 2025, the construction slowdown, higher construction costs and a weaker presale market were cited as causes of the weaker results.

How fast demand from factories and warehouses recovers may depend on whether lower rates revive project financing.

Costs versus pricing

Price increases were reported to have lifted first-half 2026 profit, but the same effect is not assured if construction and raw material costs rise again. The drop in third-quarter 2026 operating profit to KRW 594 million shows that earnings do not stay stable on pricing alone. How long price increases hold from quarter to quarter is a key variable for earnings.

Acquisitions and subsidiary execution

Consolidating a fastener maker and setting up Byuksan Property widen the business, but it may take time for new businesses to contribute core profit. One-off gains such as the bargain purchase gain in 2025 net income do not recur by definition. Whether the real estate project management unit wins outside work also remains an execution risk.

12

Overall view

Reported results show that Byuksan's operating profit has fallen for two consecutive years since its 2023 peak, and the operating margin has stayed around 4%.

Fire-safety-driven demand for inorganic insulation is cited as a support for the demand base, and the price increases and second-quarter rebound in the first half of 2026 were reported as signs of recovery.

On the other side, third-quarter 2026 sales fell year on year and operating profit dropped to KRW 594 million, showing high quarterly volatility. Part of the 2025 net income increase came from a bargain purchase gain, so it is hard to read that profit level as a core business recovery.

The price-to-book ratio sits at a steep discount to book value, but the interpretation depends on whether earnings recover. The next checkpoints are the 2026 preliminary full-year results (around late February 2027), the 2026 annual business report (due by late March 2027) and first-quarter 2027 results (mid-May 2027).
