02

Business structure

Miraeasset Maps REIT 1, formally Miraeasset Maps No. 1 Trust-Type REIT, listed on the KOSPI on August 5, 2020 as a real estate leasing and supply company. Asset management is handled by Mirae Asset Global Investments, and assets under management are reported at roughly KRW 304.2 billion.

The core asset is the Gwanggyo Central Prugio City retail property (the Gwanggyo Lotte Mall) in Suwon's Gwanggyo New City, alongside equity securities in the Gangnam KG Tower (held via Koramco No. 47 REIT) and preferred shares in Majesta City Tower 1 (via Capstone No. 42 Fund), which reduce concentration in a single physical asset.

The lease structure at the Gwanggyo Lotte Mall is layered: Miraeasset Maps REIT 1 leases to GS Retail, which subleases to Lotte Shopping, with rent set on a fixed annual escalation basis rather than tied to sales.

Under this structure, GS Retail is obligated to cover the gap between the head lease and sublease rent even if store sales weaken, making the tenant's creditworthiness the key variable for rent collection.

The master lease on the Gwanggyo Lotte Mall is structured to run through 2035, providing long-term cash flow visibility. Since its founding, the company has pursued portfolio diversification through rights offerings that added the KG Tower equity securities and the Majesta City Tower 1 preferred shares.

Its fiscal periods are split between May and November, resulting in regulatory filings twice a year. As of the September 2025 asset management reports, vacancy rates at the Gwanggyo Lotte Mall, Gangnam KG Tower, and Majesta City Tower 1 were all reported at 0%.

04

Earnings analysis

Revenue rose from KRW 5.95 billion in 2022 to KRW 10.9 billion in 2023, up 83.3% year on year and the largest figure in the 2022-2025 span. It then fell to KRW 6.88 billion in 2024 (down 36.9%) before edging up to KRW 6.92 billion in 2025 (up 0.5%), staying essentially flat.

Operating profit followed a similar path, surging 157.3% from KRW 3.15 billion in 2022 to KRW 8.1 billion in 2023, before dropping to KRW 4.01 billion in 2024 (down 50.4%) and recovering slightly to KRW 4.09 billion in 2025 (up 1.9%).

The operating margin rose from 52.9% in 2022 to 74.2% in 2023, then settled lower at 58.3% in 2024 and 59.1% in 2025.

Controlling-interest net income jumped 781.3% from KRW 633 million in 2022 to KRW 5.58 billion in 2023 at its peak, then declined for two straight years to KRW 1.53 billion in 2024 (down 72.5%) and KRW 261 million in 2025 (down 83.0%).

With no non-controlling interest, net income including non-controlling interest matched the controlling-interest figure in every year.

Total equity at the end of 2025 stood at KRW 96.1 billion (entirely attributable to controlling shareholders) against total liabilities of KRW 204.9 billion, with the debt ratio rising again from 200.0% in 2024 to 213.2% in 2025.

Operating cash flow moved more gently than net income, at KRW 2.56 billion in 2022, KRW 2.71 billion in 2023, KRW 2.38 billion in 2024, and KRW 1.85 billion in 2025, showing a softening trend since 2024.

The sum of controlling-interest net income over the last four quarters (Q3 2025 through Q2 2026) was only KRW 195 million, below the full-year 2025 figure and indicating that the profit slowdown has continued.

05

Industry analysis

The listed REIT sector in Korea is closely tied to interest rate levels, and smaller REITs tend to have lower trading liquidity and higher share price volatility.

The national average retail vacancy rate rose to 13.36% in the second quarter of 2026 from 11.96% in the first quarter of 2021, pointing to a generally challenging commercial leasing environment.

However, Miraeasset Maps REIT 1's core asset, the Gwanggyo Lotte Mall, is relatively insulated from these swings because of its master lease structure, and its vacancy rate stood at 0% as of September 2025.

Among KOSPI-listed REITs, SK Reit, Lotte Reit, Hanwha REIT, ESR Kendall Square REIT, and Shinhan Alpha REIT span a range of sectors including office, logistics, and retail and form a comparable peer group.

Compared with these larger REITs, Miraeasset Maps REIT 1 has a smaller asset base (about KRW 304.2 billion under management) and a higher concentration in a single commercial property, giving it a distinct risk-return profile in terms of asset concentration.

As with other retail-asset REITs such as Lotte Reit and E Kocref Cr-Reit, the shift toward e-commerce and the resulting slowdown in offline sales is a shared variable that can affect tenants' ability to pay rent over the long run.

Interest rate movements directly affect capitalization rates and borrowing costs across the REIT sector, and this sensitivity is relatively higher for Miraeasset Maps REIT 1 given a debt ratio above 200%.

Outlook

Because Miraeasset Maps REIT 1's fiscal periods split between May and November, it has followed a pattern of disclosing cash dividend resolutions around January and July each year.

The most recent cash dividend resolution was disclosed on July 15, 2026, and based on the established cadence the next one would be expected around January 2027.

In August 2026 the company filed its business report for the fiscal period ending May 2026, and the next full annual results are expected via the business report for the period ending November 2026, likely around February 2027.

On the asset side, because the master lease on the Gwanggyo Lotte Mall runs through 2035, short-term volatility in rental income itself is limited.

By contrast, the KG Tower equity securities and the Majesta City Tower 1 preferred shares are linked to the performance of their respective external asset managers, so vacancy rates and distribution performance should be monitored periodically through those managers' asset management reports.

No specific plans for additional asset acquisitions or capital raises were identified in public materials, so further portfolio diversification will need to be confirmed through future disclosures.

The stake held by major shareholder Mirae Asset Global Investments has gradually declined, from 11.23% in February 2026 to 10.06% in September 2026, making any further sale disclosures a variable worth watching.

08

Bull factors

Cash flow visibility from a long-term master lease

The core Gwanggyo Lotte Mall asset is covered by a master lease through 2035 with a fixed-escalation rent structure rather than a sales-linked one. Tenant GS Retail is obligated to cover any gap with the sublease rent, giving relatively high stability to rent collection even if store sales fluctuate. As of September 2025, vacancy rates at the core assets were all reported at 0%.

Portfolio diversification to reduce single-asset concentration

The company started with a single-asset structure centered on the Gwanggyo Lotte Mall but later added Gangnam KG Tower equity securities and Majesta City Tower 1 preferred shares through rights offerings.

This increased the share of equity- and preferred-share-type investment assets alongside the physical property, somewhat reducing reliance on the single retail asset. These added assets, however, still depend on the performance of external asset managers.

Operating margin holding at an elevated level

The operating margin was 59.1% in 2025, a slight improvement from 58.3% in 2024 and still above the 52.9% recorded in 2022. Given the low fixed-cost burden typical of a leasing business, the margin relative to revenue has remained stable within a defined range. This also reflects the downside rigidity built into the rent collection structure.

Bear factors

Two consecutive years of declining controlling-interest net income

Controlling-interest net income peaked at KRW 5.58 billion in 2023, then declined for two straight years to KRW 1.53 billion in 2024 (down 72.5%) and KRW 261 million in 2025 (down 83.0%).

The sum over the last four quarters (Q3 2025 through Q2 2026) was only KRW 195 million, below the full-year 2025 figure, indicating the decline has continued. While revenue and operating profit moved relatively gently, the net income line contracted far more sharply, suggesting a larger burden from non-operating costs.

Intensifying competition at the core trade area

While rent itself is protected by the master lease, store sales at the Gwanggyo Lotte Mall are reported to be on a declining trend amid intensifying competition from nearby trade areas.

If this sales weakness persists, it could put the company at a disadvantage in lease renegotiations after the contract expires in 2035. The broader offline retail environment is also not favorable, with the national average retail vacancy rate rising to 13.36% in the second quarter of 2026.

Continued stake reduction by the major shareholder

Major shareholder Mirae Asset Global Investments has sold shares on the open market on multiple occasions through 2026, with its stake falling from 11.23% in February 2026 to 10.06% in September 2026.

While selling alone does not determine share price direction, the major shareholder's ongoing stake reduction is a supply-demand variable worth watching. Further share disposal disclosures should be monitored going forward.

Risk factors

Financial and interest rate risk

The debt ratio rose again from 200.0% in 2024 to 213.2% in 2025, with total liabilities of KRW 204.9 billion more than double total equity of KRW 96.1 billion. Changes in the interest rate environment can affect both borrowing costs and the valuation of investment assets.

Operating cash flow has shown a softening trend, from KRW 2.56 billion in 2022 to KRW 1.85 billion in 2025, warranting a check on whether interest expense burdens are rising.

Tenancy structure risk

Rent collection on the core asset depends on a dual lease structure running through GS Retail and Lotte Shopping, making the creditworthiness and continued operations of these retailers a key variable.

If store-level sales remain structurally weak, the company could face unfavorable terms in lease renegotiations after the 2035 contract expiry.

The KG Tower equity securities and Majesta City Tower 1 preferred shares are also tied to the performance of external asset managers, limiting the company's independent control over those returns.

Liquidity and supply-demand risk

As a small-cap REIT, trading value and liquidity can be limited. The major shareholder's ongoing stake reduction could continue further, acting as a supply-demand variable. Given its small-cap nature, individual disclosures can trigger relatively larger share price swings.

Overall view

Thanks to the master lease structure anchored by the Gwanggyo Lotte Mall, Miraeasset Maps REIT 1's revenue and operating profit have moved relatively gently.

However, controlling-interest net income peaked at KRW 5.58 billion in 2023 and has since fallen for two straight years to KRW 1.53 billion in 2024 and KRW 261 million in 2025, with the trailing four-quarter sum remaining at around KRW 195 million, indicating the profit decline has continued.

The debt ratio rose again to 213.2% in 2025, keeping financial leverage a point to monitor.

On the asset side, holdings of KG Tower equity securities and Majesta City Tower 1 preferred shares alongside the physical Gwanggyo Lotte Mall property have somewhat reduced single-asset concentration, though these assets are also tied to the performance of external managers.

The gradual decline in the major shareholder Mirae Asset Global Investments' stake is another supply-demand variable to watch. It will be important to track changes in earnings and dividend funding capacity around the upcoming dividend resolution and the business report covering the fiscal period ending November 2026.
