Dong SUH: Operating profit holds, net income wobbles

Operating profit rose three years in a row through 2025, but quarterly operating profit and net income attributable to controlling shareholders have declined for two straight quarters since the second quarter of 2026.

02

Business structure

Dong SUH Companies was founded in 1975, listed on the KOSPI in 2016, and holds two unlisted subsidiaries.

Its business is split into a food segment (food service ingredients and imported beverages), a manufacturing segment (packaging materials, tea and coffee product packaging and sales), a trading and export segment (creamer exports and raw material purchasing agency), and other activities such as bonded warehousing and leasing.

Aggregator sites report different figures, but on a common basis the food segment is the largest at a little over half of revenue, with manufacturing and trading and export each at roughly one-fifth to one-quarter.

As the holding company, it owns 50% of the coffee affiliate, 66% of a beverage affiliate and 50% of an oils affiliate. Creamer exports and raw material purchasing agency work were 83.76% export-oriented as of December 2025.

The company is pushing smaller-size and premium products, a kombucha launch aimed at younger consumers, digital marketing and broader distribution channels. In coffee-related categories such as ready-to-drink beverages and capsule coffee, it competes with foreign brands and listed domestic food companies.

Because coffee products make up a large share of sales, raw material prices and consumer demand are the main drivers of earnings.

04

Earnings analysis

On a disclosed basis, revenue was largest in 2022 at KRW 556.5 billion and smallest in 2024 at KRW 488.9 billion. Revenue fell 12.0% to KRW 489.7 billion in 2023 and 0.2% to KRW 488.9 billion in 2024, then rebounded 9.0% to KRW 532.9 billion in 2025, still below the 2022 level.

Operating profit rose from KRW 39.2 billion in 2022 to KRW 43.8 billion in 2023 (up 11.7%), KRW 44.5 billion in 2024 (up 1.7%) and KRW 45.3 billion in 2025 (up 1.9%), an increase in each year from 2023 through 2025 that began from the 2022 base.

Operating margin went from 7.0% in 2022 to 8.9% in 2023 and 9.1% in 2024, then eased to 8.5% in 2025. Margins rose in 2023 and 2024 even as revenue fell, which may reflect changes in cost and selling expense structure, but the detail needs to be checked in the business report's cost breakdown.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders peaked at KRW 167.9 billion in 2022, then moved to KRW 144.9 billion in 2023 (down 13.7%), KRW 157.3 billion in 2024 (up 8.6%) and KRW 144.9 billion in 2025 (down 7.9%).

In 2023 operating profit rose 11.7% while controlling-shareholder net income fell 13.7%, suggesting items below operating profit weighed heavily. Net income including non-controlling interests was KRW 146.5 billion in 2025, and the gap to the controlling-shareholder figure is the non-controlling share.

Operating cash flow went from KRW 86.2 billion in 2022 to KRW 118.1 billion in 2023, then KRW 113.2 billion in 2024 and KRW 106.2 billion in 2025, staying above KRW 100 billion.

At the end of 2025, total equity was KRW 1.74 trillion, of which controlling-shareholder equity was KRW 1.72 trillion, and total liabilities were KRW 61.1 billion, bringing the debt ratio down from 5.3% in 2022 to 3.5% in 2025.

Quarterly, the company began the period in the third quarter of 2025 with revenue of KRW 137.3 billion, operating profit of KRW 13.5 billion and controlling-shareholder net income of KRW 33.8 billion.

Fourth-quarter 2025 revenue fell to KRW 129.9 billion, with operating profit of KRW 8.37 billion and controlling-shareholder net income of KRW 26.8 billion. The first quarter of 2026 marked the peak, with operating profit of KRW 10.4 billion and controlling-shareholder net income of KRW 43.1 billion.

Operating profit and controlling-shareholder net income then declined for two consecutive quarters, reaching KRW 8.58 billion and KRW 24.4 billion in the third quarter of 2026, down 36.3% and 27.7% year on year. Third-quarter revenue was KRW 120.3 billion, down 12.4% year on year.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders totaled KRW 135.1 billion over the last four quarters. Whether the third-quarter revenue drop came from volume or price cannot be separated from the disclosed figures, so the segment explanation in the next periodic report is the point to check.

05

Industry analysis

The food and beverage sector broadly underperformed in the third-quarter 2026 earnings season. Nearly half of listed companies fell short of expectations, and large semiconductor names kept lifting aggregate operating profit.

In this setting, food companies have to explain earnings changes through sector-specific drivers such as raw materials, exchange rates and consumer demand. Dong SUH sits at two points in the cycle.

Household coffee is a mature category, so the emphasis is on managing profitability through pricing and product mix such as premium, smaller-size and ready-to-drink formats rather than large share gains. The food-service ingredient and imported beverage distribution business follows restaurant and institutional demand.

Packaging demand in manufacturing is tied to output of the company's own coffee and tea products, so lower production volume also reduces profit. The trading and export business has a high export share in creamer sales and is sensitive to overseas demand and the exchange rate.

Against peers, Dong SUH is a hybrid of a coffee-focused portfolio with packaging and trading operations. Listed companies that overlap in coffee and beverage categories include Lotte Wellfood, Maeil Dairies and Namyang Dairy Products, while broader food peers include Orion, Nongshim and Samyang Foods. Precise comparisons of scale and margins require each company's periodic reports.

Outlook

The next phase of Dong SUH's earnings depends on three variables. The first is whether third-quarter 2026 revenue recovers from its drop. Revenue was KRW 135.3 billion in the first quarter of 2026 and KRW 135.7 billion in the second, then fell to KRW 120.3 billion in the third. The second is operating margin.

It was 8.5% in 2025, below 9.1% in 2024, and third-quarter 2026 operating profit of KRW 8.58 billion was down 36.3% year on year. The third is product strategy.

The company is responding to shifting demand with smaller-size and premium products, expanded online sales and new products such as a kombucha line aimed at younger consumers. Whether this translates into a revenue rebound needs to be checked against segment figures in the periodic reports.

In the first half of 2025, revenue rose while net income fell, so the divergence between sales and profit appeared early. Because the trading and export segment is sensitive to the exchange rate, the direction of the won also matters for group profit.

The 2026 annual business report will set out full-year segment revenue and operating margin, and the first-quarter 2027 quarterly report will show whether the post-peak trend continued.

Bull factors

Cumulative improvement in operating profit

Operating profit rose from KRW 39.2 billion in 2022 to KRW 45.3 billion in 2025, and operating margin climbed from 7.0% to 8.5%. Margin reached 9.1% in 2024, a year when revenue declined. The improvement is cumulative from the 2022 base in disclosed figures.

Financial headroom and cash generation

The debt ratio at the end of 2025 was 3.5%, with total liabilities of KRW 61.1 billion. Operating cash flow stayed in the KRW 100 billion range, at KRW 118.1 billion in 2023, KRW 113.2 billion in 2024 and KRW 106.2 billion in 2025. Controlling-shareholder equity of KRW 1.72 trillion makes up most of total equity of KRW 1.74 trillion.

Product mix and channel diversification

The company is launching smaller-size and premium products as well as kombucha. It is strengthening digital marketing and broadening distribution to match online growth. Its product development strength in the food-service ingredient market is also cited as an advantage.

Bear factors

Quarterly earnings have turned down

After peaking in the first quarter of 2026 at KRW 10.4 billion in operating profit and KRW 43.1 billion in controlling-shareholder net income, both declined in the second and third quarters.

Third-quarter operating profit of KRW 8.58 billion was down 36.3% year on year, and controlling-shareholder net income of KRW 24.4 billion was down 27.7%.

Operating profit and net income diverge

In 2025 operating profit rose 1.9% while controlling-shareholder net income fell 7.9%, and in 2023 operating profit rose 11.7% while the same measure fell 13.7%. Items below operating profit appear to have weighed repeatedly, which makes earnings quality harder to read. The gap between total net income and controlling-shareholder net income also needs attention.

Concentrated business and cost and FX exposure

A high share of coffee-related products in food sales makes the business sensitive to raw material prices and consumer demand. Creamer exports in the trading segment were 83.76% export-oriented as of December 2025, leaving it exposed to exchange rates and overseas demand. If revenue recovery stalls, fixed cost burden could carry into lower profit.

Risk factors

FX and raw material costs

Creamer exports in the trading segment carry a high overseas share, so currency moves pass directly into results. Imported raw materials in manufacturing and food expose cost to both exchange rates and commodity prices moving together.

In the third quarter of 2026 revenue and profit fell together, and if cost pass-through lags, margins could narrow further.

Demand and pricing pass-through

Coffee-related products account for a large share of sales, so softer demand or heavier competition quickly hits volumes. Price increases protect revenue but can reduce volume. Third-quarter 2026 revenue down 12.4% year on year is a sign this risk is already visible in the figures.

Volatility in earnings structure

Operating profit and controlling-shareholder net income often move in opposite directions, complicating interpretation. Items outside operating profit can swing net income materially. How affiliate-related gains and losses flow into consolidated results needs to be checked in the detailed items of the periodic reports.

Overall view

Dong SUH's revenue stayed in a range of roughly KRW 490 billion to KRW 560 billion from 2022 to 2025, while operating profit rose from KRW 39.2 billion to KRW 45.3 billion and operating margin held at 8.5% in 2025, above 7.0% in 2022.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders rebounded in 2024 and then fell in 2025, moving opposite to operating profit. The balance sheet looks light, with total liabilities of KRW 61.1 billion and a 3.5% debt ratio at the end of 2025, and operating cash flow stayed above KRW 100 billion.

On the other hand, quarterly results in 2026 peaked in the first quarter and then declined for two consecutive quarters, and third-quarter revenue of KRW 120.3 billion was down 12.4% year on year.

Strengths are financial stability and product mix efforts, while weaknesses are the quarterly revenue drop, net income volatility and exposure to costs and exchange rates from a coffee-centered structure.

With both sets of factors present, the next checkpoints are the fourth-quarter and full-year figures in the 2026 annual report and whether revenue recovers in the first-quarter 2027 report. Market capitalization sits at a premium to net asset value, so the pace of earnings recovery is central to reading valuation.
