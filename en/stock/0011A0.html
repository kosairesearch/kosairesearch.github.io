Axbis: Laser Equipment at a Profitability Turning Point

Axbis sharply expanded both operating profit and net income attributable to owners in 2025, but swung to an operating loss in the second quarter of 2026, making utilization recovery the key variable to watch next.

  3. 3

02

Business structure

Founded in 2009, Axbis has supplied industrial laser processing solutions and listed on KOSDAQ on March 9, 2026.

Built around its proprietary intelligent high-power laser platform VisionSCAN, the company offers four product lines: laser welding (AXWELD), cutting/notching (AXCUT), surface treatment (AXSURF), and process monitoring (AXVIEW).

According to materials disclosed during the listing process, the revenue mix as of the third quarter of 2025 was AXWELD 54.7%, AXCUT 20.8%, AXSURF 16.9%, and AXVIEW 7.5%.

The same materials showed customer concentration skewed toward a 'Group H' (six Hyundai Motor/Hyundai Mobis-affiliated companies) at 57.0% of revenue and a 'Group L' (three LG-affiliated companies) at 29.4%, with the remainder at 13.6%.

Its core applications are electric-vehicle drive-motor and battery cell/module welding, and it has recently expanded into camera-module and robot-actuator precision processing.

Notably, Axbis has been the exclusive supplier of high-power laser systems for Hyundai Mobis's hybrid and electric-vehicle electrification component manufacturing process since 2022, under a long-term supply agreement running through 2028.

CEO Kim Myung-jin is the largest shareholder, with a combined stake of 54.4% including related parties, as disclosed. The company is expanding into semiconductors, robotics, defense, and aerospace while pursuing customer diversification.

In terms of competitive positioning, Axbis is benchmarked against other listed domestic laser and precision-processing equipment makers, and the average price-to-earnings ratio of comparable companies used in its IPO pricing was presented as 27.6 times on an annualized third-quarter-2025 basis.

04

Earnings analysis

Axbis's consolidated 2025 revenue was KRW 56.6 billion, up 1.6% from KRW 55.7 billion in 2024. Over the same period, operating profit rose 78.3% from KRW 2.42 billion to KRW 4.31 billion, and the operating margin improved from 4.3% to 7.6%.

Net income attributable to owners grew 57.3% from KRW 3.19 billion to KRW 5.01 billion, with total net income (including non-controlling interests) also at KRW 5.01 billion. Operating cash flow turned positive, moving from -KRW 4.61 billion in 2024 to KRW 2.61 billion in 2025.

On a quarterly basis, Q1 2026 revenue was KRW 11.1 billion with operating profit of KRW 787 million and net income attributable to owners of KRW 1.12 billion, sustaining profitability, but Q2 2026 revenue rose 8.9% quarter over quarter to KRW 12 billion while the company swung to an operating loss of KRW 331 million and a net loss attributable to owners of KRW 268 million.

According to a Bloter report published in September 2026, Q2 2026 revenue was down from KRW 15.6 billion a year earlier.

The same report noted that consolidated first-half 2026 revenue fell 1.7% year over year to KRW 23.1 billion and operating profit dropped 66.7%, with the company attributing the decline to an equipment-industry pattern where revenue recognition is concentrated in the second half.

The sum of net income attributable to owners over the last four quarters (Q1 2025 through Q4 2025) was KRW 5.01 billion, matching the full-year 2025 figure.

05

Industry analysis

The industrial laser processing market is growing alongside rising demand for fast, high-precision processing in electric-vehicle, secondary battery, camera-module, and semiconductor manufacturing.

More recently, the spread of humanoid robots has emerged as a new demand source, with each robot containing an average of 60 to 80 actuators and laser equipment potentially being used in the stripping process that removes wire insulation on each actuator.

In line with this trend, Axbis is expanding from its existing electric-vehicle and battery-focused business into semiconductors, robotics, defense, and aerospace. However, as is typical in the equipment industry, results are heavily influenced by the capital-expenditure plans and order timing of large customers.

The company's revenue remains concentrated among a few large corporate groups—57.0% from Group H and 29.4% from Group L according to IPO disclosure materials—making it sensitive to the moves of a small number of downstream customers.

In the first half of 2026, revenue from three customers each accounting for more than 10% of sales exceeded 70% of total revenue, continuing a pattern where delivery timing on large projects drives quarterly results.

Competitively, there are other listed domestic suppliers of similar laser and precision-processing equipment, and the average price-to-earnings ratio of these peers was used as a reference metric in setting Axbis's IPO price.

06

Outlook

Axbis disclosed on May 26, 2026 a decision to invest KRW 43.3 billion in new facilities, equal to 79.83% of its 2025 year-end equity. The investment period runs from July 1, 2026 to June 30, 2028, covering a new two-basement, eight-story headquarters and plant to be built in Daehwa-dong, Daejeon.

According to a Bloter report published in September 2026, the cumulative scale of the new headquarters and manufacturing facility investment was estimated at KRW 69.7 billion, of which KRW 13.3 billion had been executed by the first half of 2026.

The same report said first-half utilization was only 25.1% but that the company expects it to rise to 70-80% once revenue recognition accelerates in the second half.

On the funding side, the report noted that a first-half capital increase raised KRW 26.1 billion, lifting consolidated equity from KRW 28.1 billion at the end of 2025 to KRW 55.1 billion and reducing the debt-to-equity ratio from 157.6% to 80.1%.

On the order front, Axbis signed a KRW 7.646 billion supply contract with J's Robotics in July 2026 and a separate KRW 8.0 billion contract in May 2026, both for laser systems used in prismatic secondary-battery manufacturing.

The company said it is proactively expanding production capacity to meet anticipated order growth in semiconductors and robotics. It was also reported to be discussing additional equipment supply contracts that could each represent more than 10% of revenue.

08

Bull factors

Profitability turnaround trajectory

From 2024 to 2025, operating profit rose from KRW 2.42 billion to KRW 4.31 billion and net income attributable to owners rose from KRW 3.19 billion to KRW 5.01 billion, while operating cash flow turned from -KRW 4.61 billion to KRW 2.61 billion.

The operating margin also improved from 4.3% to 7.6%, reflecting a strengthening profit structure. This trend continued into Q1 2026 with operating profit of KRW 787 million.

Expansion into robotics and humanoid value chains

Axbis has secured orders for laser equipment used in the robot-actuator wire-stripping process, extending its existing EV and battery-focused business into the robotics value chain.

Since humanoid robots use an average of 60 to 80 actuators each, demand for related laser processes has been cited as a potential growth area. The company is also pursuing customer diversification by expanding into semiconductors, defense, and aerospace.

IPO-funded balance sheet improvement and capacity expansion

Reports indicate that proceeds from the listing and a capital increase have boosted total equity and lowered the debt ratio. Building on this, the company is proceeding with a multi-billion-won investment in a new Daejeon headquarters and manufacturing facility to expand production capacity. Once the new facility reaches full operation, capacity to absorb additional orders could increase.

09

Bear factors

Q2 2026 swing to loss amid low utilization

Although revenue rose quarter over quarter in Q2 2026, the company swung to an operating loss of KRW 331 million and a net loss attributable to owners of KRW 268 million. Reports indicate first-half utilization was only 25.1%, reflecting low production-line usage relative to the new facility investment. Until second-half recovery is confirmed, quarter-to-quarter volatility bears watching.

Customer concentration and order-to-revenue timing lag

According to IPO-era disclosures, revenue is concentrated among top customer groups (Group H 57.0%, Group L 29.4%), making results sensitive to shifts in major customers' capital-spending plans. In the first half of 2026, three customers each exceeding 10% of revenue together accounted for over 70% of total sales.

The timing gap between order intake and revenue recognition continues to concentrate results in specific quarters.

Burden of large-scale facility investment and rising costs

The company decided on a new facility investment of KRW 43.3 billion, equal to 79.83% of its equity, with cumulative investment reportedly reaching KRW 69.7 billion. Depreciation and fixed-cost burdens may precede actual output once the new headquarters and plant become operational. R&D expenses also rose from KRW 1.2 billion to KRW 1.5 billion in the first half, adding further cost pressure.

10

Risk factors

Customer concentration risk

High revenue dependence on a specific corporate group means production plan changes or capex cuts at that customer could directly affect results. The long-term contract with Hyundai Mobis provides a stable revenue source but also deepens customer dependence. The company has stated it is pursuing customer diversification.

Utilization and demand mismatch risk

With large-scale expansion underway for the new headquarters and plant, failure to raise utilization as planned could increase fixed-cost burden. First-half utilization of only 25.1% suggests the pace of demand recovery needs monitoring. Whether second-half revenue recognition proceeds as planned remains the key factor.

Equity dilution and share overhang risk

KB Securities cited "equity dilution concerns" as a risk factor in a May 2026 report. Shares held by the largest shareholder and others at listing are subject to a lock-up period, after which the tradable float could increase.

The possibility of share count growth through additional capital increases or convertible bond issuance should also be kept in mind.

12

Overall view

Axbis showed clear profitability improvement through 2025 but faced its first major test since listing when it swung to an operating loss in Q2 2026. Utilization recovery, the payoff from the new headquarters and plant investment, and customer diversification stand out as variables that will shape future results.

Expansion into robot-actuator and humanoid applications, together with the long-term Hyundai Mobis contract, support the medium-term growth narrative.

On the other hand, revenue concentration in specific large corporate groups and quarter-to-quarter volatility from the order-to-revenue timing gap remain points to monitor. With large-scale facility investment underway, the effect of depreciation and fixed-cost burden on profitability also warrants attention.

The next periodic report will be the key point to confirm whether second-half revenue recognition and utilization improve as expected.
