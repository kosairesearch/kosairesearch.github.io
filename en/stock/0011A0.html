Axbis: Order Growth Versus Utilization Recovery

Axbis' third-quarter 2026 revenue rose 85.6% from the prior quarter to KRW 22.4 billion and returned to operating profit, but the company posted an operating loss in the second quarter, so whether the recovery holds is the key item to verify in the next periodic filings.

02

Business structure

Axbis was founded in Daejeon in 2009 as a small and medium-sized technology company focused on intelligent high-power laser solutions.

Its application equipment and process-control devices, which combine laser processing, robotics and AI-based inspection, serve manufacturing lines for advanced mobility core parts, secondary batteries and camera modules.

Its product lines are laser welding (AXWELD), cutting (AXCUT), surface treatment (AXSURF) and process monitoring (AXVIEW), with AXWELD at 54.7%, AXCUT at 20.8%, AXSURF at 16.9% and AXVIEW at 7.5% of revenue as of the third quarter of 2025.

A different securities report classified 2025 full-year revenue as 62.5% laser welding, 16.0% cutting and notching, 14.2% surface treatment and 7.3% process monitoring, so the mix differs by base period and classification.

Axbis has been the exclusive supplier of high-power laser systems for Hyundai Mobis' HEV and EV electrification parts since 2022, under a long-term supply contract that runs through 2028. The number of customers rose from 61 in 2023 to 94 in 2025, according to February 2026 IPO materials.

The company works on a project basis, moving from customer technical review and pilot development to volume equipment delivery, which creates a timing gap between order receipt and revenue recognition.

Concentration is high: in the first half of 2026, three customers accounted for KRW 17.0 billion, more than 70% of total revenue. The company is widening its scope into battery electrode drying and AI-based quality inspection, and into semiconductors, robotics, defense and aerospace.

It also supplies actuator-stripping equipment to a domestic humanoid robot maker. Axbis listed on KOSDAQ on March 9, 2026.

04

Earnings analysis

On an annual basis, revenue rose 1.6%, from KRW 55.7 billion in 2024 to KRW 56.6 billion in 2025. Operating profit grew 78.3%, from KRW 2.42 billion to KRW 4.31 billion, and the operating margin widened from 4.3% to 7.6%.

Because profit grew far faster than sales, the improvement looks driven by margin gains rather than volume expansion. Profit attributable to controlling shareholders rose 57.3%, from KRW 3.19 billion to KRW 5.01 billion.

Net profit including non-controlling interests moved over the same period from KRW 3.19 billion to KRW 5.01 billion. The debt-to-equity ratio fell from 242.3% to 157.6%. At end-2025, total equity was KRW 28.1 billion and total liabilities were KRW 44.3 billion.

Operating cash flow turned from -KRW 4.61 billion in 2024 to KRW 2.61 billion in 2025. On a quarterly basis, the first quarter of 2026 produced revenue of KRW 11.1 billion, operating profit of KRW 787 million and profit attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 1.12 billion.

In the second quarter, revenue was KRW 12 billion, up 8.9% from the prior quarter, but the company posted an operating loss of KRW 331 million and a net loss attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 268 million.

Press reports said gross profit fell and selling and administrative expenses rose, weighing on profitability, and first-half R&D spending increased from KRW 1.2 billion to KRW 1.5 billion.

In the third quarter, revenue reached KRW 22.4 billion, up 85.6% from the prior quarter, with operating profit of KRW 797 million and profit attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 146 million, returning to profit.

The company says equipment businesses typically book lower revenue in the first and second quarters and concentrate recognition in the third and fourth. Profit attributable to controlling shareholders for the four quarters of 2025 totaled KRW 5.01 billion.

05

Industry analysis

Equipment makers' quarterly results tend to cluster according to customers' capital spending and delivery and acceptance schedules. The company itself says its results improve in the second half as deliveries and acceptance inspections proceed. Demand from end markets comes from three directions.

The first is secondary batteries, where battery electrode drying and AI-based quality inspection equipment are under discussion for additional supply contracts, and press reports say large battery-equipment orders continued after the listing.

The second is automotive electrification parts, anchored by the long-term supply of laser systems for Hyundai Mobis' HEV and EV programs.

The third is new areas such as robotics, humanoids and semiconductors, where actuator-stripping equipment is already being delivered to a domestic humanoid maker and production capacity is being expanded ahead of anticipated orders.

The cycle position can only be read from the company's own quarterly trajectory, not from industry-wide indicators. Revenue rose in the second and third quarters after the first-quarter 2026 low, but first-half utilization of 25.1% suggests revenue recognition still lags installed capacity.

Axbis' differentiation lies in its project-based model, in which equipment moves from customer technical review and pilot development to volume supply, and that structure means technical qualification acts as a barrier to entry.

Within the same sector, Rainbow Robotics and Robotis overlap in robotics, while Philoptics overlaps in equipment. If customers slow their investment, deliveries can slip and quarterly results can swing, so the company is exposed to its customers' investment cycles.

06

Outlook

The company's stated task is converting its larger order book into improvements in revenue, profit and cash flow. Order backlog rose 29.2%, from KRW 21.6 billion at end-2025 to KRW 27.9 billion at the end of the first half of 2026, and two contracts combined equal about 27.7% of 2025 consolidated revenue.

Press reports say the company expects utilization to climb to 70% to 80% once second-half revenue recognition picks up, from 25.1% in the first half.

Capacity is being added through a new headquarters and manufacturing facility in Daejeon's Daewha district, a KRW 69.7 billion project, of which KRW 13.3 billion had been spent by the first half, with investment planned through 2028 funded by IPO proceeds, internal cash, asset sale proceeds and borrowings.

The company also plans to consolidate its separated headquarters and factory into one site to integrate research and manufacturing. Additional equipment supply contracts being discussed could each exceed 10% of recent revenue.

On the balance sheet, a first-half rights offering raised KRW 26.1 billion, lifting total equity to KRW 55.1 billion, and the debt-to-equity ratio fell to 80.1%. Cash generation from operations is still not stable, however, as inventory build-up weighed on cash flow. The next checkpoints are the annual utilization rate and operating cash flow in the 2026 annual report.

Bull factors

Profit recovery trend

Expanding order base

Order backlog rose 29.2%, from KRW 21.6 billion to KRW 27.9 billion over the first half of 2026. Two contracts together equal about 27.7% of 2025 consolidated revenue. Additional equipment supply contracts are also under discussion.

Balance sheet and investment capacity

A KRW 26.1 billion rights offering in the first half lifted total equity to KRW 55.1 billion, and the debt-to-equity ratio fell to 80.1%. Compared with 157.6% at end-2025, borrowing pressure has eased. The equity base now supports continued investment in the new headquarters and plant.

Bear factors

Quarterly earnings volatility

In the second quarter of 2026, revenue of KRW 12 billion came with an operating loss of KRW 331 million, a swing from the first-quarter operating profit of KRW 787 million within one quarter.

The company says revenue recognition concentrates in the third and fourth quarters, so the full-year picture depends heavily on second-half results.

Utilization and cost pressure

First-half utilization reached only 25.1%. In the second quarter, gross profit declined while selling and administrative expenses rose, lowering profitability. First-half R&D spending also rose from KRW 1.2 billion to KRW 1.5 billion.

Cash flow and customer concentration

Cash flow weakened in the first half of 2026 alongside inventory build-up. Three customers accounted for more than 70% of first-half revenue, so the timing of deliveries to any one of them can swing quarterly results sharply.

Risk factors

Customer concentration and delivery timing

Three customers account for more than 70% of first-half revenue, so the timing of large project deliveries drives quarterly results. Because orders and revenue recognition are separated in time, quarterly swings can widen. A growing backlog does not translate directly into revenue if acceptance and recognition are delayed.

Investment burden and funding

A KRW 69.7 billion headquarters and plant project runs through 2028, with KRW 13.3 billion spent by the first half. Funding is planned from IPO proceeds, internal cash, asset sales and borrowings, so unstable operating cash flow would put pressure on that funding mix.

Share supply and dilution

Pre-listing materials showed the tradable share ratio at about 33% right after listing, rising to about 46% after six months. A securities research note from May 2026 also listed dilution concerns as a risk factor. Further fundraising after listing could increase dilution pressure.

Overall view

Axbis has restored profitability, with operating profit up 78.3% in 2025 and the operating margin widening to 7.6%. In 2026, quarterly profit alternated between profit in the first quarter, a loss in the second and profit again in the third.

Third-quarter revenue of KRW 22.4 billion was up 85.6% from the second quarter, but first-half utilization of 25.1% shows that revenue recognition still lags capacity. Order backlog rose to KRW 27.9 billion by the end of the first half of 2026, and additional supply contracts are under discussion.

On the balance sheet, a KRW 26.1 billion rights offering strengthened equity and lowered the debt-to-equity ratio, but operating cash flow is not yet stable because of inventory build-up.

Of the KRW 69.7 billion headquarters project, KRW 13.3 billion had been spent by the first half, so investment burden and earnings recovery are advancing together.

The bullish factors are order growth, balance-sheet improvement and profit recovery, while the bearish factors are quarterly volatility, customer concentration and cash flow. The next checkpoints are the utilization rate and cash flow in the 2026 annual report and the fourth-quarter 2026 results.
