Report overview

Gabia Shifts Weight From Domains to AI Infrastructure

Gabia's revenue rose for three straight years through 2025 to KRW 335.7 billion, but its operating margin fell from 17.2% in 2022 to 12.1% in 2025, and quarterly operating profit has increased for two consecutive quarters since the first quarter of 2026.

  1. 1

    Revenue reached KRW 335.7 billion in 2025, up 18.9% from the prior year, and has grown for three consecutive years from 2023 to 2025.

  2. 2

    Operating profit fell to KRW 35.1 billion in 2024 before rebounding to KRW 40.5 billion in 2025, yet the 2025 operating margin of 12.1% remains below the 17.2% recorded in 2022.

  3. 3

    Quarterly operating profit has risen for two consecutive quarters from the second to the third quarter of 2026, and the third-quarter figure of KRW 12.5 billion was up 66.3% year on year.

  4. 4

  5. 5

02

Business structure

Gabia started with domain registration and web hosting and has expanded into cloud, groupware, security, desktop-as-a-service, data center (IDC) and AI infrastructure.

In a segment revenue table compiled by BOSOOP from regulatory filings, cloud and IT services account for 54.0%, IX and IDC for 29.1%, security for 14.1%, and other revenue for 2.8%.

Its groupware product Hiworks is presented as the leading domestic market-share product, alongside its in-house cloud service and DaaS offering. Major cloud services hold the Korean cloud security certification (CSAP), which supports its position in public-sector procurement.

Customers are corporate (B2B) clients and public institutions, and domain customers are cross-sold cloud and groupware services. Subsidiaries including KINX and Espsoft share the Gwacheon headquarters, letting the group bundle infrastructure, security and software.

A 2024 press report put the Gwacheon data center's contracted power capacity at 20 MW. The same coverage cited a Ministry of the Interior and Safety reference ranking Gabia fourth in public cloud share, behind KT Cloud, NHN Cloud and NAVER Cloud.

The company competes with large telecom and portal-linked cloud providers by emphasizing agility and price competitiveness for small and mid-sized clients.

04

Earnings analysis

On the disclosed annual figures, revenue rose from KRW 239.2 billion in 2022 to KRW 261.6 billion in 2023 (up 9.4%), KRW 282.4 billion in 2024 (up 8.0%) and KRW 335.7 billion in 2025 (up 18.9%), growing for three consecutive years.

Operating profit moved from KRW 41.2 billion in 2022 to KRW 42.6 billion in 2023 (up 3.4%), the largest year in the 2022–2025 period, then fell to KRW 35.1 billion in 2024 (down 17.6%), the smallest year, before recovering to KRW 40.5 billion in 2025 (up 15.3%).

Revenue grew while the operating margin declined each year, from 17.2% in 2022 to 16.3%, 12.4% and 12.1% in 2025. Press coverage credits a higher mix of cloud and security, which carry higher margins, with improving overall profitability, but the disclosed operating margin fell over the same period.

Owners' net income went from KRW 21.6 billion in 2022 to KRW 16 billion in 2023 (down 25.9%), KRW 14.6 billion in 2024 (down 8.7%) and KRW 15.8 billion in 2025 (up 8.0%).

Net income including non-controlling interests was KRW 41.6 billion in 2022 and KRW 31.1 billion in 2025, and the gap between that figure and owners' net income in 2025 reflects the non-controlling share.

The debt ratio rose from 51.8% in 2022 to 58.6% in 2023 and 83.0% in 2024 before falling to 68.0% in 2025, and the 2024 peak coincides with the completion of the Gwacheon data center at the end of 2024.

Operating cash flow was KRW 70.2 billion in 2022, KRW 58.2 billion in 2023, KRW 68.2 billion in 2024 and KRW 71.8 billion in 2025, so the 2025 figure is above 2022.

Quarterly revenue went from KRW 80.6 billion in the third quarter of 2025 to KRW 97.5 billion in the fourth quarter (up 21.0% quarter on quarter), and operating profit rose from KRW 7.54 billion to KRW 19.1 billion (up 153.6%), which press coverage attributed to cost cuts and higher utilization.

Revenue then fell to KRW 85 billion in the first quarter of 2026 (down 12.8%) with operating profit of KRW 10.7 billion (down 43.9%), before reaching KRW 88.8 billion and KRW 12.5 billion in the second quarter and KRW 91.1 billion and KRW 12.5 billion in the third quarter.

Third-quarter revenue was up 13.0% year on year, and owners' net income of KRW 3.44 billion was down 13.8% quarter on quarter. Owners' net income for the last four quarters, from the fourth quarter of 2025 to the third quarter of 2026, totals KRW 19.3 billion.

05

Industry analysis

As Korean companies expand AI adoption, demand for data centers that can host GPUs and high-power servers is growing. Gabia said it built its Gwacheon data center to accommodate high-power racks for AI servers.

A 2024 report described the planned average power density per rack at 20kW, which coverage presented as sufficient for high-power GPU operation. At the same time, large cloud and telecom operators announced AIDC conversions and new builds, and coverage expected customer competition to intensify.

KT Cloud said it would build new GPU-capable AIDC sites in Seoul's Gasan area and in Gyeongbuk, and SK Telecom was reported to be converting its Gasan data center into an AIDC. Under these conditions, design specifications alone may not be enough to differentiate Gabia.

Gabia says it is targeting the regulatory, operational and cost-management needs of domestic companies that use global CSP services, rather than competing head-on with those providers.

The approach combines its existing domain, hosting and groupware customer base with in-house data center and cloud operations capability. Because AI infrastructure takes time to monetize after initial investment, the pace of real AI demand is seen as a key variable for mid- to long-term earnings.

Coverage suggests that in public-sector AI demand, operators with security certification have an advantage. An academic has also questioned how large domestic AIDC demand will be. The public cloud ranking cited here dates from 2024 coverage and may not reflect current share.

06

Outlook

Gabia entered a joint venture agreement with Macquarie Asset Management on February 25, 2026, under which the two plan to invest KRW 600 billion jointly over the next four to six years.

The goal is a hyperscale data center platform with cumulative power capacity above 100MW at major domestic sites, starting with a 40MW Ansan data center.

Macquarie is responsible for site acquisition, permits and project financing, while Gabia and KINX handle data center design, network build-out, and operations and maintenance.

Gabia group's investment into the joint venture companies totaled KRW 10.4 billion in the first half of 2026, of which KRW 8.2 billion was executed in the second quarter. The Ansan site is planned to connect with the existing Gwacheon center to form a metropolitan-area AI infrastructure hub.

The contract value for the Gwacheon headquarters construction is reported at about KRW 56.9 billion. KINX's data center revenue in the first half of 2026 was KRW 49.7 billion, up 16.5% from a year earlier.

On the software side, Gabia launched Groupware Premium, which combines generative AI functions with its Hiworks groupware. A company official said the earnings contribution of new products would build up as new customers join and existing customers convert, rather than appearing as a one-time effect at launch.

On governance, Macquarie's side launched a tender offer for Gabia in July 2026, and coverage reported that a special committee was set up in connection with the tender offer and that a reply deadline for an activist fund was postponed.

The next checkpoints are the construction and operating schedule of the 40MW Ansan project and the pace at which joint venture investment is executed.

08

Bull factors

Three Years of Revenue Growth and Earnings Recovery

Revenue has grown for three consecutive years from 2023 to 2025, reaching KRW 335.7 billion in 2025, up 18.9%. Operating profit recovered to KRW 40.5 billion in 2025 from KRW 35.1 billion in 2024, a 15.3% increase. Owners' net income rose 8.0% to KRW 15.8 billion in 2025, reversing the decline of 2024. Supporters cite the renewed profit growth alongside a wider revenue base as the main bullish evidence.

AI Infrastructure Expansion and Joint Venture Structure

The joint venture with Macquarie Asset Management plans joint investment of KRW 600 billion over four to six years. The first project, a 40MW Ansan data center, is planned to connect with the Gwacheon center as a metropolitan AI infrastructure hub.

Under the split of roles, Macquarie handles site acquisition and project financing while Gabia and KINX run operations, which spreads capital burden. KINX's first-half 2026 data center revenue of KRW 49.7 billion was up 16.5% from a year earlier.

Operating Profit Up for Two Consecutive Quarters in 2026

Operating profit, which fell to KRW 10.7 billion in the first quarter of 2026, rose to KRW 12.5 billion in the second quarter and held at KRW 12.5 billion in the third, marking two consecutive quarterly increases. Third-quarter operating profit was up 66.3% year on year, and revenue of KRW 91.1 billion was up 13.0%. The recovery follows the fourth-quarter 2025 operating profit of KRW 19.1 billion.

09

Bear factors

Continued Decline in Operating Margin

The operating margin declined each year, from 17.2% in 2022 to 16.3% in 2023, 12.4% in 2024 and 12.1% in 2025. Operating profit of KRW 40.5 billion in 2025 was below the KRW 42.6 billion of 2023, so the larger revenue base has not translated into higher profit.

Revenue reached KRW 335.7 billion, but the lack of margin recovery in the disclosed figures is cited as a bearish factor.

Investment Burden and Capital Structure

The debt ratio rose from 51.8% in 2022 to 83.0% in 2024 before easing to 68.0% in 2025. The contract value for the Gwacheon headquarters construction is about KRW 56.9 billion, and GPU servers and high-power data centers carry heavy upfront investment and depreciation. The planned KRW 600 billion joint investment with Macquarie could add to capital requirements going forward.

Intensifying Competition and Governance Variables

Large telecom and cloud operators are converting to or building AIDC capacity, which is expected to intensify competition for customers. Competition with global CSPs is cited as a variable for profitability.

Coverage also reports that friction with an activist fund emerged as a short-term variable during Macquarie's tender offer process.

10

Risk factors

Investment and Depreciation

Data center and headquarters investment can raise depreciation and pressure the operating margin. The 2025 operating margin of 12.1% remains below the 17.2% of 2022. If utilization falls short of targets, fixed-cost pressure could persist.

Demand and Competition

If AI server demand grows more slowly than expected or GPU utilization is delayed, profitability could come under pressure. An academic has questioned how large domestic AIDC demand will be. Share competition with large cloud operators is an ongoing variable.

Governance and Capital Structure

Coverage has reported on Macquarie's tender offer and friction with an activist fund. These developments could affect the continuity of management direction and investment plans.

The large non-controlling interest means owners' share of consolidated net income appears smaller, which is another structural point to consider.

12

Overall view

Gabia's revenue grew from KRW 239.2 billion in 2022 to KRW 335.7 billion in 2025, but over the same period the operating margin fell from 17.2% to 12.1%. Owners' net income recovered from KRW 14.6 billion in 2024 to KRW 15.8 billion in 2025, yet remains below the KRW 21.6 billion of 2022.

In 2026, operating profit rose for two consecutive quarters after the first-quarter low, reaching KRW 12.5 billion in both the second and third quarters, and third-quarter operating profit was up 66.3% year on year.

The Gwacheon data center and the Macquarie joint venture are becoming the base for AI infrastructure expansion, and the planned KRW 600 billion joint investment over four to six years raises both growth potential and financial commitments.

The bullish side cites revenue growth, earnings recovery and capacity expansion through the joint venture, while the bearish side cites the falling operating margin, debt burden and competition with larger operators.

The next checkpoints are the operating margin and debt ratio in the 2026 preliminary fourth-quarter results and the 2026 annual report, and governance changes linked to the tender offer and activist dispute also merit attention.
