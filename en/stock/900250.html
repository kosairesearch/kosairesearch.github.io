02

Business structure

China Crystal New Material Holdings is a holding company incorporated in the Cayman Islands in 2012 for overseas listing purposes, controlling its Chinese operating subsidiaries through a Hong Kong intermediate holding entity.

Its core business consists of synthetic mica flake, synthetic mica powder, and fire-resistant mica tape, complemented by graphene and pearlescent pigment production and sales.

According to its fiscal-year 2022 annual report, the product mix was relatively balanced, with mica tape at 24.94%, mica flake at 19.56%, graphene powder at 19.00%, mica powder at 18.30%, and pearlescent pigments and others at 18.20%.

Synthetic mica offers superior insulation, heat resistance, and corrosion resistance, allowing it to replace natural mica in automotive coatings, cosmetics, and power equipment.

A Hunan-based mica subsidiary acquired in 2024 derives close to half of its revenue from electric vehicle makers, with CATL and BYD among the leading Chinese EV companies using its products.

In 2025, the company entered the AI industry by acquiring the entire equity of GeneVision AI Technology Holdings Ltd. through a share purchase agreement.

It now offers data-center thermal management and integrated computing/power resource management solutions by combining mica-based heat-dissipation materials with GeneVision's AI algorithms and software, with products such as the 'Lingmou' smart thermal management system and a computing power scheduling platform.

More recently, it has been expanding its product lineup through new items such as high-quality synthetic mica materials and fluorescent gold composite titanium pigments.

While the company is understood to hold a leading position within China's synthetic mica segment, the revenue contribution of its graphene and AI businesses has not yet been disclosed separately.

04

Earnings analysis

All figures below are KRW-converted. Annual revenue rose from KRW 81.5 billion in 2022 to KRW 89.5 billion in 2023 (up 9.8% year over year) and KRW 103.9 billion in 2024 (up 16.1%), before falling to KRW 70.7 billion in 2025 (down 31.9%), the smallest figure of the 2022-2025 period.

Operating profit peaked at KRW 25.3 billion in 2023 (up 25.4% year over year) after rising from KRW 20.2 billion in 2022, then declined to KRW 19.9 billion in 2024 (down 21.1%) and KRW 10.9 billion in 2025 (down 45.6%), falling for a second straight year since 2024 and marking the smallest figure of the period.

The operating margin also slid from 28.2% in 2023 to 19.2% in 2024 and 15.3% in 2025.

Controlling-interest net income fluctuated from KRW 15.0 billion in 2022 to KRW 8.2 billion in 2023 (down 45.4%) and KRW 8.98 billion in 2024 (up 9.6%), before turning into a controlling-interest net loss of KRW 1.82 billion in 2025, swinging from profit the prior year.

On a quarterly basis, operating profit fell from KRW 5.96 billion in Q2 2025 to KRW 4.51 billion in Q3 (down 24.2% quarter over quarter), then to an operating loss of KRW 2.25 billion in Q4 (turning negative), before recovering to a profit of KRW 520 million in Q1 2026 (turning positive) and KRW 2.39 billion in Q2 2026 (up 358.9% quarter over quarter but down 59.9% year over year).

Revenue likewise rose from KRW 11.0 billion in Q1 2026 to KRW 18.1 billion in Q2, up 65.0% quarter over quarter but down 38.8% year over year.

As a result, cumulative controlling-interest net losses over the last four quarters (Q3 2025 through Q2 2026) reached KRW 6.41 billion, reflecting heightened quarter-to-quarter volatility.

Operating cash flow steadily declined from KRW 73.2 billion in 2022 to KRW 21.6 billion in 2025, indicating weaker cash generation alongside the profit decline.

05

Industry analysis

The synthetic mica industry is on a trajectory of replacing natural mica, driven by natural mica resource depletion and various mining-related issues.

Demand for synthetic mica in the cosmetics industry is rising due to its low heavy-metal content, and the global synthetic mica market is reportedly growing at an annual rate of more than 20%.

In the EV battery segment, mica panels are used to enhance battery safety through insulation and fire resistance, meaning demand from Chinese EV makers directly affects the revenue of related subsidiaries.

According to reports published ahead of its 2015 KOSDAQ listing, China accounted for 95% of global synthetic mica production at the time, with China Crystal New Material Holdings commanding a 77% share of the Chinese market.

Since then, capacity additions and price competition among rivals appear to have increased volatility in the industry. The graphene business remains at an early stage of end-market penetration, with EV and secondary-battery applications most frequently cited.

'Computing-power integration' was named for the first time in China's 2026 government work report and was also included in the outline of the 15th Five-Year Plan, emerging as a key new infrastructure priority at the national level.

This policy backdrop appears to coincide with growing infrastructure demand for AI thermal management and computing-power integration businesses.

Outlook

The company decided to carry out a 4-to-1 consolidation of its common shares, stating the purpose was to maintain an appropriate float and enhance corporate value through share price stabilization.

This reverse split took effect on June 2, 2026, adjusting the total number of shares outstanding and the par value per share.

In its AI business, subsidiary GeneVision AI Technology Holdings expanded its customer base in the computing-power integration business, entering State Grid's supply chain in February, winning an order from Huadian Group in June, and securing China Mobile, a leading Chinese telecom company, as a customer in September.

However, the contract size between GeneVision and China Mobile was not disclosed, by mutual agreement between the two parties.

On the graphene front, the company has said it was reviewing a graphite mine acquisition to secure raw materials, and was also considering additional acquisitions of mica panel manufacturers in response to growth in the EV market.

That said, the specific timing and scale of any further M&A remain to be confirmed through future disclosures.

Given that revenue in both the first and second quarters of 2026 continued to decline year over year, whether revenue and profitability in both the core and new businesses recover will likely be gauged through upcoming quarterly filings.

08

Bull factors

EV Battery Mica Demand

Through its subsidiary, the company supplies mica panels to major Chinese EV makers such as CATL and BYD, positioning it to benefit from growing demand for battery safety materials. EV makers reportedly account for close to half of that subsidiary's revenue. A trend toward tighter EV battery safety regulation could provide a structural demand base for mica panels.

AI Business Customer Expansion

Through GeneVision, acquired in 2025, the company expanded its customer base from State Grid and Huadian Group to China Mobile in September 2026 under computing-power integration agreements.

There is discussion that AI thermal management and power solutions, combined with its core mica materials business, could become a new growth pillar. This also aligns with China's policy of designating computing-power integration as a key national new infrastructure priority.

Low Leverage, Solid Capital Base

At the end of 2025, the debt-to-equity ratio stood at a very low 7.1%, while total equity reached KRW 490.9 billion (converted), giving the company considerable balance-sheet capacity. Despite the contraction in business scale, its overall financial soundness appears to have been managed in a stable manner. This could provide greater funding flexibility when pursuing new-business investment or further M&A.

Bear factors

Core Business Revenue and Profit Slowdown

In 2025, revenue and operating profit fell 31.9% and 45.6% year over year, respectively, and controlling-interest net income also turned negative. Into 2026, revenue continued to decline sharply year over year in both the first and second quarters, extending the slowdown in the core business.

Cumulative controlling-interest net losses over the last four quarters reached KRW 6.41 billion, suggesting profitability has not yet clearly recovered.

Rising Quarterly Earnings Volatility

After posting an operating loss of KRW 2.25 billion in Q4 2025, the company turned to a small profit in Q1 2026 but still saw operating profit fall 59.9% year over year in Q2. With the amplitude of quarterly results widening, forecasting the next quarter has become more difficult.

This appears to reflect a structure where Chinese domestic economic conditions and end-market demand swings feed directly into results.

Unconfirmed Revenue Contribution from New AI Business

The specific revenue contribution or profit/loss scale of the AI subsidiary acquired in 2025 has not yet been clearly disclosed. While multiple contract announcements have been made public, contract sizes have often been kept confidential, making quantitative assessment difficult for investors.

Whether the new-business investment can offset the profitability slowdown in the existing mica and graphene businesses will require confirmation through additional disclosures.

Risk factors

Industry Demand Risk

The pace of substitution from natural to synthetic mica, the cyclicality of Chinese EV demand, and fluctuations in cosmetics industry demand can directly affect performance. If competitors expand capacity or intensify price competition, margin pressure could resurface.

The graphene and AI businesses remain at an early stage of market formation, carrying relatively high demand uncertainty.

FX and Consolidated Financial Risk

The company reports its financial statements in Chinese yuan, meaning the KRW-converted figures seen by domestic investors can vary with exchange rate movements.

Because the consolidated structure comprises numerous Chinese subsidiaries, segment-level profit and loss are not always disclosed in detail, limiting investor visibility. Regulatory and policy changes affecting operations in China could also be a source of earnings volatility.

Governance and New Investment Risk

The largest shareholder group's stake stands at 20.06%, which is not particularly high. The acquisition of AI subsidiary GeneVision was carried out via an in-kind capital contribution based on a discounted-cash-flow valuation, with the counterparties including specific individual and corporate shareholders.

The structure of these related-party transactions and the appropriateness of the new business's valuation are areas that warrant ongoing scrutiny through future disclosures and results.

Overall view

China Crystal New Material Holdings has seen its earnings momentum turn, with 2025 revenue and operating profit falling 31.9% and 45.6% year over year, respectively, and controlling-interest net income swinging to a loss.

The decline continued into 2026, with revenue falling year over year in both the first and second quarters, and cumulative controlling-interest net losses over the last four quarters reaching KRW 6.41 billion.

However, its balance sheet has remained relatively stable, with a low debt-to-equity ratio of 7.1% and total equity of KRW 490.9 billion (converted).

The AI subsidiary GeneVision, acquired in 2025, is attempting business diversification by expanding its customer base to include State Grid, Huadian Group, and China Mobile, though the scale of its revenue contribution has not yet been disclosed.

The existing synthetic mica and graphene businesses remain tied to demand from end markets such as EVs and cosmetics. The stock trades in a range where a conventional price-to-earnings ratio is difficult to apply due to accumulated controlling-interest net losses, and its price-to-book ratio also sits well below 1x.

Upcoming quarterly disclosures and any revelation of new-business revenue contribution are likely to be important clues for assessing the future earnings trajectory.
