02

Business structure

NRB is a concrete modular construction company that listed on KOSDAQ on July 28, 2025. According to the July 2025 securities registration statement, the 2024 revenue mix was 59.3% leasing, 22.0% services, 18.1% products, and 0.6% merchandise and other.

The business center of gravity is shifting from leasing modular buildings for set periods toward PC modular projects in which structures are produced in factories and then sold and installed.

First-half 2026 manufacturing revenue reached KRW 24.5 billion, about 6.7 times the KRW 3.6 billion of the prior-year period, and its share of total revenue rose from 15.9% to 66.1%.

By use, residential facilities generated KRW 23.9 billion, or 64.6% of total revenue, including apartment housing, dormitories and military housing.

Main customers are public procurement and education bodies such as the Public Procurement Service, education offices and the Korea Land and Housing Corporation (LH), and in December 2025 the company also disclosed a modular officers' quarters contract for an Army unit.

On the product side, NRB promotes the BridgeSchool II, which uses a reinforced concrete structure, and a relocatable modular model that separates land-use periods from building-use periods.

Production is based in Gunsan and Gimje in North Jeolla, with about 74,000 pyeong of facilities, and the Gunsan No. 1 plant has in-house concrete equipment and automated lines. The securities registration statement described NRB as the only company in Korea holding modular high-rise technology.

04

Earnings analysis

On a DART-filed annual basis, 2025 revenue was KRW 59.5 billion, up 12.7% from KRW 52.8 billion in 2024, but operating profit fell 27.6% from KRW 6.22 billion to KRW 4.5 billion.

The company attributed the decline to higher depreciation on new concrete production equipment and costs for securing modular construction specialists during the transition to concrete production, which it described as temporary costs.

As a result, the operating margin fell from 11.8% to 7.6%, and net income attributable to controlling shareholders moved from KRW 2 billion to a net loss of KRW 533 million. Net income including non-controlling interests was also a net loss of KRW 533 million in 2025.

The balance sheet improved, with the debt-to-equity ratio falling from 241.8% to 105.0% and total equity at year-end 2025 at KRW 85.5 billion. Operating cash flow, however, dropped sharply from KRW 20.1 billion to KRW 3.01 billion. Quarterly results were volatile.

Fourth-quarter 2025 revenue rose 136.7% from the prior quarter to KRW 25.8 billion, while operating profit fell 67.0% to KRW 719 million.

In the first quarter of 2026, revenue was KRW 17.2 billion and operating profit KRW 1.47 billion, yet net income attributable to controlling shareholders was a net loss of KRW 3.22 billion, a swing into loss.

In the second quarter, revenue was KRW 19.9 billion and operating profit KRW 1.87 billion, and net income attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 5.97 billion, a swing back to profit. Third-quarter 2026 revenue was KRW 18.1 billion, down 8.8% from the prior quarter but up 66.1% year on year.

Operating profit was KRW 565 million, down 69.9% from the prior quarter and 74.1% from a year earlier, and net income attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 226 million.

Because second-quarter net income exceeded operating profit and first-quarter operating profit coexisted with a net loss, non-operating items and tax lines will need to be checked in the half-year and annual reports. Net income attributable to controlling shareholders over the last four quarters totals KRW 3.31 billion.

05

Industry analysis

Demand for modular construction depends heavily on government policy. The Ministry of Land, Infrastructure and Transport announced plans to expand public modular housing orders, and NRB plans to strengthen its housing supply capacity through automated equipment and smart factories.

From 2026, government policy to activate modular housing and discussion of the OSC modular special act have continued.

The government proposed pilot projects for modular housing of 20 or more floors and plans to fund about 50% of the additional construction cost over general methods, along with pushing for a modular special act.

NRB participates in both representative high-rise pilot projects the government cited, LH's Uiwang Choepyeong A-4BL (22 floors, 381 households) and the Gyeonggi Housing and Urban Corporation's Hanam Gyoesan A1BL (25 floors, 400 households).

Residential facilities account for 79.5% of backlog, so public housing orders have become the main route to new contracts. On competition, the company points to its high-rise modular technology and to a seismic-certified joint technology applied to its concrete modular school product.

How quickly policy orders convert into revenue is still being tested quarter by quarter. Press coverage also expresses the view that the manufacturing of construction could become a core means of rapid housing supply.

06

Outlook

The largest firm indicator the company has disclosed is backlog of KRW 141 billion at end-June 2026.

The memorandum for the Hanam Gyoesan A1BL public housing project, with a construction value of KRW 83.7 billion signed in February 2026, is still before the main contract and therefore excluded from that backlog, and the company has said it will disclose the main contract separately when signed.

On production, PC modular utilization rose from 25.3% in the first quarter to 40.5% in the second quarter, and the current annualized capacity is about 4,600 modules. The company is expanding automated equipment to raise PC modular frame capacity at the Gunsan No. 1 plant.

Through a government-backed smart factory project, it is integrating production, quality, inventory and equipment data across PC modular manufacturing and developing integrated design-production-construction management for PC residential buildings of 25 floors or more.

Recent contracts include a KRW 30.21 million-won Daegu Suchang Elementary School concrete modular classroom building contract signed on September 28, 2026, running through March 31, 2027, and an 80.6 billion-won contract with the Gangwon regional procurement office signed in April 2026 running through February 10, 2027.

Press reports also described two supply contracts signed on September 28, 2026 totaling KRW 12.8 billion. The key question is whether contract execution keeps pace with production capacity.

The next checkpoints are whether the main contract for the Hanam project is disclosed and how backlog changes in the 2026 annual report.

08

Bull factors

Revenue mix shifts toward manufacturing

First-half 2026 manufacturing revenue reached KRW 24.5 billion, about 6.7 times the prior-year period, lifting its share of revenue to 66.1%. Press coverage of the half-year report said revenue rose 62.5% year on year and the operating margin improved from 7.0% to 9.0%.

Selling and administrative cost growth was below revenue growth, which coverage cites as a factor behind the margin gain.

Backlog and public housing policy tailwinds

Backlog at end-June 2026 was KRW 141 billion, of which residential facilities accounted for KRW 112.1 billion (79.5%). The government announced plans to expand public modular housing orders, and NRB participates in both high-rise pilot projects. Press reports said two supply contracts signed on September 28, 2026 totaled KRW 12.8 billion.

Utilization and capacity expansion

PC modular utilization rose from 25.3% in the first quarter to 40.5% in the second quarter. The company is expanding automated equipment at the Gunsan No. 1 plant to raise PC modular frame capacity and says it is building repeat-production and standardization capabilities to improve cost and quality competitiveness. Whether production efficiency translates into a better gross margin is the next point to check.

09

Bear factors

Sharp slowdown in quarterly earnings

Third-quarter 2026 operating profit fell 69.9% from the prior quarter to KRW 565 million, and net income attributable to controlling shareholders fell 96.2% to KRW 226 million.

For 2025 as a whole, operating profit fell 27.6% and the company swung to a net loss attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 533 million. Net income attributable to controlling shareholders over the last four quarters totals only KRW 3.31 billion.

Uncertainty over large project main contracts

The KRW 83.7 billion memorandum for the Hanam Gyoesan A1BL project is still before the main contract and is excluded from backlog. Even with expanding policy orders, the signing timing of individual projects is not fixed.

Quarterly revenue is volatile depending on execution timing: fourth-quarter 2025 revenue of KRW 25.8 billion more than doubled the KRW 10.9 billion of the third quarter, then fell 33.5% to KRW 17.2 billion in the first quarter of 2026.

Cash flow and cost burden

Operating cash flow in 2025 fell sharply to KRW 3.01 billion from KRW 20.1 billion in 2024. The company said depreciation on new equipment and costs for securing personnel increased.

The debt-to-equity ratio fell from 241.8% to 105.0%, but total liabilities at year-end 2025 of KRW 89.8 billion remain close in size to total equity of KRW 85.5 billion.

10

Risk factors

Dependence on policy and public orders

A significant part of orders depends on public procurement from bodies such as the Public Procurement Service, education offices and LH. If policy budgets or procurement schedules shift, order timing could be delayed.

The high-rise pilot support and the modular special act are still in progress, and their effect will depend on enactment and implementation.

Dilution and lock-up expiry

Stock options granted to employees across three grants total 1,031,600 shares, about 9.5% of issued shares. The combined stake of the largest shareholder and related parties was 46.49% as of end-June 2026, and the lock-up for the largest shareholder after listing is set at 1.5 to 2.5 years. When those periods end, the number of tradable shares could increase.

Design changes and cost volatility

The company has said that design changes and other contract-condition changes may arise during modular projects and can be adjusted under Public Procurement Service contract terms. In 2025, higher depreciation on new equipment and personnel costs reduced operating profit. Project margins can be affected by design changes and process delays.

  2. February 10 and March 31, 2027

  3. By March 31, 2027

  4. Timing not set (on main contract signing)

    Whether the main contract for the KRW 83.7 billion Hanam Gyoesan A1BL memorandum is signed. Once signed, the amount and timing of its addition to backlog will change.

  5. By August 14, 2027

    This is the filing deadline for the 2027 half-year report. It is the point to check how PC modular utilization and the manufacturing share of revenue change after the automated equipment expansion at the Gunsan No. 1 plant.

12

Overall view

On a DART-filed basis, NRB's 2025 revenue rose, but operating profit and net income attributable to controlling shareholders declined, with the latter turning into a loss.

In first-half 2026, the manufacturing share of revenue reached 66.1% and revenue rose 62.5% year on year, suggesting the business shift is beginning to show in results.

By contrast, third-quarter 2026 operating profit fell to KRW 565 million, and net income attributable to controlling shareholders over the last four quarters totals KRW 3.31 billion, so earnings stability is still being tested.

Backlog of KRW 141 billion and expanding policy orders support revenue visibility, but the KRW 83.7 billion Hanam project is before its main contract and is excluded from backlog. Higher PC modular utilization and automated equipment expansion are cost-side changes to watch.

Lock-up expiry for the largest shareholder and stock option share issuance remain supply-side burdens. The next checkpoints are the operating margin and backlog in the 2026 annual report and whether the Hanam main contract is disclosed.
