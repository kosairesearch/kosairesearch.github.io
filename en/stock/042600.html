Seronics: Operating Losses Persist as TV Parts Demand Softens

Seronics' sales fell to KRW 164.9 billion in 2025 as TV parts orders slowed, and it has posted operating losses in each of the four quarters from Q4 2025 through Q3 2026, although net income attributable to controlling shareholders turned positive in Q4 2025 and Q2 2026.

02

Business structure

Seronics runs two segments, Electronics and Solar. The Electronics segment centers on device units for LCD TV input, FFC cables, and kitchen TV products, and it also performs contract processing of optical sheets.

The Solar segment produces PV ribbon, the interconnect used between solar cells, and the company has said it secured ribbon technology through a technology agreement with a Japanese partner. Major customers include LG Electronics and Samsung Electro-Mechanics, and the company operates three overseas subsidiaries.

Electronics revenue moves closely with TV set demand and customer order volumes. In 2025, a slump in the TV market and tariff issues reduced orders from key customers, and sales fell 18.5% from the prior year.

In the solar business, price competition intensified in overseas markets as later entrants such as Chinese manufacturers expanded rapidly, a point raised in the first half of 2025. The company expects grid parity in solar power generation to lift demand and is pursuing growth in emerging markets.

The largest shareholder group held 65.29% as of the last change date recorded in October 2023. At the March 2026 annual general meeting, a new inside director who previously served as head of an LG Electronics subsidiary in Poland and as a senior vice president was appointed, and he now serves as CEO.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual sales fell from KRW 218.7 billion in 2022 to KRW 201.7 billion in 2023, a 7.8% decline, then edged up 0.4% to KRW 202.5 billion in 2024 before dropping 18.5% to KRW 164.9 billion in 2025. Across 2022 to 2025, 2022 was the largest year by sales and 2025 the smallest.

Operating profit was only KRW 472 million in 2022 and KRW 390 million in 2023, a 17.5% decline, before rising to KRW 7.28 billion in 2024. In 2025 the company recorded an operating loss of KRW 2.63 billion, a swing from profit, and the operating margin moved from 3.6% in 2024 to -1.6% in 2025.

The company attributed the 2025 sales decline to weaker demand from the TV market slump and tariff issues, which reduced order volumes from key customers.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders went from KRW 90.1 billion in 2022 to net losses of KRW 28.7 billion in 2023, KRW 53.9 billion in 2024, and KRW 31.5 billion in 2025, which makes three consecutive years of losses from 2023 through 2025.

The 2022 profit attributable to controlling shareholders was far larger than that year's operating profit, which points to a large non-operating contribution.

The 2025 net loss of KRW 32.1 billion was narrower than the KRW 52.9 billion loss in 2024, and the company said equity-method gains from affiliates lifted non-operating income. Operating cash flow peaked at KRW 13.3 billion in 2024 and fell to KRW 1.13 billion in 2025.

Quarterly sales went from KRW 40 billion in Q3 2025 to KRW 34.5 billion in Q4 2025, with an operating loss of KRW 63.01 million in Q4. Sales rose 31.6% quarter on quarter to KRW 45.4 billion in Q1 2026, which was the peak, while the operating loss was KRW 584 million.

The operating loss widened to KRW 4.49 billion in Q2 2026 and narrowed to KRW 2.2 billion in Q3 2026.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders swung between losses and profits, at a net loss of KRW 12.8 billion in Q3 2025, a profit of KRW 19.9 billion in Q4 2025, a loss of KRW 2.68 billion in Q1 2026, a profit of KRW 14.1 billion in Q2 2026, and a loss of KRW 7.71 billion in Q3 2026.

05

Industry analysis

Seronics' Electronics segment depends on LCD TV components, so TV set demand and the tariff environment are external variables for its results. The TV market slump and tariff issues that the company cited for the 2025 order decline have not been clearly resolved.

Within the electronic components sector, larger parts makers are defending their positions through customer diversification and higher-value products, while Seronics is small enough that the order volumes of one or two customers can move its sales sharply.

In solar, the key issue for PV ribbon is pricing, and rapid growth by later entrants such as Chinese manufacturers in overseas markets has increased price pressure. As of the first half of 2025, the company viewed grid parity in solar power generation as a driver of demand expansion.

Listed peers in the sector include Samsung Electro-Mechanics, LG Innotek, and LG Display, and Seronics differs substantially from them in scale and customer mix. Judging whether the cycle has bottomed requires tracking TV set shipments and solar ribbon order trends together.

Industry indicators alone do not explain the quarterly swings in the company's results, so checking order trends from individual customers is necessary.

Profits appeared at the net income level even while operating losses continued, which suggests that items outside core operations supported the bottom line.

Risk factors

Demand and tariff risk

The Electronics segment is directly exposed to order swings caused by the TV market slump and tariff issues. If the factors the company cited for the 2025 sales decline persist into 2026, the quarterly sales decline could last longer.

A concentrated customer base also means that one customer's order adjustment can have a large effect on results.

Solar price competition risk

Price competition in PV ribbon intensified overseas as later entrants such as Chinese manufacturers grew rapidly, a point raised as of the first half of 2025.

Expansion into emerging markets depends on efficiency and price competitiveness, and if those conditions are not met, the solar business may contribute little to sales. Whether solar can offset weakness in Electronics is the central question.

Financial structure and share price volatility risk

A higher debt ratio and lower operating cash flow are occurring together, which narrows financial room. In February 2026, the company said proceeds from the treasury share disposal would fund borrowing repayment.

Trading volatility has been high, with the stock designated as a short-term overheating stock in April 2026 after rising 77.8% over 15 trading days, so the share price may move apart from reported results.
