02

Business structure

Founded in 1986, YulChon is a precision cold-drawn steel tube manufacturer that listed on KOSDAQ in September 2023.

Using a cold-drawing process that pulls bar and pipe stock through dies for precision shaping, it produces parts used in automotive shock absorbers, suspension systems, and steering systems, supplying global automakers and parts suppliers.

Its product lineup spans automotive components such as camshafts, EGR and rack products, shock absorbers, steering column and fuel rail products, propeller shafts, brake lines, and stabilizer bars, as well as chair and desk frame gas springs, heat exchanger tubes, and mining drill rods and casing.

According to data compiled by financial data platform Butler, the segment mix based on Q2 2026 revenue was automotive parts 88%, mining 7%, other 3%, construction equipment 1%, and industrial machinery 1%, showing heavy reliance on automotive parts.

The company exports precision drawn steel tubes to roughly 40 companies across 15 countries worldwide. Its production footprint includes the Siheung, Korea headquarters and a Mexican subsidiary that has successfully localized operations, winning new customers and expanding market share in the Americas.

A new production base being built through a Polish subsidiary established in 2022 will, once completed, round out a three-nation global manufacturing network spanning Korea, Mexico, and Poland.

The company is also pursuing mass production of seamless tubes, a weld-free, high-value-added product, as part of efforts to upgrade its product mix.

Competitively, it contends with numerous domestic and overseas steel tube and forging parts makers, positioning quality certification with automakers and parts suppliers alongside its multi-region production base as key differentiators.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual revenue fell 4.2% from KRW 75.8 billion in 2022 to KRW 72.6 billion in 2023, then rose for two straight years to KRW 78.2 billion in 2024 (up 7.6%) and KRW 79.3 billion in 2025 (up 1.4%). Among 2022-2025, 2025 was the largest revenue year and 2023 the smallest.

Operating profit moved from KRW 5.15 billion in 2022 to KRW 4.12 billion in 2023 (down 20.0%), KRW 5.16 billion in 2024 (up 25.2%), and KRW 5.08 billion in 2025 (down 1.5%), with 2024 the largest and 2023 the smallest of the four years.

The operating margin dipped from 6.8% in 2022 to 5.7% in 2023, then moved between 6.6% in 2024 and 6.4% in 2025, staying in the mid-6% range. Controlling-interest net income swung to a KRW 18.1 billion loss in 2023, turned profitable at KRW 2.39 billion in 2024, and grew 36.7% to KRW 3.27 billion in 2025.

Net income including non-controlling interests also improved, from a KRW 17.9 billion loss in 2023 to KRW 2.61 billion in 2024 and KRW 3.67 billion in 2025, with the company citing increased foreign-currency translation gains on export receivables as a key driver of the 2025 improvement.

Quarterly revenue rose from KRW 19.4 billion in Q2 2025 to KRW 20.4 billion in Q3 2025 (up 5.1%), dipped to KRW 19.6 billion in Q4 2025 (down 4.1%), then climbed for two straight quarters to KRW 19.8 billion in Q1 2026 (up 1.2%) and KRW 23 billion in Q2 2026 (up 16.3% quarter-on-quarter and 18.7% year-on-year).

Operating profit followed a similar pattern, falling to KRW 634 million in Q4 2025 before rising for two consecutive quarters to KRW 909 million in Q1 2026 and KRW 2.32 billion in Q2 2026 (up 155.8% quarter-on-quarter and 166.5% year-on-year).

The sum of controlling-interest net income over the last four quarters, from Q3 2025 through Q2 2026, was KRW 3.32 billion, showing sizable quarter-to-quarter swings but a net-positive annual trend.

05

Industry analysis

The precision cold-drawn steel tube industry that YulChon operates in is an intermediate-goods sector supplying materials to varied downstream markets, including automotive suspension and steering parts, industrial machinery, and mining equipment.

The business is directly exposed to automaker production cycles, exchange rates, and steel raw material price swings, and since automotive parts account for most of revenue, results track global automotive demand.

The company uses its Mexican production base to access North American automaker and parts supplier chains, a strategy that addresses local-sourcing requirements that domestic production alone cannot meet. In Europe, a new Polish production base is intended to serve local electric vehicle and automotive parts demand.

Relative to peers listed in the same KOSDAQ metals and automotive parts category, YulChon is a small-to-mid-sized player by revenue.

High-value products such as seamless tubes differentiate on quality and durability rather than price versus standard welded steel tubes, and since few domestic producers mass-produce them, the segment is relatively niche.

However, global automotive production, the industry's key downstream market, remains exposed to structural shifts such as the EV transition and changing trade and tariff policies, meaning order flow and utilization at a small-cap supplier like YulChon can be affected by these macro variables.

06

Outlook

The company is building out facilities targeting completion of its Polish plant in the second half of 2026, after which it plans trial production followed by mass production.

The Polish base is expected to have annual capacity of 24,000 tons of tube forming and 16,000 tons of drawing, with the company projecting a contribution to results starting in the first half of 2027 once operational.

Yuanta Securities, in a report dated December 23, 2025, assessed that equipment investment at the Polish subsidiary was proceeding as planned, supporting mass production in 2026 and revenue recognition in 2027.

Domestically, the company has been building new seamless tube equipment at its Hwaseong plant, and expects meaningful revenue generation from this high-value product starting in 2027 once the buildout is complete.

Management has outlined a mid-term plan to strengthen market responsiveness by completing a three-site global production network spanning Korea, Mexico, and Poland. Executives have said they intend to continue delivering solid profit growth even while making upfront investments in new production sites.

However, investment cost burdens may persist until the new sites become operational, and the actual timing and scale of revenue contribution will depend on whether the equipment commissioning schedule is met.

08

Bull factors

Diversifying Global Production Bases

A three-country production network spanning Korea, Mexico, and Poland is set to be completed with the Polish plant's targeted commissioning in H2 2026. The Mexican subsidiary has already localized successfully, helping win new customers and expand market share in the Americas.

The company describes the Polish base as a production foundation to directly serve European EV and automotive parts demand. Diversifying production sites could improve the company's resilience to region-specific currency and trade variables.

A Q2 2026 Earnings Recovery

Q2 2026 revenue reached KRW 23 billion, up 16.3% quarter-on-quarter and 18.7% year-on-year, while operating profit rose to KRW 2.32 billion, up 155.8% quarter-on-quarter and 166.5% year-on-year. This extends a two-quarter streak of revenue and operating profit improvement following a trough in Q4 2025.

Controlling-interest net income also climbed to KRW 1.6 billion in Q2 2026, up 126.2% quarter-on-quarter and 298.4% year-on-year, underpinning the profitability recovery.

Financial Stability and Cash Generation

The debt-to-equity ratio fell from 217.4% in 2022 to 87.9% in 2024, and while it rose again to 102.4% in 2025, it remains far more stable than in 2022. Operating cash flow stayed positive throughout 2022-2025, ranging between KRW 5.31 billion and KRW 7.83 billion each year.

Total equity reached KRW 56.1 billion at the end of 2025, up substantially from prior years, with controlling-interest equity of KRW 53.1 billion providing a capital buffer.

Bear factors

Operating Margin Stuck in a Range

The annual operating margin has stayed around the mid-6% range for four straight years: 6.8% in 2022, 5.7% in 2023, 6.6% in 2024, and 6.4% in 2025. Even in years when revenue grew, margin improvement was modest, suggesting top-line growth has not translated clearly into profitability leverage.

Depreciation and early-stage operating costs from the new Poland and Hwaseong facilities could weigh on margins in the near term.

New-Site Investment Burden and Delay Risk

The Polish plant targets completion in H2 2026, but trial production and the transition to mass production will take additional time afterward. In H1 2026, the company sustained profit growth despite upfront investment costs tied to the new Polish plant, but a larger investment scale could pressure profitability. Any delay in the equipment commissioning schedule would also push back the expected 2027 revenue contribution.

Downstream Dependence and Earnings Volatility

With the large majority of revenue concentrated in automotive parts, results are vulnerable to a slowdown in global automaker production or shifts in specific customer demand.

A loss-making year in 2023, when controlling-interest net loss reached KRW 18.1 billion, illustrates how external factors like currency swings can cause sizable earnings swings. Quarterly volatility is also notable, as seen in the KRW 595 million controlling-interest net loss recorded in Q4 2025.

Risk factors

Currency and Raw Material Price Volatility

Given its heavy reliance on exports, fluctuations in the won-dollar and won-euro exchange rates directly affect revenue and earnings. Since increased foreign-currency translation gains on export receivables were a key driver of the 2025 net income gain, a reversal in currency direction could weigh on earnings. Rising steel and other raw material prices could also pressure costs.

Execution Risk at New Overseas Sites

Expanding production bases in Poland and Mexico carries execution risk from permitting, staffing, and equipment stabilization processes. Any delay in transitioning to mass production versus the planned schedule would also push back the timing of investment payback. Changes in labor and environmental regulations in these new regions could also affect the cost structure.

Small-Cap Liquidity and Funding

As a small-cap KOSDAQ stock, trading value and liquidity can be limited, which may amplify share price volatility. If additional capital is raised to fund ongoing overseas site investments, the possibility of equity dilution cannot be ruled out.

Small-cap shares also tend to see relatively large short-term price swings driven by news flow or supply-demand shifts.

Overall view

YulChon is a KOSDAQ-listed small-cap focused on precision cold-drawn steel tubes for automotive parts, posting 2025 revenue of KRW 79.3 billion and operating profit of KRW 5.08 billion, broadly similar in scale and profitability to the prior year.

After a large net loss in 2023, controlling-interest net income turned profitable in 2024-2025, and Q2 2026 saw both revenue and operating profit rise clearly versus both the prior quarter and the year-earlier period.

Over the medium term, the construction of a Polish production base alongside the existing Mexican site, plus the ramp-up of seamless tube equipment in Hwaseong, are key variables that could make a 2027 revenue contribution visible if execution stays on schedule.

On the other hand, the operating margin has remained stuck around the mid-6% range for four straight years, and cost burdens from new-site investment along with earnings volatility tied to automotive-sector dependence remain factors to monitor.

On valuation, shares trade at a discount to controlling-interest net assets, though the liquidity constraints and volatility typical of small-cap stocks warrant consideration.

The next quarterly report and the commissioning schedules for the Poland and Hwaseong facilities are the key checkpoints for gauging the path of future results.
