YulChon: Overseas Expansion Tests Earnings Recovery

YulChon is at a test stage: demand for its automotive drawn tubes is recovering, but new Polish and Hwaseong capacity is adding costs before its revenue contribution arrives.

02

Business structure

YulChon was founded in 1986 as a maker of precision cold-drawn tubes and listed on KOSDAQ in 2023. Its process draws bar or coil stock through dies to produce precision-machined steel tubes used in automotive shock absorbers, suspension and steering parts.

Although the company is classified under chemicals, its business is closer to automotive component materials. It has built a supply network across global automakers and parts suppliers, and its growth base is its Mexico production site.

The Polish subsidiary, established in 2022, is planned to have tube-forming capacity of 24,000 tons and drawing capacity of 16,000 tons.

For new growth, YulChon has secured seamless tube manufacturing technology through a government national project and installed related equipment at its Hwaseong plant, where installation was completed in October 2026.

Its headquarters is in Siheung, Gyeonggi Province, and production is organized across three bases in Korea, Mexico and Poland. The product range is still centered on automotive parts, but the company has indicated it will extend applications into industrial machinery and mining equipment. Revenue reached KRW 79.3 billion in 2025, and overseas expansion is cited as the main driver of sales growth.

Earnings analysis

Annual revenue fell from KRW 75.8 billion in 2022 to KRW 72.6 billion in 2023 (down 4.2% year on year), then rose to KRW 78.2 billion in 2024 (up 7.6%) and KRW 79.3 billion in 2025 (up 1.4%). Revenue increased in both 2024 and 2025, with 2025 the largest year and 2023 the smallest of 2022 to 2025.

Operating profit dropped from KRW 5.15 billion in 2022 to KRW 4.12 billion in 2023 (down 20.0%), recovered to KRW 5.16 billion in 2024 (up 25.2%) and was KRW 5.08 billion in 2025 (down 1.5%). The operating margin was 6.8% in 2022, 5.7% in 2023, 6.6% in 2024 and 6.4% in 2025.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders swung to a KRW 18.1 billion loss in 2023, returned to profit at KRW 2.39 billion in 2024, and rose 36.7% to KRW 3.27 billion in 2025.

Net income including non-controlling interests was KRW 3.67 billion in 2025, and media reports said the company attributed the gain partly to translation gains on foreign-currency receivables from exchange-rate moves.

Operating cash flow was KRW 5.31 billion in 2022, KRW 7.83 billion in 2023, KRW 7.66 billion in 2024 and KRW 5.52 billion in 2025. Quarterly, Q3 2025 revenue of KRW 20.4 billion and operating profit of KRW 1.52 billion were followed by a controlling-shareholder net loss of KRW 595 million in Q4 2025.

Q1 2026 returned to a KRW 708 million profit, and Q2 2026 revenue of KRW 23 billion (up 16.3% from the prior quarter) and operating profit of KRW 2.32 billion (up 155.8%) were the highest of the five filed quarters.

Q3 2026 revenue reached KRW 21.8 billion (up 6.7% year on year), but operating profit of KRW 1.02 billion fell 33.1% year on year and the controlling-shareholder result turned to a KRW 3.09 billion loss. The combined controlling-shareholder net loss for the last four quarters was KRW 1.37 billion.

The company said standalone revenue at its Korean head office rose 27% in Q3 2026 and returned to operating profit, while initial Polish plant costs and FX-related losses weighed on consolidated profitability.

The nine-month revenue and operating profit reported in the press match the first-half results plus the Q3 quarterly results, so they should be read separately from the quarterly filings.

Industry analysis

YulChon's end-market is automotive parts. The company said the market was squeezed by tariff effects in 2025, and that after adapting to the new environment, orders from existing customers increased and demand recovered.

The global auto market went through a slowdown, but YulChon grew sales by widening its overseas supply network.

In cycle terms, orders appear to be recovering in early 2026, but quarterly operating profit has ranged from KRW 634 million to KRW 2.32 billion, so the durability of the recovery is still being tested quarter by quarter.

On competition, YulChon makes precision steel tubes through cold-drawing and is building in-house seamless tube production to broaden its product range. Its existing products were centered on automotive parts, and the company has indicated it will extend into industrial machinery and mining equipment.

Overseas, the Mexico site is the growth base, and the Polish site, targeted for early 2027 operation, is aimed at European markets. The structure makes earnings sensitive to automaker order flows and trade conditions.

The CEO cited uncertainty in the external business environment, which remains a variable in assessing the industry outlook.

Outlook

The company said it aims to complete the main equipment at its Polish subsidiary within 2026 and begin full operation in early 2027.

A July 2026 report said the local plant was approaching completion in the second half of 2026, and the company expected it to contribute to earnings from the first half of 2027 once mass production starts.

In its November 2026 announcement, the company kept the position that revenue from the new production base would contribute in earnest from 2027. Once the Polish plant runs, YulChon will have a three-base production structure linking Korea, Mexico and Europe.

The seamless equipment at the Hwaseong plant completed installation in October 2026, and mass production is scheduled to begin in 2027.

On November 16, 2026, the company decided to enter a KRW 500 million share buyback trust contract with Korea Investment & Securities, running from November 16, 2026 to May 14, 2027, and said the acquired shares will all be cancelled.

The company also terminated a trust contract signed in July 2026 in August 2026, so the actual progress of this purchase should be confirmed through disclosures. The timing of the new plant operations and the progress of the trust purchases are facts to be verified through future filings.

Bull factors

Head office back in profit and demand recovery

The company said standalone revenue at its Korean head office rose 27% year on year in Q3 2026 and returned to operating profit of about KRW 815 million. It cited higher orders from existing customers as evidence of demand recovery.

Q2 2026 revenue of KRW 23 billion and operating profit of KRW 2.32 billion, the highest of the five filed quarters, are consistent with this recovery.

Expansion into Europe and seamless tubes

The Polish subsidiary is planned to have tube-forming capacity of 24,000 tons and drawing capacity of 16,000 tons, and the company said revenue contribution from Europe will ramp up from 2027. The Hwaseong plant completed seamless equipment installation in October 2026, with mass production expected to start in 2027.

The company has also outlined a plan to extend beyond automotive parts into industrial machinery and mining equipment.

Shareholder returns and cash generation

Controlling-shareholder net income rose 36.7% to KRW 3.27 billion in 2025, and operating cash flow stayed positive at KRW 5.52 billion. A KRW 500 million buyback trust decided on November 16, 2026 runs until May 14, 2027, and the company plans to cancel all acquired shares.

Media reports cited translation gains on foreign-currency receivables as a factor in the 2025 net income increase.

Bear factors

Return to quarterly net losses

The controlling-shareholder result in Q3 2026 was a KRW 3.09 billion loss, turning from a profit a year earlier. The combined controlling-shareholder net loss for the last four quarters was KRW 1.37 billion. The company said initial costs at the new Polish base and FX-related losses weighed on profitability.

Stagnant margins and shrinking operating profit

The operating margin moved from 6.8% in 2022 to 5.7% in 2023 and was 6.4% in 2025. Operating profit was KRW 5.08 billion in 2025, down 1.5% from the prior year.

Q3 2026 operating profit of KRW 1.02 billion fell 33.1% year on year even though revenue increased, a quarter in which higher sales did not translate into higher profit.

Rising leverage and external variables

The debt-to-equity ratio rose from 87.9% in 2024 to 102.4% in 2025, and total liabilities of KRW 57.4 billion at year-end 2025 exceeded total equity of KRW 56.1 billion.

Equipment investment runs through the end of 2026 ahead of 2027 operations, so the timing at which the financial burden shows up in earnings needs to be tracked. Automotive demand's sensitivity to tariffs and other trade variables is also a weakness.

Risk factors

Seamless tube mass-production ramp

Seamless (seamless-tube) products are a new growth axis built on manufacturing technology secured through a government national project, and installation of the related equipment at the Hwaseong plant was completed in October 2026.

Mass production is scheduled to begin in 2027, so whether quality verification and production stabilization proceed as planned is the key issue. If mass production is delayed or yields fall short of expectations, the timing of revenue from the new product could also slip.

Expansion into industrial and mining equipment

The company has indicated it will extend the application of its products from automotive parts into industrial machinery and mining equipment.

Because these fields involve different customer groups, separate order procedures and application validation may be required, so the speed at which new orders turn into revenue is uncertain. For this segment to offset weakness in automotive parts demand, the expansion will need to be confirmed through actual orders.

Securing European customer orders

The Polish subsidiary is aimed at European markets and targets operation in early 2027, so local customer orders to absorb its output need to coincide with the start of operations.

The company has said revenue from the new base will contribute in earnest from 2027, but the scale and timing of European customer orders after start-up have yet to be confirmed.

If utilization of the new base stays low, the fixed-cost burden could persist longer and the benefit of the three-base production structure may be limited.

Overall view

YulChon is a company centered on drawn steel tubes for automotive parts. Revenue rose for a second straight year to KRW 79.3 billion in 2025, while operating profit eased to KRW 5.08 billion.

In 2026, Q2 revenue and operating profit were the highest of the five filed quarters, and the head office returned to operating profit in Q3 on a standalone basis.

On the other hand, the controlling-shareholder result turned to a KRW 3.09 billion loss in Q3 2026, and the combined net loss for the last four quarters was KRW 1.37 billion.

Strengths include demand recovery, planned operation of new capacity and the share cancellation plan, while weaknesses include early overseas plant costs, a higher debt-to-equity ratio and quarterly earnings volatility.

The next checkpoints are completion of Polish equipment in late 2026, start of operations in early 2027, and the operating margin and debt ratio in the 2026 annual report. The key open question is whether earnings strength continues after the new capacity starts operating.
