Seowon: Operating Profit Recovers, Shareholder Losses Remain

Business structure

Seowon, founded in 1988 and listed in 1996, is a non-ferrous metals manufacturer that mainly produces brass billets, ingots and copper alloy products. The company's own profile describes its brass ingot as the only KS-marked product in Korea.

Its materials go into a broad range of industries, including automotive and machinery parts and electronic device components. On a consolidated basis, the company expands its market position through subsidiaries including Daechang.

It also runs sales activities in markets such as the United States and China through overseas affiliates. In 2025, copper alloy product sales split 46.18% domestic and 53.82% export, so overseas sales made up more than half. Brass rod in the same year was 45.79% domestic and 54.21% export.

As of June 2026, the market share of copper alloy ingots and billets was reported at 30.00%. Revenue composition also includes trading sales, processing fees and other income, and sales from industrial knowledge center units.

R&D spending in 2025 was about KRW 0.87 billion, or 0.05% of revenue, so the scale of technology investment is small. Competition is usually framed against non-ferrous peers in the same sector such as Poongsan.

Raw materials include brass and copper scrap and zinc, and changes in copper prices and the exchange rate feed into costs.

Earnings analysis

Seowon's consolidated results bottomed in 2023 and recovered clearly in 2024 and 2025. Revenue fell from KRW 275.7 billion in 2022 to KRW 233.2 billion in 2023 (down 15.4% year on year), then rose to KRW 1.23 trillion in 2024 (up 427.3%) and KRW 1.71 trillion in 2025 (up 39.4%), growing for two consecutive years.

Operating profit went from KRW 173 million in 2022 to KRW 453 million in 2023 (up 161.3%), KRW 27.6 billion in 2024 (up 6,003.9%) and KRW 53.3 billion in 2025 (up 92.9%), rising for three consecutive years from 2023.

The operating margin climbed from 0.1% in 2022 to 0.2% in 2023, 2.2% in 2024 and 3.1% in 2025, as operating profit grew faster than revenue.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders moved from a loss of KRW 10.2 billion in 2023 to a profit of KRW 49.5 billion in 2024, then back to a loss of KRW 5.64 billion in 2025. Net income including non-controlling interests was KRW 40.4 billion in 2024 and a net loss of KRW 34.37 million in 2025.

The 2025 consolidated loss being far smaller than the controlling-shareholder loss suggests non-controlling interests contributed positively that year. In 2024, the controlling-shareholder profit exceeded total net income, which points to losses at the non-controlling level.

Operating cash flow was KRW 10.9 billion in 2022, -KRW 1.13 billion in 2023, KRW 37.8 billion in 2024 and -KRW 9.7 billion in 2025, changing direction in alternate years.

The debt ratio rose from 95.4% in 2022 to 216.0% in 2025, and at the end of 2025 total equity of KRW 341.5 billion included KRW 145.9 billion attributable to controlling shareholders, against total liabilities of KRW 737.7 billion.

On a quarterly basis, operating profit rose from KRW 7.87 billion in the third quarter of 2025 to KRW 31 billion in the fourth quarter (up 293.7% from the prior quarter), held at KRW 27.4 billion and KRW 27.6 billion in the first and second quarters of 2026, and fell to KRW 11 billion in the third quarter (down 60.3% from the prior quarter).

Revenue peaked in the second quarter of 2026 at KRW 586.9 billion, up 16.7% from the prior quarter, the largest of the last five quarters.

Controlling-shareholder net income turned positive at KRW 8.13 billion in the first quarter of 2026, fell to KRW 4.34 billion in the second quarter (down 46.6%), and turned into a loss of KRW 1.32 billion in the third quarter. Controlling-shareholder net income over the last four quarters totaled KRW 10.2 billion.

The Wise Report profile attributes the 2025 improvement to higher demand for copper alloys from IT, electronics, aviation, automobiles and new energy power generation. Because quarterly operating profit swings are large, whether one-off items were included is a central question in interpreting these figures.

Industry analysis

Seowon's core end markets are automotive and machinery parts and electronic device components, so its earnings track production and capital spending in downstream industries.

The Wise Report profile cites growing copper alloy demand from IT, electronics, aviation, automobiles and new energy power generation as a driver of 2025 improvement. Greater use of low-lead, corrosion-resistant and lead-free materials was also cited as supporting product demand.

Across the steel sector, tighter carbon-neutrality rules and doubts about blast-furnace-centered production are being discussed. However, the government's 2026 growth strategy names rebar as the main restructuring target, so it does not directly overlap with Seowon's copper alloy business.

On trade, a September 2026 report in the Steel & Metal News described complaints from brass rod makers about tariff burdens and worsening export conditions. The same report noted that the rate for Daechang, Seowon and IMI fell from 8.26%, with the adjustment of the final determination also being watched.

Seowon's market share in copper alloy ingots and billets was reported at 30.00% as of June 2026, indicating a significant position in that product. Copper price swings and the exchange rate affect both costs and export economics, which adds many variables to the industry outlook.

Demand for copper alloys is sensitive to end-market conditions, so the direction of auto and electronics demand will shape future earnings.

Bear factors

Recurring shareholder losses

Controlling-shareholder net losses appeared in 2023 at KRW 10.2 billion and in 2025 at KRW 5.64 billion, and a KRW 1.32 billion loss followed in the third quarter of 2026. The KRW 49.5 billion profit of 2024 reversed into a loss the following year.

Controlling-shareholder net income over the last four quarters totaled only KRW 10.2 billion, so earnings persistence is not yet proven.

Financial leverage burden

The debt ratio rose from 95.4% in 2022 to 216.0% in 2025. Total liabilities of KRW 737.7 billion at the end of 2025 exceed five times the controlling-shareholder equity of KRW 145.9 billion. Interest costs and financial flexibility could both be affected if rates and refinancing conditions change.

Cash flow volatility

Operating cash flow shifted from KRW 37.8 billion in 2024 to -KRW 9.7 billion in 2025, and was -KRW 1.13 billion in 2023. Operating profit falling to KRW 11 billion in the third quarter of 2026 could narrow the room for cash generation.

Repeated years in which earnings and cash flow move in opposite directions weaken the basis for financial stability.

Risk factors

Trade and tariffs

Industry complaints about anti-dumping rulings and tariff burdens on brass rod and related items were reported. A September 2026 report noted that adjustment of the final determination was under watch. With 53.82% of copper alloy sales exported in 2025, trade variables carry significant weight.

Raw materials and FX

Raw materials such as brass and copper scrap and zinc are exposed to copper price and exchange rate moves. If raw material price changes reach selling prices with a lag, quarterly operating profit can swing.

The 60.3% quarter-on-quarter drop in operating profit in the third quarter of 2026 points to large quarterly margin volatility.

Balance sheet and group structure

A debt ratio of 216.0% and total liabilities of KRW 737.7 billion leave the company sensitive to interest rates and refinancing conditions. Non-controlling interests make up a large part of total equity at the end of 2025, so controlling-shareholder results and total results move differently.

Swings in the results of subsidiaries such as Daechang flow into consolidated earnings and remain a point to monitor.
