02

Business structure

Bbia is a cosmetics maker and seller centered on color cosmetics, listed on KOSDAQ on April 25, 2024 through a merger with SPAC Shinyoung No. 7. Its signature lip tint has reportedly sold more than 11 million units cumulatively since launch.

According to Butler, color cosmetics made up 78% of second-quarter 2026 revenue, merchandise 17%, and basic skincare 5%. The sales mix is clearly weighted toward color cosmetics. Overseas, reports say the products have gained popularity in China, India and Southeast Asian markets with large young populations.

Analysts also point to faster sales growth after the company set up a Japanese subsidiary, and 2024 export sales reached KRW 33.4 billion, well above KRW 21.3 billion in 2022 and KRW 22.7 billion in 2023.

The company has said it plans to expand beyond China, Japan and Southeast Asia into markets such as the Middle East. Peers in the same cosmetics industry include CLIO Cosmetics, Able C&C and It's Hanbul, which cover color and basic product lines. Revenue growth in this company depends heavily on its hero product and on expanding overseas distribution.

04

Earnings analysis

On a reported basis, revenue rose from KRW 38.3 billion in 2023 to KRW 57.6 billion in 2024, up 50.6% year on year, and to KRW 68 billion in 2025, up 17.9%. Revenue has grown for two straight years since 2024, and 2025 was the largest year of 2023 to 2025.

Operating profit was KRW 4.17 billion in 2023, KRW 5.54 billion in 2024, up 33.0%, and KRW 4.47 billion in 2025, down 19.3%, so 2024 was the largest year. Revenue grew while 2025 operating profit fell, pushing the operating margin from 10.9% in 2023 to 9.6% in 2024 and 6.6% in 2025.

Controlling-shareholder net profit dropped from KRW 7.74 billion in 2023 to KRW 608 million in 2024, down 92.1%, then recovered to KRW 4.76 billion in 2025, up 682.1%. Reports attribute the 2024 decline to other losses and higher income tax expense.

Net profit including non-controlling interests matched controlling-shareholder net profit in each year. At the end of 2025, total liabilities were KRW 7.91 billion and total equity was KRW 44.4 billion, all of it attributable to controlling shareholders.

The debt ratio was 16.0% in 2023, 20.6% in 2024 and 17.8% in 2025. Operating cash flow was KRW 6.25 billion in 2023, KRW -981 million in 2024 and KRW 3.15 billion in 2025, and reports say inventory and receivables growth drained cash in 2024.

On a quarterly basis, revenue was KRW 18.7 billion in the third quarter of 2025 and fell 12.6% quarter on quarter to KRW 16.4 billion in the fourth quarter.

It peaked in the first quarter of 2026, with revenue of KRW 20.3 billion, operating profit of KRW 2.94 billion, up 142.4% quarter on quarter, and controlling-shareholder net profit of KRW 2.33 billion. Revenue held near KRW 21.8 billion in the second quarter and KRW 20.2 billion in the third quarter.

Operating profit fell to KRW 1.77 billion in the second quarter and recovered to KRW 2.15 billion in the third, up 18.9% year on year, while controlling-shareholder net profit fell 24.0% year on year, so the two profit lines moved in different directions. Controlling-shareholder net profit totaled KRW 6.66 billion over the last four quarters.

Industry analysis

K-beauty demand growth is cited as the main driver of the cosmetics industry's expansion. FnGuide pointed to rising K-beauty demand as a factor behind Bbia's revenue growth.

Overseas demand in China, Japan and Southeast Asia is a central pillar of industry growth, and the company's export expansion lines up with that direction. The position of the industry cycle is best gauged by reading export indicators together with domestic consumption indicators.

Working-capital efficiency is weaker than the industry. According to K-ValueChain's analysis, the cash conversion cycle at the end of 2025 was 193 days, longer than the industry median of 108 days and in the bottom quartile. Cash tied up in inventory and receivables is more pronounced than the industry average.

The same analysis describes a debt ratio of about 18% and a near net-cash position, which indicates ample financial room. The most direct comparison within the industry is with CLIO Cosmetics, Able C&C and It's Hanbul.

As an indie color cosmetics brand, the company relies on its hero product and on expanding overseas distribution channels to grow.

06

Outlook

The company set a target of KRW 70 billion in revenue for 2027 while preparing for its listing, and reports say it projected annual revenue growth in the mid-18% range. Revenue of KRW 68 billion in 2025 is close to that target.

Whether the target is reached depends on quarterly revenue staying around KRW 20 billion, and 2026 quarterly revenue has been in that range at KRW 20.3 billion, KRW 21.8 billion and KRW 20.2 billion. New markets such as the Middle East are included in the expansion plan.

On the shareholder return side, a KRW 3 billion treasury share purchase trust disclosed on June 9, 2026 was terminated early on September 8, 2026, returning 391,677 common shares to the company's account.

The company said it would cancel these shares within 2026, with a cancellation amount of KRW 2.83 billion and a scheduled cancellation date of November 12, 2026. The cash dividend for 2025 was set at a total of KRW 1.96 billion, with the shareholder meeting scheduled for March 27, 2026.

The next periodic report is the 2026 annual report, which will show full-year revenue, operating margin and operating cash flow together. The first-quarter 2027 report is due around mid-May 2027 under the statutory filing deadline.

Bull factors

Wider overseas sales base

Reports say 2024 export sales reached KRW 33.4 billion, up sharply from KRW 21.3 billion in 2022 and KRW 22.7 billion in 2023. Sales growth accelerated after the Japanese subsidiary was set up, and the hero product is reported to be well known in China, India and Vietnam. Overseas sales add a demand base that is separate from domestic demand.

Earnings recovery and recent operating trend

Controlling-shareholder net profit reached KRW 4.76 billion in 2025, up 682.1% year on year. It peaked at KRW 2.33 billion in the first quarter of 2026, up 38.4% from the prior quarter. Third-quarter 2026 operating profit of KRW 2.15 billion, up 18.9% year on year, shows that operating earnings remain intact.

Financial room and shareholder returns

The debt ratio was 17.8% at the end of 2025, and total equity of KRW 44.4 billion is entirely attributable to controlling shareholders. A KRW 3 billion treasury share purchase trust was disclosed on June 9, 2026, and the 391,677 shares acquired are being cancelled. The total cash dividend for 2025 was set at KRW 1.96 billion.

Bear factors

Shrinking operating margin

The operating margin fell from 10.9% in 2023 to 9.6% in 2024 and 6.6% in 2025. In 2025 revenue rose 17.9% while operating profit fell 19.3%, so revenue growth did not translate into profit growth. Rising sales alongside a declining margin is the main bearish point.

Weaker cash generation

Operating cash flow was KRW -981 million in 2024, and reports say inventory and receivables grew. Cash inflow had been KRW 6.25 billion in 2023, and analysts described cash generation as weakened.

The cash conversion cycle of 193 days is longer than the industry median of 108 days, so cash stays tied up in inventory and receivables.

Quarterly profit slowdown and product concentration

Controlling-shareholder net profit fell for two consecutive quarters, to KRW 1.54 billion in the second quarter and KRW 1.09 billion in the third quarter of 2026. Operating profit rose in the third quarter, but controlling-shareholder net profit fell 24.0% year on year, so the two profit lines diverged.

Because the hero product has sold more than 11 million units, a shift in demand for that product could move quarterly results sharply.

Risk factors

Working capital (inventory and receivables)

In 2024, operating cash flow turned negative as inventory and receivables grew quickly. A cash conversion cycle of 193 days is longer than the industry median, so the speed of cash collection matters. Inventory write-downs or slower collection of receivables could weaken both earnings and cash flow.

Dependence on overseas markets

Export sales of KRW 33.4 billion in 2024 were more than half of the KRW 57.6 billion in revenue that year, so overseas demand is a major pillar of results. Changes in consumer demand or local regulation in China, Japan and Southeast Asia could feed directly into sales.

Running overseas subsidiaries and entering new markets such as the Middle East also adds cost and local management burden.

Earnings volatility and product concentration

Controlling-shareholder net profit swung widely, falling from KRW 7.74 billion in 2023 to KRW 608 million in 2024, down 92.1%, before recovering to KRW 4.76 billion in 2025. Reports tie the 2024 decline to other losses and higher income tax expense.

Because revenue depends on a hero product, changes in its life cycle could show up sharply in quarterly results.

Overall view

Bbia has recovered earnings, with controlling-shareholder net profit of KRW 4.76 billion in 2025, and it has financial room, with a 17.8% debt ratio and KRW 44.4 billion of controlling-shareholder equity.

Revenue grew from KRW 38.3 billion in 2023 to KRW 68 billion in 2025, and export sales expanded to KRW 33.4 billion in 2024. On the other side, the operating margin fell from 10.9% in 2023 to 6.6% in 2025, and operating cash flow was KRW -981 million in 2024.

Controlling-shareholder net profit declined for two consecutive quarters in the second and third quarters of 2026, and the 193-day cash conversion cycle is longer than the industry median of 108 days.

The valuation sits below the five-year average PER and PBR, but that comparison is most accurately read alongside the earnings recovery. The next checkpoints are the operating margin and cash flow in the 2026 annual report and whether quarterly profit rebounds in the first quarter of 2027.
