Jahwa Electronics: Profit Turnaround Meets Aggressive Capacity Expansion

Having turned profitable in 2024, Jahwa Electronics is pursuing capacity expansion in Gumi, even as operating results softened for two consecutive quarters in early 2026.

02

Business structure

Jahwa Electronics converted to a corporation in 1987 and listed on the KOSPI in 1999, building on in-house magnetic material technology to produce actuators for smartphone camera modules as its core business.

Its key products are autofocus (AF), optical image stabilization (OIS), and periscope-type folded-zoom actuators; it has long supplied Samsung Electronics smartphones and, since 2023, has also supplied key components to a leading North American customer's flagship models.

According to the 2024 annual report, telecom-equipment components accounted for KRW 626.2 billion, or 91.9% of revenue, while other parts contributed KRW 54.8 billion, or 8.1%, underscoring heavy reliance on smartphone camera components.

The other-parts segment includes HV PTC heaters and vibration motors for electric vehicles and OA equipment parts, and the company is now pursuing new items such as thin high-vibration motors for automotive massage seats and BLDC motors for robots.

In robotics components, the company generates roughly KRW 4-5 billion in customer revenue based on precision magnetic encoder and robot-joint motor technology, still a small share of total sales.

Production is diversified beyond the Cheongju headquarters in North Chungcheong Province to Vietnam, India, and Gumi in North Gyeongsang Province, with exports exceeding 90% of sales.

Within the same industry, comparable peers include Samsung Electro-Mechanics and LG Innotek, both involved in camera modules and components, though Jahwa Electronics is classified as a specialist focused specifically on actuators.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual revenue rose from KRW 292.3 billion in 2022 to KRW 503.3 billion in 2023 (up 72.2% year-on-year), KRW 681.1 billion in 2024 (up 35.3%), and KRW 848.9 billion in 2025 (up 24.6%), marking three consecutive years of growth from 2023 through 2025.

Operating results moved from an operating loss of KRW 11.7 billion in 2022 to a continued loss of KRW 16.4 billion in 2023 (loss widened), before turning to an operating profit of KRW 45 billion in 2024, then declining 6.6% year-on-year to KRW 42 billion in 2025.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders went from a loss of KRW 38.2 billion in 2022 to a continued loss of KRW 24.4 billion in 2023 (loss narrowed), turned profitable at KRW 16.6 billion in 2024, and grew 183.6% year-on-year to KRW 47.1 billion in 2025.

The operating margin improved from -4.0% in 2022 and -3.2% in 2023 to 6.6% in 2024, before easing to 4.9% in 2025. The company attributed its 2025 performance changes to higher orders from North American and Chinese customers driving revenue growth.

On a quarterly basis, revenue rose from KRW 167.8 billion in Q2 2025 to KRW 255.9 billion in Q3 2025 (up 52.5% QoQ) and KRW 264 billion in Q4 2025 (up 3.2% QoQ), then declined to KRW 260.7 billion in Q1 2026 (down 1.2% QoQ) and KRW 235.2 billion in Q2 2026 (down 9.8% QoQ, up 40.1% YoY), marking two consecutive quarterly declines after peaking in Q4 2025.

Operating profit similarly peaked at KRW 26.7 billion in Q4 2025 before falling to KRW 24.6 billion in Q1 2026 (down 7.8% QoQ) and KRW 22.4 billion in Q2 2026 (down 8.9% QoQ, turned profitable YoY), also two straight quarterly declines.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders fell from KRW 36.6 billion in Q1 2026 to KRW 17.5 billion in Q2 2026 (down 52.3% QoQ) but turned profitable versus a loss of KRW 16.1 billion a year earlier, with the trailing four quarters (Q3 2025-Q2 2026) totaling KRW 112.2 billion.

Operating cash flow improved from -KRW 58.6 billion in 2023 to KRW 107.6 billion in 2024 and KRW 103.4 billion in 2025.

Industry analysis

The key variable in the end-market is the advancement of smartphone camera modules, with folded-zoom and OIS adoption expanding particularly in premium models.

Following Samsung Electronics' 2026 launch of the Galaxy S26 series, component industry observers suggested that folded-zoom camera module adoption could broaden further.

Apple has also progressively expanded periscope-style folded-zoom cameras on its rear cameras, and interest in related component makers has tended to rise alongside new iPhone launch cycles.

According to earlier reporting, LG Innotek has not produced actuators in-house and has instead sourced them externally, differentiating its business structure from actuator specialist Jahwa Electronics.

Demand for robot and humanoid actuators and magnetic encoders is being discussed as a new growth axis, while the market for PTC heaters used in electric-vehicle electronics is also expanding.

Within the sector, Jahwa Electronics is often compared with larger diversified component makers such as Samsung Electro-Mechanics and LG Innotek, though it is classified as a mid-sized specialist focused on the actuator niche.

Given that smartphone replacement demand, premium-model mix, and currency movements all affect results simultaneously, the industry tends to exhibit relatively high quarter-to-quarter volatility.

06

Outlook

Jahwa Electronics stated it is investing roughly KRW 250 billion to quadruple OIS actuator capacity (currently around 700,000-750,000 units per week) at its existing Gumi plant, targeting mass production by July 2028, with related revenue expected to roughly double once complete.

Separately, in July 2026 the company signed a KRW 500 billion investment MOU with North Gyeongsang Province and Gumi City to build a new plant for optical drive and control products at the Gumi National Industrial Complex 1, with investment to be executed over four years from 2026 to 2029 and plans to create 400 new jobs.

Once this investment is completed, cumulative investment in the Gumi area is expected to exceed KRW 1 trillion with new employment surpassing 1,400 people. As of the end of 2025, the company indicated that a recovery in results was expected to continue, supported by expanding sales to its North American customer.

New businesses such as robot and humanoid actuators and BLDC motors remain small in scale, but the company has stated its intent to cultivate this area as a medium- to long-term growth driver.

On the brokerage side, Daishin Securities set a price target of KRW 62,000 in a June 2026 report, citing strong first-quarter results, continued growth, and business diversification. However, that figure reflects a specific point in time, and whether it incorporates subsequent Q2 2026 results warrants confirmation.

08

Bull factors

Recovering Earnings Power

After turning profitable with KRW 45 billion in operating profit in 2024, the company maintained profitability with KRW 42 billion in 2025, while net income attributable to controlling shareholders grew 183.6% year-on-year to KRW 47.1 billion in 2025.

The trailing four quarters (Q3 2025-Q2 2026) posted combined net income attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 112.2 billion, a markedly different profile from the loss-making years.

Operating cash flow also stayed above KRW 100 billion for two straight years, at KRW 107.6 billion in 2024 and KRW 103.4 billion in 2025.

Capacity Expansion Supporting Scale Growth

A KRW 250 billion investment is underway to quadruple OIS actuator capacity at the Gumi plant, with related revenue expected to roughly double upon completion. Separately, a KRW 500 billion MOU for a second Gumi plant has been signed, with investment to be executed from 2026 through 2029.

If these investments proceed as planned, cumulative investment in the Gumi area would reach roughly KRW 1 trillion, substantially expanding the production base.

Customer Diversification and New Business Lines

Since 2023 the company has supplied key components to the flagship models of a leading North American customer, and sales to Chinese customers have also grown, moderating reliance on Samsung Electronics.

New businesses including EV PTC heaters, high-vibration motors for automotive massage seats, and BLDC motors for robots are also being pursued. Customer revenue based on robot-joint motor and magnetic encoder technology has begun, indicating ongoing medium- to long-term portfolio diversification.

Bear factors

Two Consecutive Quarters of Deceleration

After peaking in Q4 2025 at KRW 264 billion in revenue and KRW 26.7 billion in operating profit, both revenue and operating profit declined quarter-on-quarter in both Q1 and Q2 2026.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders also fell 52.3% quarter-on-quarter, from KRW 36.6 billion in Q1 2026 to KRW 17.5 billion in Q2 2026. While year-on-year comparisons showed a turn to profit and growth, the sequential deceleration has continued.

Customer and End-Market Concentration

As of 2024, 91.9% of revenue came from telecom-equipment components related to smartphone camera modules, indicating very high end-market concentration. Changes in a specific customer's flagship model adoption or launch schedule can directly affect results.

Revenue from new robotics and automotive electronics components remains minor relative to the total, making near-term diversification of this reliance difficult.

Financial Burden Factors

The debt ratio eased from 82.4% in 2023 to 74.9% in 2025 but remains in the high-70% range. The possibility of exchangeable bond (EB) holders exercising put options remains, which could require additional borrowing, and in past quarters financial costs have exceeded operating profit.

With large-scale expansion investments proceeding simultaneously, borrowings and interest expenses could rise in tandem.

10

Risk factors

Financial Structure and Liquidity Risk

There are reports of a continued possibility that exchangeable bond (EB) holders could exercise additional put options, and the company has reportedly been considering further borrowing in response.

Through Q3 2025, there were quarters in which standalone financial costs exceeded operating profit, suggesting interest burden could constrain the pace of earnings improvement. With large capacity investments proceeding simultaneously, an increase in short-term borrowings could reduce financial flexibility.

Customer and Order Concentration Risk

Because the bulk of revenue depends on whether specific models from a small number of smartphone makers adopt the company's components, design changes or supply-chain shifts by customers could increase earnings volatility.

Rising revenue exposure to Chinese smartphone makers is both a growth factor and a source of exposure to China's domestic economic conditions and regulatory shifts. Competitors' technological catch-up or changes in cost competitiveness could also affect customer allocation shares.

Investment Execution and Utilization Risk

With expansion of the existing Gumi plant (KRW 250 billion) and construction of a new second Gumi plant (KRW 500 billion) proceeding simultaneously, fixed costs such as depreciation could be reflected before related revenue ramps up.

If utilization of new facilities fails to rise as planned, investment efficiency could decline and weigh on profitability. Any delay to the July 2028 mass-production target would also push back the timing of investment payback.

Overall view

Jahwa Electronics turned from losses in 2022-2023 to profitability in 2024 and maintained that trend through 2025, with trailing four-quarter net income attributable to controlling shareholders reaching KRW 112.2 billion.

However, after peaking in Q4 2025, both revenue and operating profit declined for two consecutive quarters in Q1 and Q2 2026, indicating a slowdown in growth momentum.

The company is simultaneously pursuing two large investments for medium- to long-term expansion: a fourfold capacity increase at its existing Gumi plant (KRW 250 billion) and a new second Gumi plant (KRW 500 billion).

With over 90% of revenue concentrated in smartphone camera module components, adoption decisions by specific customers and models remain the central variable for results, alongside financial variables such as exchangeable bond put options and rising borrowings.

On the brokerage side, Daishin Securities set a price target of KRW 62,000 in a June 2026 report, though whether this reflects the subsequent quarterly slowdown warrants confirmation.

Upcoming quarterly results, the pace of expansion investment execution, and changes in financial structure are likely to be the key checkpoints going forward.
