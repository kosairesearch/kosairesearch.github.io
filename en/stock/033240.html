Camera Actuator Maker: Profit Recovery Now Under Pressure

02

Business structure

Jahwa Electronics was incorporated in 1987, converted to a corporation that year, and listed on the KOSPI market in 1999 as a maker of components for communication devices.

Its core products are AF (auto focus) and OIS (optical image stabilization) actuators for smartphone cameras, along with folded zoom actuators, and the company company profile describes it as a primary vendor to a strategic customer supplying high-end actuators.

Its customers are reported to be leading global smartphone makers, so volume tends to concentrate among a few clients.

A 2017-based report stated that smartphone parts supplied to Samsung Electronics generated KRW 357.8 billion in annual revenue, about 70% of total sales; this is older data and the current customer mix may differ. The same report cited an export share of 89.6%.

New businesses extend into automotive parts such as HV PTC heaters and haptic actuators, and the company is also pursuing thin high-vibration motors for vehicle massage seats and BLDC motors for robots. Production is centered on Vietnam, with an Indian subsidiary and a Gumi plant established in 2022.

In camera module components, LG Innotek is a listed peer, and camera actuator demand is generally tied to design wins at a few large customers.

04

Earnings analysis

Revenue rose from KRW 292.3 billion in 2022 to KRW 503.3 billion in 2023 (up 72.2% year on year), KRW 681.1 billion in 2024 (up 35.3%), and KRW 848.9 billion in 2025 (up 24.6%), rising for three consecutive years from 2023 through 2025.

Operating losses of KRW 11.7 billion in 2022 and KRW 16.4 billion in 2023 continued the loss trend, before operating profit of KRW 45 billion in 2024 marked a turn to profit; in 2025 it fell to KRW 42 billion, down 6.6% year on year.

The operating margin moved from -4.0% in 2022 and -3.2% in 2023 to 6.6% in 2024 and then 4.9% in 2025, so margins did not keep pace with revenue growth.

Net loss attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 38.2 billion in 2022 and KRW 24.4 billion in 2023, before net profit of KRW 16.6 billion in 2024 turned it positive; in 2025 it reached KRW 47.1 billion, up 183.6% year on year.

Because net profit rose in 2025 while operating profit declined, the non-operating items behind that gap should be checked in the 2026 annual report. Net profit including non-controlling interests matched the controlling-shareholder figure in each year from 2022 to 2025.

Operating cash flow went from KRW 29.6 billion in 2022 to -KRW 58.6 billion in 2023, then recovered to KRW 107.6 billion in 2024 and KRW 103.4 billion in 2025.

The debt ratio rose from 47.4% in 2022 to 82.4% in 2023 and fell to 74.9% in 2025, with year-end 2025 total equity of KRW 444.7 billion and total liabilities of KRW 333.2 billion.

Quarterly revenue climbed to KRW 264 billion in Q4 2025 (up 3.2% quarter on quarter), then fell to KRW 235.2 billion in Q2 2026, and Q3 2026 revenue of KRW 252.8 billion rose 7.5% quarter on quarter but declined 1.2% year on year.

Operating profit peaked at KRW 26.7 billion in Q4 2025 and then fell to KRW 24.6 billion in Q1 2026, KRW 22.4 billion in Q2 2026, and KRW 14.4 billion in Q3 2026, a three-quarter decline.

Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders peaked at KRW 36.6 billion in Q1 2026, then fell to KRW 17.5 billion in Q2 2026 and KRW 3.16 billion in Q3 2026, a two-quarter decline. Over the last four quarters, controlling-shareholder net profit totaled KRW 91.4 billion.

05

Industry analysis

Demand for camera actuators is closely tied to the rising sophistication of smartphone cameras. According to the company profile on wisereport, Q1 2026 communication-device component revenue grew strongly as premium smartphones adopted higher-spec cameras and folded-camera demand increased.

Electrification is also lifting demand for automotive parts such as HV PTC heaters. Across the broader electronic components sector, the picture is mixed. On its Q3 2026 earnings call on October 28, 2026, Samsung Electro-Mechanics said it expected MLCC demand for its key product to grow roughly eightfold next year.

The Herald Business reported that Samsung Electro-Mechanics MLCC lead times stretched to 52 weeks and that supply shortages were spreading from premium to commodity products. This is an MLCC upcycle at a peer rather than a direct market for Jahwa Electronics, so it serves only as a read-across indicator for the sector.

Jahwa's operating profit declines since Q4 2025 have moved opposite to this sector trend, which makes customer volume and product mix worth verifying. The company describes its camera actuator franchise as globally competitive, but public data allowing a detailed share comparison with rivals is limited.

LG Innotek is a listed peer in camera module components, and both companies' earnings depend on the new-model cycles of smartphone customers.

Bull factors

Turnaround in profitability

After operating losses continued through 2023, operating profit turned to KRW 45 billion in 2024. Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders rose 183.6% to KRW 47.1 billion in 2025. The operating margin moved to 6.6% in 2024 and 4.9% in 2025, out of the loss zone.

High-value camera lineup

The company offers high-value products such as folded zoom actuators and sensor-shift OIS. Its operating commentary for Q1 2026 cites strong growth in communication-device parts from wider adoption of higher-spec cameras in premium smartphones and rising folded-camera demand. Wider adoption could support product-mix improvement.

Cash flow and balance sheet

Operating cash flow was KRW 107.6 billion in 2024 and KRW 103.4 billion in 2025, both above KRW 100 billion. The debt ratio fell from 82.4% in 2023 to 74.9% in 2025. Total equity at year-end 2025 was KRW 444.7 billion.

Bear factors

Consecutive quarterly profit declines

Operating profit peaked at KRW 26.7 billion in Q4 2025 and has declined for three consecutive quarters through Q3 2026. Controlling-shareholder net profit fell from KRW 36.6 billion in Q1 2026 to KRW 3.16 billion in Q3 2026, down 86.8% year on year in the latest quarter. The last four quarters totaled KRW 91.4 billion.

Flat revenue and customer concentration

Q3 2026 revenue of KRW 252.8 billion declined 1.2% year on year. Quarterly revenue has been below the Q4 2025 level of KRW 264 billion since. A 2017-based report stated that smartphone parts for Samsung Electronics accounted for about 70% of revenue, indicating high customer concentration.

Thin operating margin and possible reliance on non-operating items

The operating margin fell from 6.6% in 2024 to 4.9% in 2025. Operating profit declined in 2025 while controlling-shareholder net profit rose sharply, which suggests a possible contribution from non-operating items. That composition needs to be confirmed in the 2026 annual report.

10

Risk factors

Customer and export concentration

The business depends on a small number of large smartphone customers, so their new-product adoption decisions drive revenue. Reports put the export share at 89.6%. Volume shifts at a major customer or changes in the export environment could flow directly into quarterly results.

Earnings volatility

Profit has swung widely, from operating and net losses in 2022–2023 to profit in 2024–2025. Q3 2026 operating profit of KRW 14.4 billion is well below the Q4 2025 peak. Even with higher revenue, a thin margin can amplify earnings swings.

Execution of new businesses

New businesses include HV PTC heaters, haptic actuators, and BLDC motors for robots. Their revenue contribution and profitability are not separately identifiable in the disclosed results. If these businesses fail to offset declines in the camera business, the earnings recovery could be delayed.

Overall view

Jahwa Electronics has restored its earnings capacity, moving from an operating loss in 2022 to profit in 2024 and net profit attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 47.1 billion in 2025. Revenue grew for three consecutive years, from KRW 292.3 billion in 2022 to KRW 848.9 billion in 2025.

However, operating profit has declined for three straight quarters since peaking in Q4 2025, and controlling-shareholder net profit has declined for two quarters since Q1 2026, falling to KRW 3.16 billion in Q3 2026.

Supporting factors include a high-value camera actuator lineup, the turn to profit in 2024 and 2025, and operating cash flow above KRW 100 billion in both years. Negative factors include consecutive quarterly profit declines, customer concentration, and a thin operating margin.

Valuation shows a price-to-book ratio below the bottom of its past quarter-end range, but the interpretation of the earnings multiple will change if the profit trend changes.

The composition of the 2025 profit increase and the revenue contribution from new businesses are not yet fully separable in the filings, making the 2026 annual report the next key confirmation point. The nearest checkpoint is whether the next preliminary results end the three-quarter operating profit decline.
