02

Business structure

Jahwa Electronics is a telecom-device component maker that converted to a corporation in 1987 and listed on the KOSPI in 1999, operating through a headquarters and five subsidiaries with Vietnam as its main production base, while diversifying its footprint with a second plant and an Indian subsidiary in 2017 and a Gumi plant in 2022.

Its core revenue source is autofocus (AF) actuators, optical image stabilization (OIS) modules and folded-zoom actuators for smartphone camera modules, as telecom-device components form the company's core business, and telecom-device component sales accounted for 91.9% of total revenue in 2024.

Its key customer is said to include Apple, as the company is known to supply smartphone camera-module components to Apple, including folded-zoom, OIS and AF actuator parts. In terms of product mix, autofocus actuators account for roughly 93% of the relevant segment, PTC devices about 6%, and other products about 1%.

The company previously underwent restructuring by selling off most of the production equipment and raw materials at its Vinh Phuc camera-module plant in Vietnam while continuing to develop actuators for periscope-type folded-zoom modules, concentrating resources on core actuator components.

In the competitive landscape for folded-zoom actuators, Actron has been cited as the primary supplier of folded-zoom actuators produced by Samsung Electro-Mechanics for Samsung Electronics, alongside several other domestic camera-module and VCM suppliers.

More recently, robotics-sector analysis has classified Jahwa Electronics as a camera-component supplier within the humanoid value chains tied to Samsung, LG and Tesla, signaling an attempt to broaden the portfolio beyond smartphones into robotics and automotive components.

Overall, the business remains concentrated in telecom-device components while gradually exploring diversification.

04

Earnings analysis

Consolidated revenue rose for four straight years, from KRW 292.3 billion in 2022 to KRW 503.3 billion in 2023, KRW 681.1 billion in 2024 and KRW 848.9 billion in 2025, expanding rapidly in scale.

Profitability, however, swung widely: the company posted an operating loss of KRW 11.7 billion and a net loss of KRW 38.2 billion in 2022, followed by another operating loss of KRW 16.4 billion and net loss of KRW 24.4 billion in 2023, marking two consecutive years of losses.

In 2024 it turned profitable with operating profit of KRW 45.0 billion and owners' net income of KRW 16.6 billion, lifting the operating margin to 6.6%.

In 2025, despite continued revenue growth, operating profit slipped slightly to KRW 42.0 billion, pulling the operating margin down to 4.9%, yet owners' net income actually rose to KRW 47.1 billion, improving the quality of bottom-line earnings.

By quarter, Q2 2025 showed the seasonal low-season burden clearly, with revenue of KRW 167.8 billion, an operating loss of KRW 9.2 billion and a net loss of KRW 16.1 billion, before rebounding to revenue of KRW 255.9 billion and operating profit of KRW 19.4 billion in Q3, and revenue of KRW 264.0 billion and operating profit of KRW 26.7 billion in Q4; Q1 2026 extended the recovery to revenue of KRW 260.7 billion, operating profit of KRW 24.6 billion and owners' net income of KRW 36.6 billion.

According to available data, Q1 2026 consolidated revenue, operating profit and net income rose 61.7%, 389.0% and 613.2% year on year respectively, a performance attributed to expanding adoption of high-spec cameras in premium smartphones and growing demand for folded-zoom cameras driving telecom-device component sales.

In Q2 2026, however, revenue fell quarter on quarter to KRW 235.2 billion and operating profit eased slightly to KRW 22.4 billion, while owners' net income dropped sharply to KRW 17.5 billion from KRW 36.6 billion in Q1, underscoring persistent quarter-to-quarter volatility.

On the cash-flow side, operating cash flow worsened from KRW 29.6 billion in 2022 to negative KRW 58.6 billion in 2023, before recovering to large inflows of KRW 107.6 billion in 2024 and KRW 103.4 billion in 2025, showing that the earnings recovery has translated into actual cash generation.

05

Industry analysis

The VCM (voice-coil motor) actuator segment that Jahwa Electronics operates in is the mainstream technology for smartphone camera-module actuators, and this segment, which underpins autofocus and optical image stabilization, is reported to be growing at a 7.2% CAGR, faster than the broader camera-module market.

Market research indicates that the global camera-module market is expected to expand from about USD 41.3 billion in 2025 to about USD 50.8 billion by 2030, with growth shifting from pure volume expansion toward feature-rich innovation such as multi-camera arrays and folded-optics zoom.

It was also noted that after the 2024 Taiwan earthquake exposed vulnerabilities in VCM sourcing, the industry has focused more on supply-chain resilience.

In the domestic competitive landscape, multiple camera-module and VCM makers compete, and in the folded-zoom segment, Actron has been cited as the leading supplier within the Samsung Electro-Mechanics supply chain.

On the downstream smartphone market, the commercial success of the iPhone 17 drew attention to component suppliers, and ahead of Samsung Electronics' next flagship launch, expectations of expanding folded-zoom camera-module adoption were highlighted as a component-stock theme.

Market commentary has suggested that next-generation flagship models will require more high-spec components due to enhanced AI features, with expectations of expanded supply volumes for camera and thermal-management component makers.

Over the medium to long term, the robotics and humanoid actuator market has emerged as a new demand source, and while numerous domestic component and automotive suppliers have announced entry into related businesses, some analyst material has classified Jahwa Electronics as a candidate camera-component supplier within the humanoid value chain.

06

Outlook

The company's earnings improvement is tied to growth in premium-lineup sales at its smartphone customers, and industry observers note that a favorable environment for related component sales could continue if the share of high-spec camera and folded-zoom models keeps rising.

That said, the revenue and net-income slowdown seen in Q2 2026 appears to reflect quarter-to-quarter volatility tied to inventory and order adjustments around customer new-model launch cycles, and it will need to be confirmed whether this pattern continues in the next quarter's results.

The robotics/humanoid actuator business is currently discussed mainly at the level of industry value-chain analysis rather than official company guidance, and no concrete order or mass-production plan has been disclosed, leaving the timing of any new-business contribution uncertain.

Investors are likely to use the next quarterly release (Q3 2026) to assess whether the Q2 slowdown was temporary or indicative of a trend change. On the cost side, rising memory prices and other smartphone manufacturing cost pressures are cited as variables that could affect component suppliers' profitability.

Diversified production bases across Vietnam, India and Gumi are a relevant factor for customer diversification and supply-chain risk management.

Overall, the direction of 2026 earnings is likely to be shaped jointly by the premium smartphone sales cycle and progress on new businesses such as robotics and automotive components.

Bull factors

Earnings Recovery After Return to Profit

Following the return to profit in 2024-2025, quarterly operating profit rose noticeably from the second half of 2025, and operating profit in Q1 2026 increased 389.0% year on year. A growing share of higher-value actuators such as folded-zoom and OIS parts is seen as contributing to the mix improvement.

Cash generation was also supported, with annual operating cash flow remaining above the KRW 100 billion level in both 2024 and 2025.

Position in the Premium Smartphone Supply Chain

The company is known to supply smartphone camera-module components to Apple, and the commercial success of the iPhone 17 drew greater attention to component suppliers. Expectations of expanding folded-zoom adoption in Samsung Electronics' next flagship models have also been highlighted as a component-stock theme.

A supply-chain position spanning multiple global customers is cited as a factor that somewhat eases reliance on any single model.

Diversification Attempts into Robotics and Automotive

Robotics-sector value-chain analysis has cited the company as a candidate camera-component supplier within humanoid ecosystems linked to Samsung, LG and Tesla.

EV-oriented PTC devices and other non-smartphone components also make up a portion of revenue, which could be interpreted as a potential path toward reducing reliance on any single product line over the medium to long term. However, no concrete order or mass-production disclosure for the new business has been confirmed yet.

Bear factors

Quarter-to-Quarter Earnings Volatility

In Q2 2025, the company posted revenue of KRW 167.8 billion and an operating loss of KRW 9.2 billion during the seasonal low season, and in Q2 2026 both revenue and net income fell sharply from the prior quarter.

The structure, with heavy reliance on a limited product range and customer base, tends to amplify earnings swings around customer new-model launch cycles. A recurring pattern of quarterly surprises and slowdowns adds uncertainty to forward estimates.

Exposure to Cost and Currency Volatility

Component costs such as memory prices and currency fluctuations are cited as variables that can affect customers' purchasing-price negotiating power. In 2022-2023, the company incurred operating losses even as revenue grew, showing that cost pressures had a meaningful impact on profitability. If the cost environment worsens again, the recent margin recovery could slow.

New-Business Uncertainty

Exposure to robotics/humanoid actuators is currently referenced mainly at the level of industry analysis rather than confirmed company disclosure, and no concrete order or mass-production timeline has been disclosed.

Given the uncertain timing and scale of any new-business contribution, earnings are likely to remain more heavily influenced by the existing smartphone-component business cycle for the time being.

10

Risk factors

Customer and Product Concentration Risk

With telecom-device components (AF, OIS and folded-zoom actuators) forming the company's core revenue source, weaker sales or specification changes for a particular customer's new model could directly affect results. Customer diversification is underway, but reducing concentration itself may take time.

Profitability Volatility Risk

As shown by consecutive operating losses in 2022-2023 and the return to profit in 2024-2025, the operating margin swung widely over several years, ranging from -4.0% to 6.6%.

There is a history of profit and loss reversing over short periods due to changes in cost, currency and product mix, so the sustainability of earnings warrants continued monitoring.

New-Business Execution Risk

New businesses such as robotics and automotive components are still at an early stage, where investment and R&D precede any confirmed revenue contribution.

Competitors are also entering similar robotic actuator businesses, such as the unveiling of the humanoid actuator 'AXLON', suggesting that competition to secure early market positioning could intensify.

Overall view

Jahwa Electronics went through an earnings turnaround from operating losses in 2022-2023 to a return to profit in 2024-2025, with quarterly operating profit stepping up from the second half of 2025.

Its core business supplies autofocus, OIS and folded-zoom actuators to global smartphone customers including Apple, linking its performance closely to the premium smartphone camera upgrade cycle.

However, the quarter-to-quarter volatility seen in Q2 2025 and Q2 2026 reflects the characteristics of a business structure with significant reliance on specific customers and products.

New businesses such as robotics/humanoid actuators and EV-oriented PTC components point to potential portfolio diversification, but remain at an early stage with no confirmed timing for revenue contribution.

The next quarterly results, sales trends of upcoming smartphone models, and any disclosures related to new businesses are likely to be important variables in assessing the future earnings path.
