02

Business structure

Hizeaero is an aircraft parts maker founded in 2001 that focuses on processing and assembling aircraft structures, and it has been listed on KOSDAQ since 2015.

Its operations span aircraft parts manufacturing, machined parts manufacturing, and assembly and jig design, with domestic production sites in Sacheon, Jinju and Busan according to a December 2022 research report.

The company joined Boeing's first-tier supplier base in 2011 and entered the global aircraft supply chain, with main items including B787 titanium foil, wing and fuselage-related structures, and B737-family tail assemblies.

First-half 2026 revenue from the aircraft parts segment was KRW 35.6 billion, up 14.6% from KRW 31.0 billion a year earlier, according to October 2026 press coverage.

Over the same period, the apparel and accessories segment of a consolidated subsidiary rose from KRW 5.3 billion to KRW 9.9 billion, an increase of 88.7%, so part of the top-line growth came from outside aircraft parts.

Beyond Boeing's supply chain, customers identified include Korea Aerospace Industries (KAI) and Korean AIR Lines.

Reports said KAI extended a B787 structure assembly contract worth KRW 78.1 billion, and Korean AIR Lines awarded B787 structure and CH-47 military helicopter structure manufacturing and assembly work in 2026.

Defense and space are at an early expansion stage, which the company presents as a medium- to long-term growth base.

Peers such as Korea Aerospace Industries and Hanwha Aerospace run system-level programs, whereas Hizeaero supplies structures beneath them, so cost control and delivery performance are key drivers of profitability. The company is moving to bring outsourced processes in-house to lower its cost ratio.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual revenue rose from KRW 58.8 billion in 2022 to KRW 80.8 billion in 2023, an increase of 37.4% year on year, and then to KRW 86.5 billion in 2024, up 7.2%. It edged down to KRW 84.6 billion in 2025, a decline of 2.2%. Among 2022 to 2025, the largest year was 2024 and the smallest was 2022.

The operating loss narrowed from KRW 8.45 billion in 2022 to KRW 4.54 billion in 2023 and KRW 2.23 billion in 2024, before the company reported operating profit of KRW 4.6 billion in 2025, a turn to profit. The operating margin moved from -14.4% in 2022, -5.6% in 2023 and -2.6% in 2024 to 5.4% in 2025.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders turned to a profit of KRW 2.07 billion in 2025, after a net loss of KRW 9.39 billion in 2024 when the loss widened. Net income including non-controlling interests was KRW 2.36 billion in 2025, a different figure from profit attributable to controlling shareholders.

On costs, the company performed surface treatment in-house, and according to October 2026 press coverage, standalone outsourced processing costs fell from KRW 5.1 billion in 2024 to KRW 2.8 billion in 2025, while the consolidated cost of sales ratio declined from 92.8% to 85.4%.

The company attributed the 2025 return to profit to workforce efficiency and an improved cost structure.

Quarterly operating profit was KRW 802 million in 3Q 2025, KRW 1.38 billion in 4Q 2025, KRW 1.18 billion in 1Q 2026 and KRW 2.29 billion in 2Q 2026, before the company posted an operating loss of KRW 127 million in 3Q 2026.

Profit attributable to controlling shareholders climbed from KRW 444 million in 3Q 2025 to KRW 1.68 billion in 2Q 2026, then became a net loss of KRW 1.71 billion in 3Q 2026.

Because the net loss in 3Q 2026 was far larger than the operating loss, non-operating items and taxes appear to have weighed heavily, and the detailed items should be checked in the periodic report. Debt-to-equity was 126.2% in 2022, rose to 183.1% in 2024 and fell to 159.0% in 2025.

Total equity at end-2025 was KRW 45.1 billion, of which KRW 41.4 billion was attributable to controlling shareholders. Operating cash flow moved from -KRW 7.66 billion in 2022 and -KRW 4.19 billion in 2023 to KRW 1.76 billion in 2024 and KRW 6.74 billion in 2025.

In the first half of 2026, however, reported consolidated operating cash flow turned to a net outflow of KRW 1.7 billion, mainly because inventory rose from KRW 18.8 billion to KRW 24.8 billion.

05

Industry analysis

Demand for aircraft parts is tied to global civil aircraft production. A December 2022 research report noted that Boeing halted production temporarily after January 2020, and that output at the time was 31 aircraft a month, about 60% of the 52 a month recorded in 2018.

The same report projected global civil aviation demand to grow at an average annual rate of 3.8% through 2041. Because that outlook dates from 2022, current production rates should be checked against more recent data.

The company credited cost-structure improvements for its 2025 return to profit and now treats expanding aircraft parts orders as a growth pillar.

In defense, press reports of structure orders from KAI and Korean AIR Lines show Hizeaero being placed in the supply chain beneath large domestic defense and aerospace system makers.

Listed peers in the same sector that also handle defense and aircraft parts include SNT Dynamics, Firstec and AeroSpace Technology of Korea.

According to the company's own diagnosis, its price-to-book multiple at mid-2026 was below the median of the KOSDAQ industrial sector, and it attributed this to low market expectations on profitability and growth after a long period of losses.

Because defense demand moves with orders from large system makers, the company's concentration on a small number of customers is a defining feature of its position in the industry.

06

Outlook

The most concrete basis the company has presented is its backlog. Its standalone backlog was about KRW 1,183.5 billion at mid-2026, and the company expects new orders to contribute to revenue from 2027.

Identified individual contracts include a B787 structure assembly extension with KAI worth KRW 78.1 billion, and B787 structure plus CH-47 military helicopter structure manufacturing and assembly work for Korean AIR Lines.

The company said it will continue bringing outsourced processes in-house and improving its cost ratio. Its value-up plan, reported to the board on October 26, 2026, set medium- to long-term targets of a price-to-book ratio of at least 1.2 times in 2027 and a price-to-earnings ratio of about 10.5 times in 2029.

Expanding into defense and space was also presented as a growth pillar. The next checkpoint is full-year 2026 results, which will be finalized in the 2026 annual report, including the fourth-quarter operating margin and operating cash flow.

Whether the 3Q 2026 operating loss persists through year-end is the first item to check. The 1Q 2027 quarterly report will then show whether new contracts are being recognized as revenue.

With borrowings rising, whether operating cash flow recovers and whether inventory and receivables pressure eases are also points to follow.

08

Bull factors

Turn to profit and lower cost ratio

Operating profit turned positive at KRW 4.6 billion in 2025, and the operating margin rose from -14.4% in 2022 to 5.4% in 2025. According to October 2026 press coverage, the consolidated cost of sales ratio fell from 92.8% to 85.4%, and standalone outsourced processing costs also declined.

If in-house processing keeps working, there is room for the operating margin to improve further, which is the core bullish argument.

Backlog and large contracts

The standalone backlog at mid-2026 was about KRW 1,183.5 billion, well above consolidated revenue of KRW 84.6 billion in 2025. The company said it expects new orders to contribute to revenue fully from 2027. A B787 structure assembly extension with KAI worth KRW 78.1 billion was reported as a major contract.

Expansion into defense and space

Press reports said Hizeaero won CH-47 military helicopter structure manufacturing and assembly work from Korean AIR Lines. Alongside expanding aircraft parts orders, the company presented entry into defense and space as a medium- to long-term growth base.

Broadening demand to defense structures beyond aircraft parts supports the case for a more diversified business.

09

Bear factors

Operating loss in 3Q 2026

The operating loss in 3Q 2026 was KRW 127 million, reversing from operating profit of KRW 2.29 billion in 2Q 2026. The net loss attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 1.71 billion was much larger than the operating loss, suggesting that non-operating items had a large effect.

Because quarterly profit reversed sharply, whether the cost-ratio gains persist is the central bearish question.

Higher borrowings and weaker cash flow

Total liabilities rose to KRW 89.0 billion at mid-2026 from KRW 71.7 billion at end-2025, and short-term borrowings expanded to KRW 24.2 billion. Over the same period, operating cash flow shifted to a net outflow of KRW 1.7 billion and the current ratio fell from 225.3% to 159.8%. The improvement in the debt ratio rests on higher equity from the revaluation gain, which is a bearish point.

Much of the growth comes from a non-core business

In the first half of 2026, growth in top-line sales relied heavily on the fashion and accessories business of a consolidated subsidiary. That segment's revenue rose from KRW 5.3 billion to KRW 9.9 billion, or 88.7%, while the aircraft parts segment grew 14.6%. It is unclear how much the core aircraft parts business will add to earnings, which is a bearish factor.

10

Risk factors

Cost and profitability volatility

Although cost-ratio gains supported the 2025 return to profit, a company profile comment noted that in 1Q 2026 revenue rose while profitability fell because of cost pressure. Quarterly operating profit moved from KRW 2.29 billion in 2Q 2026 to an operating loss of KRW 127 million in 3Q 2026.

With a manufacturing structure that carries fixed costs, earnings may be sensitive to changes in volume and cost.

Financial health and working capital

Gross borrowings rose from KRW 52.5 billion at end-2025 to KRW 61.5 billion at mid-2026, while cash and equivalents fell from KRW 10.4 billion to KRW 9.1 billion. Inventory increased from KRW 18.8 billion to KRW 24.8 billion, adding to working capital pressure. Whether the earnings recovery can translate into a stronger financial position remains an open task.

Customer concentration and timing of revenue

Dependence on Boeing's supply chain and on a small number of customers such as KAI and Korean AIR Lines means order changes can flow directly into results. The company presented revenue recognition of new orders as becoming full from 2027. Until then, there could be a gap in top-line growth, which is a risk.

12

Overall view

Hizeaero moved from an operating loss of KRW 8.45 billion in 2022 to operating profit of KRW 4.6 billion in 2025. Revenue peaked in 2024 at KRW 86.5 billion and eased to KRW 84.6 billion in 2025, while the operating margin rose to 5.4% in 2025.

Cost-ratio gains and lower outsourcing costs are cited by the company and press reports as the main drivers of the change. However, the 3Q 2026 operating loss reversed the quarterly trend, and total liabilities rose to KRW 89.0 billion at mid-2026 while operating cash flow turned to a net outflow.

The standalone backlog of about KRW 1,183.5 billion and the plan to recognize new orders from 2027 are presented as growth support, but the pace and scale at which orders become revenue must be confirmed in results from 2027 onward.

The company set medium- to long-term targets of a price-to-book ratio of at least 1.2 times in 2027 and a price-to-earnings ratio of about 10.5 times in 2029. In summary, the turn to profit and order expansion form the bullish case, while the quarterly loss and weaker borrowing and cash flow form the bearish case.
