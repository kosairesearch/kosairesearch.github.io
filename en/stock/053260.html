02

Business structure

Keum Kang Steel was founded in 1977 and listed on KOSDAQ in 2002. It is a steel processing company that cuts and slits cold-rolled coil to customer specifications. The company is a designated processing center for POSCO and receives its raw cold-rolled coil from POSCO in full, forming a supply-dependent partnership.

Processed products go to industrial-material customers in automotive, home appliances, construction and industrial containers. The company operates modern coil slitting and cutting lines and large slitters, and it highlights equipment that removes surface defects when processing high-grade materials.

As of the second quarter of 2026 steel accounted for 100% of sales, so the business is a single-segment structure. Because sales are concentrated in one segment, domestic steel demand and price moves feed directly into results.

Competition comes mainly from other cold-rolled processors and from low-priced imports from China and Japan. Raw material prices and low-priced Chinese exports are cited as variables that determine margins. Segment-level sales figures are not part of the disclosed results, so the analysis uses total revenue trends.

With raw material sourced from a single supplier, POSCO's production and demand cycles affect both the company's volumes and its margins.

04

Earnings analysis

On a disclosed basis, revenue fell from KRW 244.2 billion in 2022 to KRW 225.4 billion in 2023 (down 7.7% year on year), KRW 201.1 billion in 2024 (down 10.8%) and KRW 177.2 billion in 2025 (down 11.9%).

Revenue has declined for three consecutive years from 2023 through 2025, with 2022 the largest year and 2025 the smallest in the 2022–2025 period.

Operating profit rose from KRW 4.89 billion in 2022 to KRW 5.29 billion in 2023 (up 8.2%), then fell to KRW 3.81 billion in 2024 (down 28.0%) and KRW 1.5 billion in 2025 (down 60.5%), a two-year decline from 2024 through 2025. The operating margin moved from 2.0% in 2022 to 2.3% in 2023, 1.9% in 2024 and 0.8% in 2025.

In 2025 the margin compression was larger than the revenue decline. The company attributed the earnings decline to lower profitability in its steel business. Net income attributable to controlling shareholders swung from KRW 6.9 billion in 2024 to a net loss of KRW 63.63 million in 2025.

Net loss for the year including non-controlling interests was KRW 175 million, and the company cited the disposal of a subsidiary as a factor. Operating cash flow went from KRW -10.7 billion in 2023 to KRW 8.15 billion in 2024 and KRW 5.5 billion in 2025.

On a quarterly basis, revenue was KRW 39.8 billion in the third quarter of 2025, KRW 42.4 billion in the fourth quarter (up 6.6% quarter on quarter), KRW 42.4 billion in the first quarter of 2026, KRW 52 billion in the second quarter (up 22.6%) and KRW 49.5 billion in the third quarter (down 4.8% quarter on quarter, up 24.3% year on year).

Operating profit was KRW 31.15 million in the first quarter of 2026, then KRW 1.18 billion in the second quarter and KRW 761 million in the third quarter, which was up 1,118.1% year on year.

The third quarter also recorded a net loss attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 14.54 million, so the operating gain did not carry through to net income. Non-operating items are likely behind the gap, in our reading. Net income attributable to controlling shareholders over the latest four quarters totals KRW 6.01 billion.

05

Industry analysis

The steel processing sector is split: construction, machinery and appliance demand has been weak, while automotive and shipbuilding demand has held up relatively well. Prolonged weakness in domestic construction and problems in project financing are cited as reasons for the company's revenue decline.

Weak appliance and machinery demand combined with rising low-priced imports from China and Japan has pressured both sales and profitability, according to analysts.

On the other side, growth in automotive and shipbuilding demand has been maintained, so processing volumes could rise if steel-consuming manufacturers increase output. Expanded low-priced exports from China are cited as a factor pressing domestic steel margins.

As a designated POSCO processing center, the company's sourcing and customer structure move with POSCO's production flow. On equipment, custom-spec processing and surface-defect removal are presented as points of differentiation.

When operating profit rose in the second quarter of 2026, market commentary said the key question was whether cost reductions would continue into the second half.

Operating profit rose again in the third quarter of 2026, but a net loss attributable to controlling shareholders meant the operating recovery did not reach net income. Signs of a sector recovery appear to depend on construction demand and the direction of import controls.

06

Outlook

The value-up plan the company disclosed in March 2026 targets stable financial soundness management and an earnings structure built on operating profit.

The same plan presented a 25% payout ratio for 2025, the most recent fiscal year, and the company said it qualifies as a high-dividend company under the Restriction of Special Taxation Act. Dividends for 2025 totaled KRW 2.67 billion, up 12.9% from KRW 2.37 billion for 2024.

The targets are directional rather than numerical, so the plan sets no explicit revenue or operating profit goals. Whether the operating-profit-based structure takes hold will therefore be judged from the profit trend in periodic reports.

Third-quarter 2026 operating profit of KRW 761 million compares with a 0.8% full-year operating margin for 2025, though the full-year margin will be confirmed in the 2026 annual report.

Third-quarter revenue of KRW 49.5 billion was 4.8% below the second quarter's KRW 52 billion, showing that demand has not recovered evenly from quarter to quarter.

The operating margin and whether net income attributable to controlling shareholders returns to profit in the next periodic report are the next points to track against these targets.

Risk factors

Demand and import risk

If weakness in construction, machinery and appliance demand persists, processing volumes could fall further. Growing low-priced imports from China and Japan could pressure selling prices and margins together. How far automotive and shipbuilding demand offsets this will shape the revenue direction.

Raw material and customer concentration risk

The company is a designated processing center that receives all of its raw material from POSCO, so changes in POSCO's production or procurement pass straight through to its volumes. Raw material price swings are also a margin variable. High reliance on one supplier may limit bargaining power and volume allocation.

Governance and financial-capacity risk

Amid ongoing management-control litigation, the largest shareholder and related parties hold 52.33% according to fnguide, with the last change dated December 12, 2025. Operating cash flow was negative at KRW -10.7 billion in 2023, so cash generation can swing with earnings. With operating profit at KRW 1.5 billion in 2025, earnings-based financial capacity has thinned.

Overall view

Keum Kang Steel is a steel processor that cuts and slits cold-rolled coil as a designated POSCO processing center. Revenue has fallen for three consecutive years from 2023 through 2025 based on disclosed results.

Operating profit declined for two consecutive years from 2024 through 2025, reaching KRW 1.5 billion and a 0.8% operating margin in 2025, and the company swung to a net loss attributable to controlling shareholders that year.

Operating profit stayed positive in the second and third quarters of 2026, at KRW 1.18 billion and KRW 761 million, but the third quarter still recorded a net loss attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 14.54 million.

On the balance sheet, the debt-to-equity ratio was 19.3% at the end of 2025 and operating cash flow stayed positive, and the March 2026 value-up plan set a 25% payout ratio for 2025.

On the other side, weak construction demand, import pressure, dependence on POSCO and repeated governance-dispute disclosures remain sources of uncertainty.

The next checkpoints are the full-year operating margin in the 2026 annual report and whether net income attributable to controlling shareholders returns to profit in the first quarter of 2027.
