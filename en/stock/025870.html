After the Loss, a Manufacturing Shift and a Listing Test

02

Business structure

Silla SG's core businesses are fish-based sausage processing, canned whelk (goebaengi) processing and livestock meat distribution.

In a June 2026 announcement, the company said its fish-based sausage line has an annual capacity of about 4,500 tons and its canned whelk business keeps output around 1.1 million cans a year. The fish-based sausage business is produced on an OEM basis for CJ CheilJedang.

In livestock distribution, the company works with Cargill Korea, SFF and TREX, including overseas local packers and Korean branches, to secure supply, and it is shifting toward U.S. frozen beef. Sales channels are expanding into online sales, e-commerce and large food-service wholesalers.

In June 2026 the company also said it had entered cured-meat sausage production for CJ CheilJedang, with an annual production target of about 1,500 tons and a sales target of about KRW 6 billion.

The company projects total manufacturing sales of about KRW 36 billion a year when combined with existing fish-based sausage sales of about KRW 25 billion and canned whelk sales of about KRW 5 billion. These are company targets, not disclosed segment results.

Because fish-based sausage is made on contract for a large food company, the business depends heavily on one customer. Diversifying overseas supply sources remains a task for the distribution segment.

In its June 2026 value-up plan, the company said it would move from a distribution-centered structure to processing and manufacturing.

04

Earnings analysis

On a full-year basis, sales rose from KRW 73.4 billion in 2022 to KRW 88.4 billion in 2023, an increase of 20.4%, making 2023 the largest year of 2022 to 2025. Sales then fell 3.1% to KRW 85.6 billion in 2024 and 18.2% to KRW 70 billion in 2025, the smallest year in the period.

Operating profit declined from KRW 2.25 billion in 2022 to KRW 1.69 billion in 2023 (down 25.1%) and KRW 1.15 billion in 2024 (down 32.0%). The operating margin moved from 3.1% to 1.9%, then 1.3%, and was -2.2% in 2025, when the company swung to an operating loss of KRW 1.56 billion.

Profit attributable to controlling shareholders fell from KRW 1.13 billion in 2022 to KRW 298 million in 2023 and KRW 121 million in 2024, before a net loss of KRW 3.34 billion in 2025. Net profit, including non-controlling interests, showed the same net loss of KRW 3.34 billion in 2025.

Operating cash flow was positive at KRW 5.22 billion in 2024 but turned to -KRW 2.1 billion in 2025. The debt-to-equity ratio rose from 164.1% in 2024 to 219.8% in 2025, when total equity was KRW 14.1 billion and total liabilities were KRW 31 billion.

On a quarterly basis, the third quarter of 2025 showed sales of KRW 20.7 billion and an operating loss of KRW 44.27 million. The fourth quarter then recorded sales of KRW 15 billion, down 27.5% from the prior quarter, and an operating loss of KRW 531 million.

In the first quarter of 2026, sales were KRW 17.6 billion, up 17.4% quarter on quarter, with an operating loss of KRW 581 million. The second quarter brought sales of KRW 20 billion and an operating profit of KRW 802 million.

Third-quarter 2026 sales were KRW 19.7 billion, down 1.8% quarter on quarter and 5.0% year on year, while operating profit was KRW 117 million, down 85.4% from the prior quarter.

Third-quarter profit attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 1.54 billion, a swing to profit from a year earlier, but the gap with operating profit is large, so the non-operating items need to be checked in the next periodic report.

Over the last four quarters, from the fourth quarter of 2025 to the third quarter of 2026, profit attributable to controlling shareholders totaled KRW 76.86 million.

05

Industry analysis

Silla SG's end markets are domestic food demand and processed food markets that are sensitive to imported raw material prices.

In its first-quarter 2025 earnings commentary, the company cited domestic political risk, lower catches, a strong exchange rate and a weak processed food market as reasons domestic demand was sluggish. Lower fish catches could add to raw material procurement pressure for its fish-based sausage and canned whelk lines.

The exchange rate feeds directly into the cost of imported livestock meat. The company says it plans to reduce costs by sourcing more raw materials directly. In competitive terms, fish-based sausage is produced on contract for a large food company, so order volumes depend on the client's strategy.

Livestock distribution, by contrast, relies on supply partnerships with global suppliers such as Cargill Korea. Expansion into online and e-commerce channels broadens the distribution reach.

In terms of cycle position, the company is in a transition from a distribution-led model to one centered on processing and manufacturing.

Large listed companies in the same consumer retail sector mostly build around the scale of their retail networks, whereas this company is built around manufacturing capacity and contract volumes.

06

Outlook

The company has set out three directions. First, the Value-Up Plan 2026 calls for shifting toward processing and manufacturing and reorganizing low-profit businesses.

Second, the cured-meat sausage business has been presented with an annual sales target of about KRW 6 billion, and total manufacturing sales of about KRW 36 billion. Third, the company plans to expand direct sourcing of raw materials to lower costs and adopt a profit-and-loss-focused management system.

For the cured-meat line, the company said it took over part of CJ CheilJedang's equipment, added only minimal investment, and has already started production. Over the medium to long term, it cites vertical integration and overseas market entry as goals.

These are company plans, and whether they are achieved can only be judged once they show up in full-year results. Third-quarter 2026 sales of KRW 19.7 billion were close to the second quarter's KRW 20 billion, so the contribution of the new line is not yet clearly visible in quarterly sales.

Operating profit fell from KRW 802 million in the second quarter to KRW 117 million in the third, so whether margin improvement can be sustained is the next question to track. The company also said it will expand regular investor relations activities.

Bull factors

A clearer restructuring direction

On June 16, 2026, the company disclosed the Value-Up Plan 2026, built on four pillars: portfolio upgrade, profit-focused operations, medium-term growth base and stronger investor communication. It includes shifting from distribution toward processing and manufacturing and reorganizing low-profit businesses. Cost reduction through direct raw material sourcing is also part of the plan.

Cured-meat sausage production started

Manufacturing equipment for cured-meat sausage is complete and production has begun, with an annual target of about 1,500 tons. The sales target is about KRW 6 billion. The company says it limited upfront investment by using existing equipment and staff.

Quarterly operating profit turned positive

Operating profit turned positive at KRW 802 million in the second quarter of 2026 and was KRW 117 million in the third quarter. Third-quarter profit attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 1.54 billion swung to a profit from a year earlier. Quarterly results have moved out of the losses recorded from the third quarter of 2025.

Bear factors

Shrinking annual sales and profit

Sales fell from KRW 88.4 billion in 2023 to KRW 70 billion in 2025. The operating margin dropped from 1.9% in 2023 to -2.2% in 2025. In 2025 the company posted an operating loss of KRW 1.56 billion and a net loss attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 3.34 billion.

Weaker capital structure and cash flow

The debt-to-equity ratio rose from 164.1% in 2024 to 219.8% in 2025. Total equity at the end of 2025 was KRW 14.1 billion against total liabilities of KRW 31 billion. Operating cash flow swung from KRW 5.22 billion in 2024 to -KRW 2.1 billion in 2025.

Size and quality of quarterly profit

Third-quarter operating profit was KRW 117 million, down 85.4% from the prior quarter. Profit attributable to controlling shareholders over the last four quarters totaled only KRW 76.86 million.

Because third-quarter net profit far exceeds operating profit, quarterly results could shift sharply depending on one-off items.

Risk factors

Listing requirements and market alerts

The company was designated a managed stock effective July 9, 2026, for failing the market-cap threshold. To stay listed, it must keep market capitalization at KRW 20 billion or more for 45 consecutive trading days.

On August 24, 2026, it was designated an investment-caution stock after the top 10 accounts' sell involvement reached 56.15%.

Financial burden

At the end of 2025, total liabilities of KRW 31 billion exceeded total equity of KRW 14.1 billion, and the debt-to-equity ratio was 219.8%. Operating cash flow was -KRW 2.1 billion in 2025. With thin earnings, debt costs are exposed directly to swings in results.

Customer concentration and cost variables

Fish-based and cured-meat sausage production is centered on contract work for CJ CheilJedang, so it is sensitive to order changes. Livestock distribution depends on overseas suppliers such as Cargill Korea. A strong exchange rate and lower fish catches could raise costs.
