Report overview

Post-Sale Liquidation Path; Payout Timeline Is Key

Koramco THE ONE REIT sold its only asset, the Hana Securities Building, in June 2026 and has moved into a special dividend and liquidation process. The payout and liquidation timeline now matters more than the 2022-2025 reported earnings when reading the share price.

02

Business structure

Koramco THE ONE REIT is an externally managed office REIT run by Koramco Asset Trust, and it listed in March 2022. Unlike most REITs that grew through asset purchases and capital raises, it held only one asset after listing, the Hana Securities Building in Yeouido.

The building was acquired in 2020 for KRW 493.2 billion. Main tenants included Hana Securities, Hana Financial Group affiliates, 3M and Intel. Hana Securities extended its lease for five years through December 2030 in May 2025.

With a single-asset structure, revenue was driven mainly by rent from this building, and a breakdown of revenue by segment was not available. Funding rested on a KRW 292.0 billion secured loan raised in November 2020 at a 2.55% fixed rate with a five-year term.

The building was sold on June 9, 2026 for KRW 811.2 billion, leaving the REIT with no assets, and the asset manager opted for shareholder payouts and liquidation rather than reinvestment.

04

Earnings analysis

On reported figures, revenue rose from KRW 6.89 billion in 2022 to KRW 7.14 billion in 2023 (up 3.6% year on year), KRW 7.42 billion in 2024 (up 3.9%) and KRW 7.57 billion in 2025 (up 2.0%), a third consecutive annual increase starting from 2023. Among 2022-2025, 2025 was the largest revenue year and 2022 the smallest.

Operating profit was KRW 4.53 billion in 2022, KRW 4.78 billion in 2023 (up 5.5%) and KRW 5.14 billion in 2024 (up 7.6%), then fell 0.8% to KRW 5.1 billion in 2025. The operating margin moved from 65.7% to 66.9% and 69.3%, then eased to 67.4% in 2025.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders grew from KRW 2.6 billion in 2022 to KRW 3.3 billion in 2024, then fell 10.6% to KRW 2.95 billion in 2025. The net income decline was larger than the operating profit decline, which suggests heavier costs below the operating line.

Operating cash flow rose from KRW 1.44 billion in 2022 to KRW 3.89 billion in 2024 and was KRW 3.6 billion in 2025, while at the end of 2025 the debt ratio was 156.8%, total equity KRW 197.4 billion and total liabilities KRW 309.6 billion.

Periodic reports for 2026 quarters have not yet been filed, and no preliminary results were found in searches. The asset manager estimates a sale gain of about KRW 318.0 billion from the June 2026 sale, a one-off item unlike the 2022-2025 earnings trend.

05

Industry analysis

Koramco Asset Trust has said that total domestic commercial real estate transactions hit a record KRW 34 trillion in 2025, while performance gaps by asset type are widening.

Seoul commercial and office transaction volume exceeded KRW 2.8 trillion in June 2026, and the Hana Securities Building deal was the largest single transaction, at about KRW 38.4 million per pyeong, a record for the Yeouido district.

The reported reasoning for liquidating rather than reinvesting is that office prices in prime Seoul districts remain high and rate volatility makes it hard to secure the previous level of returns. Other listed REITs made different choices around the same time.

Some REITs reinvested or repaid borrowings after asset sales, while Koramco Life Infra REIT and ESR Kendall Square REIT expanded asset recycling. Koramco Life Infra REIT has a similar name but is a separate listed company.

Commentators have described the case as both a successful exit and a sign of the limits of the perpetual-REIT model. The company's position in the sector has shifted from rental competition to the pace and size of payouts.

06

Outlook

The confirmed timetable is staged. The asset manager said it would discuss the liquidation agenda at an October board meeting and pursue the special dividend payment and a shareholders' meeting in November.

Residual assets are then to be distributed after creditor protection procedures and cost settlement, completing liquidation. The target for completing liquidation is February 2027.

One report says the special dividend is paid first and dissolution follows, because distributing residual assets in one go after dissolution could create a corporate tax burden.

The ex-dividend date tied to the end-August record date has already passed, and the asset manager told investors that economic value after the ex-dividend date could converge to the estimated residual asset distribution. The payout amount may change depending on the final accounts and audit results.

There is no plan to acquire new assets or run a new leasing business, so what matters is whether the dissolution resolution and distribution steps proceed on schedule rather than any earnings outlook.

08

Bull factors

A single-asset investment fully exited

The KRW 811.2 billion sale price is KRW 318.0 billion above the 2020 purchase price of KRW 493.2 billion. It is regarded as a rare case that completed the full cycle from investment and operation to exit. The asset manager chose to return the proceeds to shareholders without reinvestment risk.

A stable rental income base

On reported figures, revenue rose for a third consecutive year from 2023 through 2025. The operating margin stayed between 65.7% and 69.3% throughout 2022-2025. Operating cash flow also rose from KRW 1.44 billion in 2022 to KRW 3.6 billion in 2025, showing that the rental business kept generating cash.

Timeline and risks disclosed upfront

The asset manager communicated the ex-dividend risk and timetable in advance through a shareholder letter and website notices. The dissolution process is laid out step by step: special dividend payment, creditor protection and residual asset distribution. Investors have clear points to monitor, which keeps information asymmetry relatively small.

09

Bear factors

Dissolution and payout not yet final

Dissolution is not final because it requires a special resolution at a shareholders' meeting. The payout amount may also change depending on the final accounts and audit results. Cost settlement and tax review could make the final distribution differ from the estimate.

Post ex-dividend value tied to residual assets

The asset manager said that after the ex-dividend date the economic value of the shares in principle converges to the residual assets to be distributed. With no new asset purchases or rental business, there is no source of income beyond the distribution. The longer the process takes, the larger the relative effect of costs and delays could be.

2025 profit slowdown and rising debt ratio

Operating profit in 2025 was KRW 5.1 billion, down 0.8% year on year, and the operating margin eased from 69.3% to 67.4%. Net income attributable to controlling shareholders fell 10.6% to KRW 2.95 billion. The debt ratio kept rising from 150.8% in 2022 to 156.8% in 2025.

10

Risk factors

Process and timing risk

Several steps remain, including the special resolution on dissolution, creditor protection procedures and the final accounts and audit. A delay at any step could push distributions later than the February 2027 target. The timing of delisting and any trading halt must also be confirmed through future filings.

Tax and cost risk

Paying the special dividend first was chosen because a lump-sum distribution after dissolution could create a corporate tax burden. The reported per-share payout estimate was a figure before cost settlement and tax review. If final costs exceed expectations, the residual asset distribution could shrink.

Supply-demand and liquidity risk

If the REIT is removed from major indexes, ETFs that track those indexes may sell their holdings. As liquidation approaches, fewer participants could trade and price volatility could rise. Further share price swings may also occur during the delisting process.

12

Overall view

Koramco THE ONE REIT completed its exit in June 2026 by selling its single asset for KRW 811.2 billion, and it is pursuing a special dividend and liquidation based on an estimated sale gain of about KRW 318.0 billion.

In the 2022-2025 reported results, revenue rose for a third consecutive year from 2023 and the operating margin stayed between 65.7% and 69.3%. In 2025, however, operating profit fell 0.8%, net income attributable to controlling shareholders fell 10.6% and the debt ratio rose to 156.8%.

These past results are hard to treat as a direct basis for the share price now that the business is winding down.

The bull side points to the completed exit and the concrete payout schedule, while the bear side points to the unconfirmed dissolution, possible changes in the payout from costs and taxes, and supply-demand pressure. The P/B and dividend yield on screen may predate the sale and are hard to use on their own.

The next points to check are the November 2026 shareholders' meeting, the confirmed dividend, the residual asset distribution timing and delisting notices, with the judgment left to the reader.
