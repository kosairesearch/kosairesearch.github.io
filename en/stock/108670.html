Report overview

Q3 Loss Puts Building Materials Recovery to the Test

LX Hausys posted a sharp rebound in operating profit in the first half of 2026 but swung to a controlling-shareholder net loss in Q3. The key question is whether the housing-market recovery can outpace raw-material costs.

02

Business structure

LX Hausys runs two main businesses: building materials and automotive materials and films. The building-materials core consists of windows, flooring, wallpaper and PF insulation, sold through B2B contract channels to construction companies and through B2C channels to consumers.

In a January 2025 report, Mirae Asset Securities expected B2B results such as windows and insulation to decline as new housing sales weakened. In 2026, the recovery in housing transactions lifted B2C shipments, and a higher B2C share in windows, flooring and wallpaper was cited as improving product mix.

MMA, the raw material for artificial marble, is also a key cost factor for building-materials margins. The automotive materials and films business supplies automotive fabrics and decorative and industrial films; in Q1 2026, new car launches and export-customer diversification for industrial film lifted sales.

Diversifying overseas customers for decorative, interior and appliance films was viewed as a positive factor. Raw materials are mainly petrochemical products such as PVC, plasticizers and phenol, so the business is exposed to oil and chemical feedstock prices.

In building materials, the company competes with other specialized window and flooring makers and is directly affected by construction activity. Red Dot Design Award 2026 recognized its products in April 2026.

Overall, the B2C expansion in building materials and overseas diversification in automotive and film are the two factors reducing earnings volatility.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual revenue fell from KRW 3.61 trillion in 2022 to KRW 3.53 trillion in 2023 (down 2.4% year on year), recovered to KRW 3.57 trillion in 2024 (up 1.3%), and dropped to KRW 3.18 trillion in 2025 (down 11.0%). The largest year of 2022–2025 was 2022.

Operating profit rose from KRW 14.9 billion in 2022 to a peak of KRW 109.8 billion in 2023 (up 635.1%), then fell to KRW 97.5 billion in 2024 (down 11.3%) and KRW 13.1 billion in 2025 (down 86.6%), decreasing for two consecutive years from 2024 through 2025.

The operating margin moved from 3.1% in 2023 to 2.7% in 2024 and 0.4% in 2025. In a January 2026 report, Mirae Asset Securities cited weak results in premium building-materials products and the impact of 2025 property and lending curbs on new housing sales.

Controlling-shareholder net income swung from a loss of KRW 117.1 billion in 2022 to a profit of KRW 61.8 billion in 2023, then KRW 44.3 billion in 2024 (down 28.3%) and a loss of KRW 43.9 billion in 2025. Total net income including non-controlling interests was a loss of KRW 43.9 billion in 2025.

Operating cash flow was KRW 195.3 billion in 2025, up from KRW 184 billion in 2024, so cash generation held up despite the lower operating profit. Quarterly, Q3 2025 revenue was KRW 812.7 billion with operating profit of KRW 22.1 billion and controlling-shareholder net income of KRW 13.3 billion.

Q4 2025 revenue was KRW 765.2 billion, down 5.8% from the prior quarter, with an operating loss of KRW 28.9 billion and a controlling-shareholder net loss of KRW 54.7 billion.

Q1 2026 revenue was KRW 814.7 billion and operating profit was KRW 45.9 billion, a swing to profit helped by a 22% year-on-year rise in housing transactions and lower raw-material input prices.

Q2 2026 revenue was KRW 939.7 billion, up 15.3% quarter on quarter, with operating profit of KRW 54.9 billion and controlling-shareholder net income of KRW 47.1 billion.

Q3 2026 revenue was KRW 805.3 billion, down 14.3% quarter on quarter and 0.9% year on year, with operating profit of KRW 8.39 billion, down 84.7% quarter on quarter and 62.0% year on year, and a controlling-shareholder net loss of KRW 11.7 billion against net income of KRW 13.3 billion a year earlier.

Combined operating profit for Q1 and Q2 exceeded KRW 100 billion, but the sharp Q3 decline makes the durability of the recovery dependent on results from Q4 onward.

05

Industry analysis

Demand for building materials depends on two drivers: new housing starts and sales, and housing transactions. In a May 2026 report, Hana Securities said B2B building-materials sales were weak in Q1 2026 due to slow starts and presales, while B2C benefited from higher shipments tied to rising transaction volumes.

The same report said starts remained weak but that the transaction rise was affecting earnings. Hyundai Motor Securities said in a September 2026 report that housing transactions in Q2 2026 rose year on year despite tighter regulation.

On policy, LS Securities said in a May 2026 report that the first-half transaction increase was a temporary spillover under continuing government demand curbs, making a structural turnaround difficult.

On cycle position, Hyundai Motor Securities said in May 2026 that operating profit had bottomed in 2025 and was recovering gradually. On inputs, Mirae Asset Securities said in May 2026 that after the Iran conflict, PVC, MMA and logistics costs rose, making pass-through to selling prices the key margin variable.

Competitively, LX Hausys' B2B contract business depends on construction-company orders and is therefore exposed to weak starts, while a larger B2C share ties earnings more closely to housing transactions.

Whether the margin gains reflect an industry recovery or temporary transaction growth will depend on starts data and B2C shipment trends.

Outlook

Sell-side estimates published in May 2026 pointed to full-year 2026 operating profit above KRW 100 billion. Mirae Asset Securities projected KRW 107 billion and a second-half-weighted profit pattern, naming pass-through of raw-material prices as the key margin variable.

Hana Securities projected KRW 110 billion, and Hyundai Motor Securities projected KRW 107.9 billion. Combined Q1 and Q2 operating profit exceeded KRW 100 billion and approached that range, but with Q3 operating profit at KRW 8.39 billion, reaching the full-year level now depends on Q4 results.

LS Securities assumed in May 2026 that higher prices for phenol, PVC and plasticizers, reflected with a lag, would weigh on second-half results.

In a September 2026 report, Hyundai Motor Securities said building-materials sales could slow temporarily in the second half on loan-rate, material and freight cost increases, while non-building-material sales were supporting results.

The same report flagged the possibility of dividends resuming after the return to profit. The next scheduled regulatory filing is the 2026 annual report, which will fix full-year operating margin and controlling-shareholder net income.

The Q4 2026 preliminary results will be compared with the KRW 28.9 billion operating loss and KRW 54.7 billion controlling-shareholder net loss of Q4 2025. Results for Q1 2027 should show how much raw-material cost increases were passed through to selling prices.

08

Bull factors

First-half profit recovery

Operating profit was KRW 45.9 billion in Q1 2026, a swing from an operating loss in the prior quarter, and rose to KRW 54.9 billion in Q2, up 19.7% from Q1. The two quarters combined exceed KRW 100 billion, far above the KRW 13.1 billion full-year 2025 figure. Analysts linked the Q1 rebound to a 22% rise in housing transactions that restored B2C shipments.

B2C mix and housing-transaction rebound

A higher B2C share in windows, flooring and wallpaper was cited as improving product mix. Hyundai Motor Securities said in a September 2026 report that Q2 housing transactions rose year on year and that a strong B2C performance lifted results.

Because B2C sales track housing activity, bulls argue that continued transaction recovery leaves room for further improvement in operating metrics.

Non-building materials and overseas film diversification

Hyundai Motor Securities said in a September 2026 report that Q2 results were driven by a strong B2C performance and higher non-building-material sales.

The decorative, interior and appliance film business is broadening its overseas customer base, and export-customer diversification for industrial film contributed to Q1 2026 sales growth. Dividend resumption was raised as a possibility if profitability continues.

Bear factors

Swing to a Q3 loss

Q3 2026 operating profit was KRW 8.39 billion, down 84.7% from the prior quarter and 62.0% from a year earlier. Controlling-shareholder results swung to a net loss of KRW 11.7 billion from net income of KRW 13.3 billion in the prior-year quarter.

Because the Q2 U.S. tariff refund was a temporary item, the underlying earnings power will become clearer only with results from Q4 onward.

Raw-material and logistics cost pressure

In a May 2026 report, Mirae Asset Securities cited rising prices of key petrochemical inputs such as PVC and MMA, along with higher logistics costs, as reasons full-year profit would be weighted toward the first half.

LS Securities assumed in the same period that higher prices for phenol, PVC and plasticizers would weigh on second-half results. How much of the cost increase can be passed on to selling prices is cited as the decisive margin variable.

Weak starts and a possibly temporary transaction rebound

Hana Securities said in a May 2026 report that housing starts remained weak. LS Securities viewed the first-half increase in transactions as a temporary spillover under continuing government curbs and judged that a structural turnaround would be difficult.

Controlling-shareholder net income across the last four quarters totals only KRW 11.2 billion, leaving a thin earnings base.

10

Risk factors

Raw-material and logistics prices

Petrochemical raw-material prices such as PVC, MMA, phenol and plasticizers move with oil prices and Middle East developments. If higher input costs cannot be passed through to selling prices, margins fall and quarterly earnings become more volatile. A stronger U.S. dollar supports decorative-film exports but can also raise the cost of imported inputs.

Housing policy and interest rates

Housing-transaction policy, including the expiry of the suspension of heavier capital-gains tax on multiple-home owners, shapes sales volumes. Hyundai Motor Securities said in a September 2026 report that loan-rate burdens could rise in the second half, which could temporarily slow building-materials sales. The recovery's dependence on policy effects is cited as a limit on its durability.

Balance sheet and dividends

The debt-to-equity ratio was 160.1% at the end of 2025, with total liabilities of KRW 1.34 trillion. All KRW 834.2 billion of total equity is attributable to controlling shareholders. No dividend is being paid after the 2025 net loss of KRW 43.9 billion, and continued losses could delay any resumption. Operating cash flow of KRW 195.3 billion in 2025 was large relative to operating profit.

12

Overall view

LX Hausys' earnings fell sharply from the 2023 operating-profit peak through 2025, then recovered in the first half of 2026 on housing-transaction growth and a better B2C mix.

However, Q3 2026 swung to an operating profit of KRW 8.39 billion and a controlling-shareholder net loss of KRW 11.7 billion, so the durability of the recovery remains unproven.

Bullish factors are the housing-transaction rebound, B2C expansion, overseas diversification in non-building materials and films, and the possibility of dividends resuming after a return to profit.

Bearish factors are cost pressure from PVC, MMA and logistics, a possible temporary slowdown in building-materials sales from higher second-half loan rates, weak housing starts, and a thin earnings base with controlling-shareholder net income of KRW 11.2 billion across the last four quarters.

All broker reports identified since May 2026 carried Buy ratings, and Hyundai Motor Securities maintained a KRW 50,000 target price in September 2026. The debt-to-equity ratio fell to 160.1% at the end of 2025 from 218.5% in 2022, but leverage relative to equity remains high.

The next checkpoints are the Q4 2026 preliminary results, the full-year operating margin and dividend decision in the 2026 annual report, and the extent of raw-material pass-through in Q1 2027.
