02

Business structure

Sawnics was founded in 2000 as an RF filter specialist that began by designing and producing SAW filters. From 2015, as 5G smartphones demanded higher performance, it shifted toward RF filter foundry services, meaning contract manufacturing for customer designs.

Corporate information services describe it as the only RF filter foundry in Korea with mass-production technology for high-performance TF-SAW.

TF-SAW is a piezoelectric semiconductor built on silicon wafers and is described as a next-generation technology that improves high-frequency performance over conventional SAW filters.

Its customers are fabless communications chip companies in the United States and Greater China, and the company says it has secured more than 20 such customer relationships.

Since 2017 it has co-developed TF-SAW with a global communications chip company, and it has reportedly received strategic equipment investment from a US communications chip company and an equity stake from a Taiwanese SAW device maker.

Alongside foundry work, it runs a brand business that manufactures and sells RF filters for industrial wireless equipment.

Its new frontier is photonics foundry, which handles optical signals; in January 2025 it signed an MOU with Switzerland's LXT on mass-production foundry for TFLN-based photonic integrated circuits (PIC).

In February 2026, a foundry supply contract and strategic partnership with China's NEWSONIC for Si-SAW technology was reported and cited as a driver of the share price.

Unlike large filter design and manufacturing companies, its contract-manufacturing model suggests that the size of individual orders and customer concentration weigh heavily on earnings swings.

04

Earnings analysis

On a disclosed annual basis, revenue was KRW 15.8 billion in 2022, KRW 13.8 billion in 2023 (down 12.8% year on year), KRW 17 billion in 2024 (up 22.9% year on year), and KRW 18.2 billion in 2025 (up 7.2% year on year).

After the 2023 low, revenue grew in both 2024 and 2025, and 2025 was the largest year and 2023 the smallest among 2022 to 2025.

Over the same period, operating losses were KRW 3.4 billion in 2022, KRW 5.17 billion in 2023, KRW 4.54 billion in 2024, and KRW 6.66 billion in 2025, marking four consecutive years of operating losses from 2022 to 2025.

The operating margin moved from -21.5% in 2022 to -37.4% in 2023, improved to -26.7% in 2024, and deteriorated again to -36.6% in 2025. In 2025 revenue rose while the operating loss widened from KRW 4.54 billion to KRW 6.66 billion, so top-line growth did not translate into a smaller loss.

In the third quarter of 2025, a securities research note attributed the wider loss to weak demand from downstream customers and technology development spending.

The controlling-shareholder net loss shrank from KRW 29.5 billion in 2022 to KRW 5.12 billion in 2023 and KRW 2.24 billion in 2024, then widened to KRW 6.9 billion in 2025. Net income including non-controlling interests was disclosed at the same amounts for each year.

Quarterly revenue was KRW 4.42 billion in Q3 2025, KRW 4.7 billion in Q4 2025, KRW 4.4 billion in Q1 2026, KRW 5.87 billion in Q2 2026, and KRW 5.71 billion in Q3 2026. Q2 2026 revenue rose 33.4% from the prior quarter, and Q3 2026 revenue fell 2.6% quarter on quarter but grew 29.2% year on year.

The operating loss widened from KRW 1.67 billion in Q3 2025 to KRW 1.87 billion in Q4 2025, then narrowed to KRW 1.82 billion in Q1 2026, KRW 1.3 billion in Q2 2026, and KRW 1.09 billion in Q3 2026, and the company has posted losses for five consecutive quarters from Q3 2025 through Q3 2026.

By contrast, the controlling-shareholder net loss widened to KRW 2.8 billion in Q3 2026, well above the operating loss. The non-operating items behind this gap can be checked in the detailed notes of the next periodic report. The controlling-shareholder net loss over the last four quarters totaled KRW 6.47 billion.

At the end of 2025, total equity was KRW 34.9 billion, all attributable to controlling shareholders, and total liabilities were KRW 17.6 billion. The debt ratio rose from 60.1% in 2022 to 32.3% in 2023, 35.8% in 2024, and 50.5% in 2025.

Operating cash flow was KRW 421 million in 2022, -KRW 1.38 billion in 2023, KRW 1.18 billion in 2024, and -KRW 1.24 billion in 2025.

05

Industry analysis

Sawnics's demand base is RF filters used in communications devices such as 5G smartphones. A securities research note said the replacement of 4G phones with 5G phones tripled the number of RF filters per device and sharply increased demand for high-performance filters.

The company is moving its process from 4-inch SAW to 6-inch TF-SAW, and it says 6-inch TF-SAW filters outperform 4-inch SAW in performance and process difficulty. A corporate information site summary projects a 19% compound annual growth rate for TF-SAW through 2029.

Looking at the broader chip market, the WSTS outlook cited by the SK hynix newsroom projects global semiconductor sales growing more than 25% in 2026 to roughly USD 975 billion.

However, that growth is centered on memory driven by AI server demand, so its overlap with Sawnics's communications RF filter demand is limited. Weak demand from downstream customers is also cited as a risk factor in securities research.

On competition, Sawnics presents itself as the only RF filter foundry in Korea, but its technology and price competition against overseas filter makers will depend on how quickly it completes the move to 6-inch processes and on its ability to win customers.

06

Outlook

The core of the company's expansion is photonics foundry. On July 30, 2026, Sawnics said it had been selected as a joint research and development institution for a national program on next-generation optical computing semiconductors, led by KAIST with DGIST and NTT Research participating.

The program will receive a total of KRW 9 billion in government funding through 2030. In the second research phase starting in 2029, Sawnics plans to handle optical computing chip fabrication and prototype implementation using TFLN and TFLT-based PIC processes.

The stated goal is to convert research results into mass-production processes, which means meaningful revenue contribution is tied to the post-2029 phase.

Based on related article headlines, the company also unveiled a 30GHz millimeter-wave filter at IMS 2026 and was selected as an execution institution for a government program on high-performance quantum foundry ecosystem development.

On the earnings side, the next checkpoint is whether quarterly revenue holds near the KRW 5.87 billion in Q2 2026 and KRW 5.71 billion in Q3 2026. On financing, the KRW 10 billion private convertible bond decided on October 29 was withdrawn on November 9.

The company disclosed that it will re-pursue the issue after articles of incorporation amendments, but it has not given a timing or new issue terms. The re-issuance disclosure and any articles change are therefore the key items to watch for the financing path.

Risk factors

Downstream demand and customer dependence

Securities research cites weaker downstream demand as a risk factor. Customers are concentrated among overseas fabless companies in the United States and Greater China, so order changes from a single customer can quickly show up in quarterly revenue. The contract-manufacturing structure amplifies this volatility.

Funding and financial structure

The KRW 10 billion convertible bond decided in late October 2026 was withdrawn on November 9. The company says it will re-pursue the issue after amending its articles, but the terms remain undetermined.

Total equity is KRW 34.9 billion, yet the debt ratio rose to 50.5% in 2025, so financing costs could weigh more heavily.

Timing risk of new business

Under the government program, the photonics and optical AI chip work moves into its second research phase from 2029, and research results must still be converted into mass-production processes. Revenue from these new areas will take time to arrive, so the current loss structure could persist for a while.
