02

Business structure

Lotte Wellfood is a diversified food company spanning confectionery, ice cream and packaged food, and its results can be read through domestic operations and overseas units. Domestic third-quarter 2026 sales were KRW 918.2 billion, down 1.7%, while operating profit was KRW 69.4 billion, up 10.3%.

Within the domestic business, biscuit and confectionery sales rose 4.6% to KRW 296.9 billion, while ice cream sales fell 7.4% to KRW 225.3 billion. Overseas units posted third-quarter sales of KRW 271 billion, up 16.1%, and operating profit of KRW 23.1 billion, up 137.8%.

The overseas core is India and Kazakhstan, where Choco Pie is the main growth product and India sales rose 22.6% to KRW 90.5 billion in the third quarter.

Per press reports, Indian sales grew about 20% a year over the past three years, passed KRW 100 billion in annual sales last year, and the brand holds a market share above 70%. The company operates six plants in India and has set a target of KRW 1 trillion in Indian unit sales by 2032.

Domestically, Lotte Wellfood closed its Natuur store business and shifted to selling pints, cups and bars through existing retail channels. It is the only confectionery company in the group holding a dairy brand, Pasteur, and it has expanded interest in home meal replacement (HMR) products. The company is trimming low-efficiency items and channels to reshape the portfolio around profitability.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual sales rose from KRW 3.2 trillion in 2022 to KRW 4.07 trillion in 2023 (up 26.9%), then eased to KRW 4.04 trillion in 2024 (down 0.5%) before reaching KRW 4.22 trillion in 2025 (up 4.2%), making 2025 the largest year of the 2022–2025 span.

Operating profit peaked at KRW 177 billion in 2023 (up 57.5%) and then fell 11.3% to KRW 157.1 billion in 2024 and 30.3% to KRW 109.5 billion in 2025, a two-year decline, while the operating margin slid from 4.4% in 2023 to 2.6% in 2025.

In 2025 sales grew 4.2% but operating profit fell 30.3%, so top-line expansion did not carry through to margins.

Profit attributable to owners of the parent climbed from KRW 43.9 billion in 2022 to KRW 84.6 billion in 2024 before falling 14.6% to KRW 72.3 billion in 2025, and 2025 net income of KRW 71.4 billion was below that attributable figure.

Operating cash flow peaked at KRW 453 billion in 2023 and then declined to KRW 285.6 billion in 2024 and KRW 231.1 billion in 2025.

Quarterly results show operating profit of KRW 69.3 billion in the third quarter of 2025 turning into an operating loss of KRW 10.5 billion in the fourth quarter, followed by a return to profit at KRW 35.8 billion in the first quarter of 2026, then KRW 64.7 billion in the second quarter (up 80.8% sequentially) and KRW 89.9 billion in the third (up 38.9%), a two-quarter run of gains.

Profit attributable to owners of the parent swung from a KRW 17.8 billion loss in the fourth quarter of 2025 to KRW 17.3 billion profit in the first quarter of 2026, KRW 37.3 billion in the second and KRW 50.8 billion in the third, and the last four quarters total KRW 87.7 billion.

Sales bottomed at KRW 1.02 trillion in the fourth quarter of 2025 and rose for three consecutive quarters through the third quarter of 2026.

The third-quarter margin of 7.7% compares with 6.0% a year earlier (preliminary, announced November 11, 2026), and overseas operating profit of KRW 23.1 billion was reported at roughly a quarter of group operating profit.

Cumulative operating profit for the first three quarters reached KRW 190.4 billion, up 58.7% (preliminary, announced November 11, 2026).

05

Industry analysis

Demand in domestic food markets is diverging by category. Domestic ice cream sales fell 7.4% to KRW 225.3 billion in the third quarter of 2026, and press reports tied this to a contraction in the domestic ice cream market and the pruning of low-efficiency items.

Domestic biscuit and confectionery sales rose 4.6% to KRW 296.9 billion in the same quarter, which makes this category the more resilient profit anchor at home. On costs, reports cited Middle East conflict-related uncertainty and pressure from key raw materials.

The company has pointed to production network optimization and logistics and procurement improvements as its response. In cycle terms, domestic profit is currently being lifted by cost efficiency rather than by a sales recovery.

Competitively, Lotte Wellfood is the only confectionery company in its group holding a dairy brand, and press reports put its Indian Choco Pie market share above 70%. Widening the product mix into HMR and dairy and processed meat is a move to reduce reliance on ice cream.

Capacity expansion in India links to the 2032 target, so utilization and local sales competition are the variables to track. Market share and competitive intensity in domestic HMR and dairy are not yet disclosed in detail and can be checked in the segment disclosures of the next periodic report.

06

Outlook

The mid-to-long-term goal Lotte Wellfood unveiled in early September 2026 is to become a global comprehensive food company that connects people through the joy of delicious food.

The strategy rests on two pillars: strengthening core competitiveness to improve domestic operations, and sustaining high growth in global operations.

Domestically, the company is pursuing production network optimization, logistics and procurement innovation, data-driven management, and brand-focused marketing with portfolio management.

Overseas, it aims to consolidate its position in key markets such as India and Kazakhstan and to build core global brands including Pepero.

It also plans to deepen cooperation among Korean and Japanese Lotte food affiliates around a recently launched Singapore joint venture and to widen its overseas distribution network. The one numeric target the company has publicly cited is KRW 1 trillion in Indian unit sales by 2032.

For the first half, press reports put consolidated sales at KRW 2.18 trillion and operating profit at KRW 100.5 billion, up 7% and 98% year on year, with overseas sales up 23%. For the fourth quarter, the company plans to expand export mainstream channels and push domestic seasonal marketing.

The next checkpoint is whether the profit run that began in the first quarter of 2026 holds through the fourth-quarter preliminary results.

08

Bull factors

Rapid growth at overseas units

In Q3 2026, overseas subsidiaries' operating profit rose 137.8% to KRW 23.1 billion, and cumulative overseas operating profit for Q1–Q3 increased 105.4% to KRW 64.7 billion. India sales grew 22.6% to KRW 90.5 billion in Q3, and media reports say Choco Pie's market share there exceeds 70%.

The company set a target of KRW 1 trillion in sales for its India subsidiary by 2032. If overseas growth continues, it could become a pillar supporting consolidated operating profit.

Quarterly profit recovery

Operating profit rose for two consecutive quarters in 2026, from KRW 35.8 billion in Q1 to KRW 64.7 billion in Q2 and KRW 89.9 billion in Q3. The Q3 operating margin was 7.7%, up from 6.0% a year earlier (preliminary).

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders also rose from KRW 17.3 billion in Q1 to KRW 50.8 billion in Q3, bringing the sum of the last four quarters to KRW 87.7 billion. The fact that profitability has continued for a third straight quarter since the loss-making Q4 2025 is the basis for the bullish view.

Domestic profitability push

In the domestic business, Q3 sales fell 1.7% to KRW 918.2 billion, but operating profit rose 10.3% to KRW 69.4 billion. The company is cutting its cost structure by shutting down the Natuur store business and cleaning up low-efficiency products and sales channels.

Production network optimization and logistics and procurement innovation were presented as tasks for improving profitability. It is also a positive that dry confectionery sales grew 4.6%, giving the domestic business a relatively strong segment.

09

Bear factors

Domestic sales and ice cream weakness

Domestic sales fell 1.7% to KRW 918.2 billion in Q3 2026, and ice cream sales declined 7.4% to KRW 225.3 billion. Reports cited a shrinking domestic ice cream market and the cleanup of low-efficiency products as the background.

Ending the Natuur store business reduces the cost burden but is also a factor shrinking the top line. If domestic sales keep falling, overseas subsidiaries will account for an even larger share of consolidated profit.

Profit volatility and a short recovery

Operating profit declined from a peak of KRW 177.0 billion in 2023 to KRW 109.5 billion in 2025, and the company posted an operating loss of KRW 10.5 billion in Q4 2025. Net income attributable to controlling shareholders also swung to a net loss of KRW 17.8 billion in the same quarter.

Profits have continued since Q1 2026, but the recovery period is only three quarters. How long the profitable trend lasts needs to be confirmed in the quarterly figures of the next periodic report.

Cost pressure and financial leverage

Reports pointed to international uncertainty from the Middle East war and the burden of key raw materials as headwinds for the earnings environment. If increases in raw material costs are not sufficiently reflected in selling prices, margin pressure could continue.

10

Risk factors

Raw material and cost risk

According to reports, higher oil prices and raw material price volatility caused by the Middle East war were mentioned as cost burdens. Overseas subsidiaries are affected not only by raw materials but also by local logistics and exchange rates.

Since the operating margin fell as low as 2.6% in 2025, profit could react sensitively to cost changes. The speed at which higher costs are passed through to selling prices is the key variable.

Concentration risk in overseas units

Overseas subsidiaries' operating profit in Q3 was reported to be about one-quarter of total operating profit. Growth is concentrated in a single product, Choco Pie in India, and in two regions, India and Kazakhstan. If local competition intensifies or demand slows, profit volatility could increase. As India production capacity is being expanded, managing the utilization rate is also a task.

Balance sheet risk

At the end of 2025, total liabilities were KRW 2,287.3 billion, almost the same size as total equity of KRW 2,286.7 billion. The debt-to-equity ratio rose from 94.4% in 2022 to 100.0% in 2025. Operating cash flow fell from KRW 453.0 billion in 2023 to KRW 231.1 billion in 2025.

Along with whether the profit recovery lasts, cash generation capacity and debt management should be watched together.

12

Overall view

Lotte Wellfood's third-quarter 2026 results showed sales of KRW 1.17 trillion (up 1.5%) and operating profit of KRW 89.9 billion (up 29.7%), a quarter in which profit recovered.

The growth in profit is centered on overseas units in India and Kazakhstan, where third-quarter operating profit rose 137.8% to KRW 23.1 billion. Domestic sales fell 1.7% but domestic operating profit rose 10.3%, which shows up in the figures as the effect of the profitability-focused reshaping.

Against that, annual operating profit fell from KRW 177 billion in 2023 to KRW 109.5 billion in 2025, and the fourth quarter of 2025 posted an operating loss of KRW 10.5 billion. The debt-to-equity ratio rose to 100.0% in 2025 from 94.4% in 2022, and operating cash flow has declined since 2023.

Strengths are high overseas growth and a run of quarterly profit, while weaknesses are the domestic ice cream slump and raw material and balance-sheet pressure. The next checkpoints are the annual operating margin in the 2026 business report and overseas unit results in the first quarter of 2027.
