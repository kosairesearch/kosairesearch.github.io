02

Business structure

Seoam Machinery Industry is a member of the Hwacheon Group, controlled by major shareholder Hwacheon Machinery, alongside affiliates Hwacheon Machine Tool and Hwacheon Machinery.

Its core products are gears, chucks & cylinders, and Curvic couplings, supplying power-transmission parts widely used in machine tools, ship engines, air compressors, wind turbines, reducers, and railway vehicles.

The company's flagship gear segment is regarded as technologically strong, while the chuck & cylinder segment maintains a stable demand base.

It also has an intra-group relationship in which it performs casting work for parts supplied to Hwacheon Machine Tool and delivers certain components to both Hwacheon Machine Tool and Hwacheon Machinery, though affiliate transactions do not account for a large share of total revenue.

In June 2025 the company acquired a 90% stake in Japanese die-casting mold parts maker Suzuki Seiki and its affiliate Suzuki Seiki Busan for roughly KRW 7.6 billion, bringing them in as subsidiaries and adding a new mold-manufacturing business to diversify its portfolio.

The acquisition represented roughly 10% of total assets and about 12% of equity, a fairly significant commitment for the company.

Competitively, its long operating history since its 1978 founding underpins accumulated technical know-how in gears and power-transmission parts domestically, but results remain closely tied to order cycles in downstream machine tool, shipbuilding, and wind power industries.

As a KOSDAQ-listed company with a relatively small market capitalization and limited liquidity, it is in the process of building a growth story that is more independent of its parent group.

04

Earnings analysis

On an annual basis, revenue declined for three consecutive years from KRW 50.4 billion in 2022 to KRW 45.8 billion in 2023 and KRW 42.4 billion in 2024, before rebounding to KRW 46.5 billion in 2025.

Operating profit remained positive at KRW 1.61 billion in 2022 and KRW 614 million in 2023, turned into an operating loss of KRW 1.29 billion in 2024, and swung back to a profit of KRW 442 million in 2025.

Net income attributable to owners fell sharply from KRW 2.18 billion in 2022 and KRW 1.93 billion in 2023 to KRW 396 million in 2024, then recovered to KRW 1.19 billion in 2025, marking a substantial rebound from the 2024 trough.

The operating margin fell from 3.2% in 2022 to -3.1% in 2024 before improving to roughly breakeven at 0.1% in 2025.

The quarterly pattern shows even greater volatility: revenue of KRW 11.44 billion and operating profit of KRW 368 million in Q2 2025, followed by revenue of KRW 12.85 billion, operating profit of KRW 290 million, and owners' net income of KRW 864 million in Q4 2025, before swinging back to an operating loss of KRW 369 million on revenue of KRW 11.04 billion in Q1 2026.

Q2 2026 then posted the strongest recent quarter, with revenue of KRW 13.47 billion, operating profit of KRW 963 million, and owners' net income of KRW 937 million, underscoring a recurring up-and-down earnings pattern.

This volatility likely reflects a mix of one-off costs and consolidation effects tied to the Suzuki Seiki integration along with the irregular timing of downstream orders.

It should be noted that the Q1 and Q2 2026 figures are the most recently disclosed results and have not yet gone through full annual audit confirmation.

Industry analysis

Seoam Machinery Industry's downstream markets are diversified across machine tools, shipbuilding, air compressors, wind power, and railway vehicles, so it is not dependent on a single end market, though results are still influenced by capital-spending and order cycles in each industry.

Domestic machine tool demand tends to move in tandem with affiliates Hwacheon Machine Tool and Hwacheon Machinery, whose order conditions indirectly affect Seoam's parts orders.

Shipbuilding has seen a recovery in global orders and rising vessel prices in recent years, which could create a favorable backdrop for demand for marine gears and components.

On the nuclear front, the government's renewed push for new nuclear plants and small modular reactors (SMRs), together with high-profile order news such as the Czech Dukovany nuclear project, has broadened market attention toward nuclear and power-infrastructure related stocks.

However, this nuclear theme has largely centered on companies with direct nuclear equipment or EPC roles, such as Doosan Enerbility and KEPCO Engineering & Construction, and it is not clearly established through disclosures what specific role, if any, Seoam Machinery plays in this value chain.

The newly added Suzuki Seiki mold business belongs to a separate die-casting mold industry, which may follow a different supply-demand cycle than the existing gear and chuck & cylinder businesses.

As a small-cap KOSDAQ machinery parts stock with relatively low liquidity, price volatility tends to be driven more by individual news flow and supply-demand than by broad industry-cycle shifts.

Outlook

The Suzuki Seiki consolidation is believed to have begun materially affecting consolidated results from the second half of 2025, making it important to track how the mold business contributes to revenue and profitability once a full year of results is reflected.

Some financial information providers have noted that the Suzuki Seiki acquisition has broadened Seoam's portfolio, and that the Czech Dukovany nuclear project and SMR-related nuclear projects could serve as positive factors going forward, but it should be noted that this reflects market expectations rather than concrete contracts disclosed in the company's own filings.

Given the operating loss in Q1 2026 followed by improved results in Q2 2026, subsequent quarters will need to confirm whether this recovery is a one-off or a sustained trend.

A favorable order cycle in the company's core downstream industries of shipbuilding, wind power, and machine tools could support order stability in the gear and chuck & cylinder segments.

Conversely, a slowdown in machine tool demand at affiliates Hwacheon Machine Tool and Hwacheon Machinery, or additional costs arising from integrating the overseas Suzuki Seiki subsidiary, could reintroduce earnings volatility.

Bull factors

2025 Earnings Turnaround

Operating profit swung from a loss in 2024 to a profit in 2025, and net income also rose sharply year over year. Revenue likewise broke a three-year decline and returned to growth. Whether this improvement continues into 2026 and beyond remains to be observed.

Business Diversification via Suzuki Seiki Acquisition

In June 2025 the company acquired a 90% stake in Japanese die-casting mold maker Suzuki Seiki, adding mold manufacturing as a new business line. This represents an attempt to move beyond a single-business structure centered on gears and chucks & cylinders, potentially diversifying revenue sources. However, it will take time for the new business to translate into a stable profit contribution.

Attention from the Power/Nuclear Infrastructure Theme

Some financial information providers have cited the Czech Dukovany nuclear project and SMR-related issues as potential positive factors for Seoam Machinery.

Amid broadly heightened market attention toward domestic nuclear and power-infrastructure related stocks, there is room for thematic interest to flow into the name. However, this has not yet been confirmed through concrete company contracts or orders.

Over four years, revenue has continued to oscillate within the KRW 40-50 billion range. A clear trajectory of top-line growth has not yet been established.

Recurrence of a Quarterly Loss

After returning to profit in Q4 2025, the company posted another operating loss in Q1 2026. Results improved again in Q2, but the pattern of alternating profit and loss quarters has recurred. This suggests earnings stability has not yet been sufficiently secured.

Uncertain Substantive Link to the Nuclear Theme

Risk factors

Earnings Volatility

The operating margin has swung from 3.2% in 2022 to -3.1% in 2024 and back to 0.1% in 2025, indicating the profit structure has not yet stabilized. Quarterly results have also alternated between profit and loss, making predictability relatively low. On a small revenue base, one-off costs or shifts in order timing can materially affect results.

Overseas Subsidiary Integration Risk

Suzuki Seiki and Suzuki Seiki Busan are Japan-based entities, and the early integration phase could bring additional costs or risks related to currency fluctuations, accounting alignment, and local management oversight.

Since the acquisition represented roughly 12% of equity, any integration difficulties could have a relatively significant financial impact.

Small-Cap Liquidity and Supply-Demand Risk

As a small-cap KOSDAQ machinery parts stock, trading volume and liquidity may be limited, which can amplify price volatility in response to thematic news or shifts in supply and demand. This can result in price movements that diverge from underlying business performance.

Overall view

Seoam Machinery Industry showed clear improvement in 2025 with revenue growth, a swing to operating profit, and a recovery in net income, but quarterly earnings volatility remains high, as seen in the operating loss that recurred in Q1 2026.

As a member of the Hwacheon Group, the company has a stable business base supplying gears, chucks & cylinders, and Curvic couplings to diversified downstream industries including machine tools, shipbuilding, and wind power, and it further expanded its portfolio into mold manufacturing through the 2025 Suzuki Seiki acquisition.

Some financial information providers cite the Czech Dukovany nuclear and SMR projects as potential positive factors, but it should be noted that these remain at the level of market expectations rather than contracts confirmed through disclosures.

The price-to-book ratio appears to trade at a discount to net asset value, and the dividend yield runs below the sector average.

Going forward, the key variables for assessing the company's fundamentals will be Suzuki Seiki's earnings contribution, the stability of quarterly profits, and whether the nuclear-related theme materializes into concrete business.
