02

Business structure

Hanyang Securities operates four business segments: brokerage, proprietary trading, corporate finance, and other. Brokerage covers securities brokerage and intermediation, proprietary trading covers securities and derivatives trading, and corporate finance covers underwriting, M&A advisory and asset securitization.

In the first half of 2026, proprietary trading accounted for KRW 1.21 trillion of operating revenue, up sharply from KRW 318.7 billion a year earlier and now the center of total revenue.

Brokerage revenue rose to KRW 12.4 billion from KRW 3.7 billion, while corporate finance revenue fell to KRW 96.6 billion from KRW 115.1 billion.

The retail segment relies on commissions from equity trading and margin lending interest, and the company is expanding its product lineup with a revamped MTS, online special-offer RP and target-return funds.

In the bond and DCM business, Hanyang Securities ranked 11th in the industry with KRW 721 billion of lead-managed public corporate bonds (4 deals) through September 2026, and as of November 16, 2026 its lead-managed total stood at KRW 921 billion (5 deals) with a share of about 2.0%.

Project financing (PF) activity focuses on refinancing of high-quality assets and deals that minimize equity commitment. After KCGI became the largest shareholder in June 2025, the company restructured its portfolio under CEO Kim Byung-chul, and as of June 2026 KCGI's private equity vehicle held 29.59%.

Equity capital stood at KRW 669.5 billion at the end of June 2026, which places the company at mid-size relative to large brokerages, and it has set a vision of becoming a mid-sized securities firm with equity of at least KRW 1 trillion by 2030.

04

Earnings analysis

Based on reported results, operating profit was KRW 37.2 billion in 2022, KRW 46.3 billion in 2023 (up 24.5% year on year), KRW 54.4 billion in 2024 (up 17.6%), and KRW 75.3 billion in 2025 (up 38.3%), rising for three consecutive years from 2023 through 2025.

Over the same period, revenue fell from KRW 1,117.1 billion in 2022 to KRW 999.0 billion in 2023 (down 10.6% year on year) and KRW 833.3 billion in 2024 (down 16.6%), then turned around to KRW 909.0 billion in 2025 (up 9.1%). Of 2022 to 2025, revenue was highest in 2022 and lowest in 2024.

Even in 2023 and 2024, when revenue was shrinking, operating profit grew, so the operating margin rose to 3.3%, 4.6%, 6.5% and 8.3%.

Net income attributable to owners of the parent was KRW 24.0 billion in 2022, KRW 35.1 billion in 2023 (up 46.1% year on year), KRW 38.8 billion in 2024 (up 10.5%), and KRW 56.6 billion in 2025 (up 45.8%), and net income including non-controlling interests was also reported at KRW 56.6 billion in 2025.

The debt-to-equity ratio was 227.2% in 2022, 256.8% in 2023, 236.7% in 2024, and 215.2% in 2025, its lowest level in 2025, and at the end of 2025 total equity was KRW 581.8 billion and total liabilities were KRW 1,251.9 billion.

Cash flow from operating activities improved from KRW -0.15 billion in 2022 and KRW -132.6 billion in 2023 to KRW 106.4 billion in 2024 and KRW 260.4 billion in 2025.

By quarter, revenue grew from KRW 205.9 billion in the third quarter of 2025 to KRW 256.4 billion in the fourth quarter of 2025 (up 24.6% quarter on quarter), KRW 511.3 billion in the first quarter of 2026 (up 99.4%), and KRW 813.0 billion in the second quarter of 2026 (up 59.0%), then fell to KRW 534.0 billion in the third quarter of 2026 (down 34.3%).

Operating profit peaked at KRW 26.0 billion in the first quarter of 2026, then declined for two consecutive quarters to KRW 25.5 billion in the second quarter and KRW 10.1 billion in the third quarter, and third-quarter 2026 operating profit was down 46.7% from KRW 18.9 billion a year earlier.

Net income attributable to owners of the parent bottomed at KRW 7.6 billion in the fourth quarter of 2025 and then rose for three consecutive quarters, to KRW 18.6 billion in the first quarter of 2026, KRW 19.7 billion in the second quarter, and KRW 20.0 billion in the third quarter.

However, the third-quarter increase in net income was largely due to a gain of KRW 17.863 billion on the disposal of investment property from the sale of the Ansan headquarters building, which was recorded as non-operating income.

Third-quarter non-operating income rose from KRW 729 million a year earlier to KRW 17.478 billion, while net income from financial instruments, meaning related income minus expenses, fell from KRW 13.498 billion to KRW 2.74 billion.

Because this disposal gain is not recurring, the combined net income attributable to owners of the parent of KRW 66.0 billion over the latest four quarters should be read separately from the trend in the underlying operating business.

05

Industry analysis

Brokerage earnings in Korea move with trading volumes in the equity market and the direction of interest rates. In the first half of 2026, a strong domestic stock market lifted commissions and margin lending interest, and proprietary activities such as ETF liquidity provision expanded.

Conversely, bond markets were less favorable due to rising rates and lower institutional trading, although Hanyang's DCM business recovered on underwriting-led activity. In Q3 2026, rising market volatility and higher interest rates were again cited as headwinds.

According to the company, the industry is seeing intensifying competition driven by capital strength, and the gap with large brokerages in equity capital remains a challenge.

Large peers such as Mirae Asset Securities, Samsung Securities, NH Investment & Securities and KIWOOM Securities set the benchmark, and Hanyang is extending its position in selected product areas.

Its lead-managed DCM share of about 2.0% ranks 11th in the industry, which gives it a visible presence in bond underwriting. Preparing for an OTC derivatives license is read as an effort to add a derivatives-based revenue source beside its proprietary trading base.

Because the sector is highly sensitive to trading volumes and rate changes, the pace at which the company narrows its gap with competitors depends on capital expansion and diversification of its revenue mix.

06

Outlook

According to the company, the procedures to obtain an OTC derivatives business license are under way, led by its Financial Engineering division. The company says approval would broaden revenue sources built on derivatives transactions.

Recent asset revaluation and a KRW 50 billion rights offering have strengthened the equity base, which underpins its entry into new businesses. In retail, the MTS revamp is strengthening the non-face-to-face sales base, and the lineup is expanding with online special-offer RP and target-return funds.

The company also plans to keep widening its ETF LP, securities lending and margin lending businesses. On shareholder returns, it intends to maintain a dividend payout ratio of at least 30% in 2026, as set out in its value-up disclosure.

It has presented a vision of becoming a mid-sized securities firm with equity of at least KRW 1 trillion by 2030, which it says requires a more diversified earnings base. Few concrete schedules have been disclosed, so the timing of the OTC derivatives approval is the key milestone.

In the 2026 annual report, the next regular filing, the annual operating margin and the reliance on proprietary trading will show whether the mix has changed.

Bull factors

Improving earnings profile

Operating profit rose from KRW 37.2 billion in 2022 to KRW 75.3 billion in 2025, and operating margin climbed from 3.3% to 8.3%. Controlling-interest net profit has increased for three consecutive years from 2023 to 2025. The debt-to-equity ratio declined from 227.2% in 2022 to 215.2% in 2025.

Diversifying revenue sources

Profit from ETF liquidity provision rose, and interest income from securities lending and margin lending expanded. First-half 2026 operating revenue reached KRW 1.32 trillion, up 196.4% year on year. Efforts to move beyond reliance on a few businesses are beginning to show up in results.

Capital base and new business preparation

Asset revaluation and a KRW 50 billion rights offering widened the equity base, which stood at KRW 669.5 billion at the end of June 2026. Preparations to obtain an OTC derivatives business license are under way, led by the Financial Engineering division. As of November 16, 2026, lead-managed bond deals totaled KRW 921 billion across five transactions.

09

Bear factors

Q3 operating profit decline

Q3 2026 operating profit fell to KRW 10.1 billion, down 46.7% year on year and 60.5% quarter on quarter. Operating profit has declined for two consecutive quarters since Q1 2026. Net profit growth in Q3 was largely due to the KRW 17.86 billion building-sale gain booked as non-operating income.

Dependence on market volatility

In first-half 2026 operating revenue, gains on financial product valuation and disposal reached KRW 722 billion, or 54.5% of the total, and derivatives trading gains were KRW 439.5 billion. A large part of revenue therefore depends on price movements and interest rates. Over the same period, corporate finance revenue fell to KRW 96.6 billion from KRW 115.1 billion.

Leverage and funding burden

Total liabilities at the end of 2025 were KRW 1.25 trillion, more than twice total equity of KRW 581.8 billion. Bonds and short-term borrowings rose by about KRW 597.7 billion year on year in Q3 2026. In a rising-rate environment, funding costs and the gap in capital scale with larger rivals remain challenges.

10

Risk factors

Market and interest rate risk

Proprietary trading and derivatives make up a large share of activity, so sharp moves in share prices or interest rates can quickly change valuation and trading gains. In Q3 2026, rising volatility and higher rates were again cited as headwinds. Quarterly operating profit swings sharply, which is how this risk shows up in the numbers.

Licensing and regulatory risk

The OTC derivatives license is still in preparation, and the timing and conditions of approval have not been disclosed. A delay would push back the expected expansion of derivatives-based revenue.

Brokerages are subject to capital and business regulatory metrics, so changes in indicators such as the 862.9% net capital ratio at the end of Q3 2026 also warrant monitoring.

Security and personal data risk

Reports have emerged of personal data leakage tied to an intrusion into an external groupware system. Personal data incidents at financial firms can lead to sanctions and erosion of customer trust. Follow-up checkpoints include how the cause and recurrence-prevention measures are disclosed and carried out.

12

Overall view

Hanyang Securities has seen both controlling-interest net profit and operating profit rise since 2022, and operating margin climbing from 3.3% to 8.3% shows a visible change in its earnings profile in the filed numbers.

Its business is weighted toward proprietary trading, so market volatility and the direction of rates feed directly into results. The path from KRW 26 billion in operating profit in Q1 2026 to KRW 25.5 billion in Q2 and KRW 10.1 billion in Q3 reflects that structural sensitivity.

The 40.6% year-on-year rise in Q3 net profit came from a building-sale gain recorded as non-operating income, so it should be read separately from any improvement in the core operating base.

Positive factors include the broader revenue base from ETF LP, securities lending and margin lending, a stronger equity base, and the plan to keep a payout ratio of at least 30%.

Negative factors include earnings volatility, reliance on proprietary trading, and financial burden from high leverage and rising bond issuance. The next checkpoints are the operating margin in the 2026 annual report and whether the OTC derivatives business license is obtained.
