02

Business structure

Lightron Fiber-Optic Devices researches, develops, manufactures and sells optical transmitter, receiver and transceiver modules used in optical communication, which carries signals as light through fiber.

Its main revenue comes from communication optical-module business lines such as ODL (wireless) and FTTH, and demand is tied to investment levels of mobile carriers. A 2025 press report described Samsung Electronics and other domestic and overseas telecom equipment makers as its customers.

According to a company executive, data-center products generated revenue in 200G and 400G classes through 2025, and at a May 2026 exhibition the company highlighted 800G QSFP-DD and 1.6T OSFP modules as core items.

An October 2026 report on the semi-annual filing said 200G and 400G PAM4 products are in mass production, while 800G and 1.6T PAM4 products are still in development, and 25Gbps long-reach products extended to more than 30 km are under qualification at domestic and overseas customers.

Its PCB-assembly subsidiary, Seyoung Technology, has supplied PCB assembly and part of the back-end processing for optical module parts, according to the annual business report.

The defense business expanded after a defense division was launched at the end of 2025, and a localization project with a domestic defense contractor finished in the first half of 2026 and is in final testing.

Competition has intensified as Taiwanese and Chinese makers improved technology, and low-price Chinese module makers have added pricing pressure.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual results moved sharply on a filed basis. Revenue was KRW 52.6 billion in 2022, the largest year in the 2022–2025 window, and KRW 18.1 billion in 2024, the smallest. Revenue fell 58.7% to KRW 21.7 billion in 2023, when an operating profit of KRW 1.22 billion turned into an operating loss of KRW 9.35 billion.

In 2024 revenue fell a further 16.4% and the operating loss widened to KRW 10.3 billion. In 2025 revenue rose 75.2% to KRW 31.8 billion, but the operating loss widened to KRW 16.5 billion, leaving an operating margin of -52.0%, compared with -56.6% in 2024.

The company's executive said in May 2026 that lower wireless-sector investment brought price cuts and fierce competition, and that a sharp currency move reduced profitability in margin-generating product lines.

The net loss attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 19.6 billion in 2023, KRW 18.6 billion in 2024 and KRW 19.7 billion in 2025, and the net loss including non-controlling interests was reported at the same levels.

The debt ratio rose from 62.4% in 2022 and 30.6% in 2024 to 57.3% in 2025, when total equity was KRW 58.9 billion, of which KRW 57.8 billion belonged to controlling shareholders, and total liabilities were KRW 33.7 billion.

Operating cash flow was KRW -12.4 billion in 2023, KRW -5.77 billion in 2024 and KRW -3.4 billion in 2025.

Quarterly, the operating loss was largest in the fourth quarter of 2025 at KRW 8.31 billion, and the first quarter of 2026 showed revenue of KRW 8.46 billion, up 9.7% from the prior quarter, but still an operating loss of KRW 2.26 billion.

Revenue dropped to KRW 2.94 billion in the second quarter of 2026, down 65.3% from the prior quarter, and was KRW 3.01 billion in the third quarter, down 60.5% from a year earlier. The net loss attributable to controlling shareholders widened to KRW 3.36 billion in the third quarter of 2026 from a year earlier. A news report on the 2025 annual results said cost of sales exceeded revenue, producing a gross loss.

05

Industry analysis

Demand for optical transceivers has two sources. One is wireless and access-network demand tied to mobile carriers' capital spending, and a press report linked a cut in that investment to lower revenue at Lightron Fiber-Optic Devices.

The other is high-speed optical demand from AI data-center expansion, where optical components carry large volumes of data between servers and switches, and an October 2026 report described expectations for growth on that basis.

Mainstream data-center transmission speeds are moving toward 800G, and the company is positioning its 800G and 1.6T lineup to meet that shift. On competition, technological gains by Taiwanese and Chinese makers have intensified the optical module field, and low-price Chinese module makers add pricing pressure.

The same electronics and components sector includes large component makers such as Samsung Electro-Mechanics and LG Innotek, along with optical component makers such as TAIHAN Fiberoptics.

The company appears to be in a transition period, moving from wireless products whose sales have declined toward data-center products.

The pace of profit recovery in this industry depends on how quickly the share of higher-priced data-center products grows, which requires both industry demand and the company's mass-production schedule to line up.

06

Outlook

The core of the company's plan is a larger share of higher-priced data-center products. In May 2026 a company executive said 800G and 1.6T optical transceivers would generate full-scale revenue in the second half once testing and verification were complete.

The same executive said the company was clearing low-price products to strengthen its base and planned to increase revenue from higher-priced data-center products from 2027 to lift profitability.

However, the October 2026 report on the semi-annual filing still described the 800G and 1.6T PAM4 products as in research and development, so whether the second-half revenue target was met cannot yet be confirmed from filings. 25Gbps long-reach optical modules with transmission distances above 30 km are under qualification at domestic and overseas customers, and the company expects these to broaden its addressable market.

In the 6G field, the company was selected in September 2026 as an implementing organization for an optical communication component commercialization project run by the Ministry of Science and ICT, and is developing a 6G fronthaul BiDi optical transceiver.

The company said it secured purchase orders and demand confirmations from key customers before development, and it plans to supply domestic and overseas telecom equipment makers and carriers after development and testing by accredited labs.

In defense, the company is pursuing an integrated defense optical communication solution business and says it has completed development of high-reliability products. The next checkpoint is how the timing and scale of 800G and 1.6T revenue appear in the 2026 annual business report.

Revenue volatility and possible delays in the product shift

If wireless investment stays weak and price pressure continues, the revenue gap during the product transition could last longer.

Financing and governance questions

Convertible bonds were issued in 2026 in March (KRW 5 billion), May (KRW 8 billion) and October (KRW 3 billion), and the debt ratio at the end of 2025 was 57.3%, up from 30.6% at the end of 2024. The largest shareholder, a partnership, holds a 22.49% stake.

A Field News report said proceeds from a rights offering and convertible bonds were used to buy real estate held by affiliated companies. The factual basis of that report needs further confirmation from filings.

Dilution and financing risk

The company also says a sharp currency move reduced profitability in margin-generating product lines, which remains a cost-side variable.

Overall view

Lightron Fiber-Optic Devices is a communication-component company centered on optical transceivers. Revenue rose 75.2% in 2025, but the operating loss widened to KRW 16.5 billion, extending a run of operating losses that has lasted three consecutive years from 2023.

Revenue in 2026 moved sharply, from KRW 8.46 billion in the first quarter to KRW 3.01 billion in the third, and the third-quarter operating loss narrowed from a year earlier while the net loss attributable to controlling shareholders widened.

Data-center 800G and 1.6T products, along with 6G and defense, are presented as new growth legs, and whether the targeted second-half revenue materializes is the most direct checkpoint.

On the other side, the net loss attributable to controlling shareholders totals KRW 15.3 billion over the last four quarters, and repeated convertible bond issues, short-term market warnings and a trading halt in 2026 make liquidity and share price volatility worth monitoring.

A press report on the use of funds by the largest shareholder means the facts of capital transactions should be confirmed through filings. Strengths in the business and weaknesses in finances and governance coexist, and which side prevails will show in the revenue mix and operating results of the next periodic reports.
