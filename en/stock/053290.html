Report overview

AI Pivot and Profit Recovery Amid Shrinking Publishing

With publishing revenue shrinking, NE Neungyule returned to operating profit in 2025 and in the second quarter of 2026, yet operating losses in the first and third quarters of 2026 keep the AI learning platform pivot at the center of the profit question.

02

Business structure

NE Neungyule is an education platform company that runs textbook publishing and education services, with English learning content at its core. Its leading products are bestselling study books such as Leading Tutor, NE Neungyule VOCA and Grammar Zone, along with English school textbooks.

Its core segments cover English learning book publishing, middle and high school textbooks, ELT original-format books and education services, while non-core segments include the online learning platform Mamchal and the early-childhood books I Challenge and Kids.

The non-core segments are being wound down in stages, and the company has cited this as a main reason for the shrinking top line. Publishing revenue fell to KRW 74.2 billion in 2023, KRW 66.8 billion in 2024 and KRW 56.5 billion in 2025, according to IB Tomato.

Sales run through distributors, and in the first quarter of 2026 the company said a policy change on receivables and inventory management with distributors caused a temporary concentration of returns.

The company's main asset is more than 40 years of accumulated English education content and learning data, which it is combining with AI to move toward customized learning services. As part of this shift it moved its enterprise IT systems to an Amazon Web Services cloud environment. In March 2026, after the resignation of CEO Ju Min-hong, Lee Jung-jin became the sole CEO.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual revenue fell from KRW 80.2 billion in 2022 to KRW 79.2 billion in 2023 (down 1.3% year on year), KRW 71.7 billion in 2024 (down 9.4%) and KRW 62.7 billion in 2025 (down 12.6%), declining for three consecutive years. Within 2022 to 2025, 2022 was the largest year and 2025 the smallest.

Operating profit fell from KRW 6.77 billion in 2022 to KRW 1.11 billion in 2023 (down 83.6%), swung to an operating loss of KRW 1.05 billion in 2024, and returned to operating profit of KRW 1.07 billion in 2025. The operating margin was 8.4% in 2022, 1.4% in 2023, -1.5% in 2024 and 1.7% in 2025.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 4.54 billion in 2022 and KRW 1.07 billion in 2023 (down 76.5%), turned to a net loss of KRW 5.04 billion in 2024, and in 2025 remained a loss of KRW 387 million, with the loss narrowing.

The company disclosed lower selling and administrative expenses, including bad-debt provisions, as a main factor behind the 2025 improvement. The debt-to-equity ratio moved from 31.6% in 2022 to 24.4% in 2025, and total equity at year-end 2025 was KRW 80.1 billion.

Operating cash flow was KRW 5.61 billion in 2022, negative KRW 863 million in 2023, KRW 9.5 billion in 2024 and KRW 18.4 billion in 2025, the largest of the four years. Quarterly results are volatile.

After the fourth quarter of 2025, with revenue of KRW 24.1 billion, operating profit of KRW 4.38 billion and net income attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 3.19 billion, the first quarter of 2026 recorded revenue of KRW 1.97 billion (down 91.8% from the prior quarter), an operating loss of KRW 12.1 billion and a net loss attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 11.8 billion.

The second-quarter revenue of KRW 3.58 billion was attributed to a reduction in non-core segment sales, while operating profit improved to KRW 1.63 billion as bad-debt burden eased.

Third-quarter revenue was KRW 9.35 billion, up 161.1% from the prior quarter and down 28.2% year on year, with an operating loss of KRW 1.74 billion, and the loss widened versus the same period last year. The net loss attributable to controlling shareholders over the last four quarters totals KRW 9.57 billion.

Analysts have also noted that early investment costs of the platform transition could make a short-term rebound difficult.

05

Industry analysis

The publishing market for textbooks and study materials faces a structural squeeze from the shrinking school-age population. Some observers argue that efficiency gains alone cannot restore growth in a market where textbook sales and learning-material demand are falling.

The company is therefore moving its center of gravity from publishing toward edtech and platform businesses, a shift that could raise upfront investment burdens. In cycle terms, the company is in a phase of shrinking top-line.

Press reports describe four years of contraction, while on a disclosure basis revenue has declined for three consecutive years from 2023 to 2025. Competitively, NE Neungyule's differentiator is its long accumulation of English learning books and textbooks.

In AI learning platforms, however, it competes directly with edtech companies that already have technology and platforms, and its new platform's position in that competition has not yet been shown in reported results.

Compared with entertainment companies such as HYBE and SM Entertainment, or advertising agencies such as Cheil Worldwide and Innocean Worldwide, its business structure is clearly different.

06

Outlook

The center of the company's transition plan is an AI-based learning platform. The company said results from the platform under development are expected to become visible in stages after the second half of 2026, and it plans to launch the new learning platform in the second half to demonstrate the business model shift.

It intends to concentrate resources on core segments to complete the restructuring. The company said core-segment shipment-based revenue has stayed at a level similar to the prior year.

On shareholder returns, it decided in July 2026 on a KRW 1 billion share buyback, and results were disclosed on August 31, 2026, with 74.6% of the planned volume acquired. Treasury shares now account for 3.5% of total shares. The company is also reviewing additional buybacks and cancellation.

It said it maintains a debt-free management stance. It also said it judged that the share price does not fully reflect the growth potential from its content assets and AI transition.

The key variable is how much platform revenue can offset publishing declines as the school-age population shrinks, and this will be confirmed through periodic reports after the 2026 annual report.

08

Bull factors

Financial Cushion and Debt-Free Operation

The debt-to-equity ratio was 24.4% at year-end 2025, and total equity stood at KRW 80.1 billion. The company says it maintains a debt-free operating stance and plans to pair transition investment with shareholder-return policies.

Even during the loss period, operating cash flow reached KRW 18.4 billion in 2025, the largest of the four years.

Stable Core Revenue and Second-Quarter Return to Profit

The company says shipment-based revenue in core segments, including English learning books, middle and high school textbooks, ELT original-format books and education services, has remained similar to the prior year.

In the second quarter of 2026, operating profit returned to KRW 1.63 billion as the bad-debt burden eased. Whether the core revenue base holds after non-core segments are wound down is central to this argument.

Four Decades of Content Assets and AI Platform Pivot

The company says it is combining more than 40 years of English education content and learning data with AI to build customized learning services. It expects results from the new platform to appear in stages after the second half of 2026. The shift of its enterprise IT systems to AWS cloud is presented as the foundation for this transition.

09

Bear factors

Continued Shrinkage of the Top Line

Annual revenue declined for three consecutive years from 2023 to 2025, and third-quarter 2026 revenue of KRW 9.35 billion was down 28.2% year on year. According to IB Tomato, publishing revenue fell from KRW 74.2 billion in 2023 to KRW 56.5 billion in 2025. Some press reports describe the shrinkage as lasting four years.

Volatile Earnings

In the first quarter of 2026, the company recorded an operating loss of KRW 12.1 billion and a net loss attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 11.8 billion. The third quarter also showed an operating loss of KRW 1.74 billion.

The net loss attributable to controlling shareholders over the last four quarters totals KRW 9.57 billion, and profitable quarters have not yet come in succession.

Structural Demand Pressure and Transition Costs

Amid a shrinking school-age population and falling textbook and study-material demand, critics argue that efficiency gains alone cannot revive growth. The phased wind-down of the Mamchal online platform and the I Challenge and Kids segment is also under way. Analysts note that early transition investment could delay any short-term earnings rebound.

10

Risk factors

Shrinking School-Age Population and Demand

A structural decline in textbook and study-material demand could pull down total revenue even if core-segment shipments hold at the prior year's level. The company says core revenue is being maintained, but publishing revenue was declining through 2025. This is an external condition the company cannot easily control, so it needs separate monitoring.

Distribution and Receivables Management Risk

A policy change on receivables and inventory with distributors led to a concentration of returns in the first quarter of 2026. Although the bad-debt burden eased in the second quarter, distribution policy changes can widen quarterly swings. Because sales depend on distributors, shifts in their policies feed directly into revenue and profit.

Execution Risk in the Transition and Management Change

The new AI-based platform is scheduled for launch in the second half of 2026, but the timing of visible results remains open. After the March 2026 resignation of Ju Min-hong, Lee Jung-jin became sole CEO, changing who executes the transition strategy. Share buybacks also fell short of the planned share count because of price movements.

12

Overall view

NE Neungyule's publishing-centered revenue base is shrinking, yet profit has partly recovered, with operating profit returning to the black in 2025 and again in the second quarter of 2026.

However, the first-quarter 2026 operating loss of KRW 12.1 billion and the third-quarter operating loss of KRW 1.74 billion show wide quarterly swings, and the net loss attributable to controlling shareholders over the last four quarters totals KRW 9.57 billion, so earnings continuity is not yet confirmed.

Strengths include a debt-free operating stance and a debt-to-equity ratio of 24.4%, more than 40 years of English education content assets, and shareholder-return efforts including treasury share purchases.

Weaknesses include the publishing revenue decline tied to the shrinking school-age population, the top-line reduction from winding down non-core segments, and the upfront investment burden of the platform transition.

Which of these factors shows up in results first will become clear in the 2026 annual report and the periodic reports that follow.
