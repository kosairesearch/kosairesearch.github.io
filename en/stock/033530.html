02

Business structure

SJG Sejong is a maker of automotive exhaust system parts. Its industry classification places it under aerospace and defense, but its actual business is concentrated in automotive components.

Its main products are mufflers and converters, the exhaust gas purification devices, and in January to September 2026 these products generated KRW 1.42 trillion, or 93.6% of consolidated revenue. The remaining 6.4% (KRW 970 billion) came from other products, including electric-vehicle components.

Hyundai Motor and KIA are the core customers, and in the same period Hyundai Motor accounted for 54.6% of consolidated revenue and KIA for 24.1%. Blomter's tally of full-year 2025 showed that Hyundai Motor Group affiliates made up 87.3% of revenue.

The company is described as the number one player in the domestic exhaust system market, and one rival's exit from exhaust systems is cited as a benefit to its share. By region, the company said in November 2025 that it operates more than 31 bases in 14 countries, including the United States, Mexico, India and China.

In January to September 2026, operating profit in North America rose 79%, while operating profit in Europe and Asia fell 46.2% and 19.3%, respectively. In electrification and hydrogen, it handles SBW actuators, metal separator plates for the Nexo, and hydrogen detection sensors. Most revenue still comes from exhaust parts for internal-combustion vehicles.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual revenue fell from KRW 1.86 trillion in 2022 to KRW 1.79 trillion in 2023, down 3.6% year on year, and then rose to KRW 1.82 trillion in 2024, up 1.4%, and KRW 1.9 trillion in 2025, up 4.4%. Revenue has increased for two consecutive years since 2023, and 2025 was the largest year in the 2022 to 2025 period.

Operating profit rose from KRW 32.1 billion in 2022 to KRW 51.9 billion in 2023, KRW 60.6 billion in 2024 and KRW 85.4 billion in 2025, increasing for three consecutive years from 2023 through 2025. The operating margin moved from 1.7% in 2022 to 2.9%, 3.3% and 4.5%, so profit grew much faster than revenue.

In 2025, revenue rose 4.4% while operating profit rose 40.9%. Attributable net income climbed from KRW 1.72 billion in 2022 to KRW 4.02 billion in 2023, KRW 33.7 billion in 2024 and KRW 68.7 billion in 2025. The 738.4% jump in 2024 reflects a very low base in the prior year.

Net income including non-controlling interests was also KRW 68.7 billion in 2025. In quarterly terms, operating profit in the first quarter of 2026 was KRW 36.2 billion, rebounding sharply from KRW 1.28 billion in the fourth quarter of 2025, an increase of 2,721.9% from the prior quarter.

A corporate information service attributed that quarter's improvement to higher domestic and export sales of mufflers and converters and to higher production by major automakers.

Operating profit then fell for two consecutive quarters, to KRW 34 billion in the second quarter and KRW 25.8 billion in the third quarter of 2026.

The third-quarter attributable net loss of KRW 1.94 billion reflected a deterioration in non-operating items, and Blomter pointed to a KRW 16.6 billion foreign-currency translation loss.

Attributable net income for the last four quarters, from the fourth quarter of 2025 to the third quarter of 2026, totaled KRW 70.8 billion.

05

Industry analysis

In the domestic exhaust parts market, SJG Sejong is described as the number one player. One source put the company's domestic market share at about 28% as of June 2026 (wisereport corporate monitor), and a rival's exit from exhaust systems is cited as reinforcing its existing position.

Demand from the downstream market moves with Hyundai Motor Group's production volumes and model mix. The group's move to strengthen its hybrid lineup is cited as a favorable factor for exhaust parts.

Conversely, the spread of electric vehicles is a structural variable that could reduce demand for internal-combustion exhaust systems. The company is widening its business into SBW actuators and hydrogen-vehicle parts, but their revenue share remains small.

In North America, Hyundai Motor Group is raising hybrid vehicle production and strengthening local parts sourcing, according to a report from October 2026. In line with this, the company operates production bases in Alabama and Georgia.

On tariffs, the company said it is reducing the burden by shifting some items to its Mexican subsidiary. In overseas markets, the company presents its number of local bases as the basis of its competitiveness.

06

Outlook

The most concrete schedule the company has disclosed is its new plant in Savannah, Georgia. In October 2026 the company said it would invest KRW 15 billion in the facility to produce hybrid vehicle parts, with equipment work targeted for completion in March 2027 and first mass production in August 2027.

The plant sits near Hyundai Motor Group's HMGMA, and the company said it would expand orders to solidify its North American business base. Cumulative operating profit for January to September 2026 of KRW 96.1 billion exceeded the full-year 2025 figure of KRW 85.4 billion, according to the company.

However, quarterly operating profit has declined for two consecutive quarters since the second quarter of 2026, and the third-quarter figure was KRW 25.8 billion.

On tariffs, the company said returns at its U.S. subsidiary could partly decline, but it expects no major damage to overall profit, citing ongoing negotiations with customers on cost sharing. The company also said higher sales in Mexico and India drove improvement at its overseas operations.

The next checkpoint is whether the March 2027 completion and August 2027 first production happen as scheduled. Whether new North American orders support utilization at the plant is also worth tracking.

Bull factors

Improved earnings base

Operating profit rose for three consecutive years from KRW 32.1 billion in 2022 to KRW 85.4 billion in 2025, and the operating margin climbed from 1.7% to 4.5%.

The company announced on November 16, 2026 that cumulative operating profit for January to September 2026 of KRW 96.1 billion surpassed the full-year 2025 figure. The company attributes the gains to local production overseas combined with higher sales.

Expanding North American production

The company is building a new plant in Savannah, Georgia, with a KRW 15 billion investment to produce parts for hybrid vehicles. Operating profit in North America rose 79% in January to September 2026.

The new base is aligned with Hyundai Motor Group's push to source more parts locally in North America, and the company said it could support further orders.

Electrification and hydrogen parts

In a November 2025 announcement, the company said SBW actuator revenue grew more than 20% year on year. It also handles metal separator plates for the Nexo and hydrogen detection sensors. Most revenue still comes from exhaust parts, but a growing share from new businesses could diversify the customer base.

09

Bear factors

Customer concentration

Hyundai Motor Group affiliates accounted for 87.3% of revenue in full-year 2025, and Hyundai Motor and KIA together were 78.7% in January to September 2026. Results are structurally tied to one customer group's production and sales. Any production adjustments by the automakers feed directly into operating profit.

FX and non-operating losses

Attributable net income swung to a KRW 1.94 billion loss in the third quarter of 2026. Blomter reported that a KRW 16.6 billion foreign-currency translation loss worsened non-operating results in that quarter. Even when operating profit rises, net income can swing with exchange-rate moves, which is a bearish factor.

Weaker overseas profit and quarterly declines

In January to September 2026, operating profit in Europe fell 46.2% and in Asia 19.3%. If the tariff burden persists, the U.S. subsidiary's contribution to profit could shrink, so its profitability in the next periodic report is the point to check.

Operating profit has also declined for two consecutive quarters since the second quarter of 2026, which is a weakness in the earnings trend.

10

Risk factors

Customer concentration risk

Revenue is concentrated in Hyundai Motor and KIA, so their production plans largely determine results. If the pace of the shift to hybrid and electric vehicles differs across customers, the timing and scale of parts demand could also vary. Customer diversification depends on expanding electrification and hydrogen parts, whose share is still small.

Tariff and FX risk

The company said U.S. tariffs could partly reduce returns at its U.S. subsidiary. It is shifting some items to its Mexican subsidiary to reduce the burden, but the effect of that transfer and the outcome of cost-sharing negotiations with customers remain to be seen.

Foreign-currency translation losses from exchange-rate moves turned net income into a loss in the third quarter of 2026.

Investment and transition risk

Total liabilities at the end of 2025 were KRW 797.6 billion, with a debt ratio of 150.9%. The Savannah plant targets first mass production in August 2027, so delays in operation or North American orders below expectations could lengthen the payback period.

A revenue base centered on internal-combustion exhaust systems would face pressure if electric vehicles expand faster.

Overall view

SJG Sejong is a maker of automotive exhaust system parts. Revenue rose for two consecutive years in 2024 and 2025 after its 2023 low, and operating profit also increased for two consecutive years after its 2023 low, through 2025. The operating margin rose from 1.7% in 2022 to 4.5% in 2025.

Cumulative operating profit of KRW 96.1 billion for January to September 2026 exceeded the full-year 2025 figure, but the third-quarter attributable net loss of KRW 1.94 billion shows that FX and non-operating items remain key variables for profit.

The company says its new North American plant, targeted for equipment completion in March 2027 and first mass production in August 2027, will broaden its base by expanding local production tied to hybrid vehicle demand.

On the other side, lower operating profit in Europe and Asia, two consecutive quarterly declines in the second and third quarters of 2026, and a revenue mix centered on internal-combustion exhaust systems are bearish factors.

The industry classification places it under aerospace and defense, but the drivers of its results confirmed by filings and reports are automotive exhaust parts.
