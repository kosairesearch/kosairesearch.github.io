Namhae Chemical: Earnings Recovery Tested by Raw Material Costs

02

Business structure

Namhae Chemical makes agricultural fertilizers and chemical products such as ammonia and sulfuric acid, and is an affiliate of the Nonghyup cooperative.

According to a report by The Guru, its fertilizer production capacity is 1.36 million tonnes per year and its chemical capacity, including sulfuric acid, is 1.64 million tonnes per year. The company said in its Q1 2026 results announcement that it kept a share of about 40% of the domestic fertilizer market.

The backbone of domestic fertilizer sales is an annual tender contract with NH Nonghyup Economic Group, signed at the start of each year. Exports total roughly 600,000 tonnes a year, sold to more than 20 countries.

Based on the product sales mix in the WiseReport company profile, compound fertilizers account for about 40%, petroleum products about 33%, ammonia about 18%, and urea about 5%.

Petroleum products are a fuel business in which the company buys products from refiners and sells them through gas stations, so its character differs from the core fertilizer and chemical operations.

Ammonia is both a raw material imported from overseas and a product for sale, and the company's filings state that raw material prices affect fertilizer profitability the most. The company has set out a plan to expand ammonia storage and develop into a domestic clean ammonia logistics hub.

In export markets it competes with overseas fertilizer suppliers, and the timing gap between selling prices and costs drives quarterly profit.

04

Earnings analysis

On a reported basis, annual sales fell 26.8% from KRW 2.17 trillion in 2022 to KRW 1.59 trillion in 2023, then declined 4.3% to KRW 1.52 trillion in 2024 before rising 5.6% to KRW 1.6 trillion in 2025. Sales were largest in 2022 and smallest in 2024 across 2022 to 2025. Profit followed a different path.

Operating profit fell 82.3% from KRW 62.6 billion in 2022 to KRW 11.1 billion in 2023, then rose 228.1% to KRW 36.3 billion in 2024 and 18.9% to KRW 43.1 billion in 2025, two consecutive years of growth.

The operating margin moved from 2.9% in 2022 to 0.7% in 2023, 2.4% in 2024 and 2.7% in 2025, recovering from the 2023 low. Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders fell 74.7% to KRW 12 billion in 2023, then rose 110.2% to KRW 25.2 billion in 2024 and 15.9% to KRW 29.2 billion in 2025.

Net income including non-controlling interests, at KRW 21.5 billion in 2024 and KRW 26.9 billion in 2025, is a separate figure from the controlling-shareholder measure. Quarterly profit has swung widely since Q3 2025.

Operating profit of KRW 15.7 billion in Q3 2025 turned into an operating loss of KRW 7.42 billion in Q4 2025, then an operating loss of KRW 121 million in Q1 2026, before returning to operating profit of KRW 12.9 billion in Q2 2026 and falling to KRW 5.06 billion in Q3 2026.

Sales stayed high at KRW 493 billion, KRW 504.9 billion and KRW 463.5 billion in Q1 to Q3 2026, but Q3 sales dropped 8.2% from the prior quarter, and operating profit fell 60.9% from the prior quarter and 67.8% from a year earlier.

According to a financial media report, the company attributed the sharp Q1 2026 profit decline mainly to raw material price surges linked to the Middle East war, with ammonia up 50%, urea up 75% and sulfur up 133% from a year earlier.

On a controlling-shareholder basis, net losses of KRW 5.34 billion in Q4 2025 and KRW 2.03 billion in Q1 2026 gave way to profits of KRW 8.93 billion in Q2 2026 and KRW 2.37 billion in Q3 2026, and the last four quarters total KRW 3.93 billion.

Operating cash flow improved from negative KRW 96.1 billion in 2022 to KRW 101.9 billion in 2023 and KRW 120.3 billion in 2024, then fell to KRW 47.6 billion in 2025.

05

Industry analysis

Fertilizer is an essential input for agricultural production, and it is seen as relatively less sensitive to economic cycles. The industry estimates that about 80% of global ammonia output is used as fertilizer feedstock.

As a result, international prices of raw materials such as ammonia and urea largely determine fertilizer producers' margins. The sharp rise in raw material prices linked to the Middle East war in 2026 is the biggest variable for the sector.

In domestic fertilizer sales, the company, with a share in the 40% range, supplies volumes steadily under annual contracts with Nonghyup. This structure supports sales stability, but how quickly contract prices reflect raw material increases depends on contract terms.

Exports compete with overseas suppliers, and diversifying imported raw materials has become more important. At the end of 2025, the Malaysian prime minister said the company had agreed to supply fertilizer raw materials worth about KRW 170 billion, including phosphate rock, potash and green ammonia.

On the ammonia side, industrial demand for low-carbon ammonia is growing under carbon neutrality policies. The company has outlined a direction to develop into the country's largest clean ammonia logistics hub.

06

Outlook

The most specific guidance confirmed so far is the 2026 fertilizer supply contract with NH Nonghyup Economic Group. The contract is worth KRW 315.1 billion, and KRW 102.2 billion had been delivered by Q1 2026.

In a mid-term plan announced in 2024, the company said it would expand its controlled-release fertilizer plant from 4,000 tonnes to 16,000 tonnes by 2027 and raise its market share from 21% to 50%.

The same plan targeted raising fertilizer export volumes from about 580,000 tonnes to 750,000 tonnes and included KRW 44.7 billion of investment to replace logistics facilities at the Nakpo wharf. A semiconductor-grade sulfuric acid business has been announced as a new revenue plan.

The near-term question is how far raw material cost increases are absorbed by selling price increases. In its Q1 2026 announcement, the company said it would pursue productivity gains, expand export volumes through new customers and raise product prices in a timely way.

With Q3 2026 operating profit at KRW 5.06 billion, whether the price increases show up in margins in later quarters is the next checkpoint. In the 2026 annual report, the next periodic filing, the full-year operating margin and operating cash flow direction are the items to check. Expansion of ammonia storage facilities is linked to the clean ammonia logistics hub strategy.

08

Bull factors

Captive domestic demand and market share

The company holds a share in the 40% range of the domestic fertilizer market, and annual tender contracts with NH Nonghyup Economic Group form its demand base. The 2026 contract is worth KRW 315.1 billion, with KRW 102.2 billion delivered by Q1.

Fertilizer is an essential agricultural input seen as relatively insensitive to economic cycles. The contract structure limits swings in volume and supports a floor for sales.

Profit recovery since the 2023 low

Operating profit rose from a KRW 11.1 billion low in 2023 to KRW 36.3 billion in 2024 and KRW 43.1 billion in 2025, while the operating margin climbed from 0.7% to 2.7% over the same period. Operating cash flow stayed positive at KRW 101.9 billion in 2023 and KRW 120.3 billion in 2024, and was KRW 47.6 billion in 2025.

A decline in the debt ratio from 73.0% in 2022 to 49.2% in 2025 is cited as evidence of easing financial pressure.

Ammonia logistics and low-carbon feedstock

Ammonia storage capacity rose to 79,000 tonnes after an expansion completed around late 2024. In April 2025, Saudi-origin ultra-low-carbon ammonia arrived in Korea for the first time and went straight into the fertilizer process.

The company said it would replace 200,000 tonnes of its 620,000 tonnes of annual ammonia handling with this feedstock. It is the first case of such low-carbon ammonia being used in the agricultural sector, which is cited as a differentiator.

09

Bear factors

Sharp swings in quarterly profit

Operating profit swung from a loss of KRW 7.42 billion in Q4 2025 and a loss of KRW 121 million in Q1 2026 to KRW 12.9 billion in Q2 2026. It then fell to KRW 5.06 billion in Q3, down 60.9% from the prior quarter and 67.8% from a year earlier.

Q3 sales rose 28.6% year on year to KRW 463.5 billion, but profit did not keep pace. Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders totaling KRW 3.93 billion over the last four quarters is the main bearish point.

Pass-through pressure from raw material costs

According to a financial media report, ammonia prices in Q1 2026 rose 50% from a year earlier, urea rose 75% and sulfur rose 133%. The company said it would respond with timely product price increases and higher export volumes. How much of this cost increase has passed through to Q3 profit remains an open point. A prolonged period in which input costs rise ahead of selling prices could keep margins under pressure.

Slower cash generation and timing of new businesses

Operating cash flow dropped from KRW 120.3 billion in 2024 to KRW 47.6 billion in 2025. New businesses such as semiconductor-grade sulfuric acid and ammonia supply for hydrogen power are described as plans, so the timing of their contribution to results is unclear. Progress against previously announced expansion and export targets can be gauged from the periodic reports.

10

Risk factors

Raw materials and FX

Prices of raw materials such as ammonia, urea and sulfur, along with exchange rates, directly affect profitability. The company has described rising raw material prices and greater currency volatility as the Middle East conflict continues. If price increases lag, quarterly operating profit could return to a loss, which is the most direct risk.

Concentration in Nonghyup contracts

Domestic sales rely heavily on annual tender contracts with NH Nonghyup Economic Group, so changes in contract terms or volume feed directly into revenue. Because the largest shareholder is also part of the Nonghyup group, shifts in affiliated transaction mix and policy environment remain variables for results.

Execution of new businesses and expansion

The controlled-release fertilizer plant expansion, the export target of 750,000 tonnes and the semiconductor-grade sulfuric acid business have all been announced as plans. If execution speed or commercialization slips against these plans, the mid-term profit improvement scenario could be delayed. The gap between when plans were announced and current progress is what needs checking.

12

Overall view

Namhae Chemical is the largest domestic fertilizer supplier and an affiliate of the Nonghyup group, and it maintains its business base on a domestic market share in the 40% range and annual contracts with NH Nonghyup Economic Group.

Sales declined in 2023 and recovered in 2025, while profit grew for two consecutive years after its 2023 low. The debt ratio fell from 73.0% in 2022 to 49.2% in 2025, and total equity at end-2025 was KRW 552.6 billion. By contrast, quarterly profit swung sharply in 2026.

Operating profit returned to KRW 12.9 billion in Q2 but fell to KRW 5.06 billion in Q3, and net profit attributable to controlling shareholders totals KRW 3.93 billion over the last four quarters.

Raw material cost surges are cited as an obstacle to profit recovery, and how quickly price increases and export growth come through is the key variable. The new businesses and expansion plans have not yet shown up in reported results.

The next checkpoints are the operating margin in the 2026 annual report and the Q1 2027 results. This report is for information only and does not include a buy or sell opinion.
