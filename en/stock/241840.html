Business structure

Astory is a broadcast content producer that develops and produces dramas and supplies them to broadcasters and global OTT platforms.

The company describes its business as four lines: global OTT drama production, securing drama IP and overseas rights, selling remake rights worldwide, and secondary businesses that exploit its IP.

Its flagship title, Extraordinary Attorney Woo, is seen as the starting point of its own-IP business, and a 2022 brokerage report said the title opened the door to that model.

In the first half of 2026 the company disclosed a license agreement with the global streaming platform Viki for broadcast rights to the drama Crash Season 2. Revenue is sensitive to the number of productions, so which titles are produced and scheduled directly determines results.

Customers are domestic broadcast channels and overseas streaming operators, and sales of overseas and remake rights are revenue sources the company is looking to expand. Analysts classify Astory as a non-captive producer, one without a broadcaster affiliation, and see schedule gaps as its core risk.

In competition it is compared with Studio Dragon, widely seen as Korea's leading drama producer, and analysts note gaps in production volume and library size. Revenue of subsidiary AIMC was added separately to a 2026 revenue estimate on top of production revenue.

04

Earnings analysis

Based on filed annual results, revenue fell from KRW 71.7 billion in 2022 to KRW 56.9 billion in 2023 (down 20.7% year on year), KRW 14.8 billion in 2024 (down 74.0%) and KRW 11.8 billion in 2025 (down 20.3%), a three-consecutive-year decline from 2023 through 2025.

Among 2022 to 2025, 2022 was the largest year and 2025 the smallest.

Operating profit fell from KRW 8.5 billion in 2022 to KRW 6.95 billion in 2023 (down 18.3%), turned into an operating loss of KRW 3.5 billion in 2024, and widened to an operating loss of KRW 7.28 billion in 2025, making 2024 and 2025 two consecutive loss years.

The operating margin moved from 11.9% in 2022 and 12.2% in 2023 to -23.7% in 2024 and -61.7% in 2025.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 4.91 billion in 2023 (up 20.2%), then turned into a net loss attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 4.82 billion in 2024 and KRW 7.11 billion in 2025, and net income including non-controlling interests was reported at the same amounts in each year.

The company cited fewer drama productions and weaker production profitability as the main reasons for the 2025 changes, while brokerage commentary argued that the 2025 one-off items were accounting losses without cash outflows and were unlikely to recur in 2026 results.

On a quarterly basis, the fourth quarter of 2025 brought revenue of KRW 2.92 billion (down 21.6% from the prior quarter) and an operating loss of KRW 4.18 billion, and the first quarter of 2026 brought revenue of KRW 6.71 billion (up 129.4%) with an operating loss of KRW 1.21 billion.

Second-quarter 2026 revenue was KRW 3.46 billion (down 48.5%) with an operating loss of KRW 2.52 billion, while net income attributable to controlling shareholders turned positive at KRW 695 million.

Third-quarter 2026 revenue of KRW 9.19 billion rose 165.8% from the prior quarter and 146.3% year on year, but the operating loss of KRW 1.54 billion was wider than a year earlier.

Operating losses have run for five consecutive quarters from the third quarter of 2025 through the third quarter of 2026, and the net loss attributable to controlling shareholders over the last four quarters totals KRW 5.92 billion.

Operating cash flow alternated in sign every year: KRW 28.6 billion in 2022, -KRW 6.08 billion in 2023, KRW 21.3 billion in 2024 and -KRW 1.96 billion in 2025.

05

Industry analysis

Domestic drama production volume depends on broadcast advertising demand and platform investment. A 2025 third-quarter cumulative results summary described a global economic slowdown that reduced domestic TV advertising spending and shrank content investment budgets.

A securities analysis described how scheduling delays in 2024 and 2025, including weak industry conditions, limited broadcast slots and casting adjustments, pushed back production schedules and lengthened content gaps.

Analysts describe schedule gaps as the core risk for non-captive producers, which have no guaranteed slots at an affiliated broadcaster.

The same analysis said that securing one ENA title a year would leave only one or two additional titles needed for a profitable structure, and that producing at least two to three titles a year is the condition for stable profitability.

Studio Dragon, the leading domestic drama producer, has been assigned a higher valuation within the industry by brokers, reflecting its production capability. The same report pointed to gaps with Studio Dragon in production volume and library size.

The company is strengthening co-productions with global partners and export of its owned IP formats, and is expanding its global business through remakes. Still, how long the domestic broadcast advertising slump will constrain programming budgets remains an open variable.

Outlook

The license agreement for Crash Season 2 disclosed in the first half of 2026 is a verifiable example of OTT licensing revenue.

A securities analysis reported in the first half of 2026 estimated 2026 revenue at KRW 38.0 billion, made up of KRW 36.0 billion in production revenue from Crash Season 2 and another title, plus KRW 2.0 billion from subsidiary AIMC.

The same analysis said Crash Season 2 was in production and was scheduled for broadcast on ENA in the second half of 2026.

It judged that the two confirmed titles alone make a meaningful step up in profit difficult to expect, and said that whether additional titles, reportedly being prepared for second-half filming, enter production would determine the size of any recovery.

The same analysis added that the core basis for normalizing schedules is the cooperation structure with ENA. Filed quarterly revenue for the first three quarters of 2026 totals KRW 6.71 billion, KRW 3.46 billion and KRW 9.19 billion, roughly half of the annual revenue estimate.

Whether fourth-quarter revenue aligns with broadcast timing will therefore decide whether that estimate is reached. Because quarterly revenue has swung widely, whether the second-quarter profit reflects a one-time effect or a structural change will become clearer from later filings.

The 2026 annual general meeting was held on March 26, 2026, approving the 22nd fiscal year financial statements. The next regular checkpoint is the 2026 annual report, which will be the first confirmation of full-year results including the fourth quarter of 2026.

Bull factors

First signs of scheduling recovery

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders turned positive at KRW 695 million in the second quarter of 2026, and third-quarter revenue of KRW 9.19 billion was the largest of the last five filed quarters. Brokers see the ENA partnership as a mechanism that secures one broadcast title a year. Adding one or two more titles is described as enough to create a profitable structure.

Expansion of the IP asset base

Owned IP that began with Extraordinary Attorney Woo is extending into overseas remake rights and streaming licenses. The Crash Season 2 broadcast rights agreement disclosed in the first half of 2026 is an example of IP monetization.

A report citing Korea IR Association commentary noted that a higher share of IP-based revenue could lead to re-rating as an IP holder.

A low debt burden

Total liabilities were KRW 8.01 billion at the end of 2025, and the debt ratio fell to 16.7% from 73.1% in 2022. Total equity was KRW 48.1 billion, with equity attributable to controlling shareholders reported at the same level. A small debt load during a period of net losses can be read as capacity to absorb schedule gaps.

Risk factors

Scheduling gap risk

Non-captive producers have no guaranteed slots at an affiliated broadcaster and are exposed to schedule gaps. Fewer drama productions was the company's own explanation for the 2025 deterioration. If the planned ENA broadcast and additional productions do not follow through, the revenue base could narrow again.

Quarterly earnings volatility

Revenue rose 129.4% from the prior quarter in the first quarter of 2026, then fell 48.5% in the second quarter. Profit attributable to controlling shareholders reversed from a profit in the second quarter to a net loss of KRW 1.81 billion in the third quarter. Quarterly results depend heavily on production timing, which is the risk.

Cash flow and IP revenue uncertainty

Operating cash flow was negative in 2023 and 2025, at -KRW 1.96 billion in 2025. Overseas and remake rights sales can be booked unevenly depending on title performance and deal timing. Even with a low debt burden, production investment capacity could be constrained if cash generation does not stabilize.
