02

Business structure

SM Bexel was founded in 1961 and listed on KOSPI in 1994, and runs two business units: auto parts and batteries. The auto unit makes water pumps, aluminum cylinder heads and other auto parts for engine and drivetrain systems, supplied mainly to Hyundai Motor Group affiliates.

In the first quarter of 2026, KRW 36.6 billion of revenue split into KRW 19.6 billion (53.6%) from the auto unit and KRW 17.0 billion (46.4%) from the battery unit.

Within autos, water pumps led at KRW 10.5 billion, followed by auto parts at KRW 6.6 billion, aluminum cylinder heads at KRW 700 million and TCCM at KRW 600 million.

Within batteries, alkaline cells were the largest at KRW 11.7 billion (68.6%), followed by manganese cells at KRW 1.9 billion and ampoule batteries at KRW 1.8 billion. In the same quarter, exports were KRW 12.7 billion and domestic sales KRW 23.9 billion, with utilization at 74.5% for autos and 50.7% for batteries.

For the first half of 2026, the auto unit accounted for 57% of sales and the battery unit for 43%, according to a Value Finder report. By product, water pumps (about 31%) and alkaline cells (about 30%) were the largest, per a March 2026 breakdown.

On defense, the company completed the first phase of lithium ampoule battery supply for the Cheonmu (K239) rocket launcher, and in August 2026 signed an MOU with a drone solutions company to supply about 200 drone battery packs by the second quarter of 2027.

It is also developing a 2900Wh-class lithium-ion cell and pack for low-Earth-orbit satellites with Seoul National University, with cells now in validation for space environment testing. The largest shareholder held 50.84% of common shares as of a June 25, 2026 filing.

04

Earnings analysis

On a disclosed basis, revenue rose from KRW 136.8 billion in 2022 to a peak of KRW 202.7 billion in 2023 (up 48.2%), then fell 14.9% to KRW 172.5 billion in 2024 and 12.9% to KRW 150.3 billion in 2025, declining for two straight years.

Operating profit went from KRW 3.42 billion in 2022 to KRW 9.47 billion in 2023 (up 176.6%), then dropped 44.9% to KRW 5.22 billion in 2024 and 69.8% to KRW 1.57 billion in 2025, also falling for two straight years.

Operating margin read 2.5%, 4.7%, 3.0% and 1.0% from 2022 to 2025, and in 2025 operating profit fell far faster than revenue, making margin pressure the defining feature of the year.

Net income attributable to owners fell from KRW 9.22 billion in 2022 to KRW 687 million in 2023 (down 92.5%), then rose 62.3% to KRW 1.12 billion in 2024 and 48.0% to KRW 1.65 billion in 2025, increasing for two straight years.

The gap between operating profit and net income in 2023 is consistent with a sharp rise in income tax expense that year, per a financial data aggregator. Net income including non-controlling interests was disclosed at the same levels for each year.

Operating cash flow was KRW 4.42 billion in 2022, KRW 23.3 billion in 2023, KRW 234 million in 2024 and KRW 6.21 billion in 2025. The debt ratio fell from 123.3% in 2022 to 42.8% in 2025, and at end-2025 total equity of KRW 70.2 billion was entirely attributable to owners, against total liabilities of KRW 30 billion.

Quarterly, an operating loss of KRW 211 million in Q3 2025 and KRW 347 million in Q4 2025 gave way to an operating profit of KRW 489 million in Q1 2026. Q2 2026 operating profit was KRW 765 million (up 56.4% from the prior quarter), yet net loss attributable to owners was KRW 948 million, a swing back into loss.

Q3 2026 operating profit was KRW 130 million (down 83.1% from the prior quarter), while net income attributable to owners returned to a KRW 2.13 billion profit.

Q3 2026 revenue was KRW 38.9 billion, up 6.7% year on year, and net income attributable to owners totaled KRW 1.52 billion across the four quarters from Q4 2025 to Q3 2026.

05

Industry analysis

Demand for the auto unit depends heavily on production plans at Hyundai Motor Group affiliates. Water pumps and cylinder heads are engine and drivetrain parts, so the pace of powertrain electrification remains a variable that could reshape medium-term volumes.

Auto utilization was 74.5% in Q1 2026, which means rising volumes could absorb fixed costs, while falling volumes could turn that into a burden. Battery utilization was 50.7% in the same quarter, and because primary batteries make up much of sales, the unit tracks consumer and industrial demand.

Military, satellite and drone batteries are discussed as a separate demand stream tied to rising defense and space spending. Within the auto parts sector, a tally of 137 listed companies ranks this company 96th on revenue, so its scale is small.

Compared with peers such as Hyundai Mobis, Hanon Systems, Hyundai WIA and Sungwoo Hitech, which span modules, thermal management and body parts, its product range is narrow and centered on engine components.

The sector-wide average operating margin in the same tally is negative, so the company's margin of around 1% is high relative to that average. Its profit base is small, however, so the margin moves sharply with modest changes in amounts.

Vehicle production volumes and defense budget execution, both needed to judge the cycle, remain the next items to track.

Wider specialty battery applications

If military and aerospace demand builds, the battery unit could gain a new revenue pillar. With batteries at 43% of first-half 2026 sales, a larger specialty share would change the business mix.

Quarterly profit direction turns

After operating losses in Q3 and Q4 2025, the company posted an operating profit in Q1 2026, and in Q3 net income attributable to owners returned to a KRW 2.13 billion profit. Q3 2026 revenue of KRW 38.9 billion rose 6.7% year on year. Against 2025 annual operating profit of KRW 1.57 billion, the quarterly move from losses to profits is visible.

Improved financial structure

The debt ratio fell from 123.3% in 2022 to 42.8% in 2025, with total equity of KRW 70.2 billion and total liabilities of KRW 30 billion at end-2025. Auto utilization of 74.5% and battery utilization of 50.7% in Q1 2026 indicate room to absorb fixed costs if volumes rise. That balance sheet cushion could support equipment investment and expansion of specialty battery output.

Bear factors

Quarterly volatility

Operating profit was KRW 765 million in Q2 2026 but dropped to KRW 130 million in Q3, down 83.1% from the prior quarter. Q3 and Q4 2025 both recorded operating losses. Quarterly results have moved between profit and loss, which makes the direction of earnings hard to pin down.

Customer concentration and cash flow swings

The auto unit supplies mainly Hyundai Motor Group affiliates, so customer concentration is high. Operating cash flow swung from KRW 23.3 billion in 2023 to KRW 234 million in 2024 and then KRW 6.21 billion in 2025. Such large year-to-year changes make cash generation volatile.

Risk factors

Powertrain electrification shift

Water pumps and cylinder heads are engine and drivetrain parts, so the pace of powertrain electrification could change medium-term demand. Faster electrification could reduce engine component volumes and turn into a utilization burden for the auto unit.

Because the product range centers on engine parts, room to expand items in line with the transition is limited. Whether the company responds to electrification will be a key variable for the auto unit's trajectory.

Execution risk in defense and space

The first Cheonmu supply is complete, but follow-on orders and the drone plan through the second quarter of 2027 are still closer to plans than results. The 2900Wh satellite cell must clear space environment and long-life tests, so the timing of commercialization is open. Any delay would push back specialty battery sales growth.

Governance and share liquidity

Related-party holdings of 89.33% in a September 21, 2026 filing leave a small free float. Several insider purchase disclosures by the largest shareholder side came in 2026, but those facts are separate from the direction of the share price. Each holding change disclosure can affect trading structure.

Overall view

Operating profit was KRW 1.57 billion in 2025 with a 1.0% margin, while net income attributable to owners rose for two straight years in 2024 and 2025.
