Flat Sales and Losses as TPC Reshapes Its Portfolio

02

Business structure

TPC was founded in 1998 to make and sell auto parts and listed on KOSDAQ in 2011, according to WiseReport company profile data. Its core business makes precision steel pipes, vibration-damping parts, fuel filler systems and cooling system parts that it supplies to global automakers.

By product, WiseReport's mix shows drawn anti-vibration parts at 29.21 percent, PVC sewer, water and fire-fighting pipes at 25.61 percent, pipe assemblies at 20.20 percent, steel pipes at 13.39 percent and other items at 11.59 percent. The PVC pipe business sits in a subsidiary that handles synthetic resin piping.

On a 2024 consolidated basis, domestic sales were 87.5 percent and exports 12.5 percent, which ties the company closely to Korean vehicle production and sales.

WiseReport's December 2025 breakdown shows about 30 percent of drawn anti-vibration sales exported and about 28 percent of elevator reducers and other industrial products.

The robot-reducer subsidiary was reported to have entered mass production, but in January 2026 TPC disclosed a plan to transfer 3,653,336 shares of it for about KRW 31 billion, with a scheduled date of March 20, 2026. The company said the transfer aims to restructure its business and focus on core capabilities.

Auto parts must meet global automaker quality standards, which creates entry barriers. Peers in the same sector include Daewonkangup, Seoyon E-Hwa, Sungwoo Hitech and Hyundai WIA.

04

Earnings analysis

On the filed annual figures, sales went from KRW 81.3 billion in 2022 to KRW 79.5 billion in 2023 (down 2.3 percent year on year), KRW 80.4 billion in 2024 (up 1.2 percent) and KRW 79.4 billion in 2025 (down 1.3 percent).

05

Industry analysis

The auto parts cycle is being shaped by weak domestic sales and a shift toward electric vehicles. WiseReport's company monitor noted that domestic sales hit a low even though auto parts output passed 4 million units, as weaker consumer sentiment and economic slowdown hurt results.

With domestic sales at 87.5 percent of consolidated revenue in 2024, TPC's results track Korean automaker production and sales closely. Compared with export-heavy parts makers, there is less overseas demand to cushion the business.

Precision steel pipes and drawn anti-vibration parts require automaker quality approval, which creates entry barriers. A Korean press report quoted an industry source calling the combination of auto and industrial parts production a rare vertically integrated model.

Reports described the localization of robot reducers as meaningful for technological self-reliance, but the subsidiary stake transfer narrows the robotics scope the company holds directly.

Peers such as Daewonkangup, Seoyon E-Hwa, Sungwoo Hitech and Hyundai WIA are the natural comparison set, and the starting point is TPC's continued operating losses and high domestic concentration.

06

Outlook

The company has framed its direction as steady growth in core auto parts plus expansion into industrial and robotics parts, according to a Pinpoint News report.

However, the January 2026 disclosure on transferring the robot-reducer subsidiary stake was aimed at restructuring the business and focusing on core capabilities, so the robotics expansion has changed course with that sale.

At the March 2026 annual general meeting, shareholders approved an articles amendment adding bond, equity and fund investment to the business purpose, according to Digital Today.

The company cited expanded business capacity, but how much weight the investment business will carry relative to manufacturing needs to be confirmed in periodic reports.

As of June 2026, the consolidated subsidiary list contained only one company, the PVC pipe subsidiary, so the scope of consolidated results has changed. The 2026 annual report should therefore be read with the new consolidation scope in mind.

Because profit swung between a second-quarter gain and third-quarter loss in 2026, whether margins recover from core operations is the key question. No specific order or capacity-expansion schedule has been disclosed yet, so future filings are the next checkpoint.

Bull factors

Financial room and an improving operating trend

The debt ratio was 32.8 percent at the end of 2025, with total equity of KRW 93 billion, of which KRW 65.3 billion belongs to controlling shareholders. Operating cash flow stayed positive at KRW 3.86 billion in 2023, KRW 3.5 billion in 2024 and KRW 1.93 billion in 2025, even during loss years.

The company returned to a KRW 862 million operating profit in Q2 2026, and its Q3 2026 operating loss of KRW 243 million was narrower than a year earlier.

Business restructuring and a narrower consolidation scope

The January 2026 disclosure on transferring the robot-reducer subsidiary stake can be read as a sale of a loss-making subsidiary, and the company said it aims to focus on core capabilities. As of June 2026, only the PVC pipe subsidiary remained on the consolidated list, which simplifies the group.

Because precision steel pipes and vibration-damping parts form the main sales base, a sharper business focus is cited as a bullish argument.

An insider signal from the CEO's stake increase

The CEO bought a total of 100,000 shares on the open market from March 9 to 11, 2026, and the stake stood at 19.96 percent as of March 11, 2026, according to Digital Today. Adding shares while already holding a large stake is cited as a sign of management confidence.

However, an insider purchase is one disclosed fact and has limits as a standalone basis for any directional view.

09

Bear factors

Three straight quarters of falling sales

Q1 2026 sales were KRW 17.3 billion, down 19.1 percent from the prior quarter, followed by KRW 15.9 billion in Q2 (down 7.9 percent) and KRW 15.6 billion in Q3 (down 1.8 percent). Q3 2026 sales were also down 20.0 percent from a year earlier. With domestic sales dominating, weak domestic auto demand may have contributed.

Four straight operating loss years and a wider 2025 loss

Operating losses were KRW 1.61 billion in 2022, KRW 2.82 billion in 2023, KRW 902 million in 2024 and KRW 3.26 billion in 2025, four consecutive loss years. The 2025 loss widened from 2024 and the operating margin was -4.1 percent.

The Q2 2026 profit is a single-quarter result, so whether the annual loss pattern has changed needs the 2026 annual report.

A net profit figure that leans on a one-time gain

Controlling-shareholder net profit of KRW 18.5 billion in Q1 2026 moved against operating results, while controlling-shareholder losses were KRW 1.34 billion in both Q2 and Q3 2026. Without evidence that the profit improvement will continue, the net profit figure alone makes core business strength hard to read. Until the accounting effect of the subsidiary transfer is confirmed, the earnings trend is difficult to judge.

10

Risk factors

Domestic auto demand and the shift to electrification

Domestic sales made up 87.5 percent of consolidated revenue in 2024, so weakness in Korean automaker sales flows directly into results. WiseReport's company monitor said the shift to electric-vehicle-centered demand limits profitability gains.

If demand for the company's products changes with electrification, the speed of response becomes a key variable.

Earnings volatility and visibility after the subsidiary restructuring

One-off items can swing quarterly results sharply, as the KRW 18.5 billion controlling-shareholder profit in Q1 2026 shows, which makes the earnings trend hard to read. The subsidiary transfer and the narrower consolidation scope make direct comparison with older periods harder. Periodic reports need to show how the transfer gain was recorded and how continuing operations are separated.

Ownership concentration and trading liquidity

The CEO holds 19.96 percent, a high share of ownership, so the free float available for trading is relatively limited. With a small market capitalization, price swings can grow when trading volume is thin.

At the March 2026 general meeting, shareholders approved the financial statements and an articles amendment adding to the business purpose.

Overall view

TPC is a maker of precision steel pipes and vibration-damping parts, mainly for auto parts. Operating losses have run for four consecutive years from 2022 to 2025, and sales have stayed around KRW 80 billion.

Sales fell for three straight quarters in 2026, and operating results swung to a profit in Q2 before returning to a KRW 243 million loss in Q3, a narrower loss than a year earlier.

The KRW 18.5 billion controlling-shareholder profit in Q1 2026 moved against operating results, so whether it included one-off items, alongside the subsidiary transfer, is central to reading earnings.

Strengths include a 32.8 percent debt ratio and KRW 93 billion in total equity, positive operating cash flow and a sharper focus after narrowing the business scope.

Weaknesses include high domestic sales dependence at 87.5 percent, a wider loss in 2025, controlling-shareholder losses in Q2 and Q3 2026 and falling sales. Valuation sits at a level below net assets, while earnings-based measures lean on a one-off-looking first quarter, so readings differ.

The next checkpoints are the operating and transfer-related results in the 2026 annual report and the provisional fourth-quarter 2026 results. This report is for information only and contains no buy or sell opinion.
