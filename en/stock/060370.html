02

Business structure

LS Marine Solution is mainly engaged in laying and burying subsea power and telecom cables. The company operates three cable-laying vessels, and the GL2030 was upgraded from a 4,000-ton to a 7,000-ton-class cable capacity in an overhaul that began in April 2025.

Key projects include cable burial for the Taiwan Power Company offshore wind complex TPC2, subsea telecom cable installation in Asia-Pacific, and the Sinan Uyi offshore wind subsea cable contract with Hanwha Ocean signed on September 18, 2026.

The Sinan Uyi contract is worth KRW 156.9 billion and covers transport, laying and burial from the second half of 2026 through 2028. In subsea telecom, the company won a role in the Japan-Korea subsea network (JAKO) that Microsoft and Amazon are pursuing.

Through its onshore cable construction subsidiary, the company has also expanded into power and underground cable work, giving it construction capability spanning sea and land.

The group's cable maker LS Cable & System produces the cable while LS Marine Solution handles subsea laying and protection, a split that the company cites as the basis for large turnkey awards. Rival Taihan Cable & Solution holds both domestically delivered CLVs, making vessel ownership a key competitive variable. Consolidated backlog at the end of 3Q 2026 was about KRW 820 billion, roughly 3.4 times 2025 annual revenue.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual revenue rose from KRW 42.8 billion in 2022 to KRW 70.8 billion in 2023 (up 65.4%), KRW 130.3 billion in 2024 (up 84.1%) and KRW 244.2 billion in 2025 (up 87.4%), growing three years in a row from 2023 through 2025. Among 2022 to 2025, 2025 was the largest year.

Operating profit moved from an operating loss of KRW 6.61 billion in 2022 to KRW 13.1 billion in 2023, a swing to profit, then fell to KRW 12.4 billion in 2024 (down 5.3%) and KRW 7.01 billion in 2025 (down 43.4%), declining two years in a row.

The operating margin went from 18.5% in 2023 to 9.5% in 2024 and 2.9% in 2025. Revenue scale grew sharply, but profitability did not keep pace with the expansion.

Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders swung from a net loss of KRW 12.1 billion in 2022 to KRW 11.6 billion in 2023, then reached KRW 13.2 billion in 2024 (up 13.6%) and KRW 8.36 billion in 2025 (down 36.7%).

Net income including non-controlling interests was reported at the same amounts over the same years. Operating cash flow was -KRW 6.62 billion in 2024 before turning to KRW 19.9 billion in 2025. At end-2025, total equity was KRW 628.1 billion and total liabilities KRW 111 billion, a debt ratio of 17.7%.

On a quarterly basis, the company swung to an operating loss of KRW 1.48 billion in 4Q 2025 and back to an operating profit of KRW 4.22 billion in 1Q 2026, followed by KRW 6.12 billion in 2Q and KRW 6.87 billion in 3Q, two consecutive quarterly increases.

Revenue in 3Q 2026 was KRW 73.6 billion, down 22.9% from the prior quarter, and the company attributed this to the GL2030 vessel being unavailable for projects during its expansion.

Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders over the last four quarters totaled KRW 20.3 billion, and in some quarters it exceeded operating profit, so non-operating items also need to be considered.

05

Industry analysis

End demand comes from offshore wind, cross-border power grids (HVDC and interconnectors) and subsea telecom networks. Taiwan's government is pursuing a long-term roadmap targeting 20.6 GW of offshore wind capacity by 2035, as reported in April 2025.

In Korea, the 8.2 GW Sinan offshore wind belt is under way, and its first project, Sinan Uyi (390 MW), has a signed contract with construction scheduled from the second half of 2026. The West Coast HVDC energy expressway is also advancing as a core national grid project.

Vessel availability is a barrier to entry in subsea cable installation, and Shinhan Investment Corp. said in an August 2026 report that more large projects would intensify competition for vessels, a development it viewed as positive for vessel owners such as LS Marine Solution.

The group's cable maker LS Cable & System reportedly ran its Donghae HVDC subsea cable plant at nearly 80% utilization, and reported a record backlog of KRW 9.85 trillion at end-3Q 2026. Taihan Cable & Solution holds both domestic CLVs, so vessel ownership shapes the competitive map.

On overseas competition, the company claims that only a few European global players have a business structure similar to its own; this is the company's own assertion. Subsea cable installation at sea is also affected by winter weather, so utilization varies by season.

06

Outlook

In its August 2026 first-half results announcement, the company expected growth to continue as the Sinan Uyi and Taean offshore wind projects and the West Coast HVDC energy expressway advance in sequence. It said the Taean and Haesong projects will proceed in step with earlier projects.

The GL2030 is being deployed for the first time on the Langkawi project in Malaysia, which will put all three owned vessels to work. The company said in late October 2026 that it planned to deploy the GL2030 in earnest from the Malaysia project to expand revenue.

A 13,000-ton-class CLV began construction in July 2026, and an April 2026 report described its introduction as scheduled for 2028.

Shinhan Investment Corp., in an August 27, 2026 report, estimated 2026 revenue at KRW 331.9 billion (up 35.9%) and operating profit at KRW 16.9 billion, and projected operating profit of KRW 40.8 billion for 2027 and KRW 68.3 billion for 2028.

Against that 2026 operating profit estimate, cumulative operating profit for the first three quarters of 2026, at KRW 17.2 billion, already exceeds the figure. These brokerage numbers are forecasts rather than reported results, and the quarterly split of 2026 will be confirmed in the 4Q numbers.

The company emphasizes turnkey awards covering both subsea and onshore construction, so whether additional orders are confirmed will determine the next change in backlog.

Bull factors

Backlog equal to 3.4 times annual sales

Consolidated backlog of about KRW 820 billion equals roughly 3.4 times 2025 revenue of KRW 244.2 billion. The Sinan Uyi contract announced September 18, 2026, at KRW 156.9 billion, is the largest in the company's history. Execution covers transport, laying and burial from the second half of 2026 through 2028. How quickly orders convert into revenue will determine the next several quarters.

Vessel capacity and larger ship deployment

The company has all three vessels working, and the GL2030 has completed an upgrade to a 7,000-ton-class capacity. Shinhan Investment Corp. said in an August 2026 report that intensifying competition for vessels would benefit owners. A 13,000-ton-class vessel began construction in July 2026.

Overseas projects and subsea telecom widen reach

The TPC2 offshore wind cable burial in Taiwan, announced in April 2025, was the company's first overseas grid award, worth about KRW 22.7 billion or $15.8 million. Asia-Pacific subsea telecom installation and the JAKO network both supported results and orders. The company credited overseas large projects with driving first-half 2026 results.

09

Bear factors

Revenue decline and vessel upgrade downtime

Revenue in 4Q 2025 was KRW 55.8 billion, down 27.6% from the prior quarter, and 3Q 2026 revenue of KRW 73.6 billion fell 4.3% year on year. The company attributed the 3Q decline to the GL2030 being unavailable for projects during its expansion. Until the upgrade shows up in revenue, the pace of top-line growth is constrained.

Thin operating margin and modest profit base

Operating profit fell two years in a row from 2024 through 2025, and the operating margin dropped from 18.5% in 2023 to 2.9% in 2025. Operating cash flow was -KRW 6.62 billion in 2024. Controlling-shareholder net profit over the last four quarters totaled KRW 20.3 billion, a modest profit base relative to revenue.

Rising competition and dependence on prime contractors

Taihan Cable & Solution holds both domestic CLVs, so competition for vessels is direct. The Sinan Uyi contract counterparty is Hanwha Ocean, and award terms can shift with the prime contractor's structure.

Collaboration with the group's cable maker is cited as a competitive edge, but it also means reliance on group manufacturing capacity.

10

Risk factors

Project timing and vessel utilization

Sinan Uyi work is planned from the second half of 2026 through 2028, and the timing of the West Coast HVDC project has not been fixed. If vessel utilization drops during the GL2030 upgrade or the Malaysia deployment, revenue gaps could return. The 13,000-ton-class vessel is still under construction, so added laying capacity is limited until it arrives.

Overseas contract FX exposure and seasonality

The TPC2 contract is worth $15.8 million and is dollar-denominated, so won-dollar moves could affect revenue and profit. Overseas projects carry more local regulatory, weather and schedule variables than domestic ones.

A cable group official noted that winter weather can make at-sea installation difficult and lower utilization by season.

Durability of the profit recovery

The 2025 operating margin was only 2.9%, and 4Q 2025 swung to a KRW 1.48 billion operating loss. Quarterly operating profit has risen since KRW 4.22 billion in 1Q 2026, but whether margins hold beyond 3Q must be confirmed in reported results.

In some quarters, controlling-shareholder net profit exceeded operating profit, so the quality of earnings also needs review.

Overall view

LS Marine Solution swung from an operating loss in 2022 to profit in 2023, and revenue grew three years in a row through 2025 to KRW 244.2 billion.

Consolidated backlog at end-3Q 2026 was about KRW 820 billion, roughly 3.4 times 2025 revenue, and the KRW 156.9 billion Sinan Uyi contract is the largest in the company's history. Operating profit rose for two consecutive quarters in 2026, to KRW 4.22 billion in 1Q, KRW 6.12 billion in 2Q and KRW 6.87 billion in 3Q.

On the other side, annual operating profit fell in 2024 and 2025, and the 2025 operating margin was 2.9%. Revenue in 3Q 2026 fell 4.3% year on year as vessel upgrade downtime weighed on utilization.

The strengths are order scale, vessel capacity and overseas expansion, while the weaknesses are revenue volatility, the narrow base of the margin recovery, and competition and seasonal utilization. The next checkpoints are 4Q 2026 preliminary results and the 2026 annual report's operating margin and year-end backlog.
