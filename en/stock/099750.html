02

Business structure

ezCaretech, founded in 2001 as a spinoff from Seoul National University Hospital, is a healthcare information technology company whose core business is hospital information systems (HIS). Its main products are the on-premise HIS BESTCare 2.0, installed on hospital servers, and the web-based cloud HIS Edge & Next.

On-premise projects generate both build revenue for large general hospitals and recurring operation and maintenance revenue after go-live.

Press coverage put new BESTCare sales at about KRW 27.7 billion in the fiscal year ended March 2025, up about 19.9%, and HIS operation and maintenance revenue at about KRW 39.5 billion, up about 5.1%, out of consolidated revenue of KRW 72.6 billion.

Reported backlog at the end of that fiscal year was KRW 22.9 billion for HIS development and sales and KRW 35.6 billion for HIS operation and maintenance, both higher than a year earlier.

Its customers are mainly large domestic hospitals, including Seoul National University Bundang Hospital, Asan Medical Center and Seoul National University Hospital.

Overseas, it has built HIS systems for hospitals under the Ministry of National Guard Health Affairs (MNG-HA) in Saudi Arabia and for Anaheim Community Hospital in the United States, and it runs wholly owned subsidiaries in the United States and Japan.

In the public sector, it operates a cloud-based information system project for nine national hospitals under the Ministry of Health and Welfare and the Korea Disease Control and Prevention Agency. As of September 30, 2025, Seoul National University Hospital held 32.86% of the shares.

04

Earnings analysis

ezCaretech's annual results moved from losses to profit. For the fiscal year ended March 2023, revenue was KRW 73.2 billion, operating loss was KRW 4.12 billion (operating margin -5.6%), and net loss attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 9.59 billion.

For the fiscal year ended March 2024, revenue fell 7.2% to KRW 67.9 billion, but operating profit of KRW 236 million marked a swing to profit, and net profit attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 1.21 billion.

For the fiscal year ended March 2025, revenue rose 6.9% to KRW 72.6 billion and operating profit rose 836.5% to KRW 2.21 billion. For the fiscal year ended March 2026, revenue reached KRW 78.9 billion, operating profit KRW 5.02 billion and operating margin 6.4%.

Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders grew for two consecutive years, from KRW 2.29 billion to KRW 6.09 billion. Press coverage credited the profit recovery to a move away from low-priced volume contracts and toward add-on services.

Operating cash flow went the other way, from KRW 10.9 billion in the fiscal year ended March 2025 to -KRW 1.24 billion in the fiscal year ended March 2026.

Equity at March 2026 was KRW 44.8 billion, all attributable to controlling shareholders, against liabilities of KRW 19.4 billion, and the debt ratio rose from 33.0% to 43.4%.

Quarterly revenue peaked at KRW 26.1 billion in the first quarter of 2026, then fell to KRW 20.5 billion in the second quarter, down 21.6% from the prior quarter and up 28.5% year on year. Operating profit fell 83.9% from the prior quarter to KRW 276 million in the second quarter. Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders over the last four quarters totaled KRW 6.9 billion.

05

Industry analysis

The biggest variable in the hospital IT market is the public sector's shift to cloud.

According to a Korea IR Council report, a project to consolidate nine national hospitals onto a single cloud-based HIS is under way, with a budget of KRW 15 billion in 2026 and a target to replace the HIS at all 37 provincial medical centers with cloud systems by 2030.

Expanded adoption of domestic solutions was seen as a positive for the on-premise business as well. Public tenders require proven references, which favors companies with experience building systems for large hospitals.

By contrast, the cloud HIS demonstration project run by the National Information Society Agency (NIA) was temporarily halted over a dispute between the agency and bidders about participation eligibility. Overseas, customers are broadening from public hospitals in the Middle East to large private hospitals.

The domestic market for small and mid-sized hospitals is seen as an area where SaaS-type cloud HIS can build recurring revenue.

The company cites its large-hospital on-premise references as a strength, but its revenue base is smaller than that of larger IT services peers, so quarterly results can swing widely depending on the timing of a few large public projects.

06

Outlook

The company's guidance leans toward overseas expansion. At an investor relations briefing in August 2026, the company said overseas revenue, KRW 9.8 billion in the fiscal year ended March 2026, could rise by more than KRW 20 billion in the fiscal year ending March 2027.

In Saudi Arabia, it signed a contract for a next-generation HIS with a private hospital group, worth more than KRW 10 billion, with the cloud HIS ezEDGE.

That project is targeted to go live in the first quarter of 2027, alongside a project to build next-generation HIS systems for six veterans hospitals and three long-term care hospitals. The company also said it is pursuing exports to Japan and exploring new demand in Africa and Southeast Asia.

Domestically, it described a plan to extend cloud HIS from national hospitals to local hospitals and public health centers, which it said could reach about 3,000 small and mid-sized hospitals.

Its profitability goals include a platform revenue share above 50% by 2030 and a double-digit operating margin for the fiscal year ending March 2027. The company described its operating margin for the fiscal year ended March 2026 as around 7%, while the filed figure is 6.4%.

Press coverage reported that the company is aiming for its first annual revenue above KRW 100 billion, but this was framed as an industry view rather than a company target.

If overseas contracts and public projects line up, revenue could accelerate in the fiscal year ending March 2027; if contracts slip, quarterly results will lag.

Bull factors

Overseas contracts broaden the revenue base

A next-generation HIS contract with a Saudi private hospital group was reported at about KRW 10 billion, and the company said it expects overseas revenue of KRW 9.8 billion in the fiscal year ended March 2026 to rise by more than KRW 20 billion in the fiscal year ending March 2027.

Entry into Middle Eastern private healthcare would broaden a customer base that had been mainly public. Exports to Japan and new demand in Southeast Asia point in the same direction.

Improving profitability structure

The operating margin rose from -5.6% in the fiscal year ended March 2023 to 6.4% in the fiscal year ended March 2026, and operating profit and net profit attributable to controlling shareholders have grown for two consecutive years since the fiscal year ended March 2025.

Press coverage attributed the gain partly to fewer low-priced volume contracts and more add-on service revenue. The company has moved from losses to profit and then to a larger profit base.

Potential benefit from public cloud HIS policy

According to a Korea IR Council report, the national hospital cloud HIS program starts with a KRW 15 billion budget in 2026 and aims to move all 37 provincial medical centers to cloud systems by 2030. Expanded adoption of domestic solutions also favors the on-premise business.

The company's experience running the cloud information system for nine national hospitals could serve as a reference for follow-on bids.

Bear factors

Wider swings in quarterly profit

Operating profit in the second quarter of 2026 fell 83.9% from the prior quarter to KRW 276 million, and net profit attributable to controlling shareholders fell 68.1% over the same period. Compared with KRW 1.71 billion in the first quarter of 2026, the profit base changed sharply within a single quarter.

Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders over the last four quarters totaled KRW 6.9 billion, so quarterly swings can obscure the annual trend.

Uncertain timing of policy projects

The NIA cloud HIS demonstration project was halted after a dispute between the agency and bidders over participation eligibility. Press coverage said resuming supplier selection could be an opportunity for the company, but no restart date has been set. Prolonged delays in public tenders would reduce the visibility of cloud transition revenue.

Cash flow and leverage moving the wrong way

Operating cash flow went from KRW 10.9 billion in the fiscal year ended March 2025 to -KRW 1.24 billion in the fiscal year ended March 2026. Over the same period, the debt ratio rose from 33.0% to 43.4%.

Cash generation weakened in a year when profit grew, so working capital demands tied to new contracts could affect how results are read.

10

Risk factors

Revenue timing tied to project awards

Large on-premise HIS projects tend to recognize revenue in clusters, depending on award and go-live timing. Revenue fell from KRW 26.1 billion in the first quarter of 2026 to KRW 20.5 billion in the second quarter, an example of this variability. If recognition timing slips, the gap between quarterly results and annual targets could widen.

Execution risk in overseas contracts

In its first-quarter 2024 results, the company said delays in contracts with some Middle Eastern hospitals had a temporary effect on results. The Saudi project is targeted to go live in the first quarter of 2027, so any slippage in implementation would push back overseas revenue targets. Losses at overseas subsidiaries are also an item to monitor.

Changes in policy and bidding rules

The public-sector shift to cloud HIS depends heavily on policy budgets and tender schedules. If bidding eligibility rules change or tenders are delayed, medium-term demand expectations could be revised. The more public-sector work grows as a share of revenue, the larger the impact of such changes.

Overall view

ezCaretech has moved from losses to profit, with both revenue and earnings rising through the fiscal year ended March 2026.

The operating margin improved from -5.6% in the fiscal year ended March 2023 to 6.4% in the fiscal year ended March 2026, and net profit attributable to controlling shareholders has grown for two consecutive years.

However, operating profit fell to KRW 276 million in the second quarter of 2026, operating cash flow turned negative in the fiscal year ended March 2026, and the debt ratio rose to 43.4%. Growth drivers are the Saudi private hospital contract, overseas revenue expansion and the shift of public HIS to cloud.

On the other side, the suspension of the NIA demonstration project and the timing gaps in large project awards reduce quarterly visibility.

The company's targets of a double-digit operating margin and more than KRW 20 billion of additional overseas revenue for the fiscal year ending March 2027 are items to verify through future filings. The share price currently sits above book value per share, and the multiple is sensitive to earnings trends.

The next checkpoints are whether operating profit recovers in the third-quarter 2026 report and whether supplier selection for the NIA project resumes.
