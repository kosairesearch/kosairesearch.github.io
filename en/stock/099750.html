02

Business structure

ezCaretech, a medical IT company spun off from Seoul National University Hospital in 2001, focuses on hospital information systems (HIS) and electronic medical records (EMR).

It sells its on-premise HIS BESTCare and its cloud HIS EDGE&NEXT, and said at an August 2026 investor briefing that the two products are used at 89 hospitals. The business has two main parts: HIS development and sales, and HIS operation and maintenance.

In the year ended March 2025, new BESTCare sales were about KRW 27.7 billion, up 19.9% year on year, and HIS operation and maintenance revenue was about KRW 39.5 billion, up 5.1%, according to a press report citing the company's business report.

That maintenance revenue exceeded half of the KRW 72.6 billion total for the year. Backlog at the same point stood at KRW 22.9 billion for HIS development and KRW 35.6 billion for HIS operation.

Customers are Korean tertiary and general hospitals as well as national and public hospitals, and the company says it held the number one HIS share at Korean tertiary hospitals as of October 2024. Overseas, it has supplied HIS to hospitals in Saudi Arabia and exports to the United States and Japan.

In 2026 it signed a deal worth about KRW 10 billion with a Saudi private hospital group for a next-generation HIS. Unlike large IT service firms such as LG CNS and Hyundai Autoever, it is a hospital-specialized vendor and is widening its reach to small and mid-sized hospitals and clinics through cloud HIS.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual results started from sales of KRW 73.2 billion and an operating loss of KRW 4.12 billion in the year ended March 2023. Sales fell to KRW 67.9 billion (down 7.2% year on year) in the year ended March 2024, the trough, and operating profit of KRW 236 million marked a return to profit.

The year ended March 2025 recorded sales of KRW 72.6 billion (up 6.9%) and operating profit of KRW 2.21 billion (up 836.5%). Sales in the year ended March 2026 reached KRW 78.9 billion (up 8.6%), the largest year in the period, and operating profit was KRW 5.02 billion (up 127.0%).

The operating margin moved from -5.6% to 0.3%, 3.0% and then 6.4%. Controlling-shareholder net income moved from a net loss of KRW 9.59 billion in the year ended March 2023 to KRW 6.09 billion in the year ended March 2026, and net income including non-controlling interests followed the same path.

According to an Electronic Times report, the company attributes the margin recovery to cutting low-priced, volume-driven projects and diversifying revenue through value-added services.

Quarterly, Q2 2025 posted a controlling-shareholder net loss of KRW 88.87 million, while Q3 2025 operating profit of KRW 2.13 billion rose 1,813.6% from the prior quarter. Q4 2025 operating profit fell 50.0% to KRW 1.06 billion.

Q1 2026 recorded the highest sales among the disclosed quarters at KRW 26.1 billion, with operating profit of KRW 1.71 billion. Q2 2026 sales were KRW 20.5 billion and operating profit KRW 276 million, up 28.5% and 148.2% year on year, respectively, but down sharply from the prior quarter.

Controlling-shareholder net income in Q2 2026 was KRW 723 million, down 68.1% from the prior quarter. Operating cash flow turned to -KRW 1.24 billion in the year ended March 2026 from KRW 10.9 billion a year earlier, and the disclosed figures alone do not explain the cause.

05

Industry analysis

The Korean hospital IT market combines HIS build-out demand centered on tertiary hospitals with a shift to cloud for small and mid-sized hospitals. The company cites its number one HIS share at tertiary hospitals as a reference advantage in large-hospital projects.

New build-out revenue arrives project by project, so annual results can swing. Maintenance revenue offsets part of that variability, and operating and maintenance revenue was about KRW 39.5 billion in the year ended March 2025.

The chief executive said the government is pursuing regional, essential and public healthcare reinforcement alongside an AI-based basic healthcare strategy, which he expects to expand the role of platforms linking medical institutions and AI services.

Overseas, Saudi Arabia is the core market, and cloud HIS supply is being pursued in the United States and Japan. Large IT service companies also compete for public and large-hospital projects, and the company emphasizes its hospital-specific expertise and medical data capabilities.

Independent data comparing market or order share with rivals was not confirmed in this review, so company claims and disclosed figures are presented separately.

06

Outlook

At an August 2026 briefing, the company said it would lift the operating margin, about 7% in the year ended March 2026, to double digits in the year ended March 2027. It plans to raise overseas sales from KRW 9.8 billion in the year ended March 2026 to more than KRW 20 billion in the year ending March 2027.

It also set a target of lifting the platform revenue share to above 50% by 2030. The company said platform business revenue doubled from KRW 1.7 billion to KRW 3.7 billion, but this figure is a company statement and was not confirmed in the disclosed line items.

The next-generation HIS contract with a Saudi private hospital group, worth about KRW 10 billion, is viewed as a core base for overseas sales.

However, one project was reported to have been briefly suspended over a dispute with the client about bidder eligibility, and a restart of supplier selection within the year could create a new opportunity.

In Japan, the company is pursuing cloud HIS supply to university hospitals, and it plans to explore new projects in Southeast Asia and Africa. In the United States, it is pursuing an HIS build-out project with Aurora Healthcare. Whether these targets are met will be visible in quarterly results and order disclosures.

Bull factors

Margin recovery trend

The operating margin rose from -5.6% in the year ended March 2023 to 6.4% in the year ended March 2026, and operating profit and controlling-shareholder net income have risen for two consecutive years since the year ended March 2025.

The company attributes the improvement to cutting low-priced projects and expanding value-added services. Controlling-shareholder net income moved from a net loss of KRW 9.59 billion to KRW 6.09 billion over the same span.

Overseas sales target

The company targets raising overseas sales from KRW 9.8 billion in the year ended March 2026 to more than KRW 20 billion in the year ended March 2027.

A next-generation HIS contract with a Saudi private hospital group, worth about KRW 10 billion, serves as a base, while cloud HIS projects are being pursued in the United States and Japan. Progress toward the target will appear in quarterly results.

Recurring revenue base and backlog

At the end of the year ended March 2025, HIS development backlog stood at KRW 22.9 billion and HIS operation backlog at KRW 35.6 billion, both higher than a year earlier. HIS operation and maintenance revenue rose 5.1% to about KRW 39.5 billion, forming a recurring revenue base. The company says a larger recurring share can soften the volatility of new build-out revenue.

Bear factors

Sharp Q2 2026 quarterly decline

In Q2 2026, the drop in operating profit was far steeper than the drop in sales, so profitability weakened sharply. Controlling-shareholder net income fell 68.1% to KRW 723 million.

Against the Q1 2026 peak, quarter-to-quarter swings are large, so whether the annual improvement holds at the quarterly level is not yet confirmed.

Cash flow and leverage

The operating margin rose to 6.4% in the year ended March 2026 but remains in the low single digits. Operating cash flow turned to -KRW 1.24 billion from KRW 10.9 billion a year earlier.

The debt-to-equity ratio rose to 43.4% from 33.0%, and the gap between earnings and cash flow is not explained by the disclosed figures.

Reported delays in overseas projects

A press report said one Saudi project was briefly suspended over a dispute with the client about bidder eligibility. Because the overseas target is tied to the double-digit margin goal, delays could weigh on the wider year ending March 2027 plan. As the company cuts low-margin build-out work, a decline in that revenue could also lower near-term sales.

Risk factors

Order and project timing risk

HIS build-out revenue arrives project by project, so a large project ending or slipping can create a revenue gap. A press report of a Saudi project suspension makes its restart a key variable. The mix is heavy in public orders, so budgets and tender schedules also affect revenue.

Overseas operating environment risk

Overseas revenue depends on client procurement, regulation and policy in each country, including Saudi Arabia, the United States and Japan. The planned expansion of overseas sales requires contract timing to line up across markets. Currency movements can also affect overseas earnings.

Earnings volatility and balance-sheet risk

Q2 2026 operating profit fell to KRW 276 million, confirming earnings volatility. Operating cash flow was negative in the year ended March 2026, and the debt-to-equity ratio rose to 43.4%. The causes and persistence of these items are to be checked in the quarterly and annual reports.

Overall view

ezCaretech's revenue, operating profit and controlling-shareholder net income have risen for two consecutive years since the trough in the year ended March 2024, and the operating margin reached 6.4% in the year ended March 2026.

The company attributes the recovery to cutting low-priced projects and a maintenance revenue base. However, sharp declines in Q2 2026 sales and operating profit from the prior quarter mean the durability of the recovery is not yet confirmed at the quarterly level.

Operating cash flow turned negative in the year ended March 2026, and the debt-to-equity ratio rose to 43.4%. The overseas sales target of more than KRW 20 billion and the double-digit margin goal for the year ending March 2027 are company targets that have not yet appeared in reported results.

Strengths include the earnings recovery, the recurring revenue base and overseas contracts, while weaknesses include quarterly volatility, cash flow and the reported project suspension.

The next checkpoints are the overseas sales progress in the Q4 2026 quarterly report and the operating margin in the annual report for the year ending March 2027.
