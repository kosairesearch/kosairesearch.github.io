02

Business structure

Shinyoung Securities is a financial investment firm founded in 1956 that runs brokerage, investment banking, advisory, discretionary asset management, trust and collective investment businesses. Its unlisted affiliates include Shinyoung Asset Management and Shinyoung Real Estate Trust.

Earnings lean heavily on the trading and investment book. A 2026 credit assessment by Korea Investment Service Ratings put the reliance on that segment at roughly 60 to 70 percent of operating net revenue, and the trading book is weighted toward government bonds and high-grade corporate bonds.

For full-year 2025, reported trading-segment operating net revenue was KRW 202.0 billion against KRW 59.9 billion for investment banking. About 84 percent of liabilities are linked to customers, while self-funded debt is around 44 percent of equity.

The company's brokerage market share has been small, and in 2026 it leaned on its leadership change to a co-CEO structure from June, with IB and wealth management as the growth priorities. Disclosed segment sales figures were not confirmed in the search results, so no segment revenue mix is given here.

04

Earnings analysis

On the annual filed figures, sales rose from KRW 2.35 trillion in 2023 to KRW 2.76 trillion in 2024, an increase of 17.5 percent, then fell to KRW 2.47 trillion in 2025, a decrease of 10.8 percent. Of the 2023 to 2025 period, 2024 was the largest year for sales.

Operating profit was KRW 115.6 billion in 2023, KRW 170.4 billion in 2024, up 47.3 percent, and KRW 136.1 billion in 2025, down 20.1 percent, with operating margins of 4.9 percent, 6.2 percent and 5.5 percent.

Profit attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 96.4 billion in 2023, KRW 142.6 billion in 2024 and KRW 108.6 billion in 2025, with 2024 the largest year. Net profit including non-controlling interests was KRW 112.3 billion in 2025, a separate figure from the controlling-shareholder number.

Operating cash flow moved from KRW 518.4 billion inflow in 2023 to outflows of KRW 664.5 billion in 2024 and KRW 442.1 billion in 2025.

At the end of 2025, total equity was KRW 1.83 trillion, of which KRW 1.76 trillion belonged to controlling shareholders, and total liabilities were KRW 8.55 trillion; the debt-to-equity ratio fell from 564.3 percent in 2023 to 466.4 percent in 2025.

Quarterly, operating profit was KRW 36 billion in the second quarter of 2025 and KRW 37 billion in the third, then the fourth quarter shifted to an operating loss of KRW 12.6 billion.

The first quarter of 2026 returned to operating profit of KRW 121.1 billion on sales of KRW 1.66 trillion, up 314.6 percent from the prior quarter.

The second quarter of 2026 brought operating profit of KRW 54.2 billion, down 55.2 percent from the prior quarter and up 50.4 percent year on year, with controlling-shareholder net profit of KRW 40.5 billion.

The first-quarter swing was driven mainly by the trading book: on a separate basis for the fiscal first quarter of 2026, trading income was KRW 127.3 billion against fee income of KRW 46.7 billion.

The same reporting also shows that expanding the trading book raised losses on the separate basis for the full year to March 2026. Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders over the most recent four quarters totals KRW 151.3 billion. The cause of the second-quarter decline in 2026 was not confirmed in the search results.

Industry analysis

Within Korea's brokerage sector, Shinyoung Securities leans on trading, asset management and specialized investment banking rather than brokerage volume. The IPO market contracted in 2026.

Total public offering volume was lower than in 2025, and monthly offering amounts in August 2026 were well below the August average. Shares of six newly listed companies in August 2026 averaged a negative return of 23.6 percent from the offering price at month-end.

Shinyoung's IPO offering value rose from KRW 107.1 billion in 2023 and KRW 106.8 billion in 2024 to KRW 591.7 billion in 2025, but it had no new IPO mandates in 2026 as of September.

Among peers, NH Investment & Securities led with eight 2026 IPO mandates, followed by Mirae Asset Securities with seven, and Samsung Securities with five. Outlook

No numerical company guidance was confirmed in the search results. The clearest direction is the diversification of investment banking and an organizational reset.

The company is segmenting corporate finance coverage, strengthening securitization and advisory work, and widening its scope into private equity and venture funds, as well as developing DCM, structured finance, real estate finance and private equity investing to reduce dependence on any single business.

Since June 2026 the company has run under a co-CEO structure, with one co-CEO heading IB and the other leading wealth management and customized client solutions. An internal control committee under the board was created in 2025.

IB profitability recovery remains an open task: underwriting and arrangement fees fell 20.68 percent in 2025 to KRW 17.2 billion, and corporate finance operating profit in the first quarter of 2026 fell from KRW 8.5 billion to KRW 3.0 billion. Three items are worth tracking.

The fiscal third-quarter report covering October to December 2026 is due around mid-February 2027, the fiscal 2026 annual report is due around late June 2027, and any resumption of IPO mandates will show up in disclosures and press coverage during the remainder of 2026. Bull factors

Quarterly earnings turned around

Second-quarter 2026 operating profit of KRW 54.2 billion was up 50.4 percent from a year earlier. Supporters of the stock point to this recovery as the starting point of their case.

Balance sheet improvement

The debt-to-equity ratio fell from 564.3 percent in 2023 to 466.4 percent in 2025. Press reports cite an analysis that low-risk assets, including high-grade corporate bonds, make up about 70 percent of total assets. Supporters cite this easing in the debt structure.

ECM track record

The company acted as lead underwriter on six listings in 2023, five in 2024 and six in 2025, and offering value rose to KRW 591.7 billion in 2025. The company says it has built a differentiated equity capital markets position through careful deal selection and risk management. Supporters hinge their case on whether this converts into a steady fee stream.

Bear factors

Dependence on trading gains

An assessment puts reliance on the trading book at around 60 to 70 percent of operating net revenue. As the book expanded, losses rose alongside gains. On a separate basis at the end of March 2026, derivatives valuation and trading losses reached KRW 1.54 trillion, up from KRW 912.9 billion a year earlier. The bear case centers on earnings that swing with market conditions.

IB profitability and the IPO gap

The company had no new IPO mandates in 2026 as of a September 2026 press report. Underwriting and arrangement fees fell 20.68 percent in 2025. Corporate finance operating profit fell from KRW 8.5 billion to KRW 3.0 billion in the first quarter of 2026. Even as total company profit improved, IB profitability is a separate unresolved issue.

Low market share and fee weakness

Brokerage market share is small, and the gap with large peers in investment banking persists. Declining guarantee fees have reduced IB results. Because the core fee base is thin, total results have repeatedly swung with trading performance, according to press analysis.

Risk factors

Rates and market volatility

The large bond book makes earnings sensitive to rate moves, and the rating agency noted that rate risk needs continuous management. Derivatives trading and valuation losses have grown alongside the book, showing exposure to market swings.

Deal pipeline in corporate finance

A shrinking IPO market makes it hard for mid-sized brokers to secure new mandates, with August 2026 offering volume well below the August average. Past annual results moved sharply on a single large deal, so steady deal flow is the central question.

Internal controls and product sales

A September 2026 press report flagged outstanding Homeplus short-term bonds and raised product-explanation and sales-suitability questions. The company created an internal control committee in 2025. With total liabilities at KRW 8.55 trillion, the financial effect of any product-sales disputes merits monitoring.

Overall view

Second-quarter 2026 operating profit of KRW 54.2 billion was up 50.4 percent year on year but down 55.2 percent from the prior quarter. Much of the earnings come from the trading book, and losses on that book have grown along with its size.
