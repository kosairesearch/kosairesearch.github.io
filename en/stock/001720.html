02

Business structure

Shinyoung Securities runs brokerage, proprietary trading, corporate finance and asset management, with asset management subsidiary Shinyoung Asset Management and real estate trust affiliate Shinyoung Real Estate Trust.

In first-half fiscal 2026 (April–September 2026) disclosures, brokerage operating profit reached KRW 37.6 billion, up 85.6% year on year, and accounted for about 27% of the total operating profit increase of KRW 63.5 billion.

Commission income from brokerage rose 127.6% to KRW 32.2 billion, and commentary linked this to higher stock trading activity. Proprietary trading operating profit was KRW 57.7 billion, up 24.8%, reflecting financial product valuation and disposal gains alongside derivatives trading results.

Corporate finance operating profit rose 62.0% to KRW 16.5 billion, but underwriting and arrangement fees fell from KRW 9.6 billion to KRW 5.6 billion, so the income streams diverged.

Shinyoung Asset Management's first-half operating profit rose 37.8% to KRW 25 billion, and Shinyoung Real Estate Trust rose from KRW 1 billion to KRW 4.2 billion.

Collective investment management fees increased from KRW 8.8 billion to KRW 24.1 billion, and trust fees from KRW 13.4 billion to KRW 18.8 billion, so asset management income carries a larger share.

In July–September 2026, derivatives valuation and trading gains of KRW 613.9 billion and FX trading gains of KRW 231.1 billion were large parts of KRW 1.25 trillion in operating revenue, which shows a mix tilted toward trading and valuation gains.

Competitors include Mirae Asset Securities, Samsung Securities, NH Investment & Securities, Korea Investment Holdings and KIWOOM Securities, all contesting brokerage and proprietary trading.

04

Earnings analysis

On an annual basis, revenue went from KRW 2.35 trillion in the year ended March 2023 to KRW 2.76 trillion in the year ended March 2024 (up 17.5%), fell to KRW 2.47 trillion in the year ended March 2025 (down 10.8%), and reached KRW 3.17 trillion in the year ended March 2026 (up 28.6%), the largest year of the four.

Operating profit was KRW 115.6 billion, KRW 170.4 billion (up 47.3%), KRW 136.1 billion (down 20.1%) and KRW 195.8 billion (up 43.8%) across the same years.

Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders reached KRW 149.2 billion in the year ended March 2026, up 37.3%, while total net profit including non-controlling interests was KRW 156.1 billion.

The operating margin moved from 4.9% to 6.2%, 5.5% and 6.2%, so the year ended March 2026 returned to the level of the year ended March 2024. Operating cash flow shifted from KRW 518.4 billion in the year ended March 2023 to -KRW 664.5 billion, -KRW 442.1 billion and -KRW 978 billion in the three following years.

Broker cash flows swing with trading position size, so the gap between profit and cash flow reads as a feature of the sector. The quarterly pattern is steeper.

Revenue rose from KRW 458.5 billion in July–September 2025 to KRW 1.66 trillion in April–June 2026 before falling 24.7% to KRW 1.25 trillion in July–September 2026. Operating profit peaked at KRW 121.1 billion in April–June 2026 and fell 55.2% to KRW 54.2 billion in July–September 2026.

Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 90.8 billion and KRW 40.5 billion in those two quarters, and the four quarters from October 2025 to September 2026 total KRW 191.8 billion.

In July–September 2026, derivatives valuation and trading gains of KRW 613.9 billion (up 233.2% year on year) and FX trading gains of KRW 231.1 billion (a swing from a KRW 51 billion loss) drove revenue, and fee income rose 78.0% to KRW 74.8 billion.

Operating expenses, however, rose 183.4% to KRW 1,197.3 billion, faster than revenue, and derivatives valuation and trading losses widened to KRW 524.8 billion. Whether these items are one-off cannot be settled from the filings alone, but the direction of derivatives and FX results clearly determines quarterly profit.

Industry analysis

The brokerage sector's earnings swing sharply with trading volumes, financial market volatility and interest rate levels. First-half fiscal 2026 disclosure commentary indicates that fee and asset management income supported results even as equity market volatility rose.

Commentary repeatedly linked higher brokerage commissions to more active stock trading. Rate moves produced bond valuation losses, but bond interest income offset part of them.

In July–September 2026, FX trading moved from a KRW 51 billion loss a year earlier to a KRW 231.1 billion gain, which shows how quickly results can shift with currency and derivatives positions. Profit rose in a phase where trading and volatility increased together.

Whether brokerage commissions hold up when trading volumes weaken cannot be judged from the filings released so far. Listed peers include Mirae Asset Securities, Samsung Securities, NH Investment & Securities, Korea Investment Holdings, KIWOOM Securities, Daishin Securities and Hanwha INVESTMENT&SECURITIES.
