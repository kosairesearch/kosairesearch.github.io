Ksign: Profit Rebound and the PQC Transition Test

02

Business structure

Ksign is a information security company founded in November 1999 that runs personal data encryption, PKI authentication, integrated authentication, mobile security and AI security businesses. The company names three core product lines.

The first is PKI and authentication solutions that handle authentication and e-signatures in large user environments. The second is the HSM and cryptographic module line for data encryption and key management.

The third is an AI-based privacy protection solution that detects and de-identifies personal information in structured data. At the center of revenue is SecureDB, a database encryption product. According to a THE ELEC report, SecureDB generated KRW 25.2 billion in 2025, accounting for 52.21% of total company revenue.

Customers include public institutions, financial companies and private enterprises. In its January 2026 New Year interview, the company said it would keep public and financial sectors as its core while expanding into manufacturing, enterprise and healthcare.

Ksign is also carrying out a DB encryption upgrade project for Samsung Electronics. On technology, it has launched KSign CASE for PQC and KSign PKI for PQC, which are under pilot deployment with public institutions. Its competitive peers include Ahnlab in the security sector.

04

Earnings analysis

On a disclosed basis, annual revenue rose from KRW 43.2 billion in 2022 to KRW 47.1 billion in 2023 (up 9.1% year on year) and KRW 51.9 billion in 2024 (up 10.1%), then fell to KRW 48.4 billion in 2025 (down 6.7%). Among 2022 to 2025, 2024 was the largest year and 2022 the smallest. Profit followed a different path.

Operating profit fell from KRW 9.16 billion in 2022 to KRW 4.26 billion in 2023 (down 53.5%) and KRW 1.31 billion in 2024 (down 69.4%), then rebounded to KRW 2.9 billion in 2025 (up 121.7%). The operating margin declined from 21.2% in 2022 to 9.1% in 2023 and 2.5% in 2024, then recovered to 6.0% in 2025.

Margin was lowest in 2024 even though revenue peaked that year, and the disclosed figures do not break down cost items, so the cause cannot be split out in numbers.

Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 5.01 billion in 2022, KRW 4.55 billion in 2023 (down 9.2%), KRW 1.67 billion in 2024 (down 63.4%) and KRW 2.4 billion in 2025 (up 43.8%). Net profit including non-controlling interests fell to KRW 434 million in 2024 before rising to KRW 2.16 billion in 2025.

By quarter, the third quarter of 2025 showed revenue of KRW 7.1 billion, an operating loss of KRW 1.48 billion and a net loss attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 1.14 billion.

The fourth quarter of 2025 showed revenue of KRW 21.9 billion, operating profit of KRW 5.98 billion and net profit attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 4.56 billion, a swing into profit.

In the first quarter of 2026 revenue was KRW 7.75 billion (down 64.7% from the prior quarter), operating profit KRW 241 million and net profit attributable to controlling shareholders KRW 700 million.

The second quarter of 2026 recovered to revenue of KRW 9.94 billion, operating profit of KRW 936 million and net profit attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 1.21 billion.

The third quarter of 2026 brought revenue of KRW 10.6 billion (up 6.3% from the prior quarter and up 49.0% year on year), a second straight quarterly increase.

Operating profit of KRW 650 million was down 30.6% from the prior quarter, and the company recorded a net loss attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 19.31 million, a continued loss with a smaller deficit than a year earlier.

Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders over the last four quarters totaled KRW 6.45 billion. A Ksign subsidiary, Sands Lab, said in a 2025 report that revenue from its NDR product MNX fell short of expectations, pushing operating profit into a loss.

05

Industry analysis

The company's CEO described the 2026 security market as shifting from simple defense toward proactive prevention and structural security transformation. Ksign names post-quantum cryptography (PQC), AI security including defense against AI-driven attacks, and advanced data and key management as core technologies.

The company expects customers to invest more from a long-term security architecture perspective rather than buying individual solutions.

The institutional driver for PQC demand is the national cryptographic transition schedule, and the company has pointed to a goal of fully transitioning the national cryptographic system by 2035. A WOWTALE report cited a forecast of a 37.8% compound annual growth rate for the PQC transition market from 2026 to 2033.

Domestic DB encryption and PKI demand is tied to public security budgets and personal data protection rules, so demand tends to follow public and financial procurement timing.

The PQC market can be viewed as being in an early transition stage, and the size of demand will depend on national projects and results from public pilot deployments. On competition, Ksign is building PQC references through public pilot deployments and its role as lead institution in national projects.

Ahnlab is among the peers in the security sector used for comparison. The company said it is reviewing the possibility of overseas expansion with global security firms and partners but has not fixed a timing.

06

Outlook

The company plans to roll out upgraded versions of its existing PKI and cryptography products that apply PQC algorithms in stages during 2026. It is also preparing new security functions and products that reflect AI-driven attack scenarios, but the launch timing has not been disclosed.

According to a THE ELEC report, the Samsung Electronics DB encryption upgrade started earlier in 2026, and the company expects to recognize revenue from the fourth quarter. Because the contract size was not disclosed, the revenue contribution will become visible only in quarterly results.

The national PQC core technology project runs through December 2029 with a total of KRW 5.25 billion, of which KRW 4.5 billion is government funding, and aims to develop a platform that automates cryptographic transitions.

In the space-sector PQC pilot transition project, Ksign is carrying out PQC demonstrations across the satellite-to-ground segment together with Contec. For overseas markets, the company is reviewing entry possibilities through cooperation with global security companies and overseas partners, but no timing has been set.

A September 2026 report said the Open Charge Alliance, an international EV charging standards body, would review Ksign's fuzzing tool for EV chargers.

The next confirmation points are whether Samsung Electronics DB encryption revenue appears from the fourth quarter of 2026, and the quarterly progress of the PQC project.

08

Bull factors

Lead role in national PQC projects

Ksign was named lead institution of a national PQC core technology project in July 2026 and was also selected as lead institution for a space-sector PQC pilot transition project in the same period. The project is worth KRW 5.25 billion, of which KRW 4.5 billion is government funding.

The bull case rests on its 2025 PQC transition in the energy sector, which gives it a base for building public-sector references.

Earnings turnaround

Operating profit rebounded 121.7% to KRW 2.9 billion in 2025, and third-quarter 2026 revenue reached KRW 10.6 billion, up 49.0% from a year earlier. Operating profit of KRW 650 million in the same quarter compares with an operating loss a year earlier. If revenue growth converts into profit more consistently, earnings strength would improve.

Expansion with large customers

According to a THE ELEC report, the Samsung Electronics DB encryption upgrade was contracted separately from existing agreements, and the company expects to recognize revenue from the fourth quarter.

The contract size was not disclosed, but widening encryption coverage within an existing customer is a basis for additional orders. Combined with PQC references, the scope for new orders built on the existing customer base could widen.

Bear factors

Thin profit margins

The operating margin fell from 21.2% in 2022 to 2.5% in 2024 and reached only 6.0% in 2025. Third-quarter 2026 operating profit of KRW 650 million remains narrow against KRW 10.6 billion in revenue.

The net loss attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 19.31 million in the same quarter shows that earnings stability is not yet confirmed.

Revenue volatility and quarterly concentration

Revenue fell 6.7% to KRW 48.4 billion in 2025. Fourth-quarter 2025 revenue of KRW 21.9 billion was far above the KRW 7.1 billion of the third quarter, and first-quarter 2026 revenue fell 64.7% from the prior quarter to KRW 7.75 billion. Results depend heavily on when large projects are recognized.

Subsidiary losses and timing of new businesses

A 2025 report said that revenue from MNX, an NDR solution at subsidiary Sands Lab, fell short of expectations, pushing operating profit into a loss. The national PQC project is worth KRW 5.25 billion, and no timing has been set for overseas expansion. The bear case is that these new businesses will take time to become meaningful earnings contributors.

10

Risk factors

Customer and contract concentration

According to a THE ELEC report, SecureDB accounted for 52.21% of 2025 revenue, so product concentration is high. The Samsung Electronics order's contract size has not been disclosed, which makes its revenue timing and scale hard to gauge from outside. A change in public procurement schedules could shake quarterly results.

Competition in technology transition

The PQC transition market depends on national schedules and the finalization of standards. If the 2035 full-transition target changes, project demand could shift too. The commercial results of the automated platform will only be fully visible after the project ends in December 2029.

Subsidiary and balance sheet

Losses at subsidiary Sands Lab were cited in a 2025 report as a factor behind an operating loss, and subsidiary swings flow through to net profit attributable to controlling shareholders. Total liabilities at the end of 2025 were KRW 75.4 billion, and the debt-to-equity ratio was 62.1%. Capital is substantial, but financial headroom will feel different if earnings weaken.

Overall view

Ksign is an information security company centered on DB encryption and PKI authentication. On a disclosed basis, 2025 revenue fell to KRW 48.4 billion, but operating profit rebounded to KRW 2.9 billion.

Third-quarter 2026 revenue reached KRW 10.6 billion, up 49.0% year on year, and operating profit of KRW 650 million was in the black, but a net loss attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 19.31 million remains, so earnings stability is still being confirmed.

The bull case rests on the lead role in a national PQC core technology project, an additional Samsung Electronics DB encryption order and the growth outlook for the PQC market.

The bear case rests on thin earnings strength, with the operating margin down to 2.5% in 2024, concentrated quarterly revenue, past subsidiary losses and uncertain timing for monetizing new businesses.

On the balance sheet, the debt-to-equity ratio fell from 99.8% in 2022 to 62.1% in 2025, and total equity at the end of 2025 was KRW 121.5 billion. The share price sits below book value per share, but earnings multiples swing with quarterly results, so no single metric settles the picture.

The next checkpoints are preliminary fourth-quarter and full-year 2026 results, the operating margin and operating cash flow in the 2026 annual report, and the quarterly progress of the PQC project.
