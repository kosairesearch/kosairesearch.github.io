02

Business structure

Emnet is a digital integrated marketing company founded in 2000 and listed on KOSDAQ in 2011. Based on Q2 2026 revenue, the mix is 90% ad agency, 7% production, services and events, and 3% rental income.

The ad-agency business handles online ad execution and performance management, alongside ad production and analytics solutions. The company says its proprietary solutions are patented in Korea and Japan, and it stresses presenting ad performance in measurable terms.

In 2021 it diversified into e-commerce through Dbrooks and acquired Comas Interactive. It is now expanding AI-based ad optimization and generative-AI-driven personalized ad message creation, while search ads and performance marketing continue to grow.

By region, in 2019 the Japanese subsidiary generated KRW 12.47 billion, or 34.0% of consolidated revenue of KRW 36.7 billion. On the client side, the company plans to strengthen its large-advertiser portfolio and widen coverage of mid-sized and small advertisers through subsidiary competitiveness.

Among peers, large agencies such as Cheil Worldwide and Innocean Worldwide have broader full-service capabilities, while Emnet can be viewed as a smaller player specializing in online performance advertising. Its high ad-agency share means advertiser budget changes feed quickly into revenue.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual revenue fell from KRW 44.2 billion in 2022 to KRW 35 billion in 2023, a 20.8% decrease, then recovered to KRW 36.3 billion in 2024, a 3.7% increase. It declined again to KRW 32.3 billion in 2025, an 11.2% decrease. Within 2022 to 2025, 2022 was the largest year and 2025 the smallest.

Operating profit was KRW 9.33 billion in 2022, KRW 3.67 billion in 2023 (down 60.7%), KRW 4.76 billion in 2024 (up 29.8%), and KRW 274 million in 2025 (down 94.2%). The operating margin moved from 21.1% in 2022 to 10.5% in 2023, 13.1% in 2024, and 0.9% in 2025.

Profit attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 6.72 billion, KRW 4.04 billion, KRW 5.75 billion, and KRW 1.96 billion for 2022 to 2025, with 2025 down 65.9%. Net income including non-controlling interests was KRW 1.85 billion in 2025.

That 2025 controlling-shareholder profit far exceeded operating profit suggests non-operating items supported net income, and their composition is a point to check in the next annual report notes.

For the first nine months of 2025, the company cited weaker advertiser marketing budgets amid an economic slowdown as the reason ad-agency revenue fell. Quarterly, Q3 2025 revenue was KRW 7.93 billion with an operating loss of KRW 11.79 million.

Q4 2025 revenue rose 6.8% quarter on quarter to KRW 8.47 billion, with operating profit of KRW 606 million, a return to profit. Q1 2026 revenue was flat at KRW 8.47 billion and operating profit was KRW 237 million, down 61.0%.

Q2 2026 revenue was KRW 8.19 billion, down 3.4%, and operating profit was KRW 197 million, down 17.0%. Q3 2026 revenue was KRW 7.83 billion, down 4.4% quarter on quarter and 1.3% year on year, with an operating loss of KRW 80.02 million, a return to loss.

Profit attributable to controlling shareholders over the latest four quarters totaled KRW 2.04 billion. Operating cash flow was KRW 5.84 billion in 2022, KRW 8.32 billion in 2023, and KRW 10.7 billion in 2024, then turned to -KRW 3.77 billion in 2025.

05

Industry analysis

In its Q1 2025 results commentary, the company said the digital ad market was growing, but an economic slowdown and weaker consumer sentiment reduced its revenue. For the first nine months of 2025, cuts in advertisers' marketing budgets and ad spending were again cited as the cause of lower ad-agency revenue.

Across the industry, AI-based ad optimization and performance-focused marketing are strengthening, and generative-AI-driven ad message creation is spreading. The company's plan also references strategies aligned with digital ad trends, including RMN advertising.

Large agencies such as Cheil Worldwide and Innocean Worldwide sit in a position to absorb demand from large advertisers through full-service capabilities. Emnet, focused on online ad performance management, can be viewed as structurally sensitive to changes in advertiser budgets.

In cycle terms, after the 2025 slowdown, revenue peaked in Q1 2026 and has fallen for two consecutive quarters. The plan to expand coverage of mid-sized and small advertisers can be read as an effort to reduce reliance on large advertisers.

06

Outlook

The value-up plan disclosed on April 7, 2026 set out three main pillars: continuity of shareholder return policy, growth and profitability improvement, and strengthening the medium- to long-term growth base.

Growth tasks include strengthening the large-advertiser portfolio, expanding coverage of mid-sized and small advertisers, and improving subsidiary revenue structures.

The plan is directional and contains no numerical targets, and the company said it includes forward-looking statements that may change with market conditions. For the medium to long term, it cited upgrading AI- and data-driven marketing capabilities and responding to RMN advertising.

A September 13, 2026 disclosure showed a decision to dispose of 315,722 treasury shares, worth KRW 2.627 billion, through an off-market block trade, with a disposal window from September 14 to October 13, 2026. The stated purpose was to expand tradable supply and secure liquidity.

In March 2026, the company disclosed that submission of its audit report was delayed due to delays in audit procedures at an affiliate. The 2026 annual report will confirm full-year operating margin and controlling-shareholder profit.

Because operating results turned to a loss in Q3 2026, the direction of Q4 operating profit is the key point to watch. Progress on the value-up plan can be tracked through revenue mix and subsidiary profit changes in the annual report.

Asset base and modest leverage

Total equity at end-2025 was KRW 99.2 billion, of which KRW 95.7 billion belongs to controlling shareholders. Total liabilities were KRW 38.1 billion, and the debt ratio fell from 40.9% in 2024 to 38.4% in 2025. The book asset base is cited as a defensive factor for financial stability. Financial headroom widens operating flexibility during an ad-market slowdown.

Profit held in Q1 and Q2 2026

Operating profit was KRW 237 million in Q1 2026 and KRW 197 million in Q2, two consecutive profitable quarters. After a return to profit with KRW 606 million in Q4 2025, quarterly profit held until the Q3 2026 operating loss. Sustained quarterly profit can be read as a sign of cost discipline. Profit margins, however, have narrowed from the Q4 2025 level.

Shareholder return and value-up plan

On April 7, 2026, the company voluntarily disclosed a value-up plan that placed shareholder return continuity at its core. It stated that high-dividend status is its own determination. In September 2026 it decided to dispose of 315,722 treasury shares through an off-market block trade to expand tradable supply. Execution of the plan will be tested by results reported in annual filings.

Bear factors

Sharp operating profit decline and thin margin

Operating profit fell 94.2% to KRW 274 million in 2025, and the operating margin dropped to 0.9%. Against a 21.1% margin in 2022, the decline in profitability is steep. Profit attributable to controlling shareholders fell 65.9% to KRW 1.96 billion. With a thin profit base, small cost changes can swing results sharply.

Q3 2026 operating loss and falling revenue

Q3 2026 revenue was KRW 7.83 billion, declining for a second consecutive quarter and down 1.3% from a year earlier. The company posted an operating loss of KRW 80.02 million. Profit attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 534 million fell 27.4% quarter on quarter.

Because net profit remained positive despite the operating loss, whether non-operating items grew in weight is the point to check.

Ad budget cuts and cash flow reversal

Cumulative results through Q3 2025 show ad-agency revenue falling as advertisers cut marketing budgets. Operating cash flow fell from KRW 10.7 billion in 2024 to -KRW 3.77 billion in 2025. If operating cash generation weakens further, questions about dividend sustainability could grow.

Risk factors

Ad cycle and advertiser budgets

Ad-agency revenue accounts for close to 90% of the total, so advertiser budget changes feed directly into results. Economic weakness reduced ad spending in 2025, and quarterly revenue has fallen for two consecutive quarters since peaking in Q1 2026.

If large-advertiser wins slow or expansion among smaller advertisers is delayed, revenue recovery could be slower.

Audit and affiliate risk

In March 2026 the company disclosed that its audit report submission could be delayed because audit procedures at an affiliate were late. External audit rules require the audit report to be submitted one week before the regular shareholders' meeting.

The company said it would disclose the report immediately upon receipt. How affiliate equity investments are valued and reflected in profit could affect the reading of consolidated results.

Technology shifts and platform response

Digital ad trends are shifting quickly, including AI-based ad optimization, generative-AI message creation, and RMN advertising. The company included AI and data capability upgrades and RMN responses in its plan, but said the plan contains forward-looking statements that may change.

Because large full-service agencies and platform operators compete in similar areas, response speed is the key issue.

The April 2026 value-up plan emphasized dividend continuity and profitability led by large advertisers, but it is directional and contains no numerical targets.

On valuation, the share price sits well below book value per share, while profit attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 2.04 billion over the latest four quarters leaves a thin base, so earnings-based multiples depend on whether profit recovers.
