Report overview

Trading Slowdown Dents Q3, Annual Profit Still Climbs

Operating profit rose for a third straight year to KRW 2.35 trillion in 2025, but in the third quarter of 2026 net income attributable to controlling shareholders fell to KRW 282.3 billion on weaker investment income and foreign-currency valuation losses.

  2. 2

  3. 3

  4. 4

02

Business structure

Korea Investment Holdings is a financial holding company with subsidiaries in securities, asset management, savings banking and capital finance, and its core subsidiary is Korea Investment & Securities.

Korea Investment & Securities reports revenue across brokerage, investment banking (IB), wealth management (WM) and investment operations, and brokerage and investment operations drive most of the swings in earnings as market conditions change.

Brokerage fee income in the third quarter of 2026 was KRW 287.2 billion, down 36.5% from the prior quarter, as average daily trading value fell 29.9% from KRW 28.65 trillion to KRW 20.1 trillion.

IB income for the same quarter rose 12% to KRW 216.4 billion, supported by equity capital markets work, rights offerings and domestic bond underwriting.

Individual clients' financial product balances reached KRW 108.5 trillion at the end of the third quarter of 2026, up about KRW 3.5 trillion from the end of the second quarter, with roughly KRW 2.8 trillion of the increase in bill-issuance, repurchase agreement (RP) and Integrated Investment Account (IMA) balances.

Bill-issuance balances stood at KRW 24.5 trillion at the end of the third quarter of 2026, up 30.9% from the end of the third quarter of 2025 and about 181% of equity.

Among subsidiaries, the savings bank posted a net loss of KRW 13 billion in the third quarter of 2026 with its delinquency ratio rising to 9.07%, and the value asset management unit posted a net loss of about KRW 40 billion. The capital subsidiary, by contrast, earned KRW 21.3 billion in net income.

An October 2026 press report put the group's quarterly ETF market share at 22% to 23%, which it said keeps the group first in the industry. Against peers, the third-quarter brokerage decline compares with a drop of close to 50% reported by NH Investment & Securities and other competitors that reported earlier.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual operating profit rose from KRW 545.8 billion in 2022 to KRW 820.4 billion in 2023, KRW 1.2 trillion in 2024 and KRW 2.35 trillion in 2025, and 2025 was up 95.5% and was the largest year of the 2022–2025 period.

Operating profit grew for three consecutive years from 2023 through 2025, and net income attributable to controlling shareholders also moved in the same direction, from KRW 636.8 billion in 2022 to KRW 2.02 trillion in 2025.

Net income including non-controlling interests was KRW 2.02 trillion in 2025 and is a separate figure from net income attributable to controlling shareholders.

Revenue fell from KRW 22.9 trillion in 2022 to KRW 20.9 trillion in 2023, down 8.9%, then recovered to KRW 21.2 trillion in 2024, up 1.3%, while the operating margin rose from 2.4% to 3.9% and then 5.7% over the same period.

Operating cash flow was negative in each of the four years, at -KRW 4.59 trillion in 2022, -KRW 7.29 trillion in 2023, -KRW 7.77 trillion in 2024 and -KRW 7.61 trillion in 2025, in parallel with the growth of bill-issuance balances.

The debt-to-equity ratio at the end of 2025 was 1,025.4%, with total equity of KRW 12.1 trillion, equity attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 12 trillion and total liabilities of KRW 124.1 trillion.

Quarterly operating profit went from KRW 849.1 billion in the third quarter of 2025 to KRW 381.1 billion in the fourth quarter (down 55.1% from the prior quarter), then recovered to KRW 1.11 trillion in the first quarter of 2026 and KRW 1.34 trillion in the second quarter, before falling to KRW 300.4 billion in the third quarter (down 77.7% from the prior quarter and down 64.6% from a year earlier).

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 914.9 billion in the first quarter of 2026, KRW 995.9 billion in the second and KRW 282.3 billion in the third, and the four quarters from the fourth quarter of 2025 through the third quarter of 2026 totaled KRW 2.54 trillion.

The company cited the narrower bill-issuance margin, weaker bond investment results and foreign-currency asset valuation losses as the causes of the third-quarter decline.

In preliminary consolidated results announced on November 5, 2026, third-quarter revenue was KRW 11.2 trillion (preliminary), up 85.73% year on year, though the filing noted that figures may change before independent audit review.

For the first quarter of 2026, an analysis from May 2026 attributed the strong result to higher valuation gains at non-securities subsidiaries alongside the securities unit.

05

Industry analysis

The brokerage industry is sensitive to domestic stock trading value, and in the third quarter of 2026 the slowdown in trading appeared across the sector.

Korea Investment & Securities' average daily brokerage trading value fell 29.9% from the prior quarter, while brokerage fee declines at NH Investment & Securities and other competitors that reported earlier were close to 50%.

Rising interest rates and currency swings widened valuation losses on held assets over the same period. In bill issuance, new entrants including Samsung Securities and Meritz Securities made funding conditions harder, and the margin on bill-issuance business narrowed as a result.

In investment banking, equity capital markets and bond underwriting fees rose, which spread income sources somewhat. In May 2026, one brokerage analysis said the company, unlike some rivals, expanded credit-lending limits quickly and increased sales of asset management products.

The same analysis noted that a higher brokerage market share lifted fee income faster than trading value grew. The key variables for a sector recovery are a rebound in stock trading value, normalization of investment income and a better bill-issuance margin.

Because the group also holds savings bank, capital and asset management units, its earnings respond to market swings in a more mixed way than those of a pure securities firm.

Outlook

The direction the company has emphasized in confirmed sources is expanding funding and investment assets, centered on bill issuance and the Integrated Investment Account (IMA).

Individual clients' financial product balances were KRW 108.5 trillion at the end of the third quarter of 2026, and most of the increase came from bill-issuance, RP and IMA balances.

However, bill-issuance balances are about 181% of equity and close to the 200% funding limit, so further expansion depends on capital size and limit management.

On shareholder returns, NH Investment & Securities assessed in February 2026 that the 25.1% dividend payout ratio for 2025 met the requirements for separate taxation of dividend income.

One brokerage researcher said in a November 2026 report that the appeal of shareholder returns could partly offset the third-quarter decline.

Hana Securities, in a November 2026 report, said that even with the fourth quarter's trading volatility and seasonal costs considered, a large earnings improvement in a short period is hard to expect.

Because foreign-currency valuation gains and losses and bond investment results move with rates and exchange rates, whether investment income normalizes in the fourth quarter is the next checkpoint. Sustained ECM fee gains will determine how far income diversifies beyond brokerage.

The 2026 preliminary fourth-quarter results and the 2026 annual report are the next points at which reported results will be confirmed.

Bull factors

Annual earnings strength and margin gains

Operating profit rose from KRW 545.8 billion in 2022 to KRW 2.35 trillion in 2025 and grew for three straight years from 2023 through 2025. The operating margin improved from 2.4% in 2022 to 3.9% in 2023 and 5.7% in 2024.

In 2023 and 2024, profit growth clearly outpaced revenue growth, which shows margin gains in the reported figures.

Expanding funding and client assets

Bill-issuance balances reached KRW 24.5 trillion at the end of the third quarter of 2026, up 30.9% from the end of the third quarter of 2025. Individual clients' financial product balances rose to KRW 108.5 trillion, up about KRW 3.5 trillion from the end of the second quarter of 2026. The growth in wealth management balances points to a broader funding base.

Shareholder-return momentum

NH Investment & Securities, in a February 2026 analysis, said the 25.1% dividend payout ratio for 2025 met the requirements for separate taxation of dividend income. The same analysis viewed the move away from a previously passive stance on shareholder returns very positively.

In a November 2026 report, one brokerage researcher said the appeal of shareholder returns could partly offset the third-quarter decline.

09

Bear factors

Sharp Q3 profit drop and weak investment income

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 282.3 billion in the third quarter of 2026, down 71.7% from the prior quarter and down 58.1% from a year earlier. Operating profit was KRW 300.4 billion, down 77.7% from the prior quarter.

On a standalone basis at Korea Investment & Securities, operating net revenue from the investment unit was KRW 158.3 billion, down 51.6% from the prior quarter.

Bill-issuance margin and funding limit

The company cited a narrower net interest margin on bill issuance as a cause of weaker results. Analysts said new entrants including Samsung Securities and Meritz Securities made funding conditions harder.

With balances near 181% of equity against a 200% limit, some observers say profits have not kept pace with the larger funding base.

Foreign-currency losses and subsidiary weakness

Cumulative foreign-exchange trading income swung to a loss of KRW 204.9 billion through the third quarter of 2026, from a gain of KRW 143.6 billion in the same period of 2025.

The savings bank posted a KRW 13 billion net loss in the third quarter, and its delinquency ratio rose to 9.07% from 8.57% at the end of June 2026. The value asset management unit also posted a net loss of about KRW 40 billion.

10

Risk factors

Trading-value volatility

Brokerage income is tied directly to average daily trading value, which fell 29.9% from the prior quarter in the third quarter of 2026. If trading value falls again, commission income and IB demand could weaken together.

Competitors such as NH Investment & Securities also saw steep brokerage fee declines in the same period, so the risk is shared across the sector.

Rate and currency valuation swings

Foreign-currency asset valuation losses and weaker bond investment results pulled down third-quarter investment income. Because rising rates and currency moves feed directly into the valuation of held assets, quarterly earnings become more volatile. Investment income depends on market variables that the company cannot easily control.

Subsidiary asset quality and funding rules

The savings bank's delinquency ratio rose to 9.07%, and it set aside more provisions. Bill issuance is capped at 200% of equity, and balances are already close to that limit. If subsidiary losses coincide with changes in funding rules, group earnings could swing more widely.

Overall view

Korea Investment Holdings' reported results for 2022 through 2025 show operating profit and net income attributable to controlling shareholders rising for three straight years. Operating profit of KRW 2.35 trillion in 2025 was the largest of the period, and the operating margin rose from 2.4% in 2022 to 5.7% in 2024.

By contrast, net income attributable to controlling shareholders fell 71.7% from the prior quarter to KRW 282.3 billion in the third quarter of 2026, with weaker trading value, investment results and foreign-currency valuation losses cited as main causes.

Cumulative net income attributable to controlling shareholders for January–September 2026 was KRW 2.19 trillion, up year on year, but that figure is preliminary. Bill-issuance balances approached the funding limit at about 181% of equity, and individual clients' financial product balances continued to grow.

Operating cash flow was negative in all four reported years, and the debt-to-equity ratio was 1,025.4%. Brokerage target prices were lowered in a November 2026 round of reports.

The next checkpoints are the 2026 fourth-quarter preliminary results, the bill-issuance balance relative to the limit, and the dividend decision in the 2026 annual report.
