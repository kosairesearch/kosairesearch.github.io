02

Business structure

Socar is a shared-mobility platform that completed its IPO in August 2022, and its core business is 30-minute car sharing. Subscription and used-car commerce, the Modu Parking platform and the Ilecle micromobility unit form the other business pillars.

Car-sharing revenue in the first quarter of 2026 was KRW 72.1 billion, down 3.3% year on year. Subscription and commerce revenue fell 63.4% to KRW 18.7 billion in the same quarter, while its gross margin rose to 25.4%.

Press reports attribute this to fewer used-car disposals than in the peak season, a choice that favored profitability over volume. Modu Parking revenue grew 27% year on year in the same quarter.

At a temporary shareholders meeting in June 2026, shareholders approved amendments adding new-car sales, used-car sales and rental to the corporate purpose clause.

The Sopick long-term subscription service, launched on October 1, 2026, offers commitments of up to 60 months and allows a switch to another vehicle after at least one month. The company cites a fleet of 25,000 vehicles, 16 million members and about 1.1 million km of daily real-world driving data as its base.

Its competitive set overlaps with other car-sharing and rental operators, and Socar aims to extend into the rental and used-car markets.

04

Earnings analysis

On a reported basis, revenue rose from KRW 397.6 billion in 2022 to KRW 398.5 billion in 2023, an increase of 0.2%, then to KRW 431.8 billion in 2024, up 8.4%, and KRW 470.7 billion in 2025, up 9.0%. Revenue increased for three straight years from 2023 to 2025, and 2025 was the largest year of the 2022 to 2025 period.

Operating profit of KRW 9.52 billion in 2022 turned into an operating loss of KRW 9.69 billion in 2023, and the loss widened to KRW 9.8 billion in 2024. Operating profit returned to KRW 23.2 billion in 2025, with an operating margin of 4.9%.

Controlling-shareholder net losses were KRW 18.1 billion in 2022, KRW 42.3 billion in 2023, KRW 31 billion in 2024 and KRW 18.4 billion in 2025, a four-year losing streak in which the losses narrowed from 2024. The net loss including non-controlling interests was reported at the same amounts.

Operating cash flow moved from -KRW 62.7 billion in 2022 and -KRW 109.6 billion in 2023 to KRW 3.29 billion in 2024 and KRW 50.8 billion in 2025.

The debt-to-equity ratio climbed from 136.6% in 2022 to 220.7% in 2023, 244.2% in 2024 and 256.7% in 2025, when total liabilities were KRW 423.2 billion and total equity was KRW 164.9 billion.

Quarterly revenue fell from KRW 128.7 billion in the fourth quarter of 2025 to KRW 97.1 billion in the first quarter of 2026, down 24.6% quarter on quarter, and recovered to KRW 135.2 billion in the third quarter, up 11.7%.

Press reports tied the first-quarter revenue decline mainly to a smaller volume of used-car disposals. Operating profit rose from KRW 1.39 billion in the first quarter of 2026 to KRW 6.81 billion in the second quarter and KRW 9.76 billion in the third quarter, up 43.2% year on year.

Controlling-shareholder results moved from net losses of KRW 14.7 billion in the fourth quarter of 2025 and KRW 8.79 billion in the first quarter of 2026 to net income of KRW 6.46 billion in the second quarter and KRW 13.4 billion in the third quarter, up 743.5% year on year.

Because third-quarter net income is well above operating profit, non-operating items and financing costs are worth checking in the annual report notes. The last four quarters together show a controlling-shareholder net loss of KRW 3.58 billion.

05

Industry analysis

Socar is widely described as the leading car-sharing operator in Korea, and the company puts the domestic car-sharing market at about KRW 1 trillion. Using the same assets, the company aims to expand into a market above KRW 100 trillion that includes rental and vehicle commerce.

Press reports place the long-term rental market at about KRW 10 trillion, which is the adjacent market targeted by Sopick and the used-car trade-in service. In autonomous driving, Socar set up APX Mobility with Krafton, with registered capital of KRW 150 billion, to commercialize the technology.

The plan links the unit to autonomous car sharing and ride-hailing services. Competition overlaps with other car-sharing and rental operators, and Socar positions its fleet scale and member data as differentiators.

On demand, first-quarter 2026 revenue fell 26.1% year on year, while third-quarter 2026 revenue rose 20.9% year on year, showing a recovery. Fleet utilization reached 39.1% in the third quarter of 2025, up 4 percentage points from a year earlier, and the company credited vehicle allocation and redeployment.

As the business widens from car sharing into subscription, used cars and rental, seasonality and used-car price swings carry a larger weight in results.

06

Outlook

Socar launched the Sopick long-term subscription on October 1, 2026, offering new cars, near-new cars and used cars, available nationwide except Jeju and some offshore islands.

According to a summary of the company announcement, average daily new contracts in the first month were about four times the level of the first week.

Industry reports say Socar plans to launch its Sopick trade-in service in late November 2026, under which a used car is bought and its sale proceeds are credited as prepayment for subscription fees.

Because the proceeds of a customer's car go toward subscription fees, the next check is whether subscription contracts and used-car purchase volumes rise together.

The full-stack mobility strategy is taking shape through a product lineup of monthly and weekly subscriptions, medium-to-long-term use of one year or more, and used-car commerce.

At the June 2026 shareholders meeting, the expanded corporate purpose, covering new-car sales, rental and parts distribution, created the legal basis for these moves.

In autonomous driving, APX Mobility is upgrading end-to-end technology and linking it to autonomous car sharing and ride-hailing, but the commercialization timing is not yet reflected in reported results.

Product expansion depends on higher fleet utilization and revenue per vehicle, so those disclosed figures are the next indicators. The 2026 annual report, the next regular filing, will show the full-year operating margin and debt ratio first.

08

Bull factors

Operating profit recovery

Operating profit turned positive at KRW 23.2 billion in 2025, and third-quarter 2026 operating profit reached KRW 9.76 billion, up 43.2% year on year. In the third quarter of 2025, fleet utilization rose 4 percentage points to 39.1% and revenue per vehicle increased to KRW 1.71 million.

Indirect costs fell 14% to KRW 18.8 billion in the same period, which press reports cited as evidence of operating efficiency.

Early response to Sopick and the full-stack strategy

Sopick, launched on October 1, 2026, is a long-term subscription that allows switching models and moving between new and used cars. According to a summary of the company announcement, average daily new contracts in the first month were about four times the first-week level.

Pairing used-car purchases with subscriptions is read as a way to secure vehicle supply and retain customers at the same time.

Fleet and data assets

Socar says it holds a fleet of 25,000 vehicles, data from 16 million members and real-world driving data averaging 1.1 million km a day. Modu Parking revenue rose 27% year on year in the first quarter of 2026. The company describes these assets as the base for expanding into rental, used cars and autonomous driving.

Bear factors

Heavy financial leverage

The debt-to-equity ratio rose from 136.6% in 2022 to 256.7% in 2025, leaving total liabilities of KRW 423.2 billion well above total equity of KRW 164.9 billion at the end of 2025. Analysts reported financing costs of about KRW 13 billion in the first half of 2025, with interest burden eating into operating profit. Long-term borrowings rolling into short-term maturities were also flagged as a source of financial pressure.

Volatile net income

Controlling-shareholder net losses ran for four straight years from 2022 to 2025. In the fourth quarter of 2025 the company swung to a controlling-shareholder net loss of KRW 14.7 billion, and in the first quarter of 2026 the loss was KRW 8.79 billion. Whether controlling-shareholder net income returns to profit in the next quarter is the point to check.

Revenue swings and pricing changes

First-quarter 2026 revenue fell 26.1% year on year, and subscription and commerce revenue dropped 63.4% to KRW 18.7 billion. Car-sharing revenue also fell 10% year on year in the third quarter of 2025 after a pricing revision. Whether profits hold up during periods of falling revenue is a point to confirm in the coming quarters.

10

Risk factors

Financial and maturity risk

With debt far above equity, whether bonds maturing within one to two years can be refinanced has been flagged as the key variable. If interest costs erode operating profit, net income can turn negative even while operations are profitable. With the debt-to-equity ratio at 256.7% in 2025, changes in borrowing terms carry a large effect on earnings.

New-business execution risk

Sopick mixes new and used cars in long-term subscriptions, so residual vehicle values and reconditioning costs have to be managed. For the used-car trade-in service, the spread between purchase and resale prices will shape profitability.

APX Mobility, the autonomous driving unit, has registered capital of KRW 150 billion, and its path to revenue is not yet visible in reported results.

Revenue mix volatility

Subscription and commerce revenue moves sharply with the volume of used-car disposals, and it fell 63.4% year on year in the first quarter of 2026. The first quarter is seasonally weak, and pricing revisions can also shake car-sharing revenue. With this degree of revenue volatility, the direction of quarterly profit can change within a quarter or two.

What to watch next

  1. Late November 2026

    Industry reports point to the launch of the Sopick trade-in service, which credits used-car sale proceeds toward subscription fees. Whether used-car purchase volumes and subscription contracts rise together will signal the recovery of subscription and commerce revenue.

  2. By March 31, 2027 (2026 annual report deadline)

    The 2026 annual report will show the full-year operating margin, debt ratio, and the breakdown of non-operating items and financing costs. The reason third-quarter controlling-shareholder net income far exceeded operating profit should become clear from these items.

  3. Timing not yet set (company announcement)

    Once the commercial timeline for APX Mobility's autonomous car sharing and ride-hailing services is set, the revenue potential of this business can be gauged. Reported results do not yet separate out the revenue of this business.

12

Overall view

Reported results show Socar moving from a loss-making structure toward profitability. The controlling-shareholder net losses that ran for four straight years from 2022 ended with net income in the third quarter of 2026, but the last four quarters still total a KRW 3.58 billion net loss.

With the debt-to-equity ratio rising from 136.6% in 2022 to 256.7% in 2025, the key variable is how interest costs and the maturity profile affect earnings. Revenue peaked at KRW 470.7 billion in 2025, while first-quarter 2026 revenue fell as used-car disposals were trimmed.

Bullish factors include sustained operating profit, better fleet utilization and revenue per vehicle, and early Sopick contract momentum. Bearish factors include financial leverage, volatile net income, and revenue swings in used-car and subscription businesses.

The full-stack and autonomous driving directions are confirmed in disclosures, but their revenue scale has not yet appeared in reported results. The next checkpoints are the Sopick trade-in launch in late November 2026 and the operating margin and debt ratio in the 2026 annual report.
