Report overview

Five Straight Quarterly Losses Amid Tariff and Utilization Pressure

Hwaseung Enterprise, which depends heavily on Adidas ODM orders, has posted attributable net losses for five straight quarters through the third quarter of 2026, as U.S. tariffs and lower factory utilization hit at the same time. Operating profit stayed in the black in the second and third quarters of 2026.

02

Business structure

Hwaseung Enterprise was founded in 2015 and listed in 2016 as a footwear ODM maker that produces mainly for Adidas, with production bases in Vietnam, Indonesia and China. The company says it moved from an OEM model to an ODM model that covers planning, development and production.

Its business also spans apparel, caps and textiles. Segment disclosures differ by source: one data set puts footwear at 83.10% of revenue with distribution and other at 16.90%, while another shows footwear at 100% of revenue in 2Q 2026. Either way, footwear ODM generates most of the revenue.

Customer concentration is very high, with Adidas products at 99.7% of revenue in the first nine months of 2025. Changes in customer inventory policy pass quickly into utilization and profit, and in ODM, utilization feeds directly into margins.

In 3Q 2025, higher-priced orders rose, but defect rates and productivity worsened during product diversification.

04

Earnings analysis

On a disclosed annual basis, revenue fell from KRW 1.65 trillion in 2022 to KRW 1.21 trillion in 2023 (down 26.6% year on year), then rebounded to KRW 1.61 trillion in 2024 (up 32.6%) before easing to KRW 1.56 trillion in 2025 (down 2.8%).

Operating profit peaked at KRW 82.6 billion in 2024, the largest year of 2022 to 2025, and bottomed at KRW 13 billion in 2023, the smallest year. Operating profit fell to KRW 35.7 billion in 2025, down 56.8%, and the operating margin slipped from 5.1% to 2.3%.

The company cited deteriorating profitability from external uncertainty as the main cause of the 2025 swing. Net loss attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 28.9 billion in 2025, turning from a KRW 33.1 billion profit in 2024, while the net loss including non-controlling interests was KRW 27.1 billion.

The two figures differ because of non-controlling interests and should be read separately. Tariff pressure first appeared in the second quarter of 2025. Operating profit was KRW 143 million in 3Q 2025 and recovered to KRW 12.8 billion in 4Q 2025.

In 1Q 2026 the company posted an operating loss of KRW 4.85 billion, with utilization at 87% against 95% a year earlier. Operating profit returned to KRW 7.29 billion in 2Q 2026 and KRW 3.66 billion in 3Q 2026, yet attributable net losses were KRW 1.81 billion and KRW 18 billion in those quarters.

Because operating profit was positive in some loss quarters, the gap between operating and final income needs checking in the footnotes of the periodic report. Attributable net losses over the last four quarters total KRW 43.7 billion.

Industry analysis

Outlook data on the global footwear market projects growth from USD 404.6 billion in 2024 to USD 570 billion in 2030, as cited in a company profile summary. Company revenue, however, depends more on customer order volumes than on market growth.

U.S. tariff policy has been cited as a cause of weak results since the second quarter of 2025. Global retailers want to keep inventory below normal levels in case tariff-driven price pass-through weakens demand.

Daishin Securities lowered its 2026 and 2027 earnings estimates, citing that inventory stance and a shift in the competitive landscape of downstream industries. In 1Q 2026, core product orders did not grow much and spot orders from customers rose, cutting efficiency.

Average ODM sneaker price per pair rose 6.8% year on year in the same quarter, but volumes fell sharply. Against the apparel and fashion accessories sector average across 72 listed companies, the average operating margin is 1.57% and the average debt ratio is 111.84%.

The company's operating margin is above the sector average, while its debt ratio is well above it. Peer share and order data are not compared numerically here, so the industry position is best read through Adidas order flow and tariff policy.

08

Bull factors

Expected recovery in Adidas demand

A November 2025 Shinhan Investment Corp. report expected Originals and running products to fill the gap left by Yeezy-related sales. Because most revenue comes from Adidas, whether that recovery turns into higher order volumes is the test for this bull case.

Possible order rebound after the World Cup

Daishin Securities noted in an April 2026 report that sports brands were working through inventory ahead of the June 2026 North American World Cup. The bull argument is that customer orders could pick up once that inventory is cleared.

Operating profit of KRW 7.29 billion in 2Q 2026 and KRW 3.66 billion in 3Q 2026 is consistent with this reading.

Cash generation and funding support

Operating cash flow in 2025 was KRW 132.3 billion, the largest of 2022 to 2025. A KRW 130 billion investment in late September 2024 was earmarked to repay debt and fund operations, spreading the financial burden. Whether cash generation holds up through loss-making periods is the core of this bull case.

Bear factors

Five straight quarters of net losses

Attributable net losses were KRW 13.8 billion in 4Q 2025, KRW 10.1 billion in 1Q 2026, KRW 1.81 billion in 2Q 2026 and KRW 18 billion in 3Q 2026. The 3Q 2026 loss was larger than the 4Q 2025 loss. Cumulative losses over the last four quarters total KRW 43.7 billion, so the burden remains.

Tariffs and falling utilization

Utilization fell to 87% in 1Q 2026 from 95% a year earlier. Revenue in the same quarter fell 17% year on year to KRW 350.9 billion. If tariff effects and cautious customer inventory policy persist, a utilization recovery will be harder, which is the core of this bear case.

High leverage and customer concentration

The debt ratio rose from 149.6% in 2022 to 180.4% in 2025, and total liabilities were KRW 850.1 billion at the end of 2025. Rising debt burden since 2021 has been flagged as a continuing concern. Heavy reliance on Adidas means changes at that one customer pass straight through to the company.

10

Risk factors

Customer concentration

Adidas products accounted for 99.7% of revenue in the first nine months of 2025, so order changes at one customer affect the entire result. Adidas inventory policy, product line transitions and pricing outcomes feed directly into orders and utilization.

Tariffs and trade policy

U.S. tariff policy has been cited as a cause of weak results since the second quarter of 2025. If tariff costs are passed on to consumers, customers may cut inventory and orders further. The direction and timing of policy changes are difficult to predict at this stage.

Balance sheet and capital burden

At the end of 2025, total liabilities were KRW 850.1 billion and total equity was KRW 471.3 billion. Of the funding raised in late September 2024, KRW 110 billion was earmarked for debt repayment. If attributable net losses continue, capital headroom could narrow and the need for additional funding could grow.

Overall view

Hwaseung Enterprise's operating profit peaked at KRW 82.6 billion in 2024 and fell to KRW 35.7 billion in 2025, and quarterly results have swung widely into 2026. Attributable net losses have run for five straight quarters from 3Q 2025 through 3Q 2026, totaling KRW 43.7 billion over the last four quarters.

The bull case rests on expected Adidas demand recovery, positive operating profit in 2Q and 3Q 2026, and operating cash flow of KRW 132.3 billion in 2025. The bear case rests on tariff-driven inventory cuts by customers, 87% utilization in 1Q 2026, a 180.4% debt ratio and heavy customer concentration.

Brokers have mostly placed the timing of a earnings rebound in 2026 or later, so recovery must be confirmed through reported results. Next checkpoints are the 2026 preliminary annual results (around mid-February 2027), the 2026 annual report (by end-March 2027) and 1Q 2027 results (around mid-May 2027).
