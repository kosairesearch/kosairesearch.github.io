02

Business structure

DaeChang Steel listed on KOSDAQ on December 5, 2014, as a primary steel manufacturer. Steel accounts for 100% of sales as of Q2 2026 sales.

The company runs several plants, including Incheon, Cheonan, Asan, Paju and Seosan, providing nationwide coil center processing, and has subsidiaries and processing centers in Vietnam and China.

In the 2025 business report, domestic PO products were the largest item at KRW 84.7 billion, followed by other products at KRW 76.8 billion, CR at KRW 45.9 billion, GA at KRW 43.4 billion, EGI at KRW 32.8 billion and GI at KRW 30.6 billion.

Product sales reached KRW 318.3 billion in 2025 versus KRW 315.9 billion in 2024, while merchandise sales fell to KRW 77.2 billion from KRW 82.1 billion. A company profile states that sales through three automakers exceed 50% of total sales.

Linked sales have annual prices and volumes set under contracts between POSCO and automakers, which makes their volatility lower than in other steel sectors. The high-end double-floor product ACCESS FLOOR is tied to large projects such as the Pyeongtaek US military relocation, and its sales are growing. Peers in the steel sector include Hyundai Steel Company, Dongkuk Steel Mill, SeAH Steel and Husteel.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual sales peaked at KRW 419.8 billion in 2023, up 5.3% from the prior year, after KRW 398.5 billion in 2022. Sales then fell 4.1% in 2024 and 1.0% in 2025, a two-year decline from 2024 through 2025, ending at KRW 398.6 billion, close to the 2022 level.

Operating profit was KRW 5.07 billion in 2022 and KRW 5.73 billion in 2023, fell to KRW 2.97 billion in 2024, the smallest year, and rose 13.1% to KRW 3.36 billion in 2025. Operating margin ranged from 0.7% to 1.4% over 2022 to 2025.

Profit attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 55.7 billion in 2022, the largest year in the period, while operating profit that year was KRW 5.07 billion, so non-operating items and tax effects appear to have weighed heavily.

The figure then fell 95.4% in 2023 to KRW 2.58 billion and 70.4% in 2024 to KRW 763 million.

In 2025 it rose 274.1% to KRW 2.86 billion, and the company attributes this mainly to lower income tax expense from corporate tax refund claims covering 2019 through 2024, so the 2025 recovery in net profit should be read apart from the operating gain.

Operating cash flow moved from negative KRW 5.97 billion in 2022 and negative KRW 21.2 billion in 2023 to negative KRW 1.61 billion in 2024 and positive KRW 18.8 billion in 2025. The debt-to-equity ratio rose from 43.8% in 2022 to 70.3% in 2023, 75.4% in 2024 and 70.1% in 2025.

At year-end 2025, total equity was KRW 149.3 billion, all attributable to controlling shareholders, and total liabilities were KRW 104.6 billion.

Quarterly operating profit was KRW 1.62 billion in Q3 2025 and KRW 1.89 billion in Q4 2025, then an operating loss of KRW 8.76 million in Q1 2026 before a KRW 3.23 billion profit in Q2 2026.

Q3 2026 operating profit was KRW 1.19 billion, down 63.1% from the prior quarter, and the company posted a net loss attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 84.67 million. Profit attributable to controlling shareholders over the last four quarters totals KRW 4.27 billion.

05

Industry analysis

DaeChang Steel's end markets split between automotive and construction. In Q1 2025, weaker automotive demand and raw material price swings weighed on results. Because sales to three automakers account for more than half of total sales, vehicle production flows feed directly into revenue.

Its double-floor product, ACCESS FLOOR, serves new construction and remodeling sites, and demand for higher-value products is reported to be rising. Since the company is described as a POSCO cold-rolled service center, annual contracts between POSCO and automakers set the pricing and volume framework.

This structure reduces volatility but limits how far raw material moves pass into margins.

Compared with larger steel peers such as Hyundai Steel Company and Dongkuk Steel Mill, DaeChang Steel is centered on coil processing across a nationwide plant network, and its operating margin in a 0.7% to 1.4% range is the key comparison point.

Q2 2026 sales of KRW 125.3 billion were the highest quarterly figure in reported results, and whether this reflects a lasting rise in processing volume will show up in the following quarters' sales.

06

Outlook

The center of the company's capacity plan is the Seosan plant. With the plant completed, capacity is described as rising to 920,000 tons, spreading the load across plants. The Seosan plant is described as having slitting, rotary shear and mini shear lines, securing annual capacity of 210,000 tons.

The merger with another steel processor is described as aimed at improving management efficiency and generating synergies. At the Asan plant, the company planned to introduce electrical steel processing equipment for EV use, costing KRW 3.0 billion, with an introduction date of October 2026 given in the plan.

Moving slitting equipment to Asan after the merger is also part of expanding processing capacity. On shareholder returns, the company said in a value-enhancement plan published on March 31, 2026 that it would pursue sustainable dividends, profit-focused operations and conservative financial management.

The 2025 year-end cash dividend totaled KRW 3.17 billion and was approved at the annual general meeting held on March 31, 2026. Q3 2026 sales of KRW 113.4 billion were lower than in Q2, so when the new capacity shows up in quarterly sales and operating margin is the next point to check.

Bear factors

Profit slipped despite higher Q3 sales

Q3 2026 sales of KRW 113.4 billion rose 16.3% year on year, but operating profit fell 26.3% to KRW 1.19 billion. Profit attributable to controlling shareholders turned to a net loss of KRW 84.67 million. The bearish point is that higher sales did not translate into better margins.

Net profit reliant on tax effects

The company attributes the 2025 net profit increase mainly to lower tax expense from corporate tax refund claims. With operating margin at 0.8% in 2025, tax effects appear to have strongly shaped net profit. This effect should be read separately from the operating profit trend.

Higher leverage and volatile cash flow

The debt-to-equity ratio rose from 43.8% in 2022 to 70.3% in 2023 and stood at 70.1% in 2025. Operating cash flow was negative KRW 21.2 billion in 2023. The positive result in 2025 came after large year-to-year swings, so cash generation stability is still in the process of being confirmed.

Risk factors

End-market demand and raw material prices

In Q1 2025, weaker automotive demand and raw material price swings combined to weigh on results. With sales to three automakers above half of the total, changes in vehicle output feed directly into revenue. The timing with which raw material prices pass into processing margins is another source of profit volatility.

Balance sheet and capital structure

At year-end 2025, total equity was KRW 149.3 billion and total liabilities were KRW 104.6 billion. The debt-to-equity ratio rose from 43.8% in 2022 to 70.1% in 2025. Equity is large relative to profit, so prolonged low profitability could reduce financial flexibility.

Execution of expansion and merger

The Seosan plant completion, the merger with another steel processor and the transfer of slitting equipment to Asan are described as moving together. Turning new capacity into actual sales takes time and cost. If integration costs appear before the benefits, quarterly operating margins could come under pressure.
