OE Solutions: Narrowing Losses During an Optical Transceiver Mix Shift

OE Solutions is widening its revenue mix from wireless toward wired and datacenter products; it posted an operating profit in the third quarter of 2026, but net losses attributable to controlling shareholders from Q4 2025 through Q3 2026 still total KRW 16.1 billion.

02

Business structure

OE Solutions was founded in 2003 as a maker of optical transceivers and laser diodes and listed on KOSDAQ in 2014. An optical transceiver is a module that sends and receives optical signals, and the company is building its own laser diodes, the core optical component, to change its cost structure.

Industry reports have described it as the top domestic optical transceiver seller by sales. Its products serve 5G wireless networks, FTTH (fiber to the home), CATV/MSO, telecom and datacenter networks.

According to a Hanyang Securities analysis from December 2025, the business is moving from a wireless-centric structure toward a larger share of wired, transmission and datacom businesses.

Per iM Securities data as of March 2026, the wireless segment generated KRW 26.3 billion of the KRW 57.4 billion 2025 consolidated sales, up 31.6% year on year. Key products include 10G EPON OLT transceivers for FTTH supplied to Japan's NTT and tunable transceivers supplied to a US wired network equipment maker.

The company has said datacenter sales account for roughly 2 to 3% of the total, so the shift is at an early stage.

At OFC in March 2026 it unveiled a 1.6Tbps OSFP transceiver and the ELSFP external light source for CPO, and at AI EXPO KOREA in May 2026 it showed a 100G-class EML laser diode chip that it said was the first developed in Korea.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual sales were KRW 77 billion in 2022, KRW 46 billion in 2023 (down 40.2% year on year) and KRW 32 billion in 2024 (down 30.4%), before rebounding to KRW 57.4 billion in 2025 (up 79.2%). The largest year in the 2022 to 2025 span was 2022, and the smallest was 2024.

The company attributed the 2025 rise in sales and operating profit to the activation of 5G markets in Japan and the United States, as reported around its February 2026 disclosure.

The operating loss widened from KRW 8.71 billion in 2022 to KRW 31.1 billion in 2023, then narrowed to KRW 30.4 billion in 2024 and KRW 16 billion in 2025, marking four consecutive years of operating losses from 2022 through 2025.

The operating margin moved from -11.3% in 2022 to -67.6% in 2023 and -94.8% in 2024, then improved to -27.9% in 2025.

Net losses attributable to controlling shareholders were KRW 5.98 billion in 2022, KRW 33.8 billion in 2023, KRW 33.1 billion in 2024 and KRW 27 billion in 2025, and the net loss including non-controlling interests followed the same path.

The debt-to-equity ratio rose from 27.3% in 2022 to 36.4% in 2023, 84.4% in 2024 and 86.1% in 2025. At the end of 2025, total equity was KRW 64.3 billion, all of it attributable to controlling shareholders, against total liabilities of KRW 55.3 billion.

Operating cash flow was -KRW 7.98 billion in 2022, -KRW 7.36 billion in 2023, -KRW 24.9 billion in 2024 and -KRW 11.8 billion in 2025, negative in all four years.

Quarterly sales went from KRW 13.1 billion in Q3 2025 to KRW 14.4 billion in Q4 2025, KRW 18 billion in Q1 2026, KRW 19.7 billion in Q2 2026 and KRW 21.7 billion in Q3 2026, rising for four consecutive quarters; Q3 2026 was up 66.1% from a year earlier.

Operating results went from a KRW 2.92 billion operating loss in Q3 2025 and a KRW 3.38 billion loss in Q4 2025 to a KRW 611 million loss in Q1 2026 and an KRW 870 million loss in Q2 2026, before turning to a KRW 140 million operating profit in Q3 2026.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders showed a KRW 13.1 billion loss in Q4 2025, a KRW 1.57 billion profit in Q1 2026 after a swing to profit, and losses of KRW 2.42 billion in Q2 2026 and KRW 2.15 billion in Q3 2026.

Quarters in which operating profit and controlling-shareholder net income move in opposite directions make the gap between the two the key point to track.

05

Industry analysis

Demand for optical transceivers moves along two axes: 5G wireless capital spending and AI datacenter investment.

The company attributed its 2023 to 2024 sales decline to lower 5G wireless network investment and a revenue base concentrated in a few customers, which is why it has widened its lineup to FTTH, CATV/MSO, telecom and datacenter products.

On the AI side, a company representative at a May 2026 exhibition said 800G is the mainstream of actual demand and 1.6T began adoption in 2026. Hanyang Securities said in December 2025 that the company is moving from a wireless-centric structure toward datacenter and wired transmission businesses.

The laser diode chip accounts for about 30% of transceiver cost, and the company has invested in this component since around 2019.

Its strengths are its leading domestic sales position and in-house laser diode capability, while datacenter revenue at about 2 to 3% of the total means AI demand is still feeding into results slowly.

In May 2026, optical and telecom equipment names rallied on AI infrastructure expectations, and some market commentary cautioned that thematic buying should be separated from technology strength and customer structure.

Outlook

A May 2026 report described the company's roadmap for AI datacenter optical products in three stages. In 2026 it plans to secure a revenue base with 800G transceivers and to introduce 1.6T transceivers in earnest from the second half of 2026.

From 2027 it plans to enter the co-packaged optics (CPO) market led by the ELSFP external light source, which it sees as a long-term growth driver through 2028 to 2029. An iM Securities report from March 2026 expected ELSFP sample supply to global customers from the third quarter of 2026.

Whether those samples have converted into customer orders is an item to confirm in upcoming regular reports and investor relations materials. At OFC in March 2026, the company said the 1.6Tbps transceiver proved compatibility with InfiniBand switches for AI datacenters in internal testing.

A KRW 2.5 billion tunable transceiver mass-production order with a US wired network customer, signed in 2025, was reported to have cleared product approval in January 2026. Japanese FTTH demand also continues.

On earnings, controlling-shareholder profit turned positive in Q1 2026 and operating profit turned positive in Q3 2026, but net losses returned in Q2 2026. The next question is whether operating profit translates into controlling-shareholder net profit.

08

Bull factors

Wired and tunable transceiver orders expanding

Full-year 2025 sales rose 79.2% to KRW 57.4 billion, and the company credited the activation of 5G markets in Japan and the United States.

A KRW 2.5 billion tunable transceiver mass-production order from a US wired network equipment maker was reported to have been signed in 2025 and cleared product approval in January 2026. Quarterly sales climbed to KRW 18 billion in Q1 2026 and KRW 21.7 billion in Q3 2026, rising for four straight quarters.

Datacenter product lineup expanding

The 1.6Tbps OSFP transceiver and the CPO external light source ELSFP were unveiled at OFC in March 2026, and the company said internal testing confirmed compatibility with InfiniBand switches used in AI datacenters. A 100G-class EML chip described as the first developed in Korea was shown in May 2026. The company said 100G-class products are in mass production and 200G and 400G samples are being qualified.

In-house laser diodes and cost structure

The laser diode chip accounts for about 30% of transceiver cost, and the company has been producing it in-house since around 2019. In an April 2026 report, iM Securities said rising chip sales would strengthen operating leverage on fixed costs and could accelerate the earnings turnaround. The pace at which in-house chips feed into results is a central variable for earnings recovery.

Bear factors

Operating losses still present

The 2025 operating loss of KRW 16 billion narrowed from KRW 30.4 billion in 2024 but was still a loss, with an operating margin of -27.9%. The Q2 2026 operating loss of KRW 870 million was larger than the KRW 611 million loss in Q1 2026, and operating profit appeared only in Q3 2026.

Repeated controlling-shareholder net losses

Controlling-shareholder net losses of KRW 2.42 billion in Q2 2026 and KRW 2.15 billion in Q3 2026 mark two consecutive loss quarters. The Q4 2025 controlling-shareholder net loss of KRW 13.1 billion was especially large.

Because operating profit and controlling-shareholder net income moved in different directions, whether the improvement in operating results carries through to net income is the open question.

Financial burden and cash outflows

The debt-to-equity ratio climbed from 27.3% in 2022 to 86.1% in 2025, and total liabilities at the end of 2025 were KRW 55.3 billion. Operating cash flow was negative in every year from 2022 through 2025, reaching -KRW 24.9 billion in 2024. If operating losses persist alongside continued cash outflows, reliance on outside funding could rise.

10

Risk factors

Customer concentration

The business depends on a few large customers, including FTTH supply to Japan's NTT and orders from a US wired network equipment maker. The company itself cited concentration in a few customers as one factor behind the 2023 to 2024 sales decline. If customers change their capital spending schedules, quarterly revenue could swing sharply.

Technology transition and pricing pressure

Optical transceivers are a field where the standard moves quickly from 800G to 1.6T. If validation of new products slips or mass-production yields fall short, revenue gaps could follow. Intensifying competition could lower unit prices and slow margin improvement.

Balance sheet and funding needs

The debt-to-equity ratio reached 86.1% in 2025, up from 27.3% in 2022, and all KRW 64.3 billion of equity belongs to controlling shareholders, which leaves limited cushion. If operating cash outflows continue, the need for additional funding could increase.

Overall view

OE Solutions saw sales fall in both 2023 and 2024 before rebounding 79.2% to KRW 57.4 billion in 2025, and its operating loss narrowed to KRW 16 billion in 2025 from KRW 30.4 billion in 2024.

Sales rose for four consecutive quarters from Q4 2025 through Q3 2026, and Q3 2026 produced an operating profit of KRW 140 million. However, controlling-shareholder net income posted losses in both Q2 and Q3 2026, and the four-quarter total from Q4 2025 through Q3 2026 is KRW 16.1 billion.

Four consecutive years of operating losses and a debt-to-equity ratio of 86.1% at the end of 2025 indicate the financial structure is still recovering.

On the business side, the shift to 1.6T and the CPO-linked ELSFP are medium- to long-term initiatives, and with datacenter sales at about 2 to 3% of the total, results remain at an early stage. Because no earnings-based multiple can be calculated, the path of earnings recovery is central to reading the valuation.

The next checkpoints are the 2026 full-year preliminary results and the 2026 annual report, where the key test is whether operating profit converts into controlling-shareholder net profit.
