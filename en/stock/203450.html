02

Business structure

UNION biometrics develops and manufactures access-control equipment and related solutions based on biometric technologies such as fingerprint, face and iris recognition.

According to a report from late August 2026, biometric access-control terminals, its core product, accounted for KRW 15.1 billion, or 59.7%, of first-half 2026 revenue of KRW 25.2 billion, and about 62% of total revenue comes from domestic sales.

The company also supplies fingerprint recognition modules, enrollment devices and card-based access controllers. Its scope has widened from terminal supply to software development, server and network construction and integration with CCTV control systems, in a system integration (SI) model.

Major customers include the Ministry of National Defense headquarters, the Government Daejeon Complex, Gyeryongdae, the Marine Corps, the National Election Commission and the Military Manpower Administration, and the company is currently carrying out access-control work at the Ministry of National Defense headquarters.

Cumulative military-related orders since 2020 total about KRW 42.9 billion excluding VAT, and the company won army projects in 2020, 2022, 2024 and 2026 in succession. In the 2026 army tender, UNION biometrics and Anrobotics were the two bidders in an open competition.

Overseas, a 2025 report noted growing supply of face-recognition terminals for NEC of Japan. A June 2026 report said the company is pursuing European data-center projects with Data One of France and planned to apply its systems to new data centers in New Jersey and Indiana in the second half.

Among large component makers in the same electronics and parts classification, Samsung Electro-Mechanics and LG Innotek have very different product and customer structures.

04

Earnings analysis

Reported annual revenue rose from KRW 46.3 billion in 2022 to KRW 51.4 billion in 2023 (up 11.1% year on year) and KRW 56.7 billion in 2024 (up 10.4%), then fell to KRW 51.3 billion in 2025 (down 9.6%). Among 2022 to 2025, 2024 was the largest year for revenue and 2022 the smallest.

Operating profit grew from KRW 1.34 billion in 2022 to KRW 1.57 billion in 2023 (up 17.3%) and KRW 2.84 billion in 2024 (up 81.3%), then fell to KRW 1.02 billion in 2025 (down 64.1%), the smallest year of the period. The operating margin was 2.9% in 2022, 3.1% in 2023 and 5.0% in 2024, before easing to 2.0% in 2025.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 2.16 billion in 2022, KRW 3.07 billion in 2023 (up 42.4%), KRW 4.95 billion in 2024 (up 61.3%) and KRW 2.01 billion in 2025 (down 59.4%). Total net income was reported at the same amounts in each year, so the two lines show no difference in these figures.

The debt ratio went from 52.0% in 2022 and 51.2% in 2023 to 68.9% in 2024 and 50.8% in 2025, when total equity was KRW 41.5 billion and total liabilities were KRW 21.1 billion.

Operating cash flow moved from -KRW 2.12 billion in 2022 to KRW 9.77 billion in 2023 and KRW 11.3 billion in 2024, then turned to -KRW 12 billion in 2025.

On a quarterly basis, third-quarter 2025 revenue was KRW 13.2 billion, with operating profit of KRW 473 million and net income attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 845 million.

Fourth-quarter 2025 revenue was KRW 13.4 billion (up 1.7% from the prior quarter), operating profit fell to KRW 46.87 million (down 90.1%) and net income attributable fell to KRW 447 million (down 47.1%).

First-quarter 2026 revenue was KRW 11.4 billion (down 15.1% quarter on quarter), with an operating loss of KRW 857 million that turned the quarter into a loss.

Second-quarter 2026 revenue recovered to KRW 13.8 billion (up 20.9%), the operating loss narrowed to KRW 354 million, but a net loss attributable of KRW 368 million emerged.

Third-quarter 2026 revenue was KRW 12 billion (down 13.2% from the prior quarter and down 9.5% year on year), with an operating loss of KRW 737 million and a net loss attributable of KRW 1.55 billion, a year-on-year swing into loss.

In May 2026 the company said it concentrated strategic investment in global marketing and new business development in the first quarter. Quarterly revenue stayed in a band of roughly KRW 11 billion to 14 billion while results moved between profit and loss.

05

Industry analysis

The defense and public-sector security market is structured around procurement schedules and contract units, so quarterly revenue can bunch up and swing widely.

The October 2026 army project was put out to an urgent tender with a target of completing installation within the year, and it was run as an open competition with two bidders. The company attributes the win to its army projects since 2020 and its biometric authentication technology.

On the private side, the company judges that AI data-center expansion is lifting demand for biometric-based access control, according to a June 2026 report.

On industrial safety, its face-recognition construction safety solution HealthID has been introduced at LH public housing sites in response to tougher safety laws, and the company says it may be funded from industrial safety management costs, according to a May 2026 report.

A regulatory sandbox project for animal nose-print registration is also underway. Although classified under electronic components, its demand structure differs from the price and volume cycles that component makers face.

Competition takes place in defense and public tenders and in AI infrastructure security, where biometric and integrated security technology are the basis. Its business structure differs enough from large electronic component makers that a direct comparison is not very meaningful.

Outlook

In an October 2026 report, the company said it aims to finish delivery and installation of the army project by December 2026 and to recognize the related revenue in the fourth quarter. In the same report, a company official said this should allow a return to profit in the fourth quarter.

The company expects that the recognition of large-contract revenue together with growth in existing businesses will bring 2026 annual revenue to a record-high level and lift operating profit sharply year on year. This matches what the company said in May 2026, when it expected record annual results in the second half.

However, the third quarter of 2026 was reported with an operating loss of KRW 737 million and a net loss attributable of KRW 1.55 billion.

In new businesses, the company is preparing a next-generation construction safety platform targeted for launch in the second half, and development of the integrated security platform Alpeta X continues.

Reports said its systems are scheduled for application at new data centers in New Jersey and Indiana in the second half. At Intersec Dubai in January 2026, it showed biometric solutions aimed at Middle East construction, energy and infrastructure markets.

The company plans to secure additional large integrated security projects at critical national facilities. The next checkpoint is whether the fourth quarter returns to profit, which will be confirmed in the 2026 annual report, due within three months after the fiscal year ends, so by the end of March 2027. The same report will show whether 2026 annual revenue exceeds the KRW 56.7 billion reported for 2024.

Bull factors

Record military order and a growing order base

The approximately KRW 17.1 billion army contract disclosed in October 2026 is the largest single order in the company's history. It has won army projects four times in a row since 2020, with cumulative military orders of about KRW 42.9 billion excluding VAT.

The company points to installations at national security facilities, including the Ministry of National Defense headquarters, as the basis of its competitiveness in bids.

Company expectations for a fourth-quarter return to profit

In an October 2026 report, the company said it expects the army contract revenue, recognized in the fourth quarter, to bring a return to profit. It also expects 2026 annual revenue at a record-high level and a sharp year-on-year rise in operating profit. These are management expectations, not reported results.

Wider business scope and a rise in the CEO's stake

The company is widening its scope into data-center access control, construction safety platforms and cloud-based integrated security. According to a late-August 2026 report based on regulatory disclosures, CEO Shin Yo-sik bought roughly KRW 181 million of shares on the open market, raising his stake to 22.17%.

Bear factors

Three straight quarters of operating losses

The company has reported operating losses for three consecutive quarters from the first quarter to the third quarter of 2026. Net losses attributable to controlling shareholders total KRW 1.16 billion over the last four quarters.

The third quarter alone showed an operating loss of KRW 737 million and a net loss attributable of KRW 1.55 billion, a swing into loss year on year.

Weaker earnings base since 2025

Revenue fell 9.6% to KRW 51.3 billion in 2025, and the operating margin of 2.0% was the lowest among 2022 to 2025. Operating cash flow turned to -KRW 12 billion in 2025. Quarterly revenue in 2026 has moved between KRW 11.4 billion and KRW 13.8 billion without a steady trend.

Gap between company outlook and reported results

In May 2026 the company said it expected record results in the second half, but the reported third quarter showed an operating loss. Because recognition of the large contract is concentrated in the fourth quarter, any delay in installation would push back the earnings effect. Many new business and overseas data-center plans remain at the planning stage.

Risk factors

Contract execution and revenue timing

The army contract runs until December 24, 2026, and the company aims to finish installation by December and recognize revenue in the fourth quarter. If installation slips, fourth-quarter revenue and the timing of a return to profit would also slip.

Because it is a large SI project, terminal supply, software development and server and network construction must all be coordinated.

Dependence on public and defense procurement

Because military and public-agency orders are central to revenue, quarterly results can swing with procurement timing and bid outcomes. The 2026 army project was decided in a two-bidder competition.

With about 62% of revenue coming from domestic sales, the company is also sensitive to changes in domestic public procurement.

New-business spending and cash flow

In the first quarter of 2026 the company concentrated strategic investment in global marketing and new business development. Expanding overseas exhibitions and data-center projects requires spending before revenue arrives.

Given that operating cash flow turned to -KRW 12 billion in 2025, investment outlays could add to cash pressure.

Overall view

UNION biometrics has built an integrated security business for military and public customers on its biometric access-control equipment. The approximately KRW 17.1 billion army contract disclosed in October 2026 is its largest single order, and cumulative military orders reach about KRW 42.9 billion.

On a reported basis, operating profit peaked at KRW 2.84 billion in 2024 and fell to KRW 1.02 billion in 2025.

Operating losses have continued for three consecutive quarters from the first quarter to the third quarter of 2026, and net losses attributable to controlling shareholders total KRW 1.16 billion over the last four quarters.

The company expects a return to profit in the fourth quarter and record-high annual revenue in 2026, but these are management expectations. Strengths include the large order and wider business scope, while weaknesses include continued quarterly losses and weaker operating cash flow.
