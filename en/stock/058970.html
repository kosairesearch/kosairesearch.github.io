Report overview

Revenue Up, Profit Down in the SaaS Transition

EMRO., Incorporated grew revenue to KRW 84 billion in 2025, its third straight year of growth, yet its operating margin fell to 1.1% and operating losses have run for four consecutive quarters, leaving whether its global SRM SaaS spending converts into profit as the key open question.

02

Business structure

EMRO., Incorporated is an enterprise supply chain management software company founded in 2000, offering procurement system build-outs, consulting, technical support, software licenses and cloud services.

Its product line is grouped under the SMART suite brand, and it has broadened a lineup combining proprietary AI technology since launching its first AI software in 2019.

Its 2025 segment revenue comprised software licenses of KRW 9.75 billion (up 18.1% year on year), cloud service usage fees of KRW 6.13 billion (up 13.6%) and technology fees of KRW 18.11 billion (up 43.2%), figures the company announced in January 2026.

Taken together, these technology-based revenues totaled KRW 33.98 billion, or 40.5% of total sales. Technology fees are generated annually from customers that adopt EMRO solutions, which gives them a recurring character.

The remaining revenue appears to be centered on service work such as system build-outs and consulting. The company describes its customer base of major Korean conglomerates, built over more than 20 years, as a core asset, and a company profile cites projects with Samsung C&T on procurement supply chain optimization.

In the cumulative results for the first nine months of 2025, a next-generation procurement system operation and maintenance contract with a leading Korean IT company was cited as a driver of revenue growth.

Overseas, the company has pursued a global SRM SaaS business centered on North America under the Caidentia brand in partnership with Samsung SDS since 2024. The combined stake held by Samsung SDS and other related parties stood at about 40% as of August 2025.

04

Earnings analysis

On a disclosed-results basis, revenue rose from KRW 58.6 billion in 2022 to KRW 63.2 billion in 2023 (up 7.8% year on year), KRW 79.5 billion in 2024 (up 25.8%) and KRW 84 billion in 2025 (up 5.7%), growing for three consecutive years from 2023 through 2025. The largest year in the 2022 to 2025 span was 2025.

In January 2026 the company said 2025 revenue set a record for the seventh straight year. Operating profit was KRW 6.49 billion in 2022, KRW 4.66 billion in 2023 (down 28.1%), KRW 8.61 billion in 2024 (up 84.6%) and KRW 910 million in 2025 (down 89.4%), making 2024 the largest year and 2025 the smallest.

Operating margin moved from 11.1% in 2022 to 7.4% in 2023, 10.8% in 2024 and 1.1% in 2025.

For the 2025 decline, the company pointed to investment in global SRM SaaS development and overseas expansion, and Korea Investment & Securities also said the third quarter of 2025 reflected large-scale hiring and higher SaaS platform development costs.

The 2023 swing to a loss, with net loss attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 26.7 billion, appears tied to a KRW 29.3 billion derivative loss recorded among the non-cash add-backs in the cash flow statement, according to FnGuide.

In 2024, net income attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 18.6 billion exceeded operating profit, supported by KRW 12.6 billion of financial income according to FnGuide. Net income attributable to controlling shareholders fell 84.3% to KRW 2.93 billion in 2025.

Quarterly, revenue was KRW 21.3 billion in the third quarter of 2025 with operating profit of KRW 315 million, then KRW 20 billion in the fourth quarter with an operating loss of KRW 520 million.

The first quarter of 2026 brought revenue of KRW 14.8 billion (down 26.3% from the prior quarter) and an operating loss of KRW 2.76 billion, after which the operating losses narrowed to KRW 1.11 billion in the second quarter and KRW 324 million in the third quarter.

For the first nine months of 2025, general and administrative expenses rose 74.9% year on year and R&D expenses rose 56.4%, according to FnGuide.

05

Industry analysis

EMRO's market is enterprise SRM and SCM software that digitally manages corporate procurement and sourcing supply chains. The company says the global SRM solutions market is growing faster than other enterprise software categories (Asiae, report dated November 14, 2025).

The spread of global supply chain disruption following US tariff policy is also cited as a demand driver, and the spread of agentic AI-driven workflow automation is presented as a medium-term growth factor.

In Korea, the company says accelerating AI-centered digital transformation is prompting existing customers to upgrade EMRO systems or extend them to subsidiaries and overseas affiliates, which it interprets as a lock-in effect.

Korea Investment & Securities reported that technology-based revenue's share rose 8.2 percentage points to 39.6% in the third quarter of 2025.

On the cycle, large-enterprise IT spending and the reshaping of global supply chains appear to be the two demand pillars, though external data on market size and share are not included here.

Within Korea's supply chain management software market, the company describes itself as the domestic leader in AI-based supply chain management software. Overseas, it competes head-on with global SaaS vendors, so reference customers and local sales capability are the key factors.

Working with Samsung SDS to pursue North America and Europe can be read as a way to compensate for the limits of its own sales reach.

06

Outlook

The company has said it plans to win new customers in Europe in 2026 to accelerate its global push (Edaily, report dated January 27, 2026). It describes solution demos and proofs of concept underway with companies in energy, high-tech and manufacturing.

In a November 2025 announcement it said it is broadening its pipeline into high-tech, automotive, industrial goods, life sciences and consumer goods.

Korea Investment & Securities attributed the third-quarter 2025 decline to overseas expansion costs and the base effect of a large 2024 contract win, and said the company has room for improvement over the medium term.

The company said it expects steady medium-term growth, citing rapid expansion of the SRM market and AI-driven automation.

However, quarterly revenue in 2026 was KRW 14.8 billion in the first quarter, KRW 17.8 billion in the second and KRW 17.5 billion in the third, staying between roughly KRW 15 billion and KRW 18 billion and below the KRW 21.3 billion of the third quarter of 2025.

Whether technology fee growth can support the top line, and whether the pace of SaaS development and staffing costs keeps running ahead of revenue growth, will determine the path to a profit recovery. The fourth-quarter 2026 results will be confirmed through the annual preliminary results and the annual report.

08

Bull factors

Recurring technology-based revenue

Technology-based revenue reached KRW 33.98 billion in 2025, or 40.5% of total sales, and technology fees within it rose 43.2% to KRW 18.11 billion. Because these fees recur annually from adopting customers, the company presents them as a factor that improves revenue predictability. Cumulative data for the first nine months of 2025 also show technology fees up 30.6% year on year.

Expansion of the global SRM SaaS business

The Caidentia brand, developed with Samsung SDS, is expanding mainly in North America. Demos and proofs of concept are underway with energy, high-tech and manufacturing companies, and the company is pursuing new European customers.

The bull argument is that if overseas revenue builds up in practice, it can offset the limits of a Korea-centered revenue base.

Large-enterprise customer base and lock-in

The company has a customer base of major Korean conglomerates built over more than 20 years. In the cumulative results for the first nine months of 2025, a next-generation procurement system operation and maintenance contract with a leading Korean IT company was cited as a revenue driver.

The company says lock-in effects from existing customers, driven by AI-centered digital transformation, have helped improve results, which is a bull point because revenue can continue from current accounts.

09

Bear factors

Sharp fall in operating margin

The operating margin fell from 10.8% in 2024 to 1.1% in 2025. Losses narrowed after the first quarter of 2026, but the company remains in operating-loss territory.

Stalled revenue trend

Revenue in the first quarter of 2026 was KRW 14.8 billion, down 26.3% from the prior quarter. It recovered to KRW 17.8 billion in the second quarter and KRW 17.5 billion in the third, but both remain below the KRW 21.3 billion of the third quarter of 2025. Third-quarter 2026 revenue fell 17.9% year on year.

Upfront spending and non-operating volatility

For the first nine months of 2025, general and administrative expenses rose 74.9% and R&D expenses rose 56.4% year on year.

In 2023, a KRW 29.3 billion derivative loss added back to the cash flow statement coincided with a swing to a KRW 26.7 billion net loss attributable to controlling shareholders, and 2024 net income was partly supported by KRW 12.6 billion of financial income. Earnings that swing sharply on non-operating items are a bear point.

10

Risk factors

Earnings volatility and non-cash items

The 2023 net loss is tied to a cash flow statement that included a KRW 29.3 billion derivative loss among its add-backs, while 2024 net income relied heavily on KRW 12.6 billion of financial income.

Operating profit reflects core profitability, but net income can change direction with non-operating items, so both measures need to be read together.

Trading concentration and shareholder structure

Media reports said the Korea Exchange designated EMRO as an investment caution stock for one day on August 5, 2026, citing trading concentrated in a small number of foreign accounts.

The combined stake held by Samsung SDS and related parties was about 40% as of August 2025, so the shareholder structure can affect share supply and demand. Short-term trading concentration can amplify price swings.

Execution risk in overseas expansion

If the Caidentia business in North America and new European customers do not convert into revenue as quickly as planned, development costs may keep weighing on results. Competing with large global SaaS vendors makes the pace of reference-customer wins decisive.

Partly relying on the Samsung SDS partnership means changes in alliance terms could affect how the business develops.

12

Overall view

EMRO's disclosed results show revenue growth and deteriorating profitability at the same time. Revenue rose for three consecutive years from KRW 58.6 billion in 2022 to KRW 84 billion in 2025, and the share of technology-based revenue, anchored by technology fees, reached 40.5%.

Operating margin, however, fell from 10.8% in 2024 to 1.1% in 2025, and operating losses have continued for four consecutive quarters from the fourth quarter of 2025.

The company attributes these costs to global SRM SaaS development and overseas investment, and it is pursuing its Caidentia business in North America and new European customers.

Bull factors are the recurring nature of technology-based revenue, the large-enterprise customer base and the Samsung SDS partnership, while bear factors are the sharp drop in operating margin, stalled quarterly revenue in 2026, and rapid growth in administrative and R&D expenses.

Net income has swung widely with non-operating items, so it should be read alongside operating profit. The P/E cannot currently be calculated and the price-to-book ratio sits above 1x, so whether the expectations in the share price rest on asset value or on overseas growth has not yet been settled by earnings.

The next checkpoints are the 2026 annual preliminary results and the annual business report, and the trend in first-quarter 2027 operating results will be the first signal on whether profitability returns.
