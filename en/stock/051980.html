Report overview

Narrower Losses, but a Thin Earnings Base During Restructuring

As the building materials business is being wound down and the lithium salt joint venture remains at an early stage, Joongang Advanced Materials has narrowed its annual losses but has kept posting quarterly operating losses in 2026.

  4. 4

02

Business structure

Joongang Advanced Materials manufactures building materials such as PVC windows and ducts, telecom equipment and devices used in electric railcars, and provides sales agency services.

Telecom equipment accounted for KRW 16.5 billion, or 83.5%, of 2025 consolidated revenue, and PVC and building materials accounted for KRW 3.2 billion, or 16.1%, according to press reports.

The company added lithium salt distribution, a secondary battery base material, in 2023, but materials sales were only KRW 52 million in 2025, or 0.3% of the total. Company profile data describes its telecom business as holding a dominant position in electric railcars and public sector projects.

In defense and security, it is expanding by offering electronically scanned radar under an exclusive Korean value-added resale agreement with the UK radar maker Blighter Surveillance Systems.

The March 2026 annual shareholder meeting put forward articles amendments adding radar manufacturing and maintenance, renewable power plant operation, and non-ferrous metal smelting to the business purpose.

The board decided on October 28, 2026 to suspend the building materials business, with the suspension effective December 31, 2026. The suspended amount is about KRW 3.18 billion, equal to 16.07% of 2025 total sales of KRW 19.8 billion, and the stated reasons are continuing sales declines and operating losses.

The company said it plans to cut fixed costs and concentrate resources on its core businesses by exiting loss-making operations.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual revenue was KRW 29.1 billion in 2022, KRW 26.3 billion in 2023 (down 9.5% year on year), KRW 18.7 billion in 2024 (down 28.9%), and KRW 19.8 billion in 2025 (up 6.0%). 2022 was the largest year in the period for revenue and 2024 was the smallest.

Operating performance moved from an operating loss of KRW 5.42 billion in 2022 to an operating profit of KRW 46.94 million in 2023, a swing to profit, before turning to an operating loss of KRW 7.8 billion in 2024, a swing back to loss.

The 2025 operating loss of KRW 4.56 billion was a continued loss but smaller than in 2024. Operating margins were -18.7% in 2022, 0.2% in 2023, -41.7% in 2024 and -23.0% in 2025, and the company recorded losses for two consecutive years from 2024 to 2025.

The net loss attributable to controlling shareholders grew from KRW 18.3 billion in 2022 to KRW 48.4 billion in 2023 and KRW 76.8 billion in 2024, then narrowed to KRW 5.5 billion in 2025, a loss for four consecutive years from 2022 to 2025.

The 2024 net loss was far larger than the operating loss, which points to a large non-operating component.

On a quarterly basis, revenue in the fourth quarter of 2025 rose 76.3% from the prior quarter to KRW 7.31 billion, the operating loss narrowed to KRW 813 million, and net income attributable to controlling shareholders turned positive at KRW 4.67 billion.

In the first quarter of 2026, revenue was KRW 2.33 billion and the operating loss widened to KRW 2.32 billion, and press reports said gross profit of KRW 600 million was overwhelmed by selling and administrative expenses of KRW 2.9 billion.

In the third quarter of 2026, revenue was KRW 2.69 billion, down 35.0% year on year, and the operating loss widened again to KRW 2.18 billion. Operating cash flow went from negative KRW 11.2 billion in 2022 and negative KRW 21.2 billion in 2023 to positive KRW 7.37 billion in 2024 and KRW 3.45 billion in 2025.

05

Industry analysis

Joongang Advanced Materials is classified as a semiconductor company on the Korean exchange, but its business structure is far from chip manufacturing.

Most revenue comes from building materials and telecom equipment, so it is more exposed to construction activity and demand for public and railcar telecom infrastructure than to the semiconductor cycle.

A report on a listed construction company in which the company is the largest shareholder, dated November 4, 2026, described weak construction conditions dampening investor sentiment.

On the battery side, the lithium salt distribution business and the joint venture with Enchem exist, but they contributed only 0.3% of 2025 revenue. A market data service said demand for secondary battery and base materials is expected to grow with the expansion of the electric vehicle market.

Enchem, a maker of electrolytes and additives for secondary batteries, became the largest shareholder with a 14.53% stake in January 2026. Compared with semiconductor equipment and materials names such as HANMI Semiconductor and Soulbrain, the revenue mix and customer base differ clearly.

The defense and security radar business targets military and public demand, but its revenue scale has not yet emerged. The variables that will determine its industry position are the stability of the telecom business after the building materials exit and whether the lithium salt business reaches mass production.

06

Outlook

The one confirmed date is December 31, 2026, when the building materials business suspension takes effect.

The company disclosed that revenue from the suspended segment will inevitably decline, but that it expects better profitability over the medium to long term through lower fixed costs and a concentration of resources on its core businesses.

From 2027 the telecom segment may therefore account for a larger share of revenue. On lithium salts, a company official said the joint venture plans to begin trial operation in the first half of 2027. The company expects lithium salt distribution revenue to begin once mass production follows that trial run.

However, its loans to the joint venture, in which it holds a 50% stake, stood at KRW 11.9 billion at the end of March 2026, and it provided a joint guarantee of KRW 12.0 billion with Enchem and others to fund the joint venture's plant in Saemangeum.

Maturities on KRW 5.0 billion and KRW 2.0 billion of those loans were extended to 2027. In defense and security, the company plans to expand radar projects in critical national facilities and defense and security fields, building on its technical support and maintenance capabilities for the Blighter radar line.

The 2026 annual report, due by late March 2027, will show revenue composition and the annual operating loss trend after the suspension in numbers.

08

Bull factors

Exiting loss-making operations to cut fixed costs

The company disclosed that it will stop the building materials business, which has seen continuing sales declines and operating losses, effective December 31, 2026. It says exiting this segment should reduce fixed costs and focus resources on core operations.

The operating margin improved from -41.7% in 2024 to -23.0% in 2025, and whether that trend continues is the point to check.

Relatively stable balance sheet at the end of 2025

Total liabilities were KRW 23.5 billion at the end of 2025 against total equity of KRW 103.8 billion, a debt ratio of 22.6%. That compares with 65.1% in 2022 and 105.6% in 2023, so leverage has fallen.

Operating cash flow turned positive, moving from negative in 2022 and 2023 to KRW 7.37 billion in 2024 and KRW 3.45 billion in 2025.

Options in lithium salts and defense and security

The company says the joint venture plans trial operation in the first half of 2027, and that lithium salt distribution revenue would follow once mass production begins. It is also targeting the defense and security market through an exclusive Korean value-added resale agreement for Blighter radar. Both businesses still contribute little revenue, so execution is the point to watch.

09

Bear factors

Continued quarterly operating losses

The company has recorded operating losses for five consecutive quarters from the third quarter of 2025 through the third quarter of 2026. The operating loss widened to KRW 2.18 billion in the third quarter of 2026 from the prior quarter.

The net loss attributable to controlling shareholders narrowed year on year to KRW 3.08 billion in the third quarter of 2026, but it has been a loss for three consecutive quarters since the first quarter of 2026.

Funding and guarantee burden on affiliates

Loans to the joint venture stood at KRW 11.9 billion at the end of March 2026, and a KRW 12.0 billion joint guarantee was provided. Materials sales were still only 0.3% of revenue, so recovering these funds depends on mass production and expanded distribution.

Press reports also said about KRW 1.4 billion of cash flowed out of operating activities in the first quarter of 2026.

Pledged major shareholder stake and listing requirements

Enchem, the largest shareholder, pledged its 16,142,059 common shares, equal to a 14.53% stake, as collateral. The company has said that if the collateral is sold or ownership transfers, the largest shareholder could change.

A KOSDAQ rule that took effect July 1, 2026 can lead to administrative issue designation and delisting review if the share price stays below KRW 1,000, which led to the reverse stock split plan.

10

Risk factors

Business contraction risk

Revenue from the building materials segment will end after the suspension takes effect on December 31, 2026. The segment accounted for 16.1% of 2025 revenue. How much telecom revenue can offset the loss has not yet been shown, and whether fixed cost savings outweigh the sales decline is the key question.

Affiliate funding and guarantee risk

The joint venture's Saemangeum plant financing relies on a joint guarantee. If trial operation slips beyond the first half of 2027, the recovery of loans and the equity-method results could stay under pressure.

The company also subscribed in full to its allotment in the rights offering of a listed construction affiliate, adding to its funding commitments.

Liquidity and governance risk

Of current liabilities at the end of the first quarter of 2026, KRW 16.1 billion was current portion of long-term borrowings due within a year. If core operations do not restore cash generation, repayment pressure could increase.

Because of the pledged major shareholder stake and the reverse stock split under the low-priced stock rule, volatility in governance and share supply also needs monitoring.

12

Overall view

Joongang Advanced Materials has posted annual operating and net losses attributable to controlling shareholders since 2022, but in 2025 both losses narrowed from 2024.

The board decision on October 28, 2026 to suspend the building materials business narrows the revenue base in exchange for lower fixed costs, a structural restructuring. The debt ratio of 22.6% and total equity of KRW 103.8 billion at the end of 2025 provide the basis for its financial capacity.

On the other hand, quarterly operating losses have continued through 2026, the operating loss widened in the third quarter, and lithium salt sales remain at 0.3% of revenue, leaving a large gap between the core business and new businesses.

Loans and guarantees to the joint venture and the pledged major shareholder stake are risks to monitor. Strengths include exiting loss-making operations and a steadier balance sheet, while weaknesses include continuing quarterly losses and the funding burden on affiliates.

The next checkpoints are the revenue structure after the December 31, 2026 suspension and the joint venture's trial operation in the first half of 2027.
