02

Business structure

Iljin Power is a power plant maintenance company whose core business is routine maintenance of power generation equipment.

Scheduled maintenance work at operating plants is the main source of revenue, and the 3Q 2025 cumulative results attributed the revenue improvement to more maintenance work in the power generation division and more nuclear plant maintenance.

The plant and chemical equipment division manufactures petrochemical facilities, covering revenue beyond maintenance. The growth segment is fuel cells, operated through wholly owned subsidiaries Iljin Energy, Jinchon Green Energy and Goyang Green Energy, which build and run fuel cell power plants.

In a June 2026 report, the independent research house said the domestic power plant maintenance market is split roughly 44% to KEPCO Plant Service & Engineering and 56% to private maintenance firms including Iljin Power.

The same report cited accumulated operating know-how in routine plant maintenance and high entry barriers as the core strengths. Because the work requires long performance records and technical staff, new entrants are limited.

At the March 31, 2026 shareholders meeting, the articles of incorporation were amended to add power generation, combined heat and power, district energy supply, EPC and O&M to the business purposes.

Overseas, the company set up a Abu Dhabi subsidiary in 2024 and has taken part in maintenance work at the Barakah nuclear plant. In January 2026 it signed routine maintenance contracts with Dongducheon Dream Power, Incheon Airport Energy and the Seoul energy corporation, according to its company history.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual revenue rose 30.5% to KRW 252 billion in 2025. Revenue has grown for two consecutive years from 2024 through 2025, following the 2023 low, and 2025 was the largest year of 2022 to 2025.

Operating profit fell in 2023 and 2024, from KRW 17.9 billion in 2022 to KRW 13.1 billion and then KRW 10.3 billion, before rising 134.8% to KRW 24.1 billion in 2025. 2024 was the smallest operating profit year of 2022 to 2025.

Operating margin dropped from 9.1% in 2022 to 6.9% in 2023 and 5.3% in 2024, then recovered to 9.6% in 2025. Controlling-shareholder net income rose 87.6% to KRW 18.6 billion in 2025, while net income including non-controlling interests was KRW 19.6 billion, a separate figure.

At year-end 2025, total liabilities were KRW 177.9 billion and total equity KRW 151.2 billion, and the debt ratio climbed from 97.2% in 2024 to 117.6% in 2025. Operating cash flow fell to KRW 2.5 billion in 2024 before rising to KRW 37 billion in 2025.

Quarterly revenue went from KRW 70.4 billion in 4Q 2025 to KRW 63.8 billion in 1Q 2026 and peaked at KRW 86.4 billion in 2Q 2026, then fell 19.2% quarter on quarter to KRW 69.8 billion in 3Q 2026, though it was still up 7.8% year on year.

Quarterly operating profit peaked at KRW 8.11 billion in 4Q 2025 and then declined to KRW 7 billion in 1Q 2026 and KRW 5.6 billion in 2Q 2026, while KRW 5.87 billion in 3Q 2026 was down 21.0% year on year.

Controlling-shareholder net income peaked at KRW 7.06 billion in 4Q 2025 and has fallen for three consecutive quarters since, to KRW 3.34 billion in 3Q 2026. Net income attributable to controlling shareholders over the last four quarters totals KRW 20.3 billion.

Revenue has risen year on year while profit has fallen, which points to margin pressure, and the cost drivers behind this gap are a question for the segment cost detail in the periodic reports.

05

Industry analysis

The independent research house argued that demand for power plant maintenance is growing structurally as electricity consumption expands across industries. The spread of AI data centers and higher semiconductor capital spending were also cited as drivers of power infrastructure demand.

In the domestic plant maintenance market, KEPCO Plant Service & Engineering holds roughly 44% and private maintenance firms, including Iljin Power, share about 56%. Because the work requires long performance records and technical staff, new entrants face limits, which defines the competitive structure.

No individual share figure for Iljin Power was given, so comparison with KEPCO Plant Service & Engineering is possible only at the level of market structure. Among private maintenance firms, Iljin Power combines nuclear plant maintenance with fuel cell projects, which widens its business scope.

In the nuclear segment, its registration as a qualified supplier for general hydropower plant maintenance was confirmed in January 2026. Nuclear maintenance overseas is being pursued through the Abu Dhabi subsidiary.

On the other hand, quarterly revenue fell from KRW 86.4 billion in 2Q 2026 to KRW 69.8 billion in 3Q 2026, so the timing of maintenance volume creates wide swings between quarters. The breakdown of those swings by cause is a matter for the annual report.

06

Outlook

The independent research house estimates 2026 consolidated revenue of KRW 330.3 billion and operating profit of KRW 37.5 billion, up 31.1% and 55.6% from the prior year. The same estimates put fuel cell revenue at KRW 44.3 billion, up 81.6%, and nuclear segment revenue at KRW 35.3 billion, up 27.8%.

Given the disclosed revenue for the first three quarters of 2026, reaching the estimate requires a large amount of revenue in 4Q, which makes the 4Q result the point that decides whether the estimate is met.

Fuel cells are described as distributed power sources that can be installed near data centers or urban areas, a segment where demand is expanding. The research house expects fuel cell revenue to post the highest growth rate in 2026 once the effect of new power plant operations is fully reflected.

In March 2026 the company amended its articles to add power generation, renewable power generation, EPC and O&M to its business purposes, creating a legal basis to widen its power asset operations. Overseas, the company is pursuing market expansion centered on Barakah nuclear plant maintenance.

Whether the nuclear revenue estimate shows up in segment results is a question for the 2026 annual report. Revenue peaked at KRW 86.4 billion in 2Q 2026, yet operating profit in the same quarter was KRW 5.6 billion, and that gap is the test for this outlook.

08

Bull factors

Earnings recovery confirmed for 2025

Operating profit rose 134.8% to KRW 24.1 billion in 2025, and operating margin recovered from 5.3% in 2024 to 9.6%. Operating cash flow over the same year climbed to KRW 37 billion from KRW 2.5 billion in 2024. Profit and cash flow recovering together on a reported basis is the main bullish point.

Maintenance demand and high entry barriers

The research house said demand for power plant maintenance is growing structurally as AI data centers spread and electricity consumption rises. It also noted that long performance records and technical staff keep new entrants limited.

Having both nuclear maintenance experience and a fuel cell business widens the scope of the company's operations.

Estimated 2026 growth in fuel cells and nuclear

According to the research house's estimates, 2026 fuel cell revenue rises 81.6% to KRW 44.3 billion and nuclear revenue rises 27.8% to KRW 35.3 billion. The same estimates put 2026 operating profit at KRW 37.5 billion, up 55.6%. The January 2026 maintenance contracts and the expanded business purposes are also cited as grounds for growth.

Bear factors

Quarterly profit down for three consecutive quarters

Controlling-shareholder net income peaked at KRW 7.06 billion in 4Q 2025 and fell for three consecutive quarters to KRW 3.34 billion in 3Q 2026. Operating profit of KRW 5.87 billion in 3Q 2026 was down 21.0% year on year.

Revenue was up 7.8% over the same period, but profit did not follow, which is the main bearish point on margins.

Rising debt burden

The debt ratio rose from 97.2% in 2024 to 117.6% in 2025, and year-end 2025 total liabilities of KRW 177.9 billion exceeded total equity of KRW 151.2 billion. Operating cash flow was only KRW 2.5 billion in 2024, and the KRW 37 billion in 2025 reflects a single year of recovery. Fuel cell plant construction could add investment burden, so the funding structure needs to be checked.

Limits of the maintenance market structure

KEPCO Plant Service & Engineering holds about 44% of the domestic plant maintenance market, with private firms sharing roughly 56%. In a market where private firms compete, securing volume determines results.

Quarterly revenue fell from KRW 86.4 billion in 2Q 2026 to KRW 69.8 billion in 3Q 2026, which shows how large volume swings can be.

Risk factors

Quarterly earnings volatility

Revenue fell from KRW 86.4 billion in 2Q 2026 to KRW 69.8 billion in 3Q 2026, and operating profit declined year on year to KRW 5.87 billion in 3Q. If maintenance volume clusters in particular quarters, quarterly results can swing sharply. Because the annual estimate depends on a strong 4Q, any shortfall in that quarter is a risk.

Financial leverage

The debt ratio reached 117.6%, up from 2024, and total liabilities exceed total equity. Operating cash flow recovered to KRW 37 billion in 2025, but compared with KRW 2.5 billion in 2024 that is a single year of change. A shift in interest rates or borrowing terms could affect interest costs and the earnings structure.

Dependence on estimates and project timing

The 2026 growth in fuel cells and nuclear rests on the research house's estimates. If new power plants start operating later than planned, reaching those estimates becomes harder.

The timing of overseas nuclear maintenance work at Barakah and changes in domestic nuclear and power policy could also affect project schedules.

Overall view

Iljin Power's reported results show an earnings recovery in 2025, with revenue up 30.5% to KRW 252 billion and operating profit up 134.8% to KRW 24.1 billion.

However, controlling-shareholder net income peaked in 4Q 2025 and has declined for three consecutive quarters through 3Q 2026, and 3Q 2026 operating profit fell 21.0% year on year. The debt ratio increased to 117.6% in 2025.
