Group DX Orders Rebound as Quarterly Profit Recovers

POSCO DX posted its largest quarterly revenue of 2026 in 3Q and saw operating profit rise for two straight quarters, but annual profits have not yet recovered from the decline that followed the 2023 peak.

02

Business structure

POSCO DX is the IT services and industrial automation affiliate of the POSCO Group, and its business is split into an IT division and an automation (EIC) division.

In 3Q 2026, standalone IT division revenue was KRW 121.9 billion, up 11.0% year on year, with large group DX projects and the expansion of the group's integrated AI infrastructure lifting sales.

Automation division revenue in the same quarter was KRW 138 billion, up 7.0% from a year earlier, supported by orders for a new Gwangyang 8CGL line and expanding robotics and unmanned operations. The two divisions earn profit in very different ways.

The IT division's stable operating business, based on service-level agreements, produces steady earnings, while the automation division is larger by revenue but posted operating profit of KRW 4.4 billion at a 3.2% margin.

Of the standalone operating profit of KRW 16.6 billion, the IT division contributed KRW 12.2 billion. Customer mix is 63% POSCO, 31% other group affiliates, 3% POSCO Future M, and only 3% external customers. That makes group facility investment and DX project timing the main drivers of results.

The company plans to apply physical AI and AI agent technologies, first verified in steel plants, across group affiliates before expanding external business from 2027.

Among competitors, large IT services firms such as Samsung SDS, LG CNS and Hyundai Autoever are the reference points as the company pushes into the external market.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual results turned after the 2023 peak. Revenue rose from KRW 1.15 trillion in 2022 to KRW 1.49 trillion in 2023 (up 28.9%), eased to KRW 1.47 trillion in 2024 (down 0.8%), and fell to KRW 1.08 trillion in 2025 (down 27.0%).

Operating profit climbed from KRW 64.7 billion in 2022 to KRW 110.6 billion in 2023 (up 71.0%), then declined to KRW 109 billion in 2024 (down 1.4%) and KRW 60.4 billion in 2025 (down 44.6%). The operating margin was 7.4% in both 2023 and 2024 and fell to 5.6% in 2025.

Net profit attributable to owners of the parent rose from KRW 45.6 billion in 2022 to KRW 91.9 billion in 2023 (up 101.6%), then fell to KRW 88 billion in 2024 and KRW 52.1 billion in 2025. Net income including non-controlling interests was KRW 52.6 billion in 2025, a separate figure from the attributable amount.

The balance sheet strengthened: the debt-to-equity ratio fell from 105.7% in 2022 to 45.7% in 2025, with total equity of KRW 572.6 billion and total liabilities of KRW 261.8 billion at end-2025. Operating cash flow went from negative KRW 3.94 billion in 2022 to KRW 146 billion in 2025. The quarterly path is sharper.

After KRW 21.7 billion of operating profit in 3Q 2025, 4Q 2025 revenue of KRW 260.8 billion came with an operating loss of KRW 1.22 billion.

In 1Q 2026, revenue fell to KRW 241.5 billion, and the company attributed the drop to delayed project inputs that shifted revenue recognition, combined with AI and robotics R&D spending (as reported on April 30, 2026).

Operating profit turned to KRW 3.66 billion, then rose to KRW 9.86 billion in 2Q (up 169.6% from the prior quarter) and KRW 16.9 billion in 3Q (up 71.0%), marking two straight quarters of growth.

Provisional cumulative operating profit for January to September 2026 was KRW 30.4 billion, down 50.7% year on year, per the November 2, 2026 provisional disclosure.

05

Industry analysis

POSCO DX's business is tied to the steel industry and the investment cycle of the POSCO Group. When new facilities such as the Gwangyang 8CGL line are built, automation orders increase, and when capital spending slows, the order flow weakens quickly.

The company is extending steel plant control and automation know-how into robotics and unmanned operations, so demand is spreading from steel mills to production sites across other group affiliates.

The IT division moves with digital transformation projects such as the group's AI infrastructure build-out and the upgrade of an automated inspection platform.

AI infrastructure demand is a growth theme across Korea's IT services industry, but because most of POSCO DX's customers are group companies, project schedules within the group determine when revenue is recognized more than overall market growth does.

Peers such as Samsung SDS, LG CNS and Hyundai Autoever have larger external customer shares, so the pace of external order wins will be the main structural difference.

In physical AI and AI agents, competition among platform players such as NAVER is intensifying, so whether steel-plant automation know-how travels to outside markets is a key question. In automation, orders rising while margins fell shows that profitability on facility projects varies from project to project.

For the industry picture, the key is whether group capital spending and external orders grow together rather than whether the broader market turns.

06

Outlook

The company's disclosed and reported plans fall into three areas. First, the order backlog was about KRW 940 billion at the end of 3Q 2026, up from about KRW 700 billion a year earlier, and the KRW 960 billion level at the end of 1Q 2026 was the highest quarterly figure since 2025.

Second, the group's integrated AI infrastructure expansion and the upgrade of an automated inspection platform are included in recent IT division orders, so when these projects are recognized as revenue will shape IT division profitability in coming quarters.

Third, at its July 2026 media day the company declared a shift to becoming an AI-native company and said it will expand external business in earnest from 2027, based on physical AI and AI agent technologies verified inside the group. It also set a target of growing orders and revenue by about 30% within three years.

In automation, orders rose on the Gwangyang 8CGL line and robotics and unmanned operations, but the operating margin fell to 3.2% in 3Q, so whether order volume converts into margin recovery is the next checkpoint.

IT division orders were KRW 56.1 billion, down about 2% year on year, and whether AI infrastructure wins offset that decline will matter. External customers accounted for only 3% of revenue in 3Q 2026, so how that share moves after 2027 will be a practical measure of the target. Whether the project delays seen in 1Q 2026 recur will also determine how volatile quarterly results remain.

08

Bull factors

Consecutive quarterly profit recovery

Operating profit turned to KRW 3.66 billion in 1Q 2026, then rose to KRW 9.86 billion in 2Q and KRW 16.9 billion in 3Q, marking two straight quarters of growth. The operating margin climbed from 1.5% in 1Q to 6.4% in 3Q, according to the company's disclosure and press reports. The shift in the quarterly trend after the 4Q 2025 operating loss is cited as the basis for the recovery.

IT division profitability recovery

IT division operating profit rose from KRW 3.9 billion in 2Q to KRW 12.2 billion in 3Q, and its margin reached 10%. The group's integrated AI infrastructure expansion and an upgrade of the automated inspection platform were included in new orders.

IT division orders were KRW 56.1 billion, down about 2% year on year, but the company said it expanded orders in AI infrastructure and platforms.

Order growth and a larger backlog

New orders in 3Q 2026 were KRW 246.9 billion, up 32.7% year on year, and the order backlog of about KRW 940 billion compares with about KRW 700 billion a year earlier. Automation division orders reached KRW 184.4 billion, up about 49%, including the new Gwangyang 8CGL line. The order backlog of KRW 960 billion at end-1Q 2026 was the highest quarterly level since 2025.

09

Bear factors

Concentration in group revenue

The 3Q 2026 revenue mix was 63% POSCO, 31% other group affiliates and 3% POSCO Future M, with external customers at only 3%. If group facility spending or DX project timing is cut or delayed, revenue is affected immediately.

The company plans to expand external business from 2027, but until that shows up in results it remains an open task.

Lower margin in automation

Automation division operating profit fell to KRW 4.4 billion in 3Q from KRW 5.6 billion in 2Q, and its margin dropped from 4.3% to 3.2%. Revenue and orders rose but did not translate into profit. The cost of robotics, unmanned operations and AI investment is cited as a factor weighing on the margin.

Cumulative profit decline and project delays

Provisional cumulative operating profit for January to September 2026 was KRW 30.4 billion, down 50.7% year on year, per the November 2, 2026 disclosure. Net profit attributable to owners of the parent was KRW 52.1 billion for 2025, down 40.8%, and the company posted an operating loss in 4Q 2025. In 1Q 2026, revenue fell 18.6% year on year as project inputs were delayed.

10

Risk factors

Dependence on group investment cycle

Because most revenue comes from POSCO Group affiliates, any change in group capital spending plans shifts orders and revenue together. A cut in steel facility investment could reduce automation orders, and delays in group DX projects push back IT revenue recognition. With external customers at 3% of revenue, alternative revenue sources to offset such shocks are limited.

Shifts in revenue recognition timing

In 1Q 2026, delayed project inputs shifted revenue recognition, which cut revenue 18.6% and operating profit 84.0% year on year (as reported on April 30, 2026). Project-based revenue makes quarterly results swing with deployment schedules. If this pattern repeats, the trend of quarterly profit recovery could be interrupted.

AI and robotics investment burden and targets

The company has set an AI-native transition and a target of about 30% growth in orders and revenue within three years, but R&D and external expansion costs may weigh on margins first. In 1Q 2026, R&D spending on AI and robotics also reduced operating profit.

Until the target is reached, the gap between investment costs and when they generate revenue remains an open uncertainty.

12

Overall view

In 3Q 2026, POSCO DX booked revenue of KRW 265.2 billion, its largest quarter of 2026, and operating profit of KRW 16.9 billion, up for two straight quarters.

Compared with a year earlier, however, operating profit fell 22.2%, and provisional cumulative operating profit for January to September 2026 was KRW 30.4 billion, down 50.7% (per the November 2, 2026 provisional disclosure).

In the profit structure, the IT division's margin recovery stands out, while the automation division saw orders rise but its margin fall. Rising orders and a larger backlog are cited as supports for revenue visibility, but the fact that most revenue comes from group affiliates remains a structural weakness.

The company's external expansion plan and its target of about 30% growth within three years have not yet shown up in reported results.

On an annual basis, operating profit and net profit attributable to owners declined from the 2023 peak through 2025, while the debt-to-equity ratio fell from 105.7% in 2022 to 45.7% in 2025. Multiples read high against the last four quarters of earnings, so the pace of earnings recovery is central to interpretation. The next checkpoints are the 2026 full-year provisional results, the 2026 annual report and 1Q 2027 results.
