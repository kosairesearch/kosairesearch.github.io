02

Business structure

Fescaro, founded in 2016, is an automotive integrated cybersecurity platform company that listed on KOSDAQ in December 2025 under the technology special listing track.

Its core products span electronic control unit (ECU) security solutions, vehicle communication security solutions covering overall in-vehicle network protection, regulatory-response IT solutions including the CSMS portal, and electronic control unit development solutions.

As of 2024, security solutions (ECU, vehicle communication, regulatory response, and B2G) accounted for 88.4% of revenue, with electronic control development solutions making up 11.6%.

Within security solutions, brokerage estimates at listing showed vehicle communication security solutions at 38.5% and ECU security solutions at 34.4%, the two largest categories.

Its key customer has been KG Mobility (formerly SsangYong Motor), with analysis indicating KG Mobility accounted for more than 60% of revenue over the past three years, while the company has been broadening its customer base to include Hyundai Motor and Kia suppliers, autonomous driving company Autonomous A2Z, and agricultural machinery maker Daedong.

In 2026, Fescaro acquired electronic control unit developer MOTLAB and automotive parts manufacturer JS Automotive, building a vertically integrated structure spanning security design, electronic software development, and parts production.

JS Automotive recorded consolidated revenue of roughly KRW 40 billion in 2025 and holds three SQ quality certifications from Hyundai and Kia along with production bases in Korea, Vietnam, and China.

A notable competitor is AUTOCRYPT, which posted 2024 revenue of KRW 23.2 billion, higher than Fescaro, but an operating loss of KRW 18 billion, suggesting a relatively more stable earnings structure at Fescaro.

The company's business scope is expanding from automobiles into agricultural and construction machinery, and over the longer term it is pursuing an 'SDx' strategy extending into software-defined robots (SDR) and factories (SDF).

04

Earnings analysis

Full-year 2025 revenue reached KRW 16.6 billion, up 16.1% from KRW 14.3 billion in 2024, while operating profit fell 33.5% to KRW 879 million from KRW 1.32 billion, pushing the operating margin down from 9.2% to 5.3%.

Over the same period, net income attributable to controlling shareholders swung from a net loss of KRW 7.87 billion in 2024 to net income of KRW 2.56 billion in 2025, and net income including non-controlling interests moved to the same KRW 2.56 billion level.

The debt-to-equity ratio declined from 12.3% in 2024 to 7.8% in 2025, and at end-2025 total equity stood at KRW 61 billion (all attributable to controlling shareholders) against total liabilities of just KRW 4.76 billion, keeping the balance sheet structure stable.

Operating cash flow edged down from KRW 4.15 billion in 2024 to KRW 3.83 billion in 2025 but stayed positive for a second consecutive year.

On a quarterly basis, Q1 2026 revenue was KRW 6.55 billion with operating profit of KRW 571 million and controlling-shareholder net income of KRW 965 million, while Q2 2026 revenue reached KRW 10.5 billion (up 60.4% quarter-on-quarter), operating profit was KRW 531 million (down 6.9% quarter-on-quarter), and controlling-shareholder net income was KRW 1.28 billion (up 32.7% quarter-on-quarter).

The sharp Q2 revenue jump largely reflects the first-time consolidation of JS Automotive, whose acquisition closed in May.

The sum of controlling-shareholder net income over the last four disclosed quarters (Q1-Q4 2025) was KRW 2.56 billion, matching the full-year 2025 figure, and operating profit growth has continued to lag behind the pace of revenue growth.

The large 2024 net loss appears to have been driven substantially by one-off factors tied to the company's pre-listing financial structure, and the controlling-shareholder net income has remained in positive territory on a quarterly basis since the 2025 turnaround.

05

Industry analysis

The automotive cybersecurity industry is being driven by tightening regulations worldwide, including UN regulations (R155/R156), the EU Cyber Resilience Act (CRA, effective December 2027), and China's GB44495 and GB44496 standards (effective January 2026).

Automakers reportedly face growing compliance costs, needing to submit more than 2,000 documents per new vehicle model and maintain operation and management systems for ten years after mass production, which is increasing demand for outsourced integrated solutions.

Fescaro holds a reference achievement of supporting a customer's acquisition of four major international certifications (CSMS, SUMS, VTA, ISO/SAE 21434) in 2023, described as the first 'grand slam' in Korea.

Competitor AUTOCRYPT leads in revenue scale but has posted large operating losses, giving the two companies diverging positions within the industry on profitability.

The industry trend is tied to the shift toward software-defined vehicles (SDV), with security demand spreading beyond automobiles into agricultural machinery, construction equipment, and autonomous robots.

Fescaro established a local subsidiary in Shanghai in early 2026 to pursue business development targeting smaller Chinese automakers, and it is also working with autonomous driving company Autonomous A2Z on a security upgrade project.

Still, the automotive electronics security and software market remains in an early growth stage where the division of labor between in-house development and external suppliers is still fluid, leaving room for competitive entry and pricing pressure.

06

Outlook

Fescaro has stated that by acquiring MOTLAB in early 2026 and JS Automotive in May, it built a supply chain spanning cybersecurity, electronic control software development, and parts production within about five months of its listing.

The company said it expects synergies between the two acquired firms to become more visible from the second half of 2026, since JS Automotive has been consolidated from the second quarter.

Its business scope continues to expand from automobiles into agricultural and construction machinery, with a longer-term SDx strategy extending into software-defined robots (SDR) and factories (SDF).

Following an extraordinary general meeting on August 6, 2026 that secured distributable retained earnings, the company said it is reviewing shareholder return measures including dividends, premised on sustained profitability.

Overseas, Fescaro is pursuing business development targeting smaller Chinese automakers building on its Shanghai subsidiary established in early 2026, and it is also expanding into autonomous vehicle security through its project with Autonomous A2Z.

Whether this expansion strategy translates into revenue growth at the expected pace, and how much profitability actually improves from integrating the acquired businesses, remain matters to be confirmed through upcoming quarterly results.

08

Bull factors

Structural Demand from Expanding Global Regulation

Expanding automotive cybersecurity regulation worldwide, including the EU CRA (effective December 2027) and China's GB44495 and GB44496 standards (effective January 2026), is driving growing demand from automakers for security system development.

Fescaro is positioned to capture this demand given its reference achievement of a grand slam across four major international certifications and an 82% contract renewal rate.

Its expanding customer base beyond automobiles into agricultural machinery, construction equipment, and autonomous vehicles is also a factor broadening its revenue base.

Vertical Integration Through M&A

Through the sequential acquisitions of MOTLAB in early 2026 and JS Automotive in May, Fescaro built a supply chain covering security design, electronic software development, and parts production.

JS Automotive recorded consolidated revenue of about KRW 40 billion in 2025 and holds three SQ certifications along with production bases in Korea, Vietnam, and China, which is expected to reduce reliance on outsourced manufacturing. The 60.4% quarter-on-quarter revenue increase in Q2 2026 is understood to reflect this acquisition effect.

Stable Financial Structure

The debt-to-equity ratio remained low at 7.8% at end-2025, with total liabilities of just KRW 4.76 billion against total equity of KRW 61 billion. Operating cash flow stayed positive for two consecutive years, at KRW 4.15 billion in 2024 and KRW 3.83 billion in 2025.

Since turning from a net loss in 2024 to net income in 2025, controlling-shareholder net income has continued on a positive quarterly basis.

Bear factors

Declining Operating Margin Trend

The operating margin declined from 9.2% in 2024 to 5.3% in 2025, and Q2 2026 operating profit also fell 6.9% quarter-on-quarter. Profit growth continues to lag behind the pace of revenue growth, indicating that top-line expansion has not directly translated into margin improvement. Additional costs arising from the integration of acquired businesses cannot be ruled out.

Customer Concentration Risk

Analysis has indicated that KG Mobility accounted for more than 60% of revenue over the past three years, reflecting a history of reliance on a specific customer.

While diversification toward Hyundai and Kia suppliers and overseas customers is underway, the extent to which this concentration has structurally declined is not yet confirmed in disclosed filings. Changes in a specific customer's production or sales situation could have a relatively large impact on results.

Valuation Volatility and Share Overhang

Because the proportion of freely tradable shares increases in stages as lock-up periods expire after listing, there is a possibility that holdings by venture capital and professional investors other than the largest shareholder could be released into the market over time.

As is typical for technology special-listed companies, the gap between the valuation applied at the IPO and current trading levels has been relatively wide, resulting in notable share price volatility.

If the manufacturing segment added through M&A carries a relatively lower margin structure than the software business, consolidated profitability metrics could face dilution.

10

Risk factors

Business and Customer Risk

Because the ECU security solution business follows a project-based revenue structure tied to specific vehicle models and controllers, quarterly results can fluctuate depending on a particular customer's new-model development schedule or order timing.

If heavy revenue dependence on a specific automaker persists, weak sales or production at that company could become a direct risk.

M&A Integration Risk

The integration of organizations and systems following the MOTLAB and JS Automotive acquisitions could take more time or cost than expected.

As the manufacturing business JS Automotive is now part of the consolidated scope, the margin structure may differ from the previously software-centered business, making the future trajectory of the consolidated operating margin worth monitoring.

Regulatory and Market Environment Risk

New regulations such as China's GB44495 and GB44496, effective since January 2026, were expected to expand the demand base, but the pace and scale at which this translates into actual orders remain uncertain in the early implementation stage.

The EU CRA is not scheduled to take effect until December 2027, so its regulatory impact could remain limited in the near term.

12

Overall view

Fescaro posted 2025 revenue of KRW 16.6 billion (up 16.1%) and turned profitable with net income attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 2.56 billion, yet its operating margin declined from 9.2% to 5.3%, reflecting a divergence between top-line growth and profitability.

In 2026, the acquisitions of MOTLAB and JS Automotive drove clear revenue expansion, with Q2 revenue up 60.4% quarter-on-quarter, while operating profit over the same period actually fell 6.9%.

On the financial structure side, the company has maintained stability through a low debt ratio and consistent operating cash flow.

The automotive cybersecurity industry is in a structurally growing phase supported by tightening regulation worldwide, but revenue dependence on specific customers and the possibility of margin dilution during M&A integration are variables that warrant attention alongside this growth.

Confirming whether operating margin recovers and the actual scale of synergy between the acquired businesses, through the upcoming Q3 2026 quarterly report and the fiscal year 2026 annual results, will be an important next step.
