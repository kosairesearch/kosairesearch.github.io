02

Business structure

Fescaro was founded in 2016 as a vehicle security platform company whose core business is meeting automotive cybersecurity regulations.

Its main offerings are embedded security for electronic control units (ECUs), a key management system (KMS) and the CSMS Portal, a regulatory compliance IT system, which the company sells as one flow running from security application through certification to operations management.

Proprietary products include the FAST™-HSM vehicle security module and the FAST™-SGW security gateway. Its customers are vehicle manufacturers and parts suppliers, and when it listed in 2025 the company said it had 213 controller mass-production projects with 18 automakers, 33 vehicle models and 45 parts makers.

The same listing documents noted a high revenue dependence on one customer referred to as K. Management plans to widen the business from passenger and commercial vehicles into agricultural and construction machinery, defense and autonomous robots, pursuing electronics acquisitions alongside.

In January 2026 the company acquired MOTLAB, an automotive electronics controller developer, and then completed the acquisition of JS Automotive, a parts manufacturer. The company says the two deals give it an integrated structure linking cybersecurity, vehicle software and production.

Overseas, it set up a China subsidiary in 2025 and signed a security supply contract with a Chinese controller developer.

04

Earnings analysis

On a consolidated basis from the filed annual reports, sales rose from KRW 14.3 billion in 2024 to KRW 16.6 billion in 2025, up 16.1% year on year. Over the same period operating profit fell 33.5%, from KRW 1.32 billion to KRW 879 million, and the operating margin narrowed from 9.2% to 5.3%.

The company attributed the 2025 cost increase to R&D spending on new solutions and listing-related expenses, including non-cash share-based compensation and one-off listing costs.

Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders swung from a net loss of KRW 7.87 billion in 2024 to a profit of KRW 2.56 billion in 2025, while total net profit including non-controlling interests was also KRW 2.56 billion for 2025.

At the end of 2025 total equity stood at KRW 61 billion, all of it attributable to controlling shareholders, and total liabilities were KRW 4.76 billion, lowering the debt ratio from 12.3% to 7.8%. Operating cash flow declined from KRW 4.15 billion to KRW 3.83 billion.

Quarterly comparisons begin in 2026 because the third and fourth quarters of 2025 carry no disclosed figures. In the first quarter of 2026 sales were KRW 6.55 billion, operating profit KRW 571 million and attributable net profit KRW 965 million.

In the second quarter sales rose to KRW 10.5 billion, up 60.4% from the prior quarter, while operating profit eased 6.9% to KRW 531 million and attributable net profit rose 32.7% to KRW 1.28 billion.

In the third quarter sales reached KRW 12.8 billion, up 21.9% quarter on quarter, but the company posted an operating loss of KRW 268 million and an attributable net loss of KRW 180 million.

The company said the third-quarter sales growth was largely driven by consolidating a newly acquired business, and cited a roughly 183% year-on-year rise.

A preliminary nine-month release dated November 17, 2026 put cumulative sales at KRW 29.9 billion, up 154% year on year, and cumulative operating profit at KRW 830 million; these preliminary figures are not part of a periodic filing.

05

Industry analysis

Automotive cybersecurity is a market led by regulation. Europe's UN R155 has entered its recertification phase, Korea's automobile management act took effect in August 2025, and China is preparing to implement GB 44495-2024, according to an industry report from March 2026.

As requirements extend from development-stage certification to ongoing security operations after a vehicle reaches the market, the spread of software-defined vehicles and connected cars is driving demand for these solutions.

In October 2026 Fescaro presented a post-launch security management strategy at the Auto-ISAC Cybersecurity Summit in the United States. On barriers to entry, the company says it spent about five years and roughly KRW 13.5 billion in development costs to secure its product technology.

On certifications, it holds A-SPICE Lv2 and FIPS 140-2 and completed more than 50 compatibility tests with eight chipmakers, according to its listing materials. Hyundai Autoever was used as a comparable company in the listing review.

In robotics, an early-2026 report said the company had won a robot security project and was executing it; industry sources indicated the contract was split into product and task units, so it fell outside mandatory disclosure.

Automotive production cycles and the extent to which automakers develop software in-house remain the other variables for demand.

06

Outlook

The outlook the company has presented starts with security gateway project wins. At the December 2025 listing, CEO Hong Seok-min said ten security gateway projects had been won, with mass-production supply of about KRW 16 billion over roughly seven years from 2027 already confirmed.

At the same event he said the order backlog exceeds KRW 100 billion and set a target of more than KRW 100 billion in sales by 2030. These are management targets that have not yet been verified by 2026 results.

In an August 2026 report, the CEO said the business was proceeding on the original operating plan, as shown in second-quarter results. The same report expected synergies between the acquired companies to become visible in the second half.

To reduce the integration burden behind the third-quarter loss, the company plans to raise profitability step by step through production efficiency and deal synergies. In China, the company expects gradual sales growth from 2026, the second year after its subsidiary was set up, according to a March 2026 report.

On dividends, an extraordinary shareholders' meeting on August 6, 2026 secured distributable profit, creating the institutional basis for payouts.

08

Bull factors

Two Consecutive Quarters of Sales Growth

The company attributes much of the third-quarter increase to consolidating a newly acquired business. A preliminary cumulative release from November 17, 2026 reported sales of KRW 29.9 billion, up 154%, but these figures precede a periodic filing.

Regulation-Driven Demand

Industry reporting points to rising demand for lifecycle security operations as UN R155 enters recertification in Europe, Korea's automobile management act took effect in August 2025 and China's GB 44495-2024 nears implementation.

Fescaro received a model enterprise award at AutoSec 2026 in March 2026, and its China unit signed a supply contract with a controller developer. The company expects China sales to grow gradually from 2026.

Confirmed Mass-Production Volume and Project Base

The company says mass-production supply worth about KRW 16 billion over roughly seven years from 2027 is confirmed for its security gateway projects. Its base of 213 controller mass-production projects across 18 automakers, 33 models and 45 parts makers is presented as the foundation for executing those orders.

Management says combining it with the acquired manufacturing capability creates a structure running from development through production.

09

Bear factors

Third-Quarter Operating Loss and Integration Costs

The third quarter of 2026 turned operating profit into a KRW 268 million loss and attributable net profit into a KRW 180 million loss. The company attributes this to short-term charges during post-acquisition integration, including restructuring of an acquired business and production upgrades.

Second-quarter operating profit of KRW 531 million was also 6.9% below the first quarter, so sales growth did not translate into profit growth.

Customer Concentration and Overseas Revenue Uncertainty

Listing materials noted high revenue dependence on a customer referred to as K. A collaboration with a company referred to as H was at the research and development stage only and had not yet produced revenue.

China sales are expected to grow gradually from 2026, but the timing and scale have not yet appeared in disclosed figures.

Questions About the Quality of the Profit Turnaround

Attributable net profit turned positive in 2025, yet operating profit fell 33.5%. Operating cash flow also declined, from KRW 4.15 billion to KRW 3.83 billion. Bears point out that the profit recovery did not show up at the operating line.

10

Risk factors

Customer Concentration and Order Execution

High dependence on one customer means that customer's production plans or model changes flow directly into results. Whether mass-production supply under the ten security gateway projects starting in 2027 proceeds on schedule is central.

The KRW 100 billion-plus backlog and 2030 target are management figures, so execution has to be checked against reported results.

Acquisition Integration and Profitability

Following the MOTLAB and JS Automotive acquisitions, restructuring and production upgrades are underway, and the associated short-term costs showed up in the third-quarter loss. Owning a manufacturing business can raise fixed costs and inventory exposure, so the pace of any operating margin recovery is a key variable. The company has said it will raise profitability step by step through production efficiency.

Regulatory Timing and Overseas Markets

The pace of automotive cybersecurity regulation in Europe, Korea and China sets the speed of demand. The China unit has signed supply contracts with a controller developer, but its sales scale does not yet appear in disclosed figures.

If regulatory timing slips or local competition intensifies, expectations for overseas revenue could be revised.

Overall view

Fescaro is widening its scope from security software into vehicle controllers and manufacturing, driven by expanding automotive cybersecurity regulation.

Its 2025 annual figures show sales of KRW 16.6 billion, up 16.1%, and a turn to a KRW 2.56 billion profit attributable to controlling shareholders, but operating profit fell 33.5% to KRW 879 million, weakening the profitability picture.

Quarterly sales rose from KRW 6.55 billion in the first quarter of 2026 to KRW 12.8 billion in the third, two consecutive quarters of growth, yet the third quarter brought a KRW 268 million operating loss and a KRW 180 million attributable net loss from integration costs.

Bull points are regulation-driven demand, confirmed mass-production supply worth about KRW 16 billion from 2027 and a base of 213 controller projects; bear points are the third-quarter loss, dependence on one customer and lower operating cash flow.

The preliminary cumulative sales of KRW 29.9 billion released on November 17, 2026 broadly match the filed quarterly totals but remain preliminary ahead of a periodic filing. The 2030 sales target of more than KRW 100 billion has not yet been tested against reported results.

The next checkpoints are the operating margin in the 2026 annual report and how the acquisition effects appear in the first-quarter 2027 report.
