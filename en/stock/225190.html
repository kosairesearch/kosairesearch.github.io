Report overview

Lens Slump Keeps Losses Wide; New Businesses Under Test

Falling interchangeable-lens sales and wider operating losses ran through 2025, quarterly losses narrowed in 2026 before widening again in the third quarter, and new businesses are still too small to offset the decline.

02

Business structure

LK Samyang designs, machines, assembles and sells camera interchangeable lenses, and is reported to be the only domestic company that covers the full process in-house.

In August 2019, LK Investment Partners formed a consortium with A2 Investment Partners and acquired a 60% stake; the company was previously known as Samyang Optics.

Sales are centered on interchangeable lenses, including the MF, AF and XEEN series, which accounted for 85% of 2026 second-quarter sales, while new businesses made up 15%.

The thermal-imaging business is expanding into battery fire detection for electric vehicles and UPS and ESS systems, and the company has supplied EV fire-detection solutions to three major financial centers in Korea.

In space and aerospace, it won new orders for drone optical modules in 2022 and 2024 and is reviewing commercialization of optical modules for satellite star trackers with a Korean partner.

In drones, it moved beyond lens supply to camera modules that combine wide-angle lenses with image sensors, and in August 2026 agreed to supply two winning bidders in a Ministry of National Defense training-drone procurement.

In machine vision, it is preparing cooperation with Germany's Schneider, including a possible joint venture, based on a memorandum of understanding.

In interchangeable lenses, it competes with camera makers such as Sony, Canon, Nikon and Fujifilm, as well as third-party lens brands, while low-priced offerings from Japanese and Chinese makers have pressured margins in AF lenses.

New-business sales were KRW 2.4 billion in 2025, or 11.2% of the total, and because sales are concentrated in interchangeable lenses, shifts in demand for that category feed directly into results.

04

Earnings analysis

On a disclosed basis, sales fell from KRW 54.9 billion in 2022 to KRW 39 billion in 2023 (down 29.0% year on year), KRW 32 billion in 2024 (down 18.0%) and KRW 21.4 billion in 2025 (down 33.1%), declining for three consecutive years from 2023 to 2025.

Among 2022 to 2025, 2022 was the largest year for sales and 2025 the smallest. Operating income of KRW 11 billion in 2022 turned into an operating loss of KRW 1.13 billion in 2023, then widened to an operating loss of KRW 3.23 billion in 2024 and KRW 16.9 billion in 2025.

The operating margin moved from 20.0% in 2022 to -2.9% in 2023, -10.1% in 2024 and -78.7% in 2025. For 2025, the disclosed drivers include inventory clearance after weak sales and fixed-cost burden, and press reports cite an inventory valuation loss of KRW 3.9 billion and cost of sales exceeding revenue.

Lower sales also reflected softer demand in interchangeable lenses and supply interruptions after a major European customer went bankrupt.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders fell from KRW 8.26 billion in 2022 to KRW 729 million in 2023 (down 91.2%), then turned into a net loss of KRW 1.45 billion in 2024 and KRW 18.6 billion in 2025.

Net income including non-controlling interests moved in the same direction, and the net loss has now run for two consecutive years, 2024 and 2025.

Quarterly, sales were KRW 4.75 billion in the third quarter of 2025 with an operating loss of KRW 2.95 billion, then KRW 5.06 billion in the fourth quarter with a larger operating loss of KRW 8.75 billion.

Sales dropped to KRW 3.2 billion in the first quarter of 2026 (down 36.8% from the prior quarter) while the operating loss narrowed to KRW 2.95 billion; in the second quarter, sales rebounded to KRW 5.78 billion (up 80.9%) with an operating loss of KRW 1.94 billion.

In the third quarter of 2026, sales were KRW 4.29 billion (down 25.8% from the prior quarter and down 9.7% year on year) and the operating loss was KRW 2.38 billion. The company has posted operating losses in five consecutive quarters from the third quarter of 2025 through the third quarter of 2026.

At the end of 2025, total equity was KRW 33.3 billion and total liabilities KRW 12 billion, and the debt ratio fell from 55.7% in 2024 to 36.1% in 2025.

Operating cash flow, however, went from KRW 12.2 billion in 2022 to -KRW 1.02 billion in 2024 and -KRW 3.83 billion in 2025, a sign that internal cash generation has weakened.

05

Industry analysis

The camera market is shifting from manual-focus (MF) lenses toward autofocus (AF) lenses, and within that shift the company's MF lens sales fell from KRW 23.4 billion in 2021 to KRW 4.5 billion in 2024. A company official has said the interchangeable-lens market has been in long-term decline.

In AF lenses, low-priced offerings from Japanese and Chinese makers have added to margin pressure.

After finding limits in interchangeable lenses, the company expanded into thermal imaging, space and aerospace, machine vision and lidar, but new businesses accounted for only 13.5% of sales on a cumulative basis through the third quarter of 2025.

New-business sales are still too small to offset the decline in interchangeable-lens revenue. In thermal imaging, the company says it is shifting from one-time installations toward subscription-based relationships for long-term customers.

Its space and aerospace efforts have been tied to aerospace and autonomous-driving themes, and press reports describe repeated sharp rallies followed by pullbacks.

The in-house design-to-assembly structure is presented as a differentiator, but whether it leads to a sales recovery depends on how demand for interchangeable lenses develops.

The company is classified under electrical equipment, yet its business is optical lenses, so its end markets should be read as camera demand and optical applications in drones and aerospace rather than power-equipment demand.

06

Outlook

When it announced 2024 results, the company set a target of returning to profit in 2025, citing expanded new businesses and new product launches. It positioned the AF zoom lens lineup it began launching in 2025 as the starting point of a rebound.

However, the 2025 operating loss widened to KRW 16.9 billion, so the return-to-profit target did not carry through to results.

The company has said that even with capital injected at the end of 2025, immediate earnings improvement would be difficult, and that cost cuts in selling, general and administrative expenses and cost of sales should produce gradual improvement from the first half of 2026.

First-half 2026 consolidated sales were KRW 9 billion and the operating loss was KRW 4.9 billion, so losses remain large. In new businesses, the company is opening a subscription service portal for thermal imaging and supplying drone camera modules.

On July 7, 2026, it disclosed a five-for-one share consolidation to stabilize the share price and maintain an appropriate tradable share count, reducing issued common shares from 70,468,440 to 14,093,688.

The next checkpoints are the annual operating margin in the 2026 annual report and whether interchangeable-lens sales recover on a quarterly basis. Whether new businesses can offset the decline in interchangeable-lens sales will also show up in the quarterly revenue mix in 2027.

Bull factors

Accumulating new-business wins

The thermal-imaging business is expanding into battery fire detection for EVs and UPS and ESS systems, with a track record of supplies to major financial centers in Korea. Space and aerospace drew new drone optical-module orders in 2022 and 2024.

In August 2026, the company agreed to supply camera modules to winning bidders in a defense training-drone program. The share of new-business revenue rose from 9.7% in 2024 to 11.2% in 2025.

Direction of quarterly losses

The first-quarter 2026 operating loss of KRW 2.95 billion was narrower than KRW 8.75 billion in the fourth quarter of 2025, and the third-quarter 2026 loss of KRW 2.38 billion was smaller than KRW 2.95 billion a year earlier.

Second-quarter 2026 sales rose to KRW 5.78 billion from the first quarter, a sign of partial sales recovery. The company said cost cuts in selling and general expenses and cost of sales should produce gradual improvement from the first half of 2026. Whether this trend continues is the item to verify.

Partial easing of funding pressure

The rights offering decided in 2025, sized at KRW 24 billion, has a disclosed use of KRW 19.5 billion for operating funds and KRW 4.5 billion for debt repayment. As a result, the debt ratio fell from 55.7% in 2024 to 36.1% in 2025.

Total liabilities were KRW 12 billion and total equity KRW 33.3 billion at the end of 2025. Whether the capital translated into earnings improvement must be verified in later disclosures.

Bear factors

Structural decline in interchangeable-lens sales

Sales fell from KRW 54.9 billion in 2022 to KRW 21.4 billion in 2025, declining for three consecutive years starting in 2023. MF lens sales dropped from KRW 23.4 billion in 2021 to KRW 4.5 billion in 2024. Low-priced competition from Japanese and Chinese makers in AF lenses continues to squeeze margins. If the lens downturn lasts, sales could keep declining until new businesses fill the gap.

Cash, inventory and receivables strain

Cash and cash equivalents fell from KRW 11.8 billion in 2022 to KRW 350 million in the first half of 2025. A delayed product takeover by a US distributor in 2024 left about KRW 5.4 billion of inventory stranded, and long-aged inventory reached KRW 2.5 billion in the first half of 2025.

Collection periods for receivables have also lengthened. Operating cash flow was -KRW 1.02 billion in 2024 and -KRW 3.83 billion in 2025.

Dividend suspension and a small new-business share

Dividends have been suspended since the 2024 year-end settlement, so there is currently no cash return to shareholders. Retained earnings moved from KRW 15.9 billion at the end of 2024 to a deficit of KRW 2.2 billion at the end of 2025.

New businesses were 13.5% of sales on a cumulative basis through the third quarter of 2025 and 11.2% for full-year 2025. Some assessments say new-business spending is holding back near-term profitability.

Risk factors

Listing-maintenance and watch-list risk

The securities registration statement for the rights offering stated that without an improvement in profitability, the company could be designated for management issues and face delisting. The company disclosed a five-for-one share consolidation on July 7, 2026, reducing issued common shares to 14,093,688.

The stated aim is to stabilize the share price and keep an appropriate tradable share count. Whether the company meets listing-maintenance requirements will depend in part on the results in the 2026 annual report.

Largest-shareholder share pledge

The largest shareholder, LK, pledged 34,378,275 shares to Shinhan Bank as collateral for a KRW 40 billion loan. The company has said the bank has made no request for additional collateral or repayment.

Some observers note that a prolonged share-price decline could create default risk under the bank's collateral valuation rules. The pledge structure is also discussed as a variable in the shareholder base and in trading supply.

Inventory, receivables and customer concentration

A delayed takeover by a US distributor in 2024 left KRW 5.4 billion of inventory stranded, and the bankruptcy of a major European customer halted supply. In 2025 the company booked an inventory valuation loss of KRW 3.9 billion. Slower receivables collection has raised concerns about bad-debt exposure. Sales volatility could rise again depending on the structure of customers and distributors.

Overall view

LK Samyang has posted operating losses since 2023 as the lens market weakened, and the 2025 operating loss reached KRW 16.9 billion with an operating margin of -78.7%.

Quarterly losses narrowed during 2026, but the third-quarter operating loss was larger than in the second quarter, and the net loss attributable to controlling shareholders over the last four quarters totaled KRW 20.9 billion.

New businesses have built up orders and partnerships in thermal imaging, drones, space and machine vision, but at 11.2% of 2025 sales they have not yet offset the decline in lens sales.

On the balance sheet, the debt ratio fell to 36.1% at the end of 2025 from 55.7% at the end of 2024, yet operating cash flow was negative in both 2024 and 2025, at -KRW 1.02 billion and -KRW 3.83 billion.

The largest-shareholder pledge, the suspended dividend and the share consolidation are points to track in the shareholder structure and cash flow.

On the bullish side, new-business orders are accumulating and quarterly losses have narrowed at times, while on the bearish side, sales have declined for three consecutive years and cash remains tight.

The next checkpoints are the annual operating margin in the 2026 annual report and whether interchangeable-lens sales recover quarter by quarter in 2027.
