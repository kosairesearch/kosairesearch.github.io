Life After the Capital Impairment Scare

MoneyMove, the only online investment-linked finance operator listed on KONEX, is being assessed between a fast-growing sector and the balance-sheet strain behind its 2026 trading halt.

02

Business structure

MoneyMove operates an online investment-linked finance platform. It matches borrowers with investors and is described as using a risk-prediction model built on 297 credit and non-financial data items. The company is headquartered in Gangnam-gu, Seoul, and is led by CEO Lee Sang-kyu.

It has been listed on the KONEX market since July 1, 2013, under the other financial services category. It was formerly named LendingMachine and changed its name to MoneyMove in March 2024.

On the investor side, the product emphasizes small-lot diversification. The company promotes investment in units of KRW 10,000 and says returns can exceed those of bank deposits. Its website shows an average return of 11.82% as of February 2026.

On the borrower side, it offers a mobile app for non-face-to-face loans, from small loans to refinancing. Besides retail investors, savings-bank linked investment is a funding channel.

Under this innovative financial service, savings banks fund personal credit loans that an operator with an AI credit scoring system has arranged. No confirmed data on revenue mix by segment was found, so this is described only qualitatively.

In terms of competition, MoneyMove appears to be smaller than the sector's leading operators. KONEX has no listed peers in the same business, so direct comparison with listed banks and brokerages is difficult.

PFCT disclosed that it supplied a total of KRW 186.6 billion in personal credit loans with 17 savings banks over one year. Operators continue to compete on underwriting ability within the same savings-bank linked market.

03

Recent trends

MoneyMove's recent trajectory has been dominated by balance-sheet issues. On February 12, 2026, it disclosed a rights offering, with proceeds to be used for upgrading its AI credit scoring system and for stabilization costs.

Shares outstanding before the offering were 2,460,804, and the offering covered 289,214 new shares. A 5% discount to the reference price set the issue price at KRW 10,373. On the next day, February 13, trading was suspended after a full capital impairment arose, and the share price that day was KRW 11,500.

The suspension lasted about one month. On March 11, the KONEX market disclosed that the halt would be lifted on March 12, 2026, because the delisting ground had been resolved.

The press reported that even after reflecting MoneyMove's capital increase in March 2026, total equity at some operators in the sector was around KRW 17.8 billion. The precise path to resolution should be checked in the original disclosure.

As of October 27, 2026, the share price is KRW 6,000 and market capitalization is KRW 16.5 billion. With no change from the prior day and zero traded value, it appears no trades were executed that day. The price is less than half the KRW 12,500 close on March 11, 2026, just before trading resumed.

No confirmed disclosed figures for annual revenue and profit were found. On the sector side, reports point out that some operators have fallen into full capital impairment or have posted losses for years.

04

Outlook

The outlook splits along two axes: sector expansion and the soundness of individual operators. The sector backdrop is supportive. Personal credit loan balances at 46 operators reached KRW 435.7 billion at the end of June 2026, 10.3 times the level a year earlier.

Slower household loan growth at regulated lenders under the authorities' aggregate controls is cited as a background factor. MoneyMove, with its AI-based credit scoring, is a direct participant in the expansion of personal credit lending and savings-bank linkage.

There are supply-side constraints, however. Savings-bank linked investment counts toward the household loan aggregate cap, and some savings banks that hit their limits have halted or reduced new linked investment. A change in the regulatory environment could shake the funding structure. Experts worry that continued concentration of funds could raise potential risks such as contagion of bad loans.

Asset-quality indicators have so far been stable. The industry association said the delinquency rate on a KRW 330.9 billion savings-bank linked loan balance was 0.85% as of May 22, 2026. Yet it has been pointed out that, while loans grow fast, the current delinquency rate alone makes soundness hard to judge.

MoneyMove's own delinquency and earnings figures could not be confirmed, so periodic report disclosures are the key item to check. The point to watch is whether the capital raised for upgrading the credit scoring system leads to better underwriting quality and profitability. Growing discussion of tighter supervision is another variable.

Bull factors

Sector growth and demand for alternative finance

Total loan balances at the 46 operators grew to KRW 2.3027 trillion at the end of June 2026. Analysts say demand rejected by other lenders has moved to online investment-linked finance while household lending at regulated institutions slowed. MoneyMove, which handles mid-rate credit loans, has a structural demand base. Whether that demand converts into company profit still needs separate confirmation.

Savings-bank linkage channel

MoneyMove says it has begun contracts with multiple savings banks and plans to continue linked investment. After 29 savings banks were designated, 20 more savings banks and 10 regional agricultural cooperatives were added in January 2026.

The route for institutional funds that do not rely solely on retail investor recruitment is widening. This structure could support funding stability.

Delisting ground resolved and capital reinforced

The suspension caused by the full capital impairment was lifted on March 12, 2026, after the ground was resolved. The proceeds of the rights offering disclosed in February 2026 were planned for upgrading the AI credit scoring system and for stabilization costs.

Investment in underwriting systems could help delinquency management over the long run. This is room for improvement in business indicators, not a view on the share price.

Bear factors

A record of financial fragility

Trading was suspended in February 2026 after a full capital impairment arose. Reports said total equity at some operators in the sector stays around KRW 17.8 billion even after reflecting capital increases. A thin capital base limits loss-absorption capacity. Even after the resolution, the sustainability of capital reinforcement remains to be confirmed.

Thin liquidity and a lower share price

Traded value on October 27, 2026, is zero, and the price is KRW 6,000. The close on March 11, 2026, just before trading resumed, was KRW 12,500. As a small KONEX stock, bid and ask depth is thin, so orders may not execute when desired. At a market capitalization of KRW 16.5 billion, a few trades can move the price sharply.

Profitability uncertainty

No confirmed annual earnings figures for MoneyMove were found. Six major operators posted combined net losses of KRW 30.4 billion in 2025, reportedly about twice their combined total equity. This suggests that profitability and capital capacity do not always keep pace with sector growth. Whether MoneyMove is an exception must be verified through its periodic reports.

Risk factors

Regulatory

Savings-bank linked investment counts toward household loan aggregate controls, and some savings banks have halted or reduced new investment. A funding structure that depends on linked investment is sensitive to regulatory changes.

Calls are growing for removing weak operators and strengthening the supervisory framework. Tighter supervision could raise operating costs or restrict business.

Credit

More than 95% of borrowers in savings-bank linked loans were in the bottom 50% of credit scores, and the delinquency rate tallied by the association was 0.75%. A high share of low- and mid-credit borrowers means delinquencies could rise in a downturn.

With loans growing quickly, the current delinquency rate alone makes soundness hard to judge. MoneyMove's own delinquency rate was not confirmed.

Liquidity and listing

Traded value on October 27, 2026, was zero, so no trades were executed. Small KONEX stocks trade infrequently, which limits price discovery. The earlier suspension had been set to last until the capital impairment was resolved or the delisting ground was reviewed. If financial deterioration recurs, trading restrictions could again come into discussion.

08

Overall view

MoneyMove is the only KONEX-listed company engaged in online investment-linked finance, and it sits within the sector trend of expanding personal credit loans and savings-bank linkage. The full capital impairment and trading halt in February 2026, however, exposed the fragility of its financial base.

The halt was lifted on March 12, 2026, but as of October 27, 2026, the share price is KRW 6,000 and traded value is zero. No confirmed figures for annual revenue and profit were found, so whether the earnings structure is improving cannot be judged.

The bullish side points to sector growth and widening institutional funding channels, while the bearish side points to thin capital and low liquidity. A financial-industry source said the operating stability of these operators matters to both institutional investors and financial consumers.
