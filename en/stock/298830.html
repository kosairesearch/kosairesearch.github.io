02

Business structure

Suresofttech, founded in 2002, specializes in verifying the safety of mission-critical, high-reliability software.

It supplies code verification, system verification, model verification and future-technology verification solutions to industries such as automotive, defense/aerospace and nuclear power, where software defects can lead to critical accidents.

According to one financial broadcast report, the automotive segment is known to account for roughly 75% or more of total revenue.

Suresofttech has supplied vehicle software safety verification tools and services to the Hyundai Motor Group since 2010, and Hyundai Motor currently ranks as the company's second-largest shareholder.

In 2023 the company expanded through the acquisition of big-data and AI analytics firm Mobigen, and in December 2025 it acquired graphics solutions company Graphica to move into the digital twin business. Graphica holds 'Vehicle Twin' technology that virtually recreates vehicle motion and driving environments.

The company listed on KOSDAQ in April 2023 via a SPAC merger. In terms of competitive positioning, a stable captive market anchored by Hyundai Motor and entry barriers in verification automation technology are cited as strengths, while relatively low operating margin is noted as a weakness.

04

Earnings analysis

Consolidated revenue rose from KRW 43.4 billion in 2022 to KRW 63.3 billion in 2023 (up 45.7% year on year), KRW 88.8 billion in 2024 (up 40.3%), and KRW 93.9 billion in 2025 (up 5.8%), marking three consecutive years of growth from 2023 through 2025. Among 2022-2025, revenue was highest in 2025 and lowest in 2022.

Operating profit grew from KRW 9.67 billion in 2022 to KRW 11.5 billion in 2023 (up 18.7%), then fell to KRW 7.89 billion in 2024 (down 31.2%) before recovering to KRW 12.6 billion in 2025 (up 59.7%).

Losses at subsidiaries weighed on the 2024 operating profit decline, and the company's revenue growth that year fell short of its roughly 20% guidance, which was attributed to delays in defense and aerospace projects. Among 2022-2025, operating profit was highest in 2025 and lowest in 2024.

Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders fell from KRW 9.45 billion in 2022 to KRW 5.06 billion in 2023 (down 46.5%), rebounded to KRW 10.8 billion in 2024 (up 113.6%), then declined again to KRW 8.58 billion in 2025 (down 20.6%), making 2024 the highest and 2023 the lowest among 2022-2025.

On a quarterly basis, revenue rose from KRW 21.5 billion in Q2 2025 to KRW 23.6 billion in Q3 and KRW 31.9 billion in Q4, then fell to KRW 20.2 billion in Q1 2026 (down 36.7% quarter on quarter) before rising again to KRW 26.4 billion in Q2 2026 (up 30.8% quarter on quarter, up 23.0% year on year).

Over the same period, operating profit expanded from KRW 188 million in Q2 2025 to KRW 3.6 billion in Q3 and KRW 10.9 billion in Q4, before swinging to an operating loss of KRW 415 million in Q1 2026 and back to a KRW 2.76 billion profit in Q2 2026.

The sum of net profit attributable to controlling shareholders over the trailing four quarters (Q3 2025-Q2 2026) was KRW 11.4 billion, while the operating margin moved from 22.3% in 2022 down to 8.9% in 2024 and back up to 13.4% in 2025, with operating cash flow also improving from KRW 9.47 billion in 2024 to KRW 17.9 billion in 2025 alongside the profit recovery.

05

Industry analysis

Suresofttech's end markets consist of mission-critical fields such as automotive SDV (software-defined vehicle) transition, defense/aerospace, and nuclear power. KIA has announced plans to invest a total of KRW 38 trillion over five years through 2028 in future businesses including electrification, PBVs and SDVs.

KIA has stated it is pursuing next-generation SDV development with a target completion in 2026, aiming to unveil vehicles that integrate autonomous driving and connectivity.

In the defense sector, as cybersecurity has grown more important, Suresofttech obtained the CWE-658/659 certification, expanding its KOLAS accreditation scope from functional safety into cybersecurity.

The nuclear-related software verification business, which had been in a slump for some time, has also shown signs of reviving.

In the United States, policy discussions aimed at restricting the use of Chinese-made autonomous-driving and communications software have been raised, representing a variable that could affect related demand depending on how the regulatory environment evolves.

Within the industry, a long-standing relationship with a major automaker is seen as an entry barrier, while the verification services business as a whole shares the structural feature of operating margins fluctuating with project cycles.

06

Outlook

Following the December 2025 acquisition of Graphica, the company has been expanding its digital twin platform business, which virtually recreates vehicles, plants and cities.

Graphica has previously participated in a domestic telecom operator's AI data center digital twin project and has supplied 3D graphics solutions to a domestic Tier-1 auto parts supplier that were actually installed in mass-produced vehicles.

In October 2026, the company announced a partnership with RF connectivity solutions firm Sensorview to build an AI reliability and safety verification framework using radar and optical sensor data, jointly pursuing entry into domestic and overseas defense markets.

Around the same time, it has also been pursuing a partnership with Minerva Soft to verify the reliability and quality of AI agents for financial institutions. Through its 'Safe Intelligence' business, the company is also expanding into AI testing and trustworthy-AI certification.

One media report conveyed an outlook that as Hyundai Motor plans to move into full-scale commercialization of SDV technology from 2026, the role of software verification specialists such as Suresofttech is expected to become more important.

However, as seen in past defense and aerospace projects, businesses tied to public and defense budgets carry a risk of delayed order timing, so the point at which concrete order wins from the new digital twin and AI businesses materialize will need to be confirmed through disclosures in coming quarters.

08

Bull factors

Positioned to Benefit from SDV Transition

Hyundai Motor's position as the company's second-largest shareholder, along with a vehicle software verification partnership dating back to 2010, is viewed as a favorable business foundation amid the automotive industry's SDV transition.

KIA has announced plans to invest KRW 38 trillion through 2028 in future businesses including electrification, PBVs and SDVs. Because the automotive segment accounts for a substantial portion of revenue, the business is sensitive to this expansion in automaker investment.

Business Expansion into Digital Twin and AI Reliability Verification

The December 2025 acquisition of Graphica secured Vehicle Twin technology, broadening the company into the digital twin business. In October 2026, it expanded further into physical AI and financial-sector AI agent verification through partnerships with Sensorview and Minerva Soft. The company is also expanding into trustworthy-AI certification through its Safe Intelligence business.

Profit Recovery and Improved Cash Generation

Operating profit rebounded 59.7% to KRW 12.6 billion in 2025 after bottoming at KRW 7.89 billion in 2024. Operating cash flow also rose from KRW 9.47 billion in 2024 to KRW 17.9 billion in 2025. The debt ratio has also trended lower, falling from 74.6% in 2022 to 46.8% in 2025.

Bear factors

Wide Quarter-to-Quarter Earnings Swings

Quarter-to-quarter swings have been large, with an operating loss of KRW 415 million in Q1 2026 turning into a KRW 2.76 billion profit in Q2 2026.

In 2025 as well, operating profit was just KRW 188 million in Q2 before expanding to KRW 10.9 billion in Q4, reflecting significant quarterly fluctuation tied to project recognition timing.

History of Order and Project Delays

In 2024, revenue growth fell short of the roughly 20% guidance initially given, due to delays in defense and aerospace projects. The new digital twin and physical AI businesses are still at an early stage, and the timing of concrete order wins has yet to be confirmed.

Subsidiary Earnings Volatility

Losses at subsidiaries contributed to the decline in operating profit in 2024. As the number of acquired subsidiaries such as Mobigen and Graphica grows, the volatility of subsidiary earnings within consolidated results could increase.

10

Risk factors

Business and Order Risk

The mission-critical software verification business is structured around project-based order and acceptance schedules, which can cause revenue and profit to concentrate in specific quarters. Projects tied to public or government budgets, such as defense and aerospace, carry a persistent risk of schedule delays.

Customer Concentration Risk

Because the automotive segment accounts for a substantial portion of revenue, results can be tied to strategic shifts or the pace of SDV investment by specific customers such as Hyundai Motor and KIA. The advantage of a captive market could conversely translate into greater dependence on a single customer group.

Post-M&A Integration Risk

As successive acquisitions of Mobigen, Graphica and others increase the number of consolidated subsidiaries, additional costs or earnings volatility could arise during organizational and technical integration. The point at which the profitability of newly acquired subsidiaries stabilizes has not yet been confirmed.
