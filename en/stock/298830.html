Auto/AI Verification Growth, Earnings Volatility Remains

02

Business structure

Founded in 2002, SureSoftTech specializes in automated verification of mission-critical software and the Safe Intelligence business, with core operations in developing and supplying automation tools for safety verification of high-reliability, high-risk software, as well as third-party verification services.

Leveraging its differentiated mission-critical software verification capabilities, the company runs a Safe Intelligence business conducting AI testing and is expanding into the Trustworthy AI domain.

According to a 2025-based analysis, the business mix is roughly 63.1% software testing/verification, 36.8% big data/AI, and 0.1% other.

In August 2023, the company acquired a 43.75% stake in big data specialist Mobigen, consolidating it as a subsidiary; Mobigen supplies big data analytics and AI Ops solutions to telecom carriers, large system integrators, and public institutions.

Major revenue-contributing customers include Hyundai Motor's EV division, Hyundai Mobis's infotainment division, Hanwha's defense division, and Doosan Enerbility's nuclear division, placing automotive, defense, and nuclear power as the three core end markets.

Notably, Hyundai Motor Group is the second-largest shareholder with a 7.32% stake and has signed a software development MOU to accelerate software-defined vehicle (SDV) initiatives.

Comparable domestic listed software firms such as Emro, Douzone Bizon, and AhnLab are sometimes cited, though they operate in different niches and serve more as sector benchmarks than direct competitors.

The company has also added offline and online training services along with educational software supply as customer education demand has grown.

04

Earnings analysis

2025 consolidated revenue came in at KRW 93.938 billion, up 5.8% from KRW 88.803 billion in 2024, while operating profit rose 59.7% to KRW 12.600 billion from KRW 7.891 billion, lifting the operating margin from 8.9% to 13.4%.

However, owner-attributable net income fell to KRW 8.583 billion from KRW 10.806 billion in 2024, moving opposite to the operating profit trend, a divergence attributable to non-controlling interest and other non-operating factors.

Operating margins stood high at 22.3% and 18.1% in 2022 and 2023 respectively, dropped to 8.9% in 2024, then rebounded in 2025.

On a quarterly basis, 2Q25 revenue of KRW 21.499 billion produced an operating profit of only KRW 188 million, a margin below 1%, before improving to KRW 23.573 billion revenue and KRW 3.599 billion operating profit in 3Q25. 4Q25 delivered the year's largest quarter with KRW 31.948 billion revenue and KRW 10.902 billion operating profit, reaffirming a seasonal pattern where the fourth quarter carries a disproportionate share of annual results.

In 1Q26, revenue was KRW 20.224 billion with an operating loss of KRW 415 million, a narrower loss than the prior-year period, while net income stayed marginally positive at KRW 122 million. 2Q26 revenue reached KRW 26.448 billion with operating profit of KRW 2.755 billion and owner net income of KRW 2.778 billion, a clear swing back to profitability.

Combined owner-attributable net income over the most recent four quarters (3Q25-2Q26) totaled roughly KRW 11.402 billion, showing that despite sizable quarter-to-quarter swings, the annual earnings recovery trend has continued.

Operating cash flow rose sharply to KRW 17.902 billion in 2025 from KRW 9.468 billion in 2024, supporting the quality of the earnings improvement.

05

Industry analysis

The automotive industry is undergoing structural change as the shift toward software-defined vehicles (SDVs) accelerates, correspondingly increasing demand for software testing and verification.

Hyundai Motor Group announced in October 2022 its plan to convert all vehicle models to SDVs by 2025, and Kia has separately stated plans for large-scale investment over the next five years in electrification, purpose-built vehicles (PBVs), and SDVs.

Defense and nuclear power are sectors where high-reliability, high-safety software verification is essential, generating steady demand for verification services tied to government-funded projects and export expansion.

Korea's Ministry of Land, Infrastructure and Transport and the Korea Meteorological Administration have applied for a preliminary feasibility study on a new KRW 400 billion national R&D program running from 2027 to 2030 to advance the safety operation system for Korean urban air mobility (K-UAM), creating a potential environment for continued demand in navigation, traffic management, and vertiport operation safety verification technologies.

Across the industry, demand for certification and verification of Trustworthy AI is gaining prominence, with software verification companies observed expanding into AI safety assessment.

It is also worth noting that the domestic software verification market is structurally concentrated in a few large end-markets (automotive, defense, nuclear), which can introduce demand volatility tied to the investment cycles of those downstream industries.

06

Outlook

The company positions growing demand for automotive software testing and verification driven by the SDV transition as a core growth axis, noting that the global shift to SDVs is closely tied to its software testing and verification business.

Through its Safe Intelligence business, it is expanding into the Trustworthy AI domain, aiming to support customers' adoption of AI safety via related solutions and certification schemes.

Synergy with subsidiary Mobigen is expected to produce jointly developed AI verification technologies combining existing verification solutions with AI and big data capabilities, with vehicle big data collection, analysis, and utilization cited as a mid-to-long-term focus area.

Whether the SDV-acceleration software development MOU signed with Hyundai Motor Group translates into concrete project orders is a point to watch going forward.

In the near term, whether the swing from a 1Q26 operating loss to 2Q26 profitability can be sustained stably through the second half is an important thing to verify.

On an annual basis, whether the steady revenue growth trend from 2022 through 2025 continues, and whether the operating margin can recover further from its 2025 level of 13.4%, stand out as key points to monitor.

08

Bull factors

Core Partner Position in SDV Transition

With Hyundai Motor Group as the second-largest shareholder holding a 7.32% stake and having signed an SDV-acceleration software development MOU, the accelerating software-centric transition in the automaker industry carries potential to expand verification demand.

Kia has also outlined large-scale investment plans in electrification, PBVs, and SDVs, forming a favorable investment cycle across the downstream industry. This structural relationship is cited as a factor supporting the possibility of new project wins.

Business Expansion via Safe Intelligence and Mobigen Synergy

Built on mission-critical software verification capabilities, the Safe Intelligence business conducting AI testing is expanding into the Trustworthy AI domain.

Combined with subsidiary Mobigen's big data and AI Ops capabilities, there is room to jointly develop vehicle big data collection, analysis, and verification technologies. This is interpreted as a path toward securing new revenue sources beyond the existing verification business.

Improved Financial Structure and Stronger Cash Generation

The debt ratio fell for four consecutive years from 74.6% in 2022 to 46.8% in 2025, while operating cash flow expanded from KRW 9.340 billion to KRW 17.902 billion over the same period. The 2025 operating margin recovered to 13.4% from 8.9% in 2024.

This improvement in financial health could serve as a favorable backdrop for securing funds for new investments or M&A.

09

Bear factors

Business Structure with Large Quarterly Earnings Swings

The operating margin fell below 1% in 2Q25 but exceeded 34% in 4Q25, reflecting very large swings between quarters.

After an operating loss of KRW 415 million in 1Q26, the company swung back to profit in 2Q26, and this volatility can be felt as quarter-to-quarter uncertainty for investors focused only on annual results. A structural tendency for results to cluster around project delivery timing persists.

Net Income Diverging from Operating Profit Trend

While 2025 operating profit rose 59.7% year-over-year, owner-attributable net income actually declined to KRW 8.583 billion from KRW 10.806 billion in 2024.

This is interpreted as reflecting non-operating factors such as non-controlling interest fluctuations affecting owner-attributable earnings, indicating that operating profit improvement does not automatically translate into improved owner earnings. Continued monitoring of the non-controlling interest structure is warranted.

Dependence on a Concentrated Set of End-Markets

Top revenue-contributing customers are concentrated in a few large end-markets such as automotive, defense, and nuclear power, meaning shifts in those industries' investment cycles or project ordering schedules can directly affect performance.

If automakers' pace of SDV investment or government project timelines were to slow, near-term earnings volatility could widen.

10

Risk factors

Customer Concentration Risk

Revenue is concentrated among a small number of large customers including Hyundai Motor/Kia, Hyundai Mobis, Hanwha, and Doosan Enerbility, so a reduction in orders from any one customer or changes to internal project schedules could directly affect results.

If diversification into new customers proceeds slowly, this dependency structure could persist over the long term.

Policy and Budget Variability Risk

Government-related projects in defense, nuclear power, and UAM can see their schedules shift depending on budget allocation, the outcome of preliminary feasibility studies, and changes in policy direction.

The new K-UAM-related R&D program is still at the preliminary feasibility study application stage, with its approval and timing not yet confirmed.

Subsidiary Consolidation and Governance Risk

Earnings fluctuations at consolidated subsidiaries such as Mobigen and the non-controlling interest structure can increase the volatility of owner-attributable net income. Any further changes in equity stakes or additional M&A could alter the financial structure and governance arrangement.

12

Overall view

SureSoftTech showed a recovery in both scale and profitability in 2025 as revenue and operating profit rose together, though owner-attributable net income moved in the opposite direction from operating profit, warranting attention to non-operating factors such as non-controlling interests.

On a quarterly basis, the low margin in 2Q25 contrasted with the high margin in 4Q25, and volatility continued into 2026 with an operating loss in 1Q26 followed by a return to profit in 2Q26.

The SDV partnership with Hyundai Motor Group and the expansion into Safe Intelligence and big data through subsidiary Mobigen stand out as core pillars of the mid-to-long-term growth narrative.

At the same time, dependence on a concentrated set of end-markets—automotive, defense, and nuclear power—and the variability of government project schedules are factors that should be weighed in balance.

On the financial structure side, the declining debt ratio and expanding operating cash flow are read as positive signals, but quarterly earnings volatility may persist for some time.

Investors will need to check the sustainability of these trends through upcoming quarterly earnings disclosures and concrete order news related to SDV and AI verification.
