Revenue Steady, Profit Recovery Still the Test

dhSteel posted 2025 revenue of KRW 277.4 billion, but net losses attributable to controlling shareholders have run for five straight quarters through the third quarter of 2026, making a return to sustained profitability the central question.

  1. 1

  2. 2

  3. 3

    Operating profit turned positive in the second quarter of 2026 at KRW 1.41 billion, but the third quarter returned to an operating loss of KRW 1.27 billion.

  4. 4

02

Business structure

dhSteel is a mid-sized primary steel maker whose core products are cold-heading quality wire (CHQ wire) and CD bar. Its main customers are manufacturers of automotive fasteners, and the Chungju plant has been cited for its proximity to fastener makers in Gyeonggi and Ansan.

Trade press has described the Pohang plant, located next to a port, as handling large export orders. Combined heat-treated and non-heat-treated wire capacity at Chungju was reported at about 85,000 tons a year in an older article.

The company competes with several domestic CHQ wire producers and has emphasized quality improvement and fast delivery. Beyond steel, it is building an AI services segment based on image, video and 3D data, with AI training-data projects and the launch of an AI fashion lookbook service cited in early 2026 coverage.

The company has also referred to its acquisition of generative AI firm iOES as a way to secure future growth.

On the environmental side, the Chungju plant received a merit award in a green chemistry competition reported in November 2026, citing the full replacement of 2,862 tons of caustic soda with a low-hazard lime slurry (based on 2024 data). The largest shareholder group held 40.19% as of September 22, 2026. The business is still led by steel product sales, with the AI segment at an early expansion stage.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual revenue went from KRW 306.8 billion in 2022 to KRW 300.6 billion in 2023 (down 2.0% year on year), KRW 296.4 billion in 2024 (down 1.4%) and KRW 277.4 billion in 2025 (down 6.4%), a three-year consecutive decline from 2023 through 2025. Among 2022 to 2025, 2022 was the largest revenue year and 2025 the smallest.

Operating profit rose from KRW 902 million in 2022 to KRW 1.66 billion in 2023 (up 84.2%) and KRW 2.67 billion in 2024 (up 60.6%), then fell to KRW 2.37 billion in 2025 (down 11.1%). Operating margin was 0.3% in 2022, 0.6% in 2023, and 0.9% in both 2024 and 2025, still below 1%.

The company attributed the 2025 operating profit decline to lower revenue and higher selling, general and administrative expenses.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders was a loss of KRW 4.71 billion in 2022, KRW 3.56 billion in 2023, KRW 13 billion in 2024 and KRW 1.63 billion in 2025, a four-year consecutive loss record from 2022 through 2025.

Net income including non-controlling interests was a loss of KRW 4.22 billion in 2022, KRW 3.56 billion in 2023, KRW 13 billion in 2024 and KRW 1.85 billion in 2025, a different measure from the controlling-shareholder figure. The debt-to-equity ratio rose from 313.2% in 2022 to 351.0% in 2024 and 353.1% in 2025.

At end-2025, total liabilities were KRW 136.4 billion, total equity KRW 38.6 billion, and equity attributable to controlling shareholders KRW 38.7 billion. Operating cash flow was KRW 5.86 billion in 2022, KRW 3.13 billion in 2023, KRW 4.79 billion in 2024 and KRW 13.7 billion in 2025.

Quarterly revenue was KRW 69.7 billion in the third quarter of 2025, KRW 66.9 billion in the fourth quarter, KRW 66.7 billion in the first quarter of 2026, KRW 72.4 billion in the second quarter and KRW 71.8 billion in the third quarter, which was up 3.0% year on year.

Operating profit moved from KRW 286 million in the third quarter of 2025 to an operating loss of KRW 151 million in the fourth quarter, an operating loss of KRW 17.92 million in the first quarter of 2026, an operating profit of KRW 1.41 billion in the second quarter (a turn to profit), and an operating loss of KRW 1.27 billion in the third quarter (a turn to loss).

Net losses attributable to controlling shareholders were KRW 347 million in the third quarter of 2025, KRW 1.32 billion in the fourth quarter, KRW 638 million in the first quarter of 2026, KRW 967 million in the second quarter and KRW 1.98 billion in the third quarter, totaling KRW 4.91 billion over the latest four quarters.

Revenue rose in the third quarter of 2026, yet operating results swung back to a loss, so sales scale alone does not explain the change in profitability.

05

Industry analysis

Early-2026 coverage cited expectations of a global economic recovery and planned large-scale infrastructure investment as drivers of a rally in the steel sector.

Specialty wire demand generally tracks the cycles of end markets such as automotive and construction, and dhSteel counts automotive parts makers among its main customers. Quarterly revenue held between KRW 66.7 billion and KRW 72.4 billion through 2026, a sign that demand has been fairly stable.

Operating results alternated between profit and loss over the same period, so sales volume alone does not explain the change in profitability. Competition in the domestic CHQ wire market involves several producers, and the company says quality and delivery speed are central to that competition.

Among listed steel peers, Hyundai Steel Company, Dongkuk Steel Mill, SeAH Steel, Kiswire and KG Dongbusteel are larger, while dhSteel is a smaller producer concentrated in wire and CD bar.

Its strengths, as presented by the company, are customer responsiveness from two plants in Chungju and Pohang and investment in green chemistry and energy-efficiency equipment, which it positions as differentiators on cost and environmental compliance.

The AI services segment operates under a different business model from steel, so the two businesses are reflected together in the consolidated results.

06

Outlook

In July 2026, dhSteel was selected as the preferred bidder for the 2026 FEMS+ smart energy platform project run by the Korea Industrial Complex Corporation.

The total project cost is KRW 660 million, with KRW 600 million from government funding and KRW 60 million from the company, and full-scale work is scheduled to start in the second half of 2026.

Its scope covers automated product carbon footprint data, process energy efficiency, and linkage to the industrial complex MRV platform.

At the Chungju plant, a new dewatering system lowered the cake moisture content from 87% to 80%, cutting waste by roughly 447 tons a year, and the technology is under patent examination. On September 22, 2026, the board approved a share consolidation that halves both common and preferred shares on a 2-for-1 basis.

After the consolidation, common shares outstanding would fall from 21,704,774 to 10,852,387, and the par value would rise from KRW 2,500 to KRW 5,000. The company said the aim is to maintain an appropriate free float to stabilize the share price and raise corporate value, and that it is not a capital reduction.

The same board meeting approved a KRW 5 billion short-term borrowing to improve the financial structure and secure operating funds, with terms subject to change at loan execution. The borrowing is disclosed to raise total short-term debt to KRW 37.43 billion.

The largest shareholder group's stake rose 0.15 percentage point to 40.19% as of September 22, 2026, and CEO Kang Geun-wook raised his holding from 170,410 shares to 203,272 shares through on-market purchases on September 18, 2026.

Trading is suspended from November 16 to December 16, 2026, and the new shares are scheduled for listing on December 17, 2026.

Bear factors

Five straight quarters of controlling-shareholder net losses

Net losses attributable to controlling shareholders have continued for five straight quarters since the third quarter of 2025, totaling KRW 4.91 billion over the latest four quarters.

The third quarter of 2026 recorded an operating loss of KRW 1.27 billion, a swing from the prior-year period, and the return to loss after one profitable quarter keeps earnings stability in question.

Three years of falling revenue

Revenue has declined for three consecutive years to KRW 277.4 billion in 2025, a 6.4% fall in that year. Lower sales combined with higher SG&A expenses to reduce operating profit in 2025. When the top line shrinks while costs rise, the pace of profit recovery can slow.

High leverage and added short-term borrowing

At end-2025, total liabilities were KRW 136.4 billion against KRW 38.6 billion in total equity, a 353.1% debt-to-equity ratio.

A KRW 5 billion short-term loan approved in September 2026 raises short-term borrowings to KRW 37.43 billion, and non-financial borrowings reach KRW 17 billion, so financial burden is expanding.

Risk factors

Financial and borrowing risk

The debt-to-equity ratio of 353.1% is high, and expanding short-term borrowing could raise interest and refinancing burdens. The company has said the terms of the loan may change at execution. If operating funds continue to rely on borrowing during the loss period, financial flexibility could narrow.

Trading and share-structure risk

A 2-for-1 share consolidation will suspend trading from November 16 to December 16, 2026, with new shares scheduled for listing on December 17. The largest shareholder group holds 40.19%, which limits the number of shares actually available to trade. How liquidity changes after trading resumes will be the next point to watch.

Demand and cost volatility risk

Demand for specialty wire moves with end markets such as automotive and construction. In the third quarter of 2026, revenue rose 3.0% year on year, yet operating results swung to a loss, which suggests that cost and SG&A pressure offset sales growth and that cost control remains central.
