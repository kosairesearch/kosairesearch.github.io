Wonik Cube: Quarterly Earnings Swings Remain the Key Question

Wonik Cube's operating margin stayed at 1.2% in both 2024 and 2025, and after an operating loss in the fourth quarter of 2025, operating profit returned in each quarter from the first quarter to the third quarter of 2026, though the size of quarterly profit varied widely.

02

Business structure

Wonik Cube is a chemical and materials distribution company founded in 1979 and listed on KOSDAQ in 1996.

Its business segments are Chemicals (chemical raw materials), Building Materials (materials for wooden houses), Silicone (silicone products and raw materials), Polymer (plastics), DS (digital printing machines), and Electronic Materials (LCD films).

On a cumulative basis through the third quarter of 2025, the Polymer segment saw operating profit decline as competition intensified, pushing down selling prices and profitability. In the same period, the DS segment turned profitable as sales of high-end equipment expanded.

The Silicone segment's operating profit rose on higher earnings from high-margin functional and industrial products, and the Electronic Materials segment improved on higher sales of LCD carrier films.

Electronic Materials and Silicone face relatively high entry barriers because quality requirements are high, and the company has reportedly maintained its supply chain on quality-control know-how built through long-term trading.

The company has reportedly chosen to compete on technical support and quality rather than on price. At the regular shareholders' meeting on March 26, 2026, the articles of incorporation added liquor import and export to the business purpose and reorganized the computer peripheral item into 'Computers and Peripherals'.

With the scope of business in the articles widened, changes in segment mix are something to track in future disclosures.

04

Earnings analysis

Based on DART regular reports, annual revenue fell from KRW 246 billion in 2022 to KRW 244.4 billion in 2023 (down 0.7% year on year), then rose to KRW 286.8 billion in 2024 (up 17.4% year on year), the largest year in 2022 to 2025. Revenue in 2025 was KRW 284.8 billion (down 0.7% year on year).

However, operating profit fell to KRW 3.56 billion in 2024 (down 36.4% year on year) even as revenue rose, and the operating margin dropped from 2.3% in 2023 to 1.2% in 2024.

Operating profit peaked at KRW 5.6 billion in 2023 (up 31.6% year on year), and in 2025 it was KRW 3.31 billion (down 7.2% year on year), the smallest year in 2022 to 2025.

Profit attributable to owners of the parent was KRW 4.95 billion in 2023, then KRW 2.68 billion in 2024 (down 46.0% year on year) and KRW 2.8 billion in 2025 (up 4.8% year on year). Net income for each year was disclosed at the same amount as profit attributable to owners of the parent.

On a quarterly basis, the third quarter of 2025 showed revenue of KRW 68.8 billion and operating profit of KRW 1.07 billion.

The fourth quarter then swung to an operating loss of KRW 1.08 billion on revenue of KRW 64 billion (down 6.9% quarter on quarter), with an operating loss and a net loss attributable to owners of the parent of KRW 948 million.

In the first quarter of 2026, revenue was KRW 71.4 billion (up 11.7% quarter on quarter) and operating profit of KRW 2.53 billion marked a return to profit.

Second-quarter revenue was KRW 78.5 billion (up 9.9% quarter on quarter), with operating profit of KRW 6.24 billion (up 146.4% quarter on quarter) and profit attributable to owners of the parent of KRW 4.93 billion (up 178.1% quarter on quarter).

Third-quarter revenue rose to KRW 76.7 billion (down 2.3% quarter on quarter, up 11.5% year on year), but operating profit was KRW 985 million (down 84.2% quarter on quarter, down 7.9% year on year), a quarter in which higher sales did not translate into higher profit.

Profit attributable to owners of the parent for the last four quarters (fourth quarter of 2025 through third quarter of 2026) totaled KRW 6.83 billion.

05

Industry analysis

Wonik Cube operates a distribution-style business that handles chemical raw materials, building materials, and plastics. In such a structure, raw material prices and selling-price competition tend to drive profit margins.

The decline in selling prices in the Polymer segment as competition intensified, on a cumulative basis through the third quarter of 2025, is a representative example. By contrast, Electronic Materials and Silicone are described as areas with relatively high entry barriers because quality requirements are demanding.

Demand for LCD films in Electronic Materials depends on display industry investment and operating rates. The DS segment has been identified as an example where sales of high-end equipment such as digital printing machines lifted results.

A news report around the end of 2025 cited an industry view that earnings volatility would be relatively low in a normalizing economy. That remains a single press account and does not by itself establish where the industry cycle stands.

Competition is split between the Polymer area, where pricing pressure is intense, and the Electronic Materials and Silicone areas, where technical support and quality matter more.

06

Outlook

Quarterly results in 2026 showed a recovery trend. Operating profit turned to a profit in the first quarter, and the second quarter's KRW 6.24 billion was the largest quarterly figure in the disclosed series.

Third-quarter revenue of KRW 76.7 billion was up 11.5% year on year, but operating profit fell to KRW 985 million, so whether sales recovery translates into margin recovery remains the central question.

The addition of liquor import and export to the business purpose at the March 2026 shareholders' meeting reads as a broadening of the business scope. The revenue scale and profitability of any new business are not yet visible in the disclosed data.

Whether profitability holds in quality-driven segments such as Electronic Materials and Silicone is the key to improving the earnings structure. The Polymer segment could constrain profit recovery if price competition persists.

Operating cash flow rising to KRW 17.3 billion in 2025 indicates that cash generation improved despite profit volatility. In the 2026 annual report, the next regular filing, the operating margin versus the 1.2% recorded in 2024 and 2025 is the next checkpoint.

When the fourth-quarter 2026 preliminary results are disclosed, they will show whether the third-quarter profit decline was temporary or structural.

Bear factors

Operating margin stuck near 1%

The operating margin fell from 2.3% in 2023 to 1.2% in 2024 and stayed at 1.2% in 2025. In 2024, operating profit fell 36.4% even though revenue rose 17.4%. A structure in which profit does not follow higher sales is cited as the bearish point.

Large swings in quarterly profit

The fourth quarter of 2025 posted an operating loss of KRW 1.08 billion and a net loss attributable to owners of the parent of KRW 948 million. Third-quarter 2026 operating profit fell to KRW 985 million, down 84.2% quarter on quarter.

From the third quarter of 2025 through the third quarter of 2026, quarterly operating profit moved between losses and small profits, with only the first quarter (KRW 2.53 billion) and second quarter (KRW 6.24 billion) of 2026 standing out.

Price pressure in the Polymer segment

On a cumulative basis through the third quarter of 2025, the Polymer segment's operating profit fell as competition intensified, pushing down selling prices and profitability. If price competition persists, gains in other segments could be offset.

Profit attributable to owners of the parent for 2025 was KRW 2.8 billion, below KRW 4.95 billion in 2023, so the scale of profit recovery remains limited.

Risk factors

Industry and pricing risk

Because the business is distribution-oriented, raw material prices and selling-price changes directly affect margins. Intensifying competition in the Polymer segment was already visible in cumulative results through the third quarter of 2025.

Demand for LCD films in Electronic Materials is also tied to display industry investment flows, which adds another source of variability.

Earnings volatility and thin margins

With the operating margin near 1%, small changes in revenue can swing operating profit sharply. In the third quarter of 2026, operating profit fell 84.2% quarter on quarter even as revenue rose.

If quarterly profit alternates between profit and loss, the last four quarters' total of KRW 6.83 billion in profit attributable to owners of the parent could shift sharply with a single quarter's result.

Small-cap liquidity and pre-audit revisions

As a small-cap stock, trading volume and share price volatility can be significant. Quarterly results are first disclosed as preliminary figures and then finalized in regular reports, and figures can change during that process.

Operating cash flow of KRW 1.02 billion in 2024 remains a record to check when judging the stability of cash generation.

Overall view

Wonik Cube runs a thin-margin distribution-oriented business, with 2025 revenue of KRW 284.8 billion, operating profit of KRW 3.31 billion, and an operating margin of 1.2%.

Operating profit was positive in each quarter from the first quarter to the third quarter of 2026, but the gap between the KRW 6.24 billion of the second quarter and the KRW 985 million of the third quarter means earnings continuity is still being tested.

The debt ratio fell to 36.0% at the end of 2025, and operating cash flow rose to KRW 17.3 billion, which supports the financial picture. The share price sits well below book value per share, while the earnings base shows a low operating margin and quarterly volatility, and both facts stand side by side.

Price competition in the Polymer segment and quality competitiveness in Electronic Materials and Silicone are the factors that will shape the earnings structure.

The next checkpoints are the operating margin in the 2026 annual report, the preliminary fourth-quarter 2026 results, and operating profit in the first quarter of 2027.
