Korea Zinc: After the Quarterly Peak, U.S. Smelter in Focus

02

Business structure

Korea Zinc is the largest non-ferrous metals smelting company in Korea. It imports zinc concentrates and smelts zinc, producing by-products including gold, silver, copper, lead, specialty metals such as germanium, antimony, indium and bismuth, and sulfuric acid for semiconductor use.

Its main domestic base is the Onsan smelter, and overseas it relies on its Australian zinc smelter SMC and a U.S. resource-recycling subsidiary. It is also pursuing electrolytic copper foil, a core secondary battery material.

Revenue mix is highly sensitive to precious metal prices: Hyundai Motor Securities estimated in a March 2026 report that silver would rise to 46% of standalone revenue, up from 33% in 2025.

On competition, DB Financial Investment said Korea Zinc could be a necessary partner for U.S. gallium and germanium supply, and possibly the only zinc smelter extracting those metals in the United States.

On governance, the stock split approved at a January 2025 extraordinary shareholders meeting has stalled amid litigation from MBK Partners and Youngpoong. Operating profit direction shifts sharply from quarter to quarter with precious metal prices, zinc prices and exchange rates.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual revenue went from KRW 11.2 trillion in 2022 to KRW 9.7 trillion in 2023 (down 13.5% year on year), then rose to KRW 12.1 trillion in 2024 (up 24.2%) and KRW 16.6 trillion in 2025 (up 37.6%).

After the 2023 low, revenue grew for two consecutive years from 2024 through 2025, and 2025 was the largest year of the 2022–2025 span. Operating profit fell to KRW 659.9 billion in 2023 (down 28.2%), then recovered to KRW 723.5 billion in 2024 (up 9.6%) and KRW 1.23 trillion in 2025 (up 70.3%).

Operating margin slid from 8.2% in 2022 to 6.0% in 2024 before returning to 7.4% in 2025, so margin recovery lagged revenue growth.

Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders bottomed at KRW 300.8 billion in 2024 (down 30.4%) and reached KRW 775 billion in 2025 (up 157.6%); net profit including non-controlling interests was KRW 770.2 billion in 2025, a different figure.

Quarterly operating profit peaked at KRW 746.1 billion in Q1 2026, then fell to KRW 587.1 billion in Q2 (down 21.3% quarter on quarter) and KRW 141.7 billion in Q3 (down 75.9%), a two-quarter decline.

Q3 2026 revenue of KRW 6.09 trillion was down 4.5% quarter on quarter but up 46.3% year on year, and the company attributed the profit drop to lower precious metal and some critical mineral prices and lower exchange rates.

The company also said initial costs of Project Crucible, its U.S. smelter build, weighed on Q3 results.

Q3 net profit attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 185.3 billion rose 156.7% year on year, moving in a different direction from operating profit; the four quarters from Q4 2025 through Q3 2026 total KRW 1.1 trillion.

Debt-to-equity ratio (total liabilities to total equity) rose to 97.2% in 2024 before easing to 83.7% in 2025, with total equity at KRW 11 trillion at end-2025. Operating cash flow moved from KRW 515.8 billion in 2024 to -KRW 628.2 billion in 2025.

Industry analysis

Korea Zinc's quarterly results move sharply with precious metal prices, zinc prices and exchange rates.

In an October 1, 2026 report, Hyundai Motor Securities noted that zinc prices reflected in Q3 rose 10% from the prior quarter while silver fell 19% and gold fell 5%, and it estimated Q3 operating profit at KRW 424.6 billion. Actual Q3 operating profit of KRW 141.7 billion fell well short of that estimate.

In a November 9, 2026 report, the same brokerage expected weaker results on softer precious metal prices and a stronger won, and cut its target price from KRW 1.42 million to KRW 1.29 million.

In a June 2026 report, iM Securities expected major non-ferrous metal prices, including zinc, nickel and aluminum, to stay firm on supply disruptions and rising resource nationalism, unlike steel.

In the same June 2026 report, iM Securities cut its target price to KRW 1.7 million, citing a less favorable macro backdrop with a tightening bias. The surge in silver has raised the weight of metal price exposure in revenue, a factor that cuts both ways.

Securities analysts, as reported in March 2026, point to near-term earnings recovery from zinc and precious metals and to rare metal output and the U.S. Crucible project as medium-term value drivers. Its position rests on being Korea's largest non-ferrous metals company and its role in U.S. rare metal supply.

06

Outlook

Alongside its Q3 2026 results, the company resolved to cancel treasury shares and to re-propose a stock split. In January 2027, it plans to cancel 246,665 of its 479,737 treasury shares, using the remaining 233,072 for employee compensation over the next ten years.

The stock split will be put again to the annual general meeting in March 2027; it was approved at a January 2025 extraordinary meeting but stalled after litigation from MBK Partners and Youngpoong.

Hyundai Motor Securities said the Clarksville, Tennessee integrated smelter, referred to in DB's report as the Crucible Project, is due to start construction in Q1 2027, with zinc output in Q1 2029 and lead and copper in Q4 2029, per its November 9, 2026 report.

The same brokerage pointed to past share price moves that priced in zinc capacity expansion ahead of time, and said this investment could be a positive catalyst.

In a July 23, 2026 report, Shinhan Investment Corp. said downside would be set by existing smelting profits, while upside would depend on the silver price trend, the U.S. Crucible project, germanium and gallium, and the resolution of the control dispute.

The company has said full-scale germanium production will broaden its product portfolio and strengthen profitability. The next checkpoints are the Q4 2026 results announcement and the 2026 annual report filing; the benchmark is how next-quarter operating profit moves against KRW 141.7 billion in Q3 2026.

Bull factors

Earnings recovery in 2025

Operating profit rose 70.3% to KRW 1.23 trillion in 2025, and net profit attributable to controlling shareholders rose 157.6% to KRW 775 billion. Both were the largest years in the 2022–2025 span. Q1 2026 operating profit of KRW 746.1 billion marked the quarterly peak. Supporters point to the recovered earnings base as the core bull argument.

Firm non-ferrous metal prices

In a June 2026 report, iM Securities expected major non-ferrous metal prices to stay firm on supply disruptions and rising resource nationalism. The same firm named Korea Zinc its top sector pick.

Hyundai Motor Securities identified precious metal price trends as the key variable, given the larger silver share of standalone revenue. Bulls argue that firm metal prices would support the earnings floor.

U.S. smelter and rare metal position

DB Financial Investment, in a March 2026 report, described Korea Zinc as a necessary partner for U.S. gallium and germanium supply. The Tennessee integrated smelter is planned to start construction in Q1 2027, with zinc output targeted for Q1 2029.

The company said full-scale germanium production will broaden its product portfolio. This point supports the case for its role in rare metal supply chain restructuring.

Bear factors

Sharp Q3 profit drop

Q3 2026 operating profit of KRW 141.7 billion fell 48.2% year on year and 75.9% from the prior quarter. The company cited lower precious metal and some critical mineral prices and lower exchange rates as profitability headwinds. Two consecutive quarterly declines are cited as the core bear point. Quarterly data show profit falling even while revenue held above KRW 6 trillion.

Stronger won and target cuts

On November 9, 2026, Hyundai Motor Securities expected weaker results on softer precious metal prices and a stronger won, cutting its target from KRW 1.42 million to KRW 1.29 million. In an October 1, 2026 report, Hyundai Motor Securities had already lowered its target from KRW 1.5 million to KRW 1.42 million.

The brokerage linked the target cuts to earnings estimate cuts. Bears cite this pattern as evidence that exchange rates and precious metal prices can shake near-term earnings estimates.

Cash flow and large investment burden

Operating cash flow turned to -KRW 628.2 billion in 2025 from KRW 515.8 billion in 2024. The debt-to-equity ratio rose to 97.2% in 2024 and eased to 83.7% in 2025, still above the 25.7% of 2023. Initial costs of the U.S. Crucible project weighed on Q3 earnings, showing how large investment spending is affecting short-term profit.

10

Risk factors

Metal prices and FX

Precious metal and zinc prices directly move quarterly results. In Q3 2026, lower gold and silver prices and lower exchange rates both weighed on profit. If a stronger won persists, similar downward pressure could recur. Large price swings also widen the range of quarterly earnings outcomes.

Governance and litigation

The stock split approved at a January 2025 extraordinary meeting has stalled amid litigation from MBK Partners and Youngpoong. The company resolved to put it to the March 2027 annual general meeting. A prolonged control dispute could affect shareholder returns and decision timing. Litigation developments are a variable the company cannot fully control.

U.S. project execution

The U.S. smelter is planned to start construction in Q1 2027, with zinc output targeted for Q1 2029, leaving construction cost and schedule risk. Initial project costs were already reflected in Q3 2026 operating profit. The timing at which the investment's profitability becomes visible may shift with metal prices. Whether construction stays on schedule is a key item to monitor.

Overall view

Korea Zinc's operating profit rose 70.3% to KRW 1.23 trillion in 2025, a large recovery in the earnings base. In 2026, operating profit peaked at KRW 746.1 billion in Q1 and then fell for two consecutive quarters, to KRW 141.7 billion in Q3.

The causes the company cited, lower precious metal and critical mineral prices and lower exchange rates, reflect the metal-price exposure of its business. Q3 net profit attributable to controlling shareholders rose year on year, and the debt-to-equity ratio eased to 83.7% in 2025 from 97.2% in 2024.

Brokerage targets moved down, from KRW 1.7 million (iM Securities, June 2026) and KRW 1.6 million (Shinhan Investment Corp., July 2026) to KRW 1.29 million (Hyundai Motor Securities, November 2026).

Medium-term variables include the Tennessee smelter's Q1 2027 construction start and 2029 production start, the re-proposed stock split, and the litigation with Youngpoong and MBK Partners.

Bull points include the earnings recovery and the strategic value of rare metals, while bear points include the quarterly profit decline, metal and FX exposure, and the swing of operating cash flow into negative territory.

The next checkpoints are the Q4 2026 results, the 2026 annual report, and the outcome of the March 2027 general meeting.
