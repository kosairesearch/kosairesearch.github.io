Business structure

VC is a golf technology company that makes golf-related measurement and simulation equipment, including golf distance measuring devices, launch monitors and golf simulators.

In Q2 2026, golf distance measuring equipment accounted for 73 percent of revenue, merchandise sales for 18 percent, services and other revenue for 6 percent, and the domestic subsidiary for 3 percent, according to a Butler aggregation of that quarter.

By product type, watch-type devices were the largest category at 39 percent, followed by merchandise and other at 28 percent, laser-type at 13 percent and launch-monitor type at 11 percent, per an AlphaSquare aggregation whose reporting date is not stated.

The Q1 2026 improvement was attributed to higher overseas sales of key products and cost cuts, according to FnGuide. Golf simulators are expected to keep growing on replacement of aging equipment and new store supply. The 21st-term financial statements were approved at the March 26, 2026 annual shareholder meeting.

The company had 107 employees as of October 27, 2026, per TradingView. Customers appear to mix golf retail stores and individual demand, and overseas sales are a key driver of earnings swings. On competition, measurement accuracy, product lineup and overseas distribution channels act as the main differentiators.

Although classified under KOSDAQ machinery and equipment, VC differs sharply in revenue structure and customer base from large industrial equipment makers such as Doosan Enerbility and Doosan Bobcat.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual revenue fell from KRW 54.3 billion in 2022 to KRW 37.2 billion in 2023, a 31.5 percent decline, and then recovered to KRW 44.1 billion in 2024 (up 18.6 percent) and KRW 46.9 billion in 2025 (up 6.3 percent), rising for two consecutive years from 2024 to 2025. 2022 was the largest year among 2022 to 2025 and 2023 the smallest.

The operating margin moved from 2.3 percent in 2022 to -31.2 percent in 2023, then -10.7 percent in 2024 and -3.2 percent in 2025, so the loss rate narrowed.

Operating losses were KRW 11.6 billion in 2023, KRW 4.74 billion in 2024 and KRW 1.48 billion in 2025, a three-year run of losses from 2023 to 2025 with the loss shrinking each year.

Net loss attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 12 billion in 2023, KRW 5.87 billion in 2024 and KRW 3 billion in 2025, while the net loss including non-controlling interests was KRW 3.2 billion in 2025.

The debt-to-equity ratio rose from 94.0 percent in 2022 to 128.7 percent in 2023 and 142.7 percent in 2024 before easing to 123.9 percent in 2025. Operating cash flow was KRW -8.02 billion in 2022 and KRW -2.8 billion in 2023, turned positive at KRW 3.96 billion in 2024, and was KRW 814 million in 2025.

Quarterly, operating losses were KRW 629 million in Q3 2025 and KRW 917 million in Q4 2025, then narrowed to KRW 472 million in Q1 2026. Q2 2026 revenue of KRW 17.5 billion rose 61.6 percent from the prior quarter and operating profit of KRW 2.1 billion marked a swing to profit.

For Q1 2026, higher overseas sales and cost cuts were cited as the reasons for the narrower loss. Q3 2026 revenue of KRW 14.7 billion fell 15.6 percent from Q2 but rose 17.6 percent year on year, and operating profit of KRW 1.49 billion dropped 29.2 percent from the prior quarter.

Profit attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 1.69 billion in Q2 and KRW 355 million in Q3. The pattern, with profit rising alongside higher sales in Q2 and falling with lower sales in Q3, shows high quarterly volatility. Combined profit attributable to controlling shareholders over the latest four quarters is KRW 220 million.

Industry analysis

Demand for golf equipment carries a leisure-spending character and tends to respond to the economy and consumer sentiment. VC's revenue fell 31.5 percent in 2023 and recovered in 2024 and 2025, a path that may have moved in step with that demand cycle.

In golf equipment, measurement accuracy, software integration and store installation networks appear to act as the main competitive factors. Quarterly revenue of KRW 17.5 billion in Q2 2026 and KRW 14.7 billion in Q3 2026 suggests that sales cluster around installation and shipment timing.

On a full-year basis the company is still in a phase of narrowing losses, while on a quarterly basis it posted operating profits in Q2 and Q3 2026.

Koh Young Technology, also in the KOSDAQ machinery and equipment group, uses measurement technology but its main exposure is electronics and semiconductor inspection, so the direct comparison is limited.

Doosan Enerbility and Doosan Bobcat are large industrial equipment makers with different revenue scale and customer bases, so simple comparison with sector averages requires care. VC's position within the sector therefore centers on expanding overseas sales in a narrow golf market.

Risk factors

Sensitivity to economy and leisure spending

Golf equipment carries a leisure-spending character, so economic slowdown or weaker consumer sentiment could hit demand first. Revenue fell 31.5 percent in 2023, a period that can be read as an example of this cycle. Slower demand could delay new store supply and simulator installations.

Revenue mix and quarterly swings

Golf distance measuring equipment makes up 73 percent of revenue, so the product mix is concentrated. Quarterly revenue moved from KRW 17.5 billion to KRW 14.7 billion, consistent with earnings tied to installation and shipment timing.

Because profit flips from quarter to quarter, sustained profitability is hard to judge from one or two quarters.

Balance sheet and currency exposure

A debt-to-equity ratio of 123.9 percent and total liabilities of KRW 45.7 billion reduce the cushion against changes in interest rates or working capital. As overseas sales grow, currency moves affect won-denominated revenue and profit. If these two factors coincide, the pace of loss narrowing could slow.
