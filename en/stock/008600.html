02

Business structure

THE Willbes & is an OEM apparel company founded in 1973 and listed on the Korea Stock Exchange in 1989, built around a textile business and an education business.

The textile business produces adult and children's knitwear and swimwear on an OEM basis for global buyers including GAP and Hanesbrands, with overseas production bases in Indonesia, Cambodia, the Dominican Republic and Haiti.

By sales in Q2 2026, the textile business accounted for 59% and the education business for 40%. The education business is centered on Namu Management Academy and offers professional certification and English courses.

In the 2025 annual filing, export accounted for 98.35% of sewn-product sales, and education sales are generated entirely in Korea. The main apparel subsidiaries are in the Dominican Republic and Haiti, and the company reports 14 unlisted subsidiaries.

Textile sales depend on overseas buyer orders, while education sales rely on domestic enrollment demand. The textile business has underperformed amid economic cycles in the United States and Europe and low-price competition from emerging economies.

The education business has seen weaker profitability due to falling sales and higher cost burdens. In 2025, textile sales fell on political instability at the Haitian subsidiary. Under an OEM model, buyer order terms and pricing power drive margins, so profitability recovery remains a task in both businesses.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual sales fell from KRW 254.9 billion in 2022 to KRW 216.1 billion in 2023 (down 15.2% year on year), edged up to KRW 217.4 billion in 2024 (up 0.6%), then fell to KRW 188.3 billion in 2025 (down 13.4%). Among 2022 to 2025, 2022 was the largest year and 2025 the smallest.

Operating profit of KRW 193 million in 2022 turned into an operating loss of KRW 4.18 billion in 2023, then widened to KRW 12.5 billion in 2024 and KRW 21 billion in 2025, which makes three consecutive years of losses from 2023 to 2025.

The operating margin moved from 0.1% in 2022 to -1.9% in 2023, -5.8% in 2024 and -11.2% in 2025.

Net loss attributable to controlling shareholders widened from KRW 9.33 billion in 2022 to KRW 15.5 billion in 2023, KRW 27.4 billion in 2024 and KRW 36.4 billion in 2025, and the net loss including non-controlling interests was also KRW 36.4 billion in 2025.

The company attributed the 2025 operating loss widening to lower textile sales at its Haitian subsidiary amid political instability and higher inventory valuation provisions.

Quarterly, Q3 2025 sales were KRW 44.9 billion with an operating loss of KRW 2.31 billion; Q4 2025 sales were KRW 40.9 billion with an operating loss of KRW 18.4 billion.

Q1 2026 sales were KRW 44.8 billion with an operating loss of KRW 3.33 billion, but Q2 2026 sales were KRW 41.3 billion with an operating loss of KRW 27.9 billion and a controlling-shareholder net loss of KRW 32.5 billion, the largest quarterly loss in the last five quarters of filed results.

Q3 2026 sales were KRW 27.1 billion (down 39.6% year on year), with an operating loss of KRW 5.75 billion and a controlling-shareholder net loss of KRW 5.49 billion; the losses narrowed from the prior quarter but widened from a year earlier at the operating line.

The controlling-shareholder net loss for the last four quarters (Q4 2025 to Q3 2026) totaled KRW 69.5 billion. Operating cash flow went from KRW -6.72 billion in 2022 to KRW 5.38 billion in 2023, KRW 4.66 billion in 2024 and KRW 3.23 billion in 2025, positive since 2023 but trending lower.

The debt ratio rose from 175.1% in 2022 to 182.1% in 2023, 240.2% in 2024 and 412.3% in 2025, so accumulated losses are thinning the equity base.

05

Industry analysis

THE Willbes &'s apparel business sits in the WI26 group that combines cosmetics, apparel and toys, and it moves with demand for consumer-goods OEM production. Economic cycles in the United States and Europe are cited as a main reason for weak textile results.

Low-price competition from emerging economies adds pricing pressure. Production bases in Indonesia, Cambodia, the Dominican Republic and Haiti expose the business to regional political and trade conditions, and political instability in Haiti directly reduced textile sales in 2025.

The education business centers on domestic certification and English courses and moves on a different axis from export competition, yet falling sales and higher cost burdens have been reported to weigh on its profitability.

Under an OEM model, buyer order terms and pricing power drive margins, so a recovery in the operating margin depends on cost control and order stability. In education, course pricing and content competition shape margins.

Taken together, textile faces global consumer cycles and low-price rivals, while education faces falling sales and cost pressure.
