Report overview

Mining-and-construction holding firm faces wider operating losses

Kyungdong Invest is a holding company whose mining, plant and logistics, and construction units drive its results. After returning to operating profit in Q4 2025, it posted operating losses in Q2 and Q3 2026 even as sales rose.

02

Business structure

Kyungdong Invest and its consolidated subsidiaries are organized as strategic business units that offer different services and require different construction technologies, so each is run separately, according to its semi-annual report.

The main segments are mining and resource development, plant and logistics, construction, power generation, and holding and real estate leasing. In 2025, mining and resource development accounted for 45.95% of sales, plant and logistics 25.07%, construction 16.97%, and holding business 4.73%.

The remaining 7.28% combined power generation at 4.44% and storage and transport at 2.84%. The mix has moved over time: mining was 48.73% in 2022, dipped to 40.32% in 2023, and returned to 45.95% in 2025, while plant and logistics peaked at 30.98% in 2023 before falling to 25.07%.

Plant and logistics covers gas-related facility works, warehousing and multimodal brokerage, pipeline transport, and hazardous-material storage. Construction covers tunnels, bridges, roads, and site development, and power generation centers on electricity production and sales. All 2025 sales were booked domestically.

Per the company website, Kyungdong Invest holds 98.6% of its mining-related operating unit, 71.1% of its construction unit, and 37.0% of Kyungdong City GAS, and it wholly owns three other units. In a June 2021 FnGuide market-share table, the two operators in the mining segment held 76% and 24%.

04

Earnings analysis

On an annual basis, sales rose from KRW 275.5 billion in 2022 to KRW 312.5 billion in 2023 (up 13.4%), then fell to KRW 293.7 billion in 2024 (down 6.0%) and KRW 276.2 billion in 2025 (down 6.0%), a two-year decline after the 2023 peak.

Operating profit climbed from KRW 14.7 billion in 2022 to KRW 21.2 billion in 2023 (up 44.2%) and KRW 24.4 billion in 2024 (up 15.0%), then dropped to KRW 18.8 billion in 2025 (down 22.8%). The operating margin went from 5.3% in 2022 to 6.8% in 2023 and 8.3% in 2024, before slipping to 6.8% in 2025.

In 2025, operating profit fell faster than sales. According to FnGuide, the cost-of-sales ratio improved from 86.15% in 2022 to 82.01% in 2024 and edged up to 82.75% in 2025.

Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders rose from KRW 14.2 billion in 2022 to KRW 19.9 billion in 2023 (up 40.1%) and KRW 21.7 billion in 2024 (up 8.8%), then fell to KRW 18.5 billion in 2025 (down 14.7%).

Net profit including non-controlling interests was KRW 9.36 billion in 2022, below the attributable figure, and KRW 18.7 billion in 2025. Operating cash flow was KRW 27.2 billion in 2022, KRW 20.3 billion in 2023, and KRW 23.6 billion in 2024, then fell to KRW 4.54 billion in 2025.

Quarterly, the company moved from an operating loss of KRW 2.92 billion in Q3 2025 to operating profit of KRW 20.8 billion in Q4 2025, a Q4 figure larger than the full-year 2025 operating profit of KRW 18.8 billion.

Operating profit then fell to KRW 1.92 billion in Q1 2026, down 90.8% from the prior quarter, turned into an operating loss of KRW 2.5 billion in Q2, and widened to an operating loss of KRW 6.78 billion in Q3.

Q3 2026 sales of KRW 70.7 billion rose 13.3% from the prior quarter and 15.9% from a year earlier, but the loss widened. Whether the Q4 2025 profit was one-off can be checked in the business report's segment detail.

05

Industry analysis

Kyungdong Invest is classified under other financial services on the KOSPI, according to WiseReport, but its actual earnings come from mining and resources, plant and logistics, construction, and power generation.

Its profit drivers therefore differ from those of financial holding companies such as KB Financial Group and Shinhan Financial Group, whose results track interest rates, loan growth, and trading volumes. The mining segment's quarterly results can swing widely with raw-material prices and development schedules.

The plant and logistics segment is tied to infrastructure spending, including gas-related facility works and storage and transport. The construction segment takes on tunnels, bridges, roads, and site development, so it is affected by public and civil-works ordering schedules.

Power generation is small, at 4.44% of 2025 sales, so its effect on consolidated results is limited. Since 2023, the plant and logistics share has fallen from 30.98% to 25.07%, and the construction share has moved from 17.96% to 16.97%.

Because the business mix differs, direct profitability comparisons with financial holding companies are of limited use, and peer comparisons are only meaningful segment by segment.

08

Bull factors

Return to profit in Q4 2025

Q4 2025 operating profit was KRW 20.8 billion, turning around from an operating loss of KRW 2.92 billion in Q3 2025, and net profit attributable to controlling shareholders for that quarter was KRW 16.6 billion. That Q4 figure exceeded the full-year 2025 operating profit of KRW 18.8 billion.

Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders over the last four quarters totaled KRW 17.6 billion. The swing from loss to profit in one quarter is cited as evidence of recovered operating capacity.

Lower leverage and a larger equity base

The debt-to-equity ratio fell from 30.2% in 2022 to 23.1% in 2025. Total equity at the end of 2025 was KRW 498.3 billion, of which KRW 495.1 billion belonged to controlling shareholders. Total liabilities were KRW 115.1 billion, less than a quarter of total equity. The lower leverage could act as a buffer during periods of weaker operations.

Signs of sales recovery

Q2 2026 sales of KRW 62.5 billion rose 15.1% from the prior quarter, and Q3 2026 sales of KRW 70.7 billion rose 13.3% from Q2. Q3 sales were up 15.9% from a year earlier. Sales have grown for two straight quarters since the Q1 2026 low of KRW 54.3 billion, which is cited as a bullish point.

Whether that top-line growth translates into better operating profit must still be confirmed in upcoming quarterly results.

09

Bear factors

Operating losses widened in 2026

After an operating loss of KRW 2.5 billion in Q2 2026, the Q3 operating loss widened to KRW 6.78 billion. The Q3 loss is also larger than a year earlier, when the company was already loss-making. The deficit deepened in a quarter when sales rose, so higher revenue did not translate into profit. If this pattern continues, the timing of an earnings recovery could be pushed back.

Lower earnings and margins in 2025

Operating profit for 2025 was KRW 18.8 billion, down 22.8%, and the operating margin fell from 8.3% in 2024 to 6.8%. Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders declined 14.7% to KRW 18.5 billion. The annual figures show weaker earnings power since the 2024 peak. Profit fell more steeply than sales, so the margin decline is the more pronounced signal.

Sharp drop in operating cash flow

Operating cash flow fell from KRW 23.6 billion in 2024 to KRW 4.54 billion in 2025. It was also well below the KRW 27.2 billion recorded in 2022. Weak conversion of reported earnings into cash is cited as a bearish point. Working capital and collection of construction receivables should be checked further in the annual report.

Risk factors

Mining segment volatility

Mining and resource development made up 45.95% of 2025 sales, making it the largest pillar of consolidated results. Its share rose from 40.32% in 2023. Quarterly profit could swing sharply with raw-material prices and development progress, which is the largest risk factor.

Plant and construction costs and orders

Plant and logistics at 25.07% and construction at 16.97% together account for roughly 42% of 2025 sales. Quarterly revenue and cost ratios depend on project volumes and progress. Operating losses widened in Q3 2026 even as sales grew, which points to cost and project margin control as a key variable. A gap in orders combined with rising costs could widen earnings swings.

Cash flow and holding structure

Operating cash flow fell to KRW 4.54 billion in 2025. A holding company's attributable earnings depend on subsidiary ownership and non-controlling interests.

In 2022, net profit of KRW 9.36 billion was below attributable net profit of KRW 14.2 billion, and in 2025 net profit of KRW 18.7 billion differed from attributable profit of KRW 18.5 billion. Kyungdong City GAS is held at 37.0%, a relatively low stake.

Overall view

Kyungdong Invest is a holding company centered on mining and resource development (45.95% of 2025 sales), plant and logistics (25.07%), and construction (16.97%). Operating profit fell to KRW 18.8 billion in 2025, and the operating margin declined to 6.8%.

The company turned to operating profit of KRW 20.8 billion in Q4 2025, but operating losses followed in Q2 and Q3 2026. Q3 2026 sales of KRW 70.7 billion rose 15.9% year on year, yet the operating loss widened to KRW 6.78 billion.

The next points to check are the full-year operating margin in the 2026 annual report and the profit trend in the Q1 2027 quarterly report.
