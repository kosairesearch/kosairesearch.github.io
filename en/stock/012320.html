Holding Company Earnings Swings, Resource Development Watched

02

Business structure

Kyungdong Invest's core operations are holding-company management and real-estate leasing, while its subsidiaries run plant and logistics, power generation, construction, mining and resource development, and storage and transport businesses.

As a holding company it collects dividend income and brand-license fees from affiliates, alongside activities to enhance and protect the group brand. Its key affiliates are Kyungdong City Gas (37.04% stake), which handles city-gas distribution, Kyungdong Corp.

(98.55% stake), which handles coal and other resource trading and development, and Kyungdong Construction (71.1% stake), plus wholly owned units Kyungdong E&S, KD Powertech and Kyungdong Tank Terminal. A former subsidiary, Kyungdong Enair, was dissolved after being absorbed into Kyungdong Invest in September 2024.

Within the governance chain, Kyungdong Invest sits atop one of three Kyungdong Group family branches - the Kyungdong City Gas branch led by honorary chairman Sohn Kyung-ho - separate from the Kyungdong Navien branch led by his brother.

At the very top sits Kyungdong Holdings, which holds a 45.17% stake in Kyungdong Invest, with Chairman Sohn also holding 5.69% directly; Kyungdong Holdings' largest shareholder is his son and Kyungdong Invest vice chairman, who has largely completed the share-transfer process for third-generation succession.

Cross-shareholdings among family branches remain in place, preserving a profit-sharing structure through dividends rather than a full split.

Given this business mix, construction and plant revenue is recognized on a percentage-of-completion basis while the resource-development segment depends on sales volumes and prices, producing considerable quarter-to-quarter variability.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual revenue rose from KRW 275.5bn with operating profit of KRW 14.7bn in 2022 to KRW 312.5bn and KRW 21.2bn in 2023, then to KRW 293.7bn and KRW 24.4bn in 2024, before both revenue and operating profit slowed to KRW 276.2bn and KRW 18.8bn in 2025.

Net income attributable to owners rose for three straight years, from KRW 14.2bn in 2022 to KRW 19.9bn in 2023 and KRW 21.7bn in 2024, before slipping to KRW 18.5bn in 2025. The operating margin improved from 5.3% in 2022 to 6.8% in 2023 and 8.3% in 2024, then eased back to 6.8% in 2025.

Quarterly results show pronounced volatility: in Q2 2025 revenue was KRW 51.8bn with an operating loss of KRW 1.3bn, and in Q3 revenue was KRW 61.1bn with the operating loss widening to KRW 2.9bn while owners' net income swung to a loss of KRW 2.4bn.

Q4 2025, by contrast, saw revenue surge to KRW 96.9bn with operating profit of KRW 20.8bn and owners' net income of KRW 16.6bn, meaning a large share of annual earnings was concentrated in the fourth quarter.

Into 2026, Q1 stayed profitable with revenue of KRW 54.3bn, operating profit of KRW 1.9bn and net income of KRW 3.0bn, but Q2 reverted to an operating loss of KRW 2.5bn on revenue of KRW 62.5bn, though owners' net income remained slightly positive at KRW 0.7bn.

This pattern suggests a structural gap between operating and net results, likely reflecting equity-method gains, dividends, or other non-operating items cushioning the bottom line.

Over the trailing four quarters (Q3 2025 through Q2 2026), cumulative owners' net income stood at roughly KRW 17.9bn, underscoring the continuation of a Q4-weighted earnings pattern.

05

Industry analysis

Although classified under the KOSPI financial sector, Kyungdong Invest is in substance a diversified business holding company combining city-gas distribution, construction, logistics, power generation, and mining and resource development.

Its city-gas exposure comes largely through equity-method income from Kyungdong City Gas, a relatively stable business tied to tariff regulation and seasonal gas demand.

The construction, plant and logistics segments recognize revenue depending on domestic construction activity and project completion rates, and a slowdown in the construction cycle has been cited as a factor behind recent revenue declines.

In resource development, subsidiary Kyungdong Corp. is pursuing exploration of new minerals such as titanium in addition to its coal-trading business, and the stock has at times been grouped by the market under resource-development or rare-mineral themes.

That exploration project, however, has been the subject of repeated clarification disclosures since a related media report in 2022, and the company itself has stated that commercialization remains unconfirmed, leaving views on its progress mixed.

Separately, national attention on building non-China supply chains for strategic minerals such as tungsten and molybdenum has at times lifted market interest in related themes, but that activity has centered on an unrelated mine developer in Yeongwol, Gangwon Province, and should be distinguished from Kyungdong Invest's own operating results.

Within the holding-company peer group, Kyungdong Invest is generally viewed as a small-to-mid-sized holdco with a relatively stable cash flow base from dividends and brand fees, with its resource-development exposure noted as a point of differentiation versus similarly structured holding companies.

06

Outlook

According to company disclosures, subsidiary Kyungdong Corp. completed an additional exploratory drilling program of one hole (1,200 meters) in 2025 and requested assay analysis from the Korea Mine Reclamation and Mineral Resources Corporation, with the report expected to be received around March-April 2026.

The company set a target re-disclosure date of March 18, 2026, for this matter, while consistently stating that commercialization remains unconfirmed at any stage.

As of today, no further confirmed disclosure detailing the assay results or a subsequent commercialization decision has been identified, so this will require monitoring through future filings.

Given the holding-company structure, future results are likely to depend heavily on individual affiliate performance - equity-method income and dividends from Kyungdong City Gas, order intake and construction progress at Kyungdong Construction, and resource-trading volumes at Kyungdong Corp.

Past patterns show revenue and profit repeatedly concentrated in the fourth quarter, and whether this seasonality persists through the second half of 2026 is a point worth watching.

On the dividend front, the company appears to have paid a cash dividend every year in recent years, and whether this continues, along with its size, could signal the holding company's capital-allocation direction.

Should share-transfer activity related to third-generation succession continue, any resulting governance changes could also affect dividend policy or intra-group transaction structures, which merits attention as well.

Bull factors

Stable dividend and brand-fee income base

As a holding company, Kyungdong Invest regularly collects equity-method income, dividends, and brand-license fees from affiliates such as Kyungdong City Gas. This has historically offset some of the quarter-to-quarter earnings swings from segments like construction and plant.

Indeed, net income has repeatedly stayed positive even in quarters when operating income turned negative. This cash-generation structure is cited as a source of holding-company stability.

Growth optionality from resource development

Beyond its existing coal-trading business, subsidiary Kyungdong Corp. is pursuing exploration of new minerals such as titanium to expand its operations.

An assay report on the additional exploratory drilling completed in 2025 was flagged to arrive in the first half of 2026, potentially opening new business opportunities depending on the outcome.

The market has at times classified Kyungdong Invest under resource-development or strategic-mineral themes, keeping related attention alive, though commercialization has not yet been confirmed.

Continuing cash dividend policy

The company appears to have paid a cash dividend every year in recent years.

As a holding company that redistributes affiliate dividend income to shareholders, this policy continuity itself can be viewed as a stabilizing factor, though the payout ratio is reported to be modest relative to the scale of earnings, leaving room for potential expansion of shareholder returns as a point to watch going forward.

Bear factors

Slowing revenue and profit trend

Consolidated revenue peaked at KRW 312.5bn in 2023 before declining for two straight years to KRW 293.7bn in 2024 and KRW 276.2bn in 2025. Operating profit over the same period fell from KRW 24.4bn in 2024 to KRW 18.8bn in 2025.

This appears to reflect a combination of declining revenue in mining/resource development, plant, and logistics segments along with reduced construction revenue. Whether this slowdown continues into 2026 will need to be confirmed through quarterly results.

Low predictability of quarterly results

Operating losses persisted through Q2 and Q3 2025, and owners' net income also turned negative in Q3, while Q4 saw a sharp jump in both revenue and profit, reflecting very large quarter-to-quarter swings.

An operating loss recurred in Q2 2026 as well, which appears to stem from the business structure where results depend on project completion timing and sales recognition. This volatility makes it difficult to judge directional trends from any single quarter's results.

Limited liquidity and small-cap characteristics

As a relatively small-cap stock with a high concentration of shares held by the largest shareholder and related parties, the free float is limited. This is cited as a factor that can amplify price volatility during periods of thin trading.

Given relatively low institutional and foreign investor participation typical of such names, information accessibility can also be less favorable compared with larger-cap peers.

Risk factors

Governance and succession risk

The multi-layer governance chain from Kyungdong Holdings through Kyungdong Invest to Kyungdong City Gas and Kyungdong Corp., combined with remaining cross-shareholdings among affiliates, has drawn characterizations of a tiered, indirect control structure.

Should share transfers or group restructuring occur during the transition to third-generation ownership, aligning minority shareholder interests will be worth watching.

Given that the branches maintain independent management without a full split, market scrutiny of intra-group transaction practices is also likely to continue.

Sensitivity to business cycles

The construction and plant segments are directly exposed to a slowdown in the domestic construction cycle, while the city-gas segment is exposed to tariff regulation and seasonal shifts in gas demand. The resource-trading segment's revenue and margins can be swayed by commodity price movements.

Because these segments are exposed to different cyclical drivers, simultaneous weakness across them could weigh on consolidated results at the same time.

Uncertainty around new mineral business

Kyungdong Corp.'s titanium-related mineral exploration project has been the subject of repeated clarification disclosures since a related media report in 2022, with the company each time stating that commercialization remains unconfirmed.

The timeline for receiving the assay report could also be delayed, and if the results fall short of expectations, the actual business contribution relative to investment could be limited.

Given the nature of a new business line, additional steps such as permitting and economic feasibility assessment could require considerable further time.

Overall view

Kyungdong Invest is a business holding company combining city gas, construction, logistics, and resource development, with equity-method income and dividend/brand fees from affiliates serving as an earnings buffer.

Annual results improved through 2023-2024 before both revenue and operating profit slowed in 2025, and quarterly results have shown considerable volatility, including operating losses in Q2-Q3 2025 and a recurring concentration of results in the fourth quarter.

An assay outcome from subsidiary Kyungdong Corp.'s new mineral exploration was guided to be confirmed sometime in 2026, but commercialization remains unconfirmed, making follow-up disclosures worth watching.

On governance, a multi-layer structure topped by Kyungdong Holdings is proceeding alongside a third-generation succession process, warranting attention to potential shareholding changes. The company's policy of paying a cash dividend every year continues, though its payout ratio is reported to be limited.

Overall, the structure combines stable cash-flow factors with earnings volatility and business uncertainty, making continued monitoring of quarterly results and resource-development disclosures important going forward.
