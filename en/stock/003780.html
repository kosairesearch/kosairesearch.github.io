02

Business structure

Chin Yang Industry was founded in 1963 and makes foamed plastic molded products, led by polyurethane (PU) foam, an intermediate material.

A past securities research report indicated that PU foam accounted for nearly all sales in the foamed plastic segment, so the business is best read as a single-product structure centered on foam.

According to the FnGuide company overview, the company supplies intermediate materials to automotive interior and other industrial customers and holds about 20% of the domestic market. Its main customers sit in the automotive parts supply chain, so output at vehicle makers links directly to demand.

Overseas, the company operates three unlisted subsidiaries including a Vietnam unit, giving it production bases abroad. The 2025 results were described as benefiting from domestic sourcing of raw and subsidiary materials.

On production capacity, the subsidiary Jinrye Industry, which is 100% owned, had outsourced production of rigid urethane foam at a Gimhae plant, and on January 12, 2026 the board resolved to acquire the land and buildings for KRW 17.18 billion.

The company said in its filing that the lease could not be renewed and that it is buying the plant to prevent customer attrition. Press reports pointed out that the seller is Jinyang Mulsan, an unlisted company under Jinyang Holdings, which raised concerns about support for affiliates.

Large chemical peers such as LG Chem and Hanwha Solutions cover petrochemicals and materials broadly, so Chin Yang Industry, a foam-focused small company, differs widely in scale and product range.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual revenue rose from KRW 83.7 billion in 2022 to KRW 86.9 billion in 2023 (up 3.8% year over year), KRW 88.2 billion in 2024 (up 1.6%), and KRW 90.7 billion in 2025 (up 2.8%), making 2025 the largest year of 2022–2025.

Operating profit grew sharply from KRW 5.29 billion in 2022 to KRW 7.57 billion in 2023 (up 43.2% year over year), and continued to KRW 8.2 billion in 2024 (up 8.3%) and KRW 8.44 billion in 2025 (up 3.0%).

The operating margin rose from 6.3% in 2022 to 8.7% in 2023, 9.3% in 2024 and 9.3% in 2025, so the margin improvement was concentrated in 2023, and the margin moved sideways from 2024 onward.

Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 6.14 billion in 2022, KRW 8.5 billion in 2023 (up 38.5%) and KRW 10.0 billion in 2024 (up 18.2%), making 2024 the largest year, and then fell 30.8% to KRW 6.95 billion in 2025.

Operating profit rose 3.0% in 2025 while net profit declined, and that gap arose from items below operating profit; net income including non-controlling interests for the same year was also KRW 6.95 billion, the same as net profit attributable to controlling shareholders.

At the end of 2025, total equity was KRW 67.1 billion and total liabilities were KRW 26.6 billion, so the debt ratio fell from 61.5% in 2022 to 39.6%. Operating cash flow was KRW 6.98 billion in 2022, KRW 11.9 billion in 2023, KRW 9.3 billion in 2024 and KRW 9.22 billion in 2025.

On a quarterly basis, revenue of KRW 23.5 billion and operating profit of KRW 2.61 billion in the third quarter of 2025 fell in the fourth quarter to revenue of KRW 20.3 billion (down 13.8% from the previous quarter) and operating profit of KRW 1.09 billion (down 58.0%).

In the first quarter of 2026, revenue was KRW 22.2 billion and operating profit recovered to KRW 1.71 billion (up 56.2%), but operating profit then declined for two consecutive quarters, to KRW 1.63 billion in the second quarter and KRW 1.58 billion in the third.

Third-quarter 2026 revenue held at KRW 23.6 billion (up 0.4% from a year earlier), but net profit attributable to controlling shareholders fell 50.6% year over year to KRW 1.05 billion, and the sum of net profit attributable to controlling shareholders over the latest four quarters is KRW 4.71 billion.

Because profit has thinned while revenue has stayed at a similar level, it is necessary to check the changes in the cost and selling, general and administrative expense structure in the next periodic report.

05

Industry analysis

PU foam is an intermediate used in automotive seats and interior parts, so demand is tied directly to vehicle production and interior component orders. With the company reporting a domestic market share of about 20%, domestic PU foam demand and domestic auto output are the central variables for earnings.

Raw materials derive from petrochemical feedstocks, so when raw material prices rise, margins depend on how quickly those costs are passed through to selling prices. The 2025 results cited domestic sourcing of raw and subsidiary materials as a sales driver, which points to supply stability supporting revenue.

Intermediate makers in the automotive parts supply chain are prone to volume adjustments and price negotiations with vehicle customers.

Large peers in the chemicals sector such as LG Chem, Hanwha Solutions and Kumho Petrochemical cover petrochemicals and materials broadly, so their business scope is hard to compare directly.

Chin Yang Industry, by contrast, is a small company focused on one product, polyurethane foam, so its results may react more sharply to demand and cost in that single product line.

Because intermediate orders tend to fall when vehicle output slows, tracking automotive production is the starting point for reviewing the industry backdrop.

06

Outlook

On March 26, 2026, the company disclosed its corporate value enhancement plan and stated its goal of transforming into an eco-friendly, high-quality automotive materials company.

The same plan put forward improved profitability, higher shareholder value and stronger shareholder returns, and the company said it would increase the predictability of dividends by setting the dividend record date after the dividend amount is decided.

The plan covers both securing a medium- to long-term growth base and improving shareholder returns, and the company intends to operate a sustainable dividend policy. The dividend amount for 2025 was KRW 4.65 billion, up 27.4% from KRW 3.65 billion in 2024, and the 2025 dividend payout ratio was 66.9%.

The company stated that it qualifies as a high-dividend company under the Restriction of Special Taxation Act. At the regular general shareholders' meeting on March 12, 2026, the financial statements for the 63rd fiscal term were approved.

The acquisition of the Gimhae plant was decided at the board meeting on January 12, 2026, and the company specified its own funds and borrowings from financial institutions as the means of financing.

Because cash and cash equivalents were about KRW 4.8 billion at the end of the third quarter of 2025, media reports said the company would have to rely on borrowing to fund the acquisition payment.

Given that operating profit declined consecutively in the second and third quarters of 2026, the next point to check is how financial costs after the plant acquisition will be reflected in the operating margin.

The record date for the 2025 year-end dividend was December 31, so if the same approach is used, December 31, 2026 would be the record date for the 2026 year-end dividend.

Bear factors

Quarterly Profit Slowdown in 2026

Operating profit peaked at KRW 1.71 billion in the first quarter of 2026, then slipped to KRW 1.63 billion in the second quarter and KRW 1.58 billion in the third, a two-quarter decline.

Third-quarter operating profit fell 39.5% year on year, and profit attributable to controlling shareholders fell 50.6% to KRW 1.05 billion. Sales held at KRW 23.6 billion, but the thinner margin is the main bearish factor.

2025 Profit Decline and Sharp Q4 Drop

Profit attributable to controlling shareholders fell 30.8% in 2025 to KRW 6.95 billion from KRW 10 billion in 2024. Fourth-quarter 2025 operating profit dropped 58.0% from the prior quarter to KRW 1.09 billion.

Quarterly profit attributable to controlling shareholders has stayed around KRW 1 billion to KRW 1.5 billion in 2026.

Affiliate Transaction and Funding Burden

The KRW 17.18 billion Gimhae plant purchase has drawn criticism that it could conflict with shareholder interests because it involves buying property from an affiliate.

Cash and equivalents were about KRW 4.8 billion at the end of the third quarter of 2025, so greater borrowing could raise interest costs and weigh on operating profit.

10

Risk factors

Governance and Related-Party Dealings

Real estate dealings with the related company Jinyang Mulsan, combined with the Jinyang Holdings ownership structure, raise the possibility that minority shareholder interests and affiliate interests may diverge.

The company cited the risk of customer attrition from a production halt as the reason, and an accounting firm gave an opinion that the price was fair based on averaged appraisals. Debate over the need for and price of the deal goes beyond what the filings alone resolve.

Dividend Burden Against Earnings

The 66.9% payout ratio for 2025 was high in a year when profit declined. While quarterly profit attributable to controlling shareholders stays around KRW 1 billion, room for dividends may narrow.

The company describes its policy as sustainable, but the scale of returns could be adjusted depending on investment plans and borrowing.

Raw Material and Auto Demand Swings

PU foam is exposed to both petrochemical feedstock costs and vehicle production, so a rise in raw material prices or a slowdown in auto output can squeeze margins at the same time. With sales concentrated in one product, demand changes in that product feed straight into overall results. Weaker pricing power from customer concentration is another factor to monitor.
