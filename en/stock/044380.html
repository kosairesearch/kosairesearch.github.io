02

Business structure

Jooyontech is a first-generation PC manufacturer in Korea, founded in 1988, that sells desktops, monitors and TVs through business (B2B) and consumer (B2C) channels.

The PC business is the core, and the company said first-half 2026 revenue growth was led by monitor growth, stabilizing desktop brand PC sales and higher TV sales.

In domestic retail, the adoption of Coupang Rocket Delivery and the Naver brand store was cited as a driver of improved results in the first nine months of 2025.

The product lineup has widened to include a gaming monitor (launched June 2026), a wired membrane keyboard 'Geekstar GK03 Pro' (launched June 2026), Korea's first Pokemon PC (September 2025) and AI-solution-equipped PCs (October 2025).

By 2025, domestic sales accounted for 100% of major products such as desktop PCs and monitors, so export dependence is low.

Major contracts include a KRW 4.43 billion office desktop supply project with the Navy, confirmed on October 6, 2026, and a KRW 2.16 billion PC and monitor supply deal with a commercial counterparty signed on June 17, 2026.

Demand in the finished-PC market moves with replacement cycles and sales channels, and the company runs gaming and business desktop lines side by side while expanding online distribution. In 2026 it is also preparing to add LED signage and digital signage businesses.

04

Earnings analysis

On a disclosed annual basis, revenue fell from KRW 66.7 billion in 2022 to KRW 43.5 billion in 2023, a 34.7% decline, and then rose 22.0% to KRW 53.1 billion in 2024 and 18.0% to KRW 62.6 billion in 2025, two consecutive years of growth. 2022 was the largest year in the 2022 to 2025 period.

Operating loss was KRW 6.29 billion in 2022, KRW 4.66 billion in 2023, KRW 2.9 billion in 2024 and KRW 1.68 billion in 2025, four consecutive years of operating losses in which the loss shrank each year. Operating margin moved from -9.4% in 2022 to -10.7% in 2023, then improved to -5.5% in 2024 and -2.7% in 2025.

Net loss attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 5.64 billion in 2022, KRW 4.44 billion in 2023, KRW 2.63 billion in 2024 and KRW 1.72 billion in 2025, and the net loss including non-controlling interests was KRW 1.75 billion in 2025.

The company attributed the narrower losses in 2024 and 2025, when sales were rising, to cost control and lower selling and administrative expenses. Operating cash flow went from KRW 3.62 billion in 2022 to negative KRW 856 million in 2023 and negative KRW 2.61 billion in 2024, then turned to KRW 1.99 billion in 2025.

The debt ratio stayed at a low level, falling from 28.2% in 2022 to 21.7% in 2025.

Quarterly revenue peaked at KRW 18.8 billion in Q3 2025, fell to KRW 13.7 billion in Q4 2025 (down 27.2% from the prior quarter), recovered to KRW 17.1 billion in Q1 2026 (up 25.2%), and then declined for two straight quarters to KRW 14.8 billion in Q2 2026 (down 13.7%) and KRW 13 billion in Q3 2026 (down 12.0%).

Operating profit was KRW 414 million in Q1 2026, a swing to profit, then KRW 99.51 million in Q2 2026 (down 75.9% from the prior quarter), and Q3 2026 returned to an operating loss of KRW 162 million.

Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 621 million in Q1 2026 and KRW 296 million in Q2 2026, but Q3 2026 showed a net loss of KRW 498 million, with the loss widening year on year.

Over the last four quarters, net profit attributable to controlling shareholders totaled KRW 81.25 million, which is too small to show a steady profit trend yet.

05

Industry analysis

Reports in 2026 cite a weak PC and monitor market. A June 2026 report linked weak industry conditions and tighter listing requirements to a deterioration in the operating environment. On the demand side, expanding AI PC demand was presented as a direction for finding new customers.

Traditional Korean hardware makers are diversifying for long-term competitiveness, and Jooyontech is extending into LED signage, digital signage and keyboards.

Component-focused peers such as Samsung Electro-Mechanics and LG Innotek depend more on component price cycles, while Jooyontech's finished-PC focus makes it more directly exposed to changes in consumer and procurement demand.

The current position in the industry cycle can be gauged from the 2026 reports of weak conditions and the quarterly revenue decline. As a small-cap company, Jooyontech differentiates through gaming and business product lines and channel diversification rather than scale.

Because all sales were domestic in 2025, domestic procurement and distribution activity affects results more than overseas demand.

On capital, CEO Jang Ji-hwan said in a late May 2026 report that a rights offering to shareholders aimed at maintaining market capitalization is not under consideration. Shifts in ownership and possible capital raising

Business concentration and earnings base
