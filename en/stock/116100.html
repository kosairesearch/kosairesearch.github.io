02

Business structure

Sun Machinery was founded in May 1990 and listed on KONEX on July 1, 2013 as a maker of new automotive parts. Under Korean industry classification it falls under new engine parts manufacturing for automobiles, and the company is introduced as a Tier 1 supplier to Hyundai Motor and Kia.

Its core products are stabilizer cap mountings and bracket parts, and steady supply and quality gains in this line are cited as a driver of sales growth. The company's profile lists ISO/TS 16949 and ISO 14001 certifications.

The Wanju plant disclosed in September 2026 is meant to support mass production of parts for Hyundai Motor's LT2 one-ton eco-commercial truck project and to expand capacity. As of end-2025 the company had 88 employees and is classified as a small and mid-sized enterprise.

Paid-in capital is KRW 3.71 billion and total assets are KRW 43,085,212 thousand, which is small against large parts makers. Peers in the same sector include Hyundai Mobis and Hyundai WIA among large players, and Daewonkangup, Seoyon E-Hwa and Sungwoo Hitech among mid-sized suppliers. Its position within the OEM Tier 1 structure implies high customer concentration.

03

Recent trends

Sun Machinery's 2025 separate revenue was KRW 36,078,962 thousand, up 2.67% from KRW 35,139,146 thousand in 2024. Operating profit over the same period was KRW 1,956,244 thousand, down 5.85%, and the operating margin slipped from 5.91% in 2024 to 5.42%.

Net income reached KRW 3,244,386 thousand, versus KRW 788,714 thousand a year earlier, an increase of 311.35%. Higher non-operating income, which rose to KRW 1,466,445 thousand from KRW 604,295 thousand, and a negative income tax expense line both fed into the net income gain.

The equity ratio fell to 34.58% from 37.70%, while borrowing dependence rose to 54.36% from 47.38%. Operating cash flow was KRW 2,281,218 thousand in 2025, while investing cash flow was an outflow of KRW 3,565,763 thousand.

Paid-in capital increased from KRW 3,119,993 thousand at end-2024 to KRW 3,719,993 thousand at end-2025. The share price moved from KRW 648 on March 30, 2026 to KRW 617 intraday on September 23, after a 14.68% gain on the day, then reached KRW 630 on October 16, KRW 650 on October 26, and KRW 671 on November 9. The current price on November 11, 2026 is KRW 701, with trading value of KRW 6,714,411.

04

Outlook

In the near term, the most concrete checkpoint is progress at the Wanju plant, with completion scheduled for May 31, 2027. The KRW 8 billion investment equals 53.70% of year-end 2025 equity and is planned to be funded with internal cash and bank borrowings.

Higher borrowing feeds directly into borrowing dependence and interest burden, so the funding structure is a key variable. On the revenue side, whether eco-commercial vehicle parts reach full-scale mass production and lift sales is the central indicator.

Whether the operating margin holds at or above the 5.42% recorded in 2025 indicates the efficiency of the business.

Because tax effects and non-operating items lifted 2025 net income, the persistence of that profit depends on whether those items recur, and tracking them separately from operating profit is the accurate way to read the figures.

Hyundai Motor's eco-commercial vehicle sales and production plans are the external variable that determines the size of the capacity benefit. Open-market purchases by the CEO and a slight decline in the controlling shareholder's stake have been disclosed in succession since October 2026.

Bull factors

Capacity build-out for eco-commercial parts

Separate-basis revenue rose 11.2% in 2024, with eco-friendly vehicle part orders and steady supply of stabilizer cap mountings and bracket products cited as drivers.

The Wanju plant disclosed on September 23, 2026 is intended to support mass production of LT2 one-ton truck parts for Hyundai Motor and to expand capacity. With completion scheduled for May 31, 2027, the output gains will show up in results only after the plant enters operation.

Net income growth

Return on equity for 2025 was 20.32%, up from 4.52% in 2024, and the net profit margin on sales was 8.99%. Net income of KRW 3,244,386 thousand was up 311.35% from the prior year. However, this figure reflects higher non-operating income and a negative tax expense line, so it is separate from operating profitability. The operating margin of 5.42% was lower than the prior year.

Open-market purchases by the CEO

CEO Park Kyung-hee made five open-market purchases between October 19 and 23, 2026, lifting holdings to 607,649 shares, and a further purchase on November 2 brought the total to 624,587 shares.

Over the same period, the de facto controlling shareholder's holding fell to 1,874,782 shares, or 25.20%, as of November 6, 2026. These are disclosed facts.

06

Bear factors

Operating profit decline and thin margin

Revenue grew 2.67% in 2025, yet operating profit fell 5.85% to KRW 1,956,244 thousand. The operating margin declined from 5.91% in 2024 to 5.42%. Operating profit fell even as sales rose, which points to cost structure as an area that needs closer tracking. A margin that stays thin would also slow the payback on the capacity investment.

Higher borrowing and limited interest coverage

Borrowing dependence rose to 54.36% at end-2025 from 47.38% in 2024, and the equity ratio dropped to 34.58%. The debt ratio stood at 189.20%, indicating a debt-heavy balance sheet relative to equity. Interest coverage is also low, so operating profit does not comfortably cover interest expense. If part of the plant investment is funded by borrowing, this burden would increase.

Thin liquidity in a small KONEX listing

Sun Machinery is a small listing on KONEX with a market capitalization of KRW 5.2 billion. At the current price of KRW 701, trading value is KRW 6,714,411, which points to thin trading. In a market like this, a small number of trades can move the share price sharply. The 14.68% intraday gain on September 23, 2026 illustrates this pattern.

07

Risk factors

Macro

Interest rates and currency moves directly affect interest burden and raw material costs for a company with high borrowing. Prolonged high rates would further narrow cash flow headroom for a business with low interest coverage. A weaker won could raise imported material costs and pressure margins.

Industry

Because the company sits as a Tier 1 supplier, results track Hyundai Motor and Kia production plans and model mix. Any delay in mass production for eco-commercial vehicles would also push back the operating benefit of the Wanju plant. Intensifying competition with large and mid-sized peers could affect pricing negotiations.

Market

KONEX-listed companies typically have limited float and trading volume, which leads to sharp price swings. Ownership changes by the largest shareholder and the CEO are visible through filings, but thin trading may limit how the market responds to them. Interpretation of the disclosures may also remain concentrated among a narrow group of market participants.

08

Overall view

Sun Machinery is a company where capacity investment for Hyundai Motor's eco-commercial vehicle parts coincides with a 2025 net income rebound. Revenue edged up in 2025, but operating profit and operating margin fell, so the direction of core profitability is mixed.

The net income gain reflected tax effects and higher non-operating income together, so it is recorded separately from operating strength. On the balance sheet, borrowing dependence and the debt ratio are high and interest coverage is low, so the impact of funding the plant builds up over time.

On the ownership side, the CEO's open-market purchases and a slight decline in the controlling shareholder's stake were disclosed over the same period. Thin trading on KONEX means the share price can move on small volumes.

Overall, the business base and the expansion plan are confirmed, while the durability of profits and the financial burden remain open questions. This is a qualitative summary provided for information purposes.
