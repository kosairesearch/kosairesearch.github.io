02

Business structure

Daebo Magnetic was founded in 1976 and makes electromagnetic iron separators (EMF) that remove iron contaminants from battery materials and process streams. Its 2022 revenue mix was 93.2% EMF, 3.6% secondary-battery materials and 3.1% other products such as magnetic separators, according to a Korean media report.

EMF systems come in dry and wet types. Dry units separate powder in lithium mining and precursor, cathode and anode lines, while wet units treat slurry on cell lines. The same report said about 90% of 2022 EMF revenue came from dry systems.

Exports dominated, at 87.8% of 2022 sales, with China accounting for more than 70% of total revenue. Its customers are battery material and cell makers, and a 2022 brokerage report named Samsung SDI and BYD among them.

One report said the unit price is under KRW 100 million, which limits the number of competitors, while another described a market shared with Japanese rivals. The battery material processing business was effectively halted after a contract termination, and its staff was reduced to zero by the end of March 2026.

Equipment-segment sales fell from KRW 34.9 billion in 2023 to KRW 26.1 billion in 2025. Headcount declined from 107 at the end of 2024 to 96 at the end of 2025 and 70 at the end of March 2026.

04

Earnings analysis

On a reported basis, revenue went from KRW 108.6 billion in 2022 to KRW 40 billion in 2023 (down 63.2% year on year), KRW 28.4 billion in 2024 (down 29.0%) and KRW 26.1 billion in 2025 (down 7.9%). Among 2022 to 2025, 2022 was the largest year for sales and 2025 the smallest.

Operating profit fell from KRW 26.5 billion in 2022 to KRW 127 million in 2023 (down 99.5%), then turned into an operating loss of KRW 9.8 billion in 2024. The operating loss widened to KRW 10.4 billion in 2025. Operating margin moved from 24.4% in 2022 to 0.3% in 2023, -34.5% in 2024 and -39.9% in 2025.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders went from KRW 21.9 billion in 2022 to net losses of KRW 8.2 billion in 2023, KRW 15.8 billion in 2024 and KRW 35.5 billion in 2025. Total net income, including non-controlling interests, followed the same direction.

At the end of 2025, total equity was KRW 18 billion, all of it attributable to controlling shareholders, against total liabilities of KRW 29.5 billion, and the debt ratio had risen from 26.7% in 2022 to 164.2%.

Operating cash flow went from KRW 14.2 billion in 2022 to negative KRW 186 million in 2023 and negative KRW 903 million in 2024, then turned positive at KRW 1.32 billion in 2025.

On a quarterly basis, revenue fell from KRW 5.76 billion in the third quarter of 2025 to KRW 4.61 billion in the fourth quarter (down 20.1% from the prior quarter) and bottomed at KRW 1.14 billion in the first quarter of 2026 (down 75.3%).

Revenue rebounded to KRW 2.31 billion in the second quarter (up 102.7%) before falling to KRW 1.58 billion in the third quarter (down 31.7% from the prior quarter and down 72.7% from a year earlier).

Quarterly operating losses widened from KRW 1.88 billion in the third quarter of 2025 to KRW 4.53 billion in the fourth quarter, then narrowed to KRW 1.42 billion in the third quarter of 2026, with five consecutive quarters of operating losses from the third quarter of 2025 through the third quarter of 2026.

The fourth-quarter 2025 net loss attributable to controlling shareholders, at KRW 29.6 billion, was far larger than that quarter's operating loss of KRW 4.53 billion, which suggests non-operating or one-off items.

A March 2026 filing summary said the company recognized fixed-asset impairment and inventory valuation losses. The net loss attributable to controlling shareholders over the last four quarters, from the fourth quarter of 2025 through the third quarter of 2026, totaled KRW 35.6 billion.

05

Industry analysis

Downstream demand depends on capital spending in secondary-battery production. A prolonged slowdown in electric vehicle demand has delayed or scaled back battery process investments, and a Korean media report linked this to the sharp drop in demand for EMF.

The company also attributed its first-quarter 2026 results to a weaker downstream industry that changed its new-order environment. The iron-removal step itself is needed for battery safety and yield, according to a 2022 brokerage report, because removing metal foreign matter can reduce fire risk and improve yield.

The market is concentrated among a few suppliers. The Korea IR Council estimated the company's share of the domestic EMF market at more than 80% and of the Chinese market at around 70%, although these are estimates.

A brokerage described wet EMF as close to a monopoly in the global market, while dry EMF is reported to be shared with Japanese competitors. Chinese demand has tracked the pace of battery capacity additions, and with exports at 87.8% of 2022 sales, Chinese investment speed is a central variable.

Expansion into materials, which brokers had expected in 2022, has effectively stopped by 2026. On the cycle, order declines show up first and revenue recognition follows with a lag.

The bullish factor is the structural need for iron removal in battery safety, while the bearish factor is that slower EV demand and cuts in customer investment appeared at the same time.

06

Outlook

The company attributed the first-quarter 2026 revenue decline to its revenue recognition standard. Revenue is recorded when installation and commissioning are complete, so timing depends on project schedules, according to a June 2026 media report.

The same report said the company expects revenue recognition to become visible as commissioning and acceptance results come in. The second-quarter 2026 rise to KRW 2.31 billion appears linked to this timing. However, third-quarter revenue fell back to KRW 1.58 billion, so the durability of the rebound remains unproven.

The materials business was effectively halted after a contract termination, which narrows future revenue to the equipment business. Litigation is another burden.

Lawsuits in which the company is a defendant, including claims over payment for goods and recovery of payments, total KRW 15.7 billion according to a media report, and both were in first-instance trial as of June 2026. A DART filing summary from August 2026 said the plaintiff's claims in one suit were fully dismissed.

Headcount fell to 70 at the end of March 2026, so it is worth checking whether staffing for installation and commissioning would be adequate if equipment orders recover.

The next checkpoints are whether fourth-quarter 2026 revenue reflects completed commissioning and what operating margin and debt ratio appear in the 2026 annual business report.

Bull factors

Signs of a quarterly sales rebound

Quarterly revenue rose 102.7% from the prior quarter to KRW 2.31 billion in the second quarter of 2026. The company said revenue is recognized at installation and completion of commissioning, so acceptance results on ongoing projects could add to reported sales. Whether the first-quarter trough holds is the starting point of this bullish case.

Structural demand for iron removal in batteries

Removing metal contaminants in battery production affects fire risk and yield. A 2022 brokerage report described demand tied to this process as firm. With EMF at 93.2% of 2022 revenue, a recovery in demand would flow most directly into reported results.

Narrowing operating losses

Operating loss was KRW 1.42 billion in the third quarter of 2026, narrower than a year earlier. Quarterly operating loss had reached KRW 4.53 billion in the fourth quarter of 2025. Operating cash flow also turned positive at KRW 1.32 billion in 2025.

Bear factors

Three straight years of sales decline

Revenue fell from KRW 40 billion in 2023 to KRW 28.4 billion in 2024 and KRW 26.1 billion in 2025. Quarterly sales dropped to KRW 1.14 billion in the first quarter of 2026, down 75.3% from the prior quarter.

Equipment-segment sales, the core business, fell from KRW 34.9 billion in 2023 to KRW 26.1 billion in 2025 according to a media report.

Accumulated losses and balance-sheet strain

Attributable net losses continued for three straight years from 2023 through 2025, with the 2025 loss widening to KRW 35.5 billion. At the end of 2025, total equity was KRW 18 billion against total liabilities of KRW 29.5 billion, a debt ratio of 164.2%. The KRW 35.6 billion attributable net loss over the last four quarters is large relative to that equity base.

Litigation and shrinking business scope

Lawsuits in which the company is a defendant total KRW 15.7 billion according to a media report, with first-instance proceedings ongoing as of June 2026. The battery material processing business was effectively halted after a contract termination, and its staff was reduced to zero. With diversification gone, revenue depends more heavily on equipment demand.

Risk factors

Customer investment cycle

Delays or cuts to customer investment projects push back equipment orders and revenue recognition. The company itself cited a weaker downstream market as a factor behind the decline. With exports at a high share of sales, the pace of Chinese battery capacity additions is a key variable.

Litigation outcome

Lawsuits totaling KRW 15.7 billion compare with total equity of KRW 18 billion at the end of 2025. One suit was dismissed in August 2026 according to a DART filing summary. Other proceedings were reported as still in first-instance trial.

Balance sheet and cash flow

Debt has grown faster than equity, which has increased the financial burden. Operating cash flow was negative in 2023 and 2024 before turning positive in 2025. Tighter funding conditions could constrain capital spending and working capital.
