02

Business structure

Dasan Solueta was founded in 2003 and joined the Dasan Group in 2015, and its largest shareholder changed to Dasan Invest in 2021. In 2024 it sold its stake in a subsidiary, removing the automotive parts unit, so it now runs a single electromagnetic shielding materials business.

It exited the MB filter business for medical masks in 2023 and sold its Vietnam production unit, narrowing its scope to shielding materials. Its main products are conductive cushions and conductive tapes.

One jobs-information site lists conductive cushions at 51–53% and conductive tapes at 36–41% as of March 2026, and the sources differ on these shares. Export shares at the end of 2024 were 97.14% for conductive cushions and 70.01% for conductive tapes, so overseas sales are a large part of the mix.

R&D spending in 2024 was about KRW 2.1 billion, or 5.93% of revenue. Reporting cites weaker demand from smart-device manufacturers and the loss of control over a subsidiary as factors behind the sales decline.

Coverage says the company will focus on widening its customer base, since shielding materials can be used across many electronic devices. Press reports also point to intensifying competition in the shielding materials market.

Because the business now rests on one segment, changes in product mix feed directly into reported results. Headcount was 101 as of the end of June 2026.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual revenue rose from KRW 157.9 billion in 2022 to KRW 158.8 billion in 2023 (up 0.6% year on year), then fell to KRW 35.4 billion in 2024 and KRW 31.4 billion in 2025, two straight years of decline. The 2024 drop of 77.7% mainly reflects the subsidiary stake sale that removed the automotive parts unit.

Operating profit moved from KRW 5.67 billion in 2022 to an operating loss of KRW 149 million in 2023, then to KRW 2 billion in 2024 and KRW 1.71 billion in 2025 (down 14.2%). The operating margin stayed in the 5% range in 2024 (5.6%) and 2025 (5.5%) even as the revenue base shrank.

Controlling-shareholder net income reached KRW 25 billion in 2023, far above the operating loss, while 2024 showed a controlling-shareholder net loss of KRW 19.9 billion despite KRW 2 billion of operating profit, suggesting non-operating items carried significant weight.

The company attributed the 2025 turnaround in net profit to higher non-operating income from selling an associate stake and a swing in equity-method results. The 2025 profit therefore appears to rest more on stake sales and equity-method gains than on core operations.

The debt ratio fell from 277.2% in 2022 to 56.5% in 2025, and operating cash flow stayed positive at KRW 1.53 billion in 2024 and KRW 1.51 billion in 2025.

Quarterly results bottomed in the fourth quarter of 2025, with revenue of KRW 6.17 billion and an operating loss of KRW 972 million, then recovered through operating profit of KRW 3.8 million in the first quarter of 2026 to revenue of KRW 9.05 billion and operating profit of KRW 1.09 billion in the third quarter.

However, controlling-shareholder net loss turned to KRW 9.18 billion in the fourth quarter of 2025, the last four quarters total a controlling-shareholder net loss of KRW 2.68 billion, and controlling-shareholder net income for Q1–Q3 2026 is not yet in the reported table.

A September 7, 2026 tally showed first-half 2026 standalone revenue down 19.8% and operating profit down 89.5% year on year. Viewed on its own, the third quarter posted revenue up 4.0% and operating profit up 148.9% year on year, so decline and recovery signals coexist.

05

Industry analysis

Electromagnetic shielding materials reduce electromagnetic interference inside electronic devices, and demand depends on the new-product cycles and component specifications of device makers such as smartphone and laptop manufacturers.

In the first half of 2026, a slowdown in smartphone and electronics demand was cited as a reason for weaker core-business results. The same reporting also pointed to intensifying competition in the shielding materials market.

Before 2024 the company ran adjacent businesses such as automotive parts, but it now rests on one shielding materials segment, which raises its exposure to device-cycle demand.

A high overseas sales share, with conductive cushion exports at 97.14% at the end of 2024, makes the business sensitive to overseas device demand and export conditions. Expansion room is discussed because shielding materials can be applied across many electronic devices.

Compared with larger component makers in the sector, the company is small in both sales and market value, so customer concentration and pricing power are likely to shape its competitive position.

Revenue recovered from the first-quarter 2026 low through the third quarter, but reported results alone cannot separate whether that recovery reflects a device-cycle upturn or shifts in customer volumes.

Outlook

The company says it is working to grow its core business and improve profitability, but it has not publicly disclosed concrete plans.

At its March 2026 annual general meeting, shareholders approved amendments adding automotive parts and electrical components manufacturing, contract processing and sales, and solar power generation to the business purposes, and the company cited new-business expansion as the reason.

Whether these purposes translate into revenue is not yet visible in the segment figures of the reported results.

In June 2026 the company decided on a third-party allotment rights offering of 3,665,520 common shares with an issue amount of KRW 3.2 billion, with payment on June 10, and the proceeds were disclosed as operating funds.

In the same month, the remaining KRW 5 billion of the 8th convertible bond series was fully redeemed at maturity. A 10%-plus shareholder reported an on-market purchase of 46,939 common shares on October 30, 2026, and the holding ratio in the filing stands at 28.83%.

The next checkpoint in the periodic reports is whether fourth-quarter 2026 results extend the third-quarter recovery.

Three straight quarters of revenue growth and two straight quarters of operating profit growth would build the case for sustained core-business recovery, but the first-half 2026 decline is large, so the full-year picture will be judged from the 2026 annual report.

Bull factors

Quarterly recovery

Revenue has risen for three straight quarters since the first quarter of 2026, and operating profit has grown for two straight quarters, from KRW 3.8 million in Q1 to KRW 232 million in Q2 and KRW 1.09 billion in Q3. Third-quarter revenue of KRW 9.05 billion is the highest of the last five reported quarters. The direction of core-operating profit is the main bull argument.

Stronger balance sheet

The debt ratio fell from 277.2% in 2022 to 56.5% in 2025, with total liabilities of KRW 29.7 billion and total equity of KRW 52.6 billion at the end of 2025. The remaining KRW 5 billion of the 8th convertible bond series was redeemed at maturity in June 2026, reducing convertible-bond exposure. Improved financial stability is a bull factor.

Export-heavy product mix

Conductive cushions and conductive tapes had export shares of 97.14% and 70.01%, respectively, at the end of 2024, so the product mix absorbs overseas demand. Coverage notes room to extend applications across more electronic devices. Operating cash flow stayed positive in 2025, which points to core-business cash generation.

Bear factors

Continued shrinkage in revenue

Revenue fell to KRW 35.4 billion in 2024, down 77.7%, and to KRW 31.4 billion in 2025, down 11.4%, two straight years of decline. First-half 2026 standalone revenue was reported down 19.8% year on year. A smaller top line since the subsidiary exits is the core bear argument.

Loss quarters and cumulative net losses

In the fourth quarter of 2025 the company posted an operating loss of KRW 972 million and a controlling-shareholder net loss of KRW 9.18 billion, turning to losses. The last four quarters total a controlling-shareholder net loss of KRW 2.68 billion. First-quarter 2026 operating profit of KRW 3.8 million left a thin profit base.

Demand slowdown and rising competition

Press coverage cites a slowdown in smartphone and electronics demand and intensifying competition in shielding materials as causes of weak core results. One tally put first-quarter 2026 operating profit down 99.7% year on year. With a single business, there is no other segment to offset a downturn in device demand.

Risk factors

Listing requirements

A May 2026 report said market capitalization was below the KRW 20 billion level cited as a second-half delisting threshold, and that regulators are tightening exit standards for companies with insufficient market value. Tighter management rules for long-term low-priced stocks were also reported to be coming. The report did not specify when these standards will apply.

Single business and demand concentration

After the automotive parts unit was removed, shielding materials is effectively the only business. Demand changes at smart-device manufacturers were cited as a factor in the sales decline. A shift in demand for this single product family can translate directly into earnings swings.

Capital changes and ownership

A third-party rights offering and convertible bond redemption took place in June 2026, and an on-market purchase by a 10%-plus shareholder was reported in October 2026. Analysts noted that the subsidiary stake sales, used to address put-option exposure, came at the cost of a sharp drop in consolidated sales. Continued capital raising and share movements could keep the shareholder base changing.

Overall view

Dasan Solueta has sold stakes in subsidiaries and now operates as a single electromagnetic shielding materials business. Revenue has declined since 2024 and fell for two straight years through 2025. The company attributed the 2025 return to profit mainly to non-operating factors such as the sale of an associate stake.

Third-quarter 2026 revenue of KRW 9.05 billion and operating profit of KRW 1.09 billion show improvement on a quarterly basis. However, the controlling-shareholder net loss of KRW 2.68 billion over the last four quarters and the reported first-half 2026 decline mean the durability of the recovery is still being tested.

Bull factors are the improved balance sheet with a debt ratio of 56.5%, positive operating cash flow, and the third-quarter rebound. Bear factors are the demand slowdown, rising competition, listing-threshold pressure tied to market capitalization, and the single-business structure.

The next checkpoints are the 2026 annual report's full-year operating profit and controlling-shareholder net income, and the Q1 2027 quarterly report.
