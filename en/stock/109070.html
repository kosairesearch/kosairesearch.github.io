02

Business structure

Joosung is classified as a telecom and broadcasting equipment maker, but most of its actual revenue now comes from logistics. After BNP Jusung became the largest shareholder in December 2023, the business shifted quickly toward logistics.

The share of composite logistics brokerage revenue rose from 0% in 2023 to 76.7% in 2024 and passed 80% in 2025, according to a Dealsite report.

The logistics business is freight forwarding that arranges sea and air import and export cargo transport, and the company began its own logistics operations in 2024 using the experience of its largest shareholder's side.

Under a business transfer agreement signed in July 2026, Joosung agreed to acquire the international multimodal transport brokerage division of BNP Jusung for KRW 20 billion. The target division had annual revenue of KRW 182.12 million and accounted for 21.32% of Joosung's total revenue.

On the telecom side, the company commercialized the world's first ICS (interference cancellation system) repeater and has supplied major Japanese carriers for more than 15 years.

It has recently secured a coaxial distribution equipment business from NTT DOCOMO in Japan and a 5G medium-sized repeater business from LG Uplus, and analysts expect repeater sales to expand.

In optical communications, the company is increasing supply of high-performance optical transmission solutions tied to AI data center expansion. In logistics, the group affiliates Jusung C&Air and BNP Jusung also run composite logistics brokerage, so intra-group businesses overlap. Telecom customers are concentrated among major carriers at home and abroad.

04

Earnings analysis

On a reported basis, annual revenue fell 16.8% from KRW 28.8 billion in 2022 to KRW 23.9 billion in 2023, then rose sharply to KRW 68.8 billion in 2024 (up 187.5% year on year). Revenue reached KRW 85.4 billion in 2025, up 24.0%.

Revenue has grown for two consecutive years from the 2023 low through 2025, and 2025 was the largest year in the 2022–2025 period. Operating losses of KRW 4.81 billion in 2022 and KRW 1.34 billion in 2023 narrowed, and the company returned to operating profit of KRW 3.76 billion in 2024.

However, 2025 operating profit fell 15.9% to KRW 3.17 billion, so higher sales did not lift profit. The operating margin improved from -16.7% in 2022 to 5.5% in 2024 and then slipped to 3.7% in 2025.

Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders fell 55.7% to KRW 2.04 billion in 2025, and net profit including non-controlling interests was also KRW 2.04 billion. Operating cash flow turned negative at KRW -4.82 billion in 2025 after KRW 2.1 billion in 2024.

By quarter, the fourth quarter of 2025 posted revenue of KRW 26.4 billion and operating profit of KRW 1.29 billion, up 891.5% from the prior quarter. The first quarter of 2026 brought revenue of KRW 17.6 billion, down 33.5% from the prior quarter, and an operating loss of KRW 265 million.

The second quarter of 2026 had revenue of KRW 24.3 billion and an operating loss of KRW 267 million, with losses widening. The third quarter of 2026 brought revenue of KRW 51.5 billion, up 112.2% from the prior quarter, and operating profit of KRW 2.92 billion, a return to profit.

Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders for that quarter was KRW 244 million, down 53.5% from the prior quarter. A news report said financial income weighed heavily on first-quarter net profit, so core operating profit and non-operating items should be read separately.

A news report also said first-quarter 2026 logistics operating profit fell from KRW 1.268 billion a year earlier to KRW 28 million, suggesting logistics margin pressure is a key swing factor. Dealsite reported that first-half 2026 cost of sales rose more than 10%, cutting the gross margin from 12.5% to 6.2%.

05

Industry analysis

Logistics is strongly affected by the freight forwarding cycle and shipping rates. In April 2026, the failure of the first US–Iran ceasefire talks raised Middle East tensions, and Joosung's share price surged on expectations of higher sea freight rates and cargo concentration.

Expected freight gains and actual logistics margins are separate matters, though. A news report said logistics revenue in the first quarter of 2026 fell 15.3% to KRW 13.3 billion and operating profit dropped to KRW 28 million.

In telecom, analysts point to LTE replacement demand following the shutdown of Japan's 3G service. Optical communications is tied to rising demand for high-performance optical transmission solutions as AI data centers expand.

In Korea, the company is extending its 5G private network and customized frequency solutions toward smart factory and industrial infrastructure.

In freight forwarding, the company operates composite logistics brokerage alongside two group affiliates, and its differentiation from independent forwarders is described as rooted in intra-group cargo volume and overseas networks.

In short, the logistics segment depends on freight rates and cargo volume, while the telecom and optical segments depend on large business licenses and AI infrastructure investment.

06

Outlook

Three confirmed plans stand out. First, the acquisition of the international multimodal transport brokerage division from largest shareholder BNP Jusung adds a business with annual revenue of KRW 182.12 million.

Samduk Accounting Corporation valued the division at up to KRW 23.688 billion using a discounted cash flow method and assessed the KRW 20 billion transaction price as appropriate, according to a Numbers report.

Second, the funding is structured as KRW 30 billion of convertible bonds with a 0% coupon and 0% maturity yield, a KRW 15 billion private placement to the largest shareholder, and KRW 5 billion of short-term borrowings. The 12,406,948 new shares from the private placement are subject to a one-year lock-up from listing.

Third, the company said the 5-for-1 share consolidation is meant to keep the share count from ballooning as new shares are issued, according to the same Numbers report.

A Nate report said full conversion of the bonds would issue 20,325,203 shares based on pre-consolidation shares, and combined with the private placement shares this would exceed 60% of the shares then outstanding.

The company expects profitability to improve if it cuts overlapping costs and expands cargo volume after the acquisition. Some analysts, however, warn that a simple combination could add only funding burden and share dilution, according to the same Nate report.

The next confirmation points are therefore the operating margin of the logistics segment after the acquisition, the actual use of the raised funds, and the full-year operating margin in the 2026 annual report.

08

Bull factors

Logistics-driven revenue expansion

Revenue reached KRW 85.4 billion in 2025, up 24.0% year on year, and grew for two consecutive years after the 2024 return to profit. Composite logistics brokerage now exceeds 80% of sales, establishing logistics as the earnings base. Consolidating the logistics business agreed in July 2026 would add annual revenue of KRW 182.12 million.

Return to quarterly operating profit

Third-quarter 2026 operating profit was KRW 2.92 billion, a return to profit and up 2,149.3% from a year earlier. Third-quarter revenue of KRW 51.5 billion rose 112.2% from the prior quarter. The operating margin reached 5.5% in 2024, which is cited as evidence that margin recovery is possible.

Telecom business licenses and optical demand

Joosung secured a coaxial distribution equipment business from NTT DOCOMO and a 5G medium-sized repeater business from LG Uplus, and this is seen as expanding repeater sales. LTE replacement demand following Japan's 3G shutdown is also cited.

Rising demand for high-performance optical transmission solutions tied to AI data centers adds another growth driver.

09

Bear factors

Thin margins and volatile profit

The operating margin fell to 3.7% in 2025 from 5.5% in 2024. Operating losses continued in the first and second quarters of 2026, and a Dealsite report said the first-half 2026 gross margin fell from 12.5% to 6.2%.

Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders fell 53.5% in the third quarter from the prior quarter, leaving a four-quarter total of KRW 2.28 billion.

Sharp drop in logistics profitability

According to a news report, first-quarter 2026 logistics revenue fell 15.3% to KRW 13.3 billion, and operating profit dropped from KRW 1.268 billion to KRW 28 million. Revenue growth has repeatedly come with rising costs that did not reach profit. Because logistics exceeds 80% of sales, freight rate and cargo volume swings affect total results directly.

Funding needs and dilution

Operating cash flow turned negative at KRW -4.82 billion in 2025.

The company is pursuing KRW 30 billion of convertible bonds, a KRW 15 billion private placement and KRW 5 billion of short-term borrowings, and a news report said conversion and new shares combined could exceed 60% of shares outstanding on a pre-consolidation basis. Liquidity falls after the transfer payment, so the use of the raised funds matters.

10

Risk factors

Dilution and share supply

The 12,406,948 new shares from the private placement equal about 23.5% of shares outstanding before the issue. Conversion of the bonds would add further supply. Because the new shares are locked up for one year from listing, the lock-up expiry is the point at which supply would expand.

Freight rates and geopolitical swings

Logistics profitability is highly sensitive to sea freight rates and cargo volume. Middle East tensions once drove sharp moves in the share price on freight-rate expectations, but actual logistics margins fell sharply in the first quarter of 2026. A drop in freight rates or cargo volume could quickly pressure margins.

Financial history and governance

Trading in the company was suspended in 2020 after a disclaimer of audit opinion and capital impairment, and resumed in March 2025 after about five years.

Related-party transactions with largest shareholder BNP Jusung, including the business transfer, private placement and share consolidation, have followed one another. Their fairness and the effects of the acquired business need to be checked against financial disclosures.

12

Overall view

Joosung shifted toward logistics after 2023 and grew revenue to KRW 85.4 billion in 2025 and KRW 51.5 billion in the third quarter of 2026. Profit returned to positive in 2024, fell to KRW 3.17 billion of operating profit in 2025, and turned positive again at KRW 2.92 billion in the third quarter of 2026.

Positive factors include the logistics revenue base, the recovery in quarterly operating profit, telecom business licenses and optical demand. On the other side are thin margins of roughly 3–5%, operating losses in the first and second quarters of 2026, and sharp swings in logistics profitability.

On funding, a plan of about KRW 50 billion is under way alongside potential dilution. Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders over the last four quarters totaled KRW 2.28 billion, a small base.

The next checkpoints are the full-year operating margin in the 2026 annual report, the logistics margin in the first quarter of 2027, and share supply after the lock-up expires.
