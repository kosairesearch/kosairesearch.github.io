02

Business structure

mPlus, founded in 2003 and listed on KOSDAQ in September 2017, makes assembly-process equipment for secondary batteries. Its core offering is turnkey supply of the assembly lines for pouch-type and prismatic cells, and its main products are automated systems for tab welding, packaging, stacking and notching.

According to the company's May 2025 briefing, it holds about 140 patents, has supplied equipment to more than 80 mass-production lines, and cites overall equipment effectiveness (OEE) of 85–90%. By product, assembly equipment accounted for 64.64% of 2024 sales and other items for 35.36%.

Its customer base is anchored on pouch-type lines supplied for SK On, and a KRW 70.9 billion supply contract with the U.S. company Our Next Energy in 2023 was the entry point into prismatic-cell equipment.

The company said prismatic equipment made up about 30% of total orders in 2025 and plans to raise that share to 90% in 2026 and 96% in 2027. Its scope is widening from assembly into electrode and pack processes and into all-solid-state battery assembly lines.

It won solid-state pilot lines from a U.S. company and a domestic conglomerate across 2024–2025, though one securities analyst describes current orders as at the research or pilot stage.

Against peers, mPlus stresses its full turnkey coverage across pouch, prismatic and solid-state formats, and one analyst called its earnings recovery the clearest among domestic equipment makers. It is also pursuing new businesses such as autonomous mobile robots (AIMR) built on its battery-equipment technology.

04

Earnings analysis

On the disclosed annual figures, revenue rose from KRW 116.6 billion in 2022 to KRW 340.1 billion in 2023, a 191.6% increase, making 2023 the largest year of 2022–2025.

Revenue fell 62.1% to KRW 128.7 billion in 2024, and a securities report attributed the decline to customers adjusting investment cycles and delaying equipment deliveries. Revenue then rose 43.1% to KRW 184.2 billion in 2025.

Operating results moved from an operating loss of KRW 9.86 billion in 2022 to operating profit of KRW 23.9 billion in 2023, a turn to profit, then KRW 10.1 billion in 2024 (down 57.7%) and KRW 24.6 billion in 2025 (up 143.4%). The operating margin went from -8.5% in 2022 to 7.0% in 2023, 7.8% in 2024 and 13.3% in 2025.

Analysts linked the higher 2024 margin despite lower sales to the company avoiding low-priced orders.

Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 20 billion in 2023, KRW 11 billion in 2024 (down 45.1%) and KRW 20.6 billion in 2025 (up 87.9%), while net profit including non-controlling interests was reported at the same levels for each year.

In 2026 the first quarter brought revenue of KRW 51 billion, operating profit of KRW 10.2 billion (up 249.3% from the prior quarter) and controlling-shareholder net profit of KRW 11.8 billion. The second quarter eased to revenue of KRW 32 billion (down 37.2% on the quarter) and operating profit of KRW 4.04 billion.

In the third quarter revenue was KRW 35.9 billion (up 12.1% on the quarter, down 59.2% year on year), and the company posted an operating loss of KRW 4.48 billion and a net loss attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 9.62 billion.

Revenue had already fallen from KRW 88 billion in the third quarter of 2025 to KRW 13.4 billion in the fourth quarter (down 84.8%), so quarterly sales swing sharply. Controlling-shareholder net profit over the latest four quarters totals KRW 9.09 billion.

Operating cash flow was -KRW 36 billion in 2022, KRW 110.6 billion in 2023, -KRW 20.4 billion in 2024 and KRW 62.1 billion in 2025. The cost structure behind the third-quarter swing to a loss is a point to check in the annual report notes.

05

Industry analysis

Demand for secondary-battery equipment weakened around 2024 as the so-called chasm, a pause in demand before mass adoption, led customers to adjust investment cycles and reduce new orders.

A securities report said that in 2025, even as the equipment market paused, mPlus grew sales and profitability together through maintenance of installed equipment and customer line-conversion demand.

The same report judged mPlus to have the clearest earnings improvement and profitability among domestic equipment makers. On the demand side, prismatic cells are gaining share after EV fire concerns raised the value of structural safety, and European and North American automakers are raising their prismatic mix.

The energy storage system (ESS) market is expanding with the global energy transition and rising power demand from AI data centers, and the company cited higher overseas sales of prismatic and ESS equipment as the driver of first-quarter 2026 results.

All-solid-state batteries remain before mass production, and the company describes its current work as largely pilot-scale. Some expect equipment demand to rise quickly once mass-production investment starts.

Competitively, mPlus's strengths are its full assembly-line coverage across pouch, prismatic and solid-state formats and its extension into electrode and pack processes for turnkey delivery.

Its results depend on how fast domestic cell makers invest and on overseas order timing, so order timing has a large effect on quarterly results. The current phase looks like an adjustment period in which maintenance and line-conversion demand supports earnings while new orders slow.

06

Outlook

In a May 2025 briefing the company set targets of average annual growth above 50% through 2028 and revenue of KRW 1 trillion in 2030. It also said a new plant added to its two Cheongju facilities would raise production capacity threefold once all three are running.

The company outlined raising the prismatic share of orders from about 30% in 2025 to 90% in 2026 and 96% in 2027. Its order backlog at the end of 2025 was disclosed at KRW 172.1 billion, which underpins revenue after 2026.

An August 2026 report said the company completed a KRW 40 billion non-dilutive fundraising and will direct the proceeds to new businesses including the AIMR autonomous mobile robot and all-solid-state battery equipment.

The company attributed its first-half 2026 gross margin gain to selective order intake and higher prismatic and ESS sales. In an August 2025 report, SK Securities cited a 2026 revenue guidance of KRW 250 billion.

That level sits far above the revenue disclosed for the first three quarters of 2026, so the 2026 annual report is the next point to see where full-year revenue landed against that guidance. The January 2026 contract termination also makes the stability of the backlog worth checking.

No confirmed schedule has been disclosed for the new plant's completion, or for solid-state orders moving into mass production.

08

Bull factors

Profitability recovery and higher margins

The 2025 operating margin was 13.3%, up sharply from -8.5% in 2022, and operating profit of KRW 24.6 billion rose 143.4% from KRW 10.1 billion in 2024. The company said its first-half 2026 gross margin rose to 28.2%, up 3.7 percentage points from 24.5% a year earlier. It attributed the gain to selective order intake that prioritizes profitability.

Order mix shifting to prismatic, ESS and solid-state

Prismatic equipment was about 30% of total orders in 2025, and the company plans to raise that share to 90% in 2026. Higher overseas sales of prismatic assembly equipment and ESS equipment were cited as drivers of first-quarter 2026 revenue. The company also presents its full turnkey coverage for all-solid-state battery production as a differentiator.

Balance sheet repair and shareholder returns

The debt-to-equity ratio fell from 469.3% in 2022 to 136.7% in 2025. Operating cash flow in 2025 was KRW 62.1 billion, positive. An early-2026 securities report cited convertible bond acquisition and retirement and treasury share cancellation as positive for shareholder returns.

09

Bear factors

Quarterly profit swings into loss

The company posted an operating loss of KRW 4.48 billion and a net loss attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 9.62 billion in the third quarter of 2026. Second-quarter 2026 revenue fell 37.2% on the quarter to KRW 32 billion, and fourth-quarter 2025 revenue fell 84.8% to KRW 13.4 billion. Controlling-shareholder net profit over the latest four quarters totals KRW 9.09 billion.

Order cancellation and customer concentration

A supply contract disclosed on January 16, 2026 was with the U.S. unit of a global battery company and was terminated by mutual agreement at the counterparty's request. The termination amount was about KRW 8.5 billion, equal to 7.29% of recent revenue, and the performance rate was 50%. Revenue remains concentrated in pouch-type lines supplied for SK On, which adds customer concentration risk.

Industry slowdown and new-business costs

Revenue fell 62.1% in 2024, and a securities report attributed the drop to delayed deliveries after customers adjusted investment cycles. Solid-state orders remain at the pilot stage, and the funds raised are to be deployed into new businesses such as AIMR, which could keep near-term costs elevated. How those costs were reflected in third-quarter results can be checked in the annual report notes.

10

Risk factors

Customer investment cycles

Investment adjustments by cell makers quickly shift equipment order and delivery timing. Revenue fell 62.1% in 2024 as an example. A 2022 securities analysis put the lead time from order to revenue recognition at 9–12 months, so a drop in orders can show up in results several quarters later.

Order cancellation and reliance on key customers

The January 2026 termination shows that contracted backlog can shrink under contract terms. The termination amount equaled 7.29% of recent revenue. Heavy reliance on pouch-type lines for a few key customers means changes in one customer's orders can have a large effect on results.

Balance sheet and new-business execution

Total liabilities at the end of 2025 were KRW 139.4 billion against total equity of KRW 102 billion. The company has said it will invest the funds raised, including KRW 40 billion, in new businesses such as AIMR and solid-state equipment. If these businesses do not convert into orders, the investment may not be recovered through earnings.

12

Overall view

The disclosed figures show mPlus moving from an operating loss in 2022 to a 13.3% operating margin in 2025, and its debt-to-equity ratio fell from 469.3% to 136.7% over the same period.

On the other side, the third quarter of 2026 brought an operating loss of KRW 4.48 billion and a net loss attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 9.62 billion, so the annual profit trend did not carry through every quarter.

Strengths include the expansion of prismatic and ESS orders, turnkey capability and shareholder-return measures. Weaknesses include quarterly volatility, the January 2026 contract termination, customer investment cycles and the early stage of solid-state orders.

The first-half 2026 gross margin gain to 28.2% is a positive signal, but the reasons for the third-quarter swing cannot be fully explained by the disclosed numbers alone.

The next checkpoints are the annual operating margin and year-end backlog in the 2026 annual report, followed by whether profit recovers in the first-quarter 2027 report. This report is for information only and does not present an investment opinion or target price.
