02

Business structure

mPlus was founded in 2003 and listed on KOSDAQ in 2017. It manufactures and sells assembly-process equipment for pouch-type and prismatic EV batteries within the secondary battery production chain.

According to the company website (the reference date is not stated), battery assembly equipment accounts for 80.86% of sales and other products for 19.14%. Its main products are notching and stacking equipment, and the company says its flagship 600PPM high-speed notching machine can cut capex by 70% and opex by 30%.

The company says it is the only maker able to supply a full battery assembly line on a turnkey basis, which it argues reduces specification mismatches between machines and streamlines logistics between processes.

Its customers are mainly Korean battery and automotive groups, including Samsung SDI, Hyundai Motor and LG Energy Solution (formerly the battery unit of LG Chem).

At a May 2025 press briefing, the company said it had supplied equipment to more than 80 mass-production lines, with overall equipment effectiveness of roughly 85–90%.

The business is expanding from assembly into electrode and pack processes, and the company says it has secured technology across the full all-solid-state battery process. Its other businesses include fuel cell equipment, smart factory systems, robots and components such as precision molds and lithium-metal electrodes.

A press report from late October 2026 described an order from a European battery company, cited as an example of reaching special-purpose battery markets. Revenue, however, depends on delivery and revenue-recognition timing, so it can swing sharply by quarter, as the 3Q 2026 decline shows.

04

Earnings analysis

The disclosed annual results show a move from an operating loss in 2022 to profit in 2023, followed by a decline in 2024 and a recovery in 2025. Revenue rose from KRW 116.6 billion in 2022 to KRW 340.1 billion in 2023, up 191.6%, which was the largest year of the 2022–2025 period.

It then fell to KRW 128.7 billion in 2024, down 62.1%, and recovered to KRW 184.2 billion in 2025, up 43.1%. Operating profit turned from an operating loss of KRW 9.86 billion in 2022 to KRW 23.9 billion in 2023, dropped to KRW 10.1 billion in 2024, down 57.7%, and rose to KRW 24.6 billion in 2025, up 143.4%.

The operating margin moved from -8.5% to 7.0% and 7.8%, reaching 13.3% in 2025. The company attributes its two consecutive profitable years to cost reduction from its technological edge and its ability to win high-margin projects.

Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders was a loss of KRW 15.2 billion in 2022, a profit of KRW 20 billion in 2023 after the turnaround, KRW 11 billion in 2024, down 45.1%, and KRW 20.6 billion in 2025, up 87.9%.

Net profit including non-controlling interests is reported separately, with values of a loss of KRW 15.2 billion in 2022, and profits of KRW 20 billion, KRW 11 billion and KRW 20.6 billion in the following years, so the two measures should be read apart.

Quarterly revenue went from KRW 88 billion in 3Q 2025 to KRW 13.4 billion in 4Q 2025, down 84.8% from the prior quarter, then to KRW 51 billion in 1Q 2026, up 280.7%, KRW 32 billion in 2Q 2026, down 37.2%, and KRW 35.9 billion in 3Q 2026, up 12.1% from the prior quarter but down 59.2% year on year.

Operating profit reached KRW 10.2 billion in 1Q 2026, fell to KRW 4.04 billion in 2Q 2026, down 60.4% from the prior quarter, and turned into an operating loss of KRW 4.48 billion in 3Q 2026, a swing into loss from a year earlier.

Over the last four quarters, net profit attributable to controlling shareholders totals KRW 9.09 billion, and 3Q 2026 recorded a controlling-shareholder net loss of KRW 9.62 billion.

A one-off item often cited is the termination of an overseas equipment supply contract disclosed at the end of March 2026, reported at KRW 40.7 billion. Operating cash flow was -KRW 36 billion in 2022, KRW 110.6 billion in 2023, -KRW 20.4 billion in 2024 and KRW 62.1 billion in 2025, with large swings between years.

05

Industry analysis

The secondary battery equipment market is in a consolidation phase. A brokerage report from early 2026 noted that slower growth in global EVs reduced new equipment orders, while maintenance and production-line conversion demand supported earnings.

Construction of global battery plants has seen delays and cancellations linked to shifts in EV demand and investment plans. When customers push back investment schedules, equipment makers see deliveries and revenue recognition slip, and intensified bidding competition pressures prices and margins.

In an analysis of 33 battery equipment makers' 3Q 2026 results by THE ELEC, 19 saw revenue rise year on year. The same analysis listed five companies, including mPlus, whose revenue fell and which swung into losses.

The same early-2026 brokerage report said most secondary battery equipment makers were loss-making, with only some electrode and assembly-process makers in profit.

In competition, mPlus relies on its turnkey supply capability and high equipment efficiency for assembly lines, and is widening its scope into electrode and pack processes.

The brokerage argued that next-generation all-solid-state batteries, with high energy density and safety, could spread to industries such as humanoid robots. However, the company's solid-state orders remain at the pilot-research stage.

The 3Q 2026 swing to loss cannot be attributed solely to the industry downturn, and the revenue-recognition timing of individual projects must be considered as well.

06

Outlook

At a May 2025 press briefing, the company set a medium-term target of more than 50% average annual growth through 2028. At the same event, CEO Kim Jong-sung said the company aims to reach KRW 1 trillion in annual revenue by 2030 through high-speed electrode equipment development and new factory investment.

The company expects production capacity to triple once all three factories are running. Its expansion axis is dry electrode and pack processes, broadening the lineup from assembly toward full-process order coverage.

In all-solid-state batteries, the company says it has secured technology across the full process and is strengthening alternative equipment such as ultra-high-pressure hot press and multi-stage roll press machines.

Solid-state orders came from a US company in 2024 and a domestic company in 2025, and a move into mass production has not yet been disclosed. The key variable for the outlook is which orders convert into revenue after the overseas contract termination.

Because capacity expansion will affect earnings only once facilities start operating, whether the operating schedule lines up with revenue growth is the checkpoint.

On shareholder returns, a brokerage cited convertible bond acquisition and cancellation along with treasury share cancellation as positives, and the March 2026 shareholder meeting approved the financial statements for the 23rd fiscal year along with a cash dividend.

Risk factors

FX and contract currency

Some overseas equipment contracts are denominated in US dollars, so won-converted revenue moves with the exchange rate. As deliveries to US and European customers grow, the share of dollar-based orders may rise, raising FX exposure. A stronger won would reduce the won value of dollar-denominated contracts.

Technology development (solid-state)

Solid-state battery equipment orders are currently concentrated at the pilot stage. The timing and scale of any move to mass-production lines depend on customers' technology roadmaps, making revenue timing uncertain. The company says it has the full process technology, but commercialization pace is not set.

Capacity expansion and new-business execution

The company says production capacity will triple once all three factories are running, and it has set a KRW 1 trillion revenue target for 2030. Reaching it depends on the new factories' start-up schedule and winning electrode and pack process orders. If capex is spent before orders arrive, fixed-cost burden and cash flow pressure could follow.

Overall view

mPlus is an assembly-process equipment maker for secondary batteries that moved from an operating loss in 2022 to profit in 2023, and raised its operating margin to 13.3% by 2025.

The debt ratio at the end of 2025 was 136.7%, down sharply from 469.3% in 2022, and operating cash flow of KRW 62.1 billion in 2025 supported the profit structure.

On the other hand, 3Q 2026 revenue of KRW 35.9 billion fell 59.2% year on year, turning into an operating loss of KRW 4.48 billion and a controlling-shareholder net loss of KRW 9.62 billion.

The termination of an overseas equipment contract disclosed at the end of March 2026, along with large quarterly revenue swings, shows that order continuity is not yet stable. Strengths cited include turnkey capability, high equipment efficiency, solid-state pilot orders and shareholder return measures.

Weaknesses are the 3Q loss, the contract termination and quarterly earnings volatility, against an industry backdrop of slower new orders and delayed customer investment.

The company has set a target of more than 50% average annual growth through 2028 and KRW 1 trillion in revenue by 2030, but these are its own plans and their execution must be confirmed through future filings.

The next checkpoints are the 4Q and full-year operating margin in the 2026 annual report and whether solid-state pilot lines move to mass production.
