02

Business structure

KGA, founded in 2017, develops and manufactures secondary battery equipment, ranging from conventional lithium-ion battery equipment for LFP and NCM chemistries to next-generation equipment for solid-state batteries.

Its core products are electrode process equipment at the first stage of cell manufacturing: coaters that apply active material onto metal foil, roll presses that compress electrodes to a uniform thickness, and slitters that cut electrodes to width.

Its current business is split between this electrode process equipment and duct automation equipment. A business-profile aggregator's analysis as of March 2026 shows coaters as the largest revenue component. The company says it has secured many domestic major battery makers and global top-tier customers.

Its supplier registrations with LG Energy Solution (August 2024) and Samsung SDI (November 2024) were completed according to its corporate timeline. In new areas, the company completed a full-line lineup of front-end equipment for solid-state batteries in February 2025.

In July 2025 it announced commercialization of an aqueous process for LFP cathode active material with a partner company. Its extended power solutions for humanoid robots, FIB and FIS, are being developed with its robotics partner.

Because electrode equipment is ordered project by project as customers expand capacity, revenue moves with the timing of customer orders.

04

Earnings analysis

On the 2024 annual filings, revenue was KRW 51.4 billion, operating profit was KRW 3.41 billion, and net profit attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 2.8 billion. At its listing, the company said its 2024 consolidated revenue of about KRW 51.4 billion was its best result since its founding.

In 2025, revenue fell 64.8% year on year to KRW 18.1 billion, and the company swung to an operating loss of KRW 3.69 billion. Net loss attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 9.63 billion, a swing from profit, and the net loss including non-controlling interests was also KRW 9.63 billion.

The operating margin dropped from 6.6% in 2024 to -20.4% in 2025. Over the same period the debt-to-equity ratio fell from 201.2% to 142.5%; at year-end 2025, total liabilities were KRW 59.8 billion and total equity was KRW 42 billion. Operating cash flow moved from -KRW 2.82 billion in 2024 to KRW 1.27 billion in 2025.

Quarterly, revenue was KRW 345 million with an operating loss of KRW 1.68 billion in the third quarter of 2025, then rose to KRW 1.24 billion in the fourth quarter (up 257.8% from the prior quarter), while the operating loss widened to KRW 3 billion.

In the first quarter of 2026, revenue was KRW 899 million (down 27.2% from the prior quarter) and the operating loss narrowed to KRW 1.22 billion. In the second quarter, revenue rose to KRW 1.66 billion (up 84.5%), but the operating loss widened again to KRW 1.92 billion.

In the third quarter of 2026, revenue was KRW 870 million, up 152.0% year on year, and the operating loss narrowed to KRW 1.18 billion. Quarterly revenue has moved between the high hundreds of millions and the low billions of won, so quarterly earnings swing with delivery timing. The combined net loss attributable to controlling shareholders over the last four quarters is KRW 7.72 billion.

05

Industry analysis

KGA's end demand comes from secondary battery cell makers expanding capacity and investing in next-generation batteries, and equipment orders move with customers' capex schedules.

At the macro level, KDI expects the semiconductor-driven upturn in exports and facility investment to continue, forecasting 3.8% growth in 2026 and 3.9% in 2027. That capex momentum comes mainly from the semiconductor cycle, so it should be read separately from battery equipment orders.

KGA disclosed a KRW 2.6 billion contract on January 22, 2026 with a domestic counterparty for electrode and assembly process equipment. The equipment is slated for delivery to a U.S. company as pilot equipment, and the contract equaled 5.16% of 2024 consolidated revenue.

Solid-state and dry-process lines use different processes from conventional equipment, which creates new equipment demand. Press reports said KGA's coater and slitter deliveries to global companies had drawn market attention.

Carrying all three electrode process tools makes the company a line-level supplier, which can help in project-based orders. On aqueous processing, the company said the technology reduces costs and that many customers showed interest.

In robotics, KGA was selected as a new participant in the K-Humanoid Alliance run by the Ministry of Trade, Industry and Energy, which includes about 200 companies and institutions.

06

Outlook

The two growth axes the company has presented are solid-state battery equipment and expandable power solutions for humanoid robots. At its 2025 listing, the company said its capacity would expand by roughly four times or more once the new Pyeongtaek plant is fully operational.

According to its corporate timeline, the new Pyeongtaek plant held its completion ceremony in November 2025. At the same time, the company set a target of breaking KRW 100 billion in annual orders for the first time.

The logistics automation contract runs from November 5, 2026 to July 31, 2027, and the end date is the contractual delivery date, which may shift with the schedule. The company says the equipment will be supplied through a mix of in-house and outsourced production.

The counterparty has not been disclosed at its request to protect trade secrets, and the company has deferred related disclosure until the end of July 2027. Large solid-state equipment orders depend on customers entering mass production, according to the company.

In humanoid applications, a patent application for the frame-integrated battery developed with its robotics partner has been completed and is under examination. For waste lead-acid battery restoration equipment, the company plans to prioritize overseas markets such as Japan and Southeast Asia.

The next regular checkpoint is the 2026 annual report, which will show how the order flow translated into revenue.

Bull factors

Solid-state and next-generation process equipment lineup

In February 2025 the company said it had completed a full-line lineup of front-end equipment for solid-state batteries. Press reports pointed to its solid-state equipment development, related patents, and coater and slitter deliveries to global companies. It has also registered patents for a dry-electrode mixer. Next-generation process equipment creates demand as customers move into mass production.

New orders and payment structure

The logistics automation contract disclosed in November 2026 equals 15.45% of 2025 consolidated revenue. Payments start with a 20% advance and follow staged milestones. If the terms proceed as written, 90% of the contract value is collected before final inspection.

Press coverage noted that this helps spread the working-capital burden of building and delivering the equipment.

Customer base and technology references

Press coverage described KGA as having the largest customer pool among domestic equipment companies. In January 2026 it signed a KRW 2.6 billion electrode and assembly process equipment contract, with the equipment going to a U.S. company as pilot equipment.

In July 2025 it announced commercialization of an aqueous process for LFP cathode material with a partner company. The company says it can offer combined material and equipment solutions.

Bear factors

Continued losses and revenue contraction

Revenue fell 64.8% in 2025, and the company swung to an operating loss of KRW 3.69 billion. The company has posted losses in each of the five quarters from the third quarter of 2025 through the third quarter of 2026.

Its combined net loss attributable to controlling shareholders over the last four quarters is KRW 7.72 billion. On the filed results, no profitable quarter has yet appeared in that window.

Quarterly revenue volatility

Quarterly revenue moved sharply in 2026, at KRW 899 million in the first quarter, KRW 1.66 billion in the second, and KRW 870 million in the third. Second-quarter revenue rose 84.5% from the prior quarter, yet the operating loss widened to KRW 1.92 billion.

Because equipment supply contracts recognize results according to build, delivery, and inspection timing, revenue can land in different quarters than the orders.

Debt burden and new-business development costs

Total liabilities at year-end 2025 were KRW 59.8 billion, above total equity of KRW 42 billion, and the debt-to-equity ratio was 142.5%. Operating cash flow was KRW 1.27 billion positive in 2025, but that is a single year, so its persistence is uncertain.

If mass-production transitions in solid-state, humanoid, and restoration equipment are delayed, development costs may weigh on results first.

Risk factors

Capacity expansion and order target

The company said its capacity would expand by roughly four times or more once the new Pyeongtaek plant is fully operational. It also set a target of breaking KRW 100 billion in annual orders for the first time. If orders do not keep pace with the expanded capacity, lower utilization could weigh on results.

Because equipment orders follow customers' expansion schedules, the timing of reaching that target may depend on those schedules.

Adoption of aqueous process technology

The aqueous process for LFP cathode active material is drawing customer interest for its cost-reduction benefit, and the company will provide samples to some companies to continue collaboration. Whether this technology is adopted in customers' mass-production lines has not yet been confirmed.

If adoption is delayed, the timing of its link to equipment demand could also slip. The scope of collaboration with its joint-development partner and commercialization terms could also determine how quickly it generates revenue.

Overseas restoration equipment rollout

The company plans to prioritize overseas markets such as Japan and Southeast Asia for its waste battery restoration equipment. Revenue recognition timing may vary depending on securing overseas customers and contract terms.

If customer structures and delivery methods differ from those in Korea, the pace of expansion could be slower than expected. Because restoration equipment is presented alongside solid-state and humanoid businesses as new growth areas, resources may be spread across several businesses.

Overall view

Based on the filed results, KGA's revenue fell 64.8% in 2025, and the company swung to operating and net losses attributable to controlling shareholders, with losses continuing across the five quarters through the third quarter of 2026.

Quarterly revenue in 2026 was KRW 899 million, KRW 1.66 billion, and KRW 870 million, moving sharply with delivery timing. On the positive side, operating cash flow turned positive in 2025, and the debt-to-equity ratio fell from 201.2% in 2024 to 142.5% in 2025.

The company is broadening into solid-state electrode equipment, aqueous processing technology, logistics automation equipment, and humanoid power solutions. The KRW 2.8 billion logistics contract disclosed in November 2026 equals 15.45% of 2025 revenue.

Weak points include continued losses, revenue volatility, and a balance sheet with KRW 59.8 billion in total liabilities. Strengths include its customer base, new orders, and equipment portfolio.

Revenue recognition for new businesses depends on customer mass-production transitions and inspection schedules, which creates timing gaps.

The next checkpoints are the full-year 2026 operating margin in the 2026 annual report and the size of losses in the first-quarter 2027 report, while completion of the logistics contract delivery is due around July 31, 2027.
