02

Business structure

Korea Industrial manufactures and sells compound feed for livestock, and it is known for its Hafeed brand.

The company is reported to have introduced an automated control system, the first of its kind in the domestic compound feed industry, to standardize its products, and it positions new product development and herd management as core competitive assets.

Product sales accounted for 93.9% of 2025 revenue, and by revenue in the second quarter of 2026 the feed segment made up 93% while other businesses made up 7%. Because its raw materials depend heavily on imported grain, grain prices and the exchange rate directly drive its cost base.

Profit therefore depends less on sales volume than on how quickly the company passes costs and currency moves through to selling prices. The company also runs a meat business to secure stable sales channels, which exposes it to livestock cycles.

On competition, it is described as strengthening its market position through quality control and breeding-technology guidance rather than through price competition. Cattle and poultry output edged up in cumulative terms through the third quarter of 2025, which is cited as a demand-side positive. With non-feed sales at about 7% of revenue, portfolio diversification remains limited.

04

Earnings analysis

Based on reported results, annual revenue peaked at KRW 319.1 billion in 2023 (up 14.6% year on year) from KRW 278.4 billion in 2022, then fell for two consecutive years, to KRW 307.7 billion in 2024 (down 3.6% year on year) and KRW 297.3 billion in 2025 (down 3.4% year on year).

Operating profit rose from KRW 4.43 billion in 2022 to KRW 7.99 billion in 2023 (up 80.5% year on year) and KRW 14.3 billion in 2024 (up 78.6% year on year), then edged down to KRW 13.7 billion in 2025 (down 3.7% year on year).

The operating margin climbed from 1.6% in 2022 to 2.5% in 2023 and 4.6% in both 2024 and 2025, so margins improved even in the years when revenue declined. One brokerage summary explains that cost reductions led to improved profitability.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders grew for three straight years, from KRW 0.69 billion in 2022 to KRW 3.39 billion in 2023, KRW 4.02 billion in 2024 and KRW 9.66 billion in 2025, and in 2025 it rose 140.3% year on year.

In that same year operating profit fell while net income attributable to controlling shareholders rose, so the two moved in opposite directions, and the non-operating income and expenses and income tax items that created the gap are something to check in the next periodic report.

Net income including non-controlling interests was KRW 9.67 billion in 2025. Cash flow from operating activities turned positive, going from KRW -10.9 billion in 2024 to KRW 32.4 billion in 2025, and the debt ratio fell from 165.4% in 2024 to 120.2% in 2025.

On a quarterly basis, revenue rose for four consecutive quarters, from KRW 72.1 billion in the third quarter of 2025 to KRW 73.2 billion in the fourth quarter, KRW 75.2 billion in the first quarter of 2026, KRW 79.5 billion in the second quarter and KRW 83.6 billion in the third quarter.

Over the same period operating profit fluctuated at KRW 3.77 billion, KRW 2.66 billion, KRW 3.27 billion, KRW 4.0 billion and KRW 0.64 billion, and operating profit in the third quarter of 2026 fell 82.9% from the same period a year earlier.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders in the third quarter of 2026 was KRW 3.36 billion, up for a second consecutive quarter from the second quarter, and the sum for the most recent four quarters is KRW 7.97 billion.

According to the quarterly report, cumulative revenue for January to September 2026 came to about KRW 238.3 billion and operating profit to about KRW 7.9 billion.

05

Industry analysis

Demand for compound feed moves with livestock herd sizes, so species-level breeding trends are the starting point for earnings. Cattle and poultry output edging up in cumulative terms through the third quarter of 2025 is a positive demand signal.

On the other hand, heavy reliance on imported grain means cost pressure is a constant feature. The lag before grain prices and exchange-rate moves are reflected in selling prices is the main driver of quarterly margin swings.

The company positions automated, standardized production and breeding guidance as its differentiators. Running a meat business alongside feed secures some sales channels, which can cushion feed-demand fluctuations.

The feed sector competes mainly on price and production and logistics efficiency, so cost control drives profit gaps between companies. Herd sizes and grain price trends are the basic indicators for judging where the livestock cycle stands.

Whether the sharp drop in third-quarter 2026 operating profit reflects this cost structure is a question for line-item cost disclosures.

Bull factors

Operating margin settled in the 4% range

The operating margin rose from 1.6% in 2022 to 2.5% in 2023 and 4.6% in both 2024 and 2025. Margins held up while revenue declined in 2024 and 2025, which is cited as evidence of structural improvement. Cost reduction is also named as a driver of the profitability gain.

Stronger balance sheet and cash flow

The debt ratio fell from 165.4% in 2024 to 120.2% in 2025. Operating cash flow moved from negative KRW 10.9 billion in 2024 to KRW 32.4 billion in 2025. Year-end 2025 total equity was KRW 119.7 billion and total liabilities were KRW 143.9 billion as reported.

Four consecutive quarters of revenue growth

Revenue rose for four consecutive quarters, from KRW 72.1 billion in the third quarter of 2025 to KRW 83.6 billion in the third quarter of 2026. Third-quarter 2026 revenue was up 16.1% year on year. This is consistent with demand holding up.

Bear factors

Sharp drop in third-quarter operating profit

Third-quarter 2026 operating profit was KRW 645 million, down 82.9% year on year. Revenue rose over the same period, so sales growth did not translate into profit. Compared with KRW 4 billion in the second quarter of 2026, quarterly swings are large.

Two years of revenue decline and a smaller 2025 operating profit

Revenue has fallen for two consecutive years since its 2023 peak. Operating profit also declined 3.7% in 2025. Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders rising while operating profit fell is a point that calls for scrutiny of earnings quality.

Import dependence and currency exposure

Heavy reliance on imported grain means cost pressure is a constant feature of the business. A lag between grain and currency moves and selling prices can squeeze margins. Whether the third-quarter 2026 profit drop is linked to this structure has not been confirmed.

10

Risk factors

Raw materials and FX

Heavy reliance on imported grain means grain prices and exchange rates drive costs. If pass-through to selling prices lags, quarterly operating profit can swing sharply. The 82.9% year-on-year drop in third-quarter 2026 operating profit illustrates how such a risk can show up.

Business concentration

The feed segment accounts for about 93% of sales, so the business is concentrated on one axis. Changes in livestock herd trends or the competitive environment would flow directly into results. The meat business adds some breadth, but portfolio diversification remains limited.

Market size and trading liquidity

With a market capitalization in the range of several hundred billion won, the stock can be sensitive to price volatility and trading liquidity. Smaller companies usually receive less institutional coverage, so the share price can diverge from reported earnings. This makes it harder to read the share price from earnings figures alone.

Overall view

Korea Industrial's reported results show that revenue has declined for two consecutive years since 2023, while the operating margin rose from 1.6% in 2022 to 4.6% in 2025.

Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders rose for three consecutive years through 2025, and operating cash flow and the debt ratio both improved in 2025. In the third quarter of 2026, however, revenue rose for a fourth straight quarter while operating profit fell 82.9% year on year to KRW 645 million.

Import dependence for raw materials, currency exposure and a feed-centric business mix remain the main sources of earnings volatility. Taken together, the divergence between revenue and operating profit in 2026 suggests that durability of earnings is still being tested.

The next checkpoints are provisional fourth-quarter 2026 results, cost-line disclosures in the 2026 annual report and first-quarter 2027 results. This report does not offer an investment opinion, target price or buy or sell view.
