02

Business structure

SungEel HiTech recycles spent batteries and battery-manufacturing scrap to extract metals such as cobalt, nickel, manganese and lithium, and sells them as cathode base materials including cobalt sulfate, nickel sulfate, manganese sulfate and lithium carbonate.

Its feedstock comes from lithium-ion batteries used in EVs, phones, laptops and ESS, and how stably it collects that feedstock determines output and unit cost.

The core production base is the hydro centers 1 to 3 in the Saemangeum industrial complex in Jeollanam-do, and press reports describe the completed third plant as sized to supply material for 300,000 EVs a year.

Overseas, the company added a Hungarian subsidiary and an Indiana site in the United States, but a 2025 press report said utilization slipped while output and unit prices both fell.

Its customers are companies that make battery materials and cells; in 2023 it signed a KRW 24 billion cobalt sulfate supply contract with a cathode joint venture of Samsung SDI and Ecopro BM.

On April 18, 2025 it signed a KRW 20.8 billion cobalt and nickel sulfate supply contract with a Moroccan counterparty, which press reports said equaled about 15.27% of 2024 revenue.

Selling prices move with international metal prices and the exchange rate, so when metal prices fall the value of its materials falls too, and the company has said contract amounts may change with these movements.

On competition, the gap in profitability comes from how much spent-battery feedstock a recycler secures and how full its plants run. Because feedstock flows move with battery makers' production volumes, a slowdown in cell demand hits both supply and utilization at once.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual operating profit fell from KRW 48.3 billion in 2022 to an operating loss of KRW 8.34 billion in 2023, which turned the company into a loss-maker, followed by an operating loss of KRW 71.4 billion in 2024 and KRW 54.5 billion in 2025.

Revenue went from KRW 269.9 billion in 2022 to KRW 247.4 billion in 2023 (down 8.3%) and KRW 136.2 billion in 2024 (down 44.9%), then recovered to KRW 194.6 billion in 2025 (up 42.9%); 2022 was the largest year and 2024 the smallest in the 2022 to 2025 span.

For 2023, the company attributed the decline to lower selling prices following a drop in international metal prices and higher fixed costs tied to preparing its third plant.

An April 2025 press report said output and unit prices both fell while fixed costs grew, and even with revenue rising in 2025 the operating margin stayed deeply negative at -28.0%.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders fell from KRW 39.3 billion in 2022 to KRW 24.8 billion in 2023 (down 36.9%), turned into a net loss of KRW 110.1 billion in 2024, and narrowed to a net loss of KRW 77.6 billion in 2025.

Net income including non-controlling interests, a net loss of KRW 80.5 billion in 2025, is a different measure from the controlling-shareholder figure.

Quarterly, revenue was KRW 43.8 billion with an operating loss of KRW 12.3 billion in 3Q 2025; in 4Q 2025 revenue rose to KRW 53.4 billion (up 22.0% quarter on quarter) and the operating loss narrowed to KRW 9.34 billion.

In 1Q 2026 revenue reached KRW 60.9 billion (up 13.9%) and the operating loss shrank to KRW 4.14 million.

In 2Q 2026 revenue rose to KRW 76.8 billion (up 26.2%) and operating profit of KRW 6.33 billion marked a swing to profit, but in 3Q 2026 revenue of KRW 75.3 billion (down 1.9% quarter on quarter and up 72.0% year on year) came with an operating loss of KRW 9.26 billion, and the controlling-shareholder result moved from net income of KRW 8.37 billion in 2Q to a net loss of KRW 29.1 billion in 3Q.

Operating cash flow went from KRW 42.8 billion in 2022 to outflows of KRW 3.58 billion in 2023, KRW 55.3 billion in 2024 and KRW 22.8 billion in 2025.

At end-2025 total liabilities were KRW 566.6 billion against total equity of KRW 154.1 billion (KRW 127 billion attributable to controlling shareholders), a debt ratio of 367.6%.

05

Industry analysis

Battery recycling rose to become a leading stock-market theme as the growth of electric vehicles raised the problem of disposing of spent batteries.

Policies requiring the use of recycled raw materials, such as the EU's Critical Raw Materials Act, are taking shape, and some observers say this is widening the demand base for recyclers.

Material prices, however, were on a downtrend: the battery material SungEel HiTech produces fell from KRW 25,476 per kg in 2023 to KRW 18,227 in 2024 and KRW 16,835 in 1Q 2025.

Metal prices turned strong in 2026, and Samsung Securities said in a May 11, 2026 report that the strength in metal prices had a larger effect in 1Q 2026 than expected. On feedstock, a 2025 brokerage analysis pointed out that weaker battery output at downstream customers reduces scrap volumes.

A press report also said domestic cathode makers increased inquiries for cobalt and nickel sulfate after China tightened exports of medium and heavy rare earths.

Supply contracts with large battery-material companies, including through joint ventures, are a recurring pattern, so customers' production plans shape volumes.

Against peers, the key variable is how much feedstock a recycler can secure and how fully it runs its plants, since those gaps feed through to profit differences.

In a May 11, 2026 report, Samsung Securities estimated 2026 revenue at about KRW 356.5 billion and operating profit at KRW 7.3 billion, and projected 2027 operating profit at KRW 18.1 billion.

The same report estimated 3Q 2026 operating profit at KRW 3.0 billion, whereas the disclosed 3Q result was an operating loss of KRW 9.26 billion. In a May 26, 2026 report, a Korean brokerage pointed to higher utilization at the Saemangeum third plant and wider scrap sourcing as the drivers of improved results.

The next checkpoints are 4Q 2026 scrap volumes and third-plant utilization, and annual revenue and operating profit will be fixed in the 2026 annual report.

Bull factors

The operating loss narrowed from KRW 12.3 billion in 3Q 2025 to KRW 4.14 million in 1Q 2026, showing expansion and loss reduction at the same time.

Scrap sourcing and utilization

A Korean brokerage's May 26, 2026 report cited higher utilization at the third plant and wider scrap sourcing as drivers of improvement. The company was reported to target scrap intake rising from about 1,600 tons a month to as much as 2,600 tons by year-end.

More feedstock allows fixed costs to be spread over more output, which leaves room for the operating margin to improve.

Metal prices and recycling mandates

Samsung Securities said in a May 11, 2026 report that the strength in metal prices in 1Q 2026 was larger than expected. The EU's recycled-content requirements are taking shape, and observers see a wider demand base for recyclers. Because metal price increases feed into selling prices, price trends remain a variable for earnings recovery.

Bear factors

Return to a quarterly loss in 3Q

The operating loss in 3Q 2026 was KRW 9.26 billion, a reversal from the operating profit of 2Q 2026. Over the same period the net loss attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 29.1 billion, wider than a year earlier.

Compared with Samsung Securities' May 11, 2026 estimate of KRW 3.0 billion in 3Q operating profit, the gap between results and forecasts is clear.

Balance-sheet burden

At end-2025 the debt ratio was 367.6%, with total liabilities of KRW 566.6 billion well above total equity of KRW 154.1 billion. Operating cash flow was negative from 2023 through 2025, reaching KRW -22.8 billion in 2025.

Net losses attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 56.1 billion over the last four quarters mean borrowing costs and funding capacity bear on how quickly results can recover.

Sensitivity of costs and utilization

Selling prices for the company's battery materials fell from KRW 25,476 per kg in 2023 to KRW 16,835 in 1Q 2025, showing high sensitivity to price moves. An April 2025 press report said utilization slipped and fixed costs grew.

Utilization has been recovering in 2026, but the swing to an operating loss in 3Q shows the recovery has not been steady.

Risk factors

Raw material prices

Selling prices for cobalt and nickel sulfate track international metal prices and the exchange rate, so falling metal prices reduce both revenue and profit. Material prices fell from KRW 25,476 per kg in 2023 to KRW 16,835 in 1Q 2025. A reversal in price direction could push quarterly results back and forth between profit and loss.

Feedstock sourcing

SungEel HiTech sources battery scrap and spent-battery feedstock from outside, so lower battery output downstream reduces scrap volumes. A 2025 brokerage analysis cited this as a factor behind weak results. If the year-end target of up to 2,600 tons is not reached, plant utilization could fall again.

Financial structure

The debt ratio rose from 44.4% in 2022 to 367.6% in 2025, and operating cash flow has been negative since 2023. Net losses attributable to controlling shareholders totaled KRW 56.1 billion over the last four quarters. A change in fundraising or borrowing terms could constrain capital spending and operating flexibility.

Overall view

SungEel HiTech produces cathode base materials through spent-battery recycling, and its 2025 revenue of KRW 194.6 billion, up 42.9%, confirmed a top-line recovery.

The operating loss of KRW 54.5 billion in 2025 extended a three-year run of losses, but the 1Q 2026 loss shrank to KRW 4.14 million and 2Q 2026 turned to an operating profit of KRW 6.33 billion.

Revenue in 3Q 2026 of KRW 75.3 billion rose 72.0% year on year, yet the operating loss of KRW 9.26 billion returned the company to a loss.

Positive factors include wider scrap sourcing, higher third-plant utilization, strong metal prices and recycled-content mandates; negative factors include volatile quarterly results, a 367.6% debt ratio and net losses attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 56.1 billion over the last four quarters.

Because no earnings-based multiple can currently be calculated, the price-to-book ratio and brokerage target-price presentations serve as the main reference points. The next checkpoints are whether the year-end scrap target is met and the annual operating margin in the 2026 annual report.
