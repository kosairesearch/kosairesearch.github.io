02

Business structure

Eagle Veterinary Technology, founded in 1970, operates two core businesses: manufacturing and selling veterinary pharmaceuticals, and distributing imported companion animal food and supplies.

The veterinary pharmaceutical segment centers on antibiotics, nutritional supplements, and disinfectants for livestock such as cattle, pigs, and chickens, including African swine fever (ASF) disinfectants named Terminator, K1, and K2 that are registered as recommended products by the quarantine authority.

The companion animal segment imports and distributes premium overseas pet food brands including Now, Go, and Gather, and as of March 2026 revenue-mix data still listed Now-branded dog food items among its top-selling products.

The company's export footprint has expanded from Australia and Southeast Asia, where it entered in the early 1990s, to African markets. It has secured EU GMP certification to support entry into the European market and has pursued European exports on that basis.

In the domestic veterinary vaccine and biosecurity market, vaccine developers such as CAVAC and Komipharm compete alongside the company, prompting Eagle Veterinary Technology to differentiate itself by combining disinfectant and biosecurity products with pet food distribution.

As of March 31, 2026, the company had 108 employees, maintaining a small-to-mid-size company scale. Its headquarters is located in Yesan County, South Chungcheong Province, and it has been listed on KOSDAQ since 2000.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual revenue declined for two straight years, from KRW 43.5 billion in 2022 to KRW 42.8 billion in 2023 (down 1.7% year on year) and KRW 41.7 billion in 2024 (down 2.5% year on year), before rebounding to KRW 43.3 billion in 2025 (up 3.9% year on year).

Operating profit bottomed at KRW 3.33 billion in 2023 (down 6.5% year on year) after KRW 3.56 billion in 2022, then rose for two consecutive years to KRW 3.47 billion in 2024 (up 4.3% year on year) and KRW 4.26 billion in 2025 (up 22.9% year on year).

The operating margin also improved from 7.8% in 2023 to 8.3% in 2024 and 9.8% in 2025, with the profitability recovery more pronounced than the revenue rebound.

Net income attributable to owners rose for three consecutive years, from KRW 2.64 billion in 2022 to KRW 2.98 billion in 2023 (up 12.6%), KRW 3.71 billion in 2024 (up 24.5%), and KRW 3.95 billion in 2025 (up 6.5%).

On a quarterly basis, operating profit bottomed at KRW 286 million in the third quarter of 2025 (down 82.4% quarter on quarter), then rose for three consecutive quarters to KRW 1.08 billion in Q4 2025, KRW 1.37 billion in Q1 2026, and KRW 1.74 billion in Q2 2026.

Net income attributable to owners followed a similar path, falling to KRW 423 million in Q3 2025 before rising for three consecutive quarters to KRW 1.32 billion in Q4 2025, KRW 1.45 billion in Q1 2026, and KRW 2.26 billion in Q2 2026, with the latter up 98.2% year on year.

Net income attributable to owners over the trailing four quarters (Q3 2025 to Q2 2026) totaled KRW 5.45 billion.

Operating cash flow turned positive from negative KRW 318 million in 2022 to KRW 4.89 billion in 2023, and remained positive at KRW 4.34 billion in 2024 and KRW 5.51 billion in 2025, while the debt-to-equity ratio fell to 23.3% at the end of 2025 from 27.7% in 2022.

05

Industry analysis

Korea's veterinary pharmaceutical and biosecurity market is closely linked to the frequency of livestock disease outbreaks and government quarantine policy.

The Ministry of Agriculture, Food and Rural Affairs is running a 'special quarantine period' against highly pathogenic avian influenza (AI), foot-and-mouth disease (FMD), and African swine fever (ASF) from October 2026 to February 2027.

Overseas wild bird AI cases more than doubled in 2026 to 2,957 from 1,432 in the same period a year earlier, while domestic wild boar ASF detections rose to 197 cases from 50 over the same comparison period.

China and Mongolia have confirmed a new SAT1 strain of FMD distinct from the domestic strain, prompting quarantine authorities to expand vaccination in border regions.

For ASF, feed containing plasma protein derived from pig blood has been identified as a new transmission route, leading to tightened inspection of blood-derived feed ingredients at slaughterhouses.

On the competitive front, vaccine developers such as CAVAC and Komipharm have obtained export-only approvals for ASF vaccines, signaling a shift of biosecurity tools from disinfectants toward vaccines.

However, domestic regulations prohibit ASF vaccine production at BSL2 facilities, meaning commercialization via overseas contract manufacturing will likely take additional time.

Outlook

Disease trends during the winter special quarantine period that began in October 2026 are a variable that will directly affect demand for the company's biosecurity and disinfectant products.

The company has previously pursued European certification by conducting overseas clinical trials in the Netherlands for two ASF disinfectant products, which could open further export opportunities in developed markets if certification is completed.

In the companion animal segment, the company continues its strategy of expanding online distribution channels for imported premium pet food brands.

In Africa, the company has a history of expanding into markets such as Kenya, Uganda, and Nigeria, leaving the pace of emerging-market demand recovery as a variable for export performance.

However, since receivables management with overseas customers has previously been cited as a source of export volatility, the pace of trade receivable collection also bears watching.

The third-quarter 2026 regular report is scheduled for disclosure, and whether the three-consecutive-quarter profit growth streak continues will be the next checkpoint.

The company has decided on a year-end dividend for fiscal 2025, making the continuation of that dividend policy amid future earnings trends another point to observe.

Bull factors

Earnings Recovery Trend

Potential Expansion in Biosecurity Demand

The government is running a special quarantine period against AI, FMD, and ASF from October 2026 to February 2027. With wild bird AI cases and wild boar ASF detections both rising, demand for disinfectants and biosecurity products could be supported.

Eagle Veterinary Technology has three ASF disinfectant products registered as recommended by the quarantine authority, positioning it to potentially benefit from heightened quarantine activity.

Track Record of Export Market Diversification

The company has a history of expanding its export footprint from Australia and Southeast Asia in the early 1990s to Africa. It has obtained EU GMP certification and pursued entry into developed European markets on that basis. The companion animal segment has diversified its distribution channels around imported premium pet food brands.

Bear factors

History of Revenue Stagnation

Annual revenue declined for two straight years after peaking at KRW 43.5 billion in 2022, only returning to a level close to 2022 at KRW 43.3 billion in 2025. In years with fewer disease outbreaks, demand for biosecurity products could decline, limiting top-line growth.

The companion animal distribution business also faces intense competition, making its revenue contribution inconsistent quarter to quarter.

Demand Dependence on Disease Events

Quarterly results show significant volatility, with operating profit plunging 82.4% quarter on quarter in Q3 2025. This stems from the irregular nature of livestock disease outbreaks and the resulting biosecurity demand. The possibility of earnings being pressured again during periods of low disease incidence cannot be ruled out.

Risk of Vaccine Substitution

CAVAC and Komipharm have obtained export-only approvals for ASF vaccines, raising the possibility that biosecurity measures could shift from disinfectants toward vaccines. However, domestic regulations prohibiting vaccine production at BSL2 facilities mean commercialization will take time.

If vaccines become commercially available, Eagle Veterinary Technology's disinfectant-centered biosecurity business structure may need to adapt.

10

Risk factors

Disease Outbreak Volatility

The veterinary pharmaceutical business is heavily dependent on the frequency of livestock disease outbreaks and government quarantine policy, resulting in significant quarterly earnings volatility.

If diseases do not occur for an extended period, demand for biosecurity products could decline, slowing both revenue and profit.

Overseas Receivables Collection

There have been past instances where rising receivables from African customers led to adjustments in export shipments, reducing overseas revenue.

Given the exposure to emerging markets in its export structure, risks related to trade receivable collection tied to exchange rates and customer creditworthiness remain present.

Small-Cap Liquidity

As a small-cap stock with foreign ownership in the low single digits as a percentage, liquidity is limited. On days with low trading volume, even small trades could cause larger price swings.
