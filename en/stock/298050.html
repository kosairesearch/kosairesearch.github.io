02

Business structure

HS Hyosung Advanced Materials is an industrial fiber materials company whose core product is tire cord, the reinforcing material inside tires. Its business is split into the tire cord segment, the industrial yarn segment, and an other segment that includes super fibers such as aramid and carbon fiber.

IBK Securities put the tire cord segment at roughly 60% of revenue and industrial yarn at roughly 20% in a March 2025 report. Per Samsung Securities, tire reinforcement products made up 58% of revenue and 90% of operating profit in the first half of 2026, so profit contribution is heavily concentrated in tire cord.

The main product is PET (polyester) tire cord, and hybrid tire cord combining aramid and nylon is used on premium EV tires. Customers include global tire makers such as Michelin, Goodyear and Continental, and some long-term contracts pass quarterly raw material cost changes into the next quarter's selling price.

IBK Securities said the company held a 46% share of the global PET tire cord market in 2024, the top position and more than 35 percentage points ahead of the second-ranked supplier.

KB Securities said HS Hyosung Advanced Materials holds 48% of the global tire cord market, Kolon Industries 13%, and Korean makers together 61%. Carbon fiber is produced at plants in Jeonju, Vietnam and China, and the Jeonju and Vietnam plants are restarting in stages across the third and fourth quarters of 2026.

The company plans to invest USD 30 million, about KRW 43 billion, in an Indian subsidiary and complete a tire cord plant there in 2027.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual revenue fell from KRW 3.84 trillion in 2022 to KRW 3.2 trillion in 2023, a 16.6% decline, recovered to KRW 3.31 trillion in 2024 (up 3.4%), and eased to KRW 3.28 trillion in 2025 (down 0.9%).

Operating profit was largest in 2022 at KRW 315.1 billion and smallest in 2025 at KRW 157.4 billion, after KRW 172.4 billion in 2023 (down 45.3%) and KRW 219.7 billion in 2024 (up 27.5%).

Operating margin moved from 8.2% in 2022 to 5.4% in 2023, 6.6% in 2024 and 4.8% in 2025, so margin swings shaped profit more than revenue size did.

Profit attributable to owners of the parent fell from KRW 125.3 billion in 2022 to KRW 35.6 billion in 2023 (down 71.6%), rose to KRW 49.8 billion in 2024 (up 40.2%), and turned into a net loss of KRW 21.6 billion in 2025.

Net income including non-controlling interests was KRW 15.7 billion in 2025, a separate figure from the parent-level result. Operating cash flow declined from KRW 350.4 billion in 2022 to KRW 210.1 billion in 2025, and the debt ratio of 372.3% in 2025 was the highest of the period.

At the end of 2025, total liabilities were KRW 3.08 trillion against total equity of KRW 826.5 billion, of which KRW 807.6 billion belonged to owners of the parent.

Quarterly operating profit moved from KRW 19.3 billion in Q3 2025 to KRW 30.3 billion in Q4 2025 (up 56.7%), KRW 34.4 billion in Q1 2026 (up 13.7%) and KRW 77.6 billion in Q2 2026 (up 125.5%).

Q3 2026 operating profit was KRW 51.6 billion, down 33.5% from the prior quarter but up 167.2% year on year, while NH Investment & Securities pointed to higher input costs and one-off expenses behind the Q3 slowdown.

Profit attributable to owners of the parent went from a loss of KRW 13.6 billion in Q3 2025 and a loss of KRW 909 million in Q4 2025 (losses narrowed) to KRW 6.88 billion in Q1 2026, KRW 44.8 billion in Q2 2026 and KRW 16.5 billion in Q3 2026, totaling KRW 67.3 billion over the last four quarters.

05

Industry analysis

Many analysts expect tire cord demand to grow faster than the tire market. KB Securities projected that the tire market will grow 1.5% to 2.0% a year through 2030 and that tire cord will grow 0.5 to 1.0 percentage points faster.

The main demand driver is the spread of SUVs, whose share of global vehicle sales rose from 17% in 2009 to 51% on a cumulative basis through September 2025. Larger vehicles use wider and bigger tires, which increases tire cord usage per tire.

EVs are heavier and wear tires faster, shortening replacement cycles by one to two years versus combustion vehicles. On the supply side, global tire cord capacity is about 540,000 tons a year, and Korean makers hold 61% of the market in an oligopoly structure.

New materials need two to three years of road testing, which raises entry barriers, and KB Securities judged that rival capacity additions are limited. Raw material volatility is high: a April 2026 brokerage note said PET prices rose 45% from pre-war levels after the Middle East crisis.

Oversupply from China and a stronger won weigh on export profitability and near-term earnings. The company remains the leader in PET tire cord and produces aramid in-house, which is the hybrid tire cord feedstock, a point that sets it apart from rivals.

In carbon fiber, wind power demand in China and drone certification are cited as demand-side positives, while weak Chinese pricing persists.

06

Outlook

iM Securities estimates full-year 2026 operating profit at KRW 224.6 billion, up 42.6% from 2025, while NH Investment & Securities puts it at KRW 222 billion, up 40.8%. Both growth rates are measured against 2025 operating profit of KRW 157.4 billion.

For 2027, iM Securities projects operating profit of KRW 257.3 billion (up 14.6%) and NH Investment & Securities projects KRW 236 billion (up 6.6%). iM Securities expects fourth-quarter tire cord operating profit of KRW 56.8 billion, an improvement on the third quarter, as tire cord price increases and a recovery in carbon fiber volumes coincide.

The Jeonju and Vietnam carbon fiber plants restarting across the third and fourth quarters are expected to lift sales volumes and reduce fixed costs, according to iM Securities.

NH Investment & Securities expects earnings to rebound from the fourth quarter on the back of expanded carbon fiber capacity. iM Securities estimates operating profit in the industrial yarn and other segments rising from KRW 17.6 billion in 2026 to KRW 63 billion in 2027.

Samsung Securities argues that higher hybrid tire cord sales would also raise internal aramid demand. The India tire cord plant is scheduled for completion in 2027, the next step in capacity expansion. These estimates, however, depend on raw material prices and how quickly price increases pass through.

08

Bull factors

Tire cord leadership and oligopoly structure

PET tire cord share was 46% in 2024, the top position globally and more than 35 percentage points ahead of the second-ranked supplier, per IBK Securities. KB Securities said Korean makers hold 61% of the global tire cord market, and new materials need two to three years of road testing, which raises entry barriers. Limited rival capacity additions are cited as a supply-side strength.

Hybrid tire cord and EV replacement demand

The average selling price of hybrid tire cord is about five times that of conventional nylon cord, and it is already used on premium EVs. Samsung Securities said EV tires wear faster, shortening replacement cycles by one to two years versus combustion vehicles.

Because the company makes its own aramid, higher hybrid sales would also lift internal aramid demand, according to the same analysis.

Possible narrowing of carbon fiber losses

An April 2026 brokerage note said the China carbon fiber plant restarted in Q1 2026 and drone-grade certification was completed, lifting volumes. NH Investment & Securities said expanded Vietnam capacity and new customers keep the profitability recovery outlook valid. Power infrastructure and aviation demand are also cited as rebound drivers.

09

Bear factors

Raw material spike and pricing lag

Tire cord operating profit fell 23% from the prior quarter to KRW 49.7 billion in Q3 2026, according to iM Securities. Price increases did not fully offset raw material cost gains, and the low-cost inventory benefit seen in Q2 disappeared in Q3. Purchase prices for PET chips rose more than selling prices, the broker said.

Persistent carbon fiber and aramid losses

Other segment operating profit, covering carbon fiber and aramid, was KRW 1.9 billion in Q3 2026, down 85% from the prior quarter, per iM Securities. NH Investment & Securities said the super fiber operating margin worsened from negative 15.3% in Q2 to negative 22.1% in Q3 2026. Aramid swung to an operating loss on higher costs, and China carbon fiber prices remain weak.

Chinese oversupply and a stronger won

A stronger won, which squeezes export profitability, along with wider aramid losses and lower tire cord profitability, was cited as a driver of near-term earnings slowdown in an October 2026 report. Oversupply from China and a weak carbon fiber cycle keep profitability under pressure. Analysts also said higher-value product expansion will not quickly translate into earnings gains.

10

Risk factors

Raw materials and currency

Swings in PET chip and AN raw material prices can hit margins when price pass-through lags. Tire cord pricing is typically negotiated every three to six months against raw material costs, which creates timing gaps. A stronger won weakens export profitability.

Balance sheet

At the end of 2025, total liabilities of KRW 3.08 trillion far exceeded total equity of KRW 826.5 billion, with a debt ratio of 372.3%. Operating cash flow declined from KRW 350.4 billion in 2022 to KRW 210.1 billion in 2025. Whether profit recovery translates into stronger cash generation is a point to watch.

New capacity and Chinese supply

Initial startup costs at new Vietnam carbon fiber capacity weighed on margins, according to NH Investment & Securities. Wind-power demand in China is rising, yet oversupply keeps prices weak. The 2027 completion plan for the India tire cord plant depends on timing and utilization.

12

Overall view

HS Hyosung Advanced Materials' results have shown a recovery direction, with profits in every quarter from Q1 2026 following the 2025 swing to a loss.

Full-year 2025 operating profit fell to KRW 157.4 billion and profit attributable to owners of the parent turned into a loss of KRW 21.6 billion, but operating profit rose to KRW 77.6 billion in Q2 2026 and KRW 51.6 billion in Q3 2026.

Tire cord remains the profit center, with tire reinforcement products accounting for 58% of revenue and 90% of operating profit in the first half of 2026. In Q3, PET chip cost increases were not fully passed through to selling prices, which reduced tire cord margins, and losses in carbon fiber and aramid continued.

Brokers estimate that tire cord price increases in the fourth quarter and normalized carbon fiber output could lift full-year 2026 operating profit to around KRW 220 billion. These estimates, however, depend on raw material prices and how quickly price increases pass through.

The debt ratio of 372.3% and the decline in operating cash flow are balance-sheet points to monitor. The next checkpoints are the Q4 2026 preliminary results and the figures in the FY2026 annual report.
