02

Business structure

Volvik is a golf ball and golf equipment maker that has been listed on KONEX since 2015. According to a company profile dated December 2, 2025, it holds about 15% of the domestic golf ball market and about 80% of the color golf ball market.

Golf balls remain the core business, with the No. 1 plant in Eumseong, North Chungcheong Province, producing cores through thermoforming and the No. 2 plant handling dimpling, coating, printing and packaging.

After a fire destroyed the No. 1 plant in November 2022, the rebuilt facility restored annual capacity to 4 million dozen, of which 2 million dozen are made in-house and 2 million dozen are outsourced, according to a November 2023 report.

Park golf balls have emerged as a new growth line, and the company plans to double production capacity in 2026 from 2025 levels with dedicated equipment, targeting park golf ball sales of about KRW 4 billion.

Overseas, the company has said it will overhaul its distribution channels, including those run through its US subsidiary. In the premium segment, Volvik competes with global brands and points to color balls and new premium models as its differentiators.

Press reports tied its 2019 return to losses to heavy marketing spending and an unsuccessful diversification push, after which it exited low-priced golf balls, sports goods and badminton to concentrate on premium golf balls.

A recent report from a credit rating agency pointed to material, structure and dimple technology upgrades, the market reception of premium new products, and entry into adjacent markets as the main items to watch. However, no public figures yet show the sales scale of park golf balls or the effect of the channel overhaul.

03

Recent trends

Consolidated revenue for 2025 was KRW 38.6 billion, down 17.0% from KRW 46.5 billion a year earlier. Operating profit for the same year was KRW 78 million, a turn to profit from an operating loss in the KRW 1.6 billion range in 2024.

Non-operating expenses of about KRW 4 billion, including KRW 1.9 billion of interest expense, widened the net loss to KRW 2.8 billion, up 64.7% from the prior year.

On a separate basis, 2025 revenue was KRW 38.36 billion, operating profit KRW 671 million and net loss KRW 2.029 billion, as shown in the financial statements approved at the March 26, 2026 annual general meeting.

At the end of 2025, consolidated total equity fell 13.5% to KRW 12.6 billion while total liabilities rose 15.5% to KRW 41.6 billion, and cash and cash equivalents declined from KRW 2.5 billion to KRW 1.3 billion.

A KRW 10 billion RCPS issue disclosed on September 3, 2026 was priced at KRW 3,205, which is 91.1% above the reference price of KRW 1,677. If performance targets are missed, the conversion price is lowered to KRW 2,244, which could raise the potential conversion shares from about 3.12 million to as many as 4,456,327.

According to press reports, the stock closed at KRW 1,694 on August 10, 2026, with a market capitalization of about KRW 23.8 billion, and rose 14.62% to KRW 1,490 in early October 2026. At the current price of KRW 1,590, up 14.14% on the day, the market capitalization is KRW 22.4 billion.

Industry sources said the first-half 2026 consolidated operating margin was close to the 6% target, but the company said first-half results have not yet been officially announced.

04

Outlook

The KRW 10 billion raised through the RCPS addresses liquidity pressure, but its performance-linked terms leave the conversion price adjustment as an open variable.

Operating profit targets of KRW 5 billion for 2026 and KRW 7 billion for 2027 are the benchmarks, so whether these are met will determine the scale of dilution. If early redemption is elected, the company must pay a 7% annual compounded return in cash, adding to cash flow demands.

For the park golf ball business, capacity expansion and a KRW 4 billion sales target have been set, and how close actual sales come to that target will become clear through later disclosures and periodic reports.

Overseas, the distribution channel overhaul centered on the US subsidiary is under way, and the subsidiary's net loss and its state of full capital impairment have been cited as burdens on consolidated results.

On the listing side, a KOSDAQ transfer requires a market capitalization of KRW 30 billion, so earnings improvement will need to be accompanied by a higher market valuation.

A recent report from a credit rating agency offered no target price or investment opinion and pointed to golf ball technology upgrades and adjacent market entry as items to watch. The next checkpoint is the third-quarter 2026 quarterly report, whose statutory filing deadline is November 14, 2026.

05

Bull factors

Turn to profit and margin recovery

Consolidated operating profit of KRW 78 million in 2025 moved the company from an operating loss back to profit, and an analysis holds that restructuring and cost cuts are beginning to show in profitability.

Industry sources said the first-half 2026 consolidated operating margin approached the 6% target set at the time of the acquisition, and the company said first-half profitability improved substantially from 2025. Against a 2025 full-year consolidated operating margin of only 0.2%, the improvement is sizable.

Park golf demand and capacity expansion

The domestic park golf population is growing quickly, widening the market itself. Volvik has added dedicated equipment to double its 2026 production capacity relative to 2025 and has set a park golf ball sales target of about KRW 4 billion.

In October 2026, the company hosted a national park golf championship with a total prize pool of KRW 500 million.

Color ball share and in-house production base

According to a company profile, Volvik holds about 15% of the domestic golf ball market and about 80% of the color golf ball market. Following the reconstruction of the No. 1 plant reported in November 2023, the combined in-house and outsourced output supports annual capacity of 4 million dozen.

More than 70 domestic and overseas patents and 314 trademarks give the company an intellectual property base to build on.

06

Bear factors

Wider net loss and debt burden

The 2025 consolidated net loss widened to KRW 2.8 billion, up 64.7% from the prior year. Total equity at the end of 2025 fell 13.5% to KRW 12.6 billion while total liabilities rose 15.5% to KRW 41.6 billion.

Recent press reports put the debt ratio at 330.9%, and current liabilities exceeded current assets, leaving the current ratio at about 86.2%.

Dilution and cash burden from convertible preferred shares

The KRW 10 billion RCPS was priced above the reference share price, but it carries a repricing clause that lowers the conversion price to KRW 2,244 if performance targets are missed.

Potential conversion shares could rise from about 3.12 million to as many as 4,456,327, and commentators have flagged the resulting dilution and overhang risk for existing shareholders. If early redemption is chosen, the company must pay a 7% annual compounded return in cash, which is also viewed as a financial burden.

Falling sales and overseas channel strain

Consolidated revenue for 2025 fell 17.0% to KRW 38.6 billion. The US subsidiary's net loss of KRW 2.4 billion and its state of full capital impairment have been cited as drags on consolidated results.

The company has said it will overhaul overseas distribution channels, but no public figures yet show that the overhaul has translated into a sales recovery.

07

Risk factors

Macro

Golf equipment spending is sensitive to household spending capacity and leisure outlays. Because export volumes have driven results, changes in overseas demand and tariff policy can act as earnings variables.

US exports fell an estimated 30 to 40% during the COVID-19 period, which shows how volatile overseas demand can be.

Liquidity

Cash and cash equivalents have fallen to about KRW 1.3 billion, and cash obligations tied to the convertible preferred shares add to interest costs of KRW 1.9 billion in 2025. The interest rate environment will shape the cost of any further financing.

The company has said that additional funding has not been firmly decided but could be considered depending on circumstances.

Market and listing

Volvik, listed on KONEX, is considering a transfer to KOSDAQ, but its market capitalization remains below the KRW 30 billion requirement. Because market capitalization moves with the share price, whether the threshold is met can change over time.

An analysis holds that earnings improvement must be paired with a re-rating of the company's value in the market.

08

Overall view

Volvik has positives in its 2025 return to operating profit and signs of margin improvement in the first half of 2026. At the same time, financial negatives persist, including a wider net loss, a debt ratio of 330.9% and a decline in cash.

The KRW 10 billion RCPS addresses liquidity but comes with a conversion price adjustment to KRW 2,244 and a higher potential share count if performance targets are missed. On the business side, golf ball share, production capacity and the expanding park golf ball line are the growth pillars, but 2025 sales fell 17.0%.

On listing, the KOSDAQ market capitalization requirement and the pace of financial improvement are linked. A recent report from a credit rating agency offered no target price or investment opinion and pointed to technology upgrades and adjacent market entry as points to watch.

The items to track next are operating profit in the third-quarter 2026 report, the trend in cash and cash equivalents, and whether the performance conditions of the convertible preferred shares are met.
