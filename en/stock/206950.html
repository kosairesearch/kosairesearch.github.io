Report overview

Rebuilding After the Accounting Case

Volvik retains a distinctive colored-ball brand, but regaining credibility after an inventory-misstatement sanction and stabilizing finances after a KRW 10 billion capital raise remain the central tasks.

  1. 1

    For 2025, Volvik reported revenue of KRW 38.36 billion, operating profit of KRW 671 million and a net loss of KRW 2.029 billion. The company was profitable at the operating level but loss-making at the net level.

  2. 2

    The Securities and Futures Commission found that Volvik overstated year-end inventory from 2017 to 2021 by manipulating inbound and outbound quantities. On March 18, 2026, the Financial Services Commission imposed a fine of KRW 1.77 billion on the company.

  3. 3

    Trading in Volvik shares was suspended from January 22, 2026. Trading resumed on April 7, 2026, after the exchange determined that no delisting criterion applied.

  4. 4

    On September 2, 2026, Volvik decided on a KRW 10 billion third-party allotment of convertible redeemable preferred shares. The issue price of KRW 3,205 was set 91.1% above the reference price of KRW 1,677, and proceeds go to short-term debt repayment and working capital.

  5. 5

    Volvik is using a national park golf tournament to expand into park golf. New colored-ball and urethane-ball launches are continuing alongside.

02

Business structure

Volvik is a sporting-goods maker that listed on the KONEX market on December 30, 2015. It is known as the domestic brand that opened the colored golf ball market with its 2009 launch.

According to a trade-press report, Volvik held about 15% of the domestic golf ball market, second to Titleist, and close to 80% of the colored-ball segment. In the same report, exports were 16.8% of sales at KRW 7.6 billion, with the United States accounting for 79%.

The premium line centers on the Condor, described as the first domestic-brand urethane ball developed and produced entirely with in-house technology. In May 2026, Volvik launched the Velos Candy, an ultra-low-compression colored ball. The business is also widening into park golf.

On August 11, 2026, Volvik launched the LS Series premium park golf clubs. Apparel is run by a separate company, Wibis, under the Volvik Apparel label, and that business recorded sales of KRW 46 billion for 2025. Those figures are not part of Volvik's own results.

On the competitive front, global brands such as Titleist, Srixon and TaylorMade offer both tour-level balls and colored balls.

03

Recent trends

On a reported basis, 2025 revenue was KRW 38.36 billion, operating profit was KRW 671 million and the net loss was KRW 2.029 billion.

Press reports indicate that in 2023 Volvik posted revenue of KRW 45.4 billion and operating profit of KRW 2.0 billion, while in 2024 revenue rose to KRW 46.5 billion but an operating loss of KRW 1.7 billion emerged. Revenue in 2025 was lower than in 2023 and 2024, and operating results returned to profit.

The accounting matter dominated 2026. On January 21, 2026, the Securities and Futures Commission said Volvik had overstated inventory from 2017 to 2021. The overstatement reached KRW 17.751 billion in 2020 and KRW 15.556 billion in 2021, among other years. Trading in the shares was suspended from January 22, 2026.

On April 6, 2026, the listing committee decided to maintain the listing. Trading resumed on April 7. On March 26, 2026, CEO Hong Seung-seok resigned and Jang Seok-heon was appointed as the new CEO. The share price was KRW 1,795 on March 26, and on October 27 it stood at KRW 1,499, unchanged from the previous session. Trading value that day was only KRW 16,489, indicating very thin liquidity.

Outlook

According to Dealsite, KRW 4 billion of the proceeds repays short-term borrowings at a 10% annual rate and KRW 6 billion goes to working capital, which includes restructuring the U.S. subsidiary and expanding park golf production facilities. Lower interest costs could improve non-operating results.

Park golf is a new demand channel. Volvik aims to widen market contact through a national tournament while continuing to launch related products. 05

Bull factors

Brand Position in Colored Balls

A trade-press report says Volvik ranks second in the domestic golf ball market and holds close to 80% of the colored-ball segment. Large overseas brands also sell colored balls, but Volvik is known as the company that opened the category. The Condor S3 and S4 were renewed with a thinner Ultra Flex urethane cover. Brand recognition is an asset that supports pricing and distribution.

Funding and Room to Ease Financial Burden

The KRW 10 billion preferred-share issue secured fresh funding. Of this, KRW 4 billion goes to repaying short-term borrowings that carry a 10% annual rate. Largest shareholder TS Investment was allotted KRW 3 billion, and the Woori Venture and Everbest side also took part.

Lower interest costs could ease the financial burden, although the preferred-share redemption obligation remains.

Expansion into Park Golf

Volvik organized a national park golf event for up to 5,000 participants, described as the largest held by a golf equipment maker. Prize money is doubled for winners who use both Volvik clubs and balls. Pairing clubs and balls is a way to build equipment loyalty. How much this business contributes to earnings has not yet been confirmed in filings.

06

Bear factors

Damaged Accounting Credibility

Volvik overstated year-end inventory over five fiscal years by manipulating quantities, and it obstructed the external audit by submitting altered ledgers to the auditor. The Financial Services Commission fined the company KRW 1.78 billion and referred the company, its former CEO and a former executive to prosecutors.

The outcome of the criminal process and the possibility of further sanctions have not been confirmed. Restoring trust in past financial statements may take time.

Thin Profitability and Net Loss

Operating profit of KRW 671 million in 2025 was small relative to revenue of KRW 38.36 billion, and the net loss was KRW 2.029 billion. In 2024, an operating loss of KRW 1.7 billion arose even though revenue rose to KRW 46.5 billion. That means higher sales did not always translate into profit. Interest costs and inventory burdens appear to weigh on bottom-line results.

Financing With a Redemption Burden

The capital raise uses convertible redeemable preferred shares rather than common stock. If fully converted at the current conversion price, 3,120,123 common shares would be issued, equal to 17.53% of total shares after conversion. Investors may request early redemption from 2027. Conversion would dilute existing holders, while redemption requests would drain cash.

Risk factors

Macro

Golf ball sales depend on golf participation and consumer sentiment. Exports are concentrated in the United States, exposing Volvik to exchange rates and local economic conditions. In the trade-press report, exports were 16.8% of sales, with the United States accounting for 79% of that. Raw material and logistics costs could also affect margins, but specific figures were not confirmed.

Regulatory and Legal

The commission decided on a three-year auditor designation, dismissal-recommendation-level measures against the former CEO and a former executive, and a prosecution referral. The progress and outcome of the criminal process could affect management composition and reputation.

Trading was suspended in January 2026 after the company was designated for a substantive listing eligibility review. The listing was maintained, but disclosure compliance and internal-control improvements remain subject to scrutiny.

Liquidity and Financial

According to Dealsite, the U.S. subsidiary, Volvik USA, is in capital impairment and requires restructuring. As a KONEX stock, trading is thin, and trading value on October 27 was only KRW 16,489. Small orders can move the price, and trading at a desired time may be difficult. The possibility of early redemption requests on the preferred shares from 2027 also weighs on cash flow.

08

Overall view

Volvik is a KONEX-listed company whose business features include a strong colored-ball brand position and an expansion into park golf. In 2025 it kept operating profit positive at KRW 671 million but recorded a net loss of KRW 2.029 billion.

The year 2026 brought a sanction over inventory overstatement, a trading halt and resumption, a change of CEO, and a KRW 10 billion preferred-share issue in quick succession. The bullish side points to brand recognition, new product launches and secured funding.

The bearish side points to the accounting-credibility problem, thin profitability, the preferred-share redemption burden and sparse trading. Results after the first half of 2026 and the outcome of the criminal process would sharpen the picture once confirmed. Any investment judgment should be made by the reader, weighing these facts against personal risk tolerance.
