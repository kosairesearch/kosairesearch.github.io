Cable core plus wheel unit, earnings still volatile

02

Business structure

Dawon Cable manufactures and sells power cable, insulated wire and other cable products. In the 2025 annual report, its cable segment recorded sales of KRW 527.1 billion, and within that segment insulated wire accounted for 56%, power cable 35%, spiral cable 6%, telecom cable 2% and other products 1%.

Insulated wire is used mainly for internal wiring in vehicles and appliances, and a 2023 securities report noted its share rose from 46% in 2020 to 52% in 2022 after the company expanded automotive cable capacity in 2020.

Power cable is used for transmission and distribution, while spiral and telecom cable are mostly government-procured and carry lower profitability according to the same report.

In 2025 the company acquired the automotive aluminum wheel maker Daewon Altec as a subsidiary, so its sales now split between cable and wheel segments. Main subsidiaries include Daemyung Cable and Wihae Geumwon Cable.

Customers reportedly include Korea Electric Power, KT, Hyundai Motor and KIA, and a past media report put the combined share of Korea Electric Power and KT at about 15% of total sales. The export ratio was only 10.8% in 2024, which is lower than several listed peers.

Reliance on domestic automakers and domestic power procurement supports stability but also remains a structural challenge.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual revenue fell from KRW 562.4 billion in 2022 to KRW 515.4 billion in 2023 (down 8.4%), then rose to KRW 552.8 billion in 2024 (up 7.3%) and KRW 624.5 billion in 2025 (up 13.0%), a two-year run of increases with 2025 the largest year in 2022–2025.

Operating profit rose from KRW 1.35 billion in 2022 to KRW 13.1 billion in 2023 (up 875.4%), peaked at KRW 14.3 billion in 2024 (up 9.2%), and then fell to KRW 9.35 billion in 2025 (down 34.8%).

The operating margin climbed from 0.2% in 2022 to 2.5% in 2023 and 2.6% in 2024 before dropping to 1.5% in 2025, so revenue growth in 2025 did not carry through to profit.

Profit attributable to owners rose from KRW 1.35 billion in 2022 to KRW 9.61 billion in 2023 (up 609.9%), fell to KRW 6.79 billion in 2024 (down 29.3%) and recovered to KRW 8.78 billion in 2025 (up 29.2%), with 2023 the largest year in the period.

Net profit including non-controlling interests was KRW 12.9 billion in 2025, a different measure from profit attributable to owners. At end-2025 total equity was KRW 152 billion, of which KRW 129 billion belonged to owners of the parent, and total liabilities were KRW 194.4 billion.

The debt ratio, measured as total liabilities over total equity, fell from 168.5% in 2022 to 99.4% in 2024 and then rose to 127.9% in 2025.

Operating cash flow was KRW 31.8 billion in 2022, -KRW 5.89 billion in 2023, KRW 4.01 billion in 2024 and -KRW 30.1 billion in 2025, so cash generation turned sharply negative in 2025.

Quarterly revenue went from KRW 154.8 billion in Q3 2025 to KRW 167.1 billion in Q4 2025 (up 7.9% quarter on quarter), KRW 189.2 billion in Q1 2026 (up 13.3%) and KRW 278.1 billion in Q2 2026 (up 46.9%), then KRW 260.7 billion in Q3 2026 (down 6.3% quarter on quarter, up 68.4% year on year).

Quarterly operating profit went from KRW 1.11 billion in Q3 2025 to an operating loss of KRW 5.48 billion in Q4 2025, a swing to a loss, then KRW 14.7 billion in Q1 2026, a swing back to profit, followed by KRW 3.73 billion in Q2 2026 (down 74.6%) and KRW 9.16 billion in Q3 2026 (up 145.7% quarter on quarter, up 727.6% year on year).

Among the last five quarters, Q1 2026 had the largest operating profit. Profit attributable to owners swung to a net loss of KRW 1.13 billion in Q4 2025, peaked at KRW 8.58 billion in Q1 2026, and then declined for two consecutive quarters to KRW 4.06 billion in Q2 2026 and KRW 1.85 billion in Q3 2026.

The sum of profit attributable to owners over the latest four quarters (Q4 2025 to Q3 2026) was KRW 13.4 billion. Q2 2026 posted the sharpest revenue increase, yet operating profit was only KRW 3.73 billion, which shows a gap between sales expansion and margin.

The disclosed figures alone do not identify one-off items in individual quarters, and whether copper prices or product mix shifts drove quarterly margins was not confirmed in this review.

05

Industry analysis

The cable industry is exposed to power-grid investment and the spread of electric vehicles. A 2023 securities report expected demand for automotive insulated wire to grow with electric vehicles, alongside the wiring harness market.

Peer Taihan Cable & Solution reported preliminary Q3 2026 cumulative operating profit of KRW 152.1 billion on October 29, 2026, and disclosed that its order backlog reached KRW 4.31 trillion at the end of the third quarter, a record; these are peer figures, not Dawon Cable results.

Taihan attributed its results mainly to orders for extra-high-voltage and submarine cable projects. Dawon Cable's core lines are automotive insulated wire and power transmission cable, and this review did not confirm a material share of high-value extra-high-voltage or submarine projects in its disclosures.

Among domestic players, the 2023 securities report described Dawon Cable as a mid-sized cable maker ranked around sixth by sales. The export ratio was about 10.8% in 2024, so domestic power and automotive demand drive the business to a large degree.

Copper is the core raw material, and its price swings directly affect margins across the industry. The aluminum wheel business sits in the automotive parts market and is exposed to the same electrification and lightweighting trends.

Overall, Dawon Cable is a mid-sized domestic company whose mix of automotive wire and wheels differs from peers focused on large high-voltage projects.

Bull factors

Automotive insulated wire base

Insulated wire accounts for 56% of cable-segment sales, and its share rose after the 2020 expansion of automotive cable capacity. A 2023 securities report expected demand for automotive insulated wire to grow with electric vehicles. Supply relationships with automakers and wiring harness makers are cited as a strength.

Quarterly profit recovery in 2026

Q1 2026 operating profit of KRW 14.7 billion swung to profit, and Q3 2026 operating profit of KRW 9.16 billion rose 727.6% year on year. Q3 2026 revenue of KRW 260.7 billion was up 68.4% year on year. Compared with the Q4 2025 operating loss, recent quarterly profitability has moved in a better direction.

Diversification via subsidiary

The 2025 acquisition of the automotive aluminum wheel maker Daewon Altec split sales into cable and wheel segments. A 2025 media report cited the subsidiary's earnings as a driver of operating profit growth. Moving beyond a single cable business widens the company's earnings base.

Bear factors

Thin operating margin

The operating margin fell to 1.5% in 2025 from 2.6% in 2024. Revenue rose 13.0% while operating profit fell 34.8%. Weak profit conversion relative to sales is the main bear point.

Consecutive quarterly profit declines

Profit attributable to owners fell for two consecutive quarters, from KRW 8.58 billion in Q1 2026 to KRW 4.06 billion in Q2 and KRW 1.85 billion in Q3. Q3 2026 revenue was down 6.3% from Q2. Whether the recovered quarterly profit can be sustained is not yet confirmed.

Cash flow and balance sheet strain

Operating cash flow was negative in 2023 at -KRW 5.89 billion and in 2025 at -KRW 30.1 billion. Total liabilities stood at KRW 194.4 billion at end-2025, and the debt ratio rose to 127.9% from 99.4% in 2024. Periods in which profit does not translate into operating cash are a bear point.

Risk factors

Raw material prices

Copper prices directly affect cable manufacturing costs. The disclosures alone do not show how much copper price swings were reflected in quarterly margins. If price pass-through lags, the operating margin could fall further.

Customer and business concentration

Automotive wire is supplied to domestic automakers and wiring harness makers. Changes in vehicle production volumes or electrification schedules would affect orders. With the aluminum wheel business added, profitability differences between segments have widened.

Balance sheet and cash flow

Total equity was KRW 152 billion at end-2025, of which KRW 129 billion belonged to owners of the parent. Operating cash flow of -KRW 30.1 billion in 2025 could add pressure on working capital.

Borrowing levels were not confirmed in the disclosures reviewed, so the debt composition should be checked in the annual report.

Overall view

Dawon Cable combines a core cable business built on insulated and power cable with an automotive aluminum wheel unit added in 2025. Annual revenue reached KRW 624.5 billion in 2025, the largest year since 2022, yet the operating margin of 1.5% in 2025 was below 2.6% in 2024.

In 2026, operating profit swung to a KRW 14.7 billion profit in Q1 and recovered to KRW 9.16 billion in Q3, but profit attributable to owners declined for two consecutive quarters. Profit attributable to owners over the latest four quarters totaled KRW 13.4 billion.

Strengths include the automotive insulated wire base, the quarterly profit recovery and business diversification, while weaknesses include the thin margin, consecutive quarterly profit declines, negative operating cash flow and a high debt ratio.

The valuation sits above sector-average multiples and a large premium to net assets, so whether the profit recovery is sustained is central to interpretation. Peer Taihan Cable & Solution reported preliminary Q3 2026 cumulative operating profit of KRW 152.1 billion, but that is another company's figure.

The next checkpoints are the 2026 annual report, Q1 2027 results, and disclosures on automotive and power cable orders and capacity.
