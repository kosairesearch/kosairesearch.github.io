02

Business structure

Dawon Cable was founded in 1969 and listed in 1988 as a mid-sized cable maker. Its core business is the manufacture and sale of power cables and electric wires, and a securities data site describes it as holding a strong market share in insulated wire, giving it a stable revenue base.

It has several consolidated subsidiaries, including Daemyung Cable and Wihae Geumwon Cable. In 2025 it acquired the subsidiary Daewon Altec, adding the automotive aluminum wheel business to consolidated results.

Analysts cite growing demand for lightweight vehicles and wider electric vehicle adoption as supporting medium-term growth for aluminum wheels.

Power cables are tied to utility and industrial equipment orders, while aluminum wheels depend on the supply structure for automakers, so the two businesses face different demand patterns. Insulated wire volumes move with construction and facility investment.

Copper and other metal raw materials are passed into selling prices, so raw material price changes affect margins. Competitors include Taihan Cable & Solution, Gaon Cable and LS ELECTRIC, and Dawon Cable is classified as a mid-sized player among them.

A securities data aggregator reported that consolidated revenue and profit rose from a year earlier in the first half of 2026, which suggests the aluminum wheel consolidation added to scale.

Earnings analysis

Annual revenue for 2022 through 2025 was KRW 562.4 billion, KRW 515.4 billion, KRW 552.8 billion and KRW 624.5 billion.

Revenue fell 8.4% in 2023 to the low point of the period, then rose 7.3% in 2024 and 13.0% in 2025, so it grew for two consecutive years from 2024 through 2025. 2025 was the largest revenue year in the 2022 to 2025 span.

Operating profit rose 875.4% to KRW 13.1 billion in 2023 and reached KRW 14.3 billion in 2024, then fell 34.8% to KRW 9.35 billion in 2025. The operating margin moved from 0.2% in 2022 to 2.5% in 2023 and 2.6% in 2024 before dropping to 1.5% in 2025.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 9.61 billion in 2023, the largest of the period, fell 29.3% to KRW 6.79 billion in 2024, and rose 29.2% to KRW 8.78 billion in 2025.

Net income including non-controlling interests was KRW 12.9 billion in 2025, and the gap from the controlling-shareholder figure is read as the non-controlling share.

Total equity at the end of 2025 was KRW 152 billion, of which KRW 129 billion belonged to controlling shareholders, and total liabilities were KRW 194.4 billion. Operating cash flow swung from KRW 31.8 billion in 2022 to -KRW 5.89 billion in 2023, KRW 4.01 billion in 2024 and -KRW 30.1 billion in 2025.

Operating profit was positive in 2025, yet operating cash flow of -KRW 30.1 billion points to weak conversion of profit into cash.

By quarter, operating profit was KRW 1.11 billion in the third quarter of 2025, and the fourth quarter swung to an operating loss of KRW 5.48 billion, with a net loss attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 1.13 billion.

The first quarter of 2026 returned to profit with revenue of KRW 189.2 billion and operating profit of KRW 14.7 billion. Second-quarter revenue of KRW 278.1 billion rose 46.9% from the prior quarter, but operating profit fell 74.6% to KRW 3.73 billion, so the revenue gain did not carry through to profit.

Third-quarter revenue was KRW 260.7 billion, down 6.3% from the prior quarter, while operating profit of KRW 9.16 billion rose 145.7% from the prior quarter.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders has declined for two consecutive quarters, from the second quarter through the third quarter of 2026, to KRW 1.85 billion in the third quarter.

Nine-month operating profit of KRW 152.1 billion exceeded the 2025 full-year operating profit of KRW 128.6 billion. The company booked KRW 809.0 billion in new orders in the third quarter, lifting its cumulative backlog to KRW 4.3073 trillion, which it described as a record.

The orders were centered on extra-high-voltage cable projects in Europe, North America and Asia. Order momentum at this peer points to strong demand for extra-high-voltage and submarine cables. Dawon Cable is exposed to both the electric wire cycle and the automotive parts cycle through its aluminum wheel business.

Risk factors

Raw material prices

Copper and other metals are the main raw materials for cable products, and price moves shake margins depending on the lag in passing costs into selling prices. The second quarter of 2026 saw revenue rise sharply while operating profit fell, a pattern consistent with this structure. Raw material price swings remain a major driver of quarterly earnings changes.

Quarterly earnings volatility

Quarterly results have moved between loss and profit, as the operating and net losses in the fourth quarter of 2025 show. Even after the first-quarter 2026 return to profit, the size of quarterly earnings has not been steady. The possibility of renewed losses remains a risk.

Consolidation effect and ownership

The aluminum wheel business has contributed since its 2025 consolidation, but whether that effect persists and how well it is integrated will show up in later regular reports. The largest shareholder group, led by Gapdo Mulsan with five other holders, owns 30.43%, a high concentration.

Non-controlling interests are present, which creates a wide gap between net income and net income attributable to controlling shareholders.
