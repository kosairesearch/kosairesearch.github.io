02

Business structure

Kangwon Energy runs two main businesses, battery materials and plant equipment. The battery materials segment, operated through a subsidiary, supplies anhydrous lithium hydroxide to cathode makers.

The compound is a core raw material for lithium-ion battery cathodes, and because moisture content must stay at or below 0.1 percent, high-purity, low-moisture process technology is cited as a competitive edge.

As of March 2026, the sales mix stood at roughly 81 percent battery materials, 12 percent plant equipment such as industrial steam generators, and 4 to 5 percent battery equipment.

The plant business focuses on industrial steam generators, chemical process equipment and environmental energy facilities, with large domestic engineering and construction groups among its main customers.

The battery equipment unit designs cathode processes and supplies dryers and PD mixers, but delayed investment by downstream customers has kept new orders below expectations.

Materials results are tied to cathode makers' capacity expansion and utilization, while plant results depend on construction contractors' orders and the timing of project progress recognition.

Using piping design, construction and thermal management skills, the company plans to enter the AI data center cooling market, including cooling water distribution units, and has added permanent magnet recycling to its articles of incorporation. The company says new businesses have not yet reached the stage of contributing meaningful sales.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual sales rose from KRW 71.4 billion in 2022 to KRW 139.2 billion in 2023 (up 95.0% year on year) and KRW 224.4 billion in 2024 (up 61.2%), with 2024 the largest year of 2022 to 2025. Sales then fell 5.4% to KRW 212.3 billion in 2025.

Operating profit was KRW 2.62 billion in 2022, KRW 6.93 billion in 2023 (up 164.6%) and KRW 6.77 billion in 2024 (down 2.3%), before the company swung to an operating loss of KRW 6.1 billion in 2025. Operating margin moved from 5.0 percent in 2023 to 3.0 percent in 2024 and -2.9 percent in 2025.

Attributable net income went from KRW 5.26 billion in 2023 to an attributable net loss of KRW 582 million in 2024, and widened to an attributable net loss of KRW 8.47 billion in 2025, making two consecutive loss years.

The company attributed the 2025 weakness to delayed downstream investment from the EV slowdown and fewer plant orders, while its battery materials segment grew 34 percent that year.

Operating cash flow went from -KRW 56.72 million in 2024 to KRW 34.8 billion in 2025, a direction that differs from the net loss, so the components of that gap should be checked in the next annual report's cash flow statement.

Quarterly, Q3 2025 sales were KRW 37.3 billion with an operating loss of KRW 5.81 billion; Q4 2025 sales rose to KRW 50.7 billion (up 36.1% from the prior quarter) and operating income turned positive at KRW 111 million.

Q1 2026 sales were KRW 52.2 billion (up 2.9% quarter on quarter), but operating results swung to a loss of KRW 866 million. Q2 2026 sales were KRW 47.5 billion (down 9.0%), with the operating loss narrowing to KRW 94.53 million.

Q3 2026 sales rose to KRW 49.2 billion (up 32.0% year on year), but the operating loss widened to KRW 959 million from the prior quarter, while attributable net income turned positive at KRW 4.18 billion.

Because operating and attributable net results diverged, attributable net income for the last four quarters totals only KRW 2.55 billion.

05

Industry analysis

The battery industry is in a slowdown phase as EV demand has weakened, and customers have been delaying equipment investment. A company official said lithium materials have maintained relatively firm demand despite the investment pullback.

Separately, longer-term forecasts point to battery demand expanding as carbon neutrality and renewable energy transitions progress. Utilization at the anhydrous lithium hydroxide plant was in the mid-16 percent range in Q3 2025 and about 20 percent as of a March 2026 report, so asset utilization remains low.

In plants, the company says projects that were put on hold or delayed by oil and LNG price swings and weaker petrochemical investment are starting to resume, and aging facility replacement demand is expected.

In power, semiconductor fabs, synchronous condensers and small modular reactor collaborations have been cited as new opportunities.

On competition, the company points to its low-moisture process technology and plant design and construction capability as differentiators, while supply scale depends on the pace of customer capacity expansion.

Cathode equipment supply contracts worth KRW 43.9 billion in February 2023 and KRW 23.4 billion in February 2024 were reported, but execution has reportedly been delayed or scaled back due to the industry slowdown. Recovery in battery equipment orders therefore appears to depend on the pace of the industry's recovery.

06

Outlook

In its March 2026 annual results announcement, the company said large new customers secured in late 2025 would begin full-scale deliveries and existing customer volumes would expand in stages. The company expects operating leverage to take hold as anhydrous lithium hydroxide utilization rises sharply.

It also said cumulative lithium hydroxide orders in early 2026 were more than 2.5 times the year-earlier level.

In its Q1 2026 results announcement, the company expected orders for battery equipment and industrial plant to gain momentum from the second half, strengthening the earnings expansion trend alongside materials growth.

In disclosures, a corrected filing dated September 30, 2026 listed a cathode line supply contract of KRW 43.6 billion and a washing equipment contract of KRW 23.5 billion for a Gwangyang cathode project of a major Korean cathode maker.

A steam generation system contract disclosed on April 16, 2026, worth KRW 16.2 billion with a foreign engineering customer, lists a contract period through June 30, 2027. The company announced on November 13, 2026 that it is pursuing entry into AI data center cooling equipment, a new business.

The next checkpoints are the operating profit sign in the 2026 annual report, lithium hydroxide utilization, and whether battery equipment and plant orders translate into actual sales.

08

Bull factors

Steep growth in materials sales

Cumulative anhydrous lithium hydroxide sales reached KRW 119.1 billion for the first nine months of 2026, up 44.2%. In Q1 2026, battery materials sales rose about 65 percent year on year, offsetting much of the decline in equipment sales. The company said cumulative lithium hydroxide orders were more than 2.5 times the prior-year level.

Operating leverage from higher utilization

Utilization at the lithium hydroxide plant rose from the mid-16 percent range in Q3 2025 to about 20 percent in a March 2026 report. The company expects operating leverage to strengthen as utilization climbs. The Q3 2026 operating loss of KRW 959 million was smaller than a year earlier.

Backlog and new business pipeline

Backlog stood at KRW 43.5 billion at end-Q3 2025, mostly battery equipment and industrial plant equipment. 2026 disclosures include cathode-related contracts for a major Korean cathode maker's Gwangyang project and a KRW 16.2 billion steam generation contract with a foreign engineering firm. The company is widening its scope into AI data center cooling equipment.

09

Bear factors

Consecutive losses and a thin earnings base

Attributable net income swung to a KRW 582 million loss in 2024 and widened to a KRW 8.47 billion loss in 2025, making two consecutive loss years. Operating losses have continued for three straight quarters from Q1 through Q3 2026. Attributable net income over the last four quarters totals only KRW 2.55 billion.

Weak battery equipment and plant orders

Battery equipment supply contracts signed in 2023 and 2024 have reportedly been delayed or scaled back due to the industry slowdown. Plant sales fell from KRW 55.9 billion in 2023 to KRW 45.0 billion in 2024, and reached KRW 20.9 billion for the first nine months of 2025. A prolonged EV slowdown could delay a recovery in the equipment segment.

Leverage and ownership concentration

The debt-to-equity ratio was 129.0 percent at end-2025, after peaking at 303.4 percent in 2023. Of KRW 64 billion total equity at end-2025, KRW 34.5 billion belongs to controlling shareholders.

The controlling group holds 47.17 percent, and an August 2026 large-holding report cited a change in a share-backed loan agreement.

10

Risk factors

Customer demand and raw material prices

Materials sales move with cathode makers' production plans and EV demand. Lithium price swings can also affect profitability, and reports have cited rising lithium prices as a factor behind a recent share price rebound. If demand recovery slips, the timing of utilization gains could also slip.

Execution of new businesses

New businesses such as AI data center cooling equipment and permanent magnet recycling have not yet reached the stage of meaningful sales. The company amended its articles in 2025 to add business lines, but upfront investment costs were cited as one reason for the Q3 2025 earnings deterioration. Whether these investments convert into profit remains to be confirmed.

Balance sheet and cash flow interpretation

Total liabilities at end-2025 were KRW 82.6 billion against total equity of KRW 64 billion. Operating cash flow was positive in 2025 even though operating and net results were both negative, so the gap has to be traced through line items. The cause of that gap can be checked in the cash flow statement of the next annual report.

12

Overall view

Kangwon Energy is holding up its top line through growth in battery materials, but weaker plant and equipment results and a deterioration in earnings since 2024 leave its earnings base thin.

The 2025 operating loss of KRW 6.1 billion and attributable net loss of KRW 8.47 billion show that materials growth did not fully offset the contraction in equipment.

Operating losses have continued from Q1 through Q3 2026, but Q3 sales of KRW 49.2 billion were up 32.0 percent year on year, so the top line is larger than a year earlier.

Bullish factors are expanding lithium hydroxide demand, cumulative orders and room for higher utilization, while bearish factors are consecutive operating losses, delayed equipment orders, leverage, and execution risk in new businesses.

On valuation, the multiple screens high on a near-zero recent earnings base and carries a large premium to net asset value, so whether operating improvements translate into profit is the central question.

The company expects equipment orders to recover from the second half and is pursuing AI cooling equipment, and whether these show up in reported results is the next step.

The next checkpoints are the sign of full-year operating profit in the 2026 annual report, utilization and equipment sales in Q1 2027 results, and plant deliveries as the steam generation contract period ends on June 30, 2027.
