02

Business structure

NP was listed on KOSDAQ in 2021 and merged with Wisewig Studio in 2026, after which the company took the NP name in its current form. Its business has three arms.

The first is brand experience and marketing, covering experiential content planning and production and XR-based virtual content, operated through the NP XR STAGE studio.

The company said large projects such as the Armed Forces Day ceremony and the launch ceremony of the Daeho Kim Jong-seo vessel drove growth in this unit in the third quarter of 2026. The second is global corporate projects built on CG and VFX capabilities, which the company said have been confirmed.

The third is content IP, expanding into the MUA mind-care solution built on emotion-analysis AI and short-form drama.

The film Seven O'Clock Breakfast for Heartbroken People is confirmed for a December release, and the YouTube content New Woman, run by subsidiary A2G Entertainment, links content to product sales with single-episode merchandise sales reaching several hundred million won.

The core of revenue appears to be the project-based brand experience business, where advertising and immersive content revenue swings with contract wins. Competition overlaps with XR and virtual production firms and event video producers, while the content business is compared with film and drama producers.

Standalone third-quarter 2026 revenue of KRW 11.1 billion and operating profit of KRW 450 million cover a different scope than consolidated results and are hard to read side by side.

04

Earnings analysis

On a disclosed basis, annual revenue went from KRW 39.8 billion in 2022 to KRW 35.3 billion in 2023 (down 11.3% year on year), KRW 29.6 billion in 2024 (down 16.3%) and KRW 27.2 billion in 2025 (down 7.9%), marking three straight years of decline.

Operating losses were KRW 960 million in 2022, KRW 3.7 billion in 2023, KRW 4.15 billion in 2024 and KRW 3.05 billion in 2025, a four-year run of losses that widened in 2023 and 2024 before narrowing in 2025. Operating margins were -2.4%, -10.5%, -14.0% and -11.2% from 2022 to 2025.

Net loss attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 1.32 billion in 2022, KRW 2.37 billion in 2023, KRW 175 million in 2024 and KRW 3.87 billion in 2025, while net loss including non-controlling interests was KRW 3.84 billion in 2025.

The debt ratio rose to 64.2% at end-2025 from 58.7% in 2022, with total equity of KRW 28.9 billion, of which KRW 26.2 billion belongs to controlling shareholders.

Operating cash flow was negative in 2022 (-KRW 332 million), 2024 (-KRW 1.56 billion) and 2025 (-KRW 1.76 billion), with only 2023 positive at KRW 438 million. Quarterly, revenue rose from KRW 5.8 billion in Q3 2025 to KRW 7.9 billion in Q4, when operating profit of KRW 767 million turned the quarter profitable.

Revenue then fell 40.0% to KRW 4.74 billion in Q1 2026, rose to KRW 8.27 billion in Q2 and reached KRW 46.3 billion in Q3, two consecutive quarters of growth. Operating losses were KRW 1.97 billion, KRW 1.9 billion and KRW 2.89 billion in Q1 to Q3 2026, three straight loss quarters with the loss widening again in Q3.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders turned positive at KRW 3.67 billion in Q3 2026, which came alongside a wider operating loss, so non-operating items likely played a large role and should be checked in the periodic report.

The standalone Q3 2026 figures announced on November 12 show a profit that the consolidated figures do not, reflecting the difference in scope.

05

Industry analysis

The company is classified under Machinery and Equipment, but its actual business is brand experience and XR content production alongside video and IP operations.

Advertising and immersive content markets see revenue swing with project wins and sensitivity to economic slowdowns, which lines up with the company's quarterly volatility.

Revenue from VFX and new media at Wisewig Studio, a pre-merger subsidiary, fell from KRW 10.68 billion in 2024 to KRW 7.02 billion in 2025, showing that weaker production demand weighed on results. Meanwhile, the share of content business in that group rose over the same period, according to press reports.

The third-quarter 2026 jump in revenue from large event and public-sector projects illustrates the dependence on project wins. Competitive positioning rests on XR studio and video production capability and on the ability to bind IP to commerce.

Peers in the listed universe are mostly robotics, construction machinery and equipment makers, so direct comparison is limited. In content markets, linking commerce and securing short-form IP are cited as main monetization routes.

Since the company emphasizes AI-driven production efficiency, whether lower production costs translate into margin gains will separate its position from the broader industry.

Commerce-linked content is presented as a way to broaden the revenue base. Confirmed global corporate projects were cited as an additional revenue source, but their scale and timing were not specified in the available materials.

Second, whether operating losses break a three-quarter loss streak. Third, whether operating cash flow in the 2026 annual report moves away from the outflows seen in 2024 and 2025. Whether commerce and IP revenue becomes visible as a disclosed line item is also worth tracking.

08

Bull factors

Standalone Profit in Q3

The company said standalone operating profit turned positive at KRW 450 million in Q3 2026. It credited the brand experience and marketing unit for leading the improvement. Fixed-cost reduction from merging organizations and infrastructure, along with AI-based production efficiency, were cited as profit drivers. Whether the same trend appears on a consolidated basis is a point for the periodic report.

Quarterly Revenue Scale-Up

Consolidated revenue in Q3 2026 reached KRW 46.3 billion, far above KRW 5.8 billion in Q3 2025. From a low of KRW 4.74 billion in Q1 2026, revenue rose to KRW 8.27 billion in Q2 and KRW 46.3 billion in Q3, two straight quarters of growth.

The company attributed part of the gain to large projects such as the Armed Forces Day ceremony and the Daeho Kim Jong-seo launch ceremony. Whether this scale repeats will be gauged in Q4 2026 results.

Turn to Profit at Controlling-Shareholder Level

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders reached KRW 3.67 billion in Q3 2026, reversing a KRW 1.83 billion loss in Q3 2025. Because the operating loss widened to KRW 2.89 billion in the same quarter, non-operating items appear to have played a large role.

Total equity at end-2025 was KRW 28.9 billion, with KRW 26.2 billion attributable to controlling shareholders. Whether the profit holds beyond one quarter is the next test.

09

Bear factors

Four Straight Loss Years and a Wider Q3 Loss

Operating losses ran for four consecutive years from 2022 through 2025, with a KRW 3.05 billion loss in 2025. The Q3 2026 operating loss of KRW 2.89 billion widened from the prior quarter, extending the loss run to three quarters. Despite much higher revenue, profit did not follow, which is the main weakness. Whether the cost structure absorbs revenue gains is the key question.

Project Dependence and Cash Outflows

Revenue from advertising and immersive content depends on project wins and is vulnerable to economic slowdowns. Operating cash flow was -KRW 1.56 billion in 2024 and -KRW 1.76 billion in 2025, continuing outflows.

Q1 2026 revenue fell 40.0% quarter on quarter to KRW 4.74 billion, showing how quickly results move when project wins gap. Without a recovery in cash generation, room for expansion remains constrained.

Major Holder Stake Sale and Supply Overhang

Com2us, a holder of more than 10%, sold about 1.08 million shares off-market on November 3, 2026, reducing its stake from 31.31% to 26.81%. The disclosed buyer was Red Eyes Studio. Whether the remaining 26.81% is sold later must be tracked through disclosures. Changes in ownership structure could act as a supply-side burden on the shares.

Risk factors

Business Structure Risk

A large share of revenue depends on project-based wins, causing sharp quarterly swings. Moving from KRW 5.8 billion in Q3 2025 to KRW 46.3 billion in Q3 2026 shows low predictability. If large projects cluster in a single quarter, profit improvement may look temporary. Because the project pipeline is not quantified in public materials, sustainability is hard to gauge.

Subsidiary and IP Monetization Risk

Revenue from VFX and new media at Wisewig Studio fell from KRW 10.68 billion in 2024 to KRW 7.02 billion in 2025. Content IP and commerce linkage depend partly on episode-level results, as with New Woman. If the film release and follow-on titles underperform, the IP monetization plan could slip. The effect of subsidiary results and consolidation adjustments on group profit also needs monitoring.

Integration and Ownership Risk

The 2026 merger is still in the process of integrating NP and Wisewig Studio's organizations and infrastructure. Integration costs and staff reallocation could affect the profit structure.

With a major holder reducing its stake, governance and management direction could also change, and shareholder composition shifts affect float and trading supply.

Overall view

NP's third quarter of 2026 showed a change in both scale and earnings structure after the merger, with revenue rising to KRW 46.3 billion and net income attributable to controlling shareholders turning positive.

However, the operating loss widened to KRW 2.89 billion in the same quarter, extending the consolidated operating loss run to three quarters. On a disclosed basis, operating losses have continued for four consecutive years and revenue has declined for three straight years since 2023.

Strengths include merger synergies, large project revenue and an equity base of meaningful size. Weaknesses include reliance on project wins, cash outflows and supply-side effects from a major holder's stake reduction.

The company announced a standalone profit, but the scope differs from consolidated results, making direct comparison difficult. The next checkpoints are Q4 2026 results, the performance of the December film release, and operating cash flow in the 2026 annual report.
