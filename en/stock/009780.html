02

Business structure

MSC was founded in 1974 to supply food additives and now runs two business lines: food materials and semiconductor surface-treatment chemicals. On the food side, it supplies stabilizers such as carrageenan and agar, natural colorants such as gardenia-based pigments, and seasonings to food makers in Korea and abroad.

The 2024 domestic and export breakdown listed fish paste products, colorants, beverage products and seasonings, with fish paste products carrying the highest export share at 61.33%. The company is also widening its scope into health functional food ingredients and functional food materials.

In 2026 press coverage, MSC is described as a semiconductor chemicals maker based in the Namdong National Industrial Complex in Incheon. Its core story is the domestic development of plating chemicals that were previously fully imported.

The company describes itself as one of the top three global surface-treatment chemical makers for semiconductors, which is its own characterization. Amkor is cited as a major customer, and MSC received a conformity determination in Amkor's RBA assessment in the United States.

Overseas sales are expanding into Taiwan and China. Market data sites classify the company as a functional food material maker, while the exchange sector is KOSDAQ food and tobacco.

The structure therefore combines food materials and semiconductor chemicals, and the latest segment revenue split is best confirmed from the segment disclosures in the periodic reports.

04

Earnings analysis

On a disclosed basis, annual revenue fell from KRW 204.3 billion in 2022 to KRW 189 billion in 2023 (down 7.5% year on year), then rose to KRW 189.8 billion in 2024 (up 0.4%) and KRW 197.7 billion in 2025 (up 4.2%), marking two consecutive years of growth. 2022 was the largest year in the period and 2023 the smallest.

Operating profit fell from KRW 24.7 billion in 2022 to KRW 23.7 billion in 2023 (down 4.0%), then rose to KRW 27.5 billion in 2024 (up 16.1%) and KRW 29.7 billion in 2025 (up 7.8%), with 2025 the largest year in the period.

Operating margin rose from 12.1% in 2022 to 12.5% in 2023 even as revenue declined, and then reached 14.5% in 2024 and 15.0% in 2025. Profit growth outpaced revenue growth in the 2024 and 2025, so margin improvement was a major source of earnings growth.

Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders went from KRW 18.1 billion in 2022 to KRW 18.5 billion in 2023 (up 1.9%), KRW 21.8 billion in 2024 (up 17.8%) and KRW 23 billion in 2025 (up 5.7%), rising for three consecutive years from 2023.

Net profit including non-controlling interests shows the same yearly amounts, so there is no gap between the two lines. Operating cash flow was KRW -7.76 billion in 2022, then KRW 24.2 billion in 2023, KRW 33.5 billion in 2024 and KRW 28.2 billion in 2025, staying positive since 2023 but declining in 2025 from 2024.

The debt ratio moved from 59.4% in 2022 to 48.6% in 2023, 53.0% in 2024 and 46.5% in 2025. Total equity at end-2025 was KRW 189.2 billion, all of it attributable to controlling shareholders, against total liabilities of KRW 88 billion.

Quarterly revenue went from KRW 51.8 billion in Q3 2025 and operating profit of KRW 7.21 billion to KRW 46.8 billion (down 9.7% quarter on quarter) and KRW 5.12 billion in Q4 2025, then KRW 48.2 billion (up 3.0%) and KRW 8.03 billion in Q1 2026, and KRW 56.3 billion (up 16.8%) and KRW 11.7 billion in Q2 2026.

In Q3 2026, revenue was KRW 50.9 billion (down 9.6% quarter on quarter and down 1.8% year on year) while operating profit fell to KRW 5.1 billion (down 56.3% quarter on quarter and down 29.3% year on year).

Because the profit decline was much larger than the revenue decline, the quarterly margin dropped, and the drivers should be traced through cost lines in the periodic reports.

Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders peaked at KRW 9.69 billion in Q2 2026 and was KRW 4.35 billion in Q3 2026, with KRW 25.8 billion over the last four quarters.

05

Industry analysis

The end markets fall into two groups. In semiconductors, the core demand is surface-treatment chemicals used in packaging and electronic component production, and MSC emphasizes that it localized plating chemicals that had been fully imported.

Demand on the chip side moves with customers' production and equipment investment, which makes it an external driver of quarterly revenue. Raw material approval and notification procedures slow the introduction of new technologies, and the CEO has said these steps can take months and substantial cost.

On the food side, demand for natural ingredients and functional materials is growing. The company's own description cites rising demand for fortifying ingredients, and it aims to enter the health functional food and HMR markets as well as vegan and halal markets.

Within the KOSDAQ food and tobacco sector, food-material rivals are other food additive and ingredient makers, while in semiconductor chemicals the rivals are overseas surface-treatment chemical companies.

The EcoVadis Gold rating and the RBA conformity determination from Amkor are signals that the company meets large customers' supply-chain certification requirements. Its competitive position within the sector depends on the mix of the two businesses.

In food materials, raw material stability and certifications matter, while in semiconductor chemicals, customer qualification and localization capability are the basis of competitiveness.

Outlook

Confirmed plans include the start of construction on a new site in Yangsan, South Gyeongsang Province, aimed at health functional food and HMR markets, alongside entry into vegan and halal markets and natural-materials R&D.

The completion and start-up timing is best tracked through capital expenditure lines in the periodic reports. Overseas, CEO Kim Dong-hoon has said the company is steadily preparing to enter Texas in the United States, and press coverage reports expansion into Taiwan and China.

On ESG, MSC received an EcoVadis Gold rating in June 2026 and is pursuing RBA certification tied to Amkor's standards. The company also received a presidential commendation on May 19, 2026, which adds external recognition of its technology and social responsibility.

On earnings, the key question is whether the slowdown after the Q2 2026 peak continues in Q4 2026 and Q1 2027. Whether FY2026 operating profit exceeds KRW 29.7 billion in 2025 is the annual benchmark.

Once the new food-side site operates, the scale of health functional food ingredient revenue will be the measure of structural change. On the chip side, the challenge is how far the company broadens its customers and products after localizing plating chemicals.

Bear factors

Quarterly profit volatility

Operating profit in Q3 2026 was KRW 5.1 billion, down 56.3% quarter on quarter and 29.3% year on year. Revenue in the same quarter fell only 1.8% year on year, so profit fell far more than sales.

Profit attributable to controlling shareholders over the last four quarters totals KRW 25.8 billion, with wide gaps between quarters. Until earnings persistence is confirmed, it is hard to use as a bullish argument.

Modest top-line growth

Revenue in 2025 at KRW 197.7 billion remained below the KRW 204.3 billion of 2022. After a 7.5% decline in 2023, growth was 0.4% in 2024 and 4.2% in 2025, a gradual recovery. Q3 2026 revenue also declined year on year. If top-line growth keeps lagging profit growth for long, it becomes a bearish point.

Business focus and approval burden

With food materials and semiconductor chemicals under one roof, the business focus can look diffuse. The CEO has said raw material approval and notification procedures take months and substantial cost. Operating cash flow was KRW -7.76 billion in 2022, so cash generation stability should be judged on recent years.

Risk factors

Customer concentration and chip demand swings

If a large share of revenue comes from a few customers such as Amkor, changes in their production plans pass directly into MSC's sales. Chip packaging demand moves with equipment investment and inventory cycles. Customer diversification is best confirmed from customer disclosures in the periodic reports. Quarterly revenue swings can widen when customer concentration is high.

Regulation and raw material approvals

New raw material approval and notification procedures delay product launches and raise costs. Food materials are subject to food regulation, while semiconductor chemicals must satisfy customers' quality certification requirements. The CEO's call for easing rules points to an issue shared across the industry. Delays in approvals could push back the timing of revenue from new businesses.

Earnings volatility and small-cap traits

As a small-cap KOSDAQ name, MSC can see quarterly earnings swings amplified in its share price. A sharp quarterly profit drop, such as the Q3 2026 decline, can weigh heavily on sentiment. When institutional and foreign participation is limited, price moves can become larger. These traits can drive price changes separately from the earnings trend.
