02

Business structure

SK Securities No.14 Special Purpose Acquisition Company is a shell formed solely to merge with an unlisted company, and it has no product or service revenue of its own. It was established on May 13, 2026, is headquartered in Yeongdeungpo-gu, Seoul, and has four employees.

It raised KRW 13.0 billion through its offering at KRW 2,000 per share. The listed share count is 6.67 million, with paid-in capital of KRW 667 million and a par value of KRW 100. The deposit ratio for offering proceeds is 100%, the depository is KB Kookmin Bank, and SK Securities acted as lead underwriter.

Target sectors under review include renewable energy, biopharma and medical devices, IT convergence systems, robotics, advanced materials, auto parts and IT/semiconductors, along with entertainment and premium food. The largest shareholder is Inhwa Investment, with a 2.2% stake.

If no merger target is found within three years of listing, the shares are delisted and the company is dissolved, with principal and modest interest returned to investors. Early on, therefore, the merger process and the value of the eventual target matter more than any operating result.

Most companies grouped in the same sector classification are operating businesses such as paper makers and education firms, so the more meaningful peers are other brokerage-sponsored SPACs.

Recent trends

The company is only just listed, so there are no annual or quarterly results to report and no period over which revenue or operating profit can be compared. At its establishment date, total assets were KRW 1.994 billion, total liabilities KRW 1.548 billion and shareholders' equity KRW 445 million.

The shares listed on KOSDAQ on October 27, 2026 and ended their first session at KRW 1,946, 2.70% below the KRW 2,000 offering price. They rose as high as KRW 4,160 early in the session but kept falling and failed to hold the area around the offering price.

The volatility interruption mechanism did not apply on the listing day. At the time of writing, the price is KRW 1,890, market capitalization is KRW 13.0 billion, and trading value is recorded at KRW 271.9 billion.

04

Outlook

The key events ahead are finding a merger target, the exchange's preliminary listing review and shareholder approval. Because these steps are required, whether and when a merger is completed is cited as a main driver of the share price.

As regulators and the exchange raise KOSDAQ delisting market-cap requirements in stages, small unlisted companies increasingly prefer a regular listing or delay listing rather than a SPAC merger.

Nine companies listed on KOSDAQ via SPAC mergers from 2025 through 2026, and as of June 23, 2026 seven of them closed below their merger price.

From April 1, 2026, the Korea Financial Investment Association excluded SPAC offerings from priority allocation for lock-up commitments, easing the institutional book-building burden on underwriters.

There have been periods when venture-support policies and hopes for an IPO market recovery drew interest to the SPAC group. Sector-wide interest does not, however, guarantee that any individual SPAC will complete a merger.

For this company, the identity of a target, its business fit and the timing of merger terms all remain to be confirmed within the three-year window. If no merger is reached, the deposit-backed return of principal applies, so merger progress and the dissolution timeline will draw more attention as the deadline nears.

Bull factors

Deposit-backed principal return structure

The deposit ratio for offering proceeds is 100%, with KB Kookmin Bank as depository. If the company dissolves because no merger is reached within three years, principal and modest interest are returned to investors.

This sets it apart from an operating company, because a recovery mechanism at around the offering price exists even if no merger occurs. The timing of any recovery, however, may fall close to the end of the three-year window.

Broad range of target sectors

Target sectors range from renewable energy, biotech, IT convergence, robotics, advanced materials, auto parts and semiconductors to entertainment and premium food. The sector scope is not narrow, which leaves a wide pool of candidate companies. SK Securities served as lead underwriter.

The brokerage's investment banking network could be used to source targets, although no specific candidate has been identified.

Policy easing and sector interest

The association's decision to exclude SPAC offerings from priority allocation for lock-up commitments reduced the institutional book-building burden on brokerages. Amid venture-support policies and hopes for an IPO recovery, there were periods when interest continued in SPAC shares looking for merger targets.

The rule change can be seen as partly supporting new SPAC listings. The same reporting cautioned, however, that a price rise alone does not confirm merger prospects.

06

Bear factors

No target and no operating results

The company has no revenue or operating profit, and in the searches conducted no merger target has been disclosed. Because it does not aim to generate profit, results and growth are hard to assess, and such shells typically draw limited investor attention until a target is found.

With no business metrics to explain the price, it relies heavily on flows and sentiment. As the deadline approaches, market pressure to name a target may also grow.

Sharp early price swings

The price, which rose as high as KRW 4,160 intraday on the listing day, kept falling and closed below the offering price. Commentary has cautioned that newly listed SPACs have no momentum to support the share price. The current price of KRW 1,890 is below the KRW 2,000 offering price.

Trading value of KRW 271.9 billion against market capitalization of KRW 13.0 billion shows how large the short-term trading share was.

Tougher merger conditions, low success rate

Reports say many domestic SPACs are simply waiting out the three-year term because they cannot find a suitable company to merge with. One article's headline put the success rate at only 38.5%, noting that SPACs liquidate if no target is found within three years.

Of the nine recent SPAC-merger listings, seven closed below their merger price as of June 23, 2026. These conditions also leave post-merger share performance uncertain.

07

Risk factors

Macro

Prolonged high interest rates were cited as a core factor behind the slump, because they lowered the expected return that makes SPAC mergers attractive. Unlisted companies' appetite for SPACs can shift with interest rates and IPO market conditions.

Analysts also say that a higher approval rate for regular IPOs has reduced companies' need for a SPAC listing. If this continues, competition to secure merger targets could become tougher.

Regulatory and industry

The staged increase in KOSDAQ delisting market-cap requirements is assessed as having dealt the decisive blow to the SPAC market. Reports also say that even when a target is found, passing the exchange review has become harder. Review delays or rejections could push back the merger timeline or derail it altogether. Depending on how rules evolve, post-merger continuation requirements may also change.

Company-specific

The listing is cancelled if no merger is completed within three years. SK Securities No.9 SPAC, from the same brokerage group, was delisted after failing to file a preliminary merger-listing review application. The case shows that group affiliation does not guarantee a completed merger. With only four employees, the company relies on the underwriter's network for target sourcing.

08

Overall view

SK Securities No.14 Special Purpose Acquisition Company is a pre-merger SPAC that listed on October 27, 2026 and has no operating results to evaluate. Its KRW 13.0 billion in offering proceeds are fully deposited at KB Kookmin Bank, and it faces delisting and dissolution if no merger is completed within three years.

On the first trading day the share price swung widely between an intraday KRW 4,160 and a KRW 1,946 close, and trading value exceeded 20 times market capitalization. The bullish side points to the deposit-backed principal return structure, the broad range of target sectors and some policy easing.

The bearish side points to the absence of a target, large early price swings and a market environment with a low merger success rate. Items to monitor are whether a target is disclosed, progress of the exchange review and the merger terms, and this report offers no view on the direction of the share price.
