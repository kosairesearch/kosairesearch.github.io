02

Business structure

SK Securities No.14 Special Purpose Acquisition Company is a SPAC, a shell company formed solely to merge with an unlisted operating company. Because it uses offering proceeds to search for a target, it has no product or service segments and no segment revenue mix to report.

Its value will be set by the business quality and merger valuation of the private company it eventually combines with, so current operating metrics offer limited insight.

The company has 6,670,000 listed shares outstanding, and SK Securities Co., Ltd, the de facto controlling shareholder, reported 1,000,000 shares, or 13.05%, comprising 5,000 common shares and 995,000 convertible bonds.

The report, dated November 3, 2026, records these holdings as acquired at the October 27, 2026 listing, at KRW 1,000 per unit, funded from SK Securities' own capital of KRW 1 billion. Mason Capital Co. reported 500,000 shares, or 6.98%, as of October 29, 2026, for passive investment purposes.

Press reports indicate that in 2025 more than 90% of SPAC mergers chose the absorption structure, in which the SPAC is dissolved and the operating company survives, which lets the operating company keep its existing corporate identity and business registration.

Within the sector, this SPAC differs fundamentally from operating financial holding companies such as KB Financial Group and Shinhan Financial Group, which earn deposit, lending and fee income.

Reports say Korea Exchange began internal discussions in April 2026 on allowing multiple SPACs to merge with one unlisted company, which would widen the choice of merger partners.

03

Recent trends

On a cumulative basis for the third quarter of 2026, the company reported an operating loss of KRW 3.84 million and a net loss of KRW 19.48 million. As a SPAC with no operating revenue, these losses mainly reflect the cost of maintaining the listed shell, which is an interpretation rather than a disclosed breakdown.

The figures describe a pre-merger shell and do not represent the performance of any operating business. The shares began trading on KOSDAQ on October 27, 2026, with a first trade of KRW 1,946 against a KRW 2,000 offering price.

Press reports say the stock reached as high as KRW 4,160 intraday before declining and closing below the offering price.

On October 28, 2026, the Korea Exchange designated the stock as an investment warning issue because a single account recorded net selling of 723,023 shares, equal to 10.84% of listed shares, on a day when the close fell more than 5%.

The designation applied for one day, and the account had no same-reason designations in the prior 5 or 15 days. As of 04:45 KST on November 19, 2026, the share price was KRW 1,882, up 0.16% from the prior close, with market capitalization of KRW 12.6 billion and trading value of KRW 94,828,733.

Industry data show the SPAC merger success rate fell from 68% in 2024 to about 39% in 2025, a decline of more than 30 percentage points. Also in 2025, 24 SPACs were delisted after failing to find a merger partner, roughly three times the eight recorded in 2024.

04

Outlook

The key variable for this SPAC is whether it identifies a merger target, since a merger must be completed within three years of the October 27, 2026 listing date for the listing to be maintained. If the merger fails, the listing is terminated and offering proceeds are returned to holders with interest.

Among 78 SPACs seeking a merger at the end of 2025, those in their second year after listing made up the largest share at 44%, so many SPACs are approaching their deadlines. Industry participants expect that allowing multiple-SPAC mergers would widen the choice of merger partners.

In contrast, as of September 2026, only five companies had listed on KOSDAQ through SPAC mergers, and reports project this could mark the lowest level since four in 2022. Reports also note that higher market capitalization requirements for KOSDAQ delisting have raised the delisting risk across the SPAC sector.

If the absorption structure remains dominant, the valuation of the operating company and the fairness of the merger price will be the main points verifiable through disclosures.

The next disclosure points to confirm are whether a merger agreement is signed, changes in major shareholder stakes, and the trend in investment warning designations.

A three-year merger window measured from the listing date still remains, leaving time to pursue a merger process.

Additional institutional holder reported

Supply of SPACs and merger reform discussions

If such a measure takes effect, unlisted companies would have a wider set of SPAC partners to combine with.

Bear factors

No operating base and limited earnings visibility

Because a SPAC is not formed to generate profit, its value is difficult to assess through earnings or growth, a point often raised in commentary. The cumulative third-quarter 2026 figures of KRW 3.84 million operating loss and KRW 19.48 million net loss reflect the running costs of a pre-merger shell. Until a merger is completed, reported financials alone offer little basis for the company's value.

Falling merger success and more delistings

The SPAC merger success rate fell to about 39% in 2025 from 68% in 2024, a drop of more than 30 percentage points. Also in 2025, 24 SPACs were delisted after failing to find merger partners, roughly three times the eight in 2024.

As of September 2026, only five SPAC mergers had produced KOSDAQ listings, narrowing the route to merger completion.

Concentrated single-account selling and price swings

On October 28, 2026, net selling from a single account reached 10.84% of listed shares, leading to an investment warning designation. On the listing day the stock touched KRW 4,160 intraday before closing below the KRW 2,000 offering price. Such concentrated trading and volatility are cited as a burden for general shareholders.

Risk factors

Macro

The Financial Supervisory Service attributed the decline in SPAC listings to a strong stock market, which made regular IPOs relatively more attractive to companies. Reports cite different 2025 counts of new SPAC listings, 25 by the regulator and 11 on another basis. A preference for direct listings could limit both SPAC supply and the pool of merger targets.

Regulatory

Reports say higher market capitalization thresholds for KOSDAQ delisting have raised the risk of SPAC removals. Korea Exchange is reportedly considering measures to activate SPAC mergers, including multi-SPAC combinations. The direction and timing of these rule changes could affect both merger feasibility and continued listing.

Merger valuation

The company's value will be set by the business quality and merger price of the unlisted company it combines with. If the absorption structure remains dominant, the fairness of the merger price and the valuation of the operating company become the key points to verify through disclosures.

Disagreement over the target's valuation could delay the merger schedule. If no merger is completed within three years of the listing date, the listing will be terminated and offering proceeds will be returned with interest.

08

Overall view

SK Securities No.14 Special Purpose Acquisition Company has operated as a shell with no business activity since its October 27, 2026 listing, and its cumulative third-quarter 2026 DART figures show an operating loss of KRW 3.84 million and a net loss of KRW 19.48 million.

Positive factors include the 13.05% stake held by the de facto controlling shareholder, a three-year merger window from the listing date, and regulatory discussions aimed at widening merger options.

Negative factors include the lack of an operating base that makes earnings a weak measure of value, a sector merger success rate that fell to about 39% in 2025, and only five SPAC merger listings as of September 2026.

The one-day investment warning on October 28, 2026, triggered by concentrated single-account selling, illustrates short-term supply volatility. The higher market capitalization threshold for KOSDAQ delisting adds institutional risk if a merger fails.

The company's value ultimately depends on whether a merger target is secured and whether the merger price is fair, and the outcome cannot be readily gauged from current disclosures alone.
