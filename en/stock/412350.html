02

Business structure

Laserssel makes back-end semiconductor equipment that uses lasers to bond and reflow chip components. Its core is area-laser technology, which heats a surface rather than a point or line, and the company says this approach can solve warpage problems in conventional mass reflow processes.

Key tools include the LSR 300 FOPLP for large-panel packaging, the LSR area-laser bonder, and the LCB laser compression bonder that adds a pressing function to bonding. Applications are widening from CPO modules and FOPLP to FC-BGA AI substrates and laser sintering for SiC power semiconductors.

Customers include domestic semiconductor firms, outsourced assembly and test (OSAT) providers and memory probe card makers, while overseas customers in Taiwan, Singapore and Japan have been reported. Reports said domestic and overseas customers each accounted for roughly half of 2025 sales.

In 2026, reports said the company won a KRW 3.1 billion FOPLP LSR order from global OSAT PTI, and a contract of about KRW 900 million with a domestic substrate maker was disclosed on May 27, 2026.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual revenue on a disclosed basis was KRW 6.03 billion in 2022, KRW 6.03 billion in 2023 (down 0.1% year on year), KRW 4.02 billion in 2024 (down 33.4%) and KRW 4.68 billion in 2025 (up 16.6%). Among 2022 to 2025, 2022 was the largest year and 2024 the smallest.

Operating losses were KRW 5.03 billion in 2022, KRW 5.78 billion in 2023, KRW 9.21 billion in 2024 and KRW 13.5 billion in 2025, a loss-making run across four consecutive years in which the losses grew each year.

The operating margin deteriorated from -83.4% in 2022 to -95.9% in 2023 and -229.3% in 2024, and reached -289.2% in 2025.

The company attributed the widening loss in 2025, despite higher sales, to inventory valuation losses, and press reports said cost of sales rose more than 71% while selling and administrative expenses also increased.

Net loss attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 4.13 billion in 2022, KRW 182 million in 2023, KRW 8.5 billion in 2024 and KRW 16.2 billion in 2025, with 2023 the smallest loss. Net loss including non-controlling interests, a separate measure, was KRW 16.2 billion in 2025.

Quarterly revenue rose from KRW 213 million in the third quarter of 2025 to KRW 2.33 billion in the fourth quarter (up 992.0% from the prior quarter), fell to KRW 1.19 billion in the first quarter of 2026 (down 48.7%), and then recovered to KRW 2.06 billion in the second quarter and KRW 3.03 billion in the third quarter.

Third-quarter 2026 revenue was up 1,323.3% year on year. Operating losses narrowed from KRW 3.11 billion in the third quarter of 2025 and KRW 3.83 billion in the fourth quarter to KRW 1.71 billion, KRW 1.05 billion and KRW 881 million in the first three quarters of 2026.

Still, net losses attributable to controlling shareholders over the latest four quarters totaled KRW 13.7 billion.

At the end of 2025, total equity was KRW 18.6 billion (KRW 19.1 billion attributable to controlling shareholders), total liabilities were KRW 24.4 billion, and the debt ratio rose from 8.2% in 2022 to 131.1%.

Operating cash flow was negative every year, at KRW -3.68 billion, KRW -11.8 billion, KRW -6.84 billion and KRW -8.43 billion from 2022 to 2025. Press reports said the company raised KRW 8 billion in the first half of 2026 through a KRW 7 billion third-party rights offering and a KRW 1 billion private convertible bond.

05

Industry analysis

Laserssel's end-market demand comes from advanced packaging investment driven by AI semiconductors. Reports said the rise of AI chips and HBM has grown the advanced chip market and lifted demand for back-end equipment.

CPO is viewed as a core technology for next-generation AI data centers, and reports said global big tech firms including NVIDIA, Broadcom and Cisco are pushing toward commercialization. FOPLP has emerged as a key solution for AI and high-performance computing demand.

The company presents area-laser technology as a remedy for limits of conventional reflow and is extending the same technology to laser sintering equipment for SiC power semiconductors. On the cycle, revenue bottomed in 2024 and has been recovering since 2025.

On competition, the company stresses that it introduced volume-production equipment to a leading global CPO module maker after roughly two years of collaboration and testing.

However, reports noted that new supply has mostly come from one-off contracts, and the sales scale remains well below the KRW 70 billion-plus revenue expectations voiced at the 2022 KOSDAQ listing.

Outlook

Reports from January 2026 said the company expects 2026 revenue to grow to roughly four times the 2025 level and aims for its first year of profitability.

Revenue has risen to KRW 2.06 billion in the second quarter and KRW 3.03 billion in the third quarter of 2026, but comparing this with the target requires confirmed full-year 2026 results.

Reports from May 2026 said the company received a first volume-production LSR order from a leading global CPO module maker in the first quarter of 2026, and that follow-on LSR orders and new LCB orders are under discussion.

The CEO said he expects CPO-related orders to expand in the second half of 2026 and in 2027, and noted that several companies developing CPO have shown purchase interest.

Reports from January 2026 said talks with major Japanese substrate makers on glass-substrate and large-area packaging equipment were in their final stages.

Analysts have noted that the PTI order of KRW 3.1 billion carries an 80% advance payment within 30 days of shipment and a 20% balance due at the end of 2026, making revenue recognition within the year likely.

Capacity at the headquarters is being expanded from KRW 60 billion to KRW 120 billion a year, and the CEO has outlined a plan to reach KRW 300 billion through site expansion.

The KRW 8 billion raised in the first half of 2026 is slated for cleanroom and production line expansion, a research space and raw material procurement. The next checkpoint is whether confirmed orders and capacity additions translate into higher quarterly revenue and a better operating margin.

Bull factors

Area-laser technology entering volume production

Reports said the company received a first volume-production LSR order from a leading global CPO module maker in the first quarter of 2026. This adds to a KRW 3.1 billion FOPLP order from Taiwan-based OSAT PTI and talks with Japanese substrate makers. The CEO said several companies developing CPO have indicated interest in buying equipment.

Quarterly revenue recovery and narrowing losses

Revenue rose for two consecutive quarters after KRW 2.33 billion in the fourth quarter of 2025, reaching KRW 2.06 billion in the second quarter and KRW 3.03 billion in the third quarter of 2026. The third-quarter 2026 operating loss of KRW 881 million was the smallest of the last five quarters.

Capacity expansion and secured funding

A construction project to double headquarters capacity from KRW 60 billion to KRW 120 billion a year is underway. The CEO has outlined a plan to lift capacity to KRW 300 billion through site expansion. The KRW 8 billion raised in the first half of 2026 is slated for cleanroom and production line expansion.

Bear factors

Continuing losses and cost pressure

The 2025 operating loss widened to KRW 13.5 billion and the net loss attributable to controlling shareholders reached KRW 16.2 billion. Press reports said cost of sales rose more than 71% and selling and administrative expenses also increased. Net losses attributable to controlling shareholders over the latest four quarters totaled KRW 13.7 billion.

Balance sheet and cash flow

The debt ratio climbed from 8.2% in 2022 to 131.1% in 2025. Total liabilities of KRW 24.4 billion at the end of 2025 exceed total equity of KRW 18.6 billion. Operating cash flow was negative every year from 2022 to 2025, and external financing has continued.

One-off contracts and customer concentration

Reports have pointed out that new supply has mostly come through one-off contracts. A contract of about KRW 900 million disclosed in May 2026 equaled 19.11% of 2025 sales, so single deals carry a large weight. Compared with the KRW 70 billion-plus revenue expectations at the 2022 listing, the gap is wide.

Risk factors

Volume-production execution

CPO and FOPLP equipment requires long customer qualification and a lengthy shift to volume production. The first-quarter 2026 CPO order followed roughly two years of collaboration and testing. If the expansion of orders slips from the second half of 2026 into 2027, the pace of loss reduction could also slow.

Financing and dilution

With a 131.1% debt ratio and negative operating cash flow, funding needs are rising from capacity expansion and raw material purchases. The number of shares outstanding rose sharply from the end of 2024 to the end of 2025. Further fundraising could extend dilution for existing shareholders.

Customer and order volatility

Because revenue depends on a small number of customers and one-off contracts, a single quarter's delay in orders can swing quarterly results. As the first quarter of 2026 showed a decline from the fourth quarter of 2025, the quarterly path is not linear. With a high share of overseas customers, the company is also exposed to exchange-rate and geopolitical factors.

Overall view

Laserssel has entered the AI semiconductor back-end equipment market with area-laser technology and has secured volume-production orders and a large order in CPO and FOPLP.

Third-quarter 2026 revenue of KRW 3.03 billion was the largest of the last five quarters, and the operating loss narrowed to KRW 881 million, indicating an improving profit structure.

However, the 2025 operating loss was KRW 13.5 billion and the net loss attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 16.2 billion, and the latest four quarters total KRW 13.7 billion, so a return to profitability is not yet confirmed in disclosures.

On finances, the 131.1% debt ratio at the end of 2025 and negative operating cash flow in every year from 2022 to 2025 leave reliance on external funding.

Bullish factors include the first volume-production CPO order, capacity expansion and secured funding, while bearish factors include cost pressure, reliance on one-off contracts and financial strain. Valuation can only be compared on a price-to-book basis because losses prevent a PER calculation.

The next checkpoints are the preliminary 2026 full-year results and the operating margin in the 2026 annual report.
