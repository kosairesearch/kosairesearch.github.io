DY: Annual profit rebounded, quarterly earnings swing wider

DY's operating profit rose 34.9% in 2025, but a net loss attributable to controlling shareholders in the third quarter of 2026 has widened swings in quarterly earnings.

02

Business structure

DY runs auto parts, hydraulic equipment, industrial machinery and a holding business. The exchange classifies DY under other financial services, while the WICS industry classification places it in auto parts.

An aggregator tally of second-quarter 2026 sales puts auto parts at 58%, hydraulic equipment at 35%, industrial machinery at 7% and the holding business at 1%. The core auto parts subsidiary DY Auto makes automotive electrical components, and DY Power manufactures and sells hydraulic equipment.

In the December 2025 domestic and export breakdown, hydraulic cylinders had an 81.30% export share and electrical components and related products 65.87%, so overseas sales carry a large weight. Auto parts growth in 2025 was attributed to expanded local production abroad and rising demand for electrical components.

Hydraulic equipment rests on a recovery in construction machinery and on infrastructure investment across countries. R&D spending in 2025 equaled 2.37% of sales. Auto parts sell mainly to vehicle makers under a general supply structure, while hydraulic equipment depends on construction equipment demand. The holding business is about 1% of sales, so results are read mainly through auto parts and hydraulics.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual sales rose from KRW 1.09 trillion in 2022 to KRW 1.24 trillion in 2025, and 2025 was the largest year of 2022 to 2025. Sales in 2024 were KRW 1.17 trillion, down 1.9% from the prior year, so growth was not continuous across the period.

Operating profit was KRW 32.9 billion in 2022, KRW 43.3 billion in 2023 (up 31.5%), KRW 35.6 billion in 2024 (down 17.7%) and KRW 48.1 billion in 2025 (up 34.9%). The operating margin moved from 3.0% in 2022 to 3.9% in 2025, after dipping to 3.0% in 2024.

Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders swung from a net loss of KRW 4.32 billion in 2022 to a profit of KRW 18.2 billion in 2023, rose to KRW 22 billion in 2024 (up 21.1%) and fell to KRW 14.1 billion in 2025 (down 36.1%).

Operating profit grew 34.9% in 2025 while profit attributable to controlling shareholders fell 36.1%, so the operating-level gain did not carry through to the bottom line.

Net profit including non-controlling interests was KRW 30 billion in 2025, below KRW 35 billion in 2024, and the gap between the two measures reflects non-controlling interests. Operating cash flow declined from KRW 61.1 billion in 2022 to KRW 34.3 billion in 2024 and recovered slightly to KRW 37.9 billion in 2025.

The debt-to-equity ratio rose from 70.7% in 2022 to 75.6% in 2025, with total equity of KRW 600.6 billion at the end of 2025, of which KRW 392.3 billion belonged to controlling shareholders.

Quarterly sales went from KRW 299.3 billion with operating profit of KRW 13.1 billion in the third quarter of 2025 to KRW 370.5 billion with operating profit of KRW 24 billion in the second quarter of 2026.

Third-quarter 2026 sales were KRW 348.6 billion, down 5.9% from the prior quarter but up 16.5% from a year earlier, while operating profit was KRW 11.6 billion, down 11.2% from a year earlier.

Net loss attributable to controlling shareholders came in the fourth quarter of 2025 at KRW 1.84 billion and in the third quarter of 2026 at KRW 7.86 billion, and the last four quarters total KRW 6.71 billion.

05

Industry analysis

The auto parts market is being driven by expanded electric vehicle subsidies and rising demand for eco-friendly vehicles. Electrical component sales are tied to the shift toward electrification, so demand for them moves in the same direction as that shift.

Hydraulic equipment demand is supported by a recovery in construction machinery and by infrastructure investment and urbanization policies across countries. The two segments sit at different points in their cycles.

Auto parts respond to vehicle production and policy changes, while hydraulic equipment is sensitive to construction activity and infrastructure spending. Auto parts sales growth in 2025 was supported by expanded local production abroad.

On competitive position, a Thinkpool report on the third quarter of 2025 said DY's operating margin of 4.4% was among the lowest of its peers. Overseas production expansion helps respond to local demand but also exposes the company to exchange rate and local cost swings.

DY Auto's capital injection into its Indian subsidiary runs in the same direction as building overseas production and sales bases. If the two segments diverge in their cycles, consolidated results will move with one segment offsetting a slowdown in the other.

06

Outlook

Quarterly sales in 2026 rose from KRW 331.5 billion in the first quarter to KRW 370.5 billion in the second quarter and then fell to KRW 348.6 billion in the third quarter.

Operating profit dropped from KRW 24 billion in the second quarter to KRW 11.6 billion in the third, a fall of 51.6% against a 5.9% decline in sales from the prior quarter.

Operating profit had already fallen to KRW 3.97 billion in the fourth quarter of 2025, so the size of fourth-quarter 2026 operating profit is the next checkpoint. For auto parts, the key variable is whether electric vehicle subsidies and demand for eco-friendly vehicles continue beyond 2026.

Hydraulic equipment results depend on how long the recovery in construction machinery lasts. DY Auto disclosed a capital injection of about KRW 7.2 billion into its Indian subsidiary, which will raise its stake to 100%. R&D spending equal to 2.37% of sales points to continued investment in product development.

The 2025 annual sales of KRW 1.24 trillion serve as the annual baseline against which 2026 quarterly sales will be read. The 2026 annual report will fix full-year sales, operating profit and the segment sales mix on a regular filing basis.

Auto parts growth drivers

The capital injection that raises DY Auto's Indian subsidiary stake to 100% expands the overseas production base.
