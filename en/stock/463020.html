02

Business structure

NuenAI was founded in 2004 as RSN and later renamed; it is a social-listening company whose core business is AI-based big data analytics that collects and analyzes unstructured online text in real time to deliver consumer insights to corporate clients.

After being selected as Samsung Group's external big-data analytics provider in 2006 and Samsung Electronics' standard social big-data provider in 2013, the company built a track record with large enterprises before expanding to LG Electronics, KT, and Amorepacific.

As of 2025, its service revenue mix was composed of custom analytics Quetta_Enterprise at 37%, data preprocessing and supply Quetta_Data at 35%, standardized Quetta_Service at 13%, Insight Report at 10%, and other services at 5%.

The company formed global social data partnerships with Twitter in 2014 and Weibo in 2015, and later broadened its client base into beauty and food-and-beverage names such as Bvlgari Korea, A Twosome Place, Woowa Brothers, and Starbucks Korea.

In 2025, K-beauty and food clients including Cosmax, Dalba Global, Emart24, and Olive Young were added.

Domestically, unlike NuenAI's enterprise-focused approach, Vaiv Company's 'Sometrend' pursues both B2C and B2B channels, and due to language specificity, competition with global specialist vendors tends to be more coexistence than direct rivalry.

NuenAI listed on KOSDAQ in July 2025 and is using its own large language model 'Quetta LLMs' along with GNN and vision technologies to expand beyond social listening into vertical AI platforms for trend forecasting, fashion, insurance/healthcare, and the public sector.

It has also recently launched a dedicated public-sector brand, 'AGORA AI,' targeting subscription-based services for local governments and councils.

04

Earnings analysis

2025 consolidated revenue was KRW 18.819 billion, down from KRW 19.716 billion the prior year, and operating profit swung to a loss of KRW 1.109 billion from a KRW 429 million profit, with the operating margin falling sharply to -5.9% from 2.2%.

Net loss narrowed substantially to KRW 1.055 billion from KRW 5.620 billion a year earlier, suggesting the prior year's large one-off loss factors eased.

On a quarterly basis, 2025Q3 revenue was KRW 4.821 billion with an operating loss of KRW 149 million and a net loss of KRW 172 million, before the seasonally strong 2025Q4 delivered revenue of KRW 5.339 billion, operating profit of KRW 574 million, and net profit of KRW 553 million.

However, 2026Q1 revenue fell to KRW 4.342 billion and the operating loss widened to KRW 745 million, the weakest result of the period, before 2026Q2 returned to profit with revenue of KRW 5.310 billion, operating profit of KRW 227 million, and net profit of KRW 412 million.

This quarterly swing appears tied to a business structure where the first quarter is seasonally slow while personnel and R&D spending behave as largely fixed costs.

Summed over the trailing four quarters (2025Q3-2026Q2), owner net income was a positive KRW 500 million, meaning the combined result stayed profitable despite the alternating quarterly pattern.

Meanwhile, 2025 operating cash flow improved to KRW 972 million from KRW 56 million a year earlier; that cash generation improved despite an operating loss appears to reflect the subscription revenue model, including growth in contract liabilities (advance payments).

05

Industry analysis

Korea's AI market is growing on the back of government digital-transformation policy and expanding private-sector demand, with the market expected to grow at a 14.9% compound annual rate to reach KRW 4.5 trillion by 2027.

Social listening supply is dominated by large domestic firms and global specialist vendors, and due to language-specific nuances across markets, the competitive dynamic is often described as coexistence rather than head-to-head rivalry.

The key recent shift is that AI is rapidly automating tasks—sentiment classification, boolean query design, and analytical report writing—that used to require dedicated specialist staff, simultaneously changing incumbents' cost structures and lowering entry barriers for new entrants.

As a result, global providers are expanding low-cost tiers aimed at small and mid-sized businesses, which could add competitive pressure on NuenAI, which has run an enterprise-focused subscription model for more than two decades.

Conversely, as AI adoption improves service quality and lowers operating costs, there is potential for social-listening demand—previously concentrated among large enterprises—to broaden toward small and mid-sized companies as well.

In capital markets, a wave of AI companies including Nota, S2W, Neurophet, and NuenAI have listed on KOSDAQ since 2025, lifting investor attention to the AI theme, a dynamic that coincides with a broader sorting-out phase in which peer companies, including NuenAI, must prove their growth stories through actual results.

06

Outlook

At the time of its KOSDAQ listing, the company presented 2027 targets of KRW 44.5 billion in revenue, KRW 17.8 billion in operating profit, and KRW 14.0 billion in net income; given 2025 results (revenue of KRW 19.7 billion, operating profit of KRW 400 million, and a net loss of KRW 5.6 billion), reaching those targets would require annual revenue growth of more than 30%.

In a report dated May 20, 2026, the Korea IR Service's corporate research center forecast 2026 revenue of KRW 21.1 billion (up 12% year over year) and a turn to operating profit of KRW 700 million, estimating an operating margin of 3.3%, an improvement of 9.2 percentage points from the prior year.

The same report noted that because most costs are fixed in nature, revenue growth flows directly into profit improvement, and it pointed to a 42% year-over-year increase in first-quarter 2026 contract liabilities as a leading indicator supporting revenue growth from the second quarter onward.

As for growth drivers, Quetta_Service—currently only 13% of revenue—is seen as poised to become the key growth engine going forward.

The company plans to launch 'Quetta BeauSense,' a SaaS service analyzing K-beauty influencers and US beauty social channels, in the second half of 2026, targeting sales in the US market, building on its 2025 selection for a Ministry of Trade, Industry and Energy export voucher program to establish a local footing.

Separately, after winning a Ministry of SMEs and Startups R&D project through an 18.4-to-1 competitive selection, the company plans to develop a multi-agentic AI platform for K-beauty export marketing strategy over the next two years, backed by a total of KRW 3.5 billion including KRW 2.5 billion in government funding.

In the public sector, the company has launched a subscription (SaaS) service under the 'AGORA AI' brand targeting local governments and councils, and plans to expand around four core services covering citizen communication, public-opinion monitoring, and legislative-activity support.

08

Bull factors

Signs of Profitability Amid Quarterly Swings

The company posted operating profit of KRW 574 million and KRW 227 million, along with net profit of KRW 553 million and KRW 412 million, in 2025Q4 and 2026Q2 respectively, marking repeated profitable quarters. The trailing four-quarter sum of owner net income also stayed positive at KRW 500 million.

This suggests a partial recovery in earnings capacity after the large net loss seen in the company's first year as a listed firm, though quarterly volatility remains, as seen in the seasonally weak loss in 2026Q1.

Vertical Expansion on a Large-Enterprise Client Base

Having Samsung Electronics, LG Electronics, KT, and Amorepacific as core clients provides a stable revenue base. On top of that, the company is diversifying revenue sources by expanding into beauty (K-beauty), fashion, insurance/healthcare, and the public sector via AGORA AI. Winning government R&D funding also helps offset some of the burden of developing new services.

Improved Financial Stability Post-IPO

IPO proceeds lifted total equity from KRW 21.2 billion in 2024 to KRW 53.5 billion in 2025, while the debt ratio fell sharply from 40.9% to 7.7%. Operating cash flow also improved from KRW 56 million in 2024 to KRW 972 million in 2025, supporting cash generation even amid an operating loss.

This suggests the company has some runway to fund new-service development and overseas expansion without near-term external financing.

09

Bear factors

Revenue Stagnation Versus Ambitious Targets

2025 revenue actually declined to KRW 18.819 billion from KRW 19.716 billion the prior year. Yet reaching the 2027 target presented at listing (KRW 44.5 billion in revenue) is seen as requiring more than 30% annual revenue growth.

The trailing four-quarter revenue sum has stayed around KRW 20 billion annualized, indicating the gap between target and current trajectory has not yet narrowed meaningfully.

Limited Earnings Visibility and Seasonality

Over the recent quarters, operating profit/loss alternated markedly at -KRW 149 million, +KRW 574 million, -KRW 745 million, and +KRW 227 million. The first quarter repeatedly shows lower revenue and a wider operating loss as a seasonal pattern. This volatility makes it difficult to project the following quarter's results with confidence.

History of Post-Lockup Share Sales

Venture capital firm KB Investment reportedly completed a full exit of its stake after the post-IPO lockup expired. Profit-taking sales by early investors can weigh on share supply and demand, and there remains a possibility of similar selling pressure recurring as other stakeholders' lockups expire in sequence.

10

Risk factors

Industry Structure Risk

As AI rapidly automates tasks once performed by specialist staff—sentiment classification, query design, report writing—entry barriers in the social listening market are falling.

The trend of global providers expanding low-cost tiers aimed at small businesses could also create pricing pressure on the incumbent enterprise-focused subscription model, potentially diluting the relative value of NuenAI's two-decade-plus track record with large enterprises.

Earnings and Business Model Risk

The recurring pattern of quarterly operating profit and loss alternation leaves limited near-term earnings visibility.

Achieving the 2027 targets (KRW 44.5 billion revenue, KRW 17.8 billion operating profit) requires new vertical services to translate into meaningful revenue within a short period, and execution risk remains given many of these services are still at an early stage.

If revenue is heavily dependent on a small number of large corporate clients, changes in those clients' budget policies could directly affect results.

Supply-Demand and Shareholding Risk

With a market capitalization of roughly KRW 0.1 trillion, the stock is a small-cap name where trading liquidity could be relatively limited. As with the venture capital exit case, further lockup expirations among other early investors or employees could bring additional shares to market, creating supply-side pressure.

As a small growth stock, price volatility tied to earnings releases or new contract news may also be relatively pronounced.

12

Overall view

NuenAI is a data-analytics company that has run an enterprise-focused social listening business for more than two decades, and while its IPO substantially improved financial stability, annual revenue has yet to return to a growth trajectory.

Over the trailing four quarters (2025Q3-2026Q2), profit and loss alternated quarter to quarter, netting a modest combined profit, with a recurring pattern of seasonally strong second and fourth quarters against a weaker first quarter.

The company is expanding into new verticals—beauty, fashion, insurance/healthcare, and the public sector—and has set ambitious 2027 targets of KRW 44.5 billion in revenue and KRW 17.8 billion in operating profit, but achieving them would require more than 30% annual growth, making execution the key test.

On the industry side, AI-driven automation is lowering entry barriers in the social listening market while also potentially broadening the addressable demand base, meaning the shifting competitive landscape presents both opportunity and threat simultaneously.

The venture capital exit following lockup expiration remains a supply-side risk to monitor. Going forward, upcoming quarterly results, revenue contribution from new services, and progress in public-sector and overseas expansion will be the key variables in judging whether the company's growth story can be sustained.
