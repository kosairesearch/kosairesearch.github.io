02

Business structure

Noroo Holdings is a business holding company that owns stakes in its subsidiaries and handles group strategy and management support. Founded in 1945 on paint, ink and resin businesses, the group split its investment arm from its manufacturing and sales arm in June 2006 and has operated as a holding structure since.

The chemical cluster includes Noroo Paint & Coatings and several coatings subsidiaries covering architectural and industrial paints as well as automotive, coil and marine coatings.

The bio-agriculture cluster, consisting of Deongiban and Gibantech, handles seed development and farm products, while a distribution unit and a consulting and design unit complete the group. The holding company manages returns on its subsidiary stakes and links them to management support revenue.

Its disclosed business purposes include licensing the NOROO brand trademark, leasing owned buildings, and providing administrative support and management consulting to subsidiaries.

Coatings operations form the core of consolidated revenue, with Noroo Paint & Coatings and the chemical and automotive coatings subsidiaries identified as key units. Automotive coatings companies operate in China, India, Indonesia, the Czech Republic, the United States, Brazil and Mexico.

A 2022 press report noted that the bio-agriculture cluster had not posted an operating profit in any year since 2016, and that the holding company kept providing funds to support its finances.

04

Earnings analysis

On a disclosed basis, annual revenue rose from KRW 1.04 trillion in 2022 to KRW 1.21 trillion in 2023, up 17.0% year on year, and reached KRW 1.25 trillion in 2024, up 2.7%, which was the largest year in 2022–2025. Revenue then eased 0.7% to KRW 1.24 trillion in 2025.

Operating profit climbed from KRW 30 billion in 2022 to KRW 67.4 billion in 2023, up 125.0%, and KRW 72.6 billion in 2024, up 7.6%, before falling 11.1% to KRW 64.5 billion in 2025. Operating margin rose from 2.9% in 2022 to 5.6% in 2023, 5.8% in 2024 and 5.2% in 2025, staying in the 5% range since 2023.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders peaked at KRW 50.7 billion in 2024, up 99.3%, and fell 36.1% to KRW 32.4 billion in 2025. Net income including non-controlling interests was KRW 58.5 billion in 2025, higher than the controlling-shareholder figure because of the minority share.

On a quarterly basis, operating profit was KRW 19.7 billion in the third quarter of 2025 and KRW 8.91 billion in the fourth quarter, down 54.8% sequentially, before recovering to KRW 16.2 billion in the first quarter of 2026 and KRW 30.8 billion in the second quarter, up 89.7% sequentially.

In the third quarter of 2026, operating profit was KRW 16.7 billion, down 15.3% year on year, and the controlling-shareholder result turned to a net loss of KRW 203 million. Third-quarter revenue of KRW 313.3 billion was down 0.4% year on year, so top-line growth has stalled.

The detailed causes of the third-quarter swing to a loss, including non-operating and equity-method items, remain to be confirmed from the line items of the filing.

05

Industry analysis

Demand for paint and coatings tracks construction starts, automobile production, and appliance and metal goods output. Noroo Holdings' coatings business is split into architectural and industrial paints, automotive coatings, and coil coatings for pre-coated metal, so each unit is exposed to a different end market.

Construction demand is sensitive to interest rates and building starts, while automotive coatings depend on vehicle output and the utilization of overseas plants. Coil coatings are tied to appliance and building-material production.

Raw materials such as resins, pigments, solvents and additives are largely petrochemical-based, so input price swings flow through to cost and margins. In the domestic market the group competes with large paint makers.

The bio-agriculture cluster handles seed development, production and supply, and as of a 2022 press report it had not reached an operating profit.

Because the holding company's results are linked directly to the profitability of its coatings subsidiaries and overseas units, changes in industry conditions feed quickly into group earnings.

The company says it is widening research into new businesses, including coating materials for electronic paper, bio-based materials and marine bioplastics.

06

Outlook

The company has disclosed operating profit of KRW 16.2 billion in the first quarter of 2026, KRW 30.8 billion in the second and KRW 16.7 billion in the third.

The key question is whether the second-quarter recovery held into the third quarter, and third-quarter operating profit fell 45.8% from the prior quarter, so the sustainability of the rebound is not yet established.

Third-quarter revenue was down 0.4% year on year, so profitability rather than top-line growth is the focus. With the operating margin at 5.2% in 2025, the level the annual margin recovers to in the next annual report is a central marker.

Overseas, the automotive coatings units in the United States, Mexico, Brazil, the Czech Republic, India, Indonesia and China extend the group's footprint.

Whether funding needs at the bio-agriculture cluster ease also bears on holding-company results, and a return to operating profit there has yet to be confirmed in a periodic report.

Research into coating materials for electronic paper and bio-based materials is ongoing, and their commercial timing and revenue contribution will become clear as filings disclose them. Full-year results will be fixed in the 2026 annual business report.

08

Bull factors

Operating profit recovery

Operating profit reached KRW 30.8 billion in Q2 2026, up 89.7% from the prior quarter, after KRW 16.2 billion in Q1. Revenue rose 19.2% sequentially to KRW 356.2 billion in the same quarter.

Bulls point to the second-quarter level as evidence that profitability can rebuild beyond the 2025 annual operating profit of KRW 64.5 billion.

Improving balance sheet

The debt ratio fell from 77.4% in 2022 to 57.6% in 2025. Operating cash flow was KRW 80.1 billion in 2025, above the KRW 48.4 billion recorded in 2022. Bulls cite this balance-sheet strength, with KRW 502.8 billion of equity attributable to controlling shareholders, as a buffer against cyclical swings.

Holding structure and discount

The shares trade well below book value, and bulls note that subsidiary earnings recovery passes directly into holding-company profit. Automotive coatings units spread across several countries are also cited as diversifying exposure to any single market.

Bear factors

Q3 loss and lower operating profit

The third quarter of 2026 swung to a controlling-shareholder net loss of KRW 203 million, and operating profit of KRW 16.7 billion fell 15.3% year on year. Bears argue the momentum reversed within one quarter of the second-quarter rebound. Net income attributable to controlling shareholders over the last four quarters totals KRW 26.3 billion.

Stalled sales and thinner margin

Revenue peaked at KRW 1.25 trillion in 2024, slipped to KRW 1.24 trillion in 2025 and was down 0.4% year on year in the third quarter of 2026. Operating margin fell from 5.8% in 2024 to 5.2% in 2025. Bears question whether profitability can keep improving while the top line stays flat.

Bio-agriculture drag and minority share

A 2022 press report noted that the bio-agriculture cluster had not posted an operating profit since 2016 and that the holding company kept funding it. In 2025, net income of KRW 58.5 billion was well above the KRW 32.4 billion attributable to controlling shareholders, so the minority share is substantial. Bears see this as reducing the profit that reaches holding-company shareholders.

10

Risk factors

Raw material cost swings

Key coatings inputs such as resins, pigments, solvents and additives are often priced off petrochemical markets. When input costs rise faster than selling prices, operating margin can compress quickly. How much of the third-quarter margin decline reflects this factor needs to be confirmed from cost line items.

Overseas automotive coatings exposure

The automotive coatings units operate in China, India, Indonesia, the Czech Republic, the United States, Brazil and Mexico. Vehicle production swings and currency movements can both flow into reported results. A multi-country footprint diversifies demand but also adds management complexity.

Governance and ownership concentration

A March 2025 press report put the stake held by related parties, including chairman Han Young-jae, at 46.36% of the holding company. A high controlling stake makes decision-making and dividend policy a topic of interest for minority shareholders. Intra-group transactions and funding support within the holding structure also merit continued attention.

Overall view

Noroo Holdings lifted annual revenue from KRW 1.04 trillion in 2022 to KRW 1.25 trillion in 2024, but revenue eased 0.7% to KRW 1.24 trillion in 2025. Operating profit peaked at KRW 72.6 billion in 2024 and fell to KRW 64.5 billion in 2025, when the operating margin was 5.2%.

A rebound to KRW 30.8 billion in operating profit in Q2 2026 was followed by weaker third-quarter results, with operating profit of KRW 16.7 billion and a controlling-shareholder net loss of KRW 203 million.

On the positive side, the debt ratio has declined from 77.4% in 2022 to 57.6% in 2025, and the shares trade far below book value, a holding-company discount.

On the negative side, bears point to stalled sales, the third-quarter swing to a loss, weak profitability in bio-agriculture, and a substantial minority share of net income.

The next checkpoints are the annual operating margin confirmed in the 2026 annual business report and whether quarterly profit recovers in the 2027 quarterly reports.
