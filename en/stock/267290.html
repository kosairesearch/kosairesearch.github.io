02

Business structure

Kyungdong City GAS was spun off in 2017 from the city gas business division of Kyungdong Invest and supplies city gas through pipelines in the Ulsan Metropolitan City and Yangsan City service areas.

According to company IR material from June 2023, it is the only city gas supplier in Ulsan and Yangsan, with 631,722 households as of December 2022 and a pipeline network of 2,500 km. Penetration was reported at 96.9% in Ulsan and 85.6% in Yangsan.

Revenue comes mainly from the city gas segment, with demand split among residential and general users, industrial users, cooling and heating, and cogeneration and fuel cell users.

As a first among city gas companies, it started a pipeline leasing business, providing pipeline and safety management services for directly imported volumes for self-consumption.

It is also investing in hydrogen charging stations for vehicles and hydrogen fuel cell power generation, and the Daewon fuel cell project, with 66 units of 0.30 MW each for a total of 19.8 MW, has been in commercial operation since March 2022. An energy platform business is also under way.

Its Q1 2026 earnings commentary said that stabilized city gas prices gave it a price advantage over competing fuels, which raised usage by oil refiners. In competition, the key factor is the price gap between fuels, which drives demand.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual revenue was largest in 2022 at KRW 2.3 trillion, then fell to KRW 1.8 trillion in 2023, down 21.9% year on year.

It declined further to KRW 1.79 trillion in 2024, down 0.4%, and to KRW 1.76 trillion in 2025, down 1.8%, marking three consecutive annual declines from 2023 through 2025, with 2025 the smallest revenue year of the 2022–2025 period.

Over the same span, operating profit rose from KRW 25.3 billion in 2022 to KRW 28.3 billion in 2023, up 11.9%, fell to KRW 27 billion in 2024, down 4.6%, and recovered to KRW 33 billion in 2025, up 22.5%, the largest year of the period.

Operating profit rising while revenue fell points to an improved earnings structure rather than a larger scale. Operating margin moved from 1.1% in 2022 to 1.6% in 2023, 1.5% in 2024 and 1.9% in 2025.

Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 24.2 billion in 2022, KRW 30.6 billion in 2023 (up 26.6%), KRW 28.6 billion in 2024 (down 6.5%) and KRW 32.8 billion in 2025 (up 14.8%). Net profit including non-controlling interests was reported at the same amounts.

Operating cash flow was KRW 52.6 billion in 2022, negative KRW 11 billion in 2023, KRW 74.9 billion in 2024 and KRW 56.4 billion in 2025, turning negative only in 2023.

Quarterly revenue went from KRW 288.8 billion in Q3 2025 to KRW 446.4 billion in Q4 2025, up 54.6% quarter on quarter, and peaked at KRW 541.8 billion in Q1 2026, up 21.3%. It then fell to KRW 447.5 billion in Q2 2026, down 17.4%, and to KRW 430.9 billion in Q3 2026, down 3.7%, two straight quarterly declines.

Quarterly operating profit went from KRW 19.6 billion in Q1 2026, up 125.0% quarter on quarter, to KRW 7.09 billion in Q2 2026, down 63.8%, and KRW 7.07 billion in Q3 2026, down 0.3%.

Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders over the last four quarters (Q4 2025 to Q3 2026) totaled KRW 40 billion, and quarterly profit moved in the same direction as revenue.

05

Industry analysis

City gas supply is organized by service area, and the company holds the supplier position in Ulsan and Yangsan. Its Q1 2026 earnings commentary said that stabilized city gas prices gave it a price advantage over competing fuels, which led oil refiners to use more gas.

Its sales volume still rose year on year even as the petrochemical industry remained weak. In contrast, its Q1 2023 IR material said that higher LNG prices weakened its price competitiveness against other fuels and reduced industrial supply.

This comparison shows that relative fuel prices can change the direction of industrial demand. In a comparison with nine gas sector companies, its operating margin is below the sector average, while its debt ratio is below the sector average. Its dividend yield is somewhat above the sector average.

Listed peers in the sector include KoreaGas, Samchully, Seoul City GAS and SK GAS, and the company is among the smaller city gas operators.

Outlook

The next periodic report is the 2026 annual report, which will confirm full-year 2026 results including Q4. Revenue fell in Q2 and Q3 2026 after the Q1 peak, and operating profit moved sideways in the KRW 7 billion range.

The key checkpoint is whether Q4 2026 operating profit holds near the KRW 8.7 billion recorded in Q4 2025. The pipeline leasing business covers directly imported volumes for self-consumption, so whether those volumes continue will determine its revenue.

The Daewon fuel cell plant has operated since commercial start in March 2022, and steady contribution over its operating life matters. The annual cash dividend was approved at the regular shareholders meeting in March 2026. Any medium-term targets and timelines for new businesses should be checked against future disclosures.

Gradual margin improvement

Stronger balance sheet and new business base

The debt ratio fell from 170.3% in 2022 to 95.5% in 2025, and total equity at end-2025 was KRW 475.2 billion. The 19.8 MW Daewon fuel cell project has been in commercial operation since March 2022. Pipeline leasing and hydrogen charging are being added on top of the existing city gas infrastructure.

Bear factors

Revenue down three years running

Revenue fell from KRW 2.3 trillion in 2022 to KRW 1.76 trillion in 2025, declining three years in a row from 2023. The declines in 2024 and 2025 were small, at 0.4% and 1.8%, but continued. Quarterly revenue fell in Q2 and Q3 2026.

Profit fell after the Q1 peak

Of the KRW 40 billion in net profit attributable to controlling shareholders over the last four quarters, KRW 17.3 billion came in Q1 2026. The concentration of profit in one quarter is a bearish factor that increases volatility.

Thin margins and volatile cash flow

Operating margin was 1.9% in 2025, below the nine-company gas sector average of 3.32%. Operating cash flow was negative KRW 11 billion in 2023, then recovered to KRW 74.9 billion in 2024 and KRW 56.4 billion in 2025. Cash generation has varied considerably from year to year.

10

Risk factors

Price regulation and feedstock lag

City gas tariffs are set under government regulation, so feedstock cost changes may not pass through to selling prices immediately. In its Q1 2023 IR material, the company said higher LNG prices weakened its competitiveness against other fuels and reduced industrial supply. The timing gap between feedstock costs and tariff adjustments is a variable that can move margins.

Industrial demand and competing fuels

Industrial demand, including from oil refiners, appears to drive sales volume. If the price advantage over competing fuels narrows, industrial demand could shift away. A prolonged slump in the petrochemical industry could keep demand volatile.

New business investment and financial burden

New businesses such as pipeline leasing, hydrogen charging and fuel cells depend on long-term investment recovery, and the fuel cell business is structured for 20 years of operation after construction. Total liabilities at end-2025 were KRW 453.6 billion, a scale close to total equity of KRW 475.2 billion. The size of the financial burden could vary with the pace of new business expansion.
