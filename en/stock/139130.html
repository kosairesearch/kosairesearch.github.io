Report overview

Post-PF Earnings Base and Shareholder Returns Under Review

iM Financial Group's earnings recovered sharply from the 2024 low in 2025, but quarterly operating profit has declined for two consecutive quarters since its Q1 2026 peak, leaving capital ratios and shareholder returns as the key items to track.

02

Business structure

iM Financial Group is a holding company whose core is iM Bank, with iM Securities, iM Life (life insurance) and iM Capital (capital finance) as subsidiaries. According to preliminary H1 2026 results announced on July 27, 2026, iM Bank's net income was KRW 245.7 billion, down 4.2% from a year earlier.

Over the same period, iM Capital posted KRW 39.5 billion, iM Securities (standalone) KRW 44.9 billion, and iM Life KRW 18.2 billion, according to the company. iM Bank's won-denominated loan balance exceeded KRW 60 trillion for the first time in H1 2026, while corporate lending grew 5.9%.

Asset-quality indicators were managed at a delinquency ratio of 0.87%, a substandard-and-below ratio of 0.94%, and a credit cost ratio of 0.41%. After converting into a commercial bank, the bank is adjusting its household and corporate loan portfolio and expanding beyond Daegu and Gyeongbuk.

Recent loan growth is explained as a response to the government's productive finance policy, which steers lending toward corporates. Non-bank subsidiaries' contribution has also risen, with their share of profit reported at 34.0% in Q1 2026.

In this structure, the bank earns mainly on loan volume and lending margins, the securities and capital units on fees and growth in operating assets, and the life insurer on insurance-related income.

The group's profit center of gravity still sits with the bank, so how fast the non-bank share rises is the key measure of structural change.

04

Earnings analysis

Based on the audited annual filings, net income attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 410.5 billion in 2022, KRW 387.8 billion in 2023 (down 5.5% year on year), KRW 214.9 billion in 2024 (down 44.6%), and KRW 443.9 billion in 2025 (up 106.6%). Among 2022–2025, 2025 was the largest year and 2024 the smallest.

Net income including non-controlling interests was KRW 445.4 billion, KRW 412.2 billion, KRW 201.6 billion and KRW 457.5 billion over the same years, moving in the same direction as controlling-shareholder profit but at different amounts.

Operating profit fell from KRW 622 billion in 2022 to KRW 534.3 billion in 2023 (down 14.1%) and to KRW 263.4 billion in 2024 (down 50.7%), the smallest year, before recovering to KRW 583.2 billion in 2025 (up 121.4%).

Insurance revenue, a single account and not total company revenue, was KRW 246.9 billion in 2022, KRW 270.8 billion in 2023 (up 9.7%), KRW 255.1 billion in 2024 (down 5.8%) and KRW 265.2 billion in 2025 (up 4.0%), with 2023 the largest year. Insurance revenue alone does not explain the swings in operating profit.

Operating cash flow was -KRW 2.83 trillion in 2022, KRW 1.95 trillion in 2023, KRW 2.9 trillion in 2024 and -KRW 1.48 trillion in 2025, a large year-to-year swing.

In quarterly terms, net income attributable to controlling shareholders fell to KRW 12.2 billion in Q4 2025, down 90.0% from the prior quarter, while operating profit was KRW 19.9 billion, down 87.2%.

Q1 2026 marked the peak, with operating profit of KRW 193.9 billion (up 875.2% quarter on quarter) and controlling-shareholder net income of KRW 154.5 billion (up 1,166.9%).

Controlling-shareholder net income was KRW 141.1 billion in Q2 2026 and KRW 120.7 billion in Q3 2026 (down 1.4% year on year), and the last four quarters totaled KRW 428.5 billion.

Preliminary H1 2026 controlling-shareholder net income was KRW 295.6 billion, down 4.4% year on year, which the company attributed to higher costs from a change in the education tax rate and larger loan-loss provisions.

Preliminary nine-month 2026 controlling-shareholder net income was KRW 416.3 billion, down 3.6%, as announced on October 27, 2026.

05

Industry analysis

Earnings conditions for Korean financial holding companies depend on loan growth, provision flows and the volatility of non-interest income.

This group booked large provisions in 2024 after a revaluation of real estate project financing exposures, and securities analysts describe the current phase as normalization after the resolution of PF risk.

The company and press coverage explain that the government's productive finance policy is supporting corporate lending at the bank. A more active capital market has lifted fee income at iM Securities.

On the other side, when market volatility rises, non-interest income becomes unstable, and some analysts flagged weaker non-interest income as a factor in Q3 2026 earnings. In an April 2026 report, Meritz Securities noted that the non-bank share of revenue rose from 16% in Q1 2024 to 30% in Q1 2025 and 34% in Q1 2026.

After converting into a commercial bank, the bank competes in nationwide lending, which raises the need to combine asset growth with asset-quality discipline.

Securities analysts have described the price-to-book ratio as lower than that of major financial holding companies, and the industry expects that gap to narrow as buybacks, cancellations and tax-free dividends expand. With CET1 above 12%, management says the group has room to fund both loan growth and shareholder returns.

06

Outlook

Under a corporate value-up plan announced in October 2024, the company said it will carry out total share buybacks and cancellations of KRW 150 billion through 2027. The board's H1 2026 approval of an additional KRW 30 billion program would bring the cumulative total to KRW 130 billion once completed.

Preliminary nine-month 2026 CET1 was 12.39%, improved from the prior quarter. The CFO said the company aims to manage capital ratios steadily through year-end and reach the first target of the value-up plan ahead of schedule.

At the April 2026 results briefing, the company said a KRW 290 billion capital-reserve dividend had been secured after the March shareholder meeting. Press coverage explained that this type of dividend is paid from capital reserves, so it carries no dividend income tax burden.

Yuanta Securities, in a July 29, 2026 report, estimated the 2026 total shareholder return at 40.2%, split into a 22.1% payout ratio and an 18.1% buyback ratio. The same report judged that, given the current price-to-book level, a new shareholder return policy would likely tilt toward more buybacks than dividends.

If that view is borne out, the weight of returns could shift toward buybacks and cancellations. The next checkpoints are the full-year capital ratio and total shareholder return disclosed in the 2026 annual report.

08

Bull factors

Earnings recovery and steady loan growth

Controlling-shareholder net income rose 106.6% in 2025 to KRW 443.9 billion, and preliminary H1 2026 results reached KRW 295.6 billion. iM Bank's won-denominated loan balance exceeded KRW 60 trillion for the first time in H1 2026, with a delinquency ratio of 0.87%. Management said 2026 would show that normalized earnings are not one-off.

Higher capital ratios and expanding returns

Management said H1 2026 CET1 reached 12.27%, a record-level figure, and preliminary Q3 CET1 of 12.39% improved on the prior quarter. Cumulative buybacks and cancellations are set to reach KRW 130 billion, and a KRW 290 billion capital-reserve dividend has been secured. Yuanta Securities estimated the 2026 total shareholder return at 40.2%.

Growing contribution from non-bank units

The non-bank share reached 34.0% of profit in Q1 2026, with iM Securities, iM Life and iM Capital all contributing. iM Capital grew operating assets 13.9% in H1 2026, and iM Securities benefited from higher fee income as capital markets activity improved. Broadening earnings sources beyond bank interest income is cited as a positive factor.

09

Bear factors

Two straight quarterly declines after the Q1 peak

Operating profit peaked at KRW 193.9 billion in Q1 2026 and then declined to KRW 182.7 billion in Q2 and KRW 163.6 billion in Q3. Controlling-shareholder net income also fell to KRW 141.1 billion in Q2 and KRW 120.7 billion in Q3. Q3 controlling-shareholder net income was down 1.4% from the year-earlier quarter.

Cost and provision burden, and volatile fee income

H1 2026 net income declined from a year earlier because of higher costs from the education tax rate change and larger provisions. Some securities analysts pointed to weaker non-interest income amid higher market volatility in Q3. Non-interest income moves with market conditions, which can reduce earnings stability.

Large swings in quarterly earnings

Controlling-shareholder net income fell 90.0% quarter on quarter to KRW 12.2 billion in Q4 2025, and operating profit fell 87.2% to KRW 19.9 billion. Large quarterly swings mean it remains to be confirmed whether the annual recovery has held at the quarterly level.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders in 2024, at KRW 214.9 billion, was the smallest year among 2022–2025.

10

Risk factors

Policy direction and loan growth

Recent loan growth is explained as a result of expanding corporate lending in line with the government's productive finance policy. If that policy direction changes or corporate loan demand weakens, the pace of the bank's corporate lending growth could slow.

With won-denominated loans already above KRW 60 trillion, a slowdown in loan growth could limit the expansion of interest income.

Nationwide expansion and lending competition

After converting into a commercial bank, iM Bank faces the task of expanding its business beyond Daegu and Gyeongbuk. As it takes part in nationwide lending competition, loan rates and lending margins in new regions could come under pressure.

Initial operating costs may rise in areas where its franchise is small, and the bank must adjust its household and corporate loan portfolio at the same time.

Sustaining non-bank growth

The non-bank share of profit reached 34.0% in Q1 2026, and how fast that share rises is the key measure of structural change in the group's earnings. iM Capital earns mainly by growing operating assets, so a slowdown in asset growth or a rise in delinquencies could reduce its contribution.

Earnings at iM Securities and iM Life also depend on market conditions and insurance-related income, so their contribution can vary.

Overall view

iM Financial Group's annual filings show earnings turning from the 2024 low to a 2025 recovery, and after operating profit peaked in Q1 2026, it declined for two consecutive quarters in Q2 and Q3.

Preliminary controlling-shareholder net income for H1 2026 was KRW 295.6 billion, down 4.4% year on year, which the company attributed to an education tax rate change and higher provisions.

On the other side, CET1 of 12.27% in H1 2026 and a preliminary 12.39% at end-Q3 are near record levels, and shareholder returns including the 2025 dividend, cancellations and a capital-reserve dividend have expanded. The rise of the non-bank share of profit to 34.0% is a sign of structural change in earnings.

However, the quarterly declines, fee income volatility and provision risk all remain. Target prices and shareholder return estimates cited here are forecasts by individual brokers, not confirmed figures.

The next checkpoints are the preliminary Q4 and full-year 2026 results, the audited figures in the 2026 annual report, and the size of the year-end dividend around March 2027.
