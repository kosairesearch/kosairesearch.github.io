02

Business structure

Sewon Precision Industry makes automotive body structure parts, including front side members, cowl cross members, dash panels and radiator supports.

Sources differ on segment mix: one corporate information site reports automotive body parts at 100% of sales based on the third quarter of 2025, while Alphasquare's company summary splits sales into automotive parts at 83.24% and automotive parts molds at 16.76%.

Its core customers are Hyundai Motor and its affiliates, so sales move with completed-vehicle sales volumes and export performance. Body parts are developed and produced for specific models, so the end of a model's production run or a new model launch directly shapes the sales mix.

The company says it uses just-in-time methods to cut inventory and emphasizes cost reduction and quality stability. Listed peers in the same sector include Hyundai WIA, Sungwoo Hitech and Seoyon E-Hwa. The consolidated group includes overseas affiliate Sewon America.

On November 17, 2026, the board decided to pledge KRW 53.8 billion of time deposits against borrowings of KRW 40.05 billion from the Industrial Bank of Korea New York Branch. The pledge equals 6.67% of equity of KRW 807.1 billion as of June 30, 2026, and total collateral pledged stands at KRW 109.9 billion. The company's history also records an EcoVadis Silver sustainability rating in March 2025.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual sales rose from KRW 159.2 billion in the fiscal year ended June 2023 to KRW 162.5 billion in the fiscal year ended June 2024, up 2.1% year on year, and then to KRW 179.4 billion in the fiscal year ended June 2025, up 10.3%.

Sales then edged down to KRW 178 billion in the fiscal year ended June 2026, down 0.8%, and the fiscal year ended June 2025 was the largest sales year of the four.

Operating profit was KRW 12 billion, KRW 19.2 billion (up 59.6%), KRW 17 billion (down 11.4%) and KRW 23.6 billion (up 38.7%) across the same four fiscal years, with the fiscal year ended June 2026 the largest.

Operating margin moved from 7.6% to 11.8%, then 9.5%, and reached 13.3% in the fiscal year ended June 2026, so margin improved even in the year sales declined. In its provisional results disclosure on September 1, 2026, the company attributed the operating profit increase mainly to improved earnings at subsidiaries.

Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders went from a net loss of KRW 9.49 billion in the fiscal year ended June 2023 to KRW 55.4 billion in the fiscal year ended June 2024, a swing to profit, then KRW 46.5 billion in the fiscal year ended June 2025 (down 16.1%) and KRW 49.6 billion in the fiscal year ended June 2026 (up 6.7%).

Net profit including non-controlling interests was KRW 55.3 billion in the fiscal year ended June 2026, and the gap from the controlling-shareholder figure reflects the non-controlling share.

Operating cash flow fell from KRW 60.4 billion in the fiscal year ended June 2025 to KRW 34.8 billion in the fiscal year ended June 2026.

Quarterly, sales of KRW 39.2 billion and operating profit of KRW 7.88 billion in the quarter ended September 2025 were followed by sales of KRW 42 billion (up 7.2% quarter on quarter) and operating profit of KRW 5.12 billion (down 34.9%) in the December 2025 quarter.

The March 2026 quarter swung to an operating loss of KRW 670 million on sales of KRW 38.4 billion (down 8.5%). Sales in the June 2026 quarter rose to KRW 58.5 billion (up 52.4%) and operating profit returned to KRW 11.3 billion.

In the September 2026 quarter, sales fell to KRW 29.6 billion (down 49.4% quarter on quarter and 24.4% year on year), operating profit dropped to KRW 986 million (down 87.5% year on year), and net profit attributable to controlling shareholders turned into a loss of KRW 5.68 billion.

Whether the June quarter's recovery or the September quarter's drop represents the lasting trend will become clearer from the next quarterly figures.

05

Industry analysis

The forward demand for automotive body parts is production and sales of vehicles by automakers at home and abroad. Because Hyundai Motor and its affiliates account for a large share of Sewon Precision Industry's orders, completed-vehicle sales volumes and export performance drive its sales trend.

A corporate information page describes the expansion of SUVs, growth in eco-friendly vehicles and advances in autonomous driving technology as drivers of sustained growth in the auto industry. Whether those drivers translate into body-parts volumes depends on new model production schedules and order announcements.

Body parts are often developed and mass-produced on platform replacement cycles, so the end of a model's production or the launch of a new model can change quarterly sales sharply.

In Alphasquare's company summary, based on the fiscal year ended June 2026 and compared with 137 automotive parts companies, Sewon Precision Industry's operating margin ranked third, while the sector average operating margin was slightly negative.

The same page ranks its market capitalization 49th and its sales 89th, so profitability rank and scale rank diverge considerably. Margin figures should therefore be read alongside sales scale. Its debt ratio is also well below the sector average, which is cited as a financial strength.

With operating profit falling to KRW 986 million in the September 2026 quarter, whether that subsidiary improvement carried through at the quarterly level is the key point to watch next. A cash and in-kind dividend decision was disclosed on September 15, 2026.

Because the pledge is linked to affiliate borrowings, how its terms are renewed after October 2, 2027 is the structural point to track. Sales direction will be determined by the sales and export trends of Hyundai Motor's completed vehicles, so monthly sales releases are a useful reference.

On margins, the 13.3% operating margin for the fiscal year ended June 2026 is the benchmark against which the next annual report will be compared.

The October to December 2026 quarter is the second quarter of the company's fiscal year, and its provisional results will be the most direct test of whether the September quarter's weakness reverses.

Overall view
