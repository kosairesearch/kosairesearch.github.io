02

Business structure

Sewon Precision Industry was founded in 1989 and listed on the KOSPI in 1996 as a specialized maker of automotive body parts. Its main products are body reinforcement panels and other body components, and automotive parts account for most of revenue, with mold work making up a smaller share.

Its key customers are Hyundai Motor and its affiliates, and because it supplies body parts directly to them, its revenue tracks their vehicle sales and export volumes. On quality, the company reports a defect rate of 100 PPM or below and has been designated a Grade 1 supplier in Hyundai Motor's plant evaluations.

Its production system uses a just-in-time (JIT) approach aimed at reducing inventory and cost.

In its group structure, the company has 12 domestic affiliates and a Chinese consolidated subsidiary, Samha Sewon, and the electronic disclosure attributed the rise in operating profit for the fiscal year ended June 2026 mainly to improved results at its subsidiaries.

Its competitive set overlaps with listed auto-parts makers such as Hyundai Mobis, Hyundai WIA and Sungwoo Hitech, and its dependence on automakers is high. On the June 2026 figures, its operating margin ranks near the top of the automotive parts industry.

04

Earnings analysis

On the annual figures from the periodic reports, revenue rose from KRW 159.2 billion for the fiscal year ended June 2023 to KRW 162.5 billion for the year ended June 2024 (up 2.1%) and KRW 179.4 billion for the year ended June 2025 (up 10.3%), before edging down to KRW 178 billion for the year ended June 2026 (down 0.8%).

The year ended June 2025 was the largest revenue year in this period.

Operating profit went from KRW 12 billion in the year ended June 2023 to KRW 19.2 billion in the year ended June 2024 (up 59.6%), fell to KRW 17 billion in the year ended June 2025 (down 11.4%), and reached KRW 23.6 billion in the year ended June 2026 (up 38.7%), the largest operating profit year in the period.

The operating margin moved from 7.6% and 11.8% to 9.5% and then 13.3% in the year ended June 2026, its highest level in the period. Revenue stayed flat while margins improved, and the electronic disclosure cited improved results at subsidiaries as the main driver of higher operating profit.

Profit attributable to controlling shareholders swung from a net loss of KRW 9.49 billion in the year ended June 2023 to net income of KRW 55.4 billion in the year ended June 2024, a turnaround to profit, then fell to KRW 46.5 billion in the year ended June 2025 (down 16.1%) and rose to KRW 49.6 billion in the year ended June 2026 (up 6.7%).

Net income including non-controlling interests was KRW 55.3 billion for the year ended June 2026, and the gap from controlling-shareholder income reflects the non-controlling share, which was also large in the year ended June 2023, when the net loss was KRW 31.3 billion.

Operating cash flow was KRW 37 billion, KRW 19 billion, KRW 60.4 billion and KRW 34.8 billion across the four fiscal years, a wide swing from year to year.

Quarterly revenue went from KRW 60.6 billion in Q2 2025 to KRW 39.2 billion in Q3 2025 (down 35.4% quarter on quarter), KRW 42 billion in Q4 2025 (up 7.2%), and KRW 38.4 billion in Q1 2026 (down 8.5%) before recovering to KRW 58.5 billion in Q2 2026 (up 52.4%).

Operating profit fell from KRW 8.14 billion in Q2 2025 to KRW 7.88 billion in Q3 and KRW 5.12 billion in Q4, turned into an operating loss of KRW 670 million in Q1 2026, and returned to operating profit of KRW 11.3 billion in Q2 2026 (up 38.8% year on year).

Profit attributable to controlling shareholders rose to KRW 23.6 billion in Q2 2026 (up 1,243.2% from Q1), and the total for the last four quarters (Q3 2025 to Q2 2026) was KRW 49.6 billion.

Industry analysis

Sewon Precision's revenue is tied to automakers' vehicle sales and export volumes, so the industry backdrop depends on domestic and overseas production plans at its customers.

Company profile data point to a larger SUV mix, growth in eco-friendly vehicles and advances in autonomous driving technology as medium- to long-term growth drivers for the auto industry. These describe the sector as a whole and should be separated from the company's own volume trends.

Because a large share of volume comes from Hyundai Motor's group, sales at that customer flow directly into revenue. Shifts in vehicle mix and the move toward electrification are variables that can change the volume mix of body parts.

In the auto-parts market, Hyundai Mobis, Hyundai WIA, Sungwoo Hitech, Daewon Kang Up and Seoyon E-Hwa compete with it. On June 2026 figures, its operating margin ranks near the top of the industry, while its market capitalization and revenue are below industry averages, so it is small in scale. Whether its margin advantage holds depends on the vehicle mix of its customers and its cost structure.

Bear factors

Stalled revenue growth

Revenue for the year ended June 2026 was KRW 178 billion, down 0.8%, and did not exceed the KRW 179.4 billion of the year ended June 2025. Q2 2026 revenue of KRW 58.5 billion was also down 3.5% year on year. Part of the profit gain rests on subsidiary results, so its durability is an open question.

Quarterly earnings volatility

Revenue in Q3 2025 fell 35.4% from the prior quarter, and Q1 2026 swung to an operating loss of KRW 670 million. Quarterly swings remain large even after the return to profit in Q2 2026. Annual figures alone do not show this quarter-to-quarter variation.

Dependence on automakers and customer concentration

With key customers concentrated in Hyundai Motor and its affiliates, their sales and export trends flow straight into revenue. A slowdown in automaker sales or a model changeover could shift the delivery mix. The Chinese subsidiary also adds exposure to overseas business conditions.

Risk factors

Customer concentration risk

Because much of revenue comes from deliveries to Hyundai Motor and its affiliates, changes in their production plans feed directly into volume. A mismatch in model discontinuation or new-model timing could cause sharp swings in quarterly revenue. Whether customer diversification shows up in disclosures is the key to this risk.

Dependence on subsidiary results

The rise in operating profit for the year ended June 2026 was attributed mainly to improved subsidiary results. If subsidiary results reverse, consolidated operating profit could fall in the same direction. Changes in conditions at overseas affiliates, including the Chinese subsidiary, also affect consolidated results.

Possible revisions to preliminary figures

Media reports noted that the annual preliminary results released in early September 2026 could still be adjusted through external audit and shareholder approval. Given the wide quarterly swings, differences between preliminary and periodic-report figures are also worth tracking. This risk is resolved as periodic reports are filed.

Overall view

Sewon Precision Industry supplies body parts to Hyundai Motor and its affiliates, and its revenue for the year ended June 2026 was flat at about KRW 178 billion. Over the same period, operating profit rose 38.7% to KRW 23.6 billion and the operating margin climbed to 13.3%, a clear improvement in profitability.

The electronic disclosure attributed the main driver to improved subsidiary results, so the durability of the profit recovery depends on subsidiary performance. On the financial side, the debt ratio of 9.9% and total equity of KRW 807.1 billion indicate a light financial burden.

Quarterly, an operating loss of KRW 670 million in Q1 2026 was followed by operating profit of KRW 11.3 billion in Q2 2026, although Q2 revenue fell 3.5% year on year. On valuation, the price-to-book ratio and dividend yield can be compared as facts against industry levels, which sit at a lower level.

Strengths are the profit recovery and financial capacity, while weaknesses are flat revenue, quarterly volatility, and dependence on automakers and subsidiaries. The next checkpoint is whether the return to profit continues and how much revenue recovers in the Q3 2026 quarterly report, due November 14, 2026.
