02

Business structure

Sewon Precision Industry, founded in 1989, is a specialized manufacturer of automotive body parts headquartered in Dalseo-gu, Daegu. As a specialized auto parts maker, it participates in part development for various Hyundai Motor and Hyundai affiliate models and supplies mass-production volumes.

Its production items include body components such as front side members, cowl cross members, dash panels, and radiator supports.

Hyundai Motor designated the company a Grade-1 supplier in its factory-rating evaluation and a Level-1 supplier in its new-model development capability evaluation, cementing its base as a specialized auto parts firm.

Revenue is generated almost entirely from a single automotive body-parts segment, with no separate segment breakdown, concentrated on supplying finished-vehicle makers. Its main customers are Hyundai Motor and KIA, with most revenue tied to the two companies' domestic production volumes.

In the domestic body-parts market, multiple Tier-1 suppliers such as Sungwoo Hitech and Myoung Shin Industry compete with similar product lines. Total equity stood at KRW 712 billion at end-2025, indicating a balance sheet with asset capacity well in excess of the company's revenue scale.

04

Earnings analysis

Sewon Precision Industry's consolidated revenue rose from KRW 115.2 billion in 2022 to KRW 159.2 billion in 2023 (up 38.2% year on year), KRW 162.5 billion in 2024 (up 2.1%), and KRW 179.4 billion in 2025 (up 10.3%), marking three consecutive years of growth from 2023 through 2025.

Within this period, 2025 was the largest year for revenue and 2022 the smallest. Operating profit swung from an operating loss of KRW 10.9 billion in 2022 to operating income of KRW 12 billion in 2023, rose further to KRW 19.2 billion in 2024 (up 59.6%), then fell to KRW 17 billion in 2025 (down 11.4%).

The operating margin climbed from -9.5% in 2022 to 7.6% in 2023 and 11.8% in 2024 before easing to 9.5% in 2025, making 2024 the recent profitability peak.

Controlling-interest net income remained in loss for 2022 (a net loss of KRW 11.3 billion) and 2023 (a net loss of KRW 9.49 billion, a narrower loss), turned positive at KRW 55.4 billion in 2024, and came in at KRW 46.5 billion in 2025 (down 16.1%).

On a quarterly basis, the company swung to an operating loss of KRW 4.11 billion in Q3 2025, then rebounded in Q4 with revenue up 61.2% quarter on quarter to KRW 60.6 billion and operating income turning positive at KRW 8.14 billion (up 223.6% year on year), underscoring significant quarter-to-quarter volatility.

The sum of controlling-interest net income across the four quarters from Q1 to Q4 2025 was KRW 46.5 billion, matching the disclosed annual figure. Operating cash flow rose from KRW 24.6 billion in 2022 to KRW 60.4 billion in 2025, showing that cash generation improved even amid earnings volatility.

05

Industry analysis

Korea's auto parts industry is heavily influenced by the production and sales performance of Hyundai Motor and KIA.

The body-structure parts Sewon Precision Industry produces are common to both internal-combustion and electric vehicles, so the impact of powertrain transition is relatively more limited than for engine- or transmission-related suppliers.

However, if overall vehicle production declines or a particular model is discontinued or redesigned, related body-parts orders fall as well, so linkage to end demand remains significant.

At Hyundai Mobis, the share of revenue from KIA and its subsidiaries reached 38.2%, overtaking the 36.3% share from Hyundai Motor and its subsidiaries for the first time since such disclosure began, whereas Hyundai Motor's share had been higher a year earlier.

This illustrates how, for a supplier like Sewon Precision Industry that ships to both Hyundai Motor and KIA, shifts in volume allocation between the two customers can affect the revenue mix.

In the domestic body-parts market, companies such as Sungwoo Hitech and Myoung Shin Industry compete with similar product lines, with price competitiveness and quality certifications such as factory-grade ratings serving as key variables in order wins.

Fluctuations in raw material prices such as steel directly affect cost of goods sold, and margins can vary depending on the outcome of price negotiations with vehicle makers.

As the shift to electric vehicles accelerates, demand for lighter body structures and new materials may increase, leaving technological adaptation as a challenge for existing steel-based body-parts makers.

08

Bull factors

Earnings Turnaround and Improved Cash Generation

After turning from an operating loss of KRW 10.9 billion in 2022 to operating income of KRW 12 billion in 2023, operating profit rose to KRW 19.2 billion in 2024 and held at KRW 17 billion in 2025.

Controlling-interest net income likewise moved from losses in 2022-2023 to KRW 55.4 billion in 2024 and KRW 46.5 billion in 2025. Operating cash flow grew from KRW 24.6 billion in 2022 to KRW 60.4 billion in 2025, indicating that income-statement improvements have been backed by actual cash inflows.

Low Leverage and a Substantial Equity Base

The debt-to-equity ratio fell to 9.5% at end-2025 from 14.1% in 2022 and 16.0% in 2023, reflecting reduced financial leverage.

Total equity at the same point stood at KRW 712 billion, of which controlling-interest equity accounted for KRW 636.8 billion, indicating a balance sheet with low reliance on external borrowing.

The low debt ratio provides a cushion to respond to external shocks such as rising raw material costs or slowing demand from finished-vehicle makers.

Product Mix Relatively Insulated from Powertrain Transition

Products such as front side members, cowl cross members, dash panels, and radiator supports are body-structure parts common to both internal-combustion and electric vehicles. As a result, demand depends more on overall vehicle production trends than on the pace of EV adoption itself.

Compared with suppliers focused on engines and transmissions, the risk of demand disruption from powertrain changes is relatively lower.

Bear factors

Quarterly Volatility from Hyundai Motor and KIA Dependence

Sewon Precision Industry swung to an operating loss of KRW 4.11 billion in Q3 2025 before rebounding sharply in Q4, with revenue up 61.2% quarter on quarter and operating income jumping to KRW 8.14 billion, highlighting large quarter-to-quarter swings.

This reflects a structural feature whereby results are directly tied to Hyundai Motor's and KIA's model-specific production schedules and inventory adjustments. With limited customer diversification, changes in the vehicle makers' production plans can flow through directly to results.

Profitability Softening from the 2024 Peak

The operating margin eased from 11.8% in 2024 to 9.5% in 2025, and operating income itself fell 11.4% year on year to KRW 17 billion from KRW 19.2 billion.

Controlling-interest net income also declined 16.1% to KRW 46.5 billion from KRW 55.4 billion, suggesting that 2024 was the profitability peak of the past four years. Revenue kept rising, but profit growth failed to keep pace with revenue growth.

Disagreement Over Shareholder Return Policy

At the September 2026 AGM, the board's proposed dividend of KRW 200 per share was approved, while a shareholder proposal for KRW 1,200 per share was automatically discarded under the sequential voting method.

The sixfold gap between the two proposals shows a divergence of views over whether shareholder returns are adequate relative to the company's substantial equity base. If this dispute recurs, it could lead to governance-related uncertainty.

10

Risk factors

End-Demand and Customer Concentration Risk

Most revenue is tied to the domestic production volumes of Hyundai Motor and KIA, so weak sales or production disruptions at either company would directly affect results. Delivery volumes can temporarily decline during model discontinuations or generational changeovers. Unless customer diversification occurs, this structural dependence is likely to persist for the time being.

Raw Material Price Volatility Risk

Because body-structure parts use steel as a primary input, steel price fluctuations directly affect cost of goods sold. If pricing adjustments with vehicle makers fail to fully reflect higher material costs, margins can come under pressure.

Cost factors may have partly contributed to the decline in the operating margin in 2025 relative to the prior year.

Governance and Shareholder Return Risk

The gap between the board's dividend proposal and the shareholder proposal at the 2026 AGM reveals differing views on shareholder return policy. If this disagreement is not resolved, similar voting dynamics could recur at future annual meetings.

Changes in shareholder composition or equity stakes could alter the voting landscape and become a variable for management stability.

Overall view

Sewon Precision Industry emerged from a 2022 loss to sustain profitability through 2023-2025, posting 2025 revenue of KRW 179.4 billion and controlling-interest net income of KRW 46.5 billion.

However, the operating margin eased from 11.8% in 2024 to 9.5% in 2025, and quarterly results were volatile enough to see a loss in Q3 2025 followed by a sharp rebound in Q4.

On the balance-sheet side, a 9.5% debt-to-equity ratio and KRW 712 billion in total equity reflected low leverage alongside improving cash generation.

At the September 2026 AGM, the board's proposed dividend of KRW 200 per share was approved, but the simultaneous submission of a shareholder proposal for KRW 1,200 per share underscored differing views on shareholder returns.

Preliminary figures disclosed for the 2026 fiscal year (as of September 2026) showed roughly flat revenue alongside higher operating income, though final confirmation through a regular periodic report is still needed.

Investors may wish to monitor Hyundai Motor's and KIA's production and sales trends, the next quarterly report, and the future direction of shareholder return policy together.
