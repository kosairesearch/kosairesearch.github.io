Commax: Sales Contraction Versus Fragile Profit Turnaround

Commax's sales have fallen for three straight years through 2025, and operating income has declined for two consecutive quarters since its first-quarter 2026 turn to profit.

  2. 2

  3. 3

  4. 4

  5. 5

02

Business structure

Commax is a smart home specialist founded in 1968, offering video intercoms, wall pads, door locks, and AI and IoT-based smart home systems bundled with security technology as a total solution. The company started with intercoms and door phones and has widened its lineup to video door phones and home network systems.

An August 2026 news report described the company as redefining its identity from a home network equipment supplier to an AI and home-appliance-based smart home company, and noted that it had been acquired by Kyungdong Navien by that time.

Its traditional revenue base was home network equipment installed in apartments and homes through construction firms and distributors, which made it highly sensitive to domestic construction activity.

Overseas, a 2021 securities report described exports of home network equipment to about 120 countries, including Canada, the Middle East, and Asia. Its product mix spans video phones, intercoms, door locks, and CCTV, and the company emphasizes integrating devices into a connected smart home ecosystem.

Competition centers on domestic home network specialists, while in security cameras it overlaps with larger security equipment makers such as Hanwha Vision.

With overseas sales rising to 40.4% of the total in the first half of 2026, the business is shifting its weight away from domestic construction-driven demand toward overseas customers.

04

Earnings analysis

On a disclosed annual basis, sales went from KRW 156 billion in 2022 to KRW 137 billion in 2023 (down 12.2% year on year), KRW 117.2 billion in 2024 (down 14.5%), and KRW 85.1 billion in 2025 (down 27.3%).

Sales have declined for three consecutive years from 2023 through 2025, with 2022 the largest year and 2025 the smallest in the 2022 to 2025 period. Profitability moved in the opposite direction over the same span.

The operating loss widened from KRW 5.08 billion in 2022 to KRW 6.45 billion in 2023, KRW 12.3 billion in 2024, and KRW 14.2 billion in 2025, and the operating margin fell from -3.3% to -4.7%, -10.5%, and -16.7%.

The net loss attributable to controlling shareholders narrowed to KRW 15.2 billion in 2025 from KRW 16.8 billion in 2024, but the net loss including non-controlling interests of KRW 15.1 billion should be read separately.

Operating cash flow was negative in all four years, at -KRW 5.63 billion in 2022, -KRW 687 million in 2023, -KRW 5.92 billion in 2024, and -KRW 4.14 billion in 2025.

The debt-to-equity ratio rose from 96.0% in 2022 to 753.2% in 2025, and at the end of 2025 total equity was KRW 10.9 billion, of which KRW 10.1 billion belonged to controlling shareholders, against total liabilities of KRW 82.5 billion.

On a quarterly basis, sales fell for four consecutive quarters after KRW 23.7 billion in the third quarter of 2025, reaching KRW 17.6 billion in the third quarter of 2026.

The fourth quarter of 2025 carried the largest loss in the window, with an operating loss of KRW 9.28 billion and a controlling-shareholder net loss of KRW 11.2 billion.

Operating profit turned positive in the first quarter of 2026 at KRW 3.24 billion but then fell to KRW 2.24 billion in the second quarter and KRW 755 million in the third.

A news report indicated that domestic sales fell 30.2% in the first half of 2026 while overseas sales rose 80.1%, suggesting the sales decline was concentrated domestically, and the same report pointed to a domestic construction slowdown as a backdrop. Disclosed figures alone do not show how much of the fourth-quarter 2025 loss came from one-off items.

05

Industry analysis

The home network market moves with new construction and renovation demand, so the state of the construction industry is the central variable for the sector.

According to a news report, a domestic construction slowdown has stalled the home network market, pushing players toward global markets, renovation of existing homes, and senior care.

As demand for new construction weakens, the ability to capture equipment replacement and security demand in existing homes separates one company from another.

Competition among home network specialists has long been concentrated among a small group of firms, and recently the contest has widened into smart home platforms that combine home appliances and heating and cooling.

Commax is pursuing linked heating, cooling, and ventilation equipment with Kyungdong Navien, positioning itself to add an appliance layer to its home network expertise.

In security cameras and door systems it overlaps with larger security equipment makers such as Hanwha Vision, so it must compete on both price and technology differentiation. Overseas sales reached 40.4% of the total in the first half of 2026, making overseas channels a core pillar for defending results.

Because growing overseas sales also bring currency exposure and dependence on local distributors, whether that mix shift translates into better profitability will determine the company's standing in the sector.

06

Outlook

In an August 2026 news report, the company described its direction as a transformation into a smart home company, and its concrete product timeline includes a smart door lock launched in June 2026.

According to the report, the company is exploring combining that door lock with fire-rated doors from a Kyungdong group affiliate. Heating, cooling, and ventilation equipment from Kyungdong Navien is also being pursued for integration with Commax's home network services.

The report also said a new facial-recognition door phone is under development, but its launch timing has not been disclosed. On the financial side, the key checkpoints are whether overseas sales hold at around 40.4% of the total and whether the domestic sales decline stops as construction conditions change.

Operating income fell in both the second and third quarters of 2026, so whether the operating margin can sustain profitability in upcoming results is the central test. The 25.7% year-on-year sales decline in the third quarter of 2026 should be read alongside that margin trend.

Whether new products and overseas channels lift sales will set the direction of results for the 2026 fiscal year. The 2026 annual report will show the direction of annual sales and operating profit and loss, along with any change in the debt-to-equity ratio.

Evidence of new products translating into sales growth is more likely to appear in quarterly reports from 2027 onward.

Bull factors

Rising overseas share

According to an August 2026 news report, overseas sales rose 80.1% in the first half of 2026, lifting their share to 40.4% of the total. With domestic sales down 30.2%, overseas channels are functioning as the pillar that cushions top-line results. A higher overseas share moves the business structure away from reliance on domestic construction.

Quarterly operating profit held positive

Operating income turned positive at KRW 3.24 billion in the first quarter of 2026 and stayed positive at KRW 2.24 billion in the second quarter and KRW 755 million in the third. The third-quarter 2026 operating result moved to profit from a year earlier.

Compared with the KRW 9.28 billion operating loss in the fourth quarter of 2025, the operating line has moved out of loss territory.

Appliance and heating integration

According to a news report, heating, cooling, and ventilation equipment from Kyungdong Navien is being pursued for integration with Commax's home network services. The smart door lock launched in June 2026 is also being explored in combination with fire-rated doors.

If the expansion from home network hardware into appliance-based smart home services takes hold, the scope of linked products per household could widen.

Bear factors

Four straight quarters of sales decline

Quarterly sales fell for four consecutive quarters, from KRW 23.7 billion in the third quarter of 2025 to KRW 17.6 billion in the third quarter of 2026. Third-quarter 2026 sales were down 25.7% from a year earlier.

Annual sales have declined for three consecutive years from 2023 through 2025, so signs of a top-line recovery are not yet clear.

Sharp domestic sales decline and construction exposure

A news report indicated that domestic sales fell 30.2% in the first half of 2026. The domestic construction slowdown was cited as a reason the home network market has stalled.

With a structure that has relied on new construction demand, the bottom of domestic sales is hard to identify if construction conditions do not recover.

Strained financial structure

The debt-to-equity ratio rose from 96.0% in 2022 to 753.2% in 2025, and at the end of 2025 total equity was KRW 10.9 billion against total liabilities of KRW 82.5 billion. Operating cash flow was negative in all four years from 2022 through 2025. While losses continued, the thinner financial cushion could constrain execution of the business transformation.

Risk factors

Execution of the business transformation

The shift into an AI smart home company depends on new product launches and integration with appliance makers. The facial-recognition door phone is still in development with no disclosed launch timing. If the transformation slows, it may be difficult to offset the decline in domestic construction-related demand.

Financial leverage

A debt-to-equity ratio of 753.2% and accumulated losses could affect the company's ability to raise funds or operate flexibly. If operating losses persist with a small equity base, financial room narrows further. This is why changes in the debt structure and cash flow need to be checked in the annual report.

Overseas dependence and currency

With overseas sales at 40.4% of the total, currency movements and local distribution channels affect results more heavily. Even with strong overseas growth, the company could still see a stalled top line if that growth does not offset the domestic decline. Whether overseas expansion leads to better profitability is the key question.

Overall view

Commax's sales fell from KRW 156 billion in 2022 to KRW 85.1 billion in 2025, while the operating loss widened from KRW 5.08 billion to KRW 14.2 billion over the same period.

Operating income turned positive in the first quarter of 2026 but declined in the second and third quarters, and third-quarter 2026 sales were down 25.7% from a year earlier.
