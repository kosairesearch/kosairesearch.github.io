Revenue Rising, Operating Losses Persist at Keyeast

02

Business structure

Keyeast runs drama production, variety production, artist management, and concert and OST businesses together, making it a diversified entertainment company. For its drama unit, the company targeted four productions in 2025 and said it had a three-year lineup ready, according to March 2025 reporting.

Titles released in 2024 included a JTBC drama, a TVING original, a KBS2 weekend drama, a Coupang Play series, a tvN drama and a Disney+ original, six works in total. The variety unit was set up in 2024, and its first in-house variety program for ENA reportedly ranked near the top of Netflix charts.

Artists under management reportedly included Chae Jung-an, Park Myung-hoon, Bae Jung-nam, Han Sun-hwa, Kang Han-na and Stephanie Lee as of March 2025, and the company added Cha Seung-won and Kim Hee-ae that month.

Through a disclosure on August 26, 2026, Keyeast signed a production commissioning contract worth about KRW 22.6 billion for the KBS2 historical drama Munmu, which carries a total production budget of about KRW 29.4 billion, and it will serve as production lead.

The company plans to extend the title through sponsorship, commerce and OST income. Its customers are broadcasters and streaming platforms, so production revenue can move sharply from quarter to quarter depending on scheduling and delivery timing.

Drama specialists such as Studio Dragon and independent producers not tied to a single platform are the reference group, and in September 2024 the company said its revenue was the highest among independent producers.

04

Earnings analysis

On an annual basis, Keyeast's revenue fell from KRW 62 billion in 2022 to KRW 52 billion in 2023, down 16.2% year on year, before rising to KRW 75.8 billion in 2024, up 45.8%, which was the largest year in the 2022 to 2025 window.

Revenue dropped to KRW 15.7 billion in 2025, down 79.3%, making it the smallest year in that window. Operating losses of KRW 2.4 billion in 2022 and KRW 1.48 billion in 2023 narrowed, and the company swung to an operating profit of KRW 654 million in 2024.

It returned to an operating loss of KRW 3.94 billion in 2025, and the operating margin was -25.1%.

Net losses attributable to controlling shareholders widened from KRW 1.67 billion in 2022 and KRW 5.39 billion in 2023 to KRW 11 billion in 2024, then narrowed to KRW 2.64 billion in 2025, marking four consecutive years of losses from 2022 to 2025.

The 2024 profit swing coincided with the launch of the variety unit and an expanded drama lineup. The debt-to-equity ratio moved from 44.9% in 2022 and 41.9% in 2023 to 69.9% in 2024 before falling to 48.9% in 2025.

Operating cash flow was KRW 9.93 billion in 2022, -KRW 3.24 billion in 2023, KRW 2.43 billion in 2024 and -KRW 6.8 billion in 2025, changing sign almost every year.

Quarterly revenue rose from KRW 3.63 billion in 3Q 2025 to KRW 4.62 billion in 4Q 2025, KRW 8.54 billion in 1Q 2026, KRW 10.7 billion in 2Q 2026 and KRW 12.7 billion in 3Q 2026, four consecutive quarterly increases.

Over the same span, operating losses were KRW 1.01 billion in 3Q 2025, KRW 600 million in 4Q 2025, KRW 984 million in 1Q 2026, KRW 1.04 billion in 2Q 2026 and KRW 1.39 billion in 3Q 2026.

Parent net profit turned positive at KRW 361 million in 4Q 2025, but parent net losses returned at KRW 525 million in 1Q 2026, KRW 1.72 billion in 2Q 2026 and KRW 1.69 billion in 3Q 2026. Parent net losses over the last four quarters total KRW 3.57 billion.

Revenue growth has not yet translated into an operating profit, which is the central feature of the current results.

05

Industry analysis

The drama production market moves with broadcaster scheduling and streaming platform commissioning, and producer revenue is recognized when a title is delivered or aired.

Keyeast was reported to have raised the share of its drama production unit to 18% in 3Q 2019, and it built original-series production experience for Netflix around the same period.

In a September 2024 company release, Keyeast said its revenue was the highest among independent producers not dependent on a single platform. Studio Dragon is the representative drama specialist for comparison, but Keyeast also runs artist management and variety production, so its business mix differs.

Among listed entertainment companies, SM Entertainment, YG Entertainment, HYBE and JYP Ent. center on artist management and music, so their business axes differ from Keyeast's. Drama producers tend to see quarterly swings widen when a few large productions drive scheduling.

The Munmu contract signed in August 2026 illustrates the company's push to expand its production slate. Whether production revenue flows on to sponsorship, commerce and OST income after broadcast will determine how much the IP business contributes.

Outlook

The most concrete schedule the company has disclosed is the KBS2 historical drama Munmu. A disclosure on August 26, 2026 confirmed a production commissioning contract worth about KRW 22.6 billion and a total production budget of about KRW 29.4 billion, with production running from 2H 2026 through 1H 2027.

An August 2026 report said Munmu would premiere on KBS2 in November 2026. Keyeast has secured production support from local governments including Gyeongju and Mungyeong, and it is pursuing a regional cooperation program linked to filming in Gyeongju.

The company said it is negotiating distribution of a traditional liquor collaboration edition and considering OST and concert follow-ups. Keyeast has said it will strengthen two pillars, production revenue and IP side businesses.

Completing the repayment of its convertible bonds in June 2026 was presented as a factor easing financial pressure. In March 2025, the management unit said it would reinforce staffing and build IP around individual artists.

Because revenue recognition depends on broadcast schedules and production spending, quarterly revenue is likely to move with the Munmu timeline. Whether Munmu production revenue appears in the 4Q 2026 results is the first checkpoint for the outlook.

Bull factors

Keyeast disclosed on August 26, 2026 a production commissioning contract of about KRW 22.6 billion for the KBS2 drama Munmu, equal to 143.55% of its recent revenue. The company said the contract secures a production revenue base from 2H 2026 through 1H 2027.

If the production schedule is reflected in quarterly results, revenue scale could expand, which is the bullish factor.

Revenue Recovery and Balance Sheet Repair

Revenue has risen for four consecutive quarters from 4Q 2025 through 3Q 2026, and 3Q 2026 revenue of KRW 12.7 billion was up 250.6% year on year. The debt-to-equity ratio fell from 69.9% in 2024 to 48.9% in 2025, and total equity stood at KRW 39.2 billion at the end of 2025. The company said it completed repayment of its convertible bonds in June 2026.

Expansion of IP Side Businesses

Munmu has secured production support from local governments including Gyeongju and Mungyeong, and the company said it is pursuing follow-ups such as a traditional liquor collaboration, OST and concerts. Keyeast emphasizes an IP business structure linking drama, variety and management.

If broadcast performance carries into sponsorship and commerce income, the revenue base would diversify, which is the bullish factor.

Bear factors

Continued Operating Losses

Keyeast's operating loss in 2025 was KRW 3.94 billion, a swing back into deficit from 2024, and its operating margin was -25.1%. The 3Q 2026 operating loss of KRW 1.39 billion was wider than the prior quarter, and the company has posted five consecutive loss-making quarters from 3Q 2025. The fact that revenue growth has not yet produced an operating profit is the bearish factor.

Revenue Volatility

Annual revenue moved sharply, from KRW 62 billion in 2022 to KRW 52 billion in 2023, KRW 75.8 billion in 2024 and KRW 15.7 billion in 2025. Revenue fell 79.3% in 2025, making it the smallest year in the 2022 to 2025 window.

Because production revenue is recognized per title, a gap in scheduling can sharply reduce annual revenue, which is the bearish factor.

Cumulative Net Losses and Ownership Change

Net losses attributable to controlling shareholders widened from KRW 1.67 billion in 2022 to KRW 11 billion in 2024, then narrowed to KRW 2.64 billion in 2025, marking four consecutive years of losses.

If quarterly net losses continue, losses attributable to controlling shareholders keep accumulating, so whether the quarterly loss narrows is the next point to check. A March 2025 report said the largest shareholder's stake was being prepared for sale, so a possible change in ownership is also a bearish factor.

Risk factors

Scheduling and Performance Risk

Production revenue depends on broadcast timing and scheduling decisions, so quarterly revenue can move sharply. The fall to KRW 15.7 billion in 2025 shows this volatility. Munmu's production runs from 2H 2026 through 1H 2027, so any schedule slip would also push back the timing of revenue recognition.

Financial and Profitability Risk

Operating losses continued at KRW 984 million in 1Q 2026, KRW 1.04 billion in 2Q 2026 and KRW 1.39 billion in 3Q 2026. Parent net losses over the last four quarters total KRW 3.57 billion, and the debt-to-equity ratio reached 69.9% in 2024.

If working capital needs rise as production spending increases, operating cash flow could weaken again, which is a risk to monitor.

Ownership and Artist Contract Risk

A March 2025 report said the largest shareholder's stake was being prepared for sale, which creates the possibility of an ownership change. The management business is exposed to changes in artist contracts.

Keyeast added Cha Seung-won and Kim Hee-ae in early 2025 to strengthen its roster, but whether artists renew contracts can vary year to year.

Overall view

Keyeast posted losses from 2022 through 2025, and 2025 revenue fell 79.3% to KRW 15.7 billion.

Revenue rose for four consecutive quarters in 2026, reaching KRW 8.54 billion in 1Q, KRW 10.7 billion in 2Q and KRW 12.7 billion in 3Q, while operating losses over the same period were KRW 984 million, KRW 1.04 billion and KRW 1.39 billion.
