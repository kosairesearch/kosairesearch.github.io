Operating Profit Recovers as Quarterly Results Swing Widely

02

Business structure

MiraeING was founded in 1971 as Kukje Electronics Industry and listed on the Korea Exchange in 1999, then changed its name to the current one in 2015. Its core is the financial solutions business, which supplies SWIFT-focused management solutions, compliance solutions and foreign exchange trading solutions to banks.

Revenue centers on sales of SWIFT-centered products and maintenance, supported by infrastructure operation demand from institutions that use the SWIFT messaging system. The company is widening its entry into new markets with its in-house eNisis platform.

The migration of the international remittance messaging standard to ISO 20022 and the XML-based upgrade demand it creates are cited as new business opportunities. It is also developing an AI-based remittance pattern analysis solution and pursuing new markets using blockchain and AI.

The business is split into a financial solutions segment and a B&E segment, and the revenue scale of the B&E segment should be checked in the segment information of the periodic report.

The company profile page lists defense security and information and communication systems integration products as main businesses, so the emphasis of the business description differs across sources.

Against larger systems integrators, the company operates in a narrower, product-centered niche with a different scale and scope.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual revenue was KRW 12.8 billion in 2022, KRW 13 billion in 2023 (up 1.5% year on year), KRW 11.7 billion in 2024 (down 10.2% year on year) and KRW 14.1 billion in 2025 (up 20.7% year on year). Among 2022 to 2025, 2025 was the largest year and 2024 the smallest.

Operating profit was KRW 3.11 billion in 2022 (margin 24.2%), KRW 3.23 billion in 2023 (margin 24.8%, up 4.1%), KRW 2.01 billion in 2024 (margin 17.2%, down 37.7%) and KRW 3.8 billion in 2025 (margin 26.9%, up 88.7%).

The company attributed the 2025 operating profit increase mainly to higher sales of its own solution products.

Net loss attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 3.03 billion in 2022, KRW 9.3 billion in 2023 (wider loss), KRW 8.04 billion in 2024 and KRW 2.6 billion in 2025 (narrower loss), marking four consecutive years of losses. The company said a decline in equity-method losses contributed to the narrower 2025 loss.

Net loss including non-controlling interests followed the same pattern, at KRW 3.04 billion, KRW 9.3 billion, KRW 8.04 billion and KRW 2.6 billion for 2022 to 2025.

Quarterly results show a wide gap between operating and net results: the third quarter of 2025 posted operating profit of KRW 1.2 billion yet a net loss attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 1.42 billion.

Operating profit then fell to KRW 801 million in the first quarter of 2026 and KRW 127 million in the second quarter, before an operating loss of KRW 450 million in the third quarter. Third-quarter revenue of KRW 18.9 billion rose 388.0% year on year, but this did not carry through to operating profit.

The KRW 18 billion net profit attributable to controlling shareholders appears driven more by non-operating items than by operations, and the composition needs confirmation in the periodic report.

The debt-to-equity ratio was 40.4% in 2022, 45.3% in 2023, 46.9% in 2024 and 45.0% in 2025, with total equity of KRW 48.6 billion and total liabilities of KRW 21.9 billion at the end of 2025.

05

Industry analysis

The company's end market is IT spending by banks and financial institutions, driven by operation of the international financial messaging system and regulatory compliance. Demand for solution upgrades tied to changes in financial market conditions and regulatory compliance continues.

The migration of the international remittance messaging standard to ISO 20022 increases demand for XML-based upgrades and works as a new business opportunity. The cycle position has two sides.

Regulation and standard changes act as structural demand, but revenue has moved in a project-like pattern, falling to KRW 11.7 billion in 2024 and recovering to KRW 14.1 billion in 2025. Whether this swing came from product sales or maintenance should be checked in the revenue breakdown of the periodic report.

Within the IT and software sector, large systems integrators such as Samsung SDS, LG CNS and Hyundai Autoever operate, but few compete directly in the SWIFT segment. Financial and security solution companies such as Ahnlab, Daou Technology and NICE Information Service can serve as structural comparisons.

The company's strengths are its in-house eNisis platform and its bank customer base, and its weaknesses are concentration and scale. Whether new businesses in blockchain and AI remittance analysis will translate into revenue is hard to gauge from figures publicly available today.

06

Outlook

No numerical annual guidance from the company is available in public materials, so the outlook is organized around reported results and the confirmed business plans.

The confirmed plans are wider market reach through eNisis, XML upgrade opportunities from the ISO 20022 transition, and development of an AI-based remittance pattern analysis solution. The company is also reported to be pursuing new markets using blockchain and AI.

However, the timing and scale of revenue contribution from each plan are not presented in public materials as figures. Whether the surge in third-quarter 2026 revenue to KRW 18.9 billion is one-off or recurring cannot yet be judged from the available data.

The starting point is that revenue and operating profit both fell in the first and second quarters of 2026 before revenue reversed in the third quarter. The most direct signal will be whether fourth-quarter preliminary results maintain third-quarter revenue levels.

Whether operating profit turns positive after the fourth quarter is a separate item to watch. The 2026 annual report should set out quarterly revenue composition and non-operating income items, which will be the basis for explaining the third-quarter earnings structure.

Whether the company's explanation that 2025 operating profit improved on higher sales of its own solution products continues in 2026 will serve as the reference for the medium-term trend.

The quarter shows that the revenue jump did change the direction of reported profit, even though the operating line remained in loss.

Operating cash flow stayed positive through the loss-making years, which supports the financial capacity case.

Bear factors

Quarterly earnings volatility

Operating profit fell to KRW 800 million in the first quarter of 2026 and KRW 127 million in the second, then turned to an operating loss of KRW 450 million in the third. Third-quarter revenue rose to KRW 18.9 billion but did not carry through to operating profit. With revenue swinging sharply from quarter to quarter, it is hard to read the trend from one or two quarters.

Four consecutive years of losses

Net loss attributable to controlling shareholders continued for four years from 2022 to 2025, and the KRW 9.3 billion loss in 2023 was the largest in that span. The 2025 loss narrowed to KRW 2.6 billion, but the period of losses cannot yet be said to have ended.

A net loss of KRW 2.44 billion reappeared in the second quarter of 2026, and the third-quarter profit rests on a single quarter.

Concentration and unproven new businesses

Revenue centers on SWIFT-related products and maintenance, leaving the company highly dependent on a product family and a bank customer base. New businesses such as eNisis, blockchain and AI remittance analysis have been outlined, but their revenue contribution is hard to gauge from public figures. As a small-cap stock, its share price may also be prone to larger swings.

Risk factors

Equity-method earnings swing

The company said a decline in equity-method losses contributed to the narrower 2025 loss. Equity-method results depend on the performance of investee companies, so if this item turns back into losses, net profit attributable to controlling shareholders could return to a loss regardless of operating performance.

How much the third-quarter 2026 profit relied on this item should be confirmed from the equity-method breakdown in the periodic report. Narrowing the gap between operating and net results is a precondition for stable earnings.

ISO 20022 transition timing

The XML upgrade demand created by the migration of the international remittance messaging standard to ISO 20022 is cited as a new business opportunity. However, if the transition schedule and the adoption timing of customer banks slip, the recognition of revenue from this demand could also be pushed back.

Because the timing and scale of revenue contribution from each plan are hard to gauge, the speed at which transition demand turns into actual orders is uncertain. A shift in the timing of transition demand could directly affect the quarterly revenue trend.

Reliance on non-operating income

Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 18 billion in the third quarter of 2026 appears driven more by non-operating items than by operations. If these items were one-off in nature, net profit in later quarters could fall back to the level of operating results.

Whether the net profit-based surplus continues therefore depends on whether such items recur. Until the composition becomes clear in the periodic report, the net profit figure should be read with limited weight.

Overall view

MiraeING showed a recovery in core profitability, with 2025 operating profit of KRW 3.8 billion (up 88.7% year on year) and an operating margin of 26.9%.

Net loss attributable to controlling shareholders continued for four consecutive years from 2022 to 2025, but the loss narrowed from KRW 8.04 billion in 2024 to KRW 2.6 billion in 2025.

The third quarter of 2026 showed revenue of KRW 18.9 billion and net profit attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 18 billion, turning profit, yet an operating loss of KRW 450 million was recorded in the same quarter, so the durability of the earnings structure remains under test.

The four-quarter sum of KRW 16.4 billion leans heavily on the third quarter of 2026. The business is concentrated in SWIFT-related financial solutions sold to banks, and the ISO 20022 transition and regulatory compliance demand are cited as opportunities.

On valuation, the price-to-book ratio is below 1x, which shows where the share price stands relative to book value but does not guarantee durability of earnings recovery. The next confirmation points are the preliminary fourth-quarter 2026 results and the 2026 annual report.
