kt alpha: profit recovery as sales base narrows

kt alpha's operating profit rose for two straight years to KRW 44.2 billion in 2025, but sales declined and quarterly operating profit fell for two consecutive quarters through the third quarter of 2026.

  2. 2

  3. 3

02

Business structure

kt alpha, listed on KOSDAQ in 1999, runs two main businesses. The first is T-commerce, operating the KT alpha Shopping channel, which the company describes as the first data-broadcasting T-commerce channel in Korea. The second is the mobile voucher business centered on corporate customers.

According to a report by the Korea IR Association, of first-half 2026 standalone sales of KRW 192.5 billion, T-commerce contributed KRW 128.0 billion (66.5%) and mobile vouchers KRW 64.5 billion (33.5%).

The company once covered content and AI and digital transformation, but it spun off and sold part of its AI and DX unit in 2022 and sold its content division in December 2024 to concentrate on commerce. The 2021 absorption merger of KT M-House was part of building mobile voucher capabilities.

T-commerce reaches customers through purchases made while watching TV, and the company is emphasizing store-centric product operations and a strategy linking TV and mobile. In mobile vouchers, growth has been driven by expanded partnerships with large platforms and rising demand for integrated vouchers.

In May 2026 the company said its corporate mobile coupon service passed 200,000 corporate customers. Revenue recognition mixes gross purchasing, where the full product sale is booked as sales, with consignment, where only the commission is booked, so changes in transaction structure flow directly into reported sales.

Competition overlaps with TV home shopping, online commerce, and the platforms' own voucher and payment products, while the company points to TV-to-mobile channel linkage and exclusive brand assortments as differentiators.

04

Earnings analysis

The annual reported figures show sales and profit moving in different directions. Sales fell from KRW 469.6 billion in 2022 to KRW 430.4 billion in 2023 (down 8.3%) and KRW 388.2 billion in 2024 (down 9.8%), then rose to KRW 395.9 billion in 2025 (up 2.0%).

Among 2022 to 2025, 2022 was the largest year by sales and 2024 the smallest.

Operating profit, by contrast, fell from KRW 15.2 billion in 2022 to a low of KRW 12.3 billion in 2023 (down 19.2%), then grew to KRW 24.6 billion in 2024 (up 100.0%) and KRW 44.2 billion in 2025 (up 79.6%), rising for two consecutive years from 2024 through 2025.

The operating margin moved from 3.2% in 2022 and 2.9% in 2023 to 6.3% in 2024 and 11.2% in 2025. The company attributed the 2025 increase to higher operating profit from improved business performance.

Profit attributable to controlling shareholders rose from KRW 12.2 billion in 2022 to KRW 16.3 billion in 2023 (up 33.6%), KRW 19.7 billion in 2024 (up 20.9%) and KRW 43.6 billion in 2025 (up 121.8%), a three-year run of increases from 2023 through 2025.

Net income including non-controlling interests is reported at KRW 12.2 billion in 2022 and KRW 43.6 billion in 2025. At year-end 2025, total equity was KRW 303.1 billion and total liabilities KRW 192.8 billion, and the debt-to-equity ratio fell from 76.4% in 2022 to 63.6% in 2025, the lowest of the four years.

Operating cash flow was KRW 50.1 billion in 2022, KRW 49.6 billion in 2023, KRW 78.8 billion in 2024 and KRW 50.1 billion in 2025.

Quarterly operating profit bottomed at KRW 6.66 billion in the fourth quarter of 2025, down 41.3% from the prior quarter, and peaked at KRW 13.5 billion in the first quarter of 2026, up 103.2%.

It then declined for two consecutive quarters, to KRW 12.7 billion in the second quarter (down 6.0%) and KRW 11.5 billion in the third (down 9.5%, but up 1.5% year on year).

Profit attributable to controlling shareholders over the latest four quarters totaled KRW 42.6 billion, and quarterly swings were wide, from KRW 6.76 billion in the fourth quarter of 2025 (down 47.1%) to KRW 11.4 billion in the first quarter of 2026 (up 68.9%).

05

Industry analysis

T-commerce and mobile vouchers rest on two different types of demand, purchases made while watching TV and mobile coupon gifting. The company cites its status as the operator of Korea's first data-broadcasting T-commerce channel as the basis of its leading position.

Company-reported figures show a sharp difference between the two businesses. First-half 2026 T-commerce transaction volume rose only 3.0%, while mobile voucher transaction volume rose 14.6% to a record, according to press reports.

The preliminary third-quarter release put transaction growth at 10.2% for T-commerce and 19.6% for mobile vouchers, which the company described as double-digit growth in both businesses. That points to slower growth in the TV channel and faster growth in mobile transactions.

In mobile vouchers, expansion of corporate customers and industry-specific sales strategies contributed, and first-quarter mobile voucher sales were reported at KRW 32.4 billion, up 7.5%.

Competition in T-commerce overlaps with TV home shopping and online commerce, while mobile vouchers compete with the own voucher and simplified payment products of large platforms. The company is responding by widening partnerships with large platforms and launching subscription-type products tied to telecom plans.

Its decision to exit content and AI businesses and concentrate on commerce can be read as a choice to focus resources on one area. Industry-wide growth rates and market share data that could anchor this comparison are limited in the sources available, so the company-reported figures carry most of the weight.

06

Outlook

The company's stated direction is a shift toward a 'Connected Commerce Company' built on AI and data. It plans to fully execute its 'SHIFT 2026' management strategy, announced in July 2026, in the second half, upgrading AI search and recommendation systems and introducing AI shopping agents for live-style broadcasting.

Specifically, it plans to upgrade the AI-based real-time communication service 'Live Talk' on KT alpha Shopping and roll out an AI search solution for its corporate mobile voucher service in sequence.

On the product side, it said it will expand exclusive T-commerce brands and launch customer-selectable integrated vouchers. CEO Park Jeong-min said the company began building a foundation for business restructuring in November and aims to show visible results from early 2027.

On shareholder returns, the company has a 30% payout policy supported by a debt-free balance sheet and says it will pursue future investment alongside stable returns. Two points are worth tracking. The first is whether transaction growth converts into sales and operating profit.

Because the shift to consignment-style operations may keep reported sales from fully reflecting activity, transaction volume and operating margin need to be read together. The second is whether the AI initiatives launched around early 2027 show up in higher transaction volume and margin improvement. Whether results are disclosed and their scale will set the basis for interpreting the following quarters.

Bull factors

Structural improvement in operating profit

Operating profit rose from KRW 15.2 billion in 2022 to KRW 44.2 billion in 2025, and the operating margin moved from 3.2% to 11.2% over the same period. Operating profit rose for two straight years from 2024 through 2025, and margin gains continued even while sales were declining.

Growth in transaction volume

In its preliminary third-quarter 2026 release, the company reported transaction growth of 10.2% for T-commerce and 19.6% for mobile vouchers. Press reports said first-half 2026 mobile voucher transaction volume reached a record.

Passing 200,000 corporate customers and expanding partnerships with large platforms are cited as the base for that growth.

Debt-light balance sheet and shareholder returns

At year-end 2025, total equity was KRW 303.1 billion and total liabilities KRW 192.8 billion, and the debt-to-equity ratio fell from 76.4% in 2022 to 63.6% in 2025. The company said it operates a 30% dividend payout policy on the basis of a debt-free structure as of the end of the third quarter. Operating cash flow reached KRW 78.8 billion in 2024, which is cited as a basis for dividend capacity.

Bear factors

Shrinking reported sales

Sales fell from KRW 469.6 billion in 2022 to KRW 388.2 billion in 2024 and rose only 2.0% to KRW 395.9 billion in 2025. Third-quarter 2026 sales were KRW 93.8 billion, down 2.6% year on year.

The company attributed the decline to a shift toward consignment-style operations, which makes sales alone a weaker gauge of business scale.

Slowing quarterly profit

Operating profit peaked at KRW 13.5 billion in the first quarter of 2026 and then fell for two consecutive quarters to KRW 12.7 billion in the second and KRW 11.5 billion in the third. Third-quarter profit attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 11.1 billion, down 13.6% year on year.

The latest four quarters total KRW 42.6 billion, compared with KRW 43.6 billion for full-year 2025, so the recovery is slowing at the quarterly level.

Slow T-commerce growth

First-half 2026 T-commerce transaction volume rose only 3.0%, slower than mobile vouchers, even though T-commerce accounts for 66.5% of sales. First-quarter T-commerce sales were reported at KRW 63.8 billion, down 4.0%. Slow growth in the core business could cap the company's overall expansion.

10

Risk factors

Business structure and metric interpretation

As the transaction structure shifts from gross purchasing to consignment, reported sales no longer fully reflect transaction volume. If transaction volume and sales keep moving in different directions, assessments of the company's scale can vary with interpretation. The gap between the company's transaction metric and finalized figures after audit also needs attention.

Channel and platform dependence

T-commerce depends on TV channel placement and broadcast platform conditions, while mobile voucher volume moves with partner terms at large platforms. A change in partnership terms or platform policy could alter the growth path.

Because the company cites partnership expansion as a growth driver, changes in dependence on partners are an important point to monitor.

Execution risk and shareholder structure

The company's timeline places visible results from its AI initiatives in early 2027, so any execution delay would push back the earnings impact. Parent KT Corporation held 70.49% as of mid-2026, which leaves a low free float and may limit trading depth.

After exiting content and AI businesses, how the company builds its next growth engine remains an open question.

Overall view

kt alpha raised its operating margin from 3.2% in 2022 to 11.2% in 2025, and profit attributable to controlling shareholders rose for three consecutive years from 2023 through 2025.

Sales, however, fell from KRW 469.6 billion in 2022 to KRW 388.2 billion in 2024 before edging up to KRW 395.9 billion in 2025, and third-quarter 2026 sales were KRW 93.8 billion, down 2.6% year on year, which the company linked to its shift toward consignment operations.

Quarterly profit peaked at KRW 13.5 billion in the first quarter of 2026 and then fell in the second and third quarters, so the annual recovery has slowed at the quarterly level. Business indicators are mixed.

The preliminary third-quarter release reported transaction growth of 10.2% for T-commerce and 19.6% for mobile vouchers, but first-half 2026 T-commerce transaction growth was only 3.0%, showing a clear gap in pace between the two businesses.

On the balance sheet, the debt-to-equity ratio of 63.6% at year-end 2025 was the lowest of 2022 to 2025, and the company presents a debt-free structure and a 30% payout policy.

The next checkpoints are the 2026 annual business report, due by late March 2027, for the consignment mix and segment transaction volumes, and the first-quarter 2027 preliminary results, to see whether operating profit returns to the first-quarter 2026 level.
