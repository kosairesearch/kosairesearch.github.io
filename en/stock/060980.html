Self-Business Recovery Meets a Third-Quarter Loss

HL Holdings' self-run auto-parts distribution and logistics lifted operating profit in the first half of 2026, but the third quarter swung to an operating loss of KRW 2.6 billion and a net loss attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 19.4 billion.

02

Business structure

HL Holdings is the business holding company of the HL Group, and its website says it runs mobility, logistics and cold chain services. Its income comes from dividends and brand royalties from subsidiaries, equity-method gains, and self-run revenue booked at the parent level.

Self-run revenue is the bulk of sales, at KRW 1,273.4 billion in 2024 or 92.9% of the total. In the first three quarters of 2025, the business segment made up 90.8% of revenue, the holding segment 6.8% and other items 2.4%. Within the business segment, distribution accounted for 39.6% and logistics for 51.2%.

Distribution covers genuine and EV parts plus aftermarket sales, while logistics centers on a 2PL structure in which an affiliate runs the parent's logistics.

Volumes under that structure track customer production, and the company tied the 2025 third-quarter revenue decline to lower 2PL volumes and weaker module-customer output.

The company has also said that a logistics business focused only on auto parts, tied to customer orders, limits profit growth, and it has pointed to a change in its logistics base.

Its robotics subsidiary, a parking-robot developer set up in September 2024, booked KRW 2 billion in revenue and a net loss of KRW 15.5 billion in the first nine months of 2025.

The company also holds two listed subsidiaries, the auto-parts maker HL Mando and the construction firm HL D&I, so their equity-method and dividend flows feed into holding-company earnings.

Hankook & Company, a holding company with a tire group in the same sector, is a reference point, but its portfolio differs enough to limit direct comparison.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual revenue rose from KRW 1.28 trillion in 2022 to KRW 1.29 trillion in 2023, up 0.7% year on year, then reached KRW 1.37 trillion in 2024, up 6.7%, before easing to KRW 1.34 trillion in 2025, down 2.6%. 2024 was the largest revenue year, yet operating profit slipped 1.9% to KRW 90.4 billion.

Press coverage attributed that to losses at some affiliates that widened equity-method losses, along with higher depreciation on facilities that pushed up cost of sales.

Operating profit went from KRW 85.8 billion in 2022 to KRW 92.2 billion in 2023, up 7.4%, and has declined for two consecutive years since, to KRW 90.4 billion in 2024 and KRW 88.7 billion in 2025. The operating margin was 6.7% in 2022 and 7.2% in 2023, and 6.6% in both 2024 and 2025.

Profit attributable to controlling shareholders went from KRW 3.1 billion in 2022 to KRW 50.5 billion in 2023, up 1,530.2%, then fell 60.7% to KRW 19.8 billion in 2024 and rose 5.1% to KRW 20.8 billion in 2025.

Net income including non-controlling interests was KRW 21.5 billion in 2025, a different figure from profit attributable to controlling shareholders. Operating cash flow was KRW 48.9 billion in 2022, KRW 32.3 billion in 2023, KRW 63.4 billion in 2024 and KRW 45.2 billion in 2025.

On a quarterly basis, revenue was KRW 329.6 billion and operating profit KRW 19.8 billion in Q3 2025, and Q4 2025 operating profit rose to KRW 35.2 billion, up 78.4% from the prior quarter. Q2 2026 operating profit of KRW 37.2 billion is the highest of the five quarters disclosed.

Q3 2026 revenue was KRW 323.9 billion, down 10.0% from the prior quarter and 1.7% from a year earlier, and the company posted an operating loss of KRW 2.6 billion with a net loss attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 19.4 billion.

Profit attributable to controlling shareholders over the last four quarters totals KRW 23.2 billion, and that includes the Q4 2025 net loss of KRW 195 million and the Q3 2026 net loss.

The gap between operating profit and profit attributable to controlling shareholders comes from non-operating items such as equity-method gains and losses, so the cause of the third-quarter swing should be checked in the annual report's segment earnings.

05

Industry analysis

The auto-parts cycle affects both HL Holdings' self-run business and its subsidiaries. The auto-parts subsidiary's preliminary Q3 2026 operating profit was KRW 51.5 billion, down 45.3% year on year, and its operating margin fell 1.8 percentage points to 2.2%.

Its revenue for the first three quarters of 2026 rose 1.9% to KRW 7.13 trillion, but cumulative operating profit fell 9.1%. For the holding company, that trend can flow into equity-method income and dividend capacity, so subsidiary results pass through to parent earnings.

In late 2025, a Korean brokerage noted that customer output stagnation, lower component volumes and the halt of Russian operations had stalled growth, while also saying profitability had recovered from a Q1 low.

The construction subsidiary HL D&I's preliminary Q3 2026 operating profit was KRW 30.1 billion, up 18.1%, on revenue of KRW 471.7 billion, down 1.1%. In September 2026, another brokerage expected HL D&I's civil engineering margins to recover as new sites start generating revenue in the second half.

Auto-parts volumes depend on vehicle production cycles, while construction depends on housing and civil-works orders, so the two businesses sit at different points in their cycles.

The company has said an auto-parts-only logistics structure limits growth and has pointed to a change in its logistics base, so the speed of that shift will shape its position within the sector. Exposure to both auto parts and construction also means macro-cycle sensitivity arrives through several channels.

06

Outlook

The company's 2025 revenue outlook of KRW 1.3379 trillion implied a 2.4% decline from the prior year, and it expected the operating margin to hold at 6.6%. Reported 2025 results came in at KRW 1.34 trillion in revenue and a 6.6% operating margin, close to that outlook.

For robotics, the company set a 2025 revenue target of KRW 6.1 billion, split between Stanley Robotics at KRW 5.8 billion and HL's robotics unit at KRW 0.7 billion, and said it aimed to more than double robotics revenue each year.

Still, the robotics unit's net loss of KRW 15.5 billion in the first nine months of 2025 means any profit contribution is a longer-term matter. The company itself has said robotics earnings should be judged over a longer horizon.

In the self-run business, the key question is whether the recovery from the Q1 low continues, and because operating profit held in the KRW 30 billion range in the first half of 2026 before the Q3 loss, the cause of that reversal is the most important item for the next results.

Subsidiary earnings, equity-method income and the speed of robotics revenue growth will together likely set the direction of holding-company earnings.

Holding-company valuation is often read as the market value of the stakes in listed subsidiaries plus the value of the parent's own businesses, so subsidiary results need to be read alongside it.

The price-to-book ratio sits well below book value per share, which means little premium is paid over net asset value on the books. The market's willingness to credit the value of subsidiary stakes and real estate held inside that net asset base may explain the low multiple.

The earnings-based multiple is sensitive to quarterly swings, because profit attributable to controlling shareholders over the last four quarters totals only KRW 23.2 billion.

That profit peaked at KRW 50.5 billion in 2023, fell to KRW 20.8 billion in 2025, and turned to a net loss in Q3 2026, so whether earnings recover is central to reading the multiple.

The dividend yield reflects both the dividend policy and the share price, and with earnings down, dividend capacity depends on dividends received from subsidiaries and the holding company's cash flow.

Changes in the market value of listed subsidiary stakes feed directly into the holding company's value, so the size of the holding discount moves with those stocks. Comparisons with other holding companies' multiples are only partly useful because their portfolios differ.

08

Bull factors

Recovery in self-run operating profit

Q4 2025 operating profit rose 78.4% from the prior quarter to KRW 35.2 billion, followed by KRW 34.7 billion in Q1 2026 and KRW 37.2 billion in Q2 2026. A Korean brokerage said profitability had recovered from a Q1 low.

Operating profit stayed in the KRW 30 billion range for three straight quarters before the third-quarter swing, and that is the core of the bullish case.

Improving balance sheet

Of total equity of KRW 1.08 trillion at the end of 2025, KRW 1.07 trillion is attributable to controlling shareholders. Lower leverage gives the company more room to absorb swings in the value of its subsidiary stakes.

Improving construction subsidiary

Construction subsidiary HL D&I posted preliminary Q3 2026 operating profit of KRW 30.1 billion, up 18.1%, and cumulative operating profit of KRW 75.6 billion, up 27.2%. A Korean brokerage expected margin recovery in its civil-engineering business. Its results could partly offset weakness in auto parts at the holding-company level.

Bear factors

Third-quarter swing to losses

Q3 2026 posted an operating loss of KRW 2.6 billion and a net loss attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 19.4 billion. That compares with profit of KRW 20.3 billion in Q2 2026 and KRW 12.3 billion in Q3 2025.

Including the Q4 2025 net loss, the last four quarters total KRW 23.2 billion, a sign of large quarterly swings.

Weaker subsidiary earnings

The auto-parts subsidiary's preliminary Q3 2026 operating profit was KRW 51.5 billion, down 45.3%, and its margin fell to 2.2%. Its cumulative operating profit for the first three quarters of 2026 declined 9.1%. If equity-method income weakens, holding-company earnings could face similar pressure.

Robotics losses and affiliate drag

The robotics unit recorded KRW 2 billion in revenue and a KRW 15.5 billion net loss in the first nine months of 2025. In 2024, losses at some affiliates widened equity-method losses and pulled down operating profit. If new-business investment and affiliate losses coincide, earnings capacity could weaken further.

10

Risk factors

Demand and customer concentration

The 2PL logistics business is tied to volumes from the parent and affiliates, so a stall in customer output quickly becomes a revenue stall. In Q3 2025, lower 2PL volumes and weaker module-customer output pulled down revenue.

The decline in the auto-parts subsidiary's Q3 2026 operating profit shows the same auto cycle risk reaching the holding company.

Equity-method and affiliate volatility

Affiliate operating losses and equity-method losses move profit attributable to controlling shareholders apart from operating profit. In 2024, affiliate losses widened equity-method losses and contributed to the decline in operating profit.

The Q3 2026 net loss of KRW 19.4 billion, far larger than the KRW 2.6 billion operating loss, suggests non-operating and equity-method items weighed heavily.

New-business burden and debt

The robotics unit's net loss of KRW 15.5 billion in the first nine months of 2025 weighs on earnings. Total liabilities were KRW 1.26 trillion at the end of 2025, and the debt ratio stood at 116.7%. Equity is large, but the debt load means interest costs and earnings swings still need to be watched together.

12

Overall view

HL Holdings' disclosed revenue has stayed in a band from KRW 1.28 trillion in 2022 to KRW 1.34 trillion in 2025, so the sales base has not changed much. Operating profit peaked at KRW 92.2 billion in 2023 and fell to KRW 88.7 billion in 2025.

The first half of 2026 showed a self-run recovery, with operating profit of KRW 34.7 billion in Q1 and KRW 37.2 billion in Q2. Q3 2026 then turned to an operating loss of KRW 2.6 billion and a net loss attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 19.4 billion.

The auto-parts subsidiary's preliminary Q3 operating profit fell 45.3%, while the construction subsidiary's rose, so the subsidiaries moved in different directions. On the balance sheet, the debt ratio fell from 127.5% in 2023 to 116.7% in 2025, which is a positive.

On the other side, profit attributable to controlling shareholders over the last four quarters totals only KRW 23.2 billion, and new businesses such as robotics are still loss-making.

Because strengths and weaknesses coexist, the next checkpoints are the preliminary Q4 2026 results and the segment profit in the 2026 annual report.
