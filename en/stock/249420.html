02

Business structure

IL Dong Pharmaceutical manufactures and sells medicines, raw materials, health supplements and special nutrition foods. Its key medicines include the active vitamin brand Aronamin and the antibiotic Furamarin. The business is organized into prescription drugs (ETC), over-the-counter drugs and healthcare.

In 2026 the company widened its prescription portfolio, signing an exclusive domestic distribution and sales deal for urology drugs with Viatris Korea and taking on domestic distribution and co-promotion of the migraine drug Nurtec ODT from Pfizer Korea.

In-licensed products can generate sales faster than internally developed drugs because they use the existing sales network. On contract manufacturing, the company presented its dedicated antibiotic and anticancer facilities and raw-material supply model to overseas partners at CPhI 2026 in October.

The healthcare line includes probiotics, health functional foods and consumer brands. In June 2026 it absorbed its drug-discovery subsidiary, bringing R&D staff and pipelines in-house. No verifiable segment revenue split is disclosed, so the relative weight of each business is not quantified here.

In the P-CAB reflux market, two products already on the domestic market hold the leading positions, so the follow-on candidate ID120040002 must prove differentiation.

04

Earnings analysis

On an annual basis, revenue shrank while earnings moved from losses into profit. Revenue was highest in 2022 at KRW 637.7 billion and lowest in 2025 at KRW 566.9 billion, with KRW 600.8 billion in 2023 (down 5.8% year on year) and KRW 614.9 billion in 2024 (up 2.4%).

Operating loss narrowed from KRW 73.5 billion in 2022 to KRW 53.9 billion in 2023, then turned into operating profit of KRW 13.1 billion in 2024 and rose 48.5% to KRW 19.5 billion in 2025.

Attributable net loss shrank from KRW 141.6 billion in 2022 and KRW 78.9 billion in 2023 to KRW 4.58 billion in 2024, before turning into attributable net income of KRW 27.7 billion in 2025.

Net income including non-controlling interests was KRW 23.7 billion in 2025, a different figure because of the non-controlling share. Operating margin moved from -11.5% in 2022 to 3.4% in 2025, and the debt-to-equity ratio fell from 270.3% in 2024 to 139.2% in 2025.

At the end of 2025, total liabilities were KRW 333.5 billion and total equity was KRW 239.5 billion. Operating cash flow went from -KRW 53.5 billion in 2022 and -KRW 36.6 billion in 2023 to KRW 28.3 billion in 2024 and KRW 19 billion in 2025.

Quarterly revenue went from KRW 145.5 billion in Q3 2025 to a low of KRW 142 billion in Q1 2026, then rose to KRW 147.9 billion in Q2 and KRW 151 billion in Q3, up 3.8% year on year.

Operating profit rose from KRW 6.76 billion in Q3 2025 to KRW 7.93 billion in Q4 and KRW 9.23 billion in Q1 2026, fell to KRW 1.98 billion in Q2 (down 78.5% quarter on quarter), and recovered to KRW 7.14 billion in Q3.

Attributable net income fell from KRW 22.4 billion in Q3 2025 to KRW 7.24 billion in Q4, KRW 8.27 billion in Q1 2026 and KRW 5.7 billion in Q2, then swung to an attributable net loss of KRW 4.76 billion in Q3.

The company attributed the Q1 2026 improvement to better cost of sales from cost innovation and lower debt and interest expenses, and attributable net income over the last four quarters totaled KRW 16.4 billion.

05

Industry analysis

For the domestic drug market, the key variable in 2026 is the revision of the drug pricing system. At the 2026 New Year ceremony, the group chairman pointed to large changes such as drug price cuts and stressed preparation.

In prescription drugs, in-licensed products fill revenue in a short period, while internally developed drugs have commercialization timing set by clinical and approval outcomes. The obesity drug market is shifting from injectables toward oral products.

A brokerage report from November 2025 noted that several global competing compounds struggled with liver-related problems, which limited dose escalation or weight-loss efficacy. The same report judged that the company's candidate showed liver-related markers improving.

That assessment rests on Phase 1 results, so reproducibility in later trials is the key test. In the P-CAB market, HK inno.N and Daewoong Pharmaceutical already hold leading positions, so the follow-on reflux candidate must demonstrate differentiation.

The company's competitive strength lies in contract manufacturing backed by GMP-certified facilities, while prescription drug distribution and consumer healthcare continue to anchor earnings until new-drug results arrive.

Outlook

The company's 2026 management theme is 'ID 4.0, creating competitive advantage through results,' with two headline targets: revenue and profit performance, and securing new growth drivers. Improving profitability through cost innovation and portfolio restructuring is presented as the central task.

In new-drug R&D, the company plans to pursue out-licensing overseas and open innovation actively. The company said the oral GLP-1 obesity candidate ID110521156 showed superior efficacy and safety versus competing compounds in Phase 1.

Related reports mention plans to enter global Phase 2 in 2026 and to pursue technology-transfer talks with a big pharma company. The P-CAB reflux candidate ID120040002 is in a domestic Phase 3 trial, and the company said global commercialization discussions took place at CPhI in October 2026.

The autoimmune and fibrosis candidate IL21120033 was targeted for clinical trial application in Q3 2026, and the cyclin K degrader IL2106 for a rare cancer indication was targeted for Q4 2026. Overseas business is being diversified through contract manufacturing and OEM/ODM models, extending into healthcare and beauty.

On earnings, the next checkpoint is whether the revenue contribution from in-licensed prescription drugs and the cost-ratio gains persist in later quarters. New-drug revenue remains limited in timing and scale by clinical results and approvals.
