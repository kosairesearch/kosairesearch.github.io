Sales Grew, but Profits Remain in the Red

02

Business structure

CTR Mobility is an automotive parts maker founded in 1994 as Samjin Jeonggi. It listed on the KOSPI in November 2019 and adopted its current name in April 2023.

It produces driveline and chassis components at its Ulsan headquarters, Daegu and Seosan plants, and the Miryang plant of a subsidiary, supplying Hyundai Motor Group, domestic parts makers and overseas automakers.

Based on Q2 2026 sales compiled by Butler, the driveline segment accounts for 59% and the chassis segment for 41%. According to a report by Dealsite, the chassis share of sales exceeded 40% for the first time in Q1 2026.

Driveline parts include rotor shafts and other components tied to combustion-engine power transmission, so demand is seen shrinking as the shift to electric vehicles accelerates.

By contrast, the chassis side has drawn attention for localizing a ball screw for electronic brakes and supplying it to Tesla, according to media reports.

In June 2026 the company said it had spun off its aluminum forging business into a new subsidiary to separate decision-making, and merged its subsidiary CTR DTS to strengthen driveline synergies.

The mid-term BEYOND 3015 target announced in 2023 set KRW 1 trillion in 2030 sales and a 5% operating margin, while 2025 sales were KRW 461.5 billion. Domestic peers in chassis and driveline parts include HL Mando, Hyundai Mobis, SL and Sungwoo Hitech.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual sales moved from KRW 423.1 billion in 2022 to KRW 444.2 billion in 2023 (up 5.0% year on year), KRW 432.3 billion in 2024 (down 2.7%) and KRW 461.5 billion in 2025 (up 6.8%). Among 2022–2025, 2025 was the largest year and 2022 the smallest.

Operating results swung from an operating loss of KRW 736 million in 2022 to an operating profit of KRW 525 million in 2023, a turn to profit, then rose to KRW 3.92 billion in 2024 (up 647.2%) and KRW 5.81 billion in 2025 (up 48.3%), growing for two consecutive years from 2024 to 2025.

Operating margin moved from -0.2% to 0.1%, 0.9% and 1.3%, still in the low single digits.

Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders turned to a loss of KRW 16.9 billion in 2023, then KRW 16.6 billion in 2024 and KRW 3.23 billion in 2025, with the loss narrowing, and it has been in the red for three consecutive years from 2023 to 2025.

A May 2026 report by Vendor Zoom pointed to financing costs as a drag on earnings despite operating profit. The debt-to-equity ratio rose from 186.3% in 2022 to 314.1% in 2024 before easing to 283.6% in 2025. At the end of 2025, total liabilities were KRW 247.3 billion against total equity of KRW 87.2 billion.

Operating cash flow peaked at KRW 13.2 billion in 2024 before falling to KRW 4.52 billion in 2025.

Quarterly sales were KRW 114.6 billion in Q3 2025, KRW 108 billion in Q4 2025 (down 5.7% quarter on quarter), KRW 109.5 billion in Q1 2026, KRW 114.2 billion in Q2 2026 (up 4.2%) and KRW 99.6 billion in Q3 2026 (down 12.8%). Q3 2026 sales fell 13.1% from a year earlier.

Net losses attributable to controlling shareholders have run for five consecutive quarters from Q3 2025 through Q3 2026, with the last four quarters totaling KRW 6.67 billion. The company attributed weak short-term results to an EV market slowdown that delayed returns on earlier investments. Third-party data from WiseReport showed first-half 2026 operating profit down 49.4% year on year.

05

Industry analysis

CTR Mobility's end market is automobile production centered on Hyundai Motor Group. The company supplies Hyundai Motor Group, domestic parts makers and overseas automakers, so changes in vehicle output translate directly into parts demand.

The company attributes the EV slowdown to a temporary demand pause known as the chasm. Dealsite reported that domestic EV sales through March 2026 rose 156% year on year to 86,665 units... specifically 87,665 units. The same report said the expansion of the hybrid-centered eco-friendly vehicle market was a tailwind.

Domestic eco-friendly vehicle sales in Q1 2026 rose 37.1% year on year to 231,585 units. On the global side, US tariff policy, high interest rates and the Middle East war have reduced automobile production and weakened parts demand, according to a WiseReport commentary.

Dealsite's analysis describes a transition period in which pure EVs and hybrids split the market, which has produced uneven effects across the company's driveline and chassis lines.

The company is preparing to shift toward electrification and thermal management parts as competition in EVs moves from battery capacity to integrated thermal management systems. In thermal management the company competes with Hanon Systems, while in driveline and chassis it faces HL Mando, Hyundai Mobis and SL.

06

Outlook

The company said it is reviewing shareholder return measures, including share purchases by its largest shareholder and senior executives, and will set the scale and details after gathering market conditions and shareholder views (Seoul Economic Daily, report dated June 26, 2026).

According to the same report, the company plans to focus on making the results of its aluminum forging and electrification and thermal management parts businesses visible on the back of a recovery.

The aluminum forging unit is winning orders from global automakers and is trying to expand into lightweight parts for humanoid robots, defense and urban air mobility.

A market report from late October 2026 said news of a joint venture with the Saudi government to set up a local automotive parts company was cited as a reason for intraday strength.

Joint venture capital structure and production timing cannot be confirmed from the reporting alone and should be checked against later disclosures. On financing, a May 2026 report listed CTR Mobility as having issued KRW 20 billion in convertible bonds.

Convertible bonds can increase the number of shares outstanding if converted. The company's strategy depends on whether its business restructuring produces tangible results. The next checkpoint is whether the full-year operating margin in the 2026 annual report and quarterly profit end the run of losses.

Bull factors

Rising Chassis Share and EV-Related Supply

According to Dealsite, the chassis share of sales exceeded 40% for the first time in Q1 2026. The same report said a localized ball screw is supplied to Tesla. Sales of KRW 461.5 billion in 2025 were the largest of 2022–2025, widening the revenue base.

Two Years of Operating Profit Growth

Operating profit grew two years in a row, to KRW 3.92 billion in 2024 and KRW 5.81 billion in 2025. Operating margin improved from -0.2% in 2022 to 1.3% in 2025. Q2 2026 operating profit of KRW 1.25 billion rose 284.2% from the prior quarter, which can be read as a recovery signal.

Shareholder Return Review and Restructuring

In June 2026 the company said it was reviewing shareholder return measures, including share purchases by the largest shareholder and senior executives. The aluminum forging unit was spun off into a separate subsidiary to separate decision-making. The company also said it is preparing to shift toward electrification and thermal management parts.

Bear factors

Consecutive Net Losses and Wider Quarterly Losses

Net losses attributable to controlling shareholders have persisted for three years from 2023. The company has reported losses for five consecutive quarters from Q3 2025 to Q3 2026, with the last four quarters totaling KRW 6.67 billion.

Q3 2026 swung to an operating loss of KRW 928 million, and the net loss attributable to controlling shareholders widened to KRW 3.2 billion from the prior quarter.

Financial Burden and Financing Costs

Total liabilities were KRW 247.3 billion at the end of 2025 against total equity of KRW 87.2 billion. The debt-to-equity ratio fell from 314.1% in 2024 to 283.6% in 2025 but remains above the 186.3% of 2022. A May 2026 Vendor Zoom report pointed to financing costs as a drag on growth.

Weaker Auto Output and Driveline Demand

Analysts cite US tariffs, high interest rates and the Middle East war as factors reducing automobile output and parts demand. Driveline parts are seen facing sharper demand declines as the EV transition accelerates. WiseReport data show first-half 2026 operating profit fell 49.4% year on year.

Risk factors

Market Capitalization Listing Threshold

Press reports say the KOSPI market-cap threshold for continued listing rises to KRW 30 billion in the second half of 2026 and KRW 50 billion in 2027. Current market capitalization sits below the KRW 30 billion line, which makes compliance a key variable. Thresholds can change, so exchange notices should be checked. Reports also noted that delisting requirements after a management-issue designation were tightened.

Customer Concentration and Major Shareholder Stake

Hyundai Motor Group is a key customer, so the company is exposed to changes in group production plans. According to WiseReport, the largest shareholder and related parties held 49.28% as of September 2026, and the free float ratio was 50.38%. The shareholder structure is relevant alongside the review of share purchases by the largest shareholder.

Exchange Rates and Overseas Production

A high overseas sales share makes exchange rates and local utilization key variables for quarterly results. Q3 2026 sales fell 13.1% year on year, showing external demand changes flowing directly into results.

The pace at which new model awards become mass-production sales, and whether costs are recovered, also need monitoring.

Overall view

As of November 2026, the central issue for CTR Mobility is the gap between sales growth and profitability. Sales reached KRW 461.5 billion in 2025, the largest year of 2022–2025, and operating margin rose to 1.3%, yet net losses attributable to controlling shareholders have persisted for three years from 2023.

In 2026 the swing to an operating loss in Q3 and five consecutive quarters of net losses have weakened the earnings trend again. Bullish factors include a rising chassis share, reports of EV-related supply, and the review of share purchases by the largest shareholder and executives.

Bearish factors include total liabilities of KRW 247.3 billion, a debt-to-equity ratio of 283.6%, dependence on combustion-engine driveline parts, weaker automobile output, and a tighter market-cap listing threshold.

Some press interpretations hold that financing costs, given the debt load against KRW 87.2 billion of equity, are widening net losses. The next checkpoints are the full-year operating margin in the 2026 annual report, quarterly profit and loss in Q1 2027, and how the KOSPI market-cap threshold is applied.
