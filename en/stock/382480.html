02

Business structure

G.I.Tech is a precision coating-parts company whose core product is the slot die used in electrode processes for secondary and hydrogen batteries. A slot die applies electrode slurry evenly onto the current collector, which affects the energy density and yield of the battery.

In a February 2026 report, an independent research firm pointed to a vertically integrated structure covering design, precision machining, wear-resistant coating, inspection and repair. The business is split into a parts segment and an equipment-and-vision segment.

In Q1 2026, parts accounted for about KRW 6.33 billion (62.7%) of KRW 10.1 billion in revenue, and equipment and vision for about KRW 3.76 billion (37.3%), according to press coverage of that quarter's disclosure.

Within parts, U.S.-bound ESS slot-die sales were about KRW 2.5 billion and pump-system sales to global customers about KRW 2 billion. Pump systems have been supplied to Samsung SDI and then to LG Energy Solution's mass-production lines.

In the equipment-and-vision segment, the company absorbed the inspection and vision equipment maker MVTEC in July 2025 and won a lens-bonding machine order of about KRW 3.5 billion from a global mobile parts maker in February 2026.

In October 2026 it signed a supply contract for an automotive glass defect inspection system worth about KRW 3.1 billion. A notching die, a consumable in the electrode process, is undergoing quality verification at a global battery maker. Its customer base centers on battery makers in Korea and abroad.

04

Earnings analysis

On the basis of the disclosed annual results, revenue moved from KRW 39.6 billion in 2022 to KRW 36.6 billion in 2023 (down 7.6% year on year), KRW 37.8 billion in 2024 (up 3.1%) and KRW 36.2 billion in 2025 (down 4.1%). 2022 was the largest year and 2025 the smallest in the period.

Revenue has stayed in a roughly KRW 36 to 38 billion band since 2023, so top-line growth has been flat. Profitability weakened more clearly.

Operating profit went from KRW 6.93 billion in 2022 to KRW 4.5 billion in 2023 (down 35.0%), KRW 3.04 billion in 2024 (down 32.4%) and KRW 1.12 billion in 2025 (down 63.1%), declining three years in a row from 2023 through 2025. Operating margin fell from 17.5% to 12.3%, 8.1% and 3.1%.

Because margin dropped while revenue held roughly steady, the pattern points to rising cost and fixed-cost pressure. Parent-shareholder net income peaked at KRW 7.21 billion in 2023 (up 11.9%), then fell to KRW 6.46 billion in 2024 (down 10.5%) and KRW 1.05 billion in 2025 (down 83.8%), two straight years of decline.

Net income including non-controlling interests was KRW 950 million in 2025. Operating cash flow was KRW 7.08 billion in 2025, the highest of the four years. By quarter, Q4 2025 revenue of KRW 12.8 billion (up 101.2% from the prior quarter) and operating profit of KRW 2.47 billion marked the peak after a turn to profit.

Q1 2026 revenue was KRW 10.1 billion with operating profit of KRW 517 million (down 79.1% quarter on quarter). Q2 2026 swung to an operating loss of KRW 530 million. Q3 2026 revenue was KRW 3.4 billion (down 46.5% year on year), with an operating loss of KRW 2.71 billion and a parent net loss of KRW 6.01 billion.

Press coverage attributed the revenue drop to some September projects whose deliveries and inspections moved into Q4, and reported that selling, general and administrative expenses rose 29% year on year.

The net loss included a KRW 4.1 billion foreign-currency asset valuation loss, which the company said is a financial item with no cash outflow. The parent net loss over the last four quarters totals KRW 1.02 billion.

05

Industry analysis

G.I.Tech's main end market is battery makers' electrode-process investment. An analysis noted that as EV demand slowed, revenue has stayed in a band of roughly KRW 36 billion to 38 billion since 2023. The demand focus is shifting toward energy storage systems (ESS).

Power demand from AI data centers is driving continued ESS capex at battery makers. The key point is that LFP batteries used in ESS differ from EV batteries in slurry composition and coating thickness, so existing slot dies cannot easily be shared.

As a result, each time a customer builds or converts an ESS line, additional dedicated slot-die orders follow. The roughly KRW 2.5 billion in U.S.-bound ESS slot-die sales in Q1 2026 can be read as this dynamic showing up in results.

On the customer side, pump systems have been supplied to Samsung SDI and then to LG Energy Solution's mass-production lines, giving the company track records with two major Korean battery makers.

Electrode-process parts tend to see orders bunch up and pause with customers' investment timing, so quarterly revenue can swing sharply. The Q3 2026 revenue decline came from delivery and inspection timing, so recognition timing mattered more than underlying demand in that quarter.

Outlook

The company has said it expects results to recover from Q4 onward. Its plan is to complete the customer deliveries and inspections deferred from Q3 in Q4 and improve profitability.

A company official said that, as the secondary battery market recovers, results should recover to some extent from Q4, according to press coverage.

On the business side, a new plant under construction in Crown Point, Indiana is scheduled to begin full operation in the second half of 2026, aimed at real-time maintenance and repair service for North American customers and lower logistics costs.

A March 2026 report had planned a July start after completion in the second quarter, while a May 2026 report guided to operation in the second half.

New orders include a lens-bonding machine order of about KRW 3.5 billion in February 2026 and a supply contract for an automotive glass inspection system worth about KRW 3.1 billion in October 2026.

In late March 2026, the Korea IR Association's corporate research center estimated 2026 revenue at KRW 43 billion and operating profit at KRW 4.1 billion.

Cumulative revenue for January to September 2026 is about KRW 23.1 billion, with a cumulative operating loss of about KRW 2.7 billion, so reaching that estimate would require a large Q4 revenue figure.

In new businesses, the company signed a humanoid robot business agreement with China's JINFU Group and has entered a logistics-automation robot demonstration phase with a robotics partner.

The robot and all-solid-state battery efforts are described as focused on establishing commercial references rather than contributing to near-term earnings.

Wider equipment and vision portfolio

A lens-bonding machine order in February 2026 and an automotive glass inspection contract in October 2026 extend the business beyond batteries. Press coverage said the equipment-and-vision segment's Q1 2026 revenue rose about 80% year on year.

Balance sheet with room to absorb losses

The debt ratio was 33.4% at end-2025, with total liabilities of KRW 32.5 billion. At end-September 2026, the company reported a standalone debt ratio of 35% and a current ratio of 198%.

Consolidated equity fell from KRW 97.1 billion at the start of the year to about KRW 92.9 billion at end-September, but the equity base remains large.

Risk factors

Currency swings

Dollar deposits and receivables from exports are translated into won at quarter-end rates, so currency moves swing net income. In Q3 2026 a won-dollar move produced a KRW 4.1 billion foreign-currency valuation loss.

The company classifies this as a financial item separate from operating results, but the sensitivity of net income to quarter-end rates remains.

Revenue timing and customer concentration

Revenue is recognized when customer deliveries and inspections are complete. In Q3 2026, projects scheduled for September moved into Q4 and revenue fell as a result. Orders can bunch up or pause with the investment schedules of major battery customers. Whether the deferred volume is recognized in Q4 is the near-term point to verify.

Execution of new businesses and overseas plant

The Indiana plant is planned to run in the second half of 2026, although reports in March and May gave different start timing. Robotics and all-solid-state battery efforts are still in demonstration and partnership stages, with commercial timing not yet fixed. Costs may continue to weigh until these new businesses generate revenue.

Overall view

G.I.Tech centers on slot dies for battery electrode processes and points to the spread of LFP batteries for ESS as a driver of dedicated slot-die orders.

It returned to operating profit in Q1 2026, but in Q3 2026 revenue fell 46.5% and the company posted an operating loss of KRW 2.71 billion and a parent net loss of KRW 6.01 billion.

Since 2023 revenue has stayed in a KRW 36 billion to 38 billion range, and operating profit declined for three straight years through 2025.

Supporting factors include the expansion of ESS slot dies and the equipment-and-vision portfolio, along with a balance sheet with a debt ratio of 35% on a standalone basis at end-September 2026.

Negative factors include revenue swings driven by delivery timing, foreign-currency valuation losses, and a lower operating margin. The company expects recovery from Q4, and recognition of the deferred Q4 volume and the start of the Indiana plant in the second half of 2026 are the next checkpoints.

The full-year results confirmed in the 2026 annual report will serve as the reference point for judging the trend.
