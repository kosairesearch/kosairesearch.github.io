02

Business structure

Green Optics, founded in 1999, is a precision optics company whose main lines are defense optical systems and zinc sulfide (ZnS), an infrared optical material.

Defense made up KRW 21.5 billion, or 51.5%, of 2025 consolidated revenue of KRW 41.8 billion, and it still accounted for 56.74% of revenue in the first quarter of 2026, according to Blotter reporting.

ZnS is used in the seeker windows of guided weapons, and the company says it is the only domestic producer of the material in mass production. Its customers are major domestic weapons-system makers, and a securities report cited participation in 16 guided-weapon programs, 13 of which are exclusive supply.

Outside defense, the company supplies optical equipment and components for displays and ultra-precision lenses for semiconductor equipment, including lenses for HBM inspection tools. In space, the company says it has supplied more than 70 satellite mirrors to the Indian Space Research Organisation.

Backlog stood at KRW 76.6 billion at the end of the first quarter of 2026, of which KRW 64.2 billion, or 83.81%, was defense.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual results improved on most measures between 2024 and 2025. Revenue rose 31.6% from KRW 31.7 billion to KRW 41.8 billion, and operating profit climbed 1,028.1% from KRW 195 million to KRW 2.2 billion. The operating margin moved from 0.6% to 5.3%, so the larger top line translated into better profitability.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders rose 378.6% from KRW 428 million to KRW 2.05 billion, and net income including non-controlling interests moved from KRW 428 million to KRW 2.05 billion over the same period.

The company described 2025 as a record result and cited defense and ZnS material supply as the main drivers. The debt ratio fell from 167.6% to 52.4%, with total equity of KRW 65.6 billion and total liabilities of KRW 34.3 billion at the end of 2025.

Operating cash flow, however, moved from -KRW 2.28 billion in 2024 to -KRW 8.07 billion in 2025, a larger net outflow. The fourth quarter of 2025 was the peak: revenue of KRW 13.5 billion, operating profit of KRW 1.47 billion and net income attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 2.43 billion.

In the first quarter of 2026, revenue fell 19.7% quarter on quarter to KRW 10.9 billion, operating profit fell 62.1% to KRW 555 million, and net income attributable to controlling shareholders fell 62.3% to KRW 914 million.

Second-quarter revenue rose 41.4% to KRW 15.4 billion, but operating profit was KRW 215 million and net income attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 378 million.

Third-quarter revenue slipped 3.7% to KRW 14.8 billion, operating profit rose 573.3% to KRW 1.45 billion, and net income attributable to controlling shareholders fell 18.7% to KRW 307 million, so operating and net profit moved in opposite directions.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders for the four quarters from the fourth quarter of 2025 through the third quarter of 2026 totals KRW 4.02 billion.

05

Industry analysis

Demand for defense products has been linked to the expansion of conflict in the Middle East. The company said that after the US and Israel-Iran conflict, global defense makers increased guided-weapon output, and it expected higher sales from its exclusive ZnS supply (Newspim, March 2026).

The company also sees recurring demand for consumable defense parts as a driver of long-term profitability. In space, a report in February 2026 said the company was the only domestic firm selected for a program run by a research body under the US Space Force.

On materials, NH Investment & Securities said in a January 2026 report that the average cost ratio of its ZnS products is around 50%, a point it viewed positively for future profitability. The same report did not present an investment rating or target price.

ZnS sales reached KRW 1.628 billion in the first half of 2025, about 10% of total revenue (The Elec, October 2025). In displays, the company said its related sales fell sharply after customers cut equipment investment amid a downturn that began in 2023.

It expects domestic display makers to benefit from US export restrictions on BOE panels. Defense and display are moving in different directions, and the company and brokers both emphasize that its in-house development and mass production of ZnS in Korea sets it apart from peers.

06

Outlook

The full-year 2026 estimate the company presented at its IPO was revenue of KRW 67.67 billion and operating profit of KRW 6.42 billion (as cited in news reports).

The same reporting said first-half 2026 consolidated revenue was KRW 26.21 billion and operating profit was KRW 770 million, and that meeting the estimate would require roughly KRW 41.46 billion of additional second-half revenue and at least KRW 5.6 billion of operating profit.

In its securities filing as of October 2025, the company set a 2027 revenue target of KRW 91.3 billion. That target rests mainly on defense, as production shifts to volume, and on the ZnS business.

In 2026, the company signed a supply contract worth about KRW 8.2 billion for guided-weapon optical parts with a domestic defense firm and secured a ZnS material supply contract worth about KRW 4.7 billion with an Israeli defense company.

An October 2025 report said talks were under way with a US defense subsidiary for 500 units in 2026 and 1,000 units in 2027. The electro-optical (EO) and infrared (IR) system development is complete, and volume production is scheduled after 2027.

At OPIE 2026 in Yokohama in April 2026, the company said it would begin long-term order talks with a major Japanese company and hold working meetings on mass supply of MS-ZnS.

In astronomy, it is reviewing with the National Astronomical Observatory of Japan and the Korea Astronomy and Space Science Institute a wide-field camera for the Subaru Telescope.

The company plans to use its expanded optical-material capacity to add defense opportunities and supply more high-value solutions to semiconductor and space customers.

08

Bull factors

Defense and exclusive ZnS demand

ZnS is the only material of its kind developed and mass produced in Korea, and according to securities research the company supplies 13 of the 16 guided-weapon programs it participates in on an exclusive basis.

Of the KRW 76.6 billion backlog at the end of the first quarter of 2026, KRW 64.2 billion, or 83.81%, is defense. The company also cites higher guided-weapon output after the Middle East conflict as support for demand.

Cost ratio and margin improvement

The average cost ratio of ZnS products is reported at around 50%, and a securities report pointed to it as a factor supporting future profitability. The annual operating margin rose from 0.6% in 2024 to 5.3% in 2025, and operating profit grew 1,028.1% to KRW 2.2 billion. The shift of earnings toward defense and materials appears in these figures.

Balance sheet repair and diversification

The debt ratio fell from 167.6% in 2024 to 52.4% in 2025, with total equity of KRW 65.6 billion at year-end 2025. The current ratio reached 249.8% at the end of 2025, up from 94.2% a year earlier.

Beyond defense, the company is widening its business to optical lenses for HBM inspection equipment and a US Space Force-linked research program.

Bear factors

Slowing quarterly profit

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders fell for three consecutive quarters from the first through the third quarter of 2026, and first-quarter operating profit dropped 62.1% to KRW 555 million.

First-half 2026 operating profit was KRW 770 million, only part of the KRW 6.42 billion full-year estimate given at the IPO. Third-quarter operating profit recovered, but net income attributable to controlling shareholders was only KRW 307 million, so whether operating gains reach final profit remains to be confirmed.

Inventory and receivables burden

Consolidated inventory stood at KRW 31.67 billion at end-June 2026, about 30% of total assets and roughly double the KRW 15.88 billion at end-2023. Inventory turnover slowed from 1.78 times in 2023 to 1.62 times in 2025, and trade and other receivables reached KRW 8.44 billion. The company says the inventory does not include significant long-stored stock.

Vietnam subsidiary's finances

The Vietnamese subsidiary GREEN OPTICS VINA reported total equity of KRW 1.3 billion and total liabilities of KRW 11.55 billion at end-June 2026, with liabilities about 8.9 times equity.

The parent holds KRW 8.49 billion of claims on the subsidiary and has provided a guarantee of US$2.05 million, about KRW 2.9 billion, on its local bank loan. The company was fined twice, in January and May 2024, over export-permit matters for strategic goods shipped in 2018 and 2019.

Risk factors

Defense demand and policy concentration

Revenue and backlog are concentrated in defense, so results may depend heavily on defense budgets, procurement schedules and the course of international conflicts. Defense made up 83.81% of backlog at the end of the first quarter of 2026.

Many overseas volumes are still in negotiation, so the timing of contract signing and delivery can drive swings in quarterly profit.

New system production timing

Electro-optical (EO) and infrared (IR) systems have completed development, but volume production is scheduled after 2027.

The 2027 revenue target of KRW 91.3 billion depends on defense moving into volume production and on the ZnS business, so a delay in the new systems' production start could push back the target as well.

If customer approval or production line adjustments are needed between development completion and actual delivery, revenue recognition could also slip.

Ownership structure and lock-up

The founder, CEO Cho Hyeon-il, and related parties hold 50.54%, and shares held by the largest shareholder side are under lock-up until November 2027. Whether the amount of shares that can be sold increases after the lock-up ends should be confirmed through disclosures. With ownership concentrated in one group, limited free float may also affect share price behavior.

Overall view

Green Optics' annual operating profit rose to KRW 2.2 billion in 2025 on higher defense and ZnS material sales, and the operating margin reached 5.3%.

Since 2026, however, net income attributable to controlling shareholders has fallen for three consecutive quarters, to KRW 914 million in the first quarter, KRW 378 million in the second and KRW 307 million in the third.

Third-quarter operating profit jumped to KRW 1.45 billion, but net income attributable to controlling shareholders did not follow, so operating and final profit moved in different directions.

Strengths include the company's status as the only domestic mass producer of ZnS, a KRW 76.6 billion backlog at the end of the first quarter of 2026, and a sharp fall in the debt ratio.

Weaknesses include first-half 2026 results far below the full-year estimate given at the IPO, rising inventory and receivables, and the financial burden of the Vietnamese subsidiary.

A defense share above half of revenue makes orders more visible but also leaves results sensitive to international conflicts and policy shifts.

Valuation rests on net income attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 4.02 billion over the last four quarters, so the reading of the multiples changes with the quarterly trend. The next checkpoints are the 2026 annual report, first-quarter 2027 results, and the end of the lock-up in November 2027.
