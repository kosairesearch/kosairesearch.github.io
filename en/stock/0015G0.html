02

Business structure

Founded in 1999 and headquartered in Ochang, Cheongju, Chungcheongbuk-do, Green Optics is a precision optics specialist with an integrated production system covering optical design, processing, coating, assembly, and inspection in-house.

Its major products include zinc sulfide (ZnS) optical materials, defense optical products and camera modules, space-use camera modules and large mirrors, and precision optical systems for semiconductor and display equipment.

In defense, the company supplies seeker optics for guided missiles, laser anti-air weapon optics, and ring laser gyroscope optics mainly to LIG Nex1 and Hanwha Systems, with a core defense customer base reported at seven companies.

ZnS, once entirely import-dependent, was domestically developed and brought into mass production exclusively by Green Optics, and is used in infrared optical components for stealth aircraft and interception systems.

In space, the company has supplied multiple satellite mirrors to the Indian Space Research Organisation (ISRO) and has a history of jointly developing space camera lenses with NASA, while also collaborating with the Korea Astronomy and Space Science Institute on an off-axis aspheric telescope.

Green Optics also manufactured the high-resolution camera lens module for the Danuri lunar probe. In semiconductors, it supplies optical modules to wafer inspection equipment maker Nextin, which in turn serves memory makers such as SK hynix.

The display segment, built on Samsung Display's recognition of the company as an innovative partner in 2016, has seen its revenue share shrink since 2023 as customers cut capital spending.

The company listed on KOSDAQ in November 2025 under the technology special listing track and is pursuing a strategy of expanding its defense and ZnS material revenue while broadening into semiconductors and aerospace.

04

Earnings analysis

Green Optics' consolidated revenue rose 31.6% from KRW 31.7 billion in 2024 to KRW 41.8 billion in 2025, while operating profit surged 1,028.1% from KRW 195 million to KRW 2.2 billion.

The operating margin improved from 0.6% to 5.3%, and net income attributable to owners grew 378.6% from KRW 428 million to KRW 2.05 billion, with net income including non-controlling interests recorded at the same level.

By quarter, Q4 2025 marked a peak with revenue of KRW 13.5 billion, operating profit of KRW 1.47 billion, and net income attributable to owners of KRW 2.43 billion.

Revenue then fell 19.7% quarter-on-quarter to KRW 10.9 billion in Q1 2026, with operating profit down 62.1% to KRW 555 million and net income attributable to owners down 62.3% to KRW 914 million.

Revenue rebounded 41.4% quarter-on-quarter to KRW 15.4 billion in Q2 2026, but operating profit fell a further 61.3% to KRW 215 million and net income attributable to owners fell 58.6% to KRW 378 million, extending a two-quarter decline in profitability following the Q4 2025 peak.

Regarding why profitability softened even as revenue rebounded, reports noted that a valuation loss provision of KRW 2.45 billion was reflected in inventory on a standalone basis for the first half of 2026.

Balance-sheet health improved, with the debt-to-equity ratio falling from 167.6% in 2024 to 52.4% in 2025, while total equity stood at KRW 65.6 billion (entirely attributable to owners) and total liabilities at KRW 34.3 billion at the end of 2025.

However, operating cash flow deteriorated from -KRW 2.28 billion in 2024 to -KRW 8.07 billion in 2025, with analysis suggesting that despite the operating profit surplus, operating cash flow worsened, implying profitability did not immediately translate into cash generation, while financing cash inflows were large enough to offset the operating cash shortfall.

05

Industry analysis

The precision optics industry that Green Optics operates in is tied to demand cycles across defense, aerospace, semiconductor, and display end markets.

Amid heightened global geopolitical tensions, commentary has suggested that global defense firms have sharply increased guided weapons production, boosting expected demand for Green Optics, which exclusively supplies zinc sulfide.

However, ZnS materials and equipment are classified as strategic items subject to export licensing, and regarding a related issue in the first half of 2026 the company clarified that the matter was not a problem arising from actual sales or dealings with customers but rather a procedural export-licensing issue.

In space optics, opportunities for new satellite and telescope projects remain open given the company's track record with ISRO and NASA, and reportedly only about ten companies worldwide are known to possess similar technology to Green Optics.

The semiconductor segment is linked to growing demand for advanced processes such as HBM packaging inspection equipment, with the company indirectly embedded in the supply chain to memory makers like SK hynix through Nextin.

The display segment has contracted since 2023 due to reduced capital spending by domestic panel makers, though the company has stated that U.S. export restrictions on BOE panels are expected to benefit major domestic display makers.

In terms of competitive positioning, Green Optics is reportedly the only domestic company with in-house ZnS production capability, differentiating it from general optics component makers, though its absolute revenue scale remains small compared with large electronic component makers such as Samsung Electro-Mechanics and LG Innotek.

06

Outlook

At listing, the company presented 2026 guidance of KRW 67.6-67.7 billion in revenue and KRW 5.4-6.4 billion in operating profit, but consolidated revenue for the first half of 2026 reached only KRW 26.21 billion with operating profit of KRW 770 million, meaning roughly KRW 41.46 billion in additional revenue and over KRW 5.6 billion in additional operating profit would be needed in the second half to meet the full-year estimate presented at listing.

The ZnS business has expanded its CVD lines from two to five generations, and the company stated that full operation of the equipment is expected to generate annual revenue of KRW 10-12 billion.

Looking further ahead, the company outlined plans that combining HIP equipment with existing CVD facilities to produce and sell finished products could more than double selling prices, with HIP introduction planned for mid-2027 and full operation targeted by the end of that year.

Its electro-optical (EO) and infrared (IR) systems have completed development and are undergoing environmental testing, and the company explained that mass production is planned from 2027 for supply into a large-scale overhaul program expected to generate KRW 11.6 billion in revenue that year, though a formal contract has not yet been signed.

In semiconductors, brokerage commentary suggested that the company is developing long-term supply agreements and additional items with top-tier global equipment makers and is expected to lead the advancement of core optics for HBM inspection equipment domestically.

A germanium (Ge) material business is also underway, with a stated target of around 100 kilograms of monthly production, though the company has said it is too early to discuss financial contributions as sales have not yet materialized.

Overall, the defense- and materials-driven growth narrative remains intact, but whether the annual guidance is met appears to hinge on the pace of order intake and mass production in the second half.

08

Bull factors

Domestic Monopoly in ZnS Material Localization

Green Optics is the only domestic company with the capability to develop and mass-produce zinc sulfide (ZnS) in-house, and few companies worldwide are known to hold similar technology.

As a strategic material, ZnS reportedly continues to see global demand exceed supply, with applications diversifying into stealth aircraft and interception systems.

Having expanded CVD equipment to five generations, the company also expects higher selling prices for finished products through planned HIP equipment introduction.

Portfolio Expansion into Space and Semiconductors

Based on its supply track record with ISRO, NASA, and other overseas space agencies, Green Optics holds opportunities for additional satellite and telescope orders. In semiconductors, it supplies optical modules for HBM inspection equipment through Nextin, linking it to advanced memory process demand.

Having completed development of its electro-optical (EO) and infrared (IR) systems, the company is attempting to transition from a component supplier into a systems company.

Improving Balance Sheet

The debt-to-equity ratio fell sharply from 167.6% in 2024 to 52.4% in 2025, and total equity expanded to KRW 65.6 billion at the end of 2025.

Operating profit and net income attributable to owners continued to grow in 2025 following the 2024 turnaround to profitability, translating recovering profitability into a stronger balance sheet. Funds raised through the listing have flowed into financing cash inflows, partly offsetting working-capital burdens.

09

Bear factors

Two Consecutive Quarters of Declining Operating Profit

Operating profit and net income attributable to owners, which peaked in Q4 2025, declined for two consecutive quarters in Q1 and Q2 2026. In Q2, revenue grew 41.4% quarter-on-quarter, yet operating profit fell 61.3%, highlighting a clear divergence between revenue growth and profitability. Rising cost ratios and inventory-related charges have been cited as factors behind the margin deterioration.

Widening Operating Cash Flow Deficit

Operating cash flow was negative in both 2024 and 2025, with the deficit widening from -KRW 2.28 billion to -KRW 8.07 billion. Despite an operating profit surplus, simultaneous increases in receivables and inventory have weighed on cash generation. This has resulted in a growing reliance on external funding through financing activities.

Gap Between Annual Guidance and First-Half Results

Against the 2026 targets of KRW 67.6-67.7 billion in revenue and KRW 5.4-6.4 billion in operating profit presented at listing, first-half 2026 consolidated revenue and operating profit came in at only KRW 26.21 billion and KRW 770 million, respectively.

Meeting the guidance would require a much larger increase in revenue and profit in the second half than was achieved in the first. Whether the targets are met is likely to depend on the pace of order intake and mass production in the second half.

10

Risk factors

Inventory and Working Capital Risk

Consolidated inventory at the end of the first half of 2026 grew to roughly 30% of total assets, with more than 80% of inventory consisting of work-in-process items still undergoing processing or assembly. A valuation loss provision of KRW 2.45 billion was reflected in inventory on a standalone basis.

An internal-control issue related to export licensing procedures for strategic materials such as ZnS has also surfaced, warranting monitoring for recurrence.

Order Confirmation Uncertainty

The projected KRW 11.6 billion in revenue from the EO/IR overhaul program in 2027 remains a company estimate, as a formal contract has not yet been signed.

Brokerage estimates for 2026-2027 revenue and profit also vary across firms such as Hana Securities and Eugene Investment & Securities, and actual outcomes will depend on order timing and production pace.

Both the ramp-up of ZnS facility utilization and the germanium material business have yet to generate meaningful sales.

Post-Listing Supply-Demand and Volatility Risk

Since its November 2025 KOSDAQ listing, concerns over potential overhang from tradable share volume have been raised for Green Optics, and the stock has shown heightened volatility amid recurring swings between enthusiasm and disappointment over the defense and space themes.

As a small-cap technology special listing, its relatively limited float can lead to larger price swings from shifts in supply and demand. Trading patterns among foreign, institutional, and retail investors around earnings releases have also been cited as a factor affecting the share price.

Overall view

Following its 2024 turnaround to profitability, Green Optics backed its defense, space, and semiconductor diversification narrative with sharp revenue and operating profit growth in 2025.

However, since the Q4 2025 peak, operating profit and net income attributable to owners have declined for two consecutive quarters in 2026, and profitability softened in Q2 even as revenue rebounded.

Operating cash flow has been negative for two straight years, sustaining a structure reliant on financing activities for funding.

A substantial gap exists between the 2026 annual guidance presented at listing and actual first-half results, making the pace of order intake and mass production in the second half critical to meeting the full-year target.

Mid- to long-term growth drivers such as ZnS material localization and integration into space and semiconductor supply chains remain intact, but inventory and working-capital burdens, along with the unconfirmed formal contract for the new EO/IR system business, remain open variables.

Investors will want to watch the upcoming Q3 report and progress toward the annual guidance to assess the durability of the earnings momentum.
