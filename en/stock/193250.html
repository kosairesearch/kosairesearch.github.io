Linked: Testing a Shift From Phone Vibration Motors to Auto Motors

02

Business structure

Linked was founded in 2004 and listed on KOSDAQ in December 2014 as a maker of electronic components. The company was previously known as YJM Games until its name change in March 2024.

Its core product is coin-type vibration motors used in smartphones and e-cigarettes, and its main customers are a leading domestic smartphone maker and a global e-cigarette manufacturer. The product line covers brush and BLDC coin types, bar type, and linear AC motors.

Production runs from the Korean headquarters and plants in China and Vietnam, and the company is described as supplying a large domestic smartphone maker with a high share. Its cost competitiveness is attributed to process automation and minimizing defect rates.

Its BLDC motors, which have long life and low noise, are being pushed into more smartphone applications. The business is split into a manufacturing segment and a games segment, with the manufacturing side supplying patented BLDC coin motors.

The games business publishes PC and mobile games and VR content, and affiliates provide several VR game IPs. The company is also launching a character-licensed loungewear and homewear distribution business.

In manufacturing, it has started automotive electric motor production as a new line, beginning in the first quarter of 2026.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual revenue fell from KRW 30.2 billion in 2022 to KRW 20.2 billion in 2023 (down 33.3% year on year), KRW 14.1 billion in 2024 (down 30.2%), and KRW 10.5 billion in 2025 (down 25.7%), a three-year decline in which 2022 was the largest year and 2025 the smallest.

Operating loss widened from KRW 2.43 billion in 2022 to KRW 4.43 billion in 2023, KRW 6.06 billion in 2024, and KRW 7.21 billion in 2025, a four-year run of losses. The operating margin slid from -8.0% to -22.0%, -43.0%, and -69.0%, showing costs not adjusting as sales shrank.

The company attributed the 2025 deterioration to a slowdown in its downstream industry that reduced sales. Net loss narrowed in 2025 on improved valuation gains on financial assets and higher equity-method income from affiliates.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders swung from a KRW 6.62 billion profit in 2022 to a KRW 12.6 billion loss in 2023, widened to KRW 17.3 billion in 2024, and narrowed to a KRW 2.68 billion loss in 2025.

Net loss including non-controlling interests was KRW 2.98 billion in 2025, a different figure from the controlling-shareholder loss. Operating cash flow went from KRW 4.89 billion in 2022 and KRW 3.28 billion in 2023 to negative KRW 4.72 billion in 2024 and negative KRW 7.35 billion in 2025.

On a quarterly basis, revenue ran KRW 2 billion in the third quarter of 2025, KRW 3.47 billion in the fourth quarter (up 73.7% quarter on quarter), KRW 2.39 billion in the first quarter of 2026 (down 31.2%), KRW 4.07 billion in the second quarter (up 70.5%), and KRW 5.18 billion in the third quarter (up 27.1% quarter on quarter and 159.2% year on year).

Operating losses have run for five straight quarters from the third quarter of 2025 through the third quarter of 2026, narrowing to KRW 1.83 billion in the latest quarter.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders was profitable in the fourth quarter of 2025 (KRW 9.54 billion) and the second quarter of 2026 (KRW 3.17 billion), while the third quarter of 2026 posted a loss of KRW 3.51 billion, a wider loss than a year earlier. The sum of controlling-shareholder net income over the last four quarters is KRW 7.68 billion.

05

Industry analysis

Demand for smartphone vibration motors has been squeezed as the rapid growth of Chinese smartphone makers has slowed the expansion of domestic suppliers. Weaker demand in the e-cigarette market has added pressure on the company's core products.

The company itself cites the slowdown in its downstream industry as the main reason for the 2025 deterioration. In response, it is trying to build automotive electric motors into a new growth engine beyond its small smartphone-focused revenue base.

In vehicles, electric motors are used in air conditioning units, fuel filler necks, side mirrors, valves, and charging port mechanisms. Electrification and the spread of convenience features are broadening these applications.

The company is positioned as a supplier with a high share to a large domestic smartphone maker. Peers such as Samsung Electro-Mechanics and LG Innotek differ greatly in business scale and product mix, which makes direct comparison difficult.

Automotive parts supply generally depends on customer qualification and mass-production track records, so the pace of automotive sales may hinge on customer approvals. Whether the shift in the manufacturing business can reach revenue quickly while downstream demand is weak is the central industry question.

Outlook

The company expanded its production lines in preparation for mass production of automotive electronic components and secured an annual production capacity of 28.8 million units.

It began operating its lines in the first quarter of 2026 with samples and some component supply, and utilization is expected to rise gradually in the second half as new supply expands.

The CEO described automotive electric motors as a new growth driver for the manufacturing business that should help diversify revenue and improve profitability as supply expands. Target applications include air conditioning units and charging port mechanisms.

In games, affiliates continue to secure VR game IP, and the company participates in a global VR/AR specialist fund. The character-licensed homewear distribution business is at an early stage of expanding sales channels through B2B distribution.

Quarterly revenue has risen for two quarters since the second quarter of 2026, but the operating loss remains at KRW 1.83 billion in the third quarter, so whether higher sales narrow the losses is the next thing to watch.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders moved from profit in the second quarter of 2026 to a loss in the third quarter, so quarterly volatility also matters. The scale of automotive sales will be clarified in the segment disclosures of the next annual report. The fourth-quarter and full-year 2026 results will first appear in filings in early 2027.

Bull factors

Automotive motor ramp-up

Automotive electric motors began production in the first quarter of 2026, and reports expect utilization to rise as new supply expands in the second half. Annual capacity was secured at 28.8 million units. The company names this business as a new growth driver for manufacturing. Whether revenue diversifies beyond vibration motors is the key test.

Two quarters of sales recovery

After bottoming at KRW 2.39 billion in the first quarter of 2026, quarterly revenue rose to KRW 4.07 billion and KRW 5.18 billion in the following two quarters. Third-quarter revenue was up 159.2% from a year earlier.

The operating loss also narrowed to KRW 1.83 billion from a year earlier, showing sales recovery feeding into smaller losses.

Low leverage and sizable equity

Total equity at the end of 2025 was KRW 59.8 billion, of which KRW 59.3 billion belongs to controlling shareholders. Total liabilities were KRW 12 billion, and the debt ratio was 20.1%. The ratio rose from 13.0% in 2022 but remains low, leaving room for capacity investment.

Bear factors

Three years of falling revenue

Revenue fell from KRW 30.2 billion in 2022 to KRW 10.5 billion in 2025. Slower growth among domestic smartphone makers, tied to rising Chinese rivals, is cited as a structural burden on the core market. Weaker e-cigarette demand added to the pressure. The company attributes the 2025 deterioration to a downstream slowdown.

Persistent operating losses and cash outflows

The operating margin fell from -8.0% in 2022 to -69.0% in 2025. Operating losses have continued for four consecutive years, and the third quarter of 2026 still posted a KRW 1.83 billion loss. Operating cash flow was negative KRW 4.72 billion in 2024 and negative KRW 7.35 billion in 2025.

High volatility in quarterly profit

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders was profitable in the fourth quarter of 2025 (KRW 9.54 billion) and the second quarter of 2026 (KRW 3.17 billion), but turned into a KRW 3.51 billion loss in the third quarter of 2026, wider than a year earlier.

Whether the profitable quarters reflected core improvement or one-off items is still to be confirmed. Quarterly results may remain volatile until automotive sales scale up.

10

Risk factors

Downstream demand and customer concentration

The core business depends on a large domestic smartphone maker and e-cigarette manufacturers. If domestic suppliers lose share to growing Chinese smartphone brands, revenue could decline further. A narrow customer base means order changes from a single customer can quickly affect results.

Execution risk in the automotive shift

Automotive electric motor production began with samples and partial supply, and higher utilization depends on expanded supply in the second half. The company presents this as a diversification axis, but the scale of automotive revenue has not been disclosed. If customer approvals or mass-production schedules slip, the capacity investment burden could appear first.

Volatility from non-operating items and diversification

The 2025 narrowing of the net loss was supported by valuation gains on financial assets and higher equity-method income from affiliates. Such non-operating items can move in a different direction from operating results and blur the net income trend.

New businesses such as games, VR, and character-licensed distribution remain at an early stage of profitability.

Overall view

Linked started as a maker of vibration motors for smartphones and e-cigarettes. Its revenue fell from KRW 30.2 billion in 2022 to KRW 10.5 billion in 2025, and operating losses have persisted for four consecutive years. Operating cash flow turned negative in 2024 and 2025.

On the other hand, quarterly revenue has risen for two quarters since the second quarter of 2026, and third-quarter 2026 revenue of KRW 5.18 billion was up 159.2% from a year earlier.

Automotive electric motors began production in the first quarter of 2026, with annual capacity secured at 28.8 million units as of the end of 2025. Bullish factors include the automotive shift, the quarterly sales rebound, and low leverage.

Bearish factors include three years of falling revenue, continuing operating losses, and high quarterly volatility in net income.

The next checkpoints are the fourth-quarter and full-year 2026 preliminary results, the segment sales and automotive revenue scale in the 2026 annual report, and whether the operating loss narrows in the first-quarter 2027 report.
