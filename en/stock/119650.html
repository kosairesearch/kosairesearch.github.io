Narrower Losses and New Orders; Listing Status Is the Key Test

KC Cottrell's revenue fell from KRW 436.6 billion in 2023 to KRW 192.9 billion in 2025, and it posted operating losses for three straight years through 2025, but it returned to operating profit in the third quarter of 2026; its listing status will be decided after the exchange's improvement period ends on April 16, 2027.

02

Business structure

KC Cottrell is an environmental engineering specialist founded in November 1973, with 135 employees as of June 30, 2026 per JobKorea company data.

Its reporting structure consists of a single environmental engineering segment, and its main equipment includes electrostatic precipitators (ESP), flue gas desulfurization systems (FGD), and boiler bottom ash handling systems.

Customers disclosed in 2026 include Korea East-West Power and Korea Southern Power in Korea, Taiwan Power Company in Taiwan, the Long Phu 1 project management board in Vietnam, and TECHNO ELECTRIC AND ENGINEERING in India.

Contract sizes range from KRW 3.4 billion (Kota FGD in India, corrected disclosure in February 2026) to KRW 476.9 billion (Taichung repair project in Taiwan, corrected disclosure in February 2026).

Domestic contracts centered on ESP electrode and collector plate replacement and materials supply, typically in the KRW 6.6 billion to 8.6 billion range. The Samcheok Unit 2 bottom ash capacity expansion contract, signed on October 7, 2026, is worth KRW 6.14 billion and runs through April 30, 2027.

Disclosures state that contract amounts and periods may change with construction progress, and many contracts pay on delivery of equipment followed by progress billing. The largest shareholder is KC Green Holdings, holding 83.03% as reported in January 2026. Because a few large contracts drive the timing, quarterly revenue moves sharply.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual revenue rose from KRW 421.6 billion in 2022 to KRW 436.6 billion in 2023, up 3.6% year on year, then fell to KRW 340.9 billion in 2024, down 21.9%, and to KRW 192.9 billion in 2025, down 43.4%. 2023 was the largest year in the 2022-2025 span and 2025 the smallest, and revenue declined for two consecutive years from 2024 through 2025.

Operating income was KRW 3.63 billion in 2022, turned to an operating loss of KRW 21.1 billion in 2023, widened to an operating loss of KRW 67.8 billion in 2024, and narrowed to an operating loss of KRW 8.86 billion in 2025.

The company has posted operating losses for three consecutive years from 2023 through 2025, and its operating margin was 0.9% in 2022, -4.8% in 2023, -19.9% in 2024 and -4.6% in 2025.

Net loss attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 22 billion in 2022, KRW 44.5 billion in 2023, KRW 31.1 billion in 2024 and KRW 10.3 billion in 2025, a loss in each of the four years from 2022 through 2025.

Net loss including non-controlling interests was KRW 10.4 billion in 2025, almost identical to the controlling-shareholder figure. Operating cash flow was negative KRW 121.7 billion in 2022, negative KRW 92.8 billion in 2023, negative KRW 11.3 billion in 2024 and negative KRW 26.1 billion in 2025.

Quarterly revenue moved from KRW 47.9 billion in the third quarter of 2025 to KRW 39.7 billion in the fourth quarter (down 17.1% quarter on quarter), KRW 24.8 billion in the first quarter of 2026 (down 37.5%), KRW 15.5 billion in the second quarter (down 37.4%) and KRW 39.3 billion in the third quarter (up 152.9% quarter on quarter, down 17.9% year on year).

Quarterly operating income moved from a loss in the third quarter of 2025 to a loss in the fourth quarter, a KRW 1.59 billion profit in the first quarter of 2026, a KRW 3.01 billion loss in the second quarter and a KRW 2.74 billion profit in the third quarter.

In the first quarter of 2026, the controlling-shareholder net profit of KRW 8.58 billion was well above that quarter's operating profit.

In the third quarter of 2026, a controlling-shareholder net loss of KRW 25 billion alongside positive operating profit points to large losses below the operating line, and the breakdown is a item to check in the periodic report. The controlling-shareholder net loss over the latest four quarters totals KRW 14.1 billion.

05

Industry analysis

Demand for environmental equipment comes from tighter emission rules for coal and gas power plants and from refurbishment of existing units.

Contracts disclosed in 2026 are concentrated in power plant environmental equipment, including ESP and FGD work at Long Phu 1 in Vietnam, FGD work at Kalisindh and Kota in India, and a repair project at Taichung in Taiwan. A business summary on a financial information site cites environmental regulation as a tailwind.

In its June 2026 plan, the company cited expanding into Southeast Asian markets such as Thailand, the Philippines and India, and widening domestic steel and power project wins, built on a recovered credit rating.

Because contracts are awarded project by project, quarterly revenue swings sharply, as the 2026 figures of KRW 24.8 billion, KRW 15.5 billion and KRW 39.3 billion show. Domestic utility orders often involve replacing ESP electrodes and collector plates in existing plants, which links demand to equipment aging.

Overseas contracts, by contrast, are exposed to currency translation and host-country schedules. The company's position in bidding is best gauged by order count, client mix and the share of large projects. The company has also said it will shift from volume-based to quality-based order intake.

In the first half of 2027, restoring financial soundness and normalizing internal controls are therefore the most direct points to check.

Bull factors

Operating profit turns positive

Operating profit was positive in the first quarter of 2026 at KRW 1.59 billion and in the third quarter at KRW 2.74 billion, with the latter turning from a year-earlier operating loss. Compared with the fourth-quarter 2025 operating loss of KRW 6.59 billion, the operating trend has improved.

Quarterly profit remains in the single-digit to low tens of billions of won range, so continuity is a matter for upcoming quarterly disclosures.

Large contracts and renewed order intake

The Taichung repair project in Taiwan (KRW 476.9 billion, corrected in February 2026) and the Long Phu 1 environmental equipment project in Vietnam (KRW 74.7 billion, corrected in June 2026) enlarged overseas order intake.

Domestically, the Yeongheung Unit 5 works (KRW 8.6 billion) and materials supply (KRW 6.6 billion) were signed in July 2026, followed by the Samcheok Unit 2 contract (KRW 6.14 billion) in October. A continuing string of order disclosures shows that the company's order flow has not stopped.

Profitability plan and regulatory tailwind

In its June 2026 normalization plan, the company cited tighter project budget control, cost reduction and a shift toward higher-quality orders. Whether the operating margin improves from the 2025 level of -4.6% will show how far the plan is working.

Bear factors

Uncertain pace of revenue recovery

Revenue peaked at KRW 436.6 billion in 2023 and fell to KRW 192.9 billion in 2025, less than half the 2023 level. It dropped to KRW 15.5 billion in the second quarter of 2026 before rebounding to KRW 39.3 billion in the third quarter, but the swing is a two-quarter move rather than an established trend. Whether the recovery converts into construction progress is the next point to check.

Gap between operating profit and net loss

Operating profit was KRW 2.74 billion in the third quarter of 2026, yet the controlling-shareholder net loss was KRW 25 billion.

Controlling-shareholder losses have now run for two consecutive quarters, from the second quarter through the third quarter of 2026, and the latest four quarters total KRW 14.1 billion in losses. Whether the below-the-line losses are one-off or recurring will become clearer in the periodic reports.

Financial burden and overseas project risk

Total liabilities stood at KRW 150.7 billion at the end of 2025, and the debt ratio was reported at about 250% in the first quarter of 2026. Press reports cite losses on overseas projects and guarantee obligations as a financial burden.

Because the company is pursuing debt repayment and financial stabilization at the same time, funding conditions and guarantee execution are key variables.

Risk factors

Listing qualification

The exchange's decision on whether the shares can remain listed is still pending after the improvement period ends on April 16, 2027. A market data site showed the stock as trading-suspended as of October 2, 2026.

If financial soundness and internal controls are not demonstrated, the listing decision could weigh against the company.

Overseas project costs and guarantees

Press reports cite overseas project losses and guarantee obligations as factors behind continued net losses. Because large overseas contracts are sizable, cost changes have a large effect on results.

The company has pledged to tighten project budget management and monitoring of estimated cost changes, and the effect will show up in future quarterly results.

Audit and internal controls

The audit report for fiscal 2025 (the 16th term) was disclosed in March 2026 as delayed because the external auditor had not completed its procedures. The company has said it will reflect a Korean listed companies association recommendation in outside director selection and set up a dedicated audit unit.

Whether the filing schedule and internal control upgrades proceed as planned is an important factor in the listing review.
