E1: LPG core adds power and overseas terminals

E1 still generates 97.9% of its revenue from LPG while widening into LNG power and a Vietnam LPG terminal, and its quarterly operating profit has swung between profit and loss.

  4. 4

02

Business structure

E1 is an LS Group affiliate whose core businesses are LPG distribution and overseas LPG trading. LPG accounted for 97.9% of total revenue in the first half of 2026, and most of the remaining 2.1% came from LPG-related rental income and vessel resale sales.

Exports are larger than domestic sales, and export revenue was 59.8% of separate-basis revenue in the same period. The model has centered on buying LPG overseas and selling it to third countries, but the company is shifting toward securing local storage and supply facilities to serve host-country domestic markets.

Products split into propane for cooking, heating and industrial use, and butane for transport and petrochemical feedstock. Domestic LPG selling prices reference the CP price published by Saudi Aramco, while import benchmarks vary among Middle East CP and US Mont Belvieu prices.

The power business began when E1 acquired Pyeongtaek Energy & Power in 2024, an 833MW LNG combined-cycle plant. Competition in domestic LPG is intensifying, and SK GAS raised its June 2026 propane price by the same 30 won as E1. The company said it is not abandoning solar or wind but is focusing new business on LNG power for now.

04

Earnings analysis

On the audited basis, annual revenue was KRW 7.99 trillion in 2022, KRW 7.83 trillion in 2023 (down 2.0%), KRW 11.2 trillion in 2024 (up 43.0%) and KRW 10.4 trillion in 2025 (down 7.1%). Within 2022–2025, 2024 was the largest revenue year and 2023 the smallest.

Operating profit fell from KRW 278.7 billion in 2022 to KRW 93.2 billion in 2023 (down 66.6%), then rose to KRW 217.6 billion in 2024 (up 133.5%) and KRW 324.1 billion in 2025 (up 48.9%), increasing for two consecutive years; 2025 was the largest operating profit year in the span.

Operating margin was 3.5% in 2022, 1.2% in 2023, 1.9% in 2024 and 3.1% in 2025, a thin margin typical of a distribution model. Net profit attributable to owners was KRW 141.9 billion in 2022, KRW 213.2 billion in 2023 (up 50.3%), KRW 62.9 billion in 2024 (down 70.5%) and KRW 104.8 billion in 2025 (up 66.7%).

Net income including non-controlling interests, which is a different figure, was KRW 65.6 billion in 2024 and KRW 114.2 billion in 2025.

A brokerage's March 2025 initiation report described how the derivative gain recognized in 2023 turned into a loss in 2024, with a year-end FX spike adding foreign-exchange valuation losses on vessel lease liabilities, which weighed on pre-tax profit.

The same report linked the 2024 operating profit increase to higher LPG export volumes and the consolidation of Pyeongtaek Energy & Power. Operating cash flow went from KRW 383.4 billion in 2023 to -KRW 793.9 billion in 2024 and recovered to KRW 377.8 billion in 2025.

Debt-to-equity was 187.3% in 2022, 564.2% in 2024 and 510.0% in 2025. Quarterly revenue rose from KRW 2.42 trillion in 3Q25 to KRW 4.4 trillion in 2Q26, and reached KRW 4.27 trillion in 3Q26 (up 76.8% year on year, down 2.9% quarter on quarter).

Quarterly operating profit was KRW 90.9 billion in 3Q25 and KRW 27.3 billion in 4Q25 (down 70.0% quarter on quarter), and the fourth quarter has seasonal costs. In 1Q26 an operating loss of KRW 156.2 billion followed, and an analysis attributed the gas-segment loss to a temporary rise in costs.

Operating profit returned to KRW 117.9 billion in 2Q26, then 3Q26 posted an operating loss of KRW 172.2 billion, a swing into loss from a year earlier. Net profit attributable to owners was a loss of KRW 18.9 billion in 4Q25, then KRW 66.8 billion in 1Q26, KRW 80.1 billion in 2Q26 and KRW 21.6 billion in 3Q26. The four quarters from 4Q25 through 3Q26 total KRW 149.6 billion.

05

Industry analysis

LPG demand splits into transport and industrial/petrochemical uses, and transport demand is affected by the spread of electric vehicles and the shift to eco-friendly cars.

Total LPG vehicle registrations fell 0.5% from end-2025, but according to the company, LPG truck registrations have risen since a new 1-ton LPG truck was launched. A 2025 brokerage analysis described demand from industrial and petrochemical users as firm.

Competition in the domestic LPG market is intensifying, which adds to the case for diversifying the business. Pricing is linked to a variety of import benchmarks, including Middle East CP and US Mont Belvieu prices, which allows hedging strategies.

Derivative valuation gains and losses can be large when underlying prices move. Price adjustments are tied to government price-stability policy, and the company said it passed through only part of the remaining cost increases in its June 2026 price change.

In power, the Pyeongtaek plant began operations in 2013 and buys fuel under KoreaGas's average-price contract, which runs until 2032, so a shift to individual pricing is discussed over the long term. Raising propane prices alongside SK GAS suggests a shared pricing pattern across the industry. The timing of LPG price moves, rather than the broader cycle, is described as a key driver of quarterly profit.

06

Outlook

In its March 27, 2026 value-up disclosure, E1 set out new business investment and stronger shareholder returns as its main goals. The company is reviewing LNG power and LPG market entry in Southeast Asia as new businesses.

In Vietnam, it is building an 80,000-ton LPG cold terminal in Tien Phong Industrial Park in Quang Ninh province in northern Vietnam through a joint venture in which E1 holds 50%. Storage will consist of 50,000 tons of propane and 30,000 tons of butane.

Annual LPG import demand in northern Vietnam is cited at about 800,000 tons. The terminal is backed by a project financing agreement of USD 160 million, and construction is underway with commercial operation targeted for 2027. In domestic power, E1 is pursuing acquisition and new construction of LNG plants.

On shareholder returns, the company is reviewing the next dividend policy and treasury share use. The 2025 year-end dividend was decided by the board, and the annual shareholders' meeting approved cash dividend resolutions. Propane prices rose by 50 won in April 2026 and by a further 30 won in June.

Solar and wind businesses have not been abandoned, but a large-scale expansion has not yet reached concrete review.

08

Bull factors

Diversification moving into physical assets

The 833MW Pyeongtaek LNG plant has been consolidated since 2024, and the 80,000-ton Vietnam terminal is under construction with commercial operation targeted for 2027. A new power project in Yeosu is also in the pipeline. This gives the case for a revenue base broadening beyond LPG trading into power and storage infrastructure.

Return to profit in 2Q26

Operating profit returned to KRW 117.9 billion in 2Q26, and net profit attributable to owners was KRW 80.1 billion that quarter. The rebound followed a 1Q26 loss that one analysis tied to temporary cost increases, and this is cited as the main bullish evidence.

Shareholder return policy under review

In its March 27, 2026 value-up plan, the company said it is reviewing a next dividend policy and treasury share use. The 2025 year-end dividend has already been decided. With the three-year dividend policy ended, a new framework could be a bullish factor.

09

Bear factors

Heavy reliance on LPG revenue

LPG accounted for 97.9% of revenue in the first half of 2026, competition in domestic LPG is intensifying, and electric vehicle adoption may weigh on transport demand over the medium term. Until new businesses contribute meaningfully, revenue concentration remains a bearish factor.

Derivative swings and quarterly volatility

Derivative gains and losses on LPG are booked below operating profit and amplify pre-tax volatility. Operating losses of KRW 156.2 billion in 1Q26 and KRW 172.2 billion in 3Q26 show how uneven quarterly results have been.

High leverage and uneven cash flow

Debt-to-equity was 510.0% at end-2025, against 187.3% in 2022, with total liabilities of KRW 13.3 trillion against total equity of KRW 2.61 trillion. Operating cash flow swung to a KRW 793.9 billion outflow in 2024, and how far borrowing burdens ease as projects expand remains open.

10

Risk factors

Price policy risk

LPG retail prices are tied to government price-stability policy, and in June 2026 the company passed through only part of the remaining cost increases. If costs cannot be fully passed on, margins could thin further.

FX and derivative valuation risk

In 2024, pre-tax profit weakened on a hedge derivative loss and FX valuation losses from a year-end currency spike. Vessel lease liabilities and import costs are dollar-linked, so currency moves can shake earnings, and derivative valuation direction changes with underlying prices.

Power contract terms risk

The Pyeongtaek plant buys fuel under KoreaGas's average-price contract, which runs until 2032. Individual pricing could be possible over the long term, but the timing and terms will determine profitability, and the earnings model for new power projects has yet to be proven.

12

Overall view

E1 is moving from a distribution-centered model, where 97.9% of revenue comes from LPG, toward LNG power and a Vietnam LPG terminal.

On a DART basis, operating profit rose from KRW 93.2 billion at the 2023 low to KRW 324.1 billion in 2025, but operating losses appeared in 1Q26 and 3Q26, so quarterly earnings remain volatile.

Bullish points include consolidated earnings from the Pyeongtaek LNG plant, a return to profit in 2Q26 and the review of shareholder returns in the value-up plan. Bearish points include LPG concentration, pre-tax swings from derivatives and FX, and debt-to-equity of 510.0% at end-2025.

Valuation views split between a below-historical-average multiple and a structural sector discount. The next checkpoints are December 2026 LPG price adjustments, provisional 4Q26 results in early 2027, the 2026 annual report due by end-March 2027, and the 2027 target start of the Vietnam terminal.
