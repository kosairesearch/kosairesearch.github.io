02

Business structure

Hankook & Company is the holding company of the Hankook Tire group, combining a direct battery business with stakes in subsidiaries and affiliates. The direct business is run by the Energy Solution (ES) division, which makes automotive lead-acid batteries; its core raw material is lead, purchased domestically.

According to a summary of the 2025 annual report by OpenBizData, product and merchandise sales were KRW 1.04 trillion, or 71.7% of consolidated sales of KRW 1.46 trillion, while equity-method income was KRW 314.9 billion, or 21.6%.

The largest affiliate is Hankook Tire & Technology, in which Hankook & Company held a 30.67% stake as of December 31, 2025, making it the largest shareholder.

Hankook Tire & Technology consolidates Hanon Systems, so the group spans batteries (Hankook & Company), tires (Hankook Tire & Technology) and thermal management (Hanon Systems).

Changes in the subsidiary's volumes, selling prices and exchange rates flow through equity-method income into the holding company's operating profit, so holding-segment results track the subsidiary.

Holding-segment sales in Q3 2026 were KRW 92.2 billion, down 35.8% year on year, while trademark income rose to KRW 13.8 billion from KRW 13.1 billion. Press reports said equity-method income exceeded 80% of consolidated operating profit in Q2 2026, which shows how concentrated earnings are in that line.

The battery unit saw orders fall on U.S. tariffs and geopolitical disputes, and the company cited production disruption from a fire at its Daejeon plant.

04

Earnings analysis

On a reported basis, annual sales were KRW 1.1 trillion in 2022, KRW 1.09 trillion in 2023 (down 0.6%), KRW 1.39 trillion in 2024 (up 27.7%) and KRW 1.46 trillion in 2025 (up 4.8%). Sales rose for two consecutive years from the 2023 low, and 2025 was the largest year of 2022 to 2025.

Operating profit went from KRW 245.2 billion in 2022 to KRW 256.6 billion in 2023 (up 4.6%), KRW 418.3 billion in 2024 (up 63.0%) and KRW 411.6 billion in 2025 (down 1.6%). Operating margin was 22.4%, 23.5%, 30.1% and 28.2% across the four years, peaking in 2024.

Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders rose from KRW 163.9 billion in 2022 to KRW 350.7 billion in 2024 (up 87.3%) and eased to KRW 347.2 billion in 2025 (down 1.0%). Total net profit including non-controlling interests was also KRW 347.2 billion in 2025.

On a quarterly basis, operating profit fell from KRW 137.1 billion in Q3 2025 to KRW 73.8 billion in Q4 2025 (down 46.2% from the prior quarter). It then peaked in Q1 2026 at KRW 121.7 billion on sales of KRW 378.4 billion, with controlling-shareholder net profit of KRW 124.7 billion.

In Q2 2026 operating profit was KRW 100.1 billion, supported by equity-method income of KRW 81.6 billion (up 60.9% year on year, according to press reports).

In Q3 2026 operating profit fell to KRW 66.5 billion and controlling-shareholder net profit to KRW 46.5 billion, and press reports said non-operating items turned to a KRW 28.7 billion loss.

The reasons for the sharp Q1 2026 profit jump cannot be pinned down from quarterly figures alone, so the annual report's notes should be checked for one-off items. Operating cash flow rose from KRW 31.5 billion in 2022 to KRW 133.3 billion in 2024 and KRW 235.7 billion in 2025.

05

Industry analysis

Its core subsidiary, Hankook Tire & Technology, reportedly grew operating profit in Q2 2026 on higher sales of premium tires and better selling prices and product mix. The same subsidiary absorbed foreign-exchange losses in Q3 2026 as the won strengthened against the dollar, which cut equity-method income.

In the United States, the second Tennessee plant was reported to be due to start operations in Q3 2025, adding 6 million tires of annual capacity to the first plant's output. Tires are classed as auto parts and face a 25% tariff, but press coverage said price increases have been relatively easy to pass through.

Press reports also noted that the subsidiary's operating margin fell from 18.78% in 2024 to 8.68% in 2025, after it consolidated Hanon Systems. Hanon Systems posted a 2.2% operating margin in Q2 2025, and that weakness fed into the group's profitability.

On the battery side, the company reportedly plans to add production lines and improve efficiency to meet rising global demand for AGM batteries. However, the same unit moved into an operating loss in Q3 2026 after U.S. tariffs and geopolitical disputes reduced orders and a Daejeon plant fire disrupted output.

Compared with other listed holding companies, the structural difference is the large weight of equity-method income in group earnings.

06

Outlook

The confirmed directions are two: restoring profitability in the battery business and expanding the mobility portfolio. Press reports in 2026 said the company plans to add production lines and improve efficiency to meet rising global AGM battery demand, though this was described as a plan.

After the return of Cho Hyun-bum from parole in September 2026, press reports said the mobility expansion strategy has regained momentum. The group is reviewing a possible acquisition of Lotte Rental, a domestic car rental company, and the company said it is 'at a stage of reviewing various possibilities'.

The stated idea is to link tires, parts, rental and mobility services if Lotte Rental were brought in. However, reducing the financial burden after a large M&A is cited as an immediate task. The 2026 interim dividend was set at 10% above the prior year.

The next checkpoints are when the battery unit returns to profit, whether the Lotte Rental or other M&A plans take concrete shape, and whether the subsidiary's sensitivity to exchange rates changes.

The 2026 annual report, which will show full-year ES results and the composition of equity-method income, becomes the next reference point.

Bull factors

Recovery momentum in equity-method income

Hankook Tire & Technology has kept growing volumes and improving selling prices and product mix. Press reports said Q2 2026 equity-method income rose 60.9% year on year to KRW 81.6 billion. Because subsidiary gains flow into the holding company quickly, improvement at the subsidiary shows up in group profit.

Balance sheet and cash generation

Debt is very small relative to total equity, so the borrowing burden is limited. The next point to check is whether the increase in operating cash flow continues in later filings. The 2026 interim dividend was set 10% above the prior year.

A possible recovery path for batteries

Press reports described plans to add AGM battery lines and improve efficiency to meet rising global demand. Product and merchandise sales are the main part of consolidated revenue. The ES division swung to a loss in Q3 2026, but order recovery would give a path back to profit.

Bear factors

Equity-method income and FX sensitivity

Holding-segment equity-method income fell 45.1% year on year to KRW 63.9 billion in Q3 2026, according to press reports. Foreign-exchange losses tied to the won's decline reduced the subsidiary's net income, which shrank the equity-method line. The volatility of holding-company earnings is cited as a bearish point.

Battery unit swings to a loss

The ES division swung to an operating loss of KRW 3.0 billion in Q3 2026, from a KRW 13.6 billion profit a year earlier. U.S. tariffs and geopolitical disputes cut orders, and a Daejeon plant fire disrupted production. Against the prior quarter's operating profit of KRW 8.7 billion, the direction also reversed sharply.

Consecutive quarterly declines

Operating profit peaked at KRW 121.7 billion in Q1 2026, then fell for two straight quarters to KRW 100.1 billion and KRW 66.5 billion. Controlling-shareholder net profit over the latest four quarters totals KRW 312.4 billion, below the 2025 annual figure of KRW 347.2 billion. A swing to non-operating losses in Q3 added to the decline.

10

Risk factors

FX and equity-method volatility

Won-dollar moves hit both the subsidiary's net income and the group's equity-method income. Q3 2026 absorbed FX losses from the won's decline. Press reports also cited a gap between the exchange rates applied to sales and to costs as a burden on ES profitability.

Tariff and geopolitical risk

U.S. tariffs and geopolitical disputes affect both battery orders and tire costs. Tires face a 25% tariff, though press coverage said price increases have been relatively easy. A change in tariff policy could shift the earnings path of both businesses.

Governance and M&A

Founding-family shareholdings are concentrated, and governance and succession issues are cited as latent variables around Cho Hyun-bum's prison term, parole and return to management. If the Lotte Rental review becomes concrete, reducing the financial burden of a large deal becomes a priority. The subsidiary's margin decline after the Hanon Systems acquisition also needs monitoring.

12

Overall view

Hankook & Company's 2026 results split into two drivers: equity-method income and the battery business. In the disclosed figures, operating profit peaked at KRW 121.7 billion in Q1 2026 and then fell in Q2 and Q3, with Q3 down 51.5% year on year to KRW 66.5 billion.

The causes were a smaller equity-method contribution from the subsidiary after FX losses, and the ES division's swing to a loss. On the other side, better volumes and selling prices at the subsidiary, planned battery capacity additions, a low debt ratio and higher operating cash flow are cited as supports.

Profit remains below the 2024 peak, but the balance sheet is stable, so bullish and bearish factors coexist. The price-to-book ratio sits below 1x.

The next checkpoints are whether the ES division returns to profit and equity-method income recovers in the 2026 annual report and the Q1 2027 report, and whether M&A plans such as Lotte Rental take concrete shape.
