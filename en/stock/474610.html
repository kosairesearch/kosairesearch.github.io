02

Business structure

RF Systems is a defense-focused manufacturer founded in 2000 that uses its dip brazing technology to develop and supply radar systems, antenna systems, and environmental control systems. The company joined the Rfhic group in 2020 and listed on KOSDAQ in November 2024.

According to a Kyobo Securities report, radar systems made up about 38%, antenna systems about 26%, environmental control systems about 27%, and other items about 9% of sales in the first quarter of 2026. The same report shows defense sales of KRW 32.67 billion and civil and other sales of KRW 5.5 billion for 2025.

Hana Securities data published in January 2026 put defense at 74%, civil at 19%, and other at 7% of cumulative sales through the third quarter of 2025. LIG Nex1 is the main customer, and ARC Research reports that the company also supplies ELTA of Israel.

Products include seeker antennas used in guided missile systems such as Cheongung-II and environmental control units and core radar components for counter-battery radar. The March 2026 LIG Nex1 contract, worth about KRW 16.7 billion or roughly 51% of 2025 sales, runs from March 2026 to April 2029.

The contract sits alongside an early stage of expansion from component supply into subsystem supply, and qualification for subsystem supply is under way at a new Osan plant and test facility.

On the civil side, the company has announced entry into the atomic force microscope market and is widening its reach into low-earth-orbit satellite communication antennas and aircraft maintenance.

04

Earnings analysis

On a disclosed basis, annual sales were KRW 32.8 billion in 2023, KRW 32.7 billion in 2024 (down 0.4% year on year), and KRW 38.2 billion in 2025 (up 16.8%), making 2025 the largest year of the 2023 to 2025 period.

Operating profit was KRW 1.79 billion in 2023, KRW 1.69 billion in 2024 (down 5.7%), and KRW 4.06 billion in 2025 (up 140.6%), with 2024 the smallest year. The operating margin rose from 5.5% in 2023 and 5.2% in 2024 to 10.6% in 2025, so sales growth carried through to margin.

In 2024 operating profit stayed positive but the company swung to a net loss attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 4.1 billion.

A financial table published by Hana Securities shows other non-operating income and expenses moving from a gain of about KRW 2.2 billion in 2023 to a loss of about KRW 6.6 billion in 2024, which accounts for most of the gap.

Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 3.91 billion in 2023 and KRW 5.16 billion in 2025, and total net profit followed the same pattern.

The debt-to-equity ratio was 81.2% in 2023, 64.1% in 2024, and 67.3% in 2025, with total equity of KRW 49.4 billion and total liabilities of KRW 33.2 billion at the end of 2025. Operating cash flow was KRW 5.27 billion in 2023, KRW 1.21 billion in 2024, and KRW 4.11 billion in 2025, with 2024 the weakest.

Quarterly sales rose from KRW 7.6 billion in the third quarter of 2025 to KRW 13.5 billion in the fourth quarter (up 77.9% quarter on quarter), fell to KRW 10 billion in the first quarter of 2026 (down 26.0%), and then recovered to KRW 11.6 billion in the second quarter and KRW 11.9 billion in the third quarter.

Operating profit ran KRW 613 million, KRW 1.57 billion, KRW 1.12 billion (down 28.6% quarter on quarter), KRW 1.67 billion, and KRW 1.88 billion across the same quarters. Third-quarter 2026 sales were up 56.5% from a year earlier and operating profit was up 207.1%, so profit grew faster than sales.

Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 1.39 billion in the first quarter of 2026, KRW 2.1 billion in the second, and KRW 1.91 billion in the third, which followed two consecutive quarterly gains after the first-quarter low and dipped slightly in the third quarter.

05

Industry analysis

The defense component market that RF Systems serves is seeing steady demand amid geopolitical tension and rising defense budgets. In a March 2026 report, Hana Securities cited an outside forecast of 6.5% annual growth in the global radar and antenna market from 2026 to 2034.

The same report describes the company as a supplier of core components to major guided-missile and radar systems that stands to benefit directly from a broader order pipeline.

Hana Securities also noted that strong Korean defense exports and a sharp rise in the order backlog of LIG Nex1, the main customer, support demand for the company's parts.

On the cycle, ARC Research said in a May 2026 report that revenue recognition on orders already booked could accelerate from 2027 given defense lead times. In terms of competitive position, RF Systems sits as a component and subsystem supplier inside prime contractors' finished systems.

Hana Securities explained that as prime contractors outsource more subsystem work, unit prices and contract sizes can rise together. The same report assumes prime contractors outsource to cut costs and ensure quality compared with in-house development, so the pace of outsourcing depends on customer decisions.

ARC Research also noted that domestic defense margins rarely move into double digits. Whether overseas business and a higher share of non-defense sales can lift the margin ceiling is the key test of the company's position against its sector.

06

Outlook

The company has guided to sales of KRW 45.0 billion for 2026 and KRW 69.4 billion for 2027. In March 2026 Hana Securities described that guidance as conservative and said that the pace of new production and order recognition left room for results to exceed it.

Kyobo Securities, in a June 2026 report, estimated sales of KRW 45.35 billion for 2026 and KRW 63.36 billion for 2027, so its 2027 figure sits below company guidance. The same estimates put operating profit at KRW 5.47 billion for 2026 and KRW 9.64 billion for 2027, with operating margins of 12.1% and 15.2%.

On orders, Kyobo Securities noted that the company secured more than KRW 60 billion of new orders in 2025 and roughly KRW 30 billion in the first quarter of 2026 alone. The order backlog stood at KRW 78 billion at the end of 2025.

On product timing, the unmanned reconnaissance HTU and SAR antennas are set for mass production in 2026, a corps-level counter-battery radar order is expected in 2027, and SAR satellite antenna panels are scheduled for launch in 2030. Hana Securities expected these items to feed into results in sequence.

Subsystem supply is moving through customer qualification at the new Osan plant and test facility. Shareholder returns included the cancellation of 93,428 treasury shares on June 12, 2026.

The key questions for the outlook are how close 2026 sales come to the KRW 45.0 billion guidance and how the gap between the 2027 guidance and Kyobo Securities' estimate narrows.

08

Bull factors

Wider order visibility

The order backlog expanded to KRW 78 billion at the end of 2025, and the roughly KRW 16.7 billion contract with LIG Nex1 signed in March 2026 runs through April 2029. Kyobo Securities put new orders above KRW 60 billion in 2025 and about KRW 30 billion in the first quarter of 2026 alone. This order flow forms the base for sales from 2026 onward.

Operating leverage and margin gains

The operating margin rose from 5.5% in 2023 and 5.2% in 2024 to 10.6% in 2025. Third-quarter 2026 operating profit grew 207.1% from a year earlier, well ahead of the 56.5% gain in sales. Quarterly operating profit reached KRW 1.88 billion, the highest in the disclosed quarterly series.

Subsystem shift and defense demand

Hana Securities explained that subsystem supply raises both unit prices and contract size compared with single parts. Forecast growth in the global radar and antenna market and strong Korean defense exports are cited as demand backdrop.

If subsystem qualification is completed, the unit price structure could change, making it a central variable for future profitability.

09

Bear factors

Concentration on one prime customer

According to ARC Research, LIG Nex1 is the main customer, with ELTA of Israel also among buyers, but sales rest heavily on one prime. As a result, that prime's order and supply schedule can determine the timing of the company's revenue. The March 2026 contract is a long-term deal with the same customer and carries the same dependence.

Quarterly volatility

First-quarter 2026 sales fell 26.0% from the prior quarter and operating profit fell 28.6%. In 2024 operating profit was positive but a non-operating loss produced a net loss, and operating cash flow was weakest that year at KRW 1.21 billion. Until more quarters accumulate, a one or two quarter trend is hard to read as a durable pattern.

Execution risk in the subsystem shift

Subsystem supply depends on customer qualification and on bringing the new Osan plant and test facility into operation. Kyobo Securities' 2027 sales estimate of KRW 63.36 billion sits below the company's KRW 69.4 billion guidance. ARC Research also noted that domestic defense work rarely allows double-digit margins.

10

Risk factors

Policy and budget risk

Most of sales depends on defense demand, so the company is sensitive to how defense budgets are set and spent. If the defense spending expansion that Hana Securities pointed to slows, new order timing could slip. Even with rising export volumes, delivery schedules depend on the contract structure of prime contractors.

Qualification and capacity timing risk

If subsystem qualification and the new plant ramp up later than planned, 2027 sales could fall short of company guidance, as Kyobo Securities' estimate already implies. Because the corps-level counter-battery radar order is expected in 2027, any shift in that schedule would move the timing of results. Capacity spending that comes first could also slow the pace at which quarterly profit recovers.

Financial and shareholder structure risk

At the end of 2025 total liabilities stood at KRW 33.2 billion against total equity of KRW 49.4 billion, with a debt-to-equity ratio of 67.3%. After the 2024 net loss and the drop in operating cash flow, working capital and capital spending need to be watched against financial capacity. As a small-cap stock, supply and demand shifts can move the multiple the market applies.

Overall view

RF Systems is a defense component specialist that posted 2025 annual sales of KRW 38.2 billion and operating profit of KRW 4.06 billion, turning from a 2024 net loss to net profit attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 5.16 billion in 2025.

After the first-quarter 2026 sales low, quarterly sales and operating profit rose in both the second and third quarters, and third-quarter operating profit of KRW 1.88 billion is the largest in the disclosed quarterly series.

A backlog of KRW 78 billion at the end of 2025 and the roughly KRW 16.7 billion LIG Nex1 contract support revenue visibility through 2029. On the other side, customer concentration, quarterly volatility, and the timing of subsystem qualification and the new plant remain unproven.

The gap between the company's 2027 guidance of KRW 69.4 billion and Kyobo Securities' estimate of KRW 63.36 billion shows the width of the earnings outlook.

Valuation reads against the KRW 7.31 billion of net profit attributable to controlling shareholders over the last four quarters, so interpretation depends on whether that profit holds.

The next checkpoints are the preliminary 2026 full-year results, the 2026 annual business report, first-quarter 2027 results, and whether the corps-level counter-battery radar order is secured.
