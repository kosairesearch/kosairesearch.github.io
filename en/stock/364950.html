02

Business structure

AI Korea develops in-house its Central Electrolyte Supply System (CESS), which supplies electrolyte to battery plants used in electric vehicle battery production. Building on this, the company has widened its scope to process piping design, fabrication and installation, and process equipment.

According to the Butler data aggregator's breakdown of Q2 2026 revenue, the secondary-battery CESS infrastructure segment accounted for 88%, the system segment (including dry cleaning equipment) for 5%, other for 5%, and process piping infrastructure for 2%.

Most revenue therefore depends on one secondary-battery equipment line. Piping, ducts and fire-protection facilities, along with display and semiconductor equipment, are also among its main product lines.

Its customers include Samsung SDI, LG Energy Solution and SK On among Korea's three major battery makers, and the company says it also serves global ESS companies.

Overseas, a CESS contract worth KRW 13.16 billion for a Tesla ESS plant was disclosed in February 2026, and a KRW 24.1 billion outsourcing and infrastructure contract was signed mainly through its Hungarian subsidiary.

The Spanish subsidiary is pursuing orders of more than KRW 10 billion for a battery plant project tied to a Volkswagen affiliate.

The company has set semiconductors, physical AI and AI data centers as its three mega-project areas, and is growing data center cooling and autonomous mobile robot (AMR)-based logistics automation. At the end of 2025 it signed a strategic MOU with Envicool, a global liquid cooling company for ESS.

Competitively, the company points to its in-house CESS development and precision piping construction capability as differentiators. Listed peers in the same machinery and equipment sector include People & Technology, SPG and Taesung.

04

Earnings analysis

On a disclosed basis, revenue in 2025 was KRW 86.9 billion, down 24.1% year on year from KRW 114.6 billion in 2024. Operating profit fell 68.0%, from KRW 8.81 billion in 2024 to KRW 2.82 billion in 2025, and the operating margin declined from 7.7% to 3.2%.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders swung from KRW 12.3 billion in 2024 to a net loss of KRW 777 million in 2025, a move into loss.

Consolidated net income, which includes non-controlling interests, went from KRW 12.3 billion to a net loss of KRW 1.07 billion, and this figure differs from the controlling-shareholder measure, so the two should be read separately. The balance sheet improved, with the debt-to-equity ratio falling from 93.9% to 47.9%.

Total equity at end-2025 was KRW 56.2 billion, equity attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 57 billion, and total liabilities were KRW 27 billion. Operating cash flow moved from -KRW 12 billion in 2024 to KRW 21.3 billion in 2025.

Quarterly revenue rose from KRW 10.8 billion in Q3 2025 to KRW 15.2 billion in Q4 2025 (up 40.7% from the prior quarter), KRW 18.8 billion in Q1 2026 (up 23.8%) and KRW 21.6 billion in Q2 2026 (up 14.6%), then fell to KRW 18.9 billion in Q3 2026 (down 12.4% quarter on quarter, but up 74.8% year on year).

Operating results went from an operating loss of KRW 1.88 billion in Q3 2025 and a loss of KRW 455 million in Q4 2025 (a smaller loss) to an operating profit of KRW 600 million in Q1 2026, then back to an operating loss of KRW 2.58 billion in Q2 2026.

Q3 2026 posted an operating loss of KRW 1.89 billion, and the company has recorded two consecutive loss-making quarters.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 306 million in Q4 2025 and KRW 2.77 billion in Q1 2026 (up 807.0% quarter on quarter), then a net loss of KRW 18.61 million in Q2 2026 and a net loss of KRW 5.89 billion in Q3 2026 as losses widened.

The controlling-shareholder net loss over the last four quarters totals KRW 2.82 billion. The company attributes the revenue growth to expanded overseas projects and a profitability-focused management approach, yet the Q1 2026 profit did not carry into Q2.

The reason the Q1 2026 controlling-shareholder net income was much larger than its operating profit lies in non-operating items, and the detail should be checked in the periodic reports.

05

Industry analysis

Demand for secondary-battery equipment is being affected by a slowdown in the EV market. The company said that because EV market weakness has delayed construction of global battery plants, it developed its CESS in-house to secure technological competitiveness. The ESS infrastructure business is described as recovering.

A May 29, 2026 news report covered the recovery of its core ESS infrastructure business and expansion into new areas such as data center cooling and logistics automation.

Korea Investment & Securities analyst Yoon Cheol-hwan said the company passed its Q3 2025 earnings trough and has continued to recover through Q4 2025 and Q1 2026. He attributed the North American and European order base to the expansion of projects by Korea's three major battery makers and global ESS companies.

In the semiconductor equipment market, the company said its secondary-battery CESS construction capability has been recognized in a field with high entry barriers. In data center cooling, it is seeking market entry through its collaboration with Envicool.

Value Line noted that battery makers weigh track record heavily and cited the company's first-mass-production experience and customized equipment capability as strengths.

Listed peers in the same machinery and equipment sector include People & Technology, SPG and Taesung, and how much of their business is tied to secondary batteries should be checked in their periodic reports.

Outlook

A Korea Investment & Securities analyst also said the backlog built on North American and European projects exceeds KRW 40 billion.

The Tesla CESS contract runs from February 26, 2026 to December 31, 2026, with payment terms of 14% advance, 76% interim and 10% balance. The contract value is 9,168,094 US dollars, converted at the initial published rate of KRW 1,435.30 per dollar on February 26, 2026.

The mechanical construction order in the Yongin semiconductor cluster, announced on November 9, 2026, was described as a chance to build actual construction records in chip facilities.

The company said it aims to drive a re-rating by expanding its next-generation logistics automation solutions and data center cooling business. A May 2026 news report said second-half earnings were expected to improve on data center cooling and logistics automation.

However, the Q3 2026 disclosure showed an operating loss of KRW 1.89 billion, so whether that expectation has materialized should be checked in the annual report. Korea Investment & Securities presented a September 1, 2026 report titled around the need for results from new businesses.

The next checkpoints are the expiry of the Tesla contract period on December 31, 2026 and the 2026 annual report.

The Spanish subsidiary is also pursuing orders above KRW 10 billion for a Volkswagen affiliate's battery plant. Such orders form the base for future revenue.

Profitable quarter and record revenue quarter

The company attributes this to revenue growth from expanded overseas projects and a profitability-focused approach. Q2 2026 revenue of KRW 21.6 billion is the highest quarterly revenue in the disclosed period.

New business expansion

The company has set semiconductors, physical AI and AI data centers as its three mega-project areas. It signed a strategic MOU with Envicool at the end of 2025 to pursue the data center cooling market, and it describes AMR-based logistics automation as moving toward commercialization.

The November 2026 mechanical construction order in the Yongin semiconductor cluster was presented as a step toward building chip-facility construction records.

Bear factors

Continued losses and wider losses

After an operating loss of KRW 2.58 billion in Q2 2026, Q3 2026 posted an operating loss of KRW 1.89 billion, marking two consecutive loss-making quarters. The Q3 2026 controlling-shareholder net loss of KRW 5.89 billion was much larger than the KRW 18.61 million loss in Q2 2026.

The controlling-shareholder net loss over the last four quarters totals KRW 2.82 billion, because the Q3 2026 loss exceeded the combined Q4 2025 and Q1 2026 profits.

Concentration in secondary batteries and industry headwinds

According to the Butler data aggregator, 88% of Q2 2026 revenue came from the secondary-battery CESS infrastructure segment. Delays in global battery plant construction caused by the EV market slowdown weigh on the order pace in this segment.

The company is widening its business with Korea's three major battery makers and European projects, but its exposure to a single industry cycle remains.

Backlog figures and new business results

The company disclosed a KRW 52.3 billion cumulative backlog as of Q1 2026, while a May 2026 news report cited a Korea Investment & Securities analyst putting it at more than KRW 40 billion. A Korea Investment & Securities report dated September 1, 2026 was titled around the need for new-business results.

The revenue contribution of data center cooling and logistics automation is not yet separated in disclosed figures.

Risk factors

Earnings volatility

Revenue reached KRW 21.6 billion in Q2 2026, yet operating results alternated between profit and loss. Quarterly earnings could swing widely depending on project-level costs and installation schedules. The fact that the Q1 2026 profit did not continue into later quarters is the basis for this risk.

Customer and industry concentration

Most revenue comes from secondary-battery equipment, and key customers are concentrated among Korea's three major battery makers and global ESS companies. If slower EV demand delays battery plant investment, order timing could also slip. The revenue share of the new businesses is not yet separated in disclosed figures.

Currency and overseas subsidiary execution

The Tesla CESS contract was disclosed in won after converting a dollar-denominated amount at the rate of KRW 1,435.30 per dollar on February 26, 2026. Won-converted revenue could change if the exchange rate moves.

Execution of overseas projects through the Hungarian and Spanish subsidiaries depends on local schedules and management capacity.

Overall view

AI Korea started from secondary-battery equipment such as CESS and is widening its scope through overseas orders and new businesses in semiconductors and data centers. The KRW 52.3 billion cumulative order backlog as of Q1 2026 and the Tesla CESS contract support the revenue base.

In the disclosed results, 2025 revenue and operating profit both declined year on year, and operating losses followed in Q2 and Q3 2026 after the Q1 2026 swing to profit.

The controlling-shareholder net loss over the last four quarters totals KRW 2.82 billion, and the debt-to-equity ratio at end-2025 was 47.9%, lower than in 2024.

Strengths include order growth and a stronger balance sheet, while weaknesses include quarterly earnings volatility, concentration in the secondary-battery cycle, and backlog figures that differ across reports.

Because the company is loss-making over the last four quarters, earnings-based multiples cannot be calculated, so valuation reads mainly through asset-based measures.

The next checkpoints are the end of the Tesla contract period on December 31, 2026, and the operating margin and controlling-shareholder profit in the 2026 annual report.
