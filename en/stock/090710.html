02

Business structure

Hyulim ROBOT is a special-purpose machinery maker that was listed on KOSDAQ on December 5, 2006. Its business has long centered on Cartesian robots and robot application systems, which account for most of its sales.

Press analysis from Global Economic reports that Cartesian robot sales fell from KRW 13.5 billion in 2022 to KRW 5.7 billion in 2025, and robot application system sales dropped from KRW 10.5 billion in 2022 to KRW 4.4 billion in 2025.

The company says that starting from 2026 it is shifting away from its Cartesian robot-centered structure toward a comprehensive robot company with autonomous driving, AMR and TR manufacturing capabilities.

Its core platform is the TETRA-DSV, a modular autonomous robot that can combine robot arms, conveyors and special equipment. A pan-tilt module built on its in-house HERKULEX smart servo is positioned for humanoid and security or inspection robots, and the company frames it as a component supply business.

End demand comes from manufacturing and logistics sites, and reduced capital spending by battery makers, a key customer group, has been cited as a constraint.

04

Earnings analysis

On a disclosed annual basis, sales were KRW 55.5 billion in 2022, KRW 82.7 billion in 2023 (up 49.0% year on year), KRW 133.1 billion in 2024 (up 61.0%) and KRW 168.3 billion in 2025 (up 26.5%), rising for three consecutive years from 2023 through 2025.

Operating losses were KRW 7.47 billion in 2022, KRW 1.86 billion in 2023, KRW 4.94 billion in 2024 and KRW 17.3 billion in 2025, a four-year run of losses from 2022 through 2025.

The operating margin was -13.5% in 2022, improved to -2.3% in 2023, the smallest loss margin of the period, then widened to -3.7% in 2024 and -10.3% in 2025. Sales grew in 2025, but higher selling and administrative expenses were cited as the main driver of the larger operating loss.

The 2025 net loss including non-controlling interests was KRW 30.1 billion, versus a net loss attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 16.9 billion, and the company attributed the wider loss to lower other income and financial income.

These two measures differ: in 2023 net income including non-controlling interests was KRW 682 million, while the loss attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 3.84 billion. Operating cash flow was KRW -4.4 billion in 2022, KRW 7.64 billion in 2023, KRW -30.8 billion in 2024 and KRW -23 billion in 2025.

Quarterly sales were KRW 40.5 billion in Q3 2025, KRW 24.6 billion in Q4 2025 (down 39.2% from the prior quarter), KRW 30 billion in Q1 2026 (up 22.1%), KRW 25.4 billion in Q2 2026 (down 15.2%) and KRW 22.6 billion in Q3 2026 (down 11.0%), so sales have declined for two straight quarters after peaking in Q1 2026.

The Q4 2025 operating loss of KRW 15.9 billion was the largest among the quarters disclosed, and the Q3 2026 operating loss of KRW 3.9 billion narrowed from the prior quarter.

The subsidiary E-Cell, a battery-logistics automation business, received a KRIX delisting decision in February 2025 after about three months of ownership. Press estimates put its 2025 operating loss at KRW 14.4 billion, and the consolidated loss widened well beyond the parent's standalone loss.

05

Industry analysis

Demand for industrial robots is sensitive to domestic manufacturing capital spending, and lower facility investment by domestic manufacturers has been cited as the reason for weak supply of Cartesian robots. Press reports also flag reduced capital spending by battery makers as a demand constraint.

The government has said it will strengthen support for artificial intelligence and robotics industries, which drew investor attention to the robot theme. The K-Humanoid Alliance is a state-backed strategic program run by the Ministry of Trade, Industry and Energy.

Still, press coverage notes that the robot theme as a whole has accumulated fatigue after short-term surges. Analysts also note that share price volatility has repeatedly widened on external issues and flows rather than earnings.

An industry expert said the company's product line is technologically distinct from humanoid AI robots and cobots, and Rainbow Robotics and Doosan Robotics, both cobot-focused listed peers, have different product mixes.

Whether policy support turns into orders and sales remains the open question, since the growth shift the market expects must become visible in actual results.

06

Outlook

The company has laid out a phased expansion strategy from 2026 covering technology demonstration, product refinement, industrial application and mass production. Centered on the TETRA-DSV platform, it plans to expand its AMR and TR product lines for logistics, process, inspection and security sites from 2026.

On the component side, the pan-tilt module built on its HERKULEX servo is slated for humanoid and autonomous robot applications. The company has described 2026 as a turning point away from R&D-centered operations toward mass-production capability.

In September 2026, its CEO received a foreign ministry commendation at an international climate forum, and the company has linked its automation technology to process optimization and energy savings.

Whether the turnaround plan has reached sales is answered by Q3 2026 revenue of KRW 22.6 billion, which fell 44.0% year on year.

The next periodic report is the 2026 annual report, and the key checks are whether the Q4 2026 operating result and full-year loss compare favorably with the KRW 17.3 billion loss of 2025, and how large the component business becomes within segment disclosures.

08

Bull factors

Concrete turnaround plan

The company plans to expand its AMR and TR lineup on the TETRA-DSV platform from 2026. It says it completed technical verification of core components, autonomous driving platforms and robot module design through the K-Humanoid Alliance.

Its in-house HERKULEX servo-based pan-tilt module is cited as a basis for growing the component supply business.

Policy linkage and theme tailwinds

The government's stated support for AI and robotics has drawn attention to the robot theme. Because the K-Humanoid Alliance is a state-led program run under the Ministry of Trade, Industry and Energy, the company's work can be linked to policy initiatives. Whether this policy support converts into orders and revenue remains the condition for this bullish case.

Narrowing operating loss

The Q3 2026 operating loss of KRW 3.9 billion narrowed from KRW 5.3 billion in Q2 2026, and it is far below the KRW 15.9 billion loss in Q4 2025. The operating margin in 2023 was -2.3%, the smallest loss margin in the 2022–2025 period. Whether the quarterly narrowing continues is the bullish checkpoint.

09

Bear factors

Persistent losses and wider net loss

Losses have continued for five straight quarters from Q3 2025 through Q3 2026. The Q3 2026 net loss attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 9.07 billion widened from the prior quarter. The 2025 operating loss of KRW 17.3 billion extended a four-year run of losses.

Declining core sales

Q3 2026 sales of KRW 22.6 billion fell 44.0% year on year and have declined for two straight quarters since peaking in Q1 2026. Press figures show Cartesian robot sales peaked at KRW 13.5 billion in 2022 and fell to KRW 5.7 billion in 2025. Whether new businesses can offset the core decline is the central bearish question.

Governance and subsidiary burden

Press reports say the largest shareholder's stake fell to 0.87%. A March 2026 amended filing changed total borrowings to KRW 58 billion and total pledged collateral to KRW 74.5 billion. The delisted subsidiary's losses also weigh on consolidated results.

Risk factors

Financial capacity

Total liabilities were KRW 73.4 billion and total equity KRW 203.3 billion at end-2025, a debt ratio of 36.1%. Operating cash flow was negative for two consecutive years, at KRW -30.8 billion in 2024 and KRW -23 billion in 2025. Continued net losses could change the equity structure and the terms of future financing.

Governance and pledges

Press coverage has described the governance structure, with a small residual stake controlling a listed company, as a concern. Reports also flag the overhang from a convertible bond issue of about KRW 15 billion. These items are best tracked through verifiable filings.

Execution and theme volatility

Sales have declined for two straight quarters after reaching KRW 30 billion in Q1 2026, so the turnaround has not yet shown up in revenue. Coverage repeatedly notes that share price volatility has widened on external issues and trading flows rather than earnings. If execution slows, reliance on the theme could grow.

Overall view

Hyulim ROBOT's sales have risen for three straight years since 2023, but operating losses persisted for four straight years from 2022 through 2025. Q3 2026 sales of KRW 22.6 billion fell 44.0% year on year, and losses have continued for five straight quarters.

The operating loss narrowed to KRW 3.9 billion from the prior quarter, while the net loss attributable to controlling shareholders widened to KRW 9.07 billion. The company plans to reshape its business around autonomous driving, AMR and TR products and HERKULEX servo-based components.

On the bullish side are its participation in a state-backed program and completed technical verification; on the bearish side are declining core sales, governance concerns and the subsidiary burden. Press coverage repeatedly describes the share price as driven by the theme and trading flows rather than earnings.

The next checkpoints are the close of the 2026 fiscal year and the sales and operating loss trend in the 2026 annual report.
