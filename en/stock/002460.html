Report overview

Deep Order Backlog, but Quarterly Profits Still Volatile

HS Hwasung has improved its balance sheet and returned to profit growth in 2025 with an order backlog of KRW 2,562.2 billion as of June 2026, yet quarterly revenue has fallen in 2026 and operating losses have run for two consecutive quarters amid the regional housing slowdown.

  1. 1

    2025 results turned upward, with operating profit of KRW 43.2 billion (up 82.2%), profit attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 27.6 billion (up 130.3%), and an operating margin of 6.6%.

  2. 2

    Quarterly trends in 2026 are weak. Revenue bottomed at KRW 69 billion in the first quarter of 2026 and rose in the two quarters after, while operating losses have continued for two consecutive quarters from the second quarter of 2026.

  3. 3

    The order base is substantial. The contract backlog stood at KRW 2,562.2 billion at the end of June 2026, about six years of 2025 revenue, and the company says its roughly KRW 1.5 trillion of orders in 2025 was the largest in its history.

  4. 4

    The revenue mix has shifted toward residential sales. Sales revenue from units rose 103% in 2025 while building construction revenue fell 34.6%, and because such revenue is recognized at completion and handover, quarterly profit swings have widened.

  5. 5

    The balance sheet improved, but receivables increased. The debt-to-equity ratio fell to 69.5% at the end of 2025, while construction receivables more than doubled from KRW 29.2 billion to KRW 60.5 billion and new unit-sale receivables of KRW 13.2 billion emerged.

02

Business structure

HS Hwasung is a Daegu-based construction company that runs residential sales, building construction, civil engineering and overseas projects together.

Building construction made up 79.5% of revenue in 2023, and according to a press report citing regulatory filings, the 2024 mix split into 55.8% building construction, 25.9% civil engineering and 12.3% other.

Sales revenue from residential units rose 69.8% to KRW 158.8 billion in 2024, lifting its share of total revenue to 25.9%. In 2025, residential sales revenue rose 103% to KRW 322.3 billion and became the main driver of the revenue recovery, while building construction revenue fell 34.6% to KRW 223.6 billion.

Civil engineering revenue increased from KRW 36.5 billion to KRW 53.5 billion. Residential customers are home buyers, and civil engineering work comes from public clients and private developers. The company is described as the largest construction company in Daegu, which remains its core regional base.

Recently it has entered Seoul core redevelopment projects in Gangnam, Seocho and Seongsu under its high-end EHCRA brand. Overseas, it won a 40% stake in a BRT road project in Nairobi, Kenya, securing an order value of KRW 31.35 billion.

Competition is with local builders in Daegu and Gyeongbuk, and with other builders for redevelopment contracts in the capital area.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual revenue rose from KRW 645.7 billion in 2022 to KRW 908.1 billion in 2023 (up 40.6%), fell to KRW 612.8 billion in 2024 (down 32.5%), and recovered to KRW 659.5 billion in 2025 (up 7.6%).

Operating profit went from KRW 14.5 billion in 2022 to KRW 25.3 billion in 2023 (up 73.6%) and KRW 23.7 billion in 2024 (down 6.0%) before reaching KRW 43.2 billion in 2025 (up 82.2%); 2025 was the largest year and 2022 the smallest in the 2022 to 2025 window.

The operating margin rose from 2.3% in 2022 to 2.8% in 2023, 3.9% in 2024 and 6.6% in 2025. Profit attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 24.3 billion in 2022, KRW 21.1 billion in 2023 (down 13.4%), KRW 12 billion in 2024 (down 43.1%) and KRW 27.6 billion in 2025 (up 130.3%).

Net profit including non-controlling interests, which is a different figure, was KRW 30.9 billion in 2025. A press report said the cost of sales ratio improved from 87.9% in 2024 to 84.9% in 2025.

Operating cash flow moved from -KRW 184.6 billion in 2022 to KRW 4.28 billion in 2023, KRW 122.7 billion in 2024 and KRW 52.7 billion in 2025. On a quarterly basis, the third quarter of 2025 was the peak with revenue of KRW 295.5 billion and operating profit of KRW 33.8 billion.

The fourth quarter of 2025 brought revenue of KRW 96.7 billion (down 67.3% from the prior quarter) and an operating loss of KRW 3.5 billion.

The first quarter of 2026 returned to operating profit of KRW 191 million on revenue of KRW 69 billion, but the second quarter posted an operating loss of KRW 5.44 billion on revenue of KRW 81.6 billion, and the third quarter an operating loss of KRW 3.08 billion on revenue of KRW 93.2 billion (down 68.5% year on year).

The third-quarter loss was smaller than the second quarter's, but losses have continued for two consecutive quarters. Profit attributable to controlling shareholders for the last four quarters totals KRW 1.52 billion.

05

Industry analysis

The regional housing slowdown is the biggest variable behind the company's results. Unsold inventory and weaker transactions have reduced new sales and construction starts, and higher construction and financing costs have made it harder for regional builders to earn adequate margins.

In a May 2025 press report, a company official said Daegu project volume had fallen sharply, prompting a push for orders outside the region. A press report on first-half 2026 results then said revenue fell 43.7% year on year and turned into an operating loss.

Because residential sales revenue is recognized at completion and handover, a frozen sales market also slows revenue recognition.

In the capital area, redevelopment bidding remains competitive, and the company bid for the Changdong Sangah 1 reconstruction project in 2026 while seeking to strengthen its EHCRA brand in core Seoul districts. Its position as Daegu's leading builder is a strength, but it also ties earnings to the local housing market.

Public civil engineering work and overseas infrastructure projects backed by EDCF financing are expanding as revenue sources that follow different cycles from home sales. The company is moving from a regional housing base toward the capital area and overseas markets.

06

Outlook

In its January 2026 management strategy meeting, the company set a 2026 order target of KRW 1,400 billion. Its housing and building segment plans to select projects carefully, centered on capital-area redevelopment, while civil engineering and environmental work will weigh profitability and risk together.

The contract backlog of KRW 2,562.2 billion at the end of June 2026 is cited as the foundation for medium- to long-term results.

A disclosure on August 31, 2026 said the company was selected as the builder for the Bichwi Town street-home redevelopment in Daechi-dong, Gangnam-gu, with an expected construction value of KRW 129.6 billion, equal to 19.7% of 2025 revenue.

The company said it would disclose confirmed terms when the main contract is signed. It has won Myeonmok-bon-dong Moa Town zones 2, 3 and 5 in Jungnang-gu, Seoul, and if the adjacent zones 4 and 1 are added, it could build a Moa Town of about 1,500 households.

In Africa, the Nairobi BRT road project marks its first infrastructure entry there, and it plans to expand EDCF-backed infrastructure projects and diversify overseas work into greenhouse gas reduction and renewable energy.

At its March 2026 annual shareholders meeting, the company approved cash dividends totaling about KRW 7.1 billion, and it said it would pursue capital-area housing and redevelopment, civil engineering, environmental and overseas projects in a balanced way.

Whether quarterly results recover depends on whether the operating losses seen through the third quarter of 2026 turn back into profit.

08

Bull factors

Backlog Covers About Six Years of Revenue

The contract backlog was KRW 2,562.2 billion at the end of June 2026, about six years of 2025 revenue. The company says its roughly KRW 1.5 trillion of orders in 2025 was its largest ever. Its 2026 order target was set at KRW 1,400 billion.

Revenue follows construction starts and progress, which is why the backlog is seen as the base for medium- to long-term results.

2025 Profit Recovery and Balance Sheet Improvement

The operating margin rose from 2.3% in 2022 to 6.6% in 2025. The debt-to-equity ratio fell from 168.4% in 2023 to 69.5% in 2025. A disclosure-based report said total liabilities fell by KRW 146.2 billion from the prior year. Operating cash flow in 2025 was KRW 52.7 billion.

Seoul Redevelopment and Overseas Order Expansion

The Bichwi Town street-home redevelopment, disclosed on August 31, 2026, carries an expected construction value of KRW 129.6 billion, equal to 19.7% of 2025 revenue. The Myeonmok-bon-dong Moa Town project could grow to about 1,500 households if adjacent zones are added.

The Nairobi BRT road project secured an order value of KRW 31.35 billion through a 40% stake. These wins show the strategy to reduce reliance on the regional housing market.

09

Bear factors

Revenue Decline and Two Quarters of Operating Losses

Third-quarter 2026 revenue of KRW 93.2 billion fell 68.5% from a year earlier. A first-half 2026 press report said revenue fell 43.7% and turned into an operating loss. Operating losses have continued for two consecutive quarters from the second quarter of 2026. Profit attributable to controlling shareholders over the last four quarters totals only KRW 1.52 billion.

Regional Housing Slump and Dependence on Daegu

Reports point to unsold inventory and weaker transactions slowing new sales and construction starts in the regional housing market. Higher construction and financing costs make it harder for regional builders to earn adequate margins.

Although the company is Daegu's leading builder, its earnings remain tied to the local housing market. Its capital-area and overseas expansion is still underway, so it will take time before those projects contribute fully.

Rising Receivables and Borrowing Burden

Construction receivables more than doubled from KRW 29.2 billion to KRW 60.5 billion by the end of 2025, and new unit-sale receivables of KRW 13.2 billion appeared. A press report said short-term borrowings rose from KRW 20 billion in 2022 to KRW 156.1 billion in 2024.

Financing costs rose from KRW 8.3 billion to KRW 22.2 billion over the same period. If cash collection slows, financial pressure could build again.

10

Risk factors

Regional Housing Market

If unsold inventory and weak transactions in the Daegu and Gyeongbuk housing markets persist, new sales and construction starts will fall together. Because residential revenue is recognized at completion and handover, a market slump can delay revenue recognition.

The company is expanding into the capital area and overseas, but those segments will take time to grow in weight.

Receivables Collection and Cash Flow

Construction and unit-sale receivables increased by the end of 2025, while unbilled construction balances fell from KRW 60.1 billion to KRW 36.9 billion. Operating cash flow dropped from KRW 122.7 billion in 2024 to KRW 52.7 billion in 2025.

Because advance payments convert into revenue over time, changes in sales contracts can shift the timing of cash inflows.

Costs and Quarterly Earnings Volatility

The cost of sales ratio improved to 84.9% in 2025 but remains high. Quarterly revenue swung sharply, falling from KRW 295.5 billion in the third quarter of 2025 to KRW 96.7 billion in the fourth quarter.

The company returned to operating profit in the first quarter of 2026 but slipped back into losses in the second and third quarters. The pace and size of any profit recovery will depend on cost increases and construction progress.

Overall view

HS Hwasung showed a profit recovery direction in 2025, with operating profit up 82.2%, profit attributable to controlling shareholders up 130.3%, and the operating margin reaching 6.6%. The balance sheet also improved, with the debt-to-equity ratio at 69.5%.

The contract backlog of KRW 2,562.2 billion at the end of June 2026, about six years of revenue, is seen as the base for medium- to long-term results. On the other side, operating losses have continued for two consecutive quarters from the second quarter of 2026, and third-quarter revenue fell 68.5% year on year.

The regional housing slump, dependence on Daegu, and rising construction receivables remain factors that need to be confirmed. Bullish factors are the backlog and balance sheet improvement, while bearish factors are the quarterly earnings trend and receivables collection.

The next checkpoints are the full-year operating margin in the 2026 annual report and whether operating profit returns in the first quarter of 2027.
