02

Business structure

HANJIN Logistics runs a diversified logistics business split into parcel delivery, port handling, global, and energy logistics. In its 3Q 2026 earnings release, the company said parcel, port handling and global operations all grew steadily.

Port handling volumes rose around the Incheon and Pyeongtaek ports and supported results. The global segment operates fulfillment centers in Los Angeles, Toronto, the Netherlands and the United Kingdom, supporting K-beauty and fashion brands with overseas delivery and local distribution.

The Incheon Airport Global Distribution Center (GDC) is described by the company as having the largest customs facility among domestic logistics firms.

According to an October 2026 report, global segment revenue in the first half of 2026 reached KRW 437.4 billion, up 31.1% year on year, while segment operating profit was KRW 8.1 billion, up about 7%.

In small merchant support, the company was selected in 2026 as an operator of a government logistics program covering up to 75 firms, offering a one-click fulfillment service that combines storage, packing and delivery.

The energy business is building expertise in secondary battery materials equipment transport and power equipment logistics, and in February 2026 the company said it obtained the CEIV Lithium Batteries air cargo certification.

It operates heavy-lift vessels and a radioactive waste carrier, with track records in LNG and wind plant equipment transport. Competitively, domestic parcel delivery is described as saturated with intensifying rivalry, which makes margin improvement difficult. Overseas subsidiaries' strong results have partly offset weakness in the parcel business.

04

Earnings analysis

Based on the disclosed annual figures, revenue fell from KRW 2.85 trillion in 2022 to KRW 2.81 trillion in 2023 (down 1.5%), then rose to KRW 3.02 trillion in 2024 (up 7.4%) and KRW 3.06 trillion in 2025 (up 1.6%), making 2025 the largest year in the 2022 to 2025 period.

Operating profit was KRW 114.5 billion in 2022, KRW 122.5 billion in 2023 (up 7.0%), KRW 100.1 billion in 2024 (down 18.3%) and KRW 112.2 billion in 2025 (up 12.1%), so 2023 was the largest year and 2024 the smallest.

The operating margin stayed in a 3% to 4% band, at 4.0%, 4.4%, 3.3% and 3.7% from 2022 to 2025, so revenue growth did not translate into a wider margin.

Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders fell from KRW 47.6 billion in 2022 to KRW 26.1 billion in 2023 (down 45.1%), then turned to a net loss of KRW 4.28 billion in 2024.

A further net loss of KRW 1.55 billion in 2025 means the company has posted losses for two consecutive years, although the 2025 loss was smaller.

Net income including non-controlling interests was a loss of KRW 365 million in 2024 and a profit of KRW 985 million in 2025, and the gap between the two measures reflects the share attributable to non-controlling interests.

On a quarterly basis, 3Q 2025 revenue of KRW 799.3 billion and operating profit of KRW 30.8 billion were followed by 4Q 2025 revenue of KRW 795.7 billion (down 0.5% quarter on quarter) and operating profit of KRW 17.1 billion (down 44.7%).

Revenue bottomed at KRW 779 billion in 1Q 2026 (down 2.1%), then rose to KRW 864.1 billion in 2Q (up 10.9%) and KRW 887 billion in 3Q (up 2.6% quarter on quarter and 11.0% year on year), a two-quarter run of gains.

Operating profit rose for three consecutive quarters, reaching KRW 19.8 billion in 1Q 2026, KRW 29.8 billion in 2Q and KRW 33.7 billion in 3Q. Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders turned positive at KRW 1.95 billion in 2Q 2026 but returned to a net loss of KRW 6.03 billion in 3Q.

The last four quarters total a net loss of KRW 14.9 billion. Per an October 2026 report, the global segment absorbed higher fixed costs from new fulfillment centers, while Americas, Hong Kong and Vietnam subsidiaries supported profit.

Total liabilities were KRW 2.79 trillion and total equity was KRW 1.52 trillion at year-end 2025, and the debt-to-equity ratio rose from 166.8% in 2022 to 183.4% in 2025. Operating cash flow rose from KRW 182.4 billion in 2022 to KRW 304.4 billion in 2024 before dropping to KRW 243.8 billion in 2025.

05

Industry analysis

A 2026 October report described domestic parcel delivery as saturated, with intensifying competition that makes margin improvement difficult. Within that structure, overseas subsidiaries have partly offset weakness in parcel delivery.

On the demand side, the company says overseas demand for Korean beauty products and industrial goods is rising.

For the 4Q global shopping season, the company expects inbound volumes of K-beauty brands in local warehouses to increase, and it projects that sales volumes during peak periods could rise two to three times the normal level.

Port handling depends on cargo volumes at Incheon and Pyeongtaek, which was one of the main drivers of 3Q results. Energy logistics is positioned by the company as a growth axis tied to higher power demand from AI infrastructure.

Government programs supporting small exporters' overseas logistics are also translating into volume. Among peers, CJ Logistics and Logen compete directly in parcel delivery, and pricing in that market determines the margin of parcel operations. In global logistics, expanding fulfillment networks is the company's route to scale.

06

Outlook

At its 3Q 2026 earnings release, the company said it expects annual revenue to reach a record in 4Q as the global shopping season builds. The existing annual record is KRW 3.06 trillion in 2025, so this expectation concerns whether 2026 full-year revenue will exceed that level. Overseas capacity continues to expand.

The Los Angeles No. 2 fulfillment center was enlarged in February 2026 to about 9,500 square meters, roughly double its previous size.

In Europe, an Amsterdam fulfillment center opened in December 2025 and a Southampton site in Hampshire was added in April 2026, and the company plans to link the two sites into a pan-European network. The one-click fulfillment service for small merchants launched in January 2026.

The small merchant support program and the online export logistics program both run through December 2026, so whether volumes hold after those programs end is a checkpoint. Energy logistics is expanding around secondary battery equipment transport and power equipment logistics.

On the other side, fixed costs from new fulfillment centers could weigh on margins if overseas volumes fall short, and it may take time for new sites to reach their full profitability. With annual operating margins still in the 3% to 4% range, whether revenue growth converts into margin gains is the key test.

The next confirmation points are the 4Q 2026 results and the 2026 annual report, where operating margin and any return to positive net profit attributable to controlling shareholders will be visible.

Bull factors

Consecutive quarterly revenue gains

Revenue bottomed at KRW 779 billion in 1Q 2026 and rose to KRW 864.1 billion in 2Q and KRW 887 billion in 3Q, a two-quarter run of gains. Third-quarter revenue was up 11.0% year on year, and the company said it was the highest quarterly revenue on record. The company expects the 4Q global shopping season to support a record full-year figure.

Global segment growth and network expansion

According to an October 2026 report, global segment revenue rose 31.1% year on year in the first half of 2026. The Los Angeles No. 2 center was enlarged and sites in the Netherlands and the UK are being linked into a European network. The company says overseas subsidiaries' results have offset the fixed costs of new sites.

Recovery trend in operating profit

Operating profit rose from KRW 17.1 billion in 4Q 2025 to KRW 19.8 billion in 1Q 2026, KRW 29.8 billion in 2Q and KRW 33.7 billion in 3Q, a three-quarter run of gains. The operating margin improved to 3.7% in 2025 from 3.3% in 2024. The recovery is visible first at the operating line.

09

Bear factors

Persistent net losses for controlling shareholders

Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders recorded a loss of KRW 4.28 billion in 2024 and KRW 1.55 billion in 2025, two straight years of losses. The 3Q 2026 loss widened to KRW 6.03 billion, and the last four quarters total KRW 14.9 billion in losses. Operating improvement has not yet carried through to earnings attributable to controlling shareholders.

Thin margins and high leverage

The operating margin stayed between 3% and 4%, at 4.0% in 2022, 4.4% in 2023, 3.3% in 2024 and 3.7% in 2025. The debt-to-equity ratio rose to 183.4% in 2025 from 166.8% in 2022. Margin and balance sheet improvement has lagged the growth in revenue.

Profit pressure in parcel delivery

An October 2026 report pointed to saturation and fierce competition in domestic parcel delivery, which makes margin recovery difficult. Weakness in parcel is partly offset by overseas subsidiaries, which makes the overall profile more reliant on global results. Continued price competition at home could keep margin pressure in place.

10

Risk factors

Domestic parcel competition and pricing

Domestic parcel delivery is saturated, so price competition to defend share could persist. Weaker parcel profitability would pull down the company's total operating profit. Whether port handling and global growth can offset this is the key question.

Overseas fixed costs and volume dependence

New fulfillment centers have added fixed costs, and global profitability relies heavily on overseas subsidiaries. If fourth-quarter shopping season volumes fall short, the fixed-cost burden could weigh on margins. Swings in overseas demand could add to earnings volatility.

Balance sheet and earnings volatility

Total liabilities at year-end 2025 were KRW 2.79 trillion, well above total equity of KRW 1.52 trillion. With the debt-to-equity ratio at 183.4% and net losses continuing, financial burden could increase.

Quarterly profit attributable to controlling shareholders has moved between profit and loss, so the earnings trend is not yet stable.

Overall view

HANJIN Logistics posted KRW 887 billion of revenue in 3Q 2026, a level the company described as a quarterly record, and operating profit has risen for three quarters since the 4Q 2025 low. The global segment grew 31.1% in the first half of 2026, showing that overseas network expansion is translating into results.

On the other side, net profit attributable to controlling shareholders recorded losses in 2024 and 2025 and again in 3Q 2026, and the last four quarters total KRW 14.9 billion in losses. The operating margin has stayed between 3% and 4%, while the debt-to-equity ratio rose to 183.4% in 2025.

The next confirmation points are the 4Q 2026 preliminary results and the operating margin and net profit trend in the 2026 annual report.
