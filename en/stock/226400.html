02

Business structure

Osteonic is an orthopedic implant specialist founded in 2012 that listed on KOSDAQ in 2018. Its product lines span four areas: Sports Medicine (joints and ligaments), Trauma (upper and lower extremity fractures), CMF (craniomaxillofacial), and Spine implants.

By material, Trauma products account for the largest share within the metal segment, while Sports Medicine leads growth within the bio-material segment.

Technologically, orthopedic implant materials are classified into first-generation metal, second-generation biodegradable polymer, and third-generation biodegradable composite, and the company holds proprietary technology in the high-barrier third-generation composite category.

Its core customers are Zimmer Biomet, the world's second-largest orthopedic device maker, and Germany's B. Braun, and it has held an exclusive OEM/ODM supply agreement with Zimmer Biomet since 2020. Under this agreement, U.S.-bound Sports Medicine shipments ramped up from May 2025, lifting the export mix.

For CMF, the company signed a cranial fixation device agreement with B. Braun Korea in 2017 and an exclusive neuro-system agreement with B. Braun's German headquarters in 2018, securing global distribution.

In the first half of 2026, segment revenue stood at KRW 10.1 billion for Sports Medicine, KRW 8.1 billion for Trauma, KRW 6.1 billion for CMF, and KRW 900 million for Spine, with CMF up 7.4%, Trauma up 13%, and Spine up 58.4% year-on-year, while Sports Medicine continued its high growth at 52.2%.

Competitively, the heavier reliance on OEM/ODM supply to major global device makers rather than its own branded sales means quality, certification, and delivery capability matter more than brand-to-brand competition.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual revenue rose from KRW 19.8 billion in 2022 to KRW 27.8 billion in 2023 (+40.1% year-on-year), KRW 34.2 billion in 2024 (+23.2%), and KRW 45.4 billion in 2025 (+32.6%), marking three consecutive years of growth.

Operating profit likewise climbed from KRW 2.53 billion to KRW 4.84 billion (+91.7%), KRW 6.89 billion (+42.2%), and KRW 8.86 billion (+28.7%) over the same period, with the operating margin improving from 12.7% to 19.5%.

Controlling-interest net income grew from KRW 836 million to KRW 7.5 billion across three straight years, though the 2025 growth rate (32.7%) moderated from 2023 (337.8%) and 2024 (54.5%). The company described its 2025 results as the best in its history.

On a quarterly basis, revenue rose each quarter from KRW 11 billion in Q2 2025 to KRW 13.2 billion in Q2 2026 (Q1 2026 alone dipped 0.8% quarter-on-quarter), while controlling-interest net income peaked at KRW 3.74 billion in Q4 2025 before falling for two straight quarters to KRW 2.66 billion in Q1 2026 (-28.9%) and KRW 1.82 billion in Q2 2026 (-31.4%).

The trailing four-quarter sum of controlling-interest net income (Q3 2025 through Q2 2026) totaled KRW 10.2 billion. In Q1 2026, the Sports Medicine segment more than doubled year-on-year to KRW 5.6 billion in sales, with domestic sales up 18% while exports surged 729%, driving overall growth.

Q2 2026 operating profit rose 4.0% quarter-on-quarter but fell 11.9% year-on-year, which analysts attributed to one-time costs tied to new product development and European certification along with a timing gap in shipping higher-margin volumes.

As of year-end 2025, the debt-to-equity ratio stood at 48.6%, total equity reached KRW 67.6 billion (of which controlling interest was KRW 67.5 billion), and operating cash flow surged to KRW 9.02 billion from the prior year, pointing to a clear improvement in earnings quality as well.

05

Industry analysis

The orthopedic implant industry's structural growth driver is rising demand for devices addressing body-function impairments amid an aging population, alongside the growth of specialized hospitals.

The spine implant segment, estimated at a global market size of USD 7.5 billion (about KRW 11 trillion) as of 2021, with the domestic market at roughly KRW 100 billion, remains a relatively later entry point for Osteonic compared with Sports Medicine, Trauma, and CMF.

Competitively, large global device makers such as Zimmer Biomet, Stryker, and Medtronic dominate finished-product distribution and branding, while small and mid-sized manufacturers compete through OEM/ODM supply backed by production capability and certification.

Osteonic occupies a position as Zimmer Biomet's exclusive supply partner, benefiting from volume expansion without bearing finished-brand distribution risk. The United States, the single largest device market globally, makes FDA approval a key determinant of each product line's pace of overseas entry.

The company has completed its U.S. market entry portfolio across Sports Medicine, Trauma and Extremities, and now Spine product lines. Still, tariff and trade-policy shifts and certification costs tied to regulations such as the EU's CE MDR remain common variables across the industry.

06

Outlook

SK Securities forecast in its August 2026 report that Osteonic's 2026 revenue and operating profit would reach KRW 57.5 billion and KRW 12.1 billion respectively, rising to KRW 74.4 billion and KRW 17.4 billion in 2027 on expanded Zimmer-bound product lineups, new Trauma and CMF customer wins, and the effects of automated factory conversion.

In its August 2026 report, SK Securities maintained a Buy rating with a target price of KRW 10,300.

On new products, the SuturePacer, which received domestic regulatory approval, is expected to gain FDA approval by year-end and begin export through Zimmer's distribution network, while a new collagen patch product is also under development.

In Trauma, the company is discussing ODM supply agreements with multiple global device makers, while in CMF it is maintaining its existing B. Braun distribution channel while pursuing new sales channels. If these developments materialize, the brokerage expects all product lines to enter a phase of export expansion.

SK Securities also analyzed that Zimmer-bound volume delayed from Q2 2026 would be reflected in sales from Q3, leading to a recovery in profitability. These remain brokerage projections, however, pending confirmation through the Q3 regulatory filing.

08

Bull factors

Expanding Zimmer-linked export structure

Building on the exclusive Zimmer Biomet OEM/ODM agreement signed in 2020, U.S.-bound Sports Medicine shipments ramped up from May 2025. Sports Medicine exports surged 729% year-on-year in Q1 2026, and the segment's overall H1 revenue grew 52.2%.

SK Securities analyzed that Zimmer-bound supply items would expand from anchors to surgical tools and biomaterials from the second half, driving structural revenue growth.

Diversifying product portfolio

Beyond Sports Medicine, Trauma, and CMF, the company has also built a U.S. market entry base for Spine implants. Spine revenue rose from KRW 370 million in 2024 to KRW 1.19 billion in 2025, and the recent FDA approval of the ASTER-ACP Plate and Screw System opened further overseas supply opportunities.

New products such as the SuturePacer and a collagen patch are also moving through development and certification stages, extending the product lineup.

Improving cash flow and financial soundness

Operating cash flow jumped to KRW 9.02 billion in 2025 from KRW 4.72 billion the prior year, indicating faster cash conversion of profit. The debt-to-equity ratio eased gradually from 68.6% in 2022 to 48.6% in 2025.

SK Securities projected that automated factory conversion effects would further contribute to medium-term profitability improvement.

Bear factors

Quarterly profit softening in 2026

Controlling-interest net income peaked at KRW 3.74 billion in Q4 2025 before falling 28.9% and 31.4% in Q1 and Q2 2026, respectively, marking two consecutive quarterly declines. Q2 2026 operating profit also fell 11.9% year-on-year.

SK Securities attributed this to a combination of new product development costs, one-time European certification expenses, and delayed shipment of higher-margin volume.

Customer concentration risk

A significant portion of Sports Medicine export growth depends on OEM/ODM volume to the single customer Zimmer Biomet, and the CMF segment also relies heavily on the B. Braun distribution channel.

The Q2 2026 results showed that order policies or inventory adjustments at a specific customer can shift quarterly shipment timing. Whether new ODM agreements materialize in the Trauma segment remains key to customer diversification.

Dependence on meeting brokerage estimates

SK Securities' 2026 forecast of KRW 57.5 billion in revenue and KRW 12.1 billion in operating profit assumes that delayed Zimmer-bound volume will be reflected in the second half and that new customers will be secured.

The gap between H1 2026 cumulative results and the full-year forecast can only be assessed once second-half results are reported. Since the target price is also premised on achieving these results, ongoing comparison against actual quarterly performance is warranted.

10

Risk factors

Customer concentration

The revenue structure is heavily dependent on two global customers, Zimmer Biomet and B. Braun. Changes in these customers' order policies, inventory adjustments, or attempts to secure alternative suppliers could directly affect revenue. The Q2 2026 delay in Zimmer-bound shipments illustrated this structural risk.

Regulatory and approval risk

Overseas expansion of new products requires approval from multiple regulators including the U.S. FDA, the EU's CE MDR framework, and Korea's Ministry of Food and Drug Safety. If the year-end FDA approval timeline for the SuturePacer or similar products slips, related revenue recognition could also be delayed. Costs related to CE MDR certification were already reflected as a one-time expense in Q2 2026 results.

Trade and foreign exchange

The company has stated that the impact of U.S. tariffs has so far been limited, but trade policy remains a variable subject to future change. As the export mix grows, fluctuations in the won-dollar exchange rate could have a larger effect on revenue and profitability.

Given the sales structure relies on overseas distributors, ongoing monitoring of exchange rate and tariff changes is warranted.

Overall view

Osteonic is an orthopedic implant maker that grew both its top line and profitability across three consecutive years from 2022 through 2025, with revenue, operating profit, and controlling-interest net income all rising each year.

At the center of this growth has been expanding OEM/ODM supply to Zimmer Biomet, with U.S.-bound Sports Medicine exports that ramped up from May 2025 serving as the key driver.

However, since peaking in Q4 2025, controlling-interest net income has declined for two consecutive quarters in Q1 and Q2 2026, with new product development costs, certification expenses, and shipment timing differences emerging as clear quarterly swing factors.

FDA approval of Spine implants and the expanding new-product lineup including the SuturePacer and a collagen patch are viewed as positives for medium-term product diversification, though their actual revenue contribution has yet to be confirmed.

Brokerages project a profitability recovery from deferred-volume reflection in the second half along with revenue growth into 2026-2027, but these remain estimates pending confirmation through actual quarterly filings. Given the structural reliance on two customers, Zimmer Biomet and B.

Braun, the progress of customer diversification and new certification timelines will be worth watching in coming quarters.
