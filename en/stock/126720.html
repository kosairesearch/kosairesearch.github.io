Nuclear Maintenance Demand Holds; Q3 Swing to Loss Is the Watch Point

02

Business structure

Soosan Industries, founded in 1983, provides integrated maintenance for power plants, aiming to raise the utilization of nuclear, thermal and renewable generation assets and to prevent failures.

Based on the first half of 2024, the revenue mix was 53% nuclear, 30% thermal and 17% renewables and other, making nuclear maintenance the core of earnings.

The nuclear maintenance market was long dominated by KEPCO's maintenance affiliate, and after opening to private players, a 2024 brokerage analysis estimated a 70% share for that affiliate and 15% for Soosan Industries, the largest private participant.

Analysts have also noted that few private companies handle major plant components such as reactors and turbines.

Overseas, the company won a sole turbine overhaul contract for four units at the Barakah Nuclear Power Plant (BNPP) in the UAE, and management said expansion of this overseas business drove 2025 revenue growth.

Its high-value precision maintenance for nuclear plants and revenue from the Omisan wind farm also contributed to 2025 results. The subsidiary handling nuclear instrumentation and control equipment is pursuing small modular reactor (SMR) work as well as defense and aerospace entry.

A second subsidiary operates overseas solar power projects. In its 2026 corporate value-up plan, the company proposed expanding its scope of work from the UAE base and marketing in Eastern Europe, Africa and Southeast Asia. Its thermal business is extending into combined-cycle and cogeneration plant maintenance.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual revenue went from KRW 303.5 billion in 2022 to KRW 324.7 billion in 2023 (up 7.0%) and KRW 316.8 billion in 2024 (down 2.4%), before reaching KRW 337.7 billion in 2025 (up 6.6%), the largest year of 2022 to 2025.

Operating profit moved from KRW 50.6 billion in 2022 to KRW 50.2 billion in 2023, KRW 43.5 billion in 2024 and KRW 43.1 billion in 2025, a decline for three consecutive years from 2023. Operating margin fell over the same period from 16.7% to 15.5%, 13.7% and 12.8%, so margins compressed even as revenue grew.

The company attributed the 2025 operating profit decline to higher headcount and expenses from overseas expansion. Net income attributable to owners recovered from KRW 39.3 billion in 2024 (down 19.4%) to KRW 48.9 billion in 2025 (up 24.4%), and the company cited higher gains on financial assets as a factor.

Because that gain sits outside operations, the 2025 net profit should be read separately from operating profit. Net income including non-controlling interests was KRW 48.8 billion in 2025.

Operating cash flow fell from KRW 67.4 billion in 2024 to KRW 11.2 billion in 2025, widening the gap between reported profit and cash generation.

On a quarterly basis, Q3 2025 revenue was KRW 81.1 billion with operating profit of KRW 10.1 billion; Q4 2025 revenue rose 12.4% sequentially to KRW 91.2 billion and operating profit to KRW 12.2 billion. Q1 2026 revenue was KRW 85.7 billion with operating profit of KRW 13.6 billion, up 11.9% quarter on quarter.

Q2 2026 revenue rose to KRW 92.6 billion, but operating profit fell 57.0% to KRW 5.86 billion. Q3 2026 revenue was KRW 84.1 billion, up 3.7% year on year, while operating profit of KRW 8.86 billion declined 12.3% and the quarter recorded a net loss attributable to owners of KRW 6.4 billion, a swing into loss.

Per the company's announcement, nine-month 2026 revenue was KRW 262.4 billion (up 6.4%) and operating profit was KRW 28.3 billion (down 8.3%).

The company cited headcount and outsourcing costs at the Barakah nondestructive testing business and higher R&D at its defense and aerospace subsidiary as drags on operating profit. Net income attributable to owners totaled KRW 56.4 billion over the last four quarters, from Q4 2025 to Q3 2026.

05

Industry analysis

Nuclear plants undergo periodic planned overhauls, so maintenance volume rises and falls quarter to quarter with the number of units in service and the overhaul cycle. The company has said its quarterly results move with the timing and scope of plant maintenance.

A 2026 brokerage view holds that the 11th Basic Plan for Electricity Supply and Demand is pushing new reactor construction and SMR commercialization, while the policy of extending existing reactors' operating lives is lifting maintenance demand.

In its first-quarter 2026 results, the company said rising focus on energy security had expanded demand for nuclear maintenance. Analysts also expect nuclear maintenance volume to grow as AI and semiconductor expansion drives sharp increases in electricity demand.

On competition, a 2024 analysis estimated the incumbent utility-affiliated provider at about 70% of the nuclear maintenance market after the opening to private players, and Soosan Industries at about 15%, the top private participant.

Another analysis describes the company as the only private firm handling major plant components such as reactors and turbines. For overseas markets, a 2024 analysis argued that few sizable maintenance specialists exist to serve Korean reactor exports, which could strengthen the benefit to Soosan Industries.

These views come from the company and brokerages, so whether actual maintenance volume and contract awards support them is the key question.

06

Outlook

In March 2026 the company said it expected revenue to keep rising on contracts already secured and that high-value precision maintenance would support profitability.

Its corporate value-up plan, decided on March 31, 2026, proposed expanding the scope of work from its Barakah base in the UAE, entering high-value maintenance fields such as SMR, aerospace and defense, and pursuing M&A alongside.

For the fourth quarter, the company expected overhaul work at the Egbin power plant in Nigeria and PLC equipment deliveries for Shin-Hanul units 3 and 4 and the ARA research reactor at the Korea Atomic Energy Research Institute to lift results.

Market commentary expects nuclear maintenance demand to grow in 2027 as Saeul units 3 and 4 begin operation. Its nuclear I&C subsidiary is advancing i-SMR development step by step, and both defense equipment software and rocket engine combustion test facilities for space launch vehicles are in progress.

On shareholder returns, the company recorded a 25.9% dividend payout ratio for 2025, raised cash dividends and said it would continue shareholder-friendly policies.

The key question is whether the early headcount and outsourcing costs at Barakah, which weighed on Q3 2026 operating profit, are absorbed by higher revenue from Q4 onward.

Because the company has released fourth-quarter results alongside annual results before, the 2026 fourth-quarter figures can be checked around March 2027.

Declining operating margin

Gap between net income and cash

The company said the 2025 rise in net income attributable to owners reflected higher gains on financial assets. Operating cash flow dropped from KRW 67.4 billion in 2024 to KRW 11.2 billion in 2025.

Risk factors

Nuclear policy and overhaul timing

Nuclear maintenance volume depends on government nuclear policy, the number of units in operation and the overhaul schedule. Changes to life-extension or new-build plans could shift demand expectations. When overhauls cluster in particular quarters, swings in quarterly revenue and operating profit widen.

Early-stage costs in overseas work

The company cited early headcount and higher outsourcing costs at the Barakah nondestructive testing business as drags on Q3 2026 operating profit. Overseas work can raise staffing and expenses before revenue catches up, which may keep margins under pressure.

Whether the margin decline is a temporary expansion cost or a structural change will become clearer in the next periodic reports.

New-business investment and M&A

SMR, defense and aerospace entry, along with active M&A, appear in the 2026 corporate value-up plan. New businesses typically raise R&D spending first, which can pressure short-term profit. In Q3 2026, higher R&D at the defense and aerospace subsidiary was cited as a factor behind lower operating profit.

Overall view

Soosan Industries' 2022 to 2025 reported results combine revenue growth with a shrinking operating profit. Revenue peaked at KRW 337.7 billion in 2025, yet operating profit of KRW 43.1 billion has declined for three straight years from 2023, and operating margin fell from 16.7% in 2022 to 12.8% in 2025.

The company said gains on financial assets were a factor behind the rise in 2025 net income attributable to owners to KRW 48.9 billion. Q1 2026 operating profit of KRW 13.6 billion was the highest of the last five reported quarters, but Q3 2026 swung to a net loss attributable to owners of KRW 6.4 billion.

Per the company's announcement, nine-month 2026 revenue of KRW 262.4 billion rose 6.4%, driven by larger nuclear overhaul volume, while nine-month operating profit of KRW 28.3 billion fell 8.3%.

Positives include nuclear maintenance demand, the UAE contract, a higher dividend and the outlook for 2027 maintenance demand from new units.

Negatives include the lower operating margin, quarterly volatility, net profit that leans on financial-asset gains alongside weaker operating cash flow, and early costs in overseas work. The next checkpoints are the Q4 2026 results, the 2026 annual report and the Q1 2027 results.
