02

Business structure

Phionx was founded in 1978, listed on KOSDAQ in 2010, and changed its corporate name from Ameridge Corporation in 2026. The business has two segments, apparel and cannabis.

The apparel segment is a wholesale distribution business for men's and women's clothing in the United States, supplying about 20 wholesalers and retailers there. The cannabis segment grows, extracts, manufactures, distributes and sells cannabis for medical and recreational use.

In the first half of 2026, press reports put apparel at about 72.6 percent of sales and cannabis at about 27.4 percent. Neither segment was reported to be profitable, and apparel accounted for much of the operating loss. The apparel business is sensitive to oil prices, logistics costs and tariff policy.

Press reports from the second half of 2026 said the company wants to grow biotech and aviation businesses, but the supply items, volumes and contract values have not been specified. Annual sales were KRW 5.99 billion in 2025 (KRW-converted), so the scale is small.

The exchange classifies Phionx under automotive, but its operating businesses are apparel and cannabis, so automotive-sector peers are not direct operating comparables.

Earnings analysis

Filings are in US dollars, and the amounts in this section are KRW-converted values. Annual sales fell from KRW 68.7 billion in 2022 to KRW 39.6 billion in 2023 (down 42.4% year on year), KRW 18.7 billion in 2024 (down 52.7%) and KRW 5.99 billion in 2025 (down 68.0%), declining for three straight years from 2023.

Among 2022 to 2025, 2022 was the largest year and 2025 the smallest. Operating income of KRW 1.31 billion in 2022 turned into an operating loss of KRW 10.1 billion in 2023, widened to KRW 11.3 billion in 2024, and narrowed to KRW 7.9 billion in 2025.

The operating margin moved from 1.9% in 2022 to -25.6% in 2023, -60.3% in 2024 and -131.8% in 2025.

The 2025 operating loss narrowed even though sales fell sharply in both 2024 and 2025, which may reflect cost adjustments, but the breakdown of cost of sales and selling and administrative expenses needs to be checked in the periodic report.

The net loss attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 36.6 billion in 2023, KRW 15.8 billion in 2024 and KRW 11.2 billion in 2025, and the net loss including non-controlling interests was reported at the same amounts.

Operating cash flow went from a KRW 4.34 billion inflow in 2022 to outflows of KRW 3.58 billion in 2023, KRW 8.94 billion in 2024 and KRW 4.75 billion in 2025.

Quarterly sales were KRW 1.00 billion in the third quarter of 2025, KRW 895 million in the fourth quarter, KRW 1.23 billion in the first quarter of 2026, KRW 2.48 billion in the second quarter and KRW 1.82 billion in the third quarter of 2026.

Third-quarter 2026 sales rose 82.3% from a year earlier but fell 26.4% from the prior quarter. The operating loss narrowed to KRW 1.48 billion from KRW 1.88 billion a year earlier, while the net loss attributable to controlling shareholders widened to KRW 5.54 billion from KRW 2.2 billion.

The gap between the operating loss and the larger net loss points to non-operating items, whose details should be checked in the next annual report.

Industry analysis

The exchange classifies Phionx under automotive parts and interior sales, but its actual business is wholesale apparel distribution and cannabis distribution in the United States. The relevant end markets are therefore US apparel consumption and cannabis sales rather than automotive parts demand.

According to press reports and the company profile, the apparel segment is sensitive to oil prices, logistics costs and tariff policy, so margins depend on external variables. The sharp sales decline since 2023 may reflect both shrinking customer volumes and this cost environment.

The data here do not provide enough market-size or growth figures to place the industry in its cycle. The cannabis segment depends on sales channels and the regulatory environment. Within its competitive field, Phionx is a small operator, so its ability to pass on costs and retain customers will determine any recovery.

Automotive-sector peers such as Hyundai Motor, KIA, Hyundai Mobis and Hankook Tire & Technology differ in end markets and customer structure, so direct comparison is difficult. Against large automotive parts makers, Phionx's business profile is closer to apparel distribution.

The raise is modest against total equity of KRW 33.7 billion at the end of 2025, so it is not by itself enough for a full turnaround.

Quarterly sales rebound and narrower operating loss

Second-quarter 2026 sales were KRW 2.48 billion, up 101.1% from the prior quarter, and third-quarter 2026 sales of KRW 1.82 billion rose 82.3% from a year earlier. The third-quarter operating loss of KRW 1.48 billion was smaller than the KRW 1.88 billion a year earlier.

On an annual basis, the operating loss narrowed from KRW 11.3 billion in 2024 to KRW 7.9 billion in 2025. Sales fell from the prior quarter in the third quarter of 2026, so the rebound is not yet a confirmed trend.

Balance-sheet capacity and modest leverage

At the end of 2025, total liabilities were KRW 7.43 billion and total equity was KRW 33.7 billion, with a debt-to-equity ratio of 22.0%. The ratio rose from 13.0% in 2022 to 22.3% in 2024, but the borrowing burden is not large. Total equity remains well above total liabilities, which can be read as a financial cushion.

However, while operating losses and cash outflows continue, that cushion is gradually being used up, so it is hard to rely on it as a long-term basis.

Risk factors

Listing maintenance (market-capitalization threshold)

The market-capitalization shortfall that triggered the administrative designation applies a KRW 20 billion threshold through December 31, 2026 and KRW 30 billion from January 1, 2027.

To lift the designation, the company must stay above the threshold for at least 45 trading days within 90 trading days after designation. Even if new shares raise the capital base, market capitalization moves with the share price, so compliance may change.

According to the exchange, if the grounds for lifting become impossible to meet, the situation falls under delisting criteria.

Governance and funding

The change of largest shareholder depends on completing the subscription of new shares in the third-party allotment, and the process is still under way. The public offering only paid in 5.9% of the planned amount, so actual funding fell well short of the plan.

Treatment of unissued shares and any additional raise will be determined by later disclosures. If funding is delayed or shrinks, both new-business spending and operating funding plans may need to be adjusted.

New-business execution (biotech and aviation)

Press reports say Phionx has a strategic vendor agreement tied to its biotech and aviation plans, but the supply items, volumes and contract values have not been specified. The amount of raised capital to be allocated to new businesses and the business plan have also not been disclosed.

If the new businesses do not generate revenue, they will be hard to offset the current sales decline. The timing for judging results will depend on when the new management presents its business strategy.
