Cost Cuts Lift Profit, New-Drug Pipeline Still Thin

02

Business structure

Founded in 1897, Dongwha Pharm is Korea's longest-running pharmaceutical company, with a diversified business spanning over-the-counter (OTC) drugs, prescription drugs (ETC), medical devices, lifestyle health products, and overseas distribution.

Its core cash cows are long-standing OTC brands including the liquid digestive Hwalmyungsu line, the cold remedy Pancold line, the antifungal Itch line, and the wound-care Fusidin line, which generated KRW 21.7 billion, KRW 15.8 billion, KRW 11.6 billion and KRW 5.5 billion in sales respectively in Q1 2026.

New lifestyle-health brands such as 'Qulip' have also been growing and contributing to portfolio diversification. The medical device segment is run through subsidiary Medyssey, which makes spinal implants and surgical instruments and posted roughly KRW 8.5 billion in sales in Q2 2026.

Overseas operations are centered on Vietnamese pharmacy chain Trung Son Pharma (TS Care), acquired in 2023, which reported KRW 17.7 billion in sales in Q1 2026, though revenue declined and net losses persisted on a first-half basis, leaving profitability challenges unresolved.

In 2026 the company added a new rental-income segment after moving into its new Sunhwa-dong headquarters in central Seoul, generating KRW 1.5 billion in sales and KRW 0.9 billion in operating profit in the first half, a high-margin cash source with a 59.0% operating margin.

Within the industry, Dongwha Pharm competes with OTC and healthcare-focused peers such as Dongkook Pharmaceutical, leaning on an OTC-centric revenue mix that is relatively insulated from prescription-drug price cuts.

Its new-drug R&D is concentrated on a small number of pipeline candidates, including an improved diabetes drug and a GERD generic, leaving new drugs a limited contributor to medium- to long-term growth.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual revenue rose from KRW 340.4 billion in 2022 to KRW 361.1 billion in 2023 (up 6.1% year on year), KRW 464.9 billion in 2024 (up 28.7%), and KRW 496.4 billion in 2025 (up 6.8%), marking three consecutive years of growth from 2023 through 2025.

Operating profit, however, peaked at KRW 29.9 billion in 2022 before falling for three straight years to KRW 18.8 billion in 2023 (down 37.2%), KRW 13.4 billion in 2024 (down 28.6%), and just KRW 257 million in 2025 (down 98.1%), as the operating margin sank from 8.8% to 0.1%.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders peaked at KRW 27.4 billion in 2023 before plunging to KRW 5.56 billion in 2024 (down 79.7%) and then rebounding to KRW 8.69 billion in 2025 (up 56.1%).

On a quarterly basis, revenue bottomed at KRW 122 billion in Q3 2025 before rising for three consecutive quarters to KRW 123.7 billion in Q4 2025, KRW 130.6 billion in Q1 2026, and KRW 136.9 billion in Q2 2026.

Operating profit swung to an operating loss of KRW 4 billion in Q4 2025, then turned profitable with KRW 11.2 billion in Q1 2026, and came in at KRW 10.3 billion in Q2 2026, down 8.4% quarter on quarter but up 1,575.5% year on year.

The Q1 2026 improvement was underpinned by organizational efficiency measures that cut both SG&A expenses (KRW 43.2 billion, down 7.7% year on year) and R&D spending (KRW 5.4 billion, down 8.1%).

On a cumulative first-half 2026 basis, cost control was even more pronounced, with advertising expenses falling 39.6% from KRW 16.3 billion to KRW 9.8 billion and sales promotion expenses dropping 82.9% from KRW 2.8 billion to roughly KRW 0.5 billion.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders over the most recent four quarters (Q3 2025 through Q2 2026) totaled KRW 18.1 billion, recovering to a level above the full-year 2024 figure of KRW 5.56 billion. Operating cash flow swung from an outflow of KRW 4 billion in 2024 to a net inflow of KRW 15.7 billion in 2025.

05

Industry analysis

Unlike prescription drugs, Korea's OTC market is not directly exposed to government price-cut regulation, allowing Dongwha Pharm's long-standing OTC brands such as Hwalmyungsu, Pancold and Fusidin to serve as a stable cash cow.

At the same time, prescription-drug pricing reform is anticipated, making Innovative Pharmaceutical Company certification a variable that could affect corporate value.

Mid-sized OTC- and healthcare-focused drugmakers, including Dongkook Pharmaceutical, are increasingly expanding into overseas beauty and healthcare markets, and Dongwha Pharm's Q2 2026 revenue growth rate of 9.5% was comparable to Dongkook Pharmaceutical's 10.9%.

Overseas, Korean mid-sized pharmaceutical companies are increasingly launching consumer-style products through K-beauty and K-food distribution channels such as Amazon and Olive Young in the United States.

In new-drug development, large pharmaceutical companies and biotechs are focused on building out innovative and oncology pipelines, while Dongwha Pharm and many other mid-sized peers have instead chosen a 'fast commercialization' strategy centered on improved drugs and generics.

The GERD (P-CAB) market is entering a phase of intensifying generic competition following the original branded products, and Dongwha Pharm's DW6017 is attempting to enter this market as well.

Vietnam and other Southeast Asian pharmaceutical distribution markets are seen as difficult arenas for securing profitability given intense competition among local pharmacy chains, and the results of Korean companies' local investments there have been mixed.

06

Outlook

In August 2026 Dongwha Pharm launched the global brand 'K.O.D (Korea Original Digestive),' based on Hwalmyungsu, and began sales through U.S. marts, pharmacies, Olive Young stores and an Amazon brand store starting in September.

The company has indicated that in the early stage it will prioritize gauging consumer reaction and building brand awareness over setting sales targets, so a specific timeline for revenue contribution has not yet been given.

On the new-drug pipeline front, GERD treatment candidate DW6017 filed for domestic marketing approval in August 2026, with the company planning a launch after approval targeting a market estimated at over KRW 700 billion.

The remaining pipeline, including improved diabetes drug candidate DW6014, has completed clinical stages, with further approval procedures and launch timing to be the next items to watch.

Vietnamese subsidiary Trung Son Pharma has refocused its strategy toward profitability rather than store expansion, cutting its store count from 240 to 180 over the past year while seeking profitability gains through greater placement of its own products.

Medical device subsidiary Medyssey is pursuing a transfer listing from KONEX to KOSDAQ, though a specific timeline has not yet been formalized.

Having achieved a profit rebound through cost efficiency in the first and second quarters of 2026, the key question is whether the cost-control stance and the revenue contribution from new brands and overseas operations will continue into the second half and beyond.

08

Bull factors

Profit Rebound Driven by Cost Efficiency

Dongwha Pharm posted operating profit of KRW 11.2 billion in Q1 2026 and KRW 10.3 billion in Q2 2026, sharply higher year on year. Organizational efficiency measures that simultaneously cut SG&A and R&D spending were the direct driver.

The new rental-income business from the Sunhwa-dong headquarters also added high-margin revenue with a 59.0% operating margin, reinforcing profitability. The rise in trailing four-quarter net income attributable to controlling shareholders to KRW 18.1 billion also supports this recovery trend.

Stable Cash Generation from Legacy Brands

Long-running OTC brands such as Hwalmyungsu, Pancold, Itch, and Fusidin have maintained steady market positions, forming a stable revenue base. Unlike prescription drugs, these products are relatively insulated from price-cut risk, which helps lower earnings volatility. New lifestyle-health brands such as 'Qulip' are also contributing to portfolio diversification.

Overseas Expansion and Potential Subsidiary Re-rating

U.S. sales of the Hwalmyungsu-based 'K.O.D' brand began in September 2026, marking an attempt to shift away from a business structure historically heavily reliant on the domestic market.

Medical device subsidiary Medyssey is pursuing a KOSDAQ transfer listing, which, if completed, could allow the market to separately value the subsidiary. However, both initiatives remain at an early stage, with no confirmed revenue contribution or listing timeline yet.

Bear factors

Stalled New-Drug Pipeline

Dongwha Pharm has not produced a clear new-drug success for over a decade since launching the antibiotic 'Zabolante' in 2015. The company's currently disclosed new-drug pipeline is limited to just three candidates, including an improved diabetes drug and a GERD generic.

Its R&D strategy also leans toward 'fast commercialization' centered on health supplements and in-licensed drugs rather than innovative new drugs, leaving questions about long-term growth drivers.

Persistent Losses at Vietnamese Subsidiary

Vietnamese pharmacy chain Trung Son Pharma (TS Care) saw first-half 2026 revenue decline 14.7% year on year to KRW 35.5 billion. Net losses of KRW 3.5 billion continued over the same period.

Despite this, additional capital has been injected, including raising the equity stake from 55.99% to 65.00% following a put-option exercise.

Scars from Profitability Erosion and Balance-Sheet Strain

Operating profit shrank to just KRW 257 million in 2025, pushing the operating margin down to 0.1%, while operating cash flow also saw a temporary outflow of KRW 4 billion in 2024. The debt-to-equity ratio rose steadily from 21.8% in 2022 to 60.0% in 2025.

Q1 2026 financial costs rose 91.3% year on year to KRW 5.3 billion, reflecting a growing borrowing burden tied to expanded overseas investment.

Risk factors

Overseas Business Risk

Operating losses at the Vietnamese subsidiary, currency fluctuations, and the initial marketing and distribution costs of the new U.S. 'K.O.D' brand are all underway simultaneously, leaving the timing of an overall overseas profitability improvement uncertain.

The U.S. market is still at an early stage with no sales target yet disclosed, making it premature to assess a payback timeline. Scheduled cash outlays such as additional stake acquisitions tied to put-option exercises could also add to financial burden.

Regulatory and Drug-Pricing Risk

With prescription-drug pricing reform anticipated, whether the company retains preferential drug-pricing status may hinge on the outcome of Innovative Pharmaceutical Company re-certification.

Its OTC-centric revenue mix is relatively insulated from price cuts, but exposure could grow as the share of prescription and in-licensed drugs increases. Pipeline candidates such as DW6017, currently under regulatory review, could also see their timelines shift depending on the approval outcome.

Financial and Profitability Risk

The debt-to-equity ratio has climbed from 21.8% in 2022 to 60.0% in 2025, reflecting rising reliance on borrowing. Given the precedent of the operating margin falling to 0.1% in 2025, profitability could waver again if the current cost-efficiency effects prove unsustainable.

Rising financial costs tied to expanded overseas investment are also adding to the volatility of net income attributable to controlling shareholders.

12

Overall view

Dongwha Pharm saw revenue grow for three consecutive years from 2023 to 2025, yet operating profit declined for three consecutive years over the same period, reflecting a clear divergence between top-line growth and profitability.

After the operating margin sank to 0.1% in 2025, operating profit rebounded sharply for two straight quarters in early 2026, aided by cuts in SG&A and R&D spending along with new rental income.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders over the trailing four quarters totaled KRW 18.1 billion, surpassing the full-year 2024 figure.

However, the new-drug pipeline remains limited with no clear success since 2015, and challenges such as losses at the Vietnamese subsidiary and a rising debt-to-equity ratio persist.

The U.S.-focused 'K.O.D' brand and the pursuit of a KOSDAQ transfer listing for medical device subsidiary Medyssey could become part of a medium- to long-term growth story, but both remain at an early stage with no confirmed results yet.

Overall, the company appears to be in a phase where short-term profit improvement from cost efficiency coexists with longer-term uncertainty around new drugs and overseas operations.
