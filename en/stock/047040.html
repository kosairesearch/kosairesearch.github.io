Profit Rebound After 2025 Losses, Backlog Near KRW 55 Trillion

Daewoo Engineering & Construction swung to an operating loss of KRW 815.4 billion in 2025, but quarterly operating profit has been positive since the first quarter of 2026.

02

Business structure

Daewoo Engineering & Construction operates four segments: building, civil engineering, plant, and other consolidated subsidiaries.

In the third quarter of 2026, segment revenue was KRW 1.30 trillion for building, KRW 316.1 billion for civil engineering, KRW 187.1 billion for plant, and KRW 39.1 billion for other subsidiaries, with building accounting for about 70% of total revenue.

The building segment centers on housing sales and urban redevelopment, and third-quarter wins included the M5 block of the Cheongna International Business District (KRW 818.1 billion) and the Sinimun Station redevelopment (KRW 529.2 billion).

Urban redevelopment backlog was reported at about KRW 2.36 trillion at the end of the first quarter of 2026, accounting for more than half of domestic building wins. First-quarter 2026 housing supply was 847 units, equal to 4.7% of the annual guidance of 17,957 units.

The company said it will expand energy infrastructure such as nuclear power and LNG and strengthen overseas urban development, data center, and redevelopment orders.

On October 27, 2026, the company said the Papua New Guinea LNG CPF and Mozambique Rovuma LNG orders were imminent, but neither contract has been finalized. The backlog at the end of the third quarter of 2026 was KRW 54.9 trillion, up 8.4% from the end of 2025.

In housing and redevelopment, the company competes with large builders such as Hyundai Engineering & Construction, GS Engineering & Construction, and DL E&C.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual revenue rose from KRW 10.4 trillion in 2022 to KRW 11.6 trillion in 2023, an increase of 11.8% that marked the peak year. It then fell 9.8% to KRW 10.5 trillion in 2024 and 23.3% to KRW 8.05 trillion in 2025, a decline that makes 2025 the smallest year of the 2022 to 2025 period.

Operating profit moved from KRW 760 billion in 2022 to KRW 662.5 billion in 2023 (down 12.8%) and KRW 403.1 billion in 2024 (down 39.2%), before the company recorded an operating loss of KRW 815.4 billion in 2025. The operating margin went from 7.3% to 5.7% to 3.8% and then to -10.1% in 2025.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 504 billion in 2022, KRW 511.7 billion in 2023 (up 1.5%), and KRW 234.1 billion in 2024 (down 54.3%), before a net loss of KRW 912.3 billion attributable to controlling shareholders in 2025.

Net income including non-controlling interests, a separate measure, was a loss of KRW 916.1 billion in 2025.

Quarterly results show an operating loss of KRW 1.11 trillion in the fourth quarter of 2025, after operating profit of KRW 56.6 billion in the third quarter, even though operating profit in the first three quarters of 2025 was KRW 290.1 billion.

The company returned to operating profit of KRW 255.6 billion in the first quarter of 2026, which management attributed to completed sites that started construction during a period of rising costs. Operating profit then fell to KRW 232.2 billion in the second quarter and KRW 183.2 billion in the third quarter.

Securities analysts estimated that roughly KRW 100 billion of one-off gains from estimated cost revisions and completion settlements were included in the third quarter, while a KRW 70 billion allowance for unsold housing units raised selling and administrative costs.

Net losses attributable to controlling shareholders over the last four quarters total KRW 429.5 billion, so cumulative losses remain even after the first-quarter return to profit.

05

Industry analysis

The domestic construction market remains in a downturn. The company said it is pursuing disciplined management and overseas expansion amid the slump, and it cited a reduction in the number of active sites as the reason for lower third-quarter revenue.

This points to a shrinking top line as fewer projects start and progress. On profitability, cost ratios have stabilized. According to securities analysts, the housing segment gross profit margin improved by 12.2 percentage points year on year in the third quarter.

The same analysis said a weaker gross margin in the plant segment limited the overall profit improvement. Unsold housing units are trending lower but remain, and receivables are rising, which keeps near-term earnings uncertainty alive.

In redevelopment, the company has expanded its urban redevelopment team into three units and reportedly expects record-level orders in that business this year. Overseas orders center on technically demanding fields such as nuclear power, LNG, and ports.

Against peers such as Hyundai Engineering & Construction, GS Engineering & Construction, and DL E&C, the key benchmarks are backlog size and the share of housing and redevelopment work.

06

Outlook

The company raised its 2026 new order target from KRW 18 trillion to KRW 27 trillion when it announced first-half results. Cumulative new orders through the third quarter reached KRW 12.5 trillion, up 11.9% year on year.

Cumulative revenue through the third quarter was KRW 5.84 trillion, down 7.9% year on year, and the annual revenue guidance presented with the first-quarter 2026 results was KRW 8 trillion.

The Papua New Guinea LNG CPF and Mozambique Rovuma LNG projects are the main large contracts, and the company said both orders were imminent. Neither contract has been finalized, so whether and when they are signed is the key variable.

With the first-quarter 2026 results, the company also stated it would focus on large projects including the Czech nuclear project, the Gadeok new airport site works, and the Al Faw naval base port project in Iraq.

Securities analysts expect one-off gains to shrink gradually, and said segment gross margins remain in line with the company's expectations even after excluding them. Provisions for unsold units and rising receivables remain sources of short-term earnings uncertainty.

The next checkpoints are therefore whether the large overseas contracts are signed, the operating margin in the provisional fourth-quarter 2026 results, and the year-end backlog reported in the 2026 annual report.

08

Bull factors

Structural margin improvement

Operating profit in the first quarter of 2026 was KRW 255.6 billion, up 68.9% year on year, as completed building sites improved profitability. The cumulative operating margin for the first three quarters of 2026 reached 11.5%, a double-digit level (provisional, announced October 27, 2026). Securities analysts said the housing gross margin improvement continued the trend seen in the first half.

Backlog and visibility

The backlog being larger than at the end of 2025 forms the base of work that will later convert into revenue. The year-on-year increase in new orders is reflected in the higher backlog. About 6.8 years of annual revenue in backlog is cited as a base that can cushion earnings swings.

Large overseas projects

In its first-half announcement, the company raised its new order target from KRW 18 trillion to KRW 27 trillion, saying the prospects for the Papua New Guinea LNG CPF and Mozambique Rovuma LNG orders had become clearer.

Analysts note that converting these projects into contracts would expand new orders and revenue growth. Competitive strength in nuclear power, LNG, and ports underpins this expectation.

09

Bear factors

Continued revenue decline

Revenue has declined since the 2023 peak, continuing through 2025. Third-quarter 2026 revenue was KRW 1.84 trillion, down 7.4% year on year, and cumulative revenue fell 7.9%. The company attributed the decline to fewer active project sites.

Reliance on one-off gains

Securities analysts estimated that third-quarter operating profit included roughly KRW 100 billion of one-off gains from cost revisions and completion settlements. The same analysis pointed to a KRW 70 billion allowance for unsold units that raised selling and administrative costs. The structural margin after excluding one-off items is the next point to check.

Balance sheet burden

The debt-to-equity ratio rose to 284.5% at the end of 2025 from 192.1% a year earlier, and total liabilities were KRW 9.88 trillion. Operating cash flow was negative from 2022 through 2024 before turning to KRW 462.9 billion positive in 2025. Total equity stood at KRW 3.47 trillion.

10

Risk factors

Uncertainty in large overseas orders

The company said the Papua New Guinea LNG CPF and Mozambique Rovuma LNG orders were imminent, but neither contract is finalized. A delay or failure would narrow the path to the KRW 27 trillion new order target. Overseas projects are also exposed to external uncertainty such as geopolitical risk in the Middle East.

Unsold units and plant margins

An allowance of KRW 70 billion for unsold units raised selling and administrative costs, and remaining unsold units plus rising receivables were flagged as near-term earnings uncertainty. A weaker gross margin in the plant segment limited the overall profit improvement. The pace of recovery in these two areas will determine whether margins hold.

Construction slump and shrinking scale

Amid the construction slump and fewer active sites, revenue has declined since 2024. Even with cost ratio gains, continued shrinkage could reduce absolute operating profit. The speed at which backlog converts into revenue, along with the pace of new construction starts, should be tracked together.

12

Overall view

Daewoo Engineering & Construction posted an operating loss of KRW 815.4 billion and a net loss attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 912.3 billion in 2025, but quarterly operating profit has stayed positive in 2026, at KRW 255.6 billion in the first quarter, KRW 232.2 billion in the second, and KRW 183.2 billion in the third.

The cumulative third-quarter operating margin of 11.5% is 6.9 percentage points above the same period a year earlier. Revenue, however, has declined since 2024, and third-quarter operating profit reportedly included one-off gains.

Backlog of KRW 54.9 trillion and cumulative new orders of KRW 12.5 trillion support revenue visibility, but reaching the KRW 27 trillion annual order target requires additional fourth-quarter wins. The debt-to-equity ratio of 284.5% and total equity of KRW 3.47 trillion at the end of 2025 remain a financial burden.

Securities analysts say much of the earnings improvement appears priced into the stock, and whether large overseas LNG contracts are signed is cited as the next variable.

Overall, the margin recovery is confirmed in the figures, while the share of one-off gains and whether orders are finalized will determine how durable it is.
