Stationery Turns Profitable, Group Still in the Red

Monami's stationery business returned to profit in the first half of 2026, but losses at new businesses such as cosmetics kept consolidated operating results in the red.

02

Business structure

Monami makes and sells stationery centered on ballpoint pens, and stationery accounts for most of its revenue. Industry reports put the 2022 year-end revenue mix at 73.4% stationery and 19.4% computer supplies.

Based on 2024 data, its share of the domestic stationery market is 41%, which is cited as the highest in the industry. In the first half of 2026, the stationery business posted revenue of KRW 59.3 billion and an operating profit of KRW 0.7 billion, swinging to a profit.

The computer supplies business, which handles printer consumables, also returned to profit, with revenue of KRW 14.8 billion and operating profit of KRW 0.2 billion.

The other business unit, which includes new businesses such as cosmetics, recorded revenue of KRW 6 billion and an operating loss of KRW 4 billion for the same period, a wider loss than a year earlier.

These segment figures appear to be stated before intra-group eliminations, so they do not add up to consolidated first-half revenue of KRW 61.7 billion. The cosmetics business sits in a subsidiary, and press reports said the subsidiary's 2025 revenue was in the KRW 6 billion range.

Reports also say the cosmetics unit is pushing to expand ODM orders. Overseas revenue in Asia fell 39.6% in the first half of 2025 to KRW 6.1 billion, while Africa rose to KRW 1.9 billion.

Because revenue is concentrated in stationery, the business is directly exposed to the shrinking school-age population and lower demand for pens.

04

Earnings analysis

Consolidated revenue fell from KRW 149.5 billion in 2022 to KRW 141.5 billion in 2023, KRW 133.1 billion in 2024 and KRW 131 billion in 2025, a three-year decline running from 2023 through 2025.

Annual declines were 5.4% in 2023, 5.9% in 2024 and 1.5% in 2025, and 2022 was the largest year and 2025 the smallest in the 2022 to 2025 window.

Operating profit of KRW 6.28 billion in 2022 turned into an operating loss of KRW 2.27 billion in 2023, and the loss widened to KRW 3.81 billion in 2024 and KRW 5.85 billion in 2025. The operating margin fell from 4.2% in 2022 to -1.6% in 2023, -2.9% in 2024 and -4.5% in 2025.

Press reports tied the 2023 swing into the red mainly to losses at the cosmetics subsidiary and a sharp drop in special-order sales.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders moved from KRW 3.14 billion in 2022 to a net loss of KRW 5.84 billion in 2023, narrowed to a net loss of KRW 5.31 billion in 2024, then widened to a net loss of KRW 10.6 billion in 2025.

Net income including non-controlling interests was a net loss of KRW 10.7 billion in 2025, a separate figure from the controlling-shareholder measure.

Operating cash flow went from -KRW 9.5 billion in 2022 to KRW 3.48 billion in 2023, KRW 2.27 billion in 2024 and KRW 1.47 billion in 2025, staying positive but shrinking.

Quarterly operating losses have run for five consecutive quarters from the third quarter of 2025 through the third quarter of 2026, widening to KRW 2.72 billion in the first quarter of 2026 before narrowing to KRW 1.25 billion in the second quarter.

The third-quarter 2026 operating loss of KRW 1.54 billion was wider than the prior quarter but smaller than the KRW 2.14 billion a year earlier, while revenue of KRW 33.2 billion rose 1.9% year on year.

The third-quarter 2026 net loss attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 2.86 billion was wider than a year earlier, and the four quarters from the fourth quarter of 2025 through the third quarter of 2026 total KRW 11.4 billion in such losses.

Press coverage of the first half of 2026 attributed the wider loss mainly to weak new businesses, including cosmetics, even as stationery returned to profit.

05

Industry analysis

Industry commentary points to structural pressure on pen demand from a shrinking school-age population and the spread of smart devices.

Reports describe the domestic stationery and office-supplies market as slowing into low growth as office environments and buying habits change, with low-priced goods from China and other emerging markets intensifying competition.

Monami's revenue peaked at KRW 281.9 billion in 2011, fell to KRW 167.6 billion by 2013, and reached KRW 131 billion in 2025. Its share of the domestic stationery market was 41% in 2024, the highest in the industry, but relying on traditional stationery for more than 70% of revenue is cited as a risk factor.

Overseas, Asian revenue declined in the first half of 2025, which the company attributed to weaker global demand for consumer goods, while Africa and other regions grew on new-market efforts.

The cosmetics business was launched to supplement the pen-centered core, but industry views generally hold that it will take considerable time to offset the weakness in the core business.

In March 2026 the company said it would focus on higher profitability and financial stability through more efficient staffing and reduced loss-making sales channels. Its position combines the top share in stationery with falling revenue.

06

Outlook

Consolidated revenue for the first half of 2026 was KRW 61.7 billion, down 5.6% year on year, and the operating loss widened to KRW 4 billion. The stationery turnaround is a positive signal, but losses at the other business unit keep consolidated operating results in the red.

The cosmetics subsidiary's factory utilization is around 20%, and in a June 2026 report the company set a target of raising it to 50% by the end of 2026. A July 2026 report said the same subsidiary targets annual revenue of KRW 18 billion and a return to profit in the first half of 2028.

Reports describe growing cosmetics ODM orders, and how quickly those orders convert into revenue is the key variable for these targets. Plans to enter markets in the United States, Southeast Asia and Europe were also reported.

Third-quarter 2026 revenue rose year on year, but operating losses continued, so whether revenue recovery and a narrower loss appear together is the item to check. The 2026 annual report, the next periodic filing, will show the direction of full-year operating and controlling-shareholder results.

The first-quarter 2027 report will show whether the stationery profit holds and how large the other unit's losses are. More efficient staffing and fewer loss-making sales channels are the execution tasks the company has named.

08

Bull factors

Stationery turns profitable

The stationery business swung to a first-half 2026 operating profit of KRW 0.7 billion on revenue of KRW 59.3 billion, versus a loss a year earlier. The computer supplies business also returned to profit, with operating profit of KRW 0.2 billion.

A 41% share of the domestic stationery market in 2024 is cited as the foundation of the core business. Whether the profit carries through on a full-year basis in the 2026 annual report is the key item for the bull case.

Cosmetics utilization targets

Reports describe active orders for the cosmetics subsidiary in the first half of 2026, and the company set a target of lifting factory utilization from about 20% to 50% by the end of 2026. Annual revenue of KRW 18 billion and a return to profit in the first half of 2028 are also stated targets.

With 2025 revenue at roughly KRW 6 billion, the gap to these targets is still large. If the targets are met, the loss profile of the other business unit could change.

Equity base and cash flow

Operating cash flow moved from -KRW 9.5 billion in 2022 to positive figures from 2023, reaching KRW 1.47 billion in 2025. At end-2025, equity attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 81 billion and total liabilities were KRW 95.3 billion.

The net asset base attributable to controlling shareholders is cited as a balance-sheet strength. Whether operating cash flow stays positive is the next item to check.

Bear factors

Three years of falling revenue

Consolidated revenue fell for three straight years to KRW 141.5 billion in 2023, KRW 133.1 billion in 2024 and KRW 131 billion in 2025. Revenue of KRW 281.9 billion in 2011 has declined to KRW 131 billion, a long-running trend.

A shrinking school-age population and the spread of smart devices are cited as causes of lower pen demand. With more than 70% of revenue tied to traditional stationery, this trend constrains recovery in the core business.

Five straight loss-making quarters

Operating losses have run for five consecutive quarters from the third quarter of 2025 through the third quarter of 2026. The 2025 operating loss was KRW 5.85 billion and the controlling-shareholder net loss was KRW 10.6 billion, both wider than in 2024.

The four quarters from the fourth quarter of 2025 through the third quarter of 2026 total KRW 11.4 billion in controlling-shareholder net losses. The third-quarter 2026 controlling-shareholder net loss of KRW 2.86 billion was wider than a year earlier.

New-business losses and leverage

The other business unit recorded first-half 2026 revenue of KRW 6 billion and an operating loss of KRW 4 billion, and cosmetics factory utilization remains around 20%. The debt-to-equity ratio rose from 98.3% in 2023 to 117.8% in 2025, and total liabilities at end-2025 were KRW 95.3 billion.

The target of a return to profit in the first half of 2028 is too distant to affect the 2026 earnings structure directly. If new-business losses do not shrink, financial strain could keep building.

10

Risk factors

Demand structure

A shrinking school-age population and smart-device adoption are reducing pen demand, and more than 70% of revenue depends on traditional stationery. Even with the top market share, a shrinking market limits how much revenue can recover.

Third-quarter 2026 revenue rose year on year, but whether that continues across a full year remains to be confirmed.

New-business execution

The cosmetics subsidiary's utilization target of 50% is set against a current level of about 20%, and the timing and size of orders have not been firmly quantified. Asian overseas revenue fell 39.6% in the first half of 2025.

Weakness in new businesses was cited as a driver of the wider first-half 2026 loss, so execution risk is significant.

Financial strain and listing standards

Debt-to-equity stood at 117.8% at end-2025, with total equity of KRW 81 billion. A March 2026 report said market capitalization was below the KRW 50 billion level of the KOSPI delisting standard applying from 2029.

Price swings driven by supply and demand have been large, including a sharp one-day rise in July 2026 on retail buying.

Overall view

Monami's stationery and computer supplies businesses returned to profit in the first half of 2026, but losses at the other business unit kept consolidated operating results in the red.

On the filed figures, revenue fell for three straight years from 2023 through 2025, and operating results have been in deficit since 2023. At end-2025, total equity was KRW 81 billion and the debt-to-equity ratio was 117.8%, while operating cash flow stayed positive.

The bull points are the core-business turnaround, the cosmetics utilization targets and the size of the net asset base. The bear points are the falling revenue trend, five straight loss-making quarters and losses in new businesses.

How much weight each side carries depends on whether full-year operating results in the 2026 annual report turn profitable or the losses narrow. A report that market capitalization was below the 2029 delisting threshold, alongside supply-driven share price swings, is also part of the record.

The next checkpoints are whether the 2026 year-end utilization target is met, the 2026 annual report due by the end of March 2027, and the first-quarter 2027 report in mid-May 2027. This report contains no buy or sell opinion and is provided for information only.
