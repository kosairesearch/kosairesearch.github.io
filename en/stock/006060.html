Footwear ODM Core Business and Shareholder Return Challenges

HS Industries remains heavily tied to adidas footwear ODM orders, swung to a shareholder net loss in the third quarter of 2026, and is pursuing a higher payout target alongside a chemicals portfolio reshuffle.

02

Business structure

HS Industries supplies finished footwear and footwear components to global sports brands under its sports ODM business. According to WiseReport's compilation as of August 31, 2026, the ODM segment accounts for about 87 percent of total revenue, with production bases in Vietnam, Indonesia and China.

Based on Butler's compilation of second-quarter 2026 revenue, the business mix is footwear 87 percent, chemicals 12 percent and other 1 percent.

The chemicals segment has sold footwear adhesives, coatings and PET film as its main products, and a 2025 press report said recovery in footwear had offset weakness in chemicals.

Adidas is the central customer, and a DS Investment Securities report published in March 2025 put adidas at 91.8 percent of the company's standalone sales.

Its Vietnam affiliate Hwaseung Vina receives raw materials from HS Industries, produces finished goods, and sells them back to HS Industries, which then sells them to adidas.

According to an IB Tomato report from 2024, Hwaseung Vina's sales averaged 34.18 percent of consolidated revenue over the three years from 2021 to 2023. The group structure centers on subsidiaries including Hwaseung Enterprise, and the company had 41 consolidated subsidiaries as of August 31, 2026.

According to an IB industry report in June 2026, the company decided to sell the compound business of its Vietnam subsidiary Hwaseung Vietnam to a Chinese petrochemical company for KRW 23 billion.

Press coverage notes that the nature of footwear ODM makes it hard for rivals to take orders, while also pointing to heavy dependence on a single customer.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual revenue went from KRW 1.91 trillion in 2022 to KRW 1.47 trillion in 2023, a 23.3 percent decline, then rose 27.4 percent to KRW 1.87 trillion in 2024 before falling 4.6 percent to KRW 1.78 trillion in 2025. 2022 was the largest year and 2023 the smallest among 2022 to 2025.

Operating profit was KRW 73.3 billion in 2022, KRW 24.7 billion in 2023 (down 66.2 percent), KRW 118.2 billion in 2024 (up 377.8 percent) and KRW 76.6 billion in 2025 (down 35.2 percent), with 2024 the largest year.

The operating margin was 3.8 percent in 2022, 1.7 percent in 2023, 6.3 percent in 2024 and 4.3 percent in 2025, so it recovered in 2024 and slipped again in 2025.

Shareholder net income moved from a net loss of KRW 25 billion in 2023, a swing into loss, to KRW 39.1 billion in 2024, a swing back to profit, and then fell 50.6 percent to KRW 19.3 billion in 2025.

Net income including non-controlling interests was KRW 9.7 billion in 2025, and the gap with shareholder net income appears to come from the non-controlling share. The company cited profitability pressure from external uncertainty as the main reason for the 2025 decline.

Operating cash flow rose from KRW 74.1 billion in 2023 to KRW 97 billion in 2024 and KRW 180 billion in 2025.

Quarterly operating profit went from KRW 9.08 billion in the third quarter of 2025 to KRW 24.3 billion in the fourth quarter, up 167.9 percent, then fell to KRW 8.53 billion in the first quarter of 2026, rose to KRW 21.7 billion in the second quarter, and reached KRW 10.5 billion in the third quarter, up 16.2 percent year on year.

Third-quarter 2026 revenue was KRW 389.9 billion, down 14.9 percent from the prior quarter and down 3.7 percent year on year.

Shareholder net income reached KRW 17.9 billion in the fourth quarter of 2025 and KRW 2.03 billion and KRW 8.55 billion in the first two quarters of 2026, then turned into a shareholder net loss of KRW 13.7 billion in the third quarter.

As a result, the last four quarters total KRW 14.7 billion, with the fourth-quarter 2025 profit lifting the total and the third-quarter 2026 loss offsetting it.

WiseReport's compilation shows first-half 2026 revenue down 6.7 percent and operating profit down 30.0 percent year on year, and its net income growth figure reflects a low base and should be read alongside absolute amounts.

At the end of 2025, total liabilities stood at KRW 1.03 trillion and total equity at KRW 558.9 billion, of which shareholder equity was KRW 334.1 billion. The debt ratio fell from 193.6 percent in 2024 to 185.0 percent in 2025.

05

Industry analysis

Footwear ODM earnings move with brand order flows, so results depend heavily on the customer's sell-through and inventory adjustments. WiseReport says adidas Group is increasing its order share with the Korean group, which it expects to lift share.

The same source notes that swings in adidas Group orders led to lower ODM revenue and profitability. A June 2026 industry report said higher raw material prices, business cycle swings and a weaker currency combined to keep earnings under pressure.

Chemicals weakness has also dragged on results before, and an IB Tomato report from 2024 cited the company's explanation that the 2023 earnings decline came from the chemicals segment.

The industry view is that the nature of footwear ODM makes it hard for rivals to take orders, although diversification is widely seen as a challenge for long-term growth.

Youngone and Hansae, peers in the broader apparel and textile OEM and ODM business, have apparel-centered production structures, so HS Industries stands out for its footwear focus.

WiseReport says the company is building shorter lead times, expanding automated processes and establishing an eco-friendly product response system. Judging its position requires looking together at customer concentration and exposure to raw material and currency swings.

06

Outlook

The most direct checkpoint is the adidas order flow. WiseReport reported expectations of a higher adidas order share, and the company is pursuing shorter lead times and wider automation.

According to an IB industry report from June 2026, proceeds from the Vietnam compound sale total KRW 23 billion, and an IB official said the partial exit from chemicals would secure cash while strengthening the core footwear ODM business.

The same report raised the possibility of further restructuring, including a merger with subsidiary Hwaseung Enterprise. The value-up plan disclosed on March 5, 2026 targets a payout ratio of 40 percent or more based on the three-year average standalone net income excluding one-off items.

The payout ratio for 2025 was presented at 51 percent, and the company disclosed that it falls under the high-dividend company category. Press coverage describes shareholder activism led by a minority shareholder group as having intensified in 2025.

The fourth quarter of 2026 has not yet been filed in a regular report, so it will be confirmed in the 2026 annual report. The cause and persistence of the third-quarter 2026 net loss of KRW 13.7 billion will be checked in the notes to the 2026 annual report.

Whether the Vietnam sale closes and how the proceeds are used will come through later disclosures. Whether adidas orders continue into the first half of 2027 remains the largest variable for ODM revenue.

Bull factors

Formalized shareholder return policy

The value-up plan disclosed on March 5, 2026 set a target payout ratio of 40 percent or more based on the three-year average standalone net income. The company said dividend decisions will also consider investment plans and the business environment.

Rising operating cash flow

The reason cash generation rose even as operating profit fell to KRW 76.6 billion in 2025 can be checked in the cash flow statement of the annual report. If that cash generation continues, it could ease borrowing pressure and support shareholder returns.

Expected rise in adidas order share

Shareholder net income returned to profit at KRW 8.55 billion in the second quarter of 2026. The company is also pushing shorter lead times and wider automation.

09

Bear factors

Customer concentration risk

According to WiseReport's compilation, the ODM segment accounts for about 87 percent of revenue, and revenue and profitability fall when adidas Group orders shift. The same compilation reported first-half 2026 operating profit down 30.0 percent year on year. High dependence on one customer means order changes translate directly into earnings changes.

Third-quarter swing to loss

Shareholder net loss reached KRW 13.7 billion in the third quarter of 2026, a swing into loss. Operating profit in the same quarter was KRW 10.5 billion, up 16.2 percent year on year, yet it still ended in a net loss. The reason operating profit and shareholder net income diverged needs to be checked in the disclosure text.

Debt burden and related-party guarantees

Total liabilities were KRW 1.03 trillion at the end of 2025, with a debt ratio of 185.0 percent. According to an IB Tomato report from 2024, guarantees for Hwaseung Vina totaled KRW 163.2 billion. The same report noted that the borrowing dependence ratio had been above 40 percent since 2021.

10

Risk factors

Customer concentration

A large share of footwear ODM revenue depends on adidas, so order reductions or price term changes directly move earnings. Industry coverage cites high single-customer dependence as a constraint on sustained growth.

The pace of customer diversification can be checked in the customer composition disclosure of the annual report.

Raw materials, currency and demand cycle

A June 2026 industry report said higher raw material prices, business cycle swings and a weaker currency combined to slow earnings. The company cited external uncertainty as the main reason for the 2025 profit decline, so external variables carry significant weight. These factors act on both order pricing and the cost structure at the same time.

Related-party trade and restructuring

Hwaseung Vina, the Vietnam affiliate, receives raw materials from HS Industries and sells finished goods back through it to adidas, so related-party trade is significant. An IB Tomato report said Hwaseung Vina's sales averaged 34.18 percent of consolidated revenue over 2021 to 2023. The chemicals sale and the possibility of a subsidiary merger add uncertainty about the structure ahead.

Overall view

HS Industries sits on a footwear ODM revenue base concentrated on adidas, and operating profit fell to KRW 76.6 billion in 2025. In 2026, profit in the first and second quarters contrasted with a KRW 13.7 billion shareholder net loss in the third quarter, which increased quarterly volatility.

The last four quarters total KRW 14.7 billion in shareholder net income, with the KRW 17.9 billion fourth-quarter 2025 profit making up much of that total. On the financial side, a debt ratio of 185.0 percent and total liabilities of KRW 1.03 trillion remain burdens.

Operating cash flow, however, rose to KRW 180 billion in 2025, and a payout target of 40 percent or more and the chemicals reshuffle are under way.

Bullish factors are the expected rise in adidas order share and the shareholder return policy, while bearish factors are customer concentration and the third-quarter swing to a loss.

The next checkpoints are the fourth-quarter 2026 results and the cause of the third-quarter loss in the 2026 annual report, along with first-quarter 2027 results.
