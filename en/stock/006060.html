HS Industries: Testing Earnings Recovery Amid adidas Reliance

HS Industries' operating profit fell to KRW 76.6 billion in 2025 and the company swung to a controlling-shareholder net loss in the third quarter of 2026, although operating profit recovered to KRW 21.7 billion in the second quarter of 2026. 02

Business structure

HS Industries takes commissioned orders for footwear from global sportswear brands and manufactures to the brand's designs under an ODM model.

The ODM segment accounts for about 87% of consolidated sales according to a company profile dated August 31, 2026, with the remainder coming from chemicals and from distribution and finance.

Sales in the chemicals, distribution and finance segments fell 28.5% year on year to KRW 100.5 billion in the first quarter of 2026, according to a May 2026 press report. Customer concentration is high: adidas was reported to account for about 75% of sales.

After adidas ended its collaboration with Kanye West at the end of 2022, sales fell 23.3% in 2023. Production bases are located in Vietnam, Indonesia and China. Same-sector peers such as Youngone and Hansae also rely on contract manufacturing, but HS Industries is more heavily weighted toward footwear.

Because volumes and unit prices move together, a change in one client's orders passes through to group earnings quickly. The company has been exiting part of its chemicals operations to refocus on footwear ODM.

04

Earnings analysis

On an annual basis, sales were KRW 1.91 trillion in 2022, the largest year in the 2022 to 2025 window, and KRW 1.47 trillion in 2023, the smallest.

In 2023, sales fell 23.3% and operating profit dropped 66.2% to KRW 24.7 billion, and net income attributable to the controlling shareholder turned into a KRW 25 billion loss.

In 2024, sales rose 27.4% to KRW 1.87 trillion and operating profit rose 377.8% to KRW 118.2 billion, with controlling-shareholder net income of KRW 39.1 billion, a swing back to profit.

In 2025, sales fell 4.6% to KRW 1.78 trillion, operating profit fell 35.2% to KRW 76.6 billion, and controlling-shareholder net income fell 50.6% to KRW 19.3 billion. The company attributed the 2025 profit decline mainly to lower profitability from external uncertainty.

Operating margin was 3.8% in 2022, 1.7% in 2023, 6.3% in 2024 and 4.3% in 2025. Net income including non-controlling interests fell from KRW 46.9 billion in 2024 to KRW 9.7 billion in 2025. The debt-to-equity ratio was 169.8% in 2022, 169.4% in 2023, 193.6% in 2024 and 185.0% in 2025.

Operating cash flow rose to KRW 180 billion in 2025 from KRW 97 billion in 2024. On a quarterly basis, operating profit rose 167.9% from the prior quarter to KRW 24.3 billion in the fourth quarter of 2025, fell to KRW 8.53 billion in the first quarter of 2026, and recovered to KRW 21.7 billion in the second quarter.

Third-quarter 2026 sales were KRW 389.9 billion, down 3.7% year on year, while operating profit rose 16.2% year on year to KRW 10.5 billion. In the same quarter, net income attributable to the controlling shareholder swung to a loss of KRW 13.7 billion from a profit a year earlier.

The gap between rising operating profit and a controlling-shareholder net loss is the defining feature of that quarter. Controlling-shareholder net income for the last four quarters combined was KRW 14.7 billion.

05

Industry analysis

Footwear ODM revenue depends on how many orders global sportswear brands place and on new model launches. HS Industries leans heavily on adidas, so changes in adidas order volumes feed directly into company results.

Wisereport's company monitor says adidas Group is increasing its order share with the Korean group, which it cites as a basis for expected share gains. The same source points to shorter lead times, wider automation and a system for eco-friendly products.

It also notes that revenue and profitability fall when adidas Group order volumes move. On costs, higher raw material prices, the business cycle and a weaker exchange rate were reported as drags on first-quarter 2026 results.

Among same-sector peers, Youngone and Hansae are built mainly around apparel contract manufacturing, so HS Industries, with its higher footwear weighting, is more sensitive to the cycles of a few sportswear brands.

The company is trimming chemicals while expanding finance and investment, so the industry view should be read together with that restructuring.

06

Outlook

According to a June 2026 press report, HS Industries decided to spin off the compounds business of its Vietnam subsidiary and sell it to a Chinese petrochemical company, Shandong Dawn Polymer, for about KRW 23 billion.

Industry observers read the sale as a clearing of non-core assets and an improvement in the business structure. In 2025 the company merged its Vietnam subsidiary Hwaseung Vina with a Vietnam textile unit as part of efficiency work in manufacturing.

Domestically, it has deconsolidated food-service businesses, continuing a pullback in non-core activities. The company published a voluntary corporate value enhancement plan on March 5, 2026. Some observers expect the portfolio shift toward higher-value finance and investment businesses to accelerate.

Further restructuring, including a possible merger with Hwaseung Enterprise, has also been raised by analysts. In footwear ODM, the adidas Group's larger order share with the Korean group is seen as a positive variable.

The next points to check are how the operating margin moves from 4.3% in 2025 in the 2026 annual report, and whether the sale proceeds are actually collected.

Bull factors

Portfolio reshaping and cash generation

A June 2026 press report put the Vietnam compounds sale at about KRW 23 billion. Industry observers read it as clearing non-core assets and securing cash, which strengthens the footwear ODM core. Deconsolidation of food-service units and the Vietnam merger point to ongoing efficiency work. Narrowing chemicals while concentrating resources on footwear ODM is a positive direction.

Quarterly operating profit rebound

Operating profit rose 167.9% from the prior quarter to KRW 24.3 billion in the fourth quarter of 2025. After falling to KRW 8.53 billion in the first quarter of 2026, operating profit rose 154.1% to KRW 21.7 billion in the second quarter.

Earnings capacity has reappeared on a quarterly basis, which supports the bullish case. Whether the recovery carried into the third quarter must still be checked, since operating profit and controlling-shareholder net income diverged.

Expected rise in adidas order share

The expected share gain comes from the company monitor's view that adidas Group is shifting more orders to the Korean group. Shorter lead times, wider automation and an eco-friendly product system are also being built. Higher volumes from one client could help plant utilization and absorb fixed costs.

The expectation rests on a qualitative assessment in that monitor rather than on disclosed order-contract figures.

Bear factors

Profit decline in 2025

Operating profit fell 35.2% to KRW 76.6 billion in 2025, and controlling-shareholder net income fell 50.6% to KRW 19.3 billion. The company cited lower profitability from external uncertainty. The operating margin also declined from 6.3% in 2024 to 4.3% in 2025.

Third-quarter loss and debt burden

Controlling-shareholder net income swung to a loss of KRW 13.7 billion in the third quarter of 2026. Wisereport's company monitor says first-half 2026 sales fell 6.7% and operating profit fell 30.0% year on year.

Total liabilities were KRW 1.03 trillion at the end of 2025, with a debt-to-equity ratio of 185.0%, so the debt load remains heavy relative to equity. That burden limits financial room when earnings are falling.

Customer concentration and weak chemicals

With adidas reported at about 75% of sales, dependence on a single client is high. Sales in the chemicals, distribution and finance segments fell 28.5% year on year to KRW 100.5 billion in the first quarter of 2026. Slow diversification has left results more tied to adidas volumes. With chemicals being trimmed, that segment's contribution may shrink for a time.

Risk factors

Governance and restructuring

Analysts suggest further restructuring, including a possible merger with Hwaseung Enterprise, could be pursued. Shareholder activism led by minority-shareholder groups has become more active, which may lead the company to introduce further shareholder-value measures.

Merger terms and any change in ownership structure could affect the interests of minority and controlling shareholders. Until any restructuring is finalized, its effect on earnings is hard to gauge.

Guarantees and sale proceeds

A September 2026 disclosure covered a guarantee of KRW 27.2 billion for affiliate Hwaseung Vina, which press reports put at 9.47% of the company's equity. Guarantees can become a contingent burden if an affiliate cannot repay.

The timing and conditions for receiving the KRW 23 billion sale proceeds from the Vietnam compounds business will also affect cash flow. Neither outcome has been determined yet.

Policy and trade conditions in production countries

With production bases concentrated in Vietnam, Indonesia and China, changes in local policy and trade conditions could affect costs and delivery schedules. If export rules or tariff regimes shift in a given country, client order allocation could change.

With Vietnam merger and efficiency work under way, local labor and facility adjustment costs are also variables. These conditions have not materialized yet and can be tracked in regional disclosures in future periodic reports.

Overall view

HS Industries is structured around footwear ODM work for adidas, which accounts for most of its sales. Sales fell from KRW 1.91 trillion in 2022 to KRW 1.78 trillion in 2025, and operating profit fell from KRW 118.2 billion in 2024 to KRW 76.6 billion in 2025.

Operating profit recovered to KRW 21.7 billion in the second quarter of 2026, but the third quarter swung to a controlling-shareholder net loss of KRW 13.7 billion. The divergence between rising operating profit and that loss is pronounced.

Total liabilities were KRW 1.03 trillion at the end of 2025, with a debt-to-equity ratio of 185.0%, which is a heavy load. Portfolio reshaping, including the Vietnam compounds sale and the exit from food-service units, is under way, and a restructuring involving Hwaseung Enterprise has been raised as a possibility.

The next points to check are the 2026 annual operating margin, collection of the sale proceeds, and whether adidas order flow continues into the first quarter of 2027.
