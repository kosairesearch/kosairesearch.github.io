02

Business structure

Point Mobile was founded in 2006 as a developer and manufacturer of industrial mobile computers and listed on KOSDAQ in 2020. Its core lineup covers barcode and RFID-based industrial mobile computers, along with POS terminals, tablet PCs, industrial PDAs, RFID readers and Bluetooth scanners.

According to company materials, exports account for more than 85% of revenue and all products have received Google AER certification. The core customer is Amazon in the United States, which receives the PM86 industrial mobile computer, the PM5 wearable device and the TR54 tablet.

The company attributes the growth in cumulative 2026 third-quarter revenue mainly to expanded supply to Amazon.

Beyond Amazon, sales have grown from retail stores and rail projects in Poland, the Yamato project in Japan, warehouse and logistics products supplied to Poland's retailer Żabka, and deliveries to Whole Foods in the United States.

In the first half of 2026, orders of more than 10,000 ring scanners (PM5) in the United States and Japan were also reflected. The company is widening its scope into the RFID market, specialized tablets and the disaster-network MC PTT market.

It plans to start deliveries to the Turkish National Police in the second half of 2026 as a step into defense and public-safety communication equipment. The company also says its high-margin warranty revenue is growing.

04

Earnings analysis

On a disclosed basis, annual revenue moved from KRW 87.6 billion in 2022 to KRW 80 billion in 2023 (down 8.6% year on year) and KRW 77.3 billion in 2024 (down 3.4%), before recovering to KRW 87.2 billion in 2025 (up 12.8%). 2022 was the largest year and 2024 the smallest within 2022 to 2025.

Operating profit rose from KRW 316 million in 2022 to KRW 4.64 billion in 2023 (up 1,370.1%), then turned into an operating loss of KRW 1.41 billion in 2024 before returning to an operating profit of KRW 3.01 billion in 2025. Operating margins were 0.4% in 2022, 5.8% in 2023, -1.8% in 2024 and 3.4% in 2025.

Profit attributable to owners moved from a net loss of KRW 2.51 billion in 2022 to a profit of KRW 4.84 billion in 2023, then KRW 1.59 billion in 2024 (down 67.2%) and KRW 2.85 billion in 2025 (up 79.6%). Net income, including non-controlling interests, was reported at the same amounts in each year.

Operating cash flow went from -KRW 3.09 billion in 2022 to KRW 13.8 billion in 2023 and KRW 8.01 billion in 2024, then turned to -KRW 165 million in 2025.

The debt ratio at end-2025 was 83.2%, compared with 43.6% in 2023, with total liabilities of KRW 43.1 billion and total equity of KRW 51.9 billion, all attributable to owners.

Quarterly revenue was KRW 27.5 billion in Q3 2025 and KRW 30 billion in Q4 2025 (up 8.9% quarter on quarter), while operating profit fell from KRW 3.24 billion to KRW 2.35 billion (down 27.5%).

Q1 2026 was the trough, with revenue of KRW 18.1 billion (down 39.5% quarter on quarter) and operating profit of KRW 372 million (down 84.2%). Q2 2026 revenue rose to KRW 28.4 billion (up 56.9%) and operating profit to KRW 4.2 billion (up 1,030.4%), and revenue has risen for two consecutive quarters from Q2 to Q3 2026.

Q3 2026 revenue was KRW 30.5 billion (up 7.1% quarter on quarter and 10.7% year on year), with operating profit of KRW 3.54 billion (down 15.9% quarter on quarter, up 9.2% year on year). Profit attributable to owners fell to KRW 370 million in Q3 2026 (down 92.0% quarter on quarter and 90.2% year on year).

The reason for the wide gap between operating profit and owner-attributable profit cannot be pinned down from disclosed line items alone, and it is a point to check in the 2026 annual report's detailed income items.

Profit attributable to owners over the latest four quarters, from Q4 2025 to Q3 2026, totals KRW 10.8 billion.

Cumulative operating margin for the first three quarters of 2026 was about 10.5% on a preliminary basis; the company attributes this to securing key components before memory prices rose and to growth in high-margin warranty revenue.

05

Industry analysis

Demand for industrial mobile computers is rising as digital transformation and supply-chain optimization spread, lifting demand for AIDC-based terminals. A securities research report cited this demand expansion as a driver of the company's earnings recovery.

The spread of 5G and IoT is also mentioned as a demand factor. Terminal replacement cycles in logistics centers and retail sites line up with the company's Amazon volume flow.

The company said Amazon's last-mile service adoption is accelerating and that the new PM84P will be deployed in part of a terminal replacement project at logistics centers. On costs, rising memory prices are a variable.

The company said it secured key components ahead of memory price increases, which eased cost pressure. If prices keep rising, that structure could pressure margins.

Its position against peers can be assessed through supply relationships with global OEM and distribution customers, Google AER certification and high-margin warranty revenue. This discussion does not include a like-for-like comparison of peer revenue and margins.

Where the market sits in its cycle cannot be judged from a single indicator, and Amazon's equipment investment and logistics automation are the main forces shaping demand.

Outlook

In its first-half 2026 results announcement, the company projected Amazon-related revenue of at least KRW 45 billion for 2026. This is the company's own projection, not a confirmed annual result. The basis cited is the expansion of Amazon's last-mile service and a terminal replacement project at logistics centers.

The company expects the new PM84P to be deployed in part of a replacement pool of roughly 400,000 terminals in use at those centers. The company said it would keep growing through key customers and overseas projects in the second half of 2026.

In Europe it plans to expand retail and rail projects in Poland, and in Japan the Yamato project. For defense and public-safety, deliveries to the Turkish National Police are presented as the first step in the plan for the second half of 2026.

On shareholder returns, the company said it will continue additional share buybacks and cancellations and dividends in the second half of 2026. Amazon order timing and volumes can vary by quarter, so gaps between the annual projection and quarterly results are possible.

The next checkpoint is the 2026 annual report, which will show Q4 results, whether Amazon-related revenue reached KRW 45 billion or more for the full year, and how Turkish deliveries and defense and public-safety sales were booked.

Business diversification and shareholder return plans

Bear factors

Large swings in quarterly earnings

Q1 2026 operating profit fell to KRW 372 million and revenue to KRW 18.1 billion, marking the trough. Q3 2026 profit attributable to owners of KRW 370 million fell 90.2% year on year. The company also posted an operating loss of KRW 1.41 billion in 2024. In Q3 2026, operating profit rose year on year while profit attributable to owners fell sharply.

Concentration on Amazon volumes

Growth in 2026 has been led by expanded supply to Amazon. The company's projection of at least KRW 45 billion in Amazon-related revenue depends on replacement project schedules and new product deployment. If Amazon's order timing or volumes change, quarterly results could swing more. A structure with high customer dependence is vulnerable to changes in contract terms.

Cost pressure and small-cap characteristics

Rising memory prices have been eased by early component purchases, but continued increases could pressure margins. The company is a small cap, with its largest shareholders holding 33.84%. A free-float ratio of 65.15% means trading liquidity differs from that of large caps. These factors can widen share price movements.

Risk factors

Customer concentration risk

Much of the growth depends on Amazon volumes, so a drop in customer orders could move both revenue and factory utilization. Quarterly revenue also depends on project timing in Poland and Japan.

The company projects at least KRW 45 billion in Amazon-related revenue, and whether that pace holds quarter by quarter is a point to watch.

Memory and component price risk

The company said it secured key components before memory prices rose, easing cost pressure. The benefit of early purchasing can fade over time. If memory prices rise again, the cost structure could change and the operating margin could be affected. High-margin warranty revenue supports the margin.

Export dependence and ownership risk

According to company materials, exports account for more than 85% of revenue, so the company is exposed to exchange rates and overseas customs and tariff conditions. The largest shareholders hold 33.84%, so changes in the shareholder base could affect the free float.

New defense and public-safety businesses need certification and delivery track records before they become revenue.
