02

Business structure

WOT makes environmental control equipment that keeps temperature and humidity precisely stable inside tools used in semiconductor and display manufacturing. Its main products are ultra-precision temperature and humidity control units (THC), fan filter units (FFU) and ultra-precision thermo-chillers (TCU).

In Q1 2026, THC accounted for 56.2% of revenue and FFU for 33.2%, according to a securities report dated June 11, 2026. THC does not manage an entire cleanroom; it maintains conditions at individual process tools where precise control is required.

Its main customers are Samsung Electronics and SK hynix, and the company is registered as a first-tier supplier to both. On the display side, an analyst at a securities firm commented in January 2025 that TCU sales rose as domestic display customers expanded investment.

In competition, track equipment has mostly used environmental control devices from other vendors, but the company said it has largely completed THC evaluations for that equipment. The CEO said Samsung Electronics may cross-apply products from several suppliers to secure supply stability.

Overseas, the company is expanding exports to Taiwan and Southeast Asia and entering markets through overseas agents. Its consolidated Chinese subsidiary, WOTCHINA, sells semiconductor component equipment and provides technical services.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual results have recovered after a peak in 2022 followed by adjustment.

Revenue fell from KRW 22.8 billion in 2022 to KRW 12.8 billion in 2023 (down 43.7% year on year), then rose to KRW 15.2 billion in 2024 (up 18.2%) and was flat-to-up at KRW 15.2 billion in 2025 (up 0.2%), with two consecutive years of growth from 2024 through 2025.

Among 2022 to 2025, 2022 was the largest year for revenue and 2023 the smallest. Operating profit fell from KRW 6.71 billion in 2022 to KRW 1.47 billion in 2023 (down 78.1%), then recovered to KRW 2.03 billion in 2024 (up 38.1%) and KRW 2.48 billion in 2025 (up 22.3%).

Operating margin was 29.4% in 2022, 11.4% in 2023, 13.4% in 2024 and 16.3% in 2025, still short of the 2022 level. Profit attributable to owners of the parent was KRW 5.87 billion in 2022, KRW 2.21 billion in 2023 (down 62.4%), KRW 3.39 billion in 2024 (up 53.5%) and KRW 3.57 billion in 2025 (up 5.2%).

A securities analyst's January 2025 comment said profitability, which had been slowed by one-off costs in the first half of 2024, was normalizing in the second half. Operating cash flow was KRW 1.93 billion in 2025, down from KRW 5.86 billion in 2024.

At the end of 2025, total equity was KRW 66 billion and total liabilities KRW 2 billion, a debt ratio of 3.0%.

Quarterly results began Q3 2025 with revenue of KRW 2.42 billion and operating profit of KRW 226 million, then jumped in Q4 2025 to revenue of KRW 5.95 billion (up 146.4% quarter on quarter) and operating profit of KRW 1.26 billion.

Q1 2026 slowed to revenue of KRW 4.33 billion (down 27.2% quarter on quarter) and operating profit of KRW 955 million (down 24.0%), before two consecutive quarters of growth: Q2 revenue of KRW 7.71 billion and operating profit of KRW 2.08 billion, then Q3 revenue of KRW 8.64 billion and operating profit of KRW 2.6 billion.

Q3 2026 profit attributable to owners of the parent was KRW 2.3 billion, up 343.8% year on year. Cumulative revenue for January to September 2026 was KRW 20.7 billion and operating profit KRW 5.6 billion, as disclosed by the company on October 26, 2026.

05

Industry analysis

WOT's main end market is front-end semiconductor capital spending. As investment in AI chips and advanced processes grows, demand for equipment that precisely controls manufacturing conditions has drawn more attention.

The CEO was reported to expect equipment investment at Samsung Electronics, SK hynix and Taiwanese chipmakers to continue for about three years. SK hynix has reportedly completed THC evaluations, including for EUV-related track equipment, and is proceeding with equipment supply.

The company said new line construction and existing line expansions at Samsung Electronics and SK hynix increased THC supply and lifted 2026 results. Viewed through the cycle, orders cluster during line construction and expansion phases, so a slowdown in chip capex could reduce orders, which is a structural weakness.

A securities report also flagged a gap in front-end investment as a potential risk. On competition, track equipment has mostly used environmental control devices from other vendors, so passing evaluation could widen WOT's share at a customer.

The mention of possible cross-application by several suppliers suggests that share shifts may not happen quickly. Listed peers in the KOSDAQ machinery and equipment sector include Koh Young Technology, Taesung and People & Technology, though product and customer overlap differs by company. Entering a Taiwanese foundry is presented as a task for customer diversification.

06

Outlook

No formal annual guidance from the company was confirmed in the sources reviewed. A securities report dated June 11, 2026 forecast 2026 revenue of KRW 25.0 billion and operating profit of KRW 5.5 billion, citing expanding THC sales.

The KRW 5.5 billion operating profit forecast has already been exceeded by cumulative operating profit of KRW 5.6 billion for January to September 2026, while cumulative nine-month revenue of KRW 20.7 billion stands against the KRW 25.0 billion forecast.

In order intake, a KRW 2.1 billion Samsung Electronics supply contract disclosed on March 25, 2026 equaled 13.85% of revenue. A KRW 832 million Samsung Electronics contract disclosed on June 28, 2026 ran through October 31, 2026, and a KRW 860 million SK hynix contract was disclosed on October 21, 2026.

Reports said eight supply contracts disclosed from January 1 to August 19, 2026 totaled about KRW 12.4 billion. The CEO said a second evaluation unit for Samsung Electronics' THC has shipped and that the evaluation is expected to finish within Q1 2027.

Formal supply will depend on the second-round results and the customer's final purchase decision. Reports said deliveries to new lines such as Pyeongtaek P4 were scheduled from Q4 2025. The company is considering use of spare land at existing sites, but concrete expansion plans are not fixed.

Reports also say IPO proceeds earmarked for a second plant have gone unspent for three years, so the timing of capacity expansion is uncertain.

08

Bull factors

Consecutive quarterly improvement

Revenue and operating profit rose for two straight quarters in Q2 and Q3 2026, and Q3 operating profit grew 25.2% from the prior quarter. Cumulative nine-month operating profit of KRW 5.6 billion has already exceeded the full-year 2026 forecast of KRW 5.5 billion in a June 2026 securities report. Whether the recovery holds on a quarterly basis is central to this case.

Customer line expansion and supply contracts

A Samsung Electronics supply contract disclosed in March 2026 was KRW 2.1 billion, and an SK hynix contract disclosed in October 2026 was KRW 860 million. Reports put the eight supply contracts disclosed from January to August 19, 2026 at about KRW 12.4 billion in total. New lines and expansions at both chipmakers are the background to this order flow.

Profitability trend and balance-sheet capacity

Operating margin rose from 11.4% in 2023 to 16.3% in 2025, and the debt ratio was 3.0% at the end of 2025. With total liabilities of KRW 2 billion against KRW 66 billion in equity, the company has room to invest without relying on external borrowing. Margin remains below the 29.4% of 2022, so the direction has improved while the level has not yet returned.

09

Bear factors

Customer concentration and pending evaluation

Revenue is concentrated in Samsung Electronics and SK hynix, and Samsung's second-round THC evaluation is still in progress. The CEO noted that Samsung may cross-apply products from several suppliers. Until evaluation results and formal supply volumes are set, the scale of supply expansion remains open.

Quarterly volatility

In Q1 2026, revenue fell 27.2% and operating profit 24.0% from the prior quarter. Q3 2025 operating profit was only KRW 226 million. Annual revenue held at about KRW 15.2 billion in both 2024 and 2025, so whether quarterly gains have translated into a larger annual base still needs testing.

Unsettled expansion plan

Reports say investment in a second plant and an R&D center is not fixed, and that IPO proceeds have gone unspent for three years. Use of spare land is under review without a specific plan. If equipment demand rises faster than capacity, the speed at which orders reach reported results could be limited.

10

Risk factors

Customer capex cycle

WOT's orders depend heavily on new line construction and expansions at Samsung Electronics and SK hynix. Order gaps could arise during periods when front-end investment pauses. A slowdown in capex would reduce THC and FFU orders and directly affect revenue and operating profit.

Evaluation and supply timing

Samsung Electronics' second-round THC evaluation results and formal supply status are not yet settled. The CEO expects the evaluation to finish within Q1 2027, but that is an expectation and the schedule could slip. A delay would also push back the timing of supply expansion.

Quarterly results and expansion funding

Quarterly revenue and profit have swung sharply with customer delivery timing, as the 27.2% quarter-on-quarter revenue drop in Q1 2026 showed. With expansion plans unsettled and the timing of fund use uncertain, a gap could open between production capacity and order response.

Overall view

WOT's earnings recovered in 2024 and 2025 after a peak in 2022 and a trough in 2023, and quarterly results in 2026 have risen for two straight quarters, so the pace of recovery has picked up.

Both revenue and operating profit lean heavily on THC supply tied to new lines and expansions at Samsung Electronics and SK hynix. Bullish factors include the quarterly recovery, accumulated supply contracts and the improving operating margin trend.

Bearish factors include customer concentration, quarterly volatility, uncertainty over Samsung's second-round evaluation and formal supply, and unsettled expansion and funding plans. These two sides should be weighed equally.
