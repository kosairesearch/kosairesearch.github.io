02

Business structure

W-Scope Chungju Plant is a secondary battery separator specialist founded in 2016 in Chungju, Chungcheongbuk-do, and listed on KOSDAQ in 2022. The separator is a core material that keeps the anode and cathode from direct contact inside a battery while allowing lithium ions to pass.

Its main product is the second-generation coated wet separator for EV batteries, and it also produces ceramic-coated separators. The largest shareholder is W-SCOPE Corporation, which is listed on the Tokyo Stock Exchange, and its affiliate W-Scope Korea makes ion-exchange membranes and small cylindrical separators.

Demand has spread from EVs into ESS and small-format cells such as BBUs and power tools. In Q1 2026, a recovery in EV-related sales and higher NCA-based ESS shipments lifted revenue 57.7% from the prior quarter.

By region, Hungary export revenue reached KRW 27.1 billion in Q1 2026, up 476.5% year on year, according to a June 2026 analysis by the Korea IR Association. A July 2026 news report noted that in 2023 the Japanese parent W-SCOPE signed a memorandum of understanding with Samsung SDI to supply EV separators through 2027.

In domestic competition, the company is typically compared with SK ie technology, while low-priced offers from Chinese separator makers add pressure on pricing.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual revenue rose from KRW 257.5 billion in 2022 to KRW 305 billion in 2023 (up 18.5%) and KRW 322.1 billion in 2024 (up 5.6%), then fell to KRW 110.8 billion in 2025 (down 65.6%). Among 2022–2025, 2024 was the largest year and 2025 the smallest.

Operating profit was KRW 58 billion in 2022 (22.5% margin) and fell to KRW 46.4 billion in 2023 (down 20.1%, 15.2% margin). The company swung to an operating loss of KRW 70.9 billion in 2024 and a loss of KRW 127.6 billion in 2025, a margin of -115.2%, which makes 2024 and 2025 two consecutive loss years.

Profit attributable to owners was KRW 55.3 billion in 2022 and KRW 53.6 billion in 2023 (down 3.0%), then turned to net losses of KRW 72.2 billion in 2024 and KRW 162.1 billion in 2025.

Korea IR Association analysts attribute the 2025 deterioration to high fixed costs, because large-scale separator capacity carries heavy depreciation, so losses widen sharply when sales drop.

Operating cash flow was KRW 33.7 billion in 2022 and KRW 134.3 billion in 2023, then turned negative at -KRW 15.8 billion in 2024 and -KRW 27.5 billion in 2025. The debt ratio climbed from 15.4% in 2022 to 117.5% in 2025, when total liabilities were KRW 1.05 trillion against equity of KRW 894.5 billion.

Quarterly, revenue was KRW 29.1 billion in Q3 2025 with an operating loss of KRW 31 billion, and KRW 27.2 billion in Q4 2025 with a loss of KRW 40.2 billion. Q1 2026 revenue rose to KRW 42.9 billion (up 57.7% from the prior quarter) with an operating loss narrowed to KRW 20.5 billion.

Q2 2026 revenue was KRW 36.2 billion (down 15.5% quarter on quarter) with an operating loss of KRW 15.5 billion, while Q3 2026 revenue was KRW 43.2 billion (up 19.2% quarter on quarter and up 48.4% year on year) with an operating loss of KRW 21.9 billion.

Profit attributable to owners turned to a KRW 810 million profit in Q2 2026, then returned to a net loss of KRW 45.3 billion in Q3 2026.

Operating losses have run for five consecutive quarters from Q3 2025 through Q3 2026, and the combined net loss attributable to owners over the last four quarters is KRW 147.6 billion.

05

Industry analysis

Separators physically divide the anode and cathode and largely determine battery safety, so demand is shaped by both EVs and ESS. A July 2026 news report explained that the EV demand slowdown, often called the EV chasm, pulled utilization down and worsened profitability.

ESS has emerged as the alternative demand source, and W-Scope Chungju Plant is raising shipments of separators for NCA-based and now LFP-based ESS. The same report said that domestic makers' market share fell even as separator demand rebounded, with low-priced offers from Chinese separator makers adding pressure.

In cycle terms, the recovery signal is appearing first in ESS, while the timing of a utilization recovery in EVs remains unclear. A 2025 company analysis described Korean separator makers as holding a strategic position in a supply chain outside China.

In domestic competition, the comparison with SK ie technology is the basic reference point. The company's capacity spans Chungju and Hungary, but unless that capacity converts into utilization, fixed costs will continue to flow through as losses, which is the central question for the industry outlook.

In April 2026, some brokers saw a possible return to a quarterly profit in Q4 2026. As of a July 2026 news report, no new large supply contract disclosure had appeared, and under KOSDAQ rules a single contract worth 10% or more of the latest fiscal year's revenue requires disclosure.

The 2026 annual business report, the next periodic filing, will publish Q4 results alongside full-year figures. The key variables for the outlook are Hungary utilization, the scale of ESS shipments, and the size of new contracts that can absorb fixed costs.

Narrower losses and a quarterly profit

The Q3 2026 operating loss of KRW 21.9 billion was smaller than the KRW 31 billion loss in Q3 2025. Capacity expansion and a non-China supply chain position

Low-priced Chinese competition and share loss

Debt burden and negative operating cash flow

Operating cash flow was -KRW 15.8 billion in 2024 and -KRW 27.5 billion in 2025. With operating losses continuing, borrowing costs could limit the room for a recovery period.

Risk factors

Customer and order risk

No new large supply contract disclosure had appeared as of a July 2026 news report, so the effect of customer diversification is not yet visible in the numbers.

Under KOSDAQ rules, a single contract worth 10% or more of the latest fiscal year's revenue must be disclosed, and the timing of any such contract is uncertain. The company has relied on volumes from its early long-term supply customers, so changes in those volumes feed directly into results.

Pricing and competition risk

Low-priced offers from Chinese separator makers could put pressure on prices and limit the benefit of any utilization recovery. A longer EV demand slowdown would delay the recovery of EV-related volumes. Capacity expansion, including Hungary, would raise fixed-cost burden if demand does not keep pace.

Financial and facility risk

With total liabilities above equity and operating losses continuing, financial flexibility would narrow. A longer trial-operation period at the Hungary plant could add start-up costs and operating losses. Continued negative operating cash flow could translate into a heavier funding burden.
