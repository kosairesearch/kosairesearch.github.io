02

Business structure

Korea Asset Investment Securities was founded in January 2000 and is listed on KOSDAQ. The business centers on SME and venture financing, domestic and overseas alternative investments, and unlisted share trading (Negostock), which the company presents as its specialized areas.

The company says it registered the new technology business financing license in 2016, the first securities firm to do so, and has been designated an SME-specialized securities firm by the Financial Services Commission since 2016.

It highlights growth in its new technology business investment funds and treats venture investing as a core pillar. In October 2026 it reportedly took part in a Series A investment in Fit-in. Its largest shareholder is a private equity group, which is also the parent of its affiliated group.

As of June 30, 2026 it had 237 employees and a single branch, its head-office sales department. Total equity at end-March 2026 was KRW 99.9 billion, all attributable to owners of the parent, and total liabilities were KRW 276 billion.

Peers in the same sector include Mirae Asset Securities, NH Investment & Securities, Korea Investment Holdings, Samsung Securities, KIWOOM Securities, Hanwha INVESTMENT&SECURITIES and Shinyoung Securities, many of which are larger, full-service firms with broader businesses. A data service lists Heungkuk Securities, Next Securities and KR Investment & Securities as similar companies.

Earnings analysis

Annual revenue fell from KRW 223 billion in the year ended March 2023 to KRW 217.3 billion in the year ended March 2024 (down 2.6%), then rose sharply to KRW 268.5 billion in the year ended March 2025 (up 23.6%).

Revenue for the year ended March 2026 was KRW 261.9 billion, down 2.5%, and the year ended March 2025 was the largest year in the March 2023 to March 2026 span.

Operating results moved from an operating loss of KRW 2.08 billion in the year ended March 2023 to an operating profit of KRW 7.87 billion in the year ended March 2024, a turn to profit, then KRW 10.3 billion in the year ended March 2025 (up 30.2%) and KRW 9.15 billion in the year ended March 2026 (down 10.8%).

The operating margin was -0.9%, 3.6%, 3.8% and 3.5% across the four years, staying in the mid-3% range since the year ended March 2024.

Profit attributable to owners of the parent moved from a loss of KRW 2.77 billion in the year ended March 2023 to KRW 5.81 billion in the year ended March 2024, then KRW 7.73 billion in the year ended March 2025 (up 33.1%) and KRW 5.86 billion in the year ended March 2026 (down 24.1%).

Net profit including non-controlling interests was a loss of KRW 2.77 billion, KRW 5.81 billion, KRW 7.73 billion and KRW 5.86 billion for the four years in the same order. Operating cash flow was -KRW 36.7 billion, -KRW 33.3 billion, KRW 51.7 billion and -KRW 23.5 billion, with wide swings between years.

Quarterly revenue fell from KRW 72.4 billion in Q2 2025 to KRW 59.6 billion in Q3 2025 (down 17.7% from the prior quarter), then rose to KRW 63.6 billion in Q4 2025, KRW 66.3 billion in Q1 2026 and KRW 70.8 billion in Q2 2026, three straight quarterly increases; Q2 2026 was down 2.2% year on year.

Operating profit went from KRW 847 million in Q2 2025 to KRW 4.72 billion in Q3 2025 (up 456.7% from the prior quarter), swung to an operating loss of KRW 973 million in Q4 2025, then recovered to KRW 4.56 billion in Q1 2026 and KRW 6.22 billion in Q2 2026 (up 36.5% from the prior quarter and up 634.0% year on year).

Profit attributable to owners of the parent moved from a loss of KRW 941 million in Q4 2025 to KRW 3.09 billion in Q1 2026 and KRW 3.59 billion in Q2 2026.

Brokerage earnings depend heavily on trading value and market direction. A June 2026 research note covering a group of securities firms projected that their combined Q2 2026 profit attributable to owners would beat consensus by 13.2%.

The same note cited higher trading value from a rising stock market as a driver of brokerage profit. It also expected trading and other gains and losses to fall 11.6% from the prior quarter in Q2. Higher trading value helps commission income but adds volatility to proprietary trading and other gains.

Unlike the large firms, Korea Asset Investment Securities is a mid-sized broker focused on SME and venture finance and alternative investments. Since its designation as an SME-specialized securities firm in 2016 and its new technology business financing registration, it has built institutional footing in that niche.

The sector's upside drivers are higher trading value and rising demand for venture investment, while the downside drivers are falling trading value in market corrections and weaker capital flows into venture and unlisted markets.

Whether the Q2 2026 trend holds into a sector upcycle is a question for the Q3 2026 results. Reading the industry therefore works best alongside the operating margin and quarterly swings in profit.
