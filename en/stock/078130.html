02

Business structure

Kuk-Il Paper MFG was listed on KOSDAQ in 2004 and completed its name change from Kuk-Il Paper on October 8, 2026.

Its core products are functional and specialty industrial papers, and according to an AlphaSquare company overview (collection date not stated), paper-based products such as creped and bleached paper account for 57.4% of sales, while kraft paper products account for 34.2%.

In specialty papers, the company supplies carrier paper for semiconductors and steel-surface protection paper for industry-specific uses.

In automotive, it is expanding PET-based wire covering paper that withstands temperatures above 200 degrees Celsius for electric and combustion vehicles, a line the company says it has supplied since its first sales in 2017.

On November 2, 2026, the company announced a memorandum of understanding to acquire a battery and semiconductor production-equipment maker, which received court approval from the Daegu Rehabilitation Court.

That target has supplied energy storage system battery production equipment to LG Energy Solution, and the company says related sales make up about 70% of its total. A cooperation with SM Group's battery affiliate SM Vexel is also under review.

Separately, the company is pursuing a merger with an iron-ore producer that press reports describe as the only domestic iron ore producer, and it added 30 business purposes including mining and civil construction.

Media describe the paper industry as a declining business, so the success of this shift will depend on results from the equipment and mining segments.

04

Earnings analysis

On filed annual results, revenue fell from KRW 112.5 billion in 2022 to KRW 80.7 billion in 2023 (down 28.3% year on year) and KRW 56.4 billion in 2024 (down 30.2%), then rebounded to KRW 67.8 billion in 2025 (up 20.3%). Among 2022 to 2025, 2022 was the largest year and 2024 the smallest.

The operating loss narrowed each year, from KRW 11.1 billion in 2022 to KRW 7.44 billion in 2023, KRW 1.29 billion in 2024 and KRW 495 million in 2025, and the operating margin improved from -9.8% to -9.2%, -2.3% and -0.7%. The company recorded losses for four consecutive years from 2022 through 2025.

The net loss attributable to controlling shareholders widened from KRW 18.5 billion in 2022 to KRW 21.3 billion in 2023, then narrowed to KRW 13 billion in 2024 and KRW 1.86 billion in 2025, and the consolidated net loss was filed at the same amounts.

Operating cash flow was negative at -KRW 11.5 billion in 2022, -KRW 907 million in 2023 and -KRW 13.5 billion in 2024, then turned positive at KRW 3.59 billion in 2025.

The debt-to-equity ratio fell from 167.1% in 2022 to 11.7% in 2025, and at the end of 2025 total equity of KRW 119.9 billion was entirely attributable to controlling shareholders, against total liabilities of KRW 14 billion.

On a quarterly basis, revenue moved from KRW 15.8 billion in the third quarter of 2025 to KRW 15 billion in the fourth quarter, peaked at KRW 16.7 billion in the first quarter of 2026, then fell to KRW 15.3 billion and KRW 14.3 billion over the next two quarters.

Operating profit of KRW 25.6 million in the third quarter of 2025 turned into a KRW 601 million operating loss in the fourth quarter, narrowed to KRW 504 million and KRW 200 million in the first two quarters of 2026, and widened to KRW 480 million in the third quarter, which was a swing back to loss from the prior-year quarter.

The net loss attributable to controlling shareholders was largest in the fourth quarter of 2025 at KRW 3.48 billion, and the last four quarters total KRW 4.28 billion.

Press reports attribute the weakness partly to lower profit in the main Yongin specialty paper segment and losses in the distribution and processing segment, which fits the deterioration visible in the filed results.

05

Industry analysis

The company's core functional and specialty papers, such as tobacco-related paper and interleaf paper, serve defined end uses. However, the media describe the general paper business as a declining industry, and the company itself cites that backdrop for diversifying.

In an AlphaSquare comparison of nine paper and wood sector stocks (annual basis, collection date not stated), Kuk-Il Paper MFG ranks near the top by market capitalization but near the bottom by revenue. Other listed companies in the same sector include Hansol Paper, Hankuk Paper MFG and DaelimPaper.

The equipment target sits in the battery and semiconductor equipment market, which depends on capital spending by large battery cell makers, and it has supplied ESS battery production lines for customers including LG Energy Solution.

Automotive wire covering paper is used in both electric and combustion vehicles, and the company says the ability to withstand high-temperature processing and demanding use environments is the key competitive factor.

The mining entry route is the merger with the iron-ore producer that press reports describe as the only domestic iron ore miner. Overall, the company's position combines a mature traditional paper demand base with early-stage uncertainty in its new businesses.

06

Outlook

Among the company's announced plans, three items are confirmed. First, a merger absorbing the iron-ore producer was announced in September for effect from November 24, 2026.

Second, at the November 10, 2026 extraordinary shareholder meeting, the addition of 30 business purposes, including mining development and processing and civil and construction work, passed with 89.2% of issued shares in favor, and a plan for holding and disposing of treasury shares was also approved.

Third, the acquisition of the equipment maker followed a memorandum and court approval on November 2, 2026, with a definitive agreement targeted for mid-November after due diligence.

A company official said the remaining acquisition steps will proceed on schedule to build ESS and semiconductor equipment into a new growth engine. For automotive wire covering paper, the company plans to expand sales through more supply to existing customers and new customer development.

However, News Tomato reported that no clear turnaround in results has appeared even after past mergers, and that market concern runs ahead of expectations. The company also stated that the final decision and terms of the acquisition depend on the outcome of due diligence and the definitive agreement.

If both the merger and the acquisition close, the consolidated asset and profit structure could change, so later financial disclosures will show how much the new businesses contribute. The filed results so far reflect the paper-centered structure before those businesses are included.

Bull factors

Narrowing losses and a cash flow turn

The operating loss narrowed from KRW 11.1 billion in 2022 to KRW 495 million in 2025, and the operating margin improved from -9.8% to -0.7%. Operating cash flow turned positive at KRW 3.59 billion in 2025. The filed figures showing a narrowing loss structure form the core of the bullish case.

Diversification into ESS equipment and automotive wire covering paper

The company says related sales make up about 70% of the equipment target's revenue, which has supplied ESS battery production equipment to LG Energy Solution. Its PET wire covering paper withstands temperatures above 200 degrees Celsius and is expanding into electric and combustion vehicle applications. Moving beyond a single paper business is the core of the bullish argument.

A low-debt balance sheet

Total liabilities were KRW 14 billion at the end of 2025, and the debt-to-equity ratio fell from 167.1% in 2022 to 11.7% in 2025. Total equity of KRW 119.9 billion is entirely attributable to controlling shareholders, with no non-controlling interest burden. Financial capacity to pursue the merger and acquisition is also cited on the bullish side.

Bear factors

Four straight quarters of operating losses and falling sales

The company recorded operating losses in each of the four quarters from the fourth quarter of 2025 through the third quarter of 2026, and the net loss attributable to controlling shareholders totaled KRW 4.28 billion over the last four quarters.

Revenue fell for two straight quarters in the second and third quarters of 2026. The third-quarter 2026 operating loss of KRW 480 million marks a swing from a small operating profit a year earlier.

Short-term borrowing from an affiliate and funding pressure

News Tomato reported that the company borrowed KRW 25 billion in short-term funds from SM Group affiliate SM Hi-Plus in October 2026, with a repayment date set for the following day in the board minutes. The report said an annual rate of 6.3% applied. A company official declined to disclose details, citing confidentiality.

Proving the results of the merger and acquisition

News Tomato reported that market concern runs ahead of expectations for the merger with a loss-making mining affiliate. According to the report, profit fell in the main Yongin specialty paper segment, and the distribution and processing segment posted losses. The company still needs to show that the merger and acquisition produce measurable results.

Risk factors

Customer concentration and capex dependence

Related sales account for about 70% of the equipment target's revenue, and its main supplier relationship is with ESS battery production lines at LG Energy Solution.

Demand for battery equipment is set by the capital spending of large cell makers, so if the customer's capacity expansion schedule slips, equipment segment sales after the acquisition could fall short of expectations.

Because revenue is concentrated in one customer and one product line, earnings may remain volatile until the customer base is diversified.

Execution of the merger and acquisition

The acquisition of the equipment maker still depends on due diligence and definitive agreement terms, so the final outcome is not settled. The merger with the iron-ore producer is scheduled for November 24, 2026, and it could change consolidated profit and financial structure afterward. The contribution of the merged business is not yet reflected in the filed results.

Automotive product customer expansion

The company plans to grow automotive PET wire covering paper through more supply to existing customers and development of new customers.

High-temperature processing and durability in demanding use environments are cited as key competitive factors, so delays in customer qualification or supply expansion could limit the effect of the business shift.

The material is used in both electric and combustion vehicles, but the timing of new customer adoption has not been set.

Overall view

Kuk-Il Paper MFG recorded operating losses every year from 2022 to 2025, but the loss narrowed from KRW 11.1 billion in 2022 to KRW 495 million in 2025. Operating cash flow turned positive at KRW 3.59 billion in 2025, and the debt-to-equity ratio fell to 11.7%.

In 2026, however, revenue fell for two straight quarters after peaking in the first quarter, and the net loss attributable to controlling shareholders totaled KRW 4.28 billion over the last four quarters.

On the business side, the pursuit of an equipment acquisition, a planned merger with an iron-ore producer and an expanded business purpose list are moving the company from a paper-centered structure toward equipment and mining.

The results of this shift are not yet reflected in the filed numbers and will depend on the merger's effective date and the outcome of the definitive acquisition agreement. Media have also raised concerns about short-term borrowing from an affiliate and the execution of the merger.

Valuation is hard to read because the P/E cannot be calculated, and the P/B sits well above the sector average, so interpretation will depend on whether the business shift produces measurable results.

The next checkpoints are the fourth-quarter and full-year operating loss in the 2026 annual report and the change in consolidated profit after the merger.
