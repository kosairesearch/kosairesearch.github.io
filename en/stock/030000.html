Report overview

Ad-Market Slowdown, Non-Affiliate Push and Shareholder Returns at Cheil

Cheil Worldwide's revenue and operating profit fell year on year in the third quarter of 2026, and the company is pointing to non-affiliated client growth and a 60% payout policy as its two pillars of earnings defense.

  1. 1

    On a disclosed basis, 2025 revenue was KRW 4.55 trillion (up 4.7% year on year) and operating profit was KRW 336.9 billion (up 5.0%), the largest year of 2022 to 2025.

  2. 2

    Third-quarter 2026 revenue was KRW 1.04 trillion, down 12.6% year on year, and operating profit was KRW 77 billion, down 19.7%. The company cites lower client marketing budgets and a stronger won.

  3. 3

    Non-affiliated clients accounted for 29% of third-quarter 2026 gross profit, up 2.2 percentage points year on year.

  4. 4

    The company retired its treasury shares equal to 12% of issued shares and plans to buy back and cancel about KRW 140 billion of new shares through September 2027.

  5. 5

    Brokerage target prices were cut. Meritz Securities lowered its target to KRW 23,500 in November 2026 while keeping a Buy rating, and Samsung Securities lowered its target to KRW 21,000 in September 2026 and moved to Hold.

02

Business structure

Cheil Worldwide is a global marketing solutions company spanning digital, retail and BTL (below-the-line) advertising, and ATL (above-the-line) advertising. Its third-quarter 2026 service mix was 55% digital, 29% retail and BTL, and 16% ATL, and the 2025 annual mix had the same composition.

Digital revenue rose 10% in 2025 as the company expanded into dotcom and commerce. Because production subcontract costs are large in advertising, the company separately reports gross profit as its performance measure. Headcount at end-2025 was 7,993, up from 7,433 at end-2024, of which 6,454 were overseas.

Results are closely tied to the advertising spending of major affiliates, which makes the company sensitive to client budget changes. Non-affiliated clients made up 29% of third-quarter 2026 gross profit, up 2.2 percentage points year on year.

New clients included Amazon Ads, Temasek, Carlsberg, Kakao Commerce and Subway. Key hubs such as North America and Latin America maintained year-on-year growth.

Listed peers in the sector include Innocean Worldwide, and the competitive question is how well the company wins non-affiliated clients as affiliates tighten ad budgets.

04

Earnings analysis

On a disclosed basis, revenue was KRW 4.25 trillion in 2022, KRW 4.14 trillion in 2023 (down 2.7%), KRW 4.34 trillion in 2024 (up 5.0%) and KRW 4.55 trillion in 2025 (up 4.7%). Revenue has risen for two consecutive years since the 2023 low, and 2025 was the largest year of 2022 to 2025.

Operating profit was KRW 311.4 billion in 2022, KRW 307.5 billion in 2023 (down 1.2%), KRW 320.7 billion in 2024 and KRW 336.9 billion in 2025. The operating margin moved from 7.3% in 2022 to 7.4% in 2023 and stayed at 7.4% in 2024 and 2025.

Net profit attributable to owners of the parent was KRW 187.3 billion in 2023, rising to KRW 207.5 billion in 2024 (up 10.8%) and holding at that level in 2025. Net income including non-controlling interests, a separate figure, was KRW 208.6 billion in 2025. Gross profit for 2025 rose 8% to KRW 1.86 trillion.

The debt-to-equity ratio rose to 125.9% in 2025 from 122.7% in 2023, and operating cash flow fell from KRW 346.4 billion in 2024 to KRW 212.3 billion in 2025. Quarterly operating profit dropped sharply to KRW 36.5 billion in the first quarter of 2026 and recovered to KRW 92.7 billion in the second quarter.

Third-quarter operating profit fell 19.7% year on year, which the company attributed to reduced marketing budgets at major clients and a stronger won. Nine-month 2026 revenue was KRW 3.13 trillion, down 6.45%, and nine-month operating profit was KRW 206.2 billion, down 16.35%. Net profit attributable to owners of the parent for the last four quarters totaled KRW 200.2 billion.

05

Industry analysis

The advertising cycle is in a phase where major clients are controlling marketing spend and external growth has slowed. Meritz Securities said the slow recovery in the ad market and clients' control of marketing spending slowed top-line growth.

Major affiliates were reported to have tightened their ad budgets to spend more efficiently. Analysts linked this to higher memory prices lowering profitability at the MX division, which pushed affiliates to focus more on cost control.

As ad budgets shrink, TV and digital media spending and campaigns tied to new product launches are expected to decline. The company is defending declining traditional media with D2C dotcom and retail advertising, although some analysts think this alone cannot fully offset lower volumes.

The business is digitally centered, so changes in ad execution shift the revenue mix directly. Against listed peers such as Innocean Worldwide, the company's position is best gauged by its non-affiliated client wins and the pace of overseas growth.

The cycle position depends on client marketing spend and affiliate variables such as memory prices, so the timing of an industry recovery should be read alongside those two factors.

06

Outlook

The company plans to defend earnings by expanding its non-affiliated client base. It intends to keep D2C-related businesses such as digital, retail and BTL as core pillars.

It retired all of its treasury shares, equal to 12% of issued shares, at a book value of KRW 282.8 billion, and plans to buy back and cancel about KRW 140 billion of new shares through September 2027. The 60% payout ratio based on consolidated net income is also to be kept through 2027. External forecasts diverge.

Meritz Securities expects 2026 operating profit of KRW 284.6 billion, down 15.5%, and KRW 303.1 billion in 2027, up 6.5%. Meritz Securities lowered its target price from KRW 26,000 to KRW 23,500 and said on November 10, 2026 that it maintained its Buy rating.

Samsung Securities, in September 2026, lowered its target price from KRW 23,500 to KRW 21,000, and cut its rating from Buy to Hold. The gap between the 2026 decline and the 2027 recovery forecast is the point where views on the timing of an earnings rebound differ. The company's parallel investment in AI and other new businesses also shapes its medium-term earnings path.

Bull factors

Shareholder returns policy maintained

The 60% payout ratio based on consolidated net income is to be kept through 2027. The company plans to buy back and cancel about KRW 140 billion of new shares through September 2027. Samsung Securities was cited as viewing this policy as a support for the share price.

Rising non-affiliated client share

Non-affiliated clients made up 29% of third-quarter gross profit, up 2.2 percentage points year on year. New clients included Amazon Ads, Temasek, Carlsberg, Kakao Commerce and Subway. The company presents this expansion as the core of its earnings defense.

Overseas hubs and digital mix

Key hubs including North America and Latin America kept growing year on year in the third quarter of 2026. Digital accounts for the largest share of services at 55%. Digital results rose 10% in 2025.

Bear factors

Third-quarter revenue and profit both declined

Third-quarter 2026 revenue was KRW 1.04 trillion, down 12.61% year on year. Operating profit was KRW 77 billion, down 19.7%, and net profit attributable to owners of the parent was KRW 44.9 billion, down 30.7%. The company attributed the decline to marketing budget efficiency at major clients and a stronger won.

Dependence on affiliate ad spend and a weak market

Results are closely tied to the ad spending of major affiliates. Meritz Securities said the slow ad-market recovery cut domestic gross profit 3.2% to KRW 97.8 billion. Higher memory prices, which lowered profitability at the MX division, were also cited as a variable for ad spending.

Expected 2026 profit decline and target cuts

Meritz Securities expects 2026 operating profit of KRW 284.6 billion, down 15.5%. Samsung Securities said in September 2026 that it lowered its target price to KRW 21,000, and Meritz Securities also cut its target to KRW 23,500.

10

Risk factors

Dependence on affiliate ad budgets

Analysts identified the main cause of weak results as reduced marketing spending at major affiliates. Lower launch advertising by affiliates could reduce TV, digital and campaign volumes. Analysts also expect D2C and retail ads alone to fall short of fully offsetting lower total volume.

Currency and overseas hubs

The company itself cited a stronger won as a factor in the third-quarter operating profit decline. Overseas hubs continue to grow, but with a large overseas business, currency moves affect won-denominated results. Whether regional growth persists is the buffer against this risk.

New-business investment and personnel costs

Lower affiliate ad volumes coincide with new-business investment and personnel costs, which pressure profitability. Headcount rose to 7,993 at end-2025 from 7,433 at end-2024. If workforce investment does not align with a revenue recovery, margin pressure could persist.

12

Overall view

Cheil Worldwide's revenue and operating profit rose for two consecutive years through 2025, but both fell in the third quarter of 2026, with revenue down 12.6% and operating profit down 19.7%. The company attributes this to reduced marketing budgets at major clients and a stronger won.

Strengths include the 29% non-affiliated share of gross profit, the 60% payout policy maintained, treasury share cancellation and a plan to buy back about KRW 140 billion of new shares.

Weaknesses include reliance on affiliate ad spending, a slow ad-market recovery, the forecast 2026 profit decline and target price cuts. Brokers are split: Meritz Securities maintained its Buy rating, while Samsung Securities moved to Hold.

The next checkpoints are the 2026 full-year preliminary results in early 2027 and the 2026 annual business report due by late March 2027. How far the non-affiliated share rises from 29% will indicate whether the business mix is changing.
