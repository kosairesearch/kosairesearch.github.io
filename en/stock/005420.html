Report overview

Quarterly swings amid annual losses; battery recycling in focus

COSMO CHEMICAL CO recorded parent-shareholder net losses for three straight years through 2025, while its 2026 quarterly results have alternated between profit and loss as its battery recycling supply chain takes shape.

  1. 1

    Sales in 2025 were KRW 664.2 billion, down for two consecutive years from 2024 to 2025, and the company posted an operating loss of KRW 16 billion, its second straight year in the red.

  2. 2

  3. 3

  4. 4

02

Business structure

COSMO CHEMICAL CO operates two businesses: titanium dioxide (TiO2) and battery materials. Press coverage describes it as the only company in Korea producing titanium dioxide, a white pigment.

The battery materials business makes battery-grade cathode inputs such as cobalt sulfate, nickel sulfate and lithium carbonate, and the company says it was the first in Korea to mass-produce cobalt sulfate.

Based on the 2025 first-quarter quarterly report, titanium dioxide sales were about KRW 35.5 billion and battery materials sales were about KRW 10 billion, so titanium dioxide carries the larger revenue share. The battery materials unit produces nickel and cobalt sulfates from both ore and recycled feedstock.

In February 2026 the company signed an MOU with a large Indonesian precursor producer to discuss supplying roughly 2,000 tons a year of nickel and cobalt sulfates for 2026 to 2030, with a value of up to KRW 200 billion at current prices.

The MOU is a letter of intent that presumes a binding main contract, and specific trading terms will be set in a separate agreement.

On September 14, 2026 the company agreed with Pino, a domestic precursor maker, to build a closed loop: black mass and black powder secured and pretreated in Europe are smelted in Korea to recover nickel, cobalt and lithium, which are then supplied back as precursor and cathode feedstock.

Securing spent battery feedstock for continuous plant input and buyers for the recovered metals determines the viability of the recycling business. The company is classified as a core listed affiliate of the Cosmo Group.

04

Earnings analysis

On a disclosed basis, annual sales rose from KRW 718.2 billion in 2022 to a peak of KRW 799 billion in 2023, then fell two consecutive years from 2024 to 2025, to KRW 749.3 billion (down 6.2%) and KRW 664.2 billion (down 11.4%). Among 2022 to 2025, 2023 was the largest year and 2025 was the smallest.

Operating profit dropped from KRW 41.4 billion in 2022 to KRW 3.01 billion in 2023 (down 92.7%), and the company turned to an operating loss of KRW 2 billion in 2024. The operating loss widened to KRW 16 billion in 2025, and the company has been in the red for two consecutive years from 2024 to 2025.

Operating margin moved from 5.8% in 2022 to 0.4% in 2023, -0.3% in 2024 and -2.4% in 2025.

Parent-shareholder net profit went from KRW 11.2 billion in 2022 to a net loss of KRW 26.8 billion in 2023, widened to a net loss of KRW 64.7 billion in 2024, and narrowed to a net loss of KRW 30.3 billion in 2025, a three-year run of losses from 2023 to 2025.

Net profit including non-controlling interests, a different figure, was a net loss of KRW 31 billion in 2025. Operating cash flow went from -KRW 27.7 billion in 2024 to KRW 121.7 billion in 2025, turning positive.

Quarterly, the pass-through of higher nickel and cobalt prices into selling prices was reported to have shown up in first-quarter results immediately. Operating profit was KRW 5.99 billion in the first quarter of 2026 and KRW 7.64 billion in the second quarter, up 27.4% from the prior quarter.

In the second quarter nickel and cobalt sales volume was reported to have risen about 12% from the first quarter, while higher sulfuric acid prices increased the manufacturing cost burden on battery materials such as cobalt sulfate.

The third quarter swung to an operating loss of KRW 4.7 billion, though the loss narrowed from a year earlier. The cumulative parent-shareholder net loss over the last four quarters is KRW 19.2 billion.

05

Industry analysis

Battery materials earnings are sensitive to metal prices such as nickel and cobalt and to raw materials such as sulfuric acid. Coverage of the first-quarter 2026 provisional results stressed that higher metal prices passed quickly into selling prices.

By contrast, the second quarter brought a reported rise in cost burden from higher sulfuric acid prices, even as volumes grew. End demand comes from electric vehicle and energy storage supply chains, which the company says it has supported.

Because battery materials follow metal price cycles, earnings may respond differently when prices rise than when they fall. In spent battery recycling, securing feedstock such as black mass is central, so competition for raw material supply is a variable.

The titanium dioxide unit depends on demand for general-purpose white pigments used in paints and plastics, which moves with the economy. Being the only domestic producer shows a position in domestic supply, while price competition with overseas producers is a separate variable.

Compared with large listed petrochemical companies in the chemicals sector, the company is smaller in scale but concentrated in specific materials, namely battery inputs and white pigments.

Outlook

The company's identifiable plans fall into three areas. The quarterly report filed in May 2025 said a nickel plant expansion of about 2,000 tons is scheduled for completion by the end of December 2026.

The same filing also described an additional spent battery recycling plant of about KRW 70 billion, approved by the board in August 2023 and scheduled for completion by the end of December 2027.

Second, the MOU with the Indonesian precursor producer is still at the discussion stage, so whether a binding main contract is signed and on what terms will determine whether volumes materialize.

Third, the Pino arrangement links European black mass sourcing, domestic smelting and supply back to Pino, so the volume of feedstock secured and the buyers for recovered metals are the key points to watch. Whether the nickel expansion lifts utilization after December 2026 is another item for upcoming results.

Because the third quarter was an operating loss, the timing at which expansion and contract effects appear in operating profit will show in later quarters. The 2026 annual report is the next periodic filing, and it will show full-year operating margin and battery materials sales. Whether the additional recycling plant starts on schedule in late 2027 will indicate the pace of expansion.

08

Bull factors

Recycling-based closed supply loop

The Pino tie-up forms a loop in which European black mass is smelted domestically and recovered metals return as precursor feedstock. The company produces cobalt sulfate, nickel sulfate and lithium carbonate through its own hydrometallurgical process, and it can now link raw material supply with buyers of the products. Adding the Indonesian MOU would widen the supply geography, which supporters cite as a strength.

Pass-through of metal prices

Reports said higher nickel and cobalt prices passed into selling prices and showed up in results immediately. Operating profit was positive in both the first and second quarters of 2026, and the third-quarter operating loss narrowed from a year earlier.

If selling prices track metal prices, the company could see a faster earnings rebound when metals rise, which is the bullish argument.

Sole domestic titanium dioxide base

Being the only domestic producer of titanium dioxide, a white pigment gives the company scarcity in domestic supply. In the first quarter of 2025 the titanium dioxide unit's sales were larger than those of the battery materials unit.

If that unit generates steady cash, it can absorb part of the investment burden for battery materials expansion and recycling plants, which is a bullish point.

Bear factors

Three straight years of parent-shareholder losses

Parent-shareholder net losses persisted from 2023 through 2025, and the net loss of KRW 64.7 billion in 2024 was the largest over that span. The operating loss widened from KRW 2 billion in 2024 to KRW 16 billion in 2025.

The third-quarter 2026 operating loss returned the company to red ink, so profitable quarters have not yet strung together, which is a bearish factor.

Cost pressure and price mismatch

Coverage of the second-quarter 2026 provisional results pointed out that higher sulfuric acid prices raised the manufacturing cost of battery materials such as cobalt sulfate, which weighed on profitability. Volumes rose, yet cost pressure pulled margins down. If raw material costs rise before selling prices adjust, margins are squeezed, which is the bearish argument.

Falling sales and thin operating margin

Sales peaked at KRW 799 billion in 2023 and then fell for two consecutive years, while the operating margin was -2.4% in 2025. Quarterly sales rose in 2026, but operating results alternated between profit and loss, so durability is still hard to judge.

The Indonesian MOU is a letter of intent that presumes a binding main contract, so the timing of any revenue contribution is uncertain.

10

Risk factors

Raw material and metal price swings

Nickel and cobalt prices and sulfuric acid prices act on both selling prices and costs. When pass-through is quick, earnings improve, but when costs rise first, as with higher sulfuric acid prices, the manufacturing burden grows. Recycling feedstock and recovered metal prices can move together, which could raise earnings volatility.

Execution risk in expansion

In recycling, securing spent battery feedstock to feed the plant and buyers for recovered metals determines viability. The nickel expansion is slated for December 2026 and the additional recycling plant for December 2027, so schedule slippage could prolong fixed cost burdens. The Indonesian MOU has no confirmed terms until a main contract is signed.

Balance sheet risk

Total liabilities at the end of 2025 were KRW 584.1 billion, roughly equal to total equity of KRW 584.3 billion. Parent-shareholder equity was KRW 219.8 billion, and the debt ratio fell from 103.9% in 2024 to 100.0% in 2025 but remains around 100%.

If losses continue, how much parent-shareholder equity erodes from accumulated losses is the measure of financial room.

Overall view

COSMO CHEMICAL CO runs two businesses, titanium dioxide and battery materials, with sales and operating profit declining through 2025 and parent-shareholder net losses persisting for three consecutive years.

In 2026 the quarters have been mixed, with an operating profit in the first quarter, a wider operating profit in the second and an operating loss in the third. On the positive side, the Indonesian MOU and the Pino recycling agreement signal wider access to feedstock and sales outlets.

On the negative side, the MOU lacks binding force, raw material costs such as sulfuric acid weigh on margins, the last four quarters total a KRW 19.2 billion net loss and the debt ratio stands at 100.0%.

The share price trades above book value while the price-to-earnings ratio cannot be calculated, so the durability of the earnings recovery is the core reference point.

Next checkpoints are whether the nickel expansion is completed by the end of December 2026, the provisional fourth-quarter 2026 results in early 2027 and the full-year operating margin in the 2026 annual report.
