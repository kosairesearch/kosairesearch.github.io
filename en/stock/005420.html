02

Business structure

Founded in 1968, Cosmo Chemical is a core affiliate of the Cosmo Group, which traces its lineage to the LG Group, and is the only domestic producer of titanium dioxide (TiO2).

The TiO2 business spans general-purpose pigment grades used in paints, plastics, and paper, as well as specialty grades for fiber, food, and electronics applications, and the company has been shifting its product mix toward a larger specialty share.

The other pillar is battery materials, where Cosmo Chemical was the first in Korea to localize mass-production technology for cobalt sulfate, extracting nickel, cobalt, lithium, and manganese from both raw ore and recycled battery scrap.

Its key customers are domestic cathode material makers such as EcoPro BM and L&F, and in 2026 it signed a cobalt sulfate supply contract worth about KRW 15.47 billion with EcoPro BM.

The TiO2 segment holds a domestic monopoly position but competes globally against large chemical producers including Kronos Worldwide, Tronox, and Lomon Billions Group.

In battery materials, the company faces an ongoing challenge in maintaining its position as a raw-material supplier amid vertical integration trends among precursor and cathode producers.

Starting in 2026, Cosmo Chemical is pursuing a strategy to broaden its raw-material supply base in the Asian battery value chain through a partnership with an Indonesia-based precursor manufacturer.

Refining byproduct-linked feedstock sourcing and group-affiliate supply ties are also cited as part of its cost competitiveness.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual sales rose 11.2% from KRW 718.2 billion in 2022 to KRW 799 billion in 2023, the largest figure in the 2022-2025 span, before falling for two consecutive years to KRW 749.3 billion in 2024 (down 6.2%) and KRW 664.2 billion in 2025 (down 11.4%), making 2025 the smallest year in the period.

Operating profit plunged 92.7% from KRW 41.4 billion in 2022 to KRW 3.01 billion in 2023, turned into an operating loss of KRW 2 billion in 2024, and widened further to a loss of KRW 16 billion in 2025, marking two straight years of operating losses in 2024-2025.

Controlling-interest net income turned into a loss of KRW 26.8 billion in 2023, widened to a loss of KRW 64.7 billion in 2024, then narrowed to a loss of KRW 30.3 billion in 2025, extending losses for three consecutive years from 2023 to 2025.

The operating margin declined from 5.8% in 2022 to negative 2.4% in 2025, underscoring a multi-year erosion in profitability. Still, 2025 operating cash flow rebounded sharply to KRW 121.7 billion from negative KRW 27.7 billion a year earlier, diverging from the income-statement losses.

On a quarterly basis, sales bottomed at KRW 152 billion in the third quarter of 2025 before rising for three consecutive quarters through the second quarter of 2026, reaching KRW 206.1 billion, up 16.4% year over year.

Operating losses persisted and widened from the second through fourth quarters of 2025, before the company turned to an operating profit of KRW 5.99 billion in the first quarter of 2026 and grew it 27.4% quarter over quarter to KRW 7.64 billion in the second quarter, also marking a year-over-year swing to profit.

Controlling-interest net income likewise turned positive at KRW 2.62 billion in the first quarter of 2026 but fell 75.1% quarter over quarter to KRW 652 million in the second quarter, and the trailing four-quarter sum (Q3 2025 through Q2 2026) still stood at a net loss of KRW 19.4 billion.

05

Industry analysis

The global titanium dioxide market is expected to grow at a moderate annual rate of roughly 4% to 7%, tied to paint and construction cycles, with Asia-Pacific remaining the largest demand region.

At the same time, intensifying price competition among Chinese producers has caused regional pricing to diverge, with some reports citing simultaneous demand softness and oversupply concerns in various markets.

A case in which Tronox and other global majors raised product prices by 2% to 4% in the first quarter of 2026 suggests that supply-side discipline is still at work in parts of the market.

The battery materials segment tracks EV and ESS demand growth, but nickel and cobalt price volatility and the push by precursor and cathode makers toward raw-material self-sufficiency shape the supply-demand structure.

Cosmo Chemical holds a monopoly position domestically in TiO2 but remains a mid-sized player in global comparison, while in battery materials its combination of waste-battery recycling with ore-based production is cited as a point of differentiation.

Volatility in sulfuric acid prices, driven by geopolitical conditions, has been a direct cost burden on TiO2 production.

06

Outlook

Starting from 2026 through 2030, the company signed an MOU with an Indonesia-based precursor producer to discuss supplying roughly 2,000 tons annually of nickel and cobalt sulfate, with specific commercial terms to be finalized through separate agreements.

In battery materials, it maintains its sales base through a cobalt sulfate supply contract with EcoPro BM running from January through December 2026.

In TiO2, after completing facility maintenance at the end of 2025, the company has been raising utilization while planning price increases to pass through higher sulfuric acid costs.

It is also pursuing a strategy to increase the production share of specialty-grade TiO2, and management has stated an intention to maintain a full-year profit trend. However, many of these plans have not yet translated into binding contracts or confirmed results, warranting quarter-by-quarter verification of execution.

Some analyses suggest that the swing to profit in the first and second quarters of 2026 partly reflected a temporary lagging effect from input costs, meaning cost pressure and the speed of price pass-through will be key to profitability from the second half onward.

08

Bull factors

Two Consecutive Quarters of Operating Profit

Operating profit reached KRW 5.99 billion in the first quarter of 2026 and grew 27.4% quarter over quarter to KRW 7.64 billion in the second quarter. This marks a clear reversal from the losses recorded from the second through fourth quarters of 2025.

Sales also rose for three consecutive quarters after bottoming in the third quarter of 2025, reaching KRW 206.1 billion in the second quarter of 2026, up 16.4% year over year.

Expanding Battery Materials Supply Network

The company signed an MOU with an Indonesian precursor producer to discuss nickel and cobalt sulfate supply for 2026 through 2030. It also maintains an annual cobalt sulfate supply contract with EcoPro BM for 2026, broadening its domestic and overseas sales base.

While not yet a binding definitive agreement, this reflects an attempt to expand its footprint in the Asian battery value chain.

Marked Improvement in Operating Cash Flow

2025 operating cash flow improved sharply to KRW 121.7 billion from negative KRW 27.7 billion a year earlier. Even though the income statement still showed an operating loss and net loss in 2025, cash generation rebounded.

This can be interpreted as reflecting changes in working capital management, including inventory and receivables.

09

Bear factors

Two Straight Years of Sales Decline and Multi-Year Losses

Sales peaked at KRW 799 billion in 2023 and declined for two consecutive years to KRW 664.2 billion in 2025. Operating results posted losses for two straight years in 2024-2025, and controlling-interest net income recorded losses for three consecutive years from 2023 to 2025.

While quarterly profitability returned in 2026, offsetting the accumulated multi-year losses on an annual basis remains an ongoing task.

Trailing Four-Quarter Net Loss Persists

While controlling-interest net income turned positive in the first and second quarters of 2026, second-quarter profit fell 75.1% from the prior quarter. Summing the trailing four quarters from the third quarter of 2025 through the second quarter of 2026 still shows a net loss of KRW 19.4 billion.

Whether the quarterly swing to profit has become a stable trend requires confirmation from further quarterly results.

Exposure to Raw Material Price Volatility

Sulfuric acid, a core TiO2 feedstock, has seen price volatility driven by geopolitical conditions that has fed through into cost burdens. The battery materials segment is also structured such that quarterly profitability can swing significantly with nickel and cobalt price movements. Whether planned price increases can offset actual cost increases has not been confirmed.

10

Risk factors

Raw Materials / Cost

Sulfuric acid prices used in TiO2 production can fluctuate sharply with geopolitical conditions, creating recurring cost pressure. Battery materials also carry a cost structure linked to nickel and cobalt metal prices. If price increases fail to keep pace with rising costs, margin pressure could resurface.

Contract and Order Uncertainty

The partnership with the Indonesian precursor maker remains at the non-binding MOU stage, with specific commercial terms yet to be finalized. The cobalt sulfate supply contract with EcoPro BM is also an annual agreement whose renewal each year is uncertain.

If precursor and cathode makers expand in-house raw material production, the company's position as an external supplier could weaken.

Financial Structure and Capital Adequacy

The debt-to-equity ratio stood at 100.0% at the end of 2025, with total liabilities and total equity at similar scale. Controlling-interest equity was KRW 219.8 billion out of total equity of KRW 584.3 billion, meaning a substantial portion is non-controlling interest.

Multi-year accumulated losses could affect capital capacity, warranting continued monitoring of the financial structure.

Overall view

After two consecutive years of declining sales and multi-year operating and net losses through 2025, Cosmo Chemical turned to operating profit and controlling-interest net profit for two straight quarters in the first and second quarters of 2026.

However, second-quarter controlling-interest net income fell 75.1% from the prior quarter, and the trailing four-quarter sum still shows a net loss of KRW 19.4 billion, meaning further quarters are needed to confirm whether the swing to profit is a stable trend.

Operating cash flow improved markedly to KRW 121.7 billion in 2025, diverging from the income statement's loss trajectory.

Price increases and the expansion of specialty-grade products in TiO2, along with supply-chain expansion in Indonesia and domestically in battery materials, stand out as key variables for future results.

Volatility in sulfuric acid, nickel, and cobalt prices, along with uncertainty over contract renewals, remain persistent bearish factors. Investors should review the third-quarter regular report and the concretization of key contracts alongside any investment decision.
