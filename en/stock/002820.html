02

Business structure

SUN&L runs wood processing and living goods businesses. In 2021 it absorbed a subsidiary through a merger, adding the living goods segment, which produces and distributes plastic pump dispensers, trigger sprayers and cosmetic containers.

In the 2024 revenue-mix breakdown, wood windows, interior and furniture accounted for 60.80%, plywood, MDF, lumber and PB for 25.19%, and living goods such as pump dispensers for 21.14%, with an intercompany deduction of -7.72%.

In December 2023 the company decided to halt MDF and lumber production, shrinking its wood manufacturing base. Sales are concentrated domestically, at 90.2% in 2024 versus 9.8% from exports, and the highest export share belongs to living goods such as pump dispensers at 30.90%.

Business summaries cite rising eco-friendly product demand and a long-term supply contract with a North American customer as drivers of living goods export growth.

The interior segment is tied to the growing renovation market for older housing, but the decline in new apartment construction reduced both its top line and profitability. The company became the first in Korea's wood industry to obtain FSC-COC certification, in 2009.

The company attributed its 2025 decline to weak downstream markets, with lower sales and operating profit in wood distribution and interior. Wood and interior revenue depends heavily on construction cycles, while living goods depends on customer demand and export contracts.

Earnings analysis

Annual results combined falling revenue with continued losses. Revenue fell from KRW 506.7 billion in 2022 to KRW 441.1 billion in 2023 (down 13.0% year on year), KRW 339.3 billion in 2024 (down 23.1%) and KRW 272.4 billion in 2025 (down 19.7%), declining for three consecutive years from 2023.

Among 2022 to 2025, 2022 was the largest year by revenue and 2025 the smallest. The operating loss was KRW 17.7 billion in 2022, KRW 30.2 billion in 2023, KRW 5.86 billion in 2024 and KRW 17.9 billion in 2025, a four-year run of losses since 2022.

Operating margin was -3.5% in 2022, -6.8% in 2023, -1.7% in 2024 and -6.6% in 2025. The net loss attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 18.3 billion in 2022, KRW 160.9 billion in 2023, KRW 15.7 billion in 2024 and KRW 2.35 billion in 2025.

The 2023 net loss of KRW 160.9 billion far exceeded that year's operating loss of KRW 30.2 billion, so non-operating items may have weighed heavily.

Net loss including non-controlling interests was KRW 19.2 billion in 2022, KRW 159.2 billion in 2023, KRW 10.6 billion in 2024 and KRW 3.64 billion in 2025, a different figure from the controlling-shareholder measure.

At year-end 2025, total equity was KRW 222.9 billion, of which KRW 220.4 billion belonged to controlling shareholders, and total liabilities were KRW 250.2 billion.

Operating cash flow was -KRW 7.75 billion in 2022, -KRW 9.59 billion in 2023, -KRW 835 million in 2024 and -KRW 12.5 billion in 2025, a much wider deficit in 2025 than in 2024.

Quarterly revenue went from KRW 66 billion in Q3 2025 to KRW 57.8 billion in Q4 2025 (down 12.4% quarter on quarter) and KRW 50.6 billion in Q1 2026 (down 12.4%), then rebounded to KRW 59.5 billion in Q2 2026 (up 17.4%), and was KRW 59.4 billion in Q3 2026 (down 0.1% quarter on quarter, down 9.9% year on year).

Operating losses ran from KRW 6.85 billion in Q3 2025, KRW 6.08 billion in Q4 2025, KRW 2.77 billion in Q1 2026, KRW 1.05 billion in Q2 2026 and KRW 2.08 billion in Q3 2026, five straight loss quarters. The Q3 2026 loss widened from the prior quarter but narrowed from a year earlier.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 7.21 billion in Q3 2025, a loss of KRW 2.43 billion in Q4 2025, a loss of KRW 4.91 billion in Q1 2026, a profit of KRW 8.22 billion in Q2 2026 and a loss of KRW 1.75 billion in Q3 2026, and the last four quarters total a net loss of KRW 860 million.

Given that the Q2 2026 profit far exceeded that quarter's operating loss of KRW 1.05 billion, non-operating items appear to have contributed heavily, and details can be found in the notes to that quarterly report.

05

Industry analysis

Downstream demand is tied to construction activity. Business summaries attribute the slowdown in the interior segment to high interest rates, high prices and difficulty in project financing loans, which raised uncertainty in construction.

The decline in new apartment construction is cited as a factor that reduced both the interior segment's scale and profitability. By contrast, the renovation market for older housing is expanding and is cited as a medium-term demand base for the interior segment.

Rising demand for eco-friendly products supports exports in the living goods segment. Living goods exports to North America are growing, but a construction slump combined with the halt in wood production is cited as weighing on first-quarter 2026 results.

By industry classification the company sits with petrochemical-based chemical groups such as LG Chem and Hanwha Solutions, but its business centers on wood, building materials and cosmetic containers, so it is not directly exposed to the petrochemical cycle.

Obtaining FSC-COC certification in 2009, the first in Korea's wood industry, can be viewed as a point of differentiation in supplying eco-friendly materials. The cyclical position depends on whether signs of a construction recovery emerge, and the revenue direction is governed by construction activity.

Outlook

At the annual general meeting held on March 31, 2026, the financial statements, articles amendments and auditor appointment were all approved. The company said that business restructuring is showing signs of a turnaround, and it indicated a focus on lifestyle and beauty packaging.

The interior segment has growth drivers in the expanding renovation market for older housing, but the timing of a recovery in new construction depends on interest rates and project financing conditions.

On a quarterly basis, revenue recovered to KRW 59.5 billion in Q2 2026 and held at KRW 59.4 billion in Q3 2026, so whether that level marks a floor is the next task.
