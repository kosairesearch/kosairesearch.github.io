02

Business structure

Kukdong OIL & Chemicals was founded in 1979 and listed on the KOSPI in 1991 as a specialist in lubricants and petrochemicals. By revenue in the second quarter of 2026, petroleum wholesale and retail made up 52%, lubricants and related products 44%, asphalt 3%, and LPG and related products 2%.

The company estimated its share of the domestic lubricants market at roughly 10% in a May 2024 report, with SK group refining affiliates reportedly holding about half the market. Base oil, the main component of lubricants, is sourced 80% from S-Oil and 20% from overseas, according to the same report.

The company is S-Oil's largest partner in hydrogen development. Its Nanning-based Chinese subsidiary, which sells lubricants for tire manufacturing, is 100% owned. In 2025 the company set up two additional units to diversify its business.

Its subsidiary KD Tank Terminal, in which it holds a 51.28% stake as of year-end 2025, has been building a biodiesel plant with annual capacity of 100,000 tons in Ulsan.

A hydrogen plant in the same industrial complex, with hourly capacity of 92,000 N cubic meters, was reported in June 2024 as scheduled for completion in May 2026, with output mainly supplied to S-Oil's Shaheen project.

04

Earnings analysis

On a disclosed basis, revenue declined from KRW 1.24 trillion in 2022 to KRW 1.16 trillion in 2023 (down 6.8% year on year), KRW 1.06 trillion in 2024 (down 8.2%), and KRW 1.02 trillion in 2025 (down 3.9%). Operating profit fell from KRW 30.6 billion in 2022 to KRW 15.4 billion in 2025, a 32.5% drop in 2025 alone.

The operating margin slipped from 2.5% in 2022 to 2.4% in 2023, 2.2% in 2024 and 1.5% in 2025. A 2024 report noted the margin had hovered around 2.1% to 2.4% for several years and that high cost of sales limits profitability in this industry.

Profit attributable to controlling shareholders fell from KRW 20 billion in 2022 to KRW 1.13 billion in 2025, an 87.4% decline in the latest year. Net income including non-controlling interests was KRW 1.3 billion in 2025, a different measure that should be read separately.

Reports tied part of the pressure to cash needs for new businesses, and a September 2024 quarter saw net income drop 99.12% as funding for the biodiesel and hydrogen subsidiaries weighed. Financial structure weakened over the same period.

The debt ratio rose from 72.0% in 2022 to 116.2% in 2025, with year-end 2025 total liabilities at KRW 271 billion, total equity at KRW 233.2 billion, and equity attributable to controlling shareholders at KRW 205.3 billion.

Operating cash flow was -KRW 2.45 billion in 2022 and turned positive from 2023, reaching KRW 9.6 billion, KRW 23.9 billion and KRW 21.7 billion in 2023 to 2025.

Quarterly revenue bottomed at KRW 247.6 billion in the first quarter of 2026, then rose to KRW 257.8 billion in the second quarter (up 4.1% quarter on quarter) and KRW 284 billion in the third quarter (up 10.2%, and 8.6% year on year).

Operating profit jumped to KRW 13.1 billion in the second quarter of 2026 (up 137.7% quarter on quarter), then fell to KRW 6.11 billion in the third quarter (down 53.5%), though still 66.3% higher than a year earlier.

Profit attributable to controlling shareholders swung to a KRW 2.64 billion net loss in the third quarter of 2026, a move into the red year on year, and the last four quarters total KRW 7.41 billion. The cause of the second-quarter operating profit spike is a point to check against the quarterly filings.

05

Industry analysis

Lubricant demand is tied to domestic industrial operating rates and economic cycles. In a 2024 report, the company said lubricant earnings fluctuate each year but not by a wide margin, and that the segment is not a fast-growing one.

The company's market share is estimated at around 10%, while SK group refining affiliates are seen as holding about half of the market. Listed peers in the same sector include SK Innovation, S-Oil, Hankook Shell OIL, Michang OIL IND and Daewon.

On the supply side, roughly 80% of base oil comes from S-Oil, which concentrates sourcing in a single partner. On the other hand, long operating history has secured stable customers, and the company is viewed as having relatively stable earnings.

Large petrochemical companies came under pressure from oversupply tied to China, while the company argued that its focus on industrial lubricants sets its business apart. Crude oil prices have been hard to call, with uncertainty over US-Iran ceasefire talks cited as a variable for the energy market in a 2026 report.

With the operating margin hovering in the low 2% range for several years, any cyclical recovery in demand would still face limits from the cost structure.

06

Outlook

The company's growth push centers on two new businesses, biodiesel and hydrogen, as reported over the past two years. The hydrogen plant was reported in June 2024 as scheduled for completion in May 2026, with the Shaheen project at S-Oil named as the main outlet.

The biodiesel plant targeted completion in March 2025, with a total investment of KRW 469 billion reported in May 2024. A 2024 report said the biodiesel subsidiary had not yet generated meaningful operating cash flow.

The same report expected more capital injections before the subsidiary investment was complete, while also saying the company's strong cash generation should keep financial strain from deepening. How much these facilities contribute to revenue and profit after start-up is not yet visible in disclosed results.

After the third-quarter 2026 operating profit fell to KRW 6.11 billion and turned to a net loss attributable to controlling shareholders, whether profit recovers to the second-quarter level in the fourth quarter is the near-term test.

Crude oil volatility and base oil supply terms from S-Oil are also variables to watch. The next checkpoint is the 2026 annual report, where the operating status of the new facilities and the scale of capital spending should be laid out.

Bull factors

Signs of quarterly earnings recovery

Operating profit climbed from KRW 5.52 billion in the first quarter of 2026 to KRW 13.1 billion in the second quarter, and profit attributable to controlling shareholders rose from KRW 3.46 billion to KRW 7.87 billion over the same stretch.

Revenue has risen for two consecutive quarters since its first-quarter 2026 low. The last four quarters together total KRW 7.41 billion in profit, showing the trend is not a one-way decline.

Sustained operating cash generation

Operating cash flow was KRW 9.6 billion in 2023, KRW 23.9 billion in 2024 and KRW 21.7 billion in 2025, staying positive since 2023. Cash inflow from core operations continued while reported profit declined, which is a buffer against financial strain.

A 2024 report also took the view that the company's cash generation should keep financial pressure from deepening.

New facilities and a defined customer

The hydrogen plant targets supply to S-Oil's Shaheen project, giving it an identified customer. The biodiesel plant was designed for annual capacity of 100,000 tons. If both facilities run as planned, the business mix broadens and there is room for the operating margin to improve.

Bear factors

Three straight years of lower annual profit

Revenue has declined for three consecutive years from 2023 to 2025, and operating profit dropped from KRW 30.6 billion in 2022 to KRW 15.4 billion in 2025. Profit attributable to controlling shareholders fell to KRW 1.13 billion in 2025, down 87.4% from the year before. The operating margin declined from 2.5% in 2022 to 1.5% in 2025.

Return to a loss in the third quarter

Operating profit in the third quarter of 2026 fell to KRW 6.11 billion, down 53.5% from the prior quarter, and profit attributable to controlling shareholders swung to a KRW 2.64 billion net loss.

The company also posted a KRW 1.28 billion net loss in the fourth quarter of 2025, which points to weak quarterly stability. Whether the KRW 13.1 billion of the second quarter was a one-off remains a question for the filings.

Higher debt and funding needs for new businesses

As debt has grown, the burden of interest and the company's financial capacity have drawn more attention. A 2024 report pointed to loans to subsidiaries and participation in capital increases as adding strain.

Media reports on bribery and breach-of-trust charges, filed against the chief executive, have also raised governance concerns.

Risk factors

Financial and new-business investment risk

If new-facility investment continues with the debt ratio at 116.2%, financial headroom could narrow further. A 2024 report said several more capital injections were likely before the subsidiary investment was complete. If the facilities do not start on schedule, returns on the investment could be delayed.

Supplier and customer concentration risk

Roughly 80% of base oil, the main lubricant ingredient, comes from S-Oil, which concentrates raw material sourcing with one supplier. Hydrogen supply is also tied to S-Oil's Shaheen project, adding to dependence on a single counterparty. Any change in that partner's operations or contract terms would feed directly into the company's results.

Governance and crude oil price risk

Media reports of bribery and breach-of-trust charges against the chief executive keep governance in focus. A 2026 report noted that crude oil direction is hard to call amid uncertainty over US-Iran ceasefire talks. Oil price swings can affect inventory and margins in the petroleum distribution business.

12

Overall view

Kukdong OIL & Chemicals has seen revenue, operating profit and profit attributable to controlling shareholders decline across the disclosed years from 2022, and quarterly profit has swung sharply in 2026.

The second quarter showed a recovery, with operating profit of KRW 13.1 billion and profit attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 7.87 billion, but the third quarter reversed to KRW 6.11 billion and a KRW 2.64 billion net loss.

The debt ratio rose from 72.0% in 2022 to 116.2% in 2025, and reports have linked new-business investment to the financial strain. On the other side, operating cash flow has stayed positive since 2023, and the lubricant business rests on long-standing customers tied to industrial demand.

Heavy reliance on S-Oil for raw materials and hydrogen offtake is both a strength and a risk. The next checkpoint is the 2026 annual report due by late March 2027, where the annual operating margin, the debt ratio and the operating status of the new facilities will matter most.
