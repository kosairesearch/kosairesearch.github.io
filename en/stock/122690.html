02

Business structure

Seojin Automotive is an auto parts maker whose core products are clutch and automatic transmission (AT) parts for vehicle drivetrains. According to a 2024 report by E-Today, the revenue mix was AT parts 54%, clutches 44% and other products 2%.

In Q3 2024, consolidated revenue was split between the standalone company at 7%, subsidiary Eco Plastic at 50%, a second-tier subsidiary at 16% and another second-tier subsidiary at 12%, with other units at 15%.

The standalone company's main customers are Hyundai Motor and KIA, and it has its own sales network in the aftermarket service parts business. In 2024, standalone export revenue was larger than domestic revenue.

For electrification, hybrid parts orders were reported at KRW 1.1 trillion in 2022, with another KRW 150 billion added later and a KRW 150 billion PHEV parts order also reported. The company targets hybrid vehicles at 51% of total revenue in 2030, or 66% including electric vehicles.

After setting up a Mexico subsidiary, it expects duty-free benefits under USMCA for parts supplied to the US. In March 2026, it said it would amend its articles of incorporation to add secondary battery parts and materials and robot hardware and systems manufacturing. Peers in the same auto parts sector include Hyundai WIA, SNT Motiv and Sungwoo Hitech.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual revenue rose from KRW 2.09 trillion in 2022 to KRW 2.45 trillion in 2023 (up 17.1% year on year), KRW 2.59 trillion in 2024 (up 5.6%) and KRW 2.94 trillion in 2025 (up 13.6%), growing three years in a row from 2023 to 2025.

Over the same period operating profit peaked at KRW 73.8 billion in 2023 (up 59.1%), then fell to KRW 36.5 billion in 2024 (down 50.5%) and KRW 26.8 billion in 2025 (down 26.6%), declining two years in a row.

The operating margin slipped from 2.2% in 2022 to 3.0% in 2023, 1.4% in 2024 and 0.9% in 2025, so top-line growth did not carry through to profitability. In a March 2025 report, the company attributed the 2024 decline to costs tied to a first-instance ordinary wage lawsuit at its subsidiary Eco Plastic.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 7.1 billion in 2022, KRW 8.77 billion in 2023 (up 23.6%) and KRW 1.17 billion in 2024 (down 86.7%), then turned into a net loss of KRW 24.8 billion in 2025.

Total net income including non-controlling interests was KRW 38.3 billion in 2023, far above the controlling-shareholder figure, reflecting a large non-controlling share of earnings; it was a net loss of KRW 23 billion in 2025.

The debt-to-equity ratio stood at 392.8% in 2022, 378.1% in 2023, 476.8% in 2024 and 390.5% in 2025, and operating cash flow fell from KRW 90.8 billion in 2022 to KRW 46.6 billion in 2025.

Quarterly, operating profit of KRW 4.27 billion in Q3 2025 was followed by an operating loss of KRW 17.2 billion in Q4 2025, then a turn to operating profit of KRW 14.3 billion in Q1 2026 and KRW 23 billion in Q2 2026, up 60.3% from the prior quarter.

Q3 2026 revenue of KRW 720.9 billion rose 1.9% year on year, but the company posted an operating loss of KRW 5.75 billion, a swing back into the red. The detailed causes of quarterly swings can be traced through expense items in the quarterly reports.

05

Industry analysis

Seojin Automotive operates in an auto parts sector in transition, as drivetrains move from combustion engines toward hybrids and electric vehicles.

Clutch and AT parts are tied to transmission architecture, so demand persists while hybrids retain a transmission, but volumes of legacy items shrink as pure electric vehicles gain share. The company is responding by shifting toward electrification items such as HEV dampers and rotor parts.

Revenue in the first half of 2025 rose 16.2% year on year, with strong North American exports cited as the driver. On the vehicle side, Hyundai Motor and KIA volumes are central, so the company is exposed to automaker production and utilization.

Listed peers in the same sector include Hyundai WIA, SNT Motiv, Sungwoo Hitech, PIM Korea and Seoyon E-Hwa. Profitability can be compared on a consistent basis using each company's periodic reports.

Because a large share of standalone revenue comes from Hyundai Motor Group affiliates, allocation of group volumes affects results as much as sector conditions do.

06

Outlook

The company's stated plans center on expanding electrification items. It plans to produce HEV dampers and rotor parts and said it will relocate some lines at its Siheung plant to streamline operations and logistics.

Reports from 2023 described a KRW 150 billion PHEV damper supply deal with Hyundai Motor and a KRW 36.2 billion rotor shaft supply contract with a US electric vehicle maker. A June 2023 brokerage report cited a total parts supply contract worth KRW 1.03 trillion, running from January 2025 to December 2034.

Year-by-year revenue allocations in that 2023 report were estimates made at that time, so they should not be read as current figures; the actual contribution should be checked against revenue composition in periodic reports.

On the North American side, first-half 2025 revenue rose 16.2% year on year, and the company expects USMCA duty-free benefits through its Mexico subsidiary. Revenue growth slowed to 1.9% in Q3 2026, so whether new orders and exports translate into quarterly revenue is the key point to watch.

The revenue contribution from the new secondary battery and robotics businesses is not yet separable in reported results. The next checkpoint is the revenue mix and capital expenditure disclosed in the 2026 annual report.

08

Bull factors

A Growing Electrification Order Base

Reports put hybrid parts orders at KRW 1.1 trillion in 2022, with another KRW 150 billion added later and a KRW 150 billion PHEV parts order. A June 2023 brokerage report cited a KRW 1.03 trillion supply contract.

The plan to shift revenue from combustion parts toward hybrid parts lines up with this order flow, which is the core bullish argument.

North American Exports and the Mexico Subsidiary

First-half 2025 revenue rose 16.2% year on year, with strong North American exports cited as the driver. With the Mexico subsidiary in place, whether the USMCA duty-free benefit shows up in actual supply volumes is a point to watch alongside export growth.

Q3 2026 revenue growth of 1.9% means the export momentum still has to show up consistently in quarterly results.

Quarterly Profit Periods Have Appeared

Operating profit turned positive at KRW 14.3 billion in Q1 2026 and rose to KRW 23 billion in Q2 2026, up 60.3% from the prior quarter. Quarters of recovery have actually appeared, which is the bullish fact. Q3 2026 returned to an operating loss, so whether the recovery holds is the next point to check.

09

Bear factors

Losses Continue Through Q3 2026

The Q3 2026 operating loss of KRW 5.75 billion compares with an operating profit of KRW 4.27 billion in Q3 2025. Losses attributable to controlling shareholders were KRW 1.28 billion in Q2 2026 and KRW 14.8 billion in Q3 2026, and the last four quarters total KRW 31.2 billion. The KRW 17.2 billion operating loss in Q4 2025 shows how volatile quarterly results have been.

Dependence on Hyundai Motor Group and Combustion Parts

As of Q3 2024, Hyundai Motor affiliates accounted for about half of standalone-company revenue. Based on 2024 reporting, AT parts made up 54% of revenue and clutches 44%, so combustion-linked items remain a large share.

Because the pace of electrification depends on automaker volume allocation, changes in customer schedules can flow directly into results.

Financial Burden and Weaker Cash Flow

Total liabilities at the end of 2025 were KRW 1.62 trillion, with a debt-to-equity ratio of 390.5%. Equity attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 147.4 billion at the same date.

Operating cash flow fell from KRW 90.8 billion in 2022 to KRW 46.6 billion in 2025, and profit recovery has been limited since operating profit dropped to KRW 36.5 billion in 2024.

10

Risk factors

Pace of Electrification

A revenue mix centered on clutches and AT parts is sensitive to how fast drivetrains shift to hybrids and EVs. The company targets hybrids at 51% of revenue by 2030, but the timing of new item production and the speed of revenue recognition must be confirmed in disclosures. If new revenue does not offset declines in legacy items, the operating margin could keep falling.

Balance Sheet and Liquidity

The debt-to-equity ratio fell from 476.8% in 2024 to 390.5% in 2025 but remains high. With operating cash flow down to KRW 46.6 billion in 2025, how the company funds capital spending and debt repayment is a central question.

The fact that controlling-shareholder equity is only part of total equity also shapes how the balance sheet is read.

One-Off Costs and External Variables

Costs tied to a first-instance ordinary wage lawsuit at a subsidiary were cited as a factor in the 2024 profit decline. Expanded North American supply depends on USMCA origin requirements and exchange-rate movements. These external variables are hard for the company to control alone.

Overall view

Seojin Automotive's reported revenue rose three years in a row through 2025, but operating profit fell two years in a row from its KRW 73.8 billion peak in 2023 and the operating margin dropped to 0.9% in 2025.

A net loss attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 24.8 billion marked the swing into the red in 2025, and Q3 2026 returned to an operating loss of KRW 5.75 billion. Operating profit was positive in Q1 and Q2 2026.

Bullish factors include accumulated hybrid parts orders, strong North American exports and the duty-free expectation through the Mexico subsidiary. Bearish factors include dependence on Hyundai Motor Group, the high share of combustion-linked items, a 390.5% debt-to-equity ratio and falling operating cash flow.

The price-to-book ratio sits well below 1x, while a large part of equity belongs to non-controlling interests and the last four quarters show KRW 31.2 billion in net losses attributable to controlling shareholders.

The next checkpoints are the preliminary Q4 2026 results, the operating margin and debt structure in the 2026 annual report, and the Q1 2027 quarterly report.
