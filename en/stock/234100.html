02

Business structure

Polaris Sewon makes automotive air-conditioning parts and supplies them through global Tier-1 suppliers to major automakers, and its largest shareholder is Polaris Office with a 35.96% stake as of October 8, 2026.

The business is split into four segments: automotive air-conditioning parts, synthetic fibers and chemicals, F&C and AI convergence, and software platforms. A Q2 2026 sales breakdown put automotive parts at 36%, synthetic fibers at 34%, software platforms at 11%, F&C at 10% and IT at 8%.

Another corporate information site groups the segments differently, showing automotive air-conditioning parts at 35.64%, synthetic fibers at 35.34%, F&C and AI convergence at 18.32% and software platforms at 10.70%, so the weights depend on the classification method.

Key products include header condensers, radiator caps, bracket parts and EVAP units. A March 2023 report named Hyundai Motor, KIA, Tesla, GM, Ford, Rivian and Lucid among the customers.

Subsidiary Polaris UNO produces synthetic fibers for wigs and supplies markets in Africa and beyond, and Handysoft was consolidated into the group's results in the fourth quarter of 2025.

A March 2026 press report said the company supplies robotaxi parts to an autonomous-driving affiliate of a major global ICT company, but this was reported rather than confirmed by the company.

In air-conditioning and thermal parts, the company competes with large suppliers such as Hanon Systems, and it cites its experience supplying Hyundai Motor, EV startups and global automakers across multiple platforms as a strength.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual sales fell from KRW 176.7 billion in 2022 to KRW 160.4 billion in 2023, down 9.3% year on year, then rose to KRW 204.6 billion in 2024 (up 27.6%) and KRW 242.6 billion in 2025 (up 18.6%), growing two years in a row from 2024 through 2025. Sales in 2025 were the largest of 2022–2025.

Operating profit was KRW 8.64 billion in 2022 and KRW 7.36 billion in 2023 (down 14.8%), dropped to KRW 1.15 billion in 2024 (down 84.3%) and recovered to KRW 3.35 billion in 2025 (up 190.5%).

The operating margin ran at 4.9% in 2022, 4.6% in 2023, 0.6% in 2024 and 1.4% in 2025, so higher sales did not carry through to margin recovery.

Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 1.85 billion in 2022, KRW 4.3 billion in 2023 (up 132.6%) and KRW 8.04 billion in 2024 (up 86.8%), the largest of 2022–2025, before falling to KRW 420 million in 2025 (down 94.8%).

Net profit including non-controlling interests was KRW 2.97 billion in 2025, a separate figure from the controlling-shareholder number.

Quarterly sales were KRW 63.4 billion in Q3 2025, KRW 64.3 billion in Q4 2025 (up 1.5% quarter on quarter), KRW 54.5 billion in Q1 2026 (down 15.3%), KRW 62.4 billion in Q2 2026 (up 14.5%) and KRW 68.4 billion in Q3 2026 (up 9.7% quarter on quarter and up 7.9% year on year), with growth in two consecutive quarters since Q1 2026.

Operating profit moved to an operating loss of KRW 1.67 billion in Q4 2025, returned to a profit of KRW 1.18 billion in Q1 2026, then fell to KRW 1.01 billion in Q2 2026 (down 14.1%) and to KRW 79.6 million in Q3 2026 (down 92.1% quarter on quarter and down 96.9% year on year), declining for two consecutive quarters since Q1 2026.

Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 1.64 billion in Q3 2025, a net loss of KRW 2.23 billion in Q4 2025, a profit of KRW 1.95 billion in Q1 2026 and KRW 1.1 billion in Q2 2026, then a net loss of KRW 1.68 billion in Q3 2026 that turned into a loss year on year.

The company attributed the 2025 improvement to growth at key subsidiaries and a broader portfolio, while the causes of the Q3 2026 operating profit decline are a matter to check in the segment detail of the periodic report.

05

Industry analysis

In the end markets, the expansion of EV adoption and the shift toward eco-friendly vehicles are moving together.

A corporate information site reported that the spread of EVs is accelerating the growth of high-efficiency thermal management systems, and that wider adoption of eco-friendly vehicles in Korea is increasing the use of higher-value air-conditioning systems.

In a March 2026 announcement, the company said orders for hybrid (HEV) vehicle parts were growing quickly, and robotaxi-related supply expansion is presented as part of the same trend.

Thermal and air-conditioning parts depend heavily on technical difficulty and platform know-how, and the segment is highly competitive among large suppliers, with Hanon Systems as a key comparison point.

The company cites its experience supplying Hyundai Motor, EV startups and global automakers across varied platforms as a strength. A press report said this experience is viewed as favorable for compatibility with SDV-based integrated control.

Still, the timing of orders and revenue recognition depends on automaker production volumes and the pace of electrification, and the company's quarterly sales also declined quarter on quarter in Q1 2026.

Hyundai Mobis and Hyundai WIA are larger listed auto-parts companies with different business structures, so direct comparisons are limited.

06

Outlook

At its March 2026 earnings announcement, the company presented expanding hybrid vehicle parts orders and robotaxi-related supply as its growth pillars.

It said it had secured key parts supply rights for a next-generation electrified platform of a global automaker, with full mass production of that project slated from 2027.

In the same announcement the company said Handysoft's full-year results would be fully reflected on a consolidated basis, and that it expects larger 2026 sales and better profitability.

A corporate earnings comment reported that consolidated sales rose 1.6% year on year in the first half of 2026 while operating profit fell 9.3%.

Sales rose in Q2 and Q3 2026, but operating profit has declined for two consecutive quarters, so whether the profitability improvement the company described appears in quarterly results is the next point to check.

Revenue from new orders may build mainly after mass production starts in 2027, so the contribution could weigh more heavily on 2027 and later results than on 2026. Mass-production schedules and order volumes by customer come through periodic reports and timely disclosures.

The synthetic fiber business at subsidiary Polaris UNO depends on demand and the stabilization of its overseas production network, and its results can swing. Segment-level sales and operating margin changes in the 2026 annual report will give a firmer basis for assessing the company's outlook.

Bull factors

Growth in sales over two years

Sales rose to KRW 204.6 billion in 2024 and KRW 242.6 billion in 2025, growing two years in a row, and 2025 was the largest year of 2022–2025. Sales then rose in Q2 and Q3 2026 to KRW 62.4 billion and KRW 68.4 billion, extending the growth run for two consecutive quarters. The company describes subsidiary contributions and portfolio expansion as one driver of this growth.

Earnings recovery in 2025 and a Q1 2026 profit

Operating profit rose 190.5% in 2025 to KRW 3.35 billion from KRW 1.15 billion in 2024. After an operating loss in Q4 2025, operating profit returned to KRW 1.18 billion in Q1 2026, a quarterly sign of recovery. The operating margin improved from 0.6% in 2024 to 1.4% in 2025.

Electrification and robotaxi orders

In March 2026 the company said orders for hybrid vehicle parts were rising and robotaxi-related supply was expanding. It secured key parts supply rights for a next-generation electrified platform of a global automaker, and that project is slated for full mass production from 2027. The volume and revenue scale can be assessed in detail only once the periodic reports disclose them.

Bear factors

Q3 2026 operating profit plunged

Q3 2026 operating profit was KRW 79.6 million, down 92.1% from the prior quarter and down 96.9% year on year. Operating profit has declined for two consecutive quarters since Q2 2026, weakening after the Q1 2026 peak. Sales rose while profit fell, which points to margin pressure as the main weakness.

Net loss attributable to controlling shareholders

Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders fell from KRW 8.04 billion in 2024 to KRW 420 million in 2025, down 94.8%. A net loss of KRW 2.23 billion in Q4 2025 and a net loss of KRW 1.68 billion in Q3 2026 marked two swings into loss.

The cumulative net loss attributable to controlling shareholders over the last four quarters is KRW 869 million.

Thin margins and reliance on subsidiaries

The operating margin fell from 4.9% in 2022 to 0.6% in 2024 and was 1.4% in 2025. Much of the growth the company highlights depends on subsidiaries such as Polaris UNO and Handysoft. The profit contribution of the core automotive air-conditioning parts business is a point to separate out using segment disclosures.

Risk factors

Customer concentration and production timing

Results are sensitive to production volumes and model-change schedules at key automaker customers, given the high dependence on them. If the electrified platform's 2027 mass production is delayed, the effect of orders could reach revenue later. Robotaxi parts supply depends on volumes from a customer reported in the press, so the swing could be large.

Subsidiary and M&A dependence

The Polaris group has grown by acquiring subsidiaries through M&A. With Handysoft consolidated in Q4 2025, software results flow into consolidated profit alongside manufacturing, making it harder to isolate the core parts business. The largest shareholder, Polaris Office, held a 35.96% stake as of October 8, 2026.

Weaker profitability and small-cap characteristics

Net losses attributable to controlling shareholders over the last four quarters mean earnings-based multiples cannot be calculated. The small market capitalization can increase trading volatility, and share-price reactions to disclosed results can be large.

With operating profit down to KRW 79.6 million in Q3 2026, sustaining quarterly profitability is a key condition.

Overall view

Polaris Sewon is a group-affiliated company whose core is automotive air-conditioning parts, alongside synthetic fibers, software platforms and F&C segments. Annual sales of KRW 242.6 billion in 2025 were the largest of 2022–2025, and sales grew in both 2024 and 2025.

However, the operating margin fell from 4.9% in 2022 to 0.6% in 2024 and 1.4% in 2025, so sales growth has not fully carried through to profit. Operating profit recovered to KRW 3.35 billion in 2025, but in Q3 2026 it fell to KRW 79.6 million and net profit attributable to controlling shareholders turned into a loss.

On the bull side, hybrid parts, robotaxi-related supply and the electrified platform slated for 2027 mass production could form a medium-term revenue base. On the bear side, quarterly earnings instability and reliance on subsidiaries stand out.

On valuation, the price-to-earnings ratio cannot be calculated given net losses over the last four quarters, while the price-to-book ratio is well below 1 times, so readings differ depending on whether asset value or earnings power is weighted more.

The next points to check are the operating margin in the 2026 annual business report, whether profitability holds in Q1 2027, and the timing of electrified platform mass production. This report does not include a buy or sell opinion, and figures are based on DART periodic report disclosures.
