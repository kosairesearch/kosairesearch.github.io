02

Business structure

Company K Partners is a Korean corporate venture capital firm founded in October 2006 and listed on KOSDAQ in May 2019. The company invests in US-based founders and small and mid-sized venture companies, and its core activities are managing investment funds and holding stakes in portfolio companies.

Profit and loss for a venture capital firm arise mainly from revaluation of portfolio stakes and from exits such as sales or listings. Because of this structure, quarterly revenue and operating profit can move sharply depending on the timing of individual exits.

Total equity of KRW 82 billion at end-2025 was entirely attributable to controlling shareholders, while total liabilities were KRW 5.49 billion, so the balance sheet relies on equity rather than borrowing.

Compared with financial holding companies such as KB Financial Group and Shinhan Financial Group, or with Mirae Asset Securities, the company has a smaller deposit and fee base and depends on its investment portfolio.

Within the venture investment industry, portfolio size, exit track record and the mix of early- and late-stage investments are the main differences in results across firms. Venture exit conditions move with the IPO and M&A markets, so the business is closely tied to financial market cycles. Because the company is small, a single large exit can weigh heavily on annual earnings.

04

Earnings analysis

On a DART-filed annual basis, revenue was KRW 15.8 billion in 2022 and operating profit was KRW 6.29 billion. In 2023 revenue was KRW 15.9 billion, up 0.1%, but the company posted an operating loss of KRW 4.96 billion and a net loss attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 5.72 billion.

In 2024 revenue fell 5.0% to KRW 15.1 billion, the smallest year of 2022 to 2025, while operating profit returned to positive at KRW 3.26 billion and net income attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 2.09 billion. 2025 was the largest year of the period: revenue rose 43.3% to KRW 21.6 billion, operating profit rose 199.5% to KRW 9.77 billion, and net income attributable to controlling shareholders rose 246.3% to KRW 7.24 billion.

Operating margin moved from 39.7% in 2022 to -31.3% in 2023, 21.6% in 2024 and 45.2% in 2025, a swing driven by the timing of investment gains and losses on a small revenue base. Net income including non-controlling interests matched the attributable figure in every year.

Operating cash flow was KRW 4.48 billion in 2025, after negative KRW 2.3 billion in 2022 and KRW 8.09 billion in 2023. Quarterly swings are sharper. Revenue was KRW 4.14 billion in Q3 2025 and rose 120.6% quarter on quarter to KRW 9.12 billion in Q4 2025, when operating profit reached KRW 3.56 billion.

Q1 2026 operating profit of KRW 5.44 billion was the highest among the last five quarters. Q2 2026 revenue fell 86.6% quarter on quarter to KRW 1.05 billion, with an operating loss of KRW 3.98 billion and a net loss attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 3.15 billion.

Q3 2026 revenue of KRW 6.73 billion rose 62.8% year on year, operating profit was KRW 3.36 billion, up 75.8% year on year, and net income attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 2.64 billion.

Industry analysis

The venture investment industry creates its exit environment through the combined movement of new fund inflows, the IPO market and M&A deal volume. When exit markets are active, the likelihood of selling portfolio stakes rises, while in a downturn revaluation losses and delayed exits can appear at the same time.

Large financial holding companies in the same financial sector have broader recurring revenue bases, including net interest margin and fees, so their quarterly profit swings are relatively small.

Among securities firms, Samsung Securities and KIWOOM Securities have brokerage and asset management fee bases, which contrast with the company's investment-driven profit structure.

In Korea's venture ecosystem, policy funds and fund-of-funds commitments often support fund size, so a manager's access to capital affects its investment scale. Given its size, a revaluation change in one large investment can be significant for annual earnings at an equity base of KRW 82 billion.

Whether the 2025 earnings recovery came from sector conditions or from individual exits is a question that the investment asset detail in future periodic reports should help separate. The effect of sector conditions therefore depends on each firm's portfolio mix and exit history.

The profit direction since 2024 reads as a combination of industry conditions and company-specific factors, and the notes and investment asset schedules in periodic reports are where the two can be separated.
