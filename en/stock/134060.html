02

Business structure

e-future was founded on January 21, 2000 to develop internet English education content and software, and it listed on the KOSDAQ market on April 27, 2011.

The company focuses on ELT (English language teaching) content that aims to raise English proficiency from early childhood to adulthood, with young children and primary and secondary school students as its main customers.

Its business rests on two pillars: online education content services and the development and publishing of English textbooks.

Among the services the company presents are the phonics program Phonics Land and the English storybook program Galaxy Kids, and it publishes books covering phonics, course books, grammar and reading.

Its industry classification is textbook and study-book publishing, and its sales channels are wholesale distributors, franchise academies and individual consumers. It sells directly to large franchise academies, while it relies on wholesale distributors for end-consumer sales to keep costs down.

In large markets such as early-childhood and elementary English content, it develops its own products to replace imported content, and in smaller adult segments it imports and sells overseas publishers' products.

Overseas, it produces export textbooks tailored to local trends and expands its sales network through overseas trade fairs and English education conferences.

Unlike the platform and cloud companies in the same KOSDAQ IT and software classification, e-future's revenue comes from textbook and education content sales, which is the main structural difference.

04

Earnings analysis

On a reported basis, annual revenue fell from KRW 11.1 billion in 2022 to KRW 10.5 billion in 2023 (down 5.4% year on year), then recovered to KRW 11 billion in 2024 (up 5.0%) and KRW 11.1 billion in 2025 (up 1.0%). Revenue rose in 2024 and 2025 after the 2023 low, but the 2025 figure only returned to the 2022 level.

Profitability fell more sharply. Operating profit dropped from KRW 1.82 billion in 2022 to KRW 1 billion in 2023 (down 45.1%) and KRW 737 million in 2024 (down 26.4%), then rebounded to KRW 883 million in 2025 (up 19.9%). Operating margin moved from 16.4% in 2022 to 9.5% in 2023, 6.7% in 2024 and 7.9% in 2025.

Among 2022 to 2025, 2022 was the year with the largest operating profit and 2024 the smallest.

Profit attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 2.86 billion in 2022, KRW 1.38 billion in 2023 (down 51.6%), KRW 2.39 billion in 2024 (up 72.7%) and KRW 1.23 billion in 2025 (down 48.4%), and net profit including non-controlling interests was reported at the same amounts in each year.

On a quarterly basis, the company moved from an operating loss of KRW 222 million in the third quarter of 2025 to an operating profit of KRW 666 million in the fourth quarter, when revenue reached KRW 3.44 billion.

Operating profit then fell to KRW 125 million in the first quarter of 2026 (down 81.2% from the prior quarter), turned into an operating loss of KRW 179 million in the second quarter, and narrowed to an operating loss of KRW 30.29 million in the third quarter.

Third-quarter 2026 revenue of KRW 2.28 billion rose 1.5% year on year, but the operating loss continued. Whether the fourth-quarter 2025 revenue peak and the profit swing were linked depends on the cost structure, which needs to be checked in the periodic report text.

No one-off items are identified in the reported figures. Operating cash flow was KRW 2.35 billion in 2022, KRW 2 billion in 2023, KRW 2.12 billion in 2024 and KRW 2.54 billion in 2025, the highest of the four years.

05

Industry analysis

The English education market is structured around private academies, learning-material subscriptions and content production, and e-future sits in content production and textbook publishing.

Demand is concentrated among young children and primary and secondary school students, so changes in the school-age population directly affect medium- to long-term demand. A shrinking school-age population can limit the overall size of the market.

At the same time, competitive positions can shift depending on how well content differentiates itself on learning effectiveness even when the number of learners falls.

Anglo-American global publishers have long held strong positions in the ELT market, so domestic companies often focus on large early-childhood and elementary segments and use their own developed content to replace imported products.

The company emphasizes the product strength of content built around learners and aims to raise its in-house development share and extend to export textbooks overseas.

As online learning environments expand, whether digital education content can be combined with textbook publishing has become a competitive point in the industry.

Compared with IT and software peers in the same classification that are growing technology-based businesses such as cloud, platforms and security, e-future relies on content development capability and distribution channels, so its exposure to cycles works differently.

Its earnings volatility also reflects the tendency of small content companies to see quarterly results swing with new-product timing and sales seasonality.

Bear factors

Operating losses in 2026

Profit attributable to controlling shareholders turned to a KRW 344 million net loss in the third quarter, reversing a KRW 91.09 million profit a year earlier. The return to losses after the fourth-quarter 2025 profit is the main bearish point.

Lower profitability and still-low earnings

Stalled revenue and quarterly volatility

Annual revenue fell from KRW 11.1 billion in 2022 to KRW 10.5 billion in 2023 and only returned to the 2022 level in 2025. Quarterly revenue dropped 26.4% from KRW 3.44 billion in the fourth quarter of 2025 to KRW 2.54 billion in the first quarter of 2026. With growth not clearly visible and quarterly sales swinging, the predictability of results is low.

Risk factors

Competition

In the ELT market, Anglo-American global publishers such as Oxford, Cambridge and Pearson have held strong positions. Even when domestic companies compete with their own content, they may be at a disadvantage in brand trust and distribution scale. If online learning platforms spread further, textbook-based revenue could also be displaced.

Demand base and demographics

Because demand is concentrated among young children and primary and secondary school students, a shrinking school-age population could directly limit medium- to long-term demand. Changes in private education spending or in how parents invest in education could require adjustments to sales channels and product mix. With a narrow demand base, room for revenue expansion is limited.

Ownership concentration and liquidity

The largest shareholder, CEO Lee Ki-hyun, holds 28.64%, so ownership is concentrated in one individual. As a small-cap stock, the share price can move sharply depending on trading volume. Changes in the largest shareholder's stake or in the management lineup need to be checked through disclosures.
