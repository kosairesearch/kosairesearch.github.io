TBH Global: Online Growth and Inventory Efficiency Amid Quarterly Swings

02

Business structure

TBH Global is a KOSPI-listed fashion company that runs apparel brands including Mind Bridge and Basic House. Its flagship Mind Bridge grew 13.6% year on year in the first half of 2026, helped by an expanded womenswear line, and company-wide womenswear sales rose 14.7% over the same period.

Basic House recorded triple-digit sales growth after starting supply to Daiso in 2026. Online sales, combining external online malls and the company's own mall, reached KRW 40.4 billion in the first half of 2026, up 32.3% year on year, lifting online's share of total sales from 32.8% to 38.4%.

The company is strengthening a direct-to-consumer structure centered on its own mall. It is also widening its scope: it launched the athleisure brand rrig in July 2025 and added cosmetics manufacturing and sales to its business purpose at the March 2025 shareholder meeting.

Overseas, it liquidated its China subsidiary in 2022 and began liquidating its Malaysia unit at the end of 2024 to trim low-profitability units.

On production, a wholly owned subsidiary, TBH Global VN, runs a plant in Phu Tho province, Vietnam, with seven sewing lines in operation and capacity to expand to ten, according to the company. The plant also runs OEM production for other brands.

New brands include the licensed brand Time Out and the contemporary casual brand MARCBRICK, which is being prepared with a Chinese partner.

Competitively, the offline-heavy distribution structure and brand aging have been cited as burdens, while online and direct-sales capability and inventory efficiency are seen as points of differentiation.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual sales moved from KRW 203.1 billion in 2022 to KRW 197.1 billion in 2023 (down 2.9% year on year) and KRW 178.5 billion in 2024 (down 9.5%), before a modest recovery to KRW 182.4 billion in 2025 (up 2.2%). Among 2022 to 2025, 2022 was the largest year for sales and 2024 the smallest.

Operating profit rose from KRW 1.96 billion in 2022 to KRW 7.86 billion in 2023 (up 301.5%), then fell to KRW 3.36 billion in 2024 (down 57.2%) and KRW 3.15 billion in 2025 (down 6.3%), declining for two consecutive years from 2024 to 2025.

The operating margin was 1.0% in 2022, 4.0% in 2023, 1.9% in 2024 and 1.7% in 2025, so since 2024 it has stayed in the high-1% range to just under 2%. In 2025 sales grew but the margin slipped from 1.9% to 1.7%.

Owners' net income rose to KRW 6.74 billion in 2023 (up 36.7%), swung to an owners' net loss of KRW 724 million in 2024, and returned to owners' net income of KRW 885 million in 2025. Net income including non-controlling interests was KRW 1.55 billion in 2025, a different figure from owners' net income.

Operating cash flow went from -KRW 7.88 billion in 2022 to KRW 10.5 billion in 2023, KRW 6.5 billion in 2024 and KRW 12.9 billion in 2025, staying positive since 2023.

At the end of 2025, total equity was KRW 62.1 billion, of which KRW 60.8 billion belonged to owners, and total liabilities were KRW 42.2 billion; the debt ratio fell from 86.6% in 2022 to 68.0% in 2025.

Among the last five reported quarters, the fourth quarter of 2025 had the highest sales at KRW 58.6 billion and returned to operating profit of KRW 3.7 billion from an operating loss in the third quarter of 2025.

The company attributed the first-quarter 2026 operating loss of KRW 910 million to a big-bath effect from restructuring, including the discontinuation of a luxury business in 2025.

Operating profit returned to KRW 3.59 billion in the second quarter of 2026, while the third-quarter 2026 operating loss of KRW 294 million represents continued losses at a narrower level than a year earlier.

Owners' net income for the four quarters from the fourth quarter of 2025 through the third quarter of 2026 totaled KRW 3.64 billion.

05

Industry analysis

Fashion distribution is shifting toward online and direct-to-consumer channels, and TBH Global is strengthening its own mall and online channels to change its distribution structure. A distribution base heavily reliant on offline stores has been cited as a burden.

A December 2025 news report described athleisure as the only fashion category in Korea that has kept growing. TBH Global launched rrig in line with this trend, but the timing of athleisure's revenue contribution is still hard to gauge.

Inventory management is widely viewed as central to profitability in apparel, and the company's average inventory valuation allowance ratio in the first half of 2026 was 6.9%, down 1.45 percentage points from 8.35% a year earlier.

Seasonally, the fall-winter period is the industry's peak, and the company expects higher-priced items such as coats and light padded jackets from late November to support margins. Basic House's supply to Daiso is an example of expanding into channels beyond traditional fashion retail.

Exchange rates and financing costs are sector-wide variables; in the first half of 2026, non-cash losses from translating foreign-currency balances limited the growth of net income. By market capitalization, the company is a small-cap among listed apparel companies.

06

Outlook

The company is strengthening its brand and channel product lineups and sales strategies ahead of the fourth quarter, its peak season, and says it expects the improvement trend to continue through the fourth quarter.

From late November, sales of higher-priced winter items such as coats and light padded jackets are expected to pick up, which the company views as positive for overall margins as their share rises.

Mind Bridge has a target of KRW 150 billion in annual sales for a single brand, and the company said winter-product results could bring that goal forward.

The new brand Time Out is running an online pilot launch in four countries, Korea, Japan, the UK and China, in November 2026, and the company plans to grow it into another major brand.

In China, the company is preparing the contemporary casual brand MARCBRICK with local partner Duoyi, targeting a pop-up store in December 2026 and a permanent store from the first half of 2027.

The Vietnam plant also runs OEM work and is expected to contribute partly to consolidated results as early as the fourth quarter of 2026, with a long-term goal of producing 60% to 70% of its own-brand products there.

A news report said a higher share of in-house production should improve inventory forecasting and shorten distribution.

Exchange rates and financing costs are the key variables; the company said foreign-exchange losses and translation losses increased amid high oil prices and a strong dollar linked to the Middle East war.

The next checkpoint is the fourth-quarter and full-year 2026 results in the 2026 annual report, which will show how much the operating margin recovers and how much Vietnam OEM contributes.

08

Bull factors

Higher online and direct-sales mix

Online sales in the first half of 2026, combining external malls and the company's own mall, reached KRW 40.4 billion, up 32.3% year on year, and their share of total sales rose to 38.4%. External-mall sales grew 35.3% and own-mall sales grew 13.3%.

The company says a larger online share reduces distribution cost burdens and supports profitability. It cites the strengthened own-mall-centered direct-sales structure as the basis for this view.

Inventory efficiency and womenswear growth

The average inventory valuation allowance ratio in the first half of 2026 was 6.9%, down 1.45 percentage points from 8.35% a year earlier. Mind Bridge's womenswear sales rose 36.7% over the same period, outpacing the brand's 13.6% overall growth.

The company said inventory at the end of the third quarter of 2026 fell 10.3% year on year. Lower inventory burdens also ease the need for discount sales and inventory write-downs.

In-house production and new brands

The Vietnam plant has seven production lines running and can expand to ten. The company's long-term target is to produce 60% to 70% of its own-brand products there. The company expects new brands such as Time Out and MARCBRICK to broaden its medium-term growth base once they generate revenue. Completion of the overseas cleanup of inefficient units is also cited as part of the restructuring.

Bear factors

Volatile quarterly results

After an operating loss of KRW 1.52 billion in the third quarter of 2025, the company posted operating losses of KRW 910 million in the first quarter of 2026 and KRW 294 million in the third quarter of 2026.

Among the last five reported quarters, only two showed operating profit: the fourth quarter of 2025 and the second quarter of 2026. Profit and loss alternating from quarter to quarter makes the durability of earnings a weak point. The company said the first-quarter 2026 loss reflected a restructuring big-bath effect.

Thin operating margins

The operating margin was 1.0% in 2022, 1.9% in 2024 and 1.7% in 2025, staying in the 1% to 2% range. Sales in 2025 of KRW 182.4 billion remained below the KRW 203.1 billion of 2022. Sales grew in 2025, yet operating profit of KRW 3.15 billion fell 6.3% from the prior year. Slow margin recovery relative to sales recovery is a weak point.

Currency and financing cost pressure

Net income in the first half of 2026 grew less than operating profit because non-cash financing costs, including translation losses, rose amid high oil prices and a strong dollar linked to the Middle East war.

The company said the first-quarter 2026 owners' net loss of KRW 2.54 billion reflected one-off items such as foreign-exchange losses from the weaker won. Exchange-rate swings remain a source of volatility in net income.

Risk factors

Currency and financing costs

When the won weakens, foreign-exchange losses and translation losses arise and constrain net income. These costs are non-cash, but their size moves with exchange rates, which makes quarterly results harder to forecast. Both the first quarter and the first half of 2026 saw financing costs affect net income.

Execution of new businesses

Time Out and MARCBRICK depend on licensing and local partnerships, so results could vary with local market response. A December 2025 news report noted that the revenue contribution timing of the cosmetics business and the athleisure brand rrig is hard to gauge. If new businesses fail to offset weakness in existing brands, overall profit recovery could slow.

Seasonality and inventory dependence

Apparel earnings depend heavily on fall-winter sales, and the volume of higher-priced winter items largely determines fourth-quarter margins. If inventory forecasts miss, discounting and inventory write-down pressure can increase.

The lower inventory valuation allowance ratio in the first half of 2026 is a positive, but it may shift with seasonal demand.

Overall view

From 2022 to 2025, TBH Global's sales moved between KRW 178.5 billion and KRW 203.1 billion, and its operating margin stayed within a 1.0% to 4.0% range. Owners' net income returned to profit in 2025, and in 2026 the online sales share and inventory efficiency improved.

However, quarterly results have swung between profit and loss, with operating losses in the first and third quarters of 2026, and owners' net income for the last four quarters totaled KRW 3.64 billion.

Points in favor include online expansion, womenswear growth, inventory efficiency and the Vietnam in-house production base. Points against include a thin operating margin, financing costs tied to exchange rates, and uncertainty over when new brands will contribute revenue.

The company expects a fourth-quarter seasonal lift and Vietnam OEM contribution, which will be verified in the 2026 annual report. The next checkpoints are the fourth-quarter and full-year 2026 operating margin and the revenue scale of the new brands.
