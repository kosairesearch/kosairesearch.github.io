02

Business structure

Cenit was founded in 1995 and listed on KOSDAQ in 2000 as a maker of stainless steel thin plates. Its core product is cold-rolled stainless steel, made with precision thin-plate rolling for customers in electronics, construction, home appliances, automotive and industrial equipment.

Information sites estimate that the steel segment accounts for more than half of total revenue, but this is an outside estimate rather than a disclosed figure. Its ready-mixed concrete subsidiary, cinema operations and pharmaceutical testing services sit alongside the steel business.

Exports go to manufacturers and distributors in Europe, North America, the Middle East, Oceania and parts of Asia. Rather than competing with larger steel makers on scale, Cenit relies on specialization in precision thin gauges.

The company has said it aims to improve production and quality stability and raise profitability through old-facility upgrades, product development and sales expansion. Its new business, run through a subsidiary, extracts and recycles rare metals from spent catalysts used in oil refining.

Tighter environmental rules are expected to lift demand for spent catalyst treatment, which underpins the venture. The contribution of this business to consolidated results has not yet appeared in disclosed figures.

04

Earnings analysis

Revenue fell from KRW 160.1 billion in 2022 to KRW 140.4 billion in 2023 (down 12.3% year on year), KRW 132.4 billion in 2024 (down 5.6%) and KRW 127 billion in 2025 (down 4.1%), a three-year run of declines from 2023 through 2025.

Operating profit dropped from KRW 10 billion in 2022 to KRW 4.92 billion in 2023 (down 50.8%) and KRW 3.26 billion in 2024 (down 33.8%), then turned into an operating loss of KRW 1.08 billion in 2025. The operating margin slid from 6.2% in 2022 to 3.5% in 2023 and 2.5% in 2024, and reached -0.9% in 2025.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders fell from KRW 8.64 billion in 2022 to KRW 2.87 billion in 2023 (down 66.8%), turned into a net loss of KRW 910 million in 2024, and widened to a net loss of KRW 4.85 billion in 2025.

Net income including non-controlling interests is a separate measure, with a net loss of KRW 5.91 billion in 2025. Operating cash flow decreased from KRW 16.9 billion in 2022 to KRW 9.84 billion in 2023, KRW 3.43 billion in 2024 and KRW 3.13 billion in 2025, so cash generation weakened alongside earnings.

The debt-to-equity ratio rose to 127.5% at end-2025 from 100.9% in 2024, with total liabilities of KRW 124.7 billion against total equity of KRW 97.9 billion, of which KRW 83.4 billion belongs to controlling shareholders.

Quarterly operating profit was KRW 323 million in Q3 2025 and KRW 286 million in Q4 2025, before an operating loss of KRW 1.28 billion in Q1 2026. Q2 2026 returned to operating profit of KRW 726 million on revenue of KRW 36.5 billion, the highest of the last five quarters.

Q3 2026 revenue was KRW 35.8 billion, up 12.8% year on year, but operating profit fell to KRW 157 million (down 51.4%) and the controlling-shareholder net loss widened to KRW 1.41 billion.

A media report dated August 19, 2026 described a first-half return to operating profit driven by cost cuts and trading business expansion, but its basis may differ from the quarterly disclosures and should be checked.

05

Industry analysis

Cenit's core business depends heavily on demand for stainless steel sheet. The company's financial summary data points to weak demand in end-use industries as the background to its earnings decline.

End markets range from electronics and automotive to home appliances, construction and industrial equipment, but revenue swings widen when construction and auto and appliance demand weaken together.

Information-site analysis expects that if stainless market conditions stay weak and construction and cinema operations remain sluggish, a near-term earnings recovery could be difficult until the new business shows results.

On competition, one view holds that the shift toward higher-value stainless products favors Cenit's precision thin-gauge technology. Unlike Korea Zinc or Poongsan, which work mainly in non-ferrous metals, Cenit competes in the narrower niche of stainless thin plates.

The rare-metal venture rests on growing demand for these metals from advanced industries such as secondary batteries and semiconductors. Spent catalyst treatment demand stems from tighter environmental rules, so the market size will depend on how strict and how quickly those rules are applied.

06

Outlook

The company has outlined a plan to raise production and quality stability and profitability through old-facility upgrades, product development and more active sales. The return to operating profit in Q2 2026 was reported to reflect part of the cost-reduction effort.

Operating profit shrank to KRW 157 million in Q3 2026, so whether profitability holds quarter after quarter is the next checkpoint. The rare-metal venture is being pursued through a new subsidiary, and its scale, timing and contribution to consolidated results have not yet been specified.

Information sites say the new business is partly funded through exchangeable bond issuance, which eases financial strain but also leaves the possibility of future share supply.

For the core business, the key variable is whether end-use demand recovers, particularly construction, cinema operations and stainless market conditions. Full-year 2026 results will be confirmed through the preliminary release in early 2027 and the annual report.

Whether the new business starts to affect earnings in step with a recovery in core profitability is another item to track.

Information sites reported cost cuts and trading business expansion as drivers. The return to profit in one quarter may be read as an early sign of core-business recovery.

Rare-metal venture as an option

A subsidiary is pursuing rare-metal extraction and recycling from spent refinery catalysts. Expected tighter environmental rules and rising rare-metal demand from batteries and semiconductors form the backdrop. If it takes hold, it could become a revenue source separate from stainless steel.

Precision thin-gauge technology and export reach

The company holds precision thin-gauge rolling technology, and one analysis holds that the shift toward higher-value products favors such specialization. Its export reach spans Europe, North America, the Middle East and Oceania. When end-market demand recovers, a larger share of specialty products could widen the earnings improvement.

Overall view

Cenit is a KOSDAQ stainless steel thin-plate maker whose revenue declined for three consecutive years from 2023 through 2025. Operating profit went from KRW 10 billion in 2022 to an operating loss of KRW 1.08 billion in 2025, and the controlling-shareholder net loss persisted for two consecutive years in 2024 and 2025.
