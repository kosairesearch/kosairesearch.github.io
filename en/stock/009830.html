02

Business structure

Hanwha Solutions reports three segments: renewable energy, chemicals and advanced materials. In its Q3 2026 segment results, announced on October 29, 2026, renewable energy booked revenue of KRW 2.16 trillion and operating profit of KRW 105.1 billion.

Chemicals recorded revenue of KRW 1.34 trillion and an operating loss of KRW 44.7 billion, while advanced materials earned operating profit of KRW 38.9 billion on revenue of KRW 291.9 billion.

The solar business centers on module sales, supported by residential energy and development and EPC (engineering, procurement and construction) work. Production is concentrated in Georgia, USA, with a module plant in Dalton and an integrated plant in Cartersville covering ingot, wafer, cell and module.

A April 2026 report put the Cartersville plant's capacity at 8.4GW by 2026. In chemicals, the company produces about 150,000 tons a year of TDI, of which roughly 90% is exported, according to an April 2026 statement.

Advanced materials earns from lightweight composite orders from North American customers and steady solar materials sales. In solar, competition with Chinese modules on price is the main variable, while the US manufacturing base is cited as a differentiator under shifting policy. Chemicals remain sensitive to naphtha supply and international product prices.

04

Earnings analysis

Revenue moved from KRW 13.7 trillion in 2022 to KRW 13.3 trillion in 2023 (down 2.7% year on year) and KRW 12.4 trillion in 2024 (down 6.7%), before recovering to KRW 13.3 trillion in 2025 (up 7.6%). Among 2022 to 2025, 2022 was the largest year by revenue and 2024 the smallest.

Operating profit fell from KRW 966.2 billion in 2022 to KRW 604.5 billion in 2023 (down 37.4%), then turned into an operating loss of KRW 300.2 billion in 2024. The 2025 operating loss of KRW 364.8 billion widened, and the company has posted two consecutive years of operating losses from 2024 through 2025.

The operating margin slid from 7.1% in 2022 to 4.5% in 2023, -2.4% in 2024 and -2.7% in 2025. Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders went from KRW 359.1 billion in 2022 to a net loss of KRW 208.1 billion in 2023 and KRW 1.4 trillion in 2024, before narrowing to a net loss of KRW 650.4 billion in 2025.

Total net income including non-controlling interests, a separate figure, was a loss of KRW 615.3 billion in 2025. The operating loss widened in 2025 while the controlling-shareholder loss narrowed, so the gap should be traced to non-operating items in the income statement.

Operating cash flow turned from KRW 638.5 billion in 2024 to negative KRW 655 billion in 2025. By quarter, the company swung from an operating loss of KRW 489.7 billion in Q4 2025 to operating profit of KRW 92.6 billion in Q1 2026 and KRW 306.5 billion in Q2 2026, a 231.0% increase.

Q3 2026 operating profit of KRW 90.5 billion fell 70.5% from Q2. As announced on October 29, 2026, Q3 operating profit included an advanced manufacturing production credit (AMPC) of KRW 211.8 billion, and excluding it would leave an operating loss of KRW 121.3 billion.

05

Industry analysis

The US solar market is described as one where supply outpaces demand. Yuanta Securities, in a 2026 outlook report, estimated that US module demand would fall from 49GW in 2025 to about 40GW in 2026, while module capacity grows from 60GW to more than 73GW.

In an April 2026 earnings call, Hanwha Solutions said total US module demand is about 40GW a year, while US cell capacity would still be about 10GW short into early next year even if all planned projects are completed. The company argued that this gap should widen the premium for US-made cells.

On policy, tighter rules on Chinese modules and responses to indirect exports from Southeast Asia continue, and the company said module prices rose about 14% quarter on quarter in Q1 2026. As of late October 2026, the detailed measures under Section 232 were not yet finalized.

The chemicals business follows the petrochemical cycle: in Q2 2026 geopolitical tension lifted product prices and temporarily improved profitability, but in Q3 the geopolitical risk premium eased and crude prices fell, pushing product prices back into weakness, according to the company.

Among peers, Hanwha Solutions is cited for its US module, cell and ingot base, while price competition with Chinese manufacturers continues.

06

Outlook

On October 29, 2026, the company said Q4 may not improve on Q3 and that full-year operating profit is expected to fall short of its earlier guidance.

CFO Lee Jae-bin said module selling prices are expected to keep rising in Q4 on US trade policy changes, but noted that short-term earnings volatility is likely because of the lag in passing through prices and schedule and cost swings in EPC projects.

Q3 module shipments of 2GW came in below the 2.5GW guidance, and the company plans to sell stockpiled inventory once prices rise next year. Inventory clearance is expected to take until Q4 2026 or the first half of 2027, according to the company.

About 3-year module sales plans, the company said a small share is fixed-price, with a meaningful share of contracts able to pass through market price increases.

Section 232 is scheduled to take effect on December 4, 2026, introducing a minimum import price, and the company has referred to possible onshoring incentives for firms investing locally while staying cautious until details are finalized.

In April 2026 the company said it would raise KRW 2.4 trillion through a rights offering, using KRW 1.5 trillion to repay debt and KRW 900 billion for capital spending, with a target of a debt ratio below 150% and about 100% by 2030.

The capital spending includes KRW 100 billion for a perovskite tandem cell pilot line and KRW 800 billion to expand TOPCon capacity.

08

Bull factors

Renewable segment stays profitable

The renewable segment earned KRW 105.1 billion in operating profit in Q3 2026 (announced October 29, 2026), and the company said this extended a run of three profitable quarters. Higher US module shipments from the Georgia solar hub and better residential energy economics supported the result. The segment also swung to an operating profit of KRW 62.2 billion in Q1 2026.

US policy and local production edge

Industry and company commentary argues that the minimum import price under Section 232 will narrow the price advantage of Chinese modules. Hanwha Solutions operates production from ingot to module in the US, which is cited as a potential beneficiary of the policy shift.

The company's view that US cell capacity falls short of demand also supports the premium argument for locally made cells.

Next-generation solar technology

In April 2026 the company said it had secured efficiency certification for commercial-scale perovskite tandem cells and was preparing reliability certification. The funding plan announced the same month included KRW 100 billion for a pilot line.

Mass production and commercialization timing has not yet been confirmed, so the technology's value depends on certification and capital spending progress.

09

Bear factors

Chemicals losses return

The chemicals segment swung from KRW 87.1 billion in operating profit in Q2 2026 to a KRW 44.7 billion operating loss in Q3 2026. The company attributed this to weaker product prices and a lag effect from costly feedstock entering production. Dependence on Middle East naphtha, at about 70%, adds exposure to feedstock swings.

Q3 profit leans on tax credits

Q3 2026 operating profit of KRW 90.5 billion included an AMPC credit of KRW 211.8 billion. Excluding it, the operating loss would be KRW 121.3 billion, based on the October 29, 2026 announcement. Within renewables, EPC and development asset sales posted losses and some projects faced construction delays. This sits alongside the company's warning that Q4 may not improve on Q3.

Inventory and balance sheet strain

Q3 module shipments of 2GW fell short of the 2.5GW guidance, and the company said inventory will take until Q4 2026 or the first half of 2027 to clear. The debt ratio was 196.3% at the end of 2025, and operating cash flow was negative KRW 655 billion that year.

Whether the rights offering is executed as planned and the debt-ratio targets are met will determine how much the balance sheet strain eases.

Risk factors

Policy risk

Detailed Section 232 measures were not finalized as of late October 2026, with implementation scheduled for December 4. US tax credits and onshoring incentives may change, altering how much they contribute to earnings. Depending on the outcome, expected price increases and reliance on tax credits could both shift.

Feedstock and geopolitical risk

The chemicals segment is exposed to sharp product price moves driven by Middle East tensions and crude prices. Tension lifted prices and profits in Q2 2026, but in Q3 the easing risk premium pushed prices back into weakness. Naphtha supply and plant utilization also depend on how the company diversifies its feedstock sources.

Oversupply and project execution risk

Yuanta Securities projects US module capacity will significantly exceed demand. The company said some EPC projects are delayed and that electrical talent shortages linked to data center expansion are constraining execution. Such execution risks could delay inventory clearance and the benefit of higher prices.
