Report overview

Thinkware: Auto-electronics push meets three straight quarters of operating losses

Thinkware is trying to widen its black box-centered business into auto electronics and IP monetization, but operating losses in each of the first three quarters of 2026 make the timing of earnings recovery the central question.

  1. 1

    Operating losses have continued for three consecutive quarters from Q1 2026 to Q3 2026, and the cumulative controlling-shareholder net loss for the four quarters from Q4 2025 to Q3 2026 is KRW 41.3 billion.

  2. 2

    The decline in operating profit from its 2023 peak carried through to a controlling-shareholder net loss in 2025, which characterizes the recent earnings trend.

  3. 3

  4. 4

  5. 5

02

Business structure

Thinkware operates in three segments: home and living appliances, black boxes, and maps and platform services. According to the Q1 2026 quarterly report, segment sales were KRW 80.3 billion from home and living appliances (67.1%), KRW 36.0 billion from black boxes (30.0%) and KRW 3.4 billion from maps (2.9%).

Appliance sales centre on distributing Roborock robot vacuums, and in its Q3 2025 announcement the company said Roborock's domestic market share had expanded year on year, consolidating its position as the domestic leader.

Black box exports are the core of the overseas business, and in Q1 2026 they reached KRW 20.6 billion, effectively driving the company's total exports. Supply volumes to BMW Global more than doubled year on year in the first half of 2025.

A May 2026 press report described a supply contract with BMW Global worth about KRW 40 billion, with the range extending to cam systems, smart audio and switch controllers.

The maps business is widening its links to mobility markets such as automakers, telecom operators, taxi dispatch, substitute-driver and delivery services through its connected map platform. Its telecom-linked service Inavi WHERE and mobile map service Inavi Air are already commercialized.

Intellectual property monetization adds a further layer, with more than 2,000 domestic and overseas IP rights held as of Q1 2026. In competition, the black box market faces a variable from tighter US restrictions on Chinese-made products.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual results did not move in the same direction for sales and profit.

Sales rose from KRW 336.7 billion in 2022 to KRW 420.6 billion in 2023 (up 24.9% year on year), KRW 489.1 billion in 2024 (up 16.3%) and KRW 536.6 billion in 2025 (up 9.7%), with three consecutive years of growth from 2023 to 2025, and 2025 was the largest year of 2022 to 2025.

Operating profit rose from KRW 4.46 billion in 2022 to KRW 35.4 billion in 2023 (up 694.1%), the peak year, then fell to KRW 26 billion in 2024 (down 26.5%) and KRW 15.9 billion in 2025 (down 39.0%), a two-year decline.

The operating margin was 1.3% in 2022, 8.4% in 2023, 5.3% in 2024 and 3.0% in 2025, so margins fell in 2024 and 2025 even as sales grew. Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 15.6 billion in 2023 and KRW 10.9 billion in 2024 (down 29.9%), then turned into a net loss of KRW 25.4 billion in 2025.

Net profit including non-controlling interests also turned into a net loss of KRW 21.2 billion in 2025. Operating cash flow went from KRW 58.1 billion in 2023 and KRW 21 billion in 2024 to -KRW 40.5 billion in 2025.

At the end of 2025, total equity was KRW 217.3 billion, of which KRW 190 billion belonged to controlling shareholders, and total liabilities were KRW 152.6 billion. The debt-to-equity ratio fell from 88.5% in 2022 to 70.2% in 2025.

On a quarterly basis, Q3 2025 operating profit of KRW 3.51 billion and Q4 2025 operating profit of KRW 2.87 billion were the last profitable quarters at the operating level. Operating losses then ran for three consecutive quarters, from KRW 4.57 billion in Q1 2026 to KRW 5.11 billion in Q3 2026.

The company attributed Q1 2026 to delayed product supply that pushed sales into later periods, higher prices for semiconductor memory and other materials, and yuan strength.

In Q4 2025, the controlling-shareholder net loss of KRW 25 billion was far larger than the operating profit of that quarter, suggesting non-operating items weighed heavily. Q3 2026 sales were KRW 104.9 billion, down 21.6% from the prior quarter and down 20.5% year on year.

05

Industry analysis

The black box market sits at the intersection of two variables: the electrification of vehicles and US restrictions on Chinese-made products.

Industry observers see the rising share of in-vehicle electronics as the background to the company's strategic shift, and they note that cameras, sensors, audio equipment and control technology are becoming more important as the automotive industry moves toward software-centred design.

The same coverage read the push into future mobility businesses as a response to the possibility of slower growth in the black box market.

In an August 2026 report, the company said tighter restrictions on Chinese-made products in North America could allow its black boxes, already validated through automaker supply, to absorb related demand.

In appliances, the company cited the domestic share gains of Roborock robot vacuums and pointed to low robot vacuum penetration as a growth catalyst, as stated in its first-half 2025 results.

The company plans to extend its in-house vision AI, built on driving video data from its black boxes, to robotics and other industries.

Its patent position in autonomous driving is reflected in LexisNexis PatentSight+ rankings, where it was among the top ten Korean applicants in autonomous driving from 2019 to 2025 for seven consecutive years.

However, both black boxes and appliance distribution carry cost exposure to component prices and exchange rates, as the Q1 2026 results showed. Direct numerical comparisons with listed IT and software peers are not covered in this report.

06

Outlook

The growth agenda the company has laid out falls into three areas: auto electronics, IP and new businesses.

In auto electronics, the product range under the BMW Global supply contract is expanding to cam systems, smart audio and switch controllers, and the company has set a goal of becoming a global auto-electronics player.

In IP, it is pursuing monetization projects in autonomous and connected cars, IT and wearables, and mobility and drones, and says it aims to make this a core high-margin business over the medium to long term.

New businesses include dedicated black box supply to Mercedes-Benz Korea, ultra-compact black boxes for two-wheeled mobility, expanded online distribution of Xiaomi products, an AI-based taxi dispatch platform and overseas digital twin projects.

In Q2 2026, separate-basis operating profit turned positive at KRW 1.2 billion. The company said profitability had been hurt in the prior quarter by higher memory and other component prices, and that price adjustments for global supply and higher volumes were reflected in Q2.

Consolidated operating loss in the same quarter was KRW 3.55 billion, narrower than the KRW 4.57 billion in Q1 2026 but not yet profitable. The company says its AI black boxes, which apply Ambarella-based CV object recognition and deep learning, are commercialized.

Quantitative targets were not part of the material reviewed, so the path ahead will be judged mainly through reported results. The next checkpoints are full-year 2026 results and the operating margin and cash flow in the 2026 annual report.

Bull factors

Auto-electronics contract widens the business

According to a May 2026 press report, the company signed a supply contract with BMW Global worth about KRW 40 billion. By widening its range to cam systems, smart audio and switch controllers, it is moving from a black box-centred model toward an auto-electronics parts supplier. The company said BMW Global supply volumes more than doubled year on year in the first half of 2025.

North American rules and black box exports

In an August 2026 report, the company said tighter rules on Chinese-made products in North America could let its black boxes, already validated through automaker supply, absorb related demand. Black box exports reached KRW 20.6 billion in Q1 2026, effectively driving the company's total exports. The black box segment accounted for 30.0% of Q1 2026 sales, so the product base remains intact.

IP assets and business diversification

The company has filed 2,765 IP applications at five major patent offices worldwide and holds more than 1,580 registered patents. It was named among the top ten Korean applicants in autonomous driving for seven consecutive years, from 2019 to 2025, in an external patent-analysis ranking.

Based on this portfolio, it is pursuing IP monetization projects in IT and wearables, autonomous and connected cars, and mobility and drones.

Bear factors

Three straight quarters of operating losses

Operating losses were KRW 4.57 billion in Q1 2026, KRW 3.55 billion in Q2 and KRW 5.11 billion in Q3, a third straight quarterly loss. The Q3 loss was larger than the prior quarter.

The controlling-shareholder net loss was KRW 12.1 billion in Q3 2026, and the four-quarter total from Q4 2025 to Q3 2026 is KRW 41.3 billion.

Cost pressure and falling sales

Q1 2026 results reflected delayed shipments of some products that pushed sales into later periods, higher prices for semiconductor memory and other materials, and yuan strength. Q3 2026 sales were KRW 104.9 billion, down 20.5% year on year.

Industry observers flag the possibility of slower growth in the black box market, which makes the pace of top-line recovery in the core products an open variable.

Cash-flow deterioration and reliance on long-term projects

Operating cash flow turned to an outflow of KRW 40.5 billion in 2025, and the controlling-shareholder net loss was KRW 25.4 billion in the same year.

The company presents IP monetization as a medium- to long-term high-margin business, and the timing of its contribution to quarterly profit has not yet been specified in reported results.

Risk factors

Component costs and FX

Higher prices for semiconductor memory and other components, along with yuan strength, were directly reflected in Q1 2026 results. The company said price adjustments for global supply helped restore some profitability in Q2, but a renewed rise in input costs could reverse the separate-basis profit turn.

Execution of auto-electronics contracts

Delayed supply of some products pushed sales into later periods in Q1 2026. As the BMW Global product range widens, the ability to keep mass production and delivery on schedule will shape when revenue is recognized. Contract size and the pace of revenue recognition should be considered separately.

Financial capacity and cash flow

At the end of 2025, total liabilities were KRW 152.6 billion and total equity was KRW 217.3 billion, with a debt-to-equity ratio of 70.2%. However, operating cash flow turned to an outflow of KRW 40.5 billion in 2025, weakening the cash generated from operations. If operating losses continue on an annual basis, both the equity base and borrowing capacity could narrow.

Overall view

Thinkware is in a transition, moving its centre of gravity from black boxes, appliance distribution and map platforms toward auto electronics and IP.

Operating profit peaked at KRW 35.4 billion in 2023 and fell to KRW 15.9 billion in 2025, when the controlling-shareholder result turned into a net loss of KRW 25.4 billion. Operating losses have continued from Q1 to Q3 2026, and Q3 sales fell 20.5% year on year.

Positive factors include the supply contract with BMW Global worth about KRW 40 billion, black box exports, the IP portfolio, and the separate-basis operating profit in Q2 2026.

Negative factors include the continuing consolidated operating loss, component and yuan exposure, and the negative operating cash flow in 2025. On the financial side, the debt-to-equity ratio fell from 88.5% in 2022 to 70.2% in 2025, but the equity base should be read alongside lower earnings.

The next checkpoints are Q4 and full-year 2026 preliminary results, the operating margin and cash flow in the 2026 annual report, and how quickly BMW supply volumes flow into revenue.
