02

Business structure

Kolon Corporation is the holding company of the Kolon group under the Fair Trade Act, having converted to a holding structure through a spin-off in 2009.

Its consolidated subsidiaries include Kolon Global, the imported-car distribution group, Kolon Benit and Kolon TissueGene, while Kolon Industries and Kolon Life Science are equity-method affiliates.

The construction business sits mainly with Kolon Global, whose Q3 2026 construction revenue was KRW 624.5 billion with operating profit of KRW 31.6 billion, according to the company. Its leisure and asset management unit posted Q3 revenue of KRW 79.4 billion and operating profit of KRW 6.7 billion after a merger.

Kolon Global's cumulative new orders for 2026 reached KRW 1.78 trillion, with a growing share of high-tech projects such as Korean Air's engine maintenance facility and Tokyo Electron Korea's sites.

The distribution business held a 61% share of hybrid vehicles and 30% of electric vehicles in 2025 according to WiseReport. Kolon Industries covers chemicals such as transparent polyimide film, mPPO and phenol-epoxy resins, industrial materials including tire cord and aramid, and fashion.

The holding company's earnings depend on both consolidated subsidiaries and equity-method income from affiliates.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual revenue rose from KRW 5.66 trillion in 2022 to KRW 6.03 trillion in 2024 before declining 2.4% to KRW 5.89 trillion in 2025, with 2024 the largest year and 2022 the smallest in the 2022–2025 period.

Operating profit fell 66.9% to KRW 102.9 billion in 2023, turned into an operating loss of KRW 89.6 billion in 2024, and returned to a profit of KRW 60.5 billion in 2025. The operating margin stayed low, at 5.5% in 2022, 1.7% in 2023, -1.5% in 2024 and 1.0% in 2025.

Attributable net profit went from KRW 137.2 billion in 2022 to KRW 8.57 billion in 2023 and KRW 170.3 billion in 2024, then turned into an attributable net loss of KRW 158.5 billion in 2025.

Net profit including non-controlling interests was a loss of KRW 296.6 billion in 2025, which means the non-controlling share also absorbed losses.

In the quarters, Q4 2025 showed an operating loss of KRW 88.8 billion and an attributable net loss of KRW 111.7 billion, and Q1 2026 returned to operating profit of KRW 98.8 billion. Q2 2026 operating profit of KRW 99.8 billion edged up 1.1%, but Q3 2026 fell to KRW 41.4 billion, down 58.5% from the prior quarter.

Q3 revenue rose 20.4% year on year, while reports attributed the profit decline partly to slower new-car sales at the imported-car distribution group.

Attributable net profit of KRW 79 billion in Q3 rose sharply from a year earlier, and reports linked the net profit line to a derivative valuation gain at Kolon TissueGene.

Reports also tied the same kind of valuation swing to a net loss of KRW 44.7 billion in Q1 2026, so quarterly net profit can move independently of operating strength.

05

Industry analysis

In construction, a prolonged slump in the domestic building market has led Kolon Global to expand its non-housing and high-tech project mix. Its construction cost ratio fell from 96.8% in Q4 2025 to 89.5% in Q1 2026, 87.7% in Q2 and 87.4% in Q3, according to the company.

High-tech sites such as semiconductor and advanced manufacturing facilities recognize revenue as construction progresses, so execution speed and cost control matter for the segment. In materials, a late-2025 report said Chinese aramid capacity expansion had slowed a recovery in profitability at Kolon Industries.

Within the same business, transparent polyimide film, mPPO and phenol-epoxy resins lifted third-quarter 2026 results. The film and display business, hit by oversupply from China, became the subject of a formal sale review in May 2026.

In distribution, the hybrid and electric vehicle positions remain strong, but slower new-car sales weighed on operating profit in Q3 2026.

Because a holding company's results reflect the uneven performance of each subsidiary, the question of which business leads group earnings is the starting point for reading the numbers.

06

Outlook

Management's plans are presented subsidiary by subsidiary. Kolon Global said in May 2026 that it aims to make 2026 the first year of a earnings rebound, and it won KRW 985.4 billion in new orders in the first half, mainly in non-housing projects.

It plans to keep improving construction cost ratios and to be selective in bidding in the second half, and its cumulative new orders through Q3 reached KRW 1.78 trillion.

The imported-car distribution group plans to expand its sales and service infrastructure and to build an ecosystem covering new cars, used cars, after-sales service and data, through a recently acquired auction business.

Kolon Industries has set out a strategy to reshape its portfolio around AI and high-performance materials, with the film and display sale review as a starting point.

At group level, affiliate consolidation and restructuring continue, and the aerospace subsidiary is widening its reach into defense and space by supplying core parts to a private launch company.

The holding company's standalone net borrowings rose from about KRW 1.03 trillion at the end of 2025 to about KRW 1.11 trillion at the end of June 2026.

As a result, the debt repayment effect of any sale proceeds, mPPO mass production and cash generation, and changes in net borrowings are cited as markers of restructuring results.

Funding for gift taxes linked to a future ownership transfer, reported to run into several hundred billion won, is also cited as a task ahead.

08

Bull factors

Improving subsidiary operations

Kolon Global's construction cost ratio fell from 96.8% in Q4 2025 to 87.4% in Q3 2026. Kolon Industries grew its chemical business on higher sales of transparent polyimide film and mPPO. Better equity-method results lifted holding-company revenue to KRW 1.73 trillion in Q3, up 20.4% year on year. Bulls argue that sustained cost-ratio gains leave room for a higher operating margin.

A turn in quarterly profit direction

Attributable net profit turned from a KRW 111.7 billion net loss in Q4 2025 to KRW 13.6 billion in Q1 2026, then rose for two consecutive quarters to KRW 21.3 billion in Q2 and KRW 79 billion in Q3 2026. Bulls cite the move out of loss-making territory as evidence of earnings rebuilding. Whether this continues alongside operating profit growth is a separate question.

Portfolio reshaping and affiliate consolidation

The Kolon group cut its affiliates from 45 to 38 within a year. Kolon Industries formally began reviewing a sale of its film and display business in May 2026, a unit hit by oversupply from China. Bulls read this as a clear move toward profit-focused portfolio management. Whether sale proceeds go to debt repayment will determine how much the move matters.

09

Bear factors

Lower operating profit

Q3 2026 operating profit of KRW 41.4 billion fell 38.7% year on year and 58.5% from the prior quarter. Reports linked the decline partly to slower new-car sales at the imported-car distribution group. With the operating margin at 1.0% in 2025, revenue growth has not translated fully into profit. Bears see the key question as whether top-line expansion turns into profitability.

Volatile net profit

The four-quarter total of attributable net profit is only KRW 2.15 billion. Q3 net profit was boosted by a derivative valuation gain at Kolon TissueGene, and reports said a similar valuation swing pushed Q1 2026 into a net loss. Bears argue that net profit can move regardless of operating strength. They see the rebound as hard to treat as a sustainable trend while the underlying profit base is unclear.

High leverage and borrowing burden

The debt-to-equity ratio was 207.3% at the end of 2025, with total liabilities of KRW 5.02 trillion. Bears cite the rise in standalone net borrowings at the holding company between the end of 2025 and the end of June 2026 as evidence of the borrowing burden.

Operating cash flow turned positive only in 2025, at KRW 10.4 billion. Bears add that gift-tax funding linked to ownership transfer could further constrain financial flexibility.

10

Risk factors

Derivative and valuation swings

Derivative valuation gains and losses tied to Kolon TissueGene have swung quarterly net profit sharply. The Q3 2026 gain lifted net profit, while reports said a loss on the same instruments contributed to the Q1 2026 net loss.

Because operating profit and net profit move separately, reading the trend from attributable net profit alone can be misleading.

Construction costs and project execution

Kolon Global's construction cost ratio reached 96.8% in Q4 2025 before easing in 2026, but higher construction costs and financing expenses still pressure the industry's profitability. Revenue on high-tech sites follows project progress, so changes in execution speed can move quarterly results.

Kolon Global also has to manage contingent liabilities tied to project financing and working-capital pressure from unbilled construction work.

Chinese oversupply and succession funding

A late-2025 report said Chinese aramid expansion had slowed profitability recovery, and the film and display business has entered a sale review because of oversupply. For the holding company, prolonged price competition in materials could reduce equity-method income. Gift-tax funding of several hundred billion won tied to a future ownership transfer adds another burden.

12

Overall view

Kolon is a holding company whose consolidated results combine construction, materials and distribution subsidiaries, and its revenue rose 20.4% in Q3 2026. Operating profit in the same quarter fell 38.7% to KRW 41.4 billion, while attributable net profit of KRW 79 billion was influenced by derivative valuation gains.

The drop in the construction cost ratio from 96.8% to 87.4% and growth in the chemical business are cited as signs of improvement. The four-quarter attributable net profit total of KRW 2.15 billion and a debt-to-equity ratio of 207.3% point to financial strain and earnings volatility.

Restructuring and affiliate consolidation are under way, but the debt-reduction effect of sale proceeds and any recovery in the operating margin have not yet been confirmed.

The next checkpoints are the Q4 2026 preliminary results and the 2026 annual report, and the key question is whether operating profit and net borrowings move in the same direction in both.
