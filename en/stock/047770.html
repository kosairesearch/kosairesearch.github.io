02

Business structure

Codes Combine was founded in 1995 and makes and distributes women's casual apparel, with its own Codes Combine brand and the licensed innerwear brand JOCKEY at the core of the business. Manufacturing accounts for more than 95% of revenue, with rental income making up a smaller part.

Segment splits differ across sources: a March 2026 report put the women's casual brand at about 70% and the innerwear brand Jacquie (Jaki) at about 16%, with uniform ODM and other businesses making up the rest, while a breakdown based on Q2 2026 sales showed apparel at 77%, goods at 12%, and underwear at 11%.

The company is shifting distribution from offline-centered channels toward online and expanding exports. Online sales are about 1% of revenue, growing mainly through its own mall, with plans to add external online platforms.

In terms of market value, a February 2026 report placed the company at less than one-tenth of major sector peers such as Youngone and F&F.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual revenue went from KRW 39.1 billion in 2022 to KRW 42 billion in 2023 (up 7.4%), then fell to KRW 39.6 billion in 2024 (down 5.6%) and rose to KRW 39.7 billion in 2025 (up 0.3%). 2023 was the largest year in the 2022–2025 period and 2022 the smallest.

Because revenue stayed in a narrow band, earnings changes showed up mainly in profitability. Operating profit fell from KRW 3.86 billion in 2022 to KRW 2.93 billion in 2023 (down 24.1%) and KRW 1.06 billion in 2024 (down 63.9%), then recovered to KRW 2.42 billion in 2025 (up 128.2%).

Operating margin was 9.9% in 2022, 7.0% in 2023, 2.7% in 2024 and 6.1% in 2025. Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 7.63 billion in 2022, KRW 3.71 billion in 2023 (down 51.4%), KRW 1.86 billion in 2024 (down 49.9%) and KRW 4.46 billion in 2025 (up 139.9%).

Net profit including non-controlling interests is shown at the same amounts in each year.

Quarterly revenue was KRW 9.56 billion in Q3 2025, KRW 12.4 billion in Q4 2025 (up 29.2% quarter on quarter), KRW 9.75 billion in Q1 2026 (down 21.1%), KRW 12.3 billion in Q2 2026 (up 25.7%) and KRW 10.3 billion in Q3 2026 (down 16.3% quarter on quarter, up 7.3% year on year).

Operating profit was KRW 990 million, KRW 853 million, KRW 781 million, KRW 1.62 billion (up 107.0%) and KRW 611 million (down 38.3% year on year) across the same five quarters.

Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders rose to KRW 5.98 billion in Q2 2026 before Q3 2026 posted a net loss of KRW 3.77 billion, a swing to loss. Over the last four quarters, attributable net profit totaled KRW 4.31 billion.

The drivers of the Q2 jump and the Q3 swing to loss should be separated from the operating trend using the notes to the quarterly reports. Operating cash flow was positive every year, at KRW 3.29 billion in 2022, KRW 5.07 billion in 2023, KRW 4.01 billion in 2024 and KRW 4.56 billion in 2025.

At the end of 2025, total equity was KRW 72.3 billion (KRW 70 billion attributable to controlling shareholders) and total liabilities were KRW 20.6 billion, with the debt-to-equity ratio down from 35.0% in 2022 to 28.5% in 2025.

05

Industry analysis

Codes Combine focuses on women's casual apparel and innerwear, and its revenue base leans heavily on domestic consumption. The company has said it will restructure around profit-focused management to respond to an uncertain domestic market.

Compared with larger listed peers in the same sector, its market value is far smaller, which reflects a clear gap in market position and distribution reach. A report noted that the stock rose sharply on a day when major sector names fell, which was read as a stock-specific flow effect rather than an industry trend.

Company disclosures do not show a direct demand-cycle indicator, so the cycle position has to be judged from revenue trends and quarterly seasonality. Revenue staying between KRW 39 billion and KRW 42 billion since 2022 suggests that domestic fashion demand has not produced a clear growth driver for the company.

On channels, the shift from department-store and dealer-led offline sales toward online is becoming a competitive axis in the sector. Overseas export expansion has been presented as a way to reduce reliance on the domestic market. Relative position within the sector is best gauged through scale and the pace of channel change.

06

Outlook

The medium-term direction the company has described rests on three points. First, online sales, now about 1% of revenue, are to grow gradually as the company moves beyond its own mall to external online platforms.

Second, the company has said it expected overseas export sales to begin in Q1 2026 as a way to reduce reliance on the domestic market; whether that showed up in revenue should be checked against the 2026 annual report's revenue breakdown.

Third, product-line consolidation, lower inventory and a higher share of full-price sales are meant to improve the margin structure. Whether fewer discounted sales show up in quarterly results can be tracked through the operating margin.

In Q3 2026, revenue rose 7.3% year on year while operating profit fell 38.3%, so the margin benefit may not yet be fully reflected in profit. Whether this reflects one-off inventory clearance costs or a structural margin squeeze needs to be separated using the notes to the quarterly report.

The outlook the company has provided is mostly qualitative, centered on online expansion, exports and inventory reduction. Channel changes will take time before they appear clearly in reported results.

Bull factors

2025 profit rebound and margin push

Operating profit rose 128.2% to KRW 2.42 billion in 2025, and operating margin climbed from 2.7% in 2024 to 6.1%. The company has said that reducing inventory and selling more at full price will reshape its margin structure. Attributable net profit jumped to KRW 5.98 billion in Q2 2026, showing quarterly earnings momentum.

Light debt load

At the end of 2025, total liabilities were KRW 20.6 billion against KRW 72.3 billion of equity, and the debt-to-equity ratio had fallen from 35.0% in 2022 to 28.5%. Operating cash flow was positive in every year from 2022 to 2025, at levels above KRW 3 billion. Cash generation held up even when earnings were under pressure, which supports the financial capacity case.

Channel diversification through online and exports

Online sales are only about 1% of revenue, but the company said its own-mall sales are growing and it plans to add external online platforms. Overseas exports have been presented as a way to lower dependence on the domestic market. Wider channels could create room to change the flat-revenue pattern.

Bear factors

Stalled top line

Revenue went from KRW 39.1 billion in 2022 to KRW 39.7 billion in 2025, with little net progress. After peaking at KRW 42 billion in 2023, it fell to KRW 39.6 billion in 2024, and the 2025 recovery was only 0.3%. With the top line flat, profit gains depend on cost control.

Q3 2026 swing to a loss

Q3 2026 revenue of KRW 10.3 billion rose 7.3% year on year, but operating profit fell 38.3% to KRW 611 million. The company posted a net loss of KRW 3.77 billion attributable to controlling shareholders.

Attributable net profit over the last four quarters totaled KRW 4.31 billion, so whether this loss is one-off or a trend depends on the next quarters.

Online channel still early

A March 2026 report put online sales at about 1% of revenue. Growth from the company's own mall remains small relative to total sales. Channel shifts may take time to contribute to earnings, which limits their role as a near-term profit driver.

Risk factors

Domestic consumption

Revenue leans heavily on domestic consumption. The company itself has pointed to an uncertain domestic market and said it will restructure around profit-focused management. A prolonged slowdown could extend the flat-revenue pattern, and if exports fail to grow as hoped, the domestic dependence will remain.

Licensed brand dependence

The innerwear business is built on a domestic license for the US brand JOCKEY. Alongside the company's own brand, it forms one pillar of revenue, so changes in license terms or the contractual relationship could affect the business mix. How much the licensed business varies quarter to quarter is also worth tracking.

Earnings volatility and share price swings

Operating profit fell from KRW 3.86 billion in 2022 to KRW 1.06 billion in 2024 before rebounding to KRW 2.42 billion in 2025. Attributable net profit has also swung sharply from year to year. As a small-cap stock, it has seen price moves driven by trading flows that diverged from sector trends.

Overall view

Codes Combine's annual revenue moved from KRW 39.1 billion in 2022 to KRW 39.7 billion in 2025 with little net change, and earnings recovered in 2025 after a low in 2024. At the end of 2025 the debt-to-equity ratio was 28.5% and total equity was KRW 72.3 billion, so the debt burden is light.

However, in Q3 2026 revenue rose year on year while operating profit fell and the company posted a net loss of KRW 3.77 billion attributable to controlling shareholders, so whether the 2025 recovery continues remains open.

Positive factors include the margin recovery, a higher share of full-price sales, and efforts to diversify through online and overseas channels. Negative factors include the flat revenue trend, the Q3 2026 loss, and the early stage of online sales at about 1% of revenue.

The share price trades above net asset value, so the durability of the earnings recovery is the key variable for valuation. The next checkpoints are the revenue mix and operating margin in the 2026 annual report and whether profit recovers in the Q1 2027 quarterly report.
