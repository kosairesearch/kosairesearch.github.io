02

Business structure

Sensorview is an RF connectivity solutions company offering wired and wireless high-speed RF products, developing and manufacturing cables, connectors and antennas used in the millimeter-wave band. The company was founded in 2015 and listed on KOSDAQ in July 2023.

Its business is split into defense, civil communications and semiconductor measurement. According to a Hana Securities analysis, the first-half 2025 revenue mix was 86% civil, 13% defense and 1% semiconductor.

As defense orders grew, one data aggregator showed a second-quarter 2026 revenue mix of 61% aerospace and defense, 37% civil communications and 2% semiconductor.

Defense products reported in the press include surface-to-air defense network-link solutions, radar antennas for long-range artillery interception systems, aviation antennas and cable assemblies.

On the civil side, the company mass-produces ultra-precision coaxial connectors and cables for 5G measurement, and it has been selected as a supplier of Qualcomm's M-GROUP products for RF chip connections in 5G small cells and in-home devices.

Hana Securities named NVIDIA, Broadcom, Qualcomm and Samsung Electronics among key customers. The company says that if its products succeed in localization, profitability should improve to reflect the margin levels that overseas products have achieved.

It is also developing localized AEC cables for AI data centers, and a disclosure dated October 30, 2026 showed it was selected as lead research institution for a national 6G MIMO platform project.

04

Earnings analysis

On a filed basis, annual revenue rose from KRW 5.61 billion in 2022 to KRW 8.46 billion in 2023 (up 50.7% year on year), KRW 15.6 billion in 2024 (up 84.4%) and KRW 19 billion in 2025 (up 21.9%). Revenue has increased for three consecutive years from 2023 through 2025, and 2025 was the largest year of 2022 to 2025.

Operating losses were KRW 15.7 billion in 2022, KRW 18 billion in 2023 (losses continued and widened), KRW 15.8 billion in 2024 (losses continued and narrowed) and KRW 11.6 billion in 2025 (losses continued and narrowed), making four consecutive years of operating losses from 2022 through 2025.

The operating margin moved from -279.5% in 2022 to -213.2% in 2023, -101.2% in 2024 and -60.8% in 2025.

Net losses attributable to controlling shareholders were KRW 12.7 billion in 2022, KRW 18.3 billion in 2023, KRW 17.8 billion in 2024 and KRW 15.2 billion in 2025, and the net loss including non-controlling interests was filed at the same amounts for each year.

Dealsite reported that fixed costs and R&D spending continued even as revenue expanded, so the company did not reach a structural turn to profit. Operating cash flow was -KRW 11.8 billion in 2022, -KRW 15.6 billion in 2023, -KRW 13.5 billion in 2024 and -KRW 7.61 billion in 2025, an outflow in all four years.

The debt-to-equity ratio rose from 86.1% in 2022 to 180.5% in 2025, when total equity was KRW 13 billion (KRW 13 billion attributable to controlling shareholders) and total liabilities were KRW 23.5 billion.

Quarterly revenue was KRW 5.91 billion in the fourth quarter of 2025, up 74.4% from the prior quarter, while the operating loss narrowed to KRW 1.34 billion. First-quarter 2026 revenue fell 64.7% quarter on quarter to KRW 2.09 billion, and the operating loss widened to KRW 3.36 billion.

In the second quarter revenue was KRW 2.53 billion (up 21.2%) with an operating loss of KRW 2.2 billion. In the third quarter revenue was KRW 3.13 billion (up 23.9% quarter on quarter, down 7.6% year on year) with an operating loss of KRW 2.65 billion.

Net losses attributable to controlling shareholders over the last four quarters, from the fourth quarter of 2025 to the third quarter of 2026, totaled KRW 13.2 billion. The company said one-off project revenue in 2025 from overseas expansion created a base effect that showed up as lower first-quarter 2026 revenue.

05

Industry analysis

Sensorview's near-term demand is centered on defense. In the first quarter of 2026, it signed supply contracts with an electronic warfare operator for EJ and CDDF antennas and with a radar operator for MSAM harmonic filters. The company says its key defense development projects are moving into mass production.

Press reports said 2025 results reflected Middle East-bound surface-to-air defense network-link exports and radar antennas for long-range artillery interception systems as well as aviation antennas. Analysts also note that a large share of sales is concentrated among major domestic defense system integrators.

In cycle terms, the order backlog looks to be at an early stage of converting into revenue, but the pace at which orders become mass-production sales has to be tracked through quarterly filings.

Within its sector, Alphasquare data based on 2025 annual figures place the company 75th of 80 companies in the electrical equipment industry on revenue and 75th on operating margin. In the same dataset the industry average operating margin is -4.74%, versus -60.78% for the company. Reports indicate the AEC cable for AI data centers is still in prototype verification.

Outlook

The direction the company has set out is converting defense orders into mass-production sales. The order backlog at the end of March 2026 was about KRW 16.5 billion, up roughly 190% from about KRW 5.7 billion a year earlier and more than double the about KRW 8.2 billion at the end of December 2025.

The company expects key development projects to move gradually into mass production, which it says will expand volume sales and improve earnings visibility. On profitability, it says that once products are localized, margins should improve to reflect the levels overseas products have achieved.

In new areas, it is pursuing localized AEC cable development and supply for AI data centers, and it is widening its business in aerospace and quantum computing. On funding, a substantial part of the proceeds from the KRW 40 billion rights offering disclosed in June 2026 is expected to go into facilities and R&D.

However, KRW 8.9 billion of the KRW 11.6 billion raised in its 2023 IPO was reallocated to operating funds, so how the new proceeds are spent is a point to track. The selection as lead research institution for the 6G MIMO platform project, disclosed on October 30, 2026, is another item to follow in new businesses.

The next steps are to see whether the order backlog translates into higher revenue and a smaller operating loss in the preliminary fourth-quarter 2026 results and the 2026 annual report.

Bull factors

Expanding defense order backlog

The order backlog stood at about KRW 16.5 billion at the end of March 2026, above both the year-earlier level (about KRW 5.7 billion) and the end-2025 level (about KRW 8.2 billion). Money Today and Edaily reports from May 14, 2026 attributed the increase mainly to defense contracts.

In the first quarter of 2026 the company signed antenna and filter supply contracts with an electronic warfare operator and a radar operator.

Quarterly revenue past its trough

Revenue bottomed at KRW 2.09 billion in the first quarter of 2026 and then rose to KRW 2.53 billion and KRW 3.13 billion. The KRW 1.34 billion operating loss in the fourth quarter of 2025 was the smallest of the last five quarters. Third-quarter revenue was still 7.6% below the prior-year quarter, so the recovery is only partial.

Wider new-business projects

A disclosure dated October 30, 2026 showed the company was selected as lead research institution for a national 6G MIMO platform development project. It is pursuing localized AEC cables for AI data centers and has also focused on localizing cryogenic interconnect parts for quantum computers.

The company says successful localization would bring margins closer to the levels overseas products have achieved.

Bear factors

Four years of losses and five straight loss quarters

On filed figures, the company has posted operating losses in four consecutive years from 2022 through 2025, and its 2025 operating margin was -60.8%.

From the third quarter of 2025 through the third quarter of 2026 it recorded losses in five straight quarters, with net losses attributable to controlling shareholders totaling KRW 13.2 billion over the last four quarters.

Dealsite also pointed to fixed costs and R&D spending that have kept losses from shrinking sharply even as revenue grows.

Balance sheet and funding burden

The debt-to-equity ratio was 180.5% at the end of 2025, with total liabilities of KRW 23.5 billion exceeding total equity of KRW 13 billion. The company was in partial capital impairment as of the third quarter of 2025 and chose a free capital reduction that lowers the par value from KRW 500 to KRW 100.

Korea Financial News reported that the controlling shareholder side's stake would fall from 11.49% to 9.74% after the KRW 40 billion rights offering disclosed in June 2026.

Credibility of forecasts and use of proceeds

Operating profit was expected to turn positive in 2025 but the company posted a loss, with a deviation rate of 190.6% reported by Korea Financial News. The reallocation of KRW 8.9 billion of the KRW 11.6 billion raised in the 2023 IPO to operating funds is also cited as weakening market confidence. Third-quarter revenue down 7.6% year on year suggests the pace of recovery could fall short of expectations.

Risk factors

Funding and capital structure

The KRW 40 billion rights offering disclosed in June 2026 is more than three times the KRW 13 billion of total equity at the end of 2025, so it could materially change the capital structure.

The capital reduction is planned to take paid-in capital from KRW 25.8 billion to about KRW 5.1 billion, but whether and when it is completed must be confirmed through filings. Reports say the CEO side plans to subscribe for only about half of its allotment.

Customer concentration and mass-production timing

Analysts note that a large portion of revenue is concentrated among major domestic defense system integrators. The base effect of one-off project revenue in 2025 showed up as lower first-quarter 2026 revenue, and delays in moving development projects into mass production could open revenue gaps again.

Even with a growing backlog, there is a lag before orders are recognized as revenue, so quarter-to-quarter swings may remain large.

New-business validation and dependence on assumptions

The AEC cable is still in prototype verification, and the margin improvement the company describes depends on successful localization. Because expectations for a 2025 profit turn ended in a loss, the timing of new businesses and the margin assumptions need to be verified.

If profitability indicators do not change direction, the effect of business expansion may show up in the financial statements later than expected.

Overall view

Sensorview grew revenue from KRW 5.61 billion in 2022 to KRW 19 billion in 2025, and over the same period narrowed its operating loss from KRW 15.7 billion to KRW 11.6 billion.

However, it has posted operating losses for four straight years from 2022, and its 2025 operating margin of -60.8% shows it has not yet reached break-even.

Quarterly trends in 2026 show revenue rising for two consecutive quarters after the first-quarter trough, but the KRW 2.65 billion operating loss and KRW 3.23 billion net loss attributable to controlling shareholders in the third quarter show the loss structure remains.

The order backlog of about KRW 16.5 billion at the end of March 2026 is a bullish factor supporting the idea that defense mass production could translate into revenue.

On the other side, the 180.5% debt-to-equity ratio, total equity of KRW 13 billion at the end of 2025, the free capital reduction and the KRW 40 billion rights offering are bearish factors for the balance sheet.

The deviation rate of 190.6% against 2025 profit expectations and the reallocation of IPO proceeds also remain open questions for credibility. The points to watch are how quickly the backlog converts into higher revenue and a smaller operating loss, and how the balance sheet changes after the funding.

The next checkpoints are the preliminary fourth-quarter 2026 results around February 2027 and the 2026 annual business report due by the end of March 2027.
