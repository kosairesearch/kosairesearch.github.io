02

Business structure

QuantumRail listed on KOSDAQ on June 9, 2010, and its DART-registered business category is software development and supply. The stock is classified under games, but the core business is smart card development and security software.

Based on Q2 2026 revenue, the segment mix was smart cards at 57%, services at 28%, asset management at 10%, and goods at 5%, according to the Butler data aggregator. Because smart cards make up a majority, card supply volumes and customer order timing are the main drivers of quarterly revenue swings.

Services and goods together account for about one-third, and whether that revenue is recurring can be checked in segment disclosures. The 10% asset management share also raises the question of how much of this income is non-core.

In a October 8, 2026 press report, the company said it formed a dedicated post-quantum security development team with experts from the National Security Research Institute and from finance and security backgrounds.

The development targets are three products: Q-Proof for personal identity verification, Q-Trust for IoT data verification, and Q-SIM, a post-quantum cryptography USIM and crypto module.

The company plans to use its existing telecom customer base to diversify revenue into software licenses, security module supply, and IoT line-linked services. The products are still in development, so technical validation and securing customers will determine results.

At a July 22, 2026 extraordinary shareholder meeting, the company changed its name from Xcure to QuantumRail, and its English name became QuantumRail Inc.

04

Earnings analysis

On a DART-filed annual basis, revenue rose from KRW 10.3 billion in 2022 to KRW 34.8 billion in 2023, a 238.3% increase year on year, then fell to KRW 10.7 billion in 2024, down 69.1%. Revenue for 2025 was KRW 18.5 billion, up 72.5% year on year. Among 2022 through 2025, 2023 was the largest year and 2022 the smallest.

Revenue swung sharply from year to year, so earnings volatility is high. Operating profit moved from KRW 325 million in 2022 to an operating loss of KRW 118 million in 2023, a turn into loss, and the operating loss widened to KRW 2.18 billion in 2024 as the losses continued.

In 2025, operating profit was KRW 1.9 billion, a return to profit, with an operating margin of 10.3%. Operating margins were 3.2% in 2022, -0.3% in 2023, and -20.3% in 2024.

Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders went from KRW 891 million in 2022 to a net loss of KRW 2.67 billion in 2023 and KRW 16.1 billion in 2024, then returned to a profit of KRW 233 million in 2025.

Net loss including non-controlling interests for 2025 was KRW 498 million, which differs from the controlling-shareholder figure; the gap reflects the non-controlling interest portion. Operating cash flow moved from negative KRW 4.54 billion in 2024 to positive KRW 3.67 billion in 2025.

On a quarterly basis, Q3 2025 revenue was KRW 5.62 billion with operating profit of KRW 1.29 billion, and the DART-filed quarterly data has no entry for Q4 2025. Q1 2026 revenue was KRW 1.62 billion, with an operating loss of KRW 1.93 billion and a controlling-shareholder net loss of KRW 4.74 billion.

In Q2 2026, revenue was KRW 2.33 billion, up 43.7% from the prior quarter, the operating loss narrowed to KRW 1.55 billion, and the controlling-shareholder net loss was KRW 1.4 billion.

In Q3 2026, revenue was KRW 1.39 billion, down 40.2% from the prior quarter and 75.2% from a year earlier, the operating loss widened to KRW 2.1 billion, and the controlling-shareholder net loss was KRW 5.31 billion.

The company has recorded losses in each of the three quarters from Q1 through Q3 2026, and the controlling-shareholder net loss for the latest four quarters totals KRW 14.6 billion.

05

Industry analysis

QuantumRail's business is built on telecom authentication and smart cards, and it is extending into security products that add post-quantum cryptography.

The existing revenue base is smart card and authentication work tied to telecom customers, and the new business aims to expand from that base into financial non-face-to-face authentication and industrial IoT.

The approach that combines post-quantum cryptography with zero-knowledge proofs targets both financial institutions and the public sector. Entering the public sector involves phasing in cryptographic module verification (KCMVP) requirements.

Under DART, the business category is software development and supply, and it differs in structure from the game-sector classification the stock carries, so growth rates and margins of game companies are hard to use as a baseline here.

For peer comparison, companies focused on telecom authentication and security software fit this business structure better. Revenue rising to KRW 34.8 billion in 2023 and then falling to KRW 10.7 billion in 2024 shows that business volumes moved sharply from year to year.

New-business revenue contribution is likely to show up in results only after product validation and customer wins.

Outlook

Trading in the shares is scheduled to be suspended from December 9, 2026 through January 5, 2027, and the new shares are scheduled to list on January 6, 2027.

The reduction was presented as a way to shrink capital and improve the financial structure, but it brings in no cash, so it should be viewed separately from liquidity.

On the funding side, the board withdrew the decision to issue the 5th convertible bond worth KRW 10 billion on November 9, 2026, which changed part of the external financing plan. The withdrawn issue was first resolved on April 14, 2026, and several amended major-matter reports were filed after that.

On operations, Q3 2026 revenue fell from Q2, interrupting the recovery seen in Q2. The three post-quantum products, Q-Proof, Q-Trust, and Q-SIM, are still in development, and technical validation and customer wins are the next points to watch.

The 2026 annual report must be filed by the end of March 2027, and Q4 2026 results will be disclosed in that report.

Risk factors

Certification and regulatory risk

Entry into the public sector requires phased application of cryptographic module verification (KCMVP) requirements. For post-quantum security products to be supplied to public customers, these verification requirements must be met, so any delay in certification could push back the timing of public-sector revenue.

Because the strategy targeting both financial institutions and the public sector is tied to certification timelines, the pace of expanding new-business revenue could be constrained.

Receivables collection risk

In a structure where quarterly revenue swings sharply, delays in collecting receivables could leave cash inflows out of step with reported results.

Operating cash flow turned positive in 2025, but with operating losses continuing through the 2026 quarters, the pace of collections could determine the direction of cash flow. If collections slow while the debt-to-equity ratio is already higher, reliance on external funding could grow.

Trading and share-structure risk

Trading is scheduled to be suspended from December 9, 2026 to January 5, 2027 for the capital reduction. New shares are scheduled to list on January 6, 2027, so changes in share count and float will show up together once trading resumes.

The name change and related corrected filings mean the disclosed information has been changing and needs to be tracked.
