02

Business structure

MX On is a company whose main business is HMI-based smart factory solutions. Its main revenue comes from smart HMI products that operate and monitor equipment on manufacturing floors.

As of the December 2025 fiscal year end, smart HMI accounted for 93.80% of sales, smart SCADA for 2.40%, smart factory solution services for 2.30%, and other items for 1.50%. Revenue therefore rests heavily on one product family.

The company entered the semiconductor market in 2007, and it says semiconductor-related sales now exceed 50% of the total. Its main customers are Samsung Electronics and SK hynix, so the scale of those two companies' capex translates directly into HMI and control-system supply.

Beyond semiconductors, the company also serves automation demand in other manufacturing sites such as displays, and it is pursuing sales growth in production equipment for mobile, secondary battery and bio industries.

In August 2026 it bought new shares of MX Robotics in a third-party rights offering, lifting its stake from 21.1% to 32.24%. Through this, the company plans to extend its HMI and control-equipment business into logistics robots such as AMRs and OHTs.

04

Earnings analysis

On the disclosed annual figures, sales were KRW 41.7 billion in 2022, KRW 33 billion in 2023, KRW 36.8 billion in 2024 and KRW 62.3 billion in 2025. 2023 was the smallest year and 2025 was the largest of 2022 to 2025.

Sales grew for two consecutive years from 2024 to 2025 and rose 69.2% in 2025, yet the company swung to an operating loss of KRW 5.83 billion. Operating profit fell from KRW 11.7 billion in 2022 to KRW 5.61 billion in 2023, a 51.9% decline, then edged up 0.5% to KRW 5.64 billion in 2024 before the 2025 loss.

The operating margin slid from 28.0% in 2022 to 17.0% in 2023 and 15.3% in 2024, and reached -9.4% in 2025. Profit attributable to owners of the parent was largest in 2022 at KRW 10.5 billion and smallest in 2025 at KRW 1.7 billion.

In 2025 net loss was KRW 2.84 billion, so it moved in a different direction from parent-owner profit, which reflects losses borne by non-controlling interests. In 2022 to 2024, net profit and parent-owner profit were identical. Operating cash flow fell from KRW 8.43 billion in 2022 to -KRW 540 million in 2025.

Quarterly sales jumped from KRW 7.4 billion in Q3 2025 to KRW 36.6 billion in Q4, but that quarter also recorded an operating loss of KRW 8.21 billion, so higher sales did not immediately become profit. In Q1 2026 operating profit turned positive at KRW 502 million as sales recovered and fixed-cost pressure eased.

The company says its cost structure carries a certain level of fixed cost up front, so profitability can improve noticeably when sales rise. Q2 operating profit was thin at KRW 282 million, and Q3 returned to an operating loss of KRW 2.23 billion with a parent-owner net loss of KRW 1.62 billion. Parent-owner net profit over the last four quarters totals KRW 821 million.

05

Industry analysis

The key industry variable for MX On is the semiconductor capex cycle. The company says the resumption of investment by chip customers is driving higher sales in its core business.

For the second quarter, the company said overseas and non-semiconductor growth lifted results before the full effect of major clients' expanded capex showed up, which places the chip capex effect in the second half and beyond.

The company plans to sustain growth through next year, citing mid-to-long-term capex expansion by key customers, new chip-related products and a wider product lineup. On competition, the company argues that its HMI and control systems have become essential components of chip production equipment.

It also acknowledges that its market recognition as a semiconductor materials, parts and equipment company has been relatively low. Because customers are concentrated among a few large chipmakers, results are highly exposed to that cycle. The company is preparing to expand into logistics robots. With sales in the low KRW 60 billion range, the company is small within the electronics and components sector.

06

Outlook

The company said first-half results beat its own targets and that, if second-half growth continues, it expects record sales since its founding this year. This is management's expectation, and no firm numerical guidance was confirmed.

The key path for second-half supply is the sequential increase in HMI and control-system shipments tied to expanded capex at Samsung Electronics and SK hynix. On profitability, the company expects fixed-cost burden to ease as the business scale expands.

On products, it is preparing the new Soft PLC and robot controllers, and it is pursuing collaboration with MX Robotics and wider overseas business. In an April 2026 disclosure on its shareholder-value plan, expanding dividends was also listed as a task.

The next point to watch is whether the operating loss of KRW 2.23 billion in Q3 2026 is followed by further losses. The preliminary results for Q4 2026 and the annual operating margin confirmed in the 2026 annual report will show whether management's expectations are matched by actual results. For the robotics subsidiary, progress will become visible when order wins or product launches are announced.

08

Bull factors

Chip capex recovery feeding into sales

The company says semiconductor-related sales now exceed half of total sales. Its outlook centers on rising HMI and control-system shipments as Samsung Electronics and SK hynix expand capex in the second half. Quarterly sales holding between KRW 36 billion and KRW 40.4 billion in 2026 fits this pattern.

Room for margin gains from fixed-cost leverage

The company explains that because a fixed-cost base is incurred in advance, profitability can improve noticeably as sales rise. The swing to operating profit in Q1 2026 is the first observable case of this structure. With the operating margin at 28.0% in 2022, the company's case rests on the room for recovery in the margin.

Expansion into robotics and new products

The company plans to extend its HMI and control-equipment business into logistics robots such as AMRs and OHTs. It is also preparing the new Soft PLC and robot controllers and expanding overseas business. The company's synergy case links its semiconductor customer base with the subsidiary's robotics technology.

09

Bear factors

Unstable quarterly earnings

After an operating loss of KRW 8.21 billion in Q4 2025, results moved to profit in Q1 2026, KRW 282 million in Q2 and an operating loss of KRW 2.23 billion in Q3, changing direction each quarter. Parent-owner net profit over the last four quarters totals only KRW 821 million. The lack of steady profit accumulation despite higher sales is the core bear point.

Concentration on a few customers and timing risk

With semiconductor-related sales above half of the total, the main customers are concentrated in two companies, Samsung Electronics and SK hynix. If their capex timing slips, the expected second-half supply increase could be delayed. The company's outlook also depends on those customers executing their investment plans.

Balance-sheet strain and expanded subsidiary investment

The debt ratio rose from 6.9% in 2024 to 60.6% in 2025, with total liabilities of KRW 73.9 billion. In August 2026 the company invested roughly KRW 10 billion more in MX Robotics.

As new-business investment grows, funding pressure may increase, and subsidiary losses borne by non-controlling interests can affect the profit structure.

Risk factors

Customer capex cycle

If Samsung Electronics or SK hynix changes its capex plans, the timing of HMI and control-system supply could shift as well. The company's expected second-half supply increase and its growth plan through 2027 both depend on customers carrying out investment. A downturn in the cycle would widen sales volatility.

Sustainability of profit recovery

After an operating loss of KRW 5.83 billion in 2025, the company again posted an operating loss of KRW 2.23 billion in Q3 2026. A fixed-cost structure helps when sales rise but can widen losses when sales fall. Because the operating margin dropped from 28.0% in 2022 to -9.4% in 2025, the pace of recovery matters.

Subsidiary and new-business investment and capital structure

The MX Robotics stake was raised to 32.24%, and the 2025 net loss of KRW 2.84 billion appears to reflect losses borne by non-controlling interests. The debt ratio rose to 60.6% in 2025. If the logistics-robot business does not convert into orders, the payback period on this investment could lengthen.

Overall view

MX On's quarterly sales have held between KRW 36 billion and KRW 40.4 billion in 2026 as semiconductor customers resume investment. Sales rose 69.2% to KRW 62.3 billion in 2025, but the company swung to an operating loss of KRW 5.83 billion, and the operating margin fell from 28.0% in 2022 to -9.4% in 2025.

Even after returning to operating profit in Q1 2026, the company posted an operating loss of KRW 2.23 billion in Q3, so the quarterly profit trend is not yet stable.

Strengths include semiconductor-related sales above half of the total, management's expectation for higher HMI and control-system shipments in the second half, and room for margin gains from fixed-cost leverage.

Weaknesses include dependence on two customers' investment timing, parent-owner net profit of only KRW 821 million over the last four quarters, a debt ratio that rose to 60.6% in 2025, and the cost of subsidiary investment.

The higher stake in MX Robotics could support the logistics-robot business, but no order results have been confirmed in disclosures yet. The next checkpoints are the preliminary Q4 2026 results and the confirmed annual operating margin in the 2026 annual report.
