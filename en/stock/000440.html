02

Business structure

Joong Ang Enervis, founded in 1946, is a petroleum wholesaler and retailer that has operated under a dealership agreement with SK Energy since 1972, selling gasoline, diesel and LPG in the Seoul metropolitan area through company-run gas stations and LPG charging stations.

Based on the product sales mix compiled by WISEreport's company monitor, fuel sales account for 94.88%, service revenue for 3.03%, leasing for 2.07% and electricity for 0.03%, so fuel makes up most of revenue.

At its stations, the company bundles car washes and light repairs into a car-care service and widens its product range with lubricant sales. It also runs B2B wholesale to industrial customers such as construction companies and factories, and its head office is in Hannam-dong, Seoul.

Its consolidated subsidiary Enervis Solar runs a solar power business, which is reported under the electricity segment at 0.03% of the sales mix. The store footprint has moved toward contraction: the Wangsimni and Toegye-ro branches closed in 2020, and the Yangpyeong branch closed in August 2025 when its lease ended.

On competition, price competition in the petroleum distribution market recurs as a theme in industry descriptions. The company says it is responding with a higher service standard, conversion to self-service stations and business diversification.

Compared with large retailers in the same Distribution and Consumer Goods sector, its portfolio is concentrated in petroleum sales, leaving a narrower range of product diversification.

04

Earnings analysis

On disclosed annual results, revenue fell from KRW 71.6 billion in 2022 to KRW 55.5 billion in 2023 (down 22.5% year on year), then to KRW 49.8 billion in 2024 (down 10.2%) and KRW 49.6 billion in 2025 (down 0.4%).

Revenue declined three years in a row from 2023 to 2025, with 2022 the largest year and 2025 the smallest in the 2022–2025 period.

Operating profit of KRW 812 million in 2022 turned into an operating loss of KRW 739 million in 2023, a swing from profit to loss, and the loss continued at KRW 1.75 billion in 2024, with the loss widening, and KRW 1.74 billion in 2025, with the loss narrowing.

The operating margin slid from 1.1% in 2022 to -1.3% in 2023 and -3.5% in both 2024 and 2025. Net income attributable to controlling shareholders went from KRW 500 million in 2022 to a net loss of KRW 251 million in 2023, then to net losses of KRW 944 million in 2024 and KRW 1.12 billion in 2025.

Net income including non-controlling interests moved in the same direction, and the net loss being smaller than the operating loss reflects non-operating income and tax effects that partly offset the operating shortfall.

On a quarterly basis, revenue bottomed at KRW 11.2 billion in Q1 2026 after KRW 12.8 billion in Q3 2025 and KRW 11.8 billion in Q4 2025, then rose 33.2% to KRW 15 billion in Q2 2026 and reached KRW 15.1 billion in Q3 2026, up 1.0% quarter on quarter and 18.2% year on year.

Operating losses ran at KRW 196 million, KRW 308 million, KRW 202 million, KRW 967 million and KRW 96.6 million across the five quarters from Q3 2025 to Q3 2026, and the widest loss came in Q2 2026, the quarter when revenue rose.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders turned to a KRW 112 million profit in Q4 2025 despite an operating loss, and the company has posted losses in each of the three quarters from Q1 to Q3 2026. The last four quarters total KRW 1.04 billion in net losses attributable to controlling shareholders.

Operating cash flow fell from KRW 2.3 billion in 2022 to KRW 2.01 billion in 2023, turned negative at -KRW 521 million in 2024, and returned to KRW 513 million in 2025.

At the end of 2025, the debt ratio was 5.7%, total liabilities were KRW 2.99 billion, and total equity of KRW 52 billion was entirely controlling-shareholder equity.

05

Industry analysis

The long-term demand structure of the petroleum distribution market is seen as potentially weakening. The main premise is that growing interest in alternative energy and the spread of hybrid vehicles will reduce demand for petroleum-based energy.

On the other side, some assessments say the domestic petroleum distribution market has shown solid growth in fuel consumption for industrial and vehicle use. Recently, concerns about lower demand from oil and currency uncertainty and an economic slowdown have been raised together.

Intensifying price competition is also cited as a burden on the industry. In cycle terms, the market looks closer to a phase of preparing for structural change than to one of demand growth. The company is responding with a higher service standard, conversion to self-service stations and business diversification.

On relative position, its strengths are a stable fuel supply base built on supply from SK Energy and a station network in key Seoul-area locations. Its weakness is that its heavy reliance on petroleum sales leaves little buffer if petroleum demand falls. Compared with large retailers in the same sector, its dependence on a single product line stands out.

06

Outlook

The value-up plan the company disclosed on March 19, 2026 has two pillars. The first is a phased reduction of the fuel retail share, converting the freed-up sites into new mobility and non-fuel businesses to raise asset utilization.

The second is securing dividend funds on the back of steady profit growth to provide stable and continuing dividends. The plan cited a 25% dividend payout ratio for 2025 and marked the company as a high-dividend firm under the tax code.

However, the net loss attributable to controlling shareholders widened to KRW 1.12 billion in 2025, so the profit growth that underpins the dividend plan has not yet appeared in the results.

The specific timeline and investment size of the transition are items to check in the business reports and investor relations materials. The 2026 annual business report is expected to be filed around March 2027, when Q4 2026 results and the change in the fuel share of sales will first be confirmed on an annual basis.

How far the fuel share of revenue falls is the key gauge of conversion progress. In the Q1 2027 quarterly report, the checkpoints are whether the narrowing operating loss continues and whether revenue gains translate into margin improvement.

In the 2027 half-year report, the question is whether the pattern seen in Q2 2026, with revenue rising while losses widen, recurs.

Bull factors

Business restructuring plan disclosed

In its value-up plan dated March 19, 2026, the company said it would phase down its fuel retail share and convert the freed-up space into mobility and non-fuel businesses. The move responds to a structure in which petroleum demand is falling. The same plan includes continuing dividends premised on steady profit growth. The pace and results of the shift can be tracked through changes in the revenue mix in later business reports.

Revenue up two straight quarters in 2026

Revenue bottomed at KRW 11.2 billion in Q1 2026, then reached KRW 15 billion in Q2 and KRW 15.1 billion in Q3, rising for two straight quarters. Q3 revenue was up 18.2% from a year earlier. The Q3 operating loss of KRW 96.6 million was smaller than a year before, showing the loss narrowing. However, the Q2 loss widened to KRW 967 million, so quarterly direction is not one-way.

A low-debt capital structure

The debt ratio was 5.7% at the end of 2025, with total liabilities of KRW 2.99 billion. Total equity of KRW 52 billion is entirely controlling-shareholder equity, so financial leverage is light. Operating cash flow turned from -KRW 521 million in 2024 to KRW 513 million in 2025. The balance sheet retains financial room even while losses continue.

Bear factors

Extended run of loss quarters

Operating losses have run for five consecutive quarters from Q3 2025 to Q3 2026. As quarterly losses accumulate, net losses attributable to controlling shareholders have also built up. The Q2 2026 operating loss of KRW 967 million was the largest of the five quarters on disclosed results. Q3 2026 still carried a net loss attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 159 million.

Three years of falling revenue and thinner margins

Revenue fell from KRW 71.6 billion in 2022 to KRW 49.6 billion in 2025 and declined three years in a row from 2023 to 2025. The operating margin dropped from 1.1% in 2022 to -3.5% in 2025. Operating cash flow was negative at -KRW 521 million in 2024. A change in the earnings trend requires both revenue recovery and margin improvement.

Petroleum demand structure and price competition

Growing interest in alternative energy and the spread of hybrid vehicles are expected to reduce petroleum energy demand. Price competition in the domestic petroleum distribution market is intensifying, and oil price and currency swings can shake sales margins.

Whether station diversification and the shift to self-service can offset this trend is not yet visible in reported results.

10

Risk factors

Oil price and currency swings

Because most revenue comes from fuel sales, oil price moves feed directly into selling prices and inventory values. Currency swings and demand worries from an economic slowdown are also raised. In a market with intense price competition, passing higher oil costs through to pump prices may be difficult. Quarterly swings in the operating margin reflect this exposure.

Execution of the business shift

The reduction of fuel retail and the move into non-fuel businesses have not yet shown a clear revenue contribution in reported results. With branch closures already under way, a delayed transition could shrink the revenue base further.

Without disclosure of the profitability and investment size of mobility and non-fuel businesses, the effect of the shift is hard to gauge. Segment-level revenue changes in business reports are the item to check.

Continuing losses and dividend funding

No year since 2023 has posted an annual profit, and the latest four quarters total KRW 1.04 billion in net losses attributable to controlling shareholders. The company ties its dividend funding to steady profit growth, so a slower profit recovery would weigh on dividend continuity.

Operating cash flow returned to positive in 2025 after being negative in 2024, which shows year-to-year variability. The financial position at the time of each dividend decision should be read alongside the payout.
