CosLeaguer Global's Beauty Pivot, Profit Still Unproven

CosLeaguer Global's top line has expanded sharply through vertical integration of cosmetics ODM and brand operations, but quarterly operating results have swung between profit and loss, leaving profitability still unsettled.

02

Business structure

CosLeaguer Global was founded in 1991 and listed on KOSDAQ in 2002, and at its shareholders' meeting on March 27, 2026 it changed its name from the former Seouleaguer to its current name, citing corporate image improvement and business diversification as the reasons.

The company now operates as a business-type holding company spanning four segments: healthcare, cosmetics, green tech, and new media marketing. The healthcare segment distributes aesthetic and medical products such as botulinum toxin, fillers, and skin boosters, providing a comparatively stable revenue base.

The cosmetics segment has built a value chain centered on ODM subsidiary CosLeaguer and brand company Moments Company, with CosLeaguer having built a production facility worth roughly KRW 70 billion in the Sihwa industrial complex in Gyeonggi Province.

Moments Company's skincare brand beplain has kept its flagship mung bean cleansing line near the top of the cleansing category at CJ Olive Young. The cosmetics business expanded to roughly half of consolidated revenue as of the first quarter of 2026, becoming the company's core growth driver.

Alongside this, the company is diversifying its portfolio through a new media marketing business built around influencer-driven commerce and a green tech business handling eco-friendly products, with Sesim serving as the controlling shareholder.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual revenue rose from KRW 10.1 billion in 2022 to KRW 22.6 billion in 2023, up 123.2%, then fell 14.3% to KRW 19.3 billion in 2024, before rising 84.4% to KRW 35.7 billion in 2025, the largest of the four years.

Operating losses widened from KRW 6.59 billion in 2022 to KRW 7.54 billion in 2023, narrowed to KRW 3.35 billion in 2024, then widened again to KRW 8.33 billion in 2025, marking four consecutive loss-making years from 2022 through 2025.

Controlling-interest net loss narrowed from KRW 6.39 billion in 2022 to KRW 3.3 billion in 2023, turned to a net profit of KRW 5.38 billion in 2024, then swung back to a net loss of KRW 8.74 billion in 2025.

The debt-to-equity ratio jumped to 476.6% at the end of 2025 from 28.4% in 2024, as total liabilities expanded to KRW 187.2 billion against total equity of KRW 39.3 billion (of which controlling interest was KRW 35.7 billion).

Operating cash flow improved to an outflow of KRW 296 million in 2024 before widening again to an outflow of KRW 12.9 billion in 2025.

On a quarterly basis, revenue bottomed at KRW 9.72 billion in Q4 2025 before rising for two consecutive quarters to KRW 39.5 billion in Q1 2026 (up 306.7% quarter on quarter) and KRW 42.4 billion in Q2 2026 (up 7.2% quarter on quarter and 303.8% year on year), a pattern attributable to Moments Company's consolidation beginning to flow through from Q1 2026.

Operating losses widened from KRW 822 million in Q2 2025 to KRW 2.03 billion in Q3 2025 and KRW 4.2 billion in Q4 2025, then turned to an operating profit of KRW 1.55 billion in Q1 2026 before reverting to an operating loss of KRW 600 million in Q2 2026 (a narrower loss than a year earlier), while controlling-interest net loss ran for five consecutive quarters from KRW 1.45 billion in Q2 2025 to KRW 6.39 billion in Q2 2026, with the loss size widening as quarters progressed.

05

Industry analysis

The K-beauty industry has grown rapidly in recent years, driven by foreign tourist demand and expanding online channels. According to Financial Supervisory Service filings, CJ Olive Young posted unconsolidated 2025 revenue of KRW 5,833.5 billion, up 21.8% year on year, with operating profit up 22.5%.

In a market structure where a dominant health-and-beauty retail platform exists, the performance of indie brands such as beplain depends heavily on shelf placement and exposure within that channel.

South Korea's cosmetics ODM industry is centered on large manufacturers securing bulk orders from global brand companies, and CosLeaguer Global's cosmetics subsidiary CosLeaguer, with relatively small-scale production infrastructure, remains at an early stage of its business.

The aesthetic and medical product distribution market that the healthcare segment belongs to also features competition among numerous distributors, keeping item-level price competition intense.

The green tech and new media marketing businesses have not yet disclosed specific revenue shares or profit contributions, making it premature to gauge their maturity.

Overall, CosLeaguer Global remains a small-cap name within the sector in terms of both market value and production scale, with a clear gap versus large distribution and ODM incumbents.

06

Outlook

A company official has stated that, with the Moments Company acquisition beginning to flow through consolidated results from Q1 2026, management expects quarterly performance to trend upward based on a business structure combining brand and manufacturing.

At the time the Moments Company deal closed in late 2025, the acquisition price was reported at KRW 81.2 billion, and cosmetics ODM subsidiary CosLeaguer was expected to post revenue of around KRW 17 billion that year.

The cosmetics production facility in Gyeonggi Province's Sihwa industrial complex, spanning roughly 6,000 pyeong, has been completed to handle ODM production ranging from basic skincare to color cosmetics.

However, Q2 2026 operating profit reverted to a loss even as revenue rose from the prior quarter, indicating that top-line growth and profitability improvement have not yet moved in tandem.

In September 2026 the company issued a convertible bond earmarked for acquiring securities in another entity, and a perpetual convertible bond recognized as equity-like debt (sized at KRW 37.5 billion) was also funded the same month, reflecting continued fundraising to secure capacity for further investment.

The specific uses of these proceeds have not yet been clearly disclosed.

Whether utilization at the CosLeaguer production facility rises, and whether new product lines such as clinic-targeted products and dedicated aesthetic-clinic lines expand, are among the variables that will determine future revenue and profitability.

08

Bull factors

Cosmetics Vertical Integration Effect

Vertical integration between CosLeaguer (ODM) and Moments Company (brand) lifted quarterly revenue above KRW 40 billion from Q1 2026 onward, with year-on-year growth exceeding 300%. beplain's flagship mung bean cleansing line has stayed near the top of the cleansing category at CJ Olive Young, supporting brand recognition.

Full utilization of the Sihwa production facility could reduce reliance on outsourced manufacturing and improve the cost structure.

Surprise Q1 2026 Return to Profit

Q1 2026 operating profit of KRW 1.55 billion marked a turnaround from the prior quarter, showing that revenue growth can, at least temporarily, translate into improved operating results. This suggests the growing weight of the cosmetics business can carry some degree of operating leverage.

Controlling Shareholder's Stake Increase and Business Diversification

Controlling shareholder Sesim disclosed that it, together with related parties, raised its stake from 65.05% to 68.58% through over-the-counter purchases in September 2026.

The segmented business structure across healthcare, cosmetics, green tech, and new media marketing helps spread exposure away from dependence on any single industry cycle.

Bear factors

Quarter-to-Quarter Earnings Volatility

In Q2 2026, revenue rose 7.2% from the prior quarter, yet operating profit reverted to an operating loss of KRW 600 million. The possibility that the Q1 profit was closer to a one-off cannot be ruled out, and controlling-interest net loss actually widened to KRW 6.39 billion in the same quarter.

Surging Debt Ratio and Funding Burden

The debt-to-equity ratio jumped to 476.6% at the end of 2025 within a single year, and multiple rounds of convertible bond and perpetual bond issuance have continued since. Rising financial costs such as interest expense appear to weigh more heavily on net income than on operating profit, as seen in quarterly results.

Dependence on a Single Brand and Channel

Growth in the cosmetics segment relies heavily on a single brand, beplain, and concentration in one retail channel, CJ Olive Young. The fact that Moments Company's integration performance, now in its first year post-acquisition, has not yet been proven over multiple years is also a source of uncertainty.

Risk factors

Capital Structure and Dilution Risk

The company issued a series of convertible bonds in 2026 for purposes including acquiring securities in other entities and funding operations, with terms that allow the conversion price to be adjusted downward (repriced) if the share price falls.

If multiple convertible bonds are converted into shares in the future, the resulting increase in shares outstanding could dilute existing shareholders. The capital structure itself, with a debt-to-equity ratio raised to 476.6%, could also constrain capacity for further fundraising.

Business Integration and Goodwill Risk

The Moments Company acquisition closed less than a year ago in late 2025, meaning it has been reflected in consolidated results for only a short period and the durability of integration performance remains unproven.

Given reports that the acquisition drew valuation disputes in the market at the time, the possibility of accounting risks such as future goodwill impairment cannot be ruled out.

Industry and Channel Risk

Because the cosmetics business depends on shelf placement and exposure within specific retail channels such as CJ Olive Young, it is exposed to changes in channel strategy or intensifying competition.

The aesthetic and medical product distribution market that the healthcare segment belongs to also features numerous competing distributors, keeping pricing and margin pressure a persistent factor.

Overall view

CosLeaguer Global has rapidly expanded its revenue base by pursuing vertical integration of cosmetics ODM and brand operations following the Moments Company acquisition in late 2025. 2025 revenue reached KRW 35.7 billion, up 84.4% year on year, and quarterly revenue rose further to around KRW 40 billion in Q1 and Q2 2026, marking a broader scale of growth.

However, operating profit reverted to a loss in Q2 2026 after turning positive in Q1, and controlling-interest net loss actually widened to KRW 6.39 billion in the same quarter, showing that top-line expansion and profitability improvement have not yet moved together.

With the debt-to-equity ratio surging to 476.6% at the end of 2025 amid continued convertible and perpetual bond issuance, capital structure and dilution potential remain variables to watch.

Controlling shareholder Sesim raised its stake to 68.58% in September 2026, and the business portfolio spanning healthcare, cosmetics, green tech, and new media marketing carries both diversification benefits and integration risk.

The next quarterly report and any convertible-bond-related disclosures are likely to provide key clues on the future direction of earnings and capital structure.
