02

Business structure

Mr. Blue, established in November 2002, produces and distributes online comics and webtoons. Its businesses comprise an online comics service that converts comic content into e-books for PC and mobile, comic publishing in print, and an online game business that develops and operates game software.

By Q2 2026 revenue, comics and webtoons accounted for 74%, online games 23%, and comic publishing 3%. Its core IP is the exclusive copyright to the 'Four Heavenly Kings' martial arts line-up, and the company says it is adding romance and boys love (BL) to the martial arts genre to widen its readership.

The company has reorganized its comics menu around genres and is expanding IP goods, including limited merchandise sold at pop-up stores and season greetings products.

After absorbing a web novel subsidiary in December 2025, it now serves that subsidiary's romance and fantasy web novels directly on its own platform, which the company says reduced intermediary distribution costs.

The merger placed web novel production, distribution, platform operations and marketing under one organization, and the company says it can lower internal fee burdens and widen author revenue sharing.

Its game unit's new title EOS Black passed 2 million pre-registrations, but game subsidiary revenue fell 35.7% year on year in Q3 2026 and the game segment booked an operating loss of KRW 500 million. Excluding games, Q3 2026 revenue was KRW 13.0 billion, roughly flat year on year.

The company presents its own platform combined with IP goods as its strength, and it points to genre diversification beyond martial arts as its growth driver.

04

Earnings analysis

On a disclosed annual basis, revenue fell from KRW 77.2 billion in 2022 to KRW 74.1 billion in 2023 (down 3.9% year on year), KRW 70.3 billion in 2024 (down 5.1%), and KRW 67.5 billion in 2025 (down 4.1%).

Revenue declined for three consecutive years from 2023 to 2025, with 2022 the largest year and 2025 the smallest in the 2022 to 2025 period. Operating profit dropped from KRW 7.44 billion in 2022 to KRW 695 million in 2023 (down 90.7%), and the company swung to an operating loss of KRW 13.6 billion in 2024.

The 2025 operating loss of KRW 2.46 billion was smaller than the prior year, and the company attributed the improvement to the end of cost increases tied to a new game launch and lower marketing spend.

Operating margin ran at 9.6% in 2022, 0.9% in 2023, -19.3% in 2024 and -3.6% in 2025, so the negative margin narrowed after 2024. Operating cash flow was -KRW 6.18 billion in 2024 and recovered to KRW 2.41 billion in 2025.

Attributable net income of KRW 3.12 billion in 2022 turned into an attributable net loss of KRW 11.2 billion in 2023, which widened to KRW 16.6 billion in 2024 and KRW 18.6 billion in 2025.

The 2025 attributable net loss was far larger than the operating loss, which suggests non-operating items and asset-related charges drove much of the gap. The company said asset and equity declines in 2025 reflected goodwill impairment recognition.

Quarterly revenue has declined for four consecutive quarters from Q4 2025 (KRW 16.5 billion, down 5.5% quarter on quarter) through Q3 2026 (KRW 15.9 billion).

Quarterly operating results moved from an operating loss of KRW 714 million in Q3 2025 to an operating profit of KRW 465 million in Q4 2025, then KRW 529 million in Q1 2026, KRW 94.8 million in Q2 2026, and an operating loss of KRW 299 million in Q3 2026.

Attributable net income was KRW 14.3 million... more precisely KRW 1.43 billion in Q1 2026 and KRW 2.32 billion in Q2 2026, before an attributable net loss of KRW 1.96 billion in Q3 2026.

Press reports attributed the Q3 2026 net loss mainly to financial losses, including fair value losses on financial assets and a loss on early redemption of convertible bonds.

05

Industry analysis

The domestic webtoon and web novel market is a structure where platforms keep competing for content. Mr. Blue presents its martial arts IP combined with its own platform as its differentiator. Martial arts is the company's traditional strength, and it is widening its readership by adding romance and BL.

The company says public interest in the BL genre has risen and it aims to convert new platform users into IP fans. In web novels, distribution is shifting from supplying external platforms toward its own platform, which directly affects fee structures.

The game segment is a business where quarterly results swing sharply with new title performance, and game subsidiary revenue fell 35.7% year on year in Q3 2026. Goods extend IP consumption from fans into products, and the company reported that its 2027 season greetings sales rose 145% from the prior year.

The industry cycle position is hard to pin down with figures; the visible pattern is platform revenue declining while web novel and goods businesses expand at the same time. Webtoon-related shares have moved on news of stake deals at large platforms, so sector sentiment and company-level results can diverge.

Press coverage describes Mr. Blue as a small-cap company, which may limit information flow and trading liquidity compared with large platform operators.

Outlook

The company held a Black Friday discount event in November 2026 to convert new readers into IP fans and grow goods sales. The online goods shop offered discounts of up to 90% on popular products released over the past three years.

The company plans a second round of season greetings sales at year-end, along with pop-up store operations and planning of the 2027 goods line-up to broaden its IP revenue model. For web novels, the stated direction is to raise profitability through merger synergies and maintain a stable profit trend.

On genres, it intends to continue growth momentum by adding romance and BL alongside martial arts IP. Given reports that the Q3 2026 swing to a loss stemmed from non-operating items, whether operating profit stays positive becomes the next point to watch.

A 5-to-1 share consolidation took effect on August 5, 2026, leaving 16,615,956 shares outstanding, so price levels are hard to compare directly with pre-consolidation prices. The visible structural changes are web novel distribution consolidation, expanding goods sales, and game title performance.

The next checkpoint is the 2026 annual report, expected around March 2027, which will fix the four-quarter trend into full-year results.

08

Bull factors

Quarterly return to profit

Operating profit turned positive in Q4 2025 at KRW 465 million and reached KRW 529 million in Q1 2026. Attributable net income reached KRW 1.43 billion in Q1 2026 and KRW 2.32 billion in Q2 2026. The company credits the December 2025 merger and reduced intermediary distribution costs for the quarterly profits. The earnings path has not been one-directional toward losses, which is a positive factor.

IP goods and genre expansion

The company said 2027 season greetings sales rose 145% from the prior year. It is widening its readership by adding romance and BL to the exclusive martial arts IP. As public interest in BL rises, the company aims to turn new readers into IP fans. Goods are positioned as a channel that extends webtoon consumption into product revenue.

Cost structure improvement

Q3 2026 operating expenses were KRW 16.2 billion, down KRW 2.0 billion from KRW 18.2 billion a year earlier. Salaries and advertising costs fell 22% and 21% year on year. The operating loss narrowed to KRW 299 million from roughly KRW 700 million a year earlier. Cost cuts exceeded the revenue decline, which is a positive factor for the operating loss.

Bear factors

Continued revenue decline

Revenue has declined for three straight years from 2023 to 2025 and for four consecutive quarters from Q4 2025 to Q3 2026. Q3 2026 revenue was KRW 15.9 billion, down 9.1% year on year. Quarterly declines have narrowed, but no clear rebound has appeared yet. With a shrinking revenue base, holding profits through cost cuts alone has limits.

Cumulative attributable net losses

The 2025 attributable net loss of KRW 18.6 billion was far larger than the operating loss. Press reports cited financial losses, fair value losses and convertible bond early redemption costs as drivers of the Q3 2026 loss. If non-operating factors recur, a sustained return to profit becomes harder.

Weakness in the game unit

Game subsidiary revenue fell 35.7% year on year in Q3 2026, and the game segment posted an operating loss of KRW 500 million. EOS Black passed 2 million pre-registrations, but the weak quarterly revenue trend after launch is a concern.

Online games account for 23% of revenue, so weakness there affects the whole top line. If new title results do not translate into quarterly revenue, earnings volatility rises.

Risk factors

Share trading and volatility

On July 7, 2026, the stock was designated a short-term investment caution issue for one day due to heavy concentrated trading. Trading was suspended from August 3 to September 2, 2026 for the share consolidation. Current prices cannot be compared directly with pre-consolidation levels after the 5-to-1 consolidation. Changes in trading structure make it necessary to check price volatility against facts.

Ownership structure and dilution

CEO Cho Seung-jin, described as the de facto controlling shareholder, held 49.57% as of August 7, 2026. The same filing said 8,366,838 shares, before consolidation, were added through convertible bonds, bonds with warrants and stock option exercises. A convertible bond repurchase was also disclosed on August 13, 2026. Additional issuance and convertible securities remain a dilution factor to monitor.

Concentration and asset impairment

With comics and webtoons at 74% of revenue, the company is highly exposed to changes in platform competition. Its 2025 disclosures showed assets and equity declined after goodwill impairment recognition. Impairment risk tied to goodwill remains after the web novel subsidiary merger. In an IP-concentrated structure, shifts in the success of key titles can flow directly into results.
