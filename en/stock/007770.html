02

Business structure

Hanil Chemical IND was founded in 1972 as a specialist maker of zinc oxide and listed on KOSDAQ in 1992. The company runs three business segments: zinc oxide, coatings, and recycled plastics manufacturing.

Zinc oxide is used as a raw material in the tire, automotive rubber product, and ferrite industries, so production trends in those sectors feed directly into its main demand.

According to a FnGuide summary of a September 2024 report, the sale of stakes in a subsidiary helped secure funds and improve management efficiency. A company-level summary says the zinc oxide unit keeps a leading industry share on the back of a stable market position and quality competitiveness.

The same summary says the coatings unit grew sales through new market development and stronger marketing. Recycled plastics sales are not broken out in the public summaries, so segment revenue and mix should be read from the segment disclosure in the 2026 annual report.

Because key customers are concentrated among tire and rubber makers, changes in tire and automotive output reach zinc oxide orders. This link is a starting point for quarterly earnings swings.

Among the 121 companies in the chemical sector, the company ranked 108th by market capitalization as of December 2025 according to Alphasquare. It therefore competes in a specific product niche as a small-to-mid-sized materials maker rather than on scale with large petrochemical groups.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual revenue fell 22.0% from KRW 156.3 billion in 2022 to KRW 121.9 billion in 2023, which makes 2022 the largest year and 2023 the smallest year in the 2022 to 2025 span. Revenue rose 2.7% to KRW 125.3 billion in 2024 and then eased 0.7% to KRW 124.4 billion in 2025.

Operating profit of KRW 4.89 billion in 2022 became an operating loss of KRW 9.41 billion in 2023, and the operating margin moved from 3.1% to -7.7%. The 2024 operating loss narrowed to KRW 6.57 billion, with a margin of -5.2%, before widening to KRW 12 billion in 2025, with a margin of -9.6%.

The company recorded losses for three consecutive years from 2023 through 2025. Profit attributable to controlling shareholders turned positive at KRW 4.99 billion in 2024 but returned to losses of KRW 14.7 billion in 2023 and KRW 18.3 billion in 2025.

Net loss including non-controlling interests was KRW 20.3 billion in 2025, which is larger than the controlling-shareholder loss.

In 2024 the operating loss coexisted with a controlling-shareholder profit, so non-operating items may have had a large effect, and the years where operating and net results diverged should be read separately.

Operating cash flow went from negative figures of KRW 6.37 billion in 2022, KRW 2.23 billion in 2023 and KRW 7.44 billion in 2024 to positive KRW 2.76 billion in 2025.

The debt ratio rose from 32.1% in 2022 to 46.9% in 2025, when total liabilities were KRW 40.8 billion, total equity was KRW 87 billion, and controlling-shareholder equity was KRW 88.9 billion.

In quarterly terms, revenue of KRW 30.2 billion and an operating loss of KRW 4.69 billion in the third quarter of 2025 improved to revenue of KRW 30.9 billion and an operating loss of KRW 2.12 billion in the fourth quarter.

The first quarter of 2026 showed revenue of KRW 35.2 billion and an operating loss of KRW 816 million, and the second quarter moved to revenue of KRW 38.3 billion and an operating profit of KRW 1.51 billion.

The third quarter of 2026 recorded revenue of KRW 34.5 billion, down 10.0% from the prior quarter but up 14.4% from a year earlier, and an operating loss of KRW 2.35 billion.

Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 993 million in the second quarter of 2026 and a loss of KRW 3.48 billion in the third quarter, and the last four quarters total a controlling-shareholder net loss of KRW 12.1 billion.

Whether the second-quarter profit reflects cost improvement or one-off factors cannot be separated from the quarterly figures alone.

Bear factors

The company recorded losses for three consecutive years from 2023 through 2025, and the 2025 operating loss of KRW 12 billion pushed the operating margin to -9.6% from -5.2% in 2024. The 2025 net loss including non-controlling interests was KRW 20.3 billion, and the controlling-shareholder loss was KRW 18.3 billion.

Revenue fell from KRW 156.3 billion in 2022 to KRW 124.4 billion in 2025, and the 0.7% decline in 2025 means sales did not keep recovering.

Durability of the quarterly profit

The second-quarter turn to profit lasted only one quarter, and the third quarter returned to an operating loss of KRW 2.35 billion. Controlling-shareholder net losses over the last four quarters total KRW 12.1 billion, which the KRW 993 million profit in the second quarter of 2026 did not offset.

Until profits repeat across consecutive quarters, the swing cannot be treated as a structural change in earnings.

Rising debt ratio and shrinking equity

The debt ratio rose from 33.0% in 2024 to 46.9% in 2025. Accumulated losses since 2023 have reduced controlling-shareholder equity, so continued losses could weaken the financial buffer. Dividends per share have also declined since 2022, which adds to the weaker shareholder return outlook.
