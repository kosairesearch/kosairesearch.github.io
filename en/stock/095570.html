AJ Networks: Rental Earnings Base, Rising Distribution Mix

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders has risen three years running since 2023, while operating profit has fallen two years in a row since 2024, and a growing distribution revenue share has become the key variable between top-line growth and profitability.

02

Business structure

AJ Networks runs a B2B rental business covering items such as pallets, IT equipment, industrial safety gear and construction machinery, alongside warehousing and distribution.

The business is split into rental, warehousing and distribution, and other segments, and the 2025 first-quarter consolidated item mix was rental 65.4%, warehousing and distribution 30.8%, and other 3.8%.

On an annual basis for 2022, a report put the mix at rental 53.7%, warehousing and distribution 37.8%, and other 8.5%. The standalone entity handles B2B rental, while warehousing, distribution and other activities run through subsidiaries.

The pallet market is known to be an oligopoly shared with a pallet pool operator, and a 2023 report estimated AJ Networks' domestic share at the low 30% range.

In the first half of 2026, standalone pallet revenue reached KRW 213.0 billion, up 42% from KRW 149.7 billion a year earlier, with overseas distribution expansion cited as the driver.

Over the same period, the OA and IT equipment segment grew from KRW 120.1 billion to KRW 140.8 billion, and its operating margin improved from 8.3% to 10.7%. Distribution's share of total company revenue rose from 19% in 2023 to 35% in the first half of 2026, and distribution earns lower margins than rental.

In July 2025, subsidiary AJ Daewon was sold, removing its revenue from the consolidated base. In rental, the company competes with other listed rental operators such as LOTTE rental.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual revenue was largest in 2022 at KRW 1.21 trillion and smallest in 2023 at KRW 1 trillion, which was down 17.1% year on year. It then rose to KRW 1.01 trillion in 2024, up 1.2%, and to KRW 1.07 trillion in 2025, up 5.5%, marking two consecutive years of growth from 2024 to 2025.

Operating profit was largest in 2023 at KRW 78.4 billion, up 3.9% from KRW 75.4 billion in 2022, and then fell to KRW 72.8 billion in 2024, down 7.1%, and to KRW 71.5 billion in 2025, down 1.7%, declining two consecutive years from 2024 to 2025.

The operating margin was 6.2% in 2022, 7.8% in 2023, 7.2% in 2024 and 6.7% in 2025, so margin improvement stayed limited even in years of revenue growth.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders rose from KRW 8.77 billion in 2022 to KRW 16.5 billion in 2023, up 87.8%, to KRW 21.8 billion in 2024, up 32.0%, and to KRW 28.4 billion in 2025, up 30.6%, growing three consecutive years from 2023 to 2025.

Because net income rose in 2024 and 2025 even as operating profit declined, factors outside operating profit, such as non-operating items, likely contributed.

Net income including non-controlling interests was KRW 28.3 billion in 2025, within KRW 0.1 billion of the controlling-shareholder figure, so non-controlling interests were minimal.

Operating cash flow was KRW 18.3 billion in 2022 and -KRW 18.1 billion in 2023, turned to KRW 33.4 billion in 2024 and KRW 75.9 billion in 2025. The debt ratio fell from 309.4% in 2022 to 286.5% in 2023, then edged up to 289.1% in 2025, when total liabilities stood at KRW 1.32 trillion.

Quarterly revenue was KRW 283.5 billion in Q3 2025, KRW 272.4 billion in Q4 2025 (down 3.9% quarter on quarter), KRW 287.2 billion in Q1 2026, KRW 327.8 billion in Q2 2026 (up 14.2%), and KRW 327.1 billion in Q3 2026, staying above KRW 300 billion from Q2 2026 onward.

Quarterly operating profit was KRW 13.2 billion in Q3 2025, then KRW 25.2 billion in Q4 2025 (up 91.8% quarter on quarter), and KRW 21.9 billion, KRW 21.5 billion and KRW 22.4 billion in the first three quarters of 2026, remaining at KRW 20 billion or more since Q4 2025.

One brokerage analysis pointed to a one-off cost of KRW 7.6 billion in Q4 2025 related to exiting the US industrial safety equipment business, and said rental profitability was high excluding that item.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders moved sharply: KRW 13.3 billion in Q3 2025, KRW 4.88 billion in Q4 2025, KRW 8.56 billion in Q1 2026, KRW 28.3 billion in Q2 2026 (up 231.1% quarter on quarter), and KRW 2.77 billion in Q3 2026 (down 90.2% quarter on quarter).

Q2 2026 net income exceeded operating profit of KRW 21.5 billion, while Q3 2026 net income was far below operating profit of KRW 22.4 billion, suggesting that items below operating profit are swinging quarterly results.

The sum of controlling-shareholder net income over the last four quarters, from Q4 2025 to Q3 2026, was KRW 44.5 billion.

05

Industry analysis

B2B rental is an industry shaped by corporate equipment investment cycles and the interest rate environment. In 2024, brokerage reports noted that rate cuts would improve corporate investment sentiment and be favorable for rental demand.

In the first half of 2026, a report citing Korea Ratings data said rental demand rose as prices of OA and IT equipment increased. The improvement in that segment's operating margin from 8.3% to 10.7% can be read in the same direction.

Pallet rental is known to be an oligopoly, so market share is a key indicator for this business. By contrast, warehousing and distribution revenue rises with overseas distribution volume but carries thin margins, so its economics differ from rental.

A brokerage analysis said the warehousing and distribution segment returned to operating profit in Q3 2025 after eight quarters of losses. Among peers, listed rental operators such as LOTTE rental compete in the sector, and AJ Networks is building its distribution share on a rental profit base.

The position in the industry cycle is hard to pin down, so how rental demand and distribution volume diverge and feed into results is the central point for this company.

Whether quarterly swings in net income attributable to controlling shareholders stem from non-operating items is also a point to track.
