02

Business structure

Cheil Grinding Wheel IND makes and sells grinding wheels, which are consumables used in grinding processes. In 2025, general grinding wheels accounted for 93% of revenue, CBN grinding wheels for 6%, and raw materials and other items for 1% (JOBKOREA company data as of December 2025).

The grinding wheel product line made up 99.11% of revenue in December 2025 (FnGuide revenue mix), so results depend almost entirely on this one business.

General wheels are used for grinding work in steel, automotive, machinery and shipbuilding, while CBN wheels serve high-speed, high-performance grinding, according to the DART quarterly filing. Production is based at the Pohang headquarters plant.

The consolidated group also includes one domestic subsidiary, two Indonesian subsidiaries and a Vietnamese subsidiary (2023 quarterly filing). In 2025, domestic sales made up 79.3% and exports 20.7%.

The product behaves more like a consumable in the production line than a finished good, so customers' operating rates and maintenance and processing volumes form the base demand.

On competition, a 2024 press report described the company as the largest domestic grinding wheel maker with a share of roughly 70%, and said it spends about 10% of revenue on R&D each year.

Abrasive grain is the largest raw material input for general wheels, at about 64% of purchases in the first quarter of 2024 per the filing, followed by glass fiber fabric and resin and bond materials. The gap between product prices and raw material costs is the key variable for profit.

04

Earnings analysis

On a DART-disclosed basis, annual revenue rose from KRW 83.4 billion in 2022 to KRW 85.2 billion in 2023, a 2.2% increase, then fell 3.8% to KRW 82 billion in 2024 and another 3.8% to KRW 78.9 billion in 2025. The largest year in 2022 to 2025 was 2023, and the smallest was 2025.

Operating profit grew from KRW 5.13 billion in 2022 to KRW 7.89 billion in 2023, a 53.9% increase, and then fell 27.1% to KRW 5.75 billion in 2024 and 26.3% to KRW 4.24 billion in 2025. The operating margin moved from 6.1% in 2022 to 9.3% in 2023, 7.0% in 2024 and 5.4% in 2025.

Margin compression was steeper than the revenue decline, so profit fell faster than sales. A press report on the nine-month 2024 results attributed the decline to weaker grinding wheel sales during a market slowdown, together with higher personnel and R&D costs.

Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 2.19 billion in 2022, KRW 10.9 billion in 2023 (up 398.4%), KRW 5.99 billion in 2024 (down 45.0%) and KRW 13.6 billion in 2025 (up 127.4%), making 2025 the largest year in the period.

Total net profit including non-controlling interests was also KRW 13.6 billion in 2025. Net profit exceeding operating profit by a wide margin in 2025 suggests non-operating items contributed meaningfully, so core operations and bottom-line results moved in different directions that year.

At the end of 2025, total equity was KRW 108.8 billion, of which KRW 108.7 billion belonged to controlling shareholders, and total liabilities were KRW 12.4 billion. The debt ratio fell from 17.0% in 2024 to 11.4% in 2025.

Operating cash flow was KRW 4.49 billion in 2022, KRW 12.2 billion in 2023, KRW 6.8 billion in 2024 and KRW 8.08 billion in 2025.

On a quarterly basis, revenue of KRW 20.8 billion and operating profit of KRW 1.57 billion in 3Q 2025 gave way to revenue of KRW 19.9 billion (down 4.3% quarter on quarter) and operating profit of KRW 905 million (down 42.4%) in 4Q 2025.

In 1Q 2026, revenue of KRW 19.5 billion was 1.9% lower quarter on quarter, yet operating profit rose 69.7% to KRW 1.54 billion. In 2Q 2026, revenue reached KRW 21.6 billion (up 10.7%) and operating profit KRW 2.07 billion (up 34.8%).

In 3Q 2026, revenue was KRW 20.2 billion (down 2.9% year on year), operating profit KRW 1.59 billion (up 1.2% year on year) and net profit attributable to controlling shareholders KRW 1.29 billion (down 23.7% year on year).

Over the last four quarters, from 4Q 2025 to 3Q 2026, net profit attributable to controlling shareholders totaled KRW 6.33 billion.

Industry analysis

Grinding wheels are consumables used in grinding and cutting processes across steel, automotive, machinery and shipbuilding plants, so demand depends on the operating levels of these downstream industries. A press report on the nine-month 2024 results said weaker market conditions reduced grinding wheel sales.

By contrast, the information aggregator Wisereport said as of September 3, 2026 that higher operating rates in steel, shipbuilding, automotive and machinery were lifting grinding wheel demand. The industry picture therefore looks weak in 2024 and recovering in 2026.

This is a qualitative aggregator description rather than industry shipment or market size data. The company's market position is the domestic leader with a share of roughly 70%, based on a 2024 press report. Its leadership is likely supported by long-standing downstream customer ties and a high-performance CBN lineup.

Exports were 20.7% of 2025 sales, with general wheels exporting 22% and CBN wheels 5.5%. Overseas production runs through the Indonesian and Vietnamese subsidiaries.

On raw materials, the spread between abrasive grain costs and product prices determines margins, so pricing power and cost flow affect profit more directly than the industry cycle does.

Risk factors

Downstream cyclicality

Demand for grinding wheels moves with operating levels in steel, shipbuilding, automotive and machinery. A 2024 press report described sales weakening during a market slowdown. A renewed downturn in downstream utilization could lower revenue and profit together. Downstream utilization trends are the next thing to watch.

Raw material spread

Abrasive grain is the largest purchase item for general wheels, at about 64% of purchases in the first quarter of 2024 per the filing. If raw material costs rise and price increases lag, the operating margin can narrow. With the 2025 margin at 5.4%, changes in the spread have a sizable effect. The timing of raw material costs and price adjustments is a key variable.

Non-operating items and overseas exposure

Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 13.6 billion in 2025 far exceeded operating profit of KRW 4.24 billion, indicating non-operating items drove bottom-line results. The 23.7% year-on-year decline in 3Q 2026 net profit appears to follow the same pattern.

Overseas production through the Indonesian and Vietnamese subsidiaries and an export share of 20.7% expose the company to currency movements. Non-operating items and exchange rate trends need to be tracked together.

Overall view

Cheil Grinding Wheel IND is a domestic leader whose results depend almost entirely on grinding wheels. On a DART-disclosed basis, annual revenue peaked at KRW 85.2 billion in 2023 and then fell to KRW 82 billion in 2024 and KRW 78.9 billion in 2025.

Operating profit also declined for two straight years after reaching KRW 7.89 billion in 2023, to KRW 5.75 billion in 2024 and KRW 4.24 billion in 2025, and the operating margin fell to 5.4% in 2025.

Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders rose 127.4% to KRW 13.6 billion in 2025, but because it far exceeded operating profit, non-operating items appear to have contributed substantially.

On a quarterly basis, operating profit bottomed at KRW 905 million in 4Q 2025 and recovered through 1Q and 2Q 2026, and 3Q 2026 operating profit of KRW 1.59 billion was 1.2% above the prior-year quarter.

However, 3Q 2026 revenue fell 2.9% and net profit attributable to controlling shareholders fell 23.7% over the same period, so a recovery in sales and in net profit has not yet been confirmed.

Higher operating rates in downstream steel, shipbuilding, automotive and machinery are cited as demand support, while the raw material spread and the single-business structure remain sources of volatility.

The next checkpoints are the full-year operating margin and net profit composition in the 2026 annual business report, and the 1Q 2027 results.
