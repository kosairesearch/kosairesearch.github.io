Myoung Shin Industry: Earnings Recovery Hinges on New Business Wins

Myoung Shin Industry's sales and operating profit rose year on year in the first half of 2026, but it swung to a net loss attributable to controlling shareholders in the third quarter, so whether the recovery continues is the key open question.

  1. 1

    Sales in the first half of 2026 rose 19.9% and operating profit rose 16.1% year on year, with overseas subsidiaries cited as a main driver.

  2. 2

    Annual operating profit fell for two consecutive years after peaking in 2023, and the 2025 operating margin of 6.3% was the lowest in the 2022–2025 period.

  3. 3

  4. 4

  5. 5

02

Business structure

Myoung Shin Industry was founded in 1982 as an automotive parts maker and focuses on body parts made with hot stamping, a process that heats metal, shapes it, and then rapidly cools it. The process is seen as fitting the lightweighting needs of electric vehicles that must keep battery life long.

The company holds multiple domestic and foreign patents, which is cited as sustaining barriers to entry based on technology and capital. Its customer base centers on global electric vehicle makers, and a company official has said it supplies body parts such as door frames to them.

Volume from Hyundai Motor and KIA was also cited as a favorable factor in an April 2026 brokerage report. A 2023 news report said the company was also supplying hot-stamped lightweight body parts to Tesla. Overseas operations grew after the consolidation of the Brazilian subsidiary.

In the United States, Simwon America makes automotive body parts and is 89.7% held by a subsidiary, Simwon Tech, and at the end of 2025 its total assets equaled 15.04% of consolidated assets. The largest shareholder, MS Autotech, holds 45.45%, so the parent's results and funding are cited as variables for the company.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual sales rose from KRW 1.52 trillion in 2022 to KRW 1.74 trillion in 2023, up 14.8%, the largest year of 2022–2025. Sales then fell 9.5% to KRW 1.57 trillion in 2024 and rose 2.9% to KRW 1.62 trillion in 2025.

Operating profit peaked at KRW 208.1 billion in 2023, up 64.1%, then fell 28.8% to KRW 148.1 billion in 2024 and 30.7% to KRW 102.6 billion in 2025, a two-year decline from 2024 through 2025.

The operating margin moved from 8.4% in 2022 to 12.0% in 2023, 9.4% in 2024, and 6.3% in 2025, the lowest of the four years even though 2025 sales grew. Analysts attributed the slower 2025 growth to a temporary slowdown in front-end demand from the EV slump.

Profit attributable to controlling shareholders went from KRW 85.7 billion in 2022 to KRW 152.7 billion in 2023, then KRW 129.1 billion in 2024 and KRW 68.3 billion in 2025, down 47.1% in the latter year.

Net income including non-controlling interests was KRW 85.7 billion, KRW 152.7 billion, KRW 129.1 billion and KRW 68.3 billion over 2022–2025. At the end of 2025, total equity was KRW 768.9 billion, of which KRW 755.9 billion belonged to controlling shareholders, and total liabilities were KRW 651.8 billion.

The debt ratio fell from 142.1% in 2022 to 73.5% in 2024 and rose to 84.8% in 2025. Operating cash flow rose from KRW 97.7 billion in 2022 to KRW 246.2 billion in 2023, then declined to KRW 203.7 billion in 2024 and KRW 158.9 billion in 2025.

Quarterly, sales were KRW 443.3 billion and operating profit KRW 36.3 billion in the third quarter of 2025; in the fourth quarter sales fell to KRW 417.3 billion, down 5.9%, and operating profit to KRW 10.5 billion, down 71.1%.

Operating profit recovered to KRW 20.6 billion in the first quarter of 2026, up 96.5%, and to KRW 44.1 billion in the second quarter, up 114.2%, on sales of KRW 497.1 billion, the highest operating profit of the last five quarters.

The drivers cited were expanded supply of body parts to global EV makers and the consolidation of the Brazilian subsidiary.

In the third quarter of 2026, sales were KRW 403.3 billion, down 18.9% from the prior quarter and 9.0% year on year, operating profit was KRW 34.8 billion, down 4.4% year on year, and profit attributable to controlling shareholders turned into a net loss of KRW 1.13 billion.

05

Industry analysis

Many analysts view 2025 as a period of temporary demand slowdown in the EV market during the so-called chasm phase. In an April 2026 report, Daishin Securities judged that production weakness at North American EV customers would limit margin improvement.

The same report saw gradual improvement in earnings capacity from the recovery of production in China and stabilized costs. Stable steel prices, a steady exchange rate, and easing US tariff burdens were presented as background to the margin recovery.

In 2023, Hana Securities judged that earnings track customers' production volumes and that high growth can persist absent strong unit-price cut pressure. On the automaker side, rising production volumes at Hyundai Motor and KIA were cited as favorable.

The expansion of robotaxi and next-generation platforms at North American customers is cited as a non-automotive variable for the industry. The company's results are sensitive to specific customers' production swings, and whether a broader customer base lowers that sensitivity is an open question.

06

Outlook

In an April 2026 report, Daishin Securities analyzed that the annual operating margin could recover to around 7%, citing the recovery of production in China and the consolidation of the Brazilian subsidiary.

The same report named robotaxi production ramp-up and new non-automotive product wins as the main catalysts for a re-rating. Expectations for a full-scale robotaxi production start in the first half of 2026 were discussed.

New non-automotive wins remain a checkpoint until they are confirmed through disclosures or investor relations. On the overseas side, the US subsidiary Simwon America disclosed on October 30, 2026 that it was withdrawing its Nasdaq listing.

The original plan involved a 1.5 million share primary offering and a 1 million share secondary sale, with proceeds intended for expansion in the eastern US and in Mexico and Canada.

The sixth-series convertible bond with warrants has a remaining balance of KRW 10.935 billion, and early redemption requests become possible from November 6, 2027. The parent MS Autotech reportedly offered a 9.22% stake in Myoung Shin Industry as exchange collateral to repay group borrowings.

The next checkpoint is the annual operating margin in the 2026 annual report and the contribution from overseas subsidiaries.

08

Bull factors

Larger scale from Brazil consolidation and global EV volumes

In the first half of 2026, both sales and operating profit increased year on year. Expanded body-parts supply to global EV makers and the Brazilian subsidiary consolidation were cited as drivers. Second-quarter 2026 operating profit of KRW 44.1 billion was the highest of the last five quarters.

Robotaxi and non-automotive wins as catalysts

Expectations for robotaxi volume production were discussed. Daishin Securities named North American customer robotaxi production and non-automotive new product wins as triggers for a re-rating. Confirmed new product wins would broaden the business beyond body parts, which bulls cite.

Margin recovery outlook

Daishin Securities analyzed in an April 2026 report that the annual operating margin could recover to around 7% on easing US tariffs, the Brazilian consolidation and cost stabilization. Bulls point to the 6.3% margin in 2025 as the base. This is a brokerage estimate, not reported results.

09

Bear factors

Two years of profit declines and a third-quarter net loss

Operating profit fell two years in a row to KRW 148.1 billion in 2024 and KRW 102.6 billion in 2025, and profit attributable to controlling shareholders fell to KRW 129.1 billion and KRW 68.3 billion. The third quarter of 2026 turned to a net loss of KRW 1.13 billion. The durability of recent quarterly profit is not yet confirmed.

Weak production at North American EV customers

Daishin Securities said production weakness at North American EV customers would limit margin improvement. The same report noted that production at key North American customers was still declining in 2026. The 2025 slowdown in front-end EV demand is another bearish point.

Parent stake structure and dilution concerns

The largest shareholder, MS Autotech, holds 45.45%, and in May 2026 a KRW 66 billion exchangeable bond plan was disclosed covering a 9.22% stake. The US subsidiary's Nasdaq listing was withdrawn on October 30, 2026, but during the plan concerns about potential selling and dilution were raised.

10

Risk factors

Governance and parent funding

The parent's weak results keep its funding and balance sheet changes as a variable. The exchangeable bond and the 9.22% stake offered as collateral are cited as potential selling pressure. Given the high largest-shareholder stake, the parent's funding position can directly affect the shareholder base.

Customer concentration and EV demand

Earnings track customer production volumes, so production swings at North American EV customers feed directly into profitability. EV-slump-driven demand softness hit 2025. Strong unit-price cut pressure could limit profit recovery even if customer output grows.

Overseas funding and bond redemption

With the Nasdaq listing withdrawn, the US subsidiary's expansion funding must come from other sources. The sixth-series warrant bond has KRW 10.935 billion outstanding, with early redemption requests possible from November 6, 2027. A call option lets the company repurchase 50% of the bonds.

Overall view

Myoung Shin Industry's sales rose from KRW 1.52 trillion in 2022 to KRW 1.62 trillion in 2025, but operating profit fell for two consecutive years after peaking in 2023. Operating profit recovered in the first and second quarters of 2026, and first-half 2026 sales and operating profit rose year on year.

The third quarter of 2026 swung to a net loss of KRW 1.13 billion attributable to controlling shareholders, so the durability of the recovery is not yet confirmed. Bulls point to the Brazilian consolidation, possible robotaxi and non-automotive wins, and the outlook for margin recovery.

Bears point to weak production at North American EV customers, the EV slowdown, and dilution concerns tied to the parent stake and exchangeable bonds. The US subsidiary withdrew its Nasdaq listing on October 30, 2026, and early redemption of the warrant bond becomes possible from November 6, 2027.

The next checkpoint is the annual operating margin and overseas contribution confirmed in the 2026 annual report.
