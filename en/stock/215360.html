02

Business structure

Woory Industrial develops and manufactures HVAC (heating, ventilation and air-conditioning) components and electrical parts for automobiles.

A Samsung Securities company visit note from June 2023 put the 2022 product mix at 23.6% for HVAC actuators, 12.9% for high-voltage PTC heaters, 28.1% for control heads, 16.9% for other parts and 18.5% for CKD (completely knocked down parts kits exported overseas).

The same note described Woory Industrial as a second-tier supplier in thermal management systems and named Hanon Systems and Hyundai WIA as its key customers.

A January 2022 technical analysis report listed DENSO, Visteon and Hanon Systems as parts customers, and Tesla, Daimler, Rivian, Hyundai Motor and Kia as vehicle-maker customers. Overseas vehicle-maker deliveries to Stellantis and BMW began in 2022.

Product lines include HVAC actuators, control heads, high-voltage PTC heaters and clutch coils, and the growth case has centered on the widening role of EV thermal management. As of the 2023 data, the company itself ran its domestic plants and R&D, which were about 4% of sales.

On March 31, 2026 the company disclosed a KRW 18.16 billion loan to affiliate Woory Automotives Mexico at 4.6% interest, to fund facility investment. The loan runs from April 10, 2026 to April 10, 2031. Headcount stood at 325 as of June 30, 2026.

04

Earnings analysis

On a periodic-report basis, annual revenue rose from KRW 364.6 billion in 2022 to KRW 462.2 billion in 2023 (up 26.8% year on year), KRW 510.6 billion in 2024 (up 10.5%) and KRW 532 billion in 2025 (up 4.2%).

Revenue increased for three consecutive years from 2023 through 2025, with 2025 the largest year and 2022 the smallest in the 2022–2025 span.

Operating profit moved from an operating loss of KRW 24.7 billion in 2022 to operating profit of KRW 20.5 billion in 2023 (a swing to profit), fell to KRW 15 billion in 2024 (down 26.9%) and recovered to KRW 18.7 billion in 2025 (up 24.8%).

The operating margin was -6.8% in 2022, 4.4% in 2023, 2.9% in 2024 and 3.5% in 2025, staying in the low single digits since 2023. The 2023 return to profit was attributed to stabilizing raw material prices and lower export logistics costs.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders went from a loss of KRW 18 billion in 2022 to KRW 17.7 billion in 2023 (a swing to profit), then KRW 13.1 billion in 2024 (down 25.8%) and KRW 14 billion in 2025 (up 6.8%).

Net income including non-controlling interests was KRW 14.2 billion in 2025, a different figure from the controlling-shareholder number. Operating cash flow was -KRW 3.41 billion in 2022 and -KRW 6.78 billion in 2023, then turned positive at KRW 41.2 billion in 2024 and KRW 32.6 billion in 2025.

Quarterly, revenue went from KRW 129.3 billion with operating profit of KRW 4.78 billion in the third quarter of 2025 to KRW 132.4 billion (up 2.4% quarter on quarter) with operating profit of KRW 5.66 billion (up 18.4%) in the fourth quarter.

Revenue then fell to KRW 119.9 billion in the first quarter of 2026 (down 9.5%) with operating profit of KRW 4.28 billion (down 24.4%), before recovering to KRW 127.1 billion and KRW 4.75 billion in the second quarter.

The third quarter of 2026 brought revenue of KRW 115.5 billion (down 10.7% year on year) and operating profit of KRW 2.96 billion (down 38.0%), the lowest of the last five quarters, and a net loss attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 8.27 billion that marked a swing to loss.

Controlling-shareholder net income for the four quarters from the fourth quarter of 2025 through the third quarter of 2026 totaled KRW 8.23 billion, and the gap between operating profit of KRW 2.96 billion and that net loss is wide.

05

Industry analysis

In 2026 Korean auto parts makers face rising revenue volatility as US tariff policy and the shift to electric vehicles overlap. NICE Investors Service put its 2026 earnings outlook for auto parts makers in the lowest of its three tiers, 'deterioration' (reported January 8, 2026).

It also warned that as vehicle production shifts toward the United States, the revenue of parts makers based in Korea could become more volatile.

In the same assessment, first-tier suppliers with high overseas co-expansion were seen as less exposed to domestic effects, while second- and third-tier suppliers that depend on Korea were described as more sensitive to changes in domestic output.

The 2023 Samsung Securities note classified Woory Industrial as a second-tier thermal management supplier, which places it on the more sensitive side of that split. Hyundai Motor Group plans to raise HMGMA utilization and expand the localization rate of parts from 60% in 2025 to 80% by 2030.

NICE said this localization drive brings lower export volumes, tougher competition for new model awards and pressure on domestic procurement prices.

On raw materials, Daishin Securities said in an April 2026 industry report that higher oil prices tied to the Iran conflict were lifting resin, aluminum and steel costs, making higher production costs unavoidable.

Separately, a November 13, 2026 report said the auto parts theme strengthened on expectations for Korean automakers' quality competitiveness and expanded direct exports. Theme strength and company-level earnings are tracked as separate variables.

Outlook

The most concrete item to track is the loan to affiliate Woory Automotives Mexico, which funds facility investment from April 10, 2026 to April 10, 2031. The key question is whether overseas production capacity converts into profit.

As of the most recent closing period at disclosure, the affiliate's total assets were KRW 12.63 billion, total liabilities were KRW 21.17 billion and total equity was negative at about KRW -8.54 billion.

Whether the loan is repaid and the affiliate's capital structure improves together is therefore the next point to watch. NICE Investors Service said that large investments in electrification and in US production capacity were expected to be largely completed around 2025, easing the burden.

It also said the extent of improvement in earnings power would vary by company given uncertainty from tariff changes, a smaller EV subsidy and the global reshuffling of OEM production bases. Initial utilization of electrification facilities remains low, which NICE cited as a constraint on earnings recovery.

Operating profit of KRW 2.96 billion in the third quarter of 2026 was the lowest of the last five quarters, so the level to which quarterly operating profit returns is the key marker.

Results for the fourth quarter and full-year 2026 will be reported in the 2026 annual report, whose filing deadline is the end of March 2027. The Hyundai Motor Group target of an 80% parts localization rate by 2030 is a benchmark for how fast the domestic second-tier supply base shifts.

Financial burden at the overseas affiliate

The KRW 18.16 billion loan disclosed on March 31, 2026 carries 4.6% interest and runs to April 10, 2031. The affiliate's total equity was negative, at about KRW -8.54 billion, on its most recent closing at disclosure.

Recovery of the loan and the operating performance of the facilities are the key variables for future financial burden.

Raw material and cost volatility

Overall view

Woory Industrial moved from an operating loss in 2022 to operating profit of KRW 18.7 billion in 2025, and annual revenue has risen for three consecutive years from 2023 through 2025. However, the operating margin has stayed in a 2.9% to 4.4% range, so cost and utilization changes pass quickly into earnings.

Third-quarter 2026 revenue fell to KRW 115.5 billion and operating profit to KRW 2.96 billion, with a net loss attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 8.27 billion, so the annual recovery has wobbled at the quarterly level.

On the external side, NICE placed its 2026 earnings outlook for auto parts makers at 'deterioration', and second- and third-tier suppliers have been described as more sensitive amid tariffs and production relocation.

Supportive factors include expanding overseas OEM supply, positive operating cash flow in 2024 and 2025, and room to expand overseas production. Negative factors include the sharp third-quarter drop, negative equity at the overseas affiliate and rising raw material costs.

On valuation, the price-to-book ratio sits at a wide discount to net asset value and the P/E is below the lower end of its historical band, but the thin four-quarter earnings base frames how those readings should be interpreted.

The next points to check are preliminary full-year 2026 results, the 2026 annual report due by the end of March 2027 and the Q1 2027 quarterly report.
