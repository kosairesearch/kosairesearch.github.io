02

Business structure

SK eternix, established in 2024, develops, invests in and operates renewable power assets spanning solar, onshore wind, ESS and fuel cells.

It runs a value chain covering site planning, permits, financing, EPC and operations management, and supports corporate renewable procurement through direct PPAs and power brokerage. Its business divides into three strands: fuel cells, ESS and solar/wind.

In fuel cells it imports Bloom Energy's solid oxide fuel cells (SOFC) as the domestic main equipment supplier; after starting with a 20MW project in Cheongju in 2021, it runs 89MW across five sites according to a Sisajournal report, with 111MW under construction at three more sites that would take the total to about 200MW.

The Chungju fuel cell cluster is described as the world's largest of its kind, and because SOFCs do not burn fuel directly they emit no air pollutants and are quiet enough for installation near residential areas.

In ESS, the company is reported to run about 30 sites totaling 800MWh in Korea's C&I peak-control market and to hold the top market share. Its renewable generation operations began with the Namwon solar plant in 2008 and now exceed 500GWh, and it has built a 1.3GW offshore wind pipeline.

On the customer side, it pursues distributed power sites tied to semiconductor clusters and AI data center demand. The sharp swings in quarterly revenue reflect a business in which results cluster around the timing of completions and handovers.

04

Earnings analysis

On the disclosed annual figures, revenue rose from KRW 332.2 billion in 2024 to KRW 385.6 billion in 2025, up 16.1% year on year. Operating profit went from KRW 37.6 billion to KRW 53 billion, a 40.8% increase, and the operating margin rose from 11.3% to 13.7%.

Profit attributable to controlling shareholders increased from KRW 22.4 billion to KRW 30.7 billion, up 37.3%, while net profit including non-controlling interests is recorded at the same KRW 22.4 billion and KRW 30.7 billion for the two years.

Over the same period operating cash flow shifted from KRW 66.8 billion to -KRW 25.2 billion, a gap between rising profits and cash generation. At the end of 2025 total liabilities stood at KRW 1.04 trillion against total equity of KRW 273 billion, and the debt-to-equity ratio rose from 202.4% to 382.4%.

The large project financing behind the Chungju and Daesowon fuel cell sites, a KRW 461 billion arrangement, appears to be reflected in this debt structure. Quarterly figures are far more uneven.

In Q3 2025 revenue was KRW 46.4 billion, operating profit KRW 1.17 billion and the net loss attributable to controlling shareholders KRW 5.05 billion.

In Q4 2025 revenue jumped to KRW 248 billion, up 434.1% from the prior quarter, operating profit reached KRW 41.2 billion and profit attributable to controlling shareholders reached KRW 20.5 billion, turning profitable.

In its annual preliminary results announcement, the company cited a 180MW annual solar PPA with Solarnix, a 390MW development contract for the Sinan Uwoo offshore wind project and the delivery of a 40MW fuel cell in Chungju as Q4 achievements.

In Q1 2026 revenue fell to KRW 27.5 billion, down 88.9% from the prior quarter, operating profit was KRW 4.91 billion and the net loss attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 5.11 billion, a return to losses.

In Q2 revenue was KRW 240.9 billion and operating profit KRW 4.68 billion, but the net loss attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 2.89 billion continued, with the loss narrowing.

In Q3 revenue of KRW 228 billion rose 391.1% year on year, yet the company posted an operating loss of KRW 3.68 billion, a swing to loss from a year earlier, and the net loss attributable to controlling shareholders widened to KRW 12.3 billion.

The net loss attributable to controlling shareholders was far larger than the operating loss, suggesting that non-operating and financing costs weighed on results as well.

The Q4 2025 profit was offset by losses in the following three quarters, leaving the four-quarter total of profit attributable to controlling shareholders at KRW 185 million.

05

Industry analysis

Korea's renewable power market is in a policy transition. As the government reviews revisions to the Renewable Portfolio Standard (RPS), there is talk that the system could shift toward a contract-based market that goes a step beyond the existing scheme.

If that structure materializes, power supply through bidding or power purchase agreements (PPAs) would expand. On the demand side, the spread of AI data centers is lifting electricity demand, which raises the value of stable power sources. Fuel cells carry a structural risk, however, because they run on LNG.

Commentary suggests that as the system shifts toward clean hydrogen, gas-based fuel cell businesses could be marked down.

In response, SK eternix's SOFC units are described as using the LNG pipeline network while being designed to convert to dedicated clean-hydrogen generation without major equipment replacement once a national hydrogen pipeline network is built.

Compared with Doosan Fuel Cell in the same fuel cell industry, SK eternix differs in that it combines overseas main-equipment distribution rights with plant development, operation and PPAs in one business model.

Relative to the fuel cell capacity of one major Korean petrochemical company, reported at about 60MW, its build-out pace is described as fast. Forecasts also suggest ESS earnings could grow on long-term contracts as central contract bidding for ESS takes full effect from 2026.

06

Outlook

Guidance formally given in disclosures is limited, but the dated milestones are fuel cell capacity additions. The Icheon EcoPark (8.9MW) and Paju EcoPark 1 (9.9MW) have started commercial operation, and Paju EcoPark 2 (9.2MW) was reported to be scheduled for commercial operation in November 2026.

If these projects are completed by year-end, cumulative fuel cell capacity under operation would reach 228MW. The Icheon and Paju projects involve total project costs of about KRW 159.4 billion and are planned to produce 222GWh a year from 28MW of capacity.

In offshore wind, a 390MW development contract for the Sinan Uwoo project has been recognized, and the timing of larger construction revenue will depend on construction starts. In ESS, a 40MW BESS development contract in Jeju and the expansion of ESS operations in the United States are under way.

On ownership, SK Discovery's board decided to sell its entire 30.98% stake in SK eternix to a vehicle set up by KKR. Other reports say KKR invested about KRW 348 billion to acquire roughly 44% and became the largest shareholder, so the stake figures differ depending on the report.

The transaction is being pursued as a package with the renewable businesses of SK Innovation E&S and SK ecoplant, and a joint venture between SK and KKR is under review. A securities analyst expected SK eternix to be folded under the JV.

On credit, a domestic rating agency maintained the long- and short-term ratings at BBB+ and A3+ but placed them on review for downgrade.

08

Bull factors

Fuel cell and ESS capacity expansion

Fuel cell operating capacity, at 89MW according to a Sisajournal report, would rise to about 200MW once 111MW now under construction is added. If the Icheon and Paju projects also finish by year-end, cumulative capacity would reach 228MW.

In ESS, the company is reported to hold the top position in Korea's C&I peak-control market, giving it an established asset base.

Profit recovery in 2025 and a return to profit in Q4

Operating profit in 2025 rose 40.8%, and the operating margin climbed to 13.7% from 11.3% in 2024. Profit attributable to controlling shareholders turned positive in Q4 2025 at KRW 20.5 billion, supporting the annual improvement.

The company cited PPA signings, an offshore wind development contract and fuel cell deliveries as Q4 achievements.

PPA growth and a possible policy shift

The company signed an annual 180MW solar PPA with Solarnix and is carrying out a 390MW development contract for the Sinan Uwoo offshore wind project. As a shift of RPS toward a contract market is discussed, the share of long-term contracts based on bidding and PPAs could grow. That structure could increase the weight of recurring power-sales revenue from generation assets.

Bear factors

Losses continuing through 2026 quarters

Net losses attributable to controlling shareholders have run for three consecutive quarters, from Q1 through Q3 2026. Q3 turned to an operating loss of KRW 3.68 billion, and the KRW 228 billion of revenue in that quarter did not translate into profit. With the four-quarter total at KRW 185 million, earnings capacity is still thin.

Financial burden and weaker cash flow

The debt-to-equity ratio rose from 202.4% in 2024 to 382.4% in 2025, with total liabilities of KRW 1.04 trillion at the end of 2025. Operating cash flow turned negative at -KRW 25.2 billion in 2025.

Because the business relies heavily on project financing, it is exposed to changes in interest rates and funding conditions.

Hydrogen transition risk and ownership change

Gas-based fuel cells could be marked down if the system shifts toward clean hydrogen. A domestic rating agency kept the ratings but placed them on review for downgrade, reflecting concern that support from the SK group may weaken.

Uncertainty remains over how business direction and deal terms will be set during the KKR-centered ownership change.

Risk factors

Earnings volatility

Quarterly revenue moved sharply, from KRW 46.4 billion in Q3 2025 to KRW 248 billion in Q4 2025 and KRW 27.5 billion in Q1 2026. Because results depend on the timing of completions and handovers, continuity of earnings is hard to confirm.

Net losses attributable to controlling shareholders from Q1 through Q3 2026 mean that earnings stability still needs to be shown.

Financing and balance sheet

A debt-to-equity ratio of 382.4% and negative operating cash flow may limit financial room. Because expansion depends on external funding such as project financing, higher interest rates or changes in lender terms could feed into costs. With total equity of KRW 273 billion, how much room remains to absorb the debt load is the key question.

Policy, technology and ownership

The timing and design of any RPS shift into a contract market are not yet fixed, so the effect on revenue structure is uncertain. The hydrogen transition risk for LNG-based fuel cells remains.

The KKR-centered ownership change, the rating review and the planned SK-KKR joint venture overlap, so the direction of operations could still change.

Overall view

On the disclosed 2025 figures, SK eternix grew revenue to KRW 385.6 billion, operating profit to KRW 53 billion and profit attributable to controlling shareholders to KRW 30.7 billion. The 13.7% operating margin shows that the expansion of generation assets is partly visible in results.

However, net losses attributable to controlling shareholders have run for three consecutive quarters from Q1 through Q3 2026, and Q3 turned to an operating loss of KRW 3.68 billion.

With the four-quarter total of profit attributable to controlling shareholders at only KRW 185 million, the durability of earnings has yet to be shown. On the financial side, the debt-to-equity ratio of 382.4% and operating cash flow of -KRW 25.2 billion are burdens.

On the business side, the 228MW fuel cell target, the 390MW offshore wind development contract and PPA growth are cited as drivers. Against this, the hydrogen transition risk for LNG-based fuel cells, the rating review and the KKR-centered ownership change remain open uncertainties.

The next checkpoints are whether fuel cell capacity reaches 228MW by late December 2026, the 2026 annual results expected in early 2027, and the 2026 annual business report due by March 31, 2027.
