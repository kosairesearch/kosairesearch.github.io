Shrinking Sales and Persistent Losses; New EV Ramp Is the Key Test

Samwha Electronics, a ferrite-core focused components maker, posted losses in each of the four years from 2022 to 2025 and in each of the five quarters through the third quarter of 2026, so whether its new EV programs lift revenue is the next test.

02

Business structure

Samwha Electronics was founded in 1976 to make and sell ferrite cores and other electronic device components, and it has been listed on the KOSPI since 1987. The company is a member of the Samwha Capacitor Group, which comprises three listed and 14 unlisted companies.

Its subsidiaries include a Chinese ferrite-core manufacturer and another company, and it also holds a subsidiary that makes and sells iron oxide, linking it to upstream materials.

Its core product is the ferrite core, with applications spanning SMPS (switch-mode power supply) cores, deflection yoke (DY) cores and flyback transformer (FBT) cores.

One industry compilation shows ferrite cores and related products accounting for 100% of major business-segment sales in the second quarter of 2026, indicating heavy reliance on a single component family.

A market-share table lists a 70% global share for electromagnetic-wave absorber cores but only 2% for ferrite cores, suggesting a concentration in a niche product. On the EV side, the company describes expanding supply of key parts for the E-GMP platform.

It is much smaller than large component makers, and its position rests on niche material-based products. Exchange filings and DART documents list the legal name as Samwha Electronics Co. (Samhwa Jeonja Gongeop).

04

Earnings analysis

Annual sales declined from KRW 48.3 billion in 2022 to KRW 41.9 billion in 2023, KRW 33.4 billion in 2024 and KRW 29.6 billion in 2025. The declines were 13.3% in 2023, 20.3% in 2024 and 11.3% in 2025, making three straight years of decline from 2023 to 2025.

Of the 2022 to 2025 period, 2022 was the largest year for sales and 2025 the smallest. The operating loss grew from KRW 4.3 billion in 2022 to KRW 5 billion in 2023, KRW 7.27 billion in 2024 and KRW 7.78 billion in 2025, a loss in each of the four years.

The operating margin moved from -8.9% in 2022 to -11.9% in 2023, -21.8% in 2024 and -26.2% in 2025. An external analysis summary attributed the wider losses to a weaker cost structure as sales slumped, and also noted that a reversal of bad-debt provisions eased the selling and administrative burden.

The net loss attributable to controlling shareholders grew from KRW 5.89 billion in 2022 to KRW 6.02 billion, KRW 7.75 billion and KRW 8.24 billion in 2025, while the net loss including non-controlling interests was KRW 8.73 billion in 2025, a different figure.

Quarterly sales went from KRW 7.44 billion in the third quarter of 2025 to KRW 6.61 billion in the fourth quarter (down 11.1% from the prior quarter) and KRW 5.95 billion in the first quarter of 2026 (down 10.1%), before recovering to KRW 7.6 billion in the second quarter (up 27.8%).

Per a KRW-based industry compilation, first-quarter 2026 sales fell 26.8% year on year. Third-quarter 2026 sales were KRW 7.36 billion, down 3.2% from the prior quarter and down 1.1% year on year.

The operating loss was KRW 1.48 billion, KRW 2.81 billion, KRW 2.15 billion, KRW 1.46 billion and KRW 2.01 billion across the five quarters from the third quarter of 2025 through the third quarter of 2026, a loss in each quarter.

Over the last four quarters, the net loss attributable to controlling shareholders totaled KRW 8.96 billion. Operating cash flow deficits widened from -KRW 1.11 billion in 2022 to -KRW 7.77 billion in 2025.

05

Industry analysis

Demand for ferrite cores depends heavily on the production of downstream industries such as automotive and home appliances, and the company's sales move with that production flow.

An external analysis summary said falling output in some downstream sectors, including automotive and appliances, had weakened ferrite-core orders. The same summary expected that lower domestic automotive production, a slower pace of electrification and declining iron-oxide orders would limit any recovery in sales.

On the other side, expanding equipment investment centered on high-performance semiconductors and improving appliance demand were cited as potential drivers. Industry monitor WiseReport attributed the revenue decline in ferrite cores to slowing growth in the global EV market.

Automotive and EV demand, and automotive production data, are therefore the most direct signals for the company's business. On competition, electromagnetic-wave absorber cores are listed with a 70% global share, a strong niche position.

Ferrite cores, by contrast, are listed at 2%, so the mainstream product faces price and volume competition with large component makers.

Compared with large listed component companies in the same sector, its sales and market value are much smaller, which implies less negotiating leverage with customers and less capacity for capital spending.

The new EV supply rests on a joint development project with the world's largest EV maker, so the schedule of a single customer can weigh heavily on results.

Outlook

According to company statements, a new program co-developed with the world's largest EV maker is scheduled for mass production starting in 2026. The start date and volumes of that production are not specified in the disclosures, so the timing of any revenue contribution is not yet pinned down.

The company is also designing specialized products for power infrastructure used in AI data centers. Regarding a reported KRW 30 billion order for fast-charger and wireless-charging components, the company disclosed that no decision has been made.

It said it would re-disclose either when specific business details are decided or within six months, and the same disclosure record shows a re-disclosure dated October 8, 2026. A disclosure dated March 27, 2026 also reported a KRW 3 billion increase in short-term borrowings.

Whether the new production and orders convert into actual revenue, and how the borrowing links to working-capital needs, are the points to watch. On sales, it remains to be seen whether the 27.8% quarter-on-quarter increase in the second quarter of 2026 is an early sign of the new program's effect.

The 2026 annual business report, the company's next periodic filing, will show the direction of full-year sales and operating margin, which will serve as the reference point for the outlook.

Bull factors

Planned mass production of a new EV project

According to WiseReport's earnings commentary, a new program co-developed with the world's largest EV maker is scheduled for mass production from 2026. The company also describes expanding supply of key parts for the E-GMP EV platform.

If mass production ramps up, it could become a revenue base that partly offsets weaker ferrite-core demand. The timing and volume of that production have not yet been specified in the disclosures.

Quarterly sales recovery

Second-quarter 2026 sales rose 27.8% from the prior quarter to KRW 7.6 billion, and the operating loss narrowed to KRW 1.46 billion over the same period. Sales rebounded from KRW 5.95 billion in the first quarter of 2026. Whether this rebound holds beyond the third quarter of 2026 is the point to watch.

Third-quarter sales of KRW 7.36 billion, slightly below the prior quarter, leave open the question of how durable the recovery is.

Product design for AI data center power infrastructure

The company says it is designing specialized products aimed at the power infrastructure market for AI data centers. That area can connect with demand for magnetic materials such as ferrite cores.

It represents a possible new-business option that could widen a downstream base currently tilted toward automotive and appliances. Because the work is at the design stage, no revenue scale has been disclosed.

Bear factors

Three straight years of falling sales and four straight years of losses

Sales fell from KRW 48.3 billion in 2022 to KRW 29.6 billion in 2025, and the operating margin deteriorated from -8.9% to -26.2%. The operating loss grew from KRW 4.3 billion to KRW 7.78 billion. The loss expansion stems from a cost structure that did not keep pace as revenue declined. With losses persisting over several years, whether the business can sustain them is the central question.

Slowing demand from automotive and appliance customers

An external analysis summary expected that lower domestic automotive output, slower electrification and falling iron-oxide orders would limit any recovery in sales. WiseReport also noted that slowing EV market growth reduced ferrite-core sales. First-quarter 2026 sales were reported as down 26.8% year on year. If demand recovery is delayed, the new production effect may struggle to offset the existing decline.

Financial burden and cash outflows

Total liabilities at the end of 2025 were KRW 50.8 billion, above total equity of KRW 24.6 billion, and the debt ratio was 206.9%. Operating cash flow deficits widened from -KRW 1.11 billion in 2022 to -KRW 7.77 billion in 2025. A March 2026 disclosure also reported a KRW 3 billion increase in short-term borrowings. With less equity cushion, additional funding becomes a variable for business operations.

Risk factors

Financial and liquidity risk

Total liabilities of KRW 50.8 billion compare with total equity of KRW 24.6 billion, and operating cash outflows have widened for four consecutive years. With a short-term borrowing increase disclosed, whether working-capital funding holds is important.

If additional equity raises or borrowing become necessary, dilution or higher financing costs could follow. Signs of improvement would appear in the direction of the debt ratio and cash flow.

Customer and product concentration risk

One industry compilation shows ferrite cores and related products accounting for 100% of major business-segment sales, indicating reliance on a single component family. The new EV project also depends on joint development with a single major EV maker.

That customer's production schedule or design changes could directly affect results. If product diversification is slow, the company has little cushion against a downturn in its end markets.

Share price volatility and exchange market alerts

The exchange disclosed that the stock was designated an investment caution stock for one day from June 22, 2026, after its June 19 close rose more than 60% over five trading days. The market alert system has three stages: caution, warning and risk, and trading can be halted at warning or risk levels.

Sharp moves driven by trading flows are possible when the earnings base is weak. Any change in alert stage appears in exchange disclosures.
