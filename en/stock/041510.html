Wider Revenue Base, Narrowing Profit at SM Entertainment

SM Entertainment has widened its revenue base through concerts, merchandise and licensing, but profit attributable to controlling shareholders fell sharply in Q3 2026, leaving the durability of the profit recovery as the next item to watch.

02

Business structure

SM Entertainment has moved away from a model that relied mainly on album and digital music sales and now builds revenue from concerts, merchandise and licensing, and appearance income from broadcasts, advertising and events.

In Q2 2026, parent-company concert revenue was KRW 41.6 billion, up 23.6% year on year, merchandise and licensing revenue was KRW 77.9 billion, up 22.0%, and appearance revenue was KRW 29.7 billion, up 27.9% (preliminary, announced August 5, 2026).

Over the same quarter, parent-company album and digital music revenue fell 8.2% to KRW 90.9 billion as new album sales declined to 5.67 million copies from 6.03 million a year earlier. Concert growth has been driven by the touring activity of senior artists.

Subsidiaries are also feeding into results: the concert-production subsidiary expanded profit on more domestic shows and closer cooperation with the parent, while Dear U improved profitability through a subscription price increase and the shift to web payments.

Combined revenue of the major subsidiaries rose 25.5% to KRW 177.7 billion in Q2 2026, with operating profit up 61.2% to KRW 13 billion (unconsolidated sum, preliminary). The Japanese subsidiary returned to profitability on higher artist activity in Japan.

In China, the company's second-largest shareholder TME provides a platform and distribution infrastructure for its joint venture. In the sector, analysts point to the success of new boy groups as a key point of differentiation.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual revenue rose from KRW 850.8 billion in 2022 to KRW 961.1 billion in 2023 (up 13.0% year on year), KRW 989.7 billion in 2024 (up 3.0%) and KRW 1.17 trillion in 2025 (up 18.7%), growing for three consecutive years from 2023 through 2025.

Operating profit fell from KRW 113.5 billion in 2023 (up 24.7%) to KRW 87.3 billion in 2024 (down 23.1%), then recovered to KRW 183 billion in 2025 (up 109.7%), with the operating margin climbing from 8.8% to 15.6%.

Profit attributable to controlling shareholders was smallest in 2024 at KRW 18.3 billion (down 79.0%) and reached KRW 347.2 billion in 2025.

The 2025 figure, however, includes a one-off non-operating gain in Q1 2025 from an additional acquisition of Dear U shares, so the size of the 2025 recovery should not be read purely as core business improvement.

Net income including non-controlling interests was KRW 359.4 billion in 2025, a different figure from profit attributable to controlling shareholders; the gap reflects non-controlling interests. The debt-to-equity ratio declined from 71.2% in 2024 to 47.8% in 2025.

At year-end 2025, total equity was KRW 1.36 trillion, of which controlling shareholders' equity was KRW 1 trillion, and total liabilities were KRW 649 billion. Operating cash flow rose from KRW 114.9 billion in 2022 to KRW 194.9 billion in 2025.

On a quarterly basis, revenue peaked at KRW 349.6 billion in Q2 2026 (up 25.3% quarter on quarter) before falling to KRW 288 billion in Q3 2026 (down 17.6% quarter on quarter and down 10.5% year on year).

Operating profit fell from KRW 52.9 billion in Q2 2026 to KRW 33.6 billion in Q3 2026 (down 36.4% quarter on quarter and down 30.3% year on year).

Profit attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 6.35 billion in Q3 2026, down 84.0% year on year, and has declined for two consecutive quarters after peaking in Q1 2026. Over the last four quarters, that profit totaled KRW 98.2 billion.

In the preliminary parent-company Q3 2026 results announced on November 4, 2026, concert revenue fell 34.2% to KRW 34.5 billion, album and digital music revenue fell 51.3% to KRW 47.1 billion, and merchandise and licensing revenue rose 23.5% to KRW 62.1 billion.

05

Industry analysis

The K-pop entertainment sector is shifting its earnings base from album sales toward concerts, merchandise and licensing. SM Entertainment's parent-company revenue growth in Q2 2026 came from concerts and merchandise even as album and digital music revenue declined, which reflects the same shift.

Quarterly results remain highly sensitive to group activity schedules. iM Securities said the timing of global album releases and tours by major groups in the second half of 2026 created a visible short-term gap in earnings.

The same report noted that sector-wide valuation declines are likely to keep the share price range-bound for the time being. Drivers for 2027 include NCT WISH's 22 arena shows in the first half, RIIZE's world tour after its first-quarter full-length album, and Aespa's Asia tour.

The debut of new boy groups is also cited as a point of differentiation. In China, analysts have pointed to synergy with Tencent or an easing of restrictions on Korean content as potential catalysts.

Compared with peers, the company's results now depend more on touring and the reception of new groups than on album sales alone, which makes quarterly results more volatile.

06

Outlook

The company's largest expected driver is broader global activity in 2027. Analysts expect profit momentum to strengthen as global activity expands. The new boy group project SMTR25 was described in a September 2026 report as preparing for an official debut within the year.

In China, SM set up a joint venture, STE, with TME to manage idols aiming for local debuts within two to three years. Album releases and world tours for SM artists were scheduled for the second half of 2026, according to the company's August 5, 2026 results announcement.

Brokerage views are mixed and have trended toward cuts.

Samsung Securities said on September 9, 2026 that it lowered its target price from KRW 114,000 to KRW 105,000 and kept its Buy rating. iM Securities said on November 5, 2026 that it lowered its target price to KRW 110,000, an 8% cut reflecting the second-half earnings gap, and kept its Buy rating.

On profitability, the next regular report will show whether the operating margin recovers. Progress in China will depend on the joint venture's execution and on any easing of restrictions on Korean content.

08

Bull factors

Broader Earnings Base from Concerts and Merchandise

Parent-company concert revenue in Q2 2026 rose 23.6% year on year to KRW 41.6 billion, and merchandise and licensing revenue rose 22.0% to KRW 77.9 billion (preliminary).

Consolidated revenue and operating profit both grew in a quarter when album sales declined, which is cited as evidence of a more diversified earnings base. The structure appears less dependent on swings in album sales.

Expected Global Activity Expansion in 2027

In a November 2026 report, iM Securities pointed to NCT WISH's 22 arena shows in the first half of 2027, RIIZE's world tour and Aespa's Asia tour as earnings drivers. The same report cited expectations for new boy groups. If these activities convert into revenue, they could support a recovery after the Q3 2026 earnings gap.

Subsidiary Profitability and China Expansion

Dear U improved profitability through a subscription price increase and the shift to web payments, and the Japanese subsidiary returned to profit in Q1 2026. In China, the joint venture with TME aims for local debuts within two to three years. As profit contributions from subsidiaries grow, overall earnings volatility could decline.

Bear factors

Sharp Q3 Profit Decline and Lower Album Sales

Operating profit fell 30.3% year on year to KRW 33.6 billion in Q3 2026, and profit attributable to controlling shareholders fell 84.0% to KRW 6.35 billion. Parent-company album and digital music revenue fell 51.3% to KRW 47.1 billion (preliminary).

New album shipments dropped from 5.5 million copies in Q3 2025 to 1.67 million in Q3 2026 according to company data.

Second-Half Activity Gap and Sector Valuation Declines

iM Securities said that fewer global album releases and tours by major groups in the second half created a visible short-term earnings gap. The same report said that, given sector-wide valuation declines, the share price may trade sideways for now. A prolonged activity gap could delay a recovery in quarterly profit.

One-Off Items in 2025 Earnings

Q1 2025 net income included a one-off non-operating gain from acquiring additional Dear U shares. As a result, the 2025 growth rate does not fully show the pace of core business recovery. The year-on-year drop in Q1 2026 net income also reflects this base effect.

Risk factors

Concentration in Artist Activity Schedules

Revenue depends heavily on album releases and tour schedules of signed groups. Parent-company album and digital music revenue declined in Q2 2026, and concert revenue also fell in Q3 2026 (preliminary). Gaps in activity increase quarterly volatility, and weaker-than-expected debuts could delay medium-term growth.

Foreign Exchange Losses and Currency Moves

In its preliminary Q3 2026 results announced on November 4, 2026, the company said foreign exchange losses from a stronger won weighed on its net loss. As overseas revenue and foreign-currency balances grow, currency moves can affect net profit separately from operating profit.

Regulatory Risk and Execution in China

Expansion in China is subject to local regulation and market access. TME's platform and distribution infrastructure have been cited as a channel, but concrete results have not yet appeared in reported earnings. Whether the joint venture converts into local debuts and revenue is a medium-term variable.

12

Overall view

SM Entertainment's revenue rose for three consecutive years from 2023 through 2025, reaching KRW 1.17 trillion, and operating profit and profit attributable to controlling shareholders recovered sharply in 2025 from the 2024 low.

The 2025 earnings, however, include a one-off non-operating gain, so the pace of core business recovery should be read more narrowly. In 2026, revenue and operating profit fell in Q3 from the Q2 peak, and profit attributable to controlling shareholders has declined for two consecutive quarters.

The debt-to-equity ratio fell to 47.8% in 2025, reducing financial pressure. The earnings base is broadening from album sales toward concerts, merchandise and licensing, and subsidiary improvements and the China joint venture are additional variables.

Brokers have lowered target prices, citing the second-half earnings gap and lower sector valuations. The next checkpoints are the Q4 2026 preliminary results, the 2026 annual report, and the performance of new groups and tours in 2027.
