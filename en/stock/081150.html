Quarterly Sales Near Record Highs, Margins Still Thin

Tplex's quarterly sales have climbed to near-record levels in 2026, but its operating margin was 2.5% in 2025, so how much of the top-line growth reaches profit is the key point to track.

  3. 3

02

Business structure

Tplex processes and supplies stainless steel bars, wires and plates, and describes itself as a comprehensive stainless materials company. Bars and wires are the core products, while plates are expanding into real-demand trade through a POSCO-designated coil center.

The company says it is one of eight stainless steel (STS) designated and partner processing centers selected by POSCO and that it is the largest partner of the Changwon-based special steel maker.

Based on company figures for the first half of 2025, its share of domestic stainless bar was 31.4% and its share of wire was about 24%, the latter the highest excluding SeAH group affiliates. It serves more than 1,800 customers and was admitted as a mid-sized company in April 2025.

According to investment media, product sales were KRW 51.3 billion (80.1% of total) and merchandise sales were KRW 12.7 billion (19.8%) in Q1 2026, with domestic sales of KRW 60.4 billion (94.4%) and exports of KRW 3.6 billion. Output in the same quarter was 10,615 tons at an average utilization rate of 74.5%.

The company says it added new customers including Korea Zinc to broaden its business base. In competition, the two main tasks are holding the leading domestic share in bars and wires and expanding real-demand plate trade.

The company plans to expand its business into a comprehensive materials company through capital investment built on stainless materials and by adding new product items.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual results showed diverging trends in revenue and profit.

Revenue was highest in 2022 at KRW 257.7 billion among 2022–2025, then fell to KRW 251.1 billion in 2023 (down 2.6% year over year) and KRW 207.5 billion in 2024 (down 17.4% year over year), before recovering slightly to KRW 209.2 billion in 2025 (up 0.8% year over year).

Operating profit swung from KRW 21.3 billion in 2022 to an operating loss of KRW 1.53 billion in 2023, returned to profit at KRW 4.79 billion in 2024, and rose 9.2% to KRW 5.24 billion in 2025. The operating margin was 8.3% in 2022, -0.6% in 2023, 2.3% in 2024, and 2.5% in 2025.

The 2022 margin peak coincided with a period of declining revenue, and since 2024 the margin has risen gradually from a low level.

Net profit attributable to owners of the parent was KRW 15.6 billion in 2022, a net loss of KRW 2.23 billion in 2023 (swing to a loss), KRW 1.36 billion in 2024 (return to profit), and KRW 3.2 billion in 2025 (up 134.8% year over year).

Net profit including non-controlling interests is disclosed at the same amounts for the same years. The company explained that the reversal of valuation losses and a decrease in non-operating expenses drove the 2025 increase in net profit.

On a quarterly basis, revenue was KRW 52.5 billion in Q3 2025 and KRW 52.4 billion in Q4 (down 0.2% quarter over quarter), then KRW 63.9 billion in Q1 2026 (up 22.0% quarter over quarter), KRW 71.5 billion in Q2 (up 11.8% quarter over quarter), and KRW 69.1 billion in Q3 (down 3.3% quarter over quarter, up 31.6% year over year).

Operating profit was KRW 1.09 billion in Q3 2025, KRW 1.74 billion in Q4, and KRW 2.36 billion in Q1 2026, then declined to KRW 2.16 billion in Q2 (down 8.7% quarter over quarter) and KRW 1.26 billion in Q3 (down 41.7% quarter over quarter).

Net profit attributable to owners of the parent was KRW 1.12 billion in Q3, up 146.4% quarter over quarter; because operating profit and net profit moved in different directions, the detailed causes, including non-operating items, should be confirmed in the periodic report. The sum of net profit attributable to owners of the parent over the latest four quarters is KRW 3.93 billion.

05

Industry analysis

Tplex's downstream demand comes from semiconductors, shipbuilding, defense and nuclear power valves.

The company expects continued capital spending by Samsung Electronics and SK hynix centered on high-bandwidth memory (HBM) for AI chips, along with higher materials demand from shipbuilding and nuclear valve applications.

On the raw material side, the company says rising prices of nickel, tungsten and rare earths boosted demand for inventory stockpiling. That stockpiling demand appears to have been reflected in results during the price upswing.

Industry-wide, however, steel conditions have been described as in a downturn: a February 2026 report noted that 2025 results improved despite a broad steel slump. Tplex's earnings therefore appear to lean more on specific downstream demand than on the general steel cycle.

In competition, the most important variable is whether it keeps its domestic number one share in bars and wires. In plates, it is expanding real-demand trade through the POSCO-linked coil center, and the new customer wins, including Korea Zinc, are cited as evidence of downstream diversification.

How long this demand lasts and how inventory demand changes if raw material prices reverse are points to verify in future quarterly results.

06

Outlook

The company set a 2026 annual sales target of KRW 230 billion, which is more than 10% above the prior year. In its Q1 2026 results announcement in May, it expected solid results to continue in the second half on the back of a stronger semiconductor, shipbuilding and nuclear valve sector.

In its preliminary Q3 2026 disclosure on November 10, the company reportedly expected record annual sales and mentioned KRW 270 to 280 billion, above the original target.

Cumulative sales for the first nine months were KRW 204.5 billion, so reaching the KRW 230 billion target would require a fairly large fourth quarter. Q3 operating profit of KRW 1.26 billion fell from the prior quarter, so how second-half performance compared with the May outlook also needs checking.

For 2027 the company plans a second plant, expanded capital spending and new product items. Its outlook rests on expanded real-demand plate trade through the POSCO-linked coil center and on keeping the number one share in bars and wires.

Whether the capital investment turns into production capacity and profitability can be checked through construction and completion disclosures and later quarterly utilization rates.

Bull factors

Expanding Quarterly Sales Base

Quarterly sales reached KRW 63.9 billion in Q1 2026 and KRW 71.5 billion in Q2, high levels for the company. The company attributes the top-line growth to stronger downstream demand and new customer wins.

Downstream Demand and Inventory Buying

The company says AI-chip capital spending centered on HBM, along with demand from shipbuilding, nuclear valves and defense, has increased materials supply. Higher nickel, tungsten and rare earth prices have lifted inventory buying. The company says these two factors together drove the sales growth in the first half of 2026.

Domestic Share and Customer Base

The company says its domestic stainless bar share was 31.4% in the first half of 2025, ranking first, and its wire share was about 24%, the highest excluding SeAH group affiliates. It is one of eight STS processing centers selected by POSCO and serves more than 1,800 customers.

Bear factors

Margins Remain Thin

The 2025 operating margin was 2.5%, well below 8.3% in 2022. Cumulative operating profit for January to September 2026 rose 64.9%, but on a quarterly basis profit fell for two straight quarters after KRW 2.36 billion in Q1 2026, to KRW 2.16 billion in Q2 and KRW 1.26 billion in Q3. A low margin relative to sales volume remains the structure.

Working Capital and Debt Burden

According to investment media, inventories rose from KRW 58.5 billion to KRW 65.0 billion and receivables from KRW 45.8 billion to KRW 54.9 billion in Q1 2026. Total liabilities increased from KRW 77.7 billion at the end of 2025 to KRW 95.9 billion. Operating cash flow was -KRW 6.41 billion in 2025.

Weak Steel Cycle and Demand Concentration

The broader steel industry has been described as in a slump. The earnings improvement leans on demand from specific downstream sectors such as semiconductors, shipbuilding and defense, and domestic sales made up 94.4% of Q1 2026 revenue.

Risk factors

Raw Material Prices and Inventory Valuation

Higher nickel, tungsten and rare earth prices boosted inventory demand, so a price reversal could change both inventory buying and inventory valuation. The company cited valuation gain reversals as a factor in the 2025 profit increase, which shows that profit can be affected by valuation swings. Inventories rising to KRW 65.0 billion in Q1 2026 is linked to this exposure.

Customer and Downstream Concentration

Bars and wires depend on Tplex's position as a POSCO-designated processing center and its partnership with the Changwon-based special steel maker. If semiconductor capital spending or shipbuilding and nuclear valve demand slows, volumes could fall at the same time. New customers are growing, but reliance on existing partners remains high.

Financial Burden and Funding for Capex

The debt ratio rose to 49.4% in 2025 from 45.2% in 2024. Operating cash flow was -KRW 6.41 billion in 2025, and total liabilities increased to KRW 95.9 billion in Q1 2026. With a second plant and capex planned, whether profit and cash flow can fund the investment is the key question.

Overall view

Tplex's annual sales have stayed in the KRW 200 billion range since 2023, and quarterly sales reaching near-record levels in 2026 mark a clear change in scale.

The company attributes its results to a combination of semiconductor capital spending, shipbuilding and nuclear valve and defense demand, and inventory buying as raw material prices rose. On the other side, the operating margin was 2.5% in 2025, and operating profit fell for two consecutive quarters through Q3 2026.

Inventories and receivables rose, adding working capital pressure, and operating cash flow was negative in 2025. The company set a KRW 230 billion sales target for 2026 and then mentioned KRW 270 to 280 billion in its November disclosure, widening the range of its sales outlook.

With both strengths and weaknesses present, the operating margin and cash flow in the next annual report will show the quality of the top-line growth. This report offers no investment opinion or view on the share price direction and is based on disclosed results and public reporting.
