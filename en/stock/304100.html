02

Business structure

Saltlux combines ontology-based knowledge management with its in-house large language model LUXIA, the A.RAG retrieval solution and the Agent Studio tool for building AI agents, and it is expanding enterprise AI business on that base.

Its core system integration (SI) and AI solution projects recognize revenue according to project period and progress, so there is a timing gap between contract signing and revenue. Consolidated revenue for January to September 2026 was KRW 37.6 billion, up 71.9% year on year.

Service revenue for the same period was about KRW 30 billion, or about 79.7%, and license revenue was about KRW 5.3 billion.

Public-sector wins in the second half of 2026 included an AI configuration management project for Korea Hydro & Nuclear Power, a cloud-native transition for the National Information Society Agency, and a smart aquaculture big data project for Busan Metropolitan City.

In June 2026 the company announced a KRW 5.1 billion enterprise AI project for KEPCO Nuclear Fuel. Its own products include LUXIA 4.0, the Ontology Foundry data context platform, the hardware-integrated LUXIA On 2.0, and the subscription-based LUXIA Cloud under development.

Overseas, its Vietnam subsidiary Saltlux Technology (SLT) supplies training data for robots, and first-half 2026 revenue rose 115% to USD 876,000. That SLT result is not included in the standalone financial statements but is reflected in consolidated statements.

In the competitive landscape, Palantir also uses ontology as a core technology, and the group reports 39 ontology-related patents. Its customers are concentrated in nuclear power, finance and the public sector, and it is also pursuing an AI-based intelligent records search project for the National Archives of Korea.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual revenue went from KRW 30.3 billion in 2022 to KRW 30.8 billion in 2023 (up 1.7%), KRW 45.9 billion in 2024 (up 49.0%) and KRW 41.6 billion in 2025 (down 9.4%). Of the years 2022 to 2025, 2024 was the largest and 2022 the smallest.

Operating losses were KRW 1.96 billion in 2022, KRW 9.29 billion in 2023, KRW 6.63 billion in 2024 and KRW 8.04 billion in 2025, so every year was a loss; the loss narrowed in 2024 and widened again in 2025. Operating margins were -6.5%, -30.1%, -14.4% and -19.3% in the same years.

Net losses attributable to controlling shareholders were KRW 9.36 billion, KRW 10.7 billion, KRW 5.86 billion and KRW 10.8 billion, while the net loss including non-controlling interests was KRW 12 billion in 2025.

At the end of 2025, total equity was KRW 73.8 billion, of which KRW 70.5 billion was attributable to controlling shareholders, and total liabilities were KRW 32.7 billion; the debt ratio rose from 28.3% in 2024 to 44.4% in 2025.

Operating cash flow was -KRW 2.03 billion in 2022, -KRW 6.2 billion in 2023, -KRW 4.85 billion in 2024 and -KRW 3.28 billion in 2025, a smaller outflow in 2025 than in 2023.

Quarterly revenue was KRW 9.11 billion in Q3 2025, with an operating loss of KRW 2.65 billion; it rose to KRW 19.7 billion in Q4 2025 (up 116.6% quarter on quarter), with operating profit of KRW 3.82 billion. Q1 2026 revenue fell 54.7% to KRW 8.94 billion and the operating loss was KRW 4.86 billion.

Q2 2026 revenue was KRW 11.5 billion (up 28.9%) with an operating loss of KRW 3.52 billion, a narrower loss. Q3 2026 revenue was KRW 17.2 billion (up 49.0% quarter on quarter and 88.4% year on year), with operating profit of KRW 53.73 million. Revenue has risen for two straight quarters, from Q2 to Q3 2026.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 3.17 million... KRW 317 million in Q4 2025 and KRW 256 million in Q3 2026, and the net loss attributable to controlling shareholders over the latest four quarters totals KRW 7.21 billion.

The company attributes the revenue growth to AI projects in nuclear power and the public sector. Standalone Q3 revenue was KRW 10.84 billion, up 167.8%, and the consolidated operating margin was only about 0.3%.

Cumulative January to September consolidated operating loss narrowed to KRW 8.33 billion from KRW 11.86 billion.

05

Industry analysis

Demand from the enterprise side is shifting as generative AI and AI agents move beyond proof-of-concept into full system builds. Saltlux says it has been expanding wins in nuclear power, finance and the public sector on this trend.

On the government side, the Ministry of Science and ICT's All-Korean AI project is under way, and Saltlux is taking part in a KT consortium to build an AI agent platform and public-sector services.

The company is also reviewing AI security functions tied to the transition to the national network security framework, N2SF. The business mix of custom SI contracts alongside subscription cloud services looks like a transitional stage.

Contract wins do not translate into revenue immediately, which matters when reading the industry cycle. In competition, Palantir positions ontology as its core technology in enterprise AI.

Domestic large IT service firms such as LG CNS, Samsung SDS and Hyundai AutoEver share the SI-centered model, while Saltlux points to its in-house large language model as a differentiator. In physical AI, the company cites growing demand for robot training data as a growth driver.

06

Outlook

The company says it expects revenue growth to continue on the back of converting its second-half 2026 order backlog into sales and on overseas results including its Vietnam subsidiary. Consolidated backlog was about KRW 27.6 billion at the end of September 2026, and standalone backlog was about KRW 17.6 billion.

Because backlog is recognized as projects progress, the pace of converting contracts into revenue and controlling project costs is the key variable for Q4 results. On profitability, the company plans to expand subscription services to build recurring revenue.

LUXIA Cloud, which lets enterprise customers build and run data, AI models and agent tools in the cloud, is also under way. In the public sector, the company is considering linking AI security features to LUXIA On and LUXIA Cloud in line with the N2SF transition, with scope and timing to be set later.

The National Archives of Korea project is at the stage of pushing for testing, certification and an open demonstration environment in November 2026. Overseas, it is developing a robot foundation model and pursuing platform-based software services in North America and Southeast Asia.

The next checkpoints are the annual operating margin in the 2026 annual report and the Q4 revenue level, followed by whether profitability holds in Q1 2027.

08

Bull factors

Quarterly turnaround

The core of the Q3 turnaround is revenue growth together with operating profit turning positive. The continued revenue growth from the previous quarter coincided with the Q3 move into profit. The standalone operating margin was 16.8%, showing improved profitability at the parent.

Wider order base and public projects

Consolidated backlog was about KRW 27.6 billion at the end of September 2026, and first-half cumulative orders reached KRW 18.89 billion. Wins in nuclear power and the public sector continued, including projects with Korea Hydro & Nuclear Power, the National Information Society Agency and Busan Metropolitan City. The company says converting backlog into revenue would improve the visibility of quarterly sales.

Subscription shift and overseas data business

The company plans to add subscription-based LUXIA Cloud and AI agent supply to build recurring revenue. Revenue at Vietnam subsidiary SLT for January to September 2026 was about KRW 2.1 billion, roughly twice its full-year 2025 revenue. The result is reflected in consolidated, not standalone, financial statements.

09

Bear factors

Cumulative losses and four straight years of losses

Operating losses have run for four straight years from 2022 to 2025, with a KRW 8.04 billion operating loss and a KRW 10.8 billion net loss attributable to controlling shareholders in 2025.

The January to September 2026 consolidated operating loss of KRW 8.33 billion is smaller than a year earlier, but the loss pattern has not disappeared. Net losses attributable to controlling shareholders over the latest four quarters total KRW 7.21 billion.

Limited durability of quarterly profit

The Q3 2026 profit is small, with a consolidated operating margin of about 0.3% including subsidiaries. Net income attributable to controlling shareholders has been positive in only two quarters, Q4 2025 and Q3 2026, and Q1 2026 swung back to a KRW 4.76 billion loss. Quarterly results have shifted direction sharply within a few quarters.

Balance sheet and cash flow pressure

The debt ratio rose from 28.3% in 2024 to 44.4% in 2025, and total liabilities were KRW 32.7 billion at the end of 2025. Operating cash flow was still an outflow of KRW 3.28 billion in 2025. Equity provides a cushion, but continued losses could increase financial pressure.

10

Risk factors

Backlog conversion risk

Revenue from SI and AI solution projects is recognized according to project period and progress, so contract wins do not translate into quarterly revenue immediately. Converting contracts on schedule and controlling project costs is the key variable for Q4 results. Slower conversion could increase quarter-to-quarter revenue volatility.

Timing risk on new business

Saltlux's public-sector services under the All-Korean AI project are still at the build-out stage. Its AI security features tied to the N2SF transition have scope and timing still to be finalized. The subscription revenue from LUXIA Cloud has not yet appeared in reported results.

Concentration risk in overseas business

Data collection services for global robotics companies accounted for about 68% of SLT's first-half 2026 revenue. Overseas growth therefore depends heavily on demand from one customer type.

Because these results sit in consolidated rather than standalone statements, they add a source of variation to consolidated earnings.

12

Overall view

Saltlux posted a quarterly profit turn in Q3 2026, with revenue of KRW 17.2 billion and operating profit of KRW 53.73 million, and the company attributes the improvement to revenue recognized from nuclear and public-sector AI projects.

On an annual basis, however, operating losses and net losses attributable to controlling shareholders have run for four straight years from 2022 to 2025, and net losses over the latest four quarters total KRW 7.21 billion.

Consolidated order backlog was about KRW 27.6 billion at the end of September 2026, and the speed at which that backlog becomes revenue and profit is the central variable.

The Vietnam subsidiary's physical AI data business is growing, but its results sit outside the standalone statements and must be separated when reading consolidated figures. On the balance sheet, the debt ratio rose to 44.4% in 2025 and operating cash flow remained negative.

The next checkpoints are the operating margin and Q4 revenue in the 2026 annual report, and whether profitability holds in Q1 2027.
