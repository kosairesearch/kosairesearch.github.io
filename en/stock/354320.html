Almac: EV Aluminum Extruder Tests New Businesses After Renewed Losses

Almac returned to an operating profit in 2025 but posted an operating loss in the third quarter of 2026, so its aerospace parts and US plant projects are the next points to verify.

02

Business structure

Almac is a specialist aluminum extrusion company that moved its business from building materials into automotive parts and is described as having an integrated production chain from materials to processing.

Its core is the EV parts business, where it develops and produces EV-dedicated products such as battery module cases, battery pack frames and EV platform frames. Its customers are battery cell makers and automakers.

In 2024 it was selected as a supplier to BlueOval SK, the joint venture of SK On and Ford, and signed battery parts contracts, while rising battery output at LG Energy Solution and SK On was cited as a driver of the earnings recovery.

In January 2026 it passed the supplier registration review of Stellantis, opening the possibility of supplying bumpers, side sills and battery packs, according to the company. Its 2025 results explanation cited higher EV parts sales as the main factor behind the sales and operating profit improvement.

The new businesses fall into three areas. The first is aerospace, where a first sample supplied to a global private space company passed physical property tests and a second sample is in component-level qualification.

The second is low-carbon aluminum made from recycled scrap, which Almac was selected to develop under a Ministry of Trade, Industry and Energy materials project running through 2028. The third is high-conductivity aluminum material aimed at data centers, energy storage systems and renewable energy.

Overseas, Almac completed the acquisition of a former Nucor-affiliated plant in Eufaula, Alabama, in May 2026, and plans to introduce equipment in stages and begin supplying North American automotive, defense and energy customers from the fourth quarter of 2027.

Unlike Korea Zinc or Poongsan in the same metals sector, its profile centers on EV battery parts customers with new businesses layered on top.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual sales rose 37.8 percent from KRW 156.8 billion in 2022 to KRW 216 billion in 2023, the largest year in the 2022 to 2025 span, fell 27.4 percent to KRW 156.9 billion in 2024, and rose 19.7 percent to KRW 187.8 billion in 2025.

Operating profit peaked at KRW 15.9 billion in 2023, with an operating margin of 7.3 percent, before the company swung to an operating loss of KRW 2.74 billion in 2024, a turn from profit to loss, with a margin of -1.7 percent.

In 2025 operating profit returned to KRW 6.22 billion and the margin recovered to 3.3 percent. The company said higher EV parts sales contributed to the sales and operating profit improvement, and that a one-off loss on the disposal of derivatives at a subsidiary produced a non-operating loss.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders moved between profit and loss: KRW 5.48 billion in 2022, a net loss of KRW 6.77 billion in 2023, a profit of KRW 655 million in 2024, and a net loss of KRW 1.31 billion in 2025.

Net income including non-controlling interests was a net loss of KRW 6.13 billion in 2025, and the gap with the controlling-shareholder figure is wide enough that non-operating items and subsidiary profit structure need to be read together. The quarterly path is sharper.

In the third quarter of 2025 sales were KRW 44.5 billion and operating profit was KRW 2.25 billion; in the fourth quarter sales were KRW 44.2 billion, down 0.8 percent from the prior quarter, and the company posted an operating loss of KRW 569 million.

In the first quarter of 2026 sales were KRW 50.7 billion, up 14.6 percent from the prior quarter, and operating profit was KRW 2.81 billion. In the second quarter sales reached KRW 5.52 billion, the highest of the disclosed quarters, while operating profit was KRW 2.6 billion, down 7.5 percent.

In the third quarter of 2026 sales fell 34.1 percent to KRW 3.64 billion, down 18.3 percent year on year, with an operating loss of KRW 5.26 billion and a net loss attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 7.25 billion.

Over the last four quarters, the net loss attributable to controlling shareholders totals KRW 5.21 billion. At the end of 2025, total equity was KRW 161.2 billion, of which KRW 150.4 billion belonged to controlling shareholders, and total liabilities were KRW 181.6 billion, giving a debt ratio of 112.7 percent.

The debt ratio went from 203.7 percent in 2022 to 103.0 percent in 2023, then 123.3 percent in 2024 and 112.7 percent in 2025. Operating cash flow was -KRW 29.5 billion in 2022, KRW 24.5 billion in 2023, -KRW 4.58 billion in 2024 and KRW 27.2 billion in 2025, changing sign almost every year.

05

Industry analysis

Demand from the downstream market is tied to EV battery production. The 2025 results explanation cited the EV market's chasm, a temporary slowdown in demand, but also a view that rising battery output and closer partnerships led to the earnings improvement.

Battery cell utilization often moves ahead of parts orders, so Almac's sales are sensitive to changes in customer production plans. This structure gives upside when demand recovers and sharp pressure when it slows.

Within the metals sector, Almac sits in aluminum extrusion, a segment whose raw materials and product mix differ from Korea Zinc, Poongsan and Sama Aluminium.

On the regulatory side, the European Union's Carbon Border Adjustment Mechanism (CBAM) requires carbon management for materials including aluminum, and Almac is preparing low-carbon aluminum based on recycled scrap.

On trade, a preliminary US anti-dumping determination on aluminum extrusions in June 2024 set the rate at 0 percent, which was read as a positive signal for US export expansion.

In cycle terms, EV parts demand is passing through a chasm phase, so how quickly demand from outside EVs, such as aerospace and data centers, shows up in sales has become the key question. Brokerage views emphasize new businesses and vertical integration.

Kiwoom Securities said in a February 12, 2026 report that the company's vertical integration across aluminum materials and parts highlights expansion into aerospace and robotics.

Hana Securities said in a March 4, 2026 report that the company had entered a phase of profitability recovery by widening its portfolio into aerospace and high-power infrastructure. Neither report presented an investment rating or a target price.

06

Outlook

The most important near-term event the company cites is the aerospace customer qualification. A first sample passed physical property tests in February 2026, a second sample is in component-level qualification, and new projects were added to existing ones during testing.

In a March 2026 report Hana Securities said it expected full-scale mass production in the second half, depending on final test results in the first half.

The company had consistently said it expected qualification for a major aerospace customer to pass in the first half of 2026, and now that period has passed, the release of results is the next checkpoint.

Expectations that the Sacheon plant's full commercial production would make the 2026 earnings improvement visible were also raised in the market.

Overseas, equipment for the Alabama plant is being introduced in stages, with supply to North American customers planned from the fourth quarter of 2027, targeting US automakers and industrial customers in defense and energy.

The company said in January 2026 that its supplier registration with Stellantis opened the possibility of supplying bumpers, side sills and battery packs.

The low-carbon aluminum project, as reported in 2024, has a project size of KRW 22.5 billion, with KRW 17.7 billion in government support, running about five years through 2028, and Hyundai Motor was expected to be a demand company for it.

Backlog at the end of the third quarter of 2025 was reported at about KRW 1.1 trillion, and how that backlog converts into sales from 2026 onward is the key point to verify.

Whether the third-quarter 2026 sales decline was temporary or linked to the timing of new customer revenue cannot be separated from disclosed figures alone, so the sales mix and the cost and selling, general and administrative expense lines in the next annual report are the data to watch.

Bull factors

Recovery in operating margin

The operating margin recovered from -1.7 percent in 2024 to 3.3 percent in 2025, and operating profit turned positive in 2025. The company attributed the improvement to higher EV parts sales.

Operating profit was KRW 2.81 billion in the first quarter of 2026 and KRW 2.6 billion in the second quarter, so the first half was profitable at the operating line.

Progress in aerospace sample validation

A first sample supplied to a global private space company passed physical property tests, and a second sample is in component-level qualification.

In a March 2026 report Hana Securities assessed that the company had entered a profitability recovery phase by expanding into aerospace and high-power infrastructure, without presenting an investment rating or target price.

Customer expansion and order backlog

Backlog at the end of the third quarter of 2025 was reported at about KRW 1.1 trillion. Supplier registration with Stellantis opened the possibility of supplying bumpers, side sills and battery packs, and the Alabama plant plans to supply North American customers from the fourth quarter of 2027.

09

Bear factors

Return to losses in the third quarter

The operating profit run of the first and second quarters of 2026 was cut short in a single quarter by the third-quarter operating loss. The net loss attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 7.25 billion. Over the last four quarters that net loss totals KRW 5.21 billion, so profitability has not held steady.

Dependence on EV demand

Sales and profit are concentrated in EV battery parts, so the company is directly exposed to the EV slowdown and to adjustments in battery production. The 2025 results explanation also referred to the EV market slowdown.

Until the new businesses grow to a meaningful share of sales, concentration risk in customers and demand remains.

Financial burden and volatile cash flow

Total liabilities at the end of 2025 were KRW 181.6 billion, above total equity of KRW 161.2 billion, giving a debt ratio of 112.7 percent. Operating cash flow swung from -KRW 4.58 billion in 2024 to KRW 27.2 billion in 2025. The Alabama plant acquisition and equipment investment could add to this financial burden.

10

Risk factors

Delay in new-business validation

If qualification results for the second aerospace sample and the start of mass production are delayed, the sales contribution from new businesses will be pushed back.

The company had said it expected qualification to pass in the first half, and after that period concern over the timing of qualification has been cited as a factor behind a sharp share price decline. The US plant also depends on the timing of equipment installation and customer approvals.

Customer and demand concentration

Battery parts sales depend on the output of battery cell makers such as LG Energy Solution and SK On. If the slowdown in EV demand lasts, order adjustments could flow directly into results. High reliance on a specific customer group adds to the volatility of quarterly earnings.

Raw material costs and financial capacity

Aluminum prices and scrap costs determine margins, and how much cost benefit recycled aluminum delivers remains a point to watch. A debt ratio of 112.7 percent could become a burden if capital spending increases. Operating cash flow alternating between positive and negative across years also warrants attention.

Overall view

Almac is an aluminum extrusion company whose core is EV battery parts, and its move back to an operating profit in 2025 signaled a recovering earnings structure. Sales in 2025 were KRW 187.8 billion, operating profit was KRW 6.22 billion and the operating margin was 3.3 percent.

However, the net loss attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 1.31 billion and the net loss of KRW 6.13 billion kept the net income picture in the red.

The company made operating profits in the first and second quarters of 2026, but third-quarter sales fell to KRW 3.64 billion and an operating loss of KRW 5.26 billion made the quarterly path unsteady.

The new businesses are widening through aerospace parts qualification, Stellantis supplier registration and the Alabama plant acquisition, yet it remains hard to measure their contribution to results in numbers.

On the financial side, total liabilities at the end of 2025 were KRW 181.6 billion with a debt ratio of 112.7 percent, which could become a burden if investment rises. Because earnings are in the red, a price-to-earnings ratio cannot be calculated, and the price-to-book ratio is above 1x.

The next checkpoint is the 2026 annual report, and the aerospace qualification results and whether North American supply starts in the fourth quarter of 2027 are the core tests for the new businesses.
