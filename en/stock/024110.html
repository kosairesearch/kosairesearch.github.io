Report overview

A SME lender whose credit costs now drive the earnings debate

02

Business structure

Industrial Bank OF Korea is a policy-oriented bank focused on lending to small and mid-sized enterprises, and the government is its largest shareholder. The SME loan book stood at KRW 272 trillion at the end of September 2026, up 3.9% from the end of 2025, giving the bank a 24.5% share of the SME market.

The bank attributes this growth to its productive finance push and says it keeps a wide lead over commercial banks. Its earnings are driven mainly by net interest income.

Cumulative net interest income for the first nine months of 2026 rose 7% year on year to KRW 6.2 trillion, while non-interest income was KRW 624.5 billion, up 0.6%. Beyond the bank itself, it owns securities, capital and pension insurance subsidiaries, and their results should be read separately from the bank.

Its borrowers are mainly small and mid-sized enterprises, and at the end of the third quarter of 2026 the corporate delinquency ratio was 1.10%, above the household ratio of 0.5%. The overall delinquency ratio rose to 1.04% from 0.94% at the end of the second quarter.

The delinquency ratio for real estate and leasing loans reached 1.99%, more than double the 0.87% at the end of 2025. Against commercial banks, the bank leans on its SME market share, but expanding loans also raises provisioning needs.

04

Earnings analysis

Operating income on a DART-filed basis was KRW 3.65 trillion in 2022, KRW 3.43 trillion in 2023 (down 5.9%), KRW 3.59 trillion in 2024 (up 4.7%) and KRW 3.66 trillion in 2025 (up 1.7%). 2023 was the low point, and operating income rose for two consecutive years from 2024 through 2025, with 2025 the largest year in the 2022–2025 span.

Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 2.67 trillion in 2022 and KRW 2.67 trillion in 2023 (up 0.1%), then fell 0.9% to KRW 2.64 trillion in 2024, the smallest year. It recovered to KRW 2.71 trillion in 2025, up 2.5%, the largest year in the period.

Looking at the quarters, cumulative provisioning-before operating income for the first nine months of 2026 rose 9.1% to KRW 4.38 trillion, but net provision charges rose 38.4% to KRW 1.50 trillion, so operating income fell 1.7% to KRW 2.88 trillion.

That nine-month figure matches the sum of the filed quarterly operating income numbers. Operating income fell from KRW 976.7 billion in the third quarter of 2025 to KRW 727.4 billion in the fourth quarter, down 25.5% quarter on quarter, then recovered to KRW 932.5 billion in the first quarter of 2026, up 28.2%.

The second quarter of 2026 came in at KRW 915.1 billion, down 1.9%, and the third quarter reached KRW 1.03 trillion, up 12.7% quarter on quarter and 5.6% year on year, the highest of the last five filed quarters.

Meritz Securities noted that the third quarter absorbed proactive loan loss provisions and a KRW 70 billion loan incident at a China subsidiary, which it said were cushioned by margin improvement and currency gains.

Controlling-shareholder net profit fell 38.1% to KRW 462.5 billion in the fourth quarter of 2025 and then rebounded 61.9% to KRW 748.7 billion in the first quarter of 2026.

Insurance revenue, meaning only that account, rose from KRW 79.2 billion in 2022 to KRW 117.1 billion in 2025, up 25.3% year on year, and it increased for three consecutive years from 2023 through 2025. This is an account-level figure and not the company's total revenue.

Operating cash flow was negative in every year from 2022 through 2025, with the largest outflow of KRW 19.7 trillion in 2024. In banking, loan disbursements are classified within operating cash flow, so this line moves with loan growth.

The debt-to-equity ratio (total liabilities divided by total equity) fell from 1,373.8% in 2022 to 1,258.5% in 2025.

05

Industry analysis

In a high-rate environment, bank net interest income depends on the spread between asset and liability yields. Net interest margin in the third quarter of 2026 was 1.61%, up 0.02 percentage points from the prior quarter.

Hana Securities noted in an October 2026 report that the bank was one of the few lenders with a rising margin, citing its position outside the pressure from CORIBOR rate increases and loan-to-deposit ratio rules.

Meritz Securities also viewed the margin improvement, which differed from peer banks, as the most positive factor. Small-business conditions remain a burden, however. The bank said it built provisions early to prepare for deterioration in SME operating conditions.

Corporate delinquency running above household delinquency signals that SME credit quality is sensitive to the business cycle. In the SME lending market, the bank says its 24.5% share keeps it well ahead of commercial banks.

That share is tied to its policy role, so it is both the source of loan growth and a source of risk exposure. On the cycle, rising rates are supporting interest income while provisions for SME credit deterioration build at the same time.

Because non-interest income is a small share of the mix, earnings are relatively sensitive to the direction of rates.

06

Outlook

The bank moved ahead with a quarterly dividend plan in 2026, and Meritz Securities noted in a July 2026 report that an interim dividend was paid this year for the first time. Kyobo Securities, in a May 2026 report, described the bank's long-term goal of reaching a price-to-book ratio of 1x.

The same report outlined a strategy to improve CET1 and raise the payout ratio up to 40% through diversified revenue and cost control. Because the government is the largest shareholder, the report read the emphasis as falling on cash dividends and capital ratio improvement rather than buybacks or cancellations.

In an October 2026 report, Meritz Securities estimated net interest income growth of 8% in 2026 and 9% in 2027. That estimate depends on continued margin improvement. The annual growth in the SME loan book, which keeps expanding under policy lending, is the key variable for interest income.

The same Meritz report said the company plans to expand quarterly dividends from next year. The scale of any dividend increase will depend on how close the capital ratio moves to its target level. The 2026 annual report will show the actual dividend and capital ratio trends.

08

Bull factors

SME market share and interest income growth

Cumulative net interest income for the first nine months of 2026 rose 7% year on year to KRW 6.2 trillion, confirming growth in the core lending business. Meritz Securities said in an October 2026 report that it estimates net interest income growth of 8% in 2026 and 9% in 2027.

Recovery in quarterly operating income

Operating income in the third quarter of 2026 was KRW 1.03 trillion, up 5.6% year on year and the highest of the last five filed quarters.

From the fourth-quarter 2025 low of KRW 727.4 billion, it recovered to KRW 932.5 billion in the first quarter of 2026, and the second quarter held at KRW 915.1 billion without a sharp drop. Net interest margin in the third quarter rose 0.02 percentage points to 1.61%, which reads as a sign of margin defense.

Quarterly dividend launch and a shift in shareholder returns

The bank put its quarterly dividend plan into practice in 2026, and Meritz Securities noted in a July 2026 report that an interim dividend was paid this year. Kyobo Securities, in a May 2026 report, relayed a direction to raise the payout ratio up to 40% on the condition of an improved CET1 ratio. Meritz Securities reads the government's need for tax revenue as support for the visibility of dividends.

09

Bear factors

Rising credit costs and delinquency

The credit cost ratio in the third quarter of 2026 was 0.53%, up 0.09 percentage points year on year, and the overall delinquency ratio rose to 1.04% from 0.94% at the end of the second quarter.

The corporate delinquency ratio was 1.10%, above the household ratio of 0.5%, while the real estate and leasing delinquency ratio of 1.99% was more than double the level at the end of 2025. The bank says it built provisions early, but a further deterioration in SME conditions could keep the cost burden high.

Limited payout room and capital constraints

The CET1 ratio was 11.48% in the third quarter of 2026, roughly unchanged from the prior quarter. Meritz Securities judged in a July 2026 report that, at the current capital ratio, a payout ratio above 35% is hard to reach.

Volatility in quarterly profit

Controlling-shareholder net profit fell 38.1% quarter on quarter to KRW 462.5 billion in the fourth quarter of 2025, then rebounded to KRW 748.7 billion in the first quarter of 2026. It fell 8.7% to KRW 683.6 billion in the second quarter of 2026, so quarterly profit has been uneven.

Meritz Securities pointed to cost factors in the third quarter, including a KRW 70 billion loan incident at a China subsidiary.

Risk factors

Credit costs and asset quality

If the economic slowdown spreads into delinquencies across a KRW 272 trillion SME loan book, credit costs could rise further. The real estate and leasing delinquency ratio has risen sharply from the end of 2025, making collateral values a key watch point.

The substandard-and-below loan ratio of 1.31% is lower than a year earlier but up 0.04 percentage points from the second quarter, so the direction is mixed.

Overseas subsidiary lending

Meritz Securities identified a KRW 70 billion loan incident at a China subsidiary as a cost factor in the third quarter of 2026. Whether this proves to be a one-off will show up in later credit cost ratios and delinquency trends.

If further incidents occur, assessment of overseas lending controls could become a key factor in reading the earnings.

Policy mandate and capital constraints

As a policy lender, the bank's SME support role underpins loan growth but also consumes capital. Kyobo Securities relayed that, with the government as the largest shareholder, the focus falls on cash dividends and capital ratio improvement rather than buybacks or cancellations. The payout target therefore depends on the pace of capital ratio improvement.

Overall view

Industrial Bank of Korea (IBK) is a bank that grows its interest income on the strength of a 24.5% share of small and medium-sized enterprise (SME) loans.

On a reported basis, operating profit went from KRW 3.647 trillion in 2022 to KRW 3.6556 trillion in 2025, continuing without significant growth, and 2025 was the largest year of the period.

Third-quarter 2026 operating profit of KRW 1.0313 trillion and net profit attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 767.8 billion show that the quarterly trend is moving toward recovery.

On the other hand, the credit cost ratio rose to 0.53%, and the delinquency rate in real estate and rental businesses is significantly higher than at the end of 2025, so asset quality indicators have become a variable for earnings.

The direction of dividends became clear with the interim dividend paid in 2026, but some brokerages also take the view that a payout ratio of 35% or more will be difficult because of capital ratio constraints.

Brokerage target prices are given as KRW 29,000 from Meritz Securities (October 27, 2026) and KRW 28,500 from Hana Securities (October 12, 2026), and these are each brokerage's own assessments.

The next checkpoints are the preliminary fourth-quarter 2026 results and the 2026 annual report, at which point the credit cost ratio and the year-end dividend will become the key inputs for this report's judgment.
