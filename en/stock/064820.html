Cape Industries: Checking Profit Recovery After the Q3 2026 Loss

Cape Industries recorded a sharp profit recovery in its 2025 annual results, but net losses attributable to controlling shareholders followed in the second and third quarters of 2026, so whether margins recover is the next item to verify.

  2. 2

  3. 3

  4. 4

02

Business structure

Cape Industries makes cylinder liners used in low-speed marine engines. The export and domestic breakdown in the company's disclosed data shows a single product line, with 2025 product sales split 84.30% domestic and 15.70% export.

One securities firm described the company as the only domestic maker with an integrated production system for low-speed marine engine cylinder liners. Its main suppliers include HD-Hyundai Heavy Industries, Hanwha Engine and HD-Hyundai Marine Engine, the three domestic engine makers.

Demand comes from newbuild vessels and from replacement (after-sales) demand. Cost of sales as a share of revenue fell from 86.90% in 2022 to 77.51% in 2025, which means a larger share of each sales dollar remained as profit.

Revenue timing appears linked to the build schedules of engine customers, and the 15.70% export share points to heavy reliance on domestic customers.

04

Earnings analysis

On a disclosed annual basis, sales went from KRW 609.7 billion in 2022 to KRW 590.7 billion in 2023 (down 3.1% YoY) and KRW 516.5 billion in 2024 (down 12.6% YoY), then rose to KRW 579.8 billion in 2025 (up 12.2% YoY). Among 2022 to 2025, 2022 was the largest sales year and 2024 the smallest.

Operating profit rose from KRW 2.36 billion in 2022 to KRW 41 billion in 2023 (up 1,636.6% YoY), fell to KRW 39 billion in 2024 (down 4.8% YoY), and reached KRW 50 billion in 2025 (up 28.1% YoY). The operating margin moved from 0.4% in 2022 to 6.9%, 7.5% and 8.6% in 2025.

Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders went from KRW 228 million in 2022 to KRW 24.3 billion in 2023, KRW 15.7 billion in 2024 and KRW 59.4 billion in 2025. Net income including non-controlling interests in 2025 was KRW 64.2 billion, a different figure from the controlling-shareholder number.

Operating cash flow was KRW 43.3 billion in 2022, negative in 2023 and 2024 at -KRW 259.7 billion and -KRW 184.1 billion, and KRW 188.4 billion in 2025.

Quarterly operating profit ran from KRW 15.6 billion in Q3 2025 to an operating loss of KRW 4.71 billion in Q4 2025, then KRW 28.2 billion in Q1 2026, KRW 2.79 billion in Q2 2026 (down 90.1% QoQ) and an operating loss of KRW 3.01 billion in Q3 2026.

Sales peaked at KRW 187.7 billion in Q1 2026 and then declined for two quarters to KRW 119.9 billion in Q3 2026. Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders was a net loss of KRW 861 million in Q2 2026 and KRW 1.93 billion in Q3 2026.

The four quarters from Q4 2025 to Q3 2026 total KRW 50.6 billion for controlling-shareholder net profit. The drivers of the Q3 2026 loss are an item to check in the next periodic report.

05

Industry analysis

Global newbuilding orders are trending lower. The Export-Import Bank of Korea's overseas research institute projected that global newbuilding volume would fall another 14.6% in 2026, from 41 million CGT in 2025 to about 35 million CGT.

Korea nonetheless held its position in the global order book, with a 21% share of orders in 2025 (11.6 million CGT, 247 vessels) according to Clarksons Research, ranking second behind China. Major Korean shipbuilders had booked KRW 31.3 trillion (USD 20.68 billion) of orders as of May 24, 2026, per press reports.

Order backlogs stretch beyond three years, with a substantial share of 2028 delivery slots reported as filled. Mid-sized shipbuilders focus on vessel types that large yards do not prioritize.

Cape sits upstream as a component supplier to engine makers, so shipbuilder orders flow into engine orders and then into cylinder liner sales. Large shipbuilder order intake and engine maker production schedules both matter for the company's volume.

06

Outlook

The outlook is framed around disclosed facts and confirmed industry data. First, whether sales and operating profit keep declining after the Q1 2026 peak is the most direct check. Second, Korean shipbuilders holding enough orders supports newbuild demand for cylinder liners.

Third, a medium-term upturn in replacement demand has been cited as a factor that can support volume when newbuild volumes soften. Fourth, the projected 2026 decline in global newbuilding orders limits the upside for newbuild demand.

Fifth, if order intake weakens, pressure on newbuilding prices could weaken negotiating power and profitability over time. Full-year 2026 results will first surface in a preliminary release in early 2027, and the 2026 annual business report must be filed by the statutory deadline at the end of March 2027.

The items to confirm then are the Q4 operating margin, full-year operating cash flow for 2026, and the change in the debt ratio.

08

Bull factors

Only domestic integrated production system

Cape is described by one securities firm as the only domestic maker with an integrated production system for low-speed marine engine cylinder liners. Its role as a main supplier to three engine makers provides a demand base while newbuild volume continues.

The operating margin rose from 0.4% in 2022 to 8.6% in 2025, showing improved earnings capacity in this business.

Profit recovery in 2025

Operating profit in 2025 reached KRW 50 billion, up 28.1% YoY, and net profit attributable to controlling shareholders reached KRW 59.4 billion, up 278.4% YoY. Operating cash flow turned to a KRW 188.4 billion inflow from the prior year's outflow.

On an annual basis, 2025 was the largest year in the 2022 to 2025 span for both operating profit and controlling-shareholder net profit.

Lower cost ratio and replacement demand

Cost of sales as a share of revenue fell from 86.90% in 2022 to 77.51% in 2025. One securities firm listed a medium-term upturn in replacement demand as a key checkpoint. Replacement demand is cited as a potential cushion if newbuild volumes soften.

Bear factors

Loss turn in Q3 2026

Q3 2026 operating loss was KRW 3.01 billion and net loss attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 1.93 billion. Following a KRW 861 million net loss in Q2 2026, this marks two consecutive loss quarters, a sharp reversal from the KRW 33.3 billion controlling-shareholder profit in Q4 2025. Operating profit in Q2 2026 fell 90.1% from the prior quarter to KRW 2.79 billion.

Two straight quarters of sales decline

Sales peaked at KRW 187.7 billion in Q1 2026, then fell to KRW 161.1 billion in Q2 2026 (down 14.2% QoQ) and KRW 119.9 billion in Q3 2026 (down 25.6% QoQ). Q3 sales were also down 13.0% YoY. Continued sales declines could weigh on margins if fixed costs remain.

Global order decline and balance sheet burden

Global newbuilding volume is projected to fall another 14.6% in 2026. Total liabilities of KRW 2.38 trillion are well above total equity of KRW 309.5 billion, with a debt ratio of 770.1%. Operating cash flow was negative at -KRW 259.7 billion in 2023 and -KRW 184.1 billion in 2024.

Risk factors

Order cycle and newbuild prices

If global orders fall, lower newbuild prices could weaken shipbuilders' negotiating power and profitability. Because Cape depends on engine orders that follow shipbuilder orders, a price decline combined with lower volume would weigh on results.

Quarterly profit volatility

Operating profit swung from KRW 28.2 billion in Q1 2026 to KRW 2.79 billion in Q2 and an operating loss of KRW 3.01 billion in Q3. Q4 2025 also recorded an operating loss of KRW 4.71 billion, so quarterly swings are large. Annual totals can mask these swings, so quarterly trends need separate review.

Capital structure

The debt ratio was 884.5% in 2022, 782.3% in 2023, 826.6% in 2024 and 770.1% in 2025, staying at high levels. Total liabilities of KRW 2.38 trillion are large enough to be exposed to interest rate and refinancing conditions. If losses continue in 2026, profits may offset this burden more slowly.

Overall view

Cape Industries supplies cylinder liners for marine engines in Korea and posted an operating margin of 8.6% in 2025, showing a profit recovery.

Quarterly results were uneven within 2025, and after the Q1 2026 sales peak, both sales and profit declined in Q2 and Q3, with Q3 showing an operating loss and a net loss attributable to controlling shareholders.

Newbuild demand is supported by Korean shipbuilders' order backlogs of more than three years, but global newbuilding volume is projected to fall in 2026, so the direction is mixed. A medium-term upturn in replacement demand has been cited as a cushion.

On the financial side, a debt ratio of 770.1% and total liabilities of KRW 2.38 trillion remain burdens. The next checkpoints are the Q4 and full-year 2026 preliminary results and the 2026 annual business report, including the drivers of the Q3 loss and operating cash flow.
