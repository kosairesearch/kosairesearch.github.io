Steady Sales, Volatile Profits at a Discount-Store Operator

02

Business structure

Savezone I&C was created through a spin-off of the distribution division of a former parent company in 2002, and it was re-listed on the KOSPI the same year. Its industry classification is general retail, and its shares have been listed on the KOSPI since July 25, 2002.

The company operates six department-store-style discount stores in Korea. Its stores sell discounted carry-over and planned products from clothing brands at sale prices, and they add food, household goods and cultural-center programs so that shoppers can handle several needs in one visit.

The discount-sales format is the core of the product mix, while food, household goods and cultural programs support visit frequency. Its customer strategy relies on neighborhood-based stores, stronger customer management and lowest-price discounts aimed at lower-income households as its core repeat customers.

In competition, the company sits in offline discount retail, which faces weaker domestic consumer sentiment and competition from online operators. A company earnings note said offline retail channels face falling consumer sentiment and competition from online retailers.

Because the business centers on store operations, sales move with visitor counts, average basket size and demand for discounted products. With only six stores, the performance of any single store and its lease terms feed directly into company-wide results.

In the first half of 2025, standalone sales fell 2.8% year on year and operating profit fell 40.9%, and the same note attributed the decline to weaker domestic consumer sentiment and the growth of online operators.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual sales rose from KRW 119.4 billion in 2022 to KRW 123.8 billion in 2023 (up 3.7% year on year) and then fell to KRW 117.2 billion in 2024 (down 5.3%). Sales recovered slightly to KRW 118.1 billion in 2025 (up 0.7%), with 2023 the largest year and 2024 the smallest among 2022 to 2025.

Operating profit rose from KRW 9.22 billion in 2022 to a peak of KRW 11 billion in 2023 (up 19.8%), then fell to KRW 7.78 billion in 2024 (down 29.6%) and KRW 4.59 billion in 2025 (down 41.0%), declining for two consecutive years.

The operating margin moved from 7.7% in 2022 to 8.9% in 2023, 6.6% in 2024 and 3.9% in 2025, so margin pressure continued even though sales edged up in 2025.

Profit attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 9.18 billion in 2022, KRW 13.6 billion in 2023 (up 48.4%), KRW 12.6 billion in 2024 (down 7.2%) and KRW 7.75 billion in 2025 (down 38.6%). The filed figures for net income including non-controlling interests match these attributable figures in each year.

In 2025, attributable net income of KRW 7.75 billion exceeded operating profit of KRW 4.59 billion, so non-operating items supported earnings.

Quarterly operating profit moved from a loss of KRW 683 million in the third quarter of 2025 to KRW 1.18 billion in the fourth quarter (a swing to profit from the prior quarter), then KRW 1.3 billion in the first quarter of 2026 (up 10.4% from the prior quarter) and KRW 2.69 billion in the second quarter (up 106.2%).

Third-quarter 2026 sales of KRW 31.2 billion rose 12.2% year on year, yet the company recorded an operating loss of KRW 1.81 billion and swung into a loss from the prior quarter.

The attributable net loss for the same quarter was KRW 20.1 billion, and the company said its net income reflects changes in the fair value of financial assets tied to stock-market movements.

According to a DigitalToday report, that valuation gain is not a realized disposal gain and can move sharply with market conditions after the reporting date.

Attributable net income went from KRW 31.4 billion in the second quarter of 2026 to a net loss in the third quarter, and the total for the last four quarters, from the fourth quarter of 2025 to the third quarter of 2026, was KRW 14.9 billion.

Cumulative sales for January to September 2026 reached KRW 91.407 billion, up 5.49%, while cumulative operating profit was KRW 2.178 billion, down 36.04%, according to preliminary results disclosed on November 10, 2026.

Operating cash flow was KRW 30.1 billion in 2022, KRW 15.6 billion in 2023, -KRW 324 million in 2024 and KRW 12.4 billion in 2025. Large swings in quarterly net income reflect financial-asset valuation effects, so they are better read as recurring market-driven items than as one-off events.

Industry analysis

Offline discount retail faces two pressures at once: weaker domestic consumer sentiment and the growth of online shopping. Savezone I&C's own earnings note also cited weaker domestic consumer sentiment and the growth of online operators as causes of its profit decline.

A recent media report said that hopes for a recovery in consumer sentiment, with higher average spending per customer and stronger store competitiveness, worked positively for retail-related stocks.

The same report said Savezone I&C shares also rose, with buying extending to small and mid-sized department store and outlet-related stocks. That share-price move reflects sector expectations and should be read separately from reported operating results.

Among department stores, Hyundai Department Store and other regional department stores also gained at the same time. In terms of cycle position, signs of a consumer-sentiment recovery and the structural pressure from online channels exist together, which makes the direction hard to call.

In competition, Savezone I&C differentiates itself as a neighborhood-based discount operator with six stores rather than competing on scale with larger players such as Shinsegae, E-Mart and Lotte Shopping.

Sales stayed within KRW 117 billion to KRW 124 billion over 2022 to 2025, so top-line growth was gradual, while the operating margin fell to 3.9% in 2025, widening the gap between sales and profitability.

Operating profit improved from the fourth quarter of 2025 through the second quarter of 2026, but the third quarter of 2026 returned to an operating loss, so whether the recovery continues quarter after quarter remains the open question.

Standalone results in the first half of 2025 declined, with sales down 2.8% and operating profit down 40.9% year on year.

The next periodic report is the 2026 annual business report, due by March 31, 2027. The outlook centers on whether margins recover within the current six-store network.

Because financial-asset valuation changes can move quarterly net income sharply, the key checkpoint in the next quarterly disclosure is whether the gap between operating profit and attributable net income comes from those valuation changes.

Risk factors

Volatility from financial-asset valuation

The company disclosed that its net income reflects fair-value changes in financial assets tied to stock-market movements. It also noted that such valuation gains are not realized disposal gains and can move sharply after the reporting date.

The gap between second-quarter 2026 attributable net income of KRW 31.4 billion and the third-quarter net loss of KRW 20.1 billion illustrates this volatility.

Structural pressure on offline channels

Competition from online operators and weaker domestic consumer sentiment are cited as challenges for offline retail channels. With the operating margin down to 3.9% in 2025, a decline in average spending or visitor numbers could narrow any margin recovery.

Expectations of a consumer-sentiment recovery have shown up in share prices, but how far that translates into reported results is still being tested quarter by quarter.

Concentration in a few stores and board composition

With only six stores, a lease change or closure at a single site can have a large effect on company-wide results. As of the director elections at the March 2026 general meeting, the board had three directors, one of them an outside director, so the outside-director ratio was 33.3%. Given the small board, changes in decision-making structure are an important item to monitor.

Overall view

Savezone I&C operates six department-store-style discount stores, and its annual sales stayed within KRW 117.2 billion to KRW 123.8 billion over 2022 to 2025 without large swings.

Operating profit, by contrast, peaked at KRW 11 billion in 2023, declined for two consecutive years in 2024 and 2025, and fell to KRW 4.59 billion in 2025, with the operating margin down to 3.9%.

In 2026 quarterly operating profit recovered after the swing to profit in the fourth quarter of 2025, reaching KRW 2.69 billion in the second quarter, but the third quarter returned to an operating loss of KRW 1.81 billion.

On a cumulative basis for January to September 2026, sales rose while operating profit declined, and attributable net income moved sharply with financial-asset valuation changes.

The balance sheet looks stable, with a debt ratio of 19.5% at the end of 2025 and total equity of KRW 492.1 billion, all attributable to controlling shareholders, and the price-to-book ratio sits well below 1x.

Strengths include the capital base, the quarterly recovery in operating profit and the shareholder-return steps, while weaknesses include the margin decline, the third-quarter loss, online competition with weaker consumer sentiment, and valuation-driven earnings volatility.
