02

Business structure

INNOSIMULATION was founded in 2000 and listed on KOSDAQ in July 2023 under the technology growth company special listing route.

Its core products are XR virtual training systems for automotive, rail, heavy equipment and defense, smart mobility simulators, and VIL (Vehicle-in-the-Loop) testing solutions that connect real vehicles and sensors to virtual environments.

In its technology evaluation before listing, the company received A grades from NICE Information Service and a second rating agency. The mix has shifted.

According to a 2023 report at listing, the smart mobility simulator business was about KRW 8.8 billion and the XR virtual training business about KRW 5.5 billion in 2022, and the data aggregator Butler reports XR virtual training at 100% of revenue for the second quarter of 2026, a figure not drawn from company filings.

In January 2026 the company signed a KF-16 flight training equipment supply contract with a domestic aerospace prime worth KRW 3.4 billion, running to September 30, 2028, and an e-mobility performance test equipment contract with a Korean testing institute worth roughly KRW 700 million.

Customers are mainly B2B, including Hyundai Motor affiliates, defense and aerospace agencies and testing institutes.

At the 2026 RoboWorld show in November, the company demonstrated an autonomous driving physical twin using NVIDIA Alpamayo and a robot arm verification system developed with Vectioneer of the Netherlands. Revenue is recognized over the contract period based on progress, so quarterly results can swing widely.

Against larger Korean IT firms such as Samsung SDS and LG CNS, the scale and project-based order structure differ considerably.

04

Earnings analysis

On a disclosed basis, revenue was KRW 16.9 billion in 2022, KRW 19.5 billion in 2023, KRW 9.34 billion in 2024 and KRW 14.3 billion in 2025. 2023 was the largest year of the four and 2024 the smallest. Revenue fell 52.1% year on year in 2024 and rebounded 52.7% in 2025, but remained below the 2022 level.

Operating losses were KRW 1.8 billion in 2022, KRW 156 million in 2023, KRW 4.81 billion in 2024 and KRW 1.99 billion in 2025, a loss in each of the four years.

The 2023 narrowing and 2024 widening came as sales declined while cost burden rose, and the operating margin fell to -51.6% in 2024 before improving to -14.0% in 2025.

Net loss attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 4.81 billion in 2022, KRW 278 million in 2023, KRW 6.58 billion in 2024 and KRW 2.09 billion in 2025. Net loss including non-controlling interests matched the controlling shareholder figure in all four years.

Operating cash flow was -KRW 7.46 billion in 2022, -KRW 6.25 billion in 2023 and -KRW 1.5 billion in 2024, then turned to KRW 2.71 billion in 2025. The debt ratio went from 190.6% in 2022 to 76.8% in 2023, then 118.2% in 2024 and 90.6% in 2025.

Year-end 2025 total equity of KRW 30.2 billion was entirely attributable to controlling shareholders, against total liabilities of KRW 27.3 billion.

On a quarterly basis, revenue rose from KRW 2.51 billion in the third quarter of 2025 to KRW 4.89 billion in the fourth quarter, about double, and the operating result moved to a profit of KRW 142 million.

Revenue then fell to KRW 2.45 billion in the first quarter of 2026 with an operating loss of KRW 460 million, followed by KRW 2.16 billion (operating loss of KRW 844 million) in the second quarter and KRW 2.32 billion (operating loss of KRW 877 million) in the third quarter.

The third quarter revenue was down 7.4% year on year and the operating loss widened from the prior year. The net loss attributable to controlling shareholders over the last four quarters totals KRW 1.97 billion.

05

Industry analysis

The company's end markets combine autonomous driving and ADAS validation, defense virtual training and physical AI verification demand. Securities analysts have described the simulator-based XR market as growing quickly, supported by advances in XR technology and the commercialization of 5G networks.

At the Automotive Testing Expo 2026 in March, the company unveiled large-scale parallel simulation capable of running tens of thousands of scenarios at once, arguing that validating increasingly complex end-to-end AI models requires verification centered on large virtual environments.

In defense, expanding exports by Korean defense contractors were cited as a backdrop for XR virtual training demand in a 2023 report at listing. Demand depends on project-based orders from government, public agencies and defense buyers, so quarterly revenue moves sharply with the timing of awards.

Competitively, the company faces specialized automotive simulation equipment and software makers, while its relationship with the Varjo XR headset maker is that of a Korean distributor.

The chief executive has said the company offers the only integrated platform in Korea covering automotive, rail and heavy equipment simulators.

Large Korean IT firms such as Samsung SDS, LG CNS and Hyundai Autoever run big projects in system integration and platforms, whereas INNOSIMULATION competes in the narrower field of combined simulator hardware and software.

06

Outlook

Among the confirmed contracts, the KF-16 flight training equipment deal worth KRW 3.4 billion runs from January 21, 2026 to September 30, 2028, with payment terms of 35% advance, 50% interim and 15% balance, so revenue recognition will spread across 2026 to 2028.

Given that revenue was KRW 2.16 billion in the second quarter and KRW 2.32 billion in the third quarter of 2026, how much this contract adds to quarterly revenue is the next item to track.

In May 2025 the company raised KRW 5.7 billion through a third-party allotment, with stated use of KRW 1.7 billion in 2025, KRW 3 billion in 2026 and KRW 1 billion after 2027, mainly for personnel and materials.

Whether the physical twin and INNO ALPS platform shown at RoboWorld in November 2026 turn into orders is the key technical question. The XoundR seat and Varjo HMD line shown at KMF in June 2026 signal diversification into entertainment.

The company lists autonomous driving and ADAS validation, defense, smart cities and AI training and verification as its main areas, so which segment grows its revenue share will show how the business is changing.

No annual revenue target appears in the current disclosures, so quarterly results and contract filings should be read together. Whether the narrowing of losses comes from higher revenue or from cost restructuring should become clearer in the next annual report.

08

Bull factors

A-grade technology evaluation at listing

In its technology evaluation at listing, the company received A grades from NICE Information Service and a second rating agency. Its simulator technology spanning autonomous driving, defense and XR training has been recognized in that external review.

The fourth quarter of 2025 also produced an operating profit of KRW 142 million, a quarter in which the business moved into the black. That result is read as evidence that the technology base can translate into earnings.

Two contracts signed in early 2026

In January 2026 the company signed a KF-16 flight training equipment supply contract worth KRW 3.4 billion, running to September 30, 2028, and an e-mobility test equipment contract worth about KRW 700 million. The disclosure put the KF-16 deal at about 36% of the revenue base cited in the filing. A contract running into 2028 could add medium-term revenue visibility.

Expansion into physical AI verification

At RoboWorld in November 2026 the company demonstrated an autonomous driving physical twin built on NVIDIA Alpamayo and a robot arm verification system developed with Vectioneer, and it also introduced its INNO ALPS verification platform.

If demand for autonomous driving validation grows, companies with VIL capabilities could see wider order opportunities. Whether these demonstrations turn into actual orders is the test for the bullish case.

09

Bear factors

Four straight loss years and three quarters of operating losses

The company posted losses in each year from 2022 to 2025, including a 2025 net loss attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 2.09 billion. Operating losses continued through the first three quarters of 2026, and the third-quarter loss of KRW 877 million was larger than a year earlier. A single profitable quarter has not been followed by sustained profitability.

Gap between listing-era forecasts and actual results

At its 2023 listing the company projected 2025 revenue of KRW 67.1 billion and net income of KRW 11.5 billion. Actual 2025 revenue was KRW 14.3 billion and the net loss attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 2.09 billion. An expected turn to profit in 2023 also did not materialize. The size of the gap between the listing blueprint and disclosed results forms the core of the bearish argument.

Project-based model and stalled revenue

Revenue fell 52.1% in 2024 to KRW 9.34 billion, and third-quarter 2026 revenue was down 7.4% year on year. The company has said that its customized, project-based simulator business carries a high cost structure.

A model with heavy R&D and limited productization adds to revenue volatility, and unless that changes, the path to sustained profitability may stay constrained.

10

Risk factors

Revenue swings tied to order timing

Project-based orders mean contract timing and revenue recognition often diverge, so quarterly results can swing sharply. Revenue jumped to KRW 4.89 billion in the fourth quarter of 2025 and then fell to KRW 2.45 billion in the first quarter of 2026, less than half.

A concentration of large contracts in one quarter can make a profit look temporary, which makes quarterly figures hard to read as a trend.

Funding needs and dilution

In May 2025 the company raised KRW 5.7 billion in operating funds through a third-party allotment. The stated use is KRW 1.7 billion in 2025, KRW 3 billion in 2026 and KRW 1 billion after 2027.

Operating cash flow only turned positive in 2025, so the possibility of further funding remains, and any new issuance could dilute existing holders.

Pace of commercializing new businesses

The company has said the simulator industry does not generate near-term business profits, with many projects being development-heavy rather than productized. New areas such as physical AI verification, the XoundR entertainment seat and Varjo headset distribution have limited disclosed revenue contribution so far.

If R&D spending takes longer to turn into earnings, the loss period could extend, and the question is whether these new lines grow enough to offset existing losses.

12

Overall view

INNOSIMULATION is widening its business from XR virtual training and smart mobility simulators into defense, automotive and physical AI verification. Revenue rose 52.7% to KRW 14.3 billion in 2025, and operating cash flow turned positive at KRW 2.71 billion.

Against that, the company posted losses from 2022 through 2025, and operating losses continued for three straight quarters through the third quarter of 2026. The KF-16 contract signed in January 2026 adds revenue visibility into 2028, but quarterly revenue will keep moving with order timing.

The gap between the 2025 revenue projected at listing and the disclosed results is also a fact to weigh when judging the outlook. On valuation, the P/E cannot be calculated and the shares trade at a low multiple of net assets.

The next items to check are whether the 2026 annual report shows both a narrower operating loss and higher revenue, and whether the physical AI demonstrations convert into orders.
