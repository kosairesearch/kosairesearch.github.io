Drone orders rise as losses narrow at Alux

Alux's revenue has risen for two straight quarters on defense and education drone orders, and operating profit turned positive in the third quarter of 2026, yet attributable net losses have persisted for four consecutive quarters.

02

Business structure

Alux is a drone and robotics manufacturer founded in 2015 and listed on KOSDAQ in 2024. It started with education drones and is widening its business into defense and industrial drones.

The company says it has developed more than 10 core drive and control component families, including motors, ESCs and flight controllers, and has production capacity of more than 2 million core components a year.

A 2025 media report said the share of drone manufacturing in revenue rose from 6.7% in 2023 to 19.8% in 2024. Exports to North America are a large share of sales, and Growth Research put the North American share of finished drone exports at about 90% in 2024.

The company entered defense through the 2025 acquisition of Borasky, a defense drone specialist, and in 2026 it set up an AI smart-factory subsidiary and acquired a stake in a semiconductor equipment maker.

In July 2026 it reportedly signed a supply contract with Hanwha Aerospace for payload modules compatible with drone mission equipment. Its customers include defense procurers, education and public institutions, and large logistics, heavy industry and automotive companies.

In the finished-drone market long led by China's DJI, Alux is positioned as a non-Chinese supplier as U.S. rules tighten.

04

Earnings analysis

Based on the audited periodic filings, revenue was KRW 54.8 billion in 2023, KRW 55 billion in 2024 (up 0.4%) and KRW 63 billion in 2025 (up 14.5%). Revenue rose for two consecutive years from 2024 to 2025, and 2025 was the largest year of the 2023 to 2025 period.

The near-flat 2024 was attributed to lower budgets for public-sector outsourced projects; a digital divide bridging project saw its 2024 budget fall 52% from the prior year, according to a media report.

Operating profit fell from KRW 5.03 billion in 2023 (operating margin 9.2%) to KRW 3 billion in 2024 (margin 5.5%, down 40.2%), then turned into an operating loss of KRW 10.5 billion in 2025 (margin -16.6%).

The company attributed the 2025 swing to higher R&D and manufacturing-facility costs for its expanding drone lineup and temporarily higher early-stage production costs for new products.

Attributable net income fell from KRW 3.96 billion in 2023 to KRW 2.82 billion in 2024 (down 28.7%) and became an attributable net loss of KRW 9.39 billion in 2025; the total net loss including non-controlling interests was KRW 9.42 billion.

Quarterly revenue was KRW 15.2 billion in the first quarter of 2026 (down 25.3% from the prior quarter), KRW 21.9 billion in the second (up 43.8%) and KRW 22.9 billion in the third (up 4.6%).

Operating losses narrowed from KRW 4.26 billion in the fourth quarter of 2025 to KRW 2.51 billion and KRW 1.26 billion in the first two quarters of 2026, and the third quarter posted an operating profit of KRW 467 million.

Attributable net results moved from a profit of KRW 597 million in the third quarter of 2025 to losses in each quarter since, with KRW 2.64 million... more precisely KRW 264 million in the third quarter of 2026. Attributable net losses over the last four quarters total KRW 8.31 billion.

The gap between an operating profit and a net loss in the third quarter is a point to verify in the periodic report. Equity of KRW 45.5 billion is entirely attributable to shareholders. Operating cash flow fell from KRW 3.11 billion in 2023 and KRW 1.33 billion in 2024 to -KRW 160 million in 2025.

05

Industry analysis

The drone industry is in a phase where U.S. de-China policy is reshaping demand. Brokers have argued that U.S. restrictions on Chinese companies could displace DJI demand and accelerate growth.

A Hana Securities analyst, citing Alux's compliance with the NDAA requirements, expected a sharp rise in overseas exports, according to a report from around March 2026.

A Korea Investment & Securities analyst pointed to the company's capacity of 2 million units a year as supporting domestic defense orders and longer-term overseas expansion. On technology, a 2025 report said its lightweight drones under 250 grams compare with DJI.

An August 2026 report said U.S. 100% tariffs on Chinese drones would accelerate supply-chain realignment. In defense procurement, the level of domestic components has become a condition for winning orders, which fits the company's in-house component model.

Cheap Chinese products still pressure the market, as the company itself has acknowledged. Education-drone revenue has been volatile with public budgets, so a larger defense and industrial mix changes the character of sales.

Within the broader sector, Alux is often grouped with robotics makers such as Doosan Robotics, but its combination of finished drones and components follows a different path.

06

Outlook

The company set 2026 targets of revenue growth year on year, annual revenue of more than KRW 100 billion, a return to profit and better profitability, as announced on August 14, 2026.

With first-half revenue of KRW 37.1 billion and third-quarter revenue of KRW 22.9 billion, a simple calculation shows fourth-quarter revenue would need to exceed KRW 40 billion to reach KRW 100 billion.

The company said orders secured in the second half would be reflected in revenue in sequence and that scale-up would improve the earnings structure.

The largest confirmed order is the KRW 8.45 billion commercial education drone contract with the armed forces' central procurement office, which runs through December 27, 2026. The disclosure said the contract equals 13.40% of the latest revenue.

At a March 2026 shareholder meeting briefing, the company said it expected two large orders within the year. Among defense projects, three suppliers were reportedly selected to share a KRW 30 billion program, KRW 10 billion each.

The company also said it is discussing joint product development and production with overseas defense firms. Its subsidiaries add unmanned automation and precision inspection capabilities to the group.

The points to confirm are whether the third-quarter operating profit continues into the fourth quarter and how quickly large orders convert into revenue. Order backlog and revenue recognition timing can be checked in the periodic reports.

08

Bull factors

Defense and public orders driving revenue growth

The commercial education drone purchase disclosed on July 30, 2026, is worth KRW 8.45 billion and runs through December 27, 2026. The company said first-half 2026 revenue of KRW 37.1 billion rose about 55% from a year earlier and was its largest half-year revenue since founding. It cited its entry into defense as a main driver.

Export demand from de-China rules

U.S. restrictions on Chinese drones are creating demand for non-Chinese suppliers. Growth Research said in a 2025 report that operating margins on export drones exceed 20%, more than double those of domestic education and robotics products. Brokers see a rising export share as a possible axis of earnings improvement.

Vertical integration and quarterly margin repair

The company says it has localized core components and has capacity above 2 million units a year.

Operating losses narrowed from KRW 4.26 billion in the fourth quarter of 2025 to KRW 2.51 billion and KRW 1.26 billion in the first two quarters of 2026, and the third quarter turned to an operating profit of KRW 467 million.

09

Bear factors

Four straight quarters of attributable net losses

Attributable net losses have run from the fourth quarter of 2025 through the third quarter of 2026, totaling KRW 8.31 billion over the last four quarters. The third-quarter 2026 loss of KRW 264 million was larger than the second quarter's, and the operating profit has not yet carried through to the net line.

Dependence on public budgets and policy

In 2024, lower public-sector outsourcing budgets held revenue growth to 0.4%. A company official said delayed or cancelled government service projects reduced related revenue by about KRW 5 billion. Because defense and overseas demand depend on policy, schedule slips could increase quarterly swings.

Rising debt and volatile quarterly sales

The debt-to-equity ratio rose from 10.5% in 2024 to 105.2% in 2025, and total liabilities at year-end 2025 were KRW 47.8 billion. Quarterly revenue fell 25.3% in the first quarter of 2026 and rose 43.8% in the second, a large swing. With acquisitions and facility spending continuing, financial headroom depends on how quickly profits recover.

10

Risk factors

Policy and tariff risk

A large part of demand is tied to U.S. restrictions on Chinese drones and defense procurement rules. Easing or changes in implementation could weaken the export case. Price pressure from low-cost Chinese products also remains, as the company has acknowledged.

Subsidiary acquisition and funding risk

On August 18, 2026, the company filed corrected disclosures on a subsidiary's capital increase and on acquisitions and disposals of other companies' shares. Overlapping acquisitions and capital raises can affect the capital structure and profit recognition. Subsidiary-level results are also a point to verify.

Float and supply-demand risk

As of September 30, 2026, Lee Chi-heon and seven others held 45.88%, and the free float ratio was 52.45%. Foreign ownership was 2.16%. With a limited free float, share prices can react sharply to changes in supply and demand.

Overall view

Alux continued to grow in 2025, with revenue of KRW 63 billion, but swung to an operating loss of KRW 10.5 billion and an attributable net loss of KRW 9.39 billion. In 2026, defense and education drone orders lifted revenue, and first-half sales rose about 55% year on year.

The third quarter turned to an operating profit of KRW 467 million, but an attributable net loss of KRW 264 million remained, and the last four quarters total KRW 8.31 billion in attributable net losses.
