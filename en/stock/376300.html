02

Business structure

Dear U operates Bubble, a subscription platform where idols and actors exchange one-to-one messages with paying fans. Its core product is the message subscription, sold as a monthly pass per artist and priced at KRW 5,000 on the app.

Bubble accounts for nearly all revenue, at 98% or more in 2025 according to Alphasquare's October 20, 2026 company report, though the sources differ between 98.2% and 98.9%. Shareholders as of August 14, 2026 include SM Entertainment (45.11%) and JYP Ent.

(10.00%), so the artist IP that feeds the platform rests on these two agencies. Through a North American entity set up in May 2024, the company has run 'the bubble,' a fan service for North American fans, since September 2024.

In competition, Weverse, operated by Hybe's Weverse Company, offers DM subscriptions alongside merchandise sales, and Kakao Entertainment launched its global fan platform Veris in March 2025.

IB Tomato compared Dear U with Weverse and noted that Dear U runs only paid digital services, which avoids inventory and logistics costs.

04

Earnings analysis

On an annual basis, revenue rose from KRW 49.2 billion in 2022 to KRW 75.7 billion in 2023 (up 53.9%), eased to KRW 74.9 billion in 2024 (down 1.1%), and reached KRW 83.8 billion in 2025 (up 12.0%), the largest year of the period.

Operating profit went from KRW 16.3 billion in 2022 to KRW 28.6 billion in 2023 (up 75.8%), KRW 25.4 billion in 2024 (down 11.2%) and KRW 31.4 billion in 2025 (up 23.5%), with operating margins of 33.1%, 37.8%, 33.9% and 37.4%.

Attributable net profit peaked at KRW 26.2 billion in 2023 (up 59.7%) and then fell to KRW 24.4 billion in 2024 (down 6.9%) and KRW 18.6 billion in 2025 (down 23.6%), a two-year decline.

Alphasquare's report links the 2025 drop to KRW 11.1 billion of bad-debt-type other expenses, including impairment of prepaid expenses, booked as other costs. Operating cash flow was KRW 21 billion in 2022, KRW 12.8 billion in 2023, KRW 23.7 billion in 2024 and KRW 35.5 billion in 2025, the highest of the four years.

On a quarterly basis, revenue rose from KRW 22.3 billion in Q3 2025 to a peak of KRW 23.8 billion in Q4 2025, then declined for three straight quarters to KRW 23.4 billion in Q1 2026, KRW 23.4 billion in Q2 and KRW 22.8 billion in Q3 2026.

Operating profit followed the same path, from KRW 9.75 billion in Q4 2025 to KRW 9.7 billion, KRW 9.41 billion and KRW 9.2 billion in Q3 2026.

Attributable net profit was KRW 11.8 billion in Q1 2026, fell to KRW 4.53 billion in Q2 (down 61.5% quarter on quarter), and turned into a net loss of KRW 7.43 billion in Q3 2026.

Pre-tax income also turned into a loss of KRW 9.02 billion in the same quarter, so the operating profit stayed positive while non-operating items widened the gap. Wisereport's external tally shows Q1 2026 revenue up 33.4% year on year. Over the last four quarters, attributable net profit totaled KRW 19.2 billion.

05

Industry analysis

The fan platform market where Dear U operates is widening its user base. Alphasquare's October 20, 2026 report shows Weverse's MAU rising from 11.2 million in Q4 2025 to 14.4 million in Q2 2026. IB Tomato put Bubble's 2025 operating margin at 37.44% against 0.67% for Weverse Company, highlighting the profitability gap.

Weverse Company also runs e-commerce, which brings inventory and logistics costs, whereas Dear U carries no inventory.

Kakao Entertainment's Veris launched in March 2025, and internal reviews called its first year better than expected, yet the same coverage noted a lack of clear revenue channels beyond merchandise sales.

Overseas fandom growth is a demand driver: Wisereport attributed the Q1 2026 subscriber gains to wider K-POP fandom abroad and higher artist activity. New IP launches are the main cycle variable. DAOL said the platform lacked meaningful new IP in the third quarter of 2026.

Foreign exchange also shifted: a favorable first half turned into a drag in the third quarter, according to the same broker. On operating margin the company stands out among entertainment platforms, but its revenue scale is far smaller than large platforms such as Weverse.

06

Outlook

Alphasquare's October 20, 2026 report projects 2026 revenue of KRW 90.9 billion (up 8.5%) and operating profit of KRW 35.3 billion (up 12.5%). That estimate predates the Q3 disclosure, so it may differ from reported results.

DAOL Investment & Securities lowered its target price from KRW 33,000 to KRW 28,000 in a report dated October 21, 2026, arguing that Q3 revenue and operating profit would fall short of consensus because of currency effects and a lack of new IP.

The disclosed Q3 2026 operating profit of KRW 9.2 billion came in above the KRW 8.6 billion market estimate compiled by Yonhap Infomax.

DAOL dropped the platform premium and applied the multiples of major entertainment companies, and said it would keep that approach until core growth visibility, profitability in line with expectations, and meaningful profit from new businesses reappear.

The same report said bubble royalties from JOOX, previously expected in Q3, would now be recognized in Q4 because of administrative procedures. It also expected Q4 subscribers to grow from the prior quarter, given the high initial subscriber base.

Company priorities point toward securing new IP and expanding overseas fandom, and the growth of 'the bubble' in North America will show how much overseas revenue can contribute. The next checkpoints are how much JOOX royalty lands in Q4 and how subscriptions move after new IP launches.

08

Bull factors

Operating margin in the mid-30s range

Operating margins were 33.1%, 37.8%, 33.9% and 37.4% over 2022–2025, staying in the 30s for four straight years. A digital subscription model with no inventory or logistics is cited as the foundation. IB Tomato highlighted a wide profitability gap against Weverse Company. Operating cash flow of KRW 35.5 billion in 2025 exceeded operating profit of KRW 31.4 billion in the same year.

Light balance sheet burden

At year-end 2025, total liabilities were KRW 33.1 billion against total equity of KRW 209.9 billion, a debt-to-equity ratio of 15.8%. The ratio rose from 8.0% in 2022 but remains low. Operating cash flow grew in both 2024 and 2025. The low debt load means the company depends little on external financing.

Wider fan platform user base

Weverse's MAU rose from 11.2 million in Q4 2025 to 14.4 million in Q2 2026, indicating a broader fan platform audience. Wisereport's tally shows Q1 2026 revenue up 33.4% year on year, with overseas fandom growth cited as a driver. The North American service 'the bubble' adds another route to overseas users.

09

Bear factors

Three straight quarters of falling sales and operating profit

Sales peaked at KRW 23.8 billion and operating profit at KRW 9.75 billion in Q4 2025, and both declined through Q3 2026. Q3 operating profit was KRW 9.2 billion. Attributable net profit fell from KRW 4.53 billion in Q2 to a net loss of KRW 7.43 billion in Q3, a sign of weaker earnings strength.

Lack of new IP and currency headwinds

DAOL pointed to a lack of meaningful new IP on the platform and a currency headwind as drags on Q3 2026 results in its October 21, 2026 report. The same report lowered the target price. It described a favorable exchange rate in the first half that turned unfavorable in the third quarter.

Rising competition and concentrated revenue

Weverse runs DM subscriptions and merchandise sales with MAU at 14.4 million, while Kakao Entertainment's Veris launched in March 2025. Veris is still seen as lacking clear revenue channels beyond merchandise. Because most of Dear U's revenue comes from Bubble alone, heavier competition could hit sales directly.

10

Risk factors

Revenue concentration and IP dependence

More than 98% of revenue comes from Bubble, and the artist IP from major shareholders SM Entertainment and JYP Ent. underpins the content. Activity gaps at those agencies or delays in new IP could affect subscriber counts. A lack of new IP was cited as a Q3 2026 drag, which shows the exposure.

Exchange rate and external factors

DAOL said a favorable first-half exchange rate became a drag in the third quarter. As overseas subscriptions grow, won-denominated results become more sensitive to currency moves. Exchange-rate exposure is likely to remain a structural factor as North American and other overseas revenue increases.

Non-operating items and accounting volatility

Alphasquare's report links the 2025 profit decline to KRW 11.1 billion of bad-debt-type other expenses, including prepaid expense impairment. In Q3 2026 operating profit stayed positive, but pre-tax results turned into a loss, so non-operating items drove net profit. Such items vary in size from quarter to quarter, which makes the net profit trend harder to read.

12

Overall view

Dear U relies on a single subscription platform, Bubble, for most of its revenue, and its operating margin stayed in the 33–38% range over 2022–2025. Revenue and operating profit reached their highest levels of the period in 2025, but attributable net profit has fallen for two straight years since its 2023 peak.

From Q1 2026 through Q3 2026, revenue and operating profit declined for three consecutive quarters, and Q3 brought an attributable net loss of KRW 7.43 billion. On the strength side, the high operating margin, a light balance sheet and a growing fan platform audience stand out.

On the weakness side, quarterly slowdown, a shortage of new IP, currency effects and heavier competition weigh on the outlook. Broker views diverge: in its October 21, 2026 report, DAOL cut its target price to KRW 28,000 after removing the platform premium.

This report provides information only and offers no investment opinion or target price. The next checkpoints are Q4 2026 results and the 2026 annual report, which will show JOOX royalty recognition and the breakdown of non-operating items.
