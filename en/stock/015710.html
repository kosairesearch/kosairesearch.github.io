Kocom: Operating Losses Tied to a Slowing Housing Market

Kocom's 2025 net income rose on higher non-operating income, but operating losses have continued since the fourth quarter of 2025 as housing construction orders weakened.

  2. 2

  3. 3

02

Business structure

Kocom was founded in March 1980 and listed on KOSDAQ in September 1997 as a smart home system maker. Its core business is the manufacture and sale of smart home systems, video door phones, door phones and CCTV systems.

Built on its in-house Home Manager application, the company has commercialized IoT-based smart home systems. The housing equipment business unit is the center of the company, and its main customers are apartment construction companies.

Because apartment builders are sensitive to construction cycles, smart home system sales are strongly affected by builders' ordering patterns. The first-quarter 2026 results explanation also cited weaker demand from builders as the reason smart home system sales fell and profits deteriorated.

In its March 2026 value-up plan, the company set out premium market leadership and large project wins as priorities. The same plan includes using its export network to raise the revenue share of high-value-added businesses.

Cost efficiency, better returns on R&D spending and a continued no-borrowing management stance were also included. Revenue volatility therefore appears to depend heavily on housing market cycles and the timing of large orders.

04

Earnings analysis

On an annual basis, revenue rose from KRW 95.3 billion in 2022 to KRW 100.5 billion in 2023 (up 5.5% year on year) and KRW 105 billion in 2024 (up 4.5%), before falling to KRW 95.8 billion in 2025 (down 8.8%). Among 2022 to 2025, 2024 was the largest year and 2022 the smallest.

Operating profit moved from an operating loss of KRW 2.28 billion in 2022 to operating profit of KRW 1.91 billion in 2023, a swing back to profit, then rose to KRW 3.73 billion in 2024 (up 95.1%) before falling to KRW 3 billion in 2025 (down 19.5%).

Operating margin was -2.4% in 2022, 1.9% in 2023, 3.6% in 2024 and 3.1% in 2025. The company attributed the 2025 drop in operating profit to lower revenue and higher selling, general and administrative expenses.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders, by contrast, rose 137.8% to KRW 5.71 billion in 2025, and the company linked the increase in net income to higher non-operating income. That same profit had fallen 21.0% to KRW 2.4 billion in 2024, so the bottom line did not always move in step with operations.

Net income for 2025 was KRW 5.71 billion, the same as the controlling-shareholder figure. Operating cash flow grew from negative KRW 1.3 billion in 2022 to KRW 10.5 billion in 2025, a rise that came even as operating profit declined in 2025.

The debt-to-equity ratio was 15.2% at the end of 2025, down from 20.5% in 2024, with total equity of KRW 130.9 billion and total liabilities of KRW 20 billion.

Quarterly results show an operating profit of KRW 38.06 million in the third quarter of 2025, followed by an operating loss of KRW 207 million in the fourth quarter.

The largest quarterly loss came in the first quarter of 2026 at KRW 826 million, followed by KRW 538 million in the second quarter and KRW 215 million in the third.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders swung to KRW 1.23 billion in the second quarter of 2026 after a KRW 131 million net loss in the first quarter, then dropped to KRW 413 million in the third quarter.

05

Industry analysis

End demand comes from the housing and construction market, and Kocom's revenue is directly tied to the flow of apartment construction orders. Smart home systems and door phones are closely linked to installed buildings, so demand tends to fall when new construction volume declines.

A November 2026 press report cited a weaker housing market and reduced orders from builders as the direct cause of the decline. A financial data provider summarized the first-quarter 2026 weakness as occurring during a slowdown in construction.

Third-quarter 2026 revenue, down 13.9% from a year earlier, points in the same direction. In cycle terms, the company sits in a phase of weaker construction orders, and recovery signals have not yet clearly shown up in quarterly results.

Peers in the same KOSDAQ electronics and components sector include Samsung Electro-Mechanics and LG Innotek, which focus on large components, and Hanwha Vision, which handles security video equipment. Kocom's business structure differs clearly from these companies because it is centered on housing equipment.

The competitive gap stems from product mix and customer base, and the company has pointed to premium markets and large project wins as ways to expand its scale.

06

Outlook

The value-up plan that Kocom disclosed on March 26, 2026 sets four goals: strengthening business competitiveness, improving profitability, maintaining financial soundness and enhancing shareholder communication.

Specific tasks include securing premium market positions, winning large projects, improving cost efficiency across the business and raising the efficiency of R&D spending. The company also said it would use its export network to make high-value-added business revenue more visible and expand its revenue share.

The plan also includes optimizing financial and investment assets while maintaining a no-borrowing management stance. On shareholder returns, the payout ratio for 2025 was 45.2%, and the dividend amount for 2025 was disclosed as KRW 2.58 billion.

On governance, a press report said the founder plans to transfer 1.9 million shares to six family members. According to the report, once the transfer is completed the founder's stake would fall from 16.3% to 5.47%, while the eldest son's stake would rise from 2.38% to 5.81%.

The plan matters because succession and earnings normalization are proceeding at the same time. The next checkpoint is whether the operating margin recovers in the 2026 annual report.

08

Bull factors

Balance-sheet cushion: low leverage and a no-borrowing stance

Total equity was KRW 130.9 billion and total liabilities were KRW 20 billion at the end of 2025, giving a debt-to-equity ratio of 15.2%. That ratio is lower than 20.5% at the end of 2024 and 24.2% in 2022. The company said in its March 2026 plan that it would maintain a no-borrowing management stance. This financial room can act as a cushion while the housing slowdown keeps pressure on earnings.

Cash generation: operating cash flow has risen

Operating cash flow rose from negative KRW 1.3 billion in 2022 to KRW 4.43 billion in 2023, KRW 7.25 billion in 2024 and KRW 10.5 billion in 2025. The increase came even though operating profit fell to KRW 3 billion in 2025.

Cash generation has moved in a different direction from annual profit since the company returned to operating profit in 2023. Whether this pattern holds through the 2026 earnings slump is the next point to check.

Narrowing losses and a second-quarter swing to profit

The operating loss narrowed from KRW 826 million in the first quarter of 2026 to KRW 538 million in the second quarter and KRW 215 million in the third. Net income attributable to controlling shareholders turned to a KRW 1.23 billion profit in the second quarter of 2026.

Because profitability improvement is a core goal of the March 2026 plan, whether the narrowing loss trend translates into a return to sustained profit matters.

Bear factors

Continued revenue decline

Revenue fell 8.8% to KRW 95.8 billion in 2025, and third-quarter 2026 revenue of KRW 18.1 billion was down 13.9% from a year earlier. First-quarter 2026 revenue of KRW 18.7 billion was 14.3% lower than the prior quarter.

Weaker apartment construction orders are cited as the direct cause, and no timing for a revenue recovery has been given.

Continued operating losses and reliance on non-operating income

The company swung to an operating loss of KRW 207 million in the fourth quarter of 2025, and operating losses have continued through the third quarter of 2026. The 2025 rise in net income was attributed to higher non-operating income, while operating profit itself fell 19.5% to KRW 3 billion. The operating margin was 3.1% in 2025, below 3.6% in 2024.

Large swings in quarterly profit

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders moved sharply between quarters, from a KRW 131 million loss in the first quarter of 2026 to KRW 1.23 billion in the second and KRW 413 million in the third. Third-quarter 2026 profit fell 66.4% from the prior quarter.

Because the last four quarters total only KRW 3.27 billion, single-quarter swings have an outsized effect on the annual picture.

Risk factors

Prolonged construction slowdown

If housing orders stay weak for longer, sales of smart home systems and door phones could fall further. Third-quarter 2026 revenue was down 13.9% from a year earlier. The timing of a construction recovery is uncertain, which is the most direct risk. If revenue keeps falling, fixed-cost pressure could extend the operating losses.

Earnings quality and reliance on non-operating income

The 2025 rise in net income was attributed to higher non-operating income. Because net income increased while operating profit declined, the quality of that profit needs to be assessed. Non-operating items can be one-off in nature, so whether operating profit recovers in the next annual report matters. The operating margin was 3.1% in 2025, below 3.6% in 2024.

Ownership change and execution risk

A press report said the founder plans to transfer shares to family members, which would change the largest shareholder structure. The premium-market and large-project targets in the March 2026 plan have not yet appeared in quarterly results, since revenue kept declining through the third quarter of 2026. How the plan moves into execution will shape the nature of the management transition.

Overall view

Kocom's full-year 2025 revenue fell to KRW 95.8 billion, while net income attributable to controlling shareholders rose to KRW 5.71 billion. The company attributed the profit increase to higher non-operating income, and operating profit fell to KRW 3 billion.

Operating losses have continued since the fourth quarter of 2025, although the third-quarter 2026 loss of KRW 215 million was smaller than the prior quarter's.

Net income swung to a KRW 1.23 billion profit in the second quarter of 2026 before falling to KRW 413 million in the third, so the direction of quarterly profit is not yet stable.

The company attributes the revenue decline mainly to a construction slowdown, which makes the pace of revenue recovery the key variable for operating profit. On the financial side, the debt-to-equity ratio was 15.2% and total equity was KRW 130.9 billion at the end of 2025, indicating ample balance-sheet room.

The March 2026 value-up plan, the 45.2% payout ratio for 2025 and the planned founder share transfer show both shareholder returns and a changing management structure. The next checkpoints are the 2026 full-year preliminary results and the operating margin and revenue direction in the 2026 annual report.
