02

Business structure

Optonics, founded in 2004, makes precision optical components for inertial navigation systems (INS), specifically the core optics used in ring laser gyroscopes (RLG) and fiber-optic gyroscopes (FOG).

Reports indicate RLG parts accounted for about 67% of consolidated revenue in 2025, while FOG part sales rose roughly fivefold over the two years through 2025. The company says it is the only domestic maker that mass-produces optical parts for both RLG and FOG.

Its parts are used in multiple rocket launchers, surface-to-air guided weapons and tanks. Hanwha Aerospace performs the final INS assembly, so Optonics' direct counterparty is effectively a single customer, which accounted for 88.9% of consolidated revenue in the first half of 2026.

A new supplier must pass technical verification and system-maker certification, which keeps the entry barrier relatively high. Expansion targets include diffraction gratings for laser weapons and fiber-optic current sensors for HVDC power grids, with mass production of the sensor targeted for the second half of 2029.

A consolidated subsidiary also runs a welfare-equipment manufacturing, sales and rental business. Reports say optical operations make up 99.1% of total revenue.

03

Recent trends

Based on the DART filing, nine-month 2026 revenue was KRW 6.63 billion, up 2.8% year on year. Operating profit for the same period was KRW 658 million, up 132.9%, and net income was KRW 449 million, up 22.7%. Operating profit grew far faster than revenue, marking a notable improvement in profitability.

Net income growth trailed operating profit growth, so items below the operating line rose by less. Because this is a nine-month figure, it is not a like-for-like comparison with full-year 2025 consolidated revenue of about KRW 25.6 billion.

Reports put 2025 consolidated operating profit at KRW 3.6 billion and net income at about KRW 2.9 billion, with operating profit up 151.2% from the prior year. The operating margin rose from 5.8% in 2024 to 14.1% in 2025.

The stock opened at KRW 31,000 on its first trading day on November 17, 2026, against the KRW 12,000 IPO price. First-day closing prices differ by report, at KRW 18,040 and KRW 18,170.

On November 20, 2026 the shares closed at KRW 9,270 after a 27.12% one-day drop, close to a 52-week low of KRW 9,190, according to research published by Openfact. As of 14:34 KST on November 24, 2026, the share price was KRW 9,650 (up 4.10% from the prior close), with a market capitalization of KRW 92.3 billion.

04

Outlook

The company plans to invest KRW 14.18 billion of its IPO proceeds in a new FOG core-component line and to begin supplying parts from 2028. Backlog stood at KRW 25.66 billion at the end of September 2026, a level close to full-year 2025 revenue.

Chief Executive Lee Young-woo has said that meaningful revenue growth and margin improvement will come after the IPO-funded investment is completed in 2028. He also argued that the defense business can sustain an operating margin in the 10% range.

This is the company's own outlook, so actual results will depend on when the line starts operating and how quickly customer qualifications are completed. With the FOG share of sales rising to 28% by the end of the first quarter of 2026, missile procurement volumes now have a larger effect on revenue.

Defense demand is tied to South Korea's rising arms exports, so Hanwha Aerospace's orders and production schedule feed directly into Optonics' revenue.

The power-grid current sensor is targeted for mass production in the second half of 2029 after development and customer certification, while laser-weapon diffraction gratings are aimed at adoption in the 2030s.

The degree of revenue diversification over the medium term will depend on how these new products progress through qualification and volume production. Full-year 2026 results will be confirmed through the annual settlement disclosure.

05

Bull factors

Earnings leverage

Nine-month 2026 operating profit rose 132.9%, far above the 2.8% revenue growth. The operating margin improved from 5.8% in 2024 to 14.1% in 2025. The company has said the defense business can sustain margins in the 10% range. Supporters point to this improvement in earnings capacity as the core bullish argument.

FOG demand tailwind

Reports say the FOG share of sales rose from 19.3% at the end of 2025 to 28% at the end of the first quarter of 2026. FOG part sales are about five times their 2023 level. Demand for FOG parts used mainly in guided missiles is linked to the expansion of South Korean defense exports. The capacity plan is aimed at this demand, which supporters cite as a positive factor.

Backlog and funded capacity

Backlog stood at KRW 25.66 billion at the end of September 2026, comparable to full-year 2025 revenue. The IPO raised KRW 26.4 billion, which is planned for a new FOG line and upgrades to RLG equipment. Being the only domestic company mass-producing both RLG and FOG optics is viewed as a barrier to new entrants. This supports the durability of existing supply relationships.

06

Bear factors

Customer concentration

Hanwha Aerospace accounted for 88.9% of consolidated revenue in the first half of 2026. Because Hanwha performs INS assembly, diversifying the customer base is difficult. Strict security and export-control requirements in defense also limit the ability to widen counterparties as a commercial company would. A change in orders from one customer therefore has a large effect on results.

Earnings multiples and liquidity

Reports put the PER at 22.97x and the PBR at 3.87x at the November 20, 2026 close. Second-quarter earnings per share declined from the prior quarter. The quick ratio of 48.48% and debt ratio of 157% are cited as signs that short-term liquidity headroom is limited. Bears point to these metrics as evidence that the price already reflects growth expectations.

Post-IPO supply volatility

After rising more than 140% above the IPO price on the first trading day, the shares fell 27.12% in a single session on November 20, 2026. Shares available for trading immediately after listing equaled 22.71% of total shares.

Newly listed stocks carry the possibility of supply entering the market as lock-up arrangements expire. These flows add to short-term price volatility.

07

Risk factors

Macro

Domestic defense exports underpin demand, but export controls and security rules from the Defense Acquisition Program Administration restrict changes of counterparties and business expansion.

Adjustments to defense budgets or shifts in the timing of major export contracts can affect procurement of parts for guided missiles and tanks. Order timing can change with the external environment.

Execution

New businesses are timed for FOG supply from 2028, mass production of the grid current sensor in the second half of 2029, and laser-weapon parts in the 2030s. Any slippage in these schedules would limit the contribution of new revenue even after 2028. The company's expected margin improvement is also tied to completing this investment.

Financial and supply

A quick ratio of 48.48% and a debt ratio of 157% indicate limited buffers on short-term liquidity. Second-quarter EPS falling from KRW 65 to KRW 59 also points to volatility in earnings. Growing tradable shares after listing and large price swings could weigh on near-term supply and demand. These factors also bear on how the company funds its capacity investment.

08

Overall view

Optonics is one of the few companies in Korea that mass-produces optics for both RLG and FOG inertial navigation components, giving it a technical base in defense INS parts. The nine-month 2026 DART filing shows operating profit up 132.9%, signaling improving profitability.

However, 88.9% of consolidated revenue in the first half of 2026 came from Hanwha Aerospace, so the customer structure has changed little. The shares have moved sharply since the November 17, 2026 listing and stood at KRW 9,650 at 14:34 KST on November 24, 2026.

Reports cite a PER of 22.97x and a PBR of 3.87x at the November 20, 2026 close. The company states that the benefits of capacity expansion and new businesses will become meaningful from 2028, making medium-term execution the central variable.

Bullish factors include the earnings recovery, backlog and FOG demand, while bearish factors include customer concentration, elevated multiples and post-IPO supply volatility. At present, bullish and bearish factors coexist.
