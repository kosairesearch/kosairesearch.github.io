02

Business structure

Optonics develops and manufactures ultra-precision optical components for defense and aerospace applications.

Its core products are optical parts used in gyroscopes, a key element of inertial navigation systems (INS), and they are divided into ring laser gyroscope (RLG) parts and fiber optic gyroscope (FOG) parts according to the gyroscope type.

An INS allows missiles, aircraft and tanks to determine their position and heading without satellite navigation signals. In first-half 2026 standalone sales, RLG parts accounted for 64.0% and FOG parts for 28.1%. The RLG share was 82.1% in 2023, so the product mix is moving toward FOG.

FOG parts sales rose from KRW 1.0 billion in 2023 to KRW 2.8 billion in 2024 and KRW 4.9 billion in 2025, and reached KRW 3.3 billion in the first half of 2026. Customer concentration is dominated by a single buyer, Hanwha Aerospace.

That customer accounted for 88.9% of consolidated revenue in the first half of 2026 and 89.3% in 2025. Defense parts are developed to specific weapon system requirements, so winning new customers requires long development and qualification cycles.

On competition, the company has been reported to emphasize its ability to mass-produce both RLG and FOG optical components.

Recent trends

Consolidated revenue rose from KRW 18.9 billion in 2023 to KRW 24.8 billion in 2024 and KRW 25.6 billion in 2025, growing for three consecutive years. Operating profit for 2025 was KRW 3.6 billion, with an operating margin of 14.1%.

First-half 2026 figures are a six-month result and are not directly comparable with full-year 2025 numbers. Consolidated revenue for the first half of 2026 was about KRW 12.3 billion, with operating profit of about KRW 1.4 billion.

At the end of August 2026, the order backlog stood at KRW 27.3 billion, of which FOG parts accounted for KRW 15.1 billion and RLG parts for KRW 11.7 billion. Outlook

The next checkpoints lie in operating indicators rather than the share price. The first is whether Hanwha Aerospace's revenue share falls from the high-80% range.

The second is whether FOG sales convert from the order backlog, where FOG accounts for KRW 15.1 billion of the KRW 27.3 billion backlog at the end of August 2026. The third is whether the decline in the first-half 2026 operating margin reverses in the second half.

The fourth is whether utilization and sales rise together after the new FOG line and RLG equipment upgrades. On valuation, Korea Investment & Securities was reported to have set the offering price by applying a peer-average EV/EBITDA of 25.27 times to LTM EBITDA of KRW 527.6 million as of the first half of 2026.

On supply, the institutional lock-up commitment ratio of 5.62% and the 22.71% of shares tradable immediately after listing are disclosed facts. Tradable shares increase as each lock-up period ends, so that schedule is a reference point for reading early-stage supply.

On defense demand, domestic and international policy and export contract timing remain variables that shape the order flow.

05

Bull factors

FOG shift and order backlog

Order backlog stood at KRW 27.3 billion at the end of August 2026, with FOG parts at KRW 15.1 billion (55.3%), ahead of RLG parts at KRW 11.7 billion (42.9%). FOG sales rose from KRW 1.0 billion in 2023 to KRW 4.9 billion in 2025, so the shift in product mix is visible in reported revenue. Analysts point to this change as widening a business that once leaned on the RLG line alone.

Dual RLG and FOG production and capex

Reports describe Optonics as the only domestic company mass-producing both RLG and FOG optical components. Of the KRW 26.4 billion raised in the IPO, about KRW 15.8 billion is planned for a new FOG core-parts line and RLG equipment upgrades.

Some analysts note that long-term relationships can serve as an entry barrier in defense, so added capacity could underpin the ability to keep existing customer volumes.

Revenue growth and double-digit margin

Consolidated revenue grew from KRW 18.9 billion in 2023 to KRW 25.6 billion in 2025. The 2025 operating margin stayed in double digits at 14.1%. Coverage has pointed to this profitability as evidence of an operating base distinct from a short-term theme stock.

06

Bear factors

Single-customer concentration

Hanwha Aerospace accounted for 89.3% of consolidated revenue in 2025 and 88.9% in the first half of 2026. A change in order timing or unit pricing from this customer can swing quarterly results by a wide margin. Diversifying the customer base remains a long-term task in defense supply.

Half-year scale and profit decline

First-half 2026 revenue of KRW 12.3 billion was about half of the KRW 25.6 billion booked in 2025. Operating profit for the same period was KRW 1.4 billion, down 39.7% from a year earlier. The full-year picture will depend on second-half results, which have not yet been reported.

Post-listing supply and valuation multiple

The institutional lock-up commitment ratio stands at 5.62%, and 22.71% of shares were disclosed as tradable immediately after listing. Korea Investment & Securities was reported to have applied a peer-average EV/EBITDA of 25.27 times to LTM EBITDA of KRW 527.6 million as of the first half of 2026. Because the EBITDA base is small, the multiple has an outsized effect on the implied value.

Risk factors

Macro

Defense demand depends on national defense budgets and export contract timing, so shifts in international conditions or policy can move order timing in either direction. Exchange rates and raw material costs can affect cost ratios, with a potentially larger effect on export-linked transactions. Interest rate conditions also influence financing costs and demand for newly listed shares.

Customer concentration

About 90% of revenue is tied to Hanwha Aerospace, so order adjustments or delivery changes from that customer can move total results. New customer qualification in defense takes years, which makes it hard to add revenue sources in the short term. Order backlog spread through 2030 improves visibility, but the customer structure itself has not yet changed.

Supply and volatility

On its first trading day the stock opened at 31,000 won and reached an intraday high of 33,600 won before giving back part of the gain. The number of tradable shares increases each time a lock-up period ends. Newly listed stocks with a small tradable float often see larger price swings as supply conditions change.

08

Overall view

Optonics combines a defense optics niche with a shift toward FOG and a KRW 27.3 billion order backlog. Revenue growth since 2023 and a 14.1% operating margin in 2025 are cited as evidence of an operating base.

On the other side, about 90% of revenue is tied to Hanwha Aerospace, and first-half 2026 operating profit fell 39.7% year on year. Because half-year results are hard to compare with full-year figures, second-half results remain the key variable for the annual trend.

The stock approached twice its offering price on its first trading day, but the closing gain was smaller. The EV/EBITDA of 25.27 times used in pricing is a peer-average benchmark, and for a company with a small earnings base the multiple has a large effect on implied value.

The 5.62% lock-up commitment ratio and 22.71% of shares tradable at listing are also material facts for reading early supply. The most important indicators now are the change in Hanwha Aerospace's share, the conversion of FOG orders into sales, and whether the operating margin recovers.
