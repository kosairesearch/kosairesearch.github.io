02

Business structure

Osung Advanced Materials has built its business around functional display films and other display materials. It is classified in the secondary battery sector, but the filings and press coverage describe a business centered on display materials.

The company began this business in earnest by absorbing the affiliate Suseong Chemical in 2007, and the display materials unit accounted for about 84% of revenue in 2024 and 90.7% in 3Q 2025. According to the same coverage, the unit made up 93.3% of operating profit in 3Q 2025.

A February 4, 2026 filing set out a transfer of the unit to a newly formed subsidiary for KRW 160.0 billion, with a transfer reference date of May 10, 2026. The 100% stake in that subsidiary was agreed to be sold to a China-based buyer for about KRW 198.4 billion (950 million yuan).

The transferred unit generated KRW 107.9 billion of revenue in 2024, or 73.7% of consolidated revenue.

The remaining businesses after the sale are medical cannabis-related affiliates, residential rental housing, international logistics brokerage and money lending, and coverage describes the logistics and lending income as small in combined size.

The medical cannabis affiliate has reported losses for several years without meaningful results, and a rental subsidiary was reported to have posted a net loss of about KRW 4.0 billion. The company is pursuing new business acquisitions through consortia with several partner companies, funded by the sale proceeds.

The business is therefore moving from a display-centered operating company toward a structure with a stronger investment and holding character.

04

Earnings analysis

On a filed basis, annual revenue fell from KRW 117.1 billion in 2022 to KRW 104.9 billion in 2023, a 10.4% decline, and then rose two years in a row to KRW 146.4 billion in 2024, up 39.5%, and KRW 165.7 billion in 2025, up 13.2%.

Operating profit was KRW 12.9 billion in 2022 and KRW 12.7 billion in 2023, down 1.6%, before rising to KRW 22.2 billion in 2024, up 74.6%, and KRW 30.1 billion in 2025, up 35.5%. Operating margin rose every year, from 11.0% in 2022 to 12.1% in 2023, 15.2% in 2024 and 18.2% in 2025.

For 2025 net income growth, the company cited a higher exchange rate and a lower fixed-cost base, along with gains on disposal of property, plant and equipment and interest income.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 2.77 billion in 2022, KRW 12.3 billion in 2023, up 345.0%, KRW 12.2 billion in 2024, down 0.7%, and KRW 32.6 billion in 2025, up 166.3%.

Total net income for 2025 was KRW 32.4 billion, a different figure from controlling-shareholder net income because it includes non-controlling interests.

The debt ratio fell from 40.3% in 2022 to 21.4% in 2025, and at end-2025 total equity was KRW 282 billion, equity attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 281.3 billion, and total liabilities were KRW 60.4 billion.

Operating cash flow was KRW 31.8 billion in 2022, KRW 22.9 billion in 2023, KRW 34.2 billion in 2024 and KRW 28 billion in 2025. Quarterly results show a clear turn.

Revenue peaked at KRW 42.8 billion in 4Q 2025, up 2.0% from the prior quarter, then fell to KRW 11.5 billion in 1Q 2026, down 73.1% from the prior quarter, to KRW 11.4 billion in 2Q 2026, down 0.4%, and to KRW 9.93 billion in 3Q 2026, down 13.2%, marking three consecutive quarterly declines.

Operating profit fell from KRW 7.96 billion in 3Q 2025 and KRW 7.49 billion in 4Q 2025 to KRW 3.41 billion in 1Q 2026, and turned into an operating loss of KRW 2.38 billion in 2Q 2026. The 3Q 2026 operating profit of KRW 365 million was a return to profit but fell 95.4% year on year.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders jumped to KRW 133 billion in 2Q 2026, up 480.8% from the prior quarter, and in 3Q 2026 it turned into a net loss of KRW 16.9 billion. Of the KRW 148.5 billion four-quarter total, KRW 133 billion came from 2Q 2026.

A quarter with positive operating profit alongside a controlling-shareholder net loss points to non-operating items, whose composition should be checked in the notes to the next periodic report.

05

Industry analysis

The display materials market is in a phase of oversupply. According to the company's filings, oversupply in the display market pushed down unit prices, and the halt of legacy materials businesses along with lower sales of functional film weighed on profitability.

Press coverage says the global display market has seen profitability erode under a volume push from China and a slowdown in the industry. At the same time, a view has emerged that the industry has entered a turning phase as demand for high-resolution large-area TVs, including 4K and 8K sets, has recovered.

Because these two trends overlap, the position in the cycle reads as one where signs of demand recovery coexist with oversupply. On competition, the company's shareholder letter explained that related businesses for major customers, including polarizer operations, moved to China after 2020.

As a result, domestic materials suppliers now compete under different production and customer conditions than before. The company's end markets are display panel demand and TV demand.

Its product mix and customer base differ from those of the secondary battery materials companies in the same sector classification, so industry indicators cannot be shared directly.

Because profitability has been sensitive to display pricing, how the remaining businesses build an earnings base becomes the next benchmark.

06

Outlook

The company said it plans to use the sale proceeds to strengthen its existing display business and to keep investing in expansion into new growth areas. In a June 2026 announcement, the company said Chinese overseas direct investment approval had been completed and that it had received a KRW 106.0 billion down payment.

The company said this liquidity addresses market concerns about its funding capacity. For new business, it said it is pursuing acquisitions through consortia with several partner companies.

Filings and press coverage say the company has taken control of Filtec, merged its affiliate NSM, and is pursuing diversification through the acquisition of Cheonji Shipping. The NSM merger added plastic manufacturing for flame-retardant products to its business list.

Its participation in an acquisition consortium for SK Ocean Plant, an offshore wind and plant business, has also been reported. The timing and scale of when these acquired businesses are consolidated will be set by later filings.

Future earnings will depend on the results of M&A and the profit or loss of the remaining businesses. Two variables frame the outlook: whether the display panel market recovers, and how quickly the new acquisitions are executed.

08

Bull factors

Financial capacity and low leverage

Total equity at end-2025 was KRW 282 billion, of which KRW 281.3 billion was attributable to controlling shareholders. Total liabilities were KRW 60.4 billion, and the debt ratio fell from 40.3% in 2022 to 21.4% in 2025.

A June 2026 announcement said KRW 106.0 billion in down payment from the display unit sale had been received, and the company said it plans to use the liquidity for new investment and M&A.

Consecutive improvement in operating margin

Operating profit grew two years in a row, to KRW 22.2 billion in 2024 and KRW 30.1 billion in 2025. Net income attributable to controlling shareholders reached KRW 32.6 billion in 2025, up 166.3% from the prior year. Margin improvement sustained over several years is evidence of a change in the business profile.

Recovery in demand for large high-resolution TVs

Press coverage reports that new demand for high-resolution large-area TVs, including 4K and 8K sets, has recovered and that the display industry has entered a turning phase. The company said its results improved through securing technology and responding quickly.

Whether this demand recovery lasts long enough to offset pressure on unit prices needs to be checked against quarterly revenue in the periods ahead.

Bear factors

Sale of the core unit shrinks the revenue base

For 2024, the transferred display materials unit generated KRW 107.9 billion of revenue, or 73.7% of consolidated revenue. Press coverage reports that in 3Q 2025 the unit accounted for 90.7% of revenue and 93.3% of operating profit.

With the remaining businesses much smaller in scale after the sale, the three-quarter revenue decline in 2026 is tied to this restructuring.

Unstable operating results in 2026

The company swung to an operating loss of KRW 2.38 billion in 2Q 2026. It returned to operating profit of KRW 365 million in 3Q 2026, but that was down 95.4% year on year, and controlling-shareholder net income turned into a net loss of KRW 16.9 billion in the same quarter.

With operating profit positive and a controlling-shareholder loss in the same period, the recovery in core earnings can be read as still limited.

Loss-making remaining affiliates and uncertainty in new businesses

The medical cannabis-related affiliate that will remain after the sale has been reported to post losses for several years without meaningful results. A residential rental subsidiary was reported to have posted a net loss of about KRW 4.0 billion. The timing and scale of earnings consolidation from the new acquisitions have not yet been set.

Risk factors

M&A and business transition execution

New acquisitions proceed through consortia with several partners, so signing of definitive agreements and closing could be delayed. Receipt of the remaining display sale proceeds and completion of the transaction also depend on Chinese approvals and the debt-to-equity conversion procedure. It may take time before acquired businesses contribute to the company's earnings.

Reliance on one-off gains

Controlling-shareholder net income of KRW 133 billion in 2Q 2026 is far larger than operating results would suggest, so the next periodic report must confirm whether one-off items are included. Excluding that quarter, the four-quarter total falls sharply.

The KRW 16.9 billion controlling-shareholder net loss in 3Q 2026 also needs to be checked for links to non-operating items.

Earnings of remaining businesses and governance

Press coverage says the businesses left in the parent after the sale contribute little to earnings. A filing showed that the subsidiary's chief executive was replaced by the parent's chief executive, and the industry read this as a step to finalize the main agreement. Fund usage and related-party transactions are items to track through later disclosures.

Overall view

Osung Advanced Materials saw revenue and operating profit rise from 2022 to 2025, with operating margin climbing from 11.0% to 18.2%.

However, the display materials unit that drove that growth was set for transfer by a February 2026 filing, and quarterly revenue has declined for three consecutive quarters through 3Q 2026.

Controlling-shareholder net income of KRW 133 billion in 2Q 2026 makes up most of the four-quarter total, so the earnings trend leans heavily on a single quarter.

On the financial side, the debt ratio was 21.4% and total equity KRW 282 billion at end-2025, and a June 2026 announcement reported KRW 106.0 billion in down payment from the China-side sale.

The stock trades at a large discount to book equity, and that multiple should be read alongside whether the business transition succeeds. Strengths are financial capacity and margin improvement, while weaknesses are the sale of the core unit and the quarterly revenue decline.

The next checkpoints are the non-operating items in the 2026 annual report and the timing of new M&A consolidation.
