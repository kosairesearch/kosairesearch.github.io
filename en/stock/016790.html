Profit Recovery Against Shrinking Sales at Hyundai Feed

Hyundai Feed has posted annual operating profit since 2024, but sales fell in both 2024 and 2025, and the company swung to an operating loss in the third quarter of 2026.

    Sales peaked at KRW 160.6 billion in 2023 and fell to KRW 113 billion in 2025, with declines in both 2024 and 2025.

  2. 2

  3. 3

  4. 4

02

Business structure

Hyundai Feed, founded in 1983, manufactures compound feed and mainly sells poultry feed and swine feed. Poultry feed is the largest product line at roughly 73% to 75% of sales, swine feed accounts for about 14% to 20% depending on the data source, and other products make up the rest.

A January 2026 media report said the company specializes in layer hen feed and has grown to third place in poultry feed market share.

The company introduced Korea's first high-temperature, high-pressure hydrothermal treatment (expander) facility and is described as operating 99 bulk bins, a number reported to be among the largest per single farm in the industry.

Sales are centered on direct transactions with large farms nationwide, which the same report cited as a stable business base. Feed sits upstream in the livestock supply chain and is viewed as relatively less sensitive to economic cycles.

In research, the company is developing products such as a fly-suppressing feed using natural silicate. In 2022 and 2023, large impairment losses on intangible assets linked to a bio business weighed on its finances.

A January 2026 media report said a rebid was pursued for a stake sale of about 41.0% held by the largest shareholder side, and market participants attributed the failure of the first bidding round to the company's past entry into bio businesses.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual sales rose from KRW 158.1 billion in 2022 to KRW 160.6 billion in 2023, up 1.6%, then fell to KRW 128.5 billion in 2024, down 20.0%, and to KRW 113 billion in 2025, down 12.0%, marking two consecutive years of decline from 2024 to 2025.

Among the years from 2022 to 2025, 2023 was the largest and 2025 the smallest. The 2025 preliminary annual results announced on March 3, 2026 cited falling international grain prices and wider exchange-rate swings as the main drivers of the change in sales and earnings.

Operating losses of KRW 2.15 billion in 2022 and KRW 9.61 billion in 2023 gave way to operating profit of KRW 2.66 billion in 2024, a swing to profit. Operating profit rose 87.9% to KRW 4.99 billion in 2025, lifting the operating margin from -1.4% in 2022 and -6.0% in 2023 to 2.1% in 2024 and 4.4% in 2025.

Net losses attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 282.8 billion in 2022 and KRW 217.7 billion in 2023 turned into net income of KRW 28.4 billion in 2024.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders fell 84.6% to KRW 4.38 billion in 2025, so operating profit rose while net income fell, a pattern consistent with the large 2024 base. Whether any one-time items shaped the 2024 net income should be checked against the business report text.

Losses in 2022 and 2023 were reported to be driven largely by impairment losses on intangible assets tied to a bio business. Operating cash flow moved from -KRW 2.54 billion in 2022 and -KRW 7.91 billion in 2023 to KRW 860 million in 2024 and KRW 8.56 billion in 2025.

By quarter, the operating profit of KRW 1.7 billion in the fourth quarter of 2025 was the highest of the last five quarters, followed by KRW 370 million in the first quarter of 2026 and KRW 296 million in the second quarter, before an operating loss of KRW 204 million in the third quarter.

Third-quarter sales of KRW 29.9 billion were up 10.6% year on year, so volume grew while profitability weakened.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders turned positive at KRW 433 million in the third quarter, diverging from the operating loss, which suggests non-operating items need to be considered alongside operating results. Net income attributable to controlling shareholders for the last four quarters totals KRW 2.25 billion.

Industry analysis

Hyundai Feed's downstream market is domestic poultry and swine farms, and feed demand depends on the scale of livestock operations. Grain prices and exchange rates are the key input variables that directly affect costs and earnings.

The 2025 preliminary annual results cited falling international grain prices and wider exchange-rate swings as major drivers, which shows that the cost environment shaped earnings. A lag between input prices and selling prices can make quarterly operating margins volatile.

The cause of the third-quarter 2026 swing to an operating loss, despite higher sales, should be checked in the disclosure text to see whether cost and pricing adjustments or non-operating items played the larger role.

In competition, the company is described as the third-largest poultry feed supplier by market share, and its focus on layer hen feed is presented as a point of differentiation.

In an industry comparison by a stock information service, its operating margin is above the sector average and its debt ratio is lower than the sector average. Sector growth and the cycle position should be read alongside livestock inventory statistics.

Risk factors

Raw Materials and Exchange Rates

Grain prices and exchange rates are variables that determine the company's costs and earnings. The 2025 preliminary results cited both factors as major drivers of the change in sales and earnings. If price pass-through is delayed, the operating margin could fluctuate from quarter to quarter.

Ownership and Stake Sale

A January 2026 media report said a rebid was pursued for a stake sale of about 41.0% held by the largest shareholder side. Market participants pointed to the company's past entry into bio businesses as a reason the first round failed. Uncertainty in the sale process could translate into uncertainty about management direction.

Business Concentration and Livestock Factors

Most sales come from poultry feed, so earnings are sensitive to livestock factors such as layer hen rearing conditions and animal disease. The swine feed segment is relatively small. A structure that depends on one product line leaves limited buffer when demand shocks occur.

Overall view
