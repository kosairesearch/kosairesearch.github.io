From Secure-Facility Telecom to AI Industrial Equipment

02

Business structure

ONE4U is an AI-based company offering integrated remote services and physical security and ICT convergence solutions, founded in 1998 and listed on KONEX on October 23, 2020.

Its core customers are secure institutions where mobile phone use is restricted, including the Ministry of National Defense, the Ministry of Justice, the National Police Agency, hospitals and boarding schools.

Its flagship offerings are military public phones and video visitation services for correctional facilities, where AI speech recognition supports call monitoring.

According to company materials, long-term contracts with government bodies and proprietary technology have sustained its leading position, and the company says major telecom operators have struggled to enter this segment. That view comes from the company itself and is separate from any external market-share statistics.

Its expansion runs into AI face recognition and biometric authentication and into process management and predictive maintenance, with an integrated anomaly detection and predictive maintenance solution launched on August 12, 2026.

On September 2, 2026, it announced the 5.9 billion won acquisition of an AI inspection-equipment target. Segment revenue shares are not disclosed as figures, but remote telecom still appears to be the central revenue base. Security and control requirements at secure institutions raise barriers for new entrants.

As a result, revenue is tied to a small set of institutional buyers, while long-term contracts provide demand stability.

03

Recent trends

Based on FnGuide data, 2024 standalone results showed sales of 10 billion won, operating profit of 2.8 billion won and net income of 2.5 billion won.

Standalone statements for the 28th term, ended December 2025, reported sales of 9,049 million won, operating profit of 2,298 million won and net income of 2,428 million won. The same statements show total assets of 44,754 million won and total equity of 22,758 million won.

Sales and operating profit declined from 2024, while net income was broadly unchanged. On February 11, 2026, the company decided on a 1 billion won private convertible bond for operating funds, carrying a 6% maturity rate.

The absorption merger with a smaller affiliate took effect on October 13, 2026, issuing 2,610,584 new shares. Paid-in capital rose from 3.28 billion won to 3.54 billion won, and total liabilities rose to about 26.5 billion won from about 22.0 billion won before the merger.

The combined stake of the largest shareholder and related parties changed from 49.87% to 46.19% as reported. On October 21, 2026, a 300 million won share buyback trust contract was disclosed, with a planned purchase of 224,215 shares tied to employee performance rewards.

The same day, a filing showed executive director Ham Don-kyung's holding rose to 1,400,323 shares, or 3.96%. As of October 1, 2026, the 52-week range was 847 won to 2,550 won. At the time of this report, the share price is 1,659 won and market capitalization is 58.7 billion won.

04

Outlook

The direction of operating indicators will depend on whether secure-facility telecom keeps its profitability and how quickly the AI businesses feed into revenue.

Sales and operating profit fell in 2025, so the key question is whether a recovery in demand from secure institutions or new institutional contracts can lift the top line again.

With the operating margin near 28% in 2024, room for improvement in business metrics would come mainly from the cost structure and selling and administrative expense efficiency.

The Taekwang E&I acquisition, referred to here as an industrial equipment maker, brings both revenue and integration costs in AI inspection equipment, so its effect on future financial statements is a point of focus. However, funding for the deal and its contribution to results have not been disclosed as figures.

The merger enlarged assets and equity but also raised liabilities, so borrowing burden and the conversion terms of the convertible bond are variables for financial stability. The share buyback was disclosed as a source of funds for employee performance rewards and is linked to the company's talent strategy.

The next checkpoint is the third-quarter 2026 quarterly report, with a statutory filing deadline of November 14, 2026. That report is expected to show the asset and liability structure after the merger and acquisition, along with the revenue share of AI businesses.

05

Bull factors

A niche position in secure-facility telecom

ONE4U has built recurring demand from institutions such as the Ministry of National Defense, the Ministry of Justice and the National Police Agency, where mobile phone use is restricted. The company says major telecom operators have struggled to enter this segment, which it cites as evidence of a niche entry barrier. This assessment rests on company materials, and no external market-share figures are presented.

Strong past profitability and a sturdier balance sheet

ONE4U's operating margin was about 28% in 2024, and its debt-to-equity ratio fell to 65.6% at end-2024 from 103.8% in 2023, according to FnGuide data. Total equity reached 22,758 million won at end-2025 on a standalone basis. The 2026 absorption merger also enlarged paid-in capital, which adds to the financial buffer.

Expansion into AI products and equipment

ONE4U released an integrated process anomaly detection and predictive maintenance solution on August 12, 2026, broadening its industrial AI lineup. The acquisition adds inspection equipment to that lineup, and the point at which the acquired business starts to show up in revenue is the next thing to check.

The company also pairs AI face recognition and biometric technology with its existing remote services. No public figure yet shows how much revenue the new businesses contribute.

06

Bear factors

Lower sales and operating profit

Standalone sales were 9,049 million won in 2025, down from roughly 10 billion won in 2024. Operating profit fell to 2,298 million won from about 2.8 billion won a year earlier. If demand from secure institutions has matured, the room for top-line growth may be limited, which is the core of the bearish case.

Financing needs and dilution potential

In February 2026 ONE4U issued a 1 billion won private convertible bond for operating funds, carrying a 6% maturity rate. Total liabilities rose to about 26.5 billion won after the merger, from about 22.0 billion won before it.

Depending on the conversion terms and how the AI acquisition is funded, potential dilution and borrowing burden could increase.

Small-cap volatility and thin liquidity

The stock ranged between 847 won and 2,550 won over the 52 weeks to October 1, 2026, a wide band. As a KONEX-listed company, its trading volume is small, so prices can move sharply on modest orders.

Combined holdings of the largest shareholder and related parties stood at 46.19% after the merger, which limits the free float.

07

Risk factors

Macro

Demand for secure-facility telecom depends on the fiscal stance of defense, justice and policing budgets. Higher market interest rates could raise borrowing costs and tighten funding conditions for a small company, affecting interest on the convertible bond and the cost of the acquisition.

Industry and regulation

Changes in government procurement rules or security regulation would alter entry conditions in the secure-facility market. If major telecom operators or new entrants move into this segment, the competitive landscape could shift.

The AI inspection-equipment market is contested by established specialists, which adds competitive pressure for a later entrant.

Company-specific

Integrating the AI equipment acquisition and the absorbed affiliate could spread management resources thin and add integration costs. If the convertible bond converts into shares, existing holders' stakes could be diluted.

The high concentration of shares held by the largest shareholder also constrains minority-shareholder liquidity.

08

Overall view

ONE4U is a KONEX-listed company that has built profitability in the niche of secure-facility remote telecom, and its sales and operating profit declined in 2025 from 2024.

The bullish points include recurring demand from long-term contracts, an operating margin near 28% in 2024 and a larger equity base after the merger.

The bearish points include lower sales, the 2026 convertible bond issue and higher liabilities after the merger, and the price volatility and thin liquidity typical of small caps.

Changes to the business and capital structure, including the AI solution launch in August 2026, the acquisition in September, and the absorption merger and share buyback in October, are proceeding at the same time, so how they feed into results is the central point to watch.

The assessment of the company's position in secure-facility markets rests on company materials, and how far external data supports it remains a separate question.

The next checkpoint is whether the third-quarter 2026 quarterly report shows the asset and liability structure after the merger and acquisition, along with the revenue share of AI businesses. This verdict is a qualitative summary for information purposes only.
