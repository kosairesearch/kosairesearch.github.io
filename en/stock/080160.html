02

Business structure

MODE TOUR NETWORK INC is a comprehensive travel agency founded in 1989, focused on travel arrangement and air ticket sales.

Its core offering is package tours covering medium- and long-haul routes as well as short-haul regional products, and for 2026 it set strengthening high-value products and its medium- and long-haul route portfolio as a core direction.

The company describes 2026 as the first year of a restructuring of the package tour market and is pursuing four pillars: product competitiveness, upgrading sales and distribution channels, building a foundation for sustainable management, and expanding new overseas business.

It is widening its premium 'Hi-Class' line and places personalized travel experiences at the center of its product strategy. Sales channels include a self-operated mall focused on B2C and B2B channels such as agencies and partners, and the company plans to raise its online share through web and app upgrades.

Its customers include package travelers and independent travelers, and its promotions also offer discounted flights and hotels for independent travel. Subsidiaries include a cruise-focused business and a Modetour international unit.

Overseas bases include London and Barcelona offices, Beijing and Tokyo offices, and a visa center in Jakarta. Drawing on the Jakarta visa center's operating experience, it has extended its business to Chengdu and Wuhan in China.

In competition, it vies for package market share with listed travel peers such as Hanatour Service, Yellow Balloon Tour and Very Good Tour. In 2026 it received the travel agency category award at the Consumer-Selected Best Brand Awards for an eighth consecutive year, which points to external recognition of its brand.

04

Earnings analysis

On a disclosed annual basis, revenue rose from KRW 47.8 billion in 2022 to KRW 178.6 billion in 2023, up 273.9% year on year, and reached KRW 251.6 billion in 2024, up 40.9%, which was the largest year of 2022 to 2025. Revenue fell 16.4% in 2025 to KRW 210.4 billion.

Operating results moved from an operating loss of KRW 16.3 billion in 2022 to an operating profit of KRW 11.6 billion in 2023, a swing to profit, then declined 59.7% to KRW 4.65 billion in 2024 before rising 59.9% to KRW 7.44 billion in 2025.

Operating margin was -34.2% in 2022, 6.5% in 2023, 1.8% in 2024 and 3.5% in 2025. Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders moved from a loss of KRW 12 billion in 2022 to KRW 13.2 billion in 2023, a swing to profit, then KRW 10.8 billion in 2024, down 17.8%, and KRW 14.5 billion in 2025, up 33.6%.

In 2025 operating profit and controlling-shareholder profit both rose even as revenue fell, which points to cost factors playing a larger role than sales volume. The debt ratio fell from 230.2% in 2024 to 150.3% in 2025, with total liabilities of KRW 139 billion and total equity of KRW 92.5 billion at the end of 2025.

Operating cash flow fell from KRW 61.2 billion in 2022 to KRW 18.7 billion in 2024 before recovering to KRW 30.9 billion in 2025.

On a quarterly basis, revenue peaked at KRW 67.8 billion in the fourth quarter of 2025, up 80.4% from the prior quarter, and then fell for three consecutive quarters to KRW 61.3 billion, KRW 42.2 billion and KRW 34.9 billion.

Operating profit turned to KRW 248 million in the fourth quarter of 2025 and rose to KRW 2.83 billion in the first quarter of 2026, up 1,042.9% from the prior quarter, before an operating loss of KRW 856 million in the second quarter and KRW 893 million in the third quarter.

For the second-quarter loss, the company cited amortization tied to its next-generation system development, higher exchange rates and fuel surcharges, and product pricing policies designed to ease customer burden, according to press coverage of its August 14, 2026 disclosure.

Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders over the last four quarters, from the fourth quarter of 2025 through the third quarter of 2026, totaled KRW 4.14 billion.

05

Industry analysis

The travel industry is shaped by overseas travel demand and cost variables such as exchange rates and fuel surcharges that move together.

The company describes 2026 as the first year of a restructuring of the package market and said it aims to raise profitability by moving away from pure price competition and expanding premium product lines and semi-package offerings.

On demand, overseas bookings during the Travel Festa promotion, held from August 10 to September 11, 2026, rose 58.9% from the same period a year earlier, and sales rose 56.4%. By region, the company's count showed bookings up 42% for China, 36% for Japan, 22% for Southeast Asia and 52% for Europe.

Bookings for departures from after Chuseok through the winter peak rose 67.4%. These figures are internal company data, so they are difficult to compare directly with total industry demand.

On costs, exchange rates and fuel surcharges weighed on the second quarter of 2026, and the company said geopolitical uncertainty slowed bookings for long-haul destinations such as Europe and the Americas.

The company also said long-haul bookings for departures from mid-September 2026 onward have gradually improved after international fuel surcharges were cut.

Competitively, the company vies for package and independent travel share with Hanatour Service, Yellow Balloon Tour and Very Good Tour, and it is trying to differentiate by expanding local operations in China, Japan and Vietnam.

Outlook

For 2026 the company set four priorities: strengthening product competitiveness, upgrading sales and distribution channels, building a foundation for sustainable management, and expanding new overseas business.

It plans to strengthen high-value products and its medium- and long-haul route portfolio to raise market share, and to expand its premium 'Hi-Class' line. Overseas, it plans to expand local operations in key Asian markets including Vietnam and Japan.

Internally, it plans to upgrade business processes on its next-generation integrated system and to reorganize sales and marketing cost management to improve productivity.

In the near term, the 2026 Mega Sale runs for 39 days from November 16, 2026 to December 24, 2026, targeting winter peak-season departures and 2027 holiday departures. The company has said it will focus on improving profitability in the second half.

Because amortization tied to the next-generation system was a cost factor in the second quarter of 2026, how much of that cost continues into the second half and later quarters is a key variable for interpreting results.

Whether long-haul bookings recovery reverses the quarterly revenue decline will also show in quarterly figures. The 2026 annual report, the next periodic filing, will set the full-year results and fourth-quarter 2026 results as disclosed figures.

Overall view
