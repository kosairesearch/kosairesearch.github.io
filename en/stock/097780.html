Eco Volt: Losses Narrow as Merger Nears

Eco Volt turned operating profit positive in 2025 and narrowed its operating loss in the third quarter of 2026, but it has posted operating losses for three straight quarters amid falling sales while a merger into an RF-related listed company proceeds.

02

Business structure

Eco Volt is an automotive lighting parts company that makes automotive bulbs and LED light modules for vehicles. Its main customers are reported to include SL and Hyundai Mobis-type parts suppliers, and the company has refocused on automotive lighting since changing its name in 2023.

It holds subsidiaries including Kastio and NS Healthcare. In 2026 it is restructuring its business through the merger of two affiliates and the sale of SF INNOTEK VINA.

At a June 2026 extraordinary shareholder meeting, it added specialty film and optical film sales to its business purpose and removed rare-earth purposes that had not been pursued. In October 2026 it was reported as the company to be absorbed by a listed merger partner.

Sales move with automakers' production conditions and new model launch timing. Its stated strengths are long-term cooperation with customers and participation in new model development.

04

Earnings analysis

On an annual basis, sales rose from KRW 174.1 billion in 2022 to KRW 180.2 billion in 2023 and KRW 225.1 billion in 2024, and 2024 was the largest year in the 2022 to 2025 period. Sales in 2025 were KRW 217.4 billion, down 3.4% from the prior year.

Operating results were a loss of KRW 9.88 billion in 2022, a loss of KRW 19.2 billion in 2023 and a loss of KRW 21.7 billion in 2024 before an operating profit of KRW 1.08 billion in 2025, which turned positive.

The company attributed the 2025 change mainly to profit improvement from a change in its customers' cost structure and to the reflection of discontinued operating profit and loss for a business division following a merger.

The operating margin moved from -5.7% in 2022 and -10.6% in 2023 and -9.6% in 2024 to 0.5% in 2025. Net income attributable to controlling shareholders turned to a profit of KRW 15.6 billion in 2023, then a loss of KRW 20.6 billion in 2024, and a loss of KRW 18.3 billion in 2025, narrowing the loss.

Losses have continued for two consecutive years from 2024 to 2025. Net income including non-controlling interests was a loss of KRW 24.9 billion in 2025, larger than the controlling-shareholder loss because it includes non-controlling interests. Operating cash flow was KRW 13.2 billion in 2025, turning positive.

By quarter, operating profit rose from KRW 1.92 billion in the third quarter of 2025 to KRW 6.49 billion in the fourth quarter, while the controlling-shareholder net loss of KRW 6.1 billion in the fourth quarter turned the result into a loss.

Third-quarter 2026 sales of KRW 25.6 billion fell 52.9% from a year earlier, and the operating loss of KRW 153 million turned into a loss from a year-earlier profit.

Operating losses have continued for three consecutive quarters from the first quarter of 2026 to the third quarter, and the controlling-shareholder net loss for the last four quarters totaled KRW 5.65 billion.

05

Industry analysis

Because its core business is automotive lighting parts, the company is directly exposed to domestic vehicle production and parts export trends. It is classified in the automotive parts sector, and rising LED adoption was cited as a factor behind its 2025 return to operating profit.

Domestic automobile production and parts exports have recently been declining, and changes in automakers' production conditions and new model launch timing are cited as drivers of sales swings. The 52.9% year-on-year drop in third-quarter 2026 sales is consistent with this demand backdrop.

On the cycle, the company faces a period of falling production overlapping with gaps in new model launches. The company emphasizes long-term customer cooperation and participation in new model development as its competitive strengths.

Its profit recovery in 2025 was also linked to cost structure changes at key customers. Whether that improvement can offset weaker vehicle production will need to be checked in upcoming quarterly results.

Paid-in capital reduction was carried out in April and May 2026, and a free capital reduction with re-listing in June 2026 adjusted the number of listed shares. Growth depends on automotive lighting order flow and whether its parts are adopted in new models.

Bear factors

Three straight quarters of operating losses

Operating losses have continued from the first quarter of 2026 through the third quarter. Third-quarter sales of KRW 25.6 billion fell 52.9% from a year earlier, after KRW 28 billion in the first quarter and KRW 25.2 billion in the second. The sales base is shrinking, and operating results have not yet returned to a profit trend.

Continued controlling-shareholder net losses

Losses continued for two consecutive years from 2024 to 2025, and the controlling-shareholder net loss was KRW 18.3 billion in 2025.

Automotive demand and customer concentration

Domestic automobile production and parts exports are in a declining trend. The company's sales are sensitive to automakers' production conditions and new model launch timing. Volume swings at major customers could feed directly into results, which is a source of weakness.

Risk factors

Industry conditions and customer concentration

Declining automobile production and weaker parts exports could translate into lower sales. A gap in new model launches or a change in a customer's vehicle lineup could move quarterly sales sharply. Second- and third-quarter 2026 sales both stayed near KRW 25 billion, which reflects this sensitivity.

Changes in share count and capital structure

A paid-in capital reduction was carried out in April and May 2026, and a free capital reduction with re-listing took place in June. Disclosures also showed major shareholders' holdings being extinguished in connection with the merger. As the share count and shareholder composition change, share-based comparisons have become harder to make.
