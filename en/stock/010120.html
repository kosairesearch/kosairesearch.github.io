LS ELECTRIC: Order Growth Moving Into Earnings

LS ELECTRIC's 3Q 2026 revenue and operating profit rose sharply on North American data-center demand, while capacity execution, US data-center build timing and interest rates remain the next checkpoints.

  2. 2

  3. 3

02

Business structure

LS ELECTRIC makes power infrastructure equipment, with extra-high-voltage transformers, switchgear and gas-insulated switchgear (GIS) as its core lines. Most new orders in 1Q 2026 came from extra-high-voltage transformers and switchgear, and the backlog at that quarter-end was reported at KRW 5.6 trillion.

Extra-high-voltage transformers are described as building long-term order volume that supports earnings, while distribution systems are installed late in construction and therefore follow a different order cadence.

The CEO said in March 2026 that the company won more than KRW 1 trillion in data-center-related orders in 2025, with about 80 percent from the United States.

Its main production bases are the Cheongju plant for low- and high-voltage distribution equipment and the Busan plant for extra-high-voltage transformers, and the company said it has about three US production sites including ESS.

For DC-based power equipment, Daishin Securities reported in January 2026 ESS contracts of KRW 350 billion with a domestic battery maker and KRW 150 billion with a US fuel-cell maker for 2025. Plans to sell low-growth, low-margin businesses to reallocate resources were viewed positively.

In competition, the company faces extra-high-voltage peers such as HD Hyundai Electric and Hyosung Heavy Industries, and pricing, fast delivery and local after-sales service are cited as differentiators.

Korea Investment & Securities assessed that its data-center product delivery speed is more than 30 percent faster than competitors.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual revenue rose from KRW 3.3771 trillion in 2022 to KRW 4.2305 trillion in 2023 (up 25.3% year on year), KRW 4.5518 trillion in 2024 (up 7.6% year on year) and KRW 4.9658 trillion in 2025 (up 9.1% year on year), increasing for three consecutive years from 2023 to 2025.

Among 2022-2025, 2025 was the year with the highest revenue.

Operating profit rose from KRW 187.5 billion in 2022 to KRW 324.9 billion in 2023 (up 73.2% year on year), KRW 389.7 billion in 2024 (up 20.0% year on year) and KRW 426.4 billion in 2025 (up 9.4% year on year), increasing for three consecutive years from 2023.

The operating margin climbed from 5.6% in 2022 to 7.7% in 2023 and 8.6% in each of 2024 and 2025, staying at the same level after 2024.

Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders rose from KRW 90.3 billion in 2022 to KRW 206.0 billion in 2023 (up 128.1% year on year), KRW 238.7 billion in 2024 (up 15.9% year on year) and KRW 286.6 billion in 2025 (up 20.1% year on year).

Net income including non-controlling interests is a different figure from net profit attributable to controlling shareholders, and stood at KRW 284.3 billion in 2025. The debt-to-equity ratio was 114.5% in 2022, 116.5% in 2023, 137.3% in 2024 and 131.5% in 2025, highest in 2024 and lower in 2025.

At the end of 2025, total equity was KRW 2.1413 trillion, of which the controlling shareholders' share was KRW 2.0698 trillion, and total liabilities were KRW 2.8154 trillion.

Cash flow from operating activities turned positive, moving from KRW -145.4 billion in 2022 to KRW 214.6 billion in 2023, and then increased to KRW 230.1 billion in 2024 and KRW 299.9 billion in 2025.

On a quarterly basis, revenue of KRW 1.2163 trillion and operating profit of KRW 100.8 billion in the third quarter of 2025 rose to revenue of KRW 1.5244 trillion and operating profit of KRW 129.7 billion in the fourth quarter of 2025.

In the first quarter of 2026, results stalled, with revenue of KRW 1.3766 trillion (down 9.7% from the previous quarter) and operating profit of KRW 126.6 billion (down 2.4% from the previous quarter), and Eugene Investment & Securities analyzed in April 2026 that payments of past employee performance bonuses linked to the share price weighed on earnings.

Following revenue of KRW 1.5770 trillion and operating profit of KRW 178.5 billion in the second quarter of 2026, the third quarter posted revenue of KRW 1.8778 trillion and operating profit of KRW 233.5 billion, an increase for a second consecutive quarter.

Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders in the third quarter of 2026 was KRW 141.2 billion, up 112.6% from the same period a year earlier, and the sum of net profit attributable to controlling shareholders over the latest four quarters (fourth quarter of 2025 to third quarter of 2026) is KRW 463.6 billion.

05

Industry analysis

Power equipment is in a tight supply market, with large data-center and reshoring projects keeping demand high.

Overseas suppliers have raised prices, and NH Investment & Securities said in a May 2026 report that LS ELECTRIC also joined price increases in March and April 2026 alongside Eaton, Schneider Electric and ABB.

In the US, the replacement of aging grids has pulled forward extra-high-voltage demand, and CEO Chae Dae-seok said in March 2026 that the US market was full for four to five years. Samsung Securities said in April 2026 that this growth reflects share gains within structural demand rather than a cyclical rebound.

Korea Investment & Securities noted that data-center customers typically sign purchase contracts on the premise of supply slots of three years or more.

In competition, Yuanta-linked research from the same period was not used; instead, Eugene Investment & Securities said in April 2026 that delivery speed and responsiveness give LS ELECTRIC a growing share among data-center customers.

NH Investment & Securities also highlighted that large domestic groups including SK, GS, Samsung and Hyundai Motor plan to expand data-center investment. On the other side, some US regions have moved to restrict new data-center construction, which could delay equipment investment schedules. A faster capacity buildout by peers could also change the current pricing trend.

06

Outlook

The company raised its 2026 annual order target from KRW 4 trillion to KRW 6.5 trillion, according to media reports in October 2026.

The same reports said more than KRW 3 trillion of new orders were added in the first half of 2026 alone, and the utilization rate at the Cheongju No. 1 plant was 96.4% in the half-year report, close to full capacity.

In response, the company agreed to subscribe KRW 76.1 billion in a rights offering at its US Utah subsidiary. It plans to build the Utah plant into a complete regional production base with design and R&D functions.

Media also reported a goal for the Busan site to reach KRW 1 trillion in standalone annual revenue in 2026. CEO Chae Dae-seok said in March 2026 that the company plans double-digit revenue growth this year.

Korea Investment & Securities projects operating profit of KRW 1.1483 trillion in 2027 and KRW 1.5986 trillion in 2028, while NH Investment & Securities raised its 2026 operating profit estimate to KRW 788 billion and set 2027 at KRW 1.252 trillion.

Given a lead time of under one year for data-center products, Korea Investment & Securities said in July 2026 that first-half order growth should carry into second-half operating profit.

Hana Securities expects data-center orders to exceed 50 percent of new orders in 3Q 2026 and 4Q orders in the high KRW 1 trillion range, per its October 2026 outlook. The next checkpoints are the annual orders and operating margin in the 2026 annual report, and the output level at the Utah plant after expansion.

Brokers are split: some accept a premium valuation relative to peers on the premise of order-driven earnings growth, while others have cut targets to reflect interest rates and front-end demand risks.

NH Investment & Securities raised its operating profit estimate for the current year and its next-year estimate by 22.5%, yet cut its target price by 7.7% to reflect a higher cost of equity from rising interest rates.

When market rates rise, the discount rate used to convert future profits into present value increases, so target prices can fall even if earnings forecasts hold or improve. Hyundai Motor Securities kept its Buy rating but cut its target by 17.9%, reflecting concern over US data-center moratorium risks.

Samsung Securities said it reflected a valuation decline across the industrial sector in its target adjustment.

On the other side, NH Investment & Securities analyst Lee Min-jae said EPS growth alone is hard to use to explain the current share price, and that a clear mid- to long-term growth story is why the premium over peers is being accepted.

Whether reported quarterly results confirm the earnings estimates is the key variable for reading this valuation.

Pricing and delivery speed support orders

Daishin Securities cited price competitiveness, fast delivery and after-sales responsiveness as strengths. The company's price increases alongside overseas peers are also cited as a factor for margin improvement.

US production base expanding

Bear factors

Higher rates weigh on target prices

NH Investment & Securities cut its target by 7.7% to reflect a higher cost of equity from rising rates. Higher market rates reduce the present value of future profits, so targets can fall even when earnings forecasts hold. Many target cuts in October 2026 were tied to discount-rate changes rather than weaker earnings.

Uncertainty in US data-center investment

Some US regions have moved to limit new data-center construction over large power and water use and grid strain. Hyundai Motor Securities cut its target by 17.9% reflecting data-center moratorium concerns. Equipment investment schedules could be delayed as a result.

Soft 1Q earnings and bonus burden

Operating profit in 1Q 2026 was KRW 126.6 billion, down 2.4% from the prior quarter. Eugene Investment & Securities said in April 2026 that past stock-linked employee bonuses weighed on profit. Quarterly earnings volatility remains a weakness to watch.

Risk factors

Demand and policy risk

Restrictions on new data-center construction in parts of the US could push back demand timing. Hyundai Motor Securities reflected this in a lower target. Because orders are concentrated in data-center customers, changes in their investment schedules affect order flow directly.

Interest rate and discount-rate risk

Higher market rates raise the discount rate used to value future profits. NH Investment & Securities cut its target citing a higher cost of equity. Even with higher earnings estimates, targets can fall when discount rates change.

Capacity execution and cost risk

Utilization at the Cheongju No. 1 plant was 96.4% in the half-year report, close to full capacity. If the Utah and Busan expansions do not run on schedule, order fulfillment could slow. Whether price increases offset cost growth is a point to confirm through quarterly margins.
