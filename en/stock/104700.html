02

Business structure

Kisco is a steel producer in the steel and metals sector, and it is the company named in reports that KISCO Holdings is making a tender offer for its remaining shares.

Steel demand comes from construction, automotive, shipbuilding and general machinery, and each channel recovers at a different pace, so the company's results depend on which channel carries more weight.

Analysts broadly expect demand for construction-grade long steel to recover slowly while the construction slump persists. By contrast, demand for automotive sheet and shipbuilding plate is described as comparatively firm.

Dongkuk Steel Mill reported third-quarter 2026 long-steel sales volume of 682,000 tons, up 17% year on year, supported by large industrial projects such as semiconductor fabs and AI data centers.

Among the large domestic steelmakers, POSCO Holdings, Hyundai Steel and Dongkuk Steel Mill showed different profitability recovery paths in the third quarter of 2026 on a standalone basis, reflecting differences in product mix and end markets.

Kisco's own product lineup and segment weights should be checked against its DART annual report and IR materials. On the input side, iron ore and coking coal costs set the margin along with product prices, so how much cost can be passed through to customers is the central variable.

On governance, the terms and timing of the tender offer by KISCO Holdings could change the shareholder base and trading structure, so the filing details matter. In short, the business structure combines weak construction-linked long-steel demand with selective recovery in higher-value demand.

04

Earnings analysis

On the filed annual results, sales fell from KRW 1.06 trillion in 2022 to KRW 905.1 billion in 2023 (down 14.9% year on year), to KRW 600 billion in 2024 (down 33.7%), and to KRW 486.6 billion in 2025 (down 18.9%).

Sales declined for three consecutive years from 2023 through 2025, with 2022 the largest year and 2025 the smallest in the 2022–2025 period.

Operating profit went from KRW 121.5 billion in 2022 to KRW 86.6 billion in 2023 (down 28.8%) and KRW 1.81 billion in 2024 (down 97.9%), before turning into an operating loss of KRW 38.7 billion in 2025. The operating margin declined from 11.4% in 2022 to 9.6% in 2023, 0.3% in 2024 and -8.0% in 2025.

Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders fell from KRW 79.8 billion in 2022 to KRW 68.2 billion in 2023 (down 14.5%) and KRW 23.5 billion in 2024 (down 65.6%), then became a net loss of KRW 8.92 billion in 2025.

Net income including non-controlling interests matched these figures in each year, with a 2025 net loss of KRW 8.92 billion.

On a quarterly basis, operating losses were KRW 9.77 billion in the third quarter of 2025, KRW 15.8 billion in the fourth quarter of 2025 (wider), KRW 14.3 billion in the first quarter of 2026 (narrower), KRW 6.28 billion in the second quarter of 2026 (narrower) and KRW 7.05 billion in the third quarter of 2026 (wider quarter on quarter, but narrower year on year), making five consecutive quarters of operating losses.

Sales reached KRW 173.8 billion in the second quarter of 2026, up 55.6% from the prior quarter, but the loss did not narrow with that volume. Third-quarter 2026 sales of KRW 123.4 billion rose only 1.4% year on year.

The filed figures alone do not separate price, cost and utilization effects, so the annual report's cost and pricing data are needed to decompose the margin decline.

On the balance sheet, the debt-to-equity ratio edged up from 10.9% in 2024 to 12.4% in 2025, and operating cash flow declined from KRW 65 billion in 2023 to KRW 38.4 billion in 2024 and KRW 24.3 billion in 2025.

05

Industry analysis

Most assessments describe the domestic steel sector in 2026 as a bottoming phase, but the prevailing view is that the recovery will be limited.

Korea Ratings rated the 2026 steel industry outlook as unfavorable and the industry credit outlook as negative, citing domestic demand weakness, Chinese exports and trade protectionism as the three main threats. Samjong KPMG also rated steel as negative, the second-lowest of five levels.

The Korea Institute for Industrial Economics and Trade expects domestic steel consumption to fall 0.4% in 2026 to 43.41 million tons, while production is seen rising 0.2%. On prices, anti-dumping duties that reduced Chinese imports have been cited as the main driver of the rebound.

According to Hana Securities data as of May 11, 2026, domestic hot-rolled coil prices were KRW 960,000 per ton, up 8.8% to 21.8% from the start of the year depending on the product. Korean steel association data showed first-quarter 2026 hot-rolled imports from China falling sharply year on year.

The EU's carbon border adjustment mechanism (CBAM) has been in force since January 2026, and the Korea Chamber of Commerce and Industry estimated the certificate cost for Korean steel exports to the EU at KRW 85.1 billion in 2026, rising to KRW 558.9 billion in 2034.

The World Steel Association expects Korean steel demand to grow 1.8% in 2026 and 1.3% in 2027. Against peers, the standalone third-quarter 2026 operating margins were 4.6% at POSCO Holdings, about 3.2% at Dongkuk Steel Mill and 0.3% at Hyundai Steel.

Kisco posted an operating loss in the same quarter, so the key comparison is how much of the sector's price rebound reaches its profitability. Shipbuilding and defense were rated favorably, while construction and petrochemicals received negative outlooks.

The most concrete governance event reported so far is KISCO Holdings' tender offer for Kisco's remaining shares at KRW 13,000 per share.

The schedule and terms of the tender offer are set by the filing, and the outcome could change the number of freely tradable shares and the trading structure.

On the sector side, KIET sees the steel industry bottoming in the second half of 2026, with price increases in the first half expected to slow in the second half. Samjong KPMG projects domestic steel demand to rise 0.6% in 2026.

Demand for higher-value steel is expected from AI data centers, semiconductor fabs and energy transition projects, and peers are pursuing these markets as new businesses.

Hyundai Steel reported winning supply contracts for more than 30,000 tons of steel from seven domestic AI data centers. Whether Kisco participates in these channels should be checked in its filings and annual report.

On inputs, Samjong KPMG expects coking coal prices to rise slightly in 2026, which could weigh on margins. The next checkpoint is whether the operating margin, which was -8.0% in 2025, changes direction in the 2026 annual report.

08

Bull factors

Price rebound and lower imports

According to Hana Securities data as of May 11, 2026, domestic hot-rolled coil prices rose 8.8% to 21.8% from the start of the year. Anti-dumping duties are cited as the basis for the rebound, with first-quarter 2026 hot-rolled imports from China falling sharply year on year.

If higher selling prices offset cost pressure, the sector recovery could feed into earnings, which is the key point to watch.

Quarterly sales recovery

Sales reached KRW 173.8 billion in the second quarter of 2026, up 55.6% from the prior quarter, and KRW 123.4 billion in the third quarter, up 1.4% year on year. The third-quarter 2026 operating loss of KRW 7.05 billion was narrower than the KRW 9.77 billion loss of the third quarter of 2025. A renewed rise in sales volume can be read as a sign that operations are normalizing.

Possible change in ownership structure

Press reports say KISCO Holdings has proposed a tender offer for Kisco's remaining shares at KRW 13,000 per share. A tender offer for remaining shares raises the controlling shareholder's stake and can change the minority shareholder base and the number of freely tradable shares. The terms and schedule will be fixed in the filing, so the filing is the reference point.

Bear factors

Prolonged losses and weaker earnings base

The operating margin fell from 11.4% in 2022 to -8.0% in 2025, and operating losses have continued for five consecutive quarters from the third quarter of 2025 through the third quarter of 2026. Net loss attributable to controlling shareholders totaled KRW 14.2 billion over the last four quarters.

The operating loss persisted in the second quarter of 2026 despite a sharp sales rise, which suggests that higher volume alone has not restored profitability.

Weakening domestic demand structure

Korea Ratings pointed to domestic steel demand at its lowest level since 2010 due to the construction slump and weaker manufacturing. KIET expects domestic steel consumption to fall 0.4% in 2026.

Because the recovery in construction-grade long steel is slow, product lines tied to construction may benefit later than others.

Regulatory costs and export conditions

With the EU carbon border mechanism in force since January 2026, the Korea Chamber of Commerce and Industry estimated certificate costs for Korean steel exported to the EU at KRW 85.1 billion in 2026.

China is redirecting exports to Southeast Asia and the Middle East after losing access to markets such as Korea and Vietnam, which could intensify overseas price competition. How much carbon cost can be passed through to export prices is seen as a key variable for second-half profitability.

Risk factors

Raw material and cost risk

Samjong KPMG expects coking coal prices to rise slightly in 2026 on steady Asian demand and supply constraints tied to decarbonization. If raw-material costs rise without matching price increases, margins come under further pressure.

Operating cash flow fell to KRW 24.3 billion in 2025, so higher input costs could weaken cash generation further.

Policy and trade risk

The main driver of the price rebound was anti-dumping duties, and the effect depends on continued enforcement of trade remedies. If low-priced Chinese supply is diverted to Southeast Asia and the Middle East, the duty effect could weaken. Protectionism in the United States and the EU, together with carbon rules, keeps export conditions uncertain.

Governance and trading structure risk

If KISCO Holdings proceeds with the tender offer for remaining shares, the number of freely tradable shares and the trading structure could change. Depending on the relationship between the offer price and the market price and the tender results, trading conditions for minority shareholders may shift.

The schedule and conditions should be confirmed in the filing, since press reports alone do not show the outcome.

Overall view

Kisco's results turned from operating profit of KRW 121.5 billion in 2022 to an operating loss of KRW 38.7 billion in 2025, and the operating margin fell from 11.4% to -8.0%.

In 2026, sales jumped in the second quarter, but operating losses have persisted for five consecutive quarters, so the sales recovery has not yet become a profit recovery.

On the sector side, the hot-rolled coil price rebound and anti-dumping duties are positive, while Korea Ratings and Samjong KPMG rated the 2026 steel outlook as unfavorable or negative and domestic demand is described as the weakest since 2010.

On the balance sheet, the debt-to-equity ratio of 12.4% is low and 2025 operating cash flow stayed positive at KRW 24.3 billion, but cash flow has declined since 2023.

The price-to-book ratio is below 1, so the shares trade under book value, though whether book value supports the price depends on how well the assets earn returns. The reported tender offer by KISCO Holdings for remaining shares points to possible changes in ownership and trading structure.

The next checkpoints are the operating margin in the 2026 annual report and the size of the operating loss in the first quarter of 2027. This report does not present an investment opinion or a target price.
