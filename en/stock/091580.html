02

Business structure

SANGSIN ENERGY DISPLAY PRECISION CO makes CAN, the external can component for secondary batteries. Founded in 1992 with precision press technology that began with CRT gun parts, the company expanded into automotive and secondary battery parts and listed on KOSDAQ in May 2007.

As of Q3 2025, sales by product were 56.1% large-format CAN, 31.7% cylindrical CAN and 12.1% other. Large-format CAN goes into prismatic batteries and is strongly affected by growth in EV and ESS demand.

Cylindrical CAN is shifting from the 18650 and 21700 formats toward the 46-series, and the company produces cylindrical cans at its Cheonan plant, including 46-series.

The customer base is concentrated: the largest customer, a domestic battery maker referred to as S, accounts for about 90% of sales, and one media outlet identified this customer as Samsung SDI.

Overseas plants are in Tianjin and Xi'an in China, Malaysia, Hungary and Indiana in the United States, and the Indiana site secured in 2025 is in ramp-up and mass production. The group has nine consolidated subsidiaries.

Domestically the company is described as the top share holder among secondary battery can makers, and can manufacturing is viewed as a strength due to technology and high entry barriers.

Because utilization of production lines drives sales swings, a drop in utilization feeds directly into fixed-cost burden and earnings.

04

Earnings analysis

On a reported annual basis, sales went from KRW 290.8 billion in 2022 to KRW 301.6 billion in 2023, a 3.7% increase, and then fell 16.5% to KRW 251.8 billion in 2024 and 0.7% to KRW 250.1 billion in 2025. 2023 was the largest year and 2025 the smallest across 2022 to 2025.

Operating profit fell from KRW 32.8 billion in 2022 to KRW 30.2 billion in 2023, a 7.8% decline, then turned to an operating loss of KRW 8.22 billion in 2024 before returning to operating profit of KRW 8.65 billion in 2025.

The operating margin was 11.3% in 2022 and 10.0% in 2023, dropped to -3.3% in 2024 and recovered to 3.5% in 2025. Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders was largest in 2023 at KRW 26.2 billion, up 7.0%, and smallest in 2024 at KRW 639 million, down 97.6%.

In 2025 it recovered to KRW 2.01 billion, up 214.6%. Net profit including non-controlling interests was reported at the same amounts as net profit attributable to controlling shareholders in each year.

The recovery from the 2024 loss has been attributed to cost reduction and process improvement, and one brokerage's post-meeting note cited process improvement and lower material costs as drivers of the 2025 turnaround.

For the first nine months of 2025, weak results were attributed mainly to lower production volumes, which reduced utilization and raised fixed-cost burden.

Quarterly, sales were KRW 59.9 billion in Q3 2025 with operating profit of KRW 5.89 billion, rose to KRW 70.6 billion in Q4 2025 with operating profit of KRW 5.76 billion, a 2.3% decline quarter on quarter.

Q1 2026 sales were KRW 72.2 billion, up 2.3% quarter on quarter, while operating profit fell to KRW 4.88 billion, a 15.2% drop, marking the low point. Q2 2026 sales were KRW 86 billion, up 19.1%, and operating profit recovered to KRW 6.4 billion, up 31.0%.

Q3 2026 sales of KRW 95.7 billion, up 11.2% quarter on quarter and 59.7% year on year, and operating profit of KRW 7.29 billion, up 13.9% quarter on quarter and 23.7% year on year, are the highest of the last five quarters.

Because sales grew faster than operating profit, the operating margin was lower than a year earlier.

Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders swung to a net loss of KRW 6.54 billion in Q3 2026, so the reason for the gap between higher operating profit and the net loss needs to be checked against the items below operating profit.

The sum of net profit attributable to controlling shareholders over the last four quarters is KRW 7.03 billion. At the end of 2025, total liabilities were KRW 175.7 billion and total equity was KRW 160.1 billion, with the debt ratio at 109.7%, up from 106.2% in 2024.

Operating cash flow went from KRW 36.6 billion in 2022 and KRW 46.6 billion in 2023 to -KRW 2.26 billion in 2024 and recovered to KRW 8.08 billion in 2025.

05

Industry analysis

Cans wrap battery cells as external components, so demand is linked to cell form factors and to the battery output of customers. On the demand side, the most frequently cited driver is rapid expansion of ESS demand as power consumption in North America grows.

US policy excluding Chinese EV batteries and components, together with high tariffs, has been viewed as an opportunity for can makers with US production to gain share. On form factor, the shift toward prismatic cells and the spread of cell-to-pack designs are seen as benefiting large-format can makers in Korea.

Cylindrical cells are moving from the 18650 and 21700 formats to the 46-series, and humanoid robots are being discussed as a new application for cylindrical batteries.

On the other side, as of Q3 2025 a slowdown in EV demand in North America and Europe, along with delayed recovery in power tool demand due to high interest rates, was cited as a drag on sales.

On the cycle, the company returned to profit in Q2 2025 as utilization recovered, but the pace of recovery depends on customer volumes.

Relative to peers, the company's early overseas production footprint is cited as a strength, and one brokerage viewed its Hungary and US plants as strategic bases for winning additional customers.

However, because the largest customer accounts for a high share of sales, the speed of new customer wins will determine how the company's position within the industry changes.

Outlook

The core item to track is the ramp-up and mass production at the Indiana plant in the United States, which the company secured in 2025. A brokerage report in early 2026 noted that a large prismatic can production line expansion was under way and projected that earnings could step up clearly from Q4 2026.

A domestic brokerage report said the largest customer was preparing to introduce an LFP line with mass production targeted for the second half of 2026; that view appeared in a report around December 2025, so whether the second-half 2026 target is met is the checkpoint.

Preparation for 46-series cylindrical mass production is also under way, and the company is described as planning phased expansion of cylindrical can capacity at its Cheonan plant. Analysts also note the company is stepping up sales efforts to win new customers.

These projections were made in early 2026, so they need to be compared with later quarterly results. The nearest test is whether operating profit in Q4 2026 holds at or above the KRW 7.29 billion of Q3 2026 and whether the added capacity shows up in the sales mix.

Whether the Q3 2026 net loss attributable to controlling shareholders continues into the next quarter should also be watched.

08

Bull factors

Expanding North American ESS and prismatic demand

Q3 2026 sales of KRW 95.7 billion rose 59.7% year on year, and sales have grown for four straight quarters since Q4 2025. Large-format CAN makes up more than half of sales, so brokerage reports repeatedly cite North American ESS and prismatic battery expansion as a direct route to growth.

The Indiana plant gives the company local production in the US, which may help it respond to shifts in the policy environment.

Operating profit recovery and cost structure gains

Operating profit turned to KRW 8.65 billion in 2025, and Q3 2026 operating profit of KRW 7.29 billion rose 23.7% year on year. Process improvement and lower material costs were cited as the background to the turnaround.

Operating profit has risen for two consecutive quarters, Q2 and Q3 2026, indicating the recovery is continuing at the quarterly level.

Diversified plants and potential customer expansion

The company runs overseas plants in China, Malaysia, Hungary and the Indiana plant in the US, which gives it a base to win additional customers as global battery supply chains expand. The Cheonan plant produces cylindrical cans including 46-series, and forecasts point to broader applications such as robots. Customer diversification is cited in brokerage research as an investment point.

Bear factors

Concentration on the largest customer

The largest customer accounts for about 90% of sales, so changes in that customer's equipment operating plans feed directly into revenue. A brokerage report noted that this customer is flexibly adjusting its equipment operating plans according to market demand. Results from new customer wins still need to be confirmed in quarterly earnings.

Q3 net loss and a thin earnings base

Operating profit rose to KRW 7.29 billion in Q3 2026, but the company posted a net loss attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 6.54 billion. Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders over the last four quarters totals only KRW 7.03 billion, a thin base.

Operating profit grew 23.7% against 59.7% sales growth, so margin improvement has been limited relative to revenue expansion.

Demand slowdown and financial burden

As of Q3 2025, slower EV demand in North America and Europe, and delayed recovery in power tool demand due to high interest rates, were cited as drags on sales. The company recorded an operating loss of KRW 8.22 billion in 2024, and sales declined for two straight years in 2024 and 2025.

The debt ratio rose to 109.7% in 2025 from 99.7% in 2022, and operating cash flow was -KRW 2.26 billion in 2024.

Risk factors

Customer concentration and utilization swings

High dependence on the largest customer means production plan changes or equipment investment adjustments there can quickly reduce sales. The first nine months of 2025 showed how lower production volumes and utilization raised fixed-cost burden. If customer volumes shift utilization, the operating margin could fall again.

Execution of expansion and ramp-up

If ramp-up at the Indiana plant and the large prismatic can line does not proceed as planned, fixed-cost burden could rise again. The customer adjusting its equipment operating plans to demand is also a timing variable. Whether 46-series mass production is executed must be confirmed through quarterly results.

Balance sheet and cash flow

Total liabilities at the end of 2025 were KRW 175.7 billion, above total equity of KRW 160.1 billion. The debt ratio rose to 109.7% from 106.2% in 2024. Operating cash flow recovered to KRW 8.08 billion in 2025 after being -KRW 2.26 billion in 2024. As expansion investment rises, borrowing and cash flow management become more important.

Overall view
