Bistos: Losses Persist as Listing Test Looms

02

Business structure

Bistos (419540) is a maker of biosignal diagnostic and therapeutic medical devices founded in 2001, and it listed on KOSDAQ in October 2022.

Its core products are biosignal diagnostic and monitoring devices such as fetal monitors and patient monitors, and the line has been extended to maternal and neonatal care products and breast pumps.

The company holds patents on new products including an ADHD treatment device and an incubator, and it is developing brain-related electrical stimulation devices and a wearable healthcare baby monitor as new business areas.

The sales structure is overwhelmingly overseas: it reportedly exports to 96 countries, with overseas sales at about 80% of revenue.

Its largest shareholder is CU Medical Systems, a maker of automated external defibrillators (AED), and the combined stake of the largest shareholder and related parties was 34.20% as of June 2026.

In November 2026 the two companies jointly exhibited at MEDICA 2026 in Düsseldorf, presenting products from AEDs to maternal and neonatal care and patient monitoring. The company is also pursuing plans to offer its products through overseas distributors that already carry CU Medical Systems products.

For 2025, the company cited intensifying competition in its main sales markets and a higher exchange rate as the main reasons for lower sales and operating profit.

The export-led structure provides a base for overseas expansion, but it also leaves the business directly exposed to exchange rates and competitive pressure.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual revenue fell from KRW 24 billion in 2022 to KRW 21.3 billion in 2023 (down 11.1% year on year), KRW 20.3 billion in 2024 (down 4.9%) and KRW 17.1 billion in 2025 (down 15.6%), a decline of three straight years from 2023 through 2025. Of the 2022 to 2025 period, 2022 was the year with the largest revenue.

Operating profit went from KRW 913 million in 2022 to KRW 284 million in 2023 (down 68.9%), recovered slightly to KRW 311 million in 2024 (up 9.7%), and turned into an operating loss of KRW 1.1 billion in 2025. The operating margin slid from 3.8% in 2022 and 1.3% in 2023 and 1.5% in 2024 to -6.4% in 2025.

Profit attributable to controlling shareholders swung from a loss of KRW 4.5 billion in 2022 to a profit of KRW 806 million in 2023, rose to KRW 848 million in 2024 (up 5.1%), and returned to a loss of KRW 699 million in 2025.

In 2022, operating profit was positive at KRW 913 million while the loss attributable to controlling shareholders reached KRW 4.5 billion, which suggests that items below the operating line weighed heavily.

The company attributed the 2025 deterioration to intensified competition, lower sales and operating profit from a higher exchange rate, and higher selling and administrative expenses. Operating cash flow moved from KRW 2.44 billion in 2024 to an outflow of KRW 2.17 billion in 2025.

Quarterly, the third quarter of 2025 showed revenue of KRW 4.31 billion, an operating loss of KRW 459 million and a net loss attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 477 million.

In the fourth quarter of 2025 revenue rose 6.8% quarter on quarter to KRW 4.6 billion, the operating loss narrowed to KRW 65.84 million, and profit attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 309 million.

The first quarter of 2026 revenue fell 35.4% quarter on quarter to KRW 2.97 billion, with an operating loss of KRW 391 million. In the second quarter of 2026 revenue rose 41.2% to KRW 4.2 billion, yet the operating loss widened to KRW 856 million.

The company said the first-half 2026 swing reflected production schedule changes during facility expansion works at its Yongin and Wonju plants, with about KRW 3 billion of sales shifted into the third quarter and higher initial costs and depreciation.

Third-quarter 2026 revenue was KRW 3.63 billion, down 15.8% year on year, with an operating loss of KRW 597 million and a net loss attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 1.13 billion.

The net loss attributable to controlling shareholders over the last four quarters, from the fourth quarter of 2025 to the third quarter of 2026, totals KRW 1.66 billion.

05

Industry analysis

Bistos operates in biosignal devices for maternal and neonatal care and patient monitoring, which places it directly in export competition within global medical devices.

The company cited intensifying competition in its main sales markets as a factor behind the 2025 decline, which in practice points to tougher pricing and channel competition.

MEDICA 2026, which opened in Düsseldorf on November 16, 2026, has been described as the world's largest medical device trade fair, and Bistos held one-to-one buyer meetings there. Because overseas sales account for about 80% of revenue, exchange-rate moves feed quickly into sales and profit.

The company's citation of a higher exchange rate as a factor in the 2025 decline fits the same pattern. Evidence on where the industry sits in its cycle is limited, but the current phase combines capacity expansion with a push into overseas distribution.

Against peers, Bistos is small in revenue terms, and the company presents its shared distribution channel with the AED maker CU Medical Systems as a differentiator. Whether that channel sharing has translated into higher orders is not yet clearly visible in the figures the company has disclosed.

Outlook

In August 2026 the company said that once facility works are complete, production efficiency should improve and sales and profitability should recover gradually from the second half of 2026.

Third-quarter 2026 revenue, however, fell 15.8% year on year with an operating loss of KRW 597 million, so a gap remains between the recovery the company describes and reported results. Reports in November 2026 describe the Yongin plant expansion as ongoing, aimed at handling a rise in overseas orders steadily.

At MEDICA 2026 the company exhibited jointly with CU Medical Systems and is pursuing plans to offer its products through that partner's overseas distributors.

Among new businesses, it is developing brain-related electrical stimulation devices and a wearable healthcare baby monitor, and the timing of any sales contribution from these products is difficult to gauge from the material currently available.

On capital, an extraordinary shareholders meeting in October 2026 considered articles changes that would raise the third-party allotment limit from 20% to 50% of issued shares and lift the limits for convertible and warrant bonds from KRW 10 billion to KRW 100 billion each, and reports said the changes were approved.

In June 2026 the company entered a share buyback trust agreement worth KRW 2 billion and added KRW 1 billion in August. Trading in common shares was suspended from April 30, 2026 for a share consolidation procedure.

The next checkpoints are the annual results in the 2026 business report and whether the Yongin expansion shows up in revenue in the first quarter of 2027.

The company said sales and profitability should recover gradually from the second half of 2026 once facility works finish.

Distribution tie-up with CU Medical Systems

The company ran a joint booth with its largest shareholder CU Medical Systems at MEDICA 2026 in November 2026. Whether supply items actually expand through the overseas distributor channel is the next point to check. The idea is to share buyer networks to open new sales routes.

Capital capacity and buybacks

At the end of 2025 total liabilities were KRW 2.8 billion against total equity of KRW 17.9 billion, with a debt-to-equity ratio of 15.6%. The company entered a KRW 2 billion buyback trust in June 2026 and added KRW 1 billion in August.

An October 2026 extraordinary shareholders meeting took up articles changes that widen third-party allotment and bond limits.

Revenue fell for three straight years from 2023 through 2025, and it dropped 15.6% in 2025. The first quarter of 2026 recorded KRW 2.97 billion, the lowest of the last five quarters. Third-quarter 2026 revenue of KRW 3.63 billion was still down 15.8% year on year, so the decline has continued.

Five straight quarters of operating losses

The company has booked operating losses in each of the five quarters from the third quarter of 2025 through the third quarter of 2026. In the second quarter of 2026, the operating loss widened to KRW 856 million even though revenue rose.

The net loss attributable to controlling shareholders over the last four quarters totals KRW 1.66 billion, and the KRW 1.13 billion loss in the third quarter of 2026 was the largest of the last five quarters.

Listing pressure and dilution risk

Risk factors

Listing maintenance threshold

If market capitalization stays below KRW 20 billion for five more trading days, an administrative-issue designation becomes possible. Such a designation could weigh on funding and credibility. The company says it will keep buying back shares and take other steps to keep market capitalization above the threshold.

Exchange rates and overseas dependence

Overseas sales at about 80% of revenue make the company's results sensitive to exchange rates. The company cited a higher exchange rate as a factor behind the 2025 decline. Because sales depend on overseas distributors and partners, changes in those relationships could also disrupt supply.

Capex execution and new businesses

In the first half of 2026, facility works shifted about KRW 3 billion of sales into the third quarter. If production schedules slip again, revenue recognition could keep moving between quarters.

The new wearable healthcare and brain-related stimulation businesses are still in development, and their commercial results are not yet visible in reported figures.
