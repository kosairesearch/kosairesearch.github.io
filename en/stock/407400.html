02

Business structure

Ggumbi was founded in 2014 to manufacture and wholesale infant products and listed on KOSDAQ in February 2023. Its core categories are infant mats, bumper beds and solid-wood furniture, and its Clean Roll Mat and Giant Mat lines are described as leaders in the domestic infant-mat market.

Data platform Butler's breakdown of Q2 2026 sales shows goods at 49%, infant apparel at 24%, infant furniture at 14%, other products at 8% and services at 3%. Sales are driven by B2C e-commerce, supported by a smart logistics center in Suwon, according to FnGuide.

Group companies include Ermore, which runs the online baby-goods shop The Ergo, and Gaia Corporation, an infant-goods distributor. A distribution business added G7 coffee distribution in Q1 2026, contributing to sales growth. In August 2025 the company acquired a subsidiary that owns two installed-mat brands.

According to Seoul Shinmun, that subsidiary runs a smart-factory TPU mat line and posted 2024 revenue of KRW 7.5 billion and net income of KRW 330 million before the acquisition. The subsidiary completed a second factory in May 2026 and began full production, and the absorption merger took effect on August 1, 2026.

In Tokyo, the company supplies baby rooms and bumper beds to a metropolitan government voucher platform and said monthly volumes for key items were secured for 2026. Management says the merger consolidates manufacturing, installation and sales and aims at synergies with the group's distribution network.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual sales rose from KRW 27.2 billion in 2022 to KRW 28.9 billion in 2023, KRW 39.5 billion in 2024 and KRW 86.4 billion in 2025, up 118.8% in 2025. Sales have grown for three straight years since 2023, and 2025 was the largest year of 2022 to 2025.

Operating results moved from an operating profit of KRW 1.13 billion in 2022 to an operating loss of KRW 2.64 billion in 2023, an operating profit of KRW 403 million in 2024, and an operating loss of KRW 4.25 billion in 2025. The operating margin fell back to -4.9% in 2025 from 1.0% in 2024.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders went from KRW 1.32 billion in 2022 to a net loss of KRW 2.27 billion in 2023, KRW 2.66 billion in 2024 and KRW 15.1 billion in 2025, a three-year losing streak. Net income including non-controlling interests was also a loss of KRW 15.1 billion in 2025.

The preliminary 2025 figures disclosed on February 13, 2026 showed an operating loss of KRW 3.88 billion and a net loss of KRW 12.96 billion, and the company attributed the deterioration to amortization and impairment of intangible assets.

The final annual report shows an operating loss of KRW 4.25 billion and a net loss attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 15.1 billion.

By quarter, Q3 2025 revenue of KRW 26.5 billion with an operating loss of KRW 3.03 billion gave way to Q4 2025 revenue of KRW 31.8 billion, up 20.2% quarter on quarter, with a narrower operating loss of KRW 1.28 billion but a wider loss attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 10.4 billion.

Q1 2026 revenue was KRW 31.6 billion, down 0.7% quarter on quarter, with an operating loss of KRW 239 million. Q2 2026 revenue rose to KRW 35.8 billion, up 13.4%, with an operating profit of KRW 210 million and net income attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 4.03 billion.

Q3 2026 revenue reached KRW 37 billion, up 3.3% quarter on quarter and 39.9% year on year, but the operating loss returned at KRW 1.83 billion. Over the last four quarters, the net loss attributable to controlling shareholders totals KRW 9.12 billion.

Kiwoom Securities analyzed that the cumulative selling and administrative expenses through Q3 2025 rose sharply and that much of the increase, excluding payroll from the acquired subsidiary, was one-off.

Quarterly revenue has stayed in the KRW 300 billion range since Q4 2025, but the quarterly operating margin has alternated between positive and negative, so whether cost and expense structures stabilize is the next checkpoint.

05

Industry analysis

According to Seoul Shinmun and Money Today, the infant-mat market is being reorganized from folding mats toward TPU installed mats. Ggumbi has built its business around folding mats, so bringing installed-mat production in-house is central to its shift.

A B2C e-commerce-led structure makes margins sensitive to changes in platform fees and advertising costs. A falling number of births in Korea can act as a structural constraint on infant-product demand.

The company is trying to defend average order value by broadening categories, and Kiwoom Securities said small infant appliance sales reached KRW 18 billion in the first nine months of 2025, roughly double the prior-year period.

Overseas exposure remains small, and a 2025 report by Thinkpool cited an export share of only 1.5%. The Tokyo public voucher channel is an overseas outlet that the company describes as a highly reliable public distribution route.

Among consumer and distribution peers, Ggumbi is a small company concentrated on infant-specific product lines. Changes in its share of the installed-mat market can be traced through the product mix in upcoming periodic reports.

06

Outlook

The company expects the completion of its second factory and the start of full production to expand installed-mat capacity.

According to Seoul Shinmun, the TPU-mat subsidiary posted Q1 2026 revenue of KRW 3.38 billion and an operating profit of KRW 560 million, surpassing its full-year 2025 operating profit of KRW 520 million within a single quarter.

Shipments were delayed by more than 10,000 orders under the first factory's capacity limit, so whether the expansion restores shipment timing is a point to confirm.

Kiwoom Securities said right after the Q3 2025 results that a 2026 operating turnaround was possible if selling and administrative expense control is confirmed, and it did not present an investment rating or target price.

Quarterly operating results in 2026 have been mixed, with an operating loss of KRW 239 million in Q1, an operating profit of KRW 210 million in Q2 and an operating loss of KRW 1.83 billion in Q3. A shareholder meeting held on October 30, 2026 approved transferring KRW 20 billion of capital reserve to retained earnings.

The company presented this as a step to secure distributable profit for future treasury share purchases and cancellations, while media noted that the approval alone does not mean cash dividends or buybacks have been carried out.

The next checkpoint is the operating margin and net income attributable to controlling shareholders in the 2026 annual report, due by the end of March 2027. The 2027 Q1 quarterly report will show whether the Q2 2026 profit continues and whether second-factory output appears in sales.

08

Bull factors

Sustained Sales Growth

Sales rose for three straight years from 2023 to 2025, reaching KRW 86.4 billion in 2025. Q3 2026 revenue of KRW 37 billion was up 39.9% year on year, and quarterly sales have stayed in the KRW 300 billion range since Q4 2025. The TPU-mat subsidiary and the distribution business are cited as the main drivers of the expansion.

Installed-Mat Capacity Expansion

The TPU-mat subsidiary completed its second factory in May 2026 to expand production capacity. Its Q1 2026 revenue of KRW 3.38 billion and operating profit of KRW 560 million are subsidiary-level results. Management said rising installed-mat demand and expanded capacity together leave enough room for further growth.

Improving Cash Generation

Operating cash flow was negative from 2022 to 2024 but turned positive at KRW 1.63 billion in 2025. Total liabilities were KRW 55 billion and total equity KRW 67.7 billion at the end of 2025, and the debt ratio fell to 81.2% from 114.4% in 2024. The Q2 2026 operating profit is consistent with the same direction.

Bear factors

Recurring Operating Losses

The operating margin fell from 1.0% in 2024 to -4.9% in 2025. The 2025 operating loss was KRW 4.25 billion, and Q3 2026 returned to an operating loss of KRW 1.83 billion. The fact that the Q2 2026 operating profit did not carry into the next quarter is the basis of the bearish case.

Widening Losses

Net loss attributable to controlling shareholders grew from KRW 2.66 billion in 2024 to KRW 15.1 billion in 2025. In the preliminary 2025 disclosure, the company attributed the deterioration to amortization and impairment of intangible assets.

The last four quarters total KRW 9.12 billion of losses, so partial operating improvement has not translated into net income improvement.

Balance Sheet Burden

Total liabilities at the end of 2025 were KRW 55 billion, close to total equity of KRW 67.7 billion. The debt ratio fell from 114.4% in 2024 but remains above the 37.9% recorded in 2023. Convertible bond filings in 2026 and a treasury share disposal decision keep financing strategy as a variable.

10

Risk factors

Governance and Litigation

A petition to suspend voting rights tied to a management-control dispute was disclosed on July 30, 2026, and a court ruling or decision was disclosed on August 13, 2026. The content of the decision and its effect on management are set out in the original filings. Governance uncertainty can affect the continuity of business decisions.

Birth Rate and Channel Dependence

A falling number of births in Korea is a structural constraint on infant-product demand. Because online B2C channels account for a large share of sales, changes in platform policy or higher fees and advertising costs can pressure margins. For infant products, safety certification issues or recalls can directly damage brand trust.

Integration and Cost Burden

The company said amortization and impairment tied to subsidiary acquisitions worsened 2025 earnings. Integration costs after the 2026 merger and early costs of new categories could continue to move quarterly profit.

External analysis, including Kiwoom Securities, has pointed to control of selling and administrative expenses as the key variable.

12

Overall view

Ggumbi grew sales from KRW 27.2 billion in 2022 to KRW 86.4 billion in 2025, and sales have risen for three straight years since 2023.

Over the same period, operating results alternated between profit and loss, with a 2025 operating loss of KRW 4.25 billion and a net loss attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 15.1 billion. In 2026, a Q1 operating loss, a Q2 return to profit and a Q3 operating loss have made quarterly results uneven.

Absorbing the TPU-mat subsidiary and merging it brings installed-mat manufacturing, installation and distribution under one company, but whether that effect steadily remains in consolidated operating profit is a point for the next periodic reports.

On the financial side, the debt ratio fell from 114.4% in 2024 to 81.2% in 2025, and operating cash flow turned positive at KRW 1.63 billion in 2025.

The capital reserve transfer and the management-control litigation are facts pointing in different directions, and both are variables separate from business profit improvement. Growth in sales, the new factory and the cash-flow turn stand against recurring quarterly losses, wider net losses and governance disclosures.

Directional changes in the business will be confirmed through the figures in the 2026 annual report and the Q1 2027 quarterly report.
