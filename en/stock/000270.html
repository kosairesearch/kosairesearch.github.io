Report overview

KIA: Tariff Relief Helps, but Margin Recovery Is Unfinished

KIA's 3Q 2026 revenue and operating profit rose year on year, but the operating margin fell from 2Q as US tariff relief was partly offset by incentives and cost pressure.

  1. 1

    3Q 2026 revenue reached KRW 31.6 trillion, up 10.1% year on year, and operating profit reached KRW 1.82 trillion, up 24.3%.

  2. 2

    The US auto tariff rate was adjusted from 25% to 15%, cutting 3Q tariff costs by KRW 533 billion year on year.

  3. 3

    Versus 2Q, operating profit fell 30.8% and the operating margin dropped from 8.0% to 5.8%.

  4. 4

    Electrified vehicle retail sales rose 47% to about 300,000 units, and the US market share of 5.8% was a quarterly record.

  5. 5

    3Q operating profit was about 22% below the KRW 2.33 trillion consensus compiled by Yonhap Infomax.

02

Business structure

KIA designs and manufactures complete vehicles for sale worldwide and operates as a core affiliate of the Hyundai Motor Group, with production bases in Korea and overseas.

Global wholesale sales in 3Q 2026 reached 847,355 units, up 7.9% year on year, of which overseas sales were 706,342 units (83.4%) and domestic sales were 141,013 units (16.6%). Global retail sales, the consumer-level measure, rose 6.9% to 829,000 units.

Growth was led by electrified vehicles: xEV retail sales rose 47% to about 300,000 units, lifting their share of total retail sales to 36.2%, up 9.8 percentage points.

By powertrain, EV sales were about 123,000 units (up 75.4%) and HEV sales about 172,000 units (up 46%), while the domestic EV share rose 8.1 percentage points to 23%. EV3, EV4 and EV9 all posted higher sales. By region, Western European sales rose 16.9% to 145,000 units and India rose 38.5% to 89,000 units.

Sales in Africa and the Middle East fell 23% amid the regional conflict, while US HEV sales rose 118% to 69,000 units, far above market growth. Globally, KIA's share was 4% for January to September 2026, up from 3.6% a year earlier, and its US share of 5.8% was a quarterly record.

Profitability is sensitive to mix: a larger share of HEVs and EVs lifted the average selling price and supported revenue growth.

04

Earnings analysis

On a disclosed annual basis, revenue rose from KRW 86.6 trillion in 2022 to KRW 99.8 trillion in 2023 (up 15.3%), KRW 107.4 trillion in 2024 (up 7.7%) and KRW 114.1 trillion in 2025 (up 6.2%), extending the increase for three consecutive years from 2023 through 2025.

Among 2022 to 2025, 2025 was the largest year for revenue. Operating profit jumped to KRW 11.6 trillion in 2023 (up 60.5%), peaked at KRW 12.7 trillion in 2024 (up 9.1%), and then fell to KRW 9.08 trillion in 2025 (down 28.3%).

The operating margin moved from 8.4% in 2022 to 11.6% in 2023 and 11.8% in 2024, before declining to 8.0% in 2025. Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders fell 22.6% to KRW 7.56 trillion in 2025 from KRW 9.77 trillion in 2024.

Operating cash flow also declined to KRW 9.05 trillion in 2025 from KRW 12.6 trillion in 2024. The debt ratio fell from 87.4% in 2022 to 61.8% in 2025, reducing financial burden.

On a quarterly basis, operating profit of KRW 2.21 trillion in 1Q 2026 (up 19.7% from the prior quarter) and KRW 2.63 trillion in 2Q 2026 (up 19.2%) were the two largest of the last five disclosed quarters.

In 3Q 2026, operating profit fell 30.8% from the prior quarter to KRW 1.82 trillion, and net profit attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 1.29 trillion (down 44.7% quarter on quarter and down 9.7% year on year).

The company attributed the quarter's change to a KRW 1.56 trillion profit drag from higher incentives, price cuts, a weaker sales mix and higher material costs, partly offset by a KRW 533 billion year-on-year reduction in US tariff costs.

Revenue in the same quarter was KRW 31.6 trillion, up 10.1% year on year, but the cost of sales ratio rose 3 percentage points to 84.1%.

05

Industry analysis

The company said global industry demand fell about 6% in 3Q 2026 amid high inflation, high interest rates and the prolonged Middle East conflict. KIA's local sales still rose 6.9%, lifting its share while demand contracted.

Its global market share was 4% for January to September 2026, up from 3.6% a year earlier, and its US share of 5.8% was a quarterly record, according to the company.

Danawa reported that global EV demand has entered a slowdown, and that KIA reaffirmed a plan for 13 new EV models by 2030, down from an earlier target of 15. In the same industry, Hyundai Mobis reported 3Q 2026 operating profit of KRW 914 billion, up 17.2%, on October 30, 2026.

KIA's price competitiveness in Western Europe is supported by wider incentives for EV sales, but those costs weigh on margins. With lower African and Middle Eastern sales and higher raw material costs arriving together, regional mix and input costs remain the sector's main challenges.

06

Outlook

At its 3Q 2026 conference call, the company said it maintained its full-year 2026 sales target. On the same call, it said the Middle East conflict's effects would persist through year-end. The main near-term profitability variables are incentives and pricing.

The company described the incentive increases and price cuts cited as drags on 3Q operating profit as strategic choices to grow EV sales in Western Europe and Korea. The product baseline is a 2025 announcement, in which KIA said it would launch five new EV and hybrid models in 2026.

That plan included the first full redesign of the Seltos since its 2019 debut, with a hybrid powertrain added. The same plan delayed the US launch of the EV4 and set an early 2026 entry for the entry-level EV2 in Europe. As a long-term goal, the reaffirmed plan is a 13-model EV lineup by 2030.

Because that schedule was reported in 2025, later timing may have changed. A larger EV share lifts the average selling price, but it comes with higher incentive costs. Fourth-quarter margins will depend mainly on the Middle East fallout and the cost trend.

08

Bull factors

Tariff cut shows up in earnings

3Q tariff costs fell KRW 533 billion year on year, and the company estimated the operating margin would have been about 8% without US tariffs. Compared with the actual 5.8% margin, the size of the tariff variable is clear.

If the adjusted tariff rate holds, room for cost-structure improvement remains, which is the core bull argument.

Fast growth in electrified sales

3Q xEV retail sales rose 47% to about 300,000 units, lifting their share of total retail to 36.2%. EV sales reached about 123,000 units, up 75.4%, and KIA's EV share in Western Europe more than doubled to 43.2%. HEV sales rose 46% to about 172,000 units, diversifying the sales mix.

Financial buffer and net cash

The debt ratio fell from 87.4% in 2022 to 61.8% at the end of 2025. The company said net cash at the end of 3Q 2026 reached KRW 19.8 trillion, up KRW 158 billion from the end of 2025. Operating cash flow of KRW 9.05 trillion in 2025 supported the earnings base.

09

Bear factors

Profitability slipped in 3Q

The 3Q operating margin fell to 5.8% from 8.0% in 2Q, and operating profit dropped 30.8% from the prior quarter. The company said incentives, price cuts, a weaker mix and higher material costs created a KRW 1.56 trillion profit drag. Sales growth not translating into profit growth is the core bear argument.

Below market expectations

Compared with the KRW 2.33 trillion consensus compiled by Yonhap Infomax, 3Q operating profit was about 22% lower. Profit falling short of expectations despite higher revenue is cited as a bear argument.

Prolonged external pressures

The company said the Middle East conflict's effects would last through year-end. Sales in Africa and the Middle East fell 23% in 3Q, and raw material cost increases weighed KRW 368 billion.

Operating profit of KRW 9.08 trillion in 2025 remains below the 2024 level of KRW 12.7 trillion, so the recovery is still incomplete.

10

Risk factors

Trade and tariff policy

If the US auto tariff regime changes again, the cost structure could shift. The gap between the company's estimate of about 8% margin without tariffs and the actual 5.8% in 3Q shows the size of that exposure. Tariff changes are outside KIA's control.

Costs and geopolitics

The company estimated that raw material price increases and the Middle East conflict weighed KRW 368 billion and KRW 209 billion on 3Q operating profit, respectively. If the regional fallout runs through year-end, lower Africa and Middle East sales and cost pressure could persist together.

EV demand and incentive spending

With global EV demand slowing, the US launch of the EV4 was delayed, and the 2030 EV lineup target was reduced from 15 models to 13. Wider incentives to grow EV sales in Western Europe and Korea are a near-term drag on margins.

12

Overall view

KIA's 3Q 2026 grew both top line and profit, with revenue of KRW 31.6 trillion (up 10.1% year on year) and operating profit of KRW 1.82 trillion (up 24.3%). However, operating profit fell 30.8% from the prior quarter and the margin dropped to 5.8%, so sales growth is converting into profitability slowly.

The US tariff adjustment was the largest improvement factor, while electrified sales of about 300,000 units (up 47%) and a larger net cash position are structural strengths.

Incentives, price cuts, raw material costs and Middle East effects are weighing on near-term margins, and 3Q operating profit was about 22% below consensus. The key question is that the profit recovery seen from 1Q through 2Q 2026 reversed in 3Q, after operating profit fell 28.3% in 2025.

Bull and bear factors carry roughly equal weight, and the next checkpoints are December 2026 sales trends and the disclosed results in early 2027. This report is for information only and contains no buy or sell opinion.
