02

Business structure

PIM Korea makes precision metal parts using metal injection molding (MIM), a technology base it has built since its founding in 2001, with its headquarters in Dalseong-gun, Daegu. The company moved its listing from KONEX to KOSDAQ in April 2025.

Based on the first quarter of 2026, its sales mix was turbocharger parts at KRW 4.3 billion (53%), transmission parts at KRW 1.8 billion (22%), electrical and electronic parts at KRW 500 million (7%), and other items at KRW 1.5 billion (18%), according to press reports.

IBK Securities pointed out that automotive products accounted for 91.4% of sales in the same quarter. Customers reported in the press include LG Innotek, Samsung Electronics and Hyundai Transys.

Production is split between the Gyeongsan plant and the Vietnamese subsidiary PIM VINA, and some items were moved from Gyeongsan to Vietnam during the first quarter of 2026.

Press reports said that once the IT-dedicated line in Vietnam is complete, the subsidiary's annual capacity would rise about 130% from the previous level to roughly KRW 80 billion.

The 2021 acquisition of a mold maker brought mold design and manufacturing in-house, which is viewed as a vertically integrated setup from design through production.

New businesses include cooling solution materials for AI data centers, ultra-small reducers and materials for humanoid robots, and titanium porous transport layers (Ti PTL) for hydrogen fuel cells.

Cooling materials expanded from three supplied items to four, and the company completed development of an 8mm ultra-small reducer prototype in April 2026.

The company describes itself as a domestic No. 1 MIM technology firm and says it has won customers in IT and autonomous driving in addition to global automotive electronics companies.

04

Earnings analysis

On an annual basis, revenue slipped 1.4% from KRW 37.4 billion in 2024 to KRW 36.9 billion in 2025. The decline in sales was small, but the change in profitability was large. Operating profit fell 59.6% from KRW 3.61 billion to KRW 1.46 billion, and the operating margin moved from 9.6% to 3.9%.

A rise in the cost of sales ratio from 72.1% to 75.7% is cited as the main source of cost pressure. Non-operating items also weighed on results: financial income fell from about KRW 3.7 billion to KRW 1.2 billion, and foreign currency translation gains dropped from KRW 3.0 billion to about KRW 280 million.

As a result, net profit attributable to controlling shareholders went from KRW 2.78 billion in 2024 to a loss of KRW 54.24 million in 2025. Net profit, including non-controlling interests, moved from KRW 2.78 billion to a net loss of KRW 54.24 million over the same period.

The debt-to-equity ratio improved from 75.2% to 50.8%, with total liabilities of KRW 28.9 billion and total equity of KRW 57 billion at the end of 2025. Operating cash flow rose from KRW 3.55 billion to KRW 7.38 billion. Quarterly swings were larger.

In the third quarter of 2025, revenue was KRW 11.2 billion with operating profit of KRW 261 million; in the fourth quarter, revenue fell 27.0% quarter on quarter to KRW 8.18 billion and operating profit rose to KRW 548 million.

In the first quarter of 2026, the company posted an operating loss of KRW 747 million on revenue of KRW 8.31 billion, which it attributed to development and execution costs for entering new businesses.

In the second quarter of 2026, revenue rose 26.3% quarter on quarter to KRW 10.5 billion and operating profit turned positive at KRW 180 million.

The company said second-quarter consolidated revenue and operating profit each rose 18% and 55% year on year, respectively, in a first-half announcement dated August 19, 2026. In the third quarter of 2026, revenue was KRW 10.2 billion, down 2.6% from the prior quarter and 8.7% from a year earlier.

Operating profit was KRW 323 million, up 23.7% year on year, but the net loss attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 3.4 billion, a swing into loss.

Across the four quarters from the fourth quarter of 2025 through the third quarter of 2026, the cumulative net loss attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 1.69 billion.

The items that separate operating profit from the shareholder net result are not shown in the disclosed table, so they remain to be checked in the periodic report notes.

05

Industry analysis

Automotive parts remain the core of revenue. IBK Securities noted that automotive products made up 91.4% of sales in the first quarter of 2026. Turbocharger and transmission components are powertrain parts, so they are exposed to the shift toward electric vehicles.

The company has said it is developing metal brake parts for EVs and aluminum MIM camera module housings for autonomous driving. The growth driver in end demand is moving toward data centers. Analysts point to rising demand for cooling systems that remove heat generated by computing equipment.

For humanoid robots, an industry comment quoted in the press said 15 to 30 reducers go into each unit, but that outcome depends on reaching mass production. One brokerage expects results from the ultra-small reducer to become visible from 2028.

On competition, the company stresses MIM precision and mass-production capacity and its ability to serve global IT customers. It shares an industry classification with listed automotive parts makers such as Hyundai Mobis and Hanon Systems, but its business mix leans toward MIM parts and new-business materials.

The share price has been heavily influenced by robot-theme trading, and repeated designations as an investment-warning stock by the exchange have added to supply-and-demand volatility.

06

Outlook

Confirmed points include the company's first-half 2026 order backlog of KRW 42.4 billion, up 18.9% from a year earlier, as announced on August 19, 2026. The company said it won a large order from a global automaker in April 2026 and signed supply contracts for two new items with a domestic IT value-chain company.

It expects the backlog to feed into revenue from the third and fourth quarters. IBK Securities expects gradual growth from the second half of 2026, centered on product lines where investment has already been completed.

Cooling materials for AI data centers received an initial order in May 2026 and an additional purchase order in June 2026, and press reports tie these to a data center operated by a U.S. technology company.

The capacity plan is phased: cooling materials in 2026, humanoid parts and ultra-small reducers from 2026 through 2030, and Ti PTL from 2027 through 2030. These expansions are funded by a KRW 40 billion third series private convertible bond approved in July 2026.

If all conversion rights are exercised, new shares equal to 12.1% of the existing shares outstanding would be issued. The conversion period runs from July 20, 2027 to June 20, 2031. One brokerage projects that results from the ultra-small reducer will become visible from 2028.

The next points to check are whether operating profit remains positive in the coming quarters and whether development spending for new businesses starts to decline.

08

Bull factors

Order backlog expansion

The company attributed the increase in its order backlog to new automotive electronics contracts and expanded IT items. If the backlog converts to revenue in the third and fourth quarters, it would support an improvement in full-year 2026 results.

Entry into the AI data center materials supply chain

Cooling materials for AI data centers received an initial order in May 2026 and an additional purchase order in June 2026. Supplied items increased from three to four. Press reports say the material is used in a U.S. AI data center operated by Google, which would broaden the customer base.

Return to operating profit and balance sheet improvement

Operating profit turned positive at KRW 180 million in the second quarter of 2026 and stayed positive at KRW 323 million in the third quarter. The debt-to-equity ratio fell to 50.8% at the end of 2025 from 75.2% in 2024.

Cash and equivalents rose from KRW 3.5 billion to KRW 13.6 billion, adding a cushion for new-business investment.

Bear factors

Recurring loss-making quarters

The operating loss was KRW 747 million in the first quarter of 2026, and the net loss attributable to controlling shareholders reached KRW 3.4 billion in the third quarter. Across the four quarters from the fourth quarter of 2025 through the third quarter of 2026, the cumulative net loss was KRW 1.69 billion.

The recurring gap between positive operating profit and net losses makes the quality of earnings a point to check.

Stagnant sales and cost pressure

Revenue declined from KRW 37.4 billion in 2024 to KRW 36.9 billion in 2025, and third-quarter 2026 revenue fell 8.7% year on year. The cost of sales ratio rose from 72.1% to 75.7%. Until new businesses add meaningful revenue, top-line recovery could remain limited for a structure built mainly on automotive parts.

Investment burden and dilution risk

Consolidated acquisitions of property, plant and equipment reached KRW 10.3 billion in 2025, roughly five times the 2023 and 2024 levels. If the KRW 40 billion convertible bond is fully converted, new shares equal to 12.1% of existing shares would be issued.

The stock has been designated as an investment-warning stock several times, which adds supply-and-demand pressure.

10

Risk factors

Timing of new-business commercialization

Ultra-small reducers for humanoid robots and Ti PTL are still in development or pre-commercial stages. One brokerage expects results from the reducer to become visible from 2028. If mass production is delayed, development and capex costs could weigh on earnings for longer.

Funding and share dilution

The conversion period for the KRW 40 billion third series private convertible bond runs from July 20, 2027 to June 20, 2031. All of the proceeds are allocated to facility funding. Conversion would dilute existing shareholders, and the financial burden could grow if new businesses take longer to generate returns.

Ownership concentration and share price volatility

CEO Song Jun-ho held 32.33% as of March 31, 2026, and 48.29% including five related persons. In June 2026 he entered a share-backed loan agreement. Sharp swings tied to robot-theme trading and repeated investment-warning designations have made the share price highly volatile.

12

Overall view

PIM Korea has maintained a revenue base of roughly KRW 37 billion a year from automotive turbocharger and transmission parts. In 2025, operating profit fell 59.6% and net profit attributable to controlling shareholders turned into a loss.

In 2026, losses recurred in the first-quarter operating result and the third-quarter net result. On the other side, operating profit returned to a profit in the second quarter, and the first-half order backlog rose to KRW 42.4 billion.

Whether new businesses such as AI data center cooling materials and humanoid reducers reach mass production is still being confirmed. The investment burden has grown with the KRW 40 billion convertible bond and capacity expansion, and conversion would dilute existing shares.

The price-to-book ratio sits at a premium to net assets, while PER cannot be calculated from the last four quarters of net losses. The next points to check are the fourth-quarter results in the 2026 annual report, first-quarter 2027 earnings, and the conversion period starting in July 2027.
