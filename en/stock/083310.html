LOTVacuum: Profit Turnaround Tested by Revenue Recovery

02

Business structure

LOTVacuum was founded in March 2002 and makes and sells dry vacuum pumps, starting its business by acquiring the dry vacuum pump division of Germany's Leybold Vacuum. The company says it has since localized over 90 product types through its own R&D.

Its core products are screw-type dry vacuum pumps that evacuate gas from process chambers in semiconductor fabs, and its main revenue comes from sales of dry vacuum pumps for Fore Vacuum applications.

Most revenue is generated by pump equipment for semiconductors, and the company is also expanding equipment supply to display customers.

By contrast, general industrial segments such as secondary batteries, chemicals and pharmaceuticals remain at an early stage, and the company treats this area as a medium-term growth pillar.

Its products are described as earning praise from several global semiconductor and display makers for high exhaust capacity, low noise and power consumption, and compact size.

Customers are semiconductor device makers and display manufacturers, and earnings are directly linked to their new equipment investment and order flow. Its subsidiary makes plasma equipment for semiconductor and display processes.

Overseas bases include Austin in the United States, several sites in China, Singapore, Germany and Japan. The company competes with overseas vacuum pump makers, and it describes itself as holding the top share of the Korean dry vacuum pump market for semiconductor processes.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual revenue rose from KRW 374.2 billion in 2022 to KRW 473 billion in 2023, up 26.4% year on year, making 2023 the largest year of 2022 to 2025. It then fell to KRW 266 billion in 2024, down 43.8%, and to KRW 244.9 billion in 2025, down 7.9%, so revenue declined two years in a row from 2024 through 2025.

The company attributed the weaker revenue and profit in 2025 to delayed new equipment investment and lower orders from major customers.

Operating profit rose from KRW 29.8 billion in 2022 to KRW 67.2 billion in 2023, up 125.2%, with an operating margin of 14.2%, before turning into an operating loss of KRW 4.17 billion in 2024.

The 2025 operating loss widened to KRW 8.05 billion, with an operating margin of -3.3%, and the company has posted operating losses in two consecutive years from 2024 through 2025.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders fell from KRW 53.5 billion in 2023 to KRW 1.66 billion in 2024, down 96.9%, and then became a net loss of KRW 7.94 billion in 2025. Net income including non-controlling interests was a net loss of KRW 7.78 billion in 2025.

Operating cash flow was KRW 83 billion in 2023, KRW 4.43 billion in 2024 and KRW 6.47 billion in 2025, staying positive throughout.

Quarterly operating losses of KRW 2.21 billion, KRW 3.17 billion and KRW 5.42 billion in the third quarter of 2025, fourth quarter of 2025 and first quarter of 2026 gave way to operating profit of KRW 8.74 billion in the second quarter of 2026.

Third-quarter 2026 revenue of KRW 88.8 billion rose 9.0% from the prior quarter and 51.7% from a year earlier, while operating profit of KRW 7.82 billion fell 10.5% from the prior quarter.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders over the last four quarters totaled KRW 4.91 billion, so profit stability has yet to be confirmed even as revenue recovers.

05

Industry analysis

Most semiconductor front-end processes run under vacuum, and dry vacuum pumps are the core equipment that evacuates gas and material from process chambers. Demand for vacuum pumps therefore moves with the scale of new line investment and supplementary investment in existing lines by chip device makers.

Investment delays weighed on revenue in 2025, and a summary from a financial information site describes an increase in facility and supplementary investment in the first half of 2026 as lifting dry vacuum pump sales.

Process miniaturization is cited as another factor supporting demand, since it raises equipment requirements. Expanding supply to display customers forms a second pillar, but display investment can move separately from semiconductor investment, so the diversification effect may be limited.

Expansion into general industry, including secondary batteries, chemicals and pharmaceuticals, remains at an early stage and is a task for reducing reliance on the semiconductor investment cycle.

On competition, the company stresses that its localized dry vacuum pumps reduce dependence on overseas suppliers, but it still faces technology and service competition from leading global vacuum pump makers.

In the Korean semiconductor equipment sector, the company shares an investment cycle with peers such as Wonik IPS, PSK, Jusung Engineering and HANMI Semiconductor.

Outlook

The company's medium-term goal is to expand revenue by sustaining growth in semiconductors and displays while widening its business into general industrial applications. It has said it plans to raise the share of overseas and domestic customers and expand pump sales into general industry.

It has also set a goal of becoming a global solution provider in vacuum equipment by bundling related equipment.

On the results trend, revenue rose in the second and third quarters of 2026 after bottoming in the first quarter, and operating profit turned positive in the second quarter before easing slightly in the third. Future revenue depends on whether customers' equipment investment continues to recover.

Whether the announced medium-term plans lead to a change in the revenue mix is another point to check. The first checkpoint is whether the annual operating margin in the 2026 annual report turns positive from -3.3% in 2025. Whether the debt-to-equity ratio and operating cash flow stay at their current levels is also a point to check.

Whether the recovery is a one-off or sustained needs to be checked against future filings.

Risk factors

Revenue is directly tied to new equipment investment and order flow at major chip customers. Investment delays and lower orders reduced revenue and widened the operating loss in 2025. Whether the 2026 recovery rests on a limited set of investments can be checked against customer investment plans. Changes in those plans could widen quarterly swings.

Pace of diversification

If display investment moves differently from semiconductor investment, the diversification benefit could be limited. How quickly new segments grow as a share of revenue is central to this risk.
