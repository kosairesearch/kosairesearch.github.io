02

Business structure

Founded in 2000, NeopharmCO is a sensitive-skin cosmetics specialist built around its own brands, including Atopalm, Zeroid, Real Barrier and Dermavie. As of the end of August 2026, the sales mix was reported at 81% domestic and 19% exports.

Second-quarter 2026 sales by channel were KRW 12.3 billion online, KRW 22.6 billion offline and KRW 8.7 billion exports on a consolidated basis, according to an LS Securities report.

Exports rose 50.0% in the same quarter, with shipments to the United States up 65.1% and other regions up 89.0%, so the export base is broadening.

LS Securities estimated strong growth for Zeroid and higher Olive Young sales, and estimated that Real Barrier sales at Daiso rose sharply on wider SKU listings and store-wide ordering. The company's earnings commentary states that Atopalm has ranked first in the sensitive skincare segment for 20 consecutive years.

Marketing spend also increased, with the advertising and promotional expense ratio at 13.1% in the second quarter of 2026 versus 9.5% a year earlier. In September 2025, Yuanta Securities cited an expansion into medical aesthetics through domestic hospital distribution as a new business.

OEM sales have been shrinking, and Hana Securities said in its first-quarter 2025 analysis that this decline helped gross profit by shifting mix toward higher-margin own brands.

Competition centers on brand rivalry within the sensitive and dermo-cosmetics category, where the company overlaps with larger listed peers such as Amorepacific and LG H&H.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual revenue rose from KRW 85 billion in 2022 to KRW 97.1 billion in 2023 (up 14.2% year on year), KRW 119 billion in 2024 (up 22.6%) and KRW 132.2 billion in 2025 (up 11.1%), and it has increased for three consecutive years from 2023 through 2025.

Operating profit was KRW 21.4 billion in 2022, KRW 25.3 billion in 2023 (up 18.0%), KRW 24.7 billion in 2024 (down 2.6%) and KRW 28.8 billion in 2025 (up 16.7%).

In 2024 revenue grew 22.6% while operating profit declined, and the operating margin fell from 26.1% in 2023 to 20.7% in 2024 before recovering to 21.8% in 2025.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders rose from KRW 17 billion in 2022 to KRW 23.2 billion in 2023 (up 36.3%), eased to KRW 23.1 billion in 2024 (down 0.6%) and reached KRW 27.8 billion in 2025 (up 20.6%).

In the disclosed tables, net income for each year from 2022 through 2025 carries the same amount as net income attributable to controlling shareholders. Operating cash flow was KRW 18.2 billion in 2022, KRW 23.3 billion in 2023, KRW 18.6 billion in 2024 and KRW 28.4 billion in 2025.

The debt-to-equity ratio was 11.5% at the end of 2025, up from 9.2% in 2022, with total liabilities of KRW 22.5 billion and total equity of KRW 195.4 billion.

Quarterly revenue went from KRW 32.2 billion in the third quarter of 2025 to KRW 36.4 billion in the fourth quarter (up 13.0% quarter on quarter), then KRW 36.2 billion in the first quarter of 2026 (down 0.5%) and KRW 43.6 billion in the second quarter (up 20.3%).

Third-quarter 2026 revenue of KRW 39.4 billion fell 9.6% from the prior quarter but rose 22.3% from a year earlier. Operating profit rose for four consecutive quarters, from KRW 6.77 billion in the third quarter of 2025 to KRW 10 billion in the third quarter of 2026.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders peaked at KRW 8.62 billion in the first quarter of 2026, then fell for two consecutive quarters to KRW 8.41 billion in the second quarter and KRW 4.94 billion in the third quarter.

The gap between third-quarter operating profit and net income widened, and the preliminary pre-tax profit released on November 9, 2026 was KRW 6.319 billion. That preliminary figure was an internal settlement before external audit review and may change after review.

Because reports described the basis for quarterly results as either consolidated or separate after the subsidiary liquidation in the first quarter of 2026, quarter-on-quarter comparisons should account for any basis difference.

05

Industry analysis

NeopharmCO is a mid-sized cosmetics company anchored in the sensitive-skin and dermo-cosmetics category, with a brand scale and business structure that differ from the large listed peers in the sector.

In its August 2026 report, LS Securities said export growth and structural growth in the dermo-cosmetics category would sustain earnings momentum. On the channel side, LS Securities pointed to Olive Young and exports as the main drivers of high growth.

It estimated that Real Barrier sales at Daiso rose sharply on wider SKU listings and store-wide ordering. Hana Securities said in a 2025 analysis that overseas distribution was widening from an Amazon-centered base into offline retail.

Kolmar Korea and Cosmax operate mainly on an ODM and OEM basis, whereas NeopharmCO is increasing the weight of its own brands. Hana Securities also said in its first-quarter 2025 analysis that the shrinking OEM business, combined with growth in higher-margin brand sales, supported profitability.

The industry backdrop combines the growth of K-beauty exports with changes in domestic retail channels. For the company, the relative growth of domestic offline channels such as Olive Young and Daiso versus overseas online channels centered on Amazon determines its competitive position.

Atopalm carries the company's leadership in sensitive skincare, while medical aesthetics is the main candidate for category extension.

Top-line growth and channel diversification

Online sales of KRW 12.3 billion and offline sales of KRW 22.6 billion grew together, broadening the channel base.

Financial capacity and cash generation

LS Securities cited Olive Young and exports as drivers of high growth in the second quarter of 2026. Given that overseas sales were centered on Amazon, platform risk remains a factor to monitor.

Disclosure basis and preliminary figures

The third-quarter 2026 figures are preliminary and predate external audit review, so they may change after settlement. Following the liquidation of a subsidiary in the first quarter of 2026, reports described the basis for quarterly results as either consolidated or separate. Differences in basis affect how quarterly growth rates should be read.

Marketing and cost structure

The advertising and promotional expense ratio rose to 13.1% in the second quarter of 2026, adding cost pressure on the operating margin. If advertising efficiency declines, the margin could fall. The drop in the operating margin to 20.7% in 2024 is a past example of this risk.
