SDN: Adding ESS and AI data center projects to a solar core

After returning to an operating profit in 2025, SDN's quarterly results have swung between profit and loss through Q3 2026, so execution of its ESS, solar EPC and AIDC projects is the next checkpoint.

02

Business structure

SDN started in 1994 as an outboard engine maker and now runs an engine and shipbuilding division under a domestic distribution agreement with Honda Motor.

It entered solar in 2004 through the establishment of its renewable energy research institute and now covers solar power systems, modules and inverters, module manufacturing and development, EPC (engineering, procurement and construction), and plant maintenance.

The company produces modules under its own brand with carbon-grade ratings, while premium lines such as high-efficiency back-contact modules are sourced through partnerships with overseas manufacturers.

The solar unit is diversifying module price points and specifications to cope with volatility in the system marginal price (SMP).

The engine division supplies four-stroke, fuel-efficient outboard engines, genuine parts and marine accessories mainly to fishing, leisure and government vessel users, and has reportedly held the top share of Korea's small-vessel engine market for three consecutive years.

Fishing-use outboard engines generate replacement demand on a cycle of about five years, which underpins recurring demand. In 2026 the company has added ESS (energy storage systems) and AI data center (AIDC) infrastructure to its existing solar business as new growth pillars.

It is pursuing a 160 MW AIDC project using its own substation and a new site at the Gangjin Industrial Complex, and it was named preferred bidder for an ESS construction project of about KRW 30 billion.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual results changed direction after 2022. Revenue was KRW 104.5 billion in 2022, the largest year in the 2022 to 2025 window, and fell 27.5% to KRW 75.7 billion in 2023, the smallest year in that window. The company swung to an operating loss of KRW 7.65 billion in 2023, with an operating margin of -10.1%.

Revenue rose 11.5% to KRW 84.4 billion in 2024, but the operating loss of KRW 6.5 billion continued at a narrower level, with an operating margin of -7.7%. In 2025 revenue grew 3.7% to KRW 87.5 billion and operating profit of KRW 464 million turned the year profitable, with an operating margin of 0.5%.

Revenue has grown for two consecutive years, 2024 and 2025, after the 2023 low. On a net income basis attributable to controlling shareholders, the company posted a net loss of KRW 8.69 billion in 2023, a wider net loss of KRW 27.6 billion in 2024, and a narrower net loss of KRW 1.7 billion in 2025.

Total net income, which includes non-controlling interests, was a loss of KRW 28.5 billion in 2024, so the two measures differ. In 2025 the operating line turned positive but total net loss of KRW 2 billion remained, so operating profit did not carry through to net profit. Quarterly results were volatile.

After Q3 2025 revenue of KRW 26.1 billion, operating profit of KRW 1.76 billion and net income attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 3.62 billion, Q4 2025 revenue fell to KRW 22 billion with an operating loss of KRW 1.53 billion and a net loss attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 4.2 billion.

Q1 2026 revenue was KRW 17.2 billion, down 21.8% quarter on quarter, and Q2 2026 revenue rebounded to KRW 30 billion, up 74.4%, producing net income attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 796 million.

The company attributed the first-half improvement to lower selling and administrative expenses and lower non-operating costs.

Q3 2026 revenue was KRW 23 billion, down 12.1% year on year, and the operating result swung to a loss of KRW 185 million, while net income attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 3.04 billion. The net loss attributable to controlling shareholders for the latest four quarters totals KRW 1.27 billion.

The debt-to-equity ratio was 73.9% in 2022, 100.0% in 2023, 149.2% in 2024 and 103.6% in 2025, easing after the 2024 peak. Operating cash flow was negative in 2022 and 2023 before turning positive, at KRW 7.08 billion in 2024 and KRW 4.52 billion in 2025.

05

Industry analysis

SDN's core solar power business is exposed to volatility in the system marginal price (SMP). The company says it is diversifying module price points and specifications to respond to that volatility.

It is described as one of Korea's first-generation solar companies, covering module manufacturing through EPC and maintenance. Premium high-efficiency modules depend on partnerships with overseas manufacturers, so product competitiveness rests partly on those alliances.

In ESS, the company holds preferred bidder status for a construction project of about KRW 30 billion and plans to take part in the third 2026 ESS central contract market.

In AIDC infrastructure, securing power is the key variable, and the company's own substation at the Gangjin Industrial Complex is presented as its location advantage. A grid impact assessment is under way, and talks with domestic and overseas big tech firms, cloud service providers and AIDC operators are ongoing.

No contract signing has been announced yet. The engine and shipbuilding division is reported to rank first in Korea's small-vessel engine market, and fishing-use outboard engines generate replacement demand on a cycle of about five years.

The solar, ESS and marine engine businesses follow different demand cycles, which gives some diversification. SMP and policy variables in solar remain largely outside the company's control.

Compared with listed peers in the sector, SDN has spent a long stretch in loss-making territory, which makes earnings-based benchmarks hard to apply.

06

Outlook

The company forecasts that 2026 revenue will rise more than 20% from the prior year, citing its ESS preferred bidder selection for about KRW 30 billion and a 100 MW-class solar EPC push. Quarterly revenue in 2026 has ranged from KRW 17.2 billion to KRW 30 billion, and Q3 revenue came in below the prior-year quarter.

Against that pattern, a 20% full-year increase would require a clear rise in Q4 revenue. The company targets signing the main ESS construction contract by year-end 2026 and plans to start the project in earnest after signing and take part in the third 2026 ESS central contract market.

For the Unjeong-dong solar project in Gwangju, it expects project financing to be executed within 2026 after the city approves the implementation agreement, with cash inflow of about KRW 10 billion.

It is pushing memoranda of understanding with two or more local governments on the southern coast to sign solar EPC contracts for saltwater-affected farmland of 100 MW each.

The Gangjin AIDC project targets first construction in 2027 after a grid impact assessment, and the company is considering expanding the campus onto additional land it owns.

It is also reviewing a revaluation of owned assets, including the Gwangju plant, the Gangjin industrial complex site and its substation, which it presents as a way to improve financial structure and credit standing.

On shareholder returns, it has completed the cancellation of treasury shares worth about KRW 5.5 billion.

The next checkpoints are whether the main ESS contract is signed and the Unjeong-dong financing is executed within 2026, followed by whether full-year 2026 revenue growth in the annual report matches the company's forecast.

Bear factors

Prolonged losses and a return to quarterly losses

The company posted losses for three consecutive years from 2023 to 2025, and in Q4 2025 it recorded an operating loss of KRW 1.53 billion and a net loss attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 4.2 billion. Q3 2026 also swung to an operating loss of KRW 185 million compared with the prior-year quarter.

Revenue volatility and the gap to the annual forecast

Q3 2026 revenue was KRW 23 billion, down 12.1% from a year earlier. Reaching the 20% annual growth forecast would require a sharp rise in Q4 revenue. Revenue also fell 27.5% in 2023, which shows the top line can move sharply.

Execution stage and timing uncertainty of new projects

The ESS project is at preferred bidder stage, and the main contract has not yet been signed. The AIDC project is still undergoing a grid impact assessment and is in talks with big tech firms without a signed contract. The 2027 construction target could shift depending on contracting and permit progress.

Risk factors

Policy and price exposure

The solar business is exposed to swings in the system marginal price (SMP). The company is diversifying module price points and specifications, but it cannot fully avoid the effect of price moves on profitability. Policy changes could affect generation revenue or the pace of project permits.

Project execution risk

The AIDC project depends on a grid impact assessment and big tech contracts, and the ESS project still needs its main contract to be signed. Delays in these projects could affect the annual revenue forecast and the planned cash inflow of about KRW 10 billion. Large projects can see the timing of results slip because of financing and construction schedules.

Capital base and financial structure

Total equity was KRW 75.4 billion and total liabilities were KRW 78.1 billion at end-2025. The debt-to-equity ratio has eased, but liabilities remain close in size to equity.

The 2024 net loss attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 27.6 billion reduced the accumulated capital base, and how an asset revaluation will be reflected in financial ratios has not yet been determined.
