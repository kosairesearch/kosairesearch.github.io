02

Business structure

Dynamic Design is a tire mold specialist listed on KOSPI since March 2015, headquartered in Gwangju. Its core business is designing and manufacturing molds used to shape tires and supplying them to global tire makers.

Major customers include Pirelli and Kumho Tire, and the company said project orders from these customers increased substantially in 2025. By product mix, the company said the share of high-value passenger car (PC) and light truck (LT) products has risen.

Within KRW 80.2 billion in 2025 consolidated revenue, standalone (headquarters) revenue was reported at KRW 50.8 billion, up 29.5% year on year.

The company has a Romanian and a Chinese subsidiary abroad, and its Indonesian mold subsidiary is still in the stage of building a production base after new investment in 2025.

In the first half of 2026, standalone operating profit was reported at KRW 2.4 billion, while consolidated operating profit was KRW 660 million, and the company said delays in the Indonesian production subsidiary weighed on consolidated results.

The company cites products with its own patented technology and technical cooperation with global strategic customers as growth drivers. It also says expanding automated processes and standardized production is shortening manufacturing lead times.

Because mold orders depend on customer quality approval and repeat-production efficiency, stabilizing overseas bases is a key variable for consolidated profit.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual revenue went from KRW 58.2 billion in 2022 to KRW 67.1 billion in 2023 (up 15.3% year on year), fell to KRW 62.6 billion in 2024 (down 6.8%), and reached KRW 80.2 billion in 2025 (up 28.2%), the largest year of 2022 to 2025.

Operating losses widened from KRW 7.23 billion in 2022 to KRW 8.77 billion in 2023, then narrowed to KRW 2.55 billion in 2024 and KRW 570 million in 2025. The company has posted operating losses for four consecutive years from 2022 through 2025.

The operating margin improved from -12.4% in 2022 and -13.1% in 2023 to -4.1% in 2024 and -0.7% in 2025. Net loss attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 64.7 billion in 2022 and KRW 27.1 billion in 2023, narrowed to KRW 6.41 billion in 2024, and widened again to KRW 8.82 billion in 2025.

Net loss including non-controlling interests was KRW 8.88 billion in 2025, a different figure from the controlling-shareholder loss.

For 2025, the company attributed the operating loss partly to initial costs from new investment in its Indonesian mold subsidiary, and estimated that excluding that subsidiary's KRW 3.0 billion operating loss and KRW 1.1 billion of new-investment expense, operating profit would have been around KRW 3.5 billion.

On a quarterly basis, the company recorded an operating loss of KRW 91.02 million in the third quarter of 2025 and KRW 410 million in the fourth quarter, then an operating profit of KRW 1.12 billion in the first quarter of 2026.

Reports tied that first-quarter profit partly to a 6.9 percentage point drop in the standalone cost of sales ratio. The company then posted an operating loss of KRW 455 million in the second quarter of 2026 and KRW 908 million in the third quarter.

Reported cumulative consolidated figures for the first nine months of 2026 were revenue of KRW 70.7 billion and an operating loss of KRW 200 million, while cumulative standalone operating profit was KRW 2.3 billion, up sharply year on year.

Net loss attributable to controlling shareholders over the last four quarters totaled KRW 9.7 billion. The debt ratio rose to 166.1% at the end of 2025 from 100.3% in 2022, with total liabilities of KRW 73.3 billion and total equity of KRW 44.1 billion.

05

Industry analysis

Mold demand moves with the production plans of global tire makers and the launch schedules of new vehicles. A fire at a major domestic tire maker's plant in May 2026 was reported to have affected supply chains across automakers and tire producers.

In the same period, the company said diversified customers and overseas production bases helped it sustain revenue growth.

For the second half of 2026, mold order flow was expected to depend on production plans of automakers and tire makers, changes in tariff policy in major markets, and raw material prices and exchange rates.

Some industry observers see expanding electric and SUV launches and rising replacement-tire demand in emerging markets as possible drivers of mold demand.

Quarterly revenue moved from KRW 27 billion in the first quarter of 2026 to KRW 21.7 billion in the second and KRW 22 billion in the third, showing how order timing can cause quarter-to-quarter swings.

The company positions its high-value passenger car and light truck molds and patented-technology products as differentiators.

Its relative position in the industry is best read qualitatively, through customer diversification among global tire makers such as Pirelli and Kumho Tire and through the presence of overseas production bases.

Disclosures do not provide market share or profitability figures that compare it with peers on a like-for-like basis.

Outlook

The company set 2026 as the first year of returning to operating profit and said January 2026 revenue exceeded KRW 10 billion.

For the first half of 2026, it reported consolidated revenue of KRW 48.7 billion and an operating profit of KRW 660 million, and the external auditor's review of the half-year financial statements found no matters indicating the statements were not fairly presented in all material respects.

In a report on the third quarter, the company said it is focused on expanding equipment and stabilizing production at the Indonesian subsidiary, and plans further investment by the end of 2026 to raise local capacity and utilization.

Reports said a KRW 5.0 billion third-party allotment rights offering to the largest shareholder is under way. In February 2026, a disclosure announced a third-party allotment of 10 million common shares at an issue price of KRW 500 per share.

On the capital side, the company said its plan announced on May 6, 2026, which includes a 10-to-1 capital reduction and a transfer of capital reserves to retained earnings, would clear a standalone accumulated deficit of about KRW 152.2 billion and secure distributable reserves for future dividends.

On the listing side, the Korea Exchange granted a cure period through April 14, 2027, and the company said it is strengthening accounting transparency and internal controls while responding to the trading resumption process.

Some legal uncertainty has been resolved: a police complaint ended in a no-charge decision, and the Supreme Court's dismissal of appeals in the case involving former executives' embezzlement and tax-law violations, disclosed on October 6, 2026, finalized the second-instance ruling.
