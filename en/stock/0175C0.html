Report overview

A 5G private network vendor with mixed demand signals

02

Business structure

Eucast, founded in 2011, is an independent mobile network system manufacturer classified under the Korea Exchange sector of communications and broadcasting equipment manufacturing. Its main products are LTE and 5G communications equipment.

Its core is a full-stack Private 5G technology that covers 5G base stations, core networks and network operation software, all developed in-house. Its independent network build-out capability, which does not depend on external telecom networks, targets defense, disaster response and public infrastructure markets.

Its deployment history in the national disaster safety communication network and a shipyard-dedicated private network is cited as a technical reference. Press reports say the company points to ten years of commercial deployment as the basis for its technology.

It is also described as supplying 5G and Open RAN based equipment. Because demand is concentrated in public and special-purpose networks, revenue is likely to depend on project-level orders.

Against the large equipment market served by general mobile operators, the company frames its on-site customized independent network capability as the differentiator. Revenue for 2025 was KRW 5.93 billion.

03

Recent trends

The offering process began with an institutional book built from November 9 to 13, 2026. A total of 715 domestic and overseas institutions participated, producing a 173.42 to 1 subscription ratio, and 84.62% of participating institutions bid at or below the lower end of the indicative range of KRW 19,000 to KRW 23,000.

The indicative range had originally been KRW 21,000 to KRW 26,000 before being lowered, and the offer price was fixed at the lower end, KRW 19,000. The offering amount is KRW 15.2 billion, which is KRW 3.2 billion less than the KRW 18.4 billion maximum implied at the top of the range.

Lock-up commitments covered 0.57% of the shares requested, below the 40% threshold, so lead underwriter Eugene Investment & Securities will acquire 8,000 shares, or 1% of the offering, and hold them for six months.

The retail subscription ran from November 18 to 19, 2026 at about 0.5 to 1, the first shortfall in 3 years and 10 months, and Eugene Investment & Securities received 13,600 shares against an allocation of 160,000 shares.

Financials for 2025 (audited with a qualified opinion from Dowon Accounting Corporation, KRW-IFRS) show revenue of KRW 5.93 billion, up 43.9% year on year, an operating loss of KRW 1.83 billion and a net loss of KRW 3.56 billion, both wider than in the prior year.

For the first half of 2026 (January to June, reviewed by Dowon Accounting Corporation), revenue was KRW 2.24 billion, the operating loss KRW 1.68 billion and the net loss KRW 1.92 billion.

The 2024 figures are unaudited: revenue of KRW 4.12 billion, an operating loss of KRW 1.01 billion and a net loss of KRW 3.29 billion. Total equity at end-December 2025 was KRW -4.95 billion, a capital deficit.

04

Outlook

The listing date is November 30, 2026. According to the disclosed plan, net proceeds of KRW 14.6 billion are allocated to facilities (KRW 9.45 billion), operations (KRW 4.6 billion) and other uses (KRW 549 million).

According to IB Tomato, the offering size was set at the lower end of the band, and the company reportedly reduced production facility investment and allocated more funds to securing technology licenses.

The same outlet reported that the securities filing applied a 17.97 multiple to derive an appraised value of KRW 31,286 per share, and that the confirmed offer price is a 39.27% discount to that figure.

On the financial side, the first-half 2026 operating loss of KRW 1.68 billion is close to the full-year 2025 operating loss of KRW 1.83 billion. Total equity moved from KRW -4.95 billion at end-December 2025 to KRW 6.66 billion at end-June 2026, which marks a change in the capital structure.

According to Data Economy, the company presented a 2028 earnings target, and the question raised is how far its deployment history in the national disaster safety communication network and a shipyard-dedicated private network supports that target.

The largest shareholder's holdings are subject to a three-year lock-up, and a two-year joint holding commitment covers 17 friendly shareholders. Full-year 2026 results will be disclosed later, and the additional shares acquired by the lead underwriter are held for six months from listing.

05

Bull factors

Full-stack Private 5G technology and commercial track record

Eucast is described as holding full-stack technology that covers 5G base stations, core networks and network operation software, all developed in-house.

Its independent network capability, which does not rely on external telecom networks, matches demand from defense, disaster response and public infrastructure users that need stable communications.

Its deployments in the national disaster safety communication network and a shipyard-dedicated private network are cited as references. Press reports say ten years of commercial deployment underpin the technology claim.

Breadth of institutional participation

A total of 715 domestic and overseas institutions took part in the book, with a 173.42 to 1 ratio. Private-placement fund managers led at 59.67 to 1, discretionary investment firms recorded 57.41 to 1, and public-fund managers 34.82 to 1. Total shares requested were 104,049,000.

The lock-up ratio, which signals long-term holding intent, is a separate indicator from this subscription ratio.

Revenue growth and a shift in the balance sheet

Revenue for 2025 was KRW 5.93 billion, up 43.9% year on year. Total assets rose from KRW 6.98 billion at end-December 2025 to KRW 12.6 billion at end-June 2026, while total liabilities fell from KRW 11.9 billion to KRW 5.97 billion. Total equity turned positive, moving from KRW -4.95 billion to KRW 6.66 billion.

These are consolidated figures, and the end-June 2026 balance sheet was reviewed by Dowon Accounting Corporation.

06

Bear factors

Continuing losses and a wider loss in 2025

The 2025 operating loss was KRW 1.83 billion and the net loss KRW 3.56 billion, with both losses widening from the prior year. The 2024 net loss was KRW 3.29 billion on an unaudited basis. Losses have continued from 2024 through the first half of 2026, and the loss remains large relative to revenue.

The pace of losses in the first half of 2026

The first-half 2026 operating loss of KRW 1.68 billion is close to the full-year 2025 operating loss of KRW 1.83 billion. The first-half net loss of KRW 1.92 billion is more than half of the full-year 2025 net loss of KRW 3.56 billion.

Against first-half revenue of KRW 2.24 billion, the operating loss is large, so operating profitability has not yet reached break-even.

Weak signals from the offering

Institutions bidding at or below the lower end of the range accounted for 84.62% of participation, indicating limited price appetite. Lock-up commitments were 0.57% of shares requested, from 10 institutions, or 1.40% of participants, below the 40% threshold.

The retail subscription ratio was about 0.5 to 1, the first shortfall in 3 years and 10 months. Hankyung reported that retail investors also stayed away amid a view that the stock was overvalued.

07

Risk factors

Policy and public procurement

Because the business targets defense, disaster response and public infrastructure, the timing and scale of government and public agency orders, along with permit schedules, can shift when revenue is recognized.

With first-half 2026 revenue at KRW 2.24 billion, a delay or cancellation of a single large project could weigh heavily on quarterly results.

Investment execution and technology development

Reports say that the offering size was set at the bottom of the band, leading the company to cut production facility investment and allocate more funds to technology licenses.

Whether the 2028 earnings target built on its deployment history turns into commercial results will depend on these equipment and license outcomes. The timing and allocation of KRW 14.6 billion in net proceeds could determine how fast the plan moves.

Governance and lock-up expiry

The largest shareholder's 1,186,472 shares are subject to a three-year lock-up, and 17 friendly shareholders entered a two-year joint holding commitment. The post-listing stake of CEO Kim Jae-hyung is 16.65% per the listing notice, while press reports cite 16.56%.

Changes in the largest shareholder's holdings after the lock-up period could affect supply and governance stability.

08

Overall view

Eucast points to its full-stack private 5G technology and an institutional subscription ratio of 173.42 to 1 as strengths. Against this, losses have continued from 2024 through the first half of 2026, and the company had a capital deficit at end-December 2025.

The offer price was fixed at the lower end of the range and the offering amount was reduced to KRW 15.2 billion. Lock-up commitments of 0.57% and the retail subscription shortfall read as mixed signals on the quality of demand.

The balance sheet shows a visible change, with total equity turning positive at KRW 6.66 billion at end-June 2026. However, given the first-half 2026 operating loss of KRW 1.68 billion, the recovery in profitability will be measured through full-year results.

The deployment of KRW 14.6 billion in net proceeds and progress toward the 2028 earnings target are the most direct items for subsequent disclosures.
