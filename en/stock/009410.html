02

Business structure

Taeyoung Engineering & Construction applied for a workout in December 2023 after a default risk, and reached a debt-restructuring and asset-sale agreement with its creditor banks in October 2024, according to Dealsite's April 2026 coverage.

The business mix is moving from private development toward public works, social infrastructure (SOC) and urban redevelopment, as reported by Newsway in June 2026.

Major 2026 contracts include a Pocheon pumped-storage power plant civil works deal worth KRW 95.95 billion with a state-run hydro and nuclear utility, signed March 27, 2026, and a West Busan administrative complex construction deal worth KRW 120.82 billion with a Busan city development corporation, signed June 5, 2026.

The West Busan contract reflects a 41.74% company stake in a consortium structure. A public housing project worth KRW 77.94 billion with a state housing corporation was signed July 2, 2026, and a KRW 67.5 billion waste-to-energy facility upgrade in Suwon with a state environmental agency was signed July 16, 2026.

The company's press page also lists a KRW 218.2 billion expressway contract in Gwacheon from June 2026. The company continues apartment sales under its Desian brand, but said in September 2026 that housing-market weakness pushed sales revenue into negative territory. Management is now led by Lee Kang-seok as sole CEO following the March 2026 shareholder meeting.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual revenue rose from KRW 2.61 trillion in 2022 to a peak of KRW 3.35 trillion in 2023, then declined 19.9% to KRW 2.69 trillion in 2024 and 19.0% to KRW 2.17 trillion in 2025, marking two consecutive annual declines from 2024 through 2025. Among 2022 to 2025, 2023 was the largest year and 2025 the smallest.

Operating profit moved from KRW 91.5 billion in 2022 to an operating loss of KRW 404.5 billion in 2023, a swing back to profit at KRW 20.6 billion in 2024, and KRW 52.8 billion in 2025, up 155.7%. Operating margins were 3.5% in 2022, -12.1% in 2023, 0.8% in 2024 and 2.4% in 2025.

The 2023 loss traces to real-estate project financing problems; at the time of the workout filing, project financing loans stood at KRW 3.2 trillion and capital impairment at KRW 561.7 billion, according to Nate News reporting in September 2026.

The 2025 margin improvement came from cost control, with Dealsite reporting in April 2026 a gross margin of 8.7%, up 1.8 percentage points, and cost of sales of KRW 1.99 trillion, down about 20.6%.

Attributable net income was KRW 63.8 billion in 2025, up 82.5%, while total net income including non-controlling interests was KRW 95.8 billion, a different figure.

Quarterly operating profit went from an operating loss of KRW 5.77 billion in the third quarter of 2025 to KRW 13.2 billion in the fourth quarter, KRW 17.7 billion in the first quarter of 2026, KRW 37.2 billion in the second quarter (up 109.6% from the prior quarter) and KRW 17.6 billion in the third quarter (down 52.5% from the prior quarter).

Third-quarter 2026 revenue of KRW 348 billion fell 31.5% from a year earlier.

Attributable net income dropped from KRW 61 billion in the fourth quarter of 2025 to KRW 1.96 billion in the first quarter of 2026, then turned into an attributable net loss of KRW 7.7 billion in the second quarter and KRW 35.8 billion in the third quarter.

The sum of attributable net income over the last four quarters is KRW 19.5 billion. A Nate News report said first-half 2026 operating profit rose while financing costs of KRW 70.4 billion kept the net result in loss.

05

Industry analysis

A prolonged slump in private housing and a contraction in project financing (PF) have raised uncertainty across the construction sector, according to Newsway's June 2026 coverage.

Under these conditions Taeyoung has raised the weight of public works, social infrastructure and redevelopment projects, which it considers relatively stable. The company said in September 2026 that housing-market weakness pushed sales revenue negative, while expanded public contracts improved profitability.

Public procurement carries lower project uncertainty and steadier cash flow, though the volume of public work is limited. Competition for public contracts has also intensified, making selective bidding for profitable projects the key issue, as Smart Business reported in April 2026.

On PF clean-up, as of late May 2026, 24 of 37 continuing projects had been completed, six of 23 projects slated for restructuring had changed contractors and two had been liquidated, according to Newsdream in July 2026. The industry is therefore still working through legacy private development losses.

The debt ratio has fallen sharply from 1,154.2% at the time of the workout filing but remains above the industry average, per Smart Business in April 2026.

Industry observers expect the company to build cost-control and project-selection capabilities during the workout before widening private business after normalization, as Global Economic reported in August 2026.

Regional conditions differ, with one company press item citing a 40% drop in unsold homes in Gumi over the year to September 2026.

06

Outlook

The workout performance agreement runs through May 30, 2027, according to Newsway's June 2026 report. The maturity of unsecured borrowings excluded from the debt-to-equity conversion also falls on May 30, 2027, making that date the reference point for the exit decision, per a November 2026 Bloter report.

Debt-to-equity conversions totaling KRW 101.1 billion were approved over three months starting August 2026, reducing workout debt. However, unsecured borrowings outside those conversions mature in May 2027, and operating cash flow showed a net outflow of KRW 69.7 billion in the first half of 2026.

The asset-sale target is KRW 757.8 billion, with cumulative sales of KRW 421.5 billion through the prior year. The creditor-side expectations reported by Newsdream in July 2026 cited a debt ratio of 209.1% at year-end 2026 and 196.6% in 2027, against 337.5% on a standalone basis in the first quarter of 2026.

Coverage says the final creditor decision will depend on cash flow and refinancing results in the second half of 2026 and the first quarter of 2027. A full graduation remains possible, but a staged exit or a limited extension of the agreement is viewed as more likely.

The company plans to expand new contracts in public works, SOC and redevelopment during the second half of 2026. How far it can rebuild private business after normalization remains an open task.

08

Bull factors

Operating profit recovery

Operating profit reached KRW 52.8 billion in 2025, up 155.7%, with a 2.4% operating margin. After a swing from an operating loss of KRW 404.5 billion in 2023 to KRW 20.6 billion in 2024, profit scale expanded further.

First-quarter 2026 operating profit of KRW 17.7 billion and second-quarter profit of KRW 37.2 billion show continued earnings generation on a quarterly basis.

Balance sheet normalization

The debt ratio improved from -1,154.1% in 2023 to 720.2% in 2024 and 542.0% in 2025. Borrowings and bonds fell from KRW 1.52 trillion at the end of 2025 to KRW 1.25 trillion at the end of March 2026, according to Newsway's June 2026 report.

External creditors' debt-to-equity conversions have reached KRW 334.9 billion cumulatively, per Bloter reporting.

Expansion of public-sector orders

Since March 2026, disclosed contracts include a Pocheon power plant worth KRW 95.95 billion, a West Busan complex worth KRW 120.82 billion, an LH public housing project worth KRW 77.94 billion and a Suwon facility upgrade worth KRW 67.5 billion.

Public work is seen as carrying lower project uncertainty and steadier cash flow than private development. Commentary also notes that public works do not offer profitability far above private development.

Bear factors

Continued revenue contraction

Revenue was KRW 2.17 trillion in 2025, down 19.0%. Third-quarter 2026 revenue of KRW 348 billion fell 31.5% from a year earlier. First-half 2026 revenue was reported down about 44% year over year, per Global Economic's August 2026 coverage. Negative sales revenue from housing and the shrinking private business are cited as the causes.

Two recent quarters of losses

Attributable net losses were KRW 7.7 billion in the second quarter and KRW 35.8 billion in the third quarter of 2026, making two straight loss-making quarters. Operating profit over the same period fell from KRW 37.2 billion to KRW 17.6 billion.

First-half 2026 financing costs were reported at KRW 70.4 billion, and interest burden continues to weigh on earnings, per Nate News in September 2026.

Residual PF guarantee exposure

Guarantee limits for PF and SOC projects declined from KRW 12.84 trillion to KRW 10.39 trillion but remain in the double-digit trillions, per Smart Business in April 2026. Asset sales reached KRW 421.5 billion through 2025 against a KRW 757.8 billion target. Creditor-bank assessments noted that operating cash flow and asset sales missed targets in both 2024 and 2025.

Risk factors

Workout exit or extension uncertainty

The final creditor decision will depend on cash flow and refinancing results in the second half of 2026 and the first quarter of 2027. With unsecured borrowings maturing May 30, 2027, failing to refinance on time would raise liquidity pressure.

Financial-sector observers have also raised the possibility of requesting an extension instead of an exit, per Nate News in September 2026.

Parent company funding strain

Parent company TY Holdings reported consolidated borrowings of KRW 260.2 billion at the end of March 2026, about 97% classified as current liquidity borrowings, and a current ratio of 51.4%, lower than the company's, per Newsdream in July 2026. Its funding position may affect how much support can flow into the subsidiary during normalization.

Market conditions and public-bid competition

Weak private housing and a contraction in PF have pushed sales revenue into negative territory. Public work volume is limited and bidding competition has intensified, per Smart Business in April 2026. Even with more contracts, a rise in cost ratios could reverse the profitability recovery.

Overall view

Taeyoung Engineering & Construction restored operating profit to KRW 52.8 billion in 2025, a 2.4% margin, and cut its debt ratio from -1,154.1% in 2023 to 542.0% in 2025. Revenue has fallen since its 2023 peak, and the company is building a profitability-focused business around public-sector contracts.

Against that, attributable net losses in the second and third quarters of 2026 make two straight loss-making quarters. Positives include balance-sheet repair and expanded public orders, while negatives include shrinking sales, financing costs and parent-company funding strain.

The workout performance agreement and the maturity of unsecured borrowings both fall on May 30, 2027, so the exit-or-extension decision will be concentrated around that date.
