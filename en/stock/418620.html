02

Business structure

E8 is a physical AI operations platform company that combines digital twin technology with ontology, a method for structuring the meaning and relationships of data. It listed on KOSDAQ in February 2024.

Its product chain runs from NDX Pro for physical replication and NFLOW Ai for prediction to NAXiS for data structuring and NAX Ops for operational decisions, and NAXiS and NAX Ops were formally launched in the first half of 2026.

Alongside its existing build-type business, the company says it intends to raise the share of platform and service-based business. Its August 2026 value-up plan describes a no-code AI agent platform meant to cut staffing per project and allow repeated sales of the same product.

Target sectors are manufacturing, energy, cities and infrastructure, and distribution is widening through solution partners in building safety and telecom construction.

A digital twin BIM data contract signed on November 5, 2026 is a subcontract under a project led by LG CNS, worth KRW 199 million, equal to 12.02% of 2025 revenue. The company says it has performed similar work for LG CNS over the past three years. The current structure places a large SI firm as prime contractor and a specialist firm as data producer.

04

Earnings analysis

On a DART basis, revenue rose from KRW 309 million in 2022 to KRW 3.67 billion in 2023, an increase of 1,085.7% year on year, making 2023 the largest year of 2022 to 2025. Revenue then fell 37.6% to KRW 2.29 billion in 2024 and 27.6% to KRW 1.66 billion in 2025, a decline in each of 2024 and 2025.

The operating loss narrowed from KRW 7.71 billion in 2022 to KRW 5.25 billion in 2023, then widened to KRW 10.6 billion in 2024 and KRW 11.2 billion in 2025. Even in 2023, the peak revenue year, an operating loss of KRW 5.25 billion remained, and the loss grew as revenue shrank afterward.

As a result, the operating margin moved from -143.2% in 2023 to -463.8% in 2024 and -679.3% in 2025. The loss attributable to owners of the parent was KRW 8.12 billion in 2022 and KRW 11.4 billion in 2025, a loss in each of the four years from 2022 to 2025, and the net loss figures follow the same path.

Operating cash flow was negative every year, at -KRW 5.95 billion in 2022, -KRW 4.73 billion in 2023, -KRW 11.3 billion in 2024 and -KRW 10.1 billion in 2025. The debt ratio was negative at -525.4% in 2022 and -185.2% in 2023, then reached 247.7% in 2024 and 76.9% in 2025.

At the end of 2025, total equity was KRW 14.6 billion, total liabilities were KRW 11.2 billion, and equity attributable to owners of the parent was also KRW 14.6 billion. Quarterly, revenue went from KRW 443 million in Q3 2025 to KRW 280 million in Q4 2025 as the operating loss widened to KRW 3.25 billion.

From Q1 2026 to Q3 2026, the operating loss narrowed each quarter versus the prior quarter, to KRW 2.66 billion, KRW 2.52 billion and KRW 1.66 billion. The loss attributable to owners over the last four quarters totaled KRW 10.3 billion.

Quarterly revenue has moved between KRW 280 million and KRW 793 million, while the operating loss has stayed between about KRW 1.7 billion and KRW 3.3 billion, so the revenue swings have translated into limited profit improvement so far.

05

Industry analysis

The company says demand is rising in manufacturing, energy, cities and infrastructure for turning AI analysis into operating decisions and actions.

In its view, as simulation and prediction technology becomes common, corporate interest is shifting from how accurately to predict toward how to reflect predictions in operations. Whether this becomes business shows up in project contract size and the number of commercial contracts converted.

The company describes its model as moving from build-type projects toward platform sales. Within the IT and software sector, Samsung SDS, LG CNS and Hyundai Autoever are large IT services firms with much larger SI capabilities and financial bases.

E8 is a small firm specialized in ontology and digital twins, and it takes part in projects where a large SI firm holds the prime contract, as in the BIM subcontract. Its strengths are its own platform lineup and participation in national R&D projects that the company says internalize technology.

Its weakness is a smaller scale and reference base than large rivals. The company envisions a model in which the platform works alongside large SI firms as partners rather than only competing with them.

06

Outlook

The company has set three targets: a quarterly break-even in Q4 2026, a break-even for full-year 2027, and KRW 10 billion of contract backlog at the end of 2026.

The company cites the no-code AI agent platform, technology internalization through national R&D projects, and a shift to main contracts with large corporations as grounds for a positive view on the Q4 2026 break-even.

In February 2026 the company said projects based on NAXiS and NAX Ops were under way with some firms and that it aimed to convert them into commercial contracts within 2026.

On November 9, 2026, the company said six national research projects had brought government R&D grants of KRW 16.2 billion, including a Ministry of the Interior and Safety disaster safety project in which E8 will build AI-based wildfire detection and prediction and a digital twin integrated control system.

The LG CNS-linked BIM contract lists final inspection on April 10, 2028, so payments are staged over that period. Grants are spent over the research period and do not map directly to income statement revenue.

The checkpoints are whether the Q4 2026 quarterly break-even is reached and whether the backlog target is met, both of which can be confirmed through periodic reports and disclosures.

08

Bull factors

A no-code platform aimed at changing the revenue model

The company says a no-code, no-tuning AI agent platform would cut staffing per project and allow repeated sales of the same product. Unlike build-type projects, a product sold to many customers could leave room for operating margin improvement, which is the company's argument.

Whether NAXiS and NAX Ops, launched in the first half of 2026, convert into commercial contracts is the point that will test this argument.

Improved balance sheet and net cash

The company said in an August 2026 value-up plan that it had reduced its financial risk through a rights offering and CB redemption, securing net cash of KRW 8.8 billion.

The debt ratio fell from 247.7% in 2024 to 76.9% in 2025, and total equity at the end of 2025 of KRW 14.6 billion exceeded total liabilities of KRW 11.2 billion. Lower financial risk widens the room for business investment and staffing.

National R&D wins and public-sector demand

On November 9, 2026, the company said six national research projects had secured government R&D grants of KRW 16.2 billion, which it described as the largest in its history.

In a Ministry of the Interior and Safety project, E8 is responsible for AI-based wildfire detection and prediction and a digital twin control system. The company argues that a track record in public and disaster-safety work could extend its references into manufacturing and infrastructure sites.

Bear factors

Continuing operating losses and a larger loss

The 2025 operating loss of KRW 11.2 billion was larger than the KRW 10.6 billion of 2024, and 2025 revenue was only KRW 1.66 billion. Operating cash flow was also negative at -KRW 10.1 billion in 2025.

The loss attributable to owners over the last four quarters totaled KRW 10.3 billion, and although the Q3 2026 operating loss narrowed to KRW 1.66 billion, it remains a loss.

Unstable revenue base

Revenue peaked at KRW 3.67 billion in 2023 and fell in both 2024 and 2025. Quarterly revenue swung from KRW 280 million in Q4 2025 to KRW 793 million in Q2 2026, then dropped 25.7% from the prior quarter to KRW 589 million in Q3 2026. Large quarter-to-quarter swings make the earnings trend hard to read in one direction.

Ownership change and market warning designations

A September 2026 third-party rights offering changed the largest shareholder to an investment partnership, and the stake of the former largest shareholder group fell from 15.43% to 11.88%.

The company is under an administrative issue designation, and a one-day investment caution designation was announced for November 16, 2026, citing concentrated trading in small accounts. Ownership change and market warning designations overlap in the same period.

Risk factors

Funding and dilution

Although the company improved its finances through a rights offering and CB redemption, further fundraising remains possible while losses continue. Given operating cash flow of -KRW 10.1 billion in 2025, the funding need is not small.

The shares issued in September 2026 are subject to a one-year lock-up, so the tradable share count will rise when the lock-up ends.

Commercialization and order uncertainty

If commercial conversion of the platform and the Q4 2026 break-even target are not met, the improvement in the earnings structure will be delayed. Among recent contract disclosures, the Sejong BIM project is a subcontract worth KRW 199 million. A large project contract has not yet appeared in disclosures.

Trading rules and grant execution

The administrative issue designation continues, and a one-day investment caution designation was announced for November 16, 2026. Stocks with high trading volatility can see repeated trading restrictions and sharp price moves.

Government R&D grants are spent according to the execution period and performance reviews, so their timing can differ from revenue recognition.

Overall view

E8 is a physical AI operations platform company built on digital twin and ontology technology, and its DART-reported results still show a loss structure. Revenue peaked at KRW 3.67 billion in 2023 and fell to KRW 1.66 billion in 2025, while the operating loss widened to KRW 11.2 billion in 2025.

However, the quarterly operating loss narrowed versus the prior quarter in each quarter from Q1 2026 through Q3 2026, reaching KRW 1.66 billion in Q3 2026.

The September 2026 rights offering and change of largest shareholder altered both the capital structure and the ownership, and the debt ratio at the end of 2025 was 76.9%, lower than in 2024. The Q4 2026 break-even and KRW 10 billion backlog targets the company has set are not yet confirmed.

Strengths include the platform shift, a steadier balance sheet and national R&D wins, while weaknesses include continuing losses, revenue volatility, and ownership change alongside market warning designations. Because these factors overlap in the same period, the direction is hard to call. The next checkpoint is the 2026 annual report, which will cover Q4 2026 results.
