LG HelloVision: Shrinking Revenue and Profit Under Restructuring

LG HelloVision returned to a net profit attributable to owners in 2025, but its operating profit fell to KRW 222 million in the third quarter of 2026 as non-broadcast businesses shrank and a carriage dispute with CJ ENM escalated.

02

Business structure

LG HelloVision operates a home business built on cable system operator (SO) broadcasting, internet, and MVNO services, with rental and regional businesses alongside it. Of the third-quarter 2026 operating revenue of KRW 226.2 billion, the home business accounted for KRW 208.9 billion.

Within it, broadcasting contributed KRW 120.4 billion, internet KRW 33.1 billion, MVNO KRW 35.3 billion, and rental KRW 20.1 billion. The regional business, which includes media and B2B services, produced KRW 16.9 billion and other businesses KRW 0.4 billion, according to the company's third-quarter 2026 release.

MVNO operators sell their own-brand mobile services over the networks of the big three carriers and are generally priced below them. Broadcasting is focused on defending subscribers in a shrinking pay-TV market, and its third-quarter 2026 revenue fell 1.9% year on year.

Rental was built as a diversification engine around appliance subscriptions, but intensifying competition in home appliances and the pruning of low-margin sales channels caused revenue to drop sharply.

Samsung Electronics and LG Electronics strengthening their own appliance subscription businesses is also cited as reshaping the rental competitive landscape. The regional business includes local culture and EV charging operations, and its revenue also declined in the third quarter of 2026.

Broadcasting and internet compete with IPTV operators, and the cable SO subscriber base is shrinking as the market reorganizes around IPTV.

04

Earnings analysis

On a reported annual basis, revenue rose from KRW 1.17 trillion in 2022 to KRW 1.19 trillion in 2023 (up 1.9% year on year), KRW 1.2 trillion in 2024 (up 0.5%), and KRW 1.27 trillion in 2025 (up 5.8%).

Revenue has increased for three consecutive years since 2023, and 2025 was the largest year of 2022 to 2025 while 2022 was the smallest.

Operating profit went from KRW 53.8 billion in 2022 to KRW 47.4 billion in 2023 (down 12.0%) and KRW 13.5 billion in 2024 (down 71.5%), before rebounding to KRW 18.7 billion in 2025 (up 39.0%). Over the same span, 2022 was the largest operating profit year and 2024 the smallest.

The operating margin slid from 4.6% in 2022 to 4.0% in 2023 and 1.1% in 2024, then edged back to 1.5% in 2025. Analysts attribute the gap between rising revenue and thinner margins to slow profitability gains in newer businesses that expanded the top line.

Net income attributable to owners of the parent was a loss of KRW 26 billion in 2022, a loss of KRW 45.4 billion in 2023, and a loss of KRW 106.2 billion in 2024, before turning to a profit of KRW 1.78 billion in 2025.

Net income including non-controlling interests followed the same path, with losses from 2022 to 2024 and a KRW 1.78 billion profit in 2025.

The sharp widening of the 2024 loss relative to the drop in operating profit points to non-operating items, and the detailed components need to be confirmed in the periodic filings.

At the end of 2025, total equity stood at KRW 452.7 billion and total liabilities at KRW 813.2 billion, with the debt ratio rising from 128.3% in 2022 to 133.4% in 2023, 180.0% in 2024, and 179.6% in 2025.

Operating cash flow fell from KRW 179 billion in 2022 to KRW 84.4 billion in 2023 and KRW 87.9 billion in 2024, then recovered to KRW 121.1 billion in 2025.

Quarterly revenue peaked at KRW 299.5 billion in the fourth quarter of 2025 and then declined over three straight quarters to KRW 255.4 billion, KRW 243.3 billion, and KRW 226.2 billion in the first three quarters of 2026.

Operating profit peaked at KRW 5.1 billion in the first quarter of 2026 and then fell over two straight quarters to KRW 2.97 billion and KRW 222 million.

The company attributed the third-quarter drop to slowing industry conditions and costs tied to business restructuring, and industry reports put third-quarter EBITDA at KRW 25.2 billion, down 27.5%.

Operating costs fell 21.9% to KRW 226.0 billion, but the decline was smaller than the 24.2% revenue drop, which worsened profitability.

05

Industry analysis

According to the Korea Media and Communications Commission, pay-TV subscribers across IPTV, cable SO, and satellite platforms totaled about 36.15 million in the second half of 2025, down 76,030 from the prior half.

The market's center of gravity is shifting toward IPTV, and cable operators' subscriber base is shrinking alongside it. The company points to the spread of OTT services and lower VOD usage as the main drivers of change in broadcasting. Content costs add to the pressure.

LG HelloVision is in a legal dispute with CJ ENM over program fees; the company says CJ ENM rejected arbitration and is demanding fees at the existing level. CJ ENM and other program providers, by contrast, say pay-TV operators offered phased fee cuts and channel rearrangements that were not accepted.

While the two sides' accounts diverge, a broadcast channel association has asked the regulator to reconsider its acceptance of the channel-termination filing. In rentals, expanded in-house appliance subscription programs from Samsung Electronics and LG Electronics have intensified competition.

The Korea Exchange's first list of low price-to-book companies, published on November 2, 2026, included 151 firms, and LG HelloVision was one of only two companies in the communication services sector on the KOSPI market on that list, alongside KT Skylife.

Compared with the big three telecom operators in the same sector, the designation reflects the market's low assessment of growth in the core broadcasting business.

06

Outlook

In its third-quarter 2026 release, the company said it is focusing on stabilizing its cost structure and improving profitability through a recovery in core competitiveness and higher-quality growth. The concrete vehicle is a restructuring of the portfolio toward profit-focused rental and corporate businesses.

The company has ended its local-specialty online mall and cultural space operations, and it has pruned low-margin rental sales channels. Fixed-cost reduction through an office relocation and slimmer headcount with fewer new hires are running in parallel.

The company held its first voluntary retirement program since its founding in 2024, followed by further workforce efficiency steps in 2025 and 2026, when applications were opened to all active employees. Brokerage views diverge.

In a November 2026 report, SK Securities analyst Choi Kwan-soon expected an operating loss in the fourth quarter of 2026 given ongoing restructuring and voluntary retirement costs, and said the benefits of the portfolio shift and lower fixed costs would become visible from 2027.

NH Investment & Securities expects an operating loss for 2026 but projects a return to operating profit in 2027 on cost-cutting effects. Dividends have been suspended since fiscal year 2024 and were not resumed for fiscal year 2025, leaving the timing of a restart an open question.

On the broadcasting side, four CJ ENM channels, tvN SPORTS, tvN SHOW, Tooniverse, and Chinese TV, are scheduled to stop on November 26, 2026.

The Korea Media and Communications Commission says it will check whether protective steps, including advance notice to users and replacement channels such as ENTV, ENA Sports, Animax, and Asia N, are carried out.

The next hearing in the lawsuit between the two companies is set for December 11, 2026, and the arbitration process has been suspended.

Bull factors

Cost structure work underway

Third-quarter 2026 operating costs fell 21.9% year on year to KRW 226.0 billion. Voluntary retirement programs have run three years in a row, two low-yield operations were shut down, and an office move lowered fixed costs. The company frames these steps as reducing its cost base.

Brokers see a 2027 return to profit

NH Investment & Securities expects an operating loss for 2026 but a return to operating profit in 2027. SK Securities expects the portfolio restructuring and lower fixed-cost burden to show their effects from 2027. Both views rest on the cumulative effect of cost reductions.

2025 turnaround and cash flow recovery

Net income attributable to owners of the parent reached KRW 1.78 billion in 2025, turning positive after losses from 2022 to 2024. Operating cash flow rose from KRW 87.9 billion in 2024 to KRW 121.1 billion in 2025. Annual revenue has grown for three consecutive years since 2023.

Bear factors

Shrinking core broadcast base

Pay-TV subscribers fell by 76,030 in the second half of 2025 from the prior half. Third-quarter 2026 broadcasting revenue of KRW 120.4 billion fell 1.9% year on year, and MVNO revenue fell 8.9%. With OTT adoption and lower VOD usage both weighing, evidence for a core-business rebound is thin.

Sharp drop in rental and regional businesses

Third-quarter 2026 rental revenue fell 59.4% to KRW 20.1 billion, and regional-business revenue fell 68.1% to KRW 16.9 billion. By Deal Site's tally, these two lines accounted for 90.3% of the total revenue decline.

Expanding subscription programs by appliance makers are cited as a main cause, so the competitive picture may not change quickly.

Balance sheet strain and dividend gap

The debt ratio stood at 179.6% at the end of 2025, well above 128.3% in 2022. Third-quarter 2026 operating profit shrank to KRW 222 million. Dividends have been suspended since fiscal year 2024 and not resumed for fiscal year 2025. Room in both profitability and the balance sheet has narrowed.

Risk factors

Carriage dispute and regulatory risk

Four CJ ENM channels are scheduled to stop on November 26, 2026. The regulator accepted the terms-of-service change filing, and a channel association has demanded a review. The next lawsuit hearing is December 11, 2026, and subscriber losses and the adoption of replacement channels could affect results.

Financial and profitability risk

Total liabilities at the end of 2025 were KRW 813.2 billion against total equity of KRW 452.7 billion. The third-quarter 2026 operating margin dropped to 0.1%. SK Securities expects an operating loss in the fourth quarter of 2026 due to voluntary retirement and restructuring costs. If earnings recovery is delayed under debt burdens, financial room could narrow further.

Content cost and industry risk

The fee dispute between program providers and SO operators has moved into litigation. If fees stay at prior levels, broadcasting cost pressure keeps accumulating. As the whole pay-TV market reorganizes around IPTV, the subscriber base of cable operators continues to shrink.

12

Overall view

LG HelloVision showed positive signals in 2025, with a return to net profit attributable to owners and a recovery in operating cash flow.

In 2026, however, revenue has fallen for three straight quarters, and operating profit has declined for two straight quarters after peaking in the first quarter, reaching just KRW 222 million in the third quarter.

Sharp drops in rental and regional businesses account for most of the revenue decline, while the core broadcasting business faces limited growth as its subscriber base shrinks.

The company is pushing ahead with cost restructuring and portfolio changes, and brokers are split between a 2027 return to profit and an expected fourth-quarter operating loss. On the balance sheet, the debt ratio stands at 179.6%, above the 128.3% of 2022, and dividends have been suspended since fiscal year 2024.

The channel dispute is moving toward the November 26 channel end and the December 11 hearing, and its outcome will be an indicator of the core broadcasting outlook.

Overall, the company sits in a phase where recovery potential and further downside pressures coexist, and the next checkpoints are the annual results in the 2026 business report and the first-quarter 2027 results.
