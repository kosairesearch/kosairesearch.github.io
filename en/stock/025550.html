02

Business structure

Hankuk Steel Wire was founded in 1974 under an earlier Korean name and listed on the KOSDAQ in June 1995 as a primary steel manufacturer. Its core products are steel wire rod, including galvanized steel wire and stainless steel wire, sold in three groups: mild steel wire, hard steel wire and stainless steel wire.

Mild steel wire goes into welded wire mesh, wire mesh, crimp mesh and nails. Hard steel wire, after heat treatment, cold working and hot-dip galvanizing, is used for wire rope, agricultural materials, submarine cable, electric wire and clothing.

Stainless steel wire ranges from ultra-fine to heavy gauge and serves weaving mesh, springs and welding rod uses. The company states on its website that its galvanized steel wire holds the number one share in the domestic and Japanese markets, and that its stainless weaving wire is exported in volume to Japan.

Its consolidated subsidiaries include a pipe-fitting and valve maker for instrumentation, a plant valve manufacturer and an asset manager.

According to a company profile service, fitting and valve revenue rose in the first half of 2026 on certifications from Middle East and Southeast Asian energy companies and participation in domestic petrochemical projects.

Wire earnings depend on steel raw material costs and selling-price negotiations, while valve and fitting earnings depend on which projects the company joins, so the two businesses have different profit drivers. Listed steel and metal peers for comparison include Kiswire, Dongkuk Steel Mill and SeAH Steel.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual revenue rose from KRW 245.5 billion in 2022 to KRW 248.4 billion in 2023 (up 1.2% year on year), then fell to KRW 223.4 billion in 2024 (down 10.1%) and KRW 219.4 billion in 2025 (down 1.8%).

Operating profit fell from KRW 11.3 billion in 2022 to KRW 5.12 billion in 2023 (down 54.6%), the low point, then rose to KRW 6.03 billion in 2024 (up 17.7%) and KRW 8.17 billion in 2025 (up 35.6%), two consecutive years of increases.

The operating margin was 4.6% in 2022, 2.1% in 2023, 2.7% in 2024 and 3.7% in 2025, so margins recovered after 2024 even as revenue declined. The company attributed the 2025 improvement to a lower cost-of-sales ratio, higher financial income and lower other non-operating expenses.

Profit attributable to controlling shareholders went from KRW 13.8 billion in 2022 to a KRW 2.33 billion loss in 2023 (a swing from profit to loss), a KRW 1.23 billion loss in 2024 (losses continued at a smaller size), and a KRW 327 million profit in 2025 (a turn to profit).

Net profit including non-controlling interests was KRW 15 billion in 2022, a KRW 1.45 billion loss in 2023, KRW 483 million in 2024 and KRW 3.69 billion in 2025, and the gap with the controlling-shareholder figure in 2025 reflects non-controlling interests.

At the end of 2025, total equity was KRW 149.5 billion, of which KRW 123.8 billion belonged to controlling shareholders, and total liabilities were KRW 185.9 billion.

The debt ratio went from 117.3% in 2022 to 88.0% in 2023, 93.2% in 2024 and 124.3% in 2025, while operating cash flow was -KRW 9.67 billion in 2022, KRW 5.2 billion in 2023, -KRW 3.85 billion in 2024 and -KRW 2.94 billion in 2025.

Quarterly revenue was KRW 56 billion in the third quarter of 2025, KRW 53.9 billion in the fourth quarter (down 3.7% from the prior quarter), KRW 66.1 billion in the first quarter of 2026 (up 22.6%), KRW 71.7 billion in the second quarter (up 8.5%) and KRW 66.3 billion in the third quarter (down 7.5% from the prior quarter, up 18.4% year on year).

Quarterly operating profit moved from KRW 2.79 billion in the third quarter of 2025 to an operating loss of KRW 36.15 million in the fourth quarter (a swing into loss), then KRW 8.16 billion in the first quarter of 2026 (a swing back to profit), KRW 9.23 billion in the second quarter (up 13.1%) and KRW 5.79 billion in the third quarter (down 37.2% from the prior quarter, up 107.7% year on year).

Profit attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 1.1 billion in the third quarter of 2025, a KRW 3.33 billion loss in the fourth quarter, a KRW 2.1 billion loss in the first quarter of 2026, KRW 3 billion in the second quarter and KRW 2.44 billion in the third quarter, and the last four quarters total KRW 12.42 million.

Across the three quarters of 2026 in which operating profit exceeded KRW 5.7 billion, controlling-shareholder results still alternated between profit and loss, so the items between operating profit and controlling-shareholder profit (non-operating items, taxes and non-controlling interests) are the next thing to check.

05

Industry analysis

The vice chairman of Korea Welding Rod, a welding materials maker, told the Steel & Metal News that domestic wire demand has been on a continued downward trend, citing the shift to electric vehicles in the auto industry and a prolonged slump in construction.

In the same interview he said welding materials have less pricing power than other wire products, making price increases difficult as imported material use grows.

Hankuk Steel Wire's mild steel wire goes into construction-related uses such as welded mesh and nails, as well as agricultural applications, so it is exposed to domestic construction and farm demand.

The company claims a number one position in galvanized steel wire in both Korea and Japan, which points to a relatively strong product position in domestic competition. Japanese exports of stainless weaving wire give overseas demand a possible supporting role when domestic demand is weak.

In the domestic wire market, price competition between imports and domestic producers is the key variable, and when pricing power is weak, raw material swings tend to squeeze margins. The 2025 operating margin of 3.7% is below the 4.6% of 2022, so unit profitability has not yet returned to the 2022 level.

The company is compared with Kiswire, Dongkuk Steel Mill and SeAH Steel, but the number one share claim is the company's own assertion and should be read against independent market data. Where the industry stands in its cycle is best gauged from trends in auto production and construction investment.

Outlook

Operating profit in the first half of 2026 was KRW 8.16 billion in the first quarter and KRW 9.23 billion in the second, both above KRW 8 billion. Third-quarter operating profit was KRW 5.79 billion, down 37.2% from the prior quarter but up 107.7% year on year.

Third-quarter revenue was KRW 66.3 billion, up 18.4% year on year. A company profile service reported that fitting and valve revenue rose in the first half of 2026 on certifications from Middle East and Southeast Asian energy companies and participation in domestic petrochemical projects.

Full-year 2026 results will be fixed on a disclosed basis in the annual report, which will also set out the full-year operating margin and fourth-quarter earnings. The next checkpoint is whether fourth-quarter 2026 operating profit holds near the third-quarter level of about KRW 5.8 billion or more.

Profit attributable to controlling shareholders totaled only KRW 12.42 million over the last four quarters, so whether operating profit gains carry through to controlling-shareholder profit remains open.

Wire revenue is linked to auto and construction demand indicators, so those trends should be tracked alongside company results. Shareholder return plans and subsidiary-level results can be found in the relevant sections of the annual report.

08

Bull factors

Operating profit recovery

Operating profit reached KRW 8.17 billion in 2025, up 35.6% from the prior year, and the operating margin rose from 2.1% in 2023 to 3.7% in 2025. Operating profit was KRW 8.16 billion in the first quarter of 2026 and KRW 9.23 billion in the second, the highest quarterly levels in the last five quarters. The company attributed the 2025 improvement to a lower cost-of-sales ratio and higher financial income.

Valve and fitting subsidiaries growing

According to a company profile service, fitting and valve revenue rose in the first half of 2026, with certifications from Middle East and Southeast Asian energy companies cited as a driver. Participation in domestic petrochemical projects was also listed as a factor behind the revenue increase.

A broader business mix can partly offset the weak pricing power of the wire business, which is the main bull-side point.

Product differentiation claims

Product differentiation is the main basis for the bull case. If the company's claim of a number one galvanized wire share in Korea and Japan holds, it could defend volumes despite import competition. Exports of stainless weaving wire to Japan also partly offset weak domestic demand. The share figure is the company's own claim and should be checked against independent market data.

Bear factors

Weakening domestic wire demand

Revenue in 2025 was KRW 219.4 billion, below the 2023 peak, and fell in both 2024 and 2025, two consecutive years of decline. The vice chairman of Korea Welding Rod said in an interview that domestic wire demand keeps falling because of the auto industry's shift to electric vehicles and a prolonged construction slump. Rising imports also make price increases difficult, which is the main bear-side point.

Volatile controlling-shareholder earnings

Profit attributable to controlling shareholders went from a KRW 2.33 billion loss in 2023 and a KRW 1.23 billion loss in 2024 to a KRW 327 million profit in 2025. A KRW 3.33 billion loss was recorded in the fourth quarter of 2025 and a KRW 2.1 billion loss in the first quarter of 2026.

Over the last four quarters the total is only KRW 12.42 million, so operating profit gains did not always reach controlling shareholders.

Financial burden and cash flow

The debt ratio rose from 117.3% in 2022 to 124.3% in 2025, and total liabilities of KRW 185.9 billion exceed total equity of KRW 149.5 billion. Operating cash flow was negative in both 2024 and 2025.

If higher operating profit does not translate into cash generation, reliance on external funding could increase, which is the main bear-side point.

10

Risk factors

Imports and downstream industry shift

If price competition from imports continues, defending wire selling prices will be difficult, and if the shift to electric vehicles reduces volumes, the pace of recovery in the core business could slow. This risk depends more on external factors than on the company's pricing decisions.

Subsidiary project volatility

Fitting and valve revenue appears to depend on certifications and project participation. If projects cluster in particular periods, quarterly results could swing sharply, and subsidiary results could mask weakness in the core wire business. Segment revenue and profit need to be confirmed in the periodic reports.

Leverage and earnings gap

With the debt ratio at 124.3%, higher financing costs could make it harder for operating profit gains to reach controlling-shareholder profit.

As the gap in 2025 between KRW 3.69 billion in net profit and KRW 327 million attributable to controlling shareholders shows, a larger non-controlling share thins the profit left for controlling shareholders. This gap should be checked in the notes to the periodic reports.

Overall view

Hankuk Steel Wire's revenue peaked in 2023 and declined through 2025, while operating profit recovered from its 2023 low to a 3.7% margin in 2025. Profit attributable to controlling shareholders moved from losses in 2023 and 2024 to a KRW 327 million profit in 2025.

In 2026, operating profit was KRW 8.16 billion in the first quarter and KRW 9.23 billion in the second, then fell to KRW 5.79 billion in the third. The bull-side points are the operating profit recovery, revenue growth at the valve and fitting subsidiaries and the company's claim of a number one galvanized wire share.

The bear-side points are weaker domestic wire demand and import competition, unstable quarterly controlling-shareholder results, and a 124.3% debt ratio alongside negative operating cash flow.

Both sets of points deserve equal weight, and the main question is whether the operating profit recovery carries through to controlling-shareholder profit and cash flow.

Valuation sits in a discount range to net assets, but the small earnings base means earnings-based multiples look high, so the two measures need to be read together. The next confirmation point is the 2026 annual report, due by March 31, 2027, which will fix full-year earnings and cash flow.
