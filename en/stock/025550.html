02

Business structure

Hankuk Steel Wire was founded in 1974 and listed on KOSDAQ in 1995, with the parent company's core business centered on the manufacturing and sale of galvanized iron and steel wire and stainless steel wire, along with steel product distribution.

Galvanized steel wire is used in wire netting, wire rope, automotive parts, reinforcing materials, and submarine cable applications, while stainless steel wire, valued for its heat and corrosion resistance, supplies telecommunications equipment, semiconductor, plant, and nuclear power facilities.

Industry reports have indicated that the company held the top position in galvanized iron wire sales, fourth place in stainless steel wire, and fifth place in steel products for several consecutive years.

Its subsidiary Hansun Engineering Co., Ltd., founded in 2012 and listed on KOSDAQ in November 2024, manufactures instrumentation fittings and valves supplied to shipbuilding, offshore plants, nuclear, thermal, and hydro power facilities, semiconductors, and hydrogen energy applications.

Another subsidiary, Keysung Metal Co., Ltd., produces plant valves and has supplied eco-friendly ship engine GVUs to HD Hyundai Heavy Industries, as well as securing new project orders by developing semiconductor-use valves.

The consolidated group also includes a Japanese sales subsidiary and an asset management affiliate. The steel wire segment relies more on domestic sales than exports, while the fitting and valve segment serves a diversified customer base spanning petrochemicals, shipbuilding, and energy.

As a result, the company operates a dual structure combining traditional steel wire distribution with higher value-added precision component businesses.

04

Earnings analysis

Consolidated revenue peaked at KRW 248.4 billion in 2023, then declined for two consecutive years to KRW 223.4 billion in 2024 (down 10.1% year-on-year) and KRW 219.4 billion in 2025 (down 1.8%).

Operating profit, by contrast, bottomed at KRW 5.12 billion in 2023 and rose for two straight years to KRW 6.03 billion in 2024 (up 17.7%) and KRW 8.17 billion in 2025 (up 35.6%), showing improved profitability despite the sales slowdown.

The operating margin edged up gradually from 2.1% in 2023 to 2.7% in 2024 and 3.7% in 2025. Net income attributable to controlling interests swung to a loss of KRW 2.33 billion in 2023, remained in loss at KRW 1.23 billion in 2024 (narrower than the prior year), before turning to a profit of KRW 327 million in 2025.

Net income including non-controlling interests moved from KRW 15 billion in 2022 to a loss of KRW 1.45 billion in 2023, then KRW 483 million in 2024 and KRW 3.69 billion in 2025.

On a quarterly basis, the fourth quarter of 2025 saw an operating loss of KRW 36.15 million and a net loss attributable to controlling interests of KRW 3.33 billion, before operating profit turned positive at KRW 8.16 billion in the first quarter of 2026 and rose further for a second straight quarter to KRW 9.23 billion in the second quarter (up 13.1% quarter-on-quarter and 78.5% year-on-year).

Net income attributable to controlling interests remained in loss at KRW 2.1 billion in the first quarter of 2026 (narrower loss) before turning to a profit of KRW 3 billion in the second quarter, though that was down 20.3% year-on-year, and the sum of the trailing four quarters (third quarter of 2025 through second quarter of 2026) was still a net loss of KRW 1.33 billion.

Revenue similarly bottomed at KRW 53.9 billion in the fourth quarter of 2025 and rose for two consecutive quarters to KRW 66.1 billion in the first quarter of 2026 (up 22.6% quarter-on-quarter) and KRW 71.7 billion in the second quarter (up 8.5% quarter-on-quarter and 27.8% year-on-year).

On the balance sheet side, the debt-to-equity ratio rose from 88.0% in 2023 to 124.3% in 2025, and operating cash flow was negative in every year except 2023 (an inflow of KRW 5.2 billion), with outflows of KRW 9.67 billion in 2022, KRW 3.85 billion in 2024, and KRW 2.94 billion in 2025.

05

Industry analysis

The domestic steel wire market has been affected by sluggish construction activity and an influx of low-cost Chinese products, though industry observers note that the steel products segment could see a demand recovery as housing market activity improves and delayed redevelopment projects resume construction, even amid low-cost competition from China and a global economic slowdown.

Higher value-added wire products, such as galvanized steel wire for submarine cables, are reported to be seeing relatively firmer demand supported by power grid and offshore infrastructure investment.

In the fitting and valve market, energy infrastructure investment is a key variable, with S-OIL's KRW 9.258 trillion Shaheen project at the Onsan industrial complex in Ulsan entering the final stage of completion inspections covering 1,012 facilities including pipe-rack structures as of October 2026.

On KOSDAQ, fitting and valve stocks rallied on expectations of increased orders from downstream industries such as shipbuilding, offshore plants and petrochemicals, driven by outlooks for expanded global LNG carrier orders and energy infrastructure investment.

Within this trend, Hankuk Steel Wire and KSP were reported to have joined the rally as buying interest spread across the sector.

Brokerages have also noted that while expanded LNG carrier orders and offshore plant investment could benefit domestic fitting and valve makers' order intake, the extent of earnings improvement may vary depending on raw material prices for steel and nickel, project progress, and each company's order backlog and margins.

Industry reports indicate the company maintained the top position in galvanized iron wire sales, fourth place in stainless steel wire, and fifth place in steel products for at least three consecutive years.

In sum, the steel wire segment remains highly cyclical, while the fitting and valve segment is tied to structural growth themes in energy, semiconductors and shipbuilding, suggesting the two business lines may move through different cycles.

06

Outlook

The company's near-term earnings trajectory is likely to be significantly influenced by the progress of new business initiatives at subsidiaries Hansun Engineering and Keysung Metal.

Hansun Engineering has seen sales increase through certifications obtained from Middle Eastern and Southeast Asian energy companies and participation in domestic petrochemical projects, and its performance is reported to be improving on the back of expanded overseas orders tied to the S-OIL Shaheen project and the completion of a cleanroom facility along with the start of mass production of UHP fittings and valves for semiconductor applications.

Keysung Metal has supplied eco-friendly ship engine GVUs to HD Hyundai Heavy Industries and secured new project orders by developing semiconductor-use valves, while the steel wire segment cites rising submarine cable demand and expanded sales of higher value-added products as factors behind improved results.

However, on June 1, 2026, the company disclosed a decision to dispose of 715,564 common shares (3.72%) of Hansun Engineering to raise approximately KRW 20 billion, only to withdraw the plan after Hansun Engineering's share price fell more than 30% following the initial disclosure, triggering a legal condition for withdrawal.

As a result, the strategy of using subsidiary equity stakes to improve the balance sheet and fund shareholder returns has been put on hold for now.

Regarding the S-OIL Shaheen project, Ulju County has been working to complete completion inspections for industrial facility developments by the end of October 2026, after which the project is expected to move into trial operation, making the timing of revenue recognition for supplier companies after completion and startup a key point to watch.

Bull factors

Expansion of Fitting and Valve New Businesses

Subsidiaries Hansun Engineering and Keysung Metal are broadening their customer base across energy, semiconductor, and shipbuilding sectors.

Hansun Engineering has secured certifications from Middle Eastern and Southeast Asian energy companies and orders tied to the S-OIL Shaheen project, and has begun mass production of UHP fittings and valves for semiconductor use.

Keysung Metal has also expanded its customer roster by supplying eco-friendly ship engine GVUs to HD Hyundai Heavy Industries. These new businesses are acting as a buffer against the slowing growth of the core steel wire business.

Quarterly Profit Recovery in 2026

After posting an operating loss in the fourth quarter of 2025, operating profit rose for two consecutive quarters through the first and second quarters of 2026.

Second-quarter operating profit reached KRW 9.23 billion, up 78.5% year-on-year, and net income attributable to controlling interests also returned to a profit in the same quarter.

Revenue likewise grew for two straight quarters after bottoming in the fourth quarter of 2025, showing simultaneous recovery in both scale and profitability.

Demand for Submarine Cable and Other High Value-Added Products

In the steel wire segment, rising demand for higher value-added products such as galvanized steel wire for submarine cables is cited as a factor behind improved results.

If the sales mix shifts further toward these products amid expanding power grid and offshore infrastructure investment, it could help offset the relatively lower margins of the steel products segment.

Should signs of recovery emerge in housing and construction activity, demand in the steel products segment could also see some improvement.

Bear factors

Stagnation in the Core Steel Wire Business

Consolidated revenue peaked in 2023 and declined for two consecutive years in 2024 and 2025. Sluggish construction activity and the expanded supply of low-cost Chinese products are cited as key background factors.

Given the steel products segment's large share of revenue, delayed recovery in downstream industries could continue to constrain top-line growth.

Balance Sheet Pressure

The debt-to-equity ratio rose from 88.0% in 2023 to 124.3% in 2025. Operating cash flow was negative in three of the past four fiscal years, excluding 2023. While profitability has been improving, volatility remains in terms of cash generation and balance sheet soundness.

Quarterly Earnings Volatility

The fourth quarter of 2025 saw a simultaneous operating loss and net loss attributable to controlling interests, breaking the profitable streak of preceding quarters. Net income attributable to controlling interests also remained in loss in the first quarter of 2026.

The trailing four-quarter sum of net income attributable to controlling interests remains negative, and continued quarterly volatility cannot be ruled out.

Risk factors

Raw Material and Foreign Exchange Fluctuations

Costs for steel wire, fitting, and valve products are directly exposed to fluctuations in raw material prices such as steel and nickel. Brokerages have also noted that the extent of earnings improvement across the sector can vary depending on steel and nickel prices. Export-oriented products may also be affected by exchange rate movements.

Uncertainty in Subsidiary-Linked Financial Strategy

In June 2026, the company disclosed plans to sell a 3.72% stake in Hansun Engineering to raise approximately KRW 20 billion, but withdrew the plan after Hansun Engineering's share price plunged more than 30% following the disclosure.

This illustrates that the parent company's financial strategy, which depends on the equity value of a consolidated subsidiary, can be vulnerable to market volatility. If a similar fundraising approach is pursued again in the future, uncertainty over its execution and timing could resurface.

Major Shareholder Stake Sale Supply Pressure

Shareholder Manho Steel has reduced its holdings through the exercise of exchange rights on exchangeable bonds, with its stake disclosed as having fallen to 6.40% from 10.07% previously. Changes in affiliated shareholders' stakes can affect float and trading supply and demand. Whether the remaining stake is further disposed of in the future also bears watching.

Overall view

Hankuk Steel Wire has shown a recovery in quarterly operating profit through 2026, driven by business diversification through its fitting and valve subsidiaries even as its core steel wire business experienced a gradual decline.

The fourth quarter of 2025 saw a simultaneous operating loss and net loss attributable to controlling interests, but operating profit rose for consecutive quarters in the first and second quarters of 2026, with net income attributable to controlling interests also returning to a profit in the second quarter.

However, the debt-to-equity ratio rose to 124.3% at the end of 2025, and operating cash flow was negative in three of the past four years, warranting continued scrutiny of financial flexibility.

The withdrawal of the planned stake sale in subsidiary Hansun Engineering following a sharp share price decline illustrates the uncertainty embedded in the consolidated financial strategy.

The completion and trial-operation schedule of the S-OIL Shaheen project, along with the revenue contribution from new semiconductor- and hydrogen-related businesses, stand out as key variables for the future earnings trajectory.

It will be worth monitoring the next regular quarterly filing together with subsidiary-related disclosures to track this trend.
