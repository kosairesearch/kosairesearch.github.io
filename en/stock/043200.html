02

Business structure

Paru is a KOSDAQ-listed company, listed in 2000, specializing in solar power structures and tracking systems. Its main products are fixed-tilt structures and single-axis and dual-axis tracking systems, and most of its sales come from the solar business segment.

It has supplied roughly 400MW of tracking systems to the Alamo project in Texas, USA, which demonstrates its capability in large utility-scale projects.

Its products are exported to more than 40 countries, and the company is expanding its dealer and distribution network around EPC-type sales in which it develops projects directly. Overseas sales focus on large utility projects in India, the Middle East, Australia and Africa.

On a consolidated basis, the company holds two unlisted subsidiaries. Recently it has widened opportunities linking agriculture and energy production, such as agrivoltaic solar.

In August 2026 it reported a first order for thermal runaway barrier pads (TPSTOP) applied to an ESS project in the Americas for a domestic battery maker, and it said mass supply would start in October 2026. The company also diversified into hygiene products such as disinfection equipment in the past. Competition for tracking structure orders depends on price competitiveness and track record in large projects.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual revenue rose from KRW 40.5 billion in 2022 to KRW 42.2 billion in 2023, a 4.1% increase year on year, and then fell to KRW 40.1 billion in 2024, down 5.0%, and to KRW 28.8 billion in 2025, down 28.2%.

The company attributed the 2025 revenue decline to lower order volume and lower unit prices, and the profit decline to higher raw material prices.

Operating loss was KRW 2.33 billion in 2022 and KRW 1.92 billion in 2023, a narrower loss, before the company returned to operating profit of KRW 212 million in 2024 and swung back to an operating loss of KRW 2.73 billion in 2025.

The operating profit margin was -5.8% in 2022, -4.5% in 2023, 0.5% in 2024 and -9.5% in 2025. The 2024 profit came as revenue fell 5.0%, while in 2025 falling sales and raw material pressure together pushed the margin down to -9.5%.

Net loss attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 4.63 billion in 2022, KRW 999 million in 2023, KRW 808 million in 2024 and KRW 4.11 billion in 2025, a loss in each of those four years. Net loss including non-controlling interests was KRW 4.13 billion in 2025.

On a quarterly basis, after an operating loss of KRW 164 million in the third quarter of 2025 and KRW 288 million in the fourth quarter, the first quarter of 2026 returned to operating profit of KRW 163 million, and the second quarter recorded KRW 106 million, down 34.8% from the prior quarter.

Revenue in the third quarter of 2026 was KRW 6.31 billion, up 1.7% from the prior quarter but down 20.0% year on year, and the company recorded an operating loss of KRW 310 million.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 66.42 million in the first quarter of 2026, followed by a net loss of KRW 144 million in the second quarter and KRW 661 million in the third quarter, the second consecutive quarter of losses.

The last four quarters combined show a net loss attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 1.33 billion. The debt ratio was 129.0% in 2022, 133.4% in 2023, 112.2% in 2024 and 134.6% in 2025, rising again in 2025. Operating cash flow went from an inflow of KRW 1.97 billion in 2024 to an outflow of KRW 2.75 billion in 2025.

In 2026 Korea enacted a law establishing a legal basis for solar power generation projects, and the company has cited this as an opportunity to expand the market. The company said it plans to respond to market expansion based on its existing technology and construction experience.

Globally, on February 5, 2026, Tesla's chief executive referred to a target of 200GW of annual solar production capacity for Tesla and SpaceX by 2028, and Paru's shares rose about 28% in early trading that day.

In the same report, KB Securities researcher Jeon Woo-je questioned whether the target could be achieved, citing past overly ambitious targets. Whether production capacity targets are met is an indirect variable for power plant construction demand.

On November 17, 2026, solar-related stocks closed mixed, and Paru was among the decliners. The industry is showing differentiated moves by stock, depending on renewable energy policy and expectations for large project orders.

In a five-company solar sector comparison, Paru's 2025 operating margin of -9.5% was a smaller loss than the sector average of about -16.8%. Because order volume drives revenue, order flow is the key indicator for judging the industry cycle.

Overall view

Paru is a company with a track record in solar tracking structures and large overseas projects, but on a disclosed-results basis, net losses attributable to controlling shareholders continued from 2022 through 2025, and revenue has declined for two consecutive years since KRW 42.2 billion in 2023.

It posted operating profits in the first and second quarters of 2026, but in the third quarter losses widened again, with an operating loss of KRW 310 million and a net loss attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 661 million.
