Paru: Swinging Between Profit and Loss Amid Agrivoltaic Push

Paru is a KOSDAQ-listed company centered on solar tracking systems, and while its full-year 2025 results showed a revenue decline and a swing to an operating loss, the company briefly turned profitable in the first quarter of 2026 before slipping back into a net loss in the second quarter, underscoring continued quarter-to-quarter volatility.

02

Business structure

Founded in 1993 and listed on KOSDAQ in 2000, Paru is a renewable-energy company headquartered in Suncheon, South Jeolla Province, with solar power structures and tracking systems forming the core of its revenue.

The company designs and manufactures dual-axis, single-axis, and fixed-tilt structures in-house, supported by structural analysis technology accounting for wind, snow, and seismic loads and by public-procurement excellent-product certification.

Its hygiene and environment segment also produces disinfection equipment and automatic reels, giving it a revenue source outside solar. Among its consolidated subsidiaries, Paru wholly owns Taein Solar Power Plant, which operates solar generation assets, and beneath that sits AI Times, which manufactures film heaters.

Paru previously built an overseas track record in tracking systems by supplying a large-scale dual-axis system in the Alamo area of Texas.

More recently it has expanded into agrivoltaics, having supplied a 1MW single-axis tracking system in Yeonggwang, South Jeolla, and built and operated a 400kW demonstration site in Yeongam.

In August 2026 the company formed a consortium with AKIS and Huintech to develop an AI-based integrated agriculture-energy solution covering crop-and-generation control and open-field smart-farm IoT packages, with total project cost of about KRW2.857 billion, of which KRW2 billion is government funding.

Competitively, the domestic solar structure and tracker market involves numerous small and mid-sized players, keeping pricing pressure a persistent feature of the business.

Earnings analysis

Annual revenue rose from KRW40.5 billion in 2022 to a peak of KRW42.2 billion in 2023, then declined for two consecutive years to KRW40.1 billion in 2024 and KRW28.8 billion in 2025.

Operating profit stayed in loss at KRW2.33 billion in 2022 and KRW1.92 billion in 2023, turned positive at KRW212 million in 2024, and then swung back to an operating loss of KRW2.73 billion in 2025.

The company's disclosure attributed the 2025 swing to lower order volumes and unit-price declines on the revenue side and to rising raw-material costs on the profit side.

Net profit attributable to owners of the parent posted losses for four consecutive years from 2022 through 2025, with the loss narrowing from KRW4.63 billion in 2022 to KRW999 million in 2023 and KRW808 million in 2024, before widening again to KRW4.11 billion in 2025.

Total net loss including non-controlling interests was KRW4.13 billion in 2025, a level close to the owners' net loss figure.

On a quarterly basis, revenue rose to a peak of KRW7.97 billion in Q4 2025 before declining for two straight quarters to KRW6.91 billion in Q1 2026 and KRW6.21 billion in Q2 2026, though the Q2 2026 figure was still up 3.5% from the same quarter a year earlier.

Operating profit remained in loss from Q2 through Q4 2025, turned positive at KRW163 million in Q1 2026, and came in at KRW106 million in Q2 2026, down 34.8% quarter on quarter but representing a swing to profit versus the year-earlier loss.

Net profit attributable to owners turned positive at KRW66.42 million in Q1 2026 before swinging back to a net loss of KRW144 million in Q2 2026, and the cumulative net loss attributable to owners over the last four quarters (Q3 2025 through Q2 2026) totaled KRW1.1 billion.

Operating cash flow turned positive at KRW1.97 billion in 2024 before reversing to -KRW2.75 billion in 2025, mirroring the volatility seen in reported earnings.

05

Industry analysis

According to the company's March 2026 annual general meeting notice, Korean solar policy is shifting from large-scale centralized generation toward distributed models such as agrivoltaics, industrial-complex and building rooftop solar, and resident-participation solar.

The Ministry of Agriculture, Food and Rural Affairs is reportedly pursuing an amendment to the Farmland Act that would extend the temporary land-use permit period for agrivoltaic facilities from eight to twenty years, alongside expanded public direct-payment eligibility and new operating guidelines.

Thirty-eight months of cumulative test-bed data released by the Paru Solar Research Institute in April 2026 showed tracking systems achieving generation efficiency up to 31% higher than fixed systems in Yeongam and Wanju, suggesting the basis for selection could shift from upfront installation cost toward output.

Even so, the solar structure and tracker market involves numerous small and mid-sized competitors, keeping unit-price competition persistent, a factor the company itself cited as a direct driver of its 2025 revenue and profit decline.

Falling prices across the global solar supply chain and volatility in raw-material costs are additional variables affecting profitability across smaller domestic solar component makers broadly.

Relative to peers, Paru's differentiation lies in efficiency data accumulated through its own research institute on tracking systems, though its overall revenue scale remains at the smaller end of the industry.

06

Outlook

In August 2026, Paru formed a consortium with AKIS and Huintech to begin developing an AI precision-cultivation-based agrivoltaic variable-shading integrated solution, stating a goal of bringing a market-ready product to supply within one year of the agreement date.

The demonstration is being conducted at Paru's test site in South Jeolla Province on onions and garlic from planting through harvest, collecting crop imagery, soil moisture, weather, solar irradiance, and generation data to verify how panel angle and irrigation volume affect productivity and generation efficiency.

The company said it intends to use this project as a springboard to expand beyond equipment sales into a business structure combining AI control-platform subscriptions, farming data services, and remote maintenance.

In August 2026, largest shareholder Jibon disclosed a plan to purchase about KRW500 million worth of Paru shares for reasons described as responsible management and enhancing shareholder value, with the specific timing and quantity to be determined based on trading price and market conditions.

By October 2026, Jibon's stake was reported to have risen to 24.00%.

In August 2026 the company carried out a 2-for-1 share consolidation aimed at stabilizing the share price and enhancing corporate value, raising par value from KRW500 to KRW1,000 and reducing total shares outstanding from about 41.8 million to about 20.9 million.

Third-quarter 2026 results have not yet been disclosed through a formal regulatory filing, and no specific annual revenue or profit guidance from the company could be confirmed.

Overall view
