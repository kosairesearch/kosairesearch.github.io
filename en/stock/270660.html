Report overview

AI module sales growth versus persistent operating losses

Everybot is widening its revenue base from robot vacuums to AI autonomous-driving modules, but operating losses have continued since 2024, so the timing of a profit recovery is the next checkpoint.

02

Business structure

Everybot is a service-robot company founded in 2015 that moved its listing to KOSDAQ in 2021. Its business began with mop and all-in-one robot vacuums and is expanding into AI autonomous-driving modules, serving robots, and personal mobility.

Revenue comes from a product group that includes robot vacuums it manufactures directly, including AI autonomous-driving modules, and a merchandise group of finished goods sourced from outside manufacturers.

In a 2024 report, the main revenue mix was EDGE at 41.5%, TS300 at 25.2%, and the 31 POP merchandise line at 14.3%. Exports were 7.6% of sales at that time, with domestic sales at 92.4%, so domestic dependence was high.

The autonomous-driving module is installed in the Namuex wellness robot from SK intelliX and is produced and supplied against SK intelliX purchase orders. The company said the 2025 revenue increase came from the start of module supply, which created a new B2B revenue stream.

The X1 bedding robot vacuum sold out its initial batch within two days through a group-buying campaign after its 2024 launch. Meanwhile, the existing B2C robot vacuum business is reported to be shrinking in size because of competition from Chinese makers.

Its subsidiary Everybot Mobility handles personal mobility, and the subsidiary reported 2025 sales of about KRW 1.55 billion. In competition, Roborock and Ecovacs are far larger in sales and global penetration, while Coway and Cuckoo Homesys are the main domestic comparison points.

04

Earnings analysis

The largest year by revenue in the reported period was 2022. Revenue fell from KRW 53 billion in 2022 to KRW 31.7 billion in 2023 (down 40.2% year on year), and reached the smallest level in 2024 at KRW 29.8 billion (down 6.1% year on year). Revenue rebounded to KRW 34.4 billion in 2025 (up 15.5% year on year).

Operating profit fell from KRW 6.18 billion in 2022 to KRW 1.53 billion in 2023 (down 75.3%), and the company swung to an operating loss of KRW 2.24 billion in 2024. The 2025 operating loss widened to KRW 3.2 billion, and the company has posted losses for two consecutive years, 2024 and 2025.

The operating margin moved from 11.7% in 2022 and 4.8% in 2023 to -7.5% in 2024 and -9.3% in 2025. The company cited R&D headcount additions and higher R&D spending as the reasons for the wider 2025 operating loss.

The 2025 controlling-shareholder net loss of KRW 12.7 billion, after a swing from profit, reflected one-off costs on top of the operating loss, including a derivative valuation loss on convertible bonds and an impairment loss on an associate, according to the company.

Controlling-shareholder net income fell from KRW 6.95 billion in 2022 to KRW 312 million in 2024, and the total net loss including non-controlling interests was KRW 12.7 billion in 2025.

On a quarterly basis, revenue was KRW 8.73 billion in Q3 2025 and KRW 13.1 billion in Q4 2025 (up 49.7% from the prior quarter), followed by KRW 7.97 billion in Q1 2026 (down 39.0%), KRW 9.84 billion in Q2 2026 (up 23.4%), and KRW 7.08 billion in Q3 2026 (down 28.0% from the prior quarter and down 18.9% year on year).

Operating losses were KRW 1.78 billion in Q1 2026, KRW 1.23 billion in Q2 2026, and KRW 1.39 billion in Q3 2026.

Controlling-shareholder net income turned positive at KRW 7.61 billion in Q2 2026 even though that quarter had an operating loss of KRW 1.23 billion, which suggests non-operating items were large, and the breakdown needs to be checked in the periodic report.

The controlling-shareholder net loss for the last four quarters, from Q4 2025 to Q3 2026, totaled KRW 3.44 billion. Operating cash flow went from KRW 6.5 billion in 2022 and KRW 6.07 billion in 2023 to KRW 1.22 billion in 2024, and was -KRW 5.26 billion in 2025.

05

Industry analysis

The robot vacuum market is growing on rising fine-dust concerns and an expanding single-person household base, but competition from low-cost LiDAR components and cheap high-function models is reported to limit profitability across the sector.

Everybot's existing B2C robot vacuum business is shrinking in size because of competition from Chinese companies. Autonomous-driving modules, by contrast, create demand in a B2B segment within the robotics value chain, which shifts the company's mix toward service robots.

Because of this shift, the market is said to view Everybot more through the robotics sector lens than as a home-appliance maker. A May 2026 research report noted that Everybot's price-to-sales ratio is high compared with global robot vacuum makers Roborock and Ecovacs.

The same report added that the two peers differ widely from Everybot in sales scale and global penetration, which limits direct comparison.

In the U.S., the Federal Communications Commission on July 28, 2026 placed newly produced overseas humanoid and quadruped robots, and certain mobile robots meeting specified criteria, under its restrictions.

Everybot manufactures its robot vacuums in Korea, holds FCC registration, and sells them through Amazon and other channels, so it could benefit if new entry by Chinese robot vacuums is restricted. However, reports indicate that its U.S. sales remain small for now.

06

Outlook

In a revision filed on December 23, 2025, the company said it expects 2026 revenue of KRW 58 billion, up 61% from 2025. Reporting at the same time said module supply for the Namuex robot from SK intelliX was expected to expand to tens of thousands of units a year in 2026.

However, quarterly revenue was KRW 7.97 billion in Q1 2026, KRW 9.84 billion in Q2 2026, and KRW 7.08 billion in Q3 2026. Reaching KRW 58 billion would require Q4 2026 revenue to rise sharply on a quarterly basis.

A May 2026 research report forecast 2026 revenue of KRW 51.4 billion and an operating profit of KRW 1 billion, turning the company profitable, and the gap between that forecast and the operating losses through Q3 2026 depends on how quickly module revenue is reflected in results.

On new products, reporting as of December 2025 said the company was preparing a premium mop robot vacuum with upgraded functions and was reviewing other specialized categories. Whether and when these launch should be confirmed through company announcements.

In March 2026 the company expanded its existing AI convergence research institute into a physical AI research unit, and it is testing humanoid and senior-care robots. It agreed with an Israeli AI semiconductor company to co-develop an AI home robot and a serving and logistics robot platform by the end of 2026.

A government-funded home service robot platform project, with a KRW 5.4 billion state grant, runs through the end of 2027, and the company has said it plans to extend into kids, senior, and pet-care robots.

08

Bull factors

AI autonomous-driving modules are becoming a new revenue source

The company attributed the 2025 revenue increase to the start of AI autonomous-driving module supply to SK intelliX. Modules have been mass-produced for the Namuex robot since October 2025, and reporting from December 2025 expected supply of tens of thousands of units in 2026. The B2B revenue stream separate from robot vacuums is cited as the basis for a shift in the earnings structure.

Possible substitution opportunity for Korean-made robot vacuums in the U.S.

Everybot manufactures in Korea, holds FCC registration, and sells robot vacuums in the U.S. through Amazon and other channels. Reporting said the company could benefit if FCC restrictions introduced on July 28, 2026 limit new entry by Chinese-made products.

It is cited as one of the few listed Korean small-cap robot vacuum makers exporting to the U.S. U.S. sales, however, remain small.

Expansion into physical AI and senior care

A physical AI research unit was launched in March 2026, and talks are under way with a software company to provide the AI brain for humanoid robots. Senior-care robot trials are being run with a Samsung senior-living unit and KB Golden Life Care.

A May 2026 research report assessed that the business is at an early stage of transition but has sufficient structural growth potential.

09

Bear factors

Five consecutive quarters of operating losses and wider deficits

Operating losses have been reported for five consecutive quarters from Q3 2025 to Q3 2026. The 2025 operating loss of KRW 3.2 billion was larger than the KRW 2.24 billion loss in 2024, and the company cited R&D headcount and spending increases.

The 2025 controlling-shareholder net loss of KRW 12.7 billion is described as including one-off costs such as a derivative valuation loss on convertible bonds.

Shrinking B2C robot vacuum sales and low-price competition

The existing robot vacuum business is reported to be shrinking because of competition from Chinese companies. Low-cost LiDAR parts and cheap high-function models are cited as limiting sector profitability.

Compared with Roborock and Ecovacs, the gap in sales scale and global penetration is large, which is cited as a weakness.

Gap between the outlook and quarterly results

There is a large gap between the KRW 58 billion 2026 revenue outlook from the December 2025 revision and quarterly revenue of KRW 7.97 billion, KRW 9.84 billion, and KRW 7.08 billion for Q1 to Q3 2026.

The May 2026 report's forecast of KRW 51.4 billion in revenue and a KRW 1 billion operating profit also differs from the operating losses through Q3 2026. Reporting also indicates U.S. sales remain small, which limits the near-term effect of overseas expansion.

10

Risk factors

Financials and cash flow

Operating cash flow shifted from KRW 6.5 billion in 2022 to -KRW 5.26 billion in 2025. The debt ratio rose from 34.2% in 2023 to 76.0% in 2024 and was 75.5% in 2025. Because a derivative valuation loss on convertible bonds was reflected in net losses, share-price moves carry through to the financial statements.

Customer concentration

Autonomous-driving module revenue is produced and supplied against purchase orders from SK intelliX. Module revenue could vary sharply with that single customer's order volume. Follow-on models and additional customers should be confirmed through company announcements and remain unconfirmed for now.

Regulation and competition

Under the FCC rules, exceptions could be granted to suppliers outside China, but no confirmed decision has been reported. Low-price competition at home and overseas expansion by Chinese makers could add price pressure. Depending on how regulation changes, the opportunity in the U.S. could also narrow.

12

Overall view

Everybot is in a transition from robot vacuums toward AI autonomous-driving modules and physical AI. Revenue rose 15.5% to KRW 34.4 billion in 2025, but the operating loss widened to KRW 3.2 billion, and operating losses have been reported for five consecutive quarters through Q3 2026.

The Q2 2026 turn to positive controlling-shareholder net income appears to reflect large non-operating items, so it should be read separately from operating profitability.

Bullish factors include module supply for the Namuex robot from SK intelliX, a possible opportunity for FCC-certified Korean robot vacuums in the U.S., and physical AI research and government-funded projects.

Bearish factors include shrinking B2C sales amid competition from Chinese makers, the gap between the KRW 58 billion revenue outlook and quarterly sales, and negative operating cash flow in 2025.

The May 2026 profit forecast and the December 2025 revenue outlook both sit apart from 2026 quarterly results, so whether these are revised in later filings is the factual checkpoint. The 2026 annual report will show the full-year operating loss, the breakdown of non-operating items, and the weight of module revenue. This report does not provide an investment opinion or a target price.
