02

Business structure

Zaigle is a kitchen and healthcare appliance maker founded in 2008. Its core product is the top-heating infrared cooker 'Zaigle Grill', and the company develops and manufactures grills, ovens and griddles built on infrared heating technology.

The product line that smoke- and odor-reducing design built is the starting point, and it sold strongly through Korean home shopping channels. Later the company widened into oxygen and high-frequency medical devices as it pursued a shift toward a healthcare specialist.

In March 2023 the 'Zaigle ON medical device' received a Class 3 medical device approval from the Ministry of Food and Drug Safety, and the high-frequency device 'Nest' launched in April 2023.

In December 2025 new home-care products were launched, including the high-frequency facial care device 'Zaigle ON Silk' and the abdominal massager 'Zaigle ON Cell375'. Sales run mainly through home shopping, duty-free stores and the company's own online channels.

An external data aggregator classifies Q2 2026 revenue under a single segment, wellbeing appliances (healthcare).

In December 2022 the company acquired domestic LFP battery production assets for about KRW 7.4 billion and entered secondary batteries, but after taking a 30% stake in the US joint venture ZAICELL in July 2023, the in-kind contribution and share acquisition were fully cancelled in December 2024, and the business was reported as effectively halted.

Earnings analysis

Annual revenue fell from KRW 15 billion in 2022 to KRW 4.14 billion in 2023 (down 72.4% year on year), recovered to KRW 12.1 billion in 2024 (up 191.3%), and then declined to KRW 8.23 billion in 2025 (down 31.8%). Among 2022 to 2025, 2022 was the largest year and 2023 the smallest.

Operating losses were KRW 2.65 billion in 2022, KRW 8.67 billion in 2023, KRW 4.89 billion in 2024 and KRW 3.66 billion in 2025, marking four consecutive years of operating losses. In 2023, the year with the largest loss, revenue was at its lowest and the operating margin fell to -209.1%.

The 2025 operating loss narrowed from 2024, yet the operating margin was -44.4% against -40.5% in 2024, so the revenue decline weighed heavily.

The net loss attributable to controlling shareholders widened from KRW 3.74 billion in 2022 to KRW 10.4 billion in 2023, then narrowed to KRW 6.77 billion in 2024 and KRW 5.37 billion in 2025, and the net loss including non-controlling interests was recorded at the same amounts.

Operating cash flow was -KRW 829 million in 2022, -KRW 6.34 billion in 2023, -KRW 4.25 billion in 2024 and -KRW 6.79 billion in 2025, negative in all four years.

The debt-to-equity ratio rose from 46.4% in 2022 to 158.9% in 2025, with total equity of KRW 19.2 billion and total liabilities of KRW 30.5 billion at end-2025. Quarterly revenue rose for four consecutive quarters, from KRW 1.73 billion in Q3 2025 to KRW 3.85 billion in Q3 2026, and Q3 2026 was up 122.1% year on year.

Operating results were losses from Q3 2025 through Q1 2026, with an operating loss of KRW 502 million in Q1 2026, before Q2 2026 turned to an operating profit of KRW 721 million, and Q3 2026 reached KRW 1.06 billion, up 46.5% from the prior quarter.

Gross margin in Q1 2026 rose from 42.0% to 73.4% as cost of sales fell by more than half while revenue grew 8.0% year on year, according to an analysis of the quarterly report.

That analysis argued that the durability of the profit turn depends on whether the cost gain came from sustainable manufacturing and purchasing improvements rather than product mix or inventory sales.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders turned positive at KRW 167 million in Q1 2026, then rose to KRW 597 million in Q2 and KRW 826 million in Q3, and the four-quarter total from Q4 2025 to Q3 2026 was KRW 459 million.

Whether one-off items such as asset sale proceeds or litigation proceeds affected quarterly profit should be checked against the detailed items in the next periodic report.

Industry analysis

Under the Korea Exchange classification Zaigle sits in the KOSDAQ electrical and electronics sector, but its core products are kitchen cookers and household healthcare devices, so its earnings model differs from component makers in the same sector such as Samsung Electro-Mechanics and LG Innotek.

In kitchen appliances, the company positions its top-down infrared cooking method as its point of difference. Distribution centered on home shopping and duty-free retail tends to make revenue swing with how new products sell.

The healthcare line includes devices approved by the Ministry of Food and Drug Safety, which follow approval requirements different from those for kitchen appliances.

In secondary batteries, LFP cell prices have settled low under Chinese producers and large Korean firms are also entering, so a newcomer may face price competition, a view raised by industry participants. One report noted that industry sources treated the success of Zaigle's battery business as uncertain.

A June 2025 share price surge was attributed to the stock being grouped with secondary battery themes on expectations tied to Tesla's robotaxi plans. Research and development spending as a share of revenue fell from 6.97% in 2024 to 4.22% in 2025 and 2.84% in Q1 2026.

A securities industry source has noted that the shares tend to react to new-business keywords and that actual progress in new businesses should be checked carefully.

Because the scale and profitability gap with larger component makers in the same sector is wide, reading the company's own earnings trend is more appropriate than direct comparison.

The continuity of the recovery across quarters is the main bull argument. Annual revenue of KRW 8.23 billion in 2025 is still below KRW 15 billion in 2022.

Operating profit turn and gross margin improvement

Operating profit was KRW 721 million in Q2 2026, a turn to profit, and KRW 1.06 billion in Q3. Gross margin in Q1 2026 rose from 42.0% to 73.4% as cost of sales fell by more than half while revenue grew 8.0%. If the cost improvement persists, the base for operating profit widens, which is the main bull reading. Whether the improvement came from temporary inventory sales still needs to be separated out.

Expansion of the healthcare product line

Extending a business centered on kitchen appliances into approved healthcare products is the bull-side point. Revenue contribution from the new products is not separated in reported results, so the effect must be confirmed in later quarters.

Bear factors

Four straight years of operating losses and cash outflows

Operating losses have run for four consecutive years from 2022 through 2025, and the net loss attributable to controlling shareholders persisted over the same period. Operating cash flow was negative in all four years, and the 2025 outflow of -KRW 6.79 billion was larger than the -KRW 4.25 billion in 2024.

The 2025 operating loss narrowed to KRW 3.66 billion, but the operating margin worsened to -44.4% from -40.5%. Until quarterly profits change the annual loss pattern, the structural burden remains the main bear argument.

Shrinking equity and higher debt burden

The debt-to-equity ratio climbed each year from 46.4% in 2022 to 65.1% in 2023, 122.3% in 2024 and 158.9% in 2025. Total equity was KRW 19.2 billion and total liabilities were KRW 30.5 billion at end-2025. With equity shrinking while debt rose, the financial cushion has thinned.

Repayment or refinancing of maturing borrowings and the use of real estate sale proceeds are the variables that will determine the debt burden.

Secondary battery setback and disclosure credibility concerns

The company entered new business in December 2022 by acquiring LFP battery assets, but in December 2024 the in-kind contribution and share acquisition tied to the US joint venture were fully cancelled. In January 2025 it was designated an insincere disclosure company after the agreement with ZAICELL was withdrawn.

Press coverage pointed out that actual progress in the new business had not been verified after the announcements. Because the share price has moved on new-business expectations, disclosure credibility is cited as a bear factor.
