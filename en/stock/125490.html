02

Business structure

Hallacast began operations in 1996 as a die-casting-based precision parts maker. Built on magnesium precision processing, it has expanded from automotive parts into robot parts.

Key products include camera and lidar system housings for autonomous vehicles, electronic control unit housings, connected display frames, and robot frame components. The order backlog at end-2025 was KRW 1,134.6 billion, including KRW 641.9 billion for displays and KRW 350.6 billion for autonomous driving.

New orders in 2025 totaled KRW 541.7 billion, up 36% from the prior year. The company has reportedly been registered as a Tier 1 vendor of a global AI automaker, with cumulative backlog for that customer at KRW 118.6 billion.

Robot parts began with KRW 12.4 billion in orders in May 2025, and reports put them at about 6% of KRW 50.3 billion in new orders in Q1 2026. Segment revenue is not disclosed in the filings, so the business mix is best read through the order backlog. The company says it is strengthening technology, quality and delivery stability cooperation with its customers.

04

Earnings analysis

On the disclosed figures, 2025 revenue rose 7.9% to KRW 155.9 billion, while operating profit fell 16.1% to KRW 10.3 billion. The operating margin slipped from 8.5% in 2024 to 6.6% in 2025, and net income fell 64.5% to KRW 3.66 billion.

The company said a customer request shifted mass-production timing for some models and that listing costs, derivative valuation and interest losses were booked temporarily. On costs, Q1 2026 cost of sales rose to KRW 36.1 billion from KRW 34.1 billion a year earlier, according to press reports.

Equity stood at KRW 105.6 billion and total liabilities at KRW 87.5 billion at end-2025, and the debt-to-equity ratio fell from 2,121.3% in 2024 to 82.9%. Operating cash flow dropped from KRW 9.3 billion in 2024 to KRW 4.38 billion in 2025.

By quarter, Q4 2025 revenue of KRW 41 billion and operating profit of KRW 1.09 billion moved to Q1 2026 revenue of KRW 40.5 billion, down 1.1% quarter on quarter, and operating profit of KRW 1.5 billion, up 37.5%. Q2 2026 revenue rose 20.9% to KRW 49 billion and operating profit rose 117.5% to KRW 3.26 billion.

The company said first-half revenue of KRW 89.6 billion and operating profit of KRW 4.8 billion beat its own plan set at the start of the year.

Q3 2026 revenue reached KRW 53.1 billion, up 8.3%, but operating profit fell 33.2% to KRW 2.18 billion and net income attributable to controlling shareholders swung to a loss of KRW 1.52 billion.

Revenue has grown for two consecutive quarters since the Q1 2026 low, but that growth has not carried through to profit in a straight line. Q1 2026 operating cash flow turned into an outflow of KRW 12.8 billion.

05

Industry analysis

Even as growth in the auto market slowed, the company says new robot-part mass production and expanded mobility-part supply kept revenue growing. On demand, one research firm argues that AI vehicles and humanoid robots are lifting demand for ultra-light, high heat-dissipation precision parts.

That firm also says Hallacast was registered as a Tier 1 vendor of a global AI automaker on the strength of its magnesium die-casting technology. Commentators note that humanoid robot parts remain the core growth story, but the timing of actual mass production and revenue contribution still needs confirmation.

Customer schedule shifts are also a sector variable: in 2025 some model production timing moved, which limited top-line growth. On positioning, revenue is in the low hundreds of billions of won, small next to large automotive parts groups.

Its customer base is not concentrated in one buyer, and its magnesium technology is cited as a differentiator. Once expanded capacity comes online, the company's ability to serve customer volumes should change.

06

Outlook

The company announced on November 13 that cumulative revenue for January to September 2026 was about KRW 142.67 billion, and said Q4 supply volumes for its newer parts are expected to reach their highest level of the year.

The second plant's schedule has moved several times, from a July 2026 completion target to August completion and September operation readiness, and it is now slated to start operating in early December. Once complete, the plant is expected to lift capacity to about 150% of the prior level.

Among estimates, Korea Investment and Securities projected 2026 revenue of KRW 183.6 billion and operating profit of KRW 15.2 billion in February 2026, while a Korean brokerage's estimate reported in October 2026 put revenue at KRW 180 billion and operating profit at KRW 10 billion.

The gap in operating profit reflects the weaker first-half margins and higher prototype costs. On simple arithmetic, adding more than KRW 37 billion of Q4 revenue to the nine-month total would reach the KRW 180 billion estimate. Q4 2025 revenue of KRW 41 billion is the comparison base for the year-end quarter.

The company plans to expand revenue in the second half through full-scale mass production of robot parts and other new products. Whether the order backlog converts into revenue and profit at pace is the next checkpoint.

08

Bull factors

Order base supports top-line growth

New orders in 2025 rose 36% to KRW 541.7 billion, and the order backlog was reported at KRW 1,134.6 billion at end-2025. A backlog of this size gives visibility to top-line growth. Revenue has increased for two consecutive quarters since the Q1 2026 low.

Wider customer base in AI and robotics

The company is reportedly a Tier 1 vendor of a global AI automaker, with cumulative backlog for that customer at KRW 118.6 billion. Brokerage analysis also points to customer expansion to Hyundai Mobis. Robot parts began with KRW 12.4 billion in orders in May 2025.

First-half beat and capacity expansion

The company said first-half 2026 revenue of KRW 89.6 billion and operating profit of KRW 4.8 billion beat its plan. The company also expects Q4 supply volumes to reach the year's highest level.

09

Bear factors

Margin compression and a swing to loss

The operating margin fell from 8.5% in 2024 to 6.6% in 2025, and was 3.7% in Q1 2026. Q3 2026 operating profit fell 33.2% to KRW 2.18 billion, and net income attributable to controlling shareholders swung to a loss of KRW 1.52 billion. Revenue growth has not translated into profit growth.

Cash flow and borrowing burden

Q1 2026 operating cash flow turned into an outflow of KRW 12.8 billion, and total liabilities rose from KRW 87.5 billion at end-2025 to KRW 112.5 billion at Q1-end. Reports link this to expanded investment and borrowing. Operating cash flow had already fallen from KRW 9.3 billion in 2024 to KRW 4.38 billion in 2025.

Schedule delays and reported backlog decline

The second plant moved from a July 2026 target to an early-December start. The backlog was reported to fall from KRW 1,134.6 billion at end-2025 to KRW 920.6 billion at end-H1 2026, which makes conversion speed the key variable. The timing of robot-part mass production and revenue contribution still needs confirmation.

10

Risk factors

Production and mass-production timing

Customer-driven shifts in mass-production timing limited growth in 2025. Utilization at the new C-block of Plant 1 is being ramped gradually, and the second plant's completion date has changed several times. If new capacity is not running on time, conversion of orders into revenue could slow.

Financing and cash flow

Q1 2026 operating cash flow was an outflow of KRW 12.8 billion. Receivables and other current receivables rose from KRW 42.1 billion at end-2025 to KRW 53.3 billion at Q1-end. Expansion spending and working capital needs are rising together, raising reliance on borrowing.

Maturity of the robotics business

Robot parts are cited as the core growth story, but the timing of mass production and revenue contribution still needs confirmation. Reports put robot parts at about 6% of new orders in Q1 2026. If the robotics business does not scale as expected, the core growth argument would weaken.

  2. Q4 2026

12

Overall view

Hallacast's disclosed results show revenue growth and profit pressure at the same time. Revenue rose 7.9% in 2025, but operating profit fell 16.1%. Revenue has grown for two straight quarters in 2026, yet Q3 swung to a net loss attributable to controlling shareholders.

The KRW 1,134.6 billion order backlog at end-2025 and the Tier 1 vendor registration with a global AI automaker are cited as grounds for top-line growth. On the other side, the operating margin has declined and operating cash flow turned negative in Q1.

The next checkpoints are the second plant's start in December, the Q4 supply volumes, and the operating margin in the 2026 annual report. Brokerage estimates for full-year operating profit vary widely, so the pace of quarterly profit recovery matters most.

Robot parts are presented as a growth option, but the timing of mass production and revenue contribution has yet to be confirmed.
