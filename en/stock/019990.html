02

Business structure

Enertork, founded in 1987, manufactures industrial electric actuators and listed on KOSDAQ in February 2006. Its core business is making and selling devices that open and close valves remotely for power plants, plants, water and sewage systems, gas, steelmaking and nuclear facilities.

Revenue is concentrated in actuators and reduction gears, and in 2025 domestic sales made up 89.86% of product revenue while exports made up 10.14%. Its flagship electric actuators regulate the flow and pressure of coolant and steam in nuclear main piping and close the valve when abnormal signs appear.

Major customers are infrastructure buyers such as power utilities, plant operators and water and sewage authorities. Press reports say the company is the only domestic firm holding manufacturing technology for actuators used in nuclear power plants.

The company points to product quality, technical strength and rapid after-sales response as its advantages, and it is widening exports through a dealer network overseas.

In 2025 a private equity fund made a strategic investment, and the company has since pushed to shift from an equipment maker toward a data-driven next-generation actuator business.

In early 2026 it also announced joint development with an AI solutions firm of a data-hub smart actuator that adds communication and sensor data collection to its products. Product-level breakdown is confirmed, but segment revenue shares are not given as figures here.

04

Earnings analysis

On a disclosed annual basis, revenue was KRW 26.3 billion in 2022, KRW 26.9 billion in 2023, KRW 25.2 billion in 2024 and KRW 23.5 billion in 2025. After the 2023 peak, revenue fell for two consecutive years from 2024 to 2025, and the 2025 decline of 6.5% made 2025 the smallest year of the 2022 to 2025 period.

Operating profit went from KRW 1.01 billion in 2022 to an operating loss of KRW 882 million in 2023, turned to a profit of KRW 83.99 million in 2024, and then returned to an operating loss of KRW 4.1 billion in 2025. The operating margin was 3.8% in 2022, -3.3% in 2023, 0.3% in 2024 and -17.4% in 2025.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 723 million in 2022, a net loss of KRW 873 million in 2023, KRW 531 million in 2024 and a net loss of KRW 4.13 billion in 2025.

Net income including non-controlling interests was KRW 1.54 billion in 2022, a net loss of KRW 939 million in 2023, KRW 483 million in 2024 and a net loss of KRW 4.13 billion in 2025, a different series from the controlling-shareholder figures.

Operating cash flow was -KRW 985 million in 2022, KRW 1.86 billion in 2023, KRW 452 million in 2024 and KRW 167 million in 2025. The debt-to-equity ratio rose from 7.4% in 2024 to 20.8% at the end of 2025, when total equity was KRW 37.9 billion.

Quarterly, the operating loss was KRW 1.97 billion in the third quarter of 2025 and KRW 1.28 billion in the fourth quarter, before operating profit of KRW 72.15 million in the first quarter of 2026.

Second-quarter 2026 operating profit of KRW 354 million rose 390.1% from the prior quarter, and third-quarter operating profit of KRW 66.16 million fell 81.3% from the prior quarter but turned to profit from a year earlier.

Revenue was KRW 7.38 billion in the second quarter and KRW 5.8 billion in the third quarter of 2026, with the third quarter up 23.6% from a year earlier.

Operating profit was positive in the third quarter of 2026 while the net loss attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 287 million, so the non-operating items behind that gap are a point to check in the next periodic report.

05

Industry analysis

Demand from the downstream market rests on national infrastructure investment in power generation, water and sewage, gas, steelmaking and nuclear power.

In January 2026, reports said the president ordered sufficient public consultation on discussions for building new nuclear plants, and nuclear-related shares rose on that news. Nuclear policy remains at the discussion and consultation stage, so the timing of orders can shift with the policy schedule.

In nuclear valves and actuators, the industry expects a 'K-nuclear valve value chain' in which domestic valve and actuator makers take part. The company has said that if SMR mass production and overseas exports expand, its technology could become a global standard.

In water and sewage, replacement demand for aging underground and remote infrastructure is cited, and the company points to it as a basis for expanding in the Middle East and the United States.

The company sits in the same nuclear value chain as large nuclear equipment maker Doosan Enerbility, but its scale and order structure are different. On profit, an information-service tally shows first-half 2026 revenue up from a year earlier and operating profit turning positive.

Because the sector sits between supportive demand and policy variables, the timing of orders and project deliveries determines how visible earnings are.

Outlook

In its March 2026 corporate value-up plan, the company set out expanding Southeast Asian and overseas markets through a Vietnam plant expansion planned for the first half.

The same plan targets wider domestic and overseas nuclear orders including SMRs, development of new AI-enabled and compact products, entry into Middle East and Russian reconstruction projects, and expansion in US power and water treatment markets.

In June 2026 a contract was disclosed to supply 70 electric actuators for the ARA research reactor, with deliveries in sequence through 2028 in line with the project schedule.

At the March 2026 annual general meeting, a cash dividend on common shares was approved for fiscal 2025, even though the company posted a net loss that year. At an extraordinary shareholders meeting on July 24, 2026, articles changes and a plan for holding and disposing of treasury shares were approved as proposed.

The 40% or higher payout target depends on how much dividend capacity returns as earnings recover. Whether the Vietnam expansion is operating as planned and how much new products contribute to revenue can be checked in the next periodic report's capital expenditure and business sections. Whether treasury share disposals actually proceed is also a supply-and-demand item to monitor.

Bull factors

Third-quarter revenue rose 23.6% from a year earlier, showing a recovery in top-line growth alongside the profit turn. The change in direction after the KRW 4.1 billion operating loss in 2025 is the core of this trend.

Nuclear and SMR order base

If nuclear policy moves from discussion toward new construction, the number of order opportunities could grow. Its supply record on overseas nuclear projects is also cited.

Overseas expansion and shareholder return plan

The March 2026 value-up plan covers a Vietnam plant expansion and a push into Southeast Asian, Middle Eastern and US power and water markets. The same plan sets a target of keeping the payout ratio at 40% or higher.

At the July 2026 extraordinary meeting, a plan for holding and disposing of treasury shares was approved as proposed. With exports at 10.14% of 2025 product revenue, a rise in overseas sales could change the business mix.

Bear factors

Large 2025 loss and falling revenue

The 2025 operating loss of KRW 4.1 billion was larger than the KRW 882 million operating loss in 2023. Revenue fell for two consecutive years from 2024 to 2025, reaching KRW 23.5 billion in 2025, the smallest year of the 2022 to 2025 period.

The net loss attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 4.13 billion in 2025, and the debt-to-equity ratio rose from 7.4% in 2024 to 20.8%. Whether earnings strength recovers will need to be confirmed on an annual basis in the 2026 annual report.

Quarterly net results still in the red

Third-quarter 2026 operating profit was positive, but the net loss attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 287 million. The total for the most recent four quarters was a KRW 1.48 billion loss, so cumulative losses remain.

Third-quarter revenue fell 21.4% from the prior quarter, a sign that quarterly sales remain volatile. If operating profit does not carry through to net income, room for shareholder returns could stay limited.

Domestic concentration and nuclear policy risk

Domestic sales made up 89.86% of 2025 product revenue, so the company depends heavily on domestic power and plant investment cycles. New nuclear construction is still at the consultation stage, which makes the timing of orders hard to predict.

Exports at 10.14% of product revenue mean the overseas expansion plan has not yet fully shown up in results. As a small-cap stock, its share price is also prone to large swings.

Risk factors

Policy and order timing

If new nuclear construction decisions are delayed by hearings and consultation, order timing could also slip. The ARA project delivers in sequence through 2028, so schedule changes could affect when revenue is recognized. Concentration of orders in a few large projects can make quarterly revenue swing more widely.

Earnings volatility and profit gap

The company moved from a 2025 operating loss to quarterly operating profit in 2026, but quarterly profit has been in the range of tens of millions to a few hundred million won. Some quarters show operating profit and net results moving in opposite directions, so the quality of earnings needs checking.

With a KRW 1.48 billion net loss over the most recent four quarters, how long the profit trend lasts is uncertain.

Equity supply and capital events

Approval of a treasury share holding and disposal plan at the July 2026 extraordinary meeting creates an event that could affect future share supply. As a small-cap stock, its price can move sharply on modest trading volume.

Because a dividend was approved in a year with a net loss, the link between future dividend capacity and cash flow also matters.

Overall view

Enertork supplies electric actuators to national infrastructure in power generation, water and sewage, and nuclear power. On disclosed figures, revenue has declined since 2023, and the 2025 operating loss of KRW 4.1 billion and net loss attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 4.13 billion marked a wide loss.

In 2026, quarterly operating profit was positive in the first, second and third quarters, and one tally shows first-half revenue rising from a year earlier.

On the other side, the KRW 287 million net loss attributable to controlling shareholders in the third quarter of 2026 and the KRW 1.48 billion total for the last four quarters show the earnings recovery is not yet complete.

The ARA supply contract, the overseas expansion plan and the payout target give grounds for growth and shareholder returns. However, most revenue comes from the domestic market, and order timing can shift with nuclear policy, which also needs weighing.

The next checkpoints are the operating margin and net income attributable to controlling shareholders in the 2026 annual report, the dividend decision at the 2027 general meeting, and the first-quarter 2027 report.
