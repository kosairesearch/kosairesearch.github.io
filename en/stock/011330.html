Unichem: Testing Whether the First Quarterly Profit Holds

Unichem returned to an operating profit of KRW 651 million in the third quarter of 2026, but net losses attributable to controlling shareholders have continued for three straight quarters, so whether the recovery lasts is the next point to verify.

02

Business structure

Unichem processes leather into automotive interior parts, with automotive seat leather as its core product. It supplies domestic automakers including Hyundai Motor and KIA as a tier supplier, and it has also expanded into specialty materials by localizing flame-retardant seat material for KTX trains.

The company cited new-model deliveries for car seat parts as a driver of its 2025 revenue increase. Its business spans automotive interiors, eco-friendly and smart leather solutions, and an entertainment unit.

Its main raw material, raw hide, is largely imported, so international hide prices and exchange rates affect its costs. On the production side, automation at its Ansan plant is being pursued to expand capacity and reduce labor costs.

Winning orders depends on meeting the strict quality standards that global automakers require and keeping claims low. Because customer model schedules determine when revenue is booked, that structure showed up as earnings volatility in the first half of 2026.

In October 2026 the company signed a definitive agreement to acquire 100% of a US flexible-electronics company, and it plans to combine its material-processing skills with flexible circuit technology to target robotic e-skin, tactile sensors and smart automotive interiors.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual revenue went from KRW 120.4 billion in 2022 to KRW 108.3 billion in 2023 (down 10.1% year on year) and KRW 68.2 billion in 2024 (down 37.0%), before recovering to KRW 106.2 billion in 2025 (up 55.7%). Among 2022 to 2025, 2022 was the year with the largest revenue and 2024 the smallest.

Operating profit rose from KRW 3.27 billion in 2022 to KRW 4.74 billion in 2023 (up 45.1% year on year), then turned into an operating loss of KRW 6.27 billion in 2024. The 2025 operating loss of KRW 573 million was smaller than the prior year, and 2024 and 2025 are two consecutive years of operating losses.

The operating margin went from 2.7% in 2022 and 4.4% in 2023 to -9.2% in 2024 and -0.5% in 2025.

Profit attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 2.35 billion in 2022, then a net loss of KRW 6.25 billion in 2023, KRW 10.2 billion in 2024 and KRW 6.56 billion in 2025, which makes three consecutive years of losses from 2023 to 2025.

In 2022, net income including non-controlling interests of KRW 1.3 billion was smaller than profit attributable to controlling shareholders. At the end of 2025, total equity was KRW 131.3 billion and total liabilities were KRW 95.4 billion, with a debt-to-equity ratio of 72.7%.

That ratio fell from 212.5% in 2022 to 50.8% in 2023, then rose to 54.4% in 2024 and 72.7% in 2025. Operating cash flow was KRW 23.4 billion in 2022, -KRW 16.9 billion in 2023, KRW 3.7 billion in 2024 and -KRW 8.57 billion in 2025.

By quarter, Q3 2025 revenue was KRW 33.7 billion with operating profit of KRW 486 million, and Q4 2025 revenue fell 40.4% quarter on quarter to KRW 20.1 billion with an operating loss of KRW 2.72 billion. Q1 2026 revenue was KRW 19.6 billion with an operating loss of KRW 1.24 billion.

Q2 2026 revenue rose 58.3% quarter on quarter to KRW 31 billion, but the operating loss widened to KRW 2.93 billion. The company said volumes not supplied in the first half were deferred to the second half rather than cancelled.

Q3 2026 revenue was KRW 28.4 billion (down 8.3% quarter on quarter and down 15.6% year on year), and operating profit of KRW 651 million turned positive, up 34.1% year on year.

Net losses attributable to controlling shareholders were KRW 2.71 billion in Q1 2026, KRW 3.6 billion in Q2 and KRW 1.55 billion in Q3, three consecutive quarterly losses, and the last four quarters total KRW 16 billion.

Cumulative operating results through the first three quarters of 2026 are still a loss, so an annual operating profit depends on the fourth quarter.

Industry analysis

Unichem's downstream market is the domestic automaker production cycle. The company explained that in March 2026 sales of the Palisade were suspended in North America, and a fire at an engine valve supplier in Daejeon that same month disrupted engine supply.

It said the knock-on effect cut production of the Grandeur and Palisade and reduced related sales by about 40%, but it does not view this as a structural decline in demand.

An industry insider has noted that if early sales of new models lead to higher mass-production volumes, the earnings visibility of seat-material suppliers could improve.

Media reported that the new compact SUV from Hyundai Motor, for which Unichem supplies seat leather, recorded 15,045 contracts on the first day of contract opening in October 2026. Some data providers classify Unichem under automotive parts, so the appropriate peer group depends on the classification used.

Sector peers classified under chemicals, such as LG Chem, Hanwha Solutions, SKC, Kumho Petrochemical and Lotte Chemical, serve different demand markets such as petrochemicals and battery materials, which limits direct comparison. Raw hide prices and the exchange rate are the main cost-side industry variables.

The business is moving from automotive seat leather toward robotic e-skin and smart leather, so the demand size and competitive landscape of those new markets are a separate item to verify against company disclosures.

Outlook

The company is targeting record revenue since its founding and an annual operating profit for 2026 on a consolidated basis. The CEO described the first-half 2026 earnings trough as a temporary growing pain and said the orders secured this year are at a maximum level, with a large share concentrated in the second half.

He said production and sales returned to normal levels from mid-June 2026 and the plant is running 24 hours a day. Three new model programs, including the Avante and Tucson, entered mass production from August 2026.

The company expected revenue from deferred volumes to be fully reflected from the third quarter, and third-quarter operating profit did turn positive. It cited lower defect rates and claim costs from quality improvements as a profitability factor.

The CEO said a large second-half improvement and an annual profit turnaround are fully achievable in his view, and that this company view should be checked against actual results.

The acquisition of the US robotic e-skin company is at the definitive-agreement stage, and commercialization of e-skin technology is expected to determine medium-term growth. The company said smart leather revenue would become visible in 2027.

However, reaching an annual operating profit requires the fourth quarter to offset the cumulative operating loss of the first three quarters, so the target should be checked against full-year results.

Risk factors

Customer production and delivery timing

Quarterly revenue can swing sharply with automaker production schedules and safety issues. Q2 2026 revenue rose to KRW 31 billion, yet the operating loss widened. The company said volumes not supplied in the first half were deferred to the second half, and actual deliveries will need to confirm this.

Balance sheet and share dilution

Total liabilities at the end of 2025 were KRW 95.4 billion, with a debt-to-equity ratio of 72.7%. Reports said a KRW 30 billion warrant bond offering was fully subscribed in November 2025. Depending on the timing and size of exercise, existing shareholders' stakes could be diluted.

Execution of new businesses and use of funds

The acquisition of the US company is at the definitive-agreement stage, and the closing timing and earnings contribution must be confirmed through disclosures. In 2025 the company said about KRW 5.9 billion of the bond proceeds would go to acquiring a plant in Hwaseong and building an advanced post-processing facility. Whether new businesses can proceed alongside a recovery in the core business is the open question.

Overall view

Unichem's return to a Q3 2026 operating profit of KRW 651 million is explained by new-model deliveries combined with a normalization of production.

However, the operating losses since 2024, the losses in the first half of 2026, and three consecutive quarters of losses attributable to controlling shareholders mean the sustainability of the recovery is not yet demonstrated.

Bullish factors include order volume, new-model mass production and robotic e-skin opportunities from the Lumia acquisition. Bearish factors include reliance on automaker schedules, raw-hide costs, a rise in the debt-to-equity ratio and potential dilution.

The debt-to-equity ratio improved from 212.5% in 2022 to 72.7% in 2025, although operating cash flow in 2025 was negative. Because no earnings-based multiple can be calculated, valuation is better read through the share price relative to net assets and the capital trend.

The next checkpoints are the Q4 2026 preliminary results, the annual operating profit and net loss in the 2026 annual report, and the closing date of the Lumia acquisition.
