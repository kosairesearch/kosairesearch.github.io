Report overview

SolDefense: Order Growth Meets a Still-Thin Earnings Base

SolDefense reported KRW 10.7 billion in revenue and a KRW 1.05 billion operating profit, a return to profit, for the third quarter of 2026, yet its 2025 annual operating margin was only 4.9%, so how fast orders convert into earnings is the key point to track in the next periodic filings.

02

Business structure

SolDefense, founded in January 1999 and headquartered in Anyang, Gyeonggi Province, is a defense electronics control company. It designs and manufactures control component parts for guided weapons and avionics, and defense is the core of its business.

According to its 2025 annual report, the civilian segment supplies control boards for power plants, control parts for nuclear turbine generators, and control boards for railway systems, and civilian sales in 2025 were about KRW 900 million.

Its main products include the guidance and control computer (GCU) and navigation guidance unit (NGU) for the Chunmoo multiple rocket launcher.

It also supplies mission computers and multifunction displays for the KF-21 fighter, circuit card assemblies for the cockpit of the Light Armed Helicopter (LAH), and parts for FA-50 export aircraft. Its customers are large system integrators such as Hanwha Aerospace and Hanwha Systems.

In the 2025 annual report's customer table (separate basis), the top two customers accounted for 42.79% and 33.09%, or about 76% combined. The backlog recorded in the 2025 annual report was about KRW 33.9 billion, with avionics at about KRW 16.0 billion and guided weapons at about KRW 13.8 billion making up most of it.

Its subsidiary, which handles explosive ordnance disposal (EOD) equipment, won orders for 110 recoilless rifles in July 2026, bringing cumulative units to 234 and cumulative order value to about KRW 8.29 billion, according to the company.

The company is widening its portfolio beyond aviation and guided weapons by adding EOD equipment and development work on guidance and navigation control units (GNCU) for reusable launch vehicles.

Competitively, SolDefense is not a prime system maker but a supplier of control units and circuit parts to system integrators.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual revenue rose from KRW 15.7 billion in 2022 to KRW 16.3 billion in 2023 (up 3.8% year on year), KRW 21.8 billion in 2024 (up 33.9%), and KRW 26.8 billion in 2025 (up 23.0%). Revenue has grown for three consecutive years from 2023 through 2025, and 2025 was the largest year in the 2022 to 2025 period.

Operating losses of KRW 2.85 billion in 2022 and KRW 2.37 billion in 2023 gave way to an operating profit of KRW 3.03 billion in 2024, a turn to profit. Operating profit then fell 56.3% to KRW 1.32 billion in 2025.

The operating margin moved from -18.2% to -14.6% to 13.9% and then 4.9%, peaking in 2024 before declining in 2025. Because margins fell even as revenue grew, product mix and cost ratios look like the key variables.

Profit attributable to owners of the parent swung from a net loss of KRW 14.4 billion in 2023 to a net profit of KRW 11 billion in 2024, then fell 71.7% to KRW 3.1 billion in 2025.

In 2024 the parent-attributable profit was far larger than the operating profit of KRW 3.03 billion, which suggests items outside operations weighed heavily that year. Net income including non-controlling interests was a loss of KRW 14.4 billion in 2023, a profit of KRW 11 billion in 2024, and KRW 3.1 billion in 2025.

Operating cash flow moved from negative KRW 5.36 billion in 2022 and negative KRW 7.2 billion in 2023 to KRW 4.11 billion in 2024, then fell to KRW 1.18 billion in 2025. A Daily Union report noted that trade receivables rose from KRW 1.07 billion to KRW 3.18 billion over the same period.

Quarterly figures show a clearer trend. Revenue went from KRW 5.87 billion in the third quarter of 2025 to KRW 6.42 billion, KRW 8.69 billion, KRW 7.5 billion, and KRW 10.7 billion in the third quarter of 2026.

Operating results moved from an operating loss of KRW 194 million in the third quarter of 2025 to an operating profit of KRW 106 million in the fourth quarter, operating losses in the first and second quarters of 2026, and then an operating profit of KRW 1.05 billion in the third quarter of 2026.

The parent-attributable net loss of KRW 1.19 billion in the second quarter of 2026 was larger than the operating loss of KRW 446 million, suggesting non-operating costs widened the loss.

In the third quarter, parent-attributable net profit was only KRW 1.97 million, so the operating profit did little to lift net income. The parent-attributable net loss over the most recent four quarters totals KRW 649 million.

05

Industry analysis

In the domestic defense market, volume is distributed to suppliers as prime system makers expand exports. An industry official said that when large system makers expand global exports, subcontractors gain chances to win more orders. For the KF-21, about 65% of components are reported to be domestically produced.

Block 1 aircraft armed with air-to-air weapons are in production, and Block 2 aircraft that can carry air-to-ground missiles are scheduled for additional production. Press coverage expects follow-on orders for SolDefense as a result.

The company itself believes domestic equity flows concentrated in semiconductors have left defense stocks overlooked.

Competitively, SolDefense supplies parts to Hanwha Systems and Hanwha Aerospace, and its role differs from that of system makers in the same aerospace and defense group, such as Korea Aerospace Industries, LIG Defense&Aerospace and Hyundai-Rotem.

Its volume therefore depends on the order flow and export performance of the system makers. In cycle terms, 2026 has brought a run of contracts at the production stage. Remaining volume from the third LAH production batch is also expected to be ordered in sequence.

Defense volumes are, however, tied to the production schedules of specific platforms and are sensitive to timing shifts in orders.

06

Outlook

The core of the company's medium-term outlook is the NGU sub-system for the Chunmoo guided missile. The company expects mass production to begin in 2028 and projects about KRW 70 billion in new orders.

In the same announcement it expected about KRW 46.3 billion in avionics orders, including follow-on KF-21 production and the LAH, and said mass production of the integrated electronics unit for Chunmoo missiles could add about KRW 21 billion.

These are all company expectations, and the timing and value of formal contract disclosures will be the test of this outlook.

Confirmed contracts include the LAH third-batch circuit card assemblies worth KRW 8.62 billion in October 2026 (ending July 4, 2029), KF-21 mission computers worth KRW 1.72 billion in June 2026 (through April 20, 2027), and FA-50 export parts worth about KRW 4.27 billion in March 2026 (through January 9, 2030).

In a correction filed in November 2026, the end date of a Chunmoo launcher circuit card assembly contract with Hanwha Aerospace moved from February 10, 2027 to May 10, 2027, with the contract value of KRW 3.10 billion unchanged.

The EOD equipment business at the subsidiary and the GNCU development work for reusable launch vehicles were also presented as new growth areas. At an extraordinary shareholders' meeting on November 2, 2026, the re-election of director Lee Byung-kyung was approved, so the management structure continues.

The company says it will expand investor relations and public communication within the scope of what it can disclose. The next checkpoints are whether new orders such as the NGU convert into formal contracts, and whether quarterly operating profit keeps its profit run after 2026.

Whether widening the product range beyond defense spreads the earnings base is the point to watch.

Operating losses continued in the first and second quarters of 2026, and the parent-attributable net loss over the last four quarters totals KRW 649 million. Cash flow and dilution

In the second quarter of 2026 the parent-attributable net loss of KRW 1.19 billion exceeded the operating loss of KRW 446 million, leaving earnings quality as an open question.

A KRW 2.6 billion convertible bond was issued in 2025, a KRW 2.5 billion third-party rights offering was completed in March 2025, and convertible preferred shares were converted, so the share count may rise.

Risk factors

Governance and trading history

SolDefense's trading was suspended from February 2022 over alleged embezzlement and breach of trust by its former management, and trading resumed after the Korea Exchange's KOSDAQ committee decided to maintain its listing.

The company says it replaced the entire former management team, and director Lee Byung-kyung was re-elected at the November 2026 extraordinary shareholders' meeting. Management stability will be judged alongside the earnings recovery, so it needs ongoing monitoring.

Contract timing and customer dependence

A November 2026 correction moved the end date of the Chunmoo launcher circuit card assembly contract to May 10, 2027. Because the main customers are system makers, delivery and the timing of recognized results can slip with their ordering schedules. With NGU mass production set for 2028, there is a long lag before new orders show up in reported results.

Potential share dilution

A KRW 2.6 billion convertible bond was issued in September 2025, and in March 2025 a third-party rights offering of KRW 2.5 billion and the conversion of redeemable convertible preferred shares took place. With operating cash flow declining, a larger number of conversion shares could weigh on per-share measures.

Conversion terms and future share count changes can be checked in periodic reports and material event disclosures.

Overall view

SolDefense is a defense electronics control company that supplies avionics and guided-weapon parts to large system makers. Order disclosures continued through 2026, and the third quarter brought revenue of KRW 10.7 billion and an operating profit of KRW 1.05 billion, a return to profit.

In the annual filings, revenue grew for three straight years from 2023 to 2025, but operating profit fell to KRW 1.32 billion in 2025 and the operating margin stayed at 4.9%. On the balance sheet, the debt-to-equity ratio fell from 61.9% in 2023 to 15.0% in 2025, and total equity stands at KRW 52.2 billion.

On the other side, the four-quarter total still shows a parent-attributable net loss, and the direction of operating cash flow and trade receivables is mixed.

On the business side, the NGU sub-system, follow-on KF-21 production and remaining LAH volume form the core of the company's outlook, but all depend on formal contracts and the 2028 production schedule.

Customer concentration, the trading suspension history and share count changes including convertible bonds deserve equal attention. The current data point to a stage in which the conversion of orders into reported earnings, and whether annual profit can sustain a profitable run, will decide the outcome.

The next checkpoint is the 2026 annual results and year-end backlog in the annual report due in late March 2027.
