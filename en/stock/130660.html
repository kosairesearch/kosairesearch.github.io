Korea Electronic Power Industrial Development: Nuclear O&M Orders and the Profit Recovery Test

Korea Electronic Power Industrial Development runs power plant operation and maintenance as its core business. Revenue rose three years in a row through 2025, but the operating margin fell to 1.7% in 2025, and quarterly operating profit has stayed positive since the fourth quarter of 2025.

02

Business structure

Korea Electronic Power Industrial Development mainly provides operation and maintenance services for power plant equipment. Its power generation business covers operation and maintenance of fuel, desulfurization and ash-handling systems, while other businesses include internet billing, ESCO and ESS.

Power generation accounts for most sales, at about 93% of second-quarter 2026 revenue by one data provider's count, with other businesses at about 7%. In the first half of 2022, power generation revenue was KRW 150.22 billion and other business revenue was KRW 15.93 billion.

The business has been built on stable public-sector generation clients.

On competition, the company cites its years of construction experience and skilled technical staff, and has strengthened technical registration, R&D and training ahead of the competitive operation and maintenance market for the five state-owned generation companies.

In nuclear work, the company disclosed a KRW 27.59 billion contract with the state-owned nuclear operator for operation and maintenance of water treatment facilities at the Hanbit nuclear site, signed on August 26, 2026. The contract runs from September 1, 2026 to August 31, 2029, about two years and eleven months. Because public-sector buyers account for a large share, the business is sensitive to procurement policy.

04

Earnings analysis

On a reported basis, revenue rose from KRW 346 billion in 2022 to KRW 394.5 billion in 2025. It went to KRW 363.3 billion in 2023 (up 5.0%), KRW 367.5 billion in 2024 (up 1.2%) and KRW 394.5 billion in 2025 (up 7.4%), three consecutive years of growth from 2023 to 2025.

Operating profit peaked at KRW 26 billion in 2023 (up 39.6%), then fell to KRW 16.1 billion in 2024 (down 38.0%) and KRW 6.57 billion in 2025 (down 59.3%), two consecutive years of decline. The operating margin moved from 5.4% in 2022 to 7.2% in 2023, then to 4.4% in 2024 and 1.7% in 2025.

Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 16.3 billion in 2022, KRW 15.5 billion in 2023, KRW 11.6 billion in 2024 and KRW 4.06 billion in 2025, three consecutive years of decline. Net income including non-controlling interests shows the same yearly figures, so no gap appears between the two measures.

The debt ratio rose from 64.4% in 2022 to 96.8% in 2023, then 90.3% in 2024 and 98.5% in 2025. At the end of 2025, total equity was KRW 103.1 billion, of which controlling shareholders held KRW 103.3 billion, and total liabilities were KRW 101.5 billion.

Operating cash flow was KRW 16.1 billion in 2022, KRW 29.6 billion in 2023 and KRW 35.7 billion in 2024, then turned to -KRW 8.02 billion in 2025.

Quarterly, the third quarter of 2025 showed revenue of KRW 90.6 billion, an operating loss of KRW 16.4 billion and a net loss attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 12.9 billion. The fourth quarter of 2025 recorded revenue of KRW 104.4 billion and operating profit of KRW 1.72 billion, a swing to profit.

The first quarter of 2026 had revenue of KRW 107.4 billion and operating profit of KRW 6.33 billion, and the second quarter rose to revenue of KRW 124.7 billion (up 16.1% from the prior quarter) and operating profit of KRW 15.9 billion.

The third quarter of 2026 recorded revenue of KRW 102.7 billion (down 17.6% from the prior quarter, up 13.4% year on year) and operating profit of KRW 4.83 billion, a swing to profit from a year earlier.

Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 2.48 billion in the third quarter of 2026, and the total for the last four quarters (Q4 2025 to Q3 2026) was KRW 20.2 billion.

05

Industry analysis

Demand for domestic nuclear maintenance and power plant operation is driven by operating reactors and the need to manage aging equipment. Operation and maintenance contracts, such as the Hanbit water treatment work, run alongside the life of the plant, so orders do not end with a single award.

In early 2026, reports said the share price hit a record high on expectations for Korea-US nuclear cooperation. That upside depends on overseas nuclear orders, and revenue impact follows when contracts are disclosed.

The competitive operation and maintenance market for the five state-owned generation companies is a key variable. Competition could change the public-sector-centered order structure and pricing. The KRW 27.59 billion Hanbit contract equals about 6.99% of 2025 revenue of KRW 394.5 billion.

Expanding from power plant maintenance into nuclear operation and maintenance is cited as a positive factor. On the negative side, a 2025 operating margin of 1.7% is low. The sector picture contains both positive and negative factors.

Bull factors

Nuclear O&M contract widens the order base

The KRW 27.59 billion Hanbit water treatment operation and maintenance contract, equal to about 6.99% of revenue, was disclosed with a contract period running to August 31, 2029. The long term improves visibility on maintenance revenue. Expansion from power plant maintenance into nuclear maintenance can be read as a sign of structural change.

Quarterly operating profit has stayed positive

Operating profit turned positive at KRW 1.72 billion in the fourth quarter of 2025 and stayed in profit through the third quarter of 2026. Second-quarter 2026 operating profit rose to KRW 15.9 billion.

Profit attributable to controlling shareholders over the last four quarters totals KRW 20.2 billion, above the KRW 4.06 billion recorded for 2025 as a full year.

Public-sector client base and competitive readiness

A business built on stable public-sector generation clients supports the stability of orders. The company points to years of construction experience and skilled technical staff as strengths, and has invested in technical capacity and R&D ahead of more competitive conditions.

Early-2026 share price strength tied to expectations for Korea-US nuclear cooperation also shows market interest.

Bear factors

Declining operating margin

The operating margin fell from 7.2% in 2023 to 4.4% in 2024 and 1.7% in 2025. Operating profit fell 59.3% to KRW 6.57 billion in 2025, and profit attributable to controlling shareholders fell 65.0% to KRW 4.06 billion. Revenue rose three years in a row, but profit did not keep pace, which is the largest negative factor.

Cash flow deficit and financial burden

Operating cash flow fell from KRW 35.7 billion in 2024 to -KRW 8.02 billion in 2025. The debt ratio rose from 64.4% in 2022 to 98.5% in 2025. With total equity of KRW 103.1 billion and total liabilities of KRW 101.5 billion at the end of 2025, profit recovery is needed to build financial room.

Maintenance market competition and quarterly volatility

If competition is introduced into the operation and maintenance market of the five state-owned generation companies, the public-sector-centered order structure could change. Third-quarter 2026 revenue of KRW 102.7 billion fell 17.6% from the prior quarter, showing quarterly volatility.

The KRW 27.59 billion nuclear contract is small relative to annual revenue and cannot change the earnings direction on its own.

10

Risk factors

Profitability risk

If the operating margin stays near the 1.7% recorded in 2025 even as revenue grows, the earnings base weakens. How cost changes flow into operating profit is the key issue. Even if quarterly profit stays positive in 2026, a repeat of 2025-level margins would limit the recovery.

Policy and competition risk

The timing and form of competition in the power plant operation and maintenance market depend on policy. Because public-sector clients account for a large share, changes in procurement terms or unit prices can affect results quickly. Heavier competition could change how existing power plant maintenance volume is allocated.

Financial and cash flow risk

A 98.5% debt ratio and operating cash flow of -KRW 8.02 billion in 2025 can increase working capital pressure. The timing of payment collection on nuclear maintenance contracts will determine how fast cash flow improves. Because quarterly revenue fluctuates, the predictability of cash inflows is also limited.
