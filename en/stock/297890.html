02

Business structure

HB Solution makes and sells inspection, measurement and manufacturing equipment for displays and semiconductors. The company was founded in September 2001 and has expanded from its material-analysis and equipment-building know-how into front-end and back-end display equipment.

Front-end products include ultra-precision measurement tools such as thin-film thickness gauges, color meters and vision/3D analyzers, while back-end products cover inspection, assembly and coating automation for module production.

In a revenue breakdown compiled by the online financial data site Alphasquare, coating automation equipment (back-end) accounted for about 64%, other items (goods and customer service) about 24%, and front-end measurement equipment about 12%; the site did not state the reference period.

Past press coverage cited resin coating and bonding, inspection and material-analysis technology as core strengths. In August 2021 the company absorbed KAIMAC, a thin-film thickness gauge specialist, and adopted the HB Solution name.

Customers centre on Chinese and Korean display panel makers; Samsung Display received initial inkjet equipment supplies in the second half of 2021. In 2026 the company disclosed a KRW 15.6 billion contract with Chengdu BOE Display Technology and a KRW 24.7 billion contract with Guangzhou China Star Optoelectronics.

Export share was reported at 90.8% in coverage of first-half 2024 results. A company official said only contracts above 10% of prior-year revenue are disclosed, and smaller deals continue steadily. Late-2025 coverage also reported that the company is pursuing entry into semiconductor equipment and secondary batteries.

04

Earnings analysis

On an annual basis, revenue moved from KRW 187.6 billion in 2022 to KRW 96.2 billion in 2023, KRW 131.5 billion in 2024 and KRW 62.8 billion in 2025, which is the smallest year in the 2022–2025 span. The company attributed the 2025 decline to lower order intake.

Operating profit was KRW 27.1 billion in 2022, KRW 6.76 billion in 2023, KRW 19.3 billion in 2024 and KRW 4.4 billion in 2025. The operating margin was 14.5%, 7.0%, 14.7% and 7.0% over the same years, alternating between higher and lower years.

Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 43.6 billion in 2022 and KRW 75.6 billion in 2023, swung to a net loss of KRW 20.8 billion in 2024, and returned to a profit of KRW 26.5 billion in 2025. Total net profit including non-controlling interests followed the same path in 2025.

The company explained the 2025 swing to profit by pointing to fair-value gains on financial assets, which lifted pre-tax profit even as operating profit fell, so 2025 net profit reflects non-operating valuation effects more than an operating recovery.

By quarter, the third quarter of 2025 showed revenue of KRW 6.36 billion and an operating loss of KRW 1.97 billion. The fourth quarter of 2025 showed revenue of KRW 6.35 billion, an operating loss of KRW 326 million, and a net loss attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 407 million.

Revenue in the first quarter of 2026 reached KRW 14.7 billion, up 131.5% from the prior quarter, but the operating loss widened to KRW 1.13 billion. In the second quarter of 2026 revenue was KRW 10.2 billion and the operating loss narrowed to KRW 503 million.

The third quarter of 2026 brought revenue of KRW 16.2 billion and operating profit of KRW 2.67 billion, a swing to profit, with revenue up 154.6% year on year.

Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders for that quarter was KRW 10.2 billion, well above operating profit, which points to a sizeable non-operating component. Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders over the last four quarters totals KRW 40.2 billion.

05

Industry analysis

HB Solution's revenue has depended heavily on display investment cycles. Its first-half 2022 results were attributed to ELB equipment sales to Samsung Display. A 2025 analyst report said equipment supply for Korea's 8.6-generation IT OLED investment was largely completed in 2024.

In 2025, 8.6-generation OLED front-end equipment has been supplied mainly to Chinese customers. With the 8.6-generation IT OLED cycle winding down, large-scale domestic display investment is widely seen as having run its course.

Alongside this view, the company is expanding into semiconductor and secondary-battery equipment. Demand for ELB equipment, which applies light-leak prevention coating and inspection, depends on foldable smartphone launches and wider OLED adoption in tablets, according to a 2025 analyst report.

The same report projected roughly 8–10 million foldable phone shipments in 2026, while noting that design specifications were not yet fixed, making equipment demand hard to size. In an annual 2025 ranking compiled by Alphasquare, HB Solution ranked 35th among display and component companies by operating profit.

Continued 8.6-generation OLED investment in China is cited as a demand driver for front-end equipment. The 2025 report named whether the company enters the semiconductor equipment market as a variable that could determine any re-rating.

In competition, the company's edge is framed around differentiation in resin coating and inspection and material-analysis technology.

Outlook

A 2025 Korea IR council report projected HB Solution's 2026 revenue at KRW 97.1 billion and operating profit at KRW 10.1 billion. These are securities-analyst estimates from late 2025, not company guidance.

Quarterly revenue in each of the first three quarters of 2026 exceeded KRW 10 billion, and operating results moved from losses in the first and second quarters to a profit in the third.

A contract with Guangzhou China Star Optoelectronics disclosed on July 7, 2026 is worth KRW 24.7 billion, runs until March 4, 2027, and equals 39.28% of recent revenue. It was the first supply-contract disclosure of 2026, and none had been disclosed in the same period of 2025.

A contract with Chengdu BOE Display Technology disclosed on November 5, 2026 is worth KRW 15.6 billion and runs until December 30, 2026. It covers self-produced equipment shipped to China, with payment after shipment.

Press coverage noted that the full contract value will not necessarily be recognized as fourth-quarter revenue, since recognition depends on delivery and acceptance.

Late-2025 coverage reported the company aimed to consolidate its headquarters and plant in Sejong City in the first quarter of 2026, a move expected to raise capacity and streamline staffing. Whether the relocation delivers that effect remains to be seen in later filings.

The pace of foldable smartphone launches and ELB equipment demand remain key swing factors. The next point to check is the full-year 2026 operating margin and the composition of non-operating income in the 2026 annual report.

Bull factors

Contract disclosures resume in 2026

In July 2026 the company disclosed a KRW 24.7 billion equipment contract with Guangzhou China Star Optoelectronics, its first supply-contract disclosure of that year. In November it disclosed a KRW 15.6 billion contract with Chengdu BOE Display Technology, equal to 24.9% of 2025 consolidated revenue. Both show Chinese display investment translating into equipment orders.

Swing to quarterly operating profit

Revenue in the third quarter of 2026 was KRW 16.2 billion, up 59.2% from the prior quarter, and operating profit was KRW 2.67 billion, a turn to profit. Revenue rose 154.6% year on year. Against operating losses in the first and second quarters of 2026, the result marks a change in the quarterly profit pattern.

Capital base and low leverage

At the end of 2025 total equity was KRW 247.3 billion and total liabilities KRW 61.6 billion, giving a debt ratio of 24.9%, down from 50.0% in 2022. With the share price below book value of equity, the thick capital base is cited as a point in the bull case for financial flexibility.

Bear factors

Large swings in annual results

Revenue fell from KRW 187.6 billion in 2022 to KRW 62.8 billion in 2025, with a particularly sharp drop in 2023. Operating profit in 2025 was KRW 4.4 billion, down 77.3%. Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders swung to a KRW 20.8 billion net loss in 2024 before returning to profit in 2025. Such swings show results closely tied to the timing of orders.

Unstable quarterly results

Revenue in the first quarter of 2026 rose to KRW 14.7 billion, but the operating loss widened to KRW 1.13 billion. Revenue then fell 30.8% to KRW 10.2 billion in the second quarter.

Because quarterly revenue has moved sharply from one quarter to the next, quarterly profit can be easily disturbed by when contracts are recognized.

Reliance on non-operating valuation gains

The company pointed to fair-value gains on financial assets as the reason 2025 turned to profit, even though operating profit fell to KRW 4.4 billion that year. In the third quarter of 2026, net profit attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 10.2 billion again well exceeded operating profit of KRW 2.67 billion. Whether operating profit recovers on its own is the key test of earnings quality.

10

Risk factors

Display investment cycle and customer concentration

Supply for Korea's 8.6-generation IT OLED investment was largely completed in 2024, and orders since 2025 have centered on Chinese customers. Heavier dependence on a specific region and customer means any change in Chinese panel makers' investment schedules feeds directly into revenue. This structure is likely to persist until new business lines contribute.

Disclosure threshold and revenue timing

The company says only contracts above 10% of prior-year revenue are disclosed, so the scale of smaller undisclosed contracts is hard to gauge from outside. The BOE contract runs only until December 30, 2026, and recognition could shift depending on acceptance and inspection.

Export exposure, currency and payment terms

A high export share leaves results more exposed to currency moves. The BOE contract value is set in U.S. dollars and converted at the first posted exchange rate on November 4, 2026. Payment after shipment means currency moves and collection timing can affect both results and cash flow.

Overall view

HB Solution's annual revenue and operating profit have swung sharply since 2022, with 2025 the smallest year at KRW 62.8 billion in revenue and KRW 4.4 billion in operating profit. The return to net profit in 2025 owed much to fair-value gains on financial assets, as the company itself explained.

In 2026, equipment contracts worth KRW 24.7 billion and KRW 15.6 billion were disclosed with Chinese display makers, and operating profit turned positive in the third quarter.

However, operating losses persisted in the first and second quarters, and net profit attributable to controlling shareholders in the third quarter again well exceeded operating profit, so the earnings mix is complex.

On the balance sheet, the debt ratio was a low 24.9% at the end of 2025 and total equity stood at KRW 247.3 billion. The share price sits below book value of equity, but earnings-based multiples read differently depending on non-operating valuation gains.

The next points to check are the full-year 2026 operating margin in the annual report and the revenue recognition timing of the BOE and Guangzhou China Star contracts.
