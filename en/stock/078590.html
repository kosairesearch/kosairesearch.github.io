02

Business structure

Hyulim A-Tech makes automotive interior materials, and most of its disclosed revenue comes from automotive parts. According to a corporate profile dataset, revenue is split into carpets at 34%, trunk trim at 26%, wheel guards at 20%, undercovers at 14% and other items at 5%.

Carpets are the largest product line, and the company is developing eco-friendly woven PET and knit carpets as well as hidden-rail carpets that make better use of interior space in autonomous vehicles. On the demand side, tighter emission rules are expected to lift parts demand for hybrid and electric vehicles.

However, the core carpet and trunk trim lines have seen weaker domestic demand and higher cost burdens, which has pressured profitability. In 2025, price increases for fabric and felt materials were not passed through to supply prices, and higher fixed costs followed the operating transfer of the Asan plant.

At the regular shareholders meeting in March 2026, 16 robotics-related business purposes were added, and in May 2026 the company announced the acquisition of a robotics business division. The acquired assets include the HerkuleX smart actuator brand and the TETRA series of autonomous mobile robot platforms.

On defense, the articles of incorporation include business purposes such as manufacturing military robot parts and developing landmine clearance equipment, but an April 2026 press report said most of the added robotics businesses had not yet begun operating.

At an extraordinary shareholders meeting on November 13, 2026, the addition of 11 business purposes, including precious metal manufacturing and sales, was approved.

04

Earnings analysis

Based on reported results, revenue rose sharply from KRW 43.5 billion in 2022 to KRW 63.7 billion in 2023 (up 46.2% year on year), and reached KRW 70.3 billion in 2024 (up 10.5% year on year), the highest of the 2022–2025 period. Revenue in 2025 was KRW 64.4 billion, down 8.5% from the previous year.

Operating profit peaked at KRW 4.81 billion in 2023 (up 86.0% year on year), then fell for two consecutive years: to KRW 3.98 billion in 2024 (down 17.3% year on year) and KRW 0.25 billion in 2025 (down 93.8% year on year). The operating margin declined from 5.9% in 2022, 7.6% in 2023 and 5.7% in 2024 to 0.4% in 2025.

Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders kept shrinking, from KRW 9.73 billion in 2022 to KRW 8.2 billion in 2023 (down 15.7% year on year) and KRW 4.6 billion in 2024 (down 43.9% year on year), before turning to a net loss attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 11.1 billion in 2025.

The company cited a decline in lump-sum mold sales as the cause of the 2025 revenue decrease, and the revenue decline together with higher SG&A expenses as the cause of the operating profit decrease.

The press added that increases in raw material prices were not reflected in unit delivery prices and that the Asan plant's fixed-cost burden weighed on results. Non-operating items were also a heavy burden.

One report said non-operating losses including interest expense reached about KRW 10 billion at the end of 2025, and an analysis said derivative valuation losses were recorded at KRW 6.363 billion.

On the balance sheet, total liabilities at the end of 2025 were KRW 32.7 billion and total equity was KRW 48.9 billion (all attributable to controlling shareholders), while operating cash flow fell from KRW 11.2 billion in 2023 to KRW 3.96 billion in 2024 and KRW 0.49 billion in 2025.

On a quarterly basis, revenue peaked at KRW 16.3 billion in Q4 2025 (up 9.5% quarter on quarter), then declined for three consecutive quarters: KRW 15.8 billion in Q1 2026, KRW 14.6 billion in Q2 and KRW 12.2 billion in Q3 (down 17.8% year on year).

The company has posted operating losses for five consecutive quarters since an operating loss of KRW 0.17 billion in Q3 2025, and the operating loss in Q3 2026 is KRW 1.36 billion. The combined net loss attributable to controlling shareholders over the most recent four quarters is KRW 15.7 billion.

05

Industry analysis

The automotive interior materials market that anchors Hyulim A-Tech's core business has seen domestic demand recovery delayed. A corporate monitoring report summarized tighter regulation and rising household debt as factors slowing that recovery.

Weaker demand for carpets and trunk trim, together with higher cost burdens, is cited as the main driver of the earnings decline.

On the other side, the expansion of the eco-friendly vehicle market is raising demand for battery and motor-related parts, and the company says it is building price competitiveness through quality gains and cost reduction.

A forecast also points to rising demand for hybrid and electric vehicle parts as emission rules tighten. In cycle terms, reported revenue has declined and losses have continued since 2025, so a demand recovery has not yet shown up in the revenue trend.

Listed companies grouped under aerospace and defense include Hanwha Aerospace, Korea Aerospace Industries, LIG Defense&Aerospace and Hanwha Systems, as well as Hyundai-Rotem on the defense side and Korean AIR Lines and Asiana Airlines on the airline side.

Compared with these companies, Hyulim A-Tech does not separately report defense revenue, and its defense and robotics activities remain at the level of business purposes in its articles of incorporation.

As an auto parts maker, its earnings depend on vehicle production and domestic demand, and on whether it can pass raw material price increases through to customers.

06

Outlook

Following the robotics division acquisition, the company has said it will shift from simple parts supply toward an integrated intelligent robot solutions provider.

Its target markets are smart factories of global large companies, as well as bio (GMP) and smart farm applications, and it highlights physical AI technology and a high-payload AMR platform as strengths. The next checkpoint is how much revenue the acquired HerkuleX actuator and TETRA series will add to reported results.

On the financing side, a 1-for-10 reverse stock split was completed on May 6, 2026. A rights offering with a general public offering of unsubscribed shares followed, and based on press reports it was planned to issue 6 million new shares at an indicative price of KRW 4,915 to raise about KRW 29.5 billion.

The company has said KRW 10 billion of the proceeds will go first toward early redemption of its 16th convertible bond series. Press reports said the largest shareholder, Hyulim ROBOT, was expected to take up only about 25% of its allotment, and that its stake would fall to about 18.4% if the offering is completed.

At an extraordinary shareholders meeting on November 13, 2026, 11 business purposes including precious metal businesses were added, but the timing of revenue from these businesses has not yet appeared in disclosures. The company also said it will expand facility investment to raise production efficiency.

The next steps are the 2026 fourth-quarter preliminary results and the 2026 annual report, where operating margin and any revenue contribution from the new businesses will be visible.

Bull factors

Capital structure reset under way

A 1-for-10 reverse stock split was completed in May 2026, reducing pressure tied to listing maintenance requirements. A rights offering of about KRW 29.5 billion is planned to fund early redemption of the 16th convertible bond series and working capital. If the capital raise is executed, there is room for debt pressure to ease.

Robotics assets secured

The May 2026 robotics division acquisition brought the HerkuleX smart actuator brand and the TETRA AMR platform series. Sixteen robotics-related business purposes were added to the articles of incorporation in March 2026, broadening the direction from automotive parts toward integrated robot solutions. Smart factories of large companies are the stated target market.

Room for margin recovery

The operating margin reached 7.6% in 2023, so there is room to recover if cost and selling expenses are reduced. Net income attributable to controlling shareholders turned positive at KRW 10.45 million in the second quarter of 2026. Rising demand for eco-friendly vehicle parts is also cited as a backdrop for an improved earnings mix.

Bear factors

Core business losses continue

The operating margin fell to 0.4% in 2025. Operating losses have continued for five straight quarters from the third quarter of 2025 through the third quarter of 2026, and the third-quarter 2026 operating loss widened to KRW 1.36 billion.

Revenue has also fallen for three straight quarters since the first quarter of 2026, which weakens the case for a core business recovery.

Financial burden and weaker cash generation

The debt ratio rose from 26.9% in 2022 to 66.7% in 2025. Total liabilities were KRW 32.7 billion at the end of 2025, and operating cash flow fell from KRW 11.2 billion in 2023 to KRW 485 million in 2025. Press reports said the interest coverage ratio dropped to about 0.1x in 2025.

Weak demand and unproven new businesses

Slower recovery in domestic auto demand has reduced sales of carpets and trunk trim. The robotics, defense and precious metal businesses remain at the level of business purposes in the articles, and their revenue does not appear separately in reported results.

The decline in the stake held by the largest shareholder, Hyulim ROBOT, is also cited as a source of governance uncertainty.

10

Risk factors

Liquidity and dilution

Following 180 billion won of 14th and 15th convertible bonds in 2024, a KRW 10 billion 16th series was issued in January 2026. Analyses note that convertible bonds and derivative-linked liabilities account for a large share of short-term debt. If the rights offering proceeds, existing shareholders will face dilution from the increase in share count.

Facility investment burden

Facility investment has been set out as a means of raising production efficiency, but once spending begins it could add to equipment burdens while operating cash flow has already declined.

If part of the rights offering proceeds goes first to convertible bond redemption, the funds available for investment could be constrained. If the investment is delayed or efficiency gains do not translate into cost reductions, the narrowing of core business losses could also be slower.

Raw material pass-through

If prices of raw materials such as fabric and felt rise again and cannot be reflected in supply prices, margins on carpets and trunk trim could be squeezed further. Earnings in the auto parts business depend on whether raw material price increases can be passed through to supply prices.

During any period of delayed pass-through, cost pressure would flow directly into operating profit and could slow the recovery of the operating margin.

Overall view

Hyulim A-Tech is classed under aerospace and defense, but its revenue still centers on automotive interior materials. The operating margin fell to 0.4% in 2025, and the company swung to a net loss attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 11.1 billion.

Revenue has declined for three straight quarters through the third quarter of 2026, and operating losses have continued for five straight quarters.

On the financial side, the reverse split and the planned rights offering are reshaping the capital base, but a 66.7% debt ratio and lower operating cash flow remain burdens.

The robotics division acquisition and the added defense and precious metal business purposes show the direction of the business, but revenue from these areas does not yet appear separately in periodic reports.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders turned positive in the second quarter of 2026 but returned to a KRW 4.09 billion loss in the third quarter.

With no current PER, valuation is read through the relationship between net assets and market capitalization, and the pace of capital restructuring and core business recovery is the key to interpretation.

The next checkpoints are the 2026 fourth-quarter preliminary results and the 2026 annual report, covering operating margin, debt structure and whether new-business revenue appears.
