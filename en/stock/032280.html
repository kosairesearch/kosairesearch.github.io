Business structure

SamIl's business is split into a logistics unit that transports domestic cargo volumes, a cargo-handling unit for container work, a terminal unit that earns parking and leasing income, and a fuel unit that sells petroleum products purchased from refiners.

Its activities include general cargo trucking, dump truck transport, freight brokerage and warehousing. The segment mix differs by source.

The K5 company profile puts business revenue at 65.94%, handling revenue at 28.37% and fuel revenue at 5.7%, while JobKorea, as of March 2026, shows transport and terminal at 70%, handling at 27% and fuel at 3%. Both sources show transport and handling making up most sales, with fuel in single digits.

Annual sales were KRW 100 billion in 2025. At the end of that year, total assets were KRW 103.2 billion, total liabilities were KRW 39 billion and total equity was KRW 64.3 billion.

Listed peers in transport and logistics include CJ Logistics, HANJIN Logistics, SUN Kwang and Sebang, and SamIl is a small-to-mid-sized trucking and handling operator next to these much larger logistics groups. Transport and handling income depends on cargo volumes and the utilization of its facilities.

The fuel unit is small, but oil price moves can affect unit sales prices and margins. Vehicle operating costs and labor make up a large share of costs, so wage and fuel burdens pass quickly into margins.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual sales rose from KRW 92 billion in 2022 to KRW 99.7 billion in 2023 (up 8.4% year on year) and KRW 105.1 billion in 2024 (up 5.5%), then fell to KRW 100 billion in 2025 (down 4.9%), with 2024 the largest year of the period.

Operating profit was KRW 1.8 billion in 2022, KRW 1.72 billion in 2023 (down 4.2%), KRW 2.47 billion in 2024 (up 43.7%) and KRW 2.13 billion in 2025 (down 13.9%), and the operating margin moved within a range of 1.7% to 2.4%.

In 2024 operating profit rose while net income attributable to controlling shareholders fell 56.3% to KRW 1.24 billion, so the two measures moved in opposite directions.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders peaked at KRW 2.83 billion in 2023 (up 129.5%), then declined in both 2024 and 2025, when it reached KRW 961 million, the smallest year of the period. Net income including non-controlling interests moved in the same direction.

On the balance sheet, the debt-to-equity ratio fell to 60.6% at the end of 2025 from 70.9% in 2024, with total equity of KRW 64.3 billion and total liabilities of KRW 39 billion.

Operating cash flow was KRW 918 million in 2022, KRW 8.02 billion in 2023, KRW 3.72 billion in 2024 and KRW 2.59 billion in 2025, positive in all four years.

Quarterly sales bottomed at KRW 24.4 billion in the 2026 Q1, then rose to KRW 25.9 billion in Q2 (up 6.3% from the prior quarter) and KRW 27.1 billion in Q3 (up 4.5% from the prior quarter and up 4.0% year on year), two straight quarters of growth.

Operating profit, by contrast, fell to KRW 238 million in the 2026 Q3, down 53.7% from the prior quarter and 62.9% from a year earlier, so the sales recovery did not carry through to profit.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders swung from a KRW 101 million loss in the 2025 Q4 to KRW 791 million in the 2026 Q1, then fell in Q2 to KRW 371 million and in Q3 to KRW 38.2 million.

In the 2026 Q1, net income attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 791 million exceeded operating profit of KRW 424 million, so whether non-operating items drove the gap should be checked against the detailed line items in the filings.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders over the last four quarters, from the 2025 Q4 to the 2026 Q3, totals KRW 1.1 billion.

Industry analysis

SamIl's earnings move with cargo volumes, freight rates and handling demand within the cycle of the domestic transport and logistics industry.

The information service FnGuide summarized that, on a cumulative basis through the 2025 Q3, standalone sales fell 4.7% year on year and operating profit fell 22.9%, and characterized profitability as limited by industry conditions. The same source reported that net income declined 13.2%.

Sales turned up for two straight quarters in 2026, but 2026 Q3 operating profit fell 62.9% from a year earlier, so the profitability recovery remains limited.

Competition in transport and logistics includes listed logistics companies such as CJ Logistics, HANJIN Logistics, SUN Kwang and Sebang, along with many small and mid-sized trucking firms.

Compared with large integrated logistics groups, SamIl's sales base is small, and because its handling and terminal businesses are tied to specific sites and equipment, the utilization of those assets has a visible effect on results.

Manufacturing cargo volumes are generally a main source of demand, so industrial production trends and shipment volumes are key variables. Vehicle operating costs and labor make up a large share of transport costs, so wage and fuel burdens pass directly into operating margins.

Whether the decline in the 2026 Q3 operating profit came from volume or cost factors is a question to settle using the detailed disclosures in the filings.

Risk factors

Cargo volumes and site utilization

Transport and handling income depends on cargo volumes. Because handling and terminal work is tied to specific sites and equipment, a drop in volume can quickly lower utilization and hit profit. The sharp fall in 2026 Q3 operating profit illustrates this sensitivity.

Fuel prices and cost pressure

Depending on the source, the fuel unit accounts for roughly 3% to 6% of sales. Although small, oil price changes can affect unit prices and margins. Vehicle operating and labor costs are a large share of expenses, so wage or fuel increases can squeeze margins quickly.

Small-cap liquidity and supply-demand

As a small-cap stock, SamIl may see large share price swings. The largest shareholder group holds 34.33% according to FnGuide, which limits the free float. Supply and demand may shift after the buyback period ends on January 27, 2027, which could affect the share price.
