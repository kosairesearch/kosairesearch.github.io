OLED Inspection Specialist: Turnaround Meets Quarterly Slowdown

02

Business structure

HB Technology was founded in 1997 to produce LCD and AMOLED inspection equipment and listed on KOSDAQ in 2004. The business is split into an equipment division and a components and materials division.

The equipment division makes OLED front-end AOI (automated optical inspection) equipment, laser repair equipment, and integrated exterior inspection equipment for secondary batteries, while the components division manufactures light guide plates and diffusers for display backlight units.

A Korean financial news report put the product mix at roughly 80% display and 20% secondary battery. The same report said equipment accounts for about 40 to 50% of sales, with components making up the rest.

Secondary battery equipment supply began in 2019, and the company was described as having expanded into semiconductors in 2024. Major customers are Samsung Display, Samsung Electronics and BOE, and the company has also begun trading with Chinese manufacturers such as BOE.

Its long relationship with the Samsung group is a strength, but that high dependence has been described as a double-edged sword. A Shinhan Investment Corp. report from 2024 judged that the company holds a dominant position in OLED inspection and called it an all-rounder among equipment makers.

On the battery side, a news report said the company has secured major Korean battery makers as customers. Glass substrate inspection equipment is described as an area where inspection and repair tools matter more because of higher process difficulty.

04

Earnings analysis

On an annual basis, sales fell from KRW 148.1 billion in 2022 to KRW 111.6 billion in 2023 (down 24.6% year on year), then rose to KRW 162.4 billion in 2024 (up 45.4%), which was the largest year of 2022 to 2025. Sales were KRW 160.3 billion in 2025 (down 1.3%), and 2023 was the smallest year in that span.

Operating profit was KRW 6.86 billion in 2022, then turned into an operating loss of KRW 22.1 billion in 2023, a swing from profit to loss. The loss continued in 2024 at KRW 16.5 billion, with the loss narrowing.

In 2025 the company returned to operating profit of KRW 6.24 billion, and the operating margin was 3.9%, following 4.6% in 2022, -19.8% in 2023 and -10.2% in 2024.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 79.3 billion in 2023 (up 191.4%), then became a net loss of KRW 51.6 billion in 2024, and returned to profit of KRW 28.4 billion in 2025.

For the 2024 loss, a Korean news report pointed to the fair-value marking of the company's stake in a fund tied to HPSP, which affected the income statement; it described this as a book-value change rather than a realized loss (April 2025 report).

Because operating and net results did not move in the same direction, non-operating items appear to have carried significant weight. Quarterly sales peaked at KRW 51.8 billion in the fourth quarter of 2025, then fell to KRW 36.3 billion in the first quarter of 2026 (down 29.9% from the prior quarter).

After KRW 28.5 billion in the second quarter, sales were KRW 28.2 billion in the third quarter of 2026 with an operating loss of KRW 3.08 billion, a swing into loss, and a widening of the loss versus the year-earlier quarter.

Operating cash flow improved from KRW -14.7 billion in 2023 to KRW 3.08 billion in 2024 and KRW 12.4 billion in 2025. Quarterly net income attributable to controlling shareholders peaked at KRW 18.2 billion in the first quarter of 2026 and declined for two consecutive quarters to KRW 9.35 billion in the third quarter.

05

Industry analysis

The core demand driver in the downstream market is the expansion of OLED investment. An IR document the company released in June 2024 cited rising demand for OLED in IT devices, wider use of OLED in automotive displays, and a shift toward OLED TVs as growth drivers.

The same document introduced Samsung Display's IT innovation line investment schedule and large-line investment plans by Chinese panel makers including BOE.

A securities information site's commentary also summarizes that rising OLED production line investment by Chinese panel makers has lifted demand for display inspection equipment.

Some analysts argue that high-resolution, low-power OLED for IT devices is technically harder, which raises the importance of high-precision inspection tools. In terms of cycle position, the shift to OLED is accelerating, and how long Chinese panel makers keep investing is a key variable for display equipment demand.

For secondary battery inspection equipment, a news report said the company has secured major Korean battery makers as customers, pointing to AI-based automated inspection as its selling point.

Glass substrates are emerging as a next-generation packaging material alongside the spread of AI chips and high-performance computing, and an analysis holds that they offer better thermal stability and finer circuit implementation than organic substrates.

Competitively, the company is classified as a mid-sized firm in the KOSDAQ machinery and equipment sector, and while some evaluations credit it with a dominant position in display inspection, its customer concentration is high.

Outlook

The outlook drivers that can be confirmed are several. A securities information site's commentary named the start of 8.6-generation OLED mass production in 2026 and the launch of Apple's iPhone Fold as additional momentum.

A news report said the move toward mass production of glass substrates at SKC's Absolics unit has raised expectations for inspection equipment. An analysis holds that demand for glass substrates could grow alongside AI chips, high-performance servers and data centers.

In secondary batteries, a news report mentioned the possibility of additional orders tied to expanding global battery production capacity, though that is the report's view and no disclosed order value has been confirmed.

The company is broadening trade with Chinese manufacturers such as BOE, so customer diversification is in progress.

On results, the next checkpoint is the fourth-quarter 2026 preliminary results, which appear likely to be announced around late January 2027, based on the timing of the year-end dividend decision disclosed on January 30, 2026.

After that, the 2026 annual business report, due by the end of March 2027, will set out the full-year operating margin and the mix of net income attributable to controlling shareholders on a disclosed basis.

The first-quarter 2027 quarterly report will then show whether the three-quarter sales decline has ended and whether 8.6-generation OLED mass production revenue is being booked.

Bull factors

Recovery in operating profit

8.6-generation OLED and glass substrate drivers

An analysis holds that demand for glass substrates could grow with AI chips and high-performance computing. However, no confirmed schedule has been set for when the revenue will be booked.

Large customer base and battery diversification

Major IT customers including Samsung Display, Samsung Electronics and BOE are cited as the revenue base. A news report said secondary battery inspection equipment has secured major Korean battery makers as customers.

The debt ratio fell from 49.9% in 2022 to 34.1% in 2025, and total equity stood at KRW 285.1 billion at year-end 2025.

Bear factors

Sales declined for three consecutive quarters, from KRW 36.3 billion in the first quarter of 2026 to KRW 28.2 billion in the third quarter. Operating results swung from a small profit in the second quarter of 2026 to an operating loss of KRW 3.08 billion in the third quarter. Customer concentration and dependence

With display products cited at roughly 80% of the mix, results remain tied to the OLED investment cycle. A news report described dependence on the Samsung group as a double-edged sword. How the expansion of Chinese customers feeds into reported results remains an open question.

Volatility from fair-value gains and losses

The 2024 net loss attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 51.6 billion was reported to reflect largely the book-value marking of a fund stake tied to HPSP.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders went from KRW 3.54 billion in the fourth quarter of 2025 to KRW 18.2 billion in the first quarter of 2026, then fell to KRW 9.35 billion in the third quarter. Quarterly net income is structurally exposed to non-operating items such as fund stake valuations.

Risk factors

Customer and capex concentration

Revenue depends on when large customers such as Samsung Display invest in equipment. If customer schedules slip, equipment revenue recognition can be delayed. With display products at roughly 80% of the mix, a slowdown in OLED investment is a direct risk.

Earnings and fair-value volatility

The company swung to an operating loss of KRW 3.08 billion in the third quarter of 2026, and net income attributable to controlling shareholders has moved sharply with the fund stake valuation.

The 2024 loss was explained as a book-value change, but the direction of results could differ depending on whether gains or losses are realized. Quarterly volatility is high, so one or two quarters alone make it hard to read the trend.

Trading and shareholder flow risk

A May 2026 news report noted that the exchange had signaled designations as a caution-stock and a short-term overheating stock. A February 2026 disclosure confirmed the reduction of holdings tied to an executive's departure and a separate insider's open-market sale of 80,000 shares, worth about KRW 490 million. The largest shareholder group held 26.47% as of July 31, 2026.

Overall view

HB Technology is a KOSDAQ mid-sized equipment maker that combines display inspection equipment, secondary battery inspection equipment and components. On disclosed figures, operating profit of KRW 6.24 billion and net income attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 28.4 billion in 2025 reversed the 2024 losses.

However, sales peaked at KRW 51.8 billion in the fourth quarter of 2025 and then declined for three consecutive quarters through the third quarter of 2026, and operating results swung to a loss of KRW 3.08 billion in that quarter.

The pattern shows results depend heavily on the OLED investment cycle and the timing of capex from Samsung group customers. Positive factors include the start of 8.6-generation OLED mass production, expectations for glass substrate inspection equipment, and secured secondary battery customers.

Negative factors include the quarterly sales slowdown, customer concentration, and net income volatility from fund stake valuation. Valuation is at the stage of comparing the multiples with sector averages as facts, and because quarterly earnings move sharply, a single quarter is hard to judge from.

The next checkpoints are the fourth-quarter 2026 preliminary results, the 2026 annual business report, and the first-quarter 2027 quarterly report.
