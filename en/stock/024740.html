02

Business structure

HanilForgingIndustrial (024740) is a forging manufacturer founded in 1966. Its core automotive products are precision axle shafts and spindles.

Its main automotive customers are domestic automakers Hyundai Motor and KIA, and its company profile also lists supply contracts with overseas automakers including Ford, Stellantis, Nissan and GM. Beyond automotive power-transmission parts, the business spans industrial equipment parts and defense parts.

In defense, the company produces precision missile bodies, which its profile describes as core to domestic defense industry, along with warheads and heavy-equipment parts. Its scope also covers shipbuilding and aviation parts, so demand is not tied to a single industry.

Automotive parts appear to be the center of earnings, as the 2025 explanation pointed to automaker utilization as the key variable. Because orders move with automaker production plans, customer output changes quickly feed into plant utilization.

Among listed auto-parts peers such as Hyundai WIA, Sungwoo Hitech and Seoyon E-Hwa, the forging-plus-defense mix sets it apart. Segment sales and revenue shares can be confirmed in the segment disclosures of the annual report.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual sales rose from KRW 177.4 billion in 2022 to KRW 178.2 billion in 2023, up 0.5% year on year, before falling 21.8% to KRW 139.4 billion in 2024 and 9.2% to KRW 126.6 billion in 2025.

Operating profit went from KRW 14.6 billion in 2022 and KRW 15.2 billion in 2023 to KRW 8.29 billion in 2024, down 45.4%, and KRW 3.52 billion in 2025, down 57.5%. The operating margin moved from 8.2% in 2022 and 8.5% in 2023 to 5.9% in 2024 and 2.8% in 2025.

Profit fell much faster than sales, which points to fixed-cost pressure weighing on margins. The company attributed the 2025 decline to US tariff effects, lower plant utilization from automaker output adjustments, and higher fixed-cost burden.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders fell from KRW 7.47 billion in 2023 to KRW 6.24 billion in 2024 and KRW 1.47 billion in 2025. Net income including non-controlling interests was KRW 1.87 billion in 2025.

Operating cash flow fell from KRW 30.7 billion in 2023 to KRW 24.4 billion in 2024 and KRW 8.26 billion in 2025. Quarterly results show a swing to an operating loss of KRW 200 million in the fourth quarter of 2025, after KRW 1.55 billion of operating profit in the third quarter.

Operating profit returned to the black at KRW 2.55 billion in the first quarter of 2026, on sales of KRW 35.6 billion. Second-quarter 2026 sales reached KRW 43.1 billion, up 21.2% quarter on quarter, and operating profit rose to KRW 4.99 billion, up 95.4%.

In the third quarter of 2026, sales were KRW 38.6 billion, up 19.6% year on year, yet operating profit fell to KRW 634 million, down 59.0% year on year, so higher sales did not translate into profit.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders over the last four quarters totals KRW 2.61 billion, about one-third of the KRW 7.47 billion recorded for 2023.

Industry analysis

Automakers, the main customers for HanilForgingIndustrial, are directly exposed to US tariff policy and to output decisions by individual manufacturers. The 2025 explanation named lower plant utilization as a key cause, which shows that forging demand moves closely with automaker production cycles.

A financial summary attributes the weakness in automotive-parts sales to a global economic slowdown and weaker global demand, combined with slower steel exports and raw-material supply concerns. Because steel is the main raw material in forging, steel prices and supply shifts affect cost and margins.

On sales, the business passed through a downcycle after the 2023 peak, with declines in 2024 and 2025, and sales have risen again since the first quarter of 2026. Many listed auto-parts peers are exposed to the same automaker production cycle, so the defense-parts mix is a relative point of difference.

Defense demand rests on missile and national-defense programs, so it can offer a source of demand that does not move with vehicle output. The size of the defense contribution to earnings, however, needs to be checked in the segment information of the periodic reports.

Comparing operating margins with listed auto-parts peers is most accurate when the same-period disclosures are placed side by side.

Operating profit returned to the black at KRW 2.55 billion in the first quarter, then rose to KRW 4.99 billion in the second.

The second quarter, when higher sales carried through to profit, is read as an early sign of a turn in earnings.

Defense demand alongside autos

Beyond auto parts, the company produces precision missile bodies, warheads, heavy-equipment parts, and shipbuilding and aviation parts, so not all demand depends on automaker output. Defense demand follows defense budgets and order flows, which can run on a different timeline from the auto cycle.

How much defense contributes to earnings, however, must be confirmed from segment information in the periodic reports.

Improved financial structure

Third-quarter divergence

Third-quarter 2026 sales rose 19.6% year on year to KRW 38.6 billion, but operating profit fell 59.0% to KRW 634 million. Sales growth that does not reach profit may signal that cost and fixed-cost pressure persists. The cause needs to be checked in the cost items of the quarterly report.

Tariffs and automaker dependence

US tariff effects and automaker output adjustments were named as causes of the 2025 decline. When automakers cut production plans, plant utilization falls and the fixed-cost burden rises. Because the business depends on automaker orders, volatility in the automotive segment remains a bear factor.
