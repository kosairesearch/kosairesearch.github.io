A Rebar-Linked Holding Company With Narrowing Losses

02

Business structure

KISCO Holdings was founded in 1957 as Hankuk Steel and converted into a holding company structure in September 2008 through a spin-off of its investment business. The company's basic business is governing its subsidiaries, guiding their management, and supporting them with funds and operations.

According to its 2023 annual report, the group's segments are steel manufacturing (the core rebar subsidiary and the second rebar subsidiary), leasing (a wire-rod subsidiary), and textile sales.

A press report from early 2025 put the holding company's stakes at 40.8% in the core rebar subsidiary, 83.5% in the second rebar subsidiary, 88.7% in the wire-rod subsidiary, and 76.8% in the textile seller.

The core subsidiary uses steel scrap as its main input in electric arc furnaces and sells construction rebar to distributors and end users in Korea and abroad. The same subsidiary sells surplus liquid oxygen, high-purity liquid nitrogen and liquid argon as industrial gases.

The second rebar subsidiary joined the group through an M&A deal in 2002, and the wire-rod subsidiary is named as a key subsidiary. The 2023 annual report says the core subsidiary sits in Chungnam, serves central and southern regions, and runs 14 depots nationwide.

Revenue is shaped mainly by rebar subsidiaries, so their results largely determine the holding company's performance.

On the competitive front, a press report from August 2022 said the Fair Trade Commission referred seven domestic steel makers, including the core and second rebar subsidiaries, to prosecutors over alleged rebar bid rigging worth more than KRW 6 trillion.

04

Earnings analysis

On a disclosed basis, annual revenue went from KRW 1.81 trillion in 2022 to KRW 1.54 trillion in 2023 (down 14.8% year on year), KRW 1.01 trillion in 2024 (down 34.3%), and KRW 825.3 billion in 2025 (down 18.5%), declining for three consecutive years from 2023 to 2025.

The largest year in the 2022-2025 range was 2022, and the smallest was 2025.

Operating profit fell from KRW 197.9 billion in 2022 to KRW 149.3 billion in 2023 (down 24.6%), then turned into an operating loss of KRW 3.63 billion in 2024 and widened to a KRW 70.7 billion loss in 2025, which is two consecutive loss-making years.

The operating margin moved from 10.9% in 2022 and 9.7% in 2023 to -0.4% in 2024 and -8.6% in 2025. A press report from early 2025 quoted the company attributing the drop to a construction slump that cut subsidiary sales and operating profit.

Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders rose to KRW 91 billion in 2023 (up 14.1%), then fell to KRW 32.4 billion in 2024 (down 64.4%) and turned into a controlling-shareholder net loss of KRW 23.2 billion in 2025.

Net income including non-controlling interests was a net loss of KRW 23.2 billion in 2025, and operating cash flow declined from KRW 98.2 billion in 2022 to KRW 49.5 billion in 2025.

Quarterly, the company posted operating losses in each of the five quarters from 3Q 2025 through 3Q 2026, but the size of the loss narrowed from KRW 17.3 billion in 1Q 2026 to KRW 6.84 billion in 2Q and then widened to KRW 14.2 billion in 3Q. 3Q 2026 revenue of KRW 213.7 billion was up 4.8% from a year earlier.

The controlling-shareholder net loss over the last four quarters totaled KRW 23.3 billion. Per the 3Q 2026 quarterly report, cumulative January-September 2026 revenue was KRW 690 billion and the cumulative operating loss was KRW 38.3 billion.

05

Industry analysis

Rebar demand moves directly with construction activity. An April 2026 company profile tied to 2025 results pointed to a construction slump and the failure of small builders as factors reducing rebar demand, while higher electricity and crude oil prices raised cost burdens.

In a May 2026 report, a domestic brokerage said rebar prices had risen since the start of the year and expected demand to recover modestly this year after 2025 as the low point. In an August 2026 report, the same brokerage noted that 1Q 2026 domestic rebar demand of 1.83 million tons was roughly flat year on year.

Whether demand has passed its trough is still a question the quarterly data have not settled. In industry positioning, the company describes its core subsidiary as one of the country's leading electric-arc-furnace steel makers, combining steelmaking and rebar production.

However, alleged bid rigging among market participants led to the 2022 complaint, which remains a sector-wide regulatory variable. Steel scrap prices and power costs are the main drivers of margins for electric-arc-furnace producers.

The brokerage's core argument is that higher rebar prices can be the starting point for a recovery in subsidiary earnings.

Bull factors

A narrowing quarterly loss trend

Operating losses narrowed from KRW 17.3 billion in 1Q 2026 to KRW 6.84 billion in 2Q and KRW 14.2 billion in 3Q, and the 3Q loss was smaller than a year earlier. The controlling-shareholder result turned to a KRW 1.25 billion profit in 2Q 2026.

A domestic brokerage forecast improving subsidiary results in 2H, citing 2Q operating results of the two rebar subsidiaries.

A large equity base and low leverage

At the end of 2025, total equity was KRW 1.49 trillion, of which controlling-shareholder equity was KRW 1.14 trillion. Total liabilities were KRW 162.9 billion, and the debt ratio moved from 16.0% in 2022 to 9.6% in 2024 and 10.9% in 2025. A light debt load gives the holding company room to absorb weak subsidiary years.

A record of shareholder-return measures

A December 2024 extraordinary shareholder meeting took up a proposal to move KRW 95.0 billion of capital surplus into retained earnings. On December 27, 2024, the holding company and its core subsidiary retired treasury shares with a planned amount above KRW 100 billion, a retirement above 10% of total shares issued.

Bear factors

The company has posted losses for two consecutive years, 2024 and 2025, with the 2025 operating loss at KRW 70.7 billion. Operating losses have persisted for five consecutive quarters from 3Q 2025 through 3Q 2026.

The controlling-shareholder net loss over the last four quarters totaled KRW 23.3 billion, so the cumulative deficit remains even as the quarterly loss narrows.

Declining revenue and cash flow

Revenue fell from KRW 1.81 trillion in 2022 to KRW 825.3 billion in 2025, a three-year decline starting in 2023. Operating cash flow dropped from KRW 98.2 billion to KRW 49.5 billion over the same period. The operating margin was -0.4% in 2024 and -8.6% in 2025.

Weak construction demand and rising costs

A corporate profile tied to 2025 results describes a construction slump and small-builder failures as reducing rebar demand, while higher electricity and crude oil prices raised costs. The recovery a domestic brokerage projected has yet to show in volume, since 1Q 2026 rebar demand was roughly flat year on year.

Risk factors

Regulatory and legal risk

A 2022 press report said the Fair Trade Commission referred seven domestic steel makers, including the core and second rebar subsidiaries, to prosecutors over alleged rebar bid rigging worth more than KRW 6 trillion. The case was also described as the largest bid-rigging suspicion in public procurement. Its outcome and any sanctions are a pending variable for the group's results and reputation.

Industry and construction-cycle risk

Rebar demand depends on construction activity, so a drop in new projects and starts feeds straight into subsidiary sales. The company cited the construction slump as a cause of its 2024 decline, and demand recovery is still being tested quarter by quarter. Domestic rebar demand in 1Q 2026 was 1.83 million tons, roughly flat year on year.

Cost and subsidiary-dependence risk

Electric-arc-furnace production is sensitive to steel scrap and power costs, and higher electricity and crude oil prices were cited as drivers of the 2025 deterioration. Holding-company results lean heavily on subsidiary earnings, so weakness at one subsidiary can flow through to group losses. Subsidiary weakness was named as a background to the 2024 swing into loss.

Overall view

KISCO Holdings' results are shaped mainly by its rebar subsidiaries. On a disclosed basis, revenue has declined for three consecutive years from 2023, and operating losses have persisted since 2024.

Quarterly operating losses narrowed in 2026 to KRW 17.3 billion, KRW 6.84 billion and KRW 14.2 billion, and 2Q 2026 turned to a controlling-shareholder net profit.

Still, the controlling-shareholder net loss over the last four quarters totals KRW 23.3 billion, and five straight quarters of operating losses mean earnings recovery is not yet confirmed. Total equity stands at KRW 1.49 trillion with a debt ratio of 10.9%.

A brokerage projects a modest demand recovery in 2026, but 1Q 2026 domestic rebar demand was roughly flat year on year. The 2022 bid-rigging complaint and cost pressure from electricity and crude oil remain open variables.

The next checkpoints are the full-year 2026 operating result in the annual report and the operating trend in the 1Q 2027 quarterly report.
