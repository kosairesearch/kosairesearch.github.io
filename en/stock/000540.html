02

Business structure

Heungkuk Fire & Marine Insurance is a non-life insurer whose main lines are long-term insurance, auto insurance, and general insurance. Industry reports describe its revenue mix as tilted toward long-term insurance, which carries higher profitability efficiency.

CSM built up in long-term contracts forms the base for future insurance profit, and the CSM on the books stood at KRW 3.3674 trillion at end-September 2026.

Total revenue on an unconsolidated basis in 2025 was KRW 3.5448 trillion, which the company described as the sum of insurance operating revenue and investment operating revenue.

Because investment operating revenue accounts for a meaningful share of that total, interest-rate moves and valuation gains or losses on the asset portfolio can swing quarterly results sharply. Assets under management were KRW 10.8886 trillion in H1 2026, generating reported operating returns of KRW 161.7 billion.

On the insurance side, new CSM in H1 2026 rose to KRW 382.2 billion, but insurance service expenses grew faster than insurance operating revenue, which weakened insurance profitability. Competitively, the company faces large non-life insurers in long-term and auto insurance markets.

04

Earnings analysis

Based on reported results, insurance revenue was KRW 2,475.4 billion in 2022, KRW 2,525.4 billion in 2023 (up 2.0% year on year), KRW 2,549.8 billion in 2024 (up 1.0% year on year), and KRW 2,701.0 billion in 2025 (up 5.9% year on year), rising for three consecutive years from 2023 through 2025.

The largest year among 2022-2025 was 2025. Operating profit was KRW 263.3 billion in 2022, KRW 407.8 billion in 2023 (up 54.9% year on year), KRW 148.8 billion in 2024 (down 63.5% year on year), and KRW 187.5 billion in 2025 (up 26.0% year on year), with 2023 the largest year and 2024 the smallest.

Net income attributable to owners of the parent was KRW 207.2 billion in 2022, KRW 316.1 billion in 2023, KRW 106.7 billion in 2024 (down 66.2% year on year), and KRW 151.7 billion in 2025 (up 42.1% year on year). Revenue grew steadily, but profit plunged in 2024 and then recovered in 2025.

Over the same period, net income (including non-controlling interests) was recorded in each year's disclosure at the same amount as net income attributable to owners of the parent. The debt-to-equity ratio rose from 378.4% in 2022 to 784.2% in 2023, 1,568.9% in 2024, and 1,393.9% in 2025.

Cash flow from operating activities was KRW 356.2 billion in 2022, KRW 355.8 billion in 2023, KRW 875.0 billion in 2024, and KRW 747.4 billion in 2025.

Quarterly insurance revenue was KRW 697.6 billion in the third quarter of 2025, KRW 697.1 billion in the fourth quarter (down 0.1% quarter on quarter), KRW 734.5 billion in the first quarter of 2026 (up 5.4% quarter on quarter), KRW 740.7 billion in the second quarter (up 0.8% quarter on quarter), and KRW 798.4 billion in the third quarter (up 7.8% quarter on quarter and up 14.4% year on year).

Operating results swung from a profit of KRW 30.3 billion in the third quarter of 2025 to an operating loss of KRW 6.76 billion in the fourth quarter, returned to profit with an operating profit of KRW 35.5 billion in the second quarter of 2026, and then swung back to an operating loss of KRW 89.9 billion in the third quarter.

In the investment segment in the third quarter, net valuation losses on financial assets were reported to have widened from KRW 5.8 billion to KRW 125.0 billion, and insurance segment profit was reported to be KRW 50.1 billion, up 58.4% from a year earlier.

The experience variance loss in the first half of 2026 was KRW 103.3 billion, which the company explained was largely the result of reflecting the actuarial assumption guidelines.

Industry analysis

Korean non-life insurers face a period in which loss-ratio pressure is flowing directly into results. A company official said that, like peers, the firm saw a sharp loss-ratio deterioration reflected in expected-versus-actual claims losses.

Actuarial guidelines requiring loss ratios of 90% or more for new exposures within five years were reported to have had a large impact on insurance profit. On rates, rising market yields have turned into valuation losses on financial assets, increasing the volatility of investment results across the sector.

The company said its H1 2026 investment result turned to a loss, reflecting valuation losses from higher interest rates. The regulatory environment has also tightened, with IFRS 17 and the K-ICS solvency framework changing both profit recognition and capital management.

Heungkuk Fire & Marine Insurance highlights its long-term insurance-weighted portfolio as a strength and continues to grow its core insurance business. Industry commentary notes that long-term contracts, which run for extended periods, are well suited to building CSM.

Because sector-wide loss-ratio trends and the interest-rate path are shifting at the same time, differences between companies are likely to show up in investment management and loss-ratio control.

Second, new CSM in H1 2026 improved by KRW 151.8 billion year on year to KRW 382.2 billion, and CSM on the books reached KRW 3.3674 trillion at end-September 2026, forming the base for future insurance profit.

Third, expected-versus-actual claims losses of KRW 103.3 billion and loss-ratio pressure remain obstacles to a recovery in insurance profit. Investment results move with interest rates and valuation changes, making them the most volatile element of earnings forecasts.

Because the Q3 2026 investment loss stemmed from wider valuation losses on financial assets, the direction of those valuations should be tracked first in the next quarterly results.

On capital, the debt-to-equity ratio of 1,393.9% at end-2025 and the H1 2026 planned transfer to policy reserve funds of KRW 153.1 billion constrain dividend capacity and the pace of capital rebuilding.

Whether loss ratios improve in the core insurance business and how quickly CSM grows must both be confirmed before the continuity of the earnings recovery can be judged.

Bull factors

Continued growth in insurance revenue

Insurance revenue rose for three consecutive years from 2023 through 2025 and continued to climb for three straight quarters from Q1 through Q3 2026. Q3 2026 insurance revenue of KRW 798.4 billion was up 14.4% from a year earlier, widening the growth gap.

Reports also said insurance-segment profit rose 58.4% to KRW 50.1 billion in Q3 2026. The growing scale of the core business is the main bullish point.

CSM build-up widens the insurance profit base

New CSM in H1 2026 was KRW 382.2 billion, improving by KRW 151.8 billion year on year. CSM on the books reached KRW 3.3674 trillion at end-September 2026. Because CSM is recognized as insurance profit over the contract period, it is read as a base for future earnings. The long-term insurance-weighted structure supports this accumulation.

Quarterly returns to profit and cash generation

Operating profit returned to positive at KRW 35.5 billion in Q2 2026, and net profit attributable to controlling shareholders also turned positive at KRW 26.8 billion. Operating cash flow stayed large at KRW 875 billion in 2024 and KRW 747.4 billion in 2025. Even with loss quarters, the company retains cash-generating capacity, which is a bullish point.

Bear factors

Large volatility in investment results

Investment losses in Q3 2026 were reported at around KRW 140 billion, with net valuation losses on financial assets widening from KRW 5.8 billion to KRW 125 billion. The KRW 89.9 billion operating loss in the quarter reflects investment losses outweighing insurance profit.

Investment results also turned to a loss in H1 2026. Valuation results could swing again depending on the interest-rate path, which is the main bearish point.

Loss-ratio and expected-versus-actual claims pressure

Expected-versus-actual claims losses reached KRW 103.3 billion in H1 2026. The company said actuarial guidelines requiring a loss ratio of at least 90% for new exposures within five years had a major effect. Insurance profitability weakened as insurance service expenses grew faster than insurance operating revenue. If loss-ratio pressure persists, a recovery in insurance profit could be delayed.

High leverage and limited capital headroom

The debt-to-equity ratio rose from 378.4% in 2022 to 1,568.9% in 2024 and 1,393.9% in 2025. At end-2025, total equity was KRW 836.9 billion and total liabilities were KRW 11.7 trillion.

Reports said the planned H1 2026 transfer to policy reserve funds reached KRW 153.1 billion, leaving adjusted profit at negative KRW 139.5 billion. Limited capital headroom constrains dividends and growth investment.

10

Risk factors

Interest-rate and financial-market risk

Rising rates increase valuation losses on bonds and financial assets, pulling down investment results directly. The expansion of investment losses to around KRW 140 billion in Q3 2026 shows this risk has already materialized. With assets under management above KRW 10 trillion, a one-way rate move can swing overall quarterly results.

Loss-ratio and actuarial assumption risk

If loss ratios come in above expectations, expected-versus-actual claims losses widen and insurance profit falls. Expected-versus-actual claims losses were reported at KRW 103.3 billion in H1 2026, and the company said actuarial guidelines increased this burden. If loss-ratio control does not improve, the conversion of CSM growth into insurance profit may slow.

Capital adequacy and regulatory risk

With the debt-to-equity ratio at 1,393.9%, the K-ICS solvency framework and IFRS 17 add to capital management burdens. The policy reserve transfer pulled adjusted profit into negative territory in H1 2026. If regulatory standards tighten or losses persist, the need for capital reinforcement could grow.

Overall view

Heungkuk Fire & Marine Insurance's insurance revenue has risen for three straight quarters through Q3 2026, and CSM continues to accumulate, expanding its core business base.

However, larger investment losses pushed Q3 2026 into an operating loss of KRW 89.9 billion and a net loss attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 68.8 billion. The cumulative net loss attributable to controlling shareholders over the last four quarters totals KRW 50 billion.

The rise in expected-versus-actual claims losses to KRW 103.3 billion in H1 2026 shows that loss-ratio pressure is still weighing on profit. A debt-to-equity ratio of 1,393.9% and a smaller equity base limit room for dividends and growth investment.

On valuation, the PER cannot be calculated, while the price-to-book ratio has remained below 1 at year-ends from 2020 through 2024. The next checkpoints are the direction of investment results and the improvement in loss ratios in the 2026 annual report and the Q1 2027 quarterly report.
