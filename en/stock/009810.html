Renamed and Repositioned, Playgram Faces a Turnaround Test

Formerly an MRO and video-content company, Playgram changed its corporate and listed name in July-August 2026 as it pivoted toward IT solutions, and while quarterly operating profit turned positive starting in the first quarter of 2026, the company's annual operating loss streak continued through 2025.

  1. 1

  2. 2

  3. 3

  4. 4

02

Business structure

Playgram, founded in 1974, has historically operated an MRO (maintenance, repair and operations) procurement agency business centered on flexible industrial bulk packaging (FIBC) along with a video content business.

Reported segment weightings have shown MRO at 63.3%, management/strategic planning at 24.1%, and video content at 11.5% of revenue.

Consolidated results incorporate several subsidiaries, including MDS Intelligence, Telladyne, Ancompany, Stance, and MDS Mobility, so that software, solutions and service revenue from these units outweighs the parent's own MRO sales within the group total.

The company is a major shareholder of KOSDAQ-listed MDS Tech (086960), holding roughly a 28% stake including related parties as of February 2026, though that stake fell to around 25% after MDS Tech's share consolidation in April.

At an extraordinary shareholders' meeting on July 31, 2026, the company added embedded-systems manufacturing and leasing, IT systems and solutions consulting, and telecommunications construction as new business purposes, formalizing a pivot toward an IT-focused enterprise built on synergy with subsidiaries MDS Tech and MDS Intelligence.

Alongside this, the corporate name was changed to MDS Sphere and the listed ticker name and English notation were updated to align with the group identity.

On the competitive front, the company is compared both against traditional MRO and packaging distributors and, increasingly, against a broader set of peers as embedded and software subsidiaries contribute more to group revenue.

That said, the newly added business purposes have not yet been accompanied by disclosed acquisition targets or an execution timeline.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual revenue rose from KRW 131.3 billion in 2022 to KRW 214.4 billion in 2023 (up 63.3%) and KRW 268.3 billion in 2024 (up 25.1%), before declining to KRW 250.9 billion in 2025 (down 6.5%); among 2022-2025, 2024 was the largest year and 2022 the smallest.

Operating losses narrowed from KRW 6.86 billion in 2022 to KRW 2.59 billion in 2023, widened again to KRW 5.1 billion in 2024, and narrowed to KRW 4.4 billion in 2025 — four consecutive years of operating losses from 2022 through 2025.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders was a profit of KRW 1.31 billion in 2022 but swung to a loss of KRW 37.6 billion in 2023, narrowed to a loss of KRW 1.32 billion in 2024, and widened again to a loss of KRW 2.46 billion in 2025 — three consecutive years of losses from 2023 through 2025.

On a quarterly basis, revenue climbed from KRW 55.1 billion in the second quarter of 2025 to KRW 62.6 billion in the third quarter (up 13.5%), KRW 65.9 billion in the fourth quarter (up 5.4%), and KRW 65.7 billion in the first quarter of 2026 (down 0.4%), before jumping to KRW 81.3 billion in the second quarter of 2026 (up 23.8% quarter-on-quarter and 47.5% year-on-year).

Operating profit swung from a loss of KRW 48.31 million in the second quarter of 2025 to losses of KRW 194 million and KRW 2.71 billion in the following two quarters, before turning to a profit of KRW 1.67 billion in the first quarter of 2026 and KRW 2.54 billion in the second quarter (up 52.1% quarter-on-quarter, turning positive year-on-year).

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders, however, posted losses for four straight quarters from the third quarter of 2025 through the second quarter of 2026, totaling a loss of KRW 7.9 billion over that span, showing a gap between the recovering operating line and the still-negative bottom line.

Operating cash flow improved to KRW 11.6 billion in 2025 from negative KRW 383 million in 2024, while the debt-to-equity ratio eased to 59.1% in 2025 from 63.9% in 2024.

05

Industry analysis

The company's core MRO and industrial packaging distribution business is classified as a mature sector tied to manufacturing demand, and within packaging-sector comparisons the company's revenue scale has been recorded as below the sector average.

Separately, the embedded and software subsidiaries folded into the consolidated results are linked to Korea's embedded solutions and systems-integration market, meaning the group's end markets split between traditional manufacturing/distribution and IT infrastructure investment demand.

In the embedded software space, KOSDAQ-listed MDS Tech has reported growth in AI server and industrial automation areas, so the value of the company's stake in MDS Tech is also tied to that industry cycle.

The MRO business itself is generally seen as competing on pricing and procurement efficiency rather than technological differentiation, with trading relationships and supply networks driving competitiveness.

The video content business represents a comparatively small share of revenue, limiting its impact on group-wide results.

The embedded systems, IT solutions consulting, and telecommunications construction areas newly added as business purposes are fields often discussed in connection with expanding domestic data center and infrastructure investment, but the company has not yet disclosed revenue performance of its own in these areas.
