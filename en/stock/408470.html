02

Business structure

Hanpass is a fintech company positioned as a financial and living platform for foreign residents in Korea; it was founded in 2017 and listed on KOSDAQ in March 2026.

Its core business is small-value overseas remittance aimed at foreigners residing in Korea, delivered through a network of more than 50 global money transfer operators (MTOs) covering over 200 countries.

According to the company, monthly active remittance users are about 200,000, monthly transfers about 600,000 and monthly remittance volume about KRW 400 billion. A brokerage breakdown as of April 2026 put digital remittance at 78% of revenue, mobile wallet at 15%, prepaid cards at 5% and other at 2%.

The company describes its card business as about 30% of total revenue, but the scope of the card business differs between the company description and the brokerage breakdown, so the two figures cover different ranges.

Beyond remittance, the company reported about 4 million monthly service transactions and about 450,000 unique users excluding remittance. Its model extends from remittance customers into mobile wallet, prepaid cards, domestic and overseas payments and telecom services within a single One App platform.

The remittance method uses prefunding, in which funds are deposited in advance with overseas partners, and a brokerage described payout within about five minutes versus traditional SWIFT bank transfers, with fees of roughly KRW 5,000.

The main comparison point is the SWIFT bank transfer, and the company cites a share of about 22% in the domestic small-value overseas remittance market.

04

Earnings analysis

On a disclosed basis, revenue rose from KRW 55.3 billion in 2024 to KRW 66.4 billion in 2025, up 20.2% year on year. Operating profit grew from KRW 5.18 billion to KRW 8.23 billion, up 58.9%, and the operating margin climbed from 9.4% to 12.4%, so profit growth outpaced revenue growth.

Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders rose from KRW 4.4 billion to KRW 6.61 billion, up 50.0%, while net income including non-controlling interests was reported at KRW 4.4 billion and KRW 6.61 billion for the same years.

Operating cash flow moved from negative KRW 5.48 billion in 2024 to KRW 3.4 billion in 2025, in the same direction as profit. The 2024 debt ratio of negative 405.3% implies negative total equity in that year.

At the end of 2025, total equity was KRW 42.1 billion, all of it attributable to controlling shareholders, against total liabilities of KRW 79.8 billion, a debt ratio of 189.6%. The quarterly trend changed direction in 2026.

In the first quarter, revenue was KRW 17.1 billion and the operating loss was KRW 747 million, while net profit attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 652 million, so the operating result and the bottom line diverged.

In the second quarter, revenue fell 15.5% from the prior quarter to KRW 14.4 billion, the operating loss widened to KRW 3.12 billion, and the company posted a net loss attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 4.88 billion, a swing into loss.

In the third quarter, revenue rose 25.2% from the prior quarter to KRW 18 billion, the operating loss continued but narrowed to KRW 1.01 billion, and the net loss attributable to controlling shareholders narrowed to KRW 364 million.

The company has posted losses for three consecutive quarters from the first quarter through the third quarter of 2026, and for two consecutive quarters on a controlling-shareholder basis from the second quarter through the third quarter.

Quarterly figures for the third and fourth quarters of 2025 are not presented in the disclosures, so quarterly comparisons are made within 2026. Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders for the last four quarters (first quarter to fourth quarter of 2025) totaled KRW 6.61 billion.

05

Industry analysis

Hanpass's core market is repeat remittance demand from foreign residents in Korea. A brokerage report from April 2026 cited growth in resident foreigners from 1.96 million in 2021 to 2.78 million in 2025 as the basis for sustained demand growth.

Before and after the listing, another brokerage pointed to growth in foreign workers and students and regulatory easing as tailwinds. On the inbound visitor side, the company signed a tourism transport agreement with the Korea Tourism Organization to explore new payment-data-based businesses and joint marketing.

The share of about 22% in the domestic small-value overseas remittance market is the company's position against SWIFT bank transfers and other remittance providers. The prefunding model is the basis for its speed and fee positioning.

The company said in August 2026 that transfer counts rose more than 10% from the prior month in early August, which supports the case for sustained share. Resident-foreigner demand is sensitive to immigration policy and foreign exchange rules, so the industry outlook depends on policy variables.

Outlook

The company is extending its services from remittance customers into mobile wallet, cards, payments and telecom. It plans to use financial data accumulated through card and payment activity to upgrade loan comparison and brokerage services and to expand everyday services such as telecom.

The timing of the loan comparison and brokerage launch and its likely revenue contribution have not been disclosed in detail and will need to be checked in future announcements.

In late October 2026, the company attended a forum hosted by JB Financial Group to discuss cooperation with domestic and overseas fintech companies, and it is also pursuing partner expansion in markets including Vietnam and Indonesia.

Before and after the listing, brokerages projected revenue and operating profit growth on the back of rising resident and inbound foreign visitors. However, operating losses continued through the first to third quarters of 2026, so the gap between those projections and reported results becomes the next checkpoint.

Any improvement the company describes rests on cost structure and new-business contribution, and the weight of each needs confirmation in periodic filings. The most direct checkpoint is whether the operating margin recovers toward the 2025 level of 12.4% in the fourth quarter of 2026 and in the 2026 annual report.

Swing to a controlling-shareholder net loss

In the first quarter of 2026 there was a net profit attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 652 million, which diverged from that quarter's operating loss.

Debt load relative to equity

Total liabilities at the end of 2025 were KRW 79.8 billion, above total equity of KRW 42.1 billion, a debt ratio of 189.6%. The 2024 debt ratio of negative 405.3% implies negative equity that year. If quarterly losses continue through 2026, the burden on the equity base could grow.

Risk factors

Delayed earnings recovery

With operating losses running from the first quarter through the third quarter of 2026, the return to the 2025 profit level has not yet appeared in the disclosures. Revenue and operating profit growth projected by brokerages around the listing diverge from the actual quarterly trend. Unless cost structure and new-business contribution become visible, that gap is unlikely to close quickly.

Dependence on policy and regulation

Resident-foreigner growth, the core demand driver, can change with immigration policy and foreign exchange rules. The regulatory easing that brokerages cite as a tailwind could reverse if policy direction changes. Changes in remittance fee or transfer limit rules would affect the economics of the prefunding model.

Float supply and share price volatility

At the listing, the free float ratio was presented at 25.2% of shares, so release of lock-up shares would increase tradable supply. The share price, which rose well above the offering price on the first trading day, has since fallen sharply.

Changes in these share lock-up releases around the one-year anniversary of the listing need to be confirmed through disclosures.

Overall view

In 2025, Hanpass reported revenue of KRW 66.4 billion, up 20.2% year on year, operating profit of KRW 8.23 billion, up 58.9%, and net profit attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 6.61 billion, up 50.0%.

Monthly remittance volume of about KRW 400 billion and a share of about 22% in the domestic small-value overseas remittance market are operating indicators the company has announced.

On the other side, operating losses have continued for three consecutive quarters from the first quarter through the third quarter of 2026, and the second quarter swung to a net loss attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 4.88 billion.

In the third quarter both the operating loss and the controlling-shareholder net loss narrowed, but the losses themselves did not end.

Bullish points center on remittance volume growth and revenue diversification, while bearish points center on consecutive losses and float supply. The next checkpoints are the fourth-quarter 2026 results and the operating margin in the 2026 annual report.
