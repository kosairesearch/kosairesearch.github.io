It's Hanbul: Operating profit rises, but Q3 turns to a net loss

02

Business structure

It's Hanbul is a cosmetics manufacturer and seller founded in 1989, operating across production, R&D, marketing and distribution within one group. It runs its own brands It's Skin and Chasing Rabbit alongside brands at its subsidiary NeopharmCO.

It's Skin is pursuing new product launches and a mass-market campaign for Chasing Rabbit, and is developing digital skin-care features such as an AI-based product recommendation service and virtual makeup simulation. Distribution spans multiple domestic and overseas channels.

Domestic sales in Q1 2025 were KRW 13.6 billion, down 28% from the prior quarter, while overseas and global subsidiary sales rose 30% to KRW 9.3 billion, or 26% of the total. Overseas sales and exports in 2023 were KRW 46.1 billion, roughly flat from the prior year.

The company signed a supplementary supply agreement for China worth KRW 53.7 billion with a Chinese partner. In November 2023 it sold its domestic Eumseong production plant to NeopharmCO for KRW 17.2 billion, and NeopharmCO has since handled production for It's Hanbul products. Headcount fell from 163 in 2022 to 93 in 2025.

Earnings analysis

Annual revenue rose from KRW 130.7 billion in 2022 to KRW 139.2 billion in 2023 (up 6.5% year on year), KRW 142.4 billion in 2024 (up 2.3%) and KRW 153.4 billion in 2025 (up 7.7%), growing for three consecutive years from 2023 through 2025, with 2025 the largest year of the 2022 to 2025 period.

Operating profit went from KRW 4.81 billion in 2022 to KRW 8.05 billion in 2023 (up 67.5%), KRW 17.9 billion in 2024 (up 122.0%) and KRW 19.2 billion in 2025 (up 7.4%).

The operating margin climbed from 3.7% in 2022 and 5.8% in 2023 to 12.6% in 2024, then held at 12.5% in 2025, so the margin gains concentrated in 2024 while 2025 kept margins steady as sales grew.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders moved from a net loss of KRW 6.82 billion in 2022 to a profit of KRW 3.4 billion in 2023, KRW 16.5 billion in 2024 (up 386.2%) and KRW 16.7 billion in 2025 (up 1.3%), growing for two consecutive years from 2024 through 2025.

Net income including non-controlling interests was KRW 3.31 billion, KRW 17.4 billion, KRW 29.7 billion and KRW 32.6 billion over the same years, above the controlling-shareholder figure, with the gap reflecting the share of minority holders in consolidated subsidiaries.

At the end of 2025, total equity was KRW 506.3 billion, of which KRW 389.7 billion belonged to controlling shareholders, and the debt ratio fell from 6.6% in 2022 to 5.8% in 2025. Operating cash flow rose from KRW 7.09 billion in 2022 to KRW 28.1 billion in 2025.

Quarterly revenue went from KRW 37.8 billion in Q3 2025 to KRW 42 billion in Q4 (up 11.3% from the prior quarter), KRW 42.7 billion in Q1 2026 (up 1.5%) and KRW 49.7 billion in Q2 2026 (up 16.4%), before falling to KRW 44.8 billion in Q3 2026 (down 9.8% from the prior quarter, up 18.6% year on year).

Quarterly operating profit ran KRW 4.82 billion, KRW 4.87 billion, KRW 6.83 billion, KRW 7.33 billion and KRW 7.43 billion from Q3 2025 to Q3 2026, rising for four consecutive quarters from Q4 2025 through Q3 2026.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 7.68 billion in Q3 2025, KRW 6.6 billion in Q4, KRW 9.41 billion in Q1 2026 and KRW 9.63 billion in Q2, before a KRW 8.27 billion net loss in Q3 2026 turned the quarter into a loss. The sum for the last four quarters was KRW 17.4 billion.

Because operating profit rose while controlling-shareholder earnings turned negative, the items between operating profit and net income, including non-operating items and income tax, are the next points to check.

05

Industry analysis

Within the cosmetics sector, It's Hanbul is a mid-sized company that combines its own brands with in-house manufacturing capability through subsidiaries. In China, the rapid growth of local Chinese cosmetics brands and tighter regulation have weighed on China-bound sales.

A 2022 analysis cited fewer foreign tourists visiting Korea as a drag on duty-free and China-related sales. Offline domestic cosmetics growth slowed after masks became routine, and competition in online channels intensified.

The company has responded by expanding its own online mall and home-shopping channels and by pursuing markets outside China. Consumer interest has shifted toward derma, vegan and clean beauty, and the company has stepped up product development and marketing in those areas.

Its cumulative 2025 nine-month sales and operating profit rose from the prior year, which suggests the product strategy contributed.

Peers include large brand companies such as Amorepacific and LG H&H, contract manufacturers such as Cosmax and Kolmar Korea, and online-driven brand companies such as APR and CLIO Cosmetics.

It's Hanbul is smaller than most of these, and how that gap shows up in channel bargaining power and overseas expansion speed is the key comparison point.

Bear factors

Controlling-shareholder net loss in Q3

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders swung to a KRW 8.27 billion loss in Q3 2026, from KRW 9.63 billion of profit in the prior quarter and KRW 7.68 billion a year earlier. Revenue fell 9.8% from the prior quarter to KRW 44.8 billion.

Operating profit rose to KRW 7.43 billion, but results deteriorated below the operating line, so the items driving the swing still need to be identified.

Structural pressure from China and channels

Analyses point to rapid growth of local Chinese cosmetics brands and tighter regulation weighing on China-bound sales. A 2022 analysis noted that fewer foreign visitors to Korea reduced duty-free and China-related sales.

Offline domestic growth has slowed while online competition has intensified, adding channel and promotional costs.

Dependence on a subsidiary and standalone losses

Standalone operating profit posted losses continuously from Q3 2024 through Q3 2025. Commentary has pointed to the subsidiary NeopharmCO as the main reason consolidated profit held up.

Because the group relies on subsidiary results and dividends, changes in that subsidiary's sector conditions or dividend policy would flow through to the parent's earnings.
