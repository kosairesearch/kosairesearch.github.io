Inswave: A Financial-Sector Software Firm Tested by Its Turnaround

Inswave's revenue rose sharply in the first half of 2026 and it returned to operating profit in the third quarter, but whether full-year results turn positive depends on how much of its KRW 44.1 billion backlog is recognized as revenue in the fourth quarter of 2026.

  2. 2

    Operating profit in the third quarter of 2026 was KRW 636 million, a swing from an operating loss a year earlier. Over the last four quarters, the cumulative net loss attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 629 million.

  3. 3

  4. 4

02

Business structure

Inswave, founded in 2002, sells its WebSquare 5 UI/UX development platform and the AI-based WebSquare AI platform as licenses, and earns steady income from annual maintenance contracts.

Based on the 2025 annual report, sales were split as consulting 54.00%, licenses 29.35%, maintenance 15.73% and other 0.93%, compared with consulting 47.9%, licenses 31.1% and maintenance 17.6% in 2024, so consulting's share has risen.

A product-level breakdown compiled by a corporate data provider shows financial terminal solutions at 58.67% and the UI/UX development platform at 39.73%, and all 2025 sales were domestic.

Its customers are centered on large financial-institution and public-sector projects, and the company attributes its first-half 2026 growth to its share and technology in financial-sector projects. R&D spending was 14.84% of 2025 sales.

Inswave Japan, established in Tokyo in January 2025, is a wholly owned subsidiary with registered capital of KRW 380 million. Headcount was 181 as of December 2025. Inswave sits in the same IT and software classification as Samsung SDS, LG CNS and Hyundai Autoever, but its scale and product mix differ considerably. Concentration of revenue in individual large contracts is the key structural variable.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual revenue was KRW 43.6 billion in 2022, fell to KRW 32.6 billion in 2023 (down 25.3% year on year), declined to KRW 30.4 billion in 2024 (down 6.7%), and rebounded to KRW 39 billion in 2025 (up 28.1%). The largest year in the 2022–2025 period was 2022, and the smallest was 2024.

The operating margin moved from 16.1% in 2022 and 15.9% in 2023 to -11.8% in 2024 and -12.5% in 2025. The 2024 operating loss of KRW 3.59 billion marked a switch from profit to loss, while the 2025 operating loss of KRW 4.86 billion represented a wider loss.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 4.72 billion in 2023 (down 12.5%), then a net loss of KRW 1.96 billion in 2024 and KRW 3.48 billion in 2025. Operating cash flow was -KRW 90.84 million in 2022 and KRW 3.7 billion in 2023, then -KRW 4.52 billion in 2024 and -KRW 8.4 billion in 2025.

The debt ratio fell from 112.2% in 2022 to 24.0% in 2023 and stood at 30.2% at end-2025, when total equity was KRW 40.5 billion, all attributable to controlling shareholders, and total liabilities were KRW 12.2 billion.

Quarterly revenue was KRW 11.5 billion in the third quarter of 2025, KRW 10 billion in the fourth quarter (down 12.6% from the prior quarter), KRW 11.3 billion in the first quarter of 2026, KRW 15.3 billion in the second quarter (up 36.1%), and KRW 12.6 billion in the third quarter (down 17.7% quarter on quarter, up 10.1% year on year).

Operating results were an operating loss of KRW 884 million in the third quarter of 2025, operating profit of KRW 491 million in the fourth quarter, an operating loss of KRW 2.81 billion in the first quarter of 2026, a narrower loss of KRW 128 million in the second quarter, and operating profit of KRW 636 million in the third quarter.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 135 million in the second quarter of 2026 and KRW 510 million in the third quarter. According to the company, first-half operating loss narrowed by 34.2% and net loss by 38.5%.

05

Industry analysis

Inswave's end demand comes from financial institutions' AI transformation and digital transformation spending.

The company attributes its first-half 2026 growth to its share and technology in financial-sector projects, and the first-quarter 2026 revenue increase has been linked to demand from financial AI transformation.

Financial IT spending tends to be concentrated by project, so quarterly revenue is uneven, at KRW 15.3 billion in the second quarter of 2026 and KRW 12.6 billion in the third.

A company official said that because of the nature of its financial and public customers, investment concentrates in the second half, especially the fourth quarter, to finish projects within the year.

Larger peers in the same classification include Samsung SDS, LG CNS and Hyundai Autoever, while Inswave is a smaller specialist centered on UI/UX development platforms and financial solutions.

The company has stated an aim to enter the top ten Korean software companies, which is a management goal rather than a verified market-share figure. The sector cycle position is best read through the pace of financial-sector IT orders, which largely determines the company's order intake.

06

Outlook

The company has said that as the roughly KRW 44.1 billion backlog as of end-June 2026 is recognized as revenue in sequence, the annual improvement will be larger than the first-half gain. That is the company's own projection, and the timing of recognition depends on project completion.

Earlier in 2026, the company also said that large backlog built up from the second half of 2025 would be reflected in 2026 results. Third-quarter 2026 operating profit marked the start of the turnaround, and the fourth quarter is the period when investment is concentrated.

A June 2026 corrected filing put one contract at about KRW 21 billion, or 69.12% of sales, so the pace of that contract's execution weighs heavily on annual revenue.

Three points are to be checked: how much of the backlog is recognized in the fourth quarter of 2026, whether full-year 2026 operating profit closes in the black and whether operating cash flow moves out of negative territory after 2024, and whether new large contracts are disclosed. Inswave Japan is at an early stage, and little separate subsidiary disclosure is available.

08

Bull factors

Accelerating revenue growth

First-half 2026 revenue was about KRW 26.6 billion, up 52.0% from a year earlier, and second-quarter revenue rose 36.1% from the prior quarter. The company describes this as large orders converting into revenue rather than a one-off rebound, citing its share and technology in financial-sector projects.

Sizeable backlog

As of end-June 2026, total value of major contracts was about KRW 57 billion and the unrecognized backlog about KRW 44.1 billion, which exceeds full-year 2025 revenue. The company expects annual improvement to exceed the first-half gain as this backlog converts.

Improving profit trend

Third-quarter 2026 operating profit was KRW 636 million, a swing into the black, and second-quarter net income attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 135 million.

The company reports that first-half operating loss narrowed 34.2% and net loss 38.5%, so the quarterly data show movement from losses toward profit.

09

Bear factors

Two loss years and negative operating cash flow

Operating losses were KRW 3.59 billion in 2024 and KRW 4.86 billion in 2025, two consecutive loss years in which the 2025 loss widened. Operating cash flow was -KRW 4.52 billion in 2024 and -KRW 8.4 billion in 2025.

Profitable quarters have appeared, but whether the business generates cash on a full-year basis is not yet established.

Quarterly volatility

The first quarter of 2026 saw an operating loss of KRW 2.81 billion, and third-quarter revenue fell 17.7% from the prior quarter. The company says investment concentrates in the fourth quarter, which suggests the year-end pattern may repeat. Given how often quarterly direction has changed, full-year profitability cannot be assumed.

Dependence on a single contract

A June 2026 corrected filing put a single sales and supply contract at 69.12% of sales. A delay in executing that one contract could materially shift quarterly revenue recognition, and high per-contract concentration adds to earnings volatility even with a large backlog.

10

Risk factors

Customer and business concentration

Revenue is concentrated in large financial-institution and public-sector projects. The timing of these customers' IT orders and budget execution determines when revenue is recognized, and high reliance on specific large contracts means order delays can quickly show up as quarterly swings.

Contract execution and revenue timing

The roughly KRW 44.1 billion backlog is recognized as revenue as projects are completed. If investment concentrates in the fourth quarter as the company describes, costs may precede revenue, and if year-end projects slip, the annual improvement could fall short of the company's expectation.

Cash flow and financial capacity

Equity stands at KRW 40.5 billion and total liabilities at KRW 12.2 billion, so the debt load is modest. However, operating cash flow was negative in 2024 and 2025, and the debt ratio at end-2025 of 30.2% is higher than in 2023. Whether cash generation recovers while losses persist will determine financial capacity.

12

Overall view

After two consecutive operating loss years in 2024 and 2025 and negative operating cash flow, Inswave is in a phase where its earnings trajectory may be changing, with first-half 2026 revenue up 52.0% and a return to operating profit in the third quarter.

Bullish factors are first-half revenue growth, a backlog of roughly KRW 44.1 billion and quarterly profit improvement. Bearish factors are the full-year losses recorded, negative operating cash flow, quarterly volatility and a single contract accounting for 69.12% of sales.

Because net losses attributable to controlling shareholders totaled KRW 629 million over the last four quarters, a full-year return to profit has not yet been confirmed.

The operating margin moved from -12.5% in 2025 toward breakeven in the 2026 quarters, but the full-year margin will be confirmed in the 2026 annual report. On valuation, earnings multiples cannot be calculated, so book value and the pace of profit recovery serve as the factual reference points.

The next checkpoints are how much of the backlog is recognized in the fourth quarter of 2026, full-year profit and cash flow in the 2026 annual report, and the change in the loss in the first quarter of 2027.
