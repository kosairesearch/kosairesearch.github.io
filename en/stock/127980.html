Past the Earnings Trough, Durability Still Unproven

02

Business structure

Founded in 1991 and based in the Namdong industrial complex in Incheon, Finecircuit has long manufactured rigid printed circuit boards and listed on KOSDAQ in February 2023 through a merger with a SPAC, Shinyoung Happy Tomorrow No.6.

Its core products are double-sided and single-sided PCBs, with a past quarterly sales mix showing double-sided boards at 88.64% and single-sided boards at 9.48%, making double-sided products the overwhelming majority.

Its key customer is Samsung Electronics, with the relationship continuing steadily since Finecircuit was registered as a first-tier supplier in 2011. As of 2020, the domestic single- and double-sided PCB market was estimated at roughly KRW 650 billion, within which Finecircuit held about an 8.8% share.

In 2022 the company completed a second plant in Incheon that added automated double-sided PCB and multilayer board (MLB) production lines, which reportedly lifted annual production capacity by about 83% once operational.

The company has positioned entry into EV chargers and automotive electronics MLB as a new growth driver and has reviewed acquisitions of SQ-certified automotive-grade MLB makers, issuing a KRW 10 billion convertible bond in April 2026 for the stated purpose of acquiring securities in another company.

The founding family's roughly 83% stake keeps the ownership and control structure stable.

Within the sector it is mentioned alongside larger PCB and substrate makers such as Samsung Electro-Mechanics, Korea Circuit and Isupetasys, but in terms of revenue scale and product mix it is better classified as a smaller specialist in appliance-oriented rigid PCBs.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual revenue rose from KRW 64.4 billion in 2022 to KRW 69.2 billion in 2023 (up 7.5%) and KRW 85.9 billion in 2024 (up 24.1%), before falling 4.8% YoY to KRW 81.8 billion in 2025.

Operating profit grew modestly from KRW 4.12 billion in 2022 to KRW 4.32 billion in 2023 and KRW 4.51 billion in 2024, then swung to an operating loss of KRW 1.49 billion in 2025.

Controlling-interest net profit also rose from KRW 3.59 billion in 2022 to KRW 4.53 billion in 2024 before reversing to a controlling-interest net loss of KRW 3.98 billion in 2025, while net income including non-controlling interests likewise showed a net loss of KRW 3.77 billion in 2025.

According to disclosed explanations, the 2025 deterioration reflected a non-cash increase in selling and administrative expenses tied to bad-debt allowances as well as a non-cash increase in convertible-bond-related expenses.

The operating margin gradually declined from 6.4% in 2022 to 5.3% in 2024 before turning negative at -1.8% in 2025.

On a quarterly basis, revenue fell from KRW 23.9 billion in Q2 2025 to KRW 16.9 billion in Q3 (down 29.3%) and KRW 15.0 billion in Q4 (down 11.0%), before recovering for two straight quarters to KRW 16.6 billion in Q1 2026 (up 10.4%) and KRW 20.4 billion in Q2 2026 (up 22.7%).

Operating profit swung to a wider loss of KRW 1.14 billion in Q3 2025, narrowed to a loss of KRW 474 million in Q4, turned positive at KRW 216 million in Q1 2026, and then rose 554.9% QoQ to KRW 1.41 billion in Q2 2026.

Controlling-interest net profit likewise turned positive at KRW 507 million in Q4 2025 and rose for two consecutive quarters to KRW 521 million in Q1 2026 and KRW 931 million in Q2 2026 (up 78.5% QoQ and 207.1% YoY), though the sum of controlling-interest net profit over the trailing four quarters (Q3 2025 through Q2 2026) was only KRW 47.92 million, suggesting a full profit recovery is not yet confirmed.

05

Industry analysis

South Korea's PCB industry is in a phase where related stocks have rallied together on expectations of rising demand for high-performance, high-density boards driven by AI servers, on-device AI and data center expansion.

However, this demand growth is mainly directed at suppliers of higher value-added products such as package substrates, high-layer-count MLBs and FPCBs, which differs from Finecircuit's core double- and single-sided PCBs used in home appliances.

Indeed, in coverage of the PCB sector rally, Finecircuit was mentioned alongside Samsung Electro-Mechanics, Avico Electronics and Hyunwoo Industrial as having joined the upward move, without being singled out as a direct beneficiary of high-performance substrate earnings improvement.

Competitor Korea Circuit has seen share strength on expectations tied to AI server and high-performance semiconductor package substrate demand and has also been expanding into FPCB.

Finecircuit's core market of premium appliance PCBs tends to track the new-product launch cycle and demand trends of Samsung Electronics' Consumer Electronics division, leaving it relatively exposed to the maturity and cyclicality of that end market.

The EV charger and automotive electronics MLB businesses the company has pursued remain at an early stage whose revenue contribution has not yet been quantified in disclosed figures. Low-cost competition from Chinese makers has long been flagged as a persistent risk in the domestic general-purpose PCB market.

Outlook

The company has shown two consecutive quarters of rising operating profit and controlling-interest net profit in the first and second quarters of 2026, moving past the 2025 loss phase.

The KRW 10 billion convertible bond issued in April 2026 for the purpose of acquiring securities in another company, with a conversion price of KRW 5,151 and maturity in April 2029, means how and where these proceeds are deployed will be an important variable for future business restructuring.

The automotive-grade MLB acquisition plans the company had flagged around its listing could potentially be linked to this CB funding, but as of October 2026 no specific acquisition target or amount has been disclosed.

Despite the 2025 earnings deterioration, the company maintained dividend continuity by approving a KRW 170 per-share cash dividend at its March 2026 annual general meeting.

Looking ahead, the key points to watch are whether the recovery in appliance-related PCB demand seen through the second quarter of 2026 continues into the third quarter and beyond, and whether new automotive and EV-charger-related revenue materializes in disclosed figures.

Q2 2026 revenue of KRW 20.4 billion rose 22.7% QoQ but was still down 14.8% YoY, indicating absolute sales have not yet fully returned to the Q2 2025 level.

Bull factors

Two Straight Quarters of Profit Recovery

Both operating profit and controlling-interest net profit rose for two straight quarters from the Q4 2025 trough through Q1 and Q2 2026. Q2 2026 operating profit of KRW 1.41 billion rose 554.9% QoQ, while controlling-interest net profit of KRW 931 million rose 78.5% QoQ and 207.1% YoY. Revenue also grew for two consecutive quarters, showing improvement in both scale and profitability together.

Stable Control and Continued Dividend

The founding family's 83% stake keeps the risk of a control dispute low. Despite the 2025 loss, the company maintained a KRW 170 per-share cash dividend, demonstrating continuity in shareholder returns.

A stable governance structure can be a factor that speeds decision-making as the company pursues new businesses such as M&A.

New Business Funding Secured

In April 2026 the company issued a KRW 10 billion convertible bond for the purpose of acquiring securities in another company, securing funding that could support an acquisition in automotive MLB or related businesses.

Having long signaled intent to enter EV chargers and automotive-grade multilayer boards, a successful deployment of these proceeds into an actual acquisition could diversify the business portfolio.

Bear factors

FY2025 Swing to an Annual Loss

2025 revenue fell 4.8% YoY, operating profit swung to a loss of KRW 1.49 billion, and the controlling-interest net loss reached KRW 3.98 billion. Non-cash expenses tied to bad-debt allowances and convertible-bond-related costs contributed to the deterioration.

The sum of controlling-interest net profit over the trailing four quarters was only KRW 47.92 million, meaning the absolute scale of profit recovery remains modest.

Disconnect From the AI Substrate Theme

The recent strength across the PCB sector has been aimed at demand for AI server and high-performance semiconductor package substrates, which has limited direct relevance to Finecircuit's core appliance-oriented double- and single-sided PCBs.

Even if the share price moves with the sector theme, the actual benefit may concentrate more on competitors supplying high-layer-count substrates or FPCBs. The automotive and EV-charger new businesses have not yet shown a confirmed revenue contribution in disclosed figures.

Convertible Bond Dilution Overhang

The convertible bond issued in April 2026 carries a conversion price of KRW 5,151, and if fully converted would add new shares equal to 14.09% of total shares outstanding. If the share price approaches or exceeds the conversion price, the likelihood of conversion requests and resulting equity dilution increases. Non-cash gains or losses related to convertible bonds have previously added volatility to quarterly net profit.

Risk factors

Customer Concentration Risk

Finecircuit's revenue is known to be heavily dependent on Samsung Electronics' Consumer Electronics division. A particular customer's new-product launch cycle, inventory adjustments, or order volume changes can directly affect results.

Without sufficient customer diversification, a slowdown in end demand could translate quickly into revenue swings.

Capital Structure and Dilution Risk

The debt-to-equity ratio rose to 119.3% in 2023 before easing to 88.9% at the end of 2025, but it remains higher than past averages.

The additional convertible bond issued in 2026 carries the possibility of either redemption or conversion to shares at maturity, which could be a source of change to the capital structure and per-share metrics going forward.

It is also worth noting that non-cash gains and losses tied to convertible bonds have amplified quarterly net profit volatility.

New Business Execution Risk

The automotive MLB and EV charger businesses have been discussed for several years, but as of October 2026 no specific acquisition target or revenue contribution has been disclosed.

The timing and destination of the funds raised through the 2026 convertible bond remain uncertain, and if the planned M&A is delayed or falls through, securing a new growth driver could be pushed back further.

Given the high ownership concentration of the founding family, transparency around related-party transactions and the M&A target selection process is also a factor to monitor.

Overall view

Finecircuit experienced declining revenue, an operating loss, and a controlling-interest net loss on a full-year basis in 2025, but showed signs of passing a trough with consecutive increases in operating profit and net profit in the first and second quarters of 2026.

Still, the trailing four-quarter sum of controlling-interest net profit was only KRW 47.92 million, meaning the absolute scale of profit recovery remains modest, and Q2 2026 revenue was still down year-on-year.

The company's core business is appliance-oriented rigid PCBs concentrated around Samsung Electronics' home appliance division, which has limited direct connection to the AI server and high-performance substrate theme currently moving the broader market.

The convertible bond issued in April 2026 is intended to fund new-business M&A such as automotive MLB, but no specific target has been disclosed yet, and the potential for future equity dilution relative to the KRW 5,151 conversion price is also worth keeping in mind.

The founding family's high ownership stake supports control stability but also warrants continued attention to the transparency of related-party transactions.

On balance, the direction of quarterly results has improved, but whether this translates into a structural recovery in profitability is something to confirm further through upcoming quarterly reports and the outcome of new-business execution.
