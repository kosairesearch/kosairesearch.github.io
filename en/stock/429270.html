02

Business structure

Sigetronics is a specialty semiconductor device maker that works with both silicon and compound semiconductors. In 2024, electrostatic discharge (ESD) protection devices accounted for most of its sales, while sensors were about 8% of the total.

The company says it wants to keep ESD as a stable earnings base while reinvesting in optical sensors and foundry services.

In March 2026 it said its fab capacity had reached roughly 20,000 wafers a month, and it set four growth axes: LED lighting and displays, precision industrial motion control, smartphones and wearables, and OEM/ODM volume foundry.

Its optical sensor line serves healthcare, smartphones, LiDAR and robot vision, and in July 2026 it said it had begun volume supply to global optical sensor makers.

Its in-house M-FAB handles silicon as well as GaAs, GaN and SiC, and the company offers a one-stop service from design through packaging, reliability testing and mass production. In power and RF devices it is developing 650V-class GaN devices and high-power semiconductors for advanced radar.

At its 2023 listing, its customers were mainly overseas companies in automotive, space, aviation and healthcare, and most sales came from exports.

The company points to its own fab and epitaxy process as an edge in shortening development times, but no market share data against large foundries or ESD specialists has been presented.

In March 2026 it reorganized into three divisions, six departments, two offices and 13 teams, framing the move as a shift toward profit-focused management.

04

Earnings analysis

On an annual basis, revenue fell from KRW 14.5 billion in 2022 to KRW 12.5 billion in 2023, a 13.8% decline year on year, then slipped to KRW 12.2 billion in 2024, down 2.7%, before recovering to KRW 12.7 billion in 2025, up 4.7%. Among 2022 to 2025, 2022 was the largest year and 2024 the smallest.

Operating losses were KRW 4.85 billion, KRW 5.44 billion, KRW 6.29 billion and KRW 7.59 billion over the same years, which means four consecutive years of losses from 2022 to 2025, with the loss widening each year. The operating margin moved from -33.4% to -43.5%, -51.6% and -59.5%.

Sales edged up in 2025, yet the margin deteriorated, suggesting fixed costs were not absorbed by higher volume. A press report said fab utilization stayed around 30%, and the company cited new product development costs and higher raw material prices as cost pressures.

Net loss attributable to controlling shareholders widened from KRW 4.37 billion in 2022 to KRW 8.01 billion in 2025. The net loss including non-controlling interests was disclosed at the same values over the same years.

The debt ratio, total liabilities over total equity, was 114.9% in 2022, dropped to 36.5% in 2023, and rose to 53.9% in 2025. At the end of 2025, total equity was KRW 22.1 billion, all of it attributable to controlling shareholders, and total liabilities were KRW 11.9 billion.

Operating cash flow was -KRW 1.17 billion in 2022 and -KRW 5.04 billion in 2025. Quarterly revenue was KRW 3.92 billion in Q3 2025 and KRW 2.81 billion in Q4 2025, a 28.4% drop from the prior quarter, then KRW 3.59 billion in Q1 2026, KRW 3.78 billion in Q2 2026 and KRW 3.85 billion in Q3 2026.

Q3 2026 revenue was still 1.9% lower than a year earlier. Operating losses were KRW 1.64 billion in Q3 2025, KRW 2.51 billion in Q4 2025, KRW 2.26 billion in Q1 2026, KRW 2.14 billion in Q2 2026 and KRW 1.9 billion in Q3 2026, and the Q3 2026 loss widened from a year earlier. Net loss attributable to controlling shareholders over the last four quarters totaled KRW 9.36 billion.

05

Industry analysis

The company's demand base is widening from optical sensors to power and RF devices and to space and defense applications.

It argues that physical AI, robotics, autonomous driving and the spread of AI data centers will lift demand for specialty chips, but this is the company's own market view and no independent market-size statistic was presented.

For compound semiconductors, the company said at its 2023 listing that the wide-bandgap market would grow at more than 30% a year through 2030, a forecast that must be checked against current demand cycles.

On the cycle, the company's results appear to depend more on its own utilization and the stability of mass production than on industry conditions.

The revenue forecasts it presented at listing were KRW 26.3 billion for 2023 and KRW 51.4 billion for 2024, far above actual revenue of KRW 12.5 billion and KRW 12.2 billion, which suggests that mass production and customer wins came later than planned.

Against peers, the company positions itself on small-batch custom devices and foundry services from its own fab.

In GaN power devices it has announced joint development of 650V-class devices with DB HiTek, and a partner, ELD Electronics, is responsible for GaN epitaxial substrates, a division of labor that aims for a 6-inch process while also raising dependence on partners.

06

Outlook

Several company plans can be tracked. In August 2025 the company said it would expand compound semiconductor capacity to more than 6,000 wafers a month by 2026 and broaden its product lineup.

In March 2026 it began volume production of new devices and said it would push fab utilization above 75% in the short term by prioritizing people, equipment and R&D for its core businesses. A September 2026 press report put utilization at about 30%, so a gap remains between that target and actual operations.

In sensors, volume production ramped in the second half of 2025, and the company said it expected sensor sales in the KRW 10 billion range in 2026, a figure to be verified in the annual report once 2026 results are in.

On funding, it raised KRW 3 billion through a small public offering in September 2026, and a third-party allotment with the acquirer covers KRW 5 billion in two tranches, of which KRW 2 billion was issued on November 10, 2026.

The timing and terms of the remaining KRW 3 billion should be confirmed through further filings. On control, the acquirer bought 400,000 existing shares, and the KRW 1.5 billion balance payment is due no later than five days before the extraordinary shareholders' meeting.

If the December 15, 2026 meeting approves the articles amendment and the director and auditor appointments, the direction of the business and the management team will be set. The company has said the existing business will continue and that it will focus on stabilizing raw material procurement and supply.

08

Bull factors

Fab capacity and utilization target

In March 2026 the company said its fab capacity reached about 20,000 wafers a month and set a short-term target of lifting utilization above 75%. A September 2026 press report put utilization near 30%, so higher utilization would leave room for operating metrics to improve as fixed costs are absorbed.

The Q3 2026 operating loss of KRW 1.9 billion, down from KRW 2.51 billion in Q4 2025, moves in the same direction.

Diversifying demand in optical sensors and defense

In July 2026 the company said it had widened its optical sensor line to healthcare, smartphones, LiDAR and robot vision and started volume supply to global optical sensor makers.

In September 2026 it announced volume production of high-sensitivity APD optical devices, and it has also reported space-environment reliability testing on a domestic space verification platform. It is pursuing a strategy that combines sensors and RF devices for physical AI and defense.

Three straight quarters of revenue growth

Revenue bottomed at KRW 2.81 billion in Q4 2025 and then rose to KRW 3.59 billion in Q1 2026, KRW 3.78 billion in Q2 and KRW 3.85 billion in Q3. A press report said first-half 2026 revenue rose 22.4% from a year earlier.

The operating loss also narrowed each quarter in 2026, from KRW 2.26 billion in Q1 to KRW 2.14 billion in Q2 and KRW 1.9 billion in Q3, so the gap is closing gradually.

09

Bear factors

Persistent losses and thin margins

The company posted losses for four consecutive years from 2022 to 2025, and its operating margin fell from -33.4% to -59.5%. The Q3 2026 loss widened from a year earlier, and the net loss attributable to controlling shareholders over the last four quarters totaled KRW 9.36 billion. The main bearish factor is that higher sales still do not reach the bottom line.

Cash outflows and reliance on outside funding

A press report said first-half 2026 operating cash flow was about negative KRW 4.03 billion, and cash and equivalents fell to roughly KRW 124 million by mid-year. The company raised KRW 3 billion through a small public offering in September 2026. As long as operating cash outflows continue, further external funding may be needed.

Governance change and business continuity

Under an October 2026 agreement, KN International, a cosmetics company, acquired 400,000 existing shares, and after November's rights issue its stake reached 14.61%.

The acquirer's total assets were reported at KRW 2.4 billion at the end of 2025, and existing management other than CEO Shim Kyu-hwan was reported to be stepping down. The company says the core business will continue, but concern has been raised that business purposes unrelated to its core could be added.

10

Risk factors

Funding and dilution

If operating losses and cash outflows continue, rights issues may recur and existing shareholders' stakes will be diluted further. The issuance of 762,195 new shares in November 2026 has already changed the major shareholder structure. The timing of the remaining third-party allotment also needs to be confirmed.

Change of control and business purpose

If the December 15, 2026 extraordinary meeting approves the articles amendment and the director and auditor appointments, the management team will change.

Press reports noted that the exchange tightened its rules after listed technology-track companies repeatedly added business purposes unrelated to their core operations. This company was reported to fall outside rules that apply to companies filing preliminary listing reviews from July 2026 onward.

Listing maintenance requirements

Press coverage flagged the risk of designation as a managed stock if share price weakness and a shrinking market value persist. The company says it will respond to market-value pressure through capital raises. If continued losses coincide with managed-stock risk, financing terms could become less favorable.

Overall view

Sigetronics' revenue has risen for three consecutive quarters through Q3 2026, yet it posted losses for four consecutive years from 2022 to 2025 and has been loss-making for the last five quarters.

The operating margin fell from -33.4% in 2022 to -59.5% in 2025, so the sales recovery has not yet turned into better profitability. The debt ratio stood at 53.9% at the end of 2025, below 114.9% in 2022, but continued operating cash outflows led the company to raise equity in 2026.

The shareholder structure is also changing, with the stake held by KN International-linked holders rising to 14.61% and a change-of-control agreement in progress. Bullish points include fab capacity expansion, rising demand in optical sensors and defense, and quarterly revenue growth.

Bearish points include low utilization, cost pressure, reliance on external funding and concerns about business continuity. The key variable is whether the 75% utilization goal and the sensor revenue plan show up in actual results.

The next checkpoints are the outcome of the December 15, 2026 extraordinary shareholders' meeting and the full-year operating margin in the 2026 annual report, due by the end of March 2027.
