Report overview

Kwang Myung Electric: Shrinking Core Sales Amid Rehabilitation Proceedings

Kwang Myung Electric posted KRW 95.9 billion in 2025 revenue and a KRW 68.2 billion net loss attributable to controlling shareholders, and applied for corporate rehabilitation proceedings on April 30, 2026, so both earnings recovery and balance sheet normalization now depend on the outcome of legal proceedings.

02

Business structure

Kwang Myung Electric, founded in 1955, started out manufacturing and selling switchboards, which remain its base.

According to the company profile, its main revenue sources are sales of switchboard-related goods, construction income from solar power systems and electrical, fire-safety and mechanical facility works, and real estate rental income.

Its main products are switchboards and solar power systems, and its business list includes GIS, C-GIS, integrated supervisory control systems and busduct.

The company supplies made-to-order products tailored to customer requirements and describes product upgrades such as stronger internal arc performance and eco-friendly insulating gas as its R&D direction. Korea Electric Power Corporation and Samsung Electronics are listed as major customers.

The company says it is widening its business into renewable energy areas such as solar EPC, energy storage systems (ESS) and microgrids. It is also pursuing a shift in its production system toward digital products and new-material components that combine advanced IT and network technology with the power industry.

The company attributed the 2025 revenue decline to lower switchboard sales, which suggests switchboards remain the center of its sales.

Listed companies in the same electrical equipment sector include LS ELECTRIC, Hyosung Heavy Industries, HD Hyundai Electric and Iljin Electric, and Kwang Myung Electric is smaller in scale than these large power equipment makers.

04

Earnings analysis

Revenue rose from KRW 138.2 billion in 2022 to KRW 161.2 billion in 2023, a 16.6% increase, before falling 11.7% to KRW 142.4 billion in 2024 and 32.7% to KRW 95.9 billion in 2025. Revenue declined in both 2024 and 2025, and 2025 was the smallest year in the 2022 to 2025 period.

Operating profit of KRW 4.64 billion in 2022 turned into an operating loss of KRW 2.27 billion in 2023, followed by an operating loss of KRW 8.77 billion in 2024 and KRW 9.97 billion in 2025. The operating margin moved from 3.4% in 2022 to -1.4% in 2023, -6.2% in 2024 and -10.4% in 2025.

Profit attributable to controlling shareholders reached KRW 10.4 billion in 2023, up 88.6% year on year, but turned into a net loss of KRW 43.2 billion in 2024 and widened to a net loss of KRW 68.2 billion in 2025.

Net income including non-controlling interests, which is a different figure, was KRW 10.9 billion in 2023, a net loss of KRW 43.2 billion in 2024 and a net loss of KRW 68.2 billion in 2025.

The company attributed the 2025 revenue drop to lower switchboard sales and the operating and net losses to provisions for doubtful accounts on construction receivables and unbilled construction receivables.

Press reports tied the loss mainly to doubtful-account provisions linked to project financing, which reduced assets and increased liabilities.

The debt ratio rose from 45.0% in 2022 and 43.3% in 2023 to 97.4% in 2024 and 2,050.7% in 2025, when total liabilities were KRW 127.9 billion and total equity was KRW 6.23 billion.

Operating cash flow was negative in every year, at KRW -922 million in 2022, KRW -16.4 billion in 2023, KRW -20.7 billion in 2024 and KRW -3.77 billion in 2025.

On a quarterly basis, the company moved from revenue of KRW 30.6 billion and operating profit of KRW 1.96 billion in the third quarter of 2025 to revenue of KRW 22.1 billion and an operating loss of KRW 6.42 billion in the fourth quarter.

Revenue was KRW 12.1 billion in the first quarter of 2026 with an operating loss of KRW 2.81 billion, KRW 18.4 billion in the second quarter with an operating loss of KRW 5.03 billion, and KRW 11.7 billion in the third quarter with an operating loss of KRW 487 million.

The third-quarter loss narrowed, but losses have continued for four consecutive quarters since the fourth quarter of 2025, and the net loss attributable to controlling shareholders over the last four quarters totaled KRW 59 billion.

05

Industry analysis

Switchboards, the core product, belong to a product group tied to power facility investment, and the company says the expansion of the power market is favorable to its core business. A company official said the core business is in a very good state due to power market expansion and other factors.

By contrast, the company's first-half 2025 earnings commentary cited heightened North Korea tensions and protectionism as factors that worsened results in the switchboard segment. The 2025 revenue decline was also attributed directly to lower switchboard sales.

In terms of scale, revenue declined from 2024 through 2025, which points to a contracting phase.

Among competitors, LS ELECTRIC, Hyosung Heavy Industries, HD Hyundai Electric and Iljin Electric are listed companies in the same sector, while Kwang Myung Electric's total equity of KRW 6.23 billion at the end of 2025 leaves limited capital cushion.

The company positions the convergence of power and IT technology and renewable energy areas such as solar EPC, ESS and microgrids as growth axes. However, no disclosed figures for the revenue share or order size of these areas were identified, so the pace of this business shift is difficult to judge quantitatively.

Concentration of major customers in Korea Electric Power Corporation and Samsung Electronics also increases exposure to demand swings.

06

Outlook

The largest variable is the corporate rehabilitation process. On April 30, 2026, the company applied to the Suwon Bankruptcy Court, following a board resolution, for commencement of rehabilitation proceedings, preservation measures and a comprehensive stay order.

The company said it found it difficult to bear interest of more than KRW 4 billion a month because of project financing contingent liabilities, and that corporate rehabilitation was its path to survival.

The company decided a 5-for-1 reverse stock split of common shares on March 16, 2026, with a record date of June 18, 2026. The external auditor issued a disclaimer of opinion on April 7, 2026, and the company said it would pursue objections and re-audits to maintain its listing.

An April 2026 disclosure stated that trading remained suspended. Issues involving breach of trust tied to shareholder transactions and board resolutions have been raised, and debt-related litigation continues.

The company said it continues to generate stable profit from its switchboard business and keeps its plants running. Expansion into renewable energy and the shift toward digital products are medium-term directions the company has presented, but no disclosed order targets or capacity expansion schedules were identified.

The next checkpoints are the auditor's opinion in the 2026 annual report, progress of the rehabilitation plan, and the operating margin reported for the first quarter of 2027.

08

Bull factors

Company view on core business conditions

The company said its core business is in very good condition thanks to power market expansion. It also said the switchboard unit continues to generate steady profit and that plants are operating normally. These are company statements and should be read alongside the disclosed operating losses.

Narrowing operating loss

The operating loss narrowed to KRW 487 million in the third quarter of 2026, compared with KRW 2.81 billion in the first quarter and KRW 5.03 billion in the second. The smaller loss is a directional signal, but with quarterly revenue of KRW 11.7 billion, one quarter is not enough to establish a recovery trend.

Debt restructuring and business expansion

The company describes corporate rehabilitation as a way to reduce debt tied to project financing contingent liabilities and keep the business going. A reduction in that burden is the bullish argument. The company has also presented expansion into renewable energy areas such as solar EPC, ESS and microgrids.

09

Bear factors

Shrinking sales and persistent losses

Revenue fell to KRW 142.4 billion in 2024 and KRW 95.9 billion in 2025, and third-quarter 2026 revenue of KRW 11.7 billion was down 61.9% from a year earlier.

Operating losses have continued for three straight years from 2023, and the company has reported losses for four consecutive quarters since the fourth quarter of 2025.

Capital impairment and heavy debt burden

Total equity at the end of 2025 was KRW 6.23 billion against total liabilities of KRW 127.9 billion, a debt ratio of 2,050.7%. Press reports put current liabilities due within one year at about KRW 116.3 billion. Operating cash flow was negative in each year from 2022 through 2025.

Audit opinion and trading conditions

The external auditor issued a disclaimer of opinion on April 7, 2026, which triggered a delisting ground. An April 2026 disclosure stated that trading suspension would continue. With a capital reduction and rehabilitation both in progress, the company's value could shift significantly depending on legal outcomes.

10

Risk factors

Listing status and rehabilitation

The disclaimer of opinion created a delisting ground, and the company said it is pursuing objections and re-audits. Once rehabilitation starts, all claims and debts are frozen, which limits financing flexibility. Whether the rehabilitation plan is approved will shape the chances of keeping the listing.

Project financing contingent liabilities and interest burden

According to a company statement, project financing contingent liabilities totaled KRW 488.2 billion at the end of 2025. The company said it had difficulty bearing monthly interest of more than KRW 4 billion.

Press reports identified provisions for doubtful accounts linked to project financing as the main cause of the asset decline and liability increase.

Governance and litigation

Press coverage reported repeated sales of major shareholder stakes after March 2024. Breach-of-trust issues were raised over a board resolution and share purchase in early 2026, and debt-related lawsuits are ongoing. Minority shareholders held 65.77% at the end of 2025, a high share that leaves the voting structure dispersed.

Overall view

Kwang Myung Electric's revenue has declined for two consecutive years from its 2023 peak of KRW 161.2 billion, and it has posted operating losses for three straight years from 2023 through 2025.

Total equity was KRW 6.23 billion at the end of 2025 against a debt ratio of 2,050.7%, which points to a heavy financial burden.

The operating loss narrowed to KRW 487 million in the third quarter of 2026, a sign of a smaller loss, but the company has reported losses for four consecutive quarters since the fourth quarter of 2025, and the net loss attributable to controlling shareholders over the last four quarters totaled KRW 59 billion.

On the bullish side, the company says its core business is in good shape, the loss has narrowed, and corporate rehabilitation could reduce debt. On the bearish side, shrinking sales, capital impairment, a disclaimer of opinion with its delisting ground, trading suspension and breach-of-trust litigation are intertwined.

The direction of the business and normalization of the capital structure will depend on the outcome of the rehabilitation proceedings and the audit opinion.

The next checkpoints are the auditor's opinion in the 2026 annual report, progress of the rehabilitation plan and the operating margin for the first quarter of 2027.
