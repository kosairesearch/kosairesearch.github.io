02

Business structure

Samho Development was founded in 1976 and listed on KOSPI in May 2005 as a specialist civil engineering contractor. Its core business is civil works such as roads, railways and dams, built around specialist trades including earthworks, civil structures and reinforced concrete.

The company is described as a specialist contractor focused on major infrastructure such as roads, railways and ports, and it has recently expanded into railway and deep urban tunnel work.

Revenue is split into construction work (management fees for joint ventures, earthworks and tunnel work) and other items such as aggregate sales and property rental income, and all of these items were recorded as 100% domestic at the end of 2025.

The company also runs a venture investment segment, and its 2025 nine-month earnings commentary cited improved returns from investee companies as a driver of profitability.

Because construction revenue is recognized on a percentage-of-completion basis, the pace and cost control of individual sites drive quarterly sales and profit swings.

Its main counterparties are public infrastructure clients and large builders such as Hyundai Engineering & Construction and GS Engineering & Construction, and a contract was confirmed to be signed jointly with Hyundai Engineering & Construction.

Contracts disclosed in 2026 include a KRW 9.45 billion cogeneration project in Yongin, a KRW 27.69 billion expressway section in Hamyang-Changnyeong, a KRW 41.72 billion deep drainage tunnel in Gangnam and a KRW 55.5 billion tunnel on the Southern Inland Railway.

In competition, the company is not compared directly with large general contractors, and it is classified as a mid-sized company focused on specialist trades.

04

Earnings analysis

On a DART-disclosed basis, annual revenue was KRW 307.5 billion in 2022, KRW 361.9 billion in 2023 (up 17.7%), KRW 401.8 billion in 2024 (up 11.0%) and KRW 409.7 billion in 2025 (up 2.0%). Revenue has risen for three straight years from 2023 through 2025, but the 2025 growth rate was far slower than in 2024.

Operating profit moved from KRW 4.47 billion in 2022 to KRW 10.5 billion in 2023 (up 135.7%), fell to KRW 1.17 billion in 2024 (down 88.9%) and rose to KRW 14.5 billion in 2025 (up 1,135.6%), making 2025 the largest year of the 2022 to 2025 period.

The operating margin was 1.5% in 2022, 2.9% in 2023, 0.3% in 2024 and 3.5% in 2025, the highest of the four years. The company attributed the 2025 profit increase to the end of loss-making sites and the progress of well-performing projects, as reported after its February 6, 2026 disclosure.

Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 5.93 billion in 2022, KRW 15.1 billion in 2023, KRW 5.26 billion in 2024 and KRW 18.7 billion in 2025, with 2024 the smallest year.

Quarterly revenue was KRW 104.4 billion in the third quarter of 2025, KRW 111.3 billion in the fourth quarter, KRW 90.7 billion in the first quarter of 2026, KRW 95.6 billion in the second quarter and KRW 89.9 billion in the third quarter.

Operating profit fell from KRW 3.96 billion in the third quarter of 2025 to KRW 1.66 billion in the fourth quarter (down 58.1%) and KRW 959 million in the first quarter of 2026 (down 42.1%), then rose for two consecutive quarters to KRW 1.15 billion in the second quarter (up 20.1%) and KRW 2.97 billion in the third quarter (up 158.4%).

Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders went from KRW 1.73 billion in the fourth quarter of 2025 to KRW 3.45 billion in the second quarter of 2026 and then fell to KRW 1.86 billion in the third quarter, and the last four quarters total KRW 9.46 billion.

Operating cash flow was KRW -11.3 billion in 2022, KRW 10.6 billion in 2023 and KRW 36.7 billion in 2024, then fell to KRW 27.6 billion in 2025 despite higher profit.

The debt ratio rose from 29.0% in 2022 to 51.8% in 2025, and at the end of 2025 total equity was KRW 241.2 billion against total liabilities of KRW 124.9 billion.

05

Industry analysis

Domestic civil works demand is tied to public infrastructure budgets and tender schedules for railways, roads and urban flood control.

Samho Development's 2026 contracts fit this pattern, with the Southern Inland Railway tunnel, the Hamyang-Changnyeong expressway section 7 and the Gangnam deep drainage tunnel as representative examples.

The Gangnam drainage tunnel aims at climate-related flood prevention in a repeatedly flooded area, and experts were reported to see it as proof of the company's competitiveness in deep urban tunnel work.

The Yongin cogeneration project shows the company taking on energy facility work beyond public civil engineering, which broadens its client mix.

Confirmed contracts are concentrated in earthworks and civil structures, including the 2025 Tancheon underpass earthworks project with Hyundai Engineering & Construction and the 2026 earthworks and structures contract with GS Engineering & Construction.

Large general contractors typically hold prime contracts on major projects, while Samho Development's role has often been to take specialist trades. Civil specialist work is contested among mid-sized contractors, so securing contract size and duration matters.

The continued flow of contract disclosures alongside lower quarterly revenue in the third quarter of 2026 reflects the lag with which awarded work feeds into sales in this sector.

The Hamyang-Changnyeong expressway section 7 is disclosed with a work end date of December 31, 2026, so later revenue will depend on other sites moving forward.

Among the disclosed contracts, the Yongin, Gangnam and Southern Inland Railway contracts are confirmed to have no advance or down payment and to pay according to construction progress. Because revenue is recognized on a percentage-of-completion basis, 2027 sales will depend on how quickly these sites progress.

The next periodic report is the 2026 annual report, and the fourth-quarter 2026 results, the full-year operating margin and operating cash flow are the items to check in it.

Reports said the company's 2025 single-contract disclosures were corrected several times, so the consistency between contract disclosures and financial results needs to be checked in the next periodic report.

The Gangnam project runs to February 2030 and the railway tunnel to December 2030, which lengthens the time horizon of the work on hand. Both contracts pay according to construction progress without a down payment.

Recovery in profit scale

The company cited the end of loss-making sites and the progress of profitable projects as the reasons. Third-quarter 2026 operating profit of KRW 2.97 billion rose 158.4% from the prior quarter.

Consecutive quarterly profit gains and cash generation

Operating profit rose for two consecutive quarters after bottoming at KRW 959 million in the first quarter of 2026, reaching KRW 1.15 billion in the second quarter and KRW 2.97 billion in the third.

Operating cash flow was KRW -11.3 billion in 2022 but has been positive every year since 2023, at KRW 10.6 billion, KRW 36.7 billion and KRW 27.6 billion. Total equity of KRW 241.2 billion at the end of 2025 is larger than total liabilities of KRW 124.9 billion.

Revenue growth for full-year 2025 was only 2.0%. Contract disclosures have continued in 2026, but a clear recovery in quarterly revenue has not yet appeared.

Weaker link between profit and cash

Operating cash flow fell to KRW 27.6 billion in 2025 from KRW 36.7 billion in 2024 even as profit rose. Daily Union reported that operating cash flow declined and working capital burden grew despite higher earnings in 2025. The debt ratio rose from 29.0% in 2022 to 51.8% in 2025.

Volatile quarterly earnings

In the fourth quarter of 2025 it was KRW 1.73 billion, down 71.9% from the third quarter. The swings are large, so annual and quarterly trends point in different directions.

Risk factors

Disclosure consistency and transparency

Reports said the company's 2025 single-contract disclosures were corrected several times, which adds a verification burden on contract amounts. Because contract disclosures are expressed as a percentage of revenue, the gap between contract size and the timing of revenue recognition needs to be read alongside them. The correction history is an item to check in the next periodic report.

Site profitability and construction progress

Because revenue follows construction progress, cost increases or schedule delays at individual sites can move quarterly profit. The operating margin fell to 0.3% in 2024, and the end of loss-making sites was cited as the main driver of the 2025 profit increase. If losses recur at a particular site, the earnings recovery could weaken.

Largest shareholder stake and liquidity

Holdings including related parties stood at 33.71% as of the April 3, 2026 report, which limits the shares actually traded. This structure can amplify supply and demand swings, and changes in the largest shareholder group's stake must also be reported. The April 2026 filing recorded a reduction of 52,510 shares after an executive's departure.

Overall view

Contract disclosures in 2026, including the Yongin cogeneration project, the Hamyang-Changnyeong expressway, the Gangnam drainage tunnel and the Southern Inland Railway tunnel, run into periods from 2027 through 2030.

On the other side, third-quarter 2026 revenue of KRW 89.9 billion fell 13.9% year on year, and operating cash flow in 2025 was lower than in 2024, so the link between profit and cash is weak.

Quarterly operating profit has risen for two consecutive quarters since the first-quarter 2026 low, but net profit attributable to controlling shareholders fell in the third quarter, so the direction is mixed.

A largest shareholder stake of 33.71% and reports of repeated disclosure corrections remain issues to examine alongside information reliability and supply and demand structure.

The next checkpoints are the end of the Hamyang-Changnyeong work period on December 31, 2026 and the 2026 annual report due by March 31, 2027, where the operating margin and operating cash flow will serve as the benchmark for whether the profit recovery is sustained.
