Report overview

Order backlog up, sales down: a transition year for SK oceanplant

SK oceanplant's sales have fallen for two consecutive quarters, yet its order backlog rose to KRW 1,769.2 billion at the end of the third quarter of 2026, and the company is in a transition period in which a major shareholder sale overlaps with offshore wind project timing.

  1. 1

    Sales have declined for two consecutive quarters since the second quarter of 2026. Third-quarter 2026 sales were KRW 116.8 billion, down 60.2% from a year earlier.

  2. 2

    Profitability has held up better than sales. Third-quarter 2026 operating profit was KRW 16.9 billion, down 12.3% year on year, with a higher offshore wind share supporting the margin.

  3. 3

    The order backlog reached KRW 1,769.2 billion at the end of the third quarter of 2026, up 59.8% from the prior quarter, with a six-vessel Aframax tanker contract from a Greek owner (about KRW 600 billion) as a key driver.

  4. 4

    SK ecoplant has signed a share purchase agreement to sell its entire stake to a domestic asset manager. Analysts note that the business direction after completion remains uncertain.

  5. 5

    In a report dated November 16, 2026, Eugene Investment & Securities kept its Buy rating and lowered its target price from KRW 25,000 to KRW 20,000.

02

Business structure

SK oceanplant is built around offshore wind substructures and also builds ships and offshore structures. Its offshore wind segment accounted for 61% of third-quarter 2026 sales and lifted the operating margin to 14.5%.

The company is regarded as Asia's largest offshore wind substructure maker, with shipyard-level facilities and manufacturing capacity.

It is building a 1.57 million square meter offshore wind production base in Goseong, Gyeongsangnam-do, with a total investment of about KRW 1,000 billion and completion planned by the end of 2026.

The special vessel business has been building the second, third and fourth follow-on vessels of the Ulsan-class Batch-III for the Republic of Korea Navy. Domestic special vessel revenue has already been recognized in full, and remaining orders will be reflected in 2027 sales.

In commercial shipbuilding, the company signed a contract in late October 2026 with a Greek owner for six 115K-class Aframax crude tankers.

The order backlog at end-2025 was KRW 1,238.5 billion, split into KRW 970.2 billion offshore wind, KRW 197.3 billion special vessels, KRW 66.4 billion commercial and MRO, and KRW 4.3 billion steel pipe and plant.

Offshore wind orders depend on power producers' investment decisions, which higher interest rates have made more uncertain. Eugene Investment & Securities has described the company as one of the few builders able to cover offshore wind, shipbuilding and offshore plants together.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual sales recovered to KRW 965.4 billion in 2025, a year reported as the company's largest sales since its founding. The path was KRW 691.8 billion in 2022, KRW 925.8 billion in 2023 (up 33.8% year on year), KRW 662.6 billion in 2024 (down 28.4%), and KRW 965.4 billion in 2025 (up 45.7%).

Within 2022 to 2025, 2025 was the largest year and 2024 the smallest. Operating profit was KRW 71.9 billion in 2022 and KRW 75.6 billion in 2023 (up 5.1%), the largest year, fell to KRW 41.8 billion in 2024 (down 44.7%), the smallest year, and recovered to KRW 59.5 billion in 2025 (up 42.4%).

The operating margin declined from 10.4% in 2022 to 8.2% in 2023, 6.3% in 2024 and 6.2% in 2025, so the sales recovery did not restore margins. Analysts attribute this to the low profit contribution of special vessels versus the high margins of offshore wind.

Profit attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 22.3 billion in 2022, KRW 57.5 billion in 2023 (up 158.3%), KRW 16.4 billion in 2024 (down 71.5%) and KRW 37.8 billion in 2025 (up 130.6%).

Net income including non-controlling interests was KRW 37.5 billion in 2025, a separate figure from profit attributable to controlling shareholders. Operating cash flow was KRW 68.6 billion in 2022, negative KRW 138.2 billion in 2023, KRW 135.4 billion in 2024 and negative KRW 39.8 billion in 2025.

The debt-to-equity ratio fell from 132.0% in 2022 to 41.0% in 2025, with total equity of KRW 814.5 billion and total liabilities of KRW 333.7 billion at the end of 2025.

Quarterly sales went from KRW 293.2 billion in the third quarter of 2025 to KRW 174.5 billion in the fourth quarter (down 40.5% quarter on quarter), KRW 174.7 billion in the first quarter of 2026, KRW 170.5 billion in the second quarter, and KRW 116.8 billion in the third quarter (down 31.5% quarter on quarter and 60.2% year on year).

Sales have declined for two consecutive quarters since the second quarter of 2026. Operating profit was KRW 19.3 billion in the third quarter of 2025, KRW 13.7 billion in the fourth quarter, KRW 16.9 billion in the first quarter of 2026, KRW 19.3 billion in the second quarter and KRW 16.9 billion in the third quarter.

Profit attributable to controlling shareholders over the latest four quarters totaled KRW 47.6 billion. A one-time settlement payment of about KRW 4.0 billion from the Taiwan Penghu 1 project supported third-quarter results.

05

Industry analysis

Offshore wind substructures and commercial ship orders sit in different markets. In commercial shipping, global tanker orders in the first half of 2026 totaled 407 vessels, up sharply from 139 in the same period of 2025.

The ratio of order backlog to the existing fleet of tankers above 10,000 DWT rose above the 15.8% level of the same point in 2025. Greek owners placed 147 tanker newbuild orders in the first half of 2026.

SK oceanplant's commercial orders fit this trend, but analysts note that sustaining the commercial business depends on delivery timing, cost control and quality on the first projects.

In offshore wind, policy is a key variable, with the K-GX announcement and the 12th Basic Plan for electricity cited as supportive by brokers. Europe has begun constructing GW-scale offshore substations since late 2025, which broadens the addressable market.

Higher interest rates, however, can slow project approvals in Korea and overseas. In competition, offshore wind substructures are a field where manufacturing capacity and delivery records form entry barriers, while commercial shipbuilding is a smaller scale business where order intake speed matters.

Brokers describe offshore wind as being in a phase where the gap between order intake and revenue recognition is widening, and the shipbuilding side as an early stage of backlog expansion.

06

Outlook

The company's 2026 sales target of KRW 821.7 billion, given in a fair disclosure in early 2026, is about 14.9% below 2025 sales. The company attributes the lower target to the near completion of its special vessel projects, such as naval frigates. Sales for January to September 2026 fell 41.6% from a year earlier.

A company official said sales activities in commercial shipbuilding and plant work are continuing and that guidance could recover if new orders arrive. Analysts point to an FPSO order expected from late 2026 to early 2027, and a European offshore substation order is also being discussed.

Offshore wind carries timing risk. Delayed construction start on the Anma offshore wind project could push out the recovery in next year's results. Shinhan Investment Corp. expects new project revenue only from 2028 onward.

Eugene Investment & Securities estimates 2027 sales of KRW 739.0 billion and operating profit of KRW 73.2 billion. These are broker estimates, not company guidance. The Greek contract has a delivery schedule of 2029, so revenue from it will be recognized over several years.

The points to watch are the timing of new order confirmations, the start of the Anma project, and completion of the Goseong base.

08

Bull factors

Order backlog growth and entry into commercial shipping

The order backlog reached KRW 1,769.2 billion at the end of the third quarter, up 59.8% from the prior quarter. The Aframax contract could grow to about KRW 800 billion if two option vessels are exercised. Commercial orders are read as reducing reliance on offshore wind.

Offshore wind margin structure

The third-quarter 2026 operating margin reached 14.5%, supported by offshore wind at 61% of sales. Operating profit held up at KRW 16.9 billion despite a sharp sales decline. The margin mix shift toward offshore wind is visible in the reported results.

European expansion and policy momentum

Analysts see a likely order for an HVDC-linked offshore substation for a North Sea project. Brokers cite abundant wind policy momentum in Korea. The ability to cover offshore wind, shipbuilding and offshore plant work is also cited as a strength.

09

Bear factors

Sales decline is continuing

Third-quarter 2026 sales of KRW 116.8 billion fell 60.2% year on year and were below the market estimate of KRW 155.6 billion. Cumulative sales for January to September 2026 declined 41.6%, so reaching the company's annual target requires a sharp rise in the fourth quarter.

Project delays and a later recovery

Construction start on the Anma offshore wind project has been delayed, which may push back the recovery in next year's results. Shinhan Investment Corp. cut its earnings forecasts. Offshore wind revenue gaps lengthen when order timing slips.

Uncertain business direction after the sale

SK ecoplant, the largest shareholder, is selling its entire stake to a domestic asset manager under a signed share purchase agreement, after which the buyer becomes the largest shareholder. DS Investment noted that the medium- to long-term business direction is hard to predict even after completion. This could add uncertainty to order decisions.

10

Risk factors

Project timing risk

Offshore wind substructure revenue depends on steel cutting and construction start dates. The 2026 earnings path was expected to be back-half weighted because steel cutting on key projects slipped to the second half. Higher financing costs for power producers could delay orders and increase quarter-to-quarter volatility.

Ownership transition risk

Once the sale of the controlling stake is complete, the business strategy could change. Whether the current mix of special vessels and offshore wind will be maintained, or reshaped around commercial shipping, has not been confirmed. Order decisions and investment plans could shift around the closing.

Commercial shipbuilding execution risk

Analysts say the sustainability of commercial orders depends on delivery timing, cost control, quality management and after-sales service on the first project. Cost swings and process delays before the 2029 delivery could weigh on margins. Whether offshore wind fabrication experience carries over to commercial vessels remains to be tested.

What to watch next

  1. Late November 2026 to early 2027

    Whether the FPSO order and the European offshore substation order are confirmed. If confirmed, the next check is how the backlog mix and the offshore wind share change.

  2. End of December 2026

    Whether construction of the Goseong offshore wind production base is completed on the planned end-2026 schedule. Completion timing is directly tied to substructure production capacity.

  3. Around closing of the share transfer (date not fixed)

    After the largest shareholder change is completed, the check is whether the business strategy and management composition change.

  4. By end of March 2027

    The 2026 annual report is the next regular filing. The checks are how annual sales moved against KRW 965.4 billion in 2025 and whether the operating margin rose above 6.2%.

  5. Mid-May 2027 (typical timing for first-quarter results)

    Whether first-quarter 2027 sales recover from the third-quarter 2026 level of KRW 116.8 billion, checked alongside construction progress on the Anma offshore wind project.

12

Overall view

SK oceanplant's sales fell sharply in 2026, yet it shows two trends at once: resilient profitability and an expanding order backlog. The backlog reached KRW 1,769.2 billion at the end of the third quarter, and with offshore wind rising to 61% of sales the operating margin hit 14.5%.

Sales recovered to KRW 965.4 billion in 2025, but the operating margin declined from 10.4% in 2022 to 6.2% in 2025. The largest variable is how the business direction changes once the controlling stake sale closes. Brokers are divided.

Eugene Investment & Securities kept its Buy rating and cited intact order momentum while lowering its target price, whereas Shinhan Investment Corp. cut its earnings forecasts over project delays and placed new project revenue from 2028 onward.

The items to confirm are the FPSO and European substation orders, the start of the Anma project, completion of the Goseong base, and the operating margin in the 2026 annual report.
