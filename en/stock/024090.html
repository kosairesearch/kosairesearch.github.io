DCM: Anti-Dumping Tailwind Meets Soft Demand

DCM Corp., a laminated color steel sheet maker, saw both revenue and operating profit decline in 2025, though quarterly operating profit has shown a gradual recovery through 2026.

02

Business structure

DCM Corp., founded in 1972 and listed on the KOSPI in 1999, is a primary steel processing company whose core business is manufacturing laminated color steel sheets (VCM) by bonding synthetic resin films such as PVC, PP, and PET onto steel plates.

LAMINATED color steel sheets account for the vast majority of revenue, with industrial film and other products making up the remainder.

The main applications are exterior panels for home appliances including refrigerators, washing machines, dryers, and dishwashers, and the company has maintained its market position in the domestic refrigerator door segment—its largest appliance end market—through continuous new product development.

Customers are understood to include major domestic and overseas white-goods makers, and the product range also extends to construction interior/exterior materials, shipbuilding interiors, and furniture materials.

Its production base is located in Yangsan, Gyeongsangnam-do, sourcing steel from suppliers such as POSCO while procuring films through affiliated companies including Dorim Corp., reflecting a degree of vertical integration.

In the domestic coated and color steel market, competitors cited include Dongkuk CM, KG Steel, SeAH CM, and POSCO Steeleon. In recent years the company has pursued diversification into composite panel manufacturing and has invested in smart process automation to improve production efficiency.

With a meaningful export share, the company has expanded sales into China, Southeast Asia, India, and Turkey, and is reportedly exploring entry into European and U.S. markets.

04

Earnings analysis

On an annual basis, revenue fell from KRW 240.6 billion in 2022 to KRW 218.3 billion in 2023, a 9.3% decline, then rose 10.4% to KRW 240.9 billion in 2024, before declining again by 5.8% to KRW 227.1 billion in 2025.

Operating profit plunged 63.0% from KRW 24.5 billion in 2022 to KRW 9.06 billion in 2023, rebounded 87.4% to KRW 17 billion in 2024, then fell again by 43.6% to KRW 9.56 billion in 2025.

The operating margin likewise swung from 10.2% in 2022 down to 4.1% in 2023, back up to 7.0% in 2024, and down again to 4.2% in 2025, underscoring volatile profitability.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders swung from a profit of KRW 37.6 billion in 2022 to a loss of KRW 10.3 billion in 2023, turned profitable again at KRW 18.9 billion in 2024, and then declined 37.5% to KRW 11.8 billion in 2025.

On a quarterly basis, operating profit fell from KRW 1.96 billion in Q2 2025 into an operating loss of KRW 1.09 billion in Q3 2025, swung back to a profit of KRW 2.57 billion in Q4 2025, then came in at KRW 1.99 billion in Q1 2026 (down 22.9% sequentially) and KRW 3.26 billion in Q2 2026 (up 64.1% sequentially and 66.6% year over year), continuing a recovery trend.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders over the trailing four quarters (Q3 2025 through Q2 2026) totaled KRW 11.2 billion. Operating cash flow has steadily weakened, from KRW 31.5 billion in 2022 to KRW 7.19 billion in 2025.

The company attributed the 2025 earnings decline to volume reductions and pricing pressure stemming from changes in the steel tariff environment.

05

Industry analysis

Korea's coated and color steel market has long faced price distortion from low-priced Chinese imports, according to industry observers.

Following petitions from three domestic steelmakers—Dongkuk CM, KG Steel, and SeAH CM—the Korea Trade Commission under the Ministry of Trade, Industry and Energy conducted a dumping investigation into Chinese zinc and zinc-alloy surface-treated cold-rolled products, resulting in a decision in May 2026 to impose provisional anti-dumping duties of 22.34% to 33.67%, applied for four months from June 12 to October 12, 2026.

Industry commentary suggested the measure was more likely to support the market floor against suppressed low-price imports than to trigger a sharp price rally.

Separately, analysis indicated that the United States' 25% universal tariff on imported steel weakened the price competitiveness of Korean color steel exports to the U.S. relative to competitors such as Taiwan.

China's government is set to introduce an export licensing system covering 300 steel product categories starting in 2026, which may curb some low-price export practices, although observers note its underlying cost advantage is expected to persist.

With downstream appliance and construction demand yet to show a full recovery, DCM Corp. is simultaneously exposed to potential benefits from anti-dumping duties and a broader slowdown in global demand.

Outlook

In its corporate value-up plan disclosed in March 2026, DCM Corp. set goals of strengthening a profit-focused management structure and continuing shareholder-friendly dividend policies.

The execution plan specified pursuing profit-centered management through business diversification and expansion of production facilities.

In October 2026, the company signed a KRW 3 billion treasury stock trust contract with Shinyoung Securities, running from October 12, 2026 to April 13, 2027, with the stated purpose of enhancing shareholder value through share buybacks followed by cancellation.

At the time of the contract, treasury shares already accounted for 25.94% of shares outstanding, reflecting an already substantial buyback position.

Quarterly operating profit improved sequentially in both Q1 and Q2 2026, a pattern that may be partly linked to the provisional anti-dumping duties in effect during the first half of the year.

However, since the provisional duty period ended on October 12, 2026, whether a final ruling confirms continued duties at similar levels remains a variable that could affect the pricing environment going forward.

On the shareholder return side, the 2025 dividend payout ratio reached 59.33%, indicating a relatively high share of profit returned to shareholders.

Bull factors

Potential Pricing Environment Improvement from Anti-Dumping Duties

Provisional anti-dumping duties of up to 33.67% on Chinese coated and color steel, applied from June to October 2026, narrowed the price gap with low-cost imports. This has been cited as a factor that could improve the pricing defenses of domestic color steel producers.

If a final ruling confirms continued duties, it could support medium-term price stability. This is, however, an industry-wide structural variable rather than a factor specific to DCM Corp.

Gradual Quarterly Earnings Recovery in 2026

After an operating loss in Q3 2025, the company returned to profit in Q4 2025 and sustained operating profits through Q1 and Q2 2026. Notably, Q2 2026 operating profit rose 64.1% sequentially and 66.6% year over year, marking a clear recovery trend.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders also remained relatively solid at KRW 4.39 billion in Q1 2026 and KRW 3.28 billion in Q2 2026. Whether this trend continues, however, will need to be confirmed in upcoming quarterly results.

Continued Treasury Buyback, Cancellation, and Shareholder Returns

The KRW 3 billion treasury stock trust contract signed in October 2026 explicitly states the purpose of acquisition followed by cancellation. With the treasury share ratio already at 25.94% at the time of the contract, further cancellations could reduce shares outstanding.

The 2025 year-end dividend payout ratio reached a high 59.33%, and the 2026 corporate value-up plan set continued shareholder-friendly dividend policy as a goal. This policy stance reflects the company's ongoing attention to shareholder returns.

Bear factors

2025 Earnings Slowdown and Profit Volatility

2025 revenue fell 5.8% year over year to KRW 227.1 billion, while operating profit dropped sharply by 43.6% to KRW 9.56 billion. The operating margin also deteriorated, falling from 7.0% in 2024 to 4.2% in 2025.

The company attributed this to volume declines and pricing pressure linked to changes in the steel tariff environment.

Between 2022 and 2025, operating profit swung from KRW 24.5 billion down to KRW 9.06 billion, back up to KRW 17 billion, and down again to KRW 9.56 billion, a wide range that limits earnings predictability.

Weakening Operating Cash Flow

Operating cash flow declined from KRW 31.5 billion in 2022 to KRW 13.9 billion in 2023, KRW 19.3 billion in 2024, and further down to KRW 7.19 billion in 2025. This reflects a broader slowdown in cash generation in tandem with operating profit fluctuations.

Given ongoing shareholder returns through buybacks and dividends, continued cash flow weakness could warrant closer monitoring of financial flexibility, though the low debt ratio means it does not immediately translate into financial stability concerns.

Dual Impact of the Global Trade Environment

Analysis has indicated that the U.S. 25% universal tariff on imported steel weakened the price competitiveness of Korean color steel exports to the U.S. relative to competitors like Taiwan.

While anti-dumping duties on low-priced Chinese imports could benefit the domestic market, the U.S. tariff could act as a headwind on the export side, creating a mixed trade environment.

Additionally, since the provisional duty period ended on October 12, 2026, pricing uncertainty remains until a final ruling is issued.

Risk factors

Trade and Tariff Risk

Provisional anti-dumping duties on Chinese coated and color steel were applied temporarily from June 12 to October 12, 2026, with a final ruling and the continuation of duties not yet confirmed. The U.S. universal tariff on imported steel also remains a variable affecting export price competitiveness to the U.S. Both volume and pricing can be simultaneously affected by shifts in the trade environment.

Downstream Demand Risk

The company's core laminated color steel sheet business is heavily dependent on demand from home appliance exterior applications, and construction and appliance sector conditions have not yet shown a clear recovery.

Macro shifts such as a global slowdown or tariff conflicts could affect appliance makers' production and order volumes. Delayed demand recovery could constrain the pace of revenue recovery.

Raw Material Price Volatility Risk

DCM Corp. sources its steel raw materials from suppliers such as POSCO, meaning steel price fluctuations directly affect its cost structure. Changes in the prices of auxiliary materials like films can also influence the cost base. If raw material costs rise and price pass-through is delayed, operating margins could come under pressure.

Overall view

DCM Corp. is a specialized steel processor that has maintained a stable position in the home appliance exterior materials market through its laminated color steel sheet products, with a low debt ratio and consistent dividend payments as notable financial characteristics.

In 2025, both revenue and operating profit declined year over year amid changes in the steel tariff environment, but operating profit showed sequential improvement in both Q1 and Q2 2026.

Provisional anti-dumping duties on Chinese coated and color steel, applied from June to October 2026, may have contributed positively to the domestic pricing environment, though a final ruling has not yet been confirmed.

The treasury stock buyback-and-cancellation plan and high dividend payout ratio reflect a consistent shareholder-return policy. That said, operating cash flow has weakened over several years, and the wide swings in annual and quarterly results make it premature to draw firm conclusions about future earnings direction.

The final ruling on Chinese anti-dumping duties and upcoming quarterly results remain the key variables to watch going forward.
