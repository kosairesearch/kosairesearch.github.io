DCM: Checking Earnings Strength Amid an Appliance Steel Slowdown

DCM's operating profit fell 43.6% in 2025, and although revenue rose in the second and third quarters of 2026, the third quarter turned into a net loss attributable to controlling shareholders, so whether earnings strength is recovering remains to be confirmed.

02

Business structure

DCM's main business is manufacturing laminated steel sheet used for the exterior of home appliances. The largest domestic demand for laminated steel comes from the appliance segment, including refrigerator doors, and the company says it maintains its market share by applying new product development.

The company was listed on the KOSPI in 1999 and is classified as a primary steel manufacturer. Through two affiliated companies it also makes and sells plastic products.

As part of its diversification, it is expanding composite panel manufacturing, and in appliances it is developing a wider range of steel sheets to grow demand. On the production side, it is raising efficiency with smart processes and automation systems and expanding its environmentally friendly product lines.

Demand is tied to the cycle of the steel-consuming industries, which makes the business sensitive to outside shocks. In the first three quarters of 2025, standalone revenue fell 10.1% year on year and operating profit fell 54.6%, with the continued slump in steel-consuming industries cited as the reason.

The company attributed the full-year 2025 decline to lower shipment volumes and selling price effects from market changes such as steel product tariffs. As a result, its earnings depend heavily on appliance demand, steel prices and the tariff environment.

04

Earnings analysis

On an annual basis, revenue was highest in 2024 at KRW 240.9 billion and lowest in 2023 at KRW 218.3 billion. Revenue fell 9.3% from KRW 240.6 billion in 2022 to KRW 218.3 billion in 2023, rose 10.4% to KRW 240.9 billion in 2024, and fell 5.8% to KRW 227.1 billion in 2025.

Operating profit was highest in 2022 at KRW 24.5 billion and lowest in 2023 at KRW 9.06 billion, a 63.0% decline. It rose 87.4% to KRW 17 billion in 2024 and then fell 43.6% to KRW 9.56 billion in 2025.

The operating margin went from 10.2% in 2022 to 4.1% in 2023, recovered to 7.0% in 2024, and slipped back to 4.2% in 2025.

Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders moved from KRW 37.6 billion in 2022 to a net loss of KRW 10.3 billion in 2023, returned to a profit of KRW 18.9 billion in 2024, and fell 37.5% to KRW 11.8 billion in 2025.

Net profit and net profit attributable to controlling shareholders are reported as the same figure for each year from 2022 to 2025. The company attributed the 2025 decline to lower volumes from market changes such as steel product tariffs, together with selling price effects.

Among quarters, the third quarter of 2025 was the only quarter with an operating loss in the period, at KRW 1.09 billion. The fourth quarter of 2025 returned to operating profit of KRW 2.57 billion on revenue of KRW 55.9 billion.

First-quarter 2026 revenue was KRW 49.1 billion, down 12.3% from the previous quarter, but revenue then rose to KRW 50.2 billion in the second quarter and KRW 50.4 billion in the third, two consecutive quarterly gains.

Second-quarter 2026 operating profit rose 64.1% to KRW 3.26 billion, while third-quarter operating profit fell 48.9% to KRW 1.67 billion and net profit attributable to controlling shareholders turned into a net loss of KRW 2.7 billion.

Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders for the last four quarters totaled KRW 8.24 billion, and operating profit and that profit line moved in different directions from quarter to quarter.

05

Industry analysis

DCM's end demand is concentrated in the appliance segment, especially laminated steel for refrigerator doors. This segment is described as the largest domestic demand source for laminated steel, and the company says it is holding its share by applying new products.

By contrast, its explanation of the first nine months of 2025 pointed to global uncertainty, US-China tensions and the wars in Europe and the Middle East, which produced an economic slowdown and higher raw material prices and extended the slump in steel-consuming industries.

The company also said that policy variables such as steel product tariffs moved both volumes and selling prices. Looking only at revenue, the rise in the second and third quarters of 2026 after the first-quarter low leaves room to read it as an early stage of recovery.

Still, third-quarter revenue grew only 0.5% from the prior quarter, so the pace of growth slowed sharply. On competition, DCM is pursuing a strategy of holding its position in the narrow field of appliance laminated steel through new product development and diversification of customers.

With annual revenue of KRW 227.1 billion, changes in volumes from key customers feed directly into results. The industry cycle appears to be at an early stage of demand recovery, but continuity will depend on confirming appliance demand and steel prices together.

06

Outlook

The value-up plan DCM disclosed on March 19, 2026 aims to strengthen profit-focused management to raise earnings capacity and to continue a shareholder-friendly dividend policy. Business diversification and expansion of production facilities are listed as means of execution.

Dividends are to be decided with shareholder value and the business environment in view, within a range that does not impair the company's capacity to invest in sustained growth.

At the same shareholder meeting, the financial statements for the 39th fiscal year were approved and a cash dividend on common shares was resolved. New product development and customer diversification are presented as ongoing tasks.

Revenue rose in the second and third quarters of 2026, but third-quarter operating profit and net profit attributable to controlling shareholders declined, so an improvement in profitability has not yet been confirmed. The scale and timing of the capacity expansion will be checked against later disclosures.

The first checkpoint is whether the operating margin for 2026 in the annual business report exceeds the 4.2% recorded for 2025. The same report will also show whether net profit attributable to controlling shareholders stays positive for the full year 2026.

Bull factors

Quarterly revenue recovery

Revenue reached KRW 50.2 billion in the second quarter of 2026 and KRW 50.4 billion in the third, two consecutive quarterly gains, and third-quarter revenue rose 5.0% year on year. Operating profit was in the black in every quarter from the fourth quarter of 2025 through the third quarter of 2026.

Since the operating loss of the third quarter of 2025, revenue and operating profit have moved in the same direction for a stretch, which is a positive factor.

Low leverage and a large equity base

Total liabilities at the end of 2025 were KRW 33.1 billion against total equity of KRW 261.2 billion, a debt ratio of 12.7%. All of the equity is attributable to controlling shareholders, and the balance sheet does not depend on outside borrowing.

Financial stability through a weaker industry period gives the company room to fund capacity and to wait for demand to recover.

Stated shareholder return direction

In its March 2026 value-up plan, the company said it would continue a shareholder-friendly dividend policy. At the same month's shareholder meeting, the 39th fiscal year financial statements were approved and a cash dividend on common shares was resolved.

The disclosure states that dividends are tied to business conditions, which gives shareholders a concrete policy reference.

Bear factors

Weaker annual earnings strength

Operating profit fell from KRW 24.5 billion in 2022 to KRW 9.56 billion in 2025, a 43.6% decline in 2025 alone. The operating margin was 4.2% in 2025, less than half the 10.2% recorded in 2022. Revenue fell from KRW 240.9 billion in 2024 to KRW 227.1 billion in 2025, so both scale and profitability declined.

Third-quarter net loss attributable to controlling shareholders

Third-quarter 2026 operating profit was KRW 1.67 billion, down 48.9% from the prior quarter. Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders swung from a profit in the second quarter to a net loss of KRW 2.7 billion in the third.

Over the last four quarters that profit line totaled KRW 8.24 billion, showing how much quarterly swings shape the annual earnings path.

Shrinking cash generation

Operating cash flow fell from KRW 31.5 billion in 2022 to KRW 13.9 billion in 2023, KRW 19.3 billion in 2024 and KRW 7.19 billion in 2025. Declining profit and declining operating cash flow moved in the same direction.

With cash generation shrinking, pursuing capacity expansion and diversification at the same time could strain how the company allocates its resources.

Risk factors

Steel tariffs and selling prices

The company attributed the 2025 decline to lower volumes and selling price effects from market changes such as steel product tariffs. A change in tariff policy could move both trade volumes and prices. When selling prices fall, operating margins face pressure even if volumes hold.

Concentration in appliance demand

Because appliances are the largest domestic demand source for laminated steel, the sales trend in appliances feeds directly into results. A drop in demand in that one segment has a large effect on utilization and profit across the business. Although new products help the company hold its share, its demand remains concentrated in a single field.

Macro conditions and raw material prices

In the explanation of the first nine months of 2025, global uncertainty, US-China tensions and the wars in Europe and the Middle East were cited as causing an economic slowdown and higher raw material prices.

If the slump in steel-consuming industries continues, raw material costs and weaker demand could arrive together. These factors lie largely outside the company's direct control.

Overall view

DCM's business is concentrated in laminated steel for appliances, and its 2025 operating profit fell 43.6% to KRW 9.56 billion, with net profit attributable to controlling shareholders falling to KRW 11.8 billion.

Revenue rose in the second and third quarters of 2026, but in the third quarter operating profit of KRW 1.67 billion and a net loss of KRW 2.7 billion attributable to controlling shareholders showed earnings weakening again.

On the balance sheet, the debt ratio was 12.7% at the end of 2025 and total equity was KRW 261.2 billion, so the debt burden is low.

The next checkpoints are the 2026 full-year preliminary operating margin, the full-year trend of net profit attributable to controlling shareholders in the 2026 annual report, and whether revenue and operating profit hold in the first quarter of 2027.
