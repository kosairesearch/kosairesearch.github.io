02

Business structure

TKG Aikang is a specialist in fire-protection and heating piping materials. It makes and sells PB and C-PVC piping products and supplies water supply, heating and fire-protection piping system materials to residential complexes and construction sites.

Piping materials are the core of the business, and its results are strongly tied to construction activity. Its main PB piping products are gaining adoption among tier-1 and tier-2 builders and in the public sector on quality, but cost pressure is weighing on profitability.

C-PVC piping is growing in the fire-protection piping market thanks to its light weight and ease of installation. Because customers are centered on residential complexes and construction sites, revenue moves with construction volumes.

The company acquired a technology business in early 2025 and had hoped for synergies and an earnings recovery, but 2025 results show no improvement yet. On governance, the controlling shareholder held 47.53% before launching a tender offer in early April 2026 at KRW 900 per share to acquire the remaining shares.

The company described the move as simplifying its ownership structure and improving management efficiency. The company also said it plans six months of investor-protection measures, including off-market purchases, after any delisting.

04

Earnings analysis

On a disclosed annual basis, revenue was KRW 76.1 billion in 2022, KRW 78.1 billion in 2023, KRW 58.4 billion in 2024 and KRW 64.7 billion in 2025. 2023 was the largest year of 2022 to 2025, and 2024 was the smallest.

Revenue fell 25.3% year on year in 2024 and rose 10.8% in 2025, but the revenue rebound did not translate into an earnings recovery. Operating profit was KRW 4.53 billion in 2022 and KRW 4.44 billion in 2023, down 2.0% year on year, before turning to an operating loss of KRW 3.69 billion in 2024.

The operating loss widened to KRW 10.8 billion in 2025, a continued loss. The operating margin fell from 6.0% in 2022 and 5.7% in 2023 to -6.3% in 2024 and -16.7% in 2025.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 4.58 billion in 2022 and KRW 5.46 billion in 2023, up 19.2% year on year, before a net loss of KRW 1.93 billion in 2024 and a wider net loss of KRW 11.5 billion in 2025.

The company attributed the 2025 deterioration to a construction slowdown and higher cost burdens that lowered profitability, and said certain non-recurring costs, including asset value adjustments tied to some businesses, were reflected in the net loss.

Operating cash flow was KRW 2.16 billion in 2022 and KRW 6.55 billion in 2023, then negative at -KRW 1.26 billion in 2024 and -KRW 1 billion in 2025. One analysis flagged that 2025 receivables growth ran more than 30 percentage points above revenue growth of 10.8%.

Quarterly, Q3 2025 revenue was KRW 17.6 billion with an operating loss of KRW 2.59 billion, Q4 2025 revenue was KRW 16.7 billion with a KRW 4.1 billion operating loss, Q1 2026 revenue was KRW 17 billion with a KRW 3.16 billion operating loss, Q2 2026 revenue was KRW 21.6 billion with a KRW 2.87 billion operating loss, and Q3 2026 revenue was KRW 16.1 billion with a KRW 5.59 billion operating loss.

In Q2 2026, revenue rose 27.7% from the prior quarter but the operating loss stayed at KRW 2.87 billion. In Q3 2026, revenue fell 25.7% from the prior quarter and the operating loss widened to KRW 5.59 billion. Net loss attributable to controlling shareholders over the last four quarters totaled KRW 16.5 billion.

Industry analysis

The main demand driver is construction activity. The company cited the construction slowdown and higher cost burdens as reasons for the 2025 deterioration, suggesting that lower construction volumes weakened piping sales. On costs, PB piping products face profitability pressure from cost burdens.

On the other side, PB piping adoption is rising among public-sector clients and tier-1 and tier-2 builders, which supports the product's competitive standing. In the fire-protection piping market, C-PVC piping is growing on light weight and ease of installation.

On competition, a securities information site summary pointed to intensifying competition and the company's effort to rebound with new products.

Within the KOSDAQ chemicals sector, the structural difference from large petrochemical groups such as LG Chem and Lotte Chemical is that this company is a piping specialist tied to construction volumes.

The market had expected that a construction recovery could support growth through cross-selling, but with operating losses continuing through Q3 2026, the timing of any recovery still needs to be confirmed in reported results. The indicators to watch are the pace of construction demand recovery and whether cost pressure eases.

Risk factors

Delisting and tender offer terms

The company said it will carry out six months of investor-protection measures after delisting, but forced-purchase possibilities mean the final share acquisition could require balancing interests among shareholders. Minority shareholders have objected to delisting being chosen over an earnings recovery. Criticism has also centered on the lack of shareholder returns such as dividends over a long period.

Possible administrative designation

On August 5, 2026, the company received a market notice that a share price below KRW 1,000 could lead to designation as a watch-stage issue. The company cited the introduction of a low-priced stock exit rule as a background for its delisting plan. Trading conditions could change depending on whether that designation takes effect.

Synergy from acquired business

Synergies from the technology business acquired in early 2025 may fail to show up in the piping business's revenue and profitability. The company had expected the acquisition to improve earnings, but no improvement has yet appeared in 2025 results.

If synergies are not confirmed in the 2026 annual report, the timing of returns on the investment could be delayed. Whether cross-selling with the existing piping business proceeds as planned is also a point to monitor.
