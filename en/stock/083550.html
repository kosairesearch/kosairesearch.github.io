02

Business structure

KM was founded in 1989 and began mainly with cleanroom consumables for the semiconductor industry. Its filings list operating activities spanning masks, imports and exports, logistics, protective equipment, plastics and chemicals, so the business extends well beyond cleanroom goods.

As of the 2026 third-quarter filing, the company had six unlisted consolidated subsidiaries, two of which are major subsidiaries. Older press coverage introduced the company mainly through the manufacture and sale of protective suits and protective masks.

Its business areas are also described as cleanroom, mask and BLU-related operations. Revenue split by business segment is disclosed in the business-segment note of the annual report.

Demand for cleanroom consumables comes from manufacturers running clean processes such as semiconductor fabs, and their equipment utilization and new line investment drive order volume.

The mask business was introduced as a fine-dust mask line started in 2017, and its demand drivers differ from those of cleanroom goods. In 2025 the company restructured its production base through a factory sale, according to the company's explanation.

Although KM sits in the textiles, fashion and living sector, its business mix differs from listed peers in that sector, so direct comparison metrics are limited.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual revenue fell from KRW 151.6 billion in 2022 to KRW 127.8 billion in 2023, a 15.7% decline year on year, then rose 2.6% to KRW 131.1 billion in 2024 and 3.3% to KRW 135.4 billion in 2025, marking two consecutive years of growth after the 2023 low. 2022 was the largest year and 2023 the smallest among 2022 to 2025.

Operating profit of KRW 6.92 billion in 2022 turned into an operating loss of KRW 7.74 billion in 2023, and the company recorded operating losses for three consecutive years through 2025. The loss narrowed to KRW 3.43 billion in 2024 and KRW 393 million in 2025.

The operating margin moved from 4.6% in 2022 to -6.1% in 2023, -2.6% in 2024 and -0.3% in 2025. Even in 2024 and 2025, when revenue grew, the margin stayed negative, although the gap narrowed.

Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders went from KRW 7.63 billion in 2022 to a net loss of KRW 3.65 billion in 2023, a net loss of KRW 2.3 billion in 2024, and a profit of KRW 4.19 billion in 2025.

Net profit including non-controlling interests was KRW 4.2 billion in 2025, a different figure from the attributable amount. The company said that improved production efficiency after a factory sale and lower fixed-cost burden reduced the operating loss and turned net income positive.

Operating cash flow fell from KRW 12.2 billion in 2022 to KRW 3.69 billion in 2023, KRW 5.25 billion in 2024 and KRW 3.81 billion in 2025.

Quarterly revenue ran at KRW 34.5 billion in the third quarter of 2025, KRW 36.2 billion in the fourth quarter, KRW 33.8 billion in the first quarter of 2026, KRW 39.6 billion in the second quarter and KRW 37.3 billion in the third quarter.

Operating profit was KRW 1.67 billion in the third quarter of 2025, an operating loss of KRW 615 million in the fourth quarter, an operating loss of KRW 928 million in the first quarter of 2026, operating profit of KRW 928 million in the second quarter and KRW 1.93 billion in the third quarter of 2026.

Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 1.81 billion in the third quarter of 2025, KRW 2.29 billion in the fourth quarter, a loss of KRW 223 million in the first quarter of 2026, KRW 765 million in the second quarter and KRW 342 million in the third quarter.

The four-quarter total was KRW 3.17 billion. The reason operating profit recovered while attributable net profit fell should be checked in the income statement items of the quarterly reports.

05

Industry analysis

KM's core demand comes from manufacturers running clean processes such as semiconductor fabs, and demand for cleanroom consumables moves with equipment utilization and new line investment. Recoveries or slowdowns in the semiconductor cycle tend to show up in consumable orders with a lag.

Judgments about where the cycle stands are more accurate when read alongside equipment investment and utilization data. The mask business responds to different external conditions, so its earnings drivers differ from those of cleanroom goods.

Protective equipment, chemicals and plastics appear in the filed business list, but their individual revenue levels should be checked in the annual report. The consumables market involves price competition with domestic and overseas suppliers, which can weigh on margins.

Price swings in plastic and chemical raw materials feed directly into cost structure. The 2025 factory sale that reshaped the production base is a change factor for cost competitiveness.

Listed companies in the same sector have diverse business mixes such as apparel, fabrics, furniture and household goods, so a sector average is a limited benchmark for KM's position.

The most direct variable for KM's market position is therefore the trend in semiconductor equipment investment and cleanroom consumable orders.

Demand for cleanroom consumables depends on equipment investment and utilization at clean-process manufacturers such as semiconductor fabs. A slowdown would reduce orders, and with a sizable fixed-cost base, lower orders could quickly worsen operating results. Customer mix and segment revenue shares need to be checked in the annual report.

Governance and ownership change risk

The largest shareholder changed to Shin Ji-hoon and eight others in July 2026 through a gift between related parties. A change in controlling ownership can affect management direction and decision-making, so later director appointments and disclosures need monitoring. The effect of intra-family share transfers on trading supply and free float is also a point to examine.

Quarterly earnings volatility and cost structure risk

Raw material prices and currency movements affect costs, so quarterly profit volatility may continue for some time.
