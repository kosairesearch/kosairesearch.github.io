02

Business structure

EehwaConstruction is a general construction company founded in 1956 and listed on KOSDAQ in November 1994. A 2025 press report described the business structure as 100% contract-based construction work.

Its core areas are pharmaceutical and bio plants and cleanroom (GMP) facilities, where the industry has credited it with bidding competitiveness.

In 2022 it built a display innovation process center, and in May 2024 it signed a contract for the new headquarters building of Rainbow Robotics in Sejong, a company backed by Samsung Electronics.

In February 2025 it won a KRW 22.9 billion contract for the new building of Yonsei-affiliated Yeonsung University in Anyang, Gyeonggi, and in the same year it added construction of building D for Sijimedtech and a pedestrian bridge project in Paju Unjeong.

In new industries it is diversifying bids into cleanroom facilities for secondary batteries, displays and robotics. Sources differ on the revenue mix.

A corporate information site, as of March 2026, listed government contracts at 70% and 24% and private work at 6%, while a February 2026 report said the private share is higher than the public share.

By construction capability rating, the company ranked 134th based on 2024, far smaller in scale than large listed builders such as Samsung C&T and Hyundai Engineering & Construction. A 2026 report said the company shifted from expanding sales volume toward a focus on projects that generate stable profits. As of March 31, 2026, it had 90 employees.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual revenue fell from KRW 194.6 billion in 2022 to KRW 151.2 billion in 2023 (down 22.3% year on year), KRW 128.3 billion in 2024 (down 15.1%), and KRW 115.2 billion in 2025 (down 10.2%). 2022 was the largest year and 2025 the smallest within 2022 to 2025.

The operating margin moved from 0.1% in 2022 to -0.7% in 2023 and -15.4% in 2024, then returned to 2.9% in 2025. In 2024, operating loss reached KRW 19.8 billion and net loss attributable to controlling shareholders reached KRW 23.1 billion.

A 2025 press report said the company described the 2024 losses as a big bath, with costs recognized all at once.

In 2025, operating profit of KRW 3.29 billion and profit attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 2.13 billion marked a swing to profit, while net income including non-controlling interests was also KRW 2.13 billion.

The company attributed the turnaround to the end of low-margin legacy projects and to higher profit contribution from new sites won through selective, profitability-focused bidding.

Operating cash flow was -KRW 14.5 billion in 2022, -KRW 12.9 billion in 2023, -KRW 13 billion in 2024 and -KRW 8.76 billion in 2025, an outflow in all four years, with the smallest outflow in 2025. The debt ratio fell from 774.7% in 2024 to 464.2% in 2025, after 101.8% in 2022 and 126.1% in 2023.

On a quarterly basis, Q4 2025 was the peak of the disclosed period, with revenue of KRW 31.3 billion (up 6.2% from the prior quarter) and operating profit of KRW 1.3 billion (up 528.6%).

Revenue in Q1 2026 fell to KRW 13.2 billion (down 57.7% from the prior quarter), with an operating loss of KRW 858 million that marked a swing back to losses. In Q2 2026 revenue was KRW 10.4 billion with an operating loss of KRW 1.13 billion.

In Q3 2026 revenue dropped to KRW 859 million (down 91.7% from the prior quarter and 97.1% year on year), while the operating loss widened to KRW 1.49 billion. The controlling-shareholder net loss in Q3 2026 was KRW 1.49 billion, a larger loss than in the year-earlier period.

The net loss attributable to controlling shareholders over the last four quarters totals KRW 2.01 billion, a figure that includes the KRW 893 million profit in Q4 2025.

05

Industry analysis

In 2025, press reports described a construction downturn that led to a string of insolvency filings by mid-sized and smaller builders.

Between early 2025 and April, Shinh Dong-A Construction, Samboo Construction, Daejeo Construction, Ahngang Construction, Daewoo Shipbuilding & Marine Engineering's construction arm and Byuksan Engineering filed for court receivership, and EehwaConstruction followed in April 2025.

Industry analysts attribute the strain to sharply higher construction costs that pushed up cost ratios, leaving loss-making sites and some projects where payment was delayed. One industry official said that execution rates above 100% had accumulated losses.

Demand for the company's core business depends on pharmaceutical and bio plant investment and cleanroom facilities. In new industries, it is extending into cleanroom work for secondary batteries, displays and robotics.

A February 2026 report noted that because construction is an order-driven business, a recovery in credit standing could quickly translate into better earnings, while delays in capital raising could revive concerns about financial soundness.

Among industry peers, EehwaConstruction is a mid-sized builder ranked 134th by construction capability, so its contract scale and cost-negotiating leverage are smaller than those of large listed builders.

The company has said it will verify profitability in advance through a bid review committee and expand joint contracting with large and mid-sized builders to win more civil engineering work.

At the extraordinary shareholders meeting on March 25, 2026, the appointment of Han Ju-hee as an executive director was approved, and in the same month she was reported as the new CEO. The regular shareholders meeting on March 31, 2026 approved the financial statements for the 70th fiscal year.

A 2026 capital increase issued about 11.99 million new shares, and an analysis by a research site put the April 2026 new shares at 73.72% of the existing share count. The company has presented profitability-focused bidding and wider joint contracting as its direction.

In 2025, the company decided to sell real estate worth about KRW 7.5 billion, including part of its headquarters site, and reported receiving KRW 5 billion including interim payments. Borrowings fell from KRW 23.1 billion in Q3 2024 to KRW 5.9 billion in the first half of 2025.

The improvement period ends on April 13, 2027, and a report expects the exchange to review the FY2026 audit report and financial results in its first-half 2027 screening. The legal deadline for the FY2026 annual business report is March 31, 2027.

The company said the end of low-margin legacy projects and profit-focused selective bidding raised the contribution from new sites. A 2026 report said relatively higher-margin private projects and small-scale projects were added to bid review.

Improvement in balance sheet indicators

The debt ratio fell from 774.7% in 2024 to 464.2% in 2025. An October 2025 report said the audit opinion on the half-year report review moved from qualified to unqualified.

New largest shareholder and capital injection

The private equity firm agreed to subscribe for new shares worth KRW 6 billion immediately after the change of largest shareholder. The move is read as an effort to restore the capital base and normalize the balance sheet.

Bear factors

Revenue fell to KRW 13.2 billion in Q1 2026, KRW 10.4 billion in Q2 and KRW 859 million in Q3. Operating losses over the same quarters were KRW 858 million, KRW 1.13 billion and KRW 1.49 billion. The controlling-shareholder net loss over the last four quarters totals KRW 2.01 billion.

Thin equity and high debt ratio

History of audit opinion and governance changes

The 2024 financial statements received a disclaimer of opinion over doubts about going-concern ability. The company filed for court receivership in April 2025 and withdrew the application about three weeks later.

A free capital reduction through share consolidation took place in January 2026, and the largest-shareholder change followed.

Risk factors

Listing status and audit opinion

The improvement period tied to the disclaimer of opinion on the 2024 statements ends on April 13, 2027. A report says the exchange is likely to review the FY2026 audit report and financial results in its first-half 2027 screening. If the audit opinion does not stay unqualified, it would directly affect the decision on resuming trading.

Construction cost and receivables collection

A 2025 report said unbilled construction work and construction receivables totaled KRW 26.4 billion, about 27% of construction revenue. Industry analysts attribute the accumulated losses to sharply higher construction costs. Lawsuits over construction fees were pending, leaving the possibility of delayed collection.

Theme-driven trading and share price volatility

The stock was grouped as a theme stock tied to the 2007 presidential race, and an April 2025 report said it surged as a theme stock linked to a political figure. Theme-driven trading can amplify price swings independent of earnings. Volatility of this kind could persist after trading resumes.

Overall view

EehwaConstruction swung to annual operating profit and profit attributable to controlling shareholders in 2025, moving away from the losses of 2023 and 2024.

Revenue declined for three consecutive years, from KRW 194.6 billion in 2022 to KRW 115.2 billion in 2025, and the company attributed the turnaround to the end of low-margin projects and selective bidding.

Operating losses, however, have continued for three straight quarters from Q1 2026, and Q3 2026 revenue fell to KRW 859 million. At the end of 2025 the debt ratio stood at 464.2% and total equity at KRW 8.94 billion, which leaves a thin capital base.

Ownership and management changed substantially through the February 2026 private equity acquisition, the 2026 capital increase and the change of representative director.

The strengths are the annual profit swing and improved balance sheet indicators, while the weaknesses are continued quarterly losses and the fragile capital base. The next checkpoints on the listing side are the April 13, 2027 end of the improvement period and the audit opinion for fiscal 2026.
