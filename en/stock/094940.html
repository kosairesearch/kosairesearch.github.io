Report overview

Cobot pivot: whether quarterly profits can hold

02

Business structure

PULOON Robotics, founded in July 1997, has built its business mainly on unmanned automation equipment such as financial automation and transit automation systems.

At an extraordinary shareholder meeting in April 2026 it changed its corporate name from PULOON Technology to PULOON Robotics and said it would grow cobots and physical AI-based robot solutions into its future growth pillar.

The product sales mix shown on a third-party company profile page is 81.24% financial automation, 13.97% transit automation and 4.79% robots and specialty terminals, and the page does not state a base date.

The cobot line started with the six-axis SYMPHONY15 (15kg payload), jointly developed with a domestic automotive-parts maker in 2022, then added SYMPHONY5 and SYMPHONY10 in 2024, and the company is now preparing mass production of the higher-payload SYMPHONY20 and SYMPHONY40.

At the 2026 ROBOWORLD show held from November 4 to 7, 2026, it unveiled the 40kg-class SYMPHONY-40S and the 20kg-class SYMPHONY-25L, and the SYMPHONY-40S received a Distinction award.

Its palletizing systems target logistics and manufacturing sites that need repetitive box stacking, and it is developing unstructured palletizing linked to vision cameras.

On the transit side, a supply contract for the Sinansan Line metro automation equipment, disclosed under the former company name on May 19, 2026, is the representative case, with a contract value of KRW 3.4 billion, 14.48% of recent revenue as disclosed, and a term running from May 19, 2026 to December 31, 2028.

Consolidated subsidiaries include Pureun S&M, a sheet-metal fabrication unit, PULOON Robotics System, a robot automation systems developer, and DI Robotics, an artificial intelligence systems developer, and DI Robotics is commercializing a dental implant surgery assist robot.

The company argues it offers performance equal to foreign brands at a lower price and responds quickly to problems. Listed companies in the same sector that deal in robots include Doosan Robotics, Rainbow Robotics, Robotis and Yuilrobotics.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual revenue fell from KRW 34.1 billion in 2022 to KRW 32.3 billion in 2023 (down 5.3% year on year), recovered to KRW 34.8 billion in 2024 (up 7.7%), and dropped to KRW 23.5 billion in 2025 (down 32.5%). Within 2022 to 2025, 2024 was the largest year and 2025 the smallest.

Operating profit rose from KRW 1.02 billion in 2022 to KRW 1.85 billion in 2023 (up 80.9%) and KRW 2.28 billion in 2024 (up 23.3%), but in 2025 the company posted an operating loss of KRW 1.01 billion, a swing into deficit from the prior year.

The operating margin moved from 3.0% in 2022, 5.7% in 2023 and 6.6% in 2024 to -4.3% in 2025. Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders rose to KRW 2.59 billion in 2023 (up 1,248.1%), eased to KRW 2.06 billion in 2024 (down 20.6%), and in 2025 turned into a net loss of KRW 601 million, a swing into deficit.

Net profit including non-controlling interests, a different figure, was KRW 2.01 billion in 2024 and a net loss of KRW 654 million in 2025.

Quarterly revenue climbed from KRW 5.13 billion in the third quarter of 2025 to KRW 8.51 billion in the fourth quarter (up 65.8% quarter on quarter), dropped to a low of KRW 4.3 billion in the first quarter of 2026 (down 49.5%), then rose to KRW 6.01 billion in the second quarter (up 39.9%) and KRW 6.25 billion in the third quarter (up 3.9% quarter on quarter and 21.7% year on year), marking two consecutive quarters of growth from the second quarter through the third quarter of 2026.

Quarterly operating results were an operating loss of KRW 511 million in the third quarter of 2025, an operating profit of KRW 391 million in the fourth quarter (a swing to profit), an operating loss of KRW 532 million in the first quarter of 2026 (a swing back to loss), an operating loss of KRW 60.33 million in the second quarter (loss narrowed), and an operating profit of KRW 22.06 million in the third quarter of 2026 (a swing to profit and a swing to profit year on year).

Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders went from a loss of KRW 838 million in the third quarter of 2025 and a loss of KRW 71.92 million in the fourth quarter to a loss of KRW 840 million in the first quarter of 2026 (loss widened), a loss of KRW 141 million in the second quarter (loss narrowed), and a profit of KRW 556 million in the third quarter of 2026 (a swing to profit).

Cumulative net loss attributable to controlling shareholders over the last four quarters, from the fourth quarter of 2025 to the third quarter of 2026, was KRW 496 million. Per a third-party company profile, R&D spending in 2025 was KRW 2.514 billion, or 10.71% of revenue, versus KRW 3.543 billion, or 10.19%, in 2024.

At the end of 2025, total equity was KRW 37 billion, of which KRW 36.9 billion belonged to controlling shareholders, total liabilities were KRW 8.23 billion, and the debt ratio was 22.3% (24.0% in 2024).

Operating cash flow was KRW 3.68 billion in 2022, KRW 1.95 billion in 2023, KRW 339 million in 2024 and KRW 1.37 billion in 2025.

05

Industry analysis

Demand in the cobot market is moving toward heavier payloads. At the 2026 ROBOWORLD show, an expert commentator said the cobot industry is seeing demand for 50 to 70kg-class products.

The same discussion flagged a technical constraint: beyond 40kg, the 48V DC motors commonly used in cobots cannot generate enough force, so the design must switch to an AC power approach used in industrial robots, which increases body weight and can weaken the close-working advantage of cobots.

PULOON Robotics says the SYMPHONY-40S shows cobots can be applied to heavy transfer and stacking work that had belonged to conventional industrial robots. Its palletizing solutions target logistics and manufacturing sites at the same time.

In medical robotics, a dental implant surgery assist robot is moving toward commercialization through a subsidiary. In the physical AI area, the company joined a Ministry of Science and ICT program in April 2026 as a joint research institution, covering hardware platform development through 2029.

It also joined the K-Humanoid Alliance as a robot maker. Listed peers in the same sector that deal in cobots and robot manufacturing include Doosan Robotics, Rainbow Robotics, Robotis and Yuilrobotics, and the company's position can be gauged by comparing its payload lineup and application processes with theirs.

06

Outlook

The company has outlined three mid- to long-term directions: first, securing a competitive cobot lineup and becoming a leader in system applications such as palletizing and welding; second, upgrading hardware-based technology so physical AI companies can integrate cobots easily and establishing itself as a total robot supplier; and third, continuing its supply business of life-automation systems based on mechatronics and vision recognition.

On products, mass production of the SYMPHONY20 and SYMPHONY40 is being prepared, and the SYMPHONY-25L and SYMPHONY-40S were unveiled at the 2026 ROBOWORLD show.

On orders, the Sinansan Line metro automation supply contract disclosed in May 2026 runs through December 31, 2028, and revenue recognition is expected over the contract period.

On partnerships, the company signed an MOU with machine tool maker Smac at SIMTOS 2026 in April 2026 and plans joint work on intelligent automation combining machine tools and robots, AI and 5G smart factories, and mobile manipulator solutions.

On government projects, development of on-device AI robots and modules is planned through 2028, and physical AI demonstration work through 2029. In medical robotics, follow-on commercialization of the dental surgery assist robot is in progress.

Ahead of its 30th founding anniversary in 2027, the company has said it will reorganize around robotics as its core business. The next checkpoint is whether quarterly revenue in the KRW 6 billion range in 2026 converts into mass-production sales of high-payload models and system orders.

Bull factors

Competitiveness of high-payload cobots

The SYMPHONY-40S received a Distinction award at the 2026 ROBOWORLD show and demonstrated that a 40kg-class cobot can handle heavy transfer and stacking work that had belonged to conventional industrial robots.

With the SYMPHONY20 and SYMPHONY40 in preparation for mass production, a broader lineup could widen the range of system sales. The palletizing systems are extending their scope through unstructured stacking linked to vision cameras.

Quarterly turnaround in 2026

Revenue grew for two consecutive quarters from the second quarter through the third quarter of 2026. Operating profit of KRW 22.06 million and net profit attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 556 million in the third quarter both turned positive.

The operating loss had already narrowed to KRW 60.33 million in the second quarter, and third-quarter revenue of KRW 6.25 billion was up 21.7% year on year.

Government projects and new business expansion

The company is taking part in an on-device AI semiconductor program from the Ministry of Trade, Industry and Energy and a physical AI core technology program from the Ministry of Science and ICT, with platform development running through 2028 and 2029.

It joined the K-Humanoid Alliance as a robot maker, and its dental implant surgery assist robot is being commercialized through a subsidiary. On the balance sheet, the debt ratio was 22.3% at the end of 2025, which points to a relatively light debt burden.

Revenue in 2025 fell 32.5% to KRW 23.5 billion, and the operating margin dropped to -4.3%. Short record of profitability

Cumulative net loss attributable to controlling shareholders over the last four quarters was KRW 496 million.

Revenue dropped 49.5% quarter on quarter to KRW 4.3 billion in the first quarter of 2026, so quarterly swings remain large.

Cash generation and R&D burden

Operating cash flow fell from KRW 3.68 billion in 2022 to KRW 339 million in 2024 and was KRW 1.37 billion in 2025. Per a third-party company profile, R&D spending was 10.71% of revenue in 2025, up from 10.19% in 2024.

Financial automation accounted for 81.24% of the product sales mix on that page, so the cobot share of revenue is still small.

Risk factors

Execution risk in the business shift

Above 40kg, high-payload cobots face technical constraints such as the need to switch to AC power and added body weight. If mass production timing or yield for the SYMPHONY20 and SYMPHONY40 differs from plan, revenue recognition could be delayed. Competitors exist both at home and abroad, so the company needs to keep proving its price and performance edge.

Earnings volatility and cash generation

Quarterly revenue swung from KRW 8.51 billion in the fourth quarter of 2025 to KRW 4.3 billion in the first quarter of 2026. Operating cash flow had fallen to KRW 339 million in 2024, so whether earnings improvements translate into cash generation remains to be confirmed.

Revenue from large contracts such as transit automation may be recognized at different points quarter to quarter.

New-business spending and reliance on government projects

New businesses such as physical AI, humanoids and medical robots need continued spending on R&D and demonstration. Government projects run through 2028 and 2029, and whether commercial results continue after those projects end is not yet visible in disclosures.

If commercialization of the medical robot through a subsidiary is delayed, its cost burden could limit the recovery in earnings.

Overall view

PULOON Robotics is in the process of shifting its business from financial and transit automation toward a robotics company centered on cobots and physical AI.

Based on disclosed results, operating profit rose through 2024, then the company posted an operating loss of KRW 1.01 billion in 2025 and revenue fell to KRW 23.5 billion.

Quarterly results in 2026 show revenue growing for two consecutive quarters after the first-quarter low, and both operating profit and net profit attributable to controlling shareholders turned positive in the third quarter.

However, cumulative net loss attributable to controlling shareholders over the last four quarters was KRW 496 million, and the only profitable quarters were the fourth quarter of 2025 and the third quarter of 2026, so the track record of profitability is still short.

On the bull side, the award for the high-payload cobot, the Sinansan Line supply contract, participation in government projects and a debt ratio of 22.3% stand out.

On the bear side, revenue fell 32.5% in 2025, R&D spending equals 10.71% of revenue per a third-party profile, operating cash flow has declined, and the timing of revenue from products still in mass-production preparation is uncertain.

On valuation, the starting point is that no earnings-based multiple can be calculated and the price-to-book ratio sits below 1x. The next checkpoints are the operating margin in the 2026 annual report, due by the end of March 2027, and whether revenue recovers in the first-quarter 2027 report.
