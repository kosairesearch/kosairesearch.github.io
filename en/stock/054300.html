Panstar Enterprise Shifts Its Weight Toward Shipping and Shipbuilding

Panstar Enterprise, which began as an automotive maintenance equipment maker, is restructuring around container shipping and shipbuilding, and its quarterly results have swung between profit and loss in 2026 after a full-year loss in 2025.

  1. 1

    Logistics and cruise operations made up 46% of the KRW 33.3 billion in first-half 2026 revenue, ahead of automotive maintenance equipment at 30% and shipbuilding and marine engineering at 23%.

  2. 2

    Full-year 2025 operating loss was KRW 2.79 billion and the net loss attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 1.57 billion, a swing into the red. The 3Q 2026 operating loss was KRW 319 million.

  3. 3

    A 5-for-1 share consolidation is on the agenda for a shareholder meeting in December 2026, with the post-consolidation shares scheduled to list on January 18, 2027.

  4. 4

    Largest shareholder Kim Hyun-gyeom, chairman of the Panstar Group, disclosed plans to buy 590,235 common shares on the open market from November 30 to December 29, 2026.

  5. 5

    As of November 11, 2026, the closing price had stayed below KRW 1,000 for 25 consecutive trading days, prompting an exchange notice that designation as a managed stock is possible, and the stock was designated as an investment caution stock for one day on November 13.

02

Business structure

Panstar Enterprise started as a KOSDAQ-listed maker of automotive maintenance equipment in 2002. After joining the Panstar Group, it added stevedoring and cruise operations, and in May 2025 it absorbed a ship engineering affiliate to build out its Shipbuilding and Marine Engineering Division.

In first-half 2026, revenue of KRW 33.3 billion split into logistics and cruise at 46%, automotive maintenance equipment at 30% and shipbuilding and marine engineering at 23%, according to Newsis reporting.

The equipment business builds maintenance tools based on pneumatic and hydraulic technology, and its growth has been linked to rising vehicle registrations in Korea and demand centered on high-tech equipment.

An FnGuide summary put the company's share of Busan port ship-related business at about 63% for the first nine months of 2025, while costs weighed on operating profit over the same period.

The shipbuilding division handles ship design, conversion and maintenance, with Japan and the FSRU and FSU markets as its growth axes.

In 2026 it won eight design contracts from Japanese shipyards and shipping companies, and 47% of the remaining order backlog reported for the fourth quarter, USD 5.347 million in total, relates to FSRU and FSU projects.

The logistics business is tied to the Group's shipping operations, making that link central to the company's structure. In automotive equipment, the company operates in a niche that differs from large auto parts makers, where technology and customer base matter most. In ship design, it competes with Korean shipyards and design houses as well as Japanese shipbuilding networks.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual revenue was KRW 67 billion in 2022, KRW 69.1 billion in 2023, KRW 62.9 billion in 2024 and KRW 67.2 billion in 2025, with 2023 the largest year and 2024 the smallest across 2022 to 2025. Revenue fell 9.0% in 2024 and rose 6.8% in 2025.

Operating profit went from KRW 1.95 billion in 2022 to KRW 1.63 billion in 2023 (down 16.5%) and KRW 285 million in 2024 (down 82.5%), before turning into an operating loss of KRW 2.79 billion in 2025. The operating margin slipped from 2.9% in 2022 to 2.4% in 2023, 0.5% in 2024 and -4.2% in 2025.

The company attributed the 2025 result to delayed IMO environmental rules, which dampened shipowners' appetite for eco-friendly vessel investment and slowed sales growth, while higher raw material and labor costs pushed up expenses (per a February 2026 report on the disclosure).

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 537 million in 2022, KRW 275 million in 2023 (down 48.7%) and KRW 3.64 billion in 2024 (up 1,223.2%), a figure far above that year's KRW 285 million operating profit, so non-operating items merit a closer look.

The 2025 net loss attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 1.57 billion, and the net loss including non-controlling interests was the same amount.

Operating cash flow moved from KRW 1.37 billion in 2022 and KRW 4.29 billion in 2023 to -KRW 3.12 billion in 2024 and -KRW 7.6 billion in 2025, while the debt ratio fell from 92.8% in 2022 to 68.6% in 2025.

Quarterly, an operating loss of KRW 1.8 billion in 3Q 2025 and KRW 1.21 billion in 4Q 2025 gave way to operating profit of KRW 233 million in 1Q 2026, even though revenue fell 19.1% quarter on quarter to KRW 13.5 billion.

Revenue rose 47.7% to KRW 19.9 billion in 2Q 2026 and operating profit reached KRW 307 million, up 31.6%, before 3Q 2026 revenue of KRW 16.3 billion (down 18.2% quarter on quarter and 2.6% year on year) produced an operating loss of KRW 319 million.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 830 million in 1Q 2026 and KRW 1.25 billion in 2Q 2026, then turned into a net loss of KRW 1.35 billion in 3Q 2026, and the controlling-shareholder net loss over the last four quarters (4Q 2025 to 3Q 2026) totals KRW 1.32 billion.

05

Industry analysis

The automotive maintenance equipment market has been supported by a rising number of registered vehicles in Korea and growing demand for high-tech equipment, according to the company profile provider Wisereport.

In this segment, the key variables are vehicle registrations and equipment replacement demand in repair shops rather than automaker production volumes. In shipping, there is a view that tighter IMO greenhouse gas rules are lifting demand for eco-friendly ship design and conversion.

The company, however, said in its 2025 results explanation that delayed IMO environmental rules had dampened shipowners' investment appetite for eco-friendly vessels. The two views differ on regulatory timing, which will largely determine how quickly conversion orders arrive.

FSRU and FSU vessels are tied to LNG infrastructure demand, so design orders from Japan are the basis for growth in this division. Container shipping began when the Panstar Group joined a Korea-Japan container joint operation in July 2026, and Southeast Asian routes are the next step.

Compared with large auto parts makers, the company is far smaller in revenue and business scope, and its mix is shifting from automotive equipment, now at 30%, toward logistics and shipbuilding. Direct dealings with Japanese shipyards and shipping companies in ship design are the differentiator the company emphasizes.

06

Outlook

The company described the 5-for-1 share consolidation as the starting point of a medium- to long-term effort to establish its identity as a shipping and shipbuilding company.

The board approved the consolidation on October 29, 2026, and the shareholder meeting date in the disclosure is December 14, 2026, although some media reported a provisional shareholder meeting on December 4.

Trading will be suspended from December 28, 2026 to January 15, 2027, the effective date is December 30, 2026, and the new shares are scheduled to list on January 18, 2027. Issued shares will fall from 59,023,445 to 11,804,689 and the par value will change from KRW 1,000 to KRW 5,000.

In containers, the first target is the Vietnam route before the end of 2026, using chartered vessels while the company prepares its registration as an international regular cargo transport operator.

A memorandum of understanding has been signed with a Chinese counterpart on a cargo joint venture, and the Group plans at least two to three voyages in 2027. Selection as a pilot operator on the Arctic route, with completion of the first domestic container round trip on that route, is cited as the link toward Europe.

In shipbuilding, a joint venture agreement with a Japanese general trading company supports a planned local branch in 2027.

Consolidating overlapping functions among affiliates and clarifying cross-shareholdings are also under review, with the concrete plan to be set after board discussion and consultation with authorities.

On the capital side, conversions of the 24th series convertible bonds issued 852,500 shares in September 2026, and the unconverted balance is KRW 6.845 billion.

Bull factors

Expansion into the shipping value chain

The Panstar Group joined a Korea-Japan container joint operation in July 2026 and is pursuing Southeast Asian routes, starting with Vietnam before the end of 2026.

If the listed company runs routes with chartered vessels and completes its registration as an international regular cargo operator, its logistics business would link directly to the Group's core shipping operations.

A company representative described this as the foundation for being recognized as a shipping and shipbuilding company.

Shipbuilding order base

The company won eight design contracts from Japanese shipyards and shipping companies in 2026, and 47% of the remaining backlog reported for the fourth quarter relates to FSRU and FSU projects.

A joint venture agreement with a Japanese general trading company supports a planned local branch in 2027, building a base for wider overseas design work. The shipbuilding division accounted for 23% of first-half 2026 revenue.

Quarterly operating profit appears

Operating profit turned positive at KRW 233 million in 1Q 2026, and 2Q 2026 operating profit of KRW 307 million was up 31.6% quarter on quarter. The debt ratio fell from 92.8% in 2022 to 68.6% in 2025, a direction that eases financial burden.

Whether the operating margin stays in positive territory is the point at which the recovery in profit can be judged.

Dilution and execution uncertainty

Risk factors

Listing rule risk

A closing price below KRW 1,000 for 25 consecutive trading days creates a possibility of managed stock designation, which overlaps with the investment caution designation. Designation as a managed stock changes trading conditions and disclosure obligations, so the exchange notices need to be tracked.

Once the consolidation is completed, the number of shares and the par value change, which alters the basis on which closing prices are measured.

Execution risk in the business shift

The container business needs chartered vessel capacity, international registration and the start of Southeast Asian service to line up together. Whether the Vietnam route opens by the end of 2026 and whether two to three voyages are run in 2027 as planned is the central question.

The shipbuilding division relies on Japanese orders, so a shift in order timing would also move the timing of revenue recognition.

Capital and governance risk

Conversions of convertible bonds have increased issued shares, while the 5-for-1 consolidation and the cross-shareholding clean-up are proceeding together. The trading suspension schedule for the April 2026 share consolidation was revised once after consultation with authorities. Until the restructuring details are fixed, the terms affecting minority shareholders remain to be confirmed.

Overall view

Panstar Enterprise is moving its business weight from automotive maintenance equipment toward shipping and shipbuilding.

In first-half 2026, logistics and cruise became the largest segment at 46% of KRW 33.3 billion in revenue, followed by automotive maintenance equipment at 30% and shipbuilding and marine engineering at 23%.

Profitability has weakened, with operating profit of KRW 1.95 billion in 2022 turning into an operating loss of KRW 2.79 billion in 2025. In 2026, operating profit was positive in the first and second quarters, and the third quarter returned to an operating loss of KRW 319 million, so quarterly swings are large.

The controlling-shareholder net loss of KRW 1.32 billion over the last four quarters and operating cash flow of -KRW 7.6 billion in 2025 are the weak points.

On the other side, the debt ratio fell to 68.6% in 2025 from 92.8% in 2022, and eight Japanese design contracts and container route preparation are cited as evidence of the restructuring in progress.

The possible managed stock designation, the largest shareholder's purchase plan and the share consolidation schedule are arriving at the same time, so trading conditions are changing sharply.

The next checkpoints are the managed stock decision, the outcome of the shareholder meeting, the start of Vietnam route operations and the full-year operating margin in the 2026 annual report.
