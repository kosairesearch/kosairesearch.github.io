Report overview

Sales up three straight quarters; aviation drag keeps losses

Winix's sales have risen for three straight quarters through the third quarter of 2026, yet operating losses have continued for five consecutive quarters, so profit recovery depends on its newly added airline unit and its export-led electronics business.

02

Business structure

Winix is a home appliance maker founded in 1986 and listed on KOSDAQ in 2000. Its core products are air purifiers, dehumidifiers, mini dryers, humidifiers and water purifiers; in Korea the brand is best known for dehumidifiers, while overseas air purifiers have become the main export driver.

The company says more than half of its consolidated sales come from exports. Overseas sales run through sales subsidiaries in the United States, Canada and Europe and have expanded to Japan, Australia, Taiwan, Indonesia and India.

Its main sales channels are large North American retailers such as Costco, Amazon, Home Depot and Walmart. In Australia, cumulative sales since its 2016 entry passed USD 30 million, with an average annual growth rate of about 37%, the company said.

In Canada, citing market research firm Circana data, the company says it has held the No. 1 position since 2024. Manufacturing takes place at subsidiaries in China, Thailand and Gwangju. The business has also expanded into airline operations, where the first aircraft began flying in October 2025.

The airline business runs through Paratha Air, a subsidiary located in Yangyang; the subsidiary's revenue of KRW 34.4 billion was first included in consolidated results in the first quarter of 2026. Domestic competitors in air purifiers include Coway.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual sales rose from KRW 329 billion in 2022 to KRW 375.3 billion in 2023 (up 14.1% year on year), the largest year in the 2022-2025 window, then fell to KRW 351.2 billion in 2024 (down 6.4%) before recovering to KRW 369.5 billion in 2025 (up 5.2%).

Operating profit was KRW 6.52 billion in 2023 (up 73.8%), the last profitable year; the company swung to an operating loss of KRW 5.88 billion in 2024 and the loss widened to KRW 60.7 billion in 2025. The operating margin moved from 1.7% in 2023 to -1.7% in 2024 and -16.4% in 2025.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 10 billion in 2023, turned into a KRW 44.4 billion loss in 2024, and the loss narrowed to KRW 18.3 billion in 2025. Net income including non-controlling interests was reported as the same loss amounts in both years.

Operating cash flow went from KRW 909 million in 2024 to -KRW 51.9 billion in 2025. The company attributed 2025 sales growth to higher U.S. subsidiary air purifier sales and export growth in Japanese hot and cold water dispensers and Australian air purifiers.

In the first quarter of 2025, sales at the U.S. subsidiary temporarily rose by more than KRW 10 billion due to the Los Angeles wildfires.

Quarterly sales fell to KRW 79 billion in the fourth quarter of 2025 (down 14.0% from the prior quarter), then rose to KRW 123.2 billion in the first quarter of 2026, KRW 137.9 billion in the second and KRW 160.6 billion in the third, three consecutive quarterly gains.

Operating losses over the same stretch were KRW 38.9 billion, KRW 21.7 billion, KRW 39.5 billion and KRW 25.7 billion.

According to company statements, the electronics segment's first-quarter 2026 sales were KRW 88.7 billion (down KRW 4.4 billion year on year) and operating profit was KRW 2.9 billion (up 169%), supported by exports of Japanese hot and cold water dispensers and U.S. air purifiers and by cost reductions.

Early operating costs at the aviation unit were reflected in the same quarter's results. The combined net loss attributable to controlling shareholders over the last four quarters was KRW 133.6 billion.

05

Industry analysis

Winix's core products are strongly seasonal. The company has said that sales of air purifiers and dehumidifiers pick up noticeably in the second and third quarters. According to a company statement in July 2026, wildfire smoke from Canada spread into the U.S.

Midwest and Northeast, and in the week of July 13-19, 2026, air purifier unit sales at major North American channels such as Amazon and Costco rose by more than 2x from the prior week. U.S. sales rose 2.5 times and Canadian sales 2.8 times.

The company also said air purifier sales roughly doubled during the January 2025 Los Angeles wildfires. Because this demand follows weather events, its durability depends on whether wildfires recur. In overseas markets, cumulative Australian sales passing USD 30 million stands out.

In Korea the brand is known for dehumidifiers, while abroad air purifiers form the core line. The No. 1 Canadian ranking is based on Circana data cited by the company. Domestic competitors include Coway, and within the home appliance sector Winix's earnings generation looks weak relative to its sales scale.

06

Outlook

In July 2026 the company said it would launch new home appliances in the second half of 2026 and strengthen its direct-store sales network. It plans to focus on improving profitability in the electronics business, building on steady export growth in air purifiers and hot and cold water dispensers.

In North America, the company said it would supply air purifiers and replacement filters steadily as sales expand. Filters are a repeat-purchase item for households that own purifiers, so filter volume is a useful gauge of the quality of that sales base.

The timing of profit stabilization at the aviation unit is not yet clear. Early route-expansion costs and cost burdens at Paratha Air were cited as widening the first-quarter 2026 loss. Recent reports said the company withdrew a plan to issue exchangeable bonds backed by treasury shares.

The company had intended to use the proceeds to buy raw materials and fund early production ahead of peak demand. With fewer financing options, funding depends on operating cash flow and bank borrowing.

The next checkpoints are the fourth-quarter 2026 and full-year preliminary results, and the aviation segment's profit and loss and the electronics operating margin in the 2026 annual report.

08

Bull factors

Export recovery in electronics

According to company statements, first-quarter 2026 operating profit in the electronics segment was KRW 2.9 billion, up 169% year on year. Steady growth in exports of Japanese hot and cold water dispensers and U.S. air purifiers, together with cost reductions and efficiency measures, contributed. More than half of consolidated sales come from exports, so North American and Japanese demand drive results.

Narrowing losses at the net level

The net loss attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 13.7 billion in the third quarter of 2026, narrower than KRW 47.7 billion in the prior quarter and smaller than a year earlier.

The series ran from KRW 40.3 billion in the fourth quarter of 2025 to KRW 31.8 billion in the first quarter of 2026, KRW 47.7 billion in the second, and KRW 13.7 billion in the third. Sales rose for three consecutive quarters. Quarter-to-quarter swings are large, so a trend call needs more quarters of data.

North American demand and overseas base

The company said North American air purifier unit sales rose by more than 2x week on week in the July 13-19, 2026 period. Australian cumulative sales passed USD 30 million since its 2016 entry, growing at an average annual rate of about 37%.

In Canada, the company claims it has held the No. 1 position since 2024, citing Circana data. Its expanding overseas sales subsidiaries and retail channels form the base of its export strength.

Bear factors

Losses at the airline subsidiary

The company explained that the first-quarter 2026 operating loss of KRW 21.7 billion reflected early operating stabilization costs at the airline business. The third-quarter 2026 operating loss of KRW 25.7 billion was wider than the KRW 14.5 billion loss in the third quarter of 2025.

Commentators have noted that airlines require large upfront investment and face high entry barriers, making short-term profit hard. While operating losses persist, gains in electronics are unlikely to flow fully into consolidated earnings.

Higher financial leverage

The debt-to-equity ratio rose to 233.9% at the end of 2025 from 73.6% in 2023. Total liabilities of KRW 341.7 billion exceed total equity of KRW 146.1 billion. On a standalone basis, the current ratio fell to 79.8% at the end of the third quarter of 2025 from 95.1% at the end of 2024. Operating cash flow also turned negative at -KRW 51.9 billion in 2025.

Constrained funding capacity

Reports said the company withdrew a plan to issue exchangeable bonds backed by treasury shares. The proceeds were meant to fund raw material purchases and pre-production ahead of peak season. Analysts said liquidity room shrank as the company kept spending to support the aviation subsidiary's financial stability. While funding relies on operating cash flow and borrowing, room for investment stays limited.

Risk factors

Aviation business risk

Air transport requires heavy upfront investment and faces high entry barriers. Reports flagged as a concern that the acquired airline is a successor to a business that had previously entered court receivership. Exchange-rate and fuel-price moves directly affect operating costs. If early route-expansion costs do not decline, the drag on consolidated profit will persist.

Seasonality and one-off demand

Air purifiers and dehumidifiers are seasonal, so sales concentrate in the second and third quarters. In the first quarter of 2025, Los Angeles wildfires temporarily lifted U.S. subsidiary sales by more than KRW 10 billion, and in July 2026 wildfire smoke again drove a sharp rise in North American sales.

Demand tied to weather is not guaranteed to persist. How much of quarterly growth reflects one-off versus structural factors is the key question.

Balance sheet and currency

The debt-to-equity ratio was 233.9% at the end of 2025, and operating cash flow was -KRW 51.9 billion in 2025. With more than half of consolidated sales from exports, currency moves can affect won-denominated sales and profit.

If support obligations to the aviation subsidiary continue while liquidity room is shrinking, borrowing could rise.

Overall view

Winix's third-quarter 2026 disclosed results show sales recovery alongside narrower losses.

Sales rose for three consecutive quarters from a low of KRW 79 billion in the fourth quarter of 2025 to KRW 160.6 billion in the third quarter of 2026, and the net loss attributable to controlling shareholders narrowed to KRW 13.7 billion from the prior quarter.

However, operating losses of KRW 25.7 billion have continued for five consecutive quarters since the third quarter of 2025, and the loss was wider than a year earlier.

The core of the loss structure is the newly added airline subsidiary, whose early operating costs the company said were reflected in first-quarter 2026 results.

The electronics segment showed improvement with first-quarter 2026 operating profit of KRW 2.9 billion, and exports account for more than half of consolidated sales.

On the financial side, the debt-to-equity ratio of 233.9% and operating cash flow of -KRW 51.9 billion at the end of 2025 are burdens, and reports of a withdrawn exchangeable bond plan suggest limited funding room. The stock trades below 1x price-to-book, but no earnings-based multiple is calculated.
