Pangrim: Testing Earnings Durability After Turnaround

Pangrim returned to operating profit in the fiscal year ended September 2025, and its controlling-shareholder net income has risen for two straight quarters through Q2 2026, though revenue scale and operating margin remain low.

  3. 3

  4. 4

02

Business structure

Pangrim was founded in 1962 and listed on the KOSPI market in 1989, operating as a textile fabric maker. Its core is the cotton spinning segment's processed fabric, which includes dyed textiles.

According to JobKorea company data as of March 2026, the sales mix was 81% processed fabric, 12% raw cotton and other items, and 7% other. In its 2021 quarterly report, processed fabric accounted for 76.1% of sales and was described as used for apparel and bedding.

The same report cited long-term weakness from declining domestic textile demand and said overseas exports made up about 70% of sales. Raw cotton and other merchandise sales were also mostly export transactions.

The Vietnam-based subsidiary handles production, and raw material sales and processed fabric purchases with it are disclosed as related-party transactions. The 2021 consolidated scope also included an elderly care subsidiary.

Peers in cotton spinning such as Ilshin Spinning and Kyungbang are the natural comparison, and Pangrim's 2020 revenue was smaller than both. Because the business is concentrated in processed fabric, results track raw cotton prices, export volumes, and exchange rates closely.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual revenue fell from KRW 131.9 billion in the fiscal year ended September 2023 to KRW 110.6 billion in the year ended September 2024, a 16.2% decline, before rising to KRW 122.5 billion in the year ended September 2025, up 10.8% year on year.

The fiscal year ended September 2023 was the largest year in the period and the year ended September 2024 was the smallest.

Operating losses of KRW 4.12 billion and KRW 3.27 billion were recorded in the fiscal years ended September 2023 and 2024, and the year ended September 2025 posted operating profit of KRW 1.12 billion. Operating margin moved from -3.1% and -3.0% to 0.9%.

The narrower operating loss in the year ended September 2024 despite lower revenue points to cost structure changes, but the disclosed figures alone do not pin down the cause.

Controlling-shareholder net income moved from a loss of KRW 4.14 billion to a profit of KRW 774 million in the year ended September 2024, and then to KRW 6.72 billion in the year ended September 2025, up 768.2%.

According to Hankyung financial data, non-operating income exceeded non-operating expenses by a wide margin in the year ended September 2025, so a large part of the net income gain appears to come from outside core operations.

Quarterly controlling-shareholder net income went from a loss of KRW 1.51 billion in Q2 2025 to profits of KRW 2.63 billion in Q3 2025, KRW 2.57 billion in Q4 2025, KRW 3.09 billion in Q1 2026, and KRW 5.51 billion in Q2 2026.

Over the same stretch, quarterly operating profit was KRW 363 million in Q3 2025 and KRW 169 million in Q4 2025, followed by an operating loss of KRW 895 million in Q1 2026 and operating profit of KRW 1.58 billion in Q2 2026.

Operating cash flow declined from KRW 18.3 billion in the year ended September 2024 to KRW 5.19 billion in the year ended September 2025.

05

Industry analysis

The textile industry has faced long-term weakness in domestic demand. Pangrim's own 2021 quarterly report cited declining domestic textile demand as the reason for prolonged weakness.

As a result, Pangrim depends heavily on overseas buyers and its Vietnam production base, and export volumes and exchange rates shape results alongside the industry cycle. On 2020 data, cotton spinning peers Ilshin Spinning and Kyungbang had larger revenue than Pangrim.

Pangrim's position looks like an early recovery phase, with operating profit returning in the year ended September 2025 and quarterly profits rising through Q2 2026, rather than a clearly confirmed industry upturn. Linking this to the whole industry cycle would require sector indicators over the same period.

Against the apparel and fashion accessories sector average, Pangrim's operating margin is below the average while its debt ratio is far lower. A low debt ratio signals financial room, but profitability still trails the sector.

With processed fabric making up more than 80% of sales, product diversification is narrow, and this is the clearest difference against other listed peers in the sector. In short, Pangrim is small in scale with low financial burden, and its earnings base is at an early stage of recovery.

08

Bull factors

Earnings recovery has run for two quarters

Operating profit turned positive in the fiscal year ended September 2025 and returned to profit in Q2 2026 at KRW 1.58 billion. Controlling-shareholder net income rose from KRW 3.09 billion in Q1 2026 to KRW 5.51 billion in Q2 2026, a second straight quarterly increase.

The move from losses to profits is visible on a quarterly basis as well, which can be read as a recovery signal.

Solid financial capacity

At the end of September 2025, total equity was KRW 180.6 billion and total liabilities were KRW 48.4 billion. The debt ratio of 26.8% is low against the sector average. Most equity belongs to controlling shareholders, so the capital structure is relatively simple.

Treasury shares and shareholder return room

The company holds treasury shares equal to 21.82% of issued shares. In a January 2026 disclosure it said it would dispose of 180,333 treasury shares to fund employee bonus payments. Dividends have also been paid since the fiscal year ended September 2023. Using treasury shares for compensation and paying dividends are options within its shareholder return approach.

Bear factors

Revenue growth remains gradual

Revenue fell 16.2% in the year ended September 2024, and the 10.8% recovery in the year ended September 2025 did not fully reverse that decline. Quarterly revenue rose only 0.3% and 0.7% quarter on quarter in Q1 and Q2 2026. With revenue in the hundreds of billions of won, the scale is too small to absorb quarterly profit swings.

Quality of earnings and reliance on non-operating items

Q2 2026 controlling-shareholder net income of KRW 5.51 billion exceeded operating profit of KRW 1.58 billion. In the year ended September 2025, non-operating income also exceeded non-operating expenses by a wide margin. The weaker cash generation widens the gap with net income.

Dependence on exports and overseas production

In the 2021 quarterly report, overseas exports accounted for about 70% of sales. With domestic demand declining for an extended period, export volumes, exchange rates, and raw cotton prices can combine to widen earnings swings. Costs and utilization at the Vietnam production base also feed directly into results.

Risk factors

Earnings volatility

Quarterly operating profit moved from KRW 363 million in Q3 2025 and KRW 169 million in Q4 2025 to an operating loss of KRW 895 million in Q1 2026 and operating profit of KRW 1.58 billion in Q2 2026. Because revenue is small, modest changes in cost ratios or utilization feed directly into operating profit. Whether profitable quarters persist will determine how durable the recovery is.

Non-operating items and cash flow

When controlling-shareholder net income keeps exceeding operating profit, the earnings mix leans on non-operating items. Operating cash flow fell to KRW 5.19 billion in the year ended September 2025. If non-operating items prove one-off, earnings durability could be lower than reported net income suggests.

Export, currency, and raw material exposure

Given high export exposure and a Vietnam production base, results are sensitive to exchange rates and raw cotton prices. If domestic textile demand keeps declining, widening sales channels will be difficult.

Heavy reliance on processed fabric narrows product diversification and leaves less buffer against external shocks.

Overall view

Pangrim returned to operating profit in the fiscal year ended September 2025, and Q2 2026 extended the profitable operating result and the rise in controlling-shareholder net income. Revenue recovered to KRW 122.5 billion in the year ended September 2025, but quarterly growth has been gradual.

An operating margin of 0.9% means top-line recovery has not yet fully translated into profitability. Because controlling-shareholder net income has been running above operating profit, non-operating contributions and the gap with operating cash flow should be examined together.

The balance sheet looks stable with a debt ratio of 26.8%, and treasury share holdings and a dividend record are elements of shareholder returns. On the other side, a processed-fabric-centered business and reliance on exports increase exposure to exchange rates and raw material prices.

The next checkpoint is the Q3 2026 quarterly report, where the persistence of profitable quarters and the share of non-operating items are the key items.
