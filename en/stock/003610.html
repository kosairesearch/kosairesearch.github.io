02

Business structure

Pangrim was founded in 1962 as a cotton spinning and weaving company. It spins staple fibers such as raw cotton into yarn, then weaves and dyes or finishes that yarn into fabric. The company says its process runs from spinning through weaving, dyeing, printing and special finishing in one system.

AlphaSquare's breakdown puts processed fabric at about 82.38% of sales, raw cotton and other trading items at about 14.32%, and other items at about 3.30%, with no reference period stated. Other income comes from real estate rental and a welfare-care business.

According to Value Line, weak domestic demand has pushed exports to Europe and the United States to about 70% of sales. Production sits at the Ansan plant in Korea, which handles dyeing and printing, and at a Vietnamese subsidiary that supports raw material trading.

A domestic welfare-care subsidiary runs the welfare business, and Pangrim's stake in it was recorded at 78.95% as of August 2019. A prior broker report contrasted Pangrim with peers that have only spinning capacity, citing its dyeing process as a differentiator. Its customers are mainly global buyers.

Earnings analysis

On reported figures, revenue was KRW 131.9 billion in the year ended September 2023, the largest of the three years, then fell 16.2% to KRW 110.6 billion in the year ended September 2024 before rising 10.8% to KRW 122.5 billion in the year ended September 2025.

Operating results moved from an operating loss of KRW 4.12 billion in the year ended September 2023 and an operating loss of KRW 3.27 billion in the year ended September 2024 to operating profit of KRW 1.12 billion in the year ended September 2025, turning positive.

The operating margin moved from -3.1% and -3.0% to 0.9%. Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders went from a net loss of KRW 4.14 billion to KRW 774 million, turning positive, and then to KRW 6.72 billion in the year ended September 2025, up 768.2%.

Net profit including non-controlling interests was KRW 6.51 billion in that year, a different figure, with the gap reflecting non-controlling interests. Operating cash flow was KRW 7.41 billion, KRW 18.3 billion and KRW 5.19 billion across the three years.

Quarterly revenue rose 15.7% quarter on quarter to KRW 34.2 billion in the third quarter of 2025, then eased 8.8% to KRW 31.2 billion in the fourth quarter, and rose to KRW 31.3 billion in the first quarter of 2026 and KRW 31.5 billion in the second quarter.

Operating profit fell from KRW 1.01 billion in the second quarter of 2025 to KRW 363 million and KRW 169 million, turned into an operating loss of KRW 895 million in the first quarter of 2026, then returned to operating profit of KRW 1.58 billion in the second quarter.

Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders reached KRW 5.51 billion in the second quarter of 2026, above operating profit.

A Korea Economic Daily financial summary shows non-operating income of about KRW 11.6 billion and non-operating expenses of about KRW 3.4 billion in the year ended September 2025, which helps explain that gap. The size of any one-off items cannot be separated from the reported figures alone.

Industry analysis

The cotton spinning and fabric industry is seeing domestic demand weaken while reliance on exports grows. Value Line reports that exports make up about 70% of Pangrim's sales, mainly to Europe and the United States.

A prior broker report cited the Vietnam production base and free trade agreement benefits as strengths, but that view is dated and offers limited support for current conditions.

Compared with listed peers in the same sector, Pangrim's revenue scale is well below the sector average, and its operating margin is also lower. Raw cotton prices, overseas buyers' order volumes and exchange rates are cited as variables for profitability, but no current price data has been confirmed.

Unlike large apparel companies with branded finished goods, Pangrim focuses on fabric production and dyeing, so its earnings structure differs. No recent industry supply or demand indicator that would show the cycle position has been confirmed.

Quarterly sales and operating margin trends are therefore the more practical way to read the sector through this company.

Quarterly sales were KRW 34.2 billion in the third quarter of 2025, then KRW 31.2 billion in the fourth quarter and KRW 31.5 billion in the second quarter of 2026. Clear top-line expansion has not yet appeared.

Thin margins and quarterly swings

The operating margin was only 0.9% in the year ended September 2025. Operating profit fell to KRW 363 million in the third quarter of 2025 and KRW 169 million in the fourth quarter, then an operating loss of KRW 895 million was recorded in the first quarter of 2026. Profit and loss alternate across quarters, so the durability of the recovery is not yet confirmed.

Reliance on non-operating income

Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 5.51 billion in the second quarter of 2026 was well above operating profit of KRW 1.58 billion.

A Korea Economic Daily summary shows non-operating income of about KRW 11.6 billion and non-operating expenses of about KRW 3.4 billion for the year ended September 2025. Whether non-operating items are propping up net profit more than core operations needs checking in the quarterly reports.

10

Risk factors

Exports, FX and trade

About 70% of sales come from abroad, mainly Europe and the United States. Exchange rate moves or changes in trade conditions can affect both buyer orders and won-denominated revenue. Given the high export share, these variables can be a major source of earnings swings.

Raw material costs and margins

Raw cotton and other inputs make up a large share of cost of sales. With the operating margin at 0.9% in the year ended September 2025, a failure to pass cost increases through to prices could quickly erode profit. The latest raw material price trend has not been confirmed.

Subsidiaries, litigation and disclosure limits

The domestic subsidiary runs a welfare business, and a court loss in a related case has been reported. The contribution from the Vietnamese subsidiary was not confirmed in detailed figures. Because segment data is limited, subsidiary results and provisions need to be checked directly in the periodic report notes.
