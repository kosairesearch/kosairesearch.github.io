02

Business structure

PNC Technologies was founded in 1999 as a digital power equipment maker and listed on KOSDAQ in 2016.

Its main products are distribution automation terminal units, digital protection relays, remote supervisory control systems, fault location devices and electronic watt-hour meters, sold mainly to KEPCO across domestic and overseas power markets.

Electronic watt-hour meter sales appear to move annual results the most, and their decline was the main reason cited for the 2025 revenue drop.

In the first nine months of 2025 on a standalone basis, lower KEPCO orders and delayed overseas project awards cut distribution terminal sales, while a better product mix lifted gross profit. Concentration on a single utility customer means the timing and size of its orders are the central variable for revenue.

In January 2026 the company agreed to acquire 100% of a wholly owned-to-be subsidiary holding export-type distribution automation terminal technology for KRW 14 billion, with a KRW 1.4 billion down payment and a KRW 12.6 billion balance set for April 30, 2026.

The company said the deal would strengthen its overseas market reach. It then moved to merge that acquired unit and another subsidiary into the parent through small, no-share-issuance absorption mergers.

In April 2026 it also agreed to take a 98.4% stake in a holding company for KRW 12.3 billion, citing management efficiency. Quarterly results swing sharply with the timing of orders, which is the defining trait of the business.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual revenue fell 6.0% to KRW 20.7 billion in 2023 from KRW 22 billion in 2022, then jumped 67.9% to KRW 34.7 billion in 2024, the largest year of 2022 to 2025, before dropping 20.5% to KRW 27.6 billion in 2025.

Operating profit rose from KRW 2.39 billion in 2022 to a peak of KRW 2.72 billion in 2023 (up 13.9%), then fell 34.0% to KRW 1.79 billion in 2024 and 98.9% to KRW 20.02 million in 2025, a two-year decline running from 2024 through 2025.

The operating margin went from 10.8% in 2022 and 13.1% in 2023 to 5.2% in 2024 and 0.1% in 2025. The company explained the 2025 profit drop as lower electronic watt-hour meter sales combined with higher commission, testing and development costs.

Attributable net profit climbed to KRW 3.64 billion in 2023 (up 23.7%), fell to KRW 2.24 billion in 2024 (down 38.6%), and turned into an attributable net loss of KRW 17.9 billion in 2025. That 2025 net loss included an equity-method loss from an acquired stake and an impairment on an associate investment.

At end-2025 total liabilities were KRW 38.5 billion against total equity of KRW 43.8 billion, lifting the debt ratio to 87.9% from 13.1% in 2022. Operating cash flow was KRW 2.13 billion in 2022, negative KRW 692 million in 2023, KRW 6.21 billion in 2024 and KRW 1.11 billion in 2025.

On a quarterly basis, Q3 2025 revenue of KRW 7.35 billion with operating profit of KRW 504 million and attributable net profit of KRW 652 million gave way to Q4 2025 revenue of KRW 5.68 billion, an operating loss of KRW 318 million and an attributable net loss of KRW 18.4 billion.

Q1 2026 revenue rose 162.7% quarter on quarter to KRW 14.9 billion with operating profit of KRW 648 million, yet the attributable net loss was KRW 90.39 million. Q2 2026 revenue was KRW 7.62 billion with an operating loss of KRW 300 million and an attributable net loss of KRW 11.9 billion.

Q3 2026 revenue reached KRW 12 billion, with operating profit of KRW 3.02 billion (up 499.1% year on year) and attributable net profit of KRW 2.01 billion. Over the last four quarters, attributable net losses total KRW 28.3 billion.

05

Industry analysis

PNC Technologies' revenue and profit depend heavily on distribution-related orders from KEPCO, so domestic order flow is the central variable. On a standalone basis for the first nine months of 2025, lower KEPCO orders were cited as the cause of lower distribution automation terminal sales.

Delayed overseas project awards during the same period meant the company could not offset the domestic order gap. In late April 2026 the power equipment theme strengthened in the market, and the stock was named among related shares and rose sharply in the short term.

That theme-driven move ran separately from the earnings trend. The company is classified under KOSDAQ machinery and equipment, but peers such as Doosan Enerbility focus on power plant equipment and Hyundai Elevator on elevators, so direct comparison is limited.

The company is differentiating through export-type terminal technology aimed at widening overseas supply routes. Quarterly results in 2026 have alternated between profit and loss after the 2025 order decline, so whether domestic order recovery turns into sustained profit is the next test.

Overseas awards remain at the stage of a stated expansion plan, and their effect will appear in reported results once booked.

06

Outlook

Among the plans the company has stated, the confirmed items are securing export-type distribution terminal technology through the Huin acquisition and expanding overseas markets.

The KRW 12.6 billion balance on the Huin deal was disclosed for payment on April 30, 2026, and in April the company also resolved to take a 98.4% stake in a holding company for KRW 12.3 billion. The company said the mergers would cut unnecessary costs and make use of personnel and assets more efficient.

At the March 2026 shareholder meeting, the 2025 financial statements were approved, an executive director was newly appointed, and the standalone audit opinion was recorded as unqualified. Quarterly results show operating profit in Q1 and Q3 2026 and an operating loss in Q2.

The next test is whether Q4 2026 keeps the Q3 profit trend. On an annual basis, the 2026 business report will provide confirmed figures and show the contribution of the acquired subsidiaries alongside borrowing costs.

A court partially granted a request tied to inspection of accounting records by a major shareholder, disclosed in June 2026, and how that litigation develops could affect management stability. Whether power utility orders recover and whether overseas awards materialize are the two drivers of revenue growth.

Bull factors

Q1 2026 also posted KRW 648 million, so two quarters were profitable after the Q4 2025 operating loss of KRW 318 million. A KRW 300 million operating loss in Q2 means the trend is not yet established.

Export-type terminal technology and overseas plans

In January 2026 the company said the Huin acquisition would add export-type distribution terminal technology and widen overseas reach. The KRW 14 billion price was scheduled for full payment by April 2026.

Because overseas awards were delayed in the first nine months of 2025, whether this technology changes the award pattern is the point to watch.

Insider stake increase disclosed

In a June 2026 disclosure, director Cho Min-gi reported raising holdings to 182,000 shares, or 2.8% of issued shares. Separately, a major shareholder was disclosed buying common shares on the open market between late January and early February 2026. These are recorded facts, and their meaning should be read alongside the earnings recovery.

Reliance on utility orders and delayed overseas awards

The company attributed the 2025 annual revenue decline mainly to lower watt-hour meter sales. With KEPCO as the dominant customer, any gap in orders can limit how quickly results recover.

Risk factors

Balance sheet and borrowing burden

The debt ratio reached 87.9% at end-2025 with total liabilities of KRW 38.5 billion. The 2026 acquisition balance and stake purchase were disclosed as funded by own cash and borrowings.

If higher borrowing coincides with slower earnings recovery, financial strain could grow, and whether associate impairments recur is also a watch item.

Governance and litigation

A court partially granted a major shareholder's application to inspect and copy accounting records, disclosed in June 2026. The remaining requests were rejected, and litigation costs are split equally between the parties. A prolonged dispute between major shareholders could add to management decision-making and disclosure burdens.

Concentration on KEPCO orders and export delays

KEPCO is the main customer, so revenue moves with the timing and budget of utility orders. Lower KEPCO orders translated into lower revenue in the first nine months of 2025. Overseas project awards have already slipped once, so the timing of the export expansion plan is also uncertain.
