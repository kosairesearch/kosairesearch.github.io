02

Business structure

Dong-Ah Geological Engineering Company is a construction specialist that carries out underground and subterranean infrastructure work, including tunnels, power cable ducts and port or marine ground improvement, using Shield TBM (Tunnel Boring Machine) and DCM (Deep Cement Mixing) methods.

By segment, the Ground & Port Works division generated KRW 99 billion of first-half 2026 consolidated revenue, or 42.0%, followed by General Construction at KRW 87.6 billion (37.2%), TBM at KRW 43.4 billion (18.4%) and ENG at KRW 5.7 billion (2.4%).

Revenue is split between the Ground & Port Works and General Construction divisions rather than concentrated in one segment. Press reports describe the company as the domestic leader in TBM and DCM work.

Its main counterparties are construction companies that take part in power and road infrastructure projects, as well as public clients.

In January 2026 it signed a KRW 25.1 billion Shield TBM contract for a power cable duct project in the western Seoul metropolitan area with Namkwang Engineering & Construction Co., Ltd. In April 2026 it signed a power cable duct contract for the Yongin-Hwaseong area worth KRW 67.6 billion with KOLONGLOBAL CORPORATION.

In August 2026 it signed a KRW 24.3 billion ground improvement contract with KTC CIVIL E&C for a site expansion at the eastern part of Changi Airport in Singapore.

The competitive landscape splits between large general contractors with full construction capabilities and specialists focused on methods such as soft-ground and tunnel work, and Dong-Ah belongs to the second group.

The largest shareholder, Crescendo Equity Partners, holds a 34.23% stake, and in 2026 press reports said GS Engineering & Construction was considering acquiring that controlling stake.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual results recovered after a trough in 2022. Revenue rose from KRW 266.3 billion in 2022 to KRW 345 billion in 2023 (up 29.5% year on year), KRW 393.5 billion in 2024 (up 14.1%) and KRW 467.7 billion in 2025 (up 18.9%), marking three consecutive years of growth from 2023 through 2025.

Of the years from 2022 to 2025, 2025 was the largest.

Operating profit moved from an operating loss of KRW 30.6 billion in 2022 to operating profit of KRW 7.69 billion in 2023, a swing back to profit, and then rose to KRW 9.61 billion in 2024 (up 24.9%) and KRW 18.1 billion in 2025 (up 88.0%), two consecutive years of growth.

The operating margin went from -11.5% in 2022 to 2.2% in 2023, 2.4% in 2024 and 3.9% in 2025. Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders moved from a net loss of KRW 22.8 billion in 2022 to KRW 10.2 billion in 2023, then KRW 11.4 billion in 2024 (up 11.3%) and KRW 18.7 billion in 2025 (up 64.4%).

Press coverage attributed the 2025 improvement to rising public-sector orders and demand for eco-friendly methods such as mechanical tunneling and ground improvement. Operating cash flow was KRW 95.1 billion in 2025, up sharply from KRW 9 billion in 2024.

Quarterly revenue peaked at KRW 130.9 billion in the third quarter of 2025 and then fell over four consecutive quarters to KRW 127.5 billion in the fourth quarter of 2025, KRW 123.8 billion in the first quarter of 2026, KRW 111.9 billion in the second quarter of 2026 and KRW 103.1 billion in the third quarter of 2026.

Operating profit fell over the same four quarters, from KRW 5.24 billion in the third quarter of 2025 to KRW 4.55 billion in the third quarter of 2026. Because revenue fell 21.2% year on year while operating profit fell 13.2%, the third-quarter operating margin was higher than a year earlier.

Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders swung widely, from KRW 6.62 billion in the fourth quarter of 2025 to KRW 4.24 billion in the first quarter of 2026 and KRW 5.52 billion in the second quarter of 2026.

The debt-to-equity ratio rose from 68.0% in 2022 to 99.1% in 2025, with total liabilities of KRW 226.6 billion and total equity of KRW 228.6 billion at the end of 2025.

05

Industry analysis

In the construction order environment, expanding public-sector orders and rising infrastructure investment are cited as factors supporting the growth in new contracts.

The company's core TBM and DCM methods are linked to growing demand for eco-friendly construction methods, and press coverage cites rising demand for mechanical tunneling and ground improvement as a backdrop to the earnings recovery.

Underground power cable installation is another area the company sees as a target for expanded orders. Press reports also highlighted the Great Train eXpress (GTX) rail network in the Seoul metropolitan area as a key beneficiary. On the other side, external risks exist.

An EU safeguard investigation into Chinese PET raw materials and Chinese export controls on TBMs have been cited as risks, and according to press reports the company said Chinese-made parts account for less than 10% of its own models.

Large general contractors have broader construction scope and larger overseas order books, while Dong-Ah competes as a specialist focused on methods such as soft-ground and tunnel work.

Within the same construction and building materials sector, Samsung C&T, Hyundai Engineering & Construction, DL E&C and Daewoo Engineering & Construction form the large-cap group, and large contractors and specialists also work together on civil projects.

The direction of private-sector demand and the interest-rate environment are variables to watch at the industry level.

Outlook

In its first-half 2026 results announcement, the company said it would expand orders in the second half of 2026, focusing on domestic power cable projects and overseas infrastructure projects such as Singapore.

The chief executive said order activity would be strengthened around core projects in the second half as well. Ongoing sites include the Yongin-Hwaseong power cable project under the KOLONGLOBAL contract (April 1, 2026 to April 20, 2028) and the Shield-TBM work on Section 2 of the Gimpo-Paju expressway.

The Namkwang contract runs from January 2, 2026 to November 9, 2028, and the KTC CIVIL E&C contract runs until October 17, 2027.

On shareholder returns, a 2026 year-end dividend with a total of about KRW 19.2 billion has been proposed, and the board resolved to cancel 391,199 treasury shares, with the cancellation scheduled for November 23, 2026, subject to change depending on consultations with authorities.

A KRW 5 billion treasury share trust agreement was signed on November 16, 2026 and runs until November 15, 2027. The final dividend amount, record date and payment schedule will be set by the board and disclosed separately.

On the litigation front, a KRW 26.3 billion damages claim was received in September 2026, and progress will need to be checked through disclosures. Press reports of a possible acquisition of the controlling stake mean that ownership changes could also appear in future filings. The 2026 annual report will be the reference point for confirming full-year results.

Bull factors

Recovery in annual profit

Operating profit reached KRW 18.1 billion in 2025, compared with an operating loss of KRW 30.6 billion in 2022, and both operating profit and net profit attributable to controlling shareholders rose for two consecutive years from 2024 to 2025. The operating margin improved from -11.5% in 2022 to 3.9% in 2025. Operating cash flow rising to KRW 95.1 billion in 2025 is cited as support for the quality of earnings.

Secured order pipeline

Contracts in 2026 included a KRW 25.1 billion deal with Namkwang in January and a power cable contract worth about KRW 67.6 billion with KOLONGLOBAL in April, followed by a KRW 24.3 billion Changi Airport ground improvement contract in August.

The company said it would expand domestic power cable and overseas infrastructure orders in the second half. Several contracts run into 2028, which adds visibility to medium-term revenue.

Expanded shareholder returns

According to press reports, the company paid cash dividends of KRW 20.6 billion and bought back shares worth KRW 47.3 billion over the past three years. The 2026 year-end dividend is planned at about KRW 19.2 billion in total, and a KRW 5 billion treasury share trust was signed.

Treasury shares acquired under the earlier KRW 10 billion trust, excluding those for employee compensation, are scheduled for cancellation.

Bear factors

Consecutive quarterly declines

Revenue fell over four consecutive quarters from KRW 130.9 billion in the third quarter of 2025 to KRW 103.1 billion in the third quarter of 2026, and the latest quarter was down 21.2% year on year. Operating profit fell over the same four quarters, from KRW 5.24 billion to KRW 4.55 billion. The annual recovery is not visible in the quarterly trend, which is the main bear point.

Leverage and litigation burden

The debt-to-equity ratio rose from 68.0% in 2022 to 99.1% in 2025, and total liabilities of KRW 226.6 billion at the end of 2025 were close to total equity of KRW 228.6 billion. A KRW 26.3 billion damages claim received in September 2026 equals 11.5% of equity. The financial burden could change depending on the outcome.

Earnings volatility and thin margins

The operating margin was 3.9% in 2025, a thin level, and net profit attributable to controlling shareholders swung widely, falling 36.0% from KRW 6.62 billion in the fourth quarter of 2025 to KRW 4.24 billion in the first quarter of 2026. The third-quarter 2026 figure of KRW 4.63 billion was down 6.5% year on year. With thin margins, construction delays or cost increases can pass through to profit quickly.

Risk factors

Litigation and contingent liabilities

The KRW 26.3 billion damages claim equals 11.5% of equity, so its outcome could weigh heavily on finances. Progress on the case and whether any provisions are recorded should be checked in future disclosures and periodic reports. With the debt-to-equity ratio at 99.1%, a realized contingent loss would reduce financial flexibility.

Order concentration and project execution

Although revenue is spread across the Ground & Port Works, General Construction and TBM divisions, progress on large projects drives quarterly results. Because quarterly revenue has declined for four quarters, delays in starting new projects could weigh on second-half results. Overseas projects are also exposed to exchange rates and local execution risks.

External supply chain, regulation and ownership

An EU safeguard investigation into Chinese PET raw materials and Chinese export controls on TBMs are cited as external risks for the sector. The company said Chinese-made parts account for less than 10% of its own models, but changes in overseas equipment sourcing could affect costs and schedules.

If the sale of the largest shareholder's stake and a possible acquisition by GS Engineering & Construction materialize, the management direction could change.

Overall view

Dong-Ah Geological Engineering Company moved from an operating loss of KRW 30.6 billion in 2022 to operating profit of KRW 18.1 billion in 2025, and operating cash flow rose to KRW 95.1 billion in 2025.

Quarterly revenue and operating profit have nonetheless declined for four consecutive quarters from the third quarter of 2025, and third-quarter 2026 revenue of KRW 103.1 billion was down 21.2% year on year.

The company's first-half 2026 announcement that the operating margin rose to about 4.3% from about 3.7% a year earlier supports the case for better profitability. The debt-to-equity ratio rose to 99.1% in 2025, and the KRW 26.3 billion damages claim, equal to 11.5% of equity, remains an uncertainty.

The 2026 shareholder return plan of about KRW 24.2 billion, combining the year-end dividend and the additional share buyback, exceeds the KRW 21 billion of net profit attributable to controlling shareholders over the last four quarters.

Contracts in power cable and overseas ground improvement run into 2028, but an increase in orders large enough to reverse the quarterly revenue decline has not yet appeared in the reported figures. Press reports of a possible acquisition of the largest shareholder's stake also remain a governance variable.

The next checkpoints are the treasury share cancellation on November 23, 2026, the final year-end dividend decision and the 2026 annual report.
