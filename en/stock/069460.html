02

Business structure

Daeho AL rolls and processes aluminum sheet and has been listed on the KOSPI since November 2002. Its core line is materials for secondary batteries, including battery cell cases and ESS components.

The company says it is the only domestic producer of prismatic can-type parts among its aluminum processed battery components. A 2025 report projected that the secondary battery segment would approach about 50% of 2025 sales, and the company plans to lift that share above 55% by 2027.

Outside batteries, its main applications include A5052 series material for digital information appliances and automotive and electrical-electronic uses. Its kitchenware-related products have expanded exports to Southeast Asia on the back of high quality and short lead times.

Reported customers include supply-chain partners of large groups such as Samsung Electronics, Samsung SDI and Hyundai Rotem. The company has stressed small-batch, multi-model production and set 2026 priorities of higher-value products, customer diversification and new applications.

Export share is targeted to rise from about 19% in 2025 to 25% in 2026. Peers in the sector include Sama Aluminium, and aluminum price swings remain a key pressure on margins.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual revenue was KRW 174 billion in 2022, KRW 181.6 billion in 2023 (up 4.3% year on year), KRW 168.5 billion in 2024 (down 7.2%) and KRW 215.6 billion in 2025 (up 28.0%). Among 2022 to 2025, 2025 was the largest year and 2024 the smallest.

Operating income was KRW 9.34 billion, KRW 8.36 billion, KRW 5.83 billion and KRW 3.47 billion, declining for three consecutive years from 2023 to 2025, and the operating margin moved from 5.4% to 4.6%, 3.5% and 1.6%.

The company attributed the 2025 revenue increase to higher orders for high-value products such as battery case material, while sharp swings in LME prices and exchange rates reduced operating and net profit.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders fell from KRW 5.92 billion to KRW 3.49 billion and KRW 1.4 billion before turning into a KRW 32.1 billion loss in 2025, and total net loss including non-controlling interests was KRW 32.5 billion.

In 2025 operating income stayed positive, but reported other losses widened to KRW 29.5 billion and finance costs exceeded KRW 13 billion, which drove the loss. The debt-to-equity ratio was 110.7%, 54.0%, 58.6% and 100.9% across 2022 to 2025.

At the end of 2025 total equity was KRW 84.4 billion, of which KRW 83.3 billion was attributable to controlling shareholders, and total liabilities were KRW 85.2 billion. Operating cash flow went from KRW 5.07 billion and KRW 6.66 billion to KRW 1.35 billion and then negative KRW 2.02 billion in 2025.

Quarterly, third-quarter 2025 revenue was KRW 59.1 billion with an operating loss of KRW 3.04 billion and a net loss attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 3.36 billion.

Fourth-quarter revenue fell 7.9% to KRW 54.4 billion, with an operating loss of KRW 2.07 billion and a controlling-shareholder net loss of KRW 36.3 billion. First-quarter 2026 revenue rose 20.4% to KRW 65.4 billion and operating income turned positive at KRW 1.74 billion, while the net loss narrowed to KRW 3.7 billion.

Second-quarter revenue was KRW 71.1 billion with operating income of KRW 1.87 billion and a net loss of KRW 676 million.

Third-quarter revenue was KRW 56.5 billion, down 20.6% from the prior quarter and down 4.4% year on year, operating income was KRW 1 billion, and net income attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 2.6 billion.

The four quarters from 4Q 2025 to 3Q 2026 total a controlling-shareholder net loss of KRW 38.1 billion. The gap between a KRW 2.07 billion operating loss and a KRW 36.3 billion controlling-shareholder loss in 4Q 2025 points to a large non-operating drag in that quarter.

05

Industry analysis

Demand from the downstream sectors rests on two pillars: secondary batteries and ESS, and automotive materials. Tighter environmental rules and vehicle lightweighting have lifted demand for aluminum materials, a view shared by the company and press coverage.

A 2025 report noted that while key rivals' earnings stalled during the EV slowdown, Daeho AL supplied ESS and other battery products to three major domestic battery makers and its related sales grew. The company expects the battery industry to keep expanding alongside AI and green trends.

Aluminum is priced off LME quotes, and the company cited LME and exchange-rate swings as a reason operating profit declined in 2025. Whether demand growth converts into margins is therefore the key industry question.

The return to positive operating income from the first quarter of 2026 suggests higher-value mix and cost control are having an effect, but third-quarter 2026 revenue down year on year shows volatility.

Listed peers in the sector include Sama Aluminium, Korea Zinc and Poongsan, and direct profitability comparisons should be made using each company's filings. In aluminum materials, Daeho AL competes on quality and short lead times, but it is smaller than large producers and faces constraints on financial stability.

Industry-wide demand is improving, while company-level results depend more on raw material prices, exchange rates and governance and listing developments.

Outlook

For 2026 the company set priorities of expanding higher-value products, diversifying customers and entering new applications. It plans to strengthen development of high-strength, high-precision aluminum materials and expand supply for EV and secondary battery cases.

The company said sales of prismatic can-type battery parts should grow faster as demand rises. Export share is targeted at 25% in 2026 through supply chains centered on Poland, Hungary, Thailand and India. A medium-term goal, reported in 2025, is to lift the secondary battery share of sales above 55% by 2027.

These are company plans rather than confirmed results. On the financial side, operating income stayed positive in the second quarter of 2026 after the first-quarter turn, but fell to KRW 1 billion in the third quarter.

On governance, Daeho Sinkisul Investment Partnership acquired 12 million shares (13.78%) off-market on March 13, 2026, and press reports said the company appointed an advisor for a sale of control in July 2026.

By October 2026, shareholder-group holdings had risen to 11.09%, and the group stated that its purpose is to influence management control. The exchange decided in June 2026 that the company met delisting criteria, and the company filed an objection.

Press reports as of October 2026 described delisting and control-sale procedures running in parallel. The outcome of these two processes could change the conditions for executing the business plan, and it is the largest variable in the outlook. Progress on the company's targets is most clearly tracked through disclosed sales mix and operating margin.

Bull factors

Higher-value mix and a return to profit

Operating income turned positive at KRW 1.74 billion in the first quarter of 2026 and rose to KRW 1.87 billion in the second quarter. The company's strategy of expanding higher-value battery and ESS products is cited as the basis for the recovery.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders also turned positive at KRW 2.6 billion in the third quarter of 2026.

Demand base and export expansion

Tighter environmental rules and vehicle lightweighting are cited as driving demand for aluminum materials. The company has said it aims to raise its export share from about 19% in 2025 to 25% in 2026. It also says it is the only domestic producer of prismatic can-type battery parts.

Possible governance restructuring

The company is pursuing a public sale of control, and the shareholder group raised its stake to 11.09% in October 2026. Market analysis holds that normalization requires resolving the control dispute together with financial and accounting problems. The argument for this view is that clearer governance could reduce listing uncertainty.

Bear factors

Audit disclaimer and listing process

The 2025 annual report received a disclaimer of opinion, and trading was suspended on March 23, 2026. In June 2026 the exchange decided the company met delisting criteria, and the company filed an objection. As of October 2026 reports, the delisting process was ongoing.

Embezzlement allegations and internal controls

On March 31, 2026 the company disclosed embezzlement and breach-of-trust allegations involving KRW 14 billion against former and current executives. Further allegations followed in April, and the amount disclosed on April 9 was KRW 4.54 billion. The company said the figures are based on complaint filings and may change with investigation results.

Borrowing burden and cumulative losses

Short-term borrowings were KRW 49.8 billion at the end of the first quarter of 2026, of which KRW 38 billion was a Korea Development Bank usance facility. Including KRW 12.7 billion of convertible bonds, current financial liabilities reached KRW 63.1 billion.

Risk factors

Control sale and governance

The company is pursuing a public sale of control, and press reports said it has appointed an advisor. Shareholder-group holdings rose to 11.09% by October 2026, and the group stated a purpose of influencing management control.

The buyer, the terms and the group's role in management could change who executes the business plan and in which direction. Plans to expand EV and secondary battery case supply and to enter new applications could also be affected by that change.

Customer and supply-chain concentration

The plan to lift the secondary battery share of sales above 55% by 2027 depends on orders from supply-chain customers of large groups such as Samsung SDI. If demand from major customers including Samsung Electronics and Hyundai Rotem weakens or supply terms change, the shift toward a higher-value mix could be delayed.

The 25% export target also depends on expanding supply chains in Poland, Hungary, Thailand and India, so the pace of winning local customers remains a variable.

Raw materials and FX

Aluminum prices follow LME quotations, and exchange-rate moves affect profit. The company cited both factors when explaining the decline in 2025 operating income. Third-quarter 2026 revenue down 4.4% year on year shows demand volatility.
