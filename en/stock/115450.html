02

Business structure

HLB Therapeutics runs two business lines: drug development and cold chain storage and transport for vaccines and biologics.

In drug development, its U.S. subsidiary is running the global Phase 3 trial (SEER-2) of RGN-259 for neurotrophic keratitis, while its U.S. subsidiary Elevar Therapeutics is pursuing U.S. approval for rivoceranib combined with camrelizumab, the PD-1 antibody from China's Hengrui Pharma, as a first-line liver cancer treatment.

RGN-259 is designed for room temperature storage and dosing five times a day for four weeks, compared with refrigerated storage and six daily doses for eight weeks for Oxervate, which the group cites as the only competing product.

The group also points to FDA orphan drug designation for RGN-259 as part of the program's rationale.

The cold chain unit handles storage and transport of vaccines for the national immunization program and other biologics, and its revenue depends on distribution volumes and public procurement schedules rather than clinical outcomes.

In July 2026 the company said it had obtained an international standard certification for its cold chain quality and environmental management system.

In the same month it disclosed a shareholder meeting on a plan to spin off the electronics unit, which makes boiler motors and pumps, into a newly formed unlisted company. The company said it would still run drug development and cold chain as its two pillars after the split. Because the pipeline is still before approval, current revenue comes mainly from cold chain distribution.

04

Earnings analysis

On a disclosed basis, revenue was KRW 48.1 billion in 2022, KRW 53.7 billion in 2023, KRW 54.9 billion in 2024 and KRW 69.6 billion in 2025, rising for three straight years from 2023 through 2025, with 2025 up 26.6%.

The operating loss was KRW 7.88 billion in 2022, KRW 8.7 billion in 2023, KRW 7.62 billion in 2024 and KRW 3.53 billion in 2025, a loss in each of the four years, and the operating margin improved from -16.4% in 2022 to -5.1% in 2025.

The attributable net loss was KRW 11.4 billion in 2022, KRW 2.3 billion in 2023, KRW 22.8 billion in 2024 and KRW 9.23 billion in 2025, while the net loss including non-controlling interests was KRW 12.1 billion, KRW 3.08 billion, KRW 23.5 billion and KRW 9.67 billion for the same years.

The debt-to-equity ratio was 46.5% in 2022, 17.1% in 2023, 20.9% in 2024 and 31.5% in 2025, and at the end of 2025 total equity was KRW 173.9 billion, of which equity attributable to owners of the parent was KRW 150.3 billion, with total liabilities of KRW 54.8 billion.

Operating cash flow was -KRW 8.81 billion in 2022, KRW 3.33 billion in 2023, KRW 1.29 billion in 2024 and -KRW 8.18 billion in 2025.

On a quarterly basis, revenue was KRW 16.1 billion in Q3 2025 with an operating loss of KRW 5.38 billion, then turned to an operating profit of KRW 4.09 billion in Q4 2025 on revenue of KRW 22.8 billion.

Revenue fell 42.7% quarter on quarter to KRW 13 billion in Q1 2026, with an operating loss of KRW 3.24 billion, and the operating loss widened to KRW 4.73 billion in Q2 2026 on revenue of KRW 12.6 billion.

Q3 2026 revenue was KRW 20.6 billion, up 63.7% quarter on quarter and 28.0% year on year, and the operating loss narrowed to KRW 4.11 billion.

However, the attributable net loss of KRW 6.37 billion in Q3 2026 was larger than the KRW 5.48 billion loss in Q3 2025, so the operating and attributable results moved in different directions.

Per the 2026 half-year report, first-half consolidated revenue of KRW 25.6 billion was down 16.6% year on year, and press coverage attributed the decline to lower public vaccine distribution volumes after a shift in COVID-19 vaccine mix.

05

Industry analysis

Neurotrophic keratitis is a rare eye disease in which damage to the corneal nerves reduces corneal sensation and the cornea's natural healing ability. The group cites Oxervate as the only competing product for this indication and points to FDA orphan drug designation as part of its development rationale.

Commentary also notes that the market is not a blockbuster-scale opportunity.

In first-line liver cancer treatment, the rivoceranib and camrelizumab combination recorded median overall survival of 23.8 months in the global Phase 3 CARES-310 trial, versus 15.2 months for the sorafenib control arm, with a hazard ratio of 0.64.

Before FDA approval, the combination was included as a first-line option for advanced hepatocellular carcinoma in the 2025 BCLC guideline and the ESMO guideline.

FDA review, however, turns on manufacturing and quality requirements as well as clinical efficacy, and the July 2026 complete response letter cited cGMP inspection findings at a Chinese manufacturing facility.

The cold chain market depends on the national immunization program and public procurement, so changes in COVID-19 vaccine mix quickly flow through to distribution volumes and revenue.

Market commentary says the company's value currently reflects mainly its existing cold chain business, with the neurotrophic keratitis pipeline given limited weight. HLB Group, for its part, has said it spreads risk by keeping a separate R&D subsidiary for each therapy under its listed affiliates.

06

Outlook

The nearest checkpoint is the topline result of the RGN-259 global Phase 3 trial (SEER-2). The company said the last patient's final visit was completed in August 2026 and that topline results are planned for release in November 2026.

The rivoceranib and camrelizumab combination was resubmitted on October 9, 2026, and FDA classified it as a Class 2 resubmission with a review target date of April 9, 2027. HLB Group chairman Yang-gon Jin has said a decision could come as early as this year, but the FDA's official target date is April 9, 2027.

The company said it plans to file for RGN-259 approval in Europe after the United States. If approved, the company said it could work with a global pharmaceutical partner and use that partner's local sales organization.

The company had earlier said it expected cold chain revenue to grow 35% a year through 2026, but first-half 2026 revenue fell year on year as public vaccine volumes declined. Public vaccine procurement schedules are therefore a key variable for near-term cold chain revenue.

The spin-off of the non-bio electronics unit was disclosed in July 2026, and how the two-pillar structure appears in reported results will become visible in later filings. The next regular report is the 2026 annual report, which will confirm full-year operating loss and cash flow.

The company cites room temperature storage and a four-week, five-times-daily regimen as advantages over Oxervate. The combination was included as a first-line option in the 2025 BCLC and ESMO guidelines. Acceptance of the October 2026 resubmission as a Class 2 application restarted the full FDA review.

Cold chain base and a recent revenue rebound

The cold chain business rests on experience distributing vaccines for the national immunization program. The company obtained an international standard certification for its cold chain quality and environmental management system in July 2026. Bear factors

Approval delays linked to manufacturing quality issues

Press coverage described the current bid as the fourth U.S. attempt for the liver cancer drug. With the review target set for April 9, 2027, final approval status will not be known before then.

Lower public vaccine volumes and possible dilution from convertible securities

Lower public vaccine distribution volumes in the first half of 2026 reduced consolidated revenue to KRW 25.6 billion, down 16.6% year on year. Operating cash flow was -KRW 8.18 billion in 2025, despite total equity of KRW 173.9 billion at year-end.

Recent conversion price adjustments and corrections to convertible bond disclosures leave the possibility of dilution for existing shareholders.

10

Risk factors

Clinical and regulatory risk

If the main RGN-259 Phase 3 results fall short of expectations, the way the pipeline is valued could change. The rivoceranib and camrelizumab combination must meet manufacturing and quality requirements alongside clinical efficacy, and the decision could slip past the target date. Even with approval, separate procedures for other regions such as Europe remain.

Financial and dilution risk

Operating and attributable net losses have persisted across several quarters, so the need for external funding remains. Continued conversion price adjustments mean existing shareholders could see dilution if the bonds are converted. The debt-to-equity ratio rose to 31.5% in 2025 from 17.1% in 2023.

Business structure and group dependence risk

Cold chain revenue moves sharply with public vaccine procurement schedules and volumes. The spin-off of the non-bio unit changes the business structure, so the post-split reporting mix will need to be watched.

Delays in a parent company's drug approval have previously pushed down shares of related listed companies in the group, so group-wide events can affect the stock.
