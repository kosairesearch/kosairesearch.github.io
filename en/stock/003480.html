02

Business structure

The company is a holding company that owns stakes in subsidiaries and controls their management. After selling Hanjin Heavy Industries, the former core of the group that is now renamed HJ Heavy Industries, it reorganized its portfolio around energy and leisure.

Its main subsidiaries run city gas supply, golf course operations and power generation, all tied to everyday demand. Revenue at the holding company level comes mainly from trademark royalties paid by subsidiaries and rental income from owned property.

A April 2026 press report described this model as relatively flexible against economic cycles. Among city gas operations, Daesung Energy and GS E&R are named as subsidiaries, and city gas sales were reported to have increased.

The golf course unit saw fewer visitors due to abnormal weather, while the power and electricity unit improved. Fuel cell power generation is being expanded with a target of commercial operation in 2026. Consolidated revenue includes subsidiary results, so it moves sharply from quarter to quarter.

Revenue rose from KRW 160.9 billion in the third quarter of 2025 to KRW 484 billion in the first quarter of 2026, then fell to KRW 180.6 billion in the third quarter of 2026.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders reflects both operating profit and non-operating items, so revenue trends alone do not explain it.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual revenue fell from KRW 1.44 trillion in 2022 to KRW 1.24 trillion in 2023, a decline of 13.7% year on year, and then to KRW 1.16 trillion in 2024, down 6.3%. It recovered slightly to KRW 1.2 trillion in 2025, up 3.2%. Among 2022 to 2025, 2022 was the largest revenue year and 2024 the smallest.

Operating profit moved the other way, rising from KRW 37.1 billion in 2022 to KRW 39.4 billion in 2023, KRW 45.9 billion in 2024 and KRW 73.3 billion in 2025, an increase in each year from 2023 to 2025. The 2025 operating profit rose 59.7% year on year, far outpacing the 3.2% revenue growth.

The operating margin rose from 2.6% in 2022 to 3.2% in 2023, 3.9% in 2024 and 6.1% in 2025. Margins improved in 2023 and 2024 even as revenue declined, which is the main feature of this recovery.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders fell from KRW 14.7 billion in 2022 to KRW 7.57 billion in 2023, a decline of 48.5%, then rose to KRW 56.5 billion in 2024 and KRW 59.1 billion in 2025, two consecutive years of growth.

Net income including non-controlling interests was KRW 60.8 billion in 2024 and KRW 62.6 billion in 2025. In 2024 net income exceeded operating profit of KRW 45.9 billion, meaning non-operating items and taxes added to earnings beyond operating profit.

The debt-to-equity ratio fell from 444.2% in 2022 to 278.7% in 2025. At the end of 2025 total liabilities were KRW 1.34 trillion and total equity was KRW 479.8 billion, of which equity attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 444.7 billion.

Operating cash flow dropped to KRW 69.2 billion in 2023 before reaching KRW 124 billion in 2025, the largest of the four years. Quarterly results have been volatile.

Revenue reached KRW 346.4 billion in the fourth quarter of 2025, up 115.2% from the prior quarter, and KRW 484 billion in the first quarter of 2026, up 39.8%.

It then fell to KRW 205.7 billion in the second quarter of 2026, down 57.5%, and KRW 180.6 billion in the third quarter, down 12.2%, marking two consecutive quarterly declines.

Operating profit fell from KRW 49.6 billion in the first quarter of 2026 to KRW 3.08 billion in the second quarter, down 93.8%, and recovered to KRW 9.53 billion in the third quarter, up 209.3% from the prior quarter but down 8.4% year on year.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders over the last four quarters, from the fourth quarter of 2025 to the third quarter of 2026, totals KRW 63.8 billion, and the first quarter of 2026 alone contributed KRW 33.3 billion, more than half of that.

Third-quarter 2026 net income attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 2.34 billion, down 58.1% year on year.

Industry analysis

The subsidiary businesses span gas utilities, golf courses and power generation, and each faces different industry drivers. According to an April 2026 press report, the gas utility sector saw international gas prices stop their surge and settle into a stable range, easing cost pressure.

The same report cited the possibility of government utility tariff adjustments and expectations of higher gas power demand from rising summer electricity use as favorable factors for the sector.

Because the report dates from April 2026, the direction of gas prices and tariff policy after that date remains a separate variable. The golf course unit saw fewer visitors due to abnormal weather, while the power and electricity unit improved.

Golf course visitors are sensitive to weather and seasonal factors, which makes them a source of volatility in annual results. Fuel cell power generation is a growth axis in renewable energy, with a target of commercial operation in 2026.

Other listed holding companies in the same sector include SK Square, GS Holdings, LS, Lotte and Hyosung. Compared with these holding companies, the company's distinguishing feature is that its subsidiaries sit in energy and leisure rather than manufacturing.

Because the shipbuilding business of the former Hanjin Heavy Industries was sold, the company is not directly exposed to shipbuilding order cycles, which is another point of difference.

Bull factors

Medium-term gains in operating profit and margin

Operating profit rose from KRW 37.1 billion in 2022 to KRW 73.3 billion in 2025, increasing for three consecutive years from 2023. The operating margin climbed from 2.6% to 6.1%, improving profitability even in years when revenue declined. The annual recovery in earnings capacity is the clearest bullish point.

Lower leverage and a stronger equity base

The debt-to-equity ratio fell from 444.2% in 2022 to 278.7% in 2025. Total equity at the end of 2025 was KRW 479.8 billion, of which KRW 444.7 billion was attributable to controlling shareholders. Operating cash flow recovered from KRW 69.2 billion in 2023 to KRW 124 billion in 2025, the largest of the four years.

Holding-company income model and renewable expansion

At the holding-company level, the main income sources are trademark royalties from subsidiaries and rental income from owned property. Among subsidiaries, city gas sales increased and the power and electricity unit improved.

Fuel cell generation is expanding with a 2026 commercial operation target, adding renewable assets to everyday demand-driven businesses.

Bear factors

Sharp swings in quarterly profit

Operating profit in the second quarter of 2026 was KRW 3.08 billion, down 93.8% from the prior quarter. Third-quarter 2026 operating profit of KRW 9.53 billion was down 8.4% year on year.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders fell 58.1% year on year to KRW 2.34 billion in the third quarter, and dropped to KRW 390 million in the second quarter.

Slowing revenue momentum

Annual revenue fell 13.7% in 2023 and 6.3% in 2024 before rising only 3.2% in 2025. Quarterly revenue declined for two consecutive quarters in the second and third quarters of 2026. Third-quarter 2026 revenue of KRW 180.6 billion was up 12.2% year on year but well below the first-quarter 2026 peak of KRW 484 billion.

Earnings tied to weather and policy variables

The golf course unit saw fewer visitors due to abnormal weather. Gas subsidiaries are affected by international gas prices and tariff policy. Net income attributable to controlling shareholders fell 48.5% in 2023 to a low point, showing that earnings can swing widely with external variables.

10

Risk factors

Holding-company income dependence

Holding-company income depends on trademark royalties and property rental income received from subsidiaries. If subsidiary profits weaken and reduce their capacity to pay royalties, or rental terms change, holding-level revenue and cash flow could decline together.

If the city gas, golf course and power and electricity subsidiaries weaken at the same time, the cushioning effect of this structure would also be limited. Whether operating cash flow supports this structure is the item to monitor.

Balance sheet burden

Total liabilities at the end of 2025 were KRW 1.34 trillion, larger than total equity of KRW 479.8 billion. The debt-to-equity ratio of 278.7% is lower than 444.2% in 2022 but remains high. The maturity and interest structure of the debt is disclosed in the notes to the regular business report.

Fuel cell commercial operation timing

Fuel cell power generation is being expanded with a target of commercial operation in 2026, but if operations do not start by the target date, the earnings contribution from renewable assets could slip into the following year.

A delay in commercial operation would also slow the reflection of the improving power and electricity unit in earnings. As the business expands, a delay in operation could lengthen the payback period of investment.

Whether the plants actually operate after the target date is the practical checkpoint for the renewable growth axis.

Overall view

Hanjin Heavy Ind. & Const. Holdings is a holding company that depends on subsidiary earnings and trademark and rental income. Operating profit rose for three consecutive years from 2023 to 2025, and the operating margin climbed from 2.6% to 6.1%.

The debt-to-equity ratio fell from 444.2% to 278.7%, so the capital structure is improving. On the other hand, revenue declined for two consecutive quarters in the second and third quarters of 2026, and second-quarter operating profit fell to KRW 3.08 billion.

Third-quarter net income attributable to controlling shareholders fell 58.1% year on year. More than half of the KRW 63.8 billion four-quarter total came from the first quarter of 2026, so earnings are concentrated.

Bullish factors are the recovery in earnings capacity, lower leverage and renewable expansion including fuel cells. Bearish factors are sharp quarterly swings, the KRW 1.34 trillion liability burden, and external variables such as weather and gas policy.

The price-to-book ratio sits well below 1 times, but the multiple is sensitive to quarterly profit swings and is best read with the annual trend. The next checkpoints are fourth-quarter 2026 operating profit, preliminary disclosure in early 2027 and the annual business report due at the end of March 2027.
