Hightech Pivot: Quarterly Profit vs. Listing Hurdles

02

Business structure

Exion Group Company was founded in November 2000 and has operated as a general engineering firm handling cleanroom and piping plant construction for semiconductor and display manufacturing facilities alongside an e-commerce platform business.

In August 2024 the company changed its name from IS E-Commerce Co., Ltd. to Exion Group Company, formally accelerating a business restructuring. In August 2025 it absorbed EPC (engineering, procurement, and construction) firm MJ Tech, transforming into a structure combining construction, manufacturing, and technology.

The business now consists of a hightech engineering segment handling piping, ducts, and mechanical equipment construction for semiconductor and display production facilities, a robot automation segment that installs industrial robots, and a legacy commerce infrastructure segment built on its e-commerce base.

Its core client is Samsung Heavy Industries, for which Exion Group serves as a first-tier subcontractor, building a track record through participation in large semiconductor fab construction at Samsung Electronics' Pyeongtaek campus P3 and P4 lines.

In October 2026 the company signed a strategic cooperation agreement with semiconductor test equipment maker Semics, outlining plans to expand into materials technology and functional coatings for semiconductor inspection equipment.

The competitive landscape ranges from large integrated engineering and plant contractors to small and mid-sized specialty construction firms, and Exion Group is at a stage of expanding its footprint by leveraging its semiconductor construction track record. The legacy e-commerce business, which carries low profitability, is being scaled down.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual revenue fell 15.1% from KRW 5.48 billion in 2022 to KRW 4.65 billion in 2023, then rose for two consecutive years to KRW 4.82 billion in 2024 (up 3.8%) and KRW 24 billion in 2025 (up 397.5%), marking the largest year in the 2022-2025 span.

Over the same period, operating losses were KRW 12.1 billion in 2022, KRW 11.6 billion in 2023, KRW 10.5 billion in 2024, and KRW 5.91 billion in 2025, a fourth straight year of losses even as the loss size steadily narrowed.

Net losses attributable to controlling shareholders ran from KRW 11.1 billion in 2022 to KRW 15.3 billion in 2023, KRW 16.9 billion in 2024, and KRW 22.3 billion in 2025, a fourth consecutive year of losses in which the loss size actually widened.

Separately, net loss including non-controlling interests came to KRW 17.9 billion in 2024 and KRW 22.4 billion in 2025, figures that differ from the controlling-shareholder net losses of KRW 16.9 billion and KRW 22.3 billion in those same years.

On a quarterly basis, revenue jumped from KRW 821 million with an operating loss of KRW 1.42 billion in Q2 2025 to KRW 16.1 billion in revenue with a KRW 3.67 billion operating loss in Q4 2025, followed by Q1 2026 revenue of KRW 21.1 billion (up 31.3% quarter over quarter) with an operating loss of KRW 479 million (narrower loss), and then Q2 2026 revenue of KRW 23.9 billion (up 13.0% quarter over quarter and 2,807.8% year over year) with operating profit of KRW 765 million, turning positive both quarter over quarter and year over year — the second straight quarter of revenue growth since the Q4 2025 trough, now paired with a profit turnaround.

However, net loss attributable to controlling shareholders widened quarter over quarter from KRW 205 million in Q1 2026 to KRW 588 million in Q2 2026, even though that figure represented a narrower loss compared with the year-earlier quarter's KRW 3.6 billion loss.

The sum of controlling-shareholder net losses across the four quarters of 2025 was KRW 22.3 billion, matching the full-year figure.

Separately, for Q3 2025 — a quarter not covered in the periodic-report disclosure window — preliminary figures announced on November 7, 2025 showed revenue of KRW 6.0 billion and operating profit of KRW 1.1 billion, a year-over-year turnaround, though this was not a confirmed disclosure figure.

Cumulative first-half 2026 results were reported at KRW 44.99 billion in revenue and KRW 290 million in operating profit, continuing the profitable trend, though one outlet noted that part of this revenue increase reflected a change in the scope of consolidation.

05

Industry analysis

The hightech engineering field in which Exion Group operates is directly tied to the capital expenditure cycles of major domestic manufacturers in semiconductors, displays, and shipbuilding.

Demand for piping, duct, and mechanical equipment construction rises as semiconductor companies expand new fab construction, production line additions, and leading-edge process investment.

Some analyses suggest that if demand growth in AI data centers and high-bandwidth memory (HBM) leads domestic and overseas semiconductor firms to expand facility investment, order intake and earnings growth for related construction could accelerate further.

Exion Group's core client, Samsung Heavy Industries, is pursuing AI infrastructure-related new business separate from its core shipbuilding and offshore plant operations, having set a target of completing commercialization of its floating data center (FDC) by the second quarter of 2028, which raises the possibility of expanded order opportunities across its subcontractor ecosystem.

However, the field is a competitive market spanning large integrated engineering and plant contractors to small and mid-sized specialty firms, leaving smaller players with high dependence on specific clients exposed to order volatility.

Exion Group is at a stage of expanding from its semiconductor facility construction track record into robot automation and semiconductor materials, attempting to differentiate itself from existing competitors.

06

Outlook

At the time it pursued its business restructuring, the company set a goal of achieving annual revenue of over KRW 20 billion initially and building a revenue base of more than KRW 50 billion in 2026.

In fact, 2025 revenue of KRW 24 billion exceeded that target, and growth has continued into 2026 with quarterly revenue of KRW 21.1 billion in Q1 and KRW 23.9 billion in Q2.

New contract additions in Q1 2026 totaled KRW 32.359 billion, with a contract backlog of KRW 42.812 billion as of the end of March, suggesting an order base that should feed into future revenue.

On the order front, the company won a duct and piping construction contract worth KRW 40.6 billion from Samsung Heavy Industries in October 2025, and an additional facility and piping construction contract worth KRW 31.4 billion from the same client in February 2026, running through June 2027.

In October 2026 it signed a cooperation agreement with semiconductor test equipment maker Semics for materials and coating technology, outlining plans to expand into materials and components for semiconductor equipment.

One outlet, in a report dated late July 2026, projected that if the company achieves annual revenue in the KRW 80 billion range along with an operating profit turnaround, the results of its business restructuring could be seen as extending beyond top-line growth into profitability improvement.

The company has stated it will focus on achieving a full-year operating profit through stable order intake and tight cost management in the second half.

08

Bull factors

Revenue Growth from the Hightech Pivot

By scaling down its low-profit e-commerce business and reorganizing around piping and duct construction for semiconductor and display production facilities, Exion Group grew revenue 397.5% from KRW 4.82 billion in 2024 to KRW 24 billion in 2025.

Growth continued into 2026 with KRW 21.1 billion in Q1 and KRW 23.9 billion in Q2, marking two consecutive quarters of revenue growth since the Q4 2025 trough. Its track record building semiconductor fabs as a first-tier subcontractor to Samsung Heavy Industries underpins its new order intake.

Quarterly Operating Profit Turnaround

Q2 2026 operating profit reached KRW 765 million, turning positive both from the prior quarter's operating loss of KRW 479 million and the year-earlier quarter's operating loss of KRW 1.42 billion.

Cumulative first-half operating profit was also reported as positive, and the company has stated it intends to maintain profitability in the second half to achieve a full-year operating profit.

However, net losses attributable to controlling shareholders persist, meaning the profit turnaround has not yet fully carried through to the controlling-shareholder bottom line.

New Business Diversification and Order Expansion

Exion Group won equipment and piping construction contracts from Samsung Heavy Industries worth KRW 40.6 billion in October 2025 and KRW 31.4 billion in February 2026.

In October 2026 it signed a cooperation agreement with semiconductor test equipment maker Semics for materials and coating technology, outlining plans to expand into materials and components for semiconductor equipment.

It is also pursuing expansion into robot automation and new infrastructure areas such as AI data centers and renewable energy.

Bear factors

Continued Net Losses Attributable to Controlling Shareholders

Net losses attributable to controlling shareholders widened for four straight years, from KRW 11.1 billion in 2022 to KRW 22.3 billion in 2025. Losses continued into 2026 with KRW 205 million in Q1 and KRW 588 million in Q2, with the Q2 loss actually widening from the prior quarter.

Even though operating profit turned positive on a quarterly basis, a turnaround has not yet been confirmed at the controlling-shareholder net profit level.

Frequent Rights Issues and Governance Changes

In 2026, third-party rights issues of KRW 6 billion and KRW 8 billion, along with two general public offerings, raised roughly KRW 13.3 billion in just two months.

In the process, the largest shareholder changed to New Pacific Investment Partnership, and a previously decided rights issue was withdrawn, leading to a designation as a company with unfaithful disclosure.

Frequent capital changes and the shift in controlling shareholder remain a challenge for rebuilding market confidence in management stability.

Listing-Maintenance Requirement Risk

Exion Group was designated an administrative-issue stock on June 26, 2026 due to insufficient market capitalization, and must meet the market-cap requirement for 10 consecutive and 30 cumulative trading days within 90 trading days of designation to be released from that status.

Earlier, in March 2026, it was also designated an investor-alert stock due to a non-standard internal accounting control opinion and five consecutive fiscal years of operating losses.

Starting in January 2027, the KOSDAQ market-cap maintenance threshold is set to rise further to KRW 30 billion, making continued monitoring of compliance necessary.

Risk factors

Listing-Maintenance and Exchange Regulatory Risk

The overlap between an administrative-issue designation due to market-cap deficiency and an investor-alert designation means listing-eligibility uncertainty could persist until all grounds are resolved.

When the KOSDAQ market-cap maintenance threshold rises to KRW 30 billion in January 2027, the possibility of falling short again at current levels needs to be checked. It is also worth noting that cumulative non-compliance could lead to delisting grounds.

Financial Structure and Dilution Risk

A series of third-party and general public rights issues throughout 2026 sharply increased the share count, diluting existing shareholders' stakes. Repeated convertible bond issuances and conversion exercises leave further potential for additional share increases.

The debt-to-equity ratio of 103.8% at the end of 2025, sharply up from 29.2% a year earlier, is another burden on the financial structure.

Client Concentration and Order Volatility Risk

Much of Exion Group's revenue expansion depends on large contracts with a single client, Samsung Heavy Industries. Changes in the semiconductor or shipbuilding business cycle, or in a client's facility investment plans, could increase order and revenue volatility.

New businesses in semiconductor materials and robot automation remain at an early stage, with the timing and scale of their revenue contribution still uncertain.

Overall view

Exion Group Company restructured its once e-commerce-centered business toward semiconductor and hightech facility construction, lifting 2025 revenue 397.5% year over year to KRW 24 billion and posting an operating profit of KRW 765 million in Q2 2026, a turnaround.

However, net losses attributable to controlling shareholders persisted for four consecutive years from 2022 through 2025, widening to KRW 22.3 billion in 2025, and continued into Q1-Q2 2026.

Throughout 2026 the company has raised capital through multiple rights issues, changed its largest shareholder, and worked to resolve the grounds for its administrative-issue and investor-alert stock designations.

Business diversification continues as well, including semiconductor facility construction orders built on its first-tier subcontractor status with Samsung Heavy Industries and a materials cooperation agreement with Semics.

At the same time, the market-cap requirement, dilution from frequent capital changes, and revenue dependence on a specific client are variables that warrant continued attention.

Going forward, preliminary results from Q3 2026 onward, whether the administrative-issue designation is lifted, and confirmation of a full-year profit through the fiscal year 2026 periodic report are likely to form the basis for the next assessment.
