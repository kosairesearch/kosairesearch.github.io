Quarterly profit after the pivot, cumulative losses remain the task

02

Business structure

Exion Group Company is an engineering firm founded in 2000 that performs cleanroom and piping plant construction for semiconductor and display manufacturing lines, and it also operated an e-commerce platform.

Losses in the e-commerce business accumulated, with an operating loss of KRW 10.5 billion in 2024, and after a new largest shareholder took over in June 2024 the company pursued restructuring and M&A.

In August 2025 it absorbed an EPC (engineering, procurement and construction) contractor and entered construction contracting, while tidying up the cost structure of its low-margin commerce business.

The core of the business is now high-tech infrastructure work: as a tier-one subcontractor to a major Korean shipbuilder, it takes part in piping, duct and mechanical equipment work at large semiconductor production facilities, including Samsung Electronics Pyeongtaek Campus P3 and P4.

The company credited the expansion of high-tech sales for its return to operating profit in the first half of 2026. According to company statements, the order backlog was about KRW 42.8 billion as reported in July 2026.

Customer demand is tied to construction at Samsung-affiliated semiconductor plants, so project timing feeds directly into revenue. In competition it bids for work against large plant builders and other tier-one subcontractors.

In October 2026 it signed a strategic agreement with a semiconductor test equipment company to extend into advanced materials for chip equipment. Although it is classified in the consumer goods and distribution sector, its business structure differs from Coway or E-Mart, so direct comparison is difficult.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual revenue fell from KRW 5.48 billion in 2022 to KRW 4.65 billion in 2023, a 15.1% decline, then rose 3.8% to KRW 4.82 billion in 2024 and 397.5% to KRW 24 billion in 2025, which is two consecutive years of growth. 2025 is the largest revenue year in the 2022 to 2025 span.

The company attributes the increase to restructuring after the absorption merger with an EPC contractor. Operating losses were KRW 12.1 billion in 2022, KRW 11.6 billion in 2023, KRW 10.5 billion in 2024 and KRW 5.91 billion in 2025, a loss every year, with the loss narrowing each year.

The operating margin improved from -220.7% in 2022 to -24.6% in 2025. Net loss attributable to controlling shareholders widened from KRW 11.1 billion in 2022 to KRW 15.3 billion in 2023, KRW 16.9 billion in 2024 and KRW 22.3 billion in 2025.

The operating loss shrank while the net loss grew, which suggests non-operating costs such as interest and other expenses weighed heavily. The net loss including non-controlling interests was KRW 22.4 billion in 2025, close to the controlling-shareholder figure.

The debt ratio rose from 8.2% in 2022 and 8.0% in 2023 to 29.2% in 2024 and 103.8% in 2025, when total equity was KRW 38 billion and total liabilities were KRW 39.5 billion.

Operating cash flow was -KRW 18.5 billion in 2022, KRW 485 million in 2023, -KRW 9.58 billion in 2024 and -KRW 9.11 billion in 2025, so outflows continued apart from 2023.

By quarter, Q4 2025 showed revenue of KRW 16.1 billion, an operating loss of KRW 3.67 billion and a net loss attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 18.6 billion, the largest loss among recent quarters; the net loss far exceeded the operating loss, pointing to non-operating items.

Revenue in Q1 2026 was KRW 21.1 billion, up 31.3% quarter on quarter, Q2 was KRW 23.9 billion, up 13.0%, and Q3 was KRW 14.6 billion, down 38.7%. Even as Q3 revenue declined, operating profit rose to KRW 2.66 billion, up 247.6% from the prior quarter.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders turned positive at KRW 199 million in Q3 2026, after losses of KRW 205 million in Q1 and KRW 588 million in Q2.

According to company statements dated November 16, 2026, cumulative revenue for January to September 2026 was KRW 59.6 billion and cumulative operating profit was KRW 2.9 billion, and the company described the third quarter as its best result since its founding.

05

Industry analysis

The market Exion Group Company serves is equipment construction at semiconductor production facilities. Capacity expansion and capex schedules at chipmakers determine the volume of construction work.

In July 2026 the company said that continued expansion of semiconductor production investment and additional orders would make its earnings improvement visible.

Because equipment construction is booked project by project, quarterly revenue is volatile; Q3 2026 revenue fell 38.7% from the prior quarter even as operating profit rose.

Where the sector stands in the capex cycle cannot be settled from filings and news alone, so expansion plans announced by large chipmakers serve as the reference for order flow. Against large construction and plant companies, Exion is a smaller EPC contractor, so a few projects weigh heavily on results.

The company presented AI data centers and renewable energy infrastructure as new areas but has not given sales figures for them.

Funding conditions also matter: the Financial Services Commission raised the small public offering limit from KRW 1 billion to KRW 3 billion starting July 28, 2026, which was presented as a measure to ease funding burdens for small and mid-sized companies.

06

Outlook

When it announced its 2025 restructuring, the company said it would secure a revenue base of more than KRW 50 billion in 2026. Cumulative revenue for January to September 2026 was KRW 59.6 billion according to company statements, above that target level.

A company official said in August 2026 that the company would focus on delivering an annual operating profit, supported by steady orders and cost control in the second half. The order backlog was KRW 42.8 billion as reported in July 2026.

On the funding side, payment of KRW 6.4 billion from a third-party share issuance was completed in September 2026, and an additional KRW 8 billion issuance was under way as of July 2026 reporting.

The company plans to sell investment properties in Seoul Forest, Hanam, Incheon and Dangjin to repay borrowings and deploy the proceeds into its core and new growth businesses.

It is studying advanced materials for semiconductor equipment after signing an agreement with a semiconductor test equipment company, and it has also set out expansion into AI data center and renewable energy infrastructure. It said it would improve internal accounting controls and management transparency in parallel.

The next periodic report is the 2026 annual report; until then, quarterly filings and announcements on asset sales and share issuance will indicate direction.

If an annual operating profit is achieved, the possibility of resolving the five-year consecutive operating loss criterion cited for exchange designation would increase.

08

Bull factors

Quarterly earnings improved after the pivot

Operating results moved from a KRW 479 million loss in Q1 2026 to a KRW 765 million profit in Q2, and Q3 operating profit reached KRW 2.66 billion, up 247.6% from the prior quarter. Quarterly revenue swung across KRW 21.1 billion, KRW 23.9 billion and KRW 14.6 billion, yet operating profit improved over the period.

Bulls cite the appearance of quarterly profit after revenue expanded to KRW 24 billion in 2025 as evidence the pivot is working.

Order base and contract assets expanded

According to company statements, contract assets rose to about KRW 14.2 billion at the end of June 2026 from about KRW 5.1 billion at the end of 2025. Contract assets are progress billings on construction not yet invoiced to the client, and the company attributes the rise to a larger construction portfolio.

The order backlog was KRW 42.8 billion as reported in July 2026. Its work on Samsung Electronics Pyeongtaek Campus as a tier-one subcontractor is presented as the basis for further orders.

Balance sheet repair under way

According to company statements, the debt ratio fell to 71.5% at the end of September 2026 from 103.8% at the end of 2025, and equity rose to KRW 52.1 billion.

Payments from rights and small public offerings bolstered liquidity, with cash and equivalents rising from about KRW 1.1 billion at the end of 2025 to about KRW 13.6 billion at the end of September 2026. Planned sales of investment property to repay borrowings could ease the financial burden.

09

Bear factors

Accumulated losses and widening net losses

Net loss attributable to controlling shareholders widened from KRW 11.1 billion in 2022 to KRW 15.3 billion in 2023, KRW 16.9 billion in 2024 and KRW 22.3 billion in 2025, a larger loss in each of the last three years. The Q4 2025 loss of KRW 18.6 billion was the largest among recent quarters.

The last four quarters total KRW 19.2 billion in net losses, and the Q3 2026 return to profit is so far a single quarter.

Concentration in a few projects and revenue volatility

Revenue is tied to construction at Samsung-affiliated semiconductor plants, so results depend on a few clients and project schedules. Q3 2026 revenue of KRW 14.6 billion fell 38.7% from the prior quarter.

Large quarterly swings make it uncertain how long operating profit can be sustained, which is the main bear argument. A slowdown in chip equipment investment could change the order flow.

Frequent fundraising and governance-related corrections

Share issuance through rights offerings and small public offerings came in succession in 2026, and an additional KRW 8 billion issuance was under way.

The change-of-control agreement, the tangible asset acquisition and the funding terms were each corrected several times, and the 2026 half-year report was also corrected. A larger share count can dilute the value held by existing shareholders.

10

Risk factors

New business execution uncertainty

The company is studying advanced materials for semiconductor equipment and plans to expand into AI data center and renewable energy infrastructure. The October 2026 agreement with a semiconductor test equipment company is a starting point before commercialization.

No sales scale has been given for these new areas, so performance is hard to gauge. If the new businesses require capabilities different from existing construction work, cost burdens could rise.

Liquidity and capital raising

Total liabilities were KRW 39.5 billion at the end of 2025, with a debt ratio of 103.8%. Company statements put the ratio at 71.5% at the end of September 2026, but if the planned sales of investment property do not proceed on schedule, repayment of borrowings could be delayed. Payment of new share capital and listing timing are directly linked to dilution.

Delayed collection of construction receivables

Contract assets rose to about KRW 14.2 billion at the end of June 2026 from about KRW 5.1 billion at the end of 2025. Because contract assets are progress work not yet billed to the client, delays in billing and collection could push cash inflows later than reported profit.

Given that operating cash flow was negative in 2025, such a delay could add to liquidity pressure. Whether the balance is collected will be verified with the 2026 annual report.

12

Overall view

Exion Group Company recorded operating losses and net losses attributable to controlling shareholders from 2022 to 2025, but revenue rose to KRW 24 billion in 2025 and quarterly operating profit has appeared since Q2 2026, so the effects of the business pivot are beginning to show in the numbers.

According to company statements, cumulative revenue for January to September 2026 was KRW 59.6 billion. However, the net loss attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 22.3 billion in 2025, and the last four quarters total KRW 19.2 billion in net losses, so accumulated losses have not yet been reversed.

The Q3 2026 return to net profit of KRW 199 million is a single quarter. The debt ratio fell from 103.8% at the end of 2025 to 71.5% at the end of September 2026 according to company statements, while capital raising and asset sales continue.

Strengths include a growing revenue base and quarterly earnings improvement; weaknesses include cumulative losses, volatile quarterly revenue, frequent fundraising and governance-related corrections.

The next checkpoint is whether the 2026 annual report confirms an annual operating profit and the direction of net income attributable to controlling shareholders.
