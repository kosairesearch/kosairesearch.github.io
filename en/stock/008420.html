Business structure

Moonbae Steel was founded in 1973 as a steel distributor and processor, and according to its company profile it grew into a hot-rolled coil processor after setting up service centers in Gwangyang and Siheung in the 1990s.

It operates within POSCO's hot-rolled steel service center network and is designated as a POSCO hot-rolled product sales agent. Its product lineup covers hot-rolled coil, hot-rolled sheet, hot-rolled plate, skelp, patterned steel plate and plate.

Production sits at its Gwangyang and Siheung plants, which also house processing lines. According to a job-listing site's corporate data, as of March 2026 product sales made up 72% of revenue, trading sales 25%, rental income 2% and processing income 1%.

End uses include general structural, shipbuilding, LNG pipe and boiler applications. It is one of eight POSCO hot-rolled agents nationwide, with a market share of about 8.93% in 2024. Its main raw materials are coil and plate, and their prices follow changes in POSCO's selling prices.

A news report said a supply agreement for high-grade steel pipeline materials tied to POSCO International's Alaska LNG project had emerged, drawing attention to the stock as a related name.

As of March 25, 2026, the largest shareholder was Bae Seung-jun with 14.48%, followed by affiliate N.I Steel with 9.44% and POSCO with 4.98%.

Earnings analysis

Annual revenue fell from KRW 192.2 billion in 2022 to KRW 163.6 billion in 2023 (down 14.9% year on year), KRW 140.2 billion in 2024 (down 14.3%) and KRW 137.9 billion in 2025 (down 1.7%). Revenue declined for three consecutive years from 2023 to 2025, and 2022 was the largest year in the 2022 to 2025 range.

Operating profit of KRW 3.01 billion in 2022 turned into an operating loss of KRW 3.44 billion in 2023, followed by losses of KRW 2.13 billion in 2024 and KRW 959 million in 2025, so the loss narrowed. The operating margin moved from 1.6% in 2022 to -2.1% in 2023, -1.5% in 2024 and -0.7% in 2025.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders fell from KRW 26.2 billion in 2022 to KRW 18.6 billion in 2023 (down 29.2%), KRW 6.62 billion in 2024 (down 64.3%) and KRW 5.86 billion in 2025 (down 11.4%).

Profit stayed positive in years when operations posted losses, which points to non-operating items and taxes supporting the bottom line.

Operating cash flow went from KRW 11.2 billion in 2022 to -KRW 1.47 billion in 2023, KRW 4.59 billion in 2024 and -KRW 11.9 billion in 2025, and the 2025 outflow was far larger than the operating loss.

Quarterly revenue rose from KRW 35.4 billion in the third quarter of 2025 to KRW 37.3 billion in the fourth quarter (up 5.4% quarter on quarter), KRW 38.4 billion in the first quarter of 2026 (up 3.0%) and KRW 40.6 billion in the second quarter (up 5.6%), before falling to KRW 34.8 billion in the third quarter of 2026 (down 14.2% quarter on quarter and down 1.7% year on year).

Quarterly operating results swung from a KRW 389 million loss in the fourth quarter of 2025 to a KRW 171 million profit in the first quarter of 2026, a KRW 2.04 billion profit in the second quarter (up 1,092.6% quarter on quarter) and a KRW 519 million profit in the third quarter.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders reached KRW 3.56 billion in the second quarter of 2026, the largest of the last five quarters, and totaled KRW 8.29 billion over the last four quarters.

In the second quarter of 2026, that net income exceeded operating profit of KRW 2.04 billion, suggesting non-operating items carried a meaningful share of profit.

05

Industry analysis

Steel demand is sensitive to the business cycles of downstream industries such as automotive, construction and shipbuilding. Low-priced Chinese imports and oversupply have intensified competition, according to industry assessments.

Exports and imports move with economic cycles, and competition from later-stage producers such as China and India is also cited. Moonbae Steel is classified as a secondary processor in the production chain, a different process position from converter or electric-arc furnace steelmakers that use iron ore or scrap.

As a result, raw material prices and supply depend on policies of upstream steelmakers. Changes in POSCO's selling prices also directly affect the company's own pricing.

The company says it is strengthening competitiveness through productivity gains from factory automation and direct-delivery service across a nationwide logistics network. Being one of eight POSCO hot-rolled agents with a share of about 8.93% shows its position within POSCO's distribution channel.

POSCO and other service-center operators are cited as competitors. Based on disclosed data, third-quarter 2026 revenue fell 14.2% quarter on quarter, showing that softer conditions appeared in sales.

Overall view
