Report overview

HMM: Freight Upturn Meets Bulk Expansion in Q3

HMM's Q3 2026 operating profit of KRW 1.39 trillion reflected both higher container freight rates and faster growth in its bulk business, which is gradually reducing the company's reliance on container shipping.

  1. 1

    Q3 2026 operating profit of KRW 1.39 trillion rose 293.8% from the prior quarter and 369.8% from a year earlier.

  2. 2

    The Q3 operating margin of about 33.8% exceeded the figures the company cited for Maersk's ocean business (20.6%), ONE (21.6%) and COSCO (28.7%).

  3. 3

    Bulk operating profit for the first three quarters reached KRW 378.4 billion, up 418.5% year on year, and bulk's share of total operating profit rose from 6.4% to 18.8%.

  4. 4

    The company expects container freight rates to slow their rise in Q4 and to move sideways with route-by-route volatility.

  5. 5

    A KRW 29.1 trillion investment plan through 2030 was announced alongside a shareholder return policy with a payout ratio of 30% or more.

02

Business structure

HMM is centered on container shipping, and its business is divided into a container segment and a bulk segment.

In Q3 2026 the container segment posted revenue of KRW 3.6 trillion and operating profit of KRW 1.25 trillion, while bulk operating profit in the same quarter was KRW 139.0 billion, according to company figures.

The container segment runs a hub-and-spoke model, deploying large vessels at key hub ports and linking surrounding ports with smaller ships. Container volume for the first three quarters of 2026 reached 3,134,000 TEU, up 7.0% year on year.

The bulk fleet is being broadened from dry bulk carriers and tankers into car carriers, gas carriers and multipurpose vessels. At the end of Q3 2026 the bulk fleet numbered 60 vessels, or 82 including 22 ships awaiting delivery after ordering.

Long-term transport contracts with Brazilian miner Vale, signed twice in 2025 and once in 2026, total KRW 5.7 trillion. The growing share of bulk in operating profit reflects a shift from short-term charter-based operations toward long-term contract-based operations.

On competition, the company compared its Q3 operating margin with those of major global container carriers. Domestic bulk shipping peers include Pan Ocean, Korea Line and Heung-A Shipping.

04

Earnings analysis

On a disclosed annual basis, revenue fell from KRW 18.6 trillion in 2022 to KRW 8.4 trillion in 2023 (down 54.8% year on year), rose to KRW 11.7 trillion in 2024 (up 39.3%), and then declined to KRW 10.9 trillion in 2025 (down 6.9%). 2022 was the largest year and 2023 the smallest within the 2022 to 2025 period.

Operating profit went from KRW 9.95 trillion in 2022 to KRW 584.8 billion in 2023 (down 94.1%), then KRW 3.51 trillion in 2024 (up 500.7%) and KRW 1.46 trillion in 2025 (down 58.4%). The operating margin fell from 53.6% in 2022 to 7.0% in 2023 before recovering to 30.0% in 2024 and 13.4% in 2025.

Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 1.88 trillion in 2025, while net income including non-controlling interests was KRW 1.88 trillion, a separate figure.

Total equity at the end of 2025 was KRW 26.6 trillion and total liabilities were KRW 6.99 trillion, with the debt ratio rising from 25.5% in 2022 to 26.3% in 2025. Operating cash flow declined from KRW 11.3 trillion in 2022 to KRW 3.31 trillion in 2025.

Quarterly operating profit moved from KRW 296.8 billion in Q3 2025 to KRW 317.3 billion in Q4 2025 and KRW 269.1 billion in Q1 2026 (down 15.2% from the prior quarter), then rose to KRW 354.1 billion in Q2 2026 and KRW 1.39 trillion in Q3 2026, an increase in two consecutive quarters.

Revenue has grown for four consecutive quarters from Q4 2025 through Q3 2026, and Q3 2026 revenue of KRW 4.13 trillion was up 21.3% from the prior quarter. Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders in Q3 2026 was KRW 1.54 trillion.

The company attributed the Q3 increase to an average Shanghai Containerized Freight Index reading of 3,388 points, up 128.7% year on year, and container volume growth of 7.0%. One report put the cost of sales ratio at 63.1% in Q3, down from 84.5% in Q2.

05

Industry analysis

Container freight rates rose sharply in Q3 2026. The average Shanghai Containerized Freight Index for the quarter was 3,388 points, up 128.7% year on year, and the company pointed to an early peak season on transpacific routes and vessel supply constraints from congestion at major Asian ports.

The company said its average container freight rate rose 16.7% year on year. For Q4, the company said supply-chain disruption has weakened compared with Q3, but factors restraining supply growth such as port congestion in China remain. It expects freight rates to show route-by-route volatility and to move sideways.

Against peers, the Q3 operating margins announced were 20.6% for Maersk's ocean business, 21.6% for ONE and 28.7% for COSCO, while HMM's margin of about 33.8% was higher. External variables cited include the Middle East conflict, typhoon-related delays in China and drought affecting the Panama Canal.

In bulk, higher bulk freight was cited as a driver of earnings growth, while bulk operating profit fell from KRW 156.3 billion in Q2 to KRW 139.0 billion in Q3. Domestic bulk competitors include Pan Ocean, Korea Line and Heung-A Shipping.

06

Outlook

The company expects Q4 freight rates to move sideways but anticipates that cargo load factors will remain healthy. In containers, it plans to reorganize its small and mid-sized feeder network in the Far East and open a Mediterranean feeder service during Q4.

It is running temporary vessels on the Middle East route to maintain its business base there. In bulk, it plans to add long-term transport contracts for stable revenue, while in tankers it plans to use high freight levels to raise profitability on vessels operated in the spot market.

On investment, HMM presented a plan to invest KRW 29.1 trillion through 2030 to build a fleet of 166 container ships and 110 bulk carriers.

At its board meeting on October 15, 2026, it approved a container ship investment of KRW 2.17 trillion, covering the construction of eight 13,000 TEU-class container ships by 2029 and the purchase of four newbuildings ordered by other shipping companies.

In 2026 it invested KRW 6 trillion in fleet expansion, logistics infrastructure and future technology, and cumulative investment since the CEO took office in March 2025 has exceeded KRW 12 trillion. The shareholder return policy was raised to a payout ratio of 30% or more through 2030.

The company has set a target of 9% average annual revenue growth through 2030. The current CEO's term runs until March 26, 2027, and the company is pursuing an early re-appointment through an extraordinary shareholders' meeting.

08

Bull factors

Freight and bulk improving together

Q3 2026 operating profit of KRW 1.39 trillion rose 293.8% from the prior quarter. Container earnings were supported by higher freight indices and average rates, while bulk operating profit for the first three quarters reached KRW 378.4 billion, up 418.5%. The company describes the higher bulk share of 18.8% as a buffer against freight volatility.

Margins above cited global peers

The Q3 operating margin of about 33.8% was higher than the 20.6% for Maersk's ocean business, 21.6% for ONE and 28.7% for COSCO, as cited by the company. One media report said the cost of sales ratio fell from 84.5% in Q2 to 63.1% in Q3.

These figures rely on company statements and press reports, and differences in peers' accounting and business mix remain relevant.

Higher shareholder return floor

The company raised its shareholder return standard to a payout ratio of 30% or more through 2030. Because the standard is linked to earnings, the bull case holds that dividend amounts could grow if profit levels such as the KRW 1.54 trillion of Q3 controlling-shareholder net profit are sustained. The policy was announced alongside an earnings recovery.

Bear factors

Dependence on the freight cycle

If the expected Q4 slowdown in freight rates materializes, it could weigh on container segment earnings. Annual operating profit fell 94.1% from KRW 9.95 trillion in 2022 to KRW 584.8 billion in 2023, which the bear case cites as evidence of large earnings swings when freight falls. The operating margin also declined from 30.0% in 2024 to 13.4% in 2025.

Investment burden and rising debt ratio

A KRW 29.1 trillion investment plan through 2030 overlaps with a KRW 2.17 trillion container ship investment. The debt ratio rose from 19.9% in 2023 to 26.3% in 2025, and total liabilities at the end of 2025 were KRW 6.99 trillion.

The bear case holds that financial pressure could grow if investment spending coincides with a slower freight market.

Quarterly bulk swings and management timing

Bulk operating profit fell from KRW 156.3 billion in Q2 to KRW 139.0 billion in Q3. The CEO's current term runs until March 26, 2027, and pursuing an early re-appointment has been described as unusual compared with past practice. The move supports continuity of long-term investment, but the process itself is cited as a variable.

Risk factors

Freight and external variables

Container freight rates move with external factors such as supply-chain disruption, the Middle East conflict, typhoons in China and drought affecting the Panama Canal. The company expects Q4 rates to move sideways. In a falling freight environment, quarterly profit at the Q3 2026 level is structurally difficult to sustain.

Investment execution and finances

The KRW 29.1 trillion investment plan through 2030 brings financing needs and vessel operating cost burdens. The debt ratio stood at 26.3% in 2025, up from 19.9% in 2023. If investment pace and freight trends diverge, the financial structure could shift.

Governance and shareholder return policy

The CEO's current term runs until March 26, 2027, and the early re-appointment is being pursued through an extraordinary shareholders' meeting.

The shareholder return standard was raised to a payout ratio of 30% or more through 2030, but actual dividend amounts depend on earnings levels, so they would also shrink if profit declines.

12

Overall view

HMM's Q3 2026 results reflected higher container freight rates and growth in the bulk business at the same time. Operating profit of KRW 1.39 trillion was well above the KRW 296.8 billion of Q3 2025, and the operating margin of about 33.8% was higher than the figures the company cited for major global carriers.

Annual operating profit, however, fell from KRW 9.95 trillion in 2022 to KRW 584.8 billion in 2023 before recovering to KRW 1.46 trillion in 2025, so earnings remain highly sensitive to the freight cycle.

The rise in bulk's share of operating profit from 6.4% to 18.8% is presented by the company as the result of restructuring aimed at lowering dependence on containers.

On the other side, the expected slowdown in Q4 freight increases and the KRW 29.1 trillion investment plan through 2030 are variables that will shape future earnings and the balance sheet.

The 2026 preliminary Q4 results and the 2026 annual report are the next confirmation points, and the CEO re-appointment process is linked to the continuity of long-term investment.
