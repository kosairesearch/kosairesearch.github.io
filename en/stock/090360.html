Robostar: Sales Rebound, Net Loss Returns in Q3 2026

02

Business structure

Robostar is an industrial robot maker founded in 1999 that divides its business into robotics, semiconductor/TR, and smart factory segments. Its core products are Cartesian, SCARA and vertical articulated robots, wafer transfer robots for EFEM systems, and unmanned transport robots.

Based on the 2025 annual report, the sales mix was 53.2% robot business, 30.2% system engineering (semiconductor EFEM, process and logistics equipment), and 16.6% other (mainly after-sales service). In 2024 the same mix was 70.1% robots, 24.1% system engineering and 5.8% other, and in 2023 robots accounted for 77.8%.

Over three years the robot share fell sharply while system engineering and after-sales grew in weight. The company says it is expanding sales of products for secondary battery and semiconductor industries alongside display and automotive, reducing reliance on any single industry's investment cycle.

The business is largely order-based, so sales move with customers' investment timing. LG Electronics is the largest shareholder with a 33.40% stake.

Compared with collaborative-robot specialists such as Doosan Robotics and Rainbow Robotics in the same machinery sector, Robostar has a larger share of Cartesian robots and transfer and logistics equipment.

In December 2025 the company announced an expansion into semiconductor transfer equipment and RPS smart factory systems for mid-sized and small manufacturers.

Earnings analysis

Annual sales fell from KRW 143.2 billion in 2022 to KRW 102.7 billion in 2023 (down 28.3% year on year), KRW 89.1 billion in 2024 (down 13.2%) and KRW 75.7 billion in 2025 (down 15.0%). Sales declined for three straight years from 2023 to 2025, with 2022 the largest year and 2025 the smallest in the 2022 to 2025 range.

Operating profit fell from KRW 1.79 billion in 2022 to KRW 1.14 billion in 2023 (down 36.7%) and KRW 149 million in 2024 (down 86.9%), and the company recorded an operating loss of KRW 5.66 billion in 2025, a swing into loss.

The operating margin went from 1.3% in 2022 and 1.1% in 2023 to 0.2% in 2024 and -7.5% in 2025. One media report summarized that the 2025 cost of sales rose to 90.9% of revenue as falling volumes combined with fixed costs and low utilization.

In a January 2026 disclosure, the company said sales and profit moved because customers delayed equipment investment amid a downturn in downstream industries.

Controlling-shareholder net income went from KRW 3.28 billion in 2022 to KRW 694 million in 2023 (down 78.9%), recovered to KRW 2.22 billion in 2024 (up 219.7%), and turned into a controlling-shareholder net loss of KRW 5.2 billion in 2025.

Operating cash flow also dropped from KRW 701 million in 2022, KRW 7.32 billion in 2023 and KRW 8.86 billion in 2024 to KRW 55.69 million in 2025.

On a quarterly basis, the company posted operating losses of KRW 1.1 billion in Q3 2025 and KRW 1.36 billion in Q4 2025, then an operating profit of KRW 73.73 million in Q1 2026, KRW 500 million in Q2 (up 577.7% quarter on quarter) and KRW 219 million in Q3 (down 56.1% quarter on quarter).

Sales rose from KRW 24.6 billion in Q4 2025 (up 41.8% quarter on quarter) to KRW 28.7 billion in Q3 2026, and Q3 2026 was up 65.3% year on year.

Controlling-shareholder net income was KRW 934 million in Q1 2026 and KRW 1.1 billion in Q2, but Q3 2026 showed a controlling-shareholder net loss of KRW 1.38 billion, a larger loss than the KRW 536 million loss in Q3 2025.

Because operating profit and controlling-shareholder profit moved in opposite directions, non-operating items and income tax are the next items to check.

Industry analysis

Demand for Robostar comes from new investment in manufacturing equipment in display, semiconductor, secondary battery and automotive industries. The company attributed its 2025 weakness to a downturn in downstream industries and customers delaying equipment investment.

In its Q1 2026 results summary, reports said robot demand rose on new manufacturing investment and the expansion of advanced industries, and that smart factory results improved on rising automation demand. Sales have risen for four consecutive quarters from Q4 2025 through Q3 2026 after the investment slowdown.

Operating margins have varied widely from quarter to quarter, so how quickly sales recovery turns into profitability has to be checked against each quarter's figures. The system engineering share of sales rose from 18.8% in 2023 to 30.2% in 2025, and semiconductor transfer equipment has supported that growth.

In product terms, Robostar has a larger share of Cartesian robots and transfer equipment than collaborative-robot specialists such as Doosan Robotics and Rainbow Robotics, which means its end markets and product lines differ.

From 2026 the supply of EFEM, AGV and AMR systems began to ramp, widening its downstream exposure toward semiconductor logistics. Across the domestic robot sector, the outlook is tied to automation investment expectations and the capital expenditure cycle.
