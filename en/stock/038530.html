02

Business structure

Kbio Labs centers on a bio distribution business that supplies laboratory reagents and scientific equipment to universities, hospitals and research institutes, according to the company's own materials.

In November 2021 it took over the distribution division of a Korean biotech firm and has continued the bio distribution business since. It discontinued its fashion business in April 2023 after transferring a fashion brand. In March 2026 the company changed its name to KBIO LABS Inc.

Based on a third-party tally of Q1 2026 revenue, bio distribution accounts for about 60% of sales and ICT about 40%. A Daejeon branch was established in January 2025, and in its first-half 2026 results explanation the company cited repeat purchases by existing customers and the Daejeon branch's sales as growth drivers.

Its largest shareholder holds a stake in the 40% range, and in July 2026 the company carried out a KRW 2.0 billion capital increase targeting that shareholder. Competition in distribution depends on securing customers and supply terms.

Direct comparison with large peers such as Samsung Biologics or Celltrion is limited because their models center on manufacturing and development. Details of the ICT segment's products and amounts should be checked in the segment notes of the periodic filing.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual results show revenue expansion alongside a narrowing of losses. Revenue fell from KRW 33.6 billion in 2022 to KRW 27.3 billion in 2023 (down 18.8% year on year), then rose to KRW 76.5 billion in 2024 (up 180.0%) and KRW 87.3 billion in 2025 (up 14.1%), with 2025 the largest year of 2022 to 2025.

Operating results were an operating loss of KRW 153 million in 2022 and KRW 561 million in 2023, before an operating profit of KRW 793 million in 2024 and KRW 1.52 billion in 2025 (up 91.9%).

Operating margin was -0.5%, -2.1%, 1.0% and 1.7% for 2022 to 2025, so revenue growth has not translated into a large margin gain.

Net loss attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 7.67 billion, KRW 12.7 billion, KRW 6.79 billion and KRW 3.2 billion for 2022 to 2025, a loss in each of those four years, with the loss narrowing in 2024 and 2025.

Net loss including non-controlling interests was KRW 5.04 billion in 2025, a different figure from the controlling-shareholder number. Operating cash flow went from KRW 3.78 billion in 2022 to -KRW 1.46 billion in 2023 and -KRW 119 million in 2024, before reaching KRW 8.2 billion in 2025.

At end-2025, total equity was KRW 102.3 billion, of which KRW 53.3 billion belonged to controlling shareholders, so non-controlling interests account for close to half of total equity. The debt ratio rose from 9.4% in 2024 to 16.2% in 2025.

Quarterly revenue was KRW 20.8 billion in Q2 2025, KRW 17.6 billion in Q3, a peak of KRW 28.4 billion in Q4, then KRW 15.9 billion in Q1 2026 and KRW 5.88 billion in Q2 2026.

Operating profit turned from a KRW 808 million loss in Q1 2026 to KRW 1.13 billion in Q2, but the KRW 14.4 billion net loss attributable to controlling shareholders in Q2 far exceeds the operating result, so the non-operating items behind it should be checked in the filing notes. The net loss attributable to controlling shareholders over the last four quarters totals KRW 14.9 billion.

Industry analysis

Distribution of laboratory reagents and scientific equipment moves with research and development budgets and experimental demand at universities, hospitals and research institutes.

Demand for bio equipment and consumables is linked to domestic bio research investment, but market size and growth rates should be checked against statistical agency data.

Among listed bio and pharma companies, Samsung Biologics, Celltrion and Alteogen center on contract manufacturing or new drug and biosimilar development.

Kbio Labs operates a purchase-and-supply distribution model, so its revenue structure and asset mix differ, and the same sector label does not allow the same yardstick. Competitiveness in distribution depends on customer retention, repeat purchases, supplier contract terms and branch sales capacity.

The company's emphasis on repeat purchases and the Daejeon branch in its first-half 2026 explanation reflects this structure. Its operating margin was 1.7% in 2025, leaving a thin profit cushion on revenue, so cost control and customer expansion are the main variables behind profit swings.

On trading, a market data tally showed average trading value below the prior period as of September 7, 2026, which should be read alongside changes in investor interest.

Under KOSDAQ market-cap rules, the administrative issue designation works as a listing requirement, and for this company the market-cap scale within the sector is the direct variable determining its application.

Risk factors

Disclosure history and governance

In a March 2023 notice, the company said accounting violations for 2016 to 2019 had been reflected in its financial statements, and it pledged to cooperate with Korea Exchange procedures to resume trading.

The company said the executives subject to action had all left before 2019 and are unrelated to current management. This disclosure history remains an item to check when assessing earnings reliability.

Listing maintenance requirements

If market capitalization stays below the KRW 20.0 billion threshold for 30 consecutive trading days, the stock may be designated as an administrative issue. Such a designation can affect market reputation and funding conditions.

If the company leans more on capital increases, dilution for existing shareholders also becomes a factor to monitor.

Business concentration and reliance on the largest shareholder

Public disclosures show the largest shareholder holds a stake in the 40% range. The first-half 2026 explanation highlighted the Daejeon branch's role in sales, indicating reliance on specific branch and customer performance. Distribution earnings can swing widely with supply terms and customer concentration.
