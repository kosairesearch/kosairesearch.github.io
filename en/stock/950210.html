Revenue Up, Losses Wider at Prestige BioPharma

Revenue rose for three straight fiscal years through June 2026, but both operating and net losses widened, so whether CDMO orders narrow the core deficit is the key item to track.

02

Business structure

Prestige BioPharma handles antibody drug development, while contract development and manufacturing (CDMO) is run by subsidiary Prestige Biologics in Osong, Chungcheong Province. The group presents a full value chain spanning drug development through in-house production.

Its core commercial product is Tuznue, a biosimilar of the breast cancer therapy Herceptin, for which it obtained European marketing authorization in 2025. The pipeline includes the pancreatic cancer antibody PBP1510, the Avastin biosimilar HD204, the Humira biosimilar PBP1502, and the Prolia biosimilar PBP1601.

HD204 is reported to be in a global Phase 3 trial across 17 countries. A pancreatic cancer diagnostic kit based on PAUF protein is also being pursued.

According to press coverage of the annual report for the fiscal year ended June 2026, CDMO product sales accounted for 46.1% of revenue and other service sales for 28.9%, together about 75%. The same coverage said the share of license-out revenue declined while production, supply and CDMO service revenue grew.

Celltrion has been reported as a client under a clinical-material manufacturing contract, and the company said cumulative orders reached KRW 38 billion after additional contracts followed a Teva deal in the second half of 2025.

On competition, the reintroduced BIOSECURE Act, which restricts antibody drugs made by Chinese CDMO companies, is cited as an opportunity for domestic CDMOs with global production scale.

04

Earnings analysis

The company reports in US dollars; the amounts below are KRW translations. Revenue for the fiscal year ended June 2026 was KRW 24.2 billion, up 76.1% year on year, and has risen for three consecutive fiscal years from the year ended June 2024 through June 2026.

The operating loss went from KRW 70.8 billion in the year ended June 2023 to KRW 63.4 billion, then KRW 64.6 billion, and reached KRW 86.8 billion in the year ended June 2026, making it four consecutive years of operating losses from the year ended June 2023 through June 2026.

Press reports attributed the larger loss to higher cost of sales and SG&A expenses, and R&D spending more than doubled from the prior year.

The operating margin improved from -470.1% in the year ended June 2025 to -359.1% in the year ended June 2026, but this reflects the higher revenue base, while the absolute loss grew.

Profit attributable to controlling shareholders moved from KRW 17.9 billion in the year ended June 2023 to a net loss of KRW 33.2 billion in the year ended June 2024, returned to a profit of KRW 21.4 billion in the year ended June 2025, and then swung back to a net loss of KRW 84.9 billion in the year ended June 2026.

Net loss including non-controlling interests was KRW 114 billion in the year ended June 2026, and press analysis pointed to higher other and financial expenses.

At the end of June 2026, total equity was KRW 440 billion, of which KRW 376.9 billion was attributable to controlling shareholders, and total liabilities were KRW 203.5 billion. The debt ratio rose from 35.8% in the year ended June 2023 to 46.2% in the year ended June 2026.

Operating cash flow was negative every year from the year ended June 2023 through June 2026, at -KRW 51.8 billion in the latest year. Quarterly operating losses have continued for five consecutive quarters from Q2 2025 through Q2 2026.

Q2 2026 revenue of KRW 14.2 billion rose 2,860.7% from the prior quarter and 167.3% from a year earlier, while the Q2 2026 operating loss of KRW 20.8 billion widened versus the year-earlier quarter.

Q2 2026 net loss attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 39.3 billion widened from KRW 19.3 billion in Q1 2026, and the last four quarters total a net loss of KRW 84.9 billion.

The disclosed figures alone do not establish the cause of the Q2 revenue jump or whether it was one-off, so the revenue mix disclosed in the Q3 2026 quarterly report is the next checkpoint.

05

Industry analysis

The company says that in biosimilars, competitive advantage rests on price competitiveness, new formulation technologies that improve dosing convenience, and manufacturing efficiency. Based on that view, it has put cell line productivity gains and process efficiency at the center of its strategy.

In contract manufacturing, the reintroduced BIOSECURE Act, which restricts antibody drugs produced by Chinese CDMO companies, has been viewed as an opportunity for domestic CDMOs with global production scale and price competitiveness.

At the JP Morgan Healthcare Conference in January 2026, the company's CEO said interest in Korean biotech had grown after the law took effect.

However, at a September 2025 event, Kim Jin-woo, CEO of Prestige Biologics, said the subsidiary had built roughly KRW 400 billion of facilities over ten years but that securing contracted work meeting international quality standards and raising operating funds remained difficult.

A 2024 report linked delays in biosimilar approvals at a related company to plant utilization falling below target at the subsidiary. The clinical-material manufacturing contract with Celltrion shows that a biosimilar company can also be a CDMO customer.

Public disclosures offer limited scope for a numerical comparison of production scale with listed peers. Conversion of orders into contracts and approval timing therefore appear to be the variables that determine the company's position within the industry.

06

Outlook

The company set three goals for 2026: accelerating its pipeline, expanding in global markets, and growing revenue, according to remarks at the JP Morgan Healthcare Conference in January 2026. Key tasks cited were clinical progress for PBP1510 and advancing regulatory filings for HD204.

For Tuznue, the company said it would work with local partners to enter Asia, including China and Japan, the Middle East, and other emerging markets in sequence.

It targeted product approval and first shipments within 2026, and in the first half of 2026 it also aimed to expand Tuznue negotiations and accelerate GMP certification at its plant.

In June 2026 the company announced it would begin a follow-on clinical development roadmap for PBP1510 based on clinical data, and said it had completed abstract submission for the ESMO 2026 annual congress. For the pancreatic cancer diagnostic kit, the target is to select a global partner within 2026.

On CDMO, the company's stated aim is to broaden its portfolio through additional contracts following the Teva deal. Whether these targets are met will be confirmed through future quarterly reports and timely disclosures.

Limits in Reading Capital Metrics

One media assessment said recovery of the core business will be difficult before the pancreatic cancer drug is commercialized.

Risk factors

Financing and Liquidity

Operating cash flow has been negative every year since the year ended June 2023, so continued R&D depends on external funding conditions.

Equity attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 376.9 billion at June 2026, but part of that equity comes from a revaluation, so book capacity cannot be read as cash capacity. The timing and terms of fundraising will shape future financial strain.

Clinical and Regulatory Timelines

Progress for PBP1510 and regulatory filings for HD204 are central to the company's targets, but timing depends on clinical results and regulators. A 2024 report linked delayed biosimilar approvals at a related company to lower-than-target plant utilization at the subsidiary. Approval delays could carry over into CDMO utilization.

Quarterly Revenue Volatility

Q1 2026 revenue of KRW 478 million fell 93.5% from the prior quarter, and Q2 2026 revenue of KRW 14.2 billion rose 2,860.7% from Q1. If quarterly revenue swings this sharply with order and delivery timing, forecasting quarterly results carries high uncertainty. Whether the Q2 increase is one-off needs to be confirmed in the quarterly report.

Overall view

Prestige BioPharma reported revenue of KRW 24.2 billion for the fiscal year ended June 2026, up for three consecutive years, but the operating loss of KRW 86.8 billion and the net loss attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 84.9 billion show that the deficit deepened.

The revenue mix is shifting toward CDMO and services, and the license-out share has declined. The operating margin improved from -470.1% to -359.1%, but the absolute loss widened. Q2 2026 revenue rose sharply to KRW 14.2 billion, yet the operating loss of KRW 20.8 billion extended a five-quarter losing streak.

Negative operating cash flow and an equity increase from a revaluation coexist, so balance sheet metrics alone do not establish the financial position. In the pipeline, the follow-on PBP1510 trial, the HD204 Phase 3 program, and partner entry for Tuznue remain medium-term variables.

The reintroduced BIOSECURE Act is cited as a CDMO order opportunity, but the durability of orders has not yet shown up in quarterly results. The next checkpoints are the revenue mix in the Q3 2026 quarterly report, the operating loss trend, and whether the product approval and first shipments occur within 2026.
