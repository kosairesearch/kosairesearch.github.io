02

Business structure

Prestige BioPharma is a KOSPI-listed biopharmaceutical company developing antibody biosimilars alongside novel antibody drugs. Production is handled by its affiliate Prestige Biologics, which operates a total of 154,000 liters of antibody drug manufacturing capacity in Osong, North Chungcheong Province.

Its first commercialized product is TUZNUE (HD201), a Herceptin biosimilar that received marketing authorization from the European Commission, making it the company's first commercialized pipeline asset.

TUZNUE is supplied in Europe through partner Teva Pharmaceuticals and has since gained additional approvals in Brazil and the Caribbean, expanding its geographic footprint.

The next pipeline asset is VASFORDA (HD204), an Avastin biosimilar currently preparing for regulatory approval and commercialization in North America.

On the novel antibody side, PBP1510, which targets the pancreatic cancer factor PAUF, has received Orphan Drug and Fast Track designations from the US FDA and remains in clinical development, while PBP1710, targeting CTHRC1 in solid tumors, is being developed under a national new drug development program.

Prestige Biologics, which handles manufacturing, has historically depended heavily on intra-group orders tied to TUZNUE and is now pursuing diversification toward external clients.

The group frames its strategy around an integrated value chain linking development, manufacturing, and commercialization, with co-development and licensing deals with global pharmaceutical companies as a core part of its business model.

04

Earnings analysis

On a KRW-converted basis, annual revenue rose for two straight years, from KRW 163 million in 2023 to KRW 690 million in 2024 (up 322.4% year on year) and KRW 13.6 billion in 2025 (up 1,875.4% year on year).

The operating loss widened from KRW 65 billion in 2022 to KRW 70.2 billion in 2023, narrowed to KRW 62.8 billion in 2024, then widened again to KRW 64 billion in 2025, marking a fourth consecutive year of operating losses.

Because the revenue base is so small, the operating margin was deeply negative at -42,988.4% in 2023, -9,111.2% in 2024, and -470.1% in 2025, though the magnitude of the negative margin eased as revenue grew.

Controlling-interest net income swung from a loss of KRW 234.8 billion in 2022 to a profit of KRW 17.7 billion in 2023, back to a loss of KRW 32.9 billion in 2024, and then to a profit of KRW 21.2 billion in 2025.

Net income including non-controlling interests followed a different path, with losses of KRW 234.8 billion in 2022, KRW 2.88 billion in 2023, and KRW 55.1 billion in 2024, before turning to a profit of KRW 3.97 billion in 2025.

On a quarterly basis, the operating loss narrowed for two straight quarters from KRW 23.4 billion in the first quarter of 2025 to KRW 17.6 billion in the second quarter and KRW 9.52 billion in the third quarter, before widening again to KRW 13.6 billion in the fourth quarter, extending a streak of losses across five consecutive quarters from the fourth quarter of 2024.

Controlling-interest net income swung even more sharply by quarter, turning to a profit of KRW 55.6 billion in the third quarter of 2025 before reverting to a loss of KRW 8.36 billion in the fourth quarter.

The sum of controlling-interest net income across the last four quarters (Q1-Q4 2025) was KRW 21.2 billion, matching the annual figure, while operating cash flow stayed negative every year from 2022 to 2025 at -KRW 51 billion, -KRW 60.8 billion, -KRW 48.1 billion, and -KRW 49.6 billion respectively, underscoring that cash generation remains weak regardless of the profit figures.

05

Industry analysis

The global antibody biosimilar market continues to see expanding competition, driven by patent expirations on originator drugs and a regulatory trend toward simplified clinical pathways.

The US FDA is reviewing policy changes that would ease comparative clinical trial requirements for biosimilars, which could shorten development costs and timelines for later entrants.

In Korea, Celltrion and Samsung Bioepis secured multiple biosimilar approvals earlier, and Prestige BioPharma's TUZNUE was the second such approval among domestic listed companies, or the third overall including Samsung Bioepis, which is unlisted.

In the CDMO segment, a domestic special act supporting biomanufacturing has emerged as a potential catalyst, and affiliate Prestige Biologics, with its 154,000-liter capacity, has been cited as one of the possible beneficiaries.

However, Prestige Biologics' revenue still depends heavily on production volumes tied to its parent's TUZNUE, leaving external order wins as the key variable in converting policy tailwinds into actual results.

The pancreatic cancer treatment market is viewed as an area with limited treatment options, and interest in PAUF-targeted treatment strategies has continued at global oncology conferences.

While larger domestic biotech companies such as Samsung Biologics and Alteogen lead in contract manufacturing and platform technology, Prestige BioPharma is attempting to differentiate itself through a multi-asset pipeline strategy from a relatively small revenue base.

06

Outlook

At BIO USA 2026 in June, the company presented a strategy built on two pillars: co-development partnerships for its novel antibody drugs and expanded commercialization of its biosimilars.

VASFORDA (HD204) is undergoing regulatory preparation and commercialization readiness for North America, with affiliate Prestige Biologics also upgrading its manufacturing and quality systems to be ready for regulatory inspections.

However, in early 2026 the company disclosed it was discussing with the US FDA and European EMA the possibility of an abbreviated approval pathway based on Phase 1 data, leaving open the possibility that its originally stated filing timeline could shift.

Pancreatic cancer drug candidate PBP1510 has been targeting Phase 2a entry in the second half of 2026, with an advisory board and trial design being finalized, and at ESMO GI 2026 in October the company presented Phase 1 data from 43 enrolled patients along with the follow-on Phase 2a design.

In October the company stated that a US exemption for essential medicines from tariffs left no change to the timeline for PBP1510's US trial or VASFORDA's US cGMP preparation.

At CPHI Milan 2026 in October, TUZNUE's approval progress in Europe, Brazil, and the Caribbean was cited as the basis for pursuing new commercialization partners in regions not yet entered.

Chairman So-yeon Park stated at a June 2025 investor briefing a mid- to long-term goal of returning to profit in 2026 and reaching KRW 2.5 trillion in revenue and a KRW 30 trillion market capitalization by 2030.

08

Bull factors

Expanding Commercial Footprint for TUZNUE

TUZNUE received its first official purchase order from European partner Teva Pharmaceuticals and has since secured additional approvals in Brazil and the Caribbean.

At CPHI Milan in October 2026, the company said it is pursuing new commercialization partners in regions not yet entered, signaling continued geographic expansion. The two consecutive years of revenue growth in 2024 and 2025 were underpinned in part by this commercialization progress.

Regulatory Designations for the Antibody Drug Pipeline

Pancreatic cancer candidate PBP1510 is being developed under Orphan Drug and Fast Track designations from the US FDA, with Phase 2a trial design being finalized toward a targeted entry in the second half of 2026.

Solid-tumor candidate PBP1710 has had a US patent registered and was also selected for a national new drug development program. The company has said both candidates are the subject of ongoing co-development and licensing discussions with global pharmaceutical companies.

Integrated Value Chain and Potential CDMO Policy Tailwinds

Affiliate Prestige Biologics operates 154,000 liters of antibody drug manufacturing capacity in Osong, a scale described as unusual among mid-sized domestic CDMOs. There is a view that progress on a domestic CDMO special act could create opportunities for expanded orders from overseas clients.

The integrated value chain linking development, manufacturing, and commercialization is seen as a structure that could offer operating leverage if external order volumes increase.

Bear factors

Persistent Operating Losses

Operating losses have continued for four straight years from 2022 to 2025, widening again in 2025. Reports indicate the ratio of R&D spending to revenue rose sharply over the period, meaning revenue growth has not directly translated into improved profitability.

Operating cash flow has also been negative for four consecutive years, so a recovery in cash generation has not yet been confirmed.

Volatile Net Income

Controlling-interest net income flipped nearly every year: a loss in 2022, a profit in 2023, a loss in 2024, and a profit in 2025. Quarterly results were similarly volatile, swinging from a profit of KRW 55.6 billion in the third quarter of 2025 to a loss of KRW 8.36 billion in the fourth quarter.

With operating losses continuing while net income alternates sharply, establishing a clear trend remains difficult.

Concerns Over Optical Improvement in Leverage Metrics

The debt-to-equity ratio rose every year from 16.3% in 2022 to 46.7% in 2025. One media report noted that, as of a past quarter, the ratio had been kept at a favorable level partly because of an increase in total equity from a land asset revaluation.

The same report also pointed to an insufficient current ratio and an interest coverage ratio that had remained negative for an extended period.

Risk factors

Clinical and Regulatory Risk

Rather than the originally outlined approval timeline, the company is discussing with the FDA and EMA the possibility of an abbreviated approval pathway for VASFORDA (HD204) based on Phase 1 data, meaning the actual filing and approval timing could be delayed or subject to different conditions than initially planned.

The targeted Phase 2a entry for PBP1510 could also shift depending on patient enrollment speed and the outcome of regulatory discussions.

Financial and Liquidity Risk

With operating losses continuing for four straight years and operating cash flow negative every year, the need for additional financing to fund pipeline development and commercialization costs persists. The steadily rising debt-to-equity ratio over the period could also become a medium- to long-term financial burden.

Partner and Competitive Risk

Commercialization of TUZNUE and VASFORDA depends heavily on the sales and distribution capabilities of overseas partners including Teva Pharmaceuticals.

In the same indications, earlier entrants such as Celltrion and Samsung Bioepis already hold multiple biosimilar approvals, creating intense competitive pressure on pricing and market share.

12

Overall view

Prestige BioPharma turned to a controlling-interest net profit in 2025 and posted two straight years of revenue growth, yet operating losses have continued for four straight years and operating cash flow has been negative every year, leaving questions about the quality of the reported profit.

Progress on the pipeline side is evident, including TUZNUE's European commercialization and geographic expansion, VASFORDA's North American regulatory preparation, and regulatory designations for PBP1510 and PBP1710, but there is also timeline uncertainty given that the approval strategy for HD204 is under review.

The debt-to-equity ratio has risen every year, and some media reports have pointed to the possibility that asset revaluation effects have flattered certain financial metrics.

The CDMO capacity and potential policy tailwinds at affiliate Prestige Biologics remain a variable whose realization depends on actual external order wins.

Overall, this stock sits in a phase where the earnings volatility typical of an early-stage commercializing biotech needs to be weighed alongside pipeline progress, with the next quarterly report and the HD204 and PBP1510 timelines standing out as key points to monitor.
