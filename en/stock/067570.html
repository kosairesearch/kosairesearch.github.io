02

Business structure

NVH Korea runs two main businesses: automotive parts, mainly interior and noise, vibration and harshness (NVH) products, and environmental energy, which includes cleanroom projects. In 2024, automotive parts accounted for 63.4% of product sales and environmental energy (cleanrooms) for 31.6%.

By region, 2024 sales were 65% domestic, 18% India, 6% Poland and 6% United States. Its automotive lineup includes headliners and other NVH parts made partly from recycled and eco-friendly fiber, and it sells an integrated system covering noise, vibration and thermal management.

The EV battery business began in 2020 with battery pack assembly at a Hyundai Mobis plant in Ulsan, and the pack business was brought in-house through a merger in 2021. The EV parts operations were later moved to a subsidiary.

Customers are centered on the Hyundai Motor Group, and overseas growth has followed its expansion into India, Europe, China and Russia.

Overseas production has been extended through local subsidiaries in the United States, China and Turkey, and company analysis describes an EV battery parts plant in Georgia, United States, as under construction.

Among automotive parts suppliers, large players such as Hyundai Mobis, Hanon Systems and Hyundai WIA compete in adjacent areas, but few listed firms combine interior NVH parts with environmental energy, so direct peer comparison is narrow.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual revenue rose from KRW 1.23 trillion in 2022 to KRW 1.37 trillion in 2023 (up 11.5% year on year), KRW 1.59 trillion in 2024 (up 15.7%) and KRW 1.62 trillion in 2025 (up 2.3%), and it increased for three consecutive years from 2023 through 2025. Of the 2022 to 2025 period, 2025 was the largest year by revenue.

Operating profit grew from KRW 53.2 billion in 2022 to KRW 62.9 billion in 2023 (up 18.4%) and KRW 67.6 billion in 2024 (up 7.4%), but in 2025 it swung to an operating loss of KRW 21.9 billion, and the operating margin fell from 4.3% in 2024 to -1.3% in 2025.

The company cited cost overruns on a major subsidiary project, which led to project losses and bad-debt provisions, as the main drags on the annual result. It also recorded an impairment at its Russian unit on business-environment uncertainty; reports linked this to Hyundai Motor giving up its buy-back right on the St.

Petersburg plant. Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders moved from KRW 11.7 billion in 2024 to a loss of KRW 13.1 billion in 2025, and the net loss including non-controlling interests was larger at KRW 45 billion.

Operating cash flow fell from KRW 94.5 billion in 2022 to -KRW 7.18 billion in 2025, while the debt-to-equity ratio rose from 256.9% to 371.1%, with total liabilities of KRW 1.24 trillion at the end of 2025.

Quarterly, operating profit of KRW 11.3 billion in the third quarter of 2025 was followed by an operating loss of KRW 72.5 billion in the fourth quarter.

Operating profit then stayed positive in all three quarters of 2026, at KRW 10.5 billion, KRW 4.9 billion and KRW 10 billion, although the third-quarter figure fell 11.1% year on year.

In the same third quarter, net loss attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 27.8 billion, a swing from a profit a year earlier, and the four quarters from the fourth quarter of 2025 through the third quarter of 2026 add up to a net loss of KRW 64.5 billion.

05

Industry analysis

Demand in automotive parts tracks vehicle production plans and overseas plant utilization. The company's automotive sales depend heavily on production volumes across Hyundai Motor Group plants at home and abroad.

Overseas sales in 2024 were split between India at 18%, the United States at 6% and Poland at 6%, so changes in an automaker's overseas plant strategy pass quickly into supplier results. The Russian case shows this link: after Hyundai Motor gave up its buy-back right on the St.

Petersburg plant, an impairment was booked at the local unit. On electrification, battery pack assembly began at a Hyundai Mobis plant in Ulsan, which ties the business to EV production plans.

Against peers, Hyundai Mobis has a far larger parts scale and customer base, Hanon Systems focuses on thermal management, and Hyundai WIA occupies a different position in machinery and parts. Because the company combines interior NVH parts with cleanroom-led environmental energy, its direct peer set is limited.

Demand linked to automakers' overseas production supports automotive sales, but a withdrawal from a region or a change in plant strategy can lead to impairments at the affected unit.

06

Outlook

In March 2026, the company said it expected new mass-production vehicle awards, mainly from Hyundai Motor and Kia, and a wider range of eco-friendly vehicle parts in 2026.

At the same time, a company official said 2026 sales were expected to rise year on year and that both operating profit and net profit would turn to profit.

In practice, operating profit was positive in each quarter of 2026 through the third quarter, but net loss attributable to controlling shareholders reached KRW 27.8 billion in the third quarter, a different path from that expectation.

The company said it would focus on strengthening its financial structure rather than new investment in 2026. It paid a dividend of about KRW 6.2 billion for 2025 despite the loss, and reports said it planned a cash dividend again in 2026.

Overseas expansion includes a plan to build a production plant in Georgia, United States, and an EV battery parts plant in the United States. Company analysis describes a push to grow environmental energy revenue toward KRW 1 trillion by strengthening its cleanroom business.

The guarantee balance for the Indian subsidiary was disclosed at about KRW 446.8 billion, so overseas financial exposure will be reviewed alongside expansion. The sustainability of the Russian unit's business remains uncertain even after the 2025 impairment.

08

Bull factors

Operating profit stays positive

Operating profit was KRW 10.5 billion in the first quarter of 2026, KRW 4.9 billion in the second and KRW 10 billion in the third, so all three quarters were profitable. The third-quarter figure rose 104.7% from the prior quarter.

After an operating loss of KRW 72.5 billion in the fourth quarter of 2025, this can be read as a sign that core profitability is recovering.

Expected awards and eco-friendly parts growth

In March 2026, the company said it expected new mass-production awards centered on Hyundai Motor and Kia and wider eco-friendly vehicle parts. If those expectations materialize, sales growth and profitability could improve together. Third-quarter 2026 sales rose 2.9% year on year to KRW 409 billion.

Diversified environmental energy and overseas footprint

Environmental energy, mainly cleanroom projects, accounted for 31.6% of 2024 product sales and gives the company a revenue source beyond automotive parts. Local subsidiaries in the United States, China and Turkey, together with the planned Georgia plant, widen its ability to serve North American customers. Sales from India and Poland also make up a meaningful share of overseas revenue.

Bear factors

Net losses attributable to controlling shareholders persist

Net loss attributable to controlling shareholders reached KRW 27.8 billion in the third quarter of 2026, turning from a year-earlier profit. Over the last four quarters, from the fourth quarter of 2025 through the third quarter of 2026, the total net loss was KRW 64.5 billion. Positive operating profit has not yet prevented a net loss from continuing.

Heavy financial leverage

The debt-to-equity ratio rose from 256.9% in 2022 to 371.1% in 2025. At the end of 2025, total liabilities of KRW 1.24 trillion were well above total equity of KRW 335.2 billion. Operating cash flow turned negative at -KRW 7.18 billion in 2025.

Impairments and guarantees at overseas units

The Russian unit took an impairment in 2025, and the sustainability of its business remains uncertain. The guarantee balance for the Indian subsidiary was disclosed at about KRW 446.8 billion. That subsidiary reported a net loss of about KRW 6.8 billion for 2025.

10

Risk factors

Financial structure risk

The debt-to-equity ratio reached 371.1% and operating cash flow turned negative, which increases reliance on borrowing. If profit recovery is delayed, interest and refinancing costs could keep net profit from improving. Controlling-shareholder equity of KRW 207.7 billion also limits financial room.

Overseas units and contingent liabilities

Impairment was booked at the Russian unit, but further write-downs depend on the business environment there. The guarantee balance for the Indian subsidiary, about KRW 446.8 billion, is large. If subsidiary losses widen, the burden of guarantee obligations could pass into consolidated finances.

Customer concentration and award volatility

Automotive parts sales depend heavily on Hyundai Motor Group production plans. As the Russian case showed, a change in an automaker's regional strategy can lead to impairments at the affected unit. If new model awards are delayed, expected sales growth in 2026 would also be pushed back.

12

Overall view

NVH Korea grew revenue from KRW 1.23 trillion in 2022 to KRW 1.62 trillion in 2025, but profitability deteriorated sharply, with a 2025 operating loss of KRW 21.9 billion and a net loss attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 13.1 billion.

The company attributed the main causes to subsidiary project losses, bad-debt provisions and an impairment at its Russian unit. In 2026, quarterly operating profit stayed positive in all three quarters, and third-quarter sales rose 2.9% year on year to KRW 409 billion.

However, a third-quarter 2026 net loss attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 27.8 billion and a four-quarter total net loss of KRW 64.5 billion show that earnings recovery is not yet complete. A debt-to-equity ratio up to 371.1% and negative operating cash flow remain financial burdens.

Strengths include continued operating profit, expected new model awards, and diversification across overseas and environmental energy businesses. Weaknesses include the controlling-shareholder net loss, impairments and guarantees at overseas units, and high leverage.

The next checkpoints are the operating margin and net profit in the 2026 annual report and the first-quarter 2027 quarterly report.
