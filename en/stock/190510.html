Revenue Rebounds, Profit Recovery Still to Be Confirmed

02

Business structure

Namuga operates a single camera module business, and its core products are camera modules for smartphones and tablets. Press reports identify Samsung Electronics smartphone and tablet camera modules as its main customer.

In 3D sensing, it began ToF (Time of Flight) module development in 2011, mass-produces iToF-type modules, and has announced success in developing dToF modules.

Applications have expanded beyond phones: in 2017 it supplied modules to Sony's robot dog aibo, and reports say it has supplied in-cabin and driver monitoring (DMS) cameras to a US autonomous vehicle company. A 2025 report described plans to supply stereo cameras for an Intel RealSense-based security gate.

In early 2026 it said it was registered as a 3D sensing camera module partner on a global automaker's robot platform. It also said it was selected as sole supplier of 3D stereo camera modules for the next-generation model of a North American humanoid robot maker.

Its largest shareholder and parent company is Dreamtech, with a 33.69% stake. In smartphone camera modules it competes with large component makers such as LG Innotek and Samsung Electro-Mechanics, and it is trying to differentiate through 3D sensing and robotics and automotive applications.

04

Earnings analysis

On the audited annual basis, revenue was KRW 519.3 billion in 2022, fell to KRW 365.6 billion in 2023 (down 29.6% year on year), then rose to KRW 450.4 billion in 2024 (up 23.2%) and KRW 459.9 billion in 2025 (up 2.1%), increasing for two consecutive years.

Operating profit went from KRW 32.4 billion in 2022 to KRW 22.7 billion in 2023 (down 29.8%) and KRW 20.9 billion in 2024 (down 7.8%), before recovering to KRW 28.2 billion in 2025 (up 34.5%). The operating margin was 6.2% in 2022, 6.2% in 2023, 4.7% in 2024 and 6.1% in 2025, with 2024 the lowest.

In 2024 operating profit fell while profit attributable to controlling shareholders rose 11.0% to KRW 26.1 billion, suggesting that non-operating items affected the profit line that year.

Profit attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 31.3 billion in 2022, KRW 23.5 billion in 2023 (down 24.9%) and KRW 25.1 billion in 2025 (down 3.8%).

Quarterly revenue moved from KRW 112.7 billion in Q3 2025 to KRW 96.1 billion in Q4 2025 (down 14.8% quarter on quarter), then KRW 97.9 billion in Q1 2026 (up 1.9%) and KRW 93.2 billion in Q2 2026 (down 4.8%), before reaching KRW 127.2 billion in Q3 2026 (up 36.5% quarter on quarter and up 12.9% year on year).

Over the same quarters operating profit was KRW 7.46 billion, KRW 3.63 billion, KRW 3.88 billion, KRW 2.15 billion and KRW 7.03 billion. Q3 2026 operating profit was 5.6% lower than a year earlier, so the revenue gain did not fully carry through to profit.

Profit attributable to controlling shareholders peaked at KRW 4.5 billion in Q1 2026, then fell to KRW 2.28 billion in Q2 and KRW 1.96 billion in Q3, two consecutive quarterly declines, and Q3 was down 78.2% year on year.

A company performance comment dated June 2026 attributed weaker camera module results to a maturing global smartphone market, longer replacement cycles and smaller carrier subsidies, with higher memory chip component prices also hurting profitability.

Revenue rose in two consecutive years from 2024 to 2025, yet the 2025 operating margin of 6.1% remained just below the 6.2% of 2022.

05

Industry analysis

A May 2024 securities research report viewed the camera module market as having growth drivers from expanding AR and VR applications, even though smartphone shipments do not follow a steady trend. The same report projected the camera module market to reach about USD 8.05 billion by 2032.

Recent results, however, show heavy pressure from smartphones. A June 2026 performance comment cited the maturing smartphone market, longer replacement cycles and smaller carrier subsidies as causes of lower camera module results.

Because its main customer base is Samsung Electronics smartphone camera modules, this trend feeds directly into Namuga's earnings.

In smartphone camera modules, press coverage explains that a customer's new product launches, camera specifications and production yields drive results. 3D sensing is in a phase where applications are widening into robotics, autonomous driving and security equipment beyond phones.

The company's news describes a push into higher-value modules for robots with a dedicated line targeted for operation. In competition, LG Innotek and Samsung Electro-Mechanics are considerably larger than Namuga in camera modules and optical and component businesses.

Namuga is broadening its position from a smartphone-focused supplier toward 3D sensing and robotics, and how large those new businesses become in revenue is a point that quarterly results will need to show.

Its news page had previewed a rebound in mobile revenue and broader earnings improvement in the second half.

The Q3 2026 revenue increase points the same way as that preview, but operating profit and profit attributable to controlling shareholders both fell year on year, so the improvement has not yet reached the profit line.

In January 2026 the company was registered as a 3D sensing camera module partner on a global automaker's robot platform, and it said its partnership with Waymo, which has supplied in-cabin IR cameras, continues.

Reports say 3D stereo camera solutions for the next-generation model of a North American humanoid robot maker have been confirmed for volume supply, with robotics hardware results expected to appear gradually as the customer's commercialization schedule proceeds.

In 2025 the company also announced plans to supply stereo cameras for an Intel RealSense-based security gate product. Reports also describe a global sales MOU with Markinca covering joint sales of camera and 3D sensing modules.

Bull factors

Expanding robotics and 3D sensing applications

Namuga said it was selected as sole supplier of 3D stereo camera modules for the next-generation model of a North American humanoid robot maker. It announced in January 2026 that it was registered as a 3D sensing camera module partner on a global automaker's robot platform. The company says the ToF mass-production experience it built since 2011 also applies to robotics.

Q3 2026 revenue rebound

Q3 2026 revenue was KRW 127.2 billion, up 36.5% from Q2 and 12.9% year on year. Compared with KRW 96.1 billion in Q4 2025 and KRW 93.2 billion in Q2 2026, the quarterly top line stepped up clearly.

Improving balance sheet

The debt-to-equity ratio fell from 76.0% in 2022 to 49.9% in 2025, and total liabilities at the end of 2025 were KRW 85.7 billion. Operating cash flow was positive in every year from 2022 to 2025 and was KRW 37.8 billion in 2025. Operating profit rose 34.5% in 2025 to KRW 28.2 billion.

Bear factors

Continued profit decline

Profit attributable to controlling shareholders in Q3 2026 was KRW 1.96 billion, down 78.2% year on year. Operating profit in the same quarter was KRW 7.03 billion, down 5.6% from a year earlier.

Over the last four quarters that profit totaled KRW 12.4 billion, and it has declined for two consecutive quarters since the Q1 2026 peak.

Smartphone demand pressure and cost burden

A performance comment compiled in June 2026 said a maturing global smartphone market, longer replacement cycles and smaller carrier subsidies reduced camera module results. Higher memory chip component prices also worsened profitability in the same period. Both revenue and operating profit were lower than a year earlier in Q1 2026.

Customer concentration and unclear new-business contribution

The main customer base is Samsung Electronics for smartphone and tablet camera modules, so results depend on that customer's new product specifications and launch timing.

Robotics, automotive and security initiatives receive much company and press attention, but their revenue contribution is not yet clear in quarterly results. The pace at which these initiatives convert into volume production is central to any earnings recovery.

Risk factors

Customer concentration

Smartphone camera module revenue is tied to the new product cycle of a major customer, so changes in order volume can move quarterly results sharply. Revenue fell 14.8% quarter on quarter in Q4 2025 and rose 36.5% in Q3 2026. Swings of this size are consistent with dependence on a single customer's order flow.

Share price volatility

The share price rose 29.96% in one day on October 27, 2026, trading at the daily limit. Press coverage attributed part of the move to expectations for 3D sensing. How quickly those expectations are confirmed in reported results may determine how volatile the stock remains.

Component costs and currency

Higher memory chip component prices were cited as a factor behind weaker profitability in Q1 2026. If component procurement costs stay elevated, the pace of operating margin recovery could slow. Because a significant share of sales comes from North America and Europe, currency movements could also affect profit.

Overall view

Namuga's Q3 2026 revenue rose 12.9% year on year to KRW 127.2 billion, a sign of recovery. In the same quarter, operating profit was KRW 7.03 billion, down 5.6%, and profit attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 1.96 billion, down 78.2%.

On an annual basis, 2025 revenue was KRW 459.9 billion and operating profit was KRW 28.2 billion, both improved from 2024, and the debt-to-equity ratio fell to 49.9%. Bullish factors are the expansion into robotics and 3D stereo camera applications, the Q3 revenue rebound and improved financial strength.

Bearish factors are demand pressure in smartphone camera modules, higher memory component prices, a shrinking earnings base with KRW 12.4 billion in profit attributable to controlling shareholders over the last four quarters, and customer concentration.

The most important checkpoint is how visibly new business revenue appears in quarterly results. The next regular filing is the 2026 annual report, due by the end of March 2027. Share price movements are a separate record from the earnings trend.
