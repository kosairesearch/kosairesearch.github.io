02

Business structure

MICRO2NANO is a back-end semiconductor component maker founded in 2000 that changed its name in 2022 and listed on KOSDAQ in April 2023. Its core product is the probe card, which tests whether chips on a wafer operate correctly and is directly tied to semiconductor yield.

On a cumulative 2025 basis, NAND probe cards accounted for 78.1% of sales, DRAM probe cards for 11.7% and foundry for 9.8%, so the business is concentrated in NAND among memory segments.

The foundry segment produces customer-specified parts using MEMS technology, and the company also offers MEMS-based products such as pressure sensor chips for automotive brakes and ultra-fine microneedles for drug delivery.

In 2024, probe card sales were 77.25% domestic and 22.75% export, while foundry sales were 58.18% domestic and 41.82% export. SK hynix is a key customer, and Korea IR Council cited new supply to Solidigm in 2027 and expanded supply to Chinese customer G as growth drivers.

DRAM and CIS probe cards face entrenched overseas leaders, and at the 2023 listing they made up less than 5% of total sales, so the company is pursuing market entry together with SK hynix. In China, as of the 2023 listing, the company was working with Mingzhen to develop local customers.

In HBM, the HBM3E KGSD probe card has completed qualification and the HBM4 version is still under qualification.

04

Earnings analysis

On the disclosed annual figures, revenue fell from KRW 41.4 billion in 2022 to KRW 9.37 billion in 2023, a 77.4% decline, then rose to KRW 10.2 billion in 2024 (up 8.3%) and KRW 22.2 billion in 2025 (up 119.0%), an increase for two consecutive years after the 2023 low.

Among 2022 to 2025, 2022 was the largest year and 2023 the smallest. Operating profit of KRW 6.29 billion in 2022 turned into an operating loss of KRW 12.6 billion in 2023, followed by a loss of KRW 9.55 billion in 2024 and KRW 3.41 billion in 2025, with the losses narrowing in 2024 and 2025.

The company has been loss-making for three consecutive years from 2023 through 2025. Operating margin moved from 15.2% in 2022 to -134.2% in 2023, -94.1% in 2024 and -15.3% in 2025.

Customer production cuts and seasonal inventory adjustments were cited for the 2023 sales drop, and management attributed the 2025 improvement to expanded orders from major customers as NAND demand recovered.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 5.79 billion in 2022, then a net loss of KRW 7.84 billion in 2023, KRW 7.86 billion in 2024 and KRW 4.95 billion in 2025.

Net income including non-controlling interests, a separate measure, was KRW 5.79 billion in 2022 and net losses of KRW 7.84 billion, KRW 7.86 billion and KRW 4.95 billion in 2023 through 2025.

The debt-to-equity ratio was 89.6% in 2022, 60.0% in 2023, 74.2% in 2024 and 93.8% in 2025, with year-end 2025 total equity of KRW 25.8 billion, all attributable to controlling shareholders, and total liabilities of KRW 24.2 billion.

Operating cash flow was KRW 11.6 billion in 2022, then -KRW 9.72 billion, -KRW 7.65 billion and -KRW 2.29 billion in 2023 through 2025, so the outflow narrowed each year.

Quarterly, 3Q 2025 revenue was KRW 6.85 billion with an operating loss of KRW 782 million, and 4Q 2025 revenue fell 33.5% quarter on quarter to KRW 4.55 billion while the operating loss widened to KRW 2.03 billion.

In 1Q 2026, revenue rose 89.9% to KRW 8.65 billion and operating profit of KRW 775 million marked a swing to profit. 2Q 2026 revenue was KRW 11.2 billion (up 29.3%) with operating profit of KRW 1.13 billion (up 46.1%). 3Q 2026 revenue was KRW 10.9 billion, down 2.7% quarter on quarter but up 58.8% year on year, and operating profit of KRW 1.3 billion swung from a year-earlier loss.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 577 million in 1Q 2026, KRW 921 million in 2Q and KRW 574 million in 3Q, and the last four quarters together show a net loss of KRW 1.27 billion attributable to controlling shareholders.

05

Industry analysis

Probe card demand moves with memory investment cycles: sales fell sharply in 2023 as customers cut production, then rebounded from 2024 as NAND demand recovered. Korea IR Council expects SK hynix's conversion to 321-layer NAND (V9) to lift demand for related inspection probe cards.

Higher NAND stack counts make manufacturing and inspection more complex, which the same research sees as a business opportunity for component suppliers.

In HBM, the production process is more complex than conventional DRAM, and market commentary points to a structural rise in demand for process and inspection equipment. A 2025 news report said the company was the first among HBM3E rivals to pass qual testing.

In DRAM and CIS probe cards, overseas leaders hold early positions, so localization remains a challenge. On costs, rising raw material prices and labor costs were flagged as constraints on margin recovery.

In China, probe card demand is expanding centered on the country, making supply to Chinese customers an important overseas sales channel. Among Korean back-end component makers, the differentiator is the combination of MEMS-based probe cards and foundry services, while NAND concentration is also a constraint.

Outlook

The most direct forward indicator is an estimate from Korea IR Council, which projects 2026 revenue of KRW 39.4 billion, up 77.1% from the prior year, and an operating profit of KRW 1.9 billion that would mark a swing to profit.

The same estimate puts 2027 revenue at KRW 49.6 billion, up 26.0%, and operating profit at KRW 4.7 billion, up 144.0%. The firm nonetheless expected margin improvement to lag revenue growth, noting that higher raw material and labor costs could weigh on profitability.

This is a research estimate rather than a company disclosure, and the firm did not present a separate target price or investment opinion. On disclosed figures, quarterly revenue in 1Q through 3Q 2026 was KRW 8.65 billion, KRW 11.2 billion and KRW 10.9 billion, all above KRW 8 billion.

The key questions are whether SK hynix's NAND upgrade investment converts into actual probe card orders, and whether new customer supply, including Solidigm in 2027, flows through to revenue.

Expanded supply to Chinese customer G would raise the overseas share of sales and could reduce earnings swings tied to a single customer's investment schedule. In HBM, the HBM4 qualification must be completed and then followed by volume supply. The 2026 annual report will show whether annual revenue and operating profit match these estimates.

Bull factors

Market commentary also points to a structural rise in inspection equipment demand as HBM processes grow more complex.

Quarterly profit streak

The 2025 operating loss narrowed to KRW 3.41 billion from KRW 9.55 billion in 2024. 2025 revenue rose 119.0% to KRW 22.2 billion.

09

Bear factors

NAND concentration

NAND probe cards made up 78.1% of cumulative 2025 sales, so results depend heavily on the NAND investment cycle. DRAM at 11.7% and foundry at 9.8% are comparatively small. A delay in customer investment schedules could increase quarterly revenue volatility.

Limited margin recovery

The 2025 operating margin was -15.3%, still in loss territory. Korea IR Council expects margin improvement to lag revenue growth, with raw material and labor costs cited as burdens. If production and staffing costs rise alongside sales, margin recovery could be slow.

Competition and qualification risk

DRAM and CIS probe cards are dominated by overseas leaders, and at the 2023 listing they were under 5% of total sales. Even after HBM4 qualification, whether it leads to volume supply still needs confirmation. Technology competition with overseas rivals is also a variable for new orders.

10

Risk factors

Customer concentration

Sales depend heavily on investment schedules at major customers such as SK hynix. A change in a single customer's plans can increase quarterly earnings volatility. Expanding supply to Chinese customer G widens the overseas base, but the flow of Chinese orders is itself a variable.

Qualification and volume ramp

HBM3E qualification is complete, but HBM4 is still in qualification. Whether certification leads to volume supply must be confirmed, and any delay would push back the timing of a new growth leg. Revenue from new customer supply also depends on the timing of volume ramp-up.

Balance sheet and share price volatility

The debt-to-equity ratio rose to 93.8% at the end of 2025 from 74.2% in 2024. Operating cash flow was -KRW 2.29 billion, a narrower outflow but still negative. The one-year beta of 1.68 indicates share price moves larger than the KOSDAQ average.

Overall view

MICRO2NANO moved from an operating profit of KRW 6.29 billion in 2022 to an operating loss of KRW 12.6 billion in 2023, and has posted losses for three consecutive years through 2025 while narrowing the loss.

Quarterly operating profit has been positive since 1Q 2026, with two consecutive quarters of increase in 2Q and 3Q. The business is concentrated in NAND probe cards, which made up 78.1% of cumulative 2025 sales, so it is highly exposed to NAND investment cycles.

Bullish factors include NAND upgrade demand, expanding supply to Solidigm and Chinese customers, and completed HBM3E qualification. Bearish factors include NAND concentration, cost pressure, the unfinished HBM4 qualification, and competition from overseas leaders.

On finances, the debt-to-equity ratio rose to 93.8% at end-2025, and operating cash flow remains negative though the outflow narrowed. Because the last four quarters show a net loss, a P/E based on earnings cannot be calculated, and the price-to-book ratio sits above book value.

The next checkpoints are the 4Q 2026 preliminary results, the 2026 annual report, and the HBM4 qualification outcome along with Solidigm-related supply. This report is for information only and contains no buy or sell opinion or target price.
