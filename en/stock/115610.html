02

Business structure

Imagis is a fabless semiconductor design company founded in 2004 and listed on KOSDAQ in 2010. It designs integrated circuits without owning production lines and relies on outside manufacturing for output.

Its main products include Touch Controller ICs that process touch input in smartphones and notebooks, Haptic Driver ICs that deliver vibration feedback, SAR (Grip) Sensor ICs that detect proximity to the body to reduce phone electromagnetic emissions, and MST ICs for magnetic secure transmission.

Grip sensor products are reported as the main revenue source, and notebook touch ICs have been growing. In 2022, revenue was split 51.7 percent export and 48.3 percent domestic, and the company has widened overseas sales through distributors and direct exports.

Its major customers are domestic smartphone makers including Samsung Electronics, so revenue depends heavily on smartphone demand and on whether customers adopt its designs. Competition comes from small fabless firms in mobile touch and sensor ICs, and the company is small compared with large chipmakers.

The company has diversified around its Touch IC, SAR Sensor IC and Power and Haptic IC lines. It now plans to pair AI inference functions and application software with its hardware to move from component supply toward a solution business.

04

Earnings analysis

On the basis of regulatory annual filings, revenue fell from KRW 21.9 billion in 2022 to KRW 16.1 billion in 2023, a 26.5 percent decline, and to KRW 13 billion in 2024, a 19.3 percent decline, before edging up 1.8 percent to KRW 13.2 billion in 2025. 2022 was the largest year and 2024 the smallest within the 2022 to 2025 window.

Profitability moved from an operating profit of KRW 1.55 billion in 2022, a 7.1 percent margin, to an operating loss of KRW 2.31 billion in 2023, a -14.4 percent margin. The operating loss widened to KRW 5.21 billion in 2024, a -40.2 percent margin, and narrowed to KRW 1.99 billion in 2025, a -15.0 percent margin.

Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders went from KRW 1.81 billion in 2022 to a net loss of KRW 4.61 billion in 2023, then KRW 3.75 billion in 2024 and KRW 1.84 billion in 2025. Net income including non-controlling interests was disclosed at the same amounts in each of those years.

Operating cash flow was KRW 1.9 billion in 2022 and KRW 680 million in 2023, then turned negative at -KRW 3.33 billion in 2024 and -KRW 964 million in 2025.

The debt-to-equity ratio fell from 255.5 percent in 2023 and 198.0 percent in 2024 to 27.5 percent in 2025, when total equity stood at KRW 10.6 billion and total liabilities at KRW 2.92 billion.

Quarterly revenue rose from KRW 3.16 billion in the third quarter of 2025 to KRW 3.46 billion, KRW 3.61 billion, KRW 5.07 billion and KRW 5.64 billion in the third quarter of 2026, a run of four consecutive quarterly increases.

Operating results went from losses of KRW 597 million in the third quarter of 2025, KRW 164 million in the fourth quarter, KRW 489 million in the first quarter of 2026 and KRW 464 million in the second quarter, to an operating profit of KRW 79.36 million in the third quarter.

Net losses attributable to controlling shareholders narrowed and widened across those quarters before a net profit of KRW 17.99 million in the third quarter of 2026. The company has said it does not regard the third-quarter turn as a one-time result.

05

Industry analysis

Imagis is a fabless design company, so its business model differs from that of semiconductor equipment makers such as HANMI Semiconductor and EO Technics that supply production tools.

Because it owns no production lines and uses outside manufacturing, its results depend more on design competitiveness and customer adoption than on the equipment investment cycle. Its main demand source, smartphones, varies with component content and model adoption, and its notebook touch ICs track PC market trends.

Grip sensors connect to demand for lower phone electromagnetic emissions, so the regulatory environment is one pillar of product demand. Company materials describe an in-house TCE technology in its SAR (Grip) sensors that compensates for performance drift caused by outside temperature changes.

That claim comes from company and brokerage descriptions rather than independent verification. Mobile IC competition mixes large design houses with small fabless firms, and small firms tend to rely heavily on the adoption decisions of a few customers.

The company's push into AI tactile sensors and humanoid robot applications points toward reducing its dependence on mobile demand, but no disclosed figures yet show the revenue scale of that business.

Compared with equipment peers, Imagis carries a lighter capital investment burden, while its results are more exposed to customer concentration and product replacement cycles.

06

Outlook

The company has disclosed a 2026 full-year outlook of KRW 21 billion in revenue and a KRW 700 million operating loss. Revenue rose in each quarter from the first to the third quarter of 2026, but reaching the full-year revenue outlook would require fourth-quarter revenue to clearly exceed the third-quarter level.

On operating results, the first- and second-quarter losses were not offset by the third-quarter profit alone, so meeting the full-year operating loss outlook would need additional profit in the fourth quarter.

To sustain second-half profitability, the company plans to expand supply to existing customers, run products with a profit focus, and cut costs in parallel. It has also said it will pursue revenue growth and a stable earnings structure in 2027.

To expand into solutions, it plans to combine AI inference functions and application software tailored to customer needs.

A business cooperation MOU for entering the Indian automotive market with FutureCraft Partners was reported in company news, but no disclosed figures show the contract size or when revenue would be recognized.

Company direction also includes targeting humanoid robot applications that combine AI tactile sensors with AI Edge technology. The next checkpoints are whether the fourth-quarter profit holds and what the 2026 annual report shows for full-year results.

Bear factors

Three straight loss years and accumulated deficit

After swinging to an operating loss of KRW 2.31 billion in 2023, the company has posted losses through 2025. Net losses attributable to controlling shareholders were KRW 4.61 billion in 2023, KRW 3.75 billion in 2024 and KRW 1.84 billion in 2025.

Over the latest four quarters, the combined net loss attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 853 million keeps the company in a loss position.

Profit confined to a single quarter

The operating profit has so far appeared in only one quarter, the third quarter of 2026, after operating losses of KRW 489 million in the first quarter and KRW 464 million in the second. The first-quarter loss widened from the prior quarter.

The company's full-year operating loss outlook of KRW 700 million means that an annual profit is not yet part of its own projection.

Cash outflow and customer concentration

Operating cash flow was negative at KRW 3.33 billion in 2024 and KRW 964 million in 2025. Grip sensor products are the main revenue source and major customers are concentrated among domestic smartphone makers, so results are sensitive to orders and design adoption by a few clients. A change in orders from one or two customers could weigh heavily on quarterly results.

Risk factors

Earnings durability

The third-quarter profit is a single-quarter result, so whether profitability continues after the fourth quarter is the key variable. The outcome depends on whether cost efficiency and customer expansion turn into revenue growth.

Revenue rose for four straight quarters, yet operating losses widened in the first and second quarters of 2026, so quarterly volatility is high.

Customer and product concentration

The revenue base centers on grip sensors and touch ICs for smartphones and notebooks, so a drop in orders from a specific customer or a failed design win on a new model can feed directly into revenue. How much product diversification and AI solutions reduce this concentration has not yet appeared in reported results. A shift to in-house design by a large customer would be a similar risk.

Financing and shareholding changes

The company issued convertible bonds in 2023, and a sale of shares by the largest shareholder, who is also the chief executive, was reported in the same period. In 2025 a reduction in the chief executive's stake was also disclosed. Capital capacity and changes in ownership can add to share price volatility in a small-cap semiconductor stock.
