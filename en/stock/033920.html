Muhak: Core Soju Recovery Slow, Financial Assets and Shareholder Returns Carry Earnings

Operating profit rose for two consecutive quarters in the second and third quarters of 2026, but lower plant utilization and shrinking exports still weigh on the core soju business, and much of the first-half net profit came from financial asset gains.

02

Business structure

Muhak is a Busan, Ulsan and South Gyeongsang-based liquor company that makes and sells soju, liqueurs and fruit wines, and it also runs steel products and financial investment as other businesses. It has five consolidated subsidiaries.

In the first three quarters of 2024, 98.0% of total sales came from diluted soju products such as Chamisul-style flagship Joeun Day, with the rest from other liquors and steel products.

Joeun Day was launched as a low-alcohol soju in the early 2010s and held a 15.1% share of the national soju market, but similar low-alcohol products later multiplied and intensified competition. In the third quarter of 2024, domestic and overseas sales accounted for 88.6% and 11.4%, respectively.

Exports go to about 30 countries including China, the Philippines, Japan, the United States and Vietnam, and as of a November 2024 report Muhak was the only domestic liquor maker with a production facility in Hanoi, Vietnam. Export sales peaked at KRW 20.7 billion in 2022 and fell to KRW 13.5 billion in 2025.

Financial assets rose to about KRW 505 billion at the end of June 2026, with ELS accounting for 94.8%. The Chungju plant was positioned as a base for the capital region market, but its average utilization in the first half of 2026 was only 1.66%.

The business structure therefore reads as two pillars working together: liquor operations and financial asset management.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual revenue moved from KRW 152.8 billion in 2022 to KRW 146.6 billion in 2023 (down 4.1% year on year), KRW 152.1 billion in 2024 (up 3.7%) and KRW 143.8 billion in 2025 (down 5.4%). 2022 was the largest year in the 2022 to 2025 span and 2025 was the smallest.

Operating profit was KRW 15.6 billion in 2022, KRW 16.2 billion in 2023 (up 3.3%), KRW 16.9 billion in 2024 (up 4.4%) and KRW 10.2 billion in 2025 (down 39.4%). 2024 was the largest year and 2025 the smallest. The operating margin went from 10.2% and 11.0% to 11.1% in 2024 before falling to 7.1% in 2025.

Over the same period, profit attributable to controlling shareholders swung from a net loss of KRW 13.2 billion in 2022 to net income of KRW 65.4 billion in 2023, which marked a turn to profit, then KRW 48.4 billion in 2024 (down 26.0%) and KRW 50.3 billion in 2025 (up 3.9%).

In 2023, net income attributable to controlling shareholders far exceeded operating profit of KRW 16.2 billion, which suggests non-operating and financial asset gains played a large role.

Quarterly revenue was KRW 32.8 billion in the third quarter of 2025, KRW 34.9 billion in the fourth quarter (up 6.2% quarter on quarter), KRW 35.7 billion in the first quarter of 2026 (up 2.4%), KRW 37.9 billion in the second quarter (up 6.2%) and KRW 36.2 billion in the third quarter (down 4.4% quarter on quarter, but up 10.3% year on year).

Quarterly operating profit was KRW 915 million in the third quarter of 2025, KRW 2.16 billion in the fourth quarter, KRW 659 million in the first quarter of 2026 (down 69.5% quarter on quarter), KRW 2.41 billion in the second quarter and KRW 3.41 billion in the third quarter (up 272.1% year on year), with operating profit rising for two consecutive quarters after the first-quarter low.

Profit attributable to controlling shareholders moved from a net loss of KRW 11.6 billion in the first quarter of 2026 to KRW 25.5 billion in the second quarter, a turn to profit, and KRW 13.6 billion in the third quarter. The sum for the most recent four quarters is KRW 37 billion.

Press coverage tied the first-quarter weakness to a 78% drop in operating profit year on year. The second-quarter swing to profit was supported by financial asset gains rather than by the core business alone.

05

Industry analysis

The domestic liquor market has stalled in growth, and competition over health-oriented products is intensifying. Muhak was the first soju maker to launch a sugar-free, no-added-sugar product in 2019 and entered the zero-sugar segment earliest, but large soju makers later pushed zero-sugar lineups aggressively.

On costs, neutral grain spirit is a core raw material, and the price of fermented spirit rose 9.8% in April 2024, pressuring operating profit. Hite Jinro, a major rival, was building a plant in Vietnam targeting operations in 2026, according to a 2024 report.

A Daejeon and Sejong-based rival reported that its 2025 sales rose 9.3% year on year and that it returned to profit with operating profit of KRW 6.7 billion and net income of KRW 9.0 billion. On a full-year basis, Muhak ranks sixth among 11 companies in the beverages and liquor sector on operating margin.

Liquor makers are turning to overseas markets as domestic consumption falls, while Muhak's exports have declined since 2022. Its own production base in Vietnam is cited in the industry as room for export-specific product development. Its position within the sector depends more on whether profitability recovers than on scale.

06

Outlook

In January 2026, Muhak disclosed a corporate value-up plan that sets a medium-term target of 0.7 times price-to-book by 2027.

Under the same plan, the shareholder return ratio is to rise from 30.9% in 2025 to the mid-30% range by 2027, and about 5% of annual net income is to go to share buybacks and cancellations over three years.

The company aims to lift the export share from a single-digit level to 15% by 2027, with separate pushes into high-margin markets such as the United States and Vietnam alongside high-growth markets. Some commentators note that the strategy's earnings visibility has not yet been fully established.

On non-core asset disposals, a 2026 report said Muhak was looking for buyers for its Yongin factory and logistics center, with no price or timing set.

The company attributes the overseas slowdown to weaker global economic conditions and changing local consumer trends, and says it will pursue a turnaround through local marketing and new sales channels. Cash dividends decided for the first three quarters of 2026 total about KRW 12.3 billion.

The next checkpoints are whether operating profit growth continues after the fourth quarter of 2026, and how the export share target is tracked in filings.

08

Bull factors

Quarterly operating profit recovery

Operating profit bottomed at KRW 659 million in the first quarter of 2026 and then rose to KRW 2.41 billion in the second quarter and KRW 3.41 billion in the third, two consecutive quarterly gains. Third-quarter operating profit was up 272.1% year on year, though first-half operating profit still fell 57.1%.

Expanded shareholder return plan

The company said it would raise its shareholder return ratio from 30.9% in 2025 to the mid-30% range by 2027. It plans to put about 5% of annual net income into share buybacks and cancellations over three years.

Balance sheet stability

The debt ratio was 17.4% at the end of 2025, with total liabilities of KRW 106.5 billion against total equity of KRW 613.4 billion. After a net loss in 2022 and a turn to profit in 2023, profit attributable to controlling shareholders continued in 2024 and 2025.

09

Bear factors

Low utilization of core production assets

The Chungju plant averaged 1.66% utilization in the first half of 2026. The Changwon No. 2 plant's utilization fell from 150.3% in 2022 to 70.0% in 2025. The operating margin dropped from 11.1% in 2024 to 7.1% in 2025.

Shrinking exports and overseas losses

Export sales peaked at KRW 20.7 billion in 2022, then fell to KRW 16.3 billion in 2023, rose to KRW 17.2 billion in 2024 and fell to KRW 13.5 billion in 2025. The Vietnam subsidiary's sales were essentially flat in 2025 at KRW 1.44 billion, and it has posted net losses every year.

Reliance on financial gains

First-half 2026 operating profit fell 57.1% to KRW 30.7 million... more precisely KRW 3.07 billion, while net gains from financial instruments were about KRW 12.9 billion.

In the first quarter of 2026, operating profit fell 78% year on year and the company posted a loss of KRW 11.6 billion attributable to controlling shareholders. Core business profit alone does not yet show a clear recovery.

10

Risk factors

Financial asset valuation volatility

At the end of June 2026, financial assets at fair value through profit or loss were about KRW 505 billion, with ELS making up 94.8% of the total. Valuation losses for the same period were about KRW 13 billion.

Because these gains and losses weigh heavily on results, market swings can pass directly into earnings volatility.

Concentrated ownership

As of the end of the first quarter of 2026, chairman Choi Jae-ho held 34.78%, and Choi Nak-jun, the CEO, held 15.04%. Including related parties, insiders hold 61.44%. Each quarterly dividend therefore sends more than half of the payout to the controlling family and related parties.

Competition and input costs

Competition over health-oriented products is intensifying, and in 2024 a rise in the price of neutral grain spirit was cited as a factor behind lower operating profit. Hite Jinro's plant under construction in Vietnam adds to competition in overseas markets.

Overall view

In 2026, Muhak's operating profit rose for two consecutive quarters after the first-quarter low, yet first-half operating profit still fell 57.1% year on year.

In the core soju business, Chungju plant utilization stayed at 1.66% in the first half of 2026, and exports fell from KRW 20.7 billion in 2022 to KRW 13.5 billion in 2025. First-half net profit of about KRW 13.9 billion was heavily shaped by financial asset gains and losses of about KRW 12.9 billion.

Reading earnings quality requires separating operating profit from financial instrument results. On shareholder returns, cash dividends decided for the first three quarters of 2026 total about KRW 12.3 billion, and the company has set a 2027 return target in the mid-30% range along with share cancellations.

Strengths include the quarterly operating profit recovery, a debt ratio of 17.4% at the end of 2025, and the return plan. Weaknesses include low plant utilization, falling exports, a dividend structure concentrated in insider holdings of 61.44%, and volatility in financial assets.

The next checkpoints are the separation of operating and financial results in the 2026 annual report and the shareholder return trajectory toward 2027.
