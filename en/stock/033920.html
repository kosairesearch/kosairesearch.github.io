Core Soju Business Softens as Financial Gains Swing Earnings

02

Business structure

Muhak was founded in 1929, incorporated in 1973, and listed on the KOSPI in 2010 as a comprehensive alcoholic beverage maker based in the Gyeongnam and Busan region.

Its business is divided into a liquor segment producing diluted soju, liqueurs and fruit wines, and other segments covering steel product manufacturing and sales and financial investment, with five consolidated subsidiaries.

Its flagship brand is the low-alcohol soju 'Joeun Day,' which it upgraded and relaunched as 'All New Joeun Day' in March 2026. The new product is a 15.7% ABV low-alcohol soju featuring 100% domestic rice-based distilled liquor and a 72-hour oxygen-aging process.

It retains a 'zero added sugar' concept, aligning with health-conscious consumption trends. Beyond its liquor business, Muhak has been notable for actively investing part of its assets in financial instruments such as equity-linked securities, a practice brokerages have described as unusual for a beverage maker.

At the national level, Hite Jinro and Lotte Chilsung Beverage form a duopoly, while Muhak operates as a regional mid-sized player. On the export front, 2024 export revenue rose to KRW 17.2 billion from KRW 16.3 billion a year earlier, though the increase was modest.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual revenue moved from KRW 152.8 billion in 2022 to KRW 146.6 billion in 2023 (down 4.1%), KRW 152.1 billion in 2024 (up 3.7%), and KRW 143.8 billion in 2025 (down 5.4%).

Operating profit rose gradually from KRW 15.6 billion in 2022 to KRW 16.9 billion in 2024 before falling sharply to KRW 10.2 billion in 2025, a 39.4% decline, with the operating margin slipping from 11.1% in 2024 to 7.1% in 2025.

Net profit attributable to owners swung from a loss of KRW 13.2 billion in 2022 to a gain of KRW 65.4 billion in 2023, then KRW 48.4 billion in 2024 (down 26.0%) and KRW 50.3 billion in 2025 (up 3.9%); unlike the operating profit trend, this relative resilience in net profit reflected gains and losses on financial assets.

On a quarterly basis, revenue bottomed at KRW 32.8 billion in Q3 2025 (down 15.3% quarter-on-quarter) and then rose for three consecutive quarters through Q2 2026, reaching KRW 37.9 billion.

Operating profit, however, shrank from KRW 4.16 billion in Q2 2025 to KRW 915 million in Q3 2025 (down 78.0%) and KRW 659 million in Q1 2026 (down 69.5%) before rebounding to KRW 2.41 billion in Q2 2026.

Net profit attributable to owners was far more volatile than operating profit, swinging from an KRW 11.6 billion loss in Q1 2026 to a KRW 25.5 billion gain in Q2 2026.

According to news reports analyzing regulatory filings, the Q1 2026 loss stemmed mainly from a jump in valuation losses on financial assets measured at fair value through profit or loss, which rose from KRW 2.1 billion to KRW 19.3 billion year-on-year, including losses linked to equity-linked securities.

Conversely, the Q2 2026 net profit of KRW 25.5 billion, more than ten times the operating profit of KRW 2.41 billion, was attributable to other gains of roughly KRW 24.6 billion.

Operating cash flow declined steadily from KRW 28.1 billion in 2022 to KRW 6.28 billion in 2025, pointing to weakening cash generation from the core business.

05

Industry analysis

Domestic soju shipment volume fell for three consecutive years, from 862,000 kiloliters in 2022 to 844,000 kiloliters in 2023 and 816,000 kiloliters in 2024, dropping below 800,000 kiloliters for the first time.

In terms of market structure, Hite Jinro and Lotte Chilsung Beverage together hold 83.8% of the market, while Muhak holds 6.0%, followed by Sunyang Soju at 2.6%, Geumbokju at 2.5% and Daesun Distilling at 2.2%, with numerous smaller players splitting the remaining roughly 16%.

Even as overall soju consumption shrinks, the number of competing companies and brands has increased, and the industry expects distribution and marketing competition to intensify further.

The prospect of a major brewer entering the distribution channel is also making the competitive environment more complex for regional soju makers.

Muhak achieved its best-ever results in 2015 on the strength of the Joeun Day boom in the 2010s, but its nationwide market share, which stood above 14% as of 2014, has since fallen to the single digits.

A common trend among regional soju makers, including Muhak as well as Busan-based Daesun Distilling and Daegu-based Geumbokju, is a shrinking footprint under pressure from larger, better-capitalized rivals.

06

Outlook

Muhak has disclosed a value-up plan setting a goal of raising its price-to-book ratio, which had hovered around 0.3x, to 0.7x by 2027.

The plan includes selling non-core real estate with limited direct relevance to its operations, such as the Seoul Good Day Building, to improve capital efficiency, regularizing quarterly dividends, and expanding its shareholder return ratio above peer levels through share buybacks and cancellations.

It also pledged to allocate roughly 5% of annual standalone net profit to share buybacks and cancellations over the next three years.

However, its existing treasury shares were handled not through cancellation but through share swaps with business partners Gumbi and Samsung Gongjo, creating some divergence between the plan's stated intent and actual follow-through.

On shareholder returns, the company set a 2025 year-end dividend of KRW 176 per common share; combined with quarterly dividends of KRW 260 and KRW 130, total dividends came to roughly KRW 15.2 billion, with a payout ratio of 30.12%, meeting the high-dividend company criteria under the Special Tax Treatment Control Act.

Alongside capital efficiency reforms and enhanced shareholder returns, the company has also positioned accelerated global growth as a pillar of its value-up plan.

Bull factors

Value-Up Plan and Expanded Shareholder Returns

Muhak is pursuing a plan to achieve a price-to-book ratio of 0.7x by 2027 through non-core real estate sales, share buybacks and cancellations, and regularized quarterly dividends. It has also pledged to allocate roughly 5% of annual standalone net profit to share buybacks and cancellations over the next three years.

Its 2025 payout ratio of 30.12% met the high-dividend company criteria under the Special Tax Treatment Control Act.

Product Renewal Aligned with Low-ABV Trend

The 'All New Joeun Day' launched in March 2026 is a 15.7% ABV low-alcohol soju featuring 100% domestic rice-based distilled liquor and a 72-hour oxygen-aging process, retaining a zero-added-sugar concept. This extends the company's brand renewal strategy in response to health-conscious and low-ABV consumption trends.

Low Debt Ratio and Financial Asset Management Track Record

As of end-2025, the debt ratio stood at 17.4% and total equity at KRW 613.4 billion, indicating a relatively stable financial structure.

In periods when valuation gains on financial assets occurred, net profit has previously far exceeded operating profit, suggesting these holdings can be an additional source of earnings variability depending on market conditions.

Bear factors

Deteriorating Core Profitability

The operating margin fell from 11.1% in 2024 to 7.1% in 2025, and operating profit shrank to just KRW 659 million in Q1 2026. A three-year consecutive decline in domestic soju shipment volume is cited as a backdrop for this profitability erosion.

Weaker National and Regional Market Position

Muhak's share of the national soju market stands at around 6.0%, placing it at a disadvantage against the Hite Jinro-Lotte Chilsung Beverage duopoly that controls 83.8% of the market. Its nationwide share, which exceeded 14% as of 2014, has since shrunk to single digits.

Net Profit Volatility and Value-Up Execution Credibility

Net profit attributable to owners has swung sharply between quarterly losses and large gains depending on valuation changes in financial assets such as equity-linked securities.

While the value-up plan pledged share cancellations, existing treasury shares were instead handled through swaps with business partners, raising questions about the credibility of the plan's execution.

10

Risk factors

Industry and Competitive Risk

Domestic soju consumption is in a structural downtrend with shipment volume declining for three straight years, while competition from new products such as zero-sugar and low-ABV soju intensifies.

With a major brewer reportedly entering the distribution channel, marketing and distribution competition among players is expected to become even more intense.

Financial Asset Management Risk

Muhak has invested a significant portion of its total assets in financial instruments such as equity-linked securities, a structure in which valuation gains and losses move significantly with index fluctuations.

In Q1 2026, a decline in relevant indices amid geopolitical tensions in the Middle East led to a surge in valuation losses that drove a net loss, illustrating how market volatility can directly affect reported earnings going forward.

Governance and Execution Risk

Observers have noted that if dividend guidance execution falters or non-core asset sales are delayed, the value-up plan risks remaining a one-off announcement.

Given the precedent of swapping existing treasury shares with business partners rather than cancelling them, the actual execution of future buyback and cancellation plans warrants continued monitoring.

Overall view

Muhak's revenue fluctuated in the mid-to-upper KRW 140 billion range from 2022 to 2025, while 2025 operating profit fell 39.4% year-on-year, signaling a clear weakening in core profitability.

In contrast, net profit attributable to owners showed volatility disconnected from operating performance, swinging from a loss in Q1 2026 to a large gain in Q2 2026 under the influence of valuation changes in financial assets such as equity-linked securities.

As the price-to-book ratio has remained at a low level, the company has disclosed a value-up plan targeting a price-to-book ratio of 0.7x by 2027, pursuing non-core asset sales, regularized quarterly dividends, and share buybacks and cancellations.

However, given the precedent of swapping existing treasury shares with business partners rather than cancelling them, the actual pace and method of execution warrant continued observation.

On the industry side, domestic soju shipment volume has declined for three consecutive years while the Hite Jinro-Lotte Chilsung Beverage duopoly solidifies, making for a challenging competitive environment for regional soju makers including Muhak.

Before forming any investment judgment, it is worth tracking the operating margin trend in upcoming quarterly reports, the scale of financial asset valuation effects, and disclosures on the execution of the value-up plan.
