02

Business structure

NuriFlex is a smart energy solutions company whose core business is advanced metering infrastructure (AMI), including electricity meters.

It supplies AMI systems for electricity, water, gas and heat that use wired and wireless communication, and a company profile site says the systems have been adopted by Korea Electric Power Corporation (KEPCO) and 46 overseas utilities.

Based on Q2 2026 revenue, intelligent metering infrastructure and Green New Deal projects accounted for 50%, communication equipment development and sales for 41%, nano materials for 5%, and software for 4%.

Another breakdown that uses different categories puts the power IoT AMI business at 81.26%, the smart energy business (AMI, EMS, ESS and video security) at 9.43%, barcode and RFID industrial IoT at 5.04%, and other at 4.27%, so the weights shift with the classification method.

In overseas markets, NuriFlex signed a USD 69.55 million smart prepaid AMI supply contract with Guinea's power utility in January 2026, entering the West African market.

In March 2026 its Vietnam unit signed a rooftop solar pilot contract with a footwear manufacturer, and in May 2026 it signed a development agreement with South Africa's Joule Energy.

In February 2026 the company disclosed an off-market disposal of 241,000 treasury shares to a strategic partner to strengthen cooperation. Overseas utility orders depend on delivery track records and local partnerships, so the timing of project-based revenue recognition tends to drive swings in quarterly results.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual revenue rose from KRW 124.7 billion in 2022 to KRW 132.7 billion in 2023 (up 6.4% year on year), making 2023 the largest year among 2022 to 2025. Revenue then fell 2.6% to KRW 129.3 billion in 2024 and 21.5% to KRW 101.6 billion in 2025, a decline in each of the two years from 2024 through 2025.

Operating profit dropped from KRW 10.4 billion in 2022 to KRW 1.98 billion in 2023 (down 81.0%), swung to an operating loss of KRW 8.15 billion in 2024, and narrowed to an operating loss of KRW 6.09 billion in 2025. The operating margin was 8.3% in 2022, 1.5% in 2023, -6.3% in 2024 and -6.0% in 2025.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders reached KRW 2.94 billion in 2023 (up 369.1%), then turned into losses of KRW 4.15 billion in 2024 and KRW 6.53 billion in 2025, two straight years of losses. Net loss including non-controlling interests was KRW 6.67 billion in 2025.

In its 2025 results disclosure, the company said a bad-debt provision booked on a one-off basis drove the operating and net losses, and it also cited lower non-operating income from currency movements as a factor widening the net loss.

Operating cash flow went from KRW 25.1 billion in 2023 to -KRW 17.3 billion in 2024 and -KRW 499 million in 2025. According to fnguide data, bad-debt expenses were KRW 7.4 billion in 2024 and KRW 9.3 billion in 2025.

By quarter, Q4 2025 revenue of KRW 40.4 billion (up 82.8% from the prior quarter) was the largest of the last five quarters, yet that quarter still posted an operating loss of KRW 3.07 billion.

Q1 2026 revenue fell 62.8% quarter on quarter to KRW 15 billion, with an operating loss of KRW 3.23 billion, but net income attributable to controlling shareholders turned positive at KRW 579 million.

Fnguide data show financial asset valuation gains of KRW 2.2 billion in that quarter, suggesting non-operating items played a large part in the swing.

Q2 2026 revenue rose 68.2% to KRW 25.3 billion and the operating loss narrowed to KRW 88.62 million, with net income attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 4.88 billion.

Q3 2026 revenue was KRW 16.7 billion, down 33.9% quarter on quarter and 24.5% year on year, with an operating loss of KRW 2.22 billion and a net loss attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 7.68 billion. Over the last four quarters, the net loss attributable to controlling shareholders totaled KRW 3.54 billion.

Industry analysis

On the demand side, the need for grid digitalization and energy efficiency management in power networks is described as continuing. NuriFlex's overseas AMI business was also cited as a driver of revenue growth in its Q3 2024 announcement.

A February 2023 research report said AMI product sales recovered in both domestic and export markets as deferred demand from the COVID-19 period resumed. That analysis dates from 2023, so it offers limited evidence about where the cycle stands today.

In an Alphasquare comparison within the same IT and software industry, the company's 2025 full-year operating margin was -5.99%, above the industry average of -20.89%. In the same dataset, revenue size is below the industry average.

Competition in power AMI is understood to hinge on local utility tenders and partnerships, and NuriFlex has been adding local partners in Vietnam, South Africa and China to widen its entry routes.

Extending smart metering into an energy data platform can be read as an attempt to widen the business into power demand management. A margin above the industry average does not by itself mean earnings have recovered, because operating losses persisted from 2024 through 2025 and again in Q3 2026.

Overseas projects began with the January 2026 AMI supply contract in Guinea, followed by a rooftop solar pilot in Vietnam in March, an MOU for business cooperation with the Chongqing Group in China in April, a development agreement with Joule Energy in South Africa in May, and an O2O platform development and operation contract with the Chongqing Group in June.

The Guinea contract was disclosed at USD 69.55 million, but when and how much of it is reflected in reported revenue is the next point to watch.

Quarterly revenue in 2026 moved sharply, at KRW 15 billion, KRW 25.3 billion and KRW 16.7 billion across Q1 to Q3, so the timing of large project recognition largely determines reported results.

As financial reference points, total equity at the end of 2025 was KRW 70.7 billion and total liabilities were KRW 82.9 billion, while cash and cash equivalents fell from KRW 14.8 billion at the end of 2025 to KRW 9 billion at the end of March 2026.

The March 2026 rooftop solar pilot in Vietnam and the May 2026 development agreement in South Africa align with this direction. Risk factors

Order and collection risk

Overseas AMI projects can involve long collection periods, so continued growth in long-term receivables would weigh on earnings and cash flow. The 2025 results were hit by a one-off bad-debt provision that widened the operating loss. Bad-debt expenses were KRW 7.4 billion in 2024 and KRW 9.3 billion in 2025.

Currency and non-operating swings

The company cited lower non-operating income from currency movements as a factor widening the 2025 net loss. The Q1 2026 return to net profit also appears to have relied heavily on non-operating items such as financial asset valuation gains. Large swings in non-operating items can push core operating results and net income in opposite directions.

Liquidity and balance sheet burden

Cash and cash equivalents fell to KRW 9 billion at the end of March 2026 from KRW 14.8 billion at the end of 2025. Total liabilities stood at KRW 84.5 billion at the end of March 2026, still a large balance. Whether the company raises additional funding, and in what size, is the next item to watch.

Overall view

NuriFlex is a smart energy company centered on power AMI, and overseas utility adoption and new contracts are presented as the basis for growth. Revenue peaked at KRW 132.7 billion in 2023 and then declined in 2024 and 2025, while net income attributable to controlling shareholders was negative from 2024 through 2025.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders turned positive in Q1 and Q2 2026, but Q3 2026 swung back to a KRW 7.68 billion loss. The four-quarter total of that net loss is KRW 3.54 billion.

On the financial side, the debt ratio rose from 93.5% in 2022 to 117.3% in 2025, and operating cash flow was -KRW 17.3 billion in 2024 and -KRW 499 million in 2025.

The bull case rests on the overseas project pipeline and a share price below book equity, while the bear case rests on quarterly revenue swings and a loss-making structure.
