FPCB Turnaround Continues, Share Supply Stays Fluid

02

Business structure

Founded in 2004, EV Advanced Material is a flexible printed circuit board (FPCB) specialist organized around two business lines: FPCB and transparent display.

The FPCB business spans automotive, mobile, EV battery, and medical/healthcare product lines, while the transparent display business includes the ACTVISION-branded transparent display and LED signage products.

The company started in FPCBs for automotive and IT devices before expanding into EV battery FPCBs, and it has also entered the digital signage market through transparent displays. Its Vietnamese subsidiary, EVAM VINA, handles overseas FPCB production and serves as a hub for manufacturing efficiency and cost reduction.

More recently, the company has been investing to expand its production lines from under 750mm to over 1 meter in width, responding to a trend toward larger components in the EV, battery, and energy storage system (ESS) markets.

In 2021 it invested $8.5 million in a Series E funding round of Taiwan-based solid-state battery maker ProLogium Technology, a holding classified as a strategic investment separate from its core business.

Its controlling shareholder is Nexturn&RollKorea, part of the ROA&Co group, which also includes affiliates spanning clean energy, semiconductors, and other battery-related businesses.

Amid a slowdown (often termed a chasm) in downstream EV and battery demand, the company has pursued selective orders focused on higher-margin products to defend profitability.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual revenue rose from KRW 58.1 billion in 2022 to KRW 62.8 billion in 2023 (up 8.1% year over year) and KRW 81.9 billion in 2024 (up 30.5%), before falling 28.0% to KRW 59 billion in 2025.

Operating profit stayed in loss at KRW 2.86 billion in 2022 and KRW 5.77 billion in 2023, turned positive at KRW 2.57 billion in 2024, then swung back to a KRW 3.18 billion operating loss in 2025.

Controlling shareholder net income turned from a KRW 10.7 billion loss in 2022 to a KRW 1.56 billion profit in 2023, but then posted losses of KRW 8.29 billion in 2024 and KRW 18.7 billion in 2025, marking two consecutive years of losses.

Net income including non-controlling interests showed an even larger loss of KRW 21.6 billion in 2025 than the controlling shareholder figure.

On a quarterly basis, revenue declined from KRW 14.5 billion in Q2 2025 to KRW 13.8 billion in Q3 (down 4.5%) and KRW 11.3 billion in Q4 (down 18.6%), before rebounding for two straight quarters to KRW 15.9 billion in Q1 2026 (up 40.9%) and KRW 20.3 billion in Q2 2026 (up 28.1% quarter over quarter and 40.2% year over year).

Operating profit moved from losses of KRW 1.96 billion in Q3 2025 and KRW 1.79 billion in Q4 2025 (loss narrowing) to a turnaround of KRW 355 million in Q1 2026, then expanded to KRW 1.51 billion in Q2 2026, up 325.6% quarter over quarter and swinging from a loss a year earlier.

Controlling shareholder net income also turned positive at KRW 201 million in Q1 2026 and grew to KRW 1.45 billion in Q2 2026, up 624.4% quarter over quarter and reversing from a year-earlier loss, though the trailing four quarters from Q3 2025 through Q2 2026 still show a cumulative controlling shareholder net loss of KRW 8.02 billion.

Operating cash flow turned negative at KRW 3.49 billion in 2025, reversing from positive KRW 9.89 billion in 2024.

05

Industry analysis

The FPCB industry is a classic component materials business tied to demand for EV batteries, automotive electronics, and the miniaturization of mobile devices, making it sensitive to downstream demand cycles.

Over the past two to three years, the EV market has gone through a so-called chasm phase of slowing demand growth, which has broadly pressured utilization rates and profitability across battery and related component makers.

South Korea's battery supply chain includes large materials and cell makers such as LG Energy Solution, Samsung SDI, POSCO Future M, L&F, and Ecopro BM, with EV Advanced Material positioned further downstream as a smaller component specialist.

In the competitive landscape, quality, delivery reliability, and supply chain management (SCM) stability with customers are key variables, and the company cites strategic SCM as a source of competitiveness.

At the same time, competition in solid-state batteries, seen as a next-generation technology, is intensifying as large players like Samsung SDI and LG Energy Solution compete alongside specialized overseas firms such as Taiwan's ProLogium, which has announced entry into mass production.

EV Advanced Material has indirect exposure to this commercialization trend through its early equity investment in ProLogium, though this is largely a separate investment asset from its core FPCB manufacturing and sales business.

With the pace and scale of downstream demand recovery still uncertain, profitability among component makers is likely to diverge depending on their ability to expand the share of higher-value-added products and improve cost structures.

06

Outlook

Following consecutive quarterly profits in Q1 and Q2 2026, the company disclosed that its consolidated first-half results also showed a continued improvement trend.

A company representative said unifying production in Vietnam and improving manufacturing cost structures are expected to lift both full-year revenue and operating profit versus the prior year.

In October 2026, the company raised a total of KRW 8.5 billion through a KRW 5.5 billion third-party allotment to controlling shareholder Nexturn&RollKorea and a KRW 3 billion minor public offering, with proceeds earmarked for building large-area (1-meter-plus) FPCB production equipment for EV, battery, and ESS applications.

The newly issued 1,177,884 shares are scheduled to list on October 27, 2026, with the third-party allotment portion subject to a one-year lock-up. Earlier, in July 2026, the company decided on a 10-for-1 capital reduction to offset accumulated losses, a preemptive step to manage concerns over capital impairment.

Its investment asset ProLogium has entered mass production of 3.5-generation lithium ceramic solid-state batteries at its Taoyuan, Taiwan plant, and has stated it is building a gigafactory in Dunkirk, France targeting fourth-generation solid-state battery production in 2028 and 12 GWh of capacity by 2032.

However, these plans are based on ProLogium's own disclosures and are not a business directly reflected in EV Advanced Material's consolidated results.

The actual utilization and order performance of the new large-area FPCB line, once visible, is likely to be a key variable for gauging the company's future earnings trend.

08

Bull factors

Two Straight Quarters of Operating Profit

In Q1 2026, operating profit was KRW 355 million, followed by KRW 1.51 billion in Q2, a 325.6% increase from the previous quarter, expanding the scale of profitability.

Revenue also rose for two consecutive quarters in Q1 and Q2 2026 after bottoming out in Q4 2025, showing a trend of simultaneous improvement in top-line growth and profitability. Net income attributable to controlling interests also turned profitable for two consecutive quarters, showing a trend of narrowing losses.

Large-Area FPCB Investment and Controlling Shareholder Commitment

In October 2026, the largest shareholder, Nexton&Roll Korea, participated in a third-party allotment capital increase worth KRW 5.5 billion, raising its stake to over 25%, with the stake including special related parties expanding to 28.13%.

The raised funds are planned to be invested in building a production line for large-area FPCB of 1m or more, in response to the trend toward larger EV, battery, and ESS markets.

The structure in which the largest shareholder repeatedly injects capital directly can be interpreted as a factor demonstrating stability in management control and a commitment to business expansion.

Progress in ProLogium Equity Stake

ProLogium of Taiwan, in which $8.5 million was invested in 2021, announced that it has begun mass production of 3.5th generation lithium ceramic all-solid-state batteries at its Taoyuan plant.

ProLogium has also received investment from Mercedes-Benz, POSCO Holdings, and others, and aims to achieve mass production of 4th generation batteries by 2028 through its Dunkirk Gigafactory in France.

If the commercialization of all-solid-state batteries gains momentum, interest may continue in the value of the equity stake held by EV Advanced Materials.

Bear factors

Still Two Consecutive Years of Annual Losses

Net losses attributable to controlling interests continued for two consecutive years from 2024 to 2025, and in 2025 revenue also declined 28.0% year-over-year, resulting in a contraction in scale as well.

Over the most recent four quarters (Q3 2025 to Q2 2026), cumulative net losses attributable to controlling interests of KRW 8.02 billion have accumulated, indicating that the turn to profitability in the most recent two quarters has not yet offset the overall annual loss structure.

Given the large volatility in quarterly performance, it is necessary to verify the sustainability of the profitable trend every quarter.

Dilution and Supply Pressure from Frequent Capital Changes

Following the resolution in July 2026 for a 10-to-1 reverse stock split to cover accumulated deficits, in October an additional 1,177,884 new shares were issued combining a third-party allotment and a small-scale public offering.

The new shares are scheduled to be listed on October 27, 2026, and since the small-scale public offering shares are not subject to mandatory holding periods, there is a possibility of increased trading volume immediately after listing.

Repeated frequent capital increases and reverse stock splits reduce the comparability of per-share metrics and increase the burden on investors in tracking the capital structure.

Downstream Demand Slowdown and Cash Flow Volatility

Operating cash flow in 2025 was -KRW 3.49 billion, turning negative from the positive KRW 9.89 billion recorded in 2024. Amid the ongoing slowdown in EV market demand (chasm phase), changes in orders from downstream customers can directly affect revenue and profitability.

Since the ProLogium investment asset is classified as a non-core business, the pace of profitability recovery in the core FPCB business itself remains a more important variable.

Risk factors

Earnings Volatility and Customer Concentration

Annual operating profit/loss has frequently changed direction, repeating a pattern of loss-loss-profit-loss from 2022 to 2025. Quarterly results have also shown significant volatility, with losses widening in Q3 and Q4 2025 followed by a sharp turnaround to profitability in Q1 and Q2 2026.

If there is high dependence on supply to a specific major customer, changes in that customer's orders can directly affect performance.

Capital Structure and Governance Changes

In 2026 alone, a series of events occurred in succession, including a 10-to-1 reverse stock split, a third-party allotment capital increase, and a small-scale public offering, causing significant short-term changes in capital and the number of shares issued.

The rapid increase in the stake of the largest shareholder, Nexton&Roll Korea, from 17.3% to 28.13% (including special related parties) within a short period also demonstrates the speed of changes in governance structure.

If additional fundraising or changes in equity stakes continue, it will be necessary to continuously monitor the possibility of dilution for existing shareholders.

Uncertainty Around Non-Core Investment Assets

The ProLogium equity stake is a strategic investment made in 2021 worth $8.5 million, and ProLogium's pursuit of a Nasdaq listing or its mass production schedule is entirely dependent on ProLogium's own business progress.

Changes in the valuation of this equity stake have low direct relevance to the core FPCB business performance of EV Advanced Materials. As this is a minority equity investment in a foreign company, there is relatively greater uncertainty in information accessibility and valuation.

12

Overall view

EV Advanced Material saw declining revenue, an operating loss, and a widening controlling shareholder net loss for full-year 2025, but it has shown early signs of a turnaround with two consecutive quarters of operating profit and controlling shareholder net profit in Q1 and Q2 2026.

Still, the trailing four quarters show an accumulated controlling shareholder net loss, so it is premature to conclude that two profitable quarters have offset the annual loss structure.

Throughout 2026, a 10-for-1 capital reduction, repeated capital participation by the controlling shareholder, and fundraising for large-area FPCB equipment have occurred in quick succession, rapidly reshaping the company's capital structure and share supply.

Its early strategic stake in solid-state battery maker ProLogium has drawn market attention separate from its core business, but the value of that asset depends on ProLogium's own business progress.

For investors, it will be important to watch whether quarterly profitability continues beyond Q3 and how supply-demand dynamics evolve following the new share listing.
