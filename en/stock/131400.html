02

Business structure

EV Advanced Material is a flexible printed circuit board (FPCB) specialist, with FPCB as its core business and displays as a newer segment. As electric vehicle and battery pack structures are simplified and energy density rises, demand for large-area FPCBs that replace heavy copper wiring harnesses is growing quickly.

The large-area lines the company is adding run at 1 meter or more and serve electric vehicles, secondary batteries and energy storage systems (ESS). Its display business saw Q1 2026 sales of KRW 1.8 billion, up 125% from KRW 800 million a year earlier, and full-year 2025 display sales were KRW 2.7 billion.

In a January 19, 2026 filing, the company said it would transfer 4,140,224 shares of an engineering company for KRW 24 billion, citing balance sheet improvement and business efficiency. Its largest shareholder holds 17.3%.

Its investee ProLogium, a Taiwan solid-state battery developer, said at CES 2026 that it would develop solid-state battery modules for electric vehicles with Germany's FEV Group.

Customer mix and segment revenue shares are not disclosed in figures here, so the business is best read through its FPCB manufacturing capability and high-difficulty order intake.

Unlike cathode and anode material producers in the same sector, its core product is a component whose economics do not move directly with material price cycles.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual sales rose from KRW 58.1 billion in 2022 to KRW 62.8 billion in 2023 (up 8.1% year on year) and KRW 81.9 billion in 2024 (up 30.5%), making 2024 the largest year of 2022 to 2025, before falling to KRW 59 billion in 2025 (down 28.0%).

Operating results moved from an operating loss of KRW 2.86 billion in 2022 and KRW 5.77 billion in 2023 to an operating profit of KRW 2.57 billion in 2024, then back to an operating loss of KRW 3.18 billion in 2025. The operating margin went from 3.1% in 2024 to -5.4% in 2025.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders turned to KRW 1.56 billion in 2023 but was a loss of KRW 8.29 billion in 2024 and KRW 18.7 billion in 2025, a widening deficit. Including non-controlling interests, the 2025 net loss was KRW 21.6 billion.

Operating and bottom-line results have repeatedly pointed in different directions: 2024 posted an operating profit of KRW 2.57 billion yet a controlling-shareholder net loss of KRW 8.29 billion. The items that separate these levels should be checked in the business report's income statement and notes.

Quarterly sales fell to KRW 11.3 billion in Q4 2025 (down 18.6% from the prior quarter) and then rose to KRW 15.9 billion in Q1 2026, KRW 20.3 billion in Q2 and KRW 22.1 billion in Q3, a three-quarter increase.

Operating profit moved from an operating loss of KRW 1.79 billion in Q4 2025 to an operating profit of KRW 355 million in Q1 2026, then KRW 1.51 billion in Q2 (up 325.6% from the prior quarter) and KRW 1.11 billion in Q3 (down 26.7%).

The company attributed the first-half results to company-wide cost structure reforms and a higher-margin mix of high value-added orders. Controlling-shareholder net income went from KRW 201 million in Q1 2026 to KRW 1.45 billion in Q2 and a KRW 2.58 billion loss in Q3. Q3 2026 combined higher sales, lower operating profit and a swing to a net loss in the same quarter.

05

Industry analysis

Demand for large-area FPCBs tracks changes in EV and battery pack design. Large-area FPCBs can replace conventional wiring harnesses and their range of use is expanding with simpler and lighter battery pack structures.

The company says suppliers with high-difficulty processes and exposure equipment are limited, which it presents as an entry barrier. That is the company's own view, so market share and competitor order volumes need to be checked against independent data.

On the cycle, the company said it had gone through difficult years due to industry changes and rising costs.

On a standalone basis, operating losses of KRW 3.75 billion in 2023, KRW 760 million in 2024 and KRW 2.09 billion in 2025 persisted for three straight years, and the standalone business returned to operating profit in Q1 2026.

Compared with cathode and anode producers such as POSCO Future M, Ecopro BM and L&F, whose results track material prices, this company's earnings depend more on EV and ESS design wins and order intake for components.

Its display business remains small, so the diversification benefit will show up in the mix of future quarterly sales.

06

Outlook

The company has set a target of achieving full-year 2026 operating profit by expanding high value-added orders and sales. For the first three quarters of 2026, the company reported consolidated sales of KRW 58.3 billion, operating profit of KRW 3 billion and a net loss of KRW 900 million.

Whether the full-year target is met will be checked in the business report that includes Q4 2026. Of the KRW 8.5 billion raised in October 2026, KRW 5.5 billion will go entirely to core equipment for large-area FPCBs at the Vietnam production base, with the rest used as operating funds.

The company plans to add large-area FPCB lines for electric vehicles, secondary batteries and ESS in stages. On capital structure, the company is pursuing a 10-for-1 reverse split of common shares, and it said that the capital reduction gain will fully offset accumulated deficit with no change to total equity.

On its investee, the company said a possible Nasdaq listing of ProLogium could make the asset's potential value and the balance sheet improvement a trigger for revaluation.

A January 2026 report indicated that ProLogium's French plant was expected to start construction in 2026 and mass production in 2028, so any change to that schedule must be confirmed through later disclosures. Whether the margin improvement holds as the new capacity ramps up will set the direction of the next results.

08

Bull factors

Return to Operating Profit and Sales Growth

Sales rose for three straight quarters, from KRW 15.9 billion in Q1 2026 to KRW 22.1 billion in Q3, with Q3 up 59.3% year on year. Operating profit moved from an operating loss in Q4 2025 to a profit in Q1 2026 and reached KRW 1.51 billion in Q2. The company credits cost reforms and high value-added orders. Whether operating profit holds beyond one year is the core of the bull case.

Large-Area FPCB Expansion and Order Intake

Of the KRW 8.5 billion raised, KRW 5.5 billion goes to large-area FPCB equipment in Vietnam. Demand is rising as battery pack designs replace wiring harnesses. The company says a limited number of suppliers operate in this market. Bulls argue that once the new lines run, capacity and the range of products it can serve both widen.

Improving Balance Sheet

The debt ratio fell from 69.9% in 2022 to 32.9% at end-2025, and the company said it was 25% in the first three quarters of 2026. Total equity of KRW 102.7 billion against liabilities of KRW 33.7 billion gives room for financing. The company says the reverse split clears accumulated deficit and strengthens financial soundness.

09

Bear factors

Continued Controlling-Shareholder Net Losses

Net losses attributable to controlling shareholders widened to KRW 8.29 billion in 2024 and KRW 18.7 billion in 2025. In Q3 2026 the bottom line swung from a KRW 14.5 billion profit in Q2 to a KRW 2.58 billion loss. Over the last four quarters, the controlling-shareholder net loss totaled KRW 8.13 billion. Bears point out that operating profit has not yet carried through to net profit.

Gap Between Operating and Net Results

In 2024 the company posted an operating profit of KRW 2.57 billion yet a controlling-shareholder net loss of KRW 8.29 billion. In 2023 it reported an operating loss of KRW 5.77 billion but a controlling-shareholder net income of KRW 1.56 billion. The two levels have repeatedly moved in opposite directions. If non-operating items drive the result, improvement in core operations alone may not restore net profit.

Sales Volatility and a Small New Business

Sales fell 28.0% to KRW 59 billion in 2025 from KRW 81.9 billion in 2024. Even though Q3 2026 sales rose, operating profit fell 26.7% from the prior quarter. The display business had full-year 2025 sales of about KRW 2.7 billion, a small share of the total.

Bears will watch whether sales recovery translates into margin gains and how much the new business can contribute.

Risk factors

Funding and Dilution

In October 2026 the company raised KRW 8.5 billion through a KRW 5.5 billion third-party allotment and a KRW 3 billion public offering. Most of the proceeds go to equipment, so a financial burden remains if the expansion does not convert into sales.

The reverse split applies equally to all shareholders and leaves total equity unchanged, per the company. Its effect on share supply will need to be checked after the filing.

Reliance on an Investee Asset

The value of the ProLogium stake depends on technology development and mass production timing. A January 2026 report expected mass production at its French plant in 2028. A possible Nasdaq listing is an expectation the company has voiced, not a confirmed schedule.

Even if core operations improve, changes in the valuation of this asset could materially affect how the company's equity and value are read.

Business Concentration and Cost Structure

Because the core business is FPCB, results depend heavily on EV and ESS equipment investment and product adoption. The company names cost reform as a pillar of its turnaround, yet operating profit fell in Q3 even as sales rose. The largest shareholder holds 17.3%, which can affect free float and share price volatility. Whether cost gains hold quarter after quarter is the key risk to monitor.

Overall view

EV Advanced Material returned to operating profit in Q1 2026 and has grown sales for three straight quarters, with cumulative operating profit of about KRW 3 billion through Q3 2026. The company said its debt ratio fell from 32.9% at end-2025 to 25% in the first three quarters of 2026.

Controlling-shareholder results, however, have been loss-making since 2024 and swung to a KRW 2.58 billion net loss in Q3 2026, with the last four quarters totaling a KRW 8.13 billion loss. The recurring divergence between operating and net results is the central point in reading this company.

The open questions are whether large-area FPCB capacity and high value-added orders convert into earnings, and how the investee stake and the reverse split affect the reading of equity. The 2026 business report and the Q1 2027 quarterly report are the next checkpoints.
