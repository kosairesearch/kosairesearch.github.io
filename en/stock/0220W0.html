Report overview

Earnings Base Versus Share Price Swings

02

Business structure

Hanwha Machinery & Service Holdings is a holding company created through a spin-off from Hanwha. It began operating on August 3, 2026 and was relisted on the KOSPI on August 25. The company groups its affiliates into two pillars, Tech and Life, with 57 affiliates including sub-subsidiaries and overseas entities.

The Tech pillar includes Hanwha Vision, Hanwha Semitech, Hanwha Momentum and Hanwha Robotics, covering AI video security, semiconductor equipment and collaborative robots. Reports cite expansion of the high-bandwidth memory TC bonder business as a key growth driver in the Tech pillar.

The Life pillar includes a department store unit, a hotel and resort operator and a food service and catering affiliate, spanning department stores, hotels and resorts, and contract catering.

Per reports, the flagship Apgujeong store of the department store unit accounted for 42.5% of that unit's total transaction value in 2025. Hanwha Vision and the department store affiliate are listed on the KOSPI.

Competitively, the Tech pillar faces specialist companies in video security, semiconductor equipment and robotics, while the Life pillar faces department store, hotel and food service operators.

According to the Korea Exchange summary financials, consolidated 2025 revenue was KRW 6.0716 trillion and total assets were KRW 11.3467 trillion. Reports said the holding company's assets at the spin-off, KRW 884.7 billion, exceeded the KRW 500 billion threshold under the Fair Trade Act for holding companies.

03

Recent trends

On the filed basis, consolidated revenue for Q3 2026 year-to-date was KRW 1.21 trillion, operating profit was KRW 99.5 billion and net profit was KRW 33.2 billion.

According to the Korea Exchange summary financials released in April 2026, consolidated 2025 revenue was KRW 6.0716 trillion, operating profit KRW 170.2 billion and net profit KRW 151.5 billion.

For 2024, the same summary shows revenue of KRW 5.6427 trillion, operating profit of KRW 177.7 billion and net profit of KRW 62.7 billion.

Because the nine-month figures and the full-year figures cover different periods, a direct comparison is limited, and full-year results will be confirmed through the annual business report. The share price opened with limit-up trading on the relisting day, August 25, 2026, and touched KRW 13,000 intraday.

On October 12, 2026, it traded at KRW 7,510, up 23.52% from the prior close of KRW 6,080. As of November 13, 2026, the share price was KRW 7,890 and market capitalization was KRW 666.8 billion.

On September 23, 2026, a KRW 23 billion rights offering at Hanwha Robotics was withdrawn, and the holding company lent KRW 23 billion in operating funds the same day.

Kim Dong-seon is conducting a second tender offer at KRW 8,000 per share for about 3.38 million common shares through November 30, and reports said the stake would rise from 32.99% to 36.99% if the target is fully acquired.

04

Outlook

The investment plan presented through 2030 totals KRW 4.7 trillion, split into KRW 2.1 trillion for facilities, KRW 2.0 trillion for R&D and KRW 600 billion for M&A.

By year, the plan shows KRW 900 billion for 2026, KRW 1 trillion for 2027, KRW 800 billion for 2028, KRW 1.2 trillion for 2029 and KRW 800 billion for 2030.

Facilities spending includes the rebuilding of the Apgujeong department store and new store openings and renovations, while R&D is directed at AI and cloud technology at Hanwha Vision, semiconductor equipment, secondary battery equipment and robotics, according to reports.

Investment spending for 2026 through 2028 alone totals KRW 2.7 trillion. Funding for this plan depends on the holding company's balance sheet, its access to external financing and the cash generation of its affiliates.

At the end of Q3 2026, Hanwha Vision reported cash and equivalents of KRW 151.7 billion, down about KRW 97 billion from the end of 2025, with borrowings and bonds of KRW 740.1 billion.

The company said the collaboration among the three Life affiliates is still short, so the overall profit gain is difficult to quantify, and it will focus for now on raising profitability in existing businesses. The 2026 full-year results will be fixed in the annual business report due by the end of March 2027.

The key indicators to watch are the pace of investment execution, the level of affiliate borrowing and the recovery of operating profit in the Life pillar.

05

Bull factors

Hopes for a narrower conglomerate discount

The company and the market have framed the spin-off as a way for the Tech and Life pillars to be valued independently of the defense, energy and finance businesses that remain at Hanwha. Hanwha stated that resolving the conglomerate discount was a purpose of the split.

Limit-up trading on the relisting day was read as a reflection of these expectations. Holding growth businesses in robotics, video security and semiconductor equipment alongside consumer businesses was also cited as a reason for investor interest.

Improving profitability in Tech affiliates

Reports said Hanwha Vision's Q3 2026 year-to-date revenue and operating profit rose 16.92% and 31.73% from the same period a year earlier. Its pre-tax and net profit also moved from losses to profits. Expansion of the HBM TC bonder business is cited as a major growth driver in the Tech pillar.

The holding company's Q3 2026 year-to-date operating profit of KRW 99.5 billion is read alongside these affiliate results.

Life pillar returns to profit in the first half

Reports said the department store unit and the hotel and resort operator both returned to consolidated operating profit in the first half of 2026. The company attributed the department store gains mainly to luxury demand and rising foreign customer sales at the Seoul flagship.

Before consolidation adjustments, department store revenue rose 9.2%. Reports also noted that these first-half results predate the holding company's launch.

06

Bear factors

Heavy investment and affiliate borrowing

The KRW 4.7 trillion plan through 2030 spans facilities, R&D and M&A, creating substantial funding needs. One Tech affiliate reported borrowings and bonds of KRW 740.1 billion, with cash down about KRW 97 billion from the end of 2025.

Hanwha Robotics withdrew a KRW 23 billion rights offering and instead borrowed operating funds from the holding company. Commentary notes that the extent to which earnings gains translate into investment funding differs across affiliates.

Operating gap risk in the Life pillar

The rebuilding of the Apgujeong department store is planned with roughly KRW 900 billion, and reports flag store disruption and customer attrition during construction as challenges. The company said initial operating costs at new hotel sites and rising costs and wages weighed on profitability.

The flagship store accounted for 42.5% of department store transaction value in 2025, a high dependence on a single location. The company has said the collaboration effect across the three Life affiliates is still difficult to quantify.

Share-price volatility since relisting

The stock opened with limit-up trading on its relisting day and touched KRW 13,000 intraday, but traded at KRW 7,890 on November 13, 2026. On October 12, 2026, it rose 23.52% in a single session, and the intraday range equaled 6.25% of the prior close.

With 84,515,535 listed common shares, the share price has been sensitive to trading flows. Reports attribute the large moves to the interplay between thematic trading and expectations of revaluation.

07

Risk factors

Macro

With substantial borrowings and bonds, a rise in interest rates would increase interest burdens and the cost of funding investment. The Life pillar's department store, hotel and food service businesses are sensitive to consumer conditions.

Luxury demand and foreign customer sales are cited as key drivers of the recovery, so a slowdown in that demand would have a meaningful effect.

Regulatory and governance

Reports said the holding company's assets after the spin-off, KRW 884.7 billion, exceed the KRW 500 billion asset threshold under the Fair Trade Act. Designation as a holding company brings mandatory rules such as minimum ownership ratios for subsidiaries and sub-subsidiaries.

Within the group, another holding company has already been designated, and governance questions at the top of the structure remain under discussion.

Execution of business and investment

Executing the KRW 4.7 trillion plan requires affiliate cash generation and external financing to be secured at the same time. The withdrawal of Hanwha Robotics' rights offering shows that affiliate funding can diverge from plans.

Results from R&D and M&A may take time to appear in reported earnings, which is also a source of execution risk.

08

Overall view

Hanwha Machinery & Service Holdings is a newly formed holding company combining Tech and Life pillars, and its Q3 2026 year-to-date filed results show operating profit of KRW 99.5 billion and net profit of KRW 33.2 billion.

On the earnings side, the improved profitability at Hanwha Vision and the return to profit of the Life pillar in the first half are cited as positives.

At the same time, the KRW 4.7 trillion investment plan through 2030 and affiliate borrowing create a heavy funding load, and the withdrawn rights offering and the holding company's loan to Hanwha Robotics show ongoing demand for funds across affiliates.

Since relisting, the share price has been volatile, opening with limit-up trading and touching KRW 13,000 intraday before trading at KRW 7,890 on November 13, 2026.

The market has voiced hopes for a narrower conglomerate discount, but that outcome depends on how quickly investment results and a Life pillar recovery appear in reported earnings. This is a qualitative summary for informational purposes.
