02

Business structure

Pungkangco was founded in 1974 and listed on KOSDAQ in 2007. It makes and sells automotive assembly parts, centered on cold-forged automotive nuts, along with bolts and screws. Its customers are domestic automakers and their tier-one suppliers, including Hyundai Motor, KIA, GM Korea and Renault Korea.

Company-level commentary notes that it maintains a no-inspection direct delivery arrangement with automakers and has built long-standing relationships through high-mix, low-volume supply. Because it sits at the tier-one level, revenue depends on automaker production plans and new model programs.

Its first-quarter 2026 commentary also cited lower automaker output as the reason revenue slipped slightly. Affiliates include PK Mechatronics, SPM AUTO PARTS in Mexico, and PUNGKANG AMERICA INC, a US real estate investment vehicle.

Losses and gains at affiliates flow into consolidated results through the equity method, and the year ended August 2026 provisional report attributed the swing to a narrower affiliate operating loss and foreign-currency translation gains.

Product gross profit and affiliate equity-method results therefore both move net income. Cost reduction and lower selling and administrative expenses were the main drivers of the operating profit recovery.

Competition is at the tier-one parts supply level, where suppliers must meet the customer's quality standards and delivery terms to keep orders.

04

Earnings analysis

On a filed basis, the annual trend shows revenue and profit falling together. Revenue went from KRW 97.3 billion in the year ended August 2023 to KRW 93 billion in the year ended August 2024, down 4.4%, and then to KRW 90.3 billion in the year ended August 2025, down 3.0%.

Operating profit went from KRW 5.24 billion to KRW 1.15 billion, down 78.1%, and then to KRW 728 million, down 36.6%. Operating margin moved from 5.4% to 1.2% and then to 0.8%.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 5.58 billion in the year ended August 2023, a net loss of KRW 981 million in the year ended August 2024 that turned it into a loss, and KRW 802 million in the year ended August 2025, a return to profit.

Net income including non-controlling interests was KRW 5.63 billion, a net loss of KRW 908 million, and KRW 775 million in the three years, respectively.

At the end of August 2025, total equity was KRW 70.5 billion, of which KRW 70.3 billion belonged to controlling shareholders, and total liabilities were KRW 21.4 billion. The debt ratio rose from 24.0% to 29.6% and then to 30.4%.

Operating cash flow rose from KRW 4.78 billion to KRW 5.5 billion and then to KRW 6.99 billion over the same period, moving in the opposite direction to operating profit.

Quarterly operating profit fell from KRW 862 million in the quarter ended May 2025 to KRW 474 million and KRW 277 million in the next two quarters, then rose to KRW 1.83 billion in the quarter ended February 2026 and KRW 1.54 billion in the quarter ended May 2026.

Quarterly revenue bottomed at KRW 21 billion in the quarter ended November 2025 and recovered to KRW 23 billion in the quarter ended May 2026, though that was down 2.0% year on year.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders over the last four quarters totaled KRW 5.39 billion, with KRW 2.28 billion in the quarter ended February 2026 and KRW 2.09 billion in the quarter ended May 2026 making up most of it.

The provisional report for the year ended August 2026, disclosed October 12, 2026, shows revenue of KRW 88.18 billion, down 2.3%, operating profit of KRW 3.23 billion, up 343.4%, and net income of KRW 4.84 billion, up 524.2%. These figures are provisional and are not final until confirmed in the periodic report.

05

Industry analysis

Domestic automaker production volumes are the largest external variable for Pungkang's revenue. As its first-quarter 2026 commentary noted, lower automaker output pulls tier-one supplier revenue in the same direction.

Supply volumes are set around automaker platforms and new-model development cycles, so the position in the industry cycle is hard to judge from short-term results alone. The shift to electric vehicles changes parts content by model, so the impact depends on which models use its core nut products.

In competition, the company's high-mix, low-volume supply and no-inspection direct delivery arrangement are cited as strengths. Heavy dependence on customers is a common weakness at the tier-one level. Because its revenue base is small, a single production adjustment by a customer shows up clearly in results.

Exchange rates are also a variable, since foreign-currency translation gains contributed to the equity-method swing in the provisional year ended August 2026, and rate moves can flow through affiliate results into net income.

Provisional revenue of KRW 88.18 billion fell year on year, and the explanation that operating profit rose on better product gross profit and lower selling expenses should be backed by figures in the filed report.

Operating profit recovered in the first and second quarters of 2026, but revenue of KRW 23 billion in the quarter ended May 2026 was still down 2.0% from a year earlier. The next question is whether revenue growth follows the profit recovery.

Automaker production is the external variable that will decide revenue, and since the company cited lower output in its first-quarter 2026 commentary, customer production plans are worth tracking.

The most direct measure of margin improvement is whether the operating margin in the next periodic report exceeds the 0.8% recorded in the year ended August 2025. Dividend and treasury share disclosures should be reviewed as they are published.

Risk factors

Customer concentration and automaker output

Pungkang supplies domestic automakers and their tier-one suppliers, so production adjustments by a single customer flow straight into revenue. Its first-quarter 2026 commentary cited lower automaker output as a reason for the revenue decline. If a new-model program ends or model mix shifts, volumes can fall. Diversification beyond these customers is limited.

Equity-method results and FX

The provisional net income gain for the year ended August 2026 partly reflected an equity-method swing from narrower affiliate losses and higher foreign-currency translation gains. These results depend on affiliate operations and exchange rates, so they may not move in step with core operations.

If translation gains shrink or affiliate losses widen again, net income could reverse by more than operating profit.

Ownership concentration and float

According to a company summary on a financial information site, a major shareholder group holds 30.52% and the company also holds some treasury shares. With the controlling holder's stake above 30%, the share of tradable stock is limited.

Price volatility and trading liquidity can be affected by this structure, and governance-related disclosures should be reviewed separately.

Overall view

Profit went from a loss in the year ended August 2024 to a profit in the year ended August 2025, and provisional operating profit for the year ended August 2026 rose sharply from the prior year.
