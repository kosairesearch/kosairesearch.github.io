02

Business structure

Pungkangco was founded in 1974 to make and sell nuts, bolts and screws, and listed on KOSDAQ in 2007. Its main product is cold-forged nuts for vehicle assembly, supplied in small lots across many variants to Hyundai Motor, KIA, GM Korea and Renault Korea Motors and to tier-one suppliers.

The company has said it maintains a direct delivery system to automakers without incoming inspection, a relationship it links to long-accumulated product development capability.

It has also supplied parts for EV batteries through a tier-one battery maker, but that description rests on older material, so the current share is unconfirmed.

Affiliates include PK Mechatronics, SPM AUTO PARTS in Mexico, and PUNGKANG AMERICA INC, a U.S. real estate investment vehicle; the Mexican unit's earnings contribution could not be confirmed. Revenue splits by product or segment were not verifiable, so no figures are given.

Competition centers on cost and delivery responsiveness among domestic tier-one suppliers, and the company points to cost cuts and SG&A efficiency as margin levers. Revenue moves directly with automaker production, so customer concentration and dependence on prime contractors are high.

Industry classification differs by data provider: KOSDAQ classifies it under metals, while WICS classifies it under automobiles, and the business itself is closer to automotive parts supply.

04

Earnings analysis

On a filed basis, annual revenue was KRW 97.3 billion in the year ended August 2023, KRW 93 billion in the year ended August 2024 (down 4.4%), and KRW 90.3 billion in the year ended August 2025 (down 3.0%).

Operating profit over the same years was KRW 5.24 billion, KRW 1.15 billion (down 78.1%) and KRW 728 million (down 36.6%), and the operating margin moved from 5.4% to 1.2% to 0.8%.

Revenue declined for two consecutive fiscal years from the year ended August 2024 through the year ended August 2025, and the year ended August 2023 was the largest of the three years for both revenue and operating profit, while the year ended August 2025 was the smallest.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 5.58 billion in the year ended August 2023, a net loss of KRW 981 million in the year ended August 2024 (a swing into loss), and KRW 802 million in the year ended August 2025 (a swing back to profit).

Total net income including non-controlling interests was KRW 5.63 billion, a net loss of KRW 908 million, and KRW 775 million, a different measure from the shareholder figure.

At the end of August 2025, total equity was KRW 70.5 billion, of which KRW 70.3 billion belonged to controlling shareholders, and total liabilities were KRW 21.4 billion; the debt ratio rose from 24.0% in the year ended August 2023 to 30.4% in the year ended August 2025.

Operating cash flow was KRW 4.78 billion, KRW 5.5 billion and KRW 6.99 billion across the same three years, rising even as operating profit fell.

Quarterly revenue slid from KRW 23.4 billion in the quarter ended May 2025 to KRW 21 billion in the quarter ended November 2025, then rose to KRW 22.1 billion and KRW 23 billion in the two quarters ended February and May 2026.

Quarterly operating profit bottomed at KRW 277 million in the quarter ended November 2025 and reached KRW 1.54 billion in the quarter ended May 2026, while net income attributable to controlling shareholders swung from a KRW 136 million loss in the quarter ended August 2025 to KRW 1.16 billion in the quarter ended November 2025 and KRW 2.09 billion in the quarter ended May 2026.

The last four quarters through May 2026 total KRW 5.39 billion in controlling-shareholder net income.

The preliminary annual results for the year ended August 2026, disclosed on October 12, 2026, show revenue of KRW 88.18 billion (down 2.3%), operating profit of KRW 3.23 billion (up 343.4%) and net income of KRW 4.84 billion (up 524.2%).

The company attributed the operating profit gain to higher product margins and lower SG&A expenses, and the net income gain to smaller losses at affiliates and a swing to equity-method income from foreign currency translation gains.

Equity-method results are non-operating, which explains why net income grew faster than operating profit, and because these figures are preliminary they may change before the annual report is finalized.
