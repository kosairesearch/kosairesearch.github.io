02

Business structure

Hana Technology, founded in March 2003, makes process equipment for secondary batteries, covering assembly, formation and pack processes for cylindrical, prismatic and pouch cells.

The company positions its turnkey model, which covers precision assembly, line integration and on-site commissioning, as its main competitive edge.

Its strength lies in electrolyte injection and tab welding equipment for the assembly process; a March 2024 equity analysis by a Korean research firm noted that it supplied most of LG Energy Solution's electrolyte injectors for cylindrical cells.

The product line also includes formation process software, Z-stacking equipment and pulse charging-discharging equipment, the last technology gained through an equity stake in a pulse equipment maker.

Customers include the three major Korean cell makers and battery producers in the United States, France, Taiwan and Norway. According to the same analysis, overseas customers accounted for 75% of the KRW 350 billion in orders booked in 2023.

Based on cumulative third-quarter 2023 figures, domestic and export shares of sales were each 50%. The company said cumulative export shipments of secondary battery equipment reached KRW 59.7 billion in the first nine months of 2025, already above the KRW 42.6 billion exported in all of 2024.

In April 2022 it decided to invest KRW 49.5 billion in a second plant in Hwaseong, with capacity expected to rise from about KRW 350 billion to KRW 700 billion a year. Its position as the only domestic equipment maker serving all three major Korean cell makers is cited as a differentiator.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual revenue rose from KRW 113.9 billion in 2022 to a peak of KRW 119.9 billion in 2023 (up 5.3% year on year), then fell to KRW 94.2 billion in 2024 (down 21.5%) and KRW 93.8 billion in 2025 (down 0.4%), with declines in both 2024 and 2025.

Operating profit of KRW 11.2 billion in 2022 turned into an operating loss of KRW 6.31 billion in 2023, widened to an operating loss of KRW 26.4 billion in 2024, and narrowed to an operating loss of KRW 39.81 million in 2025. The company has reported losses for three consecutive years from 2023 through 2025.

Operating margin moved from 9.8% in 2022 to -5.3% in 2023, -28.0% in 2024 and -0.0% in 2025.

Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 1.51 billion in 2022 became a net loss of KRW 3.81 billion in 2023, widened to KRW 15.5 billion in 2024 and narrowed to KRW 5.8 billion in 2025; net profit including non-controlling interests followed the same path.

The company attributed the 2025 improvement to cost structure gains, profit-focused project management and tighter selling and administrative expenses. At the end of 2025, total liabilities were KRW 162.7 billion and total equity was KRW 100.6 billion, with the debt ratio at 161.7%, up from 105.7% in 2023.

Operating cash flow was KRW 8.2 billion in 2022, KRW 1.94 billion in 2023, -KRW 36.1 billion in 2024 and KRW 9.97 billion in 2025.

On a quarterly basis, third-quarter 2025 revenue of KRW 52.5 billion and operating profit of KRW 4.27 billion reflected delayed customer deliveries and overseas project shipments that landed in that quarter. Fourth-quarter 2025 revenue then fell to KRW 9.79 billion, an 81.3% drop from the prior quarter.

Operating losses were KRW 3.65 billion, KRW 6.37 billion and KRW 6.17 billion in the first three quarters of 2026, extending a run of three consecutive loss-making quarters.

The third-quarter 2026 net loss attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 22.8 billion is far larger than the operating loss, so the non-operating items behind that gap are a question for the annual report.

05

Industry analysis

Demand for Hana Technology's equipment depends on electric vehicle battery investment, and orders move with cell makers' capacity plans and ordering timing. A March 2024 equity analysis by a Korean research firm noted that global EV penetration rose from 4.2% in 2020 to 16.7% in 2023, with about 19% expected for 2024.

The same analysis pointed to some automakers delaying or scaling back EV investment and flagged the risk of slower battery capacity additions. Because those figures are 2024 projections, current demand should be checked against the latest capacity announcements from the major cell makers.

Within its competitive set, the company is described as the only domestic equipment maker serving all three major Korean cell makers.

The same analysis cited its overseas order strength to a domestic reference base, turnkey supply across assembly, formation and pack processes, in-house formation software and Z-stacking equipment, and local subsidiaries that provide technical support.

With a high overseas customer share, policy and investment conditions in customer countries feed directly into order flow. In its third-quarter 2025 briefing, the company said large equipment deliveries and installations in Europe increased.

Rather than judge where the equipment cycle stands, the more concrete test is whether capacity announcements from the three major cell makers and overseas customers turn into actual orders.

06

Outlook

The company's stated direction centers on expanding turnkey orders and strengthening its overseas push. In its third-quarter 2025 briefing, it said it would intensify turnkey bidding in strategic global markets and new market entry to keep improving results.

The UK contract disclosed on June 9, 2026 is a turnkey supply agreement for a secondary battery assembly line, worth about KRW 161.5 billion, or 172.09% of 2025 revenue of KRW 93.8 billion. It runs from June 8, 2026 to December 28, 2030, and the counterparty has not been disclosed at the customer's request.

The contract consists of four purchase orders, and the company said it would make additional disclosures if the amount or period changes. The pace at which this contract will be recognized as quarterly revenue is not set by the disclosure alone.

Third-quarter 2026 revenue of KRW 18.1 billion rose from KRW 5.29 billion in the second quarter but remained well below KRW 52.5 billion in the same quarter of 2025. That earlier quarter was explained by deliveries that slipped from the first half, so quarterly revenue can swing widely with delivery timing.

The next checkpoints are any change disclosed to the UK contract and the annual revenue mix in the 2026 annual report.

08

Bull factors

Large turnkey order widens the revenue base

The UK contract disclosed on June 9, 2026 is worth about KRW 161.5 billion, equal to 172.09% of 2025 revenue, and runs until December 28, 2030. The counterparty has not been disclosed, but the contract covers a turnkey assembly line, which shows the company's turnkey capability winning large orders. As this contract is recognized as revenue, the floor of annual sales could rise.

Cost efficiency cut the operating loss sharply

The 2025 operating loss narrowed to KRW 39.81 million from KRW 26.4 billion in 2024. The company credited cost structure improvements, profit-focused project management and tighter selling and administrative expenses. Operating margin improved from -28.0% in 2024 to -0.0% in 2025, a move back toward breakeven.

Export track record and customer base

Cumulative exports in the first nine months of 2025 had already exceeded the full-year 2024 total, pointing to rising overseas deliveries. Serving all three major Korean cell makers is cited as the domestic reference base behind its overseas wins.

Given the high overseas share, export shipments are a direct gauge of whether turnkey orders are turning into deliveries.

Bear factors

Three consecutive loss-making quarters and volatile sales

The company has posted operating losses in each of the first three quarters of 2026, at KRW 3.65 billion, KRW 6.37 billion and KRW 6.17 billion. Third-quarter 2026 revenue of KRW 18.1 billion fell 65.5% from a year earlier.

Quarterly sales have swung sharply, from KRW 9.79 billion in the fourth quarter of 2025 to KRW 5.29 billion in the second quarter of 2026, while losses continued.

Widening net loss and cumulative losses

The third-quarter 2026 net loss attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 22.8 billion is far larger than the operating loss of KRW 6.17 billion. Losses over the last four quarters total KRW 24.9 billion.

Net losses attributable to controlling shareholders have run from 2023 through 2025, and although the second quarter of 2026 showed a profit of KRW 1.33 billion, it did not reverse the broader trend.

Financial burden and volatile operating cash flow

The debt ratio rose to 161.7% at the end of 2025 from 105.7% in 2023. Operating cash flow was -KRW 36.1 billion in 2024 and recovered to KRW 9.97 billion in 2025, yet quarterly profits remain in the red.

With total liabilities of KRW 162.7 billion against total equity of KRW 100.6 billion, financial room could narrow if losses persist.

10

Risk factors

Order concentration and delivery timing

Third-quarter 2025 revenue was explained by customer deliveries that slipped from the first half and landed together. Turnkey orders are large, but revenue recognition can lag when installation and acceptance inspection schedules shift.

The company has said the UK contract's amount and period may change, so any amendment affects earnings visibility.

Customer investment delays and end-market demand

A March 2024 equity analysis flagged delayed or scaled-back EV investment plans at some automakers and the risk of slower battery capacity additions. Because orders follow cell makers' capacity decisions, investment adjustments by customers could translate directly into fewer orders. The high overseas customer share also leaves the company exposed to investment conditions in those countries.

Converting overseas backlog into revenue

The order list in a March 2024 equity analysis classified some UK contracts as having uncertain revenue realization. Press coverage of the same period also reported delayed revenue recognition from overseas backlog and worsening profitability.

Because the pace at which large overseas contracts convert to revenue drives earnings volatility, the gap between booked orders and recognized revenue needs to be tracked.

Overall view

Hana Technology supplies turnkey secondary battery equipment for assembly, formation and pack processes, and its 2025 operating loss narrowed to KRW 39.81 million. The UK turnkey contract disclosed in June 2026, worth about KRW 161.5 billion, equals 172.09% of 2025 revenue and runs until December 28, 2030.

On the other side, operating losses have continued through three consecutive quarters from the first quarter to the third quarter of 2026, and third-quarter 2026 revenue fell 65.5% year on year.

Net losses attributable to controlling shareholders total KRW 24.9 billion over the last four quarters, and the debt ratio stood at 161.7% at the end of 2025.

Strengths include large turnkey orders, cost efficiency gains and export results, while weaknesses include continuing quarterly losses, volatile sales and financial burden.

The next checkpoints are the non-operating items and annual revenue mix in the 2026 annual report, and whether losses continue in the first-quarter 2027 report. This report is for information only and does not present an investment opinion or target price.
