Report overview

Power-supply maker widens into EV electronics amid earnings swings

Dongyang E&P is adding an EV electronics business to its Samsung-heavy power-supply core, and a controlling-shareholder net loss in the third quarter of 2026 has made earnings stability the next item to verify.

  4. 4

02

Business structure

Dongyang E&P, founded in 1987, manufactures switching-mode power supplies (SMPS), chargers and adapters, with its headquarters and plant in the Jinwi industrial complex in Pyeongtaek, Gyeonggi Province.

Its core products are mobile-phone chargers and SMPS for digital home appliances, and its applications are widening to solar inverters, EV chargers and in-vehicle wireless chargers.

A securities-firm analysis cited in a February 2024 report estimated shares of 30–40% for mobile-phone products, 40–50% for digital appliance SMPS and 8–10% for other products.

Product-level sales, however, are reported in the filings as a single combined line item called 'electronic components and others', so segment amounts cannot be confirmed from the filings alone.

Major customers include Samsung Electronics, Hyundai Mobis, Sony and Foxconn, and Samsung Electronics and its subsidiaries accounted for 88.8% of sales in the first quarter of 2025. That concentration means changes in Samsung Electronics' demand pass through quickly to the company's revenue.

On competition, national safety certifications such as UL (Americas), CE (Europe) and CCC (China) act as entry barriers, and the company says these certifications support its technical credibility.

Samsung Electro-Mechanics and LG Innotek are listed in the same electronics components sector, but their broader component portfolios make direct product-level comparisons limited.

In June 2026 the company said it would shift from single-product manufacturing toward a business centered on automotive electronic components and power-conversion modules.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual sales moved from KRW 559.8 billion in 2022 to KRW 536.9 billion in 2023 (down 4.1% year on year), the low point of the period, before rising to KRW 548.4 billion in 2024 (up 2.1%) and KRW 596.5 billion in 2025 (up 8.8%), two consecutive years of growth.

Operating profit went from KRW 27.8 billion in 2022 to KRW 43 billion in 2023 (up 54.8%), KRW 51.6 billion in 2024 (up 20.2%) and KRW 53.6 billion in 2025 (up 3.9%), three consecutive years of increases from 2023 through 2025.

The operating margin rose from 5.0% in 2022 to 8.0% in 2023 and 9.4% in 2024, then held at 9.0% in 2025. Securities analysts around 2023 pointed to normalizing raw-material and logistics costs as a driver of the margin recovery.

Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders rose from KRW 23 billion in 2022 to KRW 47 billion in 2023 (up 104.6%) and KRW 69.6 billion in 2024 (up 48.1%), with 2024 the largest year of the 2022–2025 span. It then fell 28.9% to KRW 49.5 billion in 2025, so operating profit grew while net profit declined.

Total net profit including non-controlling interests was also KRW 49.5 billion in 2025, so minority interests do not explain the gap. The 2025 divergence needs to be traced to non-operating items and taxes, which the headline figures do not show.

Operating cash flow went from KRW 37.5 billion in 2022 and KRW 76.8 billion in 2023 to KRW 75.3 billion in 2024 and KRW 41.4 billion in 2025, a weaker cash conversion.

Quarterly sales fell from KRW 165.2 billion in the third quarter of 2025 to KRW 126.6 billion in the fourth quarter (down 23.4% quarter on quarter), KRW 122.8 billion in the first quarter of 2026 and KRW 105.7 billion in the second quarter, then rebounded to KRW 145.1 billion in the third quarter (up 37.3% quarter on quarter, down 12.2% year on year).

Operating profit slid from KRW 16.3 billion in the third quarter of 2025 to KRW 2.72 billion in the second quarter of 2026 and recovered to KRW 5.67 billion in the third quarter, still down 65.1% year on year.

Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders turned to a KRW 8.77 billion net loss in the third quarter of 2026, after KRW 23.6 billion, KRW 10.4 billion, KRW 13.3 billion and KRW 9.2 billion in the four preceding quarters.

A net loss in a quarter when sales rose calls for checking non-operating costs or one-off items. Over the last four quarters (fourth quarter 2025 to third quarter 2026), net profit attributable to controlling shareholders totaled KRW 24.2 billion.

05

Industry analysis

Power supplies are widely described as a product whose demand persists as long as electronic devices such as phones, TVs and appliances keep being sold.

The company itself said that after 2026 its core business could face a heavier load on sales and profitability from changes in end-market demand, product cycles and higher raw-material prices.

The pattern of quarterly sales falling from the first quarter to the second quarter of 2026 before rebounding in the third quarter fits that assessment. A 2023 securities-firm research note argued that the company's sales are highly correlated with the performance of the Samsung Electronics DX division.

Applications are expanding toward solar inverters, electric vehicles and robots. The company views EV power conversion and charging demand as structurally growing and plans to enter that market with automotive components and modules.

However, automotive electronics already has large component makers and specialized power-conversion companies, so the company's entry depends on the customer network and development experience of its acquisition target. Rising raw-material prices pressure margins in the core power-supply business.

The position in the cycle cannot be confirmed from the filings alone: sales fell after the fourth quarter of 2025 and recovered in the third quarter of 2026, and whether demand has passed its low point will show in the coming quarters.

Automotive results are hard to separate from the core business in the filings, so how the acquisition is consolidated becomes a new variable for the outlook.

06

Outlook

The company set out three medium-term priorities in June 2026: stabilizing the core power-supply business, building EV electronics as a new growth engine, and fully executing shareholder returns through treasury-share cancellation and additional buybacks.

On June 15, 2026 the company disclosed plans to acquire a 43% stake in the EV power-conversion company, with the stake held by SDY (23%) and Shin Dong-yang Holdings (20%). The price was set on the basis of a fair value estimated by an independent external appraiser.

The deal extends a joint-venture agreement signed on June 11, 2026. The company expects procurement scale, integrated purchasing, cross-use of global plants and cost improvements to lift profitability, and it plans to shift its product mix toward automotive components, power-conversion modules and circuit modules.

On shareholder returns, it decided on June 22, 2026 to cancel 308,342 treasury shares, about 3% of total issued shares. It is also reviewing the acquisition of 100,000 new treasury shares for use in long-term employee performance rewards.

The company said it will keep its fixed dividend while reviewing step-ups tied to sales milestones of KRW 60 billion, KRW 65 billion and KRW 70 billion.

The next points to confirm are the completion date of the acquisition, the size of automotive sales once consolidated, and the sales range and period that will trigger any dividend increase.

Bull factors

Expansion into automotive electronics

In June 2026 the company announced a 43% stake acquisition in an EV power-conversion firm. It said combining power-conversion and circuit-design skills with automotive components and modules could improve purchasing power and cost structure. A shift from single-product sales to modules could, if it materializes, soften swings in core sales.

A chain of shareholder-return measures

The company decided in June 2026 to cancel 308,342 treasury shares, about 3% of total issued shares. It is also reviewing 100,000 additional treasury shares. It is weighing step-ups in its fixed dividend tied to sales milestones, and execution will be visible through future disclosures.

A lighter balance sheet

The debt-to-equity ratio fell from 38.6% in 2022 to 20.5% in 2025. Total equity at the end of 2025 was KRW 445.1 billion. The operating margin held at 9.4% in 2024 and 9.0% in 2025. The company says its balance sheet leaves room to pursue both the acquisition and shareholder returns.

Bear factors

Weaker quarterly profit in 2026

Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders swung to an KRW 8.77 billion net loss in the third quarter of 2026. Operating profit fell to KRW 2.72 billion in the second quarter and was KRW 5.67 billion in the third, down 65.1% year on year.

Full-year 2025 net profit attributable to controlling shareholders also dropped 28.9%. Operating and net profit trends have diverged.

Concentration on Samsung Electronics

Samsung Electronics and its subsidiaries accounted for 88.8% of sales in the first quarter of 2025. A decline in Samsung's DX division demand feeds directly into the company's revenue. Whether customer diversification is progressing will need to be checked in future annual reports.

Core demand and cost pressure

The company itself said that after 2026, changes in end-market demand, product cycles and higher raw-material prices could weigh more heavily on sales and profitability. Operating cash flow fell to KRW 41.4 billion in 2025 from KRW 75.3 billion in 2024.

If core demand does not recover, a gap could open between expectations for the acquisition and reported results.

Risk factors

Customer concentration and demand

With Samsung Electronics at 88.8% of sales, a change in one customer's orders can move results sharply. A shift in appliance or mobile product cycles can show up as quarterly sales declines, as the drop in the second quarter of 2026 illustrates.

Acquisition and integration

The 43% stake in the EV power-conversion company requires combining technologies and customer bases that differ from the core business. The price was set on an external fair value, but whether profitability improves after consolidation remains to be seen. Integration costs that could erode core earnings also need monitoring.

Governance and shareholder relations

In April 2026 minority shareholders alleged that the company was deliberately holding down its share price and staged a protest before the Financial Supervisory Service, which the company denies. At the March 2026 annual meeting, shareholder proposals for a higher dividend and a stock split were both voted down. A merger possibility raised by shareholders has not been confirmed.

Overall view

The disclosed results show strengths in three consecutive years of operating profit growth from 2023 through 2025 and an operating margin held around 9%. However, net profit attributable to controlling shareholders fell 28.9% in 2025, and the third quarter of 2026 brought a controlling-shareholder net loss.

Quarterly sales fell from the first quarter to the second quarter of 2026 before rebounding in the third, so the direction of the business changes from quarter to quarter.

The 43% stake in the EV power-conversion company aims to expand into automotive electronics and shift toward modules, and results will be visible in disclosures after completion and consolidation.

Treasury-share cancellation, the review of additional buybacks and the review of dividend step-ups are shareholder-return factors, while the share-price allegations and the payout-ratio debate by minority shareholders remain. Customer concentration on Samsung Electronics is both a strength and a weakness.

The debt-to-equity ratio improved to 20.5%, but operating cash flow declined in 2025. The next points to confirm are full-year 2026 profitability in the annual report, automotive results after consolidation, and whether the sales milestones for dividend step-ups are reached.
