02

Business structure

Mason Capital was established in 1989 to engage in equipment leasing and new technology business finance, and it has been listed on KOSDAQ since 1996.

Its main businesses are loans, securities investment, new technology business finance and debt collection, and it also runs leasing, credit finance and installment finance. Lending and investment operations form its two core pillars, combining steady loan expansion with new technology investments in promising ventures.

Its consolidated subsidiaries include MASON Microfinance, which runs a small-loan business in Myanmar, Naracredit, a debt collection and credit investigation firm, and a growth investment partnership. The company says it diversified its business through the 2023 acquisition of Naracredit.

In its second-quarter 2026 results coverage, investment recoveries and valuation gains were cited as the main drivers of profit. Portfolio companies named in press coverage include Persona AI, Korea Electric Lighting and Bitel, and the company presents IPOs or share sales of these holdings as its exit strategy.

Against large financial holding companies and brokerages, its scale and business model differ, and a Jobkorea ranking places it 15th by sales within the capital and lending industry. Headcount was 18 as of the fiscal year ended March 2026.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual sales rose from KRW 4.32 billion in the fiscal year ended March 2023 to KRW 20.3 billion in the fiscal year ended March 2024, an increase of 370.1% year on year, and reached KRW 25.1 billion in the fiscal year ended March 2025, the largest year among the disclosed results, up 23.7%.

Sales fell 12.2% to KRW 22 billion in the fiscal year ended March 2026. Operating results moved from an operating loss of KRW 3.94 billion in the fiscal year ended March 2023 to an operating profit of KRW 4.3 billion in the fiscal year ended March 2024, which marked a swing from loss to profit.

The fiscal year ended March 2025 returned to an operating loss of KRW 10.1 billion, a swing back into the red, and the fiscal year ended March 2026 posted an operating loss of KRW 2.22 billion, a continued loss with a smaller deficit.

Losses have been recorded in two consecutive fiscal years, from the year ended March 2025 through the year ended March 2026.

Controlling-shareholder net income moved from KRW 4.61 billion in the fiscal year ended March 2024 to a controlling-shareholder net loss of KRW 9.51 billion in the fiscal year ended March 2025, and to a controlling-shareholder net loss of KRW 3.51 billion in the fiscal year ended March 2026.

Net loss including non-controlling interests was KRW 4.11 billion in the fiscal year ended March 2026. In the company's disclosure of the change, the reasons given were a decline in provisions and a rise in equity-method losses from joint ventures and other investees.

Operating margins were -91.3%, 21.2%, -40.2% and -10.1% across the four fiscal years. Quarterly sales rose from KRW 5.18 billion in the second quarter of 2025 to KRW 6.61 billion in the first quarter of 2026, eased 1.4% to KRW 6.51 billion in the second quarter of 2026, and were still up 25.8% from a year earlier.

Quarterly operating results went from a KRW 159 million profit in the fourth quarter of 2025 to an operating loss of KRW 1.35 billion in the first quarter of 2026, then back to an operating profit of KRW 1.81 billion in the second quarter of 2026.

The sum of controlling-shareholder net losses over the last four quarters, from the third quarter of 2025 to the second quarter of 2026, was KRW 1 billion, and the second-quarter profit offset much of the losses of the three preceding quarters.

A company press release (August 10, 2026, separate basis) put second-quarter operating profit at KRW 2.11 billion and net profit at KRW 2.12 billion, attributed to investment recoveries and fair-value gains.

Operating cash flow went from a KRW 11.1 billion inflow in the fiscal year ended March 2024 to outflows of KRW -15.3 billion and KRW -11.8 billion in the fiscal years ended March 2025 and March 2026.

Industry analysis

Mason Capital sits in the capital and lending industry, but its earnings behave more like an investment company, moving sharply with the timing of investment recoveries and fair-value marks rather than with interest income alone.

Press coverage also noted that results of investment companies can swing widely with changes in asset valuations and the timing of recoveries.

Against large financial holding companies such as KB Financial Group, Shinhan Financial Group, Hana Financial Group and Woori Financial Group, its asset base is small and its model differs.

Among listed peers in the same industry, Samsung Card, Mirae Asset Securities and NH Investment & Securities run different operations, which limits direct comparison. The Myanmar small-loan business is exposed to local operating conditions and policy shifts, a risk factor distinct from domestic lenders.

Naracredit, which handles debt collection and credit investigation, is tied to the environment for recovering loans. The most important industry variable is the health of the IPO and share sale markets, since those are the exit routes for its investments.

The company has said it will expand investments in growth areas such as AI and semiconductor materials. Whether recoveries are concentrated in a particular phase, and whether the valuation gains are one-off, can be checked in the valuation line items of upcoming quarterly reports.

Risk factors

Valuation and timing of recoveries

If the fair values of portfolio holdings fall, or if IPOs and share sales are delayed, quarterly results could slip back into losses. The disclosure for the fiscal year ended March 2026 cited lower provisions and higher equity-method losses as drivers of the sales decline and profit change, which illustrates this risk. Because exit timing varies by quarter, the range of earnings outcomes is wide.

Ownership concentration and free float

The largest shareholder held 63.73% as of September 30, 2026, leaving 36.27% as free float. In a structure with limited tradable shares, trading volume can be thin and price swings larger. Changes in the largest shareholder's stake and in the shareholder base after the capital restructuring are key items to monitor.

Capital restructuring and overseas subsidiary

The Myanmar small-loan subsidiary is exposed to local operating conditions and policy changes, and the debt collection subsidiary Naracredit is affected by changes in the environment for loan recovery.

Overall view

Mason Capital's disclosed results show a profit in the fiscal year ended March 2024, followed by losses in the fiscal years ended March 2025 and March 2026. The operating loss of KRW 2.22 billion in the fiscal year ended March 2026 was smaller than the prior year, and the operating margin improved to -10.1%.

In the second quarter of 2026 the company swung to an operating profit of KRW 1.81 billion and a controlling-shareholder net profit of KRW 2.12 billion, which it attributed to investment recoveries and fair-value gains.

On the other side, the cumulative controlling-shareholder net loss over the last four quarters is KRW 1 billion, and operating cash flow was negative in both the fiscal years ended March 2025 and March 2026.

On financial structure, total equity of KRW 80.9 billion and a debt ratio of 16.8% point to a light debt burden, but reported earnings depend heavily on valuation gains and the timing of recoveries.

That market capitalization sits well below total equity can be read as a supportive factor, but the realizable value of the investments within equity is the decisive question.

The next checkpoints are the composition of profit in the third-quarter 2026 report, exit disclosures, and operating cash flow in the annual report for the fiscal year ending March 2027.
