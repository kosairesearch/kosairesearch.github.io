02

Business structure

Mason Capital was founded in 1989 and listed on KOSDAQ in 1996. It operates equipment leasing, new technology business financing and installment financing, and holds MASON Microfinance, a microlending business in Myanmar, and a credit-collection and credit-investigation subsidiary as consolidated subsidiaries.

Its largest shareholder changed in 2025 to Leadcorp, a company engaged in wholesale trade of liquid fuels. Collections from the credit-collection subsidiary have become a main revenue source after the acquisition.

On the investment side, the company forms new funds as Co-GP alongside established managers, sources SPAC merger candidates and spreads investments across pre-IPO stocks. It manages 11 funds in total, and its Myanmar unit is described as a foothold for overseas finance.

Revenue mixes lending and collection income with investment valuation and exit gains, so which side drives profit changes from quarter to quarter. Among capital and lending companies, it ranked 15th by revenue in a 2026 tally by JobKorea.

Unlike large financial holding companies that compete on deposit and loan scale, the company earns through niche businesses such as collections and venture investment.

Earnings analysis

Annual revenue rose from KRW 4.32 billion in the fiscal year ended March 2023 to KRW 20.3 billion in the fiscal year ended March 2024, an increase of 370.1%. It then reached KRW 25.1 billion in the fiscal year ended March 2025, up 23.7%, before falling to KRW 22 billion in the fiscal year ended March 2026, down 12.2%.

Among the fiscal years from March 2023 to March 2026, the largest was the fiscal year ended March 2025 and the smallest was the fiscal year ended March 2023.

Operating results moved from an operating loss of KRW 3.94 billion to an operating profit of KRW 4.3 billion, then back to an operating loss of KRW 10.1 billion, and finally to an operating loss of KRW 2.22 billion; the company has posted losses in the fiscal years ended March 2025 and March 2026.

Operating margins were -91.3%, 21.2%, -40.2% and -10.1% in that order. Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders went from a net loss of KRW 3.83 billion to a profit of KRW 4.61 billion, then to a loss of KRW 9.51 billion and a smaller loss of KRW 3.51 billion.

Net loss including non-controlling interests was KRW 4.11 billion for the fiscal year ended March 2026, a different figure from the controlling-shareholder result. The company attributed the change in its earnings to a lower provision and higher equity-method losses from joint ventures.

Operating cash flow was KRW 11.1 billion positive in the fiscal year ended March 2024 but -KRW 15.3 billion in the fiscal year ended March 2025 and -KRW 11.8 billion in the fiscal year ended March 2026.

Quarterly revenue climbed from KRW 4.61 billion in the third quarter of 2025 to a peak of KRW 6.61 billion in the first quarter of 2026, then declined in the second and third quarters of 2026, with the third quarter down 36.7% sequentially and 10.5% year on year.

The second quarter of 2026 returned to an operating profit of KRW 1.81 billion, which the company linked to partial recovery of investments and fair-value gains on SPAC and other major assets. The company's press release of August 10, 2026 described that turnaround on a separate basis.

The third quarter of 2026 returned to an operating loss of KRW 792 million. Net loss attributable to controlling shareholders over the last four quarters totaled KRW 1.43 billion. The drivers of the third-quarter loss should be checked in the quarterly report detail.

05

Industry analysis

Korean capital companies earn mainly from collateral-backed lending and are sensitive to the interest rate environment. Mason Capital is a small player that adds venture investment exit income on top of this base. The company says it is expanding collateral-focused lending to widen its secured loan book.

Its investment arm is sensitive to the state of the IPO and stake-sale markets. The company itself has noted that investment-company results can swing with asset valuations and the timing of exits. By revenue, it ranked 15th among capital and lending companies in a 2026 tally by JobKorea.

Compared with large financial holding companies such as KB Financial Group and Shinhan Financial Group, its capital base and funding access are limited, so its direct competition with them is narrow. Its niche lies in collections and venture investment, areas large financial firms handle relatively less.

Its Myanmar microlending unit is cited as a foothold for overseas finance. Because venture exit markets drive both gains and losses on investments, the cycle position cannot be pinned down with confidence.

The company has said it plans to expand investments in growth areas such as AI, semiconductor materials and cosmetics.

Outlook

The company plans to keep forming new project funds to grow its assets under management. It has said new investments will focus on growth areas such as AI, semiconductor materials and cosmetics. On exits, it is pursuing IPOs and stake sales for portfolio companies including Persona AI, Hankook Electronics and Vuetel.

The sustainability of earnings, however, depends on how those IPO and stake-sale outcomes play out. On the financial side, the March 2026 free capital reduction and share consolidation were disclosed as aimed at offsetting accumulated deficits and improving the capital structure.

Total equity was unchanged by the capital reduction. The consolidated audit opinion for the fiscal year ended March 2026 was unqualified, with no going-concern uncertainty flagged. On governance, the largest shareholder disclosed on-market share purchases on July 21 and August 20, 2026.

Those purchases are disclosed facts, and whether further purchase disclosures follow is the next thing to watch. In the fourth quarter of 2026 results, the next checkpoints are whether revenue rebounds from the third-quarter low and whether operating results return to profit.

Total equity stayed the same because the reduction generated an equal capital surplus.

Major Shareholder Support and Investment Pipeline

If new funds and exit strategies work together, the flow of investment recoveries could continue, which is the bullish argument.

Third-quarter 2026 revenue was KRW 4.12 billion, down 36.7% from the prior quarter, and the company swung back to an operating loss of KRW 792 million. Revenue has declined for two consecutive quarters since the first-quarter 2026 peak. Losses returning right after a profitable quarter is the bearish point.

Negative Operating Cash Flow

Operating cash flow was -KRW 15.3 billion in the fiscal year ended March 2025 and -KRW 11.8 billion in the fiscal year ended March 2026. It was KRW 11.1 billion positive in the fiscal year ended March 2024, so the year-to-year swings are large.

The detailed composition should be checked in the notes to the annual report. Persistent cash outflow is cited as a bearish factor.

Dependence on External Conditions for Earnings

As the company itself describes, whether earnings persist depends on IPO and stake-sale outcomes in its major portfolio holdings. Investment-company results swing widely with valuations and the timing of exits.

The fiscal year ended March 2025 also recorded a net loss attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 9.51 billion, showing that loss years have occurred before.

10

Risk factors

Valuation Volatility of Investments

Because earnings rely on fair-value gains and asset recoveries, quarterly results can reverse direction quickly. The third-quarter 2026 operating loss following the second-quarter profit is a clear example. If portfolio IPO timelines slip or valuations fall, operating profit can easily return to a loss. This risk shows up directly in quarterly results.

Ownership Concentration and Liquidity

The largest shareholder group holds 63.73% of the shares, and the free float is 36.27%. With a thin tradable base, share prices can react strongly to modest trading volume. Changes in the largest holder's stake and on-market trades must be tracked through disclosures. After the reduction in share count, liquidity indicators should also be watched.

Overseas Subsidiary and Regulatory Exposure

The Myanmar microlending unit is exposed to local rules and operating conditions as an overseas finance business. Results of the credit-collection subsidiary flow directly into consolidated results. Lending depends on collateral values and the interest rate environment.

Subsidiary performance can be verified only at the consolidated level, so segment trends should be checked in the periodic report detail.

Overall view

Mason Capital shows both improving signals, such as the narrower operating loss for the fiscal year ended March 2026 and the second-quarter 2026 return to profit, and weakening signals, such as the third-quarter 2026 loss, the revenue decline and negative operating cash flow.

Much of its earnings rely on investment exits and fair-value gains, so quarterly results change direction often.

Total equity of KRW 80.9 billion, with KRW 77.6 billion attributable to controlling shareholders and a low debt ratio, gives a solid capital base, but the capital reduction makes per-share comparisons over time less continuous.

The largest shareholder holds most of the stock and the free float is under half, so share prices may react strongly to small trades. The company says IPO and stake-sale results will determine whether earnings persist.

The next checkpoints are the operating result direction in the fourth-quarter 2026 quarterly report, operating cash flow in the annual report for the fiscal year ending March 2027, and progress on IPOs and stake sales for portfolio companies.

On valuation, the price-to-book ratio is well below 1x while earnings-based multiples cannot be calculated, so the two measures point in different directions. This report is for information only and does not present an investment view.
