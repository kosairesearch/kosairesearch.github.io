02

Business structure

Mason Capital was founded in 1989 and operates in equipment leasing, new technology business financing and credit collection. Its main operations are loans, securities investment, new technology business financing and credit collection.

It acquired a credit-information firm in 2023, and the company says the effect has matured so that credit collection is now a principal revenue source. That subsidiary is 50% owned. Overseas, the group runs a microfinance lending business in Myanmar through MASON Microfinance, in which it holds a 99.99% stake.

On the investment side, it is widening its activities through new technology investment funds, new funds formed with co-general-partner arrangements, sponsorship of special-purpose acquisition companies, and diversified investments in pre-listing companies.

In 2025 a listed consumer lender became the largest shareholder, changing the ownership structure; as of September 2026 that holder owned 63.73%. Revenue split by business unit could not be confirmed from disclosed figures in this review, so it is described qualitatively only.

Its customers are innovative companies, small and mid-sized enterprises and small business owners, as stated in the company greeting. It competes with smaller capital, lending and collection firms, and the scale gap with large financial holding groups is wide.

Earnings analysis

On a fiscal-year basis, revenue rose from KRW 4.32 billion in the fiscal year ended March 2023 to KRW 20.3 billion in the fiscal year ended March 2024, an increase of 370.1% on the prior year.

Operating results moved from an operating loss of KRW 3.94 billion in the fiscal year ended March 2023 to an operating profit of KRW 4.3 billion a year later, then back to an operating loss of KRW 10.1 billion in the fiscal year ended March 2025 and a smaller loss of KRW 2.22 billion in the fiscal year ended March 2026.

Operating margin was -91.3%, 21.2%, -40.2% and -10.1% across the four years, and the company has posted losses for the last two fiscal years. Profit attributable to controlling shareholders was a loss of KRW 3.83 billion, then a profit of KRW 4.61 billion, then losses of KRW 9.51 billion and KRW 3.51 billion.

Net income including non-controlling interests was a loss of KRW 4.11 billion in the fiscal year ended March 2026, which is larger than the loss attributable to controlling shareholders. A news report on the filing said the company cited a lower provision and higher equity-method losses as reasons for the change.

Operating cash flow went from KRW 11.1 billion of inflow in the fiscal year ended March 2024 to outflows of -KRW 15.3 billion and -KRW 11.8 billion in the two following years.

Quarterly revenue grew from KRW 5.18 billion in the second quarter of 2025 to KRW 6.51 billion in the second quarter of 2026, up 25.8% year on year, and operating profit of KRW 1.81 billion in the latter quarter was a swing to profit from a year earlier.

Profit attributable to controlling shareholders also turned positive at KRW 2.12 billion, while the first quarter of 2026 had an operating loss of KRW 1.35 billion and a controlling-shareholder loss of KRW 1.14 billion.

05

Industry analysis

Mason Capital operates in small-scale finance, mixing credit collection, small loans, new technology business financing and fund management. Earnings in this segment are sensitive to delinquencies and recoveries, provisioning, the interest-rate environment and the timing of investment exits.

The change in profit cited in the company's filing, a lower provision and higher equity-method losses, reflects that sensitivity.

Large financial holding groups such as KB Financial Group, Shinhan Financial Group, Hana Financial Group and Woori Financial Group are far larger in earnings and capital base and have less volatile results.

Compared with other listed financial companies such as Mirae Asset Securities and Kakaopay, Mason Capital's profit base rests on a credit-collection subsidiary and an investment portfolio, so its business structure differs.

In internet banking, KakaoBank and Kbank are listed peers, while a 2025 news report said Mason Capital joined a consortium seeking a fourth internet bank license alongside its largest shareholder. The current licensing progress and investment size of that consortium could not be confirmed in this review.

Its Myanmar microfinance unit is exposed to local economic conditions and regulation. Interest-rate trends and industry-wide delinquency data were not verified in this review, so they are described without figures.

Overall view

Mason Capital's losses narrowed in the fiscal year ended March 2026, with both operating and net losses smaller than a year earlier, and in the second quarter of 2026 operating profit and profit attributable to controlling shareholders swung to positive from a year earlier.

However, both the fiscal years ended March 2025 and March 2026 were loss years, and the last four quarters together show a controlling-shareholder net loss of KRW 1 billion.

Operating cash flow has been negative for two straight years, so it is still difficult to judge whether the swing to profit has translated into cash generation. Total equity of KRW 80.9 billion and a debt-to-equity ratio of 16.8% act as a buffer, but the largest shareholder's 63.73% stake leaves a free float of 36.27%.

The business is moving toward a stage where the credit-collection subsidiary's effect is fully felt, and the company has targeted a return to profit in its 38th business term.

The points to check are operating profit and operating cash flow in the third-quarter 2026 report and the annual result for the fiscal year ending March 2027.
