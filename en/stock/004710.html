Hansol Technics: Acquisition Payoff Still to Be Proven

Revenue has risen for three straight quarters through the third quarter of 2026, yet the company swung back to a net loss attributable to controlling shareholders, so whether the semiconductor and vehicle-electronics acquisitions show up in earnings is the central question.

02

Business structure

Hansol Technics is a manufacturer within the Hansol Group that supplies TV and home appliance power boards, wireless charging modules for mobile phones, smartphone contract manufacturing (EMS), solar modules, and automotive electronic parts.

It entered semiconductor equipment parts with the 2022 acquisition of Hansol IONES, and in 2025 it took a stake in SI Materials to expand into semiconductor by-product businesses. In July 2025 it bought a 50.06% stake in a marine and robot electronics maker for about KRW 67.6 billion.

In June 2026 it acquired an 83.37% stake in a probe card maker for KRW 177.2 billion. That subsidiary posted 2025 revenue of KRW 67.4 billion and operating profit of KRW 9.6 billion, a 14.2% margin, far above the group's 1.6% operating margin in 2025.

Press reports say the company expects semiconductor revenue to rise from 15.9% to 20.2% of sales. Marine electronics nonetheless remains the core business. Production runs in factories in Korea, Vietnam, Thailand and China, and the company is consolidating products across plants to raise manufacturing efficiency.

It is also investing in a new Mexico unit to serve North American electronics customers. One analysis describes the company as the only domestic firm certified by both MAN-ES and WinGD for engine controllers.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual revenue peaked at KRW 1.65 trillion in 2022, then fell to KRW 1.3 trillion in 2023 (down 20.9%) and KRW 1.2 trillion in 2024 (down 7.9%), before rebounding to KRW 1.25 trillion in 2025 (up 4.4%).

Press reports attributed the 2024 decline to a downturn in the solar industry, and in 2023 the company exited its LCM business after losses from weakening competitiveness.

Operating profit went from KRW 55.1 billion in 2022 to KRW 48.9 billion in 2023 (down 11.2%), KRW 33.4 billion in 2024 (down 31.8%) and KRW 20.4 billion in 2025 (down 38.8%), a three-year decline from 2023 through 2025, with 2025 the smallest year in the period.

The operating margin moved from 3.3% in 2022 to 3.8% in 2023, 2.8% in 2024 and 1.6% in 2025. The company attributed the 2025 profit drop mainly to non-recurring costs, including provisions tied to restructuring its business.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders rose from KRW 14.7 billion in 2022 to KRW 43.2 billion in 2023 (up 193.8%), then fell to KRW 6.72 billion in 2024 (down 84.5%) and turned into a net loss of KRW 13.7 billion in 2025.

Total net income including non-controlling interests dropped from KRW 23.2 billion in 2024 to KRW 554 million in 2025. Operating cash flow was KRW 66.4 billion in 2022, KRW 163.8 billion in 2023, KRW 96.7 billion in 2024 and KRW 46.4 billion in 2025.

At the end of 2025, total liabilities were KRW 503.3 billion and total equity was KRW 492.7 billion, of which KRW 301.2 billion belonged to controlling shareholders. The debt-to-equity ratio fell from 143.6% in 2022 to 71.8% in 2024 before rising to 102.2% in 2025.

On a quarterly basis, operating profit went from KRW 17.7 billion in the third quarter of 2025 to an operating loss of KRW 29.3 billion in the fourth quarter as revenue fell 12.7% to KRW 289.5 billion. The first quarter of 2026 returned to operating profit of KRW 14.1 billion on revenue of KRW 316 billion.

Operating profit then reached KRW 14.2 billion in the second quarter (revenue KRW 335.1 billion) and KRW 14.8 billion in the third quarter (revenue KRW 346 billion, up 4.3% year on year but down 16.0% in operating profit).

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders went from KRW 7.85 billion in the third quarter of 2025 to a KRW 36.5 billion loss in the fourth quarter, then KRW 8.1 billion and KRW 8.12 billion in the first two quarters of 2026, before a KRW 413 million loss in the third quarter, which marked a swing from profit a year earlier.

The last four quarters together total a net loss attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 20.7 billion. Industry commentary holds that the third quarter, when the probe card subsidiary began consolidating, is the first real test of the restructuring.

05

Industry analysis

Demand from downstream markets comes from three directions. First, the shift to larger, premium TVs and wider home appliance demand supports the power board business, according to the corporate monitoring data provider Wisereport.

Second, expanded memory and foundry capital spending is lifting demand for semiconductor materials and equipment parts, and the acquired probe card maker sits in semiconductor testing.

Third, replacement demand for eco-friendly vessels and a shipbuilding upcycle support marine electronics, while electrification and greater electronic content in vehicles support automotive electronic parts. Solar runs the other way: the 2024 revenue decline was linked to a downturn in the solar industry.

Hansol IONES saw its precision machining results improve in the first half of 2025 on a semiconductor recovery, with first-half revenue of KRW 95.9 billion and operating profit of KRW 21.0 billion.

Yuanta Securities, however, concluded that Hansol IONES posted results below market expectations in 2026 because of promotion costs. On competitive position, the company's technical certifications for vessel engine controllers stand alongside its traditional TV power board and EMS businesses.

The Mexico investment is positioned to help the company respond to shifts in vehicle production in North America. Because the end markets move in different directions, quarterly earnings volatility is a structural feature of this business mix.

06

Outlook

Meritz Securities, in a May 2026 report, projected Hansol Technics' 2026 revenue at KRW 1.3637 trillion (up 8.9%) and operating profit at KRW 88.6 billion (up 334.2%). That estimate conservatively excluded the consolidation of the probe card subsidiary.

The same report named improved operating efficiency at production units as the key driver of core profitability and said the full-year contribution of the marine electronics unit would start in 2026.

However, quarterly operating profit was KRW 14.1 billion in the first quarter, KRW 14.2 billion in the second and KRW 14.8 billion in the third, all in the low teens of billions, so the gap to the full-year projection is large.

Press reports say the company expects semiconductor revenue to reach 20.2% of sales, up from 15.9%. Yuanta Securities expects the acquired businesses to be reflected on a full-year basis in 2027.

According to the same report, the probe card subsidiary posted revenue of KRW 66.7 billion and operating profit of KRW 9.2 billion in 2024, and revenue of KRW 67.4 billion and operating profit of KRW 9.6 billion in 2025, a fairly steady record before the acquisition.

Yuanta also argues that revenue growth and margin improvement at Hansol IONES could show up if promotion costs ease after the second half of 2026. Consolidation of production sites and results from the Mexico unit are expected to become visible gradually from 2027.

The next checkpoints are how the consolidated effect of the acquisitions and the operating margin move in the 2026 full-year results and the first-quarter 2027 results.

Risk factors

Integration and subsidiary earnings volatility

Full-year consolidation of the acquired businesses is expected to take effect in 2027, so integration results could be delayed. Hansol IONES was analyzed as falling short of expectations in 2026 because of promotion costs.

Subsidiary earnings depend on semiconductor capital spending, so changes in the cycle feed directly into profit.

Financial burden and cash flow

The debt-to-equity ratio rose to 102.2% at the end of 2025 from 71.8% at the end of 2024. Operating cash flow fell from KRW 96.7 billion in 2024 to KRW 46.4 billion in 2025. Because acquisition funding is concentrated, financial capacity could constrain how quickly earnings recover.

Provisional results and uneven end markets

The 2026 quarterly results are provisional figures based on internal closing and may change after external audit. Vehicle and marine electronics depend on shipbuilding conditions, and the solar business has been hit by an industry downturn before. Because end markets move in different directions, quarterly results could remain uneven.

Overall view

Hansol Technics is widening its business from TV and home appliance power boards and EMS into marine electronics, semiconductor equipment parts and semiconductor testing. Annual revenue rose to KRW 1.25 trillion in 2025, but operating profit fell to KRW 20.4 billion and the operating margin dropped to 1.6%.

In 2026, quarterly operating profit stayed in the black, ranging from KRW 14.1 billion to KRW 14.8 billion, but the third quarter swung to a net loss of KRW 413 million attributable to controlling shareholders.

Supporting factors include the full-year consolidation of acquired businesses, production-site consolidation and a stated goal of raising the semiconductor share of revenue.

Restraining factors include the gap between quarterly profit and the full-year projection, the KRW 20.7 billion net loss attributable to controlling shareholders over the last four quarters, and the 102.2% debt-to-equity ratio at the end of 2025.

On valuation, the price-to-book ratio is below 1x and the price-to-earnings ratio cannot be calculated. The next checkpoints are the provisional full-year results for fiscal 2026 in early February 2027 and the 2026 annual report due by March 31, 2027. Whether the acquisitions show up as a recovery in operating margin is the central question.
