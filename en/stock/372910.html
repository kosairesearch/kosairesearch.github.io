02

Business structure

Hancom Lifecare is a defense and safety equipment company affiliated with Hancom Inc., which acquired it in 2017. It listed on the KOSPI in 2021. The business is organized around three areas: defense, fire and safety, and unmanned robots.

In defense, the first and second batches of the ground laser target designator (GLTD) were completed in June 2026 and lifted Q2 results, while the next-generation K5 gas mask for the Republic of Korea Armed Forces is being delivered in sequence from July to October 2026.

Revenue from urban combat training and KCTC-related programs is expected to be recognized from 2027. The fire and safety unit is anchored by air respirators, with welding fire blankets and an EV and battery fire response solution added as new products in Q2 2026.

The company says it holds more than 90% of the domestic air respirator market, a claim made by the company itself.

The unmanned firefighting robot business began with a domestic supply MOU with Shark Robotics in May 2026, later expanded into a five-year exclusive domestic distribution and technology agreement covering the large Colossus firefighting and rescue robot.

As of September 2025 the company reported 14 projects across 11 countries and an opportunity worth about KRW 35.7 billion over five years. Customers include the armed forces and fire agencies at home and abroad, and the company itself describes defense delivery timing as a driver of large quarter-to-quarter swings.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual sales peaked at KRW 112.8 billion in 2023, up 12.6% year on year. Sales then fell 7.1% to KRW 104.8 billion in 2024 and 12.1% to KRW 92.1 billion in 2025, a two-year decline from 2024 through 2025. Among the years 2022 to 2025, 2023 was the largest and 2025 the smallest. Profit moved more sharply than sales.

After an operating loss of KRW 1.33 billion in 2022, the company swung to operating profit of KRW 1.83 billion in 2023 and KRW 7.49 billion in 2024, an increase of 308.8%, with an operating margin of 7.1%.

In 2025 it returned to an operating loss of KRW 727 million, and net profit attributable to controlling shareholders turned into a loss of KRW 7.73 billion, while net loss including non-controlling interests was KRW 7.67 billion.

The company attributed the 2025 weakness to a short gap when a large defense project ended and the next project was still in preparation. Reports also tied delays in fire and public-sector orders to the December 2024 martial law episode. The debt-to-equity ratio fell to 42.0% in 2023 and rose to 59.3% in 2025.

At the end of 2025, total equity was KRW 127.8 billion and total liabilities were KRW 75.8 billion. Operating cash flow was KRW -8.47 billion in 2023, then KRW 22.2 billion in 2024 and KRW 22.7 billion in 2025, staying positive even in the loss year.

Quarterly, Q4 2025 sales were KRW 34.8 billion with an operating loss of KRW 3.12 billion and a net loss attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 7.38 billion. Q1 2026 was the trough, with sales of KRW 5.99 billion and an operating loss of KRW 3.81 billion.

Q2 2026 rebounded to sales of KRW 55.1 billion and operating profit of KRW 7.61 billion. Q3 2026 sales of KRW 30.9 billion and operating profit of KRW 3.43 billion were below Q2, though up 31.2% and 14.7% year on year.

Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders in the last four quarters totals a loss of KRW 918 million. Quarters such as Q4 2025, where the net loss attributable to controlling shareholders exceeded the operating loss, call for a check of non-operating and tax items.

05

Industry analysis

Hancom Lifecare is classified in the electrical equipment sector, but the markets that drive its results are defense equipment and fire and safety equipment. Defense is a order-driven business in which quarterly revenue moves sharply with government procurement and delivery schedules.

The company attributed the 2025 third-quarter gap to the overlap of a large defense project ending and the next project still in preparation, describing it as a natural cycle typical of order-based businesses. In Q2 2026, GLTD deliveries were fully recognized, and the company entered the upswing of that cycle.

The fire and safety market is affected by public-sector order timing. Reports said fire and public orders were delayed after the December 2024 martial law episode, which pulled sales down.

In unmanned firefighting robots, the company describes the domestic market as at an early stage, and its partner Shark Robotics is presented as the world's leading firefighting robot maker by market share.

Overseas, reports in 2025 said a UAE military gas mask purchase completed local testing and a European military performance evaluation was completed, providing a foothold for entry into NATO member markets.

The company's position rests on a domestic air respirator sales and service network, with expansion into robots and defense.

06

Outlook

The company says visibility for second-half defense revenue has improved. The next-generation K5 gas mask is being delivered in sequence from July through October 2026, and remaining GLTD volume is also expected to be recognized in results.

Because Q3 2026 sales fell from the prior quarter, a first checkpoint is how much the K5 deliveries running through October and the remaining GLTD volume can support Q4 2026 revenue.

Over the medium term, the company's framing centers on revenue from urban combat training and KCTC-related programs being recognized from 2027. In unmanned firefighting robots, the company plans to integrate its in-house agentic OS into Shark Robotics' large Colossus firefighting and rescue robot within 2026.

The company aims to upgrade the product into an intelligent robot solution and combine it with its domestic fire sales network to secure an early position in the domestic market.

Overseas, it is pursuing defense orders centered on Asia and the Middle East, and reports in 2025 said it was building an air respirator factory with a major local fire and safety company in the Middle East.

The CEO said growth could widen once second-half K5 deliveries and the unmanned firefighting robot business take hold. Much of the growth the company describes depends on order timing, so whether deliveries proceed as scheduled is central to the outlook.

Durability of profit and financial burden

Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders in Q3 2026 was KRW 1.78 billion, down 22.3% year on year, showing profit declining even in a profitable quarter.

Governance dispute and weak share price

Continued reports of weak share price performance mean governance uncertainty is weighing on investor sentiment.

Risk factors

Defense delivery timing

Reports said that in Q1 2025 defense revenue fell sharply due to delays in the second GLTD project, which had a total order value of KRW 78.69 billion, and the end of a large training system program. Defense orders are a business where quarterly revenue swings with procurement timing.

If K5 deliveries and the remaining GLTD volume do not proceed on schedule, the rebound in results could be constrained.

New businesses and overseas orders

The company describes the domestic unmanned firefighting robot market as at an early stage. Integration of the agentic OS is planned for 2026, while overseas defense orders are still moving through local testing and contracting.

The timing and scale of revenue from these new businesses have not yet appeared in reported results, so delays could widen the gap between expectations and performance.

Governance and financial volatility

Reports of a second-largest shareholder seeking access to accounting books have drawn attention to management transparency. The debt-to-equity ratio rose to 59.3% in 2025, and operating cash flow moved from KRW -8.47 billion in 2023 to KRW 22.2 billion in 2024. This volatility is why cash flow and debt structure should be checked together in the next annual report.

Overall view

Hancom Lifecare's Q2 2026 results, with GLTD deliveries recognized, turned operating profit into KRW 7.61 billion and gave evidence of a rebound.

The second half still carries K5 gas mask deliveries and remaining GLTD volume, and revenue from training programs and KCTC-related work is expected to be recognized from 2027, which underpins medium-term visibility. The unmanned firefighting robot and overseas order businesses remain largely at the planning stage.

On the other side, Q3 2026 sales fell 43.9% from the prior quarter, and the last four quarters total a net loss attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 918 million. The 2025 return to a loss and the rise in the debt-to-equity ratio to 59.3% are also financial changes to weigh.

Governance issues, including the court-ordered inspection request by the second-largest shareholder and a long period of share price weakness, remain. Strength and weakness factors coexist, and the next checkpoints are the 2026 annual report and the defense revenue trend in the Q1 2027 quarterly report.
