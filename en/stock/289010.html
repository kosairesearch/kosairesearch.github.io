02

Business structure

i-Scream Edu is an edutech company whose main business is Icecream Home Run, a smart-learning service centered on elementary students.

The core product is a home self-directed learning program that combines multimedia content with a dedicated digital book, and the target audience has widened to preschoolers and middle school students. According to an Eduplus report, Icecream Home Run accounted for more than 90% of company revenue in 2024.

The revenue base is therefore concentrated in a single product and centered on business-to-consumer (B2C) sales. In August 2026 the company said it would expand this structure into business-to-business (B2B) and business-to-government (B2G) markets.

At the early September 2026 EdTech Expo, it showed AI-based personalized learning content such as 'Math Cells', 'AI Dream Teacher' and 'AI Student Record', and said it uses 16 million learning records per day.

In October 2026 it opened an 'EBSi Itus special hall' that bundles Itus lectures into the middle school service 'Icecream Home Run Middle School'. On November 10, 2026 it added a 'Literacy Complete' tab to the elementary service, grouping vocabulary, reading and dictation functions.

In public-welfare work it proposed 'Little Home Run' for special education and supplies learning devices and content to local government education-gap programs.

Competition overlaps with smart-learning providers such as Chunjae Education and Megastudy, and with elementary content providers such as Woongjin and Visang Education.

04

Earnings analysis

On the basis of the disclosed annual results, revenue fell from KRW 133.8 billion in 2022 to KRW 117.6 billion in 2023 (a 12.1% decrease from the prior year), KRW 107.6 billion in 2024 (8.5% lower) and KRW 91.6 billion in 2025 (14.9% lower).

Revenue declined for three consecutive years from 2023 to 2025, with 2022 the largest year and 2025 the smallest in the 2022 to 2025 period. Press reports show revenue rising from KRW 115.5 billion in 2020 to KRW 128.6 billion in 2021 before reaching the 2022 level.

Operating profit went from KRW 2.09 billion in 2022 to an operating loss of KRW 16.7 billion in 2023, a narrower operating loss of KRW 1.94 billion in 2024, and an operating profit of KRW 1.4 billion in 2025, which marked a return to profit.

The operating margin was 1.6% in 2022, -14.2% in 2023, -1.8% in 2024 and 1.5% in 2025.

The company said that business restructuring and operating cost efficiency, including selling, general and administrative expenses, carried out over about two years from 2024 improved profitability, and press reports noted that first-quarter selling and administrative expenses fell from about KRW 12.6 billion to KRW 8.7 billion.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders went from KRW 818 million in 2022 to a net loss of KRW 23.5 billion in 2023, a net loss of KRW 6.21 billion in 2024 and a net loss of KRW 1.06 billion in 2025. Net income including non-controlling interests followed the same pattern.

The debt ratio went from 37.9% in 2022 to 106.2% in 2023, 99.6% in 2024 and 57.3% in 2025.

Quarterly revenue went from KRW 21 billion in the third quarter of 2025 to KRW 20.3 billion in the first quarter of 2026, KRW 19.3 billion in the second quarter (down 5.0% from the prior quarter) and KRW 18.5 billion in the third quarter (down 4.1% from the prior quarter and down 11.8% from a year earlier).

Operating profit was KRW 853 million in the first quarter of 2026 and KRW 1.65 billion in the second quarter, up 93.6% from the prior quarter, while revenue fell over the same period, so profit improved alongside a smaller top line.

Third-quarter operating profit was KRW 979 million, down 40.8% from the prior quarter, and net income attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 180 million, a swing to profit from a net loss a year earlier.

There are no quarterly figures for the fourth quarter of 2025, so the annual figures are the reference point for that period.

05

Industry analysis

The end markets for i-Scream Edu are primary and secondary education services and home learning demand.

An Eduplus report cited the shift from smart learning back to private academies after the pandemic, the shrinking school-age population and intensifying competition in elementary content as reasons for the 2024 sales decline. Industry commentary said most Korean edutech companies saw their results turn down from 2023.

Analysts also said demand moved back to offline learning after the end of the pandemic. In smart learning, the company has been competing with Chunjae Education's Milkt product and Megastudy's Ellihigh, according to press coverage.

Its sister company in the same group, i-Scream Media, is built around textbooks and business-to-government sales and reported KRW 152.1 billion in revenue in 2024, a contrast in business mix within the group. The policy backdrop is partly supportive.

In July 2026 the Ministry of Education announced a plan to revitalize school reading education, and the company has been strengthening its reading challenge and literacy content in line with that direction. The reading focus matches the content direction of Icecream Home Run.

However, the shrinking school-age population remains a structural variable, and whether policy tailwinds can offset the sales decline needs to be checked against future disclosed results.

06

Outlook

The company's direction is a shift to an AI platform. According to an August 2026 report, it plans to upgrade Icecream Home Run into a K-12 AI platform connecting learning, assessment and growth, and to expand from B2C into B2B and B2G markets based on AI for essay and written assessment and education-specific AI.

It is developing education-specific AI with Seoul National University's Big Data Innovation College, the Gyeongin National University of Education AI Customized Learning Institute and the Special Education Digital Education Association.

It has also set AI assessment technology for literacy, thinking and problem-solving as a development goal, and said it would pursue collaboration with cloud and AI companies to connect education data.

Among product milestones, the upgrade of the AI Dream Teacher counseling service in September 2026, the opening of the Itus special hall in October 2026 and the addition of the Literacy Complete service in November 2026 have already been carried out.

In the middle school segment, the bundle with Itus lectures began targeting higher-performing students. On overseas expansion, a 2024 report said the launch of an AI platform for US college admissions had been postponed by a year as the company focused on Korea.

At the March 2026 annual shareholders meeting, Lee Jae-jun was newly appointed as an inside director, and the change of chief executive took effect on March 26. Whether the restructuring after this management change shows up in results is the next thing to check.

08

Bull factors

Operating profit back in the black

Operating profit turned to KRW 1.4 billion in 2025 and stayed positive in 2026, at KRW 853 million in the first quarter, KRW 1.65 billion in the second and KRW 979 million in the third. The company attributes the improvement to business restructuring and lower selling and administrative expenses.

In the second quarter of 2026 operating profit rose 93.6% from the prior quarter even as revenue declined, a bullish point on the cost side of the business.

AI platform and B2B/B2G expansion

The company plans to upgrade Icecream Home Run into a K-12 AI platform and expand business aimed at companies and government. It is developing education-specific AI with Seoul National University's Big Data Innovation College and the Gyeongin National University of Education AI Customized Learning Institute.

Product expansion is already visible, including the Literacy Complete service in November 2026 and the Itus special hall in October 2026. If revenue sources diversify, dependence on a single product could decline.

Improving financial structure

The debt ratio fell from 106.2% in 2023 to 57.3% in 2025. Operating cash flow improved from a KRW 8 billion outflow in 2023 to KRW 4.56 billion in 2024 and KRW 9.67 billion in 2025. Total equity was KRW 42.6 billion and total liabilities KRW 24.4 billion at the end of 2025. Recovering cash generation and lower debt burden are supportive points on the financial side.

09

Bear factors

Revenue decline continues

Revenue fell from KRW 133.8 billion in 2022 to KRW 91.6 billion in 2025, declining for three straight years from 2023. Third-quarter 2026 revenue was KRW 18.5 billion, down 11.8% from a year earlier, and has declined for two consecutive quarters since the second quarter of 2026.

Growth that ran from 2020 to 2022 has reversed and the pace of decline has widened. Continued shrinkage would weigh on the durability of operating profit.

Volatile earnings

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders fell 89.9% from KRW 1.79 billion in the second quarter of 2026 to KRW 180 million in the third quarter.

The company posted a KRW 1.52 billion net loss in the third quarter of 2025, and the 2025 full-year result still shows a KRW 1.06 billion loss attributable to controlling shareholders. Periods where operating profit is positive but net income is not have repeated.

Competition and shifting demand

Icecream Home Run accounts for more than 90% of revenue, and a shrinking school-age population and intensified competition in elementary content have been cited as causes of the sales decline. The company competes in smart learning with Chunjae Education and Megastudy.

Analysts have also said demand shifted to private academies after the pandemic. If the competitive environment does not improve, changing the product structure could take longer.

10

Risk factors

Revenue concentration

Icecream Home Run accounted for more than 90% of revenue in 2024, so user attrition or price competition in that product would flow directly into company-wide results. The expansion into B2B and B2G has been announced but has not yet shown up as a clear revenue share. Whether single-product dependence declines remains an ongoing check.

Financial capacity

At the end of the third quarter of 2024, convertible bonds stood at KRW 11.3 billion, short-term borrowings at KRW 3 billion and cash equivalents at about KRW 10.5 billion. Total liabilities were KRW 24.4 billion at the end of 2025.

Operating cash flow improved to KRW 9.67 billion in 2025, but borrowing and convertible bond repayment remain variables. Reduced financial capacity could affect the pace of investment and product development.

Shareholder structure and liquidity

According to a September 2024 report on the termination of a trust contract, the company's treasury share holdings rose sharply. Analysts have said the company came to rely on treasury share purchases to manage its share price.

With a small market capitalization, trading conditions and share price volatility are more sensitive. The controlling shareholder's stake and any plans to dispose of treasury shares should be confirmed through future disclosures.

12

Overall view

i-Scream Edu is showing signs of improvement, with a return to operating profit in 2025 and positive quarterly operating profit through 2026. Cost efficiency, a lower debt ratio and better operating cash flow are evidence that the financial base has strengthened.

On the other side, revenue fell from KRW 133.8 billion in 2022 to KRW 91.6 billion in 2025, declining three years in a row, and has fallen for two consecutive quarters in 2026. Net income attributable to controlling shareholders remained a loss for 2025 and swung widely quarter to quarter in 2026.

More than 90% of revenue still depends on one product. The AI platform, the expansion into B2B and B2G, and new literacy and middle school offerings are under way, but whether they have translated into a sales recovery is not yet visible in disclosed results.

Because the strengths and weaknesses carry similar weight, the next checkpoints are the revenue trend in the 2026 annual business report and the first-quarter 2027 results.
