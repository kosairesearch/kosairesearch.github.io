02

Business structure

S Polytech is a specialty engineering plastics company built on polycarbonate (PC) and PMMA (acrylic) materials. The company says it is shifting its center of gravity toward specialty materials while maintaining its market position in its traditional PC and PMMA business.

The core of this restructuring is security films for electronic passports and ID cards, along with flame-retardant materials for aircraft interiors. Security films are supplied under a customer-tailored production system.

For aircraft flame-retardant materials, the company says international safety certifications obtained in line with tightening global aviation safety rules act as a barrier to entry.

The specialty lineup includes multilayer sheets, flame-retardant PC, anti-whitening films and security films, and specialty sales reached KRW 14.9 billion in first-half 2026, or 40.1% of product sales.

On a standalone basis, cumulative specialty sales for January to September 2026 were KRW 21.89 billion, up 22.1% year on year, with a 40.3% share. Overseas sales rose to roughly 33% of consolidated sales in first-half 2026, and exports reached KRW 12.27 billion, up 33.9% year on year.

Production capacity is 44,672 tons, and domestic sales run through dealers and direct channels on a customer-order basis. General-purpose PC is exposed to selling-price swings, and in the third quarter of 2026 lower prices compounded cost pressure.

By contrast, specialty products rely on global safety certifications and customer-tailored production as their competitive base.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual results show revenue declines combined with a swing to losses. Revenue went from KRW 82.5 billion in 2022 to KRW 73.2 billion in 2023 (down 11.2% year on year), recovered to KRW 80.2 billion in 2024 (up 9.5%), and then fell to KRW 66.8 billion in 2025 (down 16.7%).

Among 2022 to 2025, 2022 was the largest year and 2025 the smallest. Operating losses of KRW 1.59 billion in 2022 and KRW 2.59 billion in 2023 were followed by operating profit of KRW 643 million in 2024, a turn to profit. In 2025 the company returned to an operating loss of KRW 4.81 billion.

The company attributed the wider 2025 loss to lower sales from industry downturns and to impairment losses on property, plant and equipment recognized after asset-impairment testing under K-IFRS.

Net loss attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 8.79 billion in 2025, after controlling-shareholder net profit of KRW 5.03 billion in 2024. The operating margin was -1.9% in 2022, -3.5% in 2023, 0.8% in 2024 and -7.2% in 2025, the lowest of the four years.

Quarterly, an operating loss of KRW 1.29 billion in Q3 2025 and KRW 1.11 billion in Q4 2025 (a smaller loss than the prior quarter) preceded operating profit of KRW 327 million in Q1 2026.

Q2 2026 operating profit of KRW 1.37 billion rose 319.7% from the prior quarter, which the company linked to a better sales mix toward high-value products. Q3 2026 swung to an operating loss of KRW 1.24 billion versus the prior quarter, though the loss narrowed from a year earlier.

Controlling-shareholder net loss for the quarter was KRW 4.1 billion, wider than a year earlier.

The company cited higher first-half raw material prices, lower general-purpose PC selling prices, construction delays from a heat wave, deferred security-film deliveries, and a coating-process problem that delayed aircraft flame-retardant sheet exports.

The preliminary first-half 2026 operating profit of KRW 17 million-level figures turned from a KRW 24 million loss a year earlier, in preliminary terms.

On the balance sheet, the debt-to-equity ratio rose from 66.0% in 2022 to 95.2% in 2025, and operating cash flow went from KRW 11.1 billion in 2022 to KRW -504 million in 2023, then KRW 980 million in 2024 and KRW 584 million in 2025.

05

Industry analysis

End demand is split across four areas: construction and domestic building materials, aviation, security documents and AI data centers. The company said domestic sales declined in 2025 amid an industry slowdown.

In summer 2026 a heat wave delayed construction sites and raised domestic inventories, according to the company. In aviation, the company says tightening global aviation safety rules underpin demand.

In AI data centers, the company has outlined a strategy to address demand for fire-safety materials tied to higher-density, higher-performance equipment. It obtained the top ASTM E84 Class A rating and is pursuing supply of flame-retardant materials for North American AI data centers on that basis.

An August 2026 report said the company had already supplied PC materials to a North American AI data center project. In security documents, sales of ID-card films rose 31.7% in first-half 2026.

On pricing, rising demand for high-value materials and falling prices for general-purpose PC are pulling in opposite directions.

On cycle position, the company's account shows margin improvement in first-half 2026 after the 2025 slowdown, but the third-quarter loss means the durability of that recovery is still to be confirmed. The differentiators the company emphasizes are global safety certification and customer-tailored production.

06

Outlook

The company said it will focus in the fourth quarter of 2026 on delivering deferred volumes and normalizing exports of aircraft flame-retardant sheets. It also plans to strengthen sales of high-value and eco-friendly new products to restore profitability.

For the second half, it outlined expanding high-value materials sales to AI data centers and aviation, based on its ASTM E84 Class A-rated PC multilayer sheets.

It also said it would accelerate entry into new B2B markets, including X-shaped structures for multilayer sheets and eco-friendly lamination films with high barrier properties.

It aims to expand share in new growth areas such as security, aviation and eco-friendly materials and make 2026 the first year of earnings turnaround. How much North American AI data center supply translates into revenue has not yet been shown in disclosed figures.

Because exports to the United States rose 78.9% in first-half 2026, US demand will be an important variable for the next results. The direction of general-purpose PC selling prices and raw material costs remains a key variable for cost pressure.

The next checkpoint is the 2026 annual report, which will include fourth-quarter 2026 results, where the effect of deferred deliveries and the normalization of aircraft flame-retardant sheet exports will appear in numbers.

Bull factors

Higher share of high-value products

The share of high-value products reached 40.1% in first-half 2026, up 3.9 percentage points from 36.2% a year earlier. Sales of flame-retardant PC for aircraft rose 167.1% year on year, and ID-card MF sales rose 31.7%. The company said the higher share of high-value products led to better profitability.

Export growth and certification base

First-half 2026 exports rose 33.9% to KRW 12.27 billion, with US exports up 78.9%. The company obtained the top ASTM E84 Class A rating and is pursuing flame-retardant supply for North American AI data centers. It also says global safety certifications act as a barrier to entry in aviation.

Quarterly profit recovery

Operating profit turned positive at KRW 327 million in Q1 2026, and Q2 2026 operating profit reached KRW 1.37 billion, up 319.7% from the prior quarter. The direction of profit changed after an operating loss of KRW 1.11 billion in Q4 2025.

Preliminary first-half 2026 operating profit of KRW 17 million, announced July 28, 2026, compares with a KRW 24 million loss a year earlier.

Bear factors

Q3 relapse and cost pressure

The operating loss in Q3 2026 was KRW 1.24 billion, a swing from profit in the prior quarter, and the controlling-shareholder net loss of KRW 4.1 billion was wider than a year earlier. The company cited higher raw material prices and lower general-purpose PC selling prices as main causes of cost pressure. The scale of any fourth-quarter improvement depends on how much deferred volume is delivered.

Shrinking revenue and impairment charges

Revenue fell from KRW 82.5 billion in 2022 to KRW 66.8 billion in 2025, and the company recorded an operating loss of KRW 4.81 billion and a controlling-shareholder net loss of KRW 8.79 billion in 2025.

The company cited the industry slowdown and recognized impairment losses on property, plant and equipment as drivers of the wider loss. Total equity fell from KRW 65.2 billion at the end of 2024 to KRW 56.4 billion at the end of 2025.

Financial capacity and cash flow

The debt-to-equity ratio rose from 66.0% in 2022 to 95.2% in 2025. Operating cash flow was KRW -504 million in 2023, KRW 980 million in 2024 and KRW 584 million in 2025, all small amounts.

Shrinking financial capacity is tied to the pace of earnings recovery, making the cash flow in the next annual report an important checkpoint.

Risk factors

Raw materials and general-purpose PC prices

When raw material prices rise and general-purpose PC selling prices fall at the same time, cost pressure increases. The company cited both factors for the deterioration in third-quarter 2026 results.

Even with a higher share of high-value products, profit volatility can remain large during periods when general-purpose products weigh heavily.

Construction and domestic demand, deferred deliveries

The company says a heat wave delayed construction work and raised domestic inventories. Some security-film volumes were also deferred. How much of the deferred volume is recognized in the fourth quarter will shape swings in quarterly results.

Export concentration and certification dependence

Exports to the United States rose 78.9% in first-half 2026, raising the share of overseas sales. However, exports of aircraft flame-retardant sheets were delayed by a problem in outsourced coating processes. Greater reliance on specific markets and certifications can magnify the impact of supply disruptions.

Overall view

S Polytech shifted its business toward specialty materials and preliminarily reported a first-half 2026 operating profit. The share of high-value products expanded to 40.1% in first-half 2026, and exports and aviation and security sales increased.

However, the third quarter of 2026 returned to losses, with an operating loss of KRW 1.24 billion and a controlling-shareholder net loss of KRW 4.1 billion, driven largely by raw material costs and general-purpose PC prices.

Revenue fell to KRW 66.8 billion in 2025 with a net loss of KRW 8.79 billion, and the debt-to-equity ratio rose to 95.2%. The price-to-book ratio is below 1x, while the price-to-earnings ratio cannot be calculated because the last four quarters were net losses.

Positive factors include the high-value product mix, overseas exports and certification base, while negative factors include the renewed Q3 loss, shrinking revenue and financial burden.

The next checkpoints are the fourth-quarter results and deferred-delivery effects in the 2026 annual report, and quarterly profit in Q1 2027.
