Report overview

Operating Profit Up, Shares Far Below IPO Price

Kido Industrial is a newly listed apparel OEM whose first-half 2026 operating profit improved, while net income fell and post-listing share supply weighed on the stock.

  3. 3

02

Business structure

Kido Industrial is a high-performance apparel OEM (original equipment manufacturer) founded in August 1980. Its main products are outdoor apparel and motorcycle apparel. It produces against orders from global brands, so reliance on its own brand is limited.

Production is spread across its Korean headquarters and six production entities in four countries: Vietnam, Myanmar, Bangladesh, and Indonesia.

Europe accounted for the largest share of 2025 revenue, at 47%. Two external customers each accounting for 10% or more of revenue made up a combined 25.5%. The company said that in 2025 it earned an operating margin of 9.3%, against averages of 5.3% for 14 woven-apparel OEMs and 3.2% for five knit-apparel OEMs. Management says it competes on technical difficulty rather than unit price.

The IPO price was set using three listed apparel peers, whose average PER was 10.01x. Press reports noted that one peer's PER of 18.84x lifted that average.

The affiliate Kido Sports was created in 2006 by spinning off Kido Industrial's helmet business, and after a later spin-off and absorption merger it became a privately held company of the owner. That separation weakened the equity-method income base, which matters when reading net income.

The KOSDAQ 'Other' category mixes paper, education, and musical-instrument companies, so direct comparison within the category is limited.

03

Recent trends

For 2025, Kido Industrial reported consolidated revenue of KRW 346.6 billion, operating profit of KRW 32.2 billion, and net income of KRW 35.3 billion. The operating margin was 9.3%. In the first quarter of 2026, operating profit was only about KRW 400 million and the company posted a net loss of KRW 3.1 billion.

The company attributed this to foreign-exchange translation losses on foreign-currency borrowings after the won weakened.

Per DART filings, cumulative first-half 2026 revenue was KRW 71.1 billion, up 0.7% year on year. Operating profit was KRW 5.91 billion, up 95.2%. Net income was KRW 4.18 billion, down 59.1%.

The gap between higher operating profit and lower net income may reflect non-operating items such as lower equity-method income after the Kido Sports separation and foreign-exchange losses, but the exact breakdown should be checked in the half-year report notes.

Quarterly figures from press reports and the cumulative half-year figures may differ in scope, so a check against the original filing is advisable.

The shares fell sharply after listing. The IPO price was fixed at KRW 28,400 after an institutional bookbuilding competition ratio of 213.3 to 1, while the retail subscription ratio was 5.5 to 1. The closing price on August 26, 2026 was KRW 15,020, which was 47.11% below the IPO price.

The entire institutional allocation of 1,241,000 shares carried no lock-up commitment. On October 27, 2026 the share price was KRW 10,200, with a market capitalization of KRW 59.4 billion and trading value of KRW 200 million.

04

Outlook

After the listing, the company presented expansion of its Bangladesh production entity as its core growth strategy. Plans call for adding 28 sewing lines in stages from early 2027, with completion by mid-2028.

It also plans to automate existing plants, lifting annual capacity from about 4 million to more than 6 million pieces. The company's target is to move from roughly KRW 350 billion of revenue in 2025 to the low KRW 500 billion range by 2030.

IPO proceeds are earmarked for overseas capacity, smart-manufacturing systems, and debt repayment. Of about KRW 37.7 billion in proceeds at the final offering terms, KRW 12.0 billion is allocated to repaying debt.

On shareholder returns, the company plans to raise its payout ratio from 22.4% in 2025 to 30% or more in 2026 and about 40% by 2028, with a minimum target dividend of KRW 2,000 per share from the fiscal 2027 year-end dividend.

One brokerage analyst assessed that quarterly orders have recovered since the second quarter, so the earlier decline could be read as a temporary supplier-adjustment gap.

The key question is whether the added capacity converts into actual order growth. An economic slowdown, order cuts, exchange-rate swings, and rising labor costs in production countries remain burdens. The controlling shareholder's 3,380,965 shares are locked up for 24 months and treasury shares for six months.

By contrast, the 1,666,000 shares allocated to public-offering investors and the 233,185 shares held by a major shareholder carry no sale restrictions. Trading value of around KRW 200 million is also a factor when reading short-term supply and demand.

05

Bull factors

Operating margin above peers

The company said its 2025 operating margin of 9.3% exceeded the averages of woven and knit OEMs. Cumulative first-half 2026 operating profit was KRW 5.91 billion, up 95.2% year on year. Operating profit rose even though revenue grew only 0.7%, so the profitability metric improved. Whether that improvement continues is the point to monitor.

Capacity expansion plan

The plan to add 28 sewing lines in Bangladesh would lift annual capacity from about 4 million to more than 6 million pieces. The company said this is to handle higher volumes from existing customers and new orders from global brands.

Its production network across Vietnam, Myanmar, Bangladesh, and Indonesia is presented as a base for responding to country-specific tariff changes. Revenue in the low KRW 500 billion range by 2030 is the company's medium-term target.

Higher payouts and debt reduction plan

The company said it will raise its payout ratio from 22.4% in 2025 to about 40% by 2028. It plans to set a minimum target dividend of KRW 2,000 per share starting with the fiscal 2027 year-end dividend. Of about KRW 37.7 billion in IPO proceeds, KRW 12.0 billion is allocated to debt repayment.

That step points toward reducing the non-operating loss volatility that stemmed from foreign-currency borrowings.

Bear factors

Lower net income and non-operating volatility

Cumulative first-half 2026 net income was KRW 4.18 billion, down 59.1% year on year. The company recorded a net loss of KRW 3.1 billion in the first quarter and cited foreign-exchange translation losses. The Kido Sports separation also weakened the equity-method income base and changed the net income structure. Periods in which operating improvement does not reach net income may recur.

Customer and regional concentration

Europe accounted for 47% of 2025 revenue, and two customers each above 10% made up a combined 25.5%. According to recent press reports, a decline in orders from a key client weighed on first-quarter 2026 results.

Order adjustments by top customers or weaker European consumption could translate directly into revenue swings. The larger the expansion, the more important it becomes to keep utilization up.

Post-listing supply pressure

The retail subscription ratio was only 5.5 to 1, and the entire institutional allocation of 1,241,000 shares had no lock-up commitment. The offering included a secondary sale of 350,000 shares by CEO Park Janghee, worth KRW 9.94 billion at the IPO price.

The share price moved from KRW 15,020 on August 26, 2026 to KRW 10,200 on October 27, 2026, less than half the IPO price. Trading value of about KRW 200 million also indicates thin liquidity, which can widen price swings.

07

Risk factors

Macro

The apparel OEM industry is sensitive to overseas brand orders and consumer demand. With a high European revenue share, weaker local consumption and exchange-rate moves can affect results. Tariff conditions and wage increases in production countries also affect costs.

Press coverage of the registration statement cited an economic slowdown, order cuts, currency swings, and labor-cost increases as burdens.

Financial

Total borrowings were KRW 90.6 billion at the end of 2025. Joint guarantees provided by the CEO for company borrowings amount to KRW 122.5 billion. Facility investment of more than KRW 45.0 billion in the Bangladesh entity was funded with debt.

Translation losses on foreign-currency borrowings were cited behind the first-quarter 2026 net loss, and the effect of repaying part of the debt with IPO proceeds should be checked in future filings.

Governance and supply

CEO Park Janghee's post-listing stake was 57.69% per the filing of August 25, 2026. After the secondary sale, he said he would hold the remaining 3,380,965 shares under a 24-month lock-up.

By contrast, shares allocated to public-offering investors and the stake of a major shareholder have no sale restrictions and can circulate in the market. The owner family separately holds Kido Sports, so the market also watches for potential conflicts of interest.

08

Overall view

Kido Industrial is a mid-sized high-performance apparel OEM that reported a 9.3% operating margin for 2025. Cumulative first-half 2026 operating profit rose 95.2% to KRW 5.91 billion, while net income fell 59.1% to KRW 4.18 billion.

The bullish arguments are the Bangladesh expansion, the 2030 revenue target, and the plan to raise payouts. The bearish arguments are non-operating volatility, customer and European concentration, the uncommitted institutional shares, and thin trading value.

The share price was KRW 10,200 on October 27, 2026, less than half the IPO price of KRW 28,400, and market capitalization was KRW 59.4 billion.

Indicators to watch are the gap between operating profit and net income from the third quarter of 2026 onward, the recovery in orders, progress on the Bangladesh expansion, and debt repayment. This report is for information only, and investment decisions are left to the reader.
