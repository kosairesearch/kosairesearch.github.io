02

Business structure

Kido Industrial, founded in 1980, is an OEM manufacturer specializing in high-tech outerwear that produces functional apparel to order for overseas brands.

Its product lines are split between outdoor apparel and motorcycle apparel, and in 2025 on a standalone basis outdoor apparel accounted for 78.9% of product sales while motorcycle apparel accounted for 20.6%.

In the first quarter of 2026 the mix shifted toward motorcycle wear, with outdoor at 69.9% and motorcycle apparel at 28.8%. Its customers include Harley-Davidson, Barbour, Jack Wolfskin and Schöffel, which are global premium brands.

Production is concentrated in Southeast Asia, with two plants in Vietnam and one each in Indonesia, Myanmar and Bangladesh. By region, 2025 revenue came from Europe at KRW 162.9 billion, Korea at KRW 111.7 billion and the United States at KRW 36.1 billion, and Europe's 47% share was the largest.

Two external customers that each exceed 10% of revenue together accounted for 25.5%, which points to meaningful customer dependence. On competition, the company cited a 2025 average operating margin of 5.3% for 14 woven-fabric OEM peers and 3.2% for five knit OEM peers, against its own 9.3%.

In November 2025 the company spun off its investment business and merged it into Kido Sports, a subsidiary that had contributed about 40% of Kido Industrial's net income, after which the helmet B2C stake moved toward the largest shareholder.

03

Recent trends

Cumulative third-quarter 2026 revenue reached KRW 112.8 billion, down 7.2% year on year. Operating profit fell 50.4% to KRW 11.3 billion, a steeper decline than revenue, while net income fell 21.3% to KRW 14.1 billion, a smaller drop than operating profit.

A simple calculation from the reported figures puts nine-month operating margin at roughly 10.0%. Full-year 2025 consolidated revenue was KRW 346.5 billion, operating profit was KRW 32.2 billion and net income was KRW 35.3 billion, with the operating margin at 9.3%.

First-quarter 2026 revenue was KRW 64.9 billion, down about 5% year on year, with operating profit of KRW 0.4 billion and a net loss of KRW 3.1 billion, which the company attributed partly to FX translation losses on foreign-currency borrowings.

Press reports said that after excluding roughly KRW 8.5 billion of equity-method income tied to Kido Sports, trailing twelve-month adjusted income attributable to controlling shareholders was about KRW 21.2 billion.

The Jack Wolfskin order value fell 99%, from USD 8.4 million in the first quarter of 2025 to about USD 94,000 in the first quarter of 2026.

Press coverage placed the KOSDAQ debut on August 21, 2026, with the offering price set at the top of the indicative band, KRW 28,400, after an institutional demand forecast of 213.31 to 1. Mandatory lock-up commitments among institutional investors were only about 0.003% of demand.

As of 14:23 KST on November 18, 2026, the share price stood at KRW 10,410, up 3.69% on the day, with a market capitalization of KRW 61.0 billion.

04

Outlook

The company said that after listing it would begin adding one production line per month at its Bangladesh plant from early 2027, with completion targeted for the middle of 2028. It also said that efficiency work at other plants could raise total capacity by about 50% from current levels.

Company management projects revenue in the low KRW 500 billion range by 2030, although this is a management projection rather than a reported result. Of the KRW 32.8 billion in net proceeds, KRW 23.5 billion is allocated to facilities, KRW 1.3 billion to operations, and KRW 8.0 billion to debt repayment.

Interest expense rose from KRW 3.3 billion in 2023 to KRW 5.5 billion in 2025, so whether debt repayment eases that burden is a key point to track.

The recovery of Jack Wolfskin orders is another critical variable, since the company said orders had begun recovering from the second quarter of 2026 while the new owner expands its China business.

Whether the core operating margin can hold without equity-method income and dividends from Kido Sports will be visible in upcoming quarterly results. The company also outlined a plan to raise its dividend payout ratio from around 20% toward 30 to 40%.

05

Bull factors

Margin Premium and High-Value Mix

The company cited a 2025 operating margin of 9.3%, above the 5.3% average for 14 woven-fabric OEM peers and 3.2% for five knit OEM peers. It attributes the gap to a portfolio centered on high-tech outerwear that requires functional fabrics and complex sewing.

The nine-month 2026 operating margin of roughly 10.0% suggests the margin level has held. The peer comparison, however, rests on figures the company itself presented.

Capacity Expansion Funded by IPO

Of the KRW 32.8 billion in net offering proceeds, KRW 23.5 billion is allocated to expanding production lines in Bangladesh. The company plans to start adding one line per month from early 2027 and complete the work by mid-2028.

Efficiency work at other plants could add roughly 50% more capacity, according to the company. Whether the added capacity translates into new orders will determine the growth outcome.

Planned Step-Up in Shareholder Returns

The company said it would raise its dividend payout ratio from around 20% to 30 to 40%. Presenting a higher payout as formal policy around the listing is a distinguishing feature. The capacity for higher payouts rests on the 2025 net income of KRW 35.3 billion. The actual size and timing of dividends will be disclosed in future filings.

06

Bear factors

Sharp Drop in Key Customer Orders

Jack Wolfskin was sold to China's Antan Sports in June 2025, after which its order flow fell sharply. Orders dropped 99%, from USD 8.4 million in the first quarter of 2025 to about USD 94,000 in the first quarter of 2026.

A company representative described the gap as a temporary pause during supplier and volume adjustments. The company says orders recovered from the second quarter of 2026, but the scale of that recovery remains to be seen in reported results.

Earnings Gap After the Kido Sports Split

Kido Sports had contributed about 40% of Kido Industrial's net income. After the November 2025 spin-off and merger, Kido Sports became controlled by the owner's side, and equity-method income and dividends stopped flowing to the listed entity.

Press reports put trailing adjusted income at about KRW 21.2 billion after deducting KRW 8.5 billion of equity-method gains from a simple figure of KRW 29.7 billion. The earnings base now rests more heavily on the core apparel business.

Rising Borrowings and Interest Burden

Total borrowings were KRW 90.6 billion at the end of 2025 and KRW 89.2 billion at the end of the first quarter of 2026, a level that remains high. The borrowing dependence ratio reached 33.4% and the debt-to-equity ratio 90.7%, both up sharply from the end of 2023.

The CEO has provided personal joint guarantees for company borrowings totaling KRW 122.5 billion. In the first quarter of 2026, FX translation losses and interest costs together produced a net loss of KRW 3.1 billion.

07

Risk factors

Macro and FX

The company's export-oriented OEM model means currency swings flow directly into non-operating results. It holds foreign-currency borrowings, and a rise in the won-dollar rate previously produced translation losses.

Slower global demand, leading to order cuts, and rising labor costs in production countries could also squeeze margins. Raw material and logistics costs are additional variables.

Customer and Regional Concentration

Europe accounted for 47% of 2025 revenue, the largest share, and two external customers that each exceed 10% of revenue together represented 25.5%. Dependence on specific regions and clients is substantial, so order swings pass straight through to results.

As the Jack Wolfskin case shows, a change in a customer's ownership can reshape its order flow. The pace of customer diversification determines how this risk evolves.

Float and Governance

The largest shareholder held about 94.81% including treasury shares before the offering, and press reports cited post-listing stakes of 57.86% and 66.92%, depending on the source. Institutional lock-up commitments were only about 0.003%, which limits the free float immediately after listing.

The largest shareholder committed to a voluntary 24-month lock-up. Supply-demand dynamics and share price volatility could be heightened under this structure.

08

Overall view

As an OEM supplier to global brands, Kido Industrial has built a model in which revenue and operating profit grew through 2025. Its nine-month 2026 operating margin of roughly 10.0% suggests profitability at the operating level has held.

However, operating profit fell 50.4%, and the Jack Wolfskin order decline and the Kido Sports split have changed the composition of earnings. Borrowings, interest costs and the CEO's personal guarantees remain defining features of the balance sheet.

Whether the IPO-funded capacity expansion and an order recovery materialize will be the central test in upcoming results. The share price is currently trading well below the offering price, which makes float and supply-demand dynamics another item to track. This is a qualitative summary for informational purposes only.
