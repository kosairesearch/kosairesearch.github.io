Report overview

Solid Fundamentals, Brutal Debut

02

Business structure

Kido Industrial has been a pioneering high-tech outer-wear OEM manufacturer since its founding in 1980, when it launched Korea's first motorcycle apparel OEM business.

Over 46 years, the company has built deep technical capabilities, including ergonomic 3D pattern design, seam sealing, ultrasonic bonding, waterproof and windproof processing, and welding—all classified as high-difficulty special processes.

The company also holds European CE certification and full Gore-Tex Pro grade certification across its entire product line, serving as meaningful barriers to competition.

The product portfolio is split between outdoor apparel (78.9% of FY2025 standalone revenue) and motorcycle apparel (20.6%), with the functional fabric processing technology differentiating the company from commodity OEM peers.

Key clients include Harley-Davidson (40+ year relationship), Barbour (20+ years), Jack Wolfskin (15+ years), Schöffel, and F&F, whose premium brand positioning supports relatively stable order relationships.

The production network spans six overseas subsidiaries across four countries—Vietnam, Myanmar, Indonesia, and Bangladesh—providing geographic flexibility to manage geopolitical or tariff disruptions.

Comparable peers cited in the IPO prospectus include Hojeon, Young One, and Nobrand; Kido's specialisation in high-difficulty functional segments has historically supported a gross margin of approximately 20% and an operating margin of approximately 9%.

In November 2025, the company completed a spin-off of its investment division—which held a 48.7% stake in its B2C helmet subsidiary Kido Sports—merging it into Kido Sports, thereby refocusing the listed entity purely on OEM manufacturing.

03

Recent trends

According to DART disclosures, Kido Industrial's consolidated revenue grew from KRW 225.6B in 2023 to KRW 272.1B in 2024 and KRW 346.6B in 2025, representing a 3-year CAGR of approximately 24%.

Operating profit expanded from KRW 21.3B to KRW 25.8B and then KRW 32.2B—a cumulative increase of 51.2%—while net profit rose from KRW 19.7B to KRW 24.9B and KRW 35.3B, roughly 1.56x over the three-year period.

However, Q1 2026 consolidated revenue fell approximately 5% year-on-year to KRW 64.9B, operating profit shrank to KRW 384M, and net income swung to a KRW 3.1B loss.

The net loss was driven primarily by foreign exchange losses on dollar-denominated borrowings amid a stronger U.S. dollar and the elimination of equity-method income following the Kido Sports spin-off.

On the operating side, the decisive blow came from Jack Wolfskin orders collapsing 99% year-on-year from USD 8.4M to USD 94,000 in Q1 2026; Jack Wolfskin had accounted for approximately 13% of standalone revenue and 28% of export outdoor revenue in 2025 before its acquisition by China's Anta Sports in May 2025 triggered a supply-chain overhaul.

On the balance sheet, front-loaded capital expenditure for the Bangladesh facility pushed total borrowings from KRW 37.0B (2023) to KRW 90.6B (2025), lifting the debt ratio from 39.7% to 82.5% and annual interest expense from KRW 3.3B to KRW 5.5B (+66.7%).

In terms of share price, the stock opened at KRW 28,000 on August 21 (down 1.41% from the KRW 28,400 IPO price), briefly touched KRW 29,500 intraday, then plunged to the KRW 19,000 range as sellers dominated; the August 22 reference price of KRW 18,580 represents a 34.58% decline from the IPO price.

First-day turnover of KRW 213.4B far exceeded market capitalisation, reflecting a structural overhang: 99.84% of institutional allocations carried no lock-up commitment and 32.41% of all shares were immediately tradeable.

04

Outlook

Kido Industrial plans to allocate KRW 23.5B (71% of IPO proceeds) to the construction of a third production building and equipment investment at its Bangladesh subsidiary KIDO DHAKA, with 28 new sewing lines to be added sequentially from early 2027.

The company targets expanding annual production capacity from approximately 4 million units to more than 6 million units—a 50%-plus increase—supporting its stated 2030 revenue goal of approximately KRW 500B.

Parallel investments in smart factory systems and AI-based production intelligence are intended to improve productivity and quality competitiveness.

On shareholder returns, the company has committed to raising the dividend payout ratio from 22.4% in FY2025 to 30% in FY2026 and ultimately to 40%, with a minimum dividend target of KRW 2,000 per share from the FY2027 payout.

The key variable is the pace of Jack Wolfskin order recovery; the company indicates that Anta Sports' aggressive expansion of the Jack Wolfskin brand within China should sustain the supply relationship, while an analyst at Dain Asset Management noted that orders appeared to be recovering from the second quarter onward, suggesting a transitional supply adjustment rather than a structural customer loss.

The remaining KRW 8.0B of IPO proceeds is earmarked for early debt repayment, expected to reduce annual interest expense by approximately KRW 400M. Global consumption trends, exchange rate volatility, and country-specific risks at production locations remain key variables for earnings.

05

Bull factors

46-Year Technical Moat and Long-Term Client Lock-In

The company has internalized high-difficulty processes—seam sealing, ultrasonic bonding, CE certification, and full Gore-Tex Pro grade certification—establishing meaningful barriers to entry versus commodity apparel OEMs.

Harley-Davidson partnerships span over 40 years and Barbour over 20 years, suggesting that switching to alternative suppliers would involve significant time and cost for global brand clients.

The approximately 20% gross margin and 9% operating margin have been relatively stable in the apparel OEM sector, reflecting pricing power from functional specialisation. As reported by iNews24, management positions the company as competing on 'product difficulty, not price,' underscoring its technology-first approach.

Three Consecutive Years of 20%+ Revenue Growth With Stable Margins

On a DART-verified basis, consolidated revenue grew at approximately 24% CAGR from 2023 to 2025, reaching KRW 346.6B, while operating profit rose 51.2% from KRW 21.3B to KRW 32.2B over the same period.

Gross and operating margins remained broadly stable at approximately 20% and 9%, respectively—demonstrating resilience in the face of cost pressures typical of OEM manufacturing.

At the IPO price of KRW 28,400, the implied 2026 estimated PER was approximately 5.7x and the 2027 estimate was approximately 5.0x, both at a discount to the comparable group average of 10.01x—a factor cited during the IPO process as a potential valuation support, according to NewStop.

Bangladesh Capacity Expansion and Enhanced Shareholder Returns

The company has committed 71% of IPO proceeds to adding 28 sewing lines at its Bangladesh subsidiary, targeting a 50%-plus increase in annual production capacity from approximately 4 million to more than 6 million units, with phased completion planned from early 2027 through mid-2028.

If expanded capacity translates to actual order growth, there is a basis for meaningful revenue and operating profit improvement, as detailed in disclosures filed with DART.

The dividend payout ratio is set to rise from 22.4% in FY2025 to 30% in FY2026 and eventually 40%, with a minimum dividend target of KRW 2,000 per share from the FY2027 settlement—indicating a commitment to progressive shareholder returns.

The controlling shareholder, CEO Park Jang-hee, voluntarily added 18 months on top of the mandatory 6-month lock-up, committing to a total 24-month holding period.

06

Bear factors

Jack Wolfskin Orders All But Eliminated

Jack Wolfskin—which accounted for approximately 13% of FY2025 standalone revenue and 28% of export outdoor revenue—saw its orders fall 99% year-on-year from USD 8.4M to USD 94,000 in Q1 2026 following its acquisition by Anta Sports, compressing operating profit to just KRW 384M.

While the company characterises this as a temporary supply-adjustment hiatus, and an analyst at Dain Asset Management suggested it was consistent with a transitional order gap rather than structural customer loss, the episode illustrates the real-world risk of vendor changes triggered by a brand ownership change.

If Jack Wolfskin orders do not recover structurally, the company faces the task of filling a substantial revenue gap through new customer acquisition or volume increases from existing clients. Confirmation of order recovery will require monitoring of upcoming quarterly results, per reporting by NewStop.

Surging Debt Load and Rising Financial Costs

Total borrowings more than doubled from KRW 37.0B in 2023 to KRW 90.6B in 2025, lifting the debt-to-total-assets ratio from 17.8% to 33.4%. Annual interest expense rose 66.7% from KRW 3.3B to KRW 5.5B over the same period, while the interest coverage ratio declined from 6.5x to 5.9x.

The current ratio fell from 209.2% at end-2023 to 129% in Q1 2026 and the debt ratio rose from 39.7% to 90.7%, indicating narrowing financial headroom.

Persistent U.S. dollar strength could generate additional foreign exchange translation losses on dollar-denominated borrowings, adding further downside risk to reported net income.

Heavy Float and Structural Supply-Demand Weakness at Listing

Of 637 institutional investors in the book-building, 636 (99.84%) received allocations without any lock-up commitment, and 32.41% of all listed shares—1.899 million of 5.86 million—were immediately tradeable on listing day, as reported by multiple financial news outlets.

The retail subscription ratio of 5.5:1 was low relative to recent KOSDAQ IPOs, and first-day turnover of KRW 213.4B far exceeded the company's market capitalisation, indicating extreme share velocity. This structural supply-demand imbalance materialised in a first-day decline of more than 34% from the IPO price.

While the controlling shareholder voluntarily committed to a 24-month lock-up, this restriction does not apply to shares held by public IPO subscribers or other holders above the 1% threshold.

07

Risk factors

Macro and Consumer Spending Risk

Premium outdoor and motorcycle apparel are high-involvement, economically sensitive consumer goods; a sustained global economic slowdown could prompt global brands to reduce orders or exert greater price pressure on OEM partners.

Because OEM manufacturers absorb demand changes with a lag, revenue and operating profit volatility can be amplified in downturns.

As reported by Bloter, the company itself lists 'economic slowdown, order reductions, exchange rate fluctuations, and rising overseas labour costs' as business risks in its IPO documentation.

As demonstrated by the Jack Wolfskin episode, a major buyer's strategic shift or ownership change can create substantial order gaps in a short period.

FX and Interest Rate Risk

Although Kido generates export revenue primarily in U.S. dollars, dollar-denominated borrowings accumulated for the Bangladesh investment totalling approximately KRW 89.2B mean that sustained dollar strength can produce large foreign exchange translation losses.

Q1 2026 net losses were partly attributed to this exact mechanism, confirming the risk is not merely theoretical. Changes in interest rates could further inflate annual interest expense—already at KRW 5.5B—and erode the interest coverage ratio.

While export revenue in dollars provides partial natural hedging, timing mismatches between revenue recognition and debt settlement leave residual net exposure.

Geopolitical and Production Country Risk

Major production facilities are located in Myanmar and Bangladesh, countries that carry meaningful political and social instability risks—including military coups, labour unrest, and natural disasters—which could disrupt supply chains without warning.

While the four-country diversified production structure provides some buffer, concentrating incremental capacity additions in Bangladesh increases exposure to country-specific risk there.

U.S. trade policy shifts or import restrictions targeting Asian manufacturing hubs could indirectly affect client ordering patterns and cost structures, with downstream consequences for OEM order volumes.

Myanmar has been in a state of sustained political instability since the 2021 coup, requiring ongoing monitoring of operational conditions at the company's production base there.

08

Overall view

Kido Industrial possesses genuine competitive assets in its 46-year-accumulated functional apparel manufacturing expertise and long-term partnerships with global premium brands such as Harley-Davidson and Barbour, having delivered a consolidated revenue CAGR of approximately 24% from 2023 to 2025 with a stable operating margin of around 9%.

The first-day decline of more than 34% from the IPO price reflects a convergence of earnings uncertainty—stemming from the near-total collapse of Jack Wolfskin orders—and structural supply-demand weakness driven by a large immediately tradeable float and near-zero institutional lock-up rates.

The bull case rests on the durability of technology-backed long-term client relationships, the stated capacity expansion plan in Bangladesh, and a progressive dividend policy.

The bear case centres on the Jack Wolfskin order vacuum and uncertain recovery timeline, rapid debt accumulation from front-loaded capex, and the overhang from immediately tradeable shares.

The pace of Jack Wolfskin order recovery, the timeline for Bangladesh expansion benefits to materialise in reported earnings, and the management of foreign exchange exposure will be the critical variables determining the earnings trajectory.

The company's 2030 revenue target of approximately KRW 500B represents a multi-condition scenario requiring simultaneous execution across order recovery, new client acquisition, and capacity ramp-up.
