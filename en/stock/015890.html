02

Business structure

Taekyung Industrial was founded in 1982 and listed on the KOSPI in 1996. The company runs seven business divisions, including lime manufacturing, carbon dioxide gas and light bulb production, and produces up to 820,000 tons of quicklime a year at its Danyang 1 and 2 plants.

Lime products accounted for 99.62% domestic sales in 2024, so almost all of that segment is sold at home. Ferroalloys and zinc oxide, a metal-materials group, were split 50.75% domestic and 49.25% export in the same year, so overseas sales are close to half.

In 2025 the company acquired Lion Chemtech and KPT, extending into artificial marble, synthetic wax and cosmetic raw materials. Consolidated subsidiaries include Taekyung BK, which handles quicklime and highway rest stops, and Taekyung Chemical, which makes liquid carbon dioxide and dry ice.

The highway rest-stop and light bulb businesses are consumer-facing and differ in nature from the lime business. Lime is described as substituting for imported products, so competition centers on domestic lime and calcium carbonate producers and on imports. Annual production capacity disclosed for 2024 was about 600,000 tons.

04

Earnings analysis

On a DART-filed basis, revenue fell 10.5% from KRW 733.4 billion in 2022 to KRW 656 billion in 2023, then rose 3.0% to KRW 675.7 billion in 2024 and 15.0% to KRW 777.1 billion in 2025, a second straight year of growth.

Operating profit went from KRW 54.9 billion in 2022 to KRW 44.1 billion in 2023, the smallest year in the period, down 19.6%, and then to KRW 55.7 billion in 2024, the largest year, up 26.2%. Operating profit in 2025 was KRW 55.4 billion, down 0.5%, so it did not keep pace with the revenue gain.

The operating margin moved from 7.5% in 2022 to 6.7%, 8.2% and 7.1% in 2025, so the margin in 2025 was below 2024.

Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders was largest in 2022 at KRW 23 billion, smallest in 2023 at KRW 16.3 billion, rose 25.4% to KRW 20.4 billion in 2024, and then fell 17.8% to KRW 16.8 billion in 2025.

Total net profit including non-controlling interests moved from KRW 40.1 billion in 2024 to KRW 38.5 billion in 2025. At the end of 2025, total equity was KRW 590.2 billion, of which controlling shareholders held KRW 307.1 billion, and total liabilities were KRW 399.3 billion.

The debt ratio rose from 43.3% in 2024 to 67.6% in 2025, a shift that coincides with the 2025 acquisitions. Operating cash flow rose from KRW 42.7 billion in 2024 to KRW 73.4 billion in 2025.

Quarterly, operating profit was KRW 13 billion in the fourth quarter of 2025, down 22.3% from the prior quarter, then KRW 17.2 billion in the first quarter of 2026, up 32.3%, and KRW 22.1 billion in the second quarter, up 28.3%.

In the third quarter, revenue of KRW 234.4 billion fell 2.6% from the prior quarter while operating profit dropped 31.7% to KRW 15.1 billion, and profit attributable to controlling shareholders fell 62.5% to KRW 4.26 billion.

Cumulative operating profit for the first three quarters of 2026 was up year on year according to the third-quarter report.

05

Industry analysis

Lime and calcium carbonate are used in steelmaking, environmental equipment, civil engineering and agriculture. A first-half 2025 disclosure summary described rising domestic import substitution for highly active calcium hydroxide and high-reactivity hygienic lime.

The same summary said eco-friendly pollution prevention and waste solidification are expanding as new businesses. A first-quarter 2025 business summary said the company is pursuing a lime method for stabilizing soft ground and expected demand for quicklime used in livestock disease control to grow.

This demand depends on construction activity, environmental regulation and livestock hygiene policy. Ferroalloys and zinc oxide are close to half exported, so they are relatively exposed to overseas demand and price swings.

Annual production capacity disclosed for 2024 combined lime plants with ferromanganese and silicomanganese output at the Yemi plant, totaling about 600,000 tons.

Even within chemicals, the company's raw materials and product mix differ from large listed firms centered on petrochemicals or battery materials, which limits direct comparison. Public industry data sufficient to place lime in its price cycle is limited.

Taekyung Industrial therefore combines a domestic substitution and environmental business axis with an export-exposed metal materials axis.

06

Outlook

In its corporate value-up plan voluntarily disclosed on March 24, 2026, the company cited efforts to secure stable earnings, expanded investment in high-value new products, stronger quality competitiveness, and a continued shareholder-friendly dividend policy.

Execution items included starting a new production line at the Onsan plant, restructuring the business divisions to improve profitability, securing dividend funds through the sale of non-operating assets, and strengthening core businesses.

The company said it qualifies as a high-dividend company under the Restriction of Special Taxation Act, and the payout ratio for fiscal 2025 was presented at 43.57%. At the regular shareholder meeting on March 31, 2026, an amendment to the articles and director appointments were approved.

After the appointments, the board has four directors, one of whom is an outside director, a 25% outside-director ratio. Cumulative operating profit for the first three quarters of 2026 rose, although third-quarter operating profit alone declined.

When the new Onsan line will show up in results and at what scale will become clear in later periodic reports. Whether asset sales translate into dividend funding will also appear in future disclosures.

No disclosed figures yet quantify the revenue share of high-value new products, so that trend can only be followed qualitatively. The operating margin and debt ratio in the next periodic report are the main indicators for judging progress on these execution items.

Bull factors

Quarterly operating profit recovery

Operating profit rose from KRW 13 billion in the fourth quarter of 2025 to KRW 17.2 billion in the first quarter of 2026 and KRW 22.1 billion in the second quarter. Second-quarter revenue of KRW 240.7 billion came with the highest operating profit of the five quarters.

Cumulative operating profit for the first three quarters of 2026 was up year on year according to the third-quarter report. Whether this run continues is a matter for the next periodic report.

Import substitution and new environmental businesses

A first-half 2025 disclosure summary recorded rising domestic import substitution for highly active calcium hydroxide and hygienic lime. Environmental new businesses such as pollution prevention and waste solidification are also expanding.

The lime method for soft-ground stabilization and quicklime demand for livestock disease control were also cited. Domestic lime sales at 99.62% in 2024 point to a solid home-market base.

Shareholder return policy and dividend funding plan

The March 2026 value-up plan included securing dividend funds through the sale of non-operating assets. The company said it qualifies as a high-dividend company, and the 2025 payout ratio was presented at 43.57%.

With the price-to-book ratio well below 1x, shareholder returns relative to the asset base are a disclosed item to follow.

Bear factors

Earnings decline and margin compression in 2025

Revenue rose 15.0% in 2025, but operating profit fell 0.5% to KRW 55.4 billion. The operating margin declined from 8.2% in 2024 to 7.1% in 2025. Profit attributable to controlling shareholders dropped 17.8% to KRW 16.8 billion, and total net profit fell from KRW 40.1 billion to KRW 38.5 billion. Revenue growth that did not turn into profit growth is the main bearish point.

Higher debt ratio and financial burden

The debt ratio rose from 43.3% in 2024 to 67.6% in 2025. Total liabilities at the end of 2025 were KRW 399.3 billion against total equity of KRW 590.2 billion. The increase coincides with the 2025 acquisitions of Lion Chemtech and KPT, so the financial burden grew after the deals. Leverage could weigh on the company while it funds both dividends and capacity investment.

High quarterly earnings volatility

Third-quarter 2026 operating profit fell 31.7% from the prior quarter to KRW 15.1 billion and declined 9.9% year on year. Profit attributable to controlling shareholders fell 62.5% quarter on quarter to KRW 4.26 billion. Operating profit also fell 22.3% in the fourth quarter of 2025. The repeated swings make one quarter's recovery hard to read as a trend.

Risk factors

Demand and cycle risk

Lime demand spans construction, steelmaking, environmental equipment and livestock hygiene, but each market moves with its own economic cycle and policy. Ferroalloys and zinc oxide had 49.25% export sales in 2024, which exposes them to overseas demand and prices. A drop in volume or price could push the operating margin lower again.

Financial leverage and acquisition integration

Total liabilities at the end of 2025 were KRW 399.3 billion and the debt ratio was 67.6%. After the acquisitions of Lion Chemtech and KPT, the business mix added artificial marble, synthetic wax and cosmetic raw materials, which makes the structure more complex.

With a sizable non-controlling share, the portion of subsidiary profit that reaches controlling shareholders also needs to be tracked.

Environmental regulation and governance

The company's sustainability report lists the Act on the Integrated Management of Environmental Pollution Facilities, the Waste Management Act and fire-prevention law as its main applicable regulations. Stricter rules could raise the cost of upgrading lime plants and waste-handling equipment.

After the March 2026 shareholder meeting, the outside-director ratio is 25%, so board independence remains a point to track.

Overall view

Taekyung Industrial is a chemical company whose core lime and calcium carbonate business combines domestic import substitution, new environmental businesses and export-exposed metal materials.

Revenue in 2025 rose to KRW 777.1 billion for a second straight year, but operating profit slipped to KRW 55.4 billion and profit attributable to controlling shareholders fell to KRW 16.8 billion.

Operating profit recovered to KRW 17.2 billion in the first quarter of 2026 and KRW 22.1 billion in the second, then fell to KRW 15.1 billion in the third. The debt ratio rose from 43.3% in 2024 to 67.6% in 2025, so leverage after the acquisitions works as both a support and a risk.

The price-to-book ratio sits well below 1x, and the high-dividend designation and disclosed dividend funding plan form the shareholder-return side of the picture. Earnings volatility, margin compression and higher leverage are equally weighty bearish points.

The next checkpoints are the operating margin and debt ratio in the 2026 annual report and whether the new Onsan line starts operating.
