Report overview

Bulk Upcycle and Long-Term Tanker Contracts Lift Earnings

Pan Ocean's operating profit of KRW 197.4 billion in the third quarter of 2026 was the highest quarterly figure since the third quarter of 2022, and its earnings base is widening from dry bulk into tanker and LNG long-term contracts.

  1. 1

    Third-quarter 2026 revenue was KRW 2.1 trillion and operating profit was KRW 197.4 billion, up 65.2% and 57.7% year on year.

  2. 2

  3. 3

    Annual operating profit rose for two consecutive years after the 2023 low, reaching KRW 471.2 billion in 2024 and KRW 491.9 billion in 2025, still below KRW 789.6 billion in 2022.

02

Business structure

Pan Ocean runs a dry bulk fleet at its core, with tanker, container, LNG carrier and grain operations alongside. Harim Holdings holds 54.72 percent of the company.

The dry bulk segment posted third-quarter 2026 operating profit of KRW 100.6 billion, up 82.6 percent year on year, with higher cargo volumes cited as the main driver.

The tanker segment booked operating profit of KRW 34.8 billion, up 71.4 percent, on VLCC deliveries under a long-term contract with SK Energy and firmer MR tanker markets. A Lead Economy report noted that the tanker segment's operating margin topped 30 percent, well above the 8 to 10 percent range of the bulk segment.

Container operating profit reached KRW 17.5 billion, up 134.8 percent, supported by port congestion at Shanghai and Ningbo. That segment fell to KRW 9.0 billion in the first quarter of 2026, a sign of sharp quarter-to-quarter swings tied to freight and capacity.

The LNG segment earned KRW 43.6 billion on long-term charters. The grain business recorded a quarterly record of KRW 678.3 billion in third-quarter 2026 revenue on 1.72 million tons of volume. Large domestic and overseas shippers under long-term contracts form a major part of the customer base.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual revenue fell from KRW 6.42 trillion in 2022 to KRW 4.36 trillion in 2023, down 32.1 percent, then rose to KRW 5.16 trillion in 2024 (up 18.3 percent) and KRW 5.43 trillion in 2025 (up 5.3 percent), growing for two consecutive years from 2024 through 2025.

Operating profit went from KRW 789.6 billion in 2022 to KRW 385.9 billion in 2023 (down 51.1 percent), then to KRW 471.2 billion in 2024 (up 22.1 percent) and KRW 491.9 billion in 2025 (up 4.4 percent). Operating margin was 12.3 percent in 2022, 8.8 percent in 2023, and 9.1 percent in both 2024 and 2025.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 677.1 billion in 2022, KRW 245 billion in 2023 (down 63.8 percent), KRW 268.1 billion in 2024 (up 9.4 percent) and KRW 301.4 billion in 2025 (up 12.4 percent). Net income including non-controlling interests is disclosed at the same yearly amounts.

The debt-to-equity ratio rose from 68.0 percent in 2022 and 66.6 percent in 2023 to 81.7 percent in 2024 and 89.6 percent in 2025. At the end of 2025, total equity was KRW 5.72 trillion, all attributable to controlling shareholders, against total liabilities of KRW 5.13 trillion.

Operating cash flow was KRW 1.41 trillion in 2022, KRW 750.8 billion in 2023, KRW 676.2 billion in 2024 and KRW 702.7 billion in 2025.

On a quarterly basis, revenue rose for four consecutive quarters from the fourth quarter of 2025 to the third quarter of 2026, from KRW 1.27 trillion in the third quarter of 2025 to KRW 2.1 trillion.

Operating profit rose 57.7 percent year on year to KRW 197.4 billion in the third quarter of 2026 from KRW 125.2 billion a year earlier.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders bottomed at KRW 48.7 billion in the fourth quarter of 2025 and then rose for three consecutive quarters to KRW 184.5 billion in the third quarter of 2026.

A Lead Economy analysis noted that the first-quarter 2026 fuel cost spike tied to the Iran conflict was offset by gains in the tanker and LNG units.

05

Industry analysis

The dry bulk market has turned firmer, with the Baltic Dry Index topping 3,000 for the first time in three years.

In the third quarter of 2026, typhoons, prolonged Suez and Panama canal transit restrictions limited effective shipping capacity, while rising steel exports and coal demand as a substitute for LNG lifted sea cargo volumes. The tanker market also saw freight rates climb across VLCC, product and bulk vessels.

KB Securities, however, judged that ocean freight had passed its peak and flagged medium-term oversupply risk from a sharp rise in newbuilding orders. The same broker cited a rise in global dry bulk and tanker orders in the first nine months of 2026 to 4.3 times and 2.4 times the year-earlier levels.

Prolonged Middle East tensions are another variable, as capacity reductions linked to Hormuz transit fees could affect shipping markets over the long term, one broker said. The cycle appears to be in a phase where newbuild ordering rises while freight remains high.

A Lead Economy report expected the energy transport share of profit to exceed 50 percent in 2027. Pan Ocean's bulk-centric portfolio, with tanker and LNG added, differs from peers that rely more on container lines.

06

Outlook

In its third-quarter 2026 earnings release, the company emphasized expanding its business portfolio and strengthening fleet competitiveness.

NH Investment and Securities, in a May 2026 report, cited plans to add 5 MR tankers, 3 newbuild VLCCs and 10 secondhand VLCCs, and said phased deliveries through 2027 would improve earnings visibility.

VLCC capacity stood at roughly 7 vessels at the end of the third quarter of 2026 and would exceed 14 vessels including chartered ships once the remaining newbuilds arrive. LS Securities expects VLCC earnings to be fully reflected in 2027.

KB Securities estimated 2026 operating profit at KRW 755.9 billion but expects earnings to slow from 2027. In grain, South American corn and Chinese-bound soybean trade and sales to Asia lifted the business. LS Securities also cited the expected announcement of a new dividend policy as a shareholder return catalyst.

The next checkpoints are the fourth-quarter 2026 preliminary results and the 2026 annual report, which will show how 2027 freight and vessel supply assumptions are reflected.

08

Bull factors

Dry bulk freight recovery

The Baltic Dry Index topping 3,000 for the first time in three years points to a freight recovery in bulk. The company said the firmer bulk market lifted results across its tanker and container units as well. Canal restrictions and reduced effective capacity in the third quarter supported rates.

Tanker and LNG earnings on long-term contracts

The LNG unit earned KRW 43.6 billion in the third quarter on long-term charters. Analysts noted that many acquired vessels are tied to long-term contracts, which dampens exposure to spot freight swings. VLCC deliveries have broadened the earnings base.

Fleet additions expected to feed 2027

NH Investment and Securities said phased vessel deliveries through 2027, combined with firm markets, would raise earnings visibility. LS Securities expects full-year VLCC earnings to be reflected in 2027. Whether freight levels hold alongside fleet additions is the premise behind these views.

09

Bear factors

Freight peak and oversupply concerns

KB Securities judged that ocean freight has passed its peak. It cited a 4.3-fold rise in global dry bulk orders in the first nine months of 2026 as grounds for medium-term oversupply. It also trimmed its 2027 operating profit estimate.

Earnings volatility from the business mix

Annual revenue fell 32.1 percent and operating profit fell 51.1 percent in 2023. Container operating profit fell 42.9 percent in the first quarter of 2026. The sharp swings in segment earnings tied to freight and capacity are a structural weakness.

Financial leverage burden

The debt-to-equity ratio rose from 68.0 percent in 2022 to 89.6 percent in 2025. Total liabilities at the end of 2025 were KRW 5.13 trillion. Investment of roughly KRW 1 trillion in vessels was flagged as a burden, and a freight downturn could magnify the financial strain.

10

Risk factors

Freight cycle

Dry bulk and tanker freight move with newbuild deliveries and effective capacity. Part of the third-quarter 2026 result came from temporary supply constraints such as canal restrictions and typhoons, and easing those conditions could reduce earnings momentum. KB Securities raised the risk of oversupply in 2027 as a key concern.

Fuel costs and geopolitics

In the first quarter of 2026, a fuel cost spike in March tied to the Middle East conflict raised cost burdens centered on bulk. Tanker and LNG gains offset this, but if fuel costs and route risks rise again, the same offset may not hold.

Conditions around the Hormuz Strait and the Suez and Panama canals could move freight rates and costs at the same time.

Balance sheet and shareholder returns

With the debt-to-equity ratio at 89.6 percent in 2025, financing costs for vessel additions draw attention alongside earnings. Expanded shareholder returns depend on a new dividend policy whose timing has not been set. Balance sheet capacity and dividend plans together will determine whether those expectations materialize.

12

Overall view

Pan Ocean's annual revenue, operating profit and net income attributable to controlling shareholders each rose for two consecutive years after the 2023 low. Quarterly revenue rose for four consecutive quarters from the fourth quarter of 2025 through the third quarter of 2026.

The two drivers of higher earnings are firm dry bulk freight and tanker and LNG long-term contract profits. KB Securities, however, sees freight past its peak and expects earnings to slow in 2027.

On the balance sheet, the rise in the debt-to-equity ratio from 68.0 percent in 2022 to 89.6 percent in 2025 is the item to watch. Broker target prices diverge widely, which shows how differently the freight cycle is being read.

The next checkpoints are the fourth-quarter 2026 preliminary results, the 2026 annual report and progress on VLCC deliveries.
