Mgame: Earnings Recovery Hinges on New Launches

Mgame's revenue peaked in the first quarter of 2026 and has since fallen for two consecutive quarters. Operating profit rose in the third quarter but stayed below the prior-year level. New server launches and the Gwihon Kiugi release in November will be the first test of whether fourth-quarter earnings can recover.

02

Business structure

Mgame develops and operates PC online MMORPGs and mobile games, with a strategy centered on relaunching long-running IPs.

The company said its flagship Yeolhyeok Gangho Online, in Greater China, and Night Online, in North America and Europe, continued to generate steady overseas revenue that supported first-half 2026 results. Night Online was co-developed with a partner studio.

On mobile, the Gwihon M title added new revenue to the 2024 annual figures, and the Gwihon-IP idle RPG Gwihon Kiugi is scheduled for release on Google Play, the Apple App Store and ONE store on November 24, 2026.

At its March 2026 vision event, the company first unveiled Project Z, a multiplatform RPG blending ancient civilization and martial-arts themes, and said a collectible turn-based RPG based on a Naver Webtoon title was targeted for release within 2026.

Its subsidiary Wizgate handles blockchain and AI-based games and Web3 platforms, and the company plans to relaunch legacy IPs such as Pungnim Hwasan in blockchain environments.

The company did not provide segment revenue shares in the disclosed figures used here, but overseas PC MMORPG revenue is described as the core of the earnings base. Non-game areas include AI-based smart tables and 3D-camera biometric data solutions still in development.

In short, the revenue base rests on long-running games while growth depends on new titles and subsidiary businesses.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual revenue rose from KRW 73.7 billion in 2022 to KRW 81.6 billion in 2023 (up 10.8% year on year), KRW 83.4 billion in 2024 (up 2.2%) and KRW 91.7 billion in 2025 (up 10.0%), with three consecutive years of growth from 2023 through 2025. 2025 was the largest year of the 2022–2025 period.

Operating profit fell from KRW 30.1 billion in 2022 to KRW 21.3 billion in 2023 (down 29.3%) and KRW 12.8 billion in 2024 (down 39.8%), before recovering to KRW 17.6 billion in 2025 (up 36.7%).

The company attributed the 2024 decline to higher new-title marketing costs and a temporary rise in second-quarter development expenses. The operating margin fell from 40.9% in 2022 to 26.1% in 2023 and 15.4% in 2024, then rebounded to 19.1% in 2025.

Profit attributable to controlling shareholders went from KRW 22.6 billion in 2022 to KRW 15.7 billion in 2024 and KRW 16 billion in 2025.

Net income including non-controlling interests was KRW 22.6 billion, KRW 21.8 billion, KRW 15.7 billion and KRW 16 billion over the same years, so the gap to controlling-shareholder profit is small.

Operating cash flow fell from KRW 37.1 billion in 2022 to KRW 7.88 billion in 2025, a steeper decline than operating profit. At the end of 2025, total equity was KRW 136.9 billion and total liabilities were KRW 20.4 billion, with the debt-to-equity ratio down from 25.0% in 2022 to 14.9%.

Quarterly revenue peaked at KRW 24.2 billion in the first quarter of 2026 after KRW 23.2 billion in the fourth quarter of 2025. The company said the first-quarter decline reflected a base effect from the North America and Europe channel expansion of Night Online in the first quarter of 2025.

Revenue then fell to KRW 21.3 billion in the second quarter and KRW 20.8 billion in the third, while third-quarter operating profit of KRW 2.7 billion was up 10.6% from the prior quarter.

Profit attributable to controlling shareholders recovered from KRW 0.721 billion in the second quarter to KRW 3.05 billion in the third, and the last four quarters total KRW 12.1 billion. Net income rose sharply from the prior quarter, which press coverage attributed partly to a higher deferred tax asset. Profit has recovered since 2025 but remains below the 2022 level.

05

Industry analysis

Mgame is a small-to-mid-sized game company, larger in scope than some mid-tier peers such as Webzen, Wemade and Com2us but well below large players such as Krafton and Netmarble.

With annual revenue of KRW 91.7 billion and quarterly revenue in the KRW 20 billion range, the performance of a single new title can move quarterly results considerably. PC MMORPGs rely on long-running IP that keeps drawing overseas players, and new servers and returning players are a main way to lift revenue.

The company said new-server pre-registrations for Night Online rose 40% compared with the November 2022 opening, when first-day revenue of USD 910,000 set a service-high daily record. That precedent suggests server expansion can revive revenue for mature titles.

In mobile idle RPGs, IP recognition often drives early user acquisition because competition is heavy.

The company's plan to relaunch legacy IP in blockchain environments runs through its Web3 subsidiary Wizgate, and that path has no disclosed revenue yet, so the effect of new titles and relaunched legacy IP needs to be read separately. The company's relative position rests on overseas PC online game revenue from older titles.

06

Outlook

The company said the fourth quarter of 2026 concentrates the Night Online servers in North America and Europe, the Gwihon Kiugi launch and the Pungnim Hwasan reopening, and it expects results to benefit from the year-end peak season. It has also set a goal of exceeding last year's performance.

The new Night Online servers opened on November 12, 2026 in North America and Europe, together with a local publisher. Gwihon Kiugi passed one million pre-registrations as of November 6, 2026, and its original Gwihon IP launched in 2005.

However, the March 2026 vision event had listed the title for release in the first half, and the official launch date has since been set for November 24, a slip of roughly two quarters. Pungnim Hwasan's PC MMORPG is set to return within the fourth quarter.

Project Z was first unveiled in March 2026, with AI incubation adopted from planning through visualization, but no launch date has been disclosed. At its first-half 2026 results announcement, the company said it would seek a rebound through second-half new launches.

The next checkpoints are how much Gwihon Kiugi revenue contributes after launch and the full-year 2026 operating margin in the annual report due by the end of March 2027.

08

Bull factors

Concentrated fourth-quarter catalysts

Night Online's new global servers opened on November 12, 2026, with pre-registrations up 40% from the 2022 opening. The 2022 opening produced a service-high first-day revenue of USD 910,000. Gwihon Kiugi and the Pungnim Hwasan reopening add to the fourth-quarter revenue sources the company has planned.

One million pre-registrations and a fixed launch date

Gwihon Kiugi passed one million pre-registrations as of November 6, 2026. The game launches simultaneously on Google Play, the Apple App Store and ONE store. The original Gwihon IP began service in 2005, and the company frames its existing fan base as a basis for early user acquisition.

Continued shareholder returns and low leverage

The company has paid cash dividends continuously since 2023. Three buyback decisions in 2026 bring the year's repurchases to about KRW 6 billion, including the October 30, 2026 decision. The debt-to-equity ratio fell to 14.9% at the end of 2025 from 25.0% in 2022.

09

Bear factors

Two consecutive quarters of revenue decline

Second-quarter 2026 revenue of KRW 21.3 billion fell 12.2% from the first quarter, and third-quarter revenue of KRW 20.8 billion fell a further 2.3%. Third-quarter revenue also fell 3.4% year on year.

Overseas PC MMORPGs supported the first half, but the decline from the first-quarter peak remains a weakness until new-title revenue appears.

Shrinking margins and cash generation

The operating margin fell from 40.9% in 2022 to 19.1% in 2025, and operating cash flow dropped from KRW 37.1 billion in 2022 to KRW 7.88 billion in 2025. Third-quarter 2026 operating profit fell 34.7% year on year. If new titles do not convert into revenue in the fourth quarter, the earnings recovery could slow.

Launch slippage and reliance on existing IP

The March 2026 vision event listed Gwihon Kiugi for a first-half release, but the official launch moved to November 24. Both Gwihon Kiugi and Pungnim Hwasan rely on existing IP, so new-title performance may depend heavily on the recognition of those franchises.

10

Risk factors

New-title performance uncertainty

Pre-registrations of one million do not guarantee paid revenue after launch. The idle RPG genre has many entrants, so retaining users after the initial wave is a challenge. The fourth-quarter revenue growth the company targets depends substantially on Gwihon Kiugi and the server effects.

Dependence on overseas revenue

Yeolhyeok Gangho Online depends on Greater China and Night Online on North America and Europe. This structure is exposed to local service policies, exchange rates and payment environments. First-half results were supported by overseas revenue from these two titles.

Earnings volatility and tax effects

Quarterly net income moves sharply with tax factors. Third-quarter 2026 net income rose substantially from the prior quarter, which coverage attributed to a higher deferred tax asset. Operating profit and net income can diverge, so core business trends should be tracked on an operating basis.

12

Overall view

Mgame's operating profit recovered in 2025, but quarterly revenue has fallen for two consecutive quarters since the first-quarter 2026 peak, and third-quarter operating profit fell 34.7% year on year.

Over a longer horizon, the operating margin declined from 40.9% in 2022 to 19.1% in 2025, and operating cash flow shrank sharply. On the other side, the debt-to-equity ratio is low at 14.9%, and the company continues to combine cash dividends with share cancellation.

The fourth quarter brings several catalysts at once, so whether earnings recover will be the first thing to watch. Bullish factors include new titles, server effects, shareholder returns and financial capacity. Bearish factors include declining revenue, lower margins, delayed launches and reliance on existing IP.

The balance between these factors may shift once post-launch Gwihon Kiugi revenue and the full-year 2026 operating margin in the annual report are visible.
