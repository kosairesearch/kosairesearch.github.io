Report overview

Rehabilitation Under Way, Earnings Recovery Is the Key Test

Youil Energy Tech has been in court-supervised rehabilitation since May 2026, with debt restructuring and business normalization running in parallel, and its operating losses ran for four straight years from 2022 to 2025. The next checkpoint is the submission of the rehabilitation plan on November 26, 2026.

  3. 3

  4. 4

02

Business structure

Youil Energy Tech develops, manufactures and sells equipment and automation systems used in secondary battery assembly and fuel cell production. Its main products are notching, stacking and tab welding machines for electric vehicle and ESS battery lines, along with module and pack line automation equipment.

The company listed on KOSDAQ in February 2021 and is headquartered in Anseong, Gyeonggi Province. At the annual general meeting on March 31, 2026, it changed its name from Yoil Energy Tech to Youil Energy Tech and appointed Oh Yun-ho as CEO.

Its customers are battery cell and module makers, so orders and revenue depend heavily on their timing of new line construction and capital spending. The company had pursued expansion into adjacent areas such as ESS, fuel cell material equipment and spent battery recycling facilities.

However, after the rehabilitation filing in April 2026, reports said its self-help measures, including entry into new businesses, were suspended. On the competitive side, a patent dispute over stacking device technology became the key variable.

In a case brought by a rival battery assembly equipment maker, the Supreme Court dismissed the appeals on July 16, 2026, confirming the appellate damages of KRW 11 billion, which the company must reflect in its rehabilitation plan.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual revenue fell from KRW 47.5 billion in 2022 to KRW 38.5 billion in 2023, down 18.9% year on year, then rose two years in a row to KRW 59.7 billion in 2024, up 55.0%, and KRW 60 billion in 2025, up 0.5%. 2025 was the largest year of 2022 to 2025, and 2023 the smallest.

Operating losses were KRW 19.3 billion in 2022, KRW 10.1 billion in 2023, KRW 16.7 billion in 2024 and KRW 2.71 billion in 2025, a loss in each of the four years, and the operating margin improved from -40.6% in 2022 to -4.5% in 2025.

In 2024 revenue rose 55.0%, yet the operating loss widened to KRW 16.7 billion, so top-line growth did not translate into better profitability.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders went from KRW 14.5 billion in 2022 to a net loss of KRW 13.2 billion in 2023, then net losses of KRW 28.7 billion in 2024 and KRW 24.8 billion in 2025, three straight years of losses.

The 2025 net loss was far larger than the operating loss, which points to sizable non-operating items and litigation-related provisions.

For 2025, the external auditor issued a disclaimer of opinion on the consolidated statements, citing continuing operating and net losses, current liabilities exceeding current assets, and a provision tied to the patent infringement suit.

The debt ratio rose from 162.2% in 2022 to 307.9% in 2024 and eased to 250.1% in 2025, with total liabilities of KRW 125.5 billion and total equity of KRW 50.2 billion at the end of 2025. Operating cash flow was negative in each of 2022 to 2024 and turned positive at KRW 2.91 billion in 2025.

On a quarterly basis, revenue jumped to KRW 32.6 billion in the fourth quarter of 2025, up 2,617.9% from the prior quarter, and operating profit of KRW 2.64 billion marked a turn to profit.

Revenue then fell to KRW 1.64 billion in the first quarter of 2026, down 95.0%, and KRW 489 million in the second quarter, down 70.2%.

Third-quarter 2026 revenue recovered to KRW 12.2 billion, up 918.3% year on year, but the operating loss was KRW 6.42 billion, the third straight quarterly loss, and the net loss attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 10.9 billion widened year on year.

05

Industry analysis

Demand for battery equipment tracks the capital spending cycle of battery makers, the company's direct customers. Reports say slowing growth in the global secondary battery industry and reduced electric vehicle subsidies in major markets led battery makers to revise their capex plans.

As schedules for new line construction slipped, demand for automated assembly equipment fell, which is cited as a factor behind the company's weak first quarter of 2026. Several specialized makers of notching, stacking and tab welding equipment compete for orders from large cell makers.

By scale, the company sits among smaller equipment makers, and its fundraising capacity and the outcome of the patent dispute are key variables for its competitiveness. After the stacking technology case was finally lost, the auditor judged that the sustainability of core businesses such as stacking had weakened.

In response, the company had pursued adjacent markets such as ESS, fuel cell material equipment and spent battery recycling facilities. The results of that expansion should be checked in the business descriptions and revenue mix of the next periodic report.

The timing of any industry recovery depends on whether battery makers resume capacity expansion, so whether equipment demand picks up is the main industry indicator to watch.

06

Outlook

In April 2026 the company said that new businesses would bring normalization no later than the following year. Later reports said self-help measures, including entry into new businesses, were suspended, so the two accounts differ.

The rehabilitation proceedings, which began after the board approved the filing in April 2026, have been under way since the court's May 8 commencement order. The deadline for the rehabilitation plan is November 26, 2026, as amended in a corrective filing.

Key variables in the plan include debt restructuring, how the KRW 11 billion patent damages claim is reflected, and new funding arrangements. Since the KRW 12.7 billion rights offering was withdrawn, a new financing route must be set within the rehabilitation process.

A shareholder group expects that the existing management's holdings will be subject to a 100% capital reduction without compensation under the plan. The 2026 annual report is due by the statutory deadline of March 31, 2027, and it will show whether the 2025 disclaimer of opinion has been resolved.

It is also the first full-year periodic report covering 2026 revenue and operating results. Future earnings should therefore be read together with the content of the approved plan and the auditor's opinion in the annual report.

Bull factors

Quarterly revenue rebound and narrower operating losses

Fourth-quarter 2025 revenue of KRW 32.6 billion rose sharply from the prior quarter, and third-quarter 2026 revenue of KRW 12.2 billion was up 918.3% year on year. The 2025 operating loss of KRW 2.71 billion was far smaller than KRW 19.3 billion in 2022, and the operating margin improved from -40.6% to -4.5%. The appearance of revenue rebounds in some quarters is the main basis for the normalization argument.

Positive operating cash flow and a fixed damages amount

Operating cash flow was negative from 2022 to 2024 but turned positive at KRW 2.91 billion in 2025. With the Supreme Court ruling final, the patent damages were fixed at KRW 11 billion, narrowing the uncertainty to a known amount. A fixed figure gives the rehabilitation plan a concrete basis for calculating debt obligations.

Adjacent-market expansion and expected debt restructuring

The company had sought to expand into adjacent areas such as ESS, fuel cell material equipment and spent battery recycling facilities. The rehabilitation process creates a framework in which debt restructuring and new funding can be considered together.

If orders in adjacent equipment are confirmed, they could partly offset weaker demand in the core business, which is the main bull argument.

Bear factors

Continuing quarterly losses and wider net losses

Operating losses have continued for three straight quarters through the third quarter of 2026, and losses attributable to controlling shareholders have run for five straight quarters from the third quarter of 2025.

The combined loss over the last four quarters is KRW 33.1 billion, and the third-quarter 2026 net loss of KRW 10.9 billion widened from the prior quarter. Even in quarters when revenue recovered, the revenue did not turn into profit.

Debt burden and liquidity pressure

Total liabilities at the end of 2025 were KRW 125.5 billion, well above total equity of KRW 50.2 billion, and the debt ratio was 250.1%. Press reports said current liabilities at the end of June 2026 were KRW 86.6 billion, exceeding current assets of KRW 50.2 billion.

Promissory notes were dishonored in April and May 2026, and during rehabilitation new share issuance requires court approval, which restricts financing routes.

Audit disclaimer and designation as a caution stock

The external auditor issued a disclaimer of opinion on the 2025 consolidated statements, and the 2026 half-year review opinion was also non-clean. As a result, a caution designation was added on August 18, 2026. With the KRW 12.7 billion rights offering withdrawn, the plan to secure operating funds was also reset.

Risk factors

Rehabilitation outcome risk

If the rehabilitation plan is not approved by the court or debt restructuring terms are unfavorable, the continuity of the business could change. The plan submission deadline was amended to November 26, 2026, and debt restructuring and new funding are the central variables.

Reports say self-help measures, including new businesses, have been suspended despite the company's normalization plan.

Patent damages and provision risk

With the Supreme Court ruling final, the KRW 11 billion damages claim must be reflected in the rehabilitation plan. The company booked a litigation provision of KRW 13.2 billion at the end of June 2026, estimating principal and default interest.

The claimant applied for a seizure order covering KRW 10.8 billion, but enforcement is paused under the commencement order.

Governance and listing-status risk

The share sale agreement for the controlling stake was cancelled after the 2025 disclaimer of opinion, and the largest shareholder reverted to former CEO Jeong Yeon-gil.

A minority shareholder group has gathered proxies for 7.57% of shares, filed a large-holding report, and considered criminal complaints against former executives. Reports note that if related allegations are formalized, a substantive delisting review could be added as a ground.

Overall view

Revenue rose two years in a row to KRW 60 billion in 2025, yet operating losses continued for three straight quarters in 2026, and the net loss attributable to controlling shareholders totaled KRW 33.1 billion over the last four quarters.

On the financial side, the debt ratio was 250.1%, with total liabilities of KRW 125.5 billion against total equity of KRW 50.2 billion at the end of 2025, and the 2025 consolidated statements received a disclaimer of opinion.

Since the rehabilitation began in April 2026, the KRW 11 billion patent damages were confirmed, and the KRW 12.7 billion rights offering was withdrawn.

Positive factors include quarterly revenue rebounds, positive operating cash flow in 2025 and reduced uncertainty from a fixed damages figure, while negative factors include continuing quarterly losses, the debt burden, the audit disclaimer and the caution designation.

The next checkpoints are the rehabilitation plan submission on November 26, 2026, the court's decision on approval, and the auditor's opinion in the 2026 annual report due by the end of March 2027.
