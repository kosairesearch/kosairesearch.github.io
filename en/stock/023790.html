Quarterly Turnaround Amid Losses as a Bar-Steel Maker Shifts to Precision Materials

Dongil Steelux swung to an operating profit in Q2 2026 and to a net profit attributable to shareholders in Q3 2026, but annual operating losses since 2023 and a heavy debt load leave the durability of the recovery as the central question.

02

Business structure

Dongil Steelux was founded in 1967 as Dongil Steel and listed on KOSDAQ in 1994. It adopted its current name in 2024. Production centers on the Hakjang-dong plant in Sasang-gu, Busan, and in 2021 the company acquired a stake in a shipbuilding affiliate to diversify its business.

According to a technology analysis report by a third-party rating agency as reported by Steel Daily, the company used to hot-roll billets itself but now sources semi-finished products externally and produces cold-drawn bar steel.

Its core products are bar steel and precision-finished bar steel, the latter made by shot-blasting round bars and wire rod and then cold drawing them to improve dimensional accuracy and surface quality. Segment shares differ by source.

Steel Daily reported that precision-finished bar steel accounted for 60% of 2025 sales, while a Butler dataset based on Q2 2026 sales shows 55% precision-finished and 45% other bar steel.

Customers are mainly in automotive and machinery applications, served through small-lot, multi-grade production under direct contracts that emphasize quality control and delivery responsiveness. Low-priced Chinese imports are cited as the main competitive pressure in the domestic market.

In 2025 the company added robotics, MRO and AAM materials to its business purpose. The bar-steel-centered structure is flagged as a weakness in the report, and moving toward precision processing beyond commodity bar steel is presented as the key challenge.

04

Earnings analysis

On a DART-reported basis, annual sales fell from KRW 32.2 billion in 2022 to KRW 21.5 billion in 2023, a 33.4% decline year on year, and then to KRW 16.6 billion in 2024, down 22.5%. Sales recovered to KRW 24 billion in 2025, up 44.6%. 2022 was the largest year and 2024 the smallest within 2022 to 2025.

Operating profit of KRW 902 million in 2022 turned into an operating loss of KRW 3.31 billion in 2023, which marked the switch to losses. The loss narrowed to KRW 1.8 billion in 2024 and widened again to KRW 2.61 billion in 2025, making three consecutive years of operating losses from 2023 to 2025.

The operating margin moved from 2.8% in 2022 to -15.4% in 2023, then -10.8% in both 2024 and 2025. In 2025, sales grew but the operating loss widened, so the revenue recovery did not translate into profit improvement.

Net losses attributable to shareholders were KRW 15.6 billion in 2022, KRW 22.3 billion in 2023, KRW 5.01 billion in 2024 and KRW 6.22 billion in 2025, which is four consecutive years of losses from 2022 to 2025. Net loss including non-controlling interests was reported at the same amounts for each year.

The debt-to-equity ratio rose from 161.7% in 2022 to 304.0% in 2023 and 321.2% in 2024, then fell to 271.0% in 2025. At the end of 2025, total equity was KRW 20.2 billion, all of it attributable to shareholders, against total liabilities of KRW 54.6 billion.

Operating cash flow went from KRW 583 million in 2022 to -KRW 3.97 billion in 2023, -KRW 5.07 billion in 2024 and -KRW 650 million in 2025.

On a quarterly basis, Q3 2025 sales were KRW 5.64 billion with an operating loss of KRW 486 million, and Q4 2025 sales were KRW 5.66 billion with an operating loss of KRW 604 million. Q1 2026 sales were KRW 6.85 billion with an operating loss of KRW 371 million.

Q2 2026 sales rose to KRW 8.62 billion with an operating profit of KRW 479 million, a swing to profit. Q3 2026 sales were KRW 6.98 billion, down 19.0% from the prior quarter but up 23.7% year on year, with an operating loss of KRW 166 million.

Net losses attributable to shareholders were KRW 1.11 billion in Q3 2025, KRW 2.38 billion in Q4 2025, KRW 1.2 billion in Q1 2026 and KRW 218 million in Q2 2026. Q3 2026 turned to a net profit attributable to shareholders of KRW 67.99 million.

Over the last four quarters, however, the cumulative net loss attributable to shareholders still totals KRW 3.74 billion.

05

Industry analysis

Demand for bar steel and precision-finished bar steel is closely tied to automotive and machinery industries. A company profile from fnguide says that stagnation in the auto industry and inflows of low-priced Chinese imports intensified domestic competition and reduced the company's sales.

The same source says that halting its own rolling process and sourcing semi-finished products externally improved the cost structure.

A technology analysis report cited by Steel Daily lists expansion of the specialty steel and AAM markets as opportunities, and more low-priced Chinese steel and weaker steel demand as threats.

Tighter environmental regulation, currency volatility, wider electrification and advances in low-carbon materials were highlighted as key political, economic, social and technological variables.

Reporting on the sector repeatedly notes that bar steel profitability depends on the combination of billet prices, exchange rates, capacity utilization and selling prices. Sales are small compared with the KOSDAQ metals sector average, which places the company at a scale disadvantage among listed peers.

The company is linked to shipbuilding through its stake in a shipbuilding affiliate, but the company's filings do not separately break out the share of core sales tied to shipbuilding.

On the industry cycle, the company sits between weak domestic auto demand and low-priced import pressure on one side and expanding specialty steel and AAM demand on the other.

06

Outlook

A technology analysis report cited by Steel Daily projects 2026 sales of KRW 247 billion, up 2.7% year on year. The same report expects gradual margin improvement based on stable sales from precision-finished bar steel and other round bars.

The company is reported to plan expansion into robot hardware, speed reducers, ultra-precision machining and MRO, and to build a future-industry portfolio through investments including an underwater drone developer.

In AAM, the company is working with an AAM specialist partner on supplying high-performance aluminum materials and joint technology development.

Reliability testing targets aircraft with a maximum payload of 200 kg and a maximum takeoff weight in the 600 kg class, and the company aims to commercialize independently in 2027.

On March 10, 2026, the company disclosed a share consolidation that changes the par value from KRW 500 to KRW 1,000 and reduces common shares outstanding from 26,139,617 to 13,069,808. Trading was suspended from April 29 to May 22, 2026, and new shares were scheduled to list on May 26, 2026.

The stated purpose was to stabilize the share price and enhance corporate value by reducing the number of shares in circulation.

Core operations showed a quarterly improvement in Q2 and Q3 2026, but Q3 sales fell from the prior quarter, so whether the sales rebound is sustainable needs to be confirmed in upcoming quarterly results. New business contributions are not yet separated in the disclosed figures.

08

Bull factors

Quarterly profit direction has turned

Operating profit of KRW 479 million in Q2 2026 turned the quarter positive, and Q3 2026 produced a net profit attributable to shareholders of KRW 67.99 million. Compared with an operating loss of KRW 486 million in Q3 2025, the Q3 2026 operating loss narrowed to KRW 166 million. Q3 2026 sales of KRW 6.98 billion were up 23.7% year on year.

Shift to a precision-focused cost structure

The company has moved to a model that sources semi-finished products externally after halting its own rolling process, and this change has been described as improving the cost structure. Steel Daily reported that precision-finished bar steel made up 60% of 2025 sales.

The technology analysis report expects gradual profit improvement supported by stable sales of precision-finished and other round bars.

New business pipeline in AAM and robotics

The company is working with an AAM specialist partner on supplying high-performance aluminum materials and joint development, aiming for independent commercialization in 2027. In 2025 it added robotics, MRO and AAM materials to its business purpose. Plans to expand into robot hardware, speed reducers and ultra-precision machining have been reported.

Operating losses have continued for three consecutive years from 2023 to 2025, and the 2025 operating loss of KRW 2.61 billion was wider than the KRW 1.8 billion loss in 2024. Net losses attributable to shareholders have continued for four consecutive years from 2022 to 2025. The cumulative net loss attributable to shareholders over the last four quarters remains at KRW 3.74 billion.

Debt burden and negative operating cash flow

Operating cash flow was -KRW 3.97 billion in 2023, -KRW 5.07 billion in 2024 and -KRW 650 million in 2025, with negative readings continuing after 2023.

Core demand slowdown and low-priced import pressure

A fnguide company profile says stagnant auto demand and inflows of low-priced Chinese imports intensified domestic competition and reduced sales. A technology analysis report lists the bar-steel-centered structure as a weakness and weaker steel demand and more low-priced Chinese steel as threats. Sales declined in both 2023, to KRW 21.5 billion, and 2024, to KRW 16.6 billion.

10

Risk factors

Financial and liquidity risk

Total liabilities of KRW 54.6 billion are large relative to total equity of KRW 20.2 billion, and operating cash flow has been negative since 2023. If profitability stays confined to individual quarters, debt repayment and working capital funding could remain a burden. The pace at which the 271.0% debt-to-equity ratio declines is the next point to monitor.

Raw material and pricing risk

Because semi-finished products are sourced externally, billet prices and exchange rates directly affect profitability. Reporting indicates that bar steel profitability depends on the combination of billet prices, exchange rates, capacity utilization and selling prices. If selling prices fail to keep pace with cost increases, the quarterly swing to profit could reverse.

New business execution and small-cap risk

AAM materials are still undergoing airframe reliability testing, with independent commercialization targeted for 2027, and their contribution is not yet separated in disclosed results. Robotics and MRO businesses remain at the planning stage.

As a small KOSDAQ company whose share count was changed by the 2026 consolidation, its share price may remain volatile.

Overall view

Dongil Steelux remains in an annual operating-loss pattern in its core bar-steel business, although Q2 and Q3 2026 each turned profitable on a quarterly basis. Sales rose 44.6% in 2025, but the operating loss widened to KRW 2.61 billion, so the revenue recovery did not yet translate into better profit.

Over the last four quarters, the cumulative net loss attributable to shareholders is KRW 3.74 billion, and total liabilities of KRW 54.6 billion and negative operating cash flow are the main financial burdens.

Supporting factors include the cost-structure change from halting in-house rolling, a product mix tilted toward precision-finished bar steel, and a pipeline in AAM and robotics. Opposing factors include weak auto demand, low-priced Chinese imports and the limits of a bar-steel-centered structure.

The 2026 share consolidation makes per-share metrics harder to compare with past figures. The points to confirm next are the 2026 full-year preliminary results in early 2027, the 2026 annual business report, Q1 2027 quarterly results and the progress of AAM commercialization.
