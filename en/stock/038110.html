02

Business structure

Ecoplastic makes automotive plastic parts and supplies Hyundai Motor, KIA and other vehicle makers; it is listed on KOSDAQ. Main sales come from TRIM products, followed by BUMPER products.

By segment, based on Q2 2026 sales, the parent accounts for 54%, the Komos unit 18%, the Aia unit 17%, ECO PLASTIC AMERICA Corp. 7% and a further domestic subsidiary 5%. Overseas operations include entities in India and Vietnam. In Q3 2025 the Jinwon subsidiary's unit Bau was brought in as a second-tier subsidiary.

The US subsidiary supplies plastic bumpers exclusively to Hyundai Motor's Georgia EV plant and was named a first-tier bumper supplier for Hyundai's North American EV plant in 2024. The Georgia plant has capacity for five models, but in a 2025 report only two were in mass production, according to a company official.

Customer concentration is high, so completed-vehicle output directly drives results. The company is also developing console products for autonomous vehicles. Listed peers in the same sector include Hyundai Mobis, Hanon Systems, Sungwoo Hitech and Seoyon E-Hwa.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual revenue rose from KRW 1.82 trillion in 2022 to KRW 2.16 trillion in 2023 (up 18.6%), KRW 2.26 trillion in 2024 (up 4.4%) and KRW 2.56 trillion in 2025 (up 13.6%). Revenue rose for three consecutive years from 2023 through 2025, and 2025 was the largest year in the 2022–2025 span.

Operating profit went from KRW 38.5 billion in 2022 to KRW 72.4 billion in 2023 (up 88.0%), the peak, before falling to KRW 32.9 billion in 2024 (down 54.6%). It recovered to KRW 35.7 billion in 2025 (up 8.7%), but the operating margin was only 1.4%.

Profit attributable to controlling shareholders peaked at KRW 43.7 billion in 2023 (up 85.0%), then fell to KRW 14.1 billion in 2024 (down 67.8%) and KRW 1.99 billion in 2025 (down 85.9%), a second straight annual decline. The gap between 2025 operating profit and net profit was driven by financing costs.

The company cited higher interest expense from North American and new-plant investment and lower foreign-currency translation gains. Interest expense rose from KRW 11.1 billion in 2022 to KRW 17.8 billion in 2023 and KRW 18.1 billion in 2024.

Quarterly, operating profit was KRW 2.29 billion in Q3 2025 and an operating loss of KRW 3.33 billion in Q4 2025, which turned into a loss from a profit. Q1 2026 posted operating profit of KRW 6.78 billion, turning positive, and Q2 2026 reached KRW 25.9 billion (up 281.8% quarter on quarter).

Q3 2026 returned to an operating loss of KRW 7.66 billion. Profit attributable to controlling shareholders went from KRW 18 billion in Q2 2026 to a net loss of KRW 44.2 billion in Q3 2026. Q3 2026 revenue was KRW 606.7 billion, down 0.9% year on year.

At end-2025, total liabilities were KRW 1.34 trillion and total equity KRW 359.7 billion, and the debt-to-equity ratio fell from 472.6% in 2024 to 371.7%. Operating cash flow declined from KRW 85.9 billion in 2022 to KRW 36.3 billion in 2025.

05

Industry analysis

Ecoplastic's results track completed-vehicle output directly. A market data service describes its auto parts business as driven by vehicle production levels. For 2026, a recovery in domestic and export demand and the start-up of new EV plants were cited as factors that could lift production.

On the other side, the EV slowdown, a temporary fall in demand, was named as a reason the US subsidiary's path to profitability has slipped. Hyundai Motor's HMGMA began producing the Ioniq 5 in October 2024 and started mass production of the Ioniq 9 in March 2025.

Because Ecoplastic supplies bumpers to this plant, local EV output is closely linked to its sales pace. The US subsidiary's 2025 revenue was KRW 191.7 billion, up sharply from KRW 16.6 billion in 2024, but it still posted a net loss of KRW 28.2 billion.

According to Alphasquare data for 2025, the auto parts industry average operating margin is slightly negative, and Ecoplastic's 1.4% margin in 2025 sits above that average.

Among listed peers such as Hyundai Mobis, Hanon Systems, Sungwoo Hitech and Seoyon E-Hwa, Ecoplastic's reliance on one customer's North American plant output appears high. However, customer diversification cannot be fully compared on disclosure data alone.

06

Outlook

The company's stated direction centers on expanding production at the US plant and developing autonomous-vehicle-ready products. A company official has said the Georgia plant will add production models. In February 2026, two guarantee decisions for the US subsidiary ECOPLASTIC AMERICA were disclosed.

The February 3 disclosure covered KRW 4.37 billion, with a guarantee period from February 5, 2026 to February 5, 2027. The February 27 disclosure covered KRW 11.28 billion, with a guarantee period from February 27, 2026 to December 31, 2032.

Both relate to loans for new plant construction following the bumper award for Hyundai Motor's Georgia EV plant. After the February 27 disclosure, the total guarantee balance stood at KRW 478.3 billion.

The company said proceeds from a treasury-share sale would fund investment, but also said the specific use had not been fixed. The buyer was the largest shareholder, Seojin Automotive.

According to WiseReport, first-half 2026 consolidated revenue fell 5.3% year on year and operating profit fell 11.2%, while net profit rose 212.7%. The detailed drivers of the Q3 2026 loss need to be confirmed in the periodic report. The next checkpoint is the operating margin and financing cost trend in the 2026 annual report.

08

Bull factors

Exclusive bumper supply to a US EV plant

The company is the sole supplier of plastic bumpers to Hyundai Motor's Georgia EV plant and was named a first-tier bumper supplier for Hyundai's North American EV plant in 2024. The plant has capacity for five models but was producing two, so adding models could expand supply volumes. The company has said it plans to expand the models produced and supplement local staffing.

Expected production recovery in 2026

WiseReport expects production to rise in 2026 on a domestic and export recovery and the start of new EV plants. First-half 2026 net profit, including non-controlling interests, rose 212.7% year on year on its tally.

Revenue and operating profit fell over the same period, so the quality of the recovery needs further confirmation.

Quarterly operating profit turned positive in 2026

Q1 2026 operating profit of KRW 6.78 billion turned positive from a prior-quarter loss, and Q2 2026 reached KRW 25.9 billion, up 281.8% quarter on quarter. The quarterly rebound shows the operating base has not disappeared. However, Q3 2026 returned to an operating loss.

09

Bear factors

Sharp contraction in profit attributable to shareholders

Profit attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 1.99 billion in 2025, down 85.9%, and fell in both 2024 and 2025. The last four quarters total a KRW 27.3 billion net loss attributable to controlling shareholders, and Q3 2026 alone posted KRW 44.2 billion. In a year when operating profit was positive, profit did not reach shareholders, which weighs on the case.

High leverage and rising financing costs

Total liabilities at end-2025 were KRW 1.34 trillion against total equity of KRW 359.7 billion. Interest expense rose from KRW 11.1 billion in 2022 to KRW 18.1 billion in 2024.

Two guarantee decisions in February 2026 brought the total guarantee balance to KRW 478.3 billion, adding to financial burden as guarantee sizes grow.

Continued losses at the US subsidiary

The US subsidiary posted 2025 revenue of KRW 191.7 billion but a net loss of KRW 28.2 billion, following a KRW 21.3 billion net loss in 2024. Earlier plans had projected 2025 revenue of KRW 313.9 billion and operating profit of KRW 6.3 billion, so the timing of profitability has slipped. The EV slowdown has been cited as a cause.

10

Risk factors

Balance sheet

The debt-to-equity ratio was 371.7% at end-2025, and the guarantee balance reached KRW 478.3 billion. These guarantees tie to loans for the US subsidiary's new plant, so slower plant ramp-up could raise repayment pressure. Proceeds from the treasury-share sale are planned for investment, but the use has not been fixed.

Customer and production concentration

Results are set by completed-vehicle production levels. Dependence on the Hyundai Motor Georgia plant is high, so production adjustments at one plant feed directly into sales. If mass-produced models at the US plant do not expand, supply volumes will stay limited.

Currency and financing costs

In 2025, exchange-rate swings reduced foreign-currency translation gains and interest costs rose, widening the gap between operating profit and net profit. Heavy overseas investment makes earnings sensitive to exchange and interest rates. Whether financing costs offset any operating recovery is the next item to watch.

Overall view

Ecoplastic's 2025 revenue of KRW 2.56 trillion extended a three-year rising trend, but profit attributable to controlling shareholders fell to KRW 1.99 billion.

The gap between 2025 operating profit of KRW 35.7 billion and net profit was attributed to higher interest expense and lower foreign-currency translation gains. Operating profit recovered in Q1 and Q2 2026, but Q3 2026 returned to an operating loss of KRW 7.66 billion.

Bullish factors include the bumper supply position at Hyundai Motor's Georgia EV plant, expected production recovery in 2026, and higher first-half 2026 net profit.

Bearish factors include the cumulative KRW 27.3 billion net loss attributable to controlling shareholders over the last four quarters, a debt-to-equity ratio of 371.7%, a guarantee balance of KRW 478.3 billion, and continued losses at the US subsidiary.

The next checkpoints are the operating margin and financing costs in the 2026 annual report, and the borrowing structure after the guarantee period ends on February 5, 2027. This report summarizes facts and bullish and bearish factors and does not include an investment opinion.
