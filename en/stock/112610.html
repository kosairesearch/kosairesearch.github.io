Report overview

CS Wind: US Onshore Demand vs. Margin Recovery

CS Wind's operating profit rose for three consecutive years through 2025, but revenue has declined for three straight quarters through the third quarter of 2026, leaving US onshore demand and subsea orders as the next checkpoints.

  1. 1

    Operating profit reached KRW 320.3 billion in 2025, up 25.4% from the prior year, while revenue fell 4.6% to KRW 2.93 trillion. The operating margin climbed from 3.1% in 2022 to 10.9% in 2025.

  2. 2

    Third-quarter 2026 operating profit of KRW 80.7 billion rose 22.8% year on year, but profit attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 46.8 billion fell 16.2%.

  3. 3

    The fourth quarter of 2025 posted a controlling-shareholder net loss of KRW 154.2 billion. Meritz Securities explained that an asset impairment of about KRW 220 billion was recognized on the closure of the Lindo yard.

  4. 4

    Cumulative new orders for 2025 reached USD 1.52 billion, 95% of the annual target, with the United States and Europe accounting for 89% of the total.

  5. 5

02

Business structure

CS Wind is the global leader in wind power towers, and securities analysts estimate that the tower segment accounts for roughly 70% of revenue. The rest comes mainly from offshore substructures, handled through its subsidiary CS Offshore and Denmark-based Bladt.

The United States accounts for about 60% of revenue, which makes the company highly exposed to US policy. Its production base includes the US, Vietnam and Türkiye, and tower output for Europe has grown with Vietnam and Türkiye capacity.

Key customers include turbine makers Vestas and Nordex; a November 2026 press report said contracts with both secure all US production volume for 2027. NH Investment & Securities' May 2026 analysis said the US plant's annual capacity is planned to rise from KRW 1.2 trillion to KRW 1.4 trillion.

Competition comes from Chinese wind companies, while in the US, rivals Broadwind and Arcosa have reduced or exited tower capacity according to market analysis.

The subsea business had a work gap in the second half of 2025, and the Lindo yard closure followed in the fourth quarter, so its contribution depends on securing new projects.

04

Earnings analysis

On a disclosed annual basis, revenue rose from KRW 1.37 trillion in 2022 to KRW 3.07 trillion in 2024, a 102.1% increase, before falling 4.6% to KRW 2.93 trillion in 2025.

Operating profit went from KRW 42.1 billion in 2022 to KRW 104.7 billion in 2023 (up 148.5%), KRW 255.5 billion in 2024 (up 144.1%) and KRW 320.3 billion in 2025 (up 25.4%), rising for three consecutive years from 2023 through 2025.

Revenue more than doubled in 2024, yet the operating margin was only 8.3%; in 2025 revenue declined while the operating margin climbed to 10.9%, which marks a recovery in profitability.

Profit attributable to controlling shareholders swung from a net loss of KRW 0.98 billion in 2022 to KRW 19.9 billion in 2023, reached KRW 142.3 billion in 2024 and fell 75.6% to KRW 34.7 billion in 2025.

Net income in 2025 was KRW 40.1 billion, higher than the controlling-shareholder figure because it includes non-controlling interests, so the two measures should be read separately.

By quarter, revenue peaked at KRW 782.7 billion in the fourth quarter of 2025, up 31.1% from the prior quarter, and then declined for three consecutive quarters through the third quarter of 2026.

The controlling-shareholder net loss of KRW 154.2 billion in the fourth quarter of 2025 is linked to an asset impairment of about KRW 220 billion tied to the closure of the Lindo yard, according to Meritz Securities.

Profit attributable to controlling shareholders returned to KRW 43.1 billion in the first quarter of 2026, and NH Investment & Securities estimated that excluding about KRW 33 billion of AMPC subsidies, the tower manufacturing margin would be around 3.2%.

Third-quarter 2026 operating profit of KRW 80.7 billion rose 22.8% year on year but fell 6.2% from the previous quarter, while controlling-shareholder net income of KRW 46.8 billion fell 16.2% year on year.

The four quarters from the fourth quarter of 2025 through the third quarter of 2026 together produced a controlling-shareholder net loss of KRW 4.75 billion. Operating cash flow rose from KRW 49.1 billion in 2024 to KRW 527.7 billion in 2025, and the debt ratio fell from 200.7% to 167.7%.

05

Industry analysis

US onshore wind demand is expected to be pulled forward ahead of the end of the AMPC subsidy. Samsung Securities expects US onshore installations to exceed 8 gigawatts (GW) a year from this year through next.

In the same period, US tower makers Broadwind and Arcosa have exited or scaled back tower capacity, which market analysts say has reduced supply. NH Investment & Securities sees US onshore orders holding up for now, given the permitting pipeline of key customer Vestas through 2027.

Shinhan Investment Corp. cited US permitting delays and deferred orders from Chinese turbine makers as reasons 2026 new orders are flat with the prior year.

In Europe, offshore wind projects continue to expand, and NH Investment & Securities said the EU's protection of its domestic wind companies could work in CS Wind's favor. Competition from Chinese companies is intensifying at the same time.

In April 2026, NH Investment & Securities said higher energy prices had improved sentiment toward renewable investment and turbine-maker valuations. In substructures, Kyobo Securities expects margins to return to normal levels from 2027 as customer incentive effects fade.

Against this backdrop, CS Wind is cited as holding an advantage as the global number one tower maker with both US capacity and a European production footprint.

06

Outlook

Shinhan Investment Corp. expects 2026 annual new orders at about USD 1.5 billion, the same level as 2025. Kyobo Securities cited delayed orders from Chinese turbine makers and a plate-steel supply disruption at its Türkiye unit in cutting its forecast from USD 1.7 billion to USD 1.5 billion.

CS Wind has secured all of its 2027 US production volume through contracts with Vestas and Nordex, and Kyobo Securities expects that volume to rise more than 10% from 2026.

The US plant's annual capacity is set to expand from KRW 1.2 trillion to KRW 1.4 trillion, with skilled workers from Taiwan and Malaysia to be deployed. The tower supply contract with Vestas American Wind Technology, worth KRW 135.33 billion, runs from February 11, 2026 to December 11, 2026 as disclosed.

For substructures, NH Investment & Securities said the two projects now secured can generate earnings through the third quarter of 2027, but additional orders are needed for sustainability after that.

New European offshore orders are cited as a variable that could reduce reliance on the US, and their outcome is the next checkpoint for the subsea business.

08

Bull factors

US onshore demand supports volume

Samsung Securities expects US onshore installations to exceed 8 GW a year from this year through next. A November 2026 press report said CS Wind has secured all 2027 US production volume under contracts with Vestas and Nordex. The US plant's annual capacity is planned to rise from KRW 1.2 trillion to KRW 1.4 trillion.

Margin recovery trend is visible

The operating margin rose from 3.1% in 2022 to 10.9% in 2025, and third-quarter 2026 operating profit grew 22.8% year on year. Operating profit rose for three consecutive years from 2023 through 2025. Profit attributable to controlling shareholders turned positive again in the first quarter of 2026.

European offshore orders could ease US reliance

NH Investment & Securities argued that CS Wind's European facilities and technology give it an advantage against Chinese competitors. The EU's protection of its domestic wind companies was cited as a supporting factor. If new substructure orders follow the two projects now secured, US revenue concentration could decline.

09

Bear factors

Revenue has fallen for three straight quarters

Revenue declined for three consecutive quarters from the first quarter through the third quarter of 2026, and third-quarter revenue of KRW 677.5 billion was 1.3% lower than the prior quarter. Controlling-shareholder net income of KRW 46.8 billion in the third quarter fell 16.2% year on year. Revenue also fell 4.6% in 2025 to KRW 2.93 trillion, so a return to top-line growth is not yet confirmed.

AMPC dependence and US concentration

The US accounts for about 60% of revenue, so US policy has a large effect on results. NH Investment & Securities estimated that excluding AMPC subsidies of about KRW 33 billion in the first quarter of 2026, the tower manufacturing margin would be about 3.2%.

A change in the subsidy framework could alter the earnings structure, so policy direction remains a bearish factor.

Slower orders and subsea margin normalization

Kyobo Securities cut its 2026 annual new-order forecast from USD 1.7 billion to USD 1.5 billion, citing delayed orders from Chinese turbine makers and a plate-steel supply disruption in Türkiye. Customer incentives in substructures are fading, and margins are expected to return to normal levels from 2027.

In the fourth quarter of 2025, an asset impairment was recorded and the controlling-shareholder net loss reached KRW 154.2 billion.

10

Risk factors

Policy and permitting

US permitting delays and changes to subsidy programs such as AMPC are variables for US revenue and profit. Shinhan Investment Corp. cited US permitting delays as a reason 2026 orders are flat with the prior year. EU policy and competition from Chinese companies remain longer-term variables.

Currency and raw materials

A weaker US dollar against the won was cited as reducing won-converted revenue and profit. A plate-steel supply disruption at the Türkiye unit could affect production schedules. Raw material and logistics costs, along with customer-specific contract terms, create gaps between order value and actual profitability.

Asset impairment and customer concentration

The Lindo yard closure in the fourth quarter of 2025 led to an asset impairment and a one-time loss. Volume is concentrated among key customers Vestas and Nordex, so their order timing drives results. Gains or losses from selling the yard and moving machinery assets are variables to monitor.

12

Overall view

CS Wind's disclosed results show operating profit and operating margin improving in 2025, but revenue has fallen for three consecutive quarters since peaking in the fourth quarter of 2025 through the third quarter of 2026.

Third-quarter 2026 operating profit of KRW 80.7 billion rose 22.8% year on year, yet controlling-shareholder net income fell 16.2%, so earnings stability is still being confirmed.

The controlling-shareholder net loss of KRW 154.2 billion in the fourth quarter of 2025 reflected a Lindo yard asset impairment; profit returned from the first quarter of 2026, but the last four quarters combined remain a loss.

Bullish factors include rising US onshore installations, US production volume for 2027 secured with Vestas and Nordex, and an expanded US plant capacity. Bearish factors include a low tower margin excluding AMPC subsidies, a cut to the 2026 new-order forecast, and margin normalization as substructure incentives fade.

The KRW 80,000 target price was proposed by Hana Securities and Kyobo Securities in a November 2026 press report and reflects those firms' judgments.

The next checkpoints are the level of annual new orders at the end of December 2026, preliminary fourth-quarter 2026 results in the first quarter of 2027, and the annual operating margin in the 2026 annual report due by the end of March 2027.
