02

Business structure

Hyvision System, founded in 2010, makes automated inspection and production equipment and holds six subsidiaries in Korea, China, Vietnam and India.

Its core is process and inspection automation equipment for IT/mobile camera modules and smart components, complemented by secondary-battery vision inspection equipment, battery production lines and 3D printers.

Based on the product sales breakdown in company-monitoring data, IT/mobile accounts for 78.63%, secondary batteries for 15.20%, automotive for 4.96%, and 3D printers and other products for 1.21%.

In Q3 2025 the IT/mobile segment was 60.2% of revenue and fell 64.1% year on year, while the secondary-battery segment grew nearly tenfold in the same quarter. The battery business began in 2022 and moved from a first ESS battery-pack line order in 2024 and a second in June 2025 to a larger contract in 2026.

A 2024 brokerage note cited internal capabilities in image processing, motion control and part assembly beyond camera inspection. R&D spending was 15.94% of 2025 revenue on a consolidated basis, up from 9.44% in 2024.

In customers, a 2023 press report said autonomous-driving sensing-camera inspection equipment was supplied to Tesla and Rivian through Samsung Electro-Mechanics. Koh Young Technology and SFA Engineering are among the domestic peers cited.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual revenue rose from KRW 197.7 billion in 2022 to KRW 348.3 billion in 2023, up 76.2% year on year, the largest year of the 2022–2025 period. Revenue then fell 10.4% to KRW 312 billion in 2024 and 45.2% to KRW 171.1 billion in 2025, two consecutive annual declines, with 2025 the smallest year of the four.

Operating profit climbed 134.4% to KRW 58.8 billion in 2023 before dropping 47.3% to KRW 31 billion in 2024, and in 2025 the company recorded an operating loss of KRW 32.6 billion. The operating margin moved from 12.7% in 2022 to 16.9% in 2023, 9.9% in 2024 and -19.0% in 2025.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders reached KRW 45.9 billion in 2023, fell 14.2% to KRW 39.4 billion in 2024, and turned into a net loss of KRW 17.1 billion in 2025. Net income including non-controlling interests was a net loss of KRW 23.7 billion in 2025.

On a quarterly basis, Q3 2025 revenue was KRW 58.7 billion with an operating loss of KRW 8.69 billion, and Q4 2025 revenue was KRW 48.4 billion with an operating loss of KRW 15.1 billion.

Company-monitoring data attributed the weakness in Q1 2025 to partly lower smartphone demand, lower revenue at a consolidated subsidiary, and higher costs from diversification and headcount growth.

Q1 2026 revenue was KRW 28 billion, down 42.2% from the prior quarter, followed by KRW 50.3 billion in Q2 and KRW 63 billion in Q3, two consecutive quarterly increases.

The operating loss narrowed to KRW 7.48 billion in Q1 and KRW 5.72 billion in Q2, and Q3 produced an operating profit of KRW 2.12 billion, a swing to profit both sequentially and year on year. Q3 2026 still carried a net loss attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 4.47 billion.

R&D reached 15.94% of revenue in 2025 against 9.44% in 2024, a ratio that rose partly because revenue fell. Operating cash flow was KRW -24.2 billion in 2022, KRW 16.2 billion in 2023, KRW 101.7 billion in 2024 and KRW -10.7 billion in 2025.

At the end of 2025 total equity was KRW 275.4 billion, of which KRW 248.8 billion belonged to controlling shareholders, and total liabilities were KRW 42.4 billion, with the debt ratio falling from 38.7% in 2022 to 15.4% in 2025.

05

Industry analysis

Demand from downstream markets runs on two tracks. In smartphone camera modules, IT/mobile revenue fell 64.1% year on year in Q3 2025, and in November 2025 brokers projected that related revenue would grow with form-factor changes and virtual devices.

In secondary batteries, rising electricity demand from AI infrastructure is lifting demand for ESS battery-pack production. Forecasts also point to a lower share of Chinese products in the US market and a larger share for domestic battery makers.

The company says it is widening its position from inspection equipment into turnkey lines that combine process, assembly, inspection and automation. Among domestic automation-inspection peers, Koh Young Technology and SFA Engineering are cited.

In wafer back-end AOI equipment, a 2024 brokerage note said the company has competitiveness against overseas leaders in that market. Battery-equipment orders tend to cluster around customers' North American plant investments, so quarterly results can swing with the timing of orders.

06

Outlook

The largest order signal is the ESS equipment business. A US-bound ESS pack assembly contract disclosed on May 11, 2026 is worth KRW 19.2 billion, equal to 11.20% of consolidated revenue in the prior year, with 57% of the contract value received as an advance.

On June 17, 2026 the company announced a line contract of about KRW 95 billion for ESS battery-pack assembly serving AI data-center demand in North America, its largest single project since entering secondary batteries. The line will be installed at a North American site of a major domestic battery maker.

The CEO said the company will press ahead to win additional production-line orders. In June 2026 Meritz Securities discussed both ESS results and recovery in the core business, and Yuanta-era analysis by Eugene Investment & Securities saw diversification widening through large ESS orders.

Indian plant No. 1 was completed in November 2025, and Eugene Investment & Securities expected full-scale equipment manufacturing and deliveries from 2026. Investing.com recorded Q2 2026 revenue of KRW 50.3 billion against an estimate of KRW 48.3 billion.

Because Q1 2026 revenue fell to KRW 28 billion, the pace at which large orders convert into quarterly revenue will shape future results. How the advance-payment structure flows through cash flow is a point to confirm in the 2026 annual report.

Bull factors

Expanding ESS equipment orders

A US-bound ESS pack assembly contract worth KRW 19.2 billion was disclosed in May 2026, and an about KRW 95 billion production-line contract followed in June. Receiving 57% of the contract value as an advance provides early cash inflow.

The battery business began in 2022 and has moved through ESS line orders in 2024 and 2025. The two large contracts support the diversification trend.

Swing to profit in Q3 2026

Revenue rose for two consecutive quarters after bottoming at KRW 28 billion in Q1 2026. The Q3 2026 operating profit of KRW 2.12 billion represented a turn to profit both sequentially and year on year. The operating loss had already narrowed from KRW 7.48 billion in Q1 to KRW 5.72 billion in Q2. The move from losses to profit is the starting point for checking the next quarters.

Low debt burden and past cash generation

Total liabilities at the end of 2025 were KRW 42.4 billion, with a debt ratio of 15.4%. Operating cash flow reached KRW 101.7 billion in 2024, a year with strong cash generation. Equity was KRW 275.4 billion, of which KRW 248.8 billion belonged to controlling shareholders. This balance-sheet capacity supports equipment investment and order execution.

Bear factors

Aftermath of the 2025 swing to loss

The company recorded an operating loss of KRW 32.6 billion and a net loss attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 17.1 billion in 2025. The four quarters from Q4 2025 through Q3 2026 total KRW 13.8 billion in net losses attributable to controlling shareholders.

Q3 2026 still carried a net loss of KRW 4.47 billion despite the operating profit. Whether the recovery holds across consecutive periods remains to be confirmed.

Exposure to smartphone demand

IT/mobile makes up 78.63% of product sales. Its revenue fell 64.1% year on year in Q3 2025. Partial weakness in smartphone demand was cited as a factor in Q1 2025 results. Changes in demand for the core segment flow heavily into total earnings.

Cash flow and revenue volatility

Operating cash flow in 2025 was KRW -10.7 billion. Q1 2026 revenue of KRW 28 billion was down 42.2% from the prior quarter. ESS equipment is booked project by project, so quarterly revenue can swing widely. Even with advance payments, earnings patterns depend on the timing of delivery and revenue recognition.

10

Risk factors

Customer and product concentration

A high share of IT/mobile revenue ties earnings to camera-module investment cycles in smartphones. In Q1 2025, partly weaker smartphone demand and lower revenue at a consolidated subsidiary reduced results together. Secondary-battery revenue is growing but was about 15% of product sales in the breakdown cited. Changes in investment by specific customers can flow quickly into quarterly revenue.

Execution of ESS turnkey lines

North American ESS lines are supplied on a turnkey basis covering process, assembly and automation equipment as well as inspection. A broader scope can increase the burden of design, delivery schedule and cost control. Even with a 57% advance, the timing of remaining payments and installation affects results. Profitability in new business areas may take time to settle.

Overseas subsidiaries and cash flow

Six subsidiaries across Korea, China, Vietnam and India mean overseas operations feed into consolidated results. India plant No. 1 was completed in November 2025, and the early utilization rate is a key point as full production begins in 2026. Operating cash flow was negative KRW 10.7 billion in 2025. R&D intensity also rose as revenue fell, leaving a heavier cost structure.

12

Overall view

Revenue peaked at KRW 348.3 billion in 2023 and fell in both 2024 and 2025, when the company swung to an operating loss of KRW 32.6 billion and a net loss attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 17.1 billion.

Revenue bottomed at KRW 28 billion in Q1 2026 and rose in Q2 and Q3, and Q3 produced an operating profit of KRW 2.12 billion. Still, Q3 2026 carried a net loss attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 4.47 billion, and the last four quarters total KRW 13.8 billion in such losses.

IT/mobile still makes up 78.63% of product sales, so smartphone demand remains the main variable. Secondary-battery ESS equipment is building a new pillar with a KRW 19.2 billion order and an about KRW 95 billion line order.

Meritz Securities and Eugene Investment & Securities both highlighted ESS orders in June 2026 as evidence of core-business recovery and wider diversification.

Strengths and weaknesses both carry weight, so the durability of profit and the conversion of ESS orders into revenue are points for the 2026 annual report and the Q1 2027 quarterly report.
