Ownership Change Brings First Quarterly Profit Signal

Union Korea Pharm, which went through a trading halt and court-led rehabilitation after an embezzlement scandal, swung to controlling-interest net profit in Q2 2026 under new owner Bukwang Pharmaceutical, even as revenue decline and operating losses have not yet ended.

02

Business structure

Union Korea Pharm is a prescription-drug manufacturer based in Munmak, Wonju, Gangwon Province, producing injectables, antibiotics, and treatments for digestive, circulatory and musculoskeletal conditions.

For fiscal 2025, revenue was split among distributed goods (43.71%), own-manufactured products (42.36%) and contract manufacturing (CMO, 13.93%), meaning own products and third-party goods distribution together made up most of sales.

The large majority of revenue is domestic, with only a single-digit share of certain antibiotic products exported. Production is split between Plant 1 and Plant 2, the latter completed in 2019 and KGMP-certified for injectable and antibiotic manufacturing.

Subsidiaries include R&D unit Korea Union Life Science and senior-care service provider Nunacare. Since 2026 the ownership structure has changed, with the company becoming a consolidated subsidiary of Bukwang Pharmaceutical and, in turn, a step-down affiliate of OCI Holdings.

Bukwang Pharmaceutical has said it intends to use the company's manufacturing infrastructure to fill gaps in its own antibiotic and injectable production capacity.

At an extraordinary shareholders' meeting on October 8, 2026, an amendment to the articles of incorporation was approved, changing the corporate name to Bukwang Unipharma Co., Ltd.

Within the KOSDAQ pharmaceutical sector, the company ranks in the lower tier by both market capitalization and revenue, positioning it as a small-to-mid-sized generic and prescription-drug manufacturer.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual revenue rose from KRW 61.3 billion in 2022 to KRW 63.2 billion in 2023, then declined for two straight years to KRW 51.3 billion in 2024 (down 18.8% year-on-year) and KRW 36.4 billion in 2025 (down 29.1%).

Operating profit of KRW 1.27 billion in 2022 turned into an operating loss of KRW 5.23 billion in 2023, and losses widened over three consecutive years to KRW 13.5 billion in 2024 and KRW 17.2 billion in 2025. The operating margin deteriorated from 2.1% in 2022 to negative 47.4% in 2025.

Controlling-interest net loss widened every year for four straight years, from KRW 932 million in 2022 to KRW 18.2 billion in 2023, KRW 20.9 billion in 2024 and KRW 30.8 billion in 2025.

The deterioration over this period coincides with the embezzlement and breach-of-trust allegations against management that surfaced in October 2024 and the auditor's disclaimer of opinion in March 2025, both of which weighed on going-concern confidence and operations broadly.

On a quarterly basis, the company posted operating losses for five consecutive quarters from Q2 2025 through Q2 2026, though the loss size narrowed from KRW 9.91 billion in Q3 2025 to KRW 2.65 billion in Q4 2025, KRW 1.57 billion in Q1 2026 and KRW 1.25 billion in Q2 2026.

Controlling-interest net loss was KRW 11.0 billion in Q3 2025 and KRW 13.1 billion in Q4 2025, narrowed to a loss of KRW 2.29 billion in Q1 2026, and then swung to a profit of KRW 2.14 billion in Q2 2026.

The swing to net profit despite a continuing operating loss likely reflects non-operating items tied to debt restructuring under the court-approved rehabilitation plan.

The sum of controlling-interest net losses over the last four quarters (Q3 2025 through Q2 2026) was KRW 24.3 billion, indicating that despite the single-quarter swing to profit, the trailing one-year figure remains in loss territory.

05

Industry analysis

The domestic generic and prescription-drug manufacturing segment faces structural margin pressure from government drug-price cuts and intensifying ingredient-level competition among small and mid-sized manufacturers.

In this environment, contract manufacturing (CMO) can act as a revenue buffer for smaller firms with weaker sales networks, though profitability varies widely with utilization rates and order stability.

Union Korea Pharm derived 13.93% of 2025 revenue from CMO, meaning swings in contract manufacturing orders directly affect results.

Larger industry players differentiate performance through biosimilar and new-drug pipelines plus overseas milestone income, while this company's heavier reliance on domestic generics and distributed goods places it in a relatively lower tier within the sector.

During the governance dispute and rehabilitation period, client attrition and revenue decline appear to have occurred simultaneously, forming the backdrop for the sharp earnings deterioration in 2024-2025.

The absorption into Bukwang Pharmaceutical can be viewed as an example of a broader industry reshuffling trend in which mid-tier drugmakers acquire distressed companies holding idle production capacity to expand their portfolios.

Manufacturing infrastructure specialized in certain dosage forms such as antibiotics and injectables could become a relative competitive factor amid rising demand for outsourced finished-dosage manufacturing, though this would need to be confirmed through an actual improvement in utilization after integration.

Outlook

The company's top priority is restoring utilization and achieving genuine profitability improvement through production integration with Bukwang Pharmaceutical.

Bukwang has stated its policy of linking Bukwang Unipharma's antibiotic and injectable production facilities with its existing prescription-drug business to expand its product portfolio and secure a stable manufacturing base.

However, specific integrated-production plans or new order volumes have not yet been disclosed and will need to be clarified through future filings and investor communications.

With the rehabilitation proceeding concluded on August 31, 2026 and the corporate name changed to Bukwang Unipharma on October 8, 2026, the management normalization process has outwardly entered its final stage.

The 3-for-1 reverse split decided in July 2026 was a measure to resolve capital impairment and improve the balance sheet, and completion of the related listing-change procedure is the next item to confirm.

The prior management's claim, made around the KRW 30 billion investment in Plant 2, that full utilization could generate over KRW 100 billion in revenue, was a statement by former management and would need to be re-validated under the new controlling shareholder.

Whether the narrowing quarterly losses and the Q2 swing to profit continue, or revert to loss, will need to be confirmed through upcoming periodic reports.

On balance, near-term direction hinges on management-normalization disclosures, while medium-term direction depends on the actual results of production linkage with Bukwang Pharmaceutical.

Bull factors

Capital and Business Support from a Strategic Controlling Shareholder

Bukwang Pharmaceutical, an OCI Group affiliate, became the largest shareholder with a 75.14% stake through a KRW 30 billion third-party share placement in May 2026.

This represents capital injection from a strategic manufacturer rather than a purely financial investor, carrying implications beyond simple liquidity support. Bukwang has stated plans to use the company's antibiotic and injectable production facilities to expand its own portfolio.

If order linkage from the parent materializes, it could open a path to profitability recovery through improved utilization.

Improving Quarterly Earnings Trend

The operating loss narrowed for four consecutive quarters, from KRW 9.91 billion in Q3 2025 to KRW 1.25 billion in Q2 2026. Controlling-interest net profit swung to KRW 2.14 billion in Q2 2026, both quarter-on-quarter and year-on-year.

This suggests the cost structure may have been partly streamlined amid the ownership change and rehabilitation process. Still, whether a single quarter's swing to profit becomes a sustained trend needs confirmation through additional quarterly results.

Conclusion of Rehabilitation and Governance Normalization

The rehabilitation proceeding opened in September 2025 was approved in May 2026 and formally concluded on August 31, 2026. An extraordinary shareholders' meeting on October 8, 2026 then confirmed the corporate name change to Bukwang Unipharma, formalizing the management structure under new ownership.

The fact that trading has continued following a stay of the delisting resolution shows the company has at least moved past the worst-case scenario. However, this marks only the starting point of management normalization, and actual profitability recovery must be verified separately.

Bear factors

Unconfirmed Rebound After Three Years of Revenue Decline Trend

Revenue peaked at KRW 63.2 billion in 2023 and then declined for two straight years to KRW 51.3 billion in 2024 and KRW 36.4 billion in 2025. Q1 and Q2 2026 revenue stood at KRW 5.9 billion and KRW 5.94 billion respectively, still down 41.4% year-on-year.

While quarterly revenue ticked up slightly (0.7%) sequentially, it remains in sharp year-on-year decline. With the revenue base itself not yet recovered, it is premature to discuss sustained earnings strength.

Residual Governance Risk from the Embezzlement Scandal

Trading was halted in October 2024 after a former co-CEO filed embezzlement and breach-of-trust complaints worth about KRW 19.4 billion against founder-chairman Baek Byung-ha and related parties, and the auditor issued a disclaimer of opinion in March 2025.

The KOSDAQ Market Committee resolved to delist the stock in April 2026, and trading has continued only because the delisting procedure was stayed pending the company's injunction request.

While Bukwang Pharmaceutical has become the new controlling shareholder, it remains unclear whether legal risks such as lawsuits and investigations tied to the past scandal have been fully resolved. Restoring governance credibility will take time.

History of Capital Impairment and Balance-Sheet Restructuring Burden

At the end of 2025, total equity was only KRW 22.9 billion against total liabilities of KRW 68.7 billion, a degree of capital impairment reflected in a debt ratio of negative 300.1%. To address this, a 3-for-1 reverse stock split was decided in July 2026.

The overlapping sequence of capital-restructuring measures—reverse split, capital increase and debt-to-equity conversion—has made historical comparison of per-share metrics difficult.

Whether the balance sheet has actually stabilized needs to be reconfirmed through financial statements disclosed after the restructuring.

Risk factors

Legal and Litigation Risk

Publicly available information provides limited visibility into whether criminal and civil proceedings related to the embezzlement and breach-of-trust allegations have been fully concluded. Additional allegations or damage claims, if they emerge, could disrupt the management-normalization timeline.

The history of cross-complaints between former and current management also remains a potential source of uncertainty.

Integration and Synergy Execution Risk

Bukwang Pharmaceutical's stated production-linkage plan remains at an early stage with no specific order volume or timeline disclosed yet. Integrating differing production systems and quality-control standards could take longer than expected.

If the anticipated synergy is delayed or falls short, the benefit to utilization improvement could also be limited.

Capital Structure and Liquidity Risk

Whether the capital-impaired financial structure as of end-2025 has been fully resolved through the reverse split and capital increase alone needs to be confirmed in future disclosures. The possibility of needing further capital raising or debt adjustment during execution of the rehabilitation plan cannot be ruled out.

Operating cash flow recorded a net outflow of KRW 1.13 billion in 2025, which also bears watching from a liquidity standpoint.

Overall view

Union Korea Pharm went through an extreme crisis—trading suspension, a delisting resolution, and court-led rehabilitation—triggered by an embezzlement and breach-of-trust scandal worth about KRW 19.4 billion that surfaced in October 2024, and the management-normalization process has outwardly entered its final stage following its 2026 acquisition by OCI Group-affiliated Bukwang Pharmaceutical and the October 8 renaming to Bukwang Unipharma.

Disclosed results remain weak, with revenue declining for two straight years after peaking in 2023 and the operating loss widening for three consecutive years, but Q2 2026 also showed a swing to controlling-interest net profit alongside four consecutive quarters of narrowing operating losses.

Still, the trailing four-quarter sum remains a controlling-interest net loss of KRW 24.3 billion, so whether the single quarter's profit marks a sustained improvement requires further confirmation.

The balance sheet, which was capital-impaired as of end-2025, is being restructured through the 3-for-1 reverse split and Bukwang Pharmaceutical's capital injection, and whether this process has actually achieved stability needs to be verified in upcoming periodic reports.

Legal risk tied to former management's embezzlement and breach of trust, and whether production integration with the new controlling shareholder will translate into actual profitability gains, both remain uncertain given the lack of detailed public plans so far.

Readers should review upcoming quarterly results and further integration-related disclosures before drawing conclusions.
