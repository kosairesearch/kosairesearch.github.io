Report overview

Loss-making AI chip IP firm must turn orders into revenue

Openedges Technology supplies NPU and memory system IP, but it posted operating losses in every year from 2022 to 2025 and in each of the five quarters from the third quarter of 2025 through the third quarter of 2026, so whether the industry recovery reaches its earnings is the key question.

02

Business structure

Openedges Technology supplies design assets (IP) used in chip design, positioning itself as an AI semiconductor IP company. Its core product is NPU IP, supplied together with memory controller and PHY IP as well as on-chip interconnect (NoC) IP.

ENLIGHT PRO, launched in April 2024, is described by the company as offering at least four times the MAC performance of the prior version, support for Transformer operations, and scalability from a minimum of 8 TOPS to several hundred TOPS.

Customer examples reported in the press include an NPU and memory system IP integrated into ADAS chips for Telechips, and a April 2023 license agreement with Aisin of the Toyota Group covering LPDDR5 memory controller and PHY IP.

A May 2023 report said the company had signed more than 50 license agreements with about 30 customers at home and abroad within six years of its founding.

As of April 2023, 83% of employees were R&D staff, with research sites in Toronto, Canada and San Jose and Austin, United States, in addition to the Korean headquarters. The company presents its integrated supply of NPU and memory system IP as a differentiator it claims is unique worldwide.

Company IR materials from March 2026 cited completed UCIe controller development, ongoing UCIe PHY work targeting the first half of 2027, and joint LP6-PIM research with a global memory maker.

Revenue rests on IP license contracts, and the revenue mix by business segment should be read only from figures confirmed in periodic filings.

04

Earnings analysis

On the disclosed annual basis, revenue was KRW 10 billion in 2022, KRW 19.6 billion in 2023 (up 95.7% year on year), KRW 15.3 billion in 2024 (down 21.8%), and KRW 16.1 billion in 2025 (up 4.8%). Among 2022 to 2025, 2023 was the largest year and 2022 the smallest.

Over the same period, the operating loss was KRW 25.3 billion in 2022, KRW 15.9 billion in 2023 (losses narrowed), KRW 24.6 billion in 2024 (losses widened), and KRW 28.9 billion in 2025 (losses widened), marking four consecutive years of operating losses from 2022 to 2025.

The operating margin was -252.3% in 2022, -81.0% in 2023, -160.6% in 2024, and -180.0% in 2025, so the loss ratio rose again in 2024 after revenue peaked in 2023. In 2025 revenue edged up, but the operating loss was the largest of the four years, meaning higher sales did not absorb the cost base.

Attributable net loss was KRW 25.2 billion in 2022, KRW 14.9 billion in 2023, KRW 27.3 billion in 2024, and KRW 30.1 billion in 2025, and net loss including non-controlling interests was reported at the same amounts each year.

A May 2023 press report quoted the company targeting a return to profitability that year and annual growth of two to three times through 2025, but 2025 revenue of KRW 16.1 billion remained well below that path from the KRW 10 billion base in 2022.

On the balance sheet, equity at the end of 2025 was KRW 17.1 billion, all attributable to controlling shareholders, against total liabilities of KRW 30.5 billion, and the debt ratio was 67.0% in 2022, 117.3% in 2023, 42.8% in 2024, and 178.5% in 2025, the highest of the four years.

Operating cash flow was negative every year, at KRW -26.7 billion in 2022, KRW -19.9 billion in 2023, KRW -10.3 billion in 2024, and KRW -18.6 billion in 2025.

On a quarterly basis, revenue moved from KRW 5.26 billion in the third quarter of 2025 to KRW 3.4 billion in the fourth quarter (down 35.2% quarter on quarter), KRW 2.67 billion in the first quarter of 2026 (down 21.5%), KRW 2.94 billion in the second quarter (up 10.0%), and KRW 1.71 billion in the third quarter (down 42.0%, and down 67.5% year on year).

The operating loss ran at KRW 5.97 billion, KRW 7.56 billion, KRW 9.02 billion, KRW 6.25 billion, and KRW 8.11 billion over the same five quarters. A notable feature of this stretch is that the sales decline in the first quarter of 2026 did not translate into a narrower loss.

05

Industry analysis

In AI chip design, the data transfer bottleneck has emerged as a bigger issue than raw compute performance, and a May 2026 securities report argued that interconnect and interface-centric IP is gaining share. Because Openedges supplies memory system IP alongside NPU IP, it sits close to this trend.

The same report said that if Korea's K-on-device AI program takes off, demand for NPU IP could increase, with the project scale estimated at roughly KRW 700 billion. That figure is an estimate for the whole program and differs from the company's own order value.

On the foundry side, company IR materials said fabless customers are diversifying supply chains amid TSMC price increases and capacity constraints, and that Samsung Electronics' 4, 5 and 8 nanometer processes are stabilizing yields.

On the memory side, the same materials said demand for LPDDR5X and newer technologies at advanced nodes of 8 nanometers and below had surged. On the negative side, several fabless companies halted or delayed projects after the US BIS rule announced in January 2025, which weighs on demand.

On competition, the company said in April 2024 that it is the only firm worldwide offering integrated NPU and memory system IP, but this is the company's own description. Press coverage also described it as the first Korean AI chip IP firm to supply global customers.

Some securities analysts describe the cycle as in the early stage of recovery, with new order flows expected to continue.

06

Outlook

Among the dated milestones in company IR materials, the clearest is the target for completing UCIe PHY development in the first half of 2027. Whether the joint LP6-PIM research with a global memory maker moves into a product stage is also a point to track.

In April 2024 the company said it expected to obtain ISO26262 certification for automotive functional safety later that year, and it planned to expand global license agreements on the back of verified autonomous driving IP.

In a report on third-quarter 2025 results published in November 2025, Korea Investment & Securities said meaningful results in NPU and ASIC could come during 2026, led by defense applications.

That view dates from November 2025, so whether it has appeared in revenue must be checked against the disclosed quarterly figures. In May 2026, Yuanta Securities said it expected benefits from business restructuring and the ASIC boom. The company has not provided a 2026 revenue or profit guidance.

Because third-quarter 2026 revenue fell to KRW 1.71 billion, the next annual filing will show how full-year revenue compares with KRW 16.1 billion in 2025, and the direction of the operating margin will show whether the operating loss is narrowing.

On cash, operating cash flow was negative every year through 2025, so funding plans and working capital trends are items to monitor. Whether customer shifts toward Samsung Electronics' foundry and actual orders under the K-on-device program connect with reported revenue is another point to track.

Bull factors

Differentiation from integrated NPU and memory IP

In April 2024 the company said its integrated supply of NPU and memory system IP is a differentiator it claims is unique worldwide. In a May 2026 report, Yuanta Securities said this integration is viewed as a core competitive advantage as interconnect and interface IP gains share. The company also said ENLIGHT PRO secured customers at launch.

Rising customer review of Samsung foundry

Company IR materials from March 2026 described rising customer review of Samsung Electronics' advanced foundry nodes amid supply chain diversification. The same materials said demand for LPDDR5X and newer memory at 8 nanometers and below surged. UCIe controller development is complete, and UCIe PHY is targeted for completion in the first half of 2027.

Expected demand from K-on-device and ASIC

In May 2026, Yuanta Securities said demand for NPU IP could expand if Korea's K-on-device program takes off. The same report cited an estimated program scale of roughly KRW 700 billion. It also said the company could benefit from the ASIC boom.

Bear factors

Four straight years and five straight quarters of losses

Operating losses ran for four consecutive years from 2022 to 2025, and the KRW 28.9 billion loss in 2025 was the largest in that span. Losses have continued for five consecutive quarters from the third quarter of 2025 through the third quarter of 2026. The third-quarter 2026 operating loss of KRW 8.11 billion was wider than a year earlier.

Revenue decline and project delay risk

Third-quarter 2026 revenue was KRW 1.71 billion, down 67.5% from a year earlier. Company IR materials say several fabless customers halted or delayed projects after the US BIS rule announced in January 2025. The company had targeted two to three times annual growth through 2025, but 2025 revenue was KRW 16.1 billion.

Debt against a thin equity base and cash outflows

At the end of 2025, total liabilities of KRW 30.5 billion exceeded equity of KRW 17.1 billion, and the debt ratio of 178.5% was the highest of the four years. Operating cash flow was negative in every year from 2022 to 2025, at KRW -18.6 billion in 2025. With a limited equity cushion, continued losses could raise the burden of funding.

10

Risk factors

Project delays and regulation

If fabless customers keep halting or delaying projects after the BIS rule, license revenue recognition shifts later. With revenue still at a small scale, a single delayed project can weigh heavily on quarterly results, as the third-quarter 2026 decline illustrates.

Financial capacity and funding

The debt ratio was 178.5% at the end of 2025, and operating cash flow has been negative every year. Attributable net loss over the last four quarters totaled KRW 33.9 billion. The way funding is raised could affect existing shareholders, so capital plans should be checked in the next periodic report.

Technology competition and customer concentration

The differentiation of integrated IP that the company emphasizes is its own claim, and its edge could narrow if competitors offer similar configurations. Publicly reported key customers are concentrated in automotive chip companies such as Telechips and Aisin. Adjustments to a single customer's project could translate directly into earnings swings.

12

Overall view

Openedges Technology is an AI semiconductor IP company that supplies integrated NPU and memory system IP. Revenue rose from KRW 10 billion in 2022 to KRW 16.1 billion in 2025, but operating losses persisted for four consecutive years.

In the third quarter of 2026, revenue was KRW 1.71 billion and the operating loss was KRW 8.11 billion, with revenue down and the loss wider year on year.

On the positive side are the differentiation of integrated IP, rising customer review of Samsung Electronics' foundry, completed UCIe controller development, and securities analyst expectations tied to K-on-device demand and the ASIC boom.

On the negative side are five straight quarters of losses, project delays after the US BIS rule, a debt ratio of 178.5% at the end of 2025, and operating cash outflows every year. On valuation, PER is not calculated because of losses, and the stock trades at a wide premium to net asset value.

The next checkpoints are the Q1 2027 quarterly report, the operating margin in the 2026 annual report, and whether UCIe PHY development is completed in the first half of 2027.
