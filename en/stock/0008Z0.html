Snsys: Growth Expansion Meets Earnings Rebalancing

Snsys grew both revenue and operating profit in 2025, but controlling-interest net profit declined, showing a divergence between top-line expansion and earnings quality.

02

Business structure

Snsys is a marine and offshore equipment specialist spun off from Samsung Heavy Industries' electrical division in 2017, and it listed on KOSDAQ in August 2025.

Its business is organized into four segments: power solutions covering marine, offshore, and land-based switchboards; eco solutions covering dual-fuel supply systems and ballast water management systems (BWMS); navigation control solutions for ship automation; and MRO solutions providing post-delivery maintenance and repair.

In 2024, revenue mix was power solutions 37.7%, eco solutions 26.5% (including dual-fuel at 5.2%), MRO solutions 22.5%, and navigation control solutions 11.5%; by the first quarter of 2025 the mix shifted to power 48.3%, MRO 24.9%, eco 17.0%, and navigation control 9.7%, with power and MRO gaining weight.

As of March 2026, the mix was reported as power solutions 49%, MRO solutions 20%, eco solutions 16%, and navigation control solutions 15%, showing a further rise in the power solutions share.

Key customers are major domestic shipyards Samsung Heavy Industries and Hanwha Ocean; revenue dependence on Samsung Heavy Industries fell from 52.3% in 2023 to 47.3% in 2024 before rising again to 63.7% in the first quarter of 2025, keeping customer concentration volatile.

The company is pursuing customer and regional diversification through expanding order volume from Hanwha Ocean, entry into China and Japan, and a production license agreement with ABB signed in February 2025.

Following the conclusion of mandatory ballast water treatment installation regulations, the business structure is shifting toward MRO and power solutions driven by maintenance demand.

In the competitive landscape, relatively few suppliers handle electrical control and power equipment within shipbuilding components, and the company is pursuing high-spec switchboard markets through collaboration with global power equipment makers.

04

Earnings analysis

For full-year 2025, revenue reached KRW 144.4 billion, up 4.6% from KRW 138.1 billion in 2024, while operating profit rose 14.6% to KRW 17.7 billion from KRW 15.5 billion, lifting the operating margin from 11.2% to 12.3%.

In contrast, controlling-interest net profit fell 20.4% to KRW 18 billion from KRW 22.7 billion in 2024, and net income including non-controlling interests declined identically from KRW 22.7 billion to KRW 18 billion.

The divergence between rising operating profit and falling net profit appears linked to changes in income tax expense; disclosed financial data show tax expense was negative (a refund-like item) in 2024 before normalizing into a positive expense in 2025.

On the balance sheet, the debt-to-equity ratio dropped sharply from 73.8% in 2024 to 34.7% in 2025, with total equity at KRW 172.7 billion (entirely attributable to controlling interests) and total liabilities at KRW 59.9 billion at the end of 2025.

Operating cash flow eased slightly from KRW 21.1 billion in 2024 to KRW 18 billion in 2025.

By quarter, revenue was KRW 33.2 billion, operating profit KRW 2.97 billion, and controlling-interest net profit KRW 2.98 billion in the third quarter of 2025, before revenue rose 14.9% quarter-on-quarter to KRW 38.2 billion and operating profit surged 105.5% to KRW 6.1 billion in the fourth quarter.

However, in the first quarter of 2026, revenue fell 10.3% quarter-on-quarter to KRW 34.3 billion and operating profit dropped 37.7% to KRW 3.8 billion, before rebounding again in the second quarter of 2026 with revenue of KRW 41.1 billion (up 19.9%), operating profit of KRW 6.07 billion (up 59.8%), and controlling-interest net profit of KRW 5.48 billion (up 49.0%), underscoring significant quarter-to-quarter volatility.

The sum of controlling-interest net profit over the most recent four quarters (Q3 2025 through Q2 2026) was KRW 17.9 billion.

05

Industry analysis

Shipbuilding has been cited as an industry with favorable conditions since the time of listing, driven by tightening environmental regulation and rising newbuild orders; Snsys itself saw a sharp rally on its first trading day relative to the offering price.

As International Maritime Organization (IMO) environmental regulations tighten, demand continues to grow for eco-friendly equipment such as LNG and methanol dual-fuel propulsion systems and ballast water management systems (BWMS).

In a March 2026 report, Shinhan Investment Corp noted that as shipping emission regulations tighten, demand for high-spec switchboards is rising alongside Chinese shipyards' expansion of high-spec vessel construction; the report did not provide an investment rating or target price.

With major domestic shipyards continuing to win orders for high-value vessels such as LNG carriers and container ships, equipment suppliers like Snsys operate in a structure closely tied to shipyards' newbuild order cycles.

In the competitive landscape, relatively few suppliers handle electrical control and power equipment within shipbuilding components, and the expansion of the MRO-serviced fleet to more than 6,000 vessels is cited as a stable revenue base.

Still, because shipbuilding itself is linked to global shipping volumes and newbuild order cycles, any downturn in the industry could affect order intake and revenue for equipment suppliers.

06

Outlook

Since its August 2025 KOSDAQ listing, Snsys has been sequentially deploying IPO proceeds into production and R&D infrastructure.

In July 2026, the company completed the purchase of roughly 2,600 pyeong of land for the 'Geoje Ocean Center' in Yeoncho-myeon, Geoje, Gyeongnam, which is planned to serve as the core base for the eco-solutions business; the company stated it will build out facilities in phases to expand production capacity for BWMS, fuel gas supply systems (FGSS), and low-flashpoint fuel supply systems (LFSS).

Prior to this, in April 2026 the company signed an MOU with Gyeongnam Province and Geoje City to establish an eco-friendly R&D center and production base. The company is also pursuing an expansion of its second Busan plant, and in December 2025 it secured a land-based switchboard production plant in Pyeongtaek.

Overseas expansion has continued as well, with a Shanghai, China subsidiary and a Singapore logistics hub established in December 2024, followed by the establishment of an additional Nantong, China subsidiary and a Singapore subsidiary in April 2026.

On the order front, the company has secured, in cooperation with Hanwha Ocean, a control solution for an LNG dual-fuel propulsion container ship for a major global shipping line and a navigation control system for a polar research icebreaker, and reports indicate it is also expanding cooperation with Conrad Shipyard in the United States in the LNG bunkering market.

In a March 2026 report, Shinhan Investment Corp projected that the company would sustain steady growth as a total marine solutions provider through expansion into China and Japan and new business lines.

08

Bull factors

Business Diversification and Customer Spread

The four business segments—power, eco, navigation control, and MRO—contribute a relatively balanced revenue mix, and the company continues working to reduce dependence on Samsung Heavy Industries through increased orders from Hanwha Ocean and entry into China and Japan.

Expansion of the MRO-serviced fleet to over 6,000 vessels is building a more stable revenue base, while the production license agreement with ABB and collaboration with other global firms provide a platform for further expansion.

Improved Balance Sheet

The debt-to-equity ratio fell sharply from 73.8% in 2024 to 34.7% in 2025, and total equity expanded to KRW 172.7 billion.

The inflow of IPO proceeds is understood to have supported this financial stability, widening the company's capacity to self-fund future investments such as the Geoje Ocean Center and the second Busan plant.

Demand Growth from Environmental Regulation

Tightening International Maritime Organization environmental regulations continue to drive demand for eco-friendly equipment such as dual-fuel propulsion systems and ballast water management systems.

The company is expanding production capacity to meet this demand through the Geoje Ocean Center and the second Busan plant, while also building global references such as a control-solution order for a Maersk vessel.

09

Bear factors

Net Profit Diverging from Operating Profit

Despite a 14.6% rise in operating profit in 2025, controlling-interest net profit fell 20.4%, showing that operational improvement did not translate directly into shareholder earnings.

Volatility in non-operating items such as income tax expense appears to be shaking earnings quality, and quarterly results have also shown periods of quarter-on-quarter decline, as in the first quarter of 2026.

High Customer Concentration

Revenue dependence on Samsung Heavy Industries reached 63.7% in the first quarter of 2025, meaning results can be significantly affected by changes in orders from a single customer.

Efforts to broaden the customer base toward Hanwha Ocean and others are underway, but concentration has not meaningfully eased in the short term. Should shipyards' newbuild order cycles slow, order intake and revenue at equipment supplier Snsys could also be affected.

Quarter-to-Quarter Earnings Volatility

Operating profit surged 105.5% quarter-on-quarter in the fourth quarter of 2025 before falling 37.7% in the first quarter of 2026, illustrating significant quarter-to-quarter swings.

Given the project-based nature of marine equipment revenue recognition, quarterly results can be uneven, making it difficult to draw firm trend conclusions from any single quarter.

10

Risk factors

Customer and Industry Concentration Risk

Both high revenue dependence on Samsung Heavy Industries and sensitivity to shipbuilding newbuild order cycles exist simultaneously. A slowdown in orders from a specific customer or a reversal in the shipbuilding cycle could directly affect results, making it necessary to monitor the pace of customer diversification.

FX and Raw Material Price Volatility

As the company expands into overseas markets such as China and Japan and increases its export share, the earnings impact of exchange rate fluctuations could grow.

Price volatility in raw materials such as copper, needed for switchboard production, is also a factor that can affect costs, and as more overseas subsidiaries are established, managing related expenses and FX exposure becomes increasingly important.

Execution Risk on New Facility Investment

With multiple production and R&D facility investments—the Geoje Ocean Center and the second Busan plant expansion among them—proceeding simultaneously, the pace of capital execution and cost management emerge as important variables.

Delays or cost overruns in investment plans funded by IPO proceeds could weigh on profitability, and the initial utilization and profit contribution timing of newly established overseas subsidiaries also warrant monitoring.

12

Overall view

Snsys showed improvement in scale and balance-sheet metrics in 2025, with both revenue and operating profit rising and the debt-to-equity ratio falling sharply, yet controlling-interest net profit declined over the same period, sending mixed signals on earnings quality.

Quarterly results have also shown significant swings, with a sharp jump in the fourth quarter of 2025 followed by a pullback in the first quarter of 2026 and a rebound in the second quarter.

Operationally, the four segments—power, eco, navigation control, and MRO—contribute a relatively balanced revenue mix, and production and market expansion are proceeding simultaneously through the Geoje Ocean Center, the second Busan plant, and new overseas subsidiaries.

Still, high revenue dependence on Samsung Heavy Industries and sensitivity to the shipbuilding order cycle remain key variables. Investors should monitor future quarterly earnings volatility, the actual progress of customer diversification, and the pace of execution on new facility investments.
