Order Growth Versus Mixed Profit Signals at S&SYS

02

Business structure

S&SYS was spun off from the electrical and electronics division of Samsung Heavy Industries in 2017 and listed on KOSDAQ in August 2025.

Its business covers power solutions centered on switchboards for ships, eco solutions including ballast water treatment systems and fuel supply equipment, navigation-control solutions that manage vessel operations, and MRO solutions for maintenance, retrofits and repairs after installation.

Based on 2024 figures, the segment mix was 37.7% power solutions, 26.5% eco solutions, 11.5% navigation-control solutions and 22.5% MRO solutions. The IPO materials presented MRO and new business expansion as the main growth axes.

Its customers include major domestic shipyards such as Samsung Heavy Industries and Hanwha Ocean, and the company is extending supply to Japanese and Chinese shipyards.

The company was once heavily dependent on Samsung Heavy Industries, but rising orders from Hanwha Ocean have been described as balancing the customer mix. A April 2026 report said the company supplies most vessel types except LNG carriers.

Its ballast water treatment system has completed IMO and US Coast Guard certification, which is cited as a barrier to entry. In navigation control, the company is described as the only domestic firm to have commercialized integrated control systems for oil tankers, container ships and LNG carriers. Its scope is also widening into land-based switchboards and naval vessel control systems.

04

Earnings analysis

On a disclosed annual basis, revenue rose from KRW 138.1 billion in 2024 to KRW 144.4 billion in 2025, up 4.6% year on year. Operating profit grew 14.6% from KRW 15.5 billion to KRW 17.7 billion, and the operating margin moved from 11.2% to 12.3%.

Over the same period, net profit attributable to owners fell 20.4% from KRW 22.7 billion to KRW 18 billion, and net profit including non-controlling interests also declined from KRW 22.7 billion to KRW 18 billion. Profitability improved at the operating line but weakened at the net profit line.

Operating cash flow fell from KRW 21.1 billion to KRW 18 billion. The balance sheet strengthened, with the debt ratio falling from 73.8% in 2024 to 34.7% in 2025. At the end of 2025, total liabilities were KRW 59.9 billion and total equity was KRW 172.7 billion, all of it attributable to owners of the parent.

On a quarterly basis, third-quarter 2025 revenue was KRW 33.2 billion with operating profit of KRW 2.97 billion and net profit attributable to owners of KRW 2.98 billion.

Fourth-quarter 2025 revenue rose to KRW 38.2 billion, up 14.9% from the prior quarter, with operating profit of KRW 6.1 billion and net profit attributable to owners of KRW 5.77 billion. First-quarter 2026 revenue was KRW 34.3 billion, down 10.3% quarter on quarter, with operating profit of KRW 3.8 billion, down 37.7%.

Second-quarter 2026 revenue recovered to KRW 41.1 billion, with operating profit of KRW 6.07 billion and net profit attributable to owners of KRW 5.48 billion. Third-quarter 2026 revenue was KRW 40.4 billion, down 1.8% quarter on quarter but up 21.5% year on year.

Operating profit of KRW 3.05 billion rose 2.9% year on year, while net profit attributable to owners of KRW 1.79 billion fell 39.9% year on year. Operating profit rose while net profit attributable to owners fell in the same quarter, so non-operating items and income tax should be checked in the annual report notes.

In its third-quarter 2025 results, the company said orders had grown but that earnings lag orders by one to two years or more.

05

Industry analysis

Demand for marine equipment is influenced by orders for LNG carriers and eco-friendly vessels. Reports have noted that growing LNG and eco-friendly vessel orders are lifting related equipment suppliers.

A December 2025 Shinhan Investment report said expanding demand for small and mid-sized vessels from Chinese shipyards could benefit order and construction volumes.

A report indicated LNG carriers accounted for roughly 50 to 55% of the company's sales by vessel type, though that figure dates from around December 2025, so the latest mix should be checked in the annual report.

China has been described as accounting for 60 to 70% of global ship construction, and a May 2026 report said China holds about 69% of global shipbuilding order backlog on a DWT basis.

Against this backdrop, S&SYS announced in May 2026 a production company in Nantong, China, and said it would expand supply to small and mid-sized vessels. Among competitors, the company is described as a domestic integrated solution provider supplying major shipyards such as Samsung Heavy Industries and Hanwha Ocean.

The equipment sector has been criticized for hardware-centric supply and price competition, and the company has said it aims to shift toward equipment combining software and hardware. In April 2026, a Korea Investment & Securities analyst said order results tied to Hanwha Ocean had roughly doubled year on year.

The next variables for the sector are whether the shipbuilding upcycle continues and how China's shipyard volumes are allocated.

06

Outlook

The company's medium-term plans emphasize production capacity and overseas footprint.

Its 2026 corporate value enhancement plan, disclosed in March, includes expanding the Busan Eco 2 plant to 1.5 times existing capacity, a new Geoje Marine Center for eco-friendly fuel systems and MRO, a Nantong production and after-sales company in China, and a Singapore MRO, after-sales and sales company.

As of an April 2026 report, the second Busan plant was expected to be completed between late 2026 and early 2027, and the Geoje Marine Center targets completion in 2027. The Nantong production company targeted starting production in the fourth quarter of 2026.

The Singapore company began local operations in June 2026 and held its opening ceremony on October 28, 2026. On the order side, the company supplies navigation-control solutions for MR tankers ordered by Hanwha Shipping and built at the Hanwha Philly Shipyard.

The contract covers four firm vessels plus six options, up to ten in total, with deliveries scheduled through March 2028. In April 2026, a supply contract of about KRW 9.9 billion with Samsung Heavy Industries for an LNG carrier equipment package was disclosed.

The company said it continues sales activity related to the MASGA project and expects additional orders. At an extraordinary shareholders meeting in October 2026, a proposal to add brokerage and repair businesses to the corporate purpose was approved.

The next checkpoints are whether the second Busan plant is completed, whether the Nantong company starts production, the Geoje Marine Center schedule, and new order disclosures.

08

Bull factors

Order flow and capacity expansion

In April 2026, a supply contract of about KRW 9.9 billion with Samsung Heavy Industries for an LNG carrier equipment package was disclosed. The MR tanker navigation-control supply for the Hanwha Philly Shipyard covers four firm vessels plus six options, up to ten in total.

The company plans to expand the Busan Eco 2 plant to 1.5 times existing capacity, which is cited as support for handling higher order volumes.

Growing MRO share and profitability

MRO solutions cover maintenance, parts sales and retrofits for installed equipment, so revenue can continue after new-build orders. MRO accounted for 22.5% of the mix based on 2024 figures, and during the listing process higher-margin MRO sales were cited as driving growth. The Singapore company's opening is also cited as a way to expand MRO and retrofit sales in Southeast Asia.

New businesses and overseas footprint

The company is extending land-based switchboards into data centers, semiconductors and bio facilities, and says its integrated naval control platform has been applied to the DDH-I retrofit and Batch-IV new builds.

The Nantong production company in China and the Singapore facility are building an overseas production and service footprint. If the new businesses reduce swings in shipbuilding orders, that would support earnings stability.

Bear factors

Profit declines and cash flow

Net profit attributable to owners fell 20.4% in 2025, and operating cash flow declined from KRW 21.1 billion to KRW 18 billion. Net profit attributable to owners also fell 39.9% year on year in the third quarter of 2026, moving in the opposite direction to operating profit.

Over the last four quarters, the cumulative total of KRW 16.7 billion reflects large swings, which is cited as a bearish point.

Customer and vessel-type concentration

The company was historically heavily dependent on Samsung Heavy Industries, and rising orders from Hanwha Ocean are described as rebalancing the customer mix. However, its results remain tied to LNG carrier order flows.

Heavy dependence on specific customers and vessel types means shipyard scheduling changes could translate directly into revenue swings.

Share supply and reliance on expectations

A March 17, 2026 off-market block trade reduced the holdings of the company's president by 19,680 shares. In April 2026, the share price moved sharply on expectations tied to the MASGA project, and reports said the price trend would depend on whether additional orders expand. If expectations have already been priced in, a delay in new orders could weigh on the shares.

Risk factors

Customer concentration and order dependence

Revenue is tied to a small number of shipbuilders, including Samsung Heavy Industries and Hanwha Ocean, so changes in their order schedules can flow directly into results. New orders are disclosed, but there is a lag before they are recognized as revenue and profit, which makes quarterly results hard to forecast. The company itself said earnings lag orders by one to two years or more.

Capacity build-out and overseas execution

As of an April 2026 report, the second Busan plant was expected to be completed between late 2026 and early 2027, and the Geoje Marine Center targets 2027. Whether the Nantong production company actually starts production is the checkpoint for overseas execution. If facility completion or start-up slips, order fulfillment and fixed-cost absorption could be affected.

New business progress and earnings volatility

Many new businesses, including land-based switchboards, naval control systems and dual-fuel systems, remain at the planning or early stage, so the timing of revenue contribution is uncertain.

Operating profit rose in 2025 while net profit attributable to owners fell, and the same pattern appeared in the third quarter of 2026. Whether below-the-line items and income tax factors persist will be key.

Overall view

S&SYS offers power, eco, navigation-control and MRO solutions for shipbuilding, and disclosed revenue grew from KRW 138.1 billion in 2024 to KRW 144.4 billion in 2025. Operating profit also rose from KRW 15.5 billion to KRW 17.7 billion, confirming improvement at the operating line.

However, net profit attributable to owners fell from KRW 22.7 billion to KRW 18 billion, and it declined 39.9% year on year in the third quarter of 2026, so profit trends diverge by line. The balance sheet improved, with the debt ratio falling from 73.8% to 34.7%.

On orders, a Samsung Heavy Industries LNG carrier supply contract and a Hanwha Philly Shipyard MR tanker navigation-control contract stand out, alongside production and overseas expansion.

Bullish factors include the order backlog, capacity expansion and the rising MRO share, while bearish factors include customer and vessel-type concentration, earnings volatility, and execution risks in facilities and overseas subsidiaries.

The next checkpoints are completion of the second Busan plant, the start of Nantong production, and the net profit trend in the 2026 annual report.
