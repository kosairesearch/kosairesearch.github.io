Cement Slump, Leisure Pivot Drive Swing to Profit

Kangdong C&L expanded into the leisure business in July 2024 by acquiring a golf course, condominium and water park complex in Gyeongju, posting KRW 71.8 billion in revenue and KRW 7.4 billion in operating profit for 2025, though the financing burden from that deal has made quarterly earnings increasingly volatile.

02

Business structure

Kangdong C&L was established in 2014 and listed on KOSDAQ the same year, built on the Portland cement production facilities of its predecessor, Korea Cement.

Its cement segment is the sole Portland cement producer in the Honam region, supplying intermediate goods to construction firms while minimizing logistics costs through a localized sales strategy.

Raw materials such as bituminous coal, granulated slag and gypsum are sourced from suppliers including Taihwa Holdings, Ssangyong C&E and Daehan Cement. The company's domestic cement market share stood at about 1.2% as of 2025, positioning it as a regional player relative to nationwide major producers.

In July 2024 the company entered the leisure business by acquiring a golf course, condominium and water park complex in Gyeongju from Blueone, a Taeyoung Group affiliate, subsequently renaming the site from Blueone to Kangdong.

The move reflects an effort to diversify the portfolio using a relatively sound balance sheet even as the core cement business continued to underperform. Based on the most recent disclosed breakdown, cement accounted for 57.73% of revenue and leisure 42.27%, making the two businesses roughly comparable in scale.

In January 2025 the largest shareholder changed from an individual to an affiliate, Kangdong Remicon HS, with another affiliate, Gangdong Ascon, also holding a significant stake.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual revenue declined from KRW 69.9 billion in 2022 to KRW 50.5 billion in 2023 (down 27.7% year-on-year) and KRW 47.4 billion in 2024 (down 6.2%), before rebounding to KRW 71.8 billion in 2025 (up 51.6%), the largest figure in the 2022-2025 period.

Operating results moved from a loss of KRW 12.6 billion in 2022 and a wider loss of KRW 17.2 billion in 2023 to a swing to profit of KRW 5.55 billion in 2024 and a further 33.4% increase to KRW 7.4 billion in 2025.

The operating margin likewise recovered from -18.1% in 2022 and -34.1% in 2023 to 11.7% in 2024 and 10.3% in 2025.

Net profit attributable to owners moved from losses of KRW 7.76 billion in 2022 and a wider KRW 15.8 billion in 2023, to a narrower loss of KRW 7.71 billion in 2024, before swinging to a profit of KRW 656 million in 2025 - a much smaller figure than the KRW 7.4 billion operating profit, reflecting financing costs tied to the leisure acquisition.

On a quarterly basis, revenue rose from KRW 19.3 billion in Q2 2025 to KRW 21.7 billion in Q3 2025 (up 12.4% quarter-on-quarter), then fell to KRW 19.3 billion in Q4 2025 (down 11.1%) and KRW 12.5 billion in Q1 2026 (down 35.0%), before rebounding to KRW 19.7 billion in Q2 2026 (up 56.7% quarter-on-quarter, up 1.8% year-on-year).

Operating profit eased from KRW 2.84 billion in Q2 2025 to KRW 2.7 billion in Q3 2025 (down 5.1%) and KRW 2.68 billion in Q4 2025 (down 0.4%), then swung to a loss of KRW 786 million in Q1 2026 before returning to a profit of KRW 2.46 billion in Q2 2026 (up quarter-on-quarter, down 13.5% year-on-year).

Net profit attributable to owners fell from KRW 1.53 billion in Q2 2025 to KRW 857 million in Q3 2025 (down 44.0%) and KRW 184 million in Q4 2025 (down 78.5%), swung to a loss of KRW 1.99 billion in Q1 2026, then returned to a profit of KRW 1.62 billion in Q2 2026 (up 6.2% year-on-year).

The sum of net profit attributable to owners over the latest four quarters (Q3 2025 through Q2 2026) was KRW 678 million, illustrating how much quarterly swings drive the cumulative annual figure.

Operating cash flow improved from -KRW 13.2 billion in 2022 to KRW 2.67 billion in 2023 and KRW 16 billion in 2024, before easing back to KRW 3.91 billion in 2025, suggesting cash generation has become more uneven since the leisure business was consolidated.

05

Industry analysis

Korea's cement industry has seen domestic shipments fall to a 34-year low as of 2024 amid a prolonged construction downturn, with a similar level expected again this year.

Capacity utilization at major plants has dropped below 60% in recent years, and because the industry is capital-intensive with heavy fixed costs, demand declines directly hurt profitability.

Compounding this, the fourth phase of the emissions trading scheme starting in 2026 cuts the average annual emissions allowance by 16.4% versus the third phase, adding to the burden of carbon-reduction investment.

Still, leading indicators have improved, with building-permit floor area in the first half of 2026 up 9% year-on-year and housing starts up 14.4%, fueling industry expectations that demand will gradually recover from 2027 as the government expands housing supply in the greater Seoul area and infrastructure investment for semiconductor fabs and AI data centers picks up.

Industry sources expect shipment volumes to begin a gradual rebound from the fourth quarter of this year, though rising electricity rates and logistics costs mean a clear earnings recovery is still difficult to call.

Some larger cement producers have already grown operating profit despite falling sales volumes through cost control, suggesting cost management remains the key differentiator within the sector until demand turns.

Kangdong C&L, with a domestic cement market share of only about 1.2%, is a regionally concentrated smaller player exposed to the same industry sensitivities as nationwide majors, though its move into the leisure business gives it a differentiated revenue structure within the sector.

Outlook

In March 2026, the company revised its co-CEO structure, newly appointing vice president Park Eul-seong as co-CEO while incumbent co-CEO Lee Hoon-chang remained in place; the new CEO previously served as production and administration division head at Korea Cement, suggesting an emphasis on strengthening core operations.

The cement segment could see a gradual rebound in shipment volumes from 2027 in line with broader industry demand-recovery expectations, though rising electricity and logistics costs alongside growing carbon-reduction investment needs make a clear near-term profitability improvement premature to assume.

The leisure segment, which operates a golf course, condominium and water park complex, is subject to significant seasonal demand swings, and the shift from an operating loss in Q1 2026 to a profit in Q2 2026 may partly reflect the onset of the peak season.

The debt-to-equity ratio tied to acquisition financing has remained elevated at 227.6% in 2024 and 218.3% in 2025, leaving interest-expense management and its effect on net profit attributable to owners as a key area to watch going forward.

No specific multi-year revenue or profit guidance from the company has been publicly confirmed to date, meaning quarterly disclosures and industry indicators remain the main gauges of earnings direction.

With limited pricing power across the cement industry broadly, whether stable leisure segment operations can cushion overall earnings volatility is a point to monitor in coming quarters.

Bull factors

Core Business Turnaround and Diversification

Operating profit swung to a gain in 2024 and grew a further 33.4% in 2025, extending a two-year run of operating profitability. The 2024 entry into the leisure business also broadened the revenue base beyond cement alone, which could reduce reliance on any single industry downturn. As of 2025, revenue is roughly split between cement and leisure, an early sign of diversification taking hold.

Sole Cement Producer Status in Honam

Kangdong C&L is the sole Portland cement producer in the Honam region, benefiting from a localized sales strategy that minimizes logistics costs. This structural advantage allows it to avoid direct logistics competition with nationwide major rivals.

The regionally concentrated supply chain can serve as a relative cost advantage even in an environment where price pass-through is difficult.

Improving Construction Leading Indicators

Construction leading indicators improved in the first half of 2026, with building-permit floor area and housing starts up 9% and 14.4% year-on-year, respectively. The industry is watching whether this improvement in starts translates into a cement shipment rebound from the fourth quarter of 2026 onward.

There is also an expectation that demand recovery could broaden from 2027 as housing supply expansion in the greater Seoul area and infrastructure investment for semiconductors and data centers accelerate.

Bear factors

Elevated Leverage and Interest Expense

The debt-to-equity ratio has remained markedly elevated at 227.6% in 2024 and 218.3% in 2025, well above the roughly 25% levels seen before the leisure acquisition in 2022-2023.

The gap between the KRW 7.4 billion operating profit and the much smaller KRW 656 million net profit attributable to owners in 2025 suggests this financial structure burden carries through to the bottom line.

Absent changes to the interest-rate environment or borrowing structure, interest expense could continue to constrain earnings capacity.

Structural Decline in Domestic Cement Demand

Domestic cement shipments fell to their lowest level in 34 years as of 2024, with capacity utilization at major plants dropping below 60%. Rising electricity rates and growing carbon-reduction investment needs are compounding cost pressure across the industry.

Given that Kangdong C&L holds only about a 1.2% domestic market share as a smaller player, a delayed industry rebound could weigh relatively more heavily on its financial resilience.

Quarterly Earnings Volatility and Trading Alerts

Quarterly results have swung sharply, with an operating loss of KRW 786 million and a net loss attributable to owners of KRW 1.99 billion in Q1 2026 followed by a return to profit in Q2 2026.

In June and July of the same year, the stock was repeatedly designated an investment-caution or investment-warning issue by the Korea Exchange following sharp price moves.

This volatility makes it difficult to judge an earnings trend from any single quarter and raises the risk of price swings driven by short-term trading flows.

Risk factors

Financial and Liquidity Risk

Total liabilities stood at KRW 293.5 billion at the end of 2025, far exceeding total equity of KRW 134.4 billion, pushing the debt-to-equity ratio to 218.3%.

Borrowing for the leisure acquisition is presumed to be the main driver, and in a rising-rate environment interest expense could offset gains from improved operating profit. Operating cash flow falling from KRW 16 billion in 2024 to KRW 3.91 billion in 2025 also points to variability in cash generation.

Industry and Regulatory Risk

A prolonged construction downturn has pushed domestic cement shipments to their lowest level in 34 years, and the fourth-phase emissions trading scheme starting in 2026 cuts the average annual emissions allowance by 16.4% versus the third phase, adding to carbon-reduction investment burdens.

Given the domestic-demand-driven structure of the industry, where price pass-through is difficult, rising electricity and logistics costs may not be fully reflected in selling prices. The leisure segment also faces significant seasonal demand swings that can affect the stability of quarterly results.

Trading and Supply-Demand Risk

The stock was repeatedly designated an investment-caution or investment-warning issue by the exchange in both June and July 2026 following sharp price surges, a history that points to elevated short-term trading volatility.

As a smaller-cap stock, trading volume and value have tended to concentrate in particular periods, which can widen price swings. A relatively concentrated ownership structure among the largest shareholder and related parties also limits free float, which can be a factor in volatility.

Overall view

Kangdong C&L has shifted to a structure where its core cement business - built on its status as the sole Portland cement producer in the Honam region - and the leisure business newly added in 2024 contribute roughly comparable shares of revenue.

The swing to operating profit in 2024-2025 and to net profit attributable to owners in 2025 are positive developments, but the rise in the debt-to-equity ratio and interest expense tied to the leisure acquisition, which sharply narrowed net profit relative to operating profit, remains a financial-structure challenge.

Through 2026, results have swung between a Q1 loss and a Q2 profit, making it difficult to draw a firm annual trend from any single quarter.

The cement industry has passed through a 34-year shipment low, with a gradual rebound possibly emerging from the fourth quarter of 2026, though rising electricity and carbon-reduction costs leave the pace of recovery uncertain.

The leisure segment's significant seasonal demand swings represent an additional source of quarterly variability.

The stock's repeated designation as an investment-caution or warning issue following sharp price moves in 2026 points to notable trading-side volatility, making it important to track upcoming quarterly disclosures together with industry indicators.
