02

Business structure

Kangdong C&L is a cement manufacturer and seller that listed on KOSDAQ in 2014.

The company runs a cement segment and a leisure segment that operates golf and condominium facilities, and it describes itself as the only Portland cement producer in the Honam region, selling under the Goryeo Cement brand since production began in 1973.

A revenue breakdown compiled from disclosures (Bosoop, 2025) shows product supply at 47.8%, leisure services at 47.6%, rental and other at 4.0%, and merchandise at 0.7%.

Because the two main businesses split revenue almost evenly, the share that leisure contributes to KRW 71.8 billion of 2025 revenue is a key reference for how much it cushions the cement cycle. Cement sales are recorded entirely in the domestic market (WISEreport, December 2025).

Ongoing research projects include clinker and cement quality improvement, mineral admixture technology, and ready-mix quality. Investment media reports that cement selling prices, bituminous coal and power costs, and regional construction starts drive profitability.

The company positions its Jeolla-region location as a competitive strength (FnGuide). The company attributed the main drivers of 2025 results to profitability gains and diversification from acquiring a new production site (Digital Today).

A co-CEO change took place in March 2026, and the largest shareholder group held 68.63% as of August 31, 2026 (WISEreport).

04

Earnings analysis

Annual revenue fell from KRW 69.9 billion in 2022 to KRW 50.5 billion in 2023 (down 27.7% year on year) and then to KRW 47.4 billion in 2024 (down 6.2%), which makes 2024 the smallest year of 2022–2025. Revenue rose to KRW 71.8 billion in 2025, up 51.6%.

The company posted operating losses of KRW 12.6 billion in 2022 and KRW 17.2 billion in 2023 before turning to operating profit of KRW 5.55 billion in 2024. Operating profit then reached KRW 7.4 billion in 2025, up 33.4%.

The operating margin moved from -18.1% in 2022 and -34.1% in 2023 to 11.7% in 2024 and 10.3% in 2025. Profit attributable to owners of the parent went from net losses of KRW 7.76 billion in 2022 and KRW 15.8 billion in 2023 to a net loss of KRW 7.71 billion in 2024, then turned positive at KRW 656 million in 2025.

Operating cash flow fell from KRW 16 billion in 2024 to KRW 3.91 billion in 2025. The debt ratio jumped from 24.3% in 2023 to 227.6% in 2024 and was 218.3% in 2025, and FnGuide data show total assets rising from KRW 114.6 billion in 2023 to KRW 439.9 billion in 2024.

Quarterly revenue bottomed at KRW 12.5 billion in Q1 2026, then rose to KRW 19.7 billion in Q2 and KRW 20.9 billion in Q3, two straight quarters of gains. Profit attributable to owners of the parent improved from a loss of KRW 1.99 billion in Q1 2026 to KRW 1.62 billion in Q2 and KRW 4.25 billion in Q3.

Q3 operating profit of KRW 1.98 billion, however, fell 26.5% year on year, so operating profit and net profit moved in different directions. The sum of profit attributable to owners of the parent over the last four quarters is KRW 407 million.

05

Industry analysis

The cement industry is centered on domestic demand, and WISEreport's June 2026 company commentary points to a slowdown in construction investment and uncertainty around interest-rate policy. The same commentary says cost pressure has weighed on industry profitability and kept the company's operating losses going.

On the other side, it expects construction investment to recover modestly, helped by government measures to expand housing supply. Investment media identify cement selling prices, bituminous coal and power costs, and regional construction starts as the main drivers of earnings.

Because energy and power are a large part of production costs, fluctuations in coal and power prices can flow through quarterly results quickly. The company's production base is the only Portland cement plant in the Honam region, so its volumes are closely tied to regional construction activity.

Its leisure business adds a second earnings base, and the 2025 acquisition of a new site was described as part of that diversification (Digital Today).

Bull factors

Return to profit in 2025 and a rebound in 2026 quarterly revenue

Operating profit rose 33.4% to KRW 7.4 billion in 2025, with the operating margin at 10.3%. Revenue bottomed at KRW 12.5 billion in Q1 2026 and then rose to KRW 19.7 billion in Q2 and KRW 20.9 billion in Q3, two straight quarterly gains. Profit attributable to owners of the parent reached KRW 4.25 billion in Q3 2026, up 395.9% year on year.

Leisure business as a second earnings base beside cement

Leisure services accounted for 47.6% of 2025 revenue in a breakdown compiled by Bosoop. Leisure income can support earnings when cement demand is weak, which forms the basis of the bullish case.

The company described the 2025 acquisition of a new site as part of its diversification, which broadens the earnings base beyond cement alone.

Expected modest recovery in construction investment from housing supply measures

WISEreport notes that government measures to expand housing supply are expected to lift construction investment modestly. Cement is a domestic product tied directly to construction starts, so a pickup in starts would be favorable for volumes. The company also cited stronger customer technical support and expanded environmental facility investment.

Bear factors

Lower Q3 2026 operating profit after a Q1 operating loss

Q1 2026 swung to an operating loss of KRW 786 million, and Q3 operating profit of KRW 1.98 billion fell 19.4% from Q2 and 26.5% from a year earlier. The slowdown in operating profit came even as profit attributable to owners of the parent rose, so the quality of earnings is a point for the next disclosures.

High leverage and slower operating cash flow

Total liabilities of KRW 293.5 billion at the end of 2025 exceed total equity of KRW 134.4 billion, leaving a 218.3% debt ratio. The ratio jumped to 227.6% in 2024 and has stayed at a high level, which keeps financial leverage elevated.

Operating cash flow fell from KRW 16 billion in 2024 to KRW 3.91 billion in 2025, which weakens the link between profit and cash generation.

Domestic-only cement exposure and cost pressure

Because cement is sold only in the domestic market, the company is directly exposed to slower construction investment and uncertain interest-rate policy. WISEreport says cost pressure has weighed on profitability and kept operating losses going.

Variables such as selling prices, coal and power costs, and regional construction starts affect earnings, so how much cost increases can be passed on to customers is the key bearish question.

Overall view

Kangdong C&L moved out of its losses after 2022, with 2025 operating profit of KRW 7.4 billion and a 10.3% operating margin, and revenue rose in both Q2 and Q3 2026.

Still, Q3 2026 operating profit fell 26.5% year on year, and profit attributable to owners of the parent totaled KRW 407 million over the last four quarters.

Total liabilities of KRW 293.5 billion against total equity of KRW 134.4 billion mean the balance sheet burden eases only if profit recovery and cash generation are confirmed together.

On the business side, the domestic-only cement base and the leisure business coexist, and a recovery in construction investment from housing measures is a bullish factor. Cost pressure and a slowdown in construction remain bearish factors.

The next checkpoints are the preliminary Q4 and full-year 2026 results and the full-year operating margin and debt ratio in the 2026 annual report.
