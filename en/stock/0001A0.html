02

Business structure

Deokyang Energen refines and reforms by-product hydrogen generated at petrochemical and chlor-alkali (CA) processes in the Yeosu, Gunsan and Ulsan industrial complexes into high-purity industrial hydrogen at 99.99% (4N) purity.

Its revenue is centered on pipeline-based direct supply, and as of the nine months through Q3 2025, the segment mix stood at roughly 83.6% pipeline, 11.7% tube trailer, 4.4% construction and services (EPC), and 0.2% rental and equipment.

Major customers are large-group affiliates in the core Yeosu and Gunsan petrochemical complexes, with the company also expanding its supplier and customer base into the Ulsan region.

In terms of competitive positioning, the company has differentiated itself by securing specialty, CA-process by-product hydrogen sources in addition to naphtha-cracking (NCC) by-product hydrogen, which it says provides a comparatively stable hydrogen supply independent of petrochemical industry cycles.

The centerpiece of its growth strategy is K&D Energen, a 50:50 joint venture with Keukdong Oil & Chemical, which serves as the exclusive hydrogen supplier to S-OIL's large-scale Shaheen petrochemical expansion project in the Ulsan Onsan area.

In 2025 the company brought Min Company, a hydrogen-plant construction specialist, under full ownership to build EPC capability and large-project references, and in April 2026 it pursued the acquisition of DKME to expand its capacity to address aging hydrogen plant replacement demand.

Over the medium to long term, the company has outlined plans to expand beyond its current gray-hydrogen-centered business into blue ammonia and ammonia-cracking-based clean hydrogen production.

This structure reflects a combination of stable traditional industrial gas supply and growth potential tied to new large-scale projects.

04

Earnings analysis

Full-year 2025 revenue came to KRW 143.2 billion, up 4.2% from KRW 137.4 billion in 2024, while operating profit rose 10.9% to KRW 6.68 billion from KRW 6.02 billion. Net income attributable to owners increased 49.3% to KRW 4.52 billion from KRW 3.02 billion, with profit growth far outpacing revenue growth.

Reports attributed this gap partly to higher net income resulting from the consolidation of a subsidiary. The operating margin edged up from 4.4% in 2024 to 4.7% in 2025.

Over the same period, the debt-to-equity ratio rose sharply from 93.7% to 137.3%, reflecting the financial burden of capital injections into joint venture K&D Energen.

Total equity at year-end 2025 stood at KRW 42.5 billion (entirely attributable to owners) against total liabilities of KRW 58.3 billion, while operating cash flow slipped slightly from KRW 8.51 billion in 2024 to KRW 7.77 billion in 2025.

On a quarterly basis, Q1 2026 revenue was KRW 37 billion with operating profit of KRW 1.39 billion and net income attributable to owners of KRW 885 million, while Q2 2026 revenue fell 4.5% quarter on quarter to KRW 35.3 billion even as operating profit jumped 129.1% to KRW 3.19 billion, yet net income attributable to owners dropped 47.8% to KRW 462 million, a notable divergence in direction.

The sum of net income attributable to owners across the four quarters from Q1 to Q4 2025 reconciled exactly with the annual disclosed figure of KRW 4.52 billion, but the fact that net income declined in Q2 2026 even as operating profit rose points to possible swings in non-operating or equity-method items that warrant a closer look in the next quarterly filing.

05

Industry analysis

The industrial hydrogen market is viewed as a structurally growing segment, supported by demand from downstream industries such as semiconductors, petrochemicals and steel, as well as the government's hydrogen economy promotion policy.

That said, the upstream business of refining and supplying by-product hydrogen is characterized by a small number of players and difficulty in storage, meaning production volumes translate almost directly into supply volumes.

Deokyang Energen is seen as having strengthened its supply stability relative to peers by securing CA-process by-product hydrogen sources, which are relatively less sensitive to petrochemical industry conditions, in addition to NCC-process by-product hydrogen.

At the same time, Korea's petrochemical industry is entering a phase of active restructuring, having submitted business realignment plans to the government that reportedly include the closure of aging naphtha-cracking facilities, raising the possibility of reduced supply from traditional NCC-linked by-product hydrogen sources.

In contrast, the Shaheen Project, on which S-OIL is investing roughly KRW 9.258 trillion in the Ulsan Onsan industrial complex, is set to be the world's first commercial application of the TC2C process that converts crude oil directly into petrochemical feedstock, and will have large-scale production capacity of 1.8 million tons of ethylene and 770,000 tons of propylene annually once operational.

The Shaheen Project reportedly completed mechanical construction in the first half of 2026 and is heading toward commercial operation in the second half, with Deokyang Energen's joint venture supplying the large hydrogen volumes the facility requires.

Industry observers note that, given the oligopolistic structure of the hydrogen supply business, a successful ramp-up of the Shaheen Project could further expand the market influence of the exclusive supplier.

06

Outlook

The company's medium-term earnings visibility is likely to hinge heavily on the actual progress of the Shaheen Project.

Joint venture K&D Energen began construction of a 92,000 Nm3/hr facility in June 2024, and has stated plans to complete installation by April 2026, conduct trial operations in July, and begin full-scale hydrogen supply in the second half of 2026.

These, however, are facility-side target dates, and the actual start of commercial operation and stabilization of initial supply volumes will need to be confirmed through subsequent quarterly filings.

Related revenue is first recognized as hydrogen sales at K&D Energen before being reflected in Deokyang Energen's results as equity-method income according to its 50% ownership stake, with an impact on earnings expected to begin in 2027.

The company is also pursuing the construction of hydrogen shipping centers in Ulsan and Daesan to expand its distribution network into the Yeongnam and metropolitan regions, reportedly targeting completion in the second half of 2026.

Separately, in April 2026 the company pursued the acquisition of DKME to build EPC capability addressing demand for replacing aging hydrogen plants, with one brokerage analyst noting that the key to the company's earnings visibility ultimately lies in Shaheen Project progress and that earnings momentum would likely strengthen around the time of trial operations.

Since around the time of listing, brokerage commentary has pointed to the possibility of sharp net income growth from the Shaheen Project and medium-term earnings improvement from new hydrogen plant orders.

It has also been noted that, should new hydrogen plant orders materialize, a separate growth track could emerge with plant completion in 2028 and related revenue beginning in 2029.

08

Bull factors

Long-Term Supply Contract Tied to Shaheen

Joint venture K&D Energen holds a 15-year exclusive hydrogen supply contract for S-OIL's Shaheen Project, which could become a stable long-term revenue base once the facility reaches normal operation.

Related gains are expected to be reflected as equity-method income starting in 2027, representing growth potential not yet captured in current results.

Given the oligopolistic structure of the hydrogen industry, a successful large-project ramp-up has been cited as a factor that could further strengthen the supplier's position.

Feedstock Diversification Independent of Cycle

In addition to NCC-process by-product hydrogen, the company has secured relatively lower-volatility CA-process by-product hydrogen sources, pursuing supply stability independent of petrochemical industry conditions.

It has also built EPC capability targeting aging hydrogen facility replacement demand through the full consolidation of Min Company and the pursuit of the DKME acquisition. This dual diversification of supply sources and business model is expected to reduce sensitivity to downstream industry cycles.

Simultaneous Revenue and Profit Growth

In 2025, revenue, operating profit and net income attributable to owners all increased year on year, with the operating margin also improving slightly. Operating cash flow has remained consistently positive, indicating relatively stable cash generation from the existing business.

Until new projects begin contributing meaningfully, the existing pipeline supply business is likely to continue serving as the earnings backbone.

Bear factors

Weaker Balance Sheet from JV Funding Obligations

Step-by-step capital injections into K&D Energen pushed the debt-to-equity ratio sharply higher from 93.7% in 2024 to 137.3% in 2025. Additional capital contribution obligations remain outstanding, which could continue to weigh on the balance sheet for some time.

Liquidity indicators are also reported to have deteriorated over the same period, making execution of the company's funding plans worth monitoring.

Diverging Operating Profit and Net Income

In Q2 2026, operating profit rose 129.1% quarter on quarter while net income attributable to owners fell 47.8% in the opposite direction.

Non-operating costs related to preparations for the new joint venture's start-up or equity-method items have been suggested as possible factors, but the details will need to be confirmed in subsequent quarterly filings.

Whether this divergence proves temporary or structural could affect how the quality of future earnings is assessed.

IPO Pricing Controversy and Share Price Volatility

At the time of listing, concerns were raised about overvaluation tied to the choice of comparable companies and the valuation methodology used, and volatility has persisted since the stock posted a sharp first-day gain over the offering price.

Supply-demand volatility typical of newly listed stocks, along with the schedule for expanding freely tradable share volume, are also cited as variables that could affect the share price. Such volatility is a separate consideration from business fundamentals when forming an investment judgment.

Risk factors

Project Execution Risk

Given it is a newly built facility, the Shaheen Project may take time to stabilize yield and supply, and any delay in commercial operation would also push back the timing of equity-method income recognition for Deokyang Energen.

Industry participants have cited uninterrupted operation and the ability to absorb output volumes as key variables until the facility reaches full capacity. As the company's dependence on this single large project increases, ongoing monitoring of its progress is warranted.

Petrochemical Industry Restructuring Risk

Korea's petrochemical industry is advancing restructuring by submitting business realignment plans to the government, reportedly including the closure of aging naphtha-cracking facilities. This could alter the traditional supply-and-demand structure for NCC-linked by-product hydrogen.

The Shaheen Project itself is also tied to industry debate over output cuts once it begins operating, making it worth watching for broader changes across the value chain.

Safety and Operational Risk

Hydrogen production and refining facilities inherently carry the risk of fire or explosion given the high-pressure, flammable materials involved, and the company's Yeosu plant has experienced related incidents in the past.

Safety incidents can lead to operational shutdowns or additional costs, making this an operational risk that requires ongoing management. As facility expansion continues, it will also be worth confirming whether safety management systems are being strengthened accordingly.

Overall view

Deokyang Energen is an industrial hydrogen specialist built on by-product hydrogen refining and supply, having posted higher revenue and profit across the board in 2025, with its long-term hydrogen supply contract for S-OIL's Shaheen Project through a joint venture standing as the centerpiece of its medium-to-long-term growth story.

However, equity-method gains tied to that project are expected to begin only in 2027, meaning they have not yet meaningfully contributed to current results.

Step-by-step capital injections into the joint venture have pushed the debt-to-equity ratio sharply higher, and Q2 2026 saw operating profit rise even as net income attributable to owners fell, leaving questions about earnings quality still to be resolved.

The listing process was accompanied by controversy over the IPO pricing methodology, and the share price has shown considerable volatility since listing.

Going forward, the key points to watch will be the actual start of Shaheen Project operations, the stabilization of hydrogen supply, and whether the related equity-method gains materialize as expected.

The pace of balance sheet improvement and the outcome of any new hydrogen plant order wins also warrant continued attention.
