Report overview

Fee income and shareholder returns broaden KB Financial's earnings base

KB Financial Group's shareholder net income reached a record level in 2025, and in 2026 fee income and large-scale shareholder returns are broadening its earnings base. Operating income, however, has declined for two straight quarters since its 1Q 2026 peak, so the direction of earnings momentum needs confirmation in upcoming results.

02

Business structure

KB Financial Group is a financial holding company that centers on banking and also operates securities, card and insurance subsidiaries. Its core banking subsidiary, the banking arm KB Kookmin Bank, posted cumulative net income of KRW 3.58 trillion for the first three quarters of 2026, up 6.3% year on year.

Among non-bank units, the securities subsidiary showed the strongest growth, with cumulative net income of KRW 1.07 trillion, up 114.9%, driven by brokerage and wealth management fees. The card subsidiary's cumulative net income reached KRW 3.32 billion, up 18.2%.

Non-bank units contributed 40.1% of cumulative net income in the first three quarters of 2026. The insurance subsidiaries reportedly posted lower earnings in the third quarter than in the prior quarter.

Net interest income remains the foundation, with cumulative net interest income of KRW 9.99 trillion, up 2.9%, supported by corporate loan growth at the bank. Net fee income in 3Q 2026 was KRW 1.13 trillion, down 29.7% from the prior quarter as stock trading volumes declined.

Among the four large financial holding companies, KB Financial is described in March 2026 reports as having a relatively thick non-bank portfolio.

04

Earnings analysis

Based on DART-filed figures, net income attributable to controlling shareholders rose from KRW 4.15 trillion in 2022 to KRW 4.63 trillion in 2023 (up 11.5%), KRW 5.08 trillion in 2024 (up 9.6%), and KRW 5.83 trillion in 2025 (up 14.9%).

It has risen for three consecutive years from 2023 through 2025, and 2025 was the largest year of the 2022-2025 period. Over the same span, operating income went from KRW 5.29 trillion in 2022 to KRW 6.44 trillion in 2023 (up 21.7%) and KRW 8.05 trillion in 2024 (up 25.0%), reaching KRW 8.52 trillion in 2025 (up 5.9%).

Growth in operating income slowed to a single-digit rate in 2025 after peaking in 2024. Insurance revenue rose from KRW 9.55 trillion in 2022 to KRW 11.7 trillion in 2025, growing 7.8%, 7.0% and 5.8% in 2023, 2024 and 2025. Insurance revenue refers only to the insurance account and is not the company's total revenue.

Net income including non-controlling interests was KRW 5.84 trillion in 2025, close to the controlling-shareholder figure. The debt-to-equity ratio was 1,172.8% in 2022, 1,115.7% in 2023, 1,167.0% in 2024 and 1,211.7% in 2025.

At the end of 2025, total equity was KRW 60.8 trillion, of which controlling-shareholder equity was KRW 59 trillion, and total liabilities were KRW 737.1 trillion. Operating cash flow fell from KRW 4.76 trillion in 2022 to KRW 4.23 trillion in 2025.

On a quarterly basis, insurance revenue rose for four consecutive quarters, from KRW 2.94 trillion in 3Q 2025 to KRW 3.21 trillion in 3Q 2026, up 9.2% year on year in the latest quarter.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders fell 57.8% quarter on quarter to KRW 711.4 billion in 4Q 2025, then rose 166.0% to KRW 1.89 trillion in 1Q 2026, and in 3Q 2026 it was KRW 1.76 trillion, down 11.7% from the prior quarter but up 4.3% year on year.

The four-quarter total from 4Q 2025 to 3Q 2026 was KRW 6.35 trillion. The third-quarter 2026 results reportedly included a one-off tax benefit of KRW 231 billion.

05

Industry analysis

Financial holding company earnings depend on both interest rates and capital market activity. In an easing rate environment, net interest margins are harder to improve, and reports note that relying on interest income alone makes earnings growth difficult.

KB Financial's group net interest margin in 3Q 2026 was 1.92%, down 0.02 percentage points from the prior quarter, which was attributed to higher funding costs. On the capital market side, stock trading volumes feed directly into securities fee income, and fee revenue can swing with market conditions.

Reported 2025 total shareholder return ratios were 52.4% for KB Financial, 50.2% for Shinhan Financial Group, 46.8% for Hana Financial Group and 36.6% for Woori Financial Group, or 39.8% when tax-free dividends are considered.

Government value-up policy and the separate taxation requirement for high-dividend companies have intensified return competition among financial holding companies. A February 2026 report noted that, excluding KB Financial, the other financial holding companies still traded below 1x price-to-book.

Household lending is expected to grow only modestly, and KB Financial has said it will shift its growth focus toward corporate lending. Among peers, KB Financial is positioned as a leading financial group with strong capital ratios and a large return package.

06

Outlook

The company has not set a specific shareholder return ratio target and has said it will return all capital above its management CET1 level to shareholders (March 2026 reports).

The return framework uses the portion of CET1 above 13% at the prior year-end for dividends and first-half buybacks, and the portion above 13.5% at mid-year for second-half additional returns. The company said the first 2026 package of KRW 2.82 trillion corresponds to CET1 capital above 13% at the end of 2025.

In April 2026, the board resolved to cancel 14.26 million treasury shares, worth about KRW 2.3 trillion. In June 2026, KB Kookmin Bank paid an interim dividend of KRW 1.4 trillion to the holding company, and reports said this would underpin second-half return measures.

Financial industry observers expected buybacks and cancellations of about KRW 600 billion in the second half. Market commentary pointed to a total shareholder return ratio above 56% in 2026 and gradual expansion toward about 60% by 2028.

An LS Securities analyst said in a June 2026 report that the capital ratio should recover quickly and that the 2026 return ratio would be around 57%. The company also announced plans to pursue tax-free dividends through a reduction of capital surplus. The items to watch are the execution size of second-half additional returns and the year-end CET1 ratio.

08

Bull factors

Broader earnings base

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders grew for three consecutive years, from KRW 4.15 trillion in 2022 to KRW 5.83 trillion in 2025. Cumulative net fee income rose 38.5% in the first three quarters of 2026, and quarterly net fee income stayed above KRW 1 trillion for four straight quarters.

Non-bank units contributed 40.1% of cumulative net income, reflecting a shift away from reliance on interest income.

Capital base and return framework

The CET1 ratio was reported at 13.80% at the end of September 2026, and a May 2026 Kyobo Securities report described it as among the industry's highest. The company has said it will return all capital above its management CET1 target to shareholders, with no upper limit. In April 2026, the board resolved to cancel 14.26 million treasury shares.

Rising total shareholder return

The 2025 total shareholder return ratio of 52.4% was reported as the highest among the four large financial holding companies. Total returns for 2025 were KRW 3.06 trillion, and the first 2026 package is KRW 2.82 trillion. Market commentary expects the 2026 return ratio to exceed 56%.

09

Bear factors

Slowing operating income growth

Operating income growth slowed from 21.7% in 2023 and 25.0% in 2024 to 5.9% in 2025. Operating income peaked in 1Q 2026 at KRW 2.73 trillion and then declined in 2Q and 3Q. In 3Q 2026 it reached KRW 2.37 trillion, up only 1.4% year on year.

Dependence on market-driven fees

Third-quarter net fee income of KRW 1.13 trillion fell 29.7% from the prior quarter as stock trading volumes declined. Fee income that expanded during a market rally can swing with market conditions, which is cited as a burden factor. Because the securities unit grew fastest, a slowdown in trading could weigh more heavily on earnings.

Rate and margin pressure

The group net interest margin fell 0.02 percentage points to 1.92% in 3Q 2026 from the prior quarter, which was attributed to funding costs. Some reports say interest income may be hard to improve in an easing rate environment.

Household lending is expected to grow only modestly, so the effect of the shift toward corporate lending still needs to be confirmed.

10

Risk factors

Insurance subsidiary earnings

The insurance subsidiaries reportedly posted lower earnings in 3Q 2026 than in the prior quarter. Insurance revenue has risen for four straight quarters, but that growth in insurance account income may not translate into higher subsidiary earnings.

If insurance earnings slow, the composition of the 40.1% non-bank contribution to net income could shift. Insurance revenue reflects the insurance account and is not total company revenue, so its earnings contribution should be checked separately.

Corporate lending credit risk

KB Financial has said it will shift its growth focus toward corporate lending. A larger share of corporate loans can make asset quality more sensitive to changes in borrowers' business conditions. If corporate repayment capacity weakens with an economic slowdown, credit costs could rise and reduce earnings.

Whether this strategy can grow lending without weakening capital ratios or earnings stability still needs to be confirmed.

Conditions of the return policy

Additional returns depend on mid-year CET1 and board resolutions, so uncertainty remains until amounts are finalized. The one-off tax benefit of KRW 231 billion included in the third-quarter 2026 results may not recur. If the policy stance changes, the pace of expansion in the total shareholder return ratio could also change.

Overall view

On a DART-filed basis, KB Financial Group's net income attributable to controlling shareholders rose each year from 2023 through 2025, with 2025 the largest year of the period.

Cumulative net fee income rose 38.5% in the first three quarters of 2026, and non-bank units contributed 40.1% of cumulative net income, so the earnings mix is broadening beyond interest income.

On capital, the CET1 ratio was reported at 13.80% at the end of September 2026, and the 2025 total shareholder return ratio was 52.4%. On the other side, operating income has declined for two straight quarters since its 1Q 2026 peak, and the net interest margin edged lower on higher funding costs.

A slowdown in stock trading could also make quarterly fee income swing. The next checkpoints are the execution of second-half additional returns and the year-end CET1 ratio, the 4Q 2026 results release, and the 2026 annual report.
