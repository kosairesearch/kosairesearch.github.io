Report overview

Egg-processing growth versus feed-cost pressure

KC Feed grew 2025 operating profit to KRW 12.2 billion by pairing animal feed with egg processing, but Q3 2026 operating profit fell 26.5% year on year, making profit durability the next item to watch.

  2. 2

  4. 4

02

Business structure

KC Feed was founded in 1970 as a livestock company in North Gyeongsang Province and has produced compound animal feed since.

Its feed unit makes poultry and cattle feed and sells mainly in the Yeongnam region, and the company said that through the first nine months of 2025 it responded to a law reducing laying-hen floor space and opened special dealerships, raising sales efficiency.

After absorbing a food company in 2017, the group built a food unit that processes eggs into liquid egg products. The food unit focuses mainly on bakery and confectionery ingredient markets, with customers including food manufacturers, bakery and confectionery makers, and franchise restaurant operators.

Per Etoday's report, 2025 liquid egg sales were about 3,573 tons, an estimated 4.4 percent share of total liquid egg output. Its farm subsidiary operates egg farms and improved profitability through higher selling prices and business stabilization. The group also holds a lending subsidiary.

Feed is described as a stable revenue base but a mature business with limited profit volatility, while liquid egg processing is gaining weight in the company as demand keeps rising. In its 2025 disclosure, the company attributed profit growth to larger transaction volumes with new and existing customers.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual results rose across sales and profit from 2022 to 2025. Sales went from KRW 88.5 billion in 2022 to KRW 92.6 billion in 2023 (up 4.6% year on year), KRW 103 billion in 2024 (up 11.2%), and KRW 121.8 billion in 2025 (up 18.3%).

Operating profit rose from KRW 4.87 billion in 2022 to KRW 6.05 billion in 2023, KRW 8.1 billion in 2024, and KRW 12.2 billion in 2025, while the operating margin moved from 5.5 percent to 6.5 percent, 7.9 percent and 10.0 percent.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders went from KRW 4.2 billion in 2022 to KRW 10.3 billion in 2025, up 51.2 percent in 2025. Net income including non-controlling interests was KRW 10.6 billion in 2025, a different figure from controlling-shareholder net income.

For 2025, the company cited larger sales volumes from new and existing customers at KC Feed and higher selling prices plus business stabilization at its farm subsidiary. Operating cash flow rose from KRW 846 million in 2022 to KRW 14 billion in 2025.

Quarterly, Q3 2025 operating profit of KRW 4.31 billion was the highest of the last five quarters, and Q4 2025 fell to KRW 2.79 billion, down 35.2 percent from the prior quarter. Q1 2026 operating profit was KRW 1.63 billion, down 41.7 percent sequentially, and press coverage linked that quarter to higher feed costs.

Q2 2026 operating profit rose 72.6 percent sequentially to KRW 2.81 billion, and Q3 2026 rose 12.6 percent to KRW 3.17 billion. However, Q3 2026 operating profit was down 26.5 percent from a year earlier, and sales of KRW 30.7 billion fell 3.9 percent year on year.

Controlling-shareholder net income of KRW 3.12 billion dropped 12.5 percent from the prior-year quarter. Quarterly profit swings appear to depend jointly on sales volume, selling prices and feed costs.

05

Industry analysis

The compound feed industry is a mature business in which volumes track livestock inventories. Etoday reports that feed earns relatively stable margins, and that whether higher input costs are passed through to selling prices determines profitability.

In its 2025 disclosure, the company also cited efficiency gains from the law reducing laying-hen floor space. On the egg side, highly pathogenic avian influenza is a recurring variable that temporarily reduces output.

Coverage of Q1 2026 noted that egg output fell temporarily due to avian flu while food-unit sales still rose. Demand for liquid eggs is linked to growth in high-protein foods and convenience foods. Etoday expects the liquid egg market to grow at a compound annual rate of about 5.45 percent.

KC Feed's 2025 liquid egg sales were about 3,573 tons, an estimated 4.4 percent share of total output. Because the food unit targets bakery and confectionery ingredient makers, its results track food manufacturing demand.

Combining feed with egg products is a common structure among livestock-based companies in the food and beverage sector.

06

Outlook

The most concrete growth plan is the expansion of production facilities at the Geochang plant. The company said the new plant will be about 50 percent of existing capacity, and that total capacity will reach roughly 1.5 times the current level once the expansion is complete.

The same company official said the liquid egg plant is not yet running at full capacity. The profit impact will therefore depend on how quickly utilization rises. The investment period is set to run to August 2027, so the gap between completion and full operation is an item to watch.

On the feed side, the Q1 2026 cost increase was reported as a drag on operating profit, and how costs and selling prices were balanced in later quarters is the next checkpoint. Operating profit rose sequentially in Q2 and Q3 2026, but Q3 2026 at KRW 3.17 billion remained below Q3 2025 at KRW 4.31 billion.

Egg prices and avian influenza outbreaks remain variables for food-unit results. Demand for liquid eggs is tied to high-protein and convenience foods, which supports the medium-term demand case for this unit.

The next annual report will show whether the 2026 operating margin holds near the 10.0 percent recorded in 2025.

08

Bull factors

Stronger cash generation and balance sheet

Between 2022 and 2025, cash coming in from operations grew alongside the improvement in financial capacity. The liabilities-to-equity ratio fell over the same period from 59.4% to 23.8%. Total equity at the end of 2025 was KRW 81 billion, of which KRW 80.1 billion belonged to controlling shareholders. Coverage describes the feed business as a stable source of cash.

Egg-processing expansion widens the growth base

KRW 13.4 billion is going into the Geochang plant expansion, equal to about 16.7 percent of equity. The new line is about half the size of existing capacity, and completion would lift total capacity to roughly 1.5 times today's level.

Rising demand from food makers, bakery and confectionery producers and franchise restaurants is increasing the unit's weight in the company. Coverage also reports expectations that the center of earnings will shift toward egg processing.

Wider customer base and feed sales efficiency

The company attributed its 2025 profit growth to larger transaction volumes with new and existing customers. Through the first nine months of 2025, the feed unit gained sales efficiency by responding to a law on laying-hen floor space and opening special dealerships. The food unit's results improved through stronger promotion and more diversified sales channels.

Bear factors

Quarterly operating profit down year on year

Q3 2026 operating profit was KRW 3.17 billion, down 26.5% from a year earlier. Sales of KRW 30.7 billion in the same quarter fell 3.9% year on year, and net income attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 3.12 billion dropped 12.5%. Press coverage linked the Q1 2026 profit decline to higher feed costs. Sequential recovery and year-on-year declines are appearing at the same time.

Large swings in quarterly profit

Q4 2025 operating profit was KRW 2.79 billion, down 35.2 percent sequentially, and controlling-shareholder net income fell 43.4 percent to KRW 2.02 billion. Q1 2026 operating profit of KRW 1.63 billion was down 41.7 percent from the prior quarter and was the lowest of the last five quarters.

Because quarterly swings are large, annual trends and quarterly flows sometimes point in different directions. Controlling-shareholder net income totaled KRW 9.91 billion over the last four quarters.

Feed costs and egg-supply volatility

Feed is considered a mature business with limited profit volatility, yet when costs rise, how far they are passed into selling prices decides results. The food unit has been affected by highly pathogenic avian influenza, which temporarily reduces egg output.

In Q1 2026, food-unit sales rose even as egg output fell, but coverage said profitability stayed limited because of higher costs.

10

Risk factors

Feed cost pressure

Higher feed costs were reported as a factor behind the lower Q1 2026 operating profit. How much selling-price increases offset those costs shows up in quarterly operating profit. If cost pressure persists, the feed business's contribution to profit could shrink.

Poultry disease and egg supply

When highly pathogenic avian influenza occurs, egg output falls and affects food-unit volumes. Q1 2026 coverage cites a temporary output decline from the same cause. If raw egg supply becomes unstable, processing costs and profit volatility could rise together.

Expansion execution and utilization

The KRW 13.4 billion expansion equals about 16.7 percent of equity. The egg plant is not yet at full capacity, and the 1.5-times capacity target depends on completion by August 2027. If demand does not keep pace with the expansion, the payback period could lengthen.

12

Overall view

KC Feed paired its animal feed core with egg processing and grew sales, operating profit and controlling-shareholder net income in every year from 2022 to 2025.

In 2025 it recorded sales of KRW 121.8 billion, operating profit of KRW 12.2 billion and controlling-shareholder net income of KRW 10.3 billion, with a 10.0 percent operating margin.

The liabilities-to-equity ratio was 23.8 percent at the end of 2025 and operating cash flow was KRW 14 billion, so the balance sheet also strengthened. However, higher feed costs reduced Q1 2026 operating profit, and Q3 2026 operating profit was down 26.5 percent from a year earlier.

The Geochang expansion targets about 1.5 times current egg-processing capacity, but the pace of utilization and egg-supply swings are variables. The bullish factors are earnings capacity and financial structure, while the bearish factors are the year-on-year quarterly decline and cost and disease risks.

The next checkpoints are the full-year operating margin in the 2026 annual report, Q1 2027 results expected around May 2027, and completion of the expansion by August 2027.
