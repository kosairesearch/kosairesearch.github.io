02

Business structure

HYTC is a maker of ultra-precision components for secondary battery manufacturing equipment, founded in 2005 and listed on KOSDAQ in 2022.

Its main products include PX-SHAFT and KNIFE UNIT and KNIFE-SPACER parts used in electrode process equipment, and winding cores, CUTTERs, ultrasonic horns, ANVILs and insulation mold parts used in assembly process equipment.

Assembly-process parts are consumables such as ultrasonic horns and ANVILs that require periodic replacement, so revenue tends to rise with equipment utilization as electric vehicle demand grows. Conversely, when secondary battery makers cut equipment investment and adjust inventories, consumable demand falls first.

According to a financial data site, cumulative revenue for the first nine months of 2025 fell 8.3% year on year, with lower assembly-part demand and higher costs cited as the cause.

Overseas subsidiaries are located in China, Poland, the United States and Indonesia, and a August 2026 report described seven sites across five countries, including Georgia and Ohio in the United States, Poland, China, Indonesia and Korea.

The overseas units support battery makers' factory expansions abroad with dispatched staff and technical support. On competition, the company cited intensifying market bidding competition as a reason for the 2025 operating profit decline.

For new businesses, it plans to extend its precision machining know-how into AI data centers and robotics. Revenue shares by segment and by customer have not been disclosed in figures available here, so they remain items to confirm in the segment notes of the periodic report.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual revenue rose from KRW 34.2 billion in 2022 to KRW 40.6 billion in 2023, the largest year in the period (up 18.8% year on year), before falling to KRW 32.3 billion in 2024 (down 20.4%) and KRW 30.1 billion in 2025 (down 6.9%), a two-year decline running from 2024 through 2025.

Operating profit rose from KRW 1.98 billion in 2022 to KRW 3.57 billion in 2023 (up 80.3%), then fell to KRW 424 million in 2024 (down 88.1%) and turned into an operating loss of KRW 3.17 billion in 2025. The operating margin peaked at 8.8% in 2023 and then declined to 1.3% in 2024 and -10.5% in 2025.

For 2025, the company attributed the profit decline to higher raw material costs and intensifying market bidding competition.

Owner-attributable net income rose 32.2% to KRW 3.45 billion in the same year, which the company linked to gains from the disposal and valuation of financial assets lifting non-operating income, so the net income increase should not be read as an operating recovery.

Operating cash flow also fell from KRW 6.54 billion in 2024 to KRW 1.23 billion in 2025.

Quarterly, an operating loss of KRW 583 million in the third quarter of 2025 was followed by an operating profit of KRW 36.27 million in the fourth quarter, but the first quarter of 2026 returned to an operating loss of KRW 1.17 billion.

In the second quarter of 2026, revenue reached KRW 10.3 billion (up 23.8% from the prior quarter) and the operating loss narrowed to KRW 53.74 million.

Third-quarter 2026 revenue of KRW 12.2 billion was up 48.5% year on year, but an operating loss of KRW 251 million continued, and the owner-attributable net loss of KRW 6.94 billion marked a swing into loss from a year earlier.

The second-quarter 2026 owner-attributable net income of KRW 17.2 billion is far larger than that quarter's small operating loss, which points to sizable non-operating one-off items to be checked in the detailed income statement lines of the periodic report.

05

Industry analysis

Equipment investment by secondary battery makers, the main end market, weighed on consumable demand in the third quarter of 2025 as capital spending cuts and inventory adjustments overlapped.

On the other side, rising equipment utilization from electric vehicle growth is cited as a driver of replacement demand for assembly-process consumables. A summary of first-half 2026 results showed revenue up 46.7% year on year and the operating loss down 53.3%.

Whether the 2026 revenue rebound reflects renewed equipment investment or the replacement cycle of consumables cannot be separated from disclosures alone.

On competition, intensifying market bidding competition was named as a reason for the 2025 decline in operating profit, which points to pricing and delivery pressure. Higher raw material prices also added to cost burdens.

Compared with larger listed companies in the machinery and equipment sector, HYTC is far smaller in revenue and equity, which makes its earnings more volatile.

Because it is pursuing robotics, it may find points of overlap in precision machining and component supply with robotics-focused listed companies such as Rainbow Robotics and Doosan Robotics. However, the revenue contribution of the new businesses has not been shown in figures.

Demand for battery equipment consumables ultimately depends on capacity expansion plans of large battery makers, so overseas factory utilization is a key variable.

Outlook

In a statement following the August 2026 half-year report, company officials set a goal of a full-year operating profit in the second half of 2026.

Because operating losses were recorded from the first quarter through the third quarter of 2026, the goal requires fourth-quarter operating profit large enough to offset that cumulative loss, which makes the fourth-quarter result the key variable.

The company has hired an IT and cloud executive with an Amazon headquarters background to build an ERP and workflow automation system linking seven sites in five countries. Plans also include an AI-based drawing review system and an AI automated quotation system.

For new businesses, it plans to expand into AI data centers and robotics. Expanding overseas sites should strengthen local responsiveness, but new customer wins and order sizes have not been shown in disclosed figures.

On revenue, the most direct check is whether the increases in the second and third quarters of 2026 continue. On operating profit, the key question is whether the third-quarter 2026 operating loss of KRW 251 million turns into a profit in the fourth quarter.

Whether new-business revenue appears as a separate line in the business segment information of the annual report is another checkpoint.

Bull factors

Revenue rebound

Revenue rose from the prior quarter in both the second and third quarters of 2026, extending the run of increases. Third-quarter revenue of KRW 12.2 billion was up 48.5% year on year. The narrower first-half operating loss is also cited as evidence of improving profit structure.

Stable balance sheet

The debt ratio fell from 22.5% in 2022 to 16.6% in 2025. Total liabilities at the end of 2025 were KRW 10.8 billion, against total equity of KRW 64.7 billion. A stable balance sheet may leave room for restructuring and new-business investment.

Management upgrade and new businesses

The company has hired an IT and cloud executive with an Amazon headquarters background to pursue digital transformation including ERP, workflow automation and AI-based drawing review. Its new-business plan extends precision machining into AI data centers and robotics. If these businesses generate revenue, dependence on the secondary battery cycle could decline.

Bear factors

Operating losses persist

The company swung to an operating loss of KRW 3.17 billion in 2025, with an operating margin of -10.5%. Operating losses have continued for three consecutive quarters from the first quarter through the third quarter of 2026.

The owner-attributable net loss of KRW 6.94 billion in the third quarter of 2026 marked a swing into loss from a year earlier, so revenue growth has not yet translated into profit.

Dependence on equipment investment cycle

When secondary battery makers cut equipment investment and adjust inventories, consumable demand falls first. Cumulative revenue for the first nine months of 2025 declined year on year for this reason. The durability of the industry recovery is the largest external variable behind earnings swings.

Cost and bidding pressure

For 2025, the company cited higher raw material costs and intensifying market bidding competition as reasons for lower operating profit. With limited pricing power, it is difficult to pass cost increases on to customers.

The operating margin fell from 1.3% in 2024 to -10.5% in 2025, showing that this pressure reached profitability.

Risk factors

Reliance on non-operating items

Owner-attributable net income in 2025 was affected by gains from disposal and valuation of financial assets. The second-quarter 2026 figure of KRW 17.2 billion also diverges sharply from that quarter's small operating loss.

Profits that rely on one-off non-operating items have low repeatability and add uncertainty to earnings analysis.

New business execution

Plans for new businesses in AI data centers and robotics were described in an August 2026 report. However, the revenue scale and order record of these businesses have not been shown in disclosed figures. If new-business investment widens the core operating loss, it could become a financial burden.

Small-cap liquidity and ownership concentration

The largest shareholder and related parties hold about 39% of the company, so the freely tradable share base is limited. The free float ratio is about 43%. If volatility rises or trading volume shrinks, price discovery could weaken.
