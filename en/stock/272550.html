02

Business structure

Samyang Packaging is a PET packaging specialist within the Samyang Group, focused on PET containers and aseptic (sterile filling) products, and it is building its recycled PET business as a medium- to long-term growth driver.

According to the company profile, it commercialized the first PET bottle in Korea in 1979 and has since led the domestic PET packaging market, and media coverage describes it as the country's No. 1 PET packaging company.

Its PET container lines, more than 30 in number, can produce about 3.2 billion containers a year, and its six aseptic lines can produce more than 1 billion bottles a year. The company says it holds a share of about 70% of the domestic aseptic market.

It is actively pursuing new demand in containers and aseptic products, and it attributes higher unit sales in the first half of 2026 to that effort. It produces recycled PET flake and R-Chip, and it says the recycling market's full-scale formation has widened its sales base.

Its core raw material, PET chip, became more expensive as higher crude oil prices, driven by geopolitical risk in the Middle East, pushed costs up. The company says higher sales, process improvements and more efficient plant operations are offsetting that pressure.

Its competitive base rests on scale of facilities and aseptic share, together with price and cost competitiveness, and it plans to keep improving production processes and plant efficiency.

04

Earnings analysis

On a disclosed annual basis, sales rose from KRW 407.4 billion in 2022 to KRW 421.7 billion in 2023 (up 3.5% year on year) and KRW 448.1 billion in 2024 (up 6.3%), then fell to KRW 420.2 billion in 2025 (down 6.2%). Among 2022 to 2025, 2024 was the largest year for sales and 2022 was the smallest.

Operating profit went from KRW 23.7 billion in 2022 to KRW 32.5 billion in 2023 (up 37.0%) and KRW 33.8 billion in 2024 (up 4.0%), then fell to KRW 24.6 billion in 2025 (down 27.0%).

The operating margin rose from 5.8% in 2022 to 7.7% in 2023, then eased to 7.5% in 2024 and 5.9% in 2025, and media reports said the 2025 annual deterioration was attributed in the disclosure to lower sales in the container segment.

Attributable net income to controlling shareholders rose from KRW 12.1 billion in 2022 to KRW 25.4 billion in 2023 (up 110.4%), then fell for two consecutive years to KRW 20.4 billion in 2024 (down 19.6%) and KRW 17.7 billion in 2025 (down 13.6%).

Net income including non-controlling interests is reported at the same figures for each year. Operating cash flow rose from KRW 30.9 billion in 2022 to KRW 61.7 billion in 2024 before falling to KRW 48.3 billion in 2025, and the debt ratio declined from 78.0% in 2022 to 68.2% in 2025.

On a quarterly basis, the company moved from operating profit of KRW 15.8 billion in the third quarter of 2025 to an operating loss of KRW 3.6 billion in the fourth quarter on sales of KRW 82.8 billion, a 33.9% drop from the prior quarter, which marked a shift into loss.

It then returned to operating profit of KRW 454 million in the first quarter of 2026, KRW 17 billion in the second quarter on sales of KRW 131.5 billion, and KRW 12.9 billion in the third quarter on sales of KRW 135.7 billion, which was down 24.5% from the second quarter.

For the second quarter of 2026, the company attributed the 21.4% year-on-year profit increase to new demand in containers and aseptic products, lower fixed cost per unit, higher selling prices, and a 6.5% decline in selling and administrative expenses in the first half.

Attributable net income was KRW 13.6 billion in the second quarter and KRW 10.9 billion in the third quarter, and the total for the last four quarters was KRW 18.5 billion.

05

Industry analysis

In the domestic PET packaging market, the company is described as the No. 1 player, and its share of about 70% in the aseptic segment is cited as the core basis of its position.

The company says that higher crude oil prices pushed up the price of PET chip, its core raw material, and cost pressure was still widening on a nine-month 2026 basis. On the demand side, the company says new demand it developed in containers and aseptic products translated into higher unit sales.

It views the recycled PET market as one that is taking full shape, and it credits recycled flake and R-Chip sales with contributing to first-half 2026 revenue growth. In terms of the cycle, price increases supported earnings while raw material prices were rising.

On competition, the company has pledged to strengthen price competitiveness and has cited process improvements and facility efficiency as ways to manage costs.

If crude oil moves raw material and selling prices together, the speed at which cost increases can be passed through to selling prices becomes the key variable for profit swings.

For recycled PET to become a medium- to long-term growth driver, sourcing recycled feedstock must keep pace with sales expansion, and that is the central issue.

06

Outlook

On November 10, 2026, the company set a target of KRW 500 billion in sales by 2029, a figure larger than the KRW 420.2 billion recorded for 2025. On shareholder returns, it said it is pursuing an annual dividend payout ratio of at least 40% and the introduction of a minimum per-share dividend over the medium term.

When it reported first-half 2026 results, the company said it would expand sales in line with market demand in the second half and maintain stable profitability through raw material and selling price management and cost efficiency.

In sustainability, it plans to expand production of recycled PET flake and R-Chip and to develop recycled PET from used PET bottles as a medium- to long-term growth driver.

Raising the share of higher value-added products and improving productivity and operating efficiency are also part of the medium-term direction it has presented. Because the 2029 target is set on sales, its significance depends on margins improving alongside growth.

For results to follow the company's plan, higher sales volume must absorb rising raw material costs, and the next point to check is whether the third-quarter 2026 profit decline reflects a temporary cost factor or a structural one.

The timing and form of the shareholder return policy are likely to become clearer through periodic filings and shareholder meeting agenda items.

Market position anchored in aseptic products

Its PET container segment runs more than 30 production lines, and its aseptic segment runs six lines, and this capacity is described as the base for absorbing new demand. Its recycled PET business, which it is developing as a sustainability growth driver, adds to the breadth of its portfolio.

Bear factors

Third-quarter profit decline

Third-quarter 2026 operating profit of KRW 12.9 billion fell 18.7% from a year earlier and 24.5% from the prior quarter. Attributable net income to controlling shareholders of KRW 10.9 billion was down 15.4% year on year.

Sales rose 8.3%, but profit did not keep pace, which suggests that sales growth is translating into earnings less effectively.

Raw material cost burden

Its core raw material, PET chip, became more expensive as crude oil prices rose on Middle East geopolitical risk, raising cost pressure. The company says higher sales, process improvements and plant efficiency offset the pressure, but operating profit in the third quarter was below the second quarter. How far selling price increases can pass through cost increases will determine margins.

2025 profit decline and loss quarter

Operating profit for 2025 was KRW 24.6 billion, down 27.0% year on year, and attributable net income declined for two consecutive years from 2024 through 2025. The fourth quarter of 2025 posted an operating loss of KRW 3.6 billion and an attributable net loss of KRW 4.59 billion.

Media reports said the decline in container segment sales was cited as the reason for the 2025 annual deterioration.

Risk factors

Raw materials and crude oil

PET chip prices move with crude oil and geopolitical risk, and the extent to which cost increases can be passed through to selling prices determines margins. The company said cost pressure widened on a nine-month 2026 basis.

If raw material prices rise sharply again, maintaining second-quarter 2026 operating profit levels could become difficult.

Dependence on volume and pricing

The company attributed the second-quarter 2026 earnings improvement to higher sales volume and selling price increases. Whether new demand and the formation of the recycling market continue is the key question. If demand slows, fixed costs per unit could rise again and profit could decline quickly.

Financing and shareholder return execution

Total liabilities at the end of 2025 were KRW 263.2 billion, with a debt ratio of 68.2%. Operating cash flow fell from KRW 61.7 billion in 2024 to KRW 48.3 billion in 2025.

Because the shareholder return policy has been described as under pursuit, the timing and form of its introduction could change the execution burden and financial headroom.
