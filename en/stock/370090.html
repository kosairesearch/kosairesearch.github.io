02

Business structure

Furonteer is an automation equipment specialist for camera module production, founded in 2009 and listed on KOSDAQ in 2022. The business runs in two divisions.

The equipment division develops and sells assembly and inspection tools for automotive camera modules, while the parts division makes core components for automation equipment, including industrial PCs and LED light sources. Revenue mix varies by data provider.

Jobkorea company information, as of March 2026, shows parts such as industrial PCs and LED lighting at 56%, automotive equipment at 32%, and other equipment at 11%. Alphasquare lists its main revenue sources as industrial PCs, inspection LED light sources, and automation components.

A research report published in early 2023 showed equipment at 55.5% and parts at 44.5% of cumulative revenue for the first nine months of 2022. External analysis (FnGuide) cited weaker demand for automotive camera process equipment and investment delays by major customers as reasons for the 2025 equipment slowdown.

The parts side depends on demand from industrial automation and inspection facilities. In new products, Furonteer completed development of process equipment for automotive lidar and ToF modules and then recorded first sales.

According to Wisereport, orders for industrial PC and LED light parts and automotive camera equipment increased in the first quarter of 2026. Competitive positioning depends on customer investment plans and equipment performance.

04

Earnings analysis

On a disclosed basis, annual revenue rose from KRW 26.9 billion in 2022 to a peak of KRW 46.5 billion in 2023, up 73.0% year on year, before falling 45.6% to KRW 25.3 billion in 2024 and 52.1% to KRW 12.1 billion in 2025. Revenue fell in 2024 and 2025, two years in a row.

Among 2022 to 2025, 2023 was the largest year and 2025 the smallest. Operating profit rose from KRW 1.63 billion in 2022 to KRW 7.16 billion in 2023, up 338.8%, then turned into an operating loss of KRW 3.75 billion in 2024 and widened to an operating loss of KRW 7.16 billion in 2025.

The operating margin fell from 15.4% in 2023 to -14.8% in 2024 and -59.0% in 2025. Profit attributable to controlling shareholders dropped from KRW 7.52 billion in 2023 to KRW 62.57 million in 2024, down 99.2%, and turned into a net loss of KRW 6.15 billion in 2025.

Net income including non-controlling interests was also a net loss of KRW 6.15 billion in 2025, the same amount.

Total equity fell from KRW 47.3 billion at the end of 2024, according to a Nate News report on the separate financial statements, to KRW 41.2 billion at the end of 2025, with total liabilities of KRW 3.39 billion and a debt ratio of 8.2%.

Operating cash flow went from an inflow of KRW 12.7 billion in 2023 to an outflow of KRW 706 million in 2024 and KRW 5.48 billion in 2025. Quarterly revenue was KRW 1.91 billion in the third quarter of 2025 with an operating loss of KRW 1.98 billion.

It rose to KRW 3.05 billion in the fourth quarter, up 59.4% from the prior quarter, and KRW 3.38 billion in the first quarter of 2026, up 10.8%, then jumped to KRW 8.63 billion in the second quarter, up 155.2%. Third-quarter revenue of KRW 6.19 billion fell 28.3% from the prior quarter but rose 223.3% year on year.

The third-quarter operating loss of KRW 274 million was the smallest of the five quarters. However, the net loss attributable to controlling shareholders widened to KRW 573 million from KRW 135 million in the second quarter.

The sum of the net losses attributable to controlling shareholders over the last four quarters was KRW 4.25 billion. Because quarterly revenue swings widely, results appear to depend heavily on the timing of customer orders.

05

Industry analysis

Furonteer's demand comes from two directions. On the automotive side, its assembly and inspection tools go into camera production lines, and on the industrial side, its industrial PCs and LED light sources go into automation equipment.

Automotive camera equipment relies on the view that manufacturers will increase investment as ADAS and autonomous driving technology grows. FnGuide summarized that this trend is expected to drive rapid growth in the related market. However, equipment revenue in 2025 fell on customer investment delays and softer demand.

From a cycle perspective, the company appears to have passed a 2025 investment adjustment phase and entered a 2026 stage of order growth and revenue rebound. First sales of lidar and ToF module process equipment are an indicator of whether a new demand pillar will take hold.

Among peers in the same electronics and components sector, large parts makers such as Samsung Electro-Mechanics and LG Innotek differ greatly in scale. Furonteer does not make camera modules itself but supplies the equipment and parts used in their production.

In an Alphasquare sector comparison of 96 electronic equipment and device stocks on an annual basis, Furonteer ranks 88th in revenue and 87th in operating margin, placing it near the bottom. Its position within the sector is likely to depend on how quickly profit margins recover.

Bull factors

Quarterly revenue rebound

Revenue in the second quarter of 2026 reached KRW 8.63 billion, up 155.2% from the prior quarter, which marked a break from the weakness of 2025. Wisereport's tally of rising orders in the first quarter of 2026 fits with this trend.

Third-quarter revenue of KRW 6.19 billion was 223.3% higher than a year earlier, so the year-on-year recovery direction held in the third quarter.

Narrowing operating losses

The operating loss narrowed from KRW 1.98 billion in the third quarter of 2025 to KRW 274 million in the third quarter of 2026. Losses have run for five quarters but have become smaller in each quarter.

The 2025 annual operating margin was -59.0%, and the business still has room to improve margins, so higher revenue could increase the ability to absorb losses.

New products and financial capacity

Process equipment for automotive lidar and ToF modules has moved past development and recorded first sales, and it could become a new source of equipment demand as automotive sensor use grows. The debt ratio was 8.2% at the end of 2025 and total equity was KRW 41.2 billion, so the financial burden is relatively light.

Bear factors

Revenue volatility and long decline

Annual revenue fell from KRW 46.5 billion in 2023 to KRW 12.1 billion in 2025. Quarterly revenue fell 28.3% from KRW 8.63 billion in the second quarter of 2026 to KRW 6.19 billion in the third quarter, so quarterly volatility remains large. A rebound over one or two quarters may still be insufficient to reverse the annual decline.

Persistent losses and wider net loss

Operating losses ran for two years in a row in 2024 and 2025, and the 2025 net loss attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 6.15 billion. The third-quarter net loss attributable to controlling shareholders widened to KRW 573 million from KRW 135 million in the second quarter.

The sum of the last four quarters' net losses attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 4.25 billion, and operating cash flow in 2025 was an outflow of KRW 5.48 billion.

Dependence on customer investment

External analysis attributed the 2025 decline in automotive camera process equipment sales to softer demand combined with investment delays at major customers. If equipment revenue depends on the investment timing of a few customers, earnings swings grow sharply whenever those customers delay spending.

Risk factors

Customer investment cycle

Orders for automotive camera equipment move with the capital spending plans of automakers and component makers. Revenue fell in 2025 on investment delays, so if the investment cycle slows again, the rebound in quarterly results could be short-lived.

Timing of new product revenue

Lidar and ToF module process equipment has recorded first sales, but whether it translates into mass-production revenue is not yet clearly visible in quarterly results. If new product sales do not grow as expected, there will be too few other sources to offset swings in equipment revenue.

Equity decline and cash outflow

Total equity fell from KRW 47.3 billion at the end of 2024 to KRW 41.2 billion at the end of 2025. Operating cash flow was an outflow of KRW 5.48 billion in 2025. If losses continue, the decline in equity could accelerate, so changes in financial capacity need to be tracked together.

The 2025 annual operating loss was KRW 7.16 billion and the net loss attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 6.15 billion.
