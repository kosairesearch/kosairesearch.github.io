S-Chem: Direct-Supply Shift Tested by Consecutive Losses

02

Business structure

S-Chem is a fine chemicals materials company founded in 2014 that listed on KOSDAQ in 2024. Its core business is display materials: it synthesizes finished OLED materials and intermediates and runs a recycling-purification business that refines used OLED organic materials for reuse.

The second pillar is bio and pharmaceutical materials, including reagents for in-vitro diagnostics (IVD) and life-science protein separation and purification, pharmaceutical intermediates made in a non-GMP facility, and animal vitamins. The company has described its OLED materials business as its main business.

Customer structure is shifting: from 2025 supply to a major Korean-listed global display maker moved to direct delivery, whereas previously supply ran through second-tier suppliers, according to press reports.

In 2026 the company said it is expanding from synthesis into a total solution covering purification and quality evaluation. New areas include semiconductor materials, aerospace and white bio (cosmetics), and it entered semiconductor additive materials in June 2026.

In OLED intermediates and purification, customer qualification and supply stability matter, which limits quick entry by new suppliers. Its scale is far smaller than large chemical peers such as LG Chem and Hanwha Solutions. Shareholding by the largest shareholder and related parties was 40.46% as of a September 2025 change.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual sales were KRW 24.4 billion in 2023, rose 35.6% to KRW 33.1 billion in 2024, then fell 33.2% to KRW 22.1 billion in 2025. Among 2023-2025, 2024 was the largest year for sales and 2025 the smallest.

Operating profit rose 16.6% from KRW 1.92 billion in 2023 to KRW 2.24 billion in 2024, but 2025 turned to an operating loss of KRW 2.03 billion. The operating margin moved from 7.9% in 2023 and 6.8% in 2024 to -9.2% in 2025.

Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders went from a loss of KRW 862 million in 2023 to a profit of KRW 1.02 billion in 2024, then to a loss of KRW 2.17 billion in 2025, and total net income including non-controlling interests was reported at the same amounts in each year.

For 2025, the company attributed the weakness to a gap created when its supply structure changed during the move into a global display maker's supply chain, with some revenue gaps and profitability adjustments.

By quarter, third-quarter 2025 sales of KRW 6.55 billion and an operating loss of KRW 980 million narrowed in the fourth quarter to flat sales of KRW 6.55 billion (up 0.1% from the prior quarter) and an operating loss of KRW 484 million.

First-quarter 2026 sales rose 1.8% to KRW 6.67 billion and operating profit of KRW 302 million marked a return to profit, with net profit attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 488 million.

Second-quarter 2026 sales fell 2.1% to KRW 6.53 billion with an operating loss of KRW 1.01 billion, and third-quarter sales fell 18.1% from the prior quarter and 18.3% year on year to KRW 5.35 billion with an operating loss of KRW 549 million.

Operating losses have now run for two consecutive quarters, from the second quarter of 2026 through the third, and net loss attributable to controlling shareholders totaled KRW 1.96 billion over the last four quarters.

Total equity at the end of 2025 was KRW 36.1 billion, all attributable to controlling shareholders, against total liabilities of KRW 3.59 billion and a debt ratio of 9.9%. Operating cash flow moved from KRW 3.88 billion in 2024 to -KRW 971 million in 2025.

05

Industry analysis

S-Chem's end market is the OLED panel market. According to a FnGuide summary, growth in 2025 was expected to come from replacement demand tied to AI features and wider OLED adoption in premium tablets and notebooks.

In early 2025, Meritz Securities pointed to expanding M14 panel shipments as a beneficiary but expected results to lag the guidance given at listing because of fourth-quarter inventory adjustments. The same report projected 2025 sales of KRW 40.5 billion, while reported 2025 sales were KRW 22.1 billion.

That gap suggests the customer supply-structure transition weighed on revenue more than panel shipments, so industry and company-specific factors need to be separated.

In cycle terms, the display supply chain is in a transition period, and customer qualification and a stable direct-supply setup are prerequisites for a revenue recovery.

In OLED intermediates and purification, the company's strengths are cited as quality evaluation capability and joint development tailored to customers.

Against large chemical peers such as LG Chem and Hanwha Solutions, however, its OLED materials revenue base is far smaller, and dependence on a specific customer and panel generation is high.

New areas such as semiconductor materials and aerospace appear to be at an early stage with limited earnings contribution so far.

08

Bull factors

Direct-supply stabilization and first-quarter profit

First-quarter 2026 sales of KRW 6.67 billion were the highest of the last five quarters, and operating profit of KRW 302 million marked a return to profit. The company said completing its supply-chain transition in 2025 should bring the benefits of a stabilized direct-supply system from 2026.

The argument for the bull case starts from the claim that direct supply has improved customer response speed and supply efficiency.

Completed upgrade of recycling-purification equipment

The company completed its recycling-purification upgrade and said related revenue would be reflected from the second quarter. Recycling refines used OLED organic materials, and the company says demand is linked to the ESG agendas of global clients.

In early 2025, Meritz Securities also cited the growth potential of eco-friendly purification as an investment point.

Expansion into new materials

A new materials research center opened in Jecheon in March 2026, and in June 2026 the company entered semiconductor additive materials. Building on its high-purity synthesis and purification technology, it is pursuing expansion into semiconductor materials, aerospace and white bio. Diversifying beyond a single OLED product line could broaden its customer and product base.

09

Bear factors

Two straight quarters of losses and falling sales

Second-quarter 2026 returned to an operating loss of KRW 1.01 billion, and third-quarter sales fell 18.1% from the prior quarter to KRW 5.35 billion. The third-quarter operating loss narrowed to KRW 549 million, but sales were down 18.3% year on year.

The bear case centers on the fact that the benefits of a stabilized direct-supply system have not yet translated into a sales recovery.

Large gap between earlier forecasts and results

In early 2025, Meritz Securities projected 2025 sales of KRW 40.5 billion and operating profit of KRW 5.8 billion. The same report expected results below the listing guidance because of fourth-quarter inventory adjustments, but actual 2025 sales were KRW 22.1 billion and operating loss was KRW 2.03 billion. The wide gap between forecast and actual results shows how uncertain the business outlook has been.

Dependence on one customer and supply gaps

The 2025 weakness was attributed to a supply gap that arose while the company moved into a global Korean display maker's supply chain. The direct-supply shift is a strategy to build a more stable revenue base over time, but the transition period brought revenue gaps and profitability adjustments.

While the business depends on one customer and specific panel generations, supply swings feed straight into results.

10

Risk factors

Customer and supply-chain concentration

OLED materials revenue is tied to the direct-supply arrangement with a global Korean display maker and to panel shipments. If the customer changes panel production plans or generation transitions, volumes could fall quickly.

Because the company already experienced supply gaps and profitability adjustments in 2025, the supply-chain transition is still at an early stage.

Cash flow and financial capacity

The turn of operating cash flow to negative is a burden. Net loss attributable to controlling shareholders totaled KRW 1.96 billion over the last four quarters, so capital is building slowly from earnings.

Total equity of KRW 36.1 billion and a debt ratio of 9.9% indicate financial room, but a prolonged loss would narrow the capacity to fund facility and new-business spending from internal cash.

Execution of new businesses and capex

Several projects are running at once, including semiconductor additive materials, natural-product new materials and a fourth plant. New fields often take time for customer qualification and volume production, so revenue contributions could be delayed. Given the company's small scale, pursuing several initiatives in parallel adds pressure on resource allocation.

Overall view

S-Chem posted weak 2025 results, with sales of KRW 22.1 billion and an operating loss of KRW 2.03 billion, before returning to operating profit in the first quarter of 2026.

Operating losses then ran for two consecutive quarters in the second and third quarters, and third-quarter sales fell 18.1% from the prior quarter. Net loss attributable to controlling shareholders totaled KRW 1.96 billion over the last four quarters, a period in which the P/E ratio cannot be calculated.

Bullish factors are the stabilization of direct supply, the upgraded recycling-purification equipment and expansion into new materials such as semiconductor additives. Bearish factors are dependence on one customer, a wide gap between earlier forecasts and actual results, and weaker operating cash flow.

The balance sheet carries little debt, but unless earnings recover, the price-to-book level will not translate into an earnings-based multiple.

The next checkpoints are the fourth-quarter results in the 2026 annual report and whether direct-supply volumes and new-material revenue end the losses in the first-quarter 2027 report.
