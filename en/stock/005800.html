02

Business structure

Shinyoung Wacoal began as Shinyoung Textile Dyeing Company in 1954, incorporated in 1968, and adopted its current name in 1994 when Japan's Wacoal took an equity stake, becoming Korea's leading women's foundation and lingerie specialist.

The company operates multiple foundation and lingerie brands including Venus, Wacoal, Solb, Motherpia, Orhwa, Salute, Remamma, Aneta, and Arbo, with Remamma—a dedicated underwear brand for women who have undergone breast cancer surgery—being the only such category offering in the domestic market.

As a women's underwear specialist, the company holds a 54.1% market share within department-store sales, preserving a dominant position in the traditional retail channel.

Control rests with the founding family, with Chairman Lee Eui-pyeong and his son, CEO Lee Sung-won, among related parties holding stakes under a co-CEO structure, while Japan's Wacoal Holdings remains the second-largest shareholder with a 25% stake acquired through a 1994 rights offering.

More recently, the company has focused on customized product development using CRM systems and 3D body scanners, while pursuing channel expansion into online and live-commerce platforms to bolster competitiveness.

On the competitive front, traditional innerwear makers such as BYC and Ssangbangwool's Vivien are expanding seamless and wireless bra lines, while fashion companies like Shinsegae International have also entered the comfort-focused innerwear segment, intensifying competition.

Notably, majority shareholder Japan Wacoal Holdings has separately announced plans to enter the automotive interior materials market using its 3D-molding textile technology, though this is a strategic move by a distinct corporate entity and does not directly alter Shinyoung Wacoal's own domestic business structure.

Given its domestically focused business, the company remains sensitive to consumer sentiment and the broader economy, though underwear's status as a near-essential good supports baseline demand, and growing health and wellness interest is cited as a relative tailwind for premium and functional product lines.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual revenue rose slightly from KRW 201.4 billion in 2022 to KRW 202.8 billion in 2023, before declining for two consecutive years to KRW 196.7 billion in 2024 and KRW 185.2 billion in 2025, a roughly 8% drop over four years.

Operating margin fluctuated from 3.3% in 2022 to 1.1% in 2023, 1.4% in 2024, and 0.7% in 2025, showing an overall thinning trend.

In 2025, revenue fell 5.9% year-on-year to KRW 185.15 billion from KRW 196.68 billion, and operating profit plunged 53.2% to KRW 1.29 billion from KRW 2.76 billion, which the company attributed to lower revenue and rising raw material costs.

In contrast, 2025 net income attributable to owners rose 60.8% year-on-year to KRW 6.27 billion from KRW 3.90 billion, which the company said reflected temporary non-operating gains from subsidiaries and affiliates.

Looking at the quarterly pattern, the company posted revenue of KRW 57.89 billion with an operating loss of KRW 0.13 billion in 2Q 2025, then swung to an operating profit of KRW 2.78 billion on revenue of KRW 47.54 billion in 3Q 2025, before revenue collapsed to KRW 33.52 billion in 4Q 2025 alongside a sizable operating loss of KRW 3.34 billion and a net loss of KRW 3.40 billion.

In 2026, the company returned to small-scale profitability with revenue of KRW 44.20 billion and operating and net profit of KRW 0.18 billion each in 1Q, while 2Q revenue climbed to KRW 59.37 billion even as operating profit slipped to a small loss of KRW 0.045 billion, with net income of KRW 0.21 billion.

As a result, net income attributable to owners over the most recent four quarters (3Q 2025 through 2Q 2026) totaled roughly KRW 1.63 billion, well below full-year 2025 net income of KRW 6.27 billion, underscoring how the large 4Q 2025 loss weighed on the recent earnings trend.

Overall, core operating profitability has structurally thinned in recent years, while net income defense has increasingly relied on factors outside the core business.

05

Industry analysis

According to a survey by the Korea Fashion Industry Association, the domestic underwear market has been estimated at roughly KRW 2 trillion, a mature market with limited growth competed for by numerous players.

The global lingerie market was estimated at USD 66 billion in 2025 and is projected to grow at a 5.7% compound annual rate between 2026 and 2035, suggesting relatively more growth headroom overseas than domestically.

The ongoing shift in consumer purchasing channels from department stores and franchise outlets toward online and home-shopping platforms remains a persistent challenge for Shinyoung Wacoal, which has traditionally held a strong position in department-store channels.

Competitors are responding with comfort-focused seamless and wireless bralette products, with BYC and Ssangbangwool's Vivien expanding related lines and fashion companies such as Shinsegae International also entering the market with growing bralette sales.

This rise in new entrants adds to the burden of defending market share for traditional underwear specialists. Raw material price volatility directly affects the cost structure across the industry, and Shinyoung Wacoal itself cited rising raw material costs as a key driver behind its weaker 2025 operating performance.

Given its heavy reliance on large offline retail networks such as department stores and franchise agencies, the business remains sensitive to macro factors like offline foot traffic trends and consumer sentiment.

Outlook

The company attributed its weak 2025 performance to declining revenue and rising raw material costs, suggesting cost management will remain a key challenge into 2026. Both 1Q and 2Q 2026 operating profit hovered near breakeven, pointing more toward a gradual stabilization phase than a clear margin recovery signal.

According to corporate information providers such as FnGuide, the company is responding through CRM- and 3D body scanner-based customized products along with expansion into online and live-commerce channels, and the actual revenue contribution of this channel diversification will likely be a key point to monitor going forward.

Given that the fourth quarter has proven seasonally weak (with an operating and net loss in 4Q 2025), whether 4Q 2026 repeats a similar pattern is a central item to watch.

No specific mid- to long-term capacity expansion, major investment, or overseas expansion plans have been disclosed; the company appears focused on maintaining its existing brand portfolio and distribution network while pursuing gradual channel transition.

On the net income side, the presence or absence of one-time gains from subsidiaries and affiliates is likely to continue driving quarter-to-quarter volatility, making it important going forward to distinguish core operating profit from net income that includes non-recurring items.

Bull factors

Department-Store Channel Dominance and Brand Strength

The company holds a 54.1% market share within department-store sales, preserving a dominant position in the traditional retail channel. Its flagship brand Venus previously ranked first in brand power in the underwear and lingerie category for 25 consecutive years in a past survey, underscoring strong brand equity.

It also owns Remamma, the only domestic underwear brand dedicated to women who have undergone breast cancer surgery, giving it a hard-to-replicate niche position.

Stable Financial Structure and Cash Flow

The debt ratio is very low, in the single-to-low-double-digit range, providing significant financial flexibility. Operating cash flow has remained positive in all of the most recent four fiscal years, demonstrating stable cash generation. This low leverage can serve as a buffer against short-term shocks such as raw material cost increases.

Attempted Distribution Channel Diversification

The company is described as responding to shifting consumption patterns through customized product development using CRM systems and 3D body scanners, along with expanding online and live-commerce channels. This can be viewed as an attempt to offset the weakness of its offline-dependent structure.

On the ESG front, the company has also continued sustainability-related initiatives such as its bra recycling campaign.

Bear factors

Four Years of Revenue Stagnation and Margin Compression

Revenue peaked at KRW 202.8 billion in 2023 before declining in both 2024 and 2025 to KRW 185.2 billion. Operating margin also thinned markedly, from 3.3% in 2022 to 0.7% in 2025. This suggests a structural weakening in the core business's profit-generating capacity.

Net Income Defense Reliant on Non-Core Gains

While 2025 net income rose 60.8% year-on-year, the company itself attributed this to temporary non-operating gains from subsidiaries and affiliates. Operating profit over the same period actually fell 53.2%, meaning core business results and net income diverged in direction. This reliance on one-off factors could continue to amplify earnings volatility going forward.

Intensifying Competition and Channel Structure Risk

BYC, Ssangbangwool's Vivien, and Shinsegae International have entered the market with comfort-focused seamless and bralette products, intensifying competition.

The shift in consumption channels from department stores toward online and home shopping poses a structural burden for a company heavily reliant on department stores. In 4Q 2025, revenue collapsed to KRW 33.5 billion alongside a large operating and net loss, exposing seasonal vulnerability as well.

Risk factors

Raw Material and Cost Risk

The company explicitly cited rising raw material costs as a direct driver of the sharp decline in 2025 operating profit. Fluctuations in global prices for yarns and materials can directly impact operating results. If cost pressures persist, the already-thin operating margin could face further compression.

Distribution Channel Transition Risk

Given the company's heavy reliance on department-store sales, the trend of consumers shifting purchases toward online and home-shopping channels continues.

While the company is pursuing expansion of online and live-commerce channels, the profitability and revenue contribution of these channels relative to offline sales remain unproven. If this channel transition lags, the revenue decline trend could persist longer than expected.

Governance and Shareholder Structure Risk

Japan's Wacoal Holdings remains the second-largest shareholder with a 25% stake, and brand identity controversy has surfaced in the past during periods of rising anti-Japanese sentiment.

The controlling shareholder side consists of the founding family and affiliated companies holding stakes in a distributed manner rather than being highly concentrated in a single individual or entity.

The fact that majority shareholder Japan Wacoal Holdings has announced diversification into automotive parts is also a factor worth monitoring for potential shifts in brand partnership priorities going forward.

Overall view

Shinyoung Wacoal maintains a distinctive position in Korea's women's underwear market, underpinned by high market share within department-store channels and long-accumulated brand power.

However, revenue has declined for two straight years after peaking in 2023, and operating margin has thinned from 3.3% in 2022 to 0.7% in 2025, pointing to a structural weakening in core profit generation.

While 2025 net income rose sharply year-on-year, the company itself attributed this to temporary non-operating gains from subsidiaries and affiliates, a signal that should be interpreted separately from core business improvement.

Quarterly results have shown pronounced volatility rather than a clear direction, mixing a large 4Q 2025 loss with modest profits in the first half of 2026.

The financial structure remains relatively stable, supported by a low debt ratio and consistently positive operating cash flow, providing some buffer against short-term shocks.

Distribution channel shifts, raw material cost trends, and whether reliance on one-off gains eases going forward remain the key points to watch in assessing this company's performance.
