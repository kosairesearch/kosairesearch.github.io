Business structure

04

Earnings analysis

On a reported basis, consolidated revenue was KRW 201.4 billion in 2022 and KRW 202.8 billion in 2023 (up 0.7% year on year), then fell 3.0% to KRW 196.7 billion in 2024 and 5.9% to KRW 185.2 billion in 2025, a two-year decline since the 2023 peak.

The company attributed the 2025 operating profit decline to lower sales and higher raw material prices. Operating profit went from KRW 6.61 billion in 2022 to KRW 2.16 billion in 2023, KRW 2.76 billion in 2024 and KRW 1.29 billion in 2025, a 53.2% fall that makes 2025 the smallest year in the 2022 to 2025 range.

Net income for 2025 rose 60.8% to KRW 6.27 billion, but the company explained this as a temporary rise in non-operating income at subsidiaries and associates, so profit growth did not come from core operations.

Operating cash flow rose from KRW 775 million in 2023 to KRW 8.95 billion in 2025, moving in the opposite direction to operating profit.

Quarterly results show Q3 2025 revenue of KRW 47.5 billion with operating profit of KRW 2.78 billion, followed by Q4 2025 revenue of KRW 33.5 billion (down 29.5% from the prior quarter) and an operating loss of KRW 3.34 billion.

Q1 2026 returned to operating profit of KRW 180 million on revenue of KRW 44.2 billion, while Q2 2026 revenue of KRW 59.4 billion came with an operating loss of KRW 45.06 million.

Q3 2026 revenue was KRW 44.3 billion, down 6.8% year on year, with an operating loss of KRW 1.32 billion, and a news report put the cumulative January to September 2026 operating loss at KRW 1.2 billion. Net income attributable to controlling shareholders reached KRW 55.8 billion in Q3 2026, up 1,100.6% year on year.

This coincides with a fixed-asset disposal disclosed on August 7, 2026, and with the parent Wacoal Holdings saying it would record its share of a Korean affiliate's fixed-asset sale gain as investment income. The exact breakdown should be checked in the quarterly report notes.

Industry analysis

Shinyoung Wacoal operates in innerwear, a category sensitive to department store and online consumption trends. Revenue has fallen for two consecutive years since the 2023 peak, which points to a downward phase in the sales cycle.

Rising raw material prices are the factor the company itself cited for the 2025 operating profit decline. The parent group's picture is mixed: Wacoal Holdings said e-commerce was strong in the United States and Europe for April to September 2026, while Japan lagged due to store closures.

In the same announcement, China revenue rose on currency effects, but profit fell because of clearance sales and restructuring costs. These are group-level comments and do not directly describe the Korean business.

On competition, BYC is a direct innerwear peer, and Handsome, F&F and LF share the fashion retail backdrop. Shinyoung Wacoal is leaning on its own online mall CRM and body measurement services to use customer data in channel competition. Direction in this industry can be gauged by whether revenue declines stop in upcoming quarterly reports.

Risk factors

Costs and Raw Materials

The company cited higher prices of key raw materials as a factor in the 2025 operating profit decline. If costs cannot be passed on to selling prices, margins could narrow further. Raw material trends and pricing adjustments will show up in future operating margins.

Dividend Sustainability

The company pledged continuous dividends for the next three years, while its target of keeping operating profit in the black for 2026 depends on Q4 results. The 2025 payout ratio of 25.1% shows that dividends were set as a share of earnings.

Maintaining dividends while operating losses persist could widen the gap between reported profit and cash returned to shareholders. The link between core earnings and dividend policy should be checked around the fiscal 2026 dividend resolution in March 2027.

Investment Execution Costs

The CRM build for the company's online mall and the additional 3D body scanners are the sales recovery tools the company cited. If the costs of building and running these two projects are booked in selling and administrative expenses, they could add pressure to an operating profit that is already in loss.

If the sales effect arrives later than planned, the cost burden could appear first. The scale of execution costs and their timing should be tracked through quarterly operating expense trends.
