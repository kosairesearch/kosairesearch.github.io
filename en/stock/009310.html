02

Business structure

Charm Engineering, founded in 1973, is a maker of flat panel display (FPD) and semiconductor equipment that has been listed on the KOSPI since 1987.

Its core product is a laser repair tool that fixes manufacturing defects in OLED and other display panels, and industry profiles describe it as holding the top share of the domestic OLED repair equipment market.

The business is split into a domestic segment covering development and production of FPD defect repair equipment and an overseas segment covering procurement and maintenance of parts through the Shanghai unit Charm Trading Shanghai.

Segment revenue shares should be checked in the segment information of the business report. Its new-growth businesses include metal 3D printers and atmospheric-pressure scanning electron microscopes built on laser and charged-particle beam control technology.

Micro LED repair products are described as already delivered to customers, and flexible display inspection equipment is described as having completed technology development. Customers are display panel makers, and equipment demand moves with their facility investment and repair needs.

On April 27, 2026, the company disclosed two copper supply contracts: KRW 10.35 billion with Hanmin, equal to 17.3% of recent revenue, and KRW 5.18 billion with C-Metal, equal to 8.65%.

Both were classified as product supply, so how much of 2026 quarterly revenue growth they explain should be checked in the quarterly report's revenue breakdown. Management also changed: after the March 26, 2026 general meeting, CEO Lee Kyung-hee resigned and Ahn Kyung-hwan was appointed CEO.

Earnings analysis

Annual results show revenue falling while losses narrowed. Revenue dropped from KRW 170.6 billion in 2022 to KRW 76.3 billion in 2023 (down 55.3% year on year), then to KRW 64.9 billion in 2024 (down 14.9%) and KRW 59.8 billion in 2025 (down 7.8%), a three-year decline from 2023 through 2025.

Within 2022 to 2025, 2022 was the largest year for revenue and 2025 the smallest.

Operating profit of KRW 1.31 billion in 2022 turned into an operating loss of KRW 28.1 billion in 2023, the loss widened to KRW 35.8 billion in 2024, and it narrowed to KRW 7.53 billion in 2025, making 2023 through 2025 three consecutive loss years.

Operating margins were 0.8% in 2022, -36.8% in 2023, -55.2% in 2024 and -12.6% in 2025. Net loss attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 2.27 billion in 2022, KRW 26 billion in 2023, KRW 24.9 billion in 2024 and KRW 13.6 billion in 2025, which makes 2022 through 2025 four consecutive loss years.

Net income including non-controlling interests is reported at the same amounts for each year. For 2025, the company attributed the smaller loss mainly to the effect of financial restructuring at subsidiaries, according to its provisional disclosure dated February 24, 2026.

That effect alone did not lift the consolidated operating margin into profit, which stood at -12.6% in 2025. The debt ratio rose from 759.0% in 2022 to 1,154.4% in 2025. Operating cash flow went from KRW 8.64 billion in 2022 to outflows of KRW 8.32 billion in 2023, KRW 11.1 billion in 2024 and KRW 11.9 billion in 2025.

Quarterly revenue bottomed at KRW 13.2 billion in 2025 Q4 (down 27.6% from the prior quarter), then rose to KRW 22.6 billion in 2026 Q1 (up 71.3%), KRW 33.8 billion in Q2 (up 49.9%) and KRW 34.7 billion in Q3 (up 2.6% quarter on quarter and up 90.8% year on year). Profit moved around more than revenue did.

Operating profit of KRW 5.38 billion in 2026 Q1 was followed by an operating loss of KRW 2.46 billion in Q2 and an operating profit of KRW 1.46 billion in Q3. Net income attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 4.19 billion in Q1 and KRW 666 million in Q3, with a net loss of KRW 862 million in Q2.

Over the last four quarters, net income attributable to controlling shareholders totals KRW 956 million, so the growth in revenue has not built up steadily into profit and quarter-to-quarter swings remain large.

Industry analysis

Demand for the company's equipment is tied to facility investment and repair needs at OLED and other display panel makers.

Fnguide company data show that cumulative consolidated revenue for the first nine months of 2025 fell 9.3% year on year and the operating loss shrank 70.4%, with OLED market expansion and intensifying competition from China cited as the background.

The company therefore sits between the benefit of a growing OLED market and the pressure of price competition. Where the sector stands in the cycle should be judged alongside panel makers' facility investment announcements in 2026.

On competition, the company is described as the top holder of the domestic OLED repair equipment market, though that claim comes from an industry profile. Competition from Chinese suppliers can affect the pricing and win rates of OLED equipment.

Micro LED repair and flexible display inspection are newer areas that may contribute revenue later than the core FPD repair business.

Compared with large listed companies in the electronics and components sector, Charm Engineering is a small-cap by market value, and its business is concentrated on FPD repair equipment plus two new lines, a niche structure within the sector.
