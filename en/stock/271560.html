Report overview

Overseas Growth Widens as Profit Trends Diverge

Orion's revenue keeps rising on growth at its China, Vietnam and Russia units, but net profit attributable to controlling shareholders fell 46.4% year on year in the third quarter of 2026, so top-line and bottom-line trends are diverging.

  2. 2

02

Business structure

Orion makes and sells snack and confectionery products, including Choco Pie, Poca Chip, Swing Chip and Castella, across Korea, China, Vietnam, Russia and India. Overseas sales were KRW 2.19 trillion in 2025, or 65.7% of consolidated revenue of KRW 3.33 trillion.

China was the largest market at KRW 1.32 trillion, about 39% of the total, while Vietnam contributed KRW 538.1 billion, about 16%, and China's share has fallen from 49% in 2020.

In China, the company has pushed dedicated products for snack shops and e-commerce alongside potato snacks, pies and jelly, and third-quarter 2026 China revenue was KRW 384.0 billion, up 13.8% year on year.

Vietnam's third-quarter 2026 revenue rose 8.4% to KRW 130.6 billion, but operating profit fell 0.8% to KRW 21.1 billion as raw material prices increased.

In Russia, the company broadened its range beyond Choco Pie to include Watermelon Pie, Fresh Pie, Chambungeo-ppang and jelly, and first-half 2026 revenue rose 32.1% to KRW 195.5 billion. Major Russian customers include X5 and Tander, large retail chains.

Domestic rivals include Lotte Wellfood and Nongshim, while overseas the company competes with local confectionery makers. The Vietnam unit also serves as an export base for Southeast Asia.

04

Earnings analysis

On a disclosed annual basis, revenue rose from KRW 2.87 trillion in 2022 to KRW 3.33 trillion in 2025, with three straight years of increases from 2023 to 2025. Operating profit grew from KRW 466.7 billion in 2022 to KRW 558.3 billion in 2025, but the 2025 gain of 2.7% trailed revenue growth of 7.3%.

As a result, operating margin peaked at 17.5% in 2024 and eased to 16.8% in 2025. A Chosun report citing the business report attributed the 2025 operating profit declines at the China unit (0.9%) and the Vietnam unit (3.6%) directly to raw material cost pressure.

Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders was KRW 392.4 billion in 2022, KRW 376.6 billion in 2023 (down 4.0%), KRW 524.6 billion in 2024 (up 39.3%) and KRW 382.7 billion in 2025 (down 27.0%); 2024 was the largest year and 2023 the smallest in the period.

Net profit including non-controlling interests was KRW 390.6 billion in 2025, a different figure from the attributable number. The debt ratio fell from 27.8% in 2022 to 17.5% in 2025, and total equity at year-end 2025 was KRW 3.91 trillion.

Operating cash flow was KRW 537.3 billion in 2025, down from KRW 651.6 billion in 2024.

Quarterly revenue rose to KRW 924.6 billion in the fourth quarter of 2025 (up 11.5% from the prior quarter), then moved to KRW 930.4 billion in the first quarter of 2026, KRW 893.6 billion in the second (down 4.0%) and KRW 922.7 billion in the third (up 3.3% quarter on quarter and 11.3% year on year).

Operating profit fell from KRW 165.5 billion in the first quarter of 2026 to KRW 132.6 billion in the second (down 19.9%) before recovering to KRW 150.9 billion in the third (up 13.8%). In its first-half 2026 results, the company said manufacturing and selling expense burdens had increased.

Attributable net profit peaked at KRW 124.5 billion in the first quarter of 2026, then fell to KRW 86.2 billion and KRW 55.1 billion over the next two quarters. Third-quarter operating profit rose 9.4% while attributable net profit fell 46.4%, so the two measures moved in opposite directions. Over the last four quarters, attributable net profit totaled KRW 371.6 billion.

05

Industry analysis

Overseas sales accounted for 65.7% of revenue in 2025, so the center of gravity of growth sits abroad. In China, the snack-shop channel is expanding quickly.

Hana Securities estimated in a September 14, 2026 report that the snack-shop channel rose from roughly 2% of sales in 2020 to 26% in 2025 and would exceed 30% in 2026.

Traditional retail channels in China are shrinking, and LS Securities said in a November 4, 2026 report that new listings in snack shops are offsetting that decline.

On the production side, Swing Chip line utilization in China topped 150%, and the company plans three additional lines next year to expand capacity by about 90%.

In Russia, the second Tver plant expansion is targeted for September 2027, and Kyobo Securities said in an April 2026 report that production lines would rise from 15 to 31.

On inputs, Shinhan Securities expected lower cocoa prices to ease costs in the second half of 2026, while Kiwoom Securities flagged rising packaging costs. On currency, the strengthening won reduces the won value of overseas earnings.

According to a Chosun report, Vietnam's operating margin in the first quarter of 2026 preliminary results was 18.8%, above China's 14.1%.

06

Outlook

In its first-half 2026 results, the company said it expects to bring forward the goal of KRW 5 trillion in revenue and KRW 1 trillion in operating profit. The same release said that new snack, pie and jelly lines starting operations in the second half would accelerate growth.

A growth-enhancement plan disclosed on March 26, 2026 included domestic and overseas production investment of KRW 830 billion or more, expansion of global business and export markets.

In Vietnam, the Hanoi No. 3 plant and the Da Nang logistics center began operating from the fourth quarter of 2026, and construction of the Ho Chi Minh No. 4 plant is planned for 2027. The company has said it will expand annual capacity in Vietnam to roughly KRW 1 trillion.

In Russia, the new Tver plant is expected to be completed in 2027 and lift annual capacity to about KRW 750 billion, roughly double the current level, according to Daily Korea. On shareholder returns, the company paid its first quarterly dividend since its founding and has said it will keep raising the dividend rate.

Among brokerages, Hana Securities set a target price of KRW 220,000 in a September 14, 2026 report, and NH Investment & Securities set KRW 170,000 the same day. Kiwoom Securities cut its target from KRW 175,000 to KRW 155,000 on September 14, 2026, citing the stronger won. Shinhan Securities set KRW 160,000 on August 11, 2026, and LS Securities set KRW 180,000 on November 4, 2026.

08

Bull factors

Double-digit growth at overseas units

The company said Chinese, Vietnamese and Russian units all posted double-digit growth in both sales and operating profit in the first half of 2026. Hana Securities estimated the snack-shop share in China would exceed 30% in 2026, which it cited as evidence the growth channel is intact.

LS Securities analyzed that Russian sales grew more than 20% in local-currency terms in the third quarter of 2026. New lines that began operating in the fourth quarter of 2026 are expected to widen room to sell products that are in short supply.

Possible easing of cost pressure

Shinhan Securities said in an August 11, 2026 report that lower cocoa prices and a stronger won could ease external cost burdens. Daol Investment & Securities said in a February 2026 report that continued declines in raw material prices, such as gelatin, whole milk powder and potatoes, could support margin improvement.

The same report noted that manufacturing cost ratios at the China and Russia units improved year on year. How much of this easing shows up in second-half 2026 results is a matter for quarterly disclosures.

Expanding shareholder returns

Orion paid its first quarterly dividend since its founding and said it will keep raising the dividend rate. Kiwoom Securities noted that the payout ratio and dividend per share have been raised steadily over the past three years. LS Securities described the shareholder-return push as clear within the food sector. The 2026 growth-enhancement plan also referenced stronger shareholder returns.

09

Bear factors

Third-quarter profit decline and diverging metrics

Attributable net profit for the third quarter of 2026 was KRW 55.1 billion, down 46.4% year on year, and has fallen for two consecutive quarters. Over the same period operating profit rose 9.4%, but the Korean and Vietnamese units saw operating profit decline as energy and raw material costs rose.

Whether this divergence is one-off or structural is a question for the non-operating items in the 2026 annual report.

Stronger won and concern over China growth

A stronger won reduces the won value of overseas earnings, and Kiwoom Securities cut its target price from KRW 175,000 to KRW 155,000 in a September 14, 2026 report. LS Securities named the slowdown in China sales growth and higher-than-expected costs as the main concerns in July to September results. The shrinking traditional retail channel in China is also cited as a negative.

Cost pressure and Middle East conflict effects

The company said that prolonged Middle East conflict pushed up energy, logistics and raw material costs, adding to expense pressure in the first half of 2026. Kiwoom Securities said rising packaging costs make a possible price increase over the medium term worth watching. As capacity expansion continues, depreciation and utilization management also remain challenges.

10

Risk factors

Currency

A stronger won reduces the won value of overseas sales and operating profit. Growth in local-currency terms can still translate into smaller figures in won. Kiwoom Securities cut its earnings estimates in a September 2026 report to reflect the stronger won.

Because the company has large exposure to local currencies in Vietnam and Russia, currency swings can have a sizable effect on quarterly results.

Raw material and energy costs

Swings in cocoa, packaging and energy costs largely determine operating margin. The company cited higher energy and logistics costs from the prolonged Middle East conflict as a burden in the first half of 2026. The Vietnam unit's operating profit fell in the third quarter of 2026 because of higher raw material prices. Without price increases, the pace of margin recovery could be constrained.

China channel structure and competition

China still accounts for about 39% of revenue, making it the largest market. As traditional retail shrinks and sales shift toward snack shops and e-commerce, competition within those channels could intensify.

LS Securities noted that last year also passed through a phase of stronger competition after the Lunar New Year effect, and judged that a structural slowdown this year is too early to call. Whether new production lines expand in step with utilization is also worth tracking.

Overall view

Orion's revenue rose to KRW 3.33 trillion in 2025 on growth at its China, Vietnam and Russia units, and overseas sales accounted for 65.7% of the total that year. Operating profit rose to KRW 558.3 billion in 2025, but operating margin eased from 17.5% in 2024 to 16.8% in 2025.

In the third quarter of 2026, revenue of KRW 922.7 billion and operating profit of KRW 150.9 billion were both higher than a year earlier, yet attributable net profit fell 46.4% to KRW 55.1 billion, so the earnings picture is mixed.

Strengths include snack-channel expansion, overseas capacity additions, the possibility of easing cost pressure and the introduction of quarterly dividends.

Weaknesses include two consecutive quarterly declines in attributable net profit, a stronger won that reduces the won value of overseas results, raw material, energy and packaging cost pressure, and concern about slowing growth in China.

Brokerages all carried buy ratings between August and November 2026, but target prices varied by firm, such as Hana Securities at KRW 220,000 and Kiwoom Securities at KRW 155,000.

The next checkpoints are the 2026 fourth-quarter preliminary results, the non-operating items in the 2026 annual report, and whether new production lines in China and the Russian expansion come online.
