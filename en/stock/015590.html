Governance Reset Meets Earnings Volatility

02

Business structure

Founded in Ulsan in 1981 and listed on KOSPI in 1989, DKME is a chemical equipment manufacturer that changed its corporate name from KIB Plug Energy to DKME in May 2025.

Its core products are shell-and-tube heat exchangers, pressure vessels, reactors, and towers/columns, key equipment used in petrochemical, LNG, and gas plants. Per regulatory filings, revenue is reported under a single chemical-equipment segment, which accounts for 100% of sales.

Exports represent roughly 85% of revenue, with supply to global EPC (engineering, procurement, and construction) contractors forming the core of its business.

In June 2026, the company signed a supply contract with global EPC firm TECHNIP for a Qatar LNG plant, re-establishing its presence in a major overseas project supply chain.

Dukyang Energen (industrial hydrogen production) and Wolf (industrial boiler manufacturing), both Ulsan-based energy and plant equipment companies that acquired control in May 2026, have publicly stated plans to expand a hydrogen and plant value chain combined with DKME's manufacturing capabilities.

The competitive landscape consists of order-based competition among domestic and overseas chemical equipment and heat-exchanger makers, and because revenue is project-based, quarterly results in this industry tend to be volatile.

The company holds shell-and-tube heat exchanger technology; a prior acquisition proposal had floated integrating next-generation PCHE (printed circuit heat exchanger) technology, but with the change in the eventual acquirer, the current status of that plan is unconfirmed.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual revenue rose sharply from KRW 105.7 billion in 2022 to KRW 137.3 billion in 2023 (+29.9%), then edged down for two consecutive years to KRW 136.6 billion in 2024 and KRW 133.4 billion in 2025.

The operating margin declined from 12.2% in 2022 to 9.4% in 2023, 7.3% in 2024, and then stabilized slightly higher at 7.5% in 2025.

Net income attributable to owners followed a different path: after peaking at KRW 12.75 billion in 2023, it fell sharply to KRW 2.28 billion in 2024, and in 2025, despite operating profit holding near the 2024 level, net income swung to a loss of KRW -2.88 billion.

This widening gap between operating profit and net income suggests a growing impact from below-the-line items such as financial costs and foreign-exchange effects.

Cash flow told a similar story: operating cash flow moved from net inflows of KRW 14.3 billion in 2022 and KRW 6.5 billion in 2023 to net outflows of KRW -6.1 billion in 2024 and KRW -4.4 billion in 2025, widening the gap between reported earnings and cash generation.

On a quarterly basis, both revenue and operating profit declined in tandem from KRW 35.6 billion and KRW 5.8 billion in 2Q25 to KRW 33.4 billion and KRW 0.3 billion in 3Q25, and KRW 27.2 billion and KRW 0.4 billion in 4Q25. 1Q26 showed improved net income (revenue KRW 20.4 billion, operating profit KRW 1.2 billion, net income KRW 4.6 billion), but 2Q26 revenue fell further to KRW 16.5 billion with an operating loss of roughly KRW -5.5 billion and a net loss of about KRW -5.8 billion, marking the most volatile recent quarter.

This period overlaps with the confusion surrounding the ownership transfer and trading halt, raising the possibility that production and order activity were affected.

05

Industry analysis

The chemical equipment and heat-exchanger industry is an order-driven business tied to global petrochemical, LNG, and gas plant expansion cycles, with revenue recognition spread out according to project award timing.

In June 2026, equipment orders tied to QatarEnergy's LNG and NGL plant expansion, awarded through TECHNIP, flowed to Korean chemical equipment makers, resulting in DKME's large order win.

Industry participants have noted that heat-exchanger demand is rising alongside power-infrastructure expansion driven by AI adoption, and the possibility of entering small modular reactor (SMR)-related business has also been discussed, though this has not yet been confirmed through a specific contract.

New controlling shareholder Dukyang Energen holds a stable demand base including S-Oil's 'Shaheen Project' and has stated it is pursuing EPC business expansion targeting domestic aging hydrogen plant replacement demand.

Competitively, DKME competes for orders against domestic and overseas chemical equipment and heat-exchanger makers, and the recent ownership change is intended to link Wolf's plant construction capabilities and Dukyang Energen's hydrogen production and supply capabilities with DKME's manufacturing.

From a cycle perspective, the resumption of overseas LNG order flow and the recovery of domestic petrochemical capital investment stand out as key variables for future order trends.

06

Outlook

The company faces progress-based revenue recognition on its roughly KRW 38.1 billion Qatar LNG plant equipment contract with TECHNIP, signed in June 2026 and running through February 2028, making the timing of this revenue flowing into quarterly results a key point to watch.

Industry observers have suggested that follow-on orders tied to Qatar's LNG expansion remain pending in the market, implying potential for additional order wins.

The new controlling consortium, Dukyang Energen and Wolf, which completed the ownership transfer in May 2026, has stated management stabilization and the building of a mid-to-long-term growth foundation through linkage with existing businesses as formal goals.

However, a September 2026 change in the controlling shareholder structure to a "Dukyang Energen and three others" arrangement, with the group's combined stake falling from 38.54% to 20.33%, suggests further shareholding shifts are possible before governance fully settles.

The company also disclosed filings related to a control-dispute lawsuit as recently as July 2026, indicating that legal follow-up from the earlier dispute remains ongoing.

The next regular filing, the 3Q26 report, is due by the statutory deadline of November 16, 2026, which should show whether the 2Q26 operating loss persists and how much of the Qatar order has begun flowing into revenue.

Bull factors

Large Overseas Order Secured

The roughly KRW 38.1 billion Qatar LNG plant equipment contract signed with TECHNIP in June 2026 equals about 28.5% of FY2025 revenue. Follow-on orders tied to QatarEnergy's LNG and NGL expansion project are considered pending in the market, implying room for additional order wins.

Given the company's 85% export ratio, re-entry into this overseas large-project supply chain can be viewed as a positive factor for expanding its revenue base.

New Controlling Shareholders' Business Linkage Strategy

Dukyang Energen and Wolf, which acquired control in May 2026, are both Ulsan-based energy and plant equipment companies with industrial hydrogen production and industrial boiler manufacturing capabilities, respectively.

The two companies have stated an official goal of expanding EPC business by combining DKME's chemical equipment manufacturing capabilities with existing demand bases such as S-Oil's Shaheen Project.

If the strategy targeting aging hydrogen plant replacement demand materializes, it could open up additional order channels.

Potential Industry Demand Expansion

Industry participants have noted that heat-exchanger demand is rising alongside power-infrastructure expansion driven by AI adoption. The possibility of entering small modular reactor (SMR)-related business has also been discussed, though this has not yet been confirmed through a specific contract.

If power and energy infrastructure investment continues, order opportunities across the chemical equipment industry could increase.

Bear factors

Sharp Recent-Quarter Revenue and Profitability Decline

2Q26 revenue fell to about KRW 16.5 billion, less than half of the KRW 35.6 billion recorded in 2Q25, while operating profit swung from a KRW 5.8 billion surplus to roughly a KRW -5.5 billion loss over the same period. Revenue has declined in consecutive quarters from 3Q25 through 2Q26.

This period overlaps with confusion surrounding the ownership transfer and trading halt, raising the possibility that production and order activity were affected.

History of Governance Instability

DKME experienced a trading halt from November 2024 due to listing eligibility review stemming from former management's alleged embezzlement and breach of trust and major-shareholder qualification issues, during which the largest shareholder changed five times.

While the ownership transfer was completed in May 2026, the controlling shareholder group's combined stake was further reduced from 38.54% to 20.33% in a September 2026 change, and a control-dispute lawsuit was also disclosed in July. It may still be premature to conclude that governance has fully stabilized.

Widening Gap Between Operating Profit and Net Income

Although FY2025 operating profit held near the FY2024 level of around KRW 10 billion, net income swung to a loss of about KRW -2.88 billion. Operating cash flow also posted consecutive net outflows in both 2024 and 2025, widening the gap between reported earnings and cash generation.

If volatility in non-operating items continues, there is a risk that operating profit improvement may not translate into net income.

10

Risk factors

Legal and Governance Risk

A control-dispute lawsuit filing and ruling were disclosed as recently as July 2026, indicating legal follow-up from the earlier dispute is still ongoing.

The controlling shareholder group's stake structure was also changed again in September 2026, falling to 20.33%, and further shareholding shifts or renewed disputes cannot be ruled out. This uncertainty could affect the consistency of management decision-making.

Revenue Concentration and FX Risk

With exports accounting for roughly 85% of revenue and sales concentrated in a single chemical-equipment segment, delays or cancellations in orders from specific regions or customers could have an outsized impact on results.

The Qatar LNG contract was converted to won at a rate of KRW 1,522.40 per USD; given the contract runs through February 2028, exchange-rate fluctuations over this long period could affect actual won-denominated revenue and margins.

Execution Risk of Order-Based Revenue

DKME's revenue is based on project-level orders recognized on a progress basis as construction advances, making quarterly results highly volatile.

Periods such as 2Q26, where revenue decline and operating loss occurred simultaneously, could recur, and cost management and delivery schedule adherence on the new large contract are variables that could affect future profitability.

12

Overall view

DKME has restructured its governance under the Dukyang Energen-Wolf consortium as of May 2026 following a prolonged control dispute and trading halt, but governance stabilization remains a work in progress, as the controlling shareholder group's stake was reduced again in September 2026 and a related lawsuit was disclosed in July.

Annual results moved from a 2023 net income peak to a sharp 2024 decline and a 2025 swing to loss, while the first half of 2026 saw significant quarterly swings in revenue and operating profit, with the most recent quarter showing an expanded operating and net loss simultaneously.

On the other hand, the Qatar LNG order booked with TECHNIP in June 2026, equal to about 28.5% of FY2025 revenue, could mark a turning point for revenue recovery, and the industry is viewed as having room for follow-on orders.

Whether the new controlling shareholders' stated hydrogen and plant value-chain strategy translates into concrete order wins, and whether revenue and operating profit rebound from 3Q26 onward, are the key variables that will shape the earnings trajectory going forward.

The stock can be factually compared as trading at a discount to net asset value, and profitability-related valuation metrics are likely to become more meaningful once earnings stabilize. Governance risk, order execution risk, and currency exposure should all be weighed together before forming any investment judgment.
