02

Business structure

DKME was founded in 1981 and listed on the KOSPI in 1989, manufacturing shell-and-tube heat exchangers, pressure vessels, reactors, and towers/columns at three plants in the petrochemical complex of Ulsan.

Production capacity spans roughly 4,900 pyeong at Plant 1 for small-to-mid-size equipment, 5,560 pyeong at Plant 2 for mid-size equipment, and about 14,670 pyeong at Plant 3 for mid-to-large equipment.

The company's name changed from Daekyung Machinery Technology to Curo, then to KIB Plug Energy, before becoming DKME in May 2025. Its product lineup has broadened from heat exchangers for refining, petrochemical, and gas plants to industrial boilers, heat-recovery steam generators (HRSG), and cogeneration boilers.

Customers consist of global EPC (engineering, procurement, construction) contractors and end users worldwide, with an export share reported at around 85%, underscoring reliance on overseas plant projects. In June 2026, DKME signed an LNG plant equipment supply contract with global EPC firm Technip.

Domestic heat exchanger and chemical equipment makers such as BHI operate in similar business areas. In 2026, Dukyang Energen, seeking to expand its EPC business, acquired a stake in DKME citing the aim of securing heat exchanger manufacturing capability.

04

Earnings analysis

DKME's consolidated revenue rose 29.9% from KRW 105.7 billion in 2022 to a peak of KRW 137.3 billion in 2023, then fell for two straight years to KRW 136.6 billion in 2024 (down 0.5%) and KRW 133.4 billion in 2025 (down 2.4%).

Operating profit was essentially flat at KRW 12.9 billion in both 2022 and 2023 (down 0.4%), then dropped to KRW 10 billion in 2024 (down 22.2%) and stayed at KRW 10 billion in 2025 (unchanged). The operating margin slipped from 12.2% in 2022 to 9.4% in 2023 and 7.3% in 2024, before ticking up to 7.5% in 2025.

Controlling-interest net profit rose from KRW 11.3 billion in 2022 to KRW 12.8 billion in 2023 (up 13.1%), then plunged to KRW 2.28 billion in 2024 (down 82.1%) and swung to a controlling-interest net loss of KRW 2.88 billion in 2025.

The quarterly trajectory deteriorated more sharply: revenue fell for four straight quarters, from KRW 35.6 billion in Q2 2025 to KRW 33.4 billion in Q3 2025 (down 6.2%), KRW 27.2 billion in Q4 2025 (down 18.6%), KRW 20.4 billion in Q1 2026 (down 25.1%), and KRW 16.5 billion in Q2 2026 (down 19.1% quarter-on-quarter and 53.8% year-on-year).

Operating profit tumbled from KRW 5.84 billion in Q2 2025 to KRW 289 million in Q3 2025 (down 95.0%), recovered modestly to KRW 378 million in Q4 2025 and KRW 1.23 billion in Q1 2026, then swung to an operating loss of KRW 5.48 billion in Q2 2026, a reversal from both the prior quarter and the year-ago period.

Controlling-interest net profit turned positive at KRW 4.6 billion in Q1 2026 before swinging back to a controlling-interest net loss of KRW 5.81 billion in Q2 2026, continuing a loss streak with a wider deficit; the cumulative controlling-interest net loss over the last four quarters (Q3 2025 to Q2 2026) totaled KRW 4 billion.

Operating cash flow was positive at KRW 14.3 billion in 2022 and KRW 6.53 billion in 2023, then turned negative for two straight years at -KRW 6.06 billion in 2024 and -KRW 4.37 billion in 2025, tracking the broader profit slowdown.

05

Industry analysis

The chemical equipment industry is a classic order-driven business tied to new-build investment and turnaround maintenance cycles in refining, petrochemical, and gas (LNG) plants, as well as replacement and expansion cycles in power-generation plants.

Investment-banking circles have characterized the plant industry as entering a structural upcycle. In Korea, replacement demand for aging petrochemical and hydrogen plants is gaining attention, a factor cited as the strategic rationale behind Dukyang Energen's acquisition of a stake in DKME.

Overseas, orders for LNG and gas plant equipment placed through global EPC contractors continue to flow, and DKME itself secured an LNG plant equipment order worth roughly KRW 38.06 billion in June 2026.

Rising interest in power-facility expansion tied to artificial intelligence (AI) in the United States and elsewhere is also cited as lifting heat exchanger demand, with potential opportunities in small modular reactor (SMR)-related business also mentioned.

However, the order-driven nature of the business means quarterly revenue can swing significantly depending on the delivery timing of specific large projects. Domestic chemical equipment and heat exchanger makers such as BHI operate in similar business areas, competing and coexisting in the same space.

Outlook

In 2026, DKME moved to exit its governance dispute through third-party capital increases involving Dukyang Energen and Wolf and a transfer of controlling shares. Dukyang Energen cited the aim of securing DKME's heat exchanger manufacturing capability to address replacement demand for aging domestic hydrogen plants.

In June 2026, DKME signed a supply contract with Technip for LNG plant equipment running through February 2028, securing order backlog, though the spread-out delivery schedule may limit any reduction in quarterly revenue volatility.

The company worked on strengthening management transparency and governance to resolve a trading halt that had been in place since November 2024, and as of October 2026 shares appear to be trading again on the KOSPI.

Still, after multiple capital raises and a share consolidation that repeatedly altered the shareholder base and share count, further capital raising and potential dilution remain variables to watch.

The outcome of an investigation into the former CEO's alleged embezzlement and breach of trust, along with related governance litigation, also remains a factor that could affect governance stability.

Bull factors

Governance Risk Easing

After several changes of controlling shareholder following the November 2024 trading halt, ownership consolidated around Dukyang Energen and Wolf in 2026, moving the governance dispute toward resolution.

Parallel efforts to strengthen management transparency and internal controls have also eased pressure related to the exchange's listing eligibility review. Litigation involving the former CEO continues, but governance uncertainty appears to have diminished under the new controlling shareholder structure.

Order Win From A Global EPC Client

In June 2026, DKME signed an LNG plant equipment supply contract worth roughly KRW 38.06 billion with global EPC firm Technip, equivalent to about 28.5% of 2025 annual revenue and providing order backlog running through February 2028.

Given the company's export-heavy business structure, continued large overseas EPC orders could help support its revenue base.

Improved Balance Sheet Leverage

The debt-to-equity ratio fell from 75.4% in 2022 to 49.9% in 2025, an improving trend in financial stability. Capital stood at KRW 125.2 billion at the end of 2025, and capital raises conducted during 2026 further broadened the equity base. This could help cushion working-capital pressure inherent to an order-driven business.

Annual revenue declined for two straight years after peaking in 2023, and quarterly revenue fell for four consecutive quarters starting in Q2 2025. The Q2 2026 decline widened to 53.8% year-on-year.

Given the order-driven nature of the business, where revenue hinges on specific project delivery timing, quarterly volatility is likely to persist for the time being.

Quarterly Swing To Losses

The Q2 2026 operating loss of KRW 5.48 billion marked a swing from both the prior quarter and the year-earlier period.

Controlling-interest net profit also alternated between gains and losses on a quarterly basis before posting a controlling-interest net loss of KRW 5.81 billion in Q2 2026, continuing a loss streak with a widening deficit.

Share Dilution And Governance Uncertainty

Throughout 2026, multiple third-party capital increases and a 5-for-1 share consolidation repeatedly altered the share count and shareholder composition.

Whether the new controlling shareholder structure will stabilize remains to be seen, and investigations into the former CEO's alleged embezzlement and breach of trust, along with related litigation, are still ongoing. Further capital raises could increase dilution for existing shareholders.

Risk factors

Governance And Legal Risk

Investigations into the former CEO's alleged embezzlement and breach of trust, along with litigation tied to the governance dispute, remain ongoing.

The controlling shareholder has changed multiple times since 2024, and legal disputes including allegations of document forgery have also surfaced, meaning full normalization may take time.

Depending on litigation outcomes, there is a possibility of further instability for the new management or additional disclosure risk.

Order Concentration And Revenue Volatility Risk

DKME's revenue structure depends heavily on the timing of orders and deliveries for large plant projects, meaning delays or reductions in specific contracts can directly affect results.

When a single contract, such as the Technip agreement secured in June 2026, accounts for a substantial share of annual revenue, quarterly results can become more volatile depending on how that contract progresses.

Given the export-heavy business structure, currency fluctuations and delays in overseas clients' investment decisions could also be variables.

Cash Flow And Further Capital-Raising Risk

Operating cash flow was negative for two straight years in 2024 and 2025, revealing a gap between reported profit and cash generation. If this trend continues, the need for additional borrowing or capital increases to fund working capital could grow.

Having already gone through multiple rounds of capital raising, the impact of any further increases on existing shareholders' equity value also warrants attention.

Overall view

DKME went through governance turmoil in 2026 with multiple changes of controlling shareholder, but moved into a phase of partially eased risk following share acquisitions and capital raises involving Dukyang Energen and Wolf.

Operating results, however, declined for two straight years after peaking in 2023, and Q2 2026 saw revenue fall 53.8% year-on-year alongside an operating loss of KRW 5.48 billion.

On an annual basis, the 2025 controlling-interest net loss of KRW 2.88 billion extended the sharp profit decline seen in 2024 into an outright loss.

The pace of governance stabilization, progress on order fulfillment, and whether losses continue in upcoming quarterly results are all factors worth watching together.
