Report overview

Revenue Decline and Losses; Recovery Is the Key Question

Revenue has declined since its 2023 peak and the last four quarters carry cumulative attributable net losses, while the post-acquisition restructuring plan reported in April 2026 is the next checkpoint for DKME.

02

Business structure

DKME was founded in 1981 and listed in 1989 as a manufacturer of chemical process equipment.

Its core business is the chemical equipment segment, which makes heat exchangers, pressure vessels and storage tanks, and the company points to about 40 years of operating history and its position in overseas markets as strengths.

It has technology tie-ups with several licensors to develop specialized equipment in-house and raise quality, according to the company profile.

Orders come both through EPC (engineering, procurement and construction) contractors on projects and directly from end users in each country, and the company says it is expanding the direct channel.

By region, the Middle East rose from about one-third of sales in 2022 to about two-thirds in 2025 on a compiled basis from Simply Wall St-style data aggregator StockAnalysis, so concentration in one region has become pronounced. In the same compilation, Americas and South Korea sales swing widely from year to year.

DKME is a plant equipment maker that is much smaller than large power equipment groups, and in bidding, project-level engineering capability and EPC partnerships matter.

According to an Asia Economy report in April 2026, Energin signed a share purchase agreement with the former largest shareholder, DKME Inc., and completed the acquisition of management control.

04

Earnings analysis

Revenue rose from KRW 105.7 billion in 2022 to KRW 137.3 billion in 2023, up 29.9% year on year, making 2023 the largest year of 2022 to 2025. After that peak, revenue fell to KRW 136.6 billion in 2024 (down 0.5%) and KRW 133.4 billion in 2025 (down 2.4%), a two-year consecutive decline from 2024 through 2025.

Operating profit was KRW 12.9 billion in 2022 and KRW 12.9 billion in 2023 (down 0.4%), then dropped to KRW 10 billion in 2024 (down 22.2%), and was flat at KRW 10 billion in 2025. The operating margin fell from 12.2% in 2022 to 9.4% in 2023 and 7.3% in 2024, then edged up to 7.5% in 2025.

The slight margin recovery during a period of falling revenue suggests some stabilization in the cost structure.

Attributable net income was KRW 11.3 billion in 2022 and KRW 12.8 billion in 2023 (up 13.1%), then fell to KRW 2.28 billion in 2024 (down 82.1%) and turned into an attributable net loss of KRW 2.88 billion in 2025. The 2025 net loss was also KRW 2.88 billion.

Operating profit of KRW 10 billion against net income of KRW 2.28 billion in 2024 shows that items below operating profit, such as non-operating gains and losses and taxes, grew in weight.

Operating cash flow was KRW 14.3 billion in 2022 and KRW 6.53 billion in 2023, then turned negative at -KRW 6.06 billion in 2024 and -KRW 4.37 billion in 2025.

The debt-to-equity ratio declined from 75.4% in 2022 to 49.9% at year-end 2025, when total equity was KRW 125.2 billion, all of it attributable to shareholders of the parent, and total liabilities were KRW 62.4 billion.

Quarterly revenue moved from KRW 33.4 billion in Q3 2025 to KRW 27.2 billion in Q4 2025 (down 18.6% quarter on quarter), KRW 20.4 billion in Q1 2026 (down 25.1%), KRW 16.5 billion in Q2 2026 (down 19.1%) and KRW 13.4 billion in Q3 2026 (down 19.0%, and down 60.1% year on year).

Operating profit was KRW 289 million in Q3 2025 and KRW 378 million in Q4 2025, recovered to KRW 1.23 billion in Q1 2026 (up 227.0% quarter on quarter), then swung to an operating loss of KRW 5.48 billion in Q2 2026 before returning to an operating profit of KRW 147 million in Q3 2026 (down 49.3% year on year).

Attributable net results were a net income of KRW 3.13 billion in Q3 2025, a net loss of KRW 5.92 billion in Q4 2025, a net income of KRW 4.6 billion in Q1 2026, and net losses of KRW 5.81 billion in Q2 2026 and KRW 5.84 billion in Q3 2026.

Net losses have now occurred in two consecutive quarters, and the last four quarters total KRW 13 billion in attributable net losses. In Q1 2026, a foreign exchange gain of about KRW 1.6 billion appears in F&Guide's compiled data, which helps explain why that quarter's net income exceeded its operating profit.

In Q3 2026, an operating profit coexisted with a large attributable net loss, so the lines below operating profit now drive the reading of earnings.

05

Industry analysis

Demand from downstream customers is centered on power infrastructure expansion. A March 2026 Asia Economy report said that, with AI-driven interest in expanding power facilities in the United States and globally, demand for heat exchangers is rising, according to the company.

The same report mentioned the possibility of entering small modular reactor (SMR) related business. However, DKME's revenue has declined from its 2023 peak through Q3 2026, so the expansion in downstream demand has not yet translated into higher sales.

The cycle position can be read as a period in which demand expectations and reported sales are moving in opposite directions. The sharp quarterly swings in revenue are consistent with a project-by-project order structure.

By region, the Middle East rose from about one-third of sales in 2022 to about two-thirds in 2025 in StockAnalysis's compilation, so the company is more exposed to Middle East project cycles.

On competitive positioning, DKME's strengths are about 40 years of operating history, technology tie-ups and a direct overseas order channel.

The company also said it still delivered results in 2025 despite a management dispute and a trading suspension, which indicates that external events weighed on its operating conditions.

Separately, an April 2026 report said the acquirer plans to combine DKME's shell-and-tube technology with PCHE technology to improve competitiveness. Industry observers cited in the same reporting see this combination as a possible route into new energy equipment markets.

Outlook

According to an April 2026 Asia Economy report, Energin signed a share purchase agreement with DKME Inc. and completed the acquisition of management control.

The report said the management improvement conditions requested by the exchange include the participation of a sound acquirer, a substantive capital injection and governance improvement, and that the acquisition is expected to satisfy them.

The acquirer also said it is reviewing a funding plan to strengthen the financial structure. On the business side, a plan was presented to combine DKME's shell-and-tube technology with PCHE technology to raise competitiveness.

The trading suspension was reported to stem from a substantive review of listing eligibility tied to reasons involving the former major shareholder, and the acquisition was said to open a path to resolving that issue.

The materials used for this report do not give the size or timing of the capital injection in specific figures. On revenue, the first checkpoint is whether the four-quarter decline that ran through Q3 2026 comes to a halt.

On profitability, the question is whether the Q3 2026 operating profit of KRW 147 million is followed by further operating profits after the operating loss of KRW 5.48 billion in Q2 2026.

The 2026 annual report will confirm Q4 2026 results and the full-year operating margin, and until then the 7.5% margin for 2025 serves as the reference point. On cash flow, the checkpoint is whether operating cash flow, negative since 2024, turns positive.

F&Guide's compiled data show operating cash flow of KRW 8 billion in Q1 2026, but one quarter is too short to establish a trend.

Bull factors

Improved capital structure

The debt-to-equity ratio fell from 75.4% in 2022 to 49.9% in 2025, and total equity at year-end 2025 was KRW 125.2 billion. Total liabilities at the same date were KRW 62.4 billion, below total equity. Supporters point to this financial capacity as a buffer while earnings recover.

Q1 2026 profit and operating recovery signal

Attributable net income turned positive in Q1 2026 at KRW 4.6 billion, and operating profit rose 227.0% quarter on quarter to KRW 1.23 billion. Operating profit stayed positive in Q3 2026 at KRW 147 million.

Attributable net income was positive in only that one quarter, which should be read alongside the operating trend.

Change of control and technology combination plan

According to an April 2026 Asia Economy report, the acquirer completed its takeover of control and signaled capital injection and management reform. A plan was presented to combine DKME's shell-and-tube technology with PCHE technology to raise competitiveness.

Industry commentary cited in the same reporting links AI-driven power facility expansion to rising heat exchanger demand.

Bear factors

Four consecutive quarters of revenue decline

Q3 2026 revenue of KRW 13.4 billion was down 60.1% year on year. Revenue has fallen for four consecutive quarters starting in Q4 2025, and annual revenue declined in both 2024 and 2025. With the revenue base shrinking, absorbing fixed costs may become harder, which is the core of the bearish case.

Continued net losses and negative cash flow

The company posted net losses in two consecutive quarters, Q2 and Q3 2026. Operating cash flow was -KRW 6.06 billion in 2024 and -KRW 4.37 billion in 2025.

Governance uncertainty and trading suspension history

The company said it still delivered results in 2025 despite a management dispute and a trading suspension. The acquisition was completed in April 2026, but reports did not give specific figures for the size or timing of the funding to improve the financial structure. Further variables remain until the governance structure settles, which is a bearish factor.

10

Risk factors

Order gaps and continued revenue decline

Revenue has fallen for four consecutive quarters since Q4 2025, reaching KRW 13.4 billion in Q3 2026. Under a project-based order structure, the absence of large contracts causes sharp quarterly swings. This risk remains until expected downstream demand shows up in order results.

Governance transition and capital deployment

With the change of control, whether the financial restructuring funds and the management improvement plan are carried out has become the key variable. Delayed or small capital injections could slow any improvement in operating conditions. Given the trading suspension and management dispute history, the pace of disclosed follow-through matters.

Concentration in the Middle East and project volatility

On StockAnalysis's compiled data, the Middle East rose from about one-third of sales in 2022 to about two-thirds in 2025. A drop in orders or changes in the local business environment in that region could feed directly into results. Without geographic diversification, this concentration can amplify earnings volatility.

Overall view

DKME's annual revenue peaked at KRW 137.3 billion in 2023 and then declined for two consecutive years through 2025, while quarterly revenue has fallen for four consecutive quarters from Q4 2025 through Q3 2026.

The operating margin dropped from 12.2% in 2022 to 7.5% in 2025, and attributable net income turned into a net loss of KRW 2.88 billion in 2025. Attributable net losses over the last four quarters total KRW 13 billion.

On the other side, the debt-to-equity ratio fell to 49.9% at year-end 2025, and the return to profit in Q1 2026 and the operating profit in Q3 2026 are cited as signs of recovery.

As of an April 2026 report, the acquisition of management control has been completed, with a capital injection and a technology combination plan presented, and industry commentary points to rising heat exchanger demand from AI-driven power facility expansion. Both strengths and weaknesses are present.

The next checkpoints are full-year 2026 revenue and operating margin in the annual report, disclosures on capital injection, and the quarterly revenue trend into Q1 2027.
