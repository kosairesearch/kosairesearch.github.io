02

Business structure

RoboRobo was founded in May 2000 and develops, manufactures and sells educational robots and related products. Its flagship product is the Roborobo Kit, and its in-house GUI programming tool ROGIC received a new software award for educational robot software.

The product line covers educational robots, learning software for robots and related textbooks, including products for elementary students and young children such as Yuilro, Robokids and Coding Story.

By business unit, outsourcing accounted for 54% of Q2 2026 revenue, robot and education products for 42% and academy operations for 4%, according to Butler's aggregation.

The company has about 60 regional partner sites in Korea and roughly 20,000 students, and it has a 10-year long-term supply contract with Beijing Roborobo Education Technology in China. Its subsidiaries handle after-school outsourced education services and academy operations.

When offline education demand fell during COVID-19, the company responded with a new product, refreshed existing products and digital education training programs. It has since invested in AI, metaverse and autonomous driving technologies and is widening its scope toward an edtech business.

It is discontinuing products with stalled growth and launching new ones to diversify in digital education. Competition in the after-school school market has intensified as the child population shrinks, which the company's results commentary cites as a factor behind revenue declines.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual revenue rose from KRW 11.9 billion in 2022 to KRW 14.3 billion in 2023, a 20.2% increase, and then fell to KRW 13.5 billion in 2024 (down 5.4%) and KRW 13.4 billion in 2025 (down 1.0%), declining for two straight years from 2024 to 2025.

Operating results swung from an operating loss of KRW 182 million in 2022 to an operating profit of KRW 221 million in 2023, then back to an operating loss of KRW 1.54 billion in 2024 and a wider operating loss of KRW 1.96 billion in 2025.

The operating margin moved from -1.5% in 2022 to 1.5% in 2023, -11.4% in 2024 and -14.7% in 2025, and the company has posted operating losses for two straight years from 2024 to 2025.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders turned positive at KRW 1.39 billion in 2023 but was a net loss of KRW 107 million in 2024 and KRW 1.36 billion in 2025. Net income including non-controlling interests (a different figure) was KRW 1.42 billion in 2023 and a net loss of KRW 1.35 billion in 2025.

Higher selling, general and administrative and personnel costs, alongside lower sales, have been cited as the drivers of the operating profit decline. Operating cash flow was KRW 792 million in 2022, KRW 1.03 billion in 2023, -KRW 1.64 billion in 2024 and KRW 574 million in 2025, so 2024 was the only year of outflow.

At the end of 2025, total equity was KRW 29.8 billion, of which KRW 29.7 billion belonged to controlling shareholders, and total liabilities were KRW 2.1 billion.

Quarterly revenue went from KRW 3.03 billion in Q3 2025 to KRW 3.7 billion in Q4 2025 (up 22.4% quarter on quarter), then fell to KRW 2.8 billion in Q1 2026 (down 24.4%) and KRW 2.22 billion in Q2 2026 (down 20.8%), before rebounding to KRW 3.01 billion in Q3 2026 (up 35.5% quarter on quarter, but down 0.7% year on year).

Quarterly operating losses widened from KRW 543 million in Q3 2025 to KRW 777 million, KRW 1.02 billion and KRW 1.21 billion in Q2 2026, then narrowed to KRW 6.52 million in Q3 2026, and the company has been in the red for five consecutive quarters from Q3 2025 to Q3 2026.

Net losses attributable to controlling shareholders narrowed from KRW 1.32 billion in Q2 2026 to KRW 216 million in Q3 2026, yet the Q3 2026 loss was larger than the KRW 39.28 million loss in Q3 2025. The combined controlling-shareholder net loss for the last four quarters was KRW 3.25 billion.

Whether the Q3 2026 improvement reflects a structural change in costs or a one-time factor should be checked against quarterly cost trends in the next periodic report.

05

Industry analysis

RoboRobo's core demand comes from after-school classes and private academies. As the child population shrinks, competition in the after-school school market has intensified and continues to pressure sales.

When offline education demand dropped during COVID-19, the company adapted its products and training programs to a digital education environment.

The company itself attributes part of its revenue decline to a transition period of product discontinuations and new product launches, so how far new products rebuild the revenue base is the central industry question.

Its domestic network of about 60 partner sites and its long-term supply contract in China are its main channel strengths. On the technology side, its in-house ROGIC software received a new software award.

Because companies with similar names, such as Robotis and Robostar, have different business models, direct comparisons should be made within the same product category.

Data providers also classify the company differently: fnguide compares it with the KOSDAQ electronics sector, while wisereport places it in media and education, so sector-average comparisons depend on the chosen classification.

The next periodic report is the 2026 annual report, and the first checkpoint is whether the 2026 operating margin improves from -14.7% in 2025.

The Q1 2027 quarterly report is due by mid-May 2027, which will show whether the loss-narrowing trend since Q3 2026 continues. Whether new products rebuild revenue and whether the cost structure has changed are the two linked tasks.

Bull factors

Quarterly loss narrowing

Track record of a profitable year

In 2023 the company turned profitable, with operating profit of KRW 221 million and net income attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 1.39 billion. Revenue of KRW 14.3 billion that year was the largest of the 2022 to 2025 period. Whether a cost structure like that of 2023 can return is the key to an earnings recovery.

Sales network and balance sheet

The company has about 60 regional partner sites in Korea, roughly 20,000 students, and a 10-year supply contract with a Chinese company. Total liabilities at the end of 2025 were KRW 2.1 billion, a debt ratio of 7.1%. Once the product transition is completed, the sales network could serve as a base for a revenue recovery.

Bear factors

Five straight quarters of losses

The company has reported operating losses and controlling-shareholder net losses for five consecutive quarters from Q3 2025 to Q3 2026. The Q2 2026 operating loss of KRW 1.21 billion was the largest of those five quarters.

Continued revenue decline

Annual revenue declined for two straight years, to KRW 13.5 billion in 2024 and KRW 13.4 billion in 2025. First-half 2026 revenue fell 24.3% year on year according to an external aggregation. The operating margin was -14.7% in 2025, showing a deeper loss structure.

Transition results not yet visible

Results commentary cites the transition from discontinued products to new launches, a shrinking child population and intensifying after-school market competition as reasons for lower revenue. Digital education shifts and AI education commercialization have not yet translated into a profitable structure. Whether new business investment reduces losses must be checked in the next periodic reports.

10

Risk factors

Market and demographics

A shrinking child population and intensifying competition in the after-school market directly pressure product demand. Because annual revenue fell in both 2024 and 2025, whether this market contraction is structural is the core risk.

Whether the company can respond by changing sales channels or product pricing should be checked through changes in the revenue mix.

Execution of the business transition

If product discontinuations, new launches and digital shifts do not translate into revenue, losses could persist for longer. The range of investment areas, including AI education, metaverse and autonomous driving, places a heavy burden on resource allocation.

Restructuring the business portfolio, including subsidiaries in after-school outsourcing and academy operations, is also an execution risk.

Liquidity and trading flows

As a small-cap stock, it is vulnerable to swings in quotes when trading volume is thin. The short interest was 136,989 shares as of November 4, 2026, equal to 0.67% of market capitalization. Short selling and institutional and foreign flows could increase short-term volatility.

Overall view

RoboRobo is a company built on educational robots. After turning profitable in 2023, its revenue fell for two straight years in 2024 and 2025 and operating losses continued. In Q3 2026, revenue rose 35.5% quarter on quarter and the operating loss narrowed to KRW 6.52 million, signaling improvement on a quarterly basis.

However, the company has posted losses for five consecutive quarters from Q3 2025, the controlling-shareholder net loss in Q3 2026 was larger than a year earlier, and first-half 2026 revenue fell 24.3% according to an external aggregation.

Its debt ratio of 7.1% and total equity of KRW 29.8 billion indicate a light debt burden, but earnings capacity is not yet clear. Bullish factors include its record of a profitable year, its domestic partner network, its China supply contract and the narrowing quarterly loss.

Bearish factors include the shrinking child population, competition in the after-school market, the product transition and two straight years of revenue decline. On valuation, PER cannot be calculated and the price-to-book ratio reflects a premium, so interpretation depends on whether earnings recover.

The next checkpoints are the 2026 annual operating margin in the annual report and the loss trend in the Q1 2027 quarterly report.
