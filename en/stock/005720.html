02

Business structure

Nexen is a business holding company with affiliated operations, and it completed the inclusion of a subsidiary on December 30, 2025. The business is organized into a rubber segment and a logistics segment.

The rubber segment makes tires and other rubber products for automakers and is one pillar of the group's operations. The company is expanding supply to premium automakers through a global production base, and it is pursuing quality and productivity gains as its European plant enters full operation.

The logistics segment draws on 10 domestic logistics sites and a network spanning the United States, Europe, Asia and the Middle East to expand overseas expansion with group companies. Automakers are the core customers, and the main competition comes from global tire makers in a contest over price and quality.

Raw material prices and exchange rates directly affect manufacturing costs. In the Q1 2026 business summary, growing demand for eco-friendly tires was cited as the reason for improved results.

Production efficiency in the rubber segment and utilization at overseas plants can be read as key variables linked to margin swings.

Earnings analysis

Annual sales went from KRW 3 trillion in 2022 to KRW 3.04 trillion in 2023 (up 1.1%), KRW 3.21 trillion in 2024 (up 5.9%) and KRW 3.59 trillion in 2025 (up 11.6%), rising three straight years from 2023 through 2025. 2025 was the largest year and 2022 the smallest in the 2022 to 2025 period.

Operating profit swung from an operating loss of KRW 22.5 billion in 2022 to KRW 221 billion in 2023, then fell to KRW 209.7 billion in 2024 (down 5.1%) and KRW 204.2 billion in 2025 (down 2.6%), a two-year decline from 2024 through 2025. Operating margin was -0.8% in 2022, 7.3% in 2023, 6.5% in 2024 and 5.7% in 2025.

Sales kept growing in 2024 and 2025, but profit did not keep pace, so margins narrowed. Net profit attributable to owners rose from KRW 17.5 billion in 2022 to KRW 41.6 billion in 2023 (up 138.3%), KRW 85.9 billion in 2024 (up 106.3%) and KRW 91.2 billion in 2025 (up 6.2%), a three-year rise from 2023 through 2025.

Owner-attributable profit grew in 2024 and 2025 even as operating profit fell, which points to items outside operating profit such as non-operating items or taxes; the breakdown is a point to confirm in the annual report's income statement detail.

Net profit including non-controlling interests was KRW 1.78 billion in 2022, KRW 100.4 billion in 2023, KRW 158.4 billion in 2024 and KRW 177.9 billion in 2025, and the gap from owner-attributable profit reflects the non-controlling share.

Quarterly operating profit peaked at KRW 67.6 billion in Q1 2026 and then fell for two consecutive quarters, to KRW 49.1 billion in Q2 and KRW 26 billion in Q3 2026. Q3 2026 sales were KRW 947.3 billion, down 6.1% from the prior quarter but up 6.8% year on year.

Owner-attributable net profit swung to a KRW 21 billion loss in Q3 2026, and the last four quarters total KRW 63.7 billion. Operating cash flow went from -KRW 29.1 billion in 2022 to KRW 360.4 billion in 2025, and the debt ratio at end-2025 was 113.4%, down from 123.6% in 2024.

05

Industry analysis

Tire demand splits between original equipment supply to automakers and the replacement market, and replacement demand depends on vehicle usage and the age of cars on the road. In explaining its Q1 2026 improvement, the company cited growing demand for eco-friendly tires.

Electric vehicle adoption is also being discussed in the industry as lifting demand for durable, low-noise tires that cope with vehicle weight and torque.

Raw materials such as synthetic rubber and carbon black are petroleum-based, and their prices drive cost pressure, while the extent to which that cost can be passed on to finished-product prices creates quarter-to-quarter differences in margins. On competition, global major tire makers and Asian rivals press on prices.

Within the chemical sector, Nexen's structure is built around tires and logistics rather than the synthetic rubber or petrochemical products that larger peers focus on.

Industry direction shifts each quarter with the mix of replacement demand and raw material prices, and the sharp drop in Q3 2026 operating profit should be read alongside those external conditions.

On the bullish side, eco-friendly demand and premium automaker supply expansion stand out; on the bearish side, cost pressure and price competition push back.

European production base and eco-friendly tire demand

The bullish argument holds that overseas production expansion combined with eco-friendly demand supports the durability of sales growth.

Bear factors

Balance sheet and disclosure issue

Risk factors

Raw materials and FX

Tire manufacturing is heavily exposed to petroleum-based raw materials such as synthetic rubber and carbon black. With overseas production and export exposure, exchange rate swings are also a profit variable. Quarterly margins can move sharply depending on how much cost increases can be passed on to selling prices.

Balance sheet burden

Total liabilities of KRW 2.98 trillion exceed total equity of KRW 2.63 trillion, and the debt ratio at end-2025 was 113.4%. When operating profit is declining, interest and debt repayment burdens can weigh relatively more. Whether improved operating cash flow offsets this burden is the key question.

Quarterly volatility

Quarterly operating profit moved sharply in a short span in 2026, from KRW 67.6 billion in Q1 to KRW 26 billion in Q3. Owner-attributable net profit also swung from KRW 38.4 billion in Q1 to a KRW 21 billion loss in Q3, a sizable quarter-to-quarter volatility. Because earnings power varies each quarter, a single quarter is not enough to define the trend.
