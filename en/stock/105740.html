Q3 2026 Loss as Semiconductor and Aviation Qualifications Advance

DK-Lok returned to operating profit in 2025 but posted an operating loss and a net loss attributable to controlling shareholders in Q3 2026, so the qualification and mass-production timeline of its semiconductor and aviation businesses is the next checkpoint.

02

Business structure

DK-Lok was founded in 1986 as a maker of instrumentation fittings and valves, renamed itself DK-Lok in 2010 and listed on KOSDAQ. The instrumentation fittings and valves business accounts for about 90% of total sales and moves with capex cycles in oil and gas, refining and chemicals, and power and plant.

Its products serve shipbuilding, offshore plants and power facilities, and about 70% of sales are exports. A December 2025 KB Securities report said oil and gas made up 55% of total sales, while semiconductors were described as about 10%.

Samsung Electronics and SK hynix are cited as semiconductor customers, with ultra-high-purity fittings, valves and gas filters as core products.

In aerospace and defense, DK-Lok has supplied qualified parts for the KF-21 and FA-50 programs since 2020, and it delivers hydraulic fittings for defense ground vehicles to Hyundai Rotem. The company plans to lift aerospace and defense to 15% or more of sales within five years.

It runs a vertically integrated chain from forging and machining to surface treatment and testing, and aviation qualifications and mass-production capability are cited as key supplier-selection factors.

Analysts also note that profitability differs widely by region, with fittings and valves in the Americas and Europe reported at 15 to 20% operating margins (Korea IR Council, 2025).

04

Earnings analysis

On a filed basis, annual sales were KRW 109.9 billion in 2022, KRW 111.4 billion in 2023 (up 1.4%), KRW 98.4 billion in 2024 (down 11.7%) and KRW 128.8 billion in 2025 (up 30.8%). Among 2022 to 2025, 2025 was the largest year and 2024 the smallest.

Operating profit was KRW 11.4 billion in 2022 and KRW 11.8 billion in 2023 (up 3.2%), then turned into an operating loss of KRW 188 million in 2024 before returning to a profit of KRW 11.1 billion in 2025. Operating margin moved from 10.4% in 2022 and 10.6% in 2023 to -0.2% in 2024 and recovered to 8.6% in 2025.

Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders peaked at KRW 11.6 billion in 2023 (up 97.6%), was smallest at KRW 2.13 billion in 2024 (down 81.6%), and reached KRW 6.75 billion in 2025 (up 216.8%).

Net income including non-controlling interests was KRW 6.74 billion in 2025, a different figure from the controlling-shareholder line. The company attributed the 2025 sales recovery to a rebound in overseas demand.

The debt ratio rose to 65.8% in 2025 from 53.3% in 2022, 44.0% in 2023 and 49.9% in 2024, with total liabilities of KRW 89.8 billion and total equity of KRW 136.4 billion at year-end 2025.

Operating cash flow went from KRW 1.55 billion in 2022, KRW 6.28 billion in 2023 and KRW 5.87 billion in 2024 to an outflow of KRW 1.31 billion in 2025.

Quarterly sales were KRW 37.6 billion in Q3 2025, KRW 39.4 billion in Q4 2025, KRW 32.8 billion in Q1 2026, KRW 39.5 billion in Q2 2026 and KRW 32.5 billion in Q3 2026.

Operating profit fell from KRW 6.73 billion in Q3 2025 to KRW 2.61 billion in Q4 2025 and KRW 238 million in Q1 2026, recovered to KRW 3.81 billion in Q2 2026, and turned into an operating loss of KRW 13.34 million in Q3 2026.

The company cited delayed recognition of export sales due to Middle East shipping disruption, a lower share of high-value products and higher costs, and said upfront expenses for semiconductor and aviation projects were also reflected.

Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders swung to a loss of KRW 4.59 billion in Q3 2026, and the company said foreign-exchange losses and FX valuation losses affected net income. Net profit attributable to controlling shareholders for the last four quarters (Q4 2025 to Q3 2026) totaled KRW 2.76 billion.

05

Industry analysis

Demand from downstream markets rests on two pillars: energy capex and shipbuilding and offshore plants. In November 2025 the company expected North American and Middle Eastern oil and gas investment and supply-chain investment demand to continue into 2026.

In shipbuilding and offshore, it supplies FPSO, FPU and rig platforms in Korea and China and plans to address demand tied to the US shipbuilding revival program (MASGA).

In semiconductors, a December 2025 KB Securities report expected orders for semiconductor fittings and valves to grow if AI data-center investment continues, while noting that the contribution of ultra-high-purity products to sales was still limited.

In aerospace and defense, expansion is expected on the back of KF-21 prototype supply. The Korea IR Council (2025) described DK-Lok as a company that had been viewed as sensitive to the EPC project cycle, and said a larger share from semiconductors and aerospace could act as a re-rating factor.

In Q3 2026 the company cited Middle East shipping disruption as a reason export sales recognition was delayed. Because exports are a large share of sales, currency volatility is also a factor to watch.

On competition, the company's certification record and specialized processes in instrumentation fittings and valves are presented as its differentiators.

06

Outlook

The company set a target of KRW 300 billion in annual sales and KRW 45 billion in operating profit by 2030 (as stated in September 2025), with aerospace and defense rising to about 15% of sales over the same period.

In semiconductors, it plans to expand from Hastelloy fittings and valves into regulators, filters and integrated gas systems (IGS) after the Company A qualification.

Its domestically developed semiconductor gas filter has completed mounting tests with Samsung Electronics, and mass-production supply is targeted for 2027 in line with the construction schedule of Samsung's Pyeongtaek P5 campus.

In aviation, DK-Lok has signed an NDA with a global commercial airframer (Company B) and is negotiating supply of aviation fittings, but no supply contract has been finalized. The company is also pursuing Nadcap certification, a global aerospace quality accreditation for chemical processing and coatings, in 2027.

On funding, the board on November 11, 2026 approved KRW 17.5 billion in borrowings to secure aviation raw materials, product development, testing and validation, and mass-production capacity.

The next checkpoints are whether a supply contract with Company B is signed, whether mass-production supply of the P5 gas filter starts in 2027, and how fast orders recover in the energy business. The order in which these reach reported earnings will shape how 2027 profit trends are read.

Bull factors

Semiconductor qualification progress

DK-Lok obtained a qualification from Company A and plans to widen its range from fittings and valves into regulators, filters and IGS. Its domestically developed gas filter has completed mounting tests with Samsung Electronics, with mass-production supply targeted for 2027. In 2025, analysts said earnings visibility could rise quickly once customers' capex resumes.

Aerospace and defense supply record

DK-Lok has supplied qualified parts for the KF-21 and FA-50 since 2020, and in November 2026 signed an NDA with commercial airframer Company B. The company targets aerospace and defense at about 15% of sales by 2030.

KB Securities cited its position as the only domestic aviation-fitting supplier as a source of structural growth in December 2025.

Earnings recovery and payout target

Operating profit returned to KRW 11.1 billion in 2025, with operating margin recovering to 8.6%. The 2025 fiscal-year payout ratio was 45.28%, and the company disclosed in April 2026 a target of keeping the payout ratio at 25% or more of consolidated net income. The dividend was approved at the March 2026 annual general meeting.

Bear factors

Q3 swing to losses

Q3 2026 posted an operating loss of KRW 13.34 million and a net loss attributable to controlling shareholders of KRW 4.59 billion. Over the last four quarters, controlling-shareholder net profit totaled only KRW 2.76 billion, a thin earnings base.

The company pointed to logistics disruption and upfront costs, and whether cost pressure and shipping delays persist beyond Q4 is the open question.

Rising financial burden

The debt ratio was 65.8% at year-end 2025, up from 53.3% in 2022. After the KRW 17.5 billion borrowing approved on November 11, 2026, short-term borrowings will total KRW 62.9 billion. Operating cash flow turned into an outflow of KRW 1.31 billion in 2025.

Timing of new businesses and share price volatility

Commercial aircraft supply is not yet contracted, and mass production of the semiconductor gas filter is targeted for 2027. Upfront costs and R&D for new businesses may weigh on profitability for some time.

The stock rose more than 24% on November 10, 2026 without a disclosed catalyst and fell more than 10% intraday on November 16, showing high short-term volatility.

10

Risk factors

Logistics and FX

Middle East shipping disruption delayed export revenue recognition, and because exports are a large share of sales, currency moves have led to FX and valuation losses. If both factors persist beyond Q4, operating and net profit could be affected. Whether logistics normalizes will be most directly visible in quarterly results.

New-business qualification and mass production

Aviation parts require substantial time for certification and production validation, and whether military supply experience carries into commercial aircraft is the key question.

The semiconductor gas filter is still in the pre-mass-production stage for 2027, and customer qualification and supply negotiations carry uncertainty. If new-business revenue is delayed, the upfront investment burden could hit earnings first.

Financial capacity and share price swings

The debt ratio rose to 65.8% in 2025, and the KRW 17.5 billion borrowing lifts short-term borrowings to KRW 62.9 billion. With operating cash flow turning negative in 2025, how the borrowed funds are deployed and recovered matters more. Repeated price swings without disclosed catalysts could widen the gap between share prices and earnings trends.

Overall view

DK-Lok disclosed a return to profit in 2025, with annual sales of KRW 128.8 billion and operating profit of KRW 11.1 billion.

In Q3 2026, however, it swung back to an operating loss and a net loss attributable to controlling shareholders, and net profit attributable to controlling shareholders over the last four quarters totaled KRW 2.76 billion.

The company cited Middle East shipping disruption, a lower share of high-value products, higher costs, and upfront semiconductor and aviation expenses.

Positive factors include the qualification from Company A, planned 2027 mass-production supply of the gas filter for Samsung Electronics, the KF-21 and FA-50 supply record, and ongoing talks with Company B.

Negative factors include the lack of a finalized supply contract, a rising debt ratio alongside the KRW 17.5 billion borrowing, the 2025 operating cash outflow, and sharp short-term price moves.

The 2030 targets of KRW 300 billion in sales and KRW 45 billion in operating profit are company goals, and reported results will determine whether they are reached.

The next checkpoints are the full-year operating margin in the 2026 annual report, whether logistics normalizes in Q1 2027, and the start of mass production of the P5-related gas filter.
