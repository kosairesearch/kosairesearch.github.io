DL: Quarterly Profit Recovery, Petrochemical Pricing in Focus

DL posted operating profit of KRW 256.5 billion in the second quarter of 2026 and KRW 174 billion in the third quarter, but the KRW 88 billion net loss attributable to controlling shareholders in 2025 and the temporary nature of petrochemical price gains remain items to track.

02

Business structure

DL (DL Holdings) has operated as a pure holding company since its January 2021 restructuring, when the construction arm was spun off as DL E&C and the petrochemical business became DL Chemical. DL E&C, DL Chemical and DL Energy each serve as intermediate holding companies for construction, petrochemicals and energy.

DL E&C is classified as an associate in DL's DART quarterly filing, so its construction revenue is not consolidated into DL's revenue. In the 2024 figures, the petrochemical and materials manufacturing segment accounted for 86.3% of total simple-sum revenue, and exports made up 73.9% of that total.

Key products include polybutene (PB), HDPE and LLDPE polyethylene, SBS and SEBS polymers and IR latex. On the same basis, the automotive parts unit DL Motors contributed 5.6% (1.4% domestic, 4.2% export), while investment and other activities, including real estate leasing, contributed 4.8%.

A 2024 press report described DL Chemical's PB and Cariflex's IR latex as global number-one positions, and Kraton's SBC as number one in the United States and Europe. In energy, DL Energy holds the Niles and Fairview generation assets in the United States, and the hotel business is run by Glad. Petrochemical competition has been shaped since 2022 by low-priced supply from China.

04

Earnings analysis

Annual revenue was KRW 5.17 trillion in 2022, KRW 5.02 trillion in 2023 (down 3.0%), KRW 5.62 trillion in 2024 (up 11.9%) and KRW 5.33 trillion in 2025 (down 5.1%), with 2024 the largest year of the 2022-2025 period.

Operating profit fell from KRW 287.5 billion in 2022 to KRW 150.7 billion in 2023 (down 47.6%), rose to KRW 412.5 billion in 2024 (up 173.7%) and was KRW 298.5 billion in 2025 (down 27.6%). The operating margin moved from 5.6% in 2022 to 3.0% in 2023, 7.3% in 2024 and 5.6% in 2025.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders went from KRW 72.3 billion in 2022 to a net loss of KRW 133.4 billion in 2023, a return to profit of KRW 89.4 billion in 2024, and a net loss of KRW 88 billion in 2025.

Total net income including non-controlling interests was KRW 101.8 billion in 2022, a net loss of KRW 121 billion in 2023, KRW 94.8 billion in 2024 and a net loss of KRW 96.3 billion in 2025.

In 2025 operating profit was positive while net results were negative, so items below operating profit and the flow of subsidiary earnings matter for interpreting the bottom line. The debt-to-equity ratio fell from 160.3% in 2022 and 167.3% in 2023 to 161.9% in 2024 and 147.6% in 2025.

At the end of 2025, total equity was KRW 4.92 trillion, of which controlling shareholders' equity was KRW 4.02 trillion, and total liabilities were KRW 7.26 trillion. Operating cash flow was KRW 227.9 billion in 2022, KRW 325 billion in 2023, KRW 557.1 billion in 2024 and KRW 514.9 billion in 2025.

On a quarterly basis, operating profit was KRW 109.4 billion in the third quarter of 2025, KRW 11.8 billion in the fourth quarter (down 89.2% from the prior quarter), KRW 112.9 billion in the first quarter of 2026 (up 858.1%), KRW 256.5 billion in the second quarter (up 127.3%) and KRW 174 billion in the third quarter (down 32.2% sequentially, up 59.1% year on year).

DL said in its second-quarter 2026 preliminary release that both revenue and operating profit were record quarterly highs. In the second quarter, higher polybutene margins at DL Chemical and Middle East-related product price increases were reflected together.

In the third quarter, that temporary price effect faded and DL Chemical's profitability slowed.

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders moved from a net loss of KRW 5.94 billion in the fourth quarter of 2025 to a profit of KRW 7.22 billion in the first quarter of 2026, then KRW 125.2 billion in the second quarter and KRW 132.4 billion in the third, marking two consecutive quarters of growth.

The last four quarters (fourth quarter of 2025 to third quarter of 2026) add up to KRW 258.9 billion on the same attributable basis.

05

Industry analysis

Petrochemicals have faced a weak cycle since 2022, when low-priced supply from China pushed down product margins, and a 2024 press report cited this as a core cause of the downturn. Construction has faced cost increases and a property slump.

In 2026, press reports attributed higher polyethylene prices to the Strait of Hormuz blockade, and DL said its specialty product competitiveness and overseas footprint kept earnings stable despite a broad industry slowdown.

In energy, tight US power market supply and the peak season pushed DL Energy's third-quarter 2026 operating profit to KRW 62.1 billion, a record quarterly high. The same unit also cited rising global power demand and higher US capacity payments as drivers of second-quarter results.

The hotel unit Glad posted record results in the third quarter of 2026 on rising foreign tourist arrivals. In construction, a 2024 press report noted that associate DL E&C's operating profit declined from KRW 956.7 billion in 2021 to KRW 331.2 billion in 2023.

The same report said petrochemicals, once more profitable than construction, weakened further after the holding company transition, prompting the group to rework how construction generates returns. Other listed holding companies in the same sector include SK Square, LG, GS Holdings and Lotte.

06

Outlook

In its third-quarter 2026 release, DL said it would strengthen stable earnings and cash generation by upgrading a profit-focused business structure. In its first-quarter 2026 release, DL also said it would continue cost reduction, operating efficiency gains and a portfolio shift toward high value-added products.

Press reports expected the improvement in petrochemical results to continue for some time, supported by supply stability at the North American and European plants.

However, DL Chemical said its profitability slowed in the third quarter of 2026 as the Middle East-related price gains faded, so the durability of this improvement may depend on pricing conditions.

Kraton's third-quarter 2026 operating profit was KRW 73.1 billion, up KRW 70.1 billion from a year earlier, with cumulative operating profit of KRW 209 billion. DL Energy cited higher power sales at US gas-fired plants and better generation profitability as third-quarter drivers.

Glad reported a 38.6% operating margin in the third quarter of 2026 as foreign tourist arrivals lifted room rates and occupancy. DL said it would pursue medium- to long-term competitiveness through diversification of its overseas portfolio.

The direction the company has set is business structure upgrading, so the next items to track are how each subsidiary's earnings contribution changes in the coming reports.

Bull factors

Specialty mix improvement in petrochemicals

DL Chemical's PB margins and Kraton's restructuring and efficiency measures lifted results in the second and third quarters of 2026. Kraton's third-quarter 2026 operating profit was KRW 73.1 billion, up KRW 70.1 billion year on year, with cumulative operating profit of KRW 209 billion.

DL said upgrading the portfolio toward high value-added specialty products is translating into better profitability.

Record results at energy and hotel subsidiaries

DL Energy posted third-quarter 2026 operating profit of KRW 62.1 billion, a quarterly record. Glad recorded a record result in the same quarter with a 38.6% operating margin.

Both units depend on external drivers such as US power market tightness and rising foreign tourist arrivals, and they now form a meaningful part of holding-level earnings.

Return to profit at the attributable level

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders moved from a KRW 5.94 billion loss in the fourth quarter of 2025 to a profit in the first quarter of 2026, then rose for two consecutive quarters. The debt-to-equity ratio declined from 160.3% in 2022 to 147.6% in 2025. Controlling shareholders' equity at the end of 2025 was KRW 4.02 trillion.

Bear factors

Fading of temporary price gains

DL Chemical said its profitability slowed in the third quarter of 2026 as temporary Middle East-related price gains faded and a lagging effect appeared. Third-quarter operating profit of KRW 174 billion was down 32.2% from KRW 256.5 billion in the second quarter.

The petrochemical industry has been described as facing structural weakness, which leaves the durability of profits open to question.

Annual losses and high earnings volatility

Net income attributable to controlling shareholders swung to losses in 2023 (KRW 133.4 billion) and 2025 (KRW 88 billion). Operating profit in 2025 was KRW 298.5 billion, down 27.6%, and the operating margin was 5.6%.

Quarterly results ranged from KRW 11.8 billion in the fourth quarter of 2025 to KRW 256.5 billion in the second quarter of 2026, so earnings stability has yet to be tested over a full cycle.

Weak construction associate and limited group support

A 2024 press report noted that associate DL E&C's operating profit fell from KRW 956.7 billion in 2021 to KRW 331.2 billion in 2023. The same report said that, because DL E&C is the group's core construction arm, support from other affiliates would be hard to expect in a crisis. Recovery in construction reaches holding-level results only indirectly.

Risk factors

Feedstock, product pricing and geopolitical risk

Press reports attributed the first-quarter 2026 polyethylene price increase to the Strait of Hormuz blockade. DL has pointed to disrupted global supply chains as a factor affecting results.

If supply disruptions ease, product price gains could reverse quickly, leaving petrochemical margins sensitive to external conditions.

Concentration in subsidiary earnings

In the third quarter of 2026, DL Energy and Glad each posted record operating profit and supported consolidated results. When a few subsidiaries drive quarterly results, changes in US power market conditions or tourism demand can increase volatility at the holding level. Shifts in each subsidiary's earnings contribution are the next point to track.

Balance sheet burden and reliance on associates

Total liabilities at the end of 2025 were KRW 7.26 trillion, above total equity of KRW 4.92 trillion. The debt-to-equity ratio fell to 147.6% in 2025, but the absolute debt load can weigh on results during earnings swings.

The performance and contingent liabilities of associate DL E&C are also factors in assessing the holding company's financial position.

Overall view

DL is a holding company with petrochemical, energy and hotel subsidiaries. Annual operating profit rose from KRW 150.7 billion in 2023 to KRW 412.5 billion in 2024 and then fell to KRW 298.5 billion in 2025, while net income attributable to controlling shareholders recorded losses in 2023 and 2025.

In 2026, quarterly operating profit reached KRW 256.5 billion in the second quarter and KRW 174 billion in the third, and attributable net income grew for two consecutive quarters after turning positive in the first quarter.

Supportive factors include the specialty-focused improvement in petrochemicals, earnings from the energy subsidiary's US generation assets, hotel results and a lower debt-to-equity ratio.

Negative factors include DL Chemical's description of price gains as temporary, the annual net losses in 2023 and 2025, and the declining operating profit at associate DL E&C. Weighing these equally, the quarterly recovery is visible, but its durability depends on petrochemical pricing and subsidiary performance.

The next checkpoints are the fourth-quarter 2026 preliminary results and the 2026 annual report. This report does not offer an investment opinion or a target price and is provided for information only.
