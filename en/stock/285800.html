02

Business structure

Jinyoung was founded in 2013 as a plastic sheet manufacturer and listed on KOSDAQ in 2023. It produces high-function sheets based on plastic materials such as ASA, ABS, PP and PMMA, and develops decorative sheets and functional films for furniture, construction and electronics.

Its main customers are furniture makers, to which it supplies furniture sheets, while it also supplies LG Electronics with refrigerator interior parts and air conditioner exterior parts. It is expanding exports to overseas markets. Furniture sheet sales have grown as aging housing stock and remodeling demand increase.

Since the third quarter of 2025, industrial film sales that began that quarter have offset part of the shrinkage in the core business. The pyrolysis oil unit began with the acquisition of a plant site and related permits in Nonsan, South Chungcheong Province.

The plan is to run 14 pyrolysis oil production lines by adding 10 units to the existing four. A securities analyst expects the unit's contribution to revenue to become full-scale from 2027, once capacity expansion and stabilization are complete.

In competitive terms, Jinyoung supplies sheet and film materials rather than finished products, and its revenue tracks the production volumes of its furniture and appliance customers.

04

Earnings analysis

On the disclosed annual results, revenue fell from KRW 48.1 billion in 2022 to KRW 30.9 billion in 2023, a 35.8% decline, before rising 10.7% to KRW 34.2 billion in 2024 and falling 5.3% to KRW 32.4 billion in 2025. 2022 was the largest year in the period and 2023 the smallest.

Profitability turned in 2023: operating profit of KRW 6.45 billion in 2022 became an operating loss of KRW 2.36 billion, and the operating margin fell from 13.4% to -7.6%.

A securities analyst attributed the period's deterioration to the burden of higher fixed costs, which turned operating profit into a loss and caused a sharp margin decline. Operating losses continued at KRW 2.94 billion in 2024, with the loss widening, and KRW 2.66 billion in 2025, with the loss narrowing.

Operating losses have therefore run for three consecutive years from 2023 through 2025. Profit attributable to controlling shareholders swung from KRW 5.12 billion in 2022 to a net loss of KRW 1.87 billion in 2023, followed by net losses of KRW 3.2 billion in 2024 and KRW 2.77 billion in 2025.

Net income including non-controlling interests was a loss of KRW 3.55 billion in 2024, larger than the controlling-shareholder loss.

At end-2025, total equity was KRW 38.3 billion, of which KRW 35.1 billion belonged to controlling shareholders, and total liabilities were KRW 31.8 billion, a debt-to-equity ratio of 83.1%.

On a quarterly basis, the third-quarter 2025 result was a loss because equipment repair and safety inspections were not completed until late October.

Operating loss of KRW 1.24 billion in the fourth quarter of 2025 narrowed to KRW 90.24 million in the first quarter of 2026, and the second quarter swung to an operating profit of KRW 78.57 million.

Second-quarter controlling-shareholder profit of KRW 2.7 billion far exceeded that operating profit, suggesting non-operating items played a large role. The third quarter of 2026 recorded revenue of KRW 7.92 billion and an operating loss of KRW 520 million, with the loss widening from a year earlier. Controlling-shareholder net income over the last four quarters totals KRW 873 million.

05

Industry analysis

Jinyoung's core business tracks demand for interior materials in Korean furniture, appliances and construction. A securities analysis as of the third quarter of 2025 noted that the downturn in domestic downstream industries continued, and customer sales also kept declining.

Conversely, aging housing stock and rising remodeling demand favor furniture sheets. Given that third-quarter 2026 revenue of KRW 7.92 billion fell 7.7% from a year earlier, the disclosed numbers do not yet show that the downstream cycle has turned.

The appliance business supplies LG Electronics, so results are sensitive to its production volumes. Export expansion partly offsets weak domestic demand.

Plastic sheets and functional films require material processing capability and customer qualification, which creates some entry barriers, but high customer concentration means order changes from a few furniture or appliance customers flow directly into revenue.

The pyrolysis oil business is a process business whose returns depend on feedstock sourcing and plant utilization, so it differs in character from the core business. Direct comparison with listed electronics component makers is limited because their business structures differ.

Whether the downstream recovery coincides with pyrolysis oil utilization will shape the company's position within the sector.

06

Outlook

The company's medium-term growth driver is the pyrolysis oil business. A securities report from December 2025 said that the establishment of a newly formed subsidiary is planned during 2026.

The report presented a setup of a total of 14 pyrolysis oil production lines on a consolidated basis, adding 10 units to the existing four treatment facilities. The challenge identified was ramping up the added capacity stably.

The same report cited 2027 pyrolysis oil revenue guidance of about KRW 3 billion; because this figure dates from December 2025, it may change with the pace of expansion. Operations resumed normal running from November 2025. In the core business, remodeling demand supports furniture sheet sales.

However, third-quarter 2026 revenue fell to KRW 7.92 billion and the operating loss widened to KRW 520 million. Even if the new lines run, the disclosed results will need to show how far pyrolysis revenue offsets core-business declines.

In April 2026 the company issued KRW 8 billion of convertible bonds, and it has since disclosed a plan to buy back and retire KRW 4 billion of the remaining balance off-exchange. How the company combines financing with a return to profit is the point to check.

Bull factors

Room to expand the pyrolysis oil business

The company plans to run 14 lines by adding 10 units to four existing ones. A December 2025 securities report cited 2027 pyrolysis oil revenue guidance of about KRW 3 billion. If this unit becomes a new revenue pillar offsetting core declines, the earnings structure could change, provided the facilities stabilize.

Early signs of core-business recovery

Furniture sheet sales rose as aging housing stock and remodeling demand grew. The operating loss narrowed from KRW 1.24 billion in the fourth quarter of 2025 to KRW 90.24 million in the first quarter of 2026, and the second quarter swung to an operating profit of KRW 78.57 million. Quarters in which losses narrowed show the potential for earnings to recover.

Market value relative to net assets

Total equity at end-2025 was KRW 38.3 billion, with KRW 35.1 billion attributable to controlling shareholders. Market capitalization sits below that controlling-shareholder equity, a discount to book net assets.

A sizable asset base could become a basis for reinterpreting the share price if restructuring or capital efficiency improves.

Bear factors

Three straight years of operating losses and a wider loss

Operating losses were KRW 2.36 billion in 2023, KRW 2.94 billion in 2024 and KRW 2.66 billion in 2025, a three-year run. The third quarter of 2026 posted a KRW 520 million operating loss, wider than a year earlier. The 2025 operating margin of -8.2% contrasts sharply with 13.4% in 2022. The fact that profit has appeared only at the quarterly level is a bearish factor.

Downstream demand weakness and falling revenue

A securities analysis as of the third quarter of 2025 noted that the downturn in domestic downstream industries continued and customer sales were falling. Third-quarter 2026 revenue of KRW 7.92 billion fell 7.7% from a year earlier.

With high customer concentration, reduced orders from key customers pass straight through to revenue, which is a weakness.

Thin earnings base and financial burden

Controlling-shareholder net income over the last four quarters totals only KRW 873 million. The debt-to-equity ratio rose from 64.0% at end-2024 to 83.1% at end-2025.

Of the KRW 8 billion convertible bonds issued in April 2026, KRW 4 billion is planned for buyback and retirement, and the remaining KRW 4 billion could add shares on conversion. Financing needs overlap with a thin earnings base.

Risk factors

Pyrolysis facility operation and expansion risk

In the third quarter of 2025, equipment repair and safety inspections ran until late October, leading to a net loss. The expansion schedule has also slipped before. If the new subsidiary and the additional 10 units do not proceed on plan during 2026, the premise behind the 2027 revenue guidance could be affected.

Financing and dilution risk

The convertible bonds issued in April 2026 total KRW 8 billion. The company disclosed plans to buy back KRW 4 billion off-exchange with its own funds and retire it in full. The remaining KRW 4 billion could convert into 418,628 common shares at the conversion price disclosed. Conversion would increase the share count and dilute per-share metrics.

Downstream demand and customer concentration risk

The company depends heavily on furniture and appliance customers. If the domestic downstream industry weakens further, reduced customer sales feed through to Jinyoung's revenue.

New products such as industrial films have partly cushioned revenue declines, but the third-quarter 2026 decline suggests that cushion may not be sufficient.

12

Overall view

Jinyoung's disclosed results show a structure of operating losses persisting since 2023. The second-quarter 2026 swing to operating profit is a quarterly recovery signal, but the third-quarter loss widening again leaves the direction of the core business unconfirmed.

Furniture sheet demand from remodeling and appliance supply to LG Electronics are the core supports, while a weak downstream industry and customer concentration are the weak points.

The pyrolysis oil business offers a growth path through 14 lines and 2027 revenue guidance, but that figure dates from December 2025 and needs verifying against actual plant output.

On the financial side, the debt-to-equity ratio rose to 83.1% and convertible bonds remain outstanding, so financing and dilution factors coexist.

Market capitalization sits below controlling-shareholder equity, but with four-quarter controlling-shareholder net income of KRW 873 million, earnings-based interpretation remains limited.

The next checkpoints are the operating margin in the 2026 annual report and the pyrolysis revenue contribution in the first-quarter 2027 report.
