Core Business and Pyrolysis Oil Both Turn Profitable—Durability Untested

02

Business structure

Jinyoung Co., Ltd. was founded in 1996 and listed on KOSDAQ in June 2023 as a manufacturer of plastic sheets and films.

Most of its revenue comes from furniture sheets and furniture edge bands, and the company collaborated with LG Chem, the world's largest ASA resin supplier, to develop ASA-based decorative sheets under its proprietary brand.

Using ABS, PP, ASA and PMMA materials, it produces functional surface-finishing materials for furniture, appliances and building materials, with major customers including LG Electronics, Hanssem, Hyundai Livart and Hansol Homedeco.

Its production base of eight sheet lines and four edge-band lines is the largest of its kind in Korea. On a consolidated basis its subsidiaries are Korea Eco Energy (78.7% owned), which produces pyrolysis oil, and Necero (51.0% owned), a waste-treatment company.

Jinyoung entered the waste-plastic pyrolysis business in 2023 by acquiring a 47.74% stake in Korea Eco Energy and subsequently signed a pyrolysis-oil supply contract with a domestic refiner.

During 2026 it plans to establish a new subsidiary, Jinyoung Eco Energy, having taken over a plant site and permits in Nonsan, South Chungcheong Province. On a standalone basis, Q3 2025 revenue was composed of furniture sheets (45.8%), edge bands (30.5%), appliance sheets (12.3%) and industrial sheets (9.8%).

04

Earnings analysis

Consolidated revenue fell 35.8% from KRW 48.1 billion in 2022 to KRW 30.9 billion in 2023, rebounded 10.7% to KRW 34.2 billion in 2024, then declined 5.3% to KRW 32.4 billion in 2025.

Operating profit swung from a KRW 6.45 billion profit in 2022 to an operating loss of KRW 2.36 billion in 2023, widened to a loss of KRW 2.94 billion in 2024, and narrowed to a loss of KRW 2.66 billion in 2025, marking three consecutive loss years from 2023 through 2025.

Controlling-interest net profit followed a similar path, turning from a KRW 5.12 billion profit in 2022 to a net loss of KRW 1.87 billion in 2023, widening to a loss of KRW 3.2 billion in 2024, and narrowing to a loss of KRW 2.77 billion in 2025.

Total net income including non-controlling interests moved from KRW 5.12 billion in 2022 to -KRW 1.87 billion in 2023, -KRW 3.55 billion in 2024 and -KRW 3.2 billion in 2025, tracing a different path from the controlling-interest figure.

The operating margin fell from 13.4% in 2022 to -7.6%, -8.6% and -8.2% in 2023, 2024 and 2025, respectively.

On a quarterly basis, revenue rose from KRW 8.02 billion in Q2 2025 to KRW 8.58 billion in Q3 (+7.0% quarter-on-quarter), dipped to KRW 8.47 billion in Q4 (-1.2%), then rose to KRW 8.52 billion in Q1 2026 (+0.6%) and KRW 8.59 billion in Q2 2026 (+0.8% quarter-on-quarter, +7.2% year-on-year), extending two consecutive quarters of growth after the Q4 2025 trough.

Quarterly operating results moved from a loss of KRW 250 million in Q2 2025 to a loss of KRW 127 million in Q3 (narrower), a loss of KRW 1.24 billion in Q4 (wider), and a loss of KRW 90.24 million in Q1 2026 (narrower), before turning to a profit of KRW 78.57 million in Q2 2026, a period during which gross margin was reported to have risen from 12.8% in Q1 to 18.6% in Q2.

Controlling-interest net profit also turned from a loss of KRW 979 million in Q1 2026 to a profit of KRW 2.7 billion in Q2 2026, bringing the sum over the last four quarters (Q3 2025 to Q2 2026) to KRW 1.71 billion; the fact that net profit rose by far more than the KRW 78.57 million operating profit suggests non-operating items may have also contributed, details that warrant confirmation in the forthcoming regular filing.

05

Industry analysis

Jinyoung's core end-markets are domestic and overseas housing and furniture demand, which are directly exposed to construction-sector slowdowns and weakness in the Chinese property market.

At the same time, growing remodeling demand tied to an aging housing stock has been cited as a factor supporting furniture-sheet sales. Within the chemicals sector, Jinyoung ranks around 109th out of 122 comparable companies by revenue, placing it among smaller players.

The pyrolysis-oil market is growing as waste-treatment demand and refiners' need for recycled feedstock converge, and Korea Eco Energy has already signed a supply contract with a refiner and expanded into equipment sales to outside operators.

Government support policies aimed at expanding waste-plastic pyrolysis technology are also expected to extend the duration of such partnerships.

However, early-stage new businesses carry significant operational volatility, as illustrated by the July 2025 fire at Korea Eco Energy's plant that halted operations until normalization in November.

Pyrolysis oil still accounts for only a single-digit share of consolidated revenue, meaning conditions in the core sheet and film business remain the central driver of overall performance.

06

Outlook

Korea Eco Energy signed a KRW 5.7 billion equipment-supply contract with a domestic pyrolysis-oil operator in September 2026, the largest contract in its history and equivalent to 17.6% of Jinyoung's roughly KRW 32.4 billion consolidated revenue in 2025.

The company is pursuing commercialization of its plant technology by expanding from in-house pyrolysis-oil production into equipment supply.

During 2026 it also plans to add production lines beyond Korea Eco Energy's existing facility through the newly established subsidiary Jinyoung Eco Energy, which has taken over a plant site and permits in Nonsan, South Chungcheong Province.

On the financing side, Jinyoung issued an KRW 8 billion convertible bond in April 2026 for operating capital and debt repayment, and in August listed 491,641 new shares (issued at KRW 1,017 each) from a third-party share placement.

In September it completed a par-value consolidation from KRW 100 to KRW 500 per share and resumed trading following the re-listing.

As this financing has continued, the debt ratio has climbed from 19.6% in 2023 to 83.1% in 2025, leaving a balance between new-business investment and financial burden as an ongoing challenge.

In its core sheet and film business, the company is working to expand new revenue sources such as industrial film, though the timing of a recovery in domestic and overseas construction activity remains uncertain.

08

Bull factors

Signs of a Turn to Profitability

Consolidated operating profit turned positive in Q2 2026, and gross margin was reported to have improved from 12.8% in Q1 to 18.6% in Q2. Controlling-interest net profit also turned positive at KRW 2.7 billion, bringing the trailing four-quarter sum to KRW 1.71 billion. This is the first quarter to show a clear break from the loss pattern of recent years.

Expansion of the Pyrolysis Oil and Waste-Treatment Business

Korea Eco Energy won its largest-ever equipment supply contract worth KRW 5.7 billion in September 2026, broadening its business beyond in-house production into equipment sales. The company plans to add further pyrolysis production lines through Jinyoung Eco Energy, to be established in 2026. Government support policies for pyrolysis technology are also cited as a favorable backdrop.

Diversification of the Product Portfolio

The company is working to reduce its dependence on the construction cycle by expanding into new revenue sources such as industrial film, beyond its traditional furniture and appliance sheet lines.

Its domestically largest production base of eight sheet lines and four edge-band lines gives it flexibility to shift product mix. Its proprietary ASA-based decorative sheet, co-developed with LG Chem, also maintains brand-level competitiveness.

09

Bear factors

A History of Sustained Losses

The company recorded three consecutive years of operating losses from 2023 through 2025, with controlling-interest net losses persisting over the same period. A single profitable quarter in Q2 2026 is not sufficient to confirm a sustained recovery in profitability. Of the last five quarters, only Q4 2025 and Q2 2026 were profitable.

Rising Financial Burden

The debt ratio rose from 19.6% in 2023 to 83.1% in 2025, and reliance on external funding increased further in 2026 through a convertible bond issuance and a share placement. Conversion of the bond or further new share issuance could act as a future dilution factor.

Uncertainty in End-Market Demand

Continued weakness in domestic and overseas housing and construction activity has kept demand from the furniture and appliance makers that are Jinyoung's main customers subdued. The timing of a recovery in overseas sales, including the Chinese market, also remains uncertain.

10

Risk factors

Execution Risk

As seen in the July 2025 fire at Korea Eco Energy's plant, which halted operations until they normalized in November, early-stage equipment outages or delays could recur in the new businesses. Other subsidiaries such as Necero are also still in an early operational stabilization phase.

Financial Risk

The rising debt ratio and successive rounds of external financing (an KRW 8 billion convertible bond and a share placement) have increased financial leverage. Full-year 2025 operating cash flow turned positive, but it has reportedly remained volatile on a quarterly basis.

Industry-Cycle Risk

The furniture and appliance market is closely tied to domestic and overseas housing transaction volumes, making it highly cycle-sensitive. While expanding remodeling demand is a favorable factor, a delayed construction recovery could slow the rebound in Jinyoung's core business revenue.

12

Overall view

Jinyoung showed a signal of change in Q2 2026, with both consolidated operating profit and controlling-interest net profit turning positive after three consecutive loss years from 2023 through 2025.

Revenue has grown for two straight quarters since the Q4 2025 trough, and subsidiary Korea Eco Energy is attempting to expand beyond pyrolysis-oil production by winning a KRW 5.7 billion equipment supply contract.

Still, the profitable quarter remains a single instance, and the gap between the scale of operating profit and net profit growth in 2026 raises the possibility that one-off items played a role.

The convertible bond issuance, share placement and par-value consolidation have together reshaped the capital structure, while the debt ratio climbed from 19.6% in 2023 to 83.1% in 2025, adding to the financial burden.

The core sheet and film business remains exposed to slowing domestic and overseas construction and furniture demand, so the durability of the recovery is something to verify through the next regular filing and operational indicators from the new businesses.

With new-business expansion and changes in financial structure proceeding in parallel, how these two trends interact going forward is the key point to watch.
